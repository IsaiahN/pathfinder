## Two things in the Medium article that bear directly on the GR/QM pitch

1. **"Three mappings offered and declined"** is the article's own precedent for refusing a mapping
   that resembles the framework without aligning with it. Bayesian updating was declined because
   *"there is no prior, no likelihood and no posterior anywhere in the mechanism."* The GR/QM
   mapping in the draft email has the same problem unless provenance is shown. Cite this section
   when explaining why the mapping is not being asserted.
2. **The Bekenstein/Hawking/'t Hooft/Susskind entry is tagged *an instance*** — *"a case the
   framework describes, arrived at independently by someone else"* — and the article says the
   triangulation claim *"fits four cases already known and has not been tested forward."* So
   black-hole thermodynamics is a **retrodiction**, not a result the framework produced. Do not
   present it as a derivation.

## Distribution: offer, do not push

Figure-grounded reasons:

- **Fig 4 :22–25** — *"Only methods cross up. Only head starts come down. A recording carried upward looks like knowledge and is a description of one occasion."* Sending the full architecture before anyone has described a residual sends a recording, not a generator. Lead with the refuter and the shape claim; offer the links after.
- **Fig 10 :42** — *"A verifier that can reconstruct the claim cannot verify it."* Seat 4 "must not hold the interiors." A recipient who has absorbed the entire framework before being asked for a verdict has joined the frame being checked.
- **Fig 9 :27–28** — *"describe it before you go looking, or any frame you pick will seem to fit."* Ask what they would expect to see if the shape claim were true before giving them the article.
- **Seat Map :163** — *"High correlation is disqualifying for a verdict and efficient for a convention."* A mass send selected for people who already agree produces a convention, not corroboration.

**Recommended order in a reply:** (1) the shape claim and its refuter; (2) ask what they would count as a failure; (3) then the three links, with the note that the figures — not the summary — are the authority.

## The two-stage offer (author's clarification, 2026-11-22)

The proposition is now: **(1)** validate the output against the ground, **(2)** only if curious,
read the theory that produced it, **(3)** use the formula recursively on other problems. This is
the right order and it follows from the figures:

- **Stage 1 before stage 2** is Fig 10 :52 — *"Bring a characterised residual, never the source"* —
  and Fig 2 :45 — *"Frames range against the ground. They do not negotiate with it."* The recipient
  checks the output without holding the framework's interior.
- **Stage 2 is opt-in by construction.** Seat 4 must not hold the interiors; once a recipient has
  absorbed the theory, their agreement stops being evidence. So their verdict on stage 1 must be
  recorded *before* any theory is shown, and their later agreement is never pooled as corroboration
  (Fig 2 :40 — *"agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool"*).
- **Stage 3 sends a method, which is permitted.** Fig 4 :22 — *"Only methods cross up. Only head
  starts come down."* The formula is a generator, so giving it away does not give away an answer.
- **Stage 3 still needs a ground per problem.** `THE_FORMULA.md` "Direction": a frame *"cannot check
  the direction of its own question, because the shape a frame is searching for is not a term it
  holds."* The Medium article's own warning: outputs *"could never be verified if the solutions
  presented were not contrived to fit the formula."* So each user must write down what would refute
  their result before running it, and label the mode of what they get.

**Hard precondition:** stages 1–3 only work if the stage-1 output survives. As currently drafted it
does not (F1–F3 in `01_figure_grounded_audit.md`: it joins two states directly, uses a glyph the
figures do not define, and adds an ordering the figure disclaims). Repair before sending.
