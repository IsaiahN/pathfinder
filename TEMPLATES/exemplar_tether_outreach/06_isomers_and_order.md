### Q2 — Are the two cores first-order?

- Fig 13: *"Everything else is a composition across the two kinds, never within one."*
- A first-order molecule is `state → structure → state`, one crossing.
- Fig 13: *"A gradient over space and one over time are different terms, because which state it is
  bonded to is part of what it is."* ⇒ at least two distinct first-order gradient molecules are
  licensed by the figure itself.
- GR core `Space → Gradient → Time`: first-order, legal under Rule 1.
- QM core `Energy → Spectrum → Time`: first-order, legal under Rule 1.
- Physics check (not a figure line): in QM, time is a parameter and the Hamiltonian generates time
  evolution — a gradient-over-time is also present. So QM may require **two** first-order molecules,
  not one. Mark `specified`, unmeasured. Triangulated for legality; single-source for the physics.

### Q3 — Are they isomers?

- Fig 12 :117: **"isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is
  invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."**
- Fig 12 :124: **"functional group. A substructure whose presence fixes the outcome. The group says
  what kind; the qualifier says which member."**
- As the record writes them — GR `{Space, Gradient, Time}`, QM `{Energy, Spectrum, Time}` — the two
  share **only Time**. By Fig 12's definition they are **not isomers**; they are two members of the
  same **functional group** (`state → structure → state`) with different atoms in the slots.
- The stronger version: if both theories are written over the **full shared atom set**
  `{Space, Time, Energy, Gradient, Spectrum, Network}`, differing only in which structure mediates
  which state pair, then they **are** isomers — same atoms, different arrangement, different
  substance. That is the claim worth testing.
- **Falsifier, written before the run:** if either theory can be written without an atom the other
  requires (e.g. QM with no Space, or GR with no Spectrum), the isomer claim fails and the
  functional-group reading stands instead.

### Q4 — What order is the union?

- Fig 12 :104: **"A settled molecule becomes an atom for whatever composes over it. So irreducible is
  a fact about a level and never about the world."**
- GR and QM are each settled against the ground (decades of experiment). Each can therefore serve as
  an atom at the next level.
- Their comparison ⋛ — the record's Threshold claim — is thus a **second-order composition**:
  `(settled GR molecule) ⋛ (settled QM molecule)`. It is legal only *because* both first-order
  molecules settled. This is the recursion Fig 12 names, not an import.
- Type caveat, still open: the Operators table defines ⋛ as *"a threshold relation between two
  quantities."* At second order the operands are molecules-now-atoms; level-relativity permits the
  type only if they count as quantities at that level. This needs an instrument, not an argument.
  Triangulated for the recursion; strained-for-now on the operand type.

## CONCLUSION

The two cores are **first-order derivatives** of the six-term set. Their union is **second-order**,
and is licensed by Fig 12's settled-molecule rule rather than by any new primitive. On the isomer
question the record as written fails the figure's definition (only Time is shared), but the stronger
form — both theories over the full shared atom set, arranged differently — is a real isomer claim
with a stated falsifier.

This framing **rescues** what was defensible in the record without the part that failed: the order
and the isomer test need no new primitive, no import that moves the wall, and no naming of a seventh
term. `metric tensor = Gradient` remains an unproven binding; `GR and QM are first-order isomers over
a shared atom set` is a checkable structural claim.
