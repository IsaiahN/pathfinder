## QUESTION

Is life likely to emerge wherever conditions are suitable, or could its origin require a very rare chain of events? Restated in effect: with one observed site and a prediction that conditions of kind C yield life, is the residual a high-rate outcome of a closure or the mark of an atom that closure lacks? Option A leads to convergent repeatable arrival and makes a second origin probable. Option B leads to contingency governed by the arrival rate of a missing element. The sought-for shape is a number with a named discriminator; the figures return the discriminator and withhold the number.

## DOMAIN CENSUS

R by arity, symmetry and scale, then one line per domain giving actors, currency and anchor, partition or union, and order by dependency.

- **R, the residual.** Arity: one observed instance indexed by site, asking for a distribution. Fig 1 :13 "a global R near zero with one live slot residual is a legal state." Symmetry: site predicates are exchangeable, but the question is a threshold comparison over counts while the chain is a production sequence, so two different bonds are in play. Scale: molecular replication, protocell assembly, planetary habitat, and the inference across them.
- **D1, prebiotic chemistry.** Actors: molecules, mineral surfaces, free energy inflows, gradients, cycles. Currency: reaction flux, composition, concentration. Anchor: experiment and laboratory synthesis. Admitted.
- **D2, evolutionary and habitat.** Actors: replicators, lineages, environments, relations. Currency: survival, reproduction, cascade. Anchor: the fossil and genomic record. Admitted. Consumes D1 because a replicator must exist before selection can run.
- **D3, observation and inference.** Actors: observers, instruments, sites. Currency: detections and non-detections. Anchor: the actual count of independent origins. Admitted.
- **X1, free-floating origin probability.** Actors: rate parameters. Currency: numbers. Anchor: none nameable for the origin term. Not a domain yet. The public literature records estimates for the Drake origin term spanning "many hundreds of orders of magnitude".
- **X2, a vital organising principle supervening on chemistry.** No nameable anchor, absorbed into D1 and D2 by Fig 11 :39 "So the answerable question is not what intelligence is, but what makes a system capable. And capability is a property of agent-and-habitat, never of the agent alone."

**Union, not partition.** D1, D2 and D3 are ordered by dependency, and the phrase conditions suitable is itself a bundle of overlapping conditions (Fig 9 :55). The whole is a union of arrival and prevalence, with a separate inference frame on top. **Order by dependency:** enumerate the habitat before naming conditions; settle arrival before selection; describe the residual before choosing a frame; refuse before mint.

**Strain test applied.** Convergent evolution appears to say arrival is likely, while Fig 11 :36 says selection runs only after a lineage exists. Re-census assigns convergence to D2 and the origin to D1, so the two figures govern different objects. No contradiction remains.

## VERDICT (from the figures)

The either/or is refused as posed. The figures return a shape with a hole. Life is either a reachable arrangement inside the closure that suitable conditions supply, so that search under the same conditions can find it and different routes can converge on the same functional result, or it requires an atom that closure lacks, so that no amount of search adds it and the rate is set by import. Closure and import are different question types with different currencies and are never averaged. The condition set is a union, arrival is unmaintained and precedes selection, the habitat is enumerated rather than composed, and non-detection is not rarity. The number is a declared `gap` of the no-anchor kind. Mode `specified` on the structure, `general` on the shape, not `grounded`. Verdicts per domain are kept separate and never averaged.

- **cost if wrong:** a misdirected search and a false symmetry. If the import case holds and we treat it as a search problem, we spend decades increasing observation time inside a closure that cannot produce the required atom. If the closure case holds and we treat it as an import problem, we invent a rare chain that was never needed and price a wrong atom above starting from primitives. The outreach cost is also real: asking for agreement before a verdict spends the independence of the only people who could refute the shape. Both costs are bounded because the refuters and the lineage are recorded.
- **undoable:** yes. Nothing is deleted if the claim fails. A refutation is added to the record and the reach falls because something was added rather than because anything was struck out. The shape, its six falsifiers and its provenance are in `01` and `08`, so a later reader can locate the error rather than only feel it. A `gap` can be closed by an instrument without withdrawing the structural findings.
- **reversed by:** any of the six pre-registered refuters. Most decisively: an instrument returning an independent-origin rate over a defined ensemble would close X1; a fixed fully inventoried closure producing a new atom under longer search would break idempotence; a natural pre-replicator mechanism in which differential persistence supplies a missing atom would collapse the D1/D2 boundary; one condition moving the rate across every intersection would dissolve the union; a sealed and completely instrumented search proving absence would break Fig 8 :39; and a measured second origin with a discriminated pathway would replace the analogy with a reading. Agreement, prestige and the number of publications are not among the reversing conditions.
