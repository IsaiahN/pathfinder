## 1. A shape is a constraint with a hole in it

The canonical README says the framework returns *"what a missing thing would have to look like (a shape, not a name)."* Fig 9 :32 *"you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself"* says the same thing inside the figures. The Library of Babel essay gives the operational form: *"you can cut your search short if the set of books returned is small and descriptive enough that it provides a general shape of the answer that satisfies your question."*

For ORI-0093 the shape is:

> Life is either an arrangement reachable from the atoms a closure supplies, or it requires an atom the closure lacks. The first case is governed by search inside the closure. The second is governed by import. `P(L | C)` is not returned.

The hole is the number and the identity of any missing atom. A shape with a hole is still an answer about what a candidate answer must look like. It is not an estimate with the digits removed.

## 2. How the shape is obtained

- **Narrow by proximity.** Library of Babel: use the vocabulary already closest to the thing, then *"With each new term gained, you narrow the space and deepen your vocabulary."* Here the near vocabulary is closure, atom, arrangement, import and habitat, not probability.
- **Describe the effect, not the cause.** Fig 6 :30 *"The description travels. Fails by being sharpened into terms only we can read. Cast it in effect terms."* So the rare chain is described as *an element outside the closure that must arrive*, not named as a particular isotope, mineral or cycle.
- **Bind the sought-for shape as an ordinary term.** The formula's Direction paragraph: a frame cannot check the direction of its own question because the shape is a preference, not a term. Writing down what an answer would look like, as section 0 of `01_figure_grounded_audit.md` does, makes the preference refusable.
- **Stop when the finish line is the starting place.** Library of Babel: *"You will know that you are done because the finish line will also be the starting place."* Here the stop occurs when the same four constraints, closure, import, unbundled condition set and unmaintained arrival, keep returning and no new constraint appears.

## 3. The union error is part of the shape

Library of Babel: *"Perhaps your problem was a bundle or conflation of several problems masquerading as one?"* The question's phrase *conditions suitable* is exactly such a bundle. Fig 9 :55 *"A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones."* So the shape includes a structural refusal of the premise. A shape that accepted the premise would be fitting the question instead of answering it.

## 4. What licenses a name

Only two things, both outside the shape itself.

1. **The ground settles it.** Fig 2 :29 defines the anchor as *"A measurement, an experiment, a counter, a market, a patient outcome."* The Recursive Self-Improvement Trap says *"the output is only as good as the validator. The agent, or the tool, or the frame, or the theory, cannot validate itself."* A second independent origin, or a laboratory demonstration that a fixed closure repeatedly produces life, is such a validator. Agreement among astrobiologists is not.
2. **A provenanced import from a differing closure.** Fig 6 :23 *"No frame escapes its own closure. The bound moves only on contact with a frame whose closure differs."* If a requirement such as wet-dry cycling is named, the record must show where the name came from and which gap it closes.

The Hard Problem is About Source gives the discriminator rule. Metamerism: an instrument reads green and *"cannot comment on the source."* The essay's conclusion is *"The answer is unreached until a better instrument exists."* For this question the instrument would have to distinguish a closure case from an import case at a site. A count of habitable planets cannot do it; neither can a laboratory synthesis alone, because a successful synthesis shows reachability, not the natural rate.

## 5. Correction to my own first reading

I initially wrote that the figures must return either a high or a low probability and that failing to do so was a `gap` of the whole question. That overstated the case. The figures refuse the premise as posed, but they do return a determinate structural result: closure and import are different question types, arrival precedes selection, and the condition set is a union. So the honest verdict is two verdicts kept separate: `gap` on the number, `specified` on the structure. Both are recorded, and neither is silently erased. The same correction is in `01_figure_grounded_audit.md` section 7.

A second correction: the *shape, not a name* rule governs a missing primitive, not an already-held binding. Naming a condition already present in the chemistry frame, such as a gradient or a mineral surface, is provenance. It becomes forbidden only when it is presented as the framework's own derivation of the missing atom. That narrowing is carried from the exemplar's own retraction and applies here unchanged.

## 6. Two consequences for this question

**Prestige is not action.** The Recursive Self-Improvement Trap: *"Prestige can fund an action. It can give an action a budget to be tried. But it cannot settle an action. Only the ground can settle it."* A survey of eminent scientists on whether life is common funds the question; it does not answer it. Public contact points in `03_public_contact_points.csv` were chosen as places where a claim can be tried and refused, not as name lists.

**My own audit is a simulation, not a ground reading.** Same essay: LLMs *"can quickly simulate an approximate answer/output with the formula"* but *"the truly testable output would need to be derived from the real architecture or working the formula yourself."* So `01_figure_grounded_audit.md` is a simulated derivation checked for internal consistency against the figures. It is not a test of whether life is common. It cannot settle anything it reports on.
