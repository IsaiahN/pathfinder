# Expert verification packet, ORI-0093

**What this is:** a claim in a form an origins-of-life researcher or astrobiologist can check. Mode, derivation and refuters are stated before the conclusion, so the reader knows what to look for and what would settle it.

**Mode: `specified` on the structure, `general` on the shape, `gap` on the number. Not `grounded`.** Simulated derivation checked for internal consistency against the figures, not a ground reading.

## 1. The claim, in one paragraph

The question's either/or is a category mistake. *Likely* is a threshold comparison; *rare chain* asserts a production sequence requiring an import. The figures return that life is either an arrangement reachable from the atoms a closure supplies, governed by search, or an outcome requiring an atom that closure lacks, governed by import. These have different currencies and different grounds and cannot be averaged into one probability. The condition set is a union of overlapping conditions, so a single lever will not clear it. Arrival is unmaintained and precedes selection, so convergent evolution cannot be projected back to the origin. Non-detection is not rarity. No instrument currently returns an independent-origin rate, so the number is a declared `gap` with a stated discriminator.

## 2. Three checkable sub-claims

### C1, framework-internal; checkable by reading Figures 13, 8 and 6

- **Statement:** closure and import are two question types, not two values of one rate. Under a fixed closure, longer search cannot add a missing atom.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 13 :115 *"The set is closed. The arrangements are not. That gap is why import exists."* Fig 8 :32 *"Closure is idempotent. Composing inside a closed set never leaves it, so no search, however long, adds an atom."* Fig 6 :23 *"No frame escapes its own closure. The bound moves only on contact with a frame whose closure differs."*
- **Refuter:** a fixed, fully inventoried closure under extended search produces a genuinely new atom; or a single instrument returns an independent-origin rate over a defined ensemble and the two cases are shown to be one rate after all.

### C2, chemistry and astrobiology facing; checkable against the origin literature

- **Statement:** the condition set is a union, and arrival precedes selection. Convergent evolution is evidence about the second, not the first.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 9 :55 *"A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones."* Fig 11 :56 *"Unmaintained. No maintainer and no live frame in the seat. The habitat is pure contact, nothing selects, and what survives is what the ground kept."* Fig 11 :36 *"Where nothing maintains anything, intelligence is a label applied afterwards to a lineage that met its ground. Nothing was intelligent; selection ran."*
- **What to check:** does any current origin model require differential persistence before a heritable replicator exists? If so, the D1/D2 boundary drawn here is wrong. Are the listed suitable conditions separable, or do they overlap so that changing one changes the others? If separable, the union finding weakens.
- **Refuter:** a demonstrated natural pre-replicator mechanism in which selection-like differential persistence supplies the missing atom; or one manipulable condition that moves the origin rate across every intersection of the bundle.

### C3, inference facing; checkable by an observational astronomer or statistician

- **Statement:** the origin rate has no anchor, so a universal probability is a gap rather than an estimate; non-detection cannot select the rare-chain option.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 2 :29 *"An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks. A measurement, an experiment, a counter, a market, a patient outcome."* Fig 8 :39 *"So a search that finds nothing is never a proof of absence."* Fig 1 :19 *"The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated."*
- **What to check:** is there any current instrument that returns an independent-origin count over a defined ensemble? If yes, the gap is closed. Is the per-slot indexing claim compatible with the way exoplanet occurrence rates are actually estimated? If occurrence-rate methods already provide a per-slot residual for this quantity, the objection is answered and the verdict changes.
- **Refuter:** a sealed and completely instrumented search space in which non-detection proves absence; or an ensemble estimate that binds `f_l` without the overconfidence the public literature records.

## 3. What is explicitly not claimed

- Not a probability, not a bound, not a bet on common or rare.
- Not that any named ingredient, mineral, cycle or environment is the missing atom.
- Not that convergent evolution makes abiogenesis likely.
- Not that Earth's single origin was typical.
- Not that the absence of a second origin is evidence of rarity.
- Not grounded, and not a claim that the number is unknowable in principle. It is unreached at this instrument set.

## 5. What would settle it

A source-discriminating instrument, not a further argument.

1. A second independent origin with ancestry determined, which doubles the sample and starts an actual rate estimate.
2. A repeated laboratory closure test showing whether arrival is reachable under a fully inventoried atom set.
3. An instrument that distinguishes a closure pathway from an import pathway at a site rather than reading the end state and naming a source from a projection.
4. A natural pre-replicator mechanism in which differential persistence supplies the missing atom, which would collapse the arrival boundary.

## 6. What an expert can check in twenty minutes

1. Read Fig 13 :115, Fig 8 :32, Fig 9 :55, Fig 11 :36 and :56, Fig 2 :29 and Fig 8 :39. Seven short passages.
2. Check C2 against their own field: is there a pre-replicator selection mechanism they would accept, and are the suitable conditions separable?
3. Name one instrument that would distinguish a closure pathway from an import pathway at a site, or say why none is possible.
4. Send the refutation. A failure is more useful than an endorsement.
