**Use:** one-to-one, or as a cover letter attached to a submission through the public venues in `03_public_contact_points.csv`. **Not** for bulk send.

**Mode of everything below:** `specified` on the structure, `gap` on the number, not `grounded`. This is a simulated derivation checked for internal consistency against the figures, not a ground reading.

**Subject:** One checkable output on the probability of life emerging

> Hi [name],
>
> I have one specific output I would like you to check against what you already know. I am not asking you to read the framework first, and I am not asking you to agree with it. If the output does not survive, there is nothing else to look at.
>
> **The output (`specified`, not `grounded`).** The question asks whether life is likely wherever conditions are suitable, or whether its origin needs a rare chain. The framework returns the same structural split from two different routes. Under one route, life is an arrangement reachable from the atoms a set of conditions supplies. Under the other, life needs an atom that set does not supply. These are not two values of one probability. They have different currencies and different grounds: the first is governed by search inside a closure, the second by the arrival rate of an import. Fig 13 :115 says *"The set is closed. The arrangements are not. That gap is why import exists."* Fig 8 :32 says *"Closure is idempotent. Composing inside a closed set never leaves it, so no search, however long, adds an atom."*
>
> Three consequences follow, and they are the part I would like checked.
>
> 1. **The number is a gap, not an estimate.** Fig 2 :29: *"An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks. A measurement, an experiment, a counter, a market, a patient outcome."* Nobody has an instrument that returns an independent-origin rate. The public literature records estimates spanning hundreds of orders of magnitude for the Drake origin term. So I report `gap`. I am not claiming life is common or rare.
> 2. **The premise is a bundle.** Fig 9 :55: *"A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones."* Liquid water, free energy, building blocks, concentration mechanisms and mineral surfaces are overlapping conditions, not a single switch. Finding one kind of habitable planet does not clear the intersections.
> 3. **Arrival is not selection.** Fig 11 :36: *"Where nothing maintains anything, intelligence is a label applied afterwards to a lineage that met its ground. Nothing was intelligent; selection ran."* Convergent evolution explains what happens after a replicator exists. It cannot be used as evidence that the origin transition itself is likely. In a laboratory a seat exists and arrival is worked on; on a young planet there is no seat.
>
> **What I am not claiming.** I am not claiming a number, not claiming that abiogenesis is easy or hard, not claiming to have identified the missing atom if one exists, and not claiming that non-detection elsewhere shows rarity. Fig 8 :39 says *"So a search that finds nothing is never a proof of absence."* If I name a specific requirement, I will record where that name came from and which gap it explains. Until then the slot stays marked.
>
> **What would refute the shape claim.** I wrote these before sending this:
> 1. An instrument returns an independent-origin rate over a defined ensemble of sites, so the `gap` closes.
> 2. A fixed, fully inventoried closure under longer search produces a genuinely new atom.
> 3. A natural pre-replicator mechanism is demonstrated in which differential persistence supplies the missing atom, so selection can work before a replicator exists.
> 4. One manipulable condition is shown to move the origin rate across every intersection of the bundle.
> 5. A sealed and completely instrumented search space is found, and non-detection in it proves absence.
>
> If any one holds, the shape is wrong and I will retract it. A one-line "this fails because..." is more useful to me than agreement. If you would rather not engage, no reply is needed.
