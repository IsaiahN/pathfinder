# Union, order and isomers in the conservation question

**Mode: `specified` on the structural claims, `general` on the bond and order rules.** This is a simulated derivation checked against the figures, not a ground reading (see `05_what_shape_means.md` section 7).

## RESIDUAL

The program must act so that a biodiversity ground improves and a rights-and-knowledge ground is not overridden. The question supplies two conditions with different anchors. Two supporting questions are owed: (1) what **order** is licensed among enumerate, refuse, prescribe and settle; and (2) are the two familiar program forms, exclusion and co-management, **isomers** over a shared atom set, or different functional groups?

## DERIVATION

### Q1 - Union, not a partition

Fig 9 :55: *"A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones. A single lever clears the region where only one condition holds and leaves the intersections standing, because the second condition still binds."*
Fig 9 :57: *"a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either"*

The biodiversity-only region and the rights-only region can each be cleared by a single lever. Their intersection cannot. That is why "maximise protection, then add safeguards" is a sequence that leaves the compound standing. Union status: `specified`, triangulated across Fig 9 and Fig 2 :36-38.

**Falsifier:** one lever that clears both conditions in an overlapping case with both grounds independently registered.

### Q2 - The order is decided by dependency, not by preference

Fig 12 :111-113: *"A vocabulary without bonds can name a state and cannot name a route to one. A plan is a sequence. A goal is a comparison. Progress is a subtraction. A refutation is a negation, and that one is not a bond."*
Fig 12 :70-74 (Operators :29-33): the sequence bond `→` means *"A then B; B needs A to have happened"*, with the test *"swap them. Does it become a different thing? Yes."*
Fig 11 :43: *"The habitat is enumerated, never composed."*
Fig 9 :27-28: *"describe it before you go looking, or any frame you pick will seem to fit"*

The dependency order is therefore:

1. **enumerate** the habitat and the people in contact with it (Fig 11 :43-44);
2. **describe** the residual in effect terms before choosing a frame (Fig 9 :27-28);
3. **refuse** the term that is wrong before minting the prescription (Formula step 2, Operators :58 *"a refutation is a negation, which is why a vocabulary without it can only accumulate"*);
4. **mint** the governance prescription only if support, reachability and novelty pass (Formula step 3);
5. **settle** each site slot against both grounds (Fig 1 :19);
6. **compose** the regional or programme claim only from settled molecules (Fig 12 :104 *"A settled molecule becomes an atom for whatever composes over it"*).

The FPIC term *"prior"* is literally a `→` constraint: consent must happen before authorisation. The Wikipedia FPIC page quotes the FAO definition that *"Prior implies that consent is sought sufficiently in advance of any activities being either commenced or authorised."* A program that asks for consent after the boundary is drawn has the same atoms in a different order, therefore a different term, not a minor variant of the same one.

**Falsifier:** if prior and post-hoc consent are behaviourally indistinguishable at the ground, with the program's own stake removed from the reading, the sequence-bond reading fails.

### Q3 - Is exclusion and co-management an isomer pair?

Fig 12 :117: *"isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."*
Fig 12 :123: *"functional group. A substructure whose presence fixes the outcome. The group says what kind; the qualifier says which member."*
Fig 12 :5: *"A set of ingredients is not one. The bond is the rest of the information, and it is the half nothing was recording."*

Written over the same atom set `{habitat, actors, conditions, relations, rules in use, monitor, benefit, ground reading}`, the two program forms differ by bond:

| arrangement | bond pattern | figure reading |
|---|---|---|
| fortress / exclusion | maintainer seat bonded to a substituted habitat; people bonded by subtraction `-`; monitor inside the program | an isomer of the same atoms if the atom set really is shared; Fig 12 :117 makes the arrangement, not the ingredient list, the substance |
| co-management | maintainer seat bonded to the enumerated habitat; people bonded by conjunction `+` and by consent `¬` where they refuse; monitor shared | same atoms, different bond, different substance |

This is a `specified` isomer claim with a condition: it holds only if both forms can be written without an atom the other does not require. If exclusion needs coercion as a primitive that co-management does not have, the pair is **not** an isomer; they are two members of a functional group `maintainer + habitat + rules + ground` with different qualifiers (Fig 12 :123). The weaker functional-group reading then stands, and the stronger isomer reading is withdrawn.

World corroboration is careful rather than enthusiastic. Berkes 2007 states that *"community-based conservation as a panacea, like government-based conservation as a panacea, ignores the necessity of managing commons at multiple levels, with vertical and horizontal interplay among institutions"* and that *"the false dichotomy of the state vs. the community can be discarded."* That is a functional-group claim about a shared structure, not a claim that the two forms are interchangeable.

**Falsifier:** exhibit one form that requires an atom absent from the other, or show that the two forms are behaviourally identical over the same ground.

### Q4 - "Consult then decide" versus "decide then consult"

Same atoms, ordered bond `→`, different term (Fig 12 :70-74). This is the cleanest of the three order claims because it needs no new atom:

- **consult → decide:** the refusal term is admitted before mint; the prescription is built under the possibility of being refused;
- **decide → consult:** the prescription is built first; consultation is a report about the already-chosen outcome.

Fig 12 :48-49: *"A chain is the degenerate case: one bond type, no branching. Nothing about a chain is a different kind of object."* So the difference is not a new kind of object, it is a different arrangement of one. Fig 2 :34 warns that when the reference updates on the frames, *"everything drifts together, and the drift is coherent, which is why nobody notices"*; under decide-then-consult, the consultation becomes another frame that updates on the decision.

**Falsifier:** a randomized comparison at matched sites in which the two orders return the same distribution of both grounds, with independent measurement of each.

### Q5 - Structures that matter: catalyst, cycle and functional group

Fig 12 :119: *"catalyst. A part that survives its own use. Spent and regenerating parts behave differently across uses, and nothing records which a term is."* Local monitors who keep their standing after a monitoring round are catalysts; monitors whose authority is spent by enforcing one rule are spent parts. The figure says nothing records which a term is, so a program must record it explicitly.

Fig 12 :121: *"cycle. The output re-enters as input. The sign of the loop decides whether it closes, and a chain cannot close by construction."* A harvest rule whose monitoring output re-enters as the next season's input is a cycle. A program that collects monitoring data for a donor report is a chain, which cannot close.

Fig 12 :123 (above) gives the functional group that both program forms share.

**Falsifier:** a program in which a spent part behaves like a catalyst across repeated uses with no change in standing, or a chain that closes without its output re-entering as input.

### Q6 - Two currencies, not one effectiveness score

Fig 12 :125-127: *"A composition costs in two currencies, and they do not add. Bits price the description. Actions price finding out whether it holds."*
Fig 9 :60: *"Use filters for the budget and witnesses for the verdict. Never let a filter hand you a verdict."*

A simple governance rule is cheap in bits and may be expensive in actions at a remote site. An elaborate rule is expensive in bits and may be cheap to test. The two readings come apart in both directions, so no single "program effectiveness" number is legitimate. A budget filter decides where to try; it never decides whether the two conditions were met.

**Falsifier:** a single scalar that predicts both description cost and action cost across sites without losing the direction of either.

## CONCLUSION

The licensed order is enumerate, describe, refuse, mint, settle per slot, then compose. Prior consent is a `→` constraint, not an adjective, and reversing the order produces a different term. The two familiar program forms are isomers only under a shared-atom condition that the world literature does not yet settle; the defensible reading is that they share a functional group and differ by bond. Local monitors divide into catalysts and spent parts, and harvest monitoring is a cycle only if its output re-enters as input. Description cost and action cost stay in two columns. None of this names a site arrangement; the `gap` in F11 of `01_figure_grounded_audit.md` remains.
