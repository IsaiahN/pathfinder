## QUESTION

Decide, in effect terms, what the Tether says about a conservation program that must bring about two outcomes. Effect A: a biodiversity ground registers improvement that does not move when the program rewrites its own report. Effect B: the rights and knowledge of local communities are not overridden, displaced or used without standing, with human judgment as a legitimate anchor because the question is about people. Option A: exclusion, which removes people from the protected area. Option B: maintained habitat with two independent knowledge frames and a refusal seat. What each leads to: A substitutes a habitat and inherits actors it did not choose, with two silent failures, plus a severed rights ground. B returns a checkable shape with per-slot verdicts and pre-registered refuters, but no named arrangement, boundary or allocation.

## DOMAIN CENSUS

R by arity, symmetry and scale, then one line per domain giving actors, currency and anchor, partition or union, and order by dependency.

- **R (the residual).** Arity: two conditions. Symmetry: not symmetric, because the biodiversity anchor is an instrument and the rights anchor is human judgment. Scale: site slot, region and global commons at once. Fig 9 :57 says *"a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either"*, so this is a union compound with its own loop, not one dial.
- **Domain 1, habitat and biodiversity.** Actors: species, habitats, conditions of light, water, season and terrain, relations of pollination, predation and competition, and the program as maintainer seat. Currency: contact and settlement, indexed per site slot. Anchor: a measured biodiversity outcome that does not update on what the program thinks. Question type: partition boundary, which compositions of actors and conditions are possible.
- **Domain 2, livelihoods and rights.** Actors: local communities, customary institutions, knowledge holders, program staff, courts and consent procedures. Currency: rights, consent, benefit distribution, trust and displacement cost. Anchor: human judgment about rights, legitimate because the question is constitutively about people. Question type: union, the rights condition binds in the intersection with D1.
- **Domain 3, knowledge and epistemics.** Actors: formal conservation science, local and traditional ecological knowledge, monitoring instruments. Currency: predictions, residuals and provenance. Anchor: the D1 ground for ecological claims and the D2 anchor for rights claims, never the frames themselves. Question type: union requiring two low-overlap frames. If they share one evidence pool they are one frame wearing two names.
- **Domain 4, verification and outreach.** Actors: expert readers in conservation and livelihoods research. Currency: agreement and residual. Anchor: the ground, never agreement. In this simulated run all seats draw on one evidence pool, so this domain's independence is zero and its agreement is internal consistency.
- **Order by dependency.** Enumerate the habitat before choosing a lever (Fig 11 :44). Describe the residual before naming a frame (Fig 9 :27-28). Admit refusal before mint (Formula step 2). Settle each site slot before composing a regional claim (Fig 12 :104).

Strain test applied: Fig 11 :61 says exclusion is unavailable while states legally exclude people. Re-census resolves it: the figure governs contact and habitat, not legal permission; a legal exclusion substitutes a habitat and does not delete contact. No figure line needed a strained reading.

## VERDICT (from the figures)

The shape survives; a site arrangement does not. The question is a union of two conditions with different anchors, so verdicts stay per site and per condition and are never averaged. Enumerate the habitat with people in it; treat their valence as residual-relative; treat exclusion as habitat substitution with two silent failures; keep formal science and local knowledge as two independent frames with recorded provenance; install consent as a prior refusal term; transfer a method rather than a recipe. The framework returns no governance form, boundary or benefit split, and that absence is reported as `unreachable`, not silence. Every substantive claim is `specified`, ungrounded and `candidate`.

- **cost if wrong:** bounded and locatable, because the packet states mode, sub-claims and refuters before the conclusion. The larger avoidable cost is spending an outside reader's independence by sending the whole framework before their stage-1 verdict. Fig 2 :42 says "Buy independence in proportion to the cost of being wrong." That cost is paid by the sender, not by the ground.
- **undoable:** yes. Any of the three sub-claims can be corrected without withdrawing the union structure, and a site verdict can be superseded by a measured one. No primitive has to be invented or removed, and a refused term never leaves the record.
- **reversed by:** a ground reading, not agreement. An independent biodiversity instrument that does not update on the program's report, an independent rights judgment with a refusal seat the program did not author, provenance separating the two knowledge frames, or a source-discriminating reading that separates consent from co-option would all move the verdict. A matched-site result in which prior and post-hoc consent behave identically on both grounds would break C3. An exclusion program with no substitution failures would break C1. Two overlapping frames returning the same surplus would break C2. Until one of these fires, the verdict stands as `candidate`.
