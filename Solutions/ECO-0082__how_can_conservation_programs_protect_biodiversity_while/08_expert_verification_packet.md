# Expert verification packet - the Tether on conservation, rights and local knowledge

**What this is:** a claim in a form a conservation or livelihoods researcher can check. Mode, derivation and refuters are stated before the conclusion, so a reader knows what to look for and what would settle it.

**Mode: `specified` on the three sub-claims, `general` on the structural union, `gap` on the named arrangement. Not `grounded`.** No site measurement is claimed.

**Standing:** a simulated derivation checked for internal consistency against the figures, not a ground reading. It cannot settle the question it reports on.

## 1. The claim, in one paragraph

A conservation program faces two conditions with different anchors, so the question is a union compound and verdicts must stay per site slot and per condition. The habitat must be enumerated with local people, conditions and second-order contacts in it; no standing friend or enemy list survives a change of residual. Excluding people does not remove them from the habitat but substitutes another one, with two silent failures: what the goal required is not reproduced and what arrives is not chosen. Formal conservation science and local knowledge are two frames whose surplus exists only where their evidence pools differ, so merging them into one project report destroys the very thing that makes agreement informative. Consent is a negation term that must be admitted before the prescription, not a participation score written afterwards. The framework returns that shape and a set of pre-registered refuters; it does not name a governance arrangement, a boundary or a benefit split.

## 2. Three checkable sub-claims

### C1 - framework-internal plus ecology-facing; checkable by reading Fig 11 and a habitat census

- **Statement:** the habitat is enumerated, never composed, and local people are actors in it with residual-relative valence. Exclusion is substitution, not removal, and contact cascades beyond a first-order boundary.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 11 :44 *"List everything in contact with the residual, then what is in contact with those, and outward until the cascade stops mattering."* Fig 11 :49 *"An actor's valence is relative to the residual, not a property it carries."* Fig 11 :66 *"Isolation is not removal of the habitat. It is substitution of one habitat for another."* Fig 11 :68 *"what you failed to reproduce is invisible until the goal fails"* and Fig 11 :69 *"what you unintentionally introduced is invisible until it acts"*.
- **What to check:** take one protected area you know. List the human actors and conditions in contact with the biodiversity residual, including what happens to people after a boundary is drawn. Does an exclusion decision actually remove contact, or move it? Which required relations were not reproduced, and which unchosen actors arrived?
- **Refuter:** an exclusion program that reproduces everything the goal required, leaves no displaced pressure and settles the rights claim at a human anchor it did not author.
- **Status:** `specified`, triangulated across Fig 11 and Fig 4 :25 (habitat law and recording-versus-method are different figures with low overlap). World corroboration: Berkes 2007 in *PNAS* on multilevel commons and path dependency, and the documented displacement cases in the opened *Fortress conservation* page. Tertiary pages are leads, not decisive witnesses.

### C2 - epistemics-facing; checkable by comparing two knowledge frames

- **Statement:** the knowledge surplus requires independence. Formal science and local knowledge are triangulated only while they range against their grounds separately; a program-authored report about community views is one evidence pool, not two frames.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 8 :45 *"Two frames together reach what neither reaches alone."* Fig 8 :48 *"How much two frames draw on the same evidence decides both whether their agreement means anything and whether their union has any surplus."* Fig 2 :35 *"Alignment is triangulation, not negotiation. A mirror cannot correct you."* Fig 8 :52 *"Convergent derivation and adopted import look identical in the contents. Only the record of where each came from separates them"*.
- **What to check:** for one site, identify which predictions came from ecological measurement and which from local knowledge. Are the two datasets collected by the same staff under the same incentive? If the program elicits local knowledge and then scores its own report, has the independence been spent?
- **Refuter:** two frames with substantial evidence overlap that return the same surplus, or a program's own report about community views that counts as corroboration at the ground.
- **Status:** `specified`, triangulated across Fig 8 and Fig 2. World corroboration: Berkes 2007, *"The global lens of biodiversity conservation... is therefore different from the local lens on biodiversity (local commons for livelihoods)"*, with the note that differences often reflect the level at which information was obtained. The framework essays agree but share the framework author's pool, so they are not independent witnesses.

### C3 - governance-facing; checkable by checking an actual decision sequence

- **Statement:** consent is a unary refusal operator and must precede authorisation. A post-hoc consultation has the same atoms in a different order and is therefore a different term, not a milder version of the same one. A participation metric is a convention, not a verdict.
- **Source, verbatim:** Operators :58 *"a refutation is a negation, which is why a vocabulary without it can only accumulate"*. Fig 10 :47 *"a convention that decides an outcome has stopped being one"*. Fig 12 :71 *"A then B; B needs A to have happened"*. Fig 10 :59 *"an interest in the outcome is unavoidable, an interest in the reading is disqualifying"*.
- **What to check:** in one program, find the decision at which the boundary or the harvest rule was fixed. Where does refusal sit relative to that decision? Who authors the reading of whether consent was given? Does that author benefit from the outcome?
- **Refuter:** a post-authorisation consultation with the same refusal power as prior consent, with no program interest entering the reading.
- **Status:** `specified`, triangulated across the Operators table (unary negation) and Fig 10 (seat and convention rules). World corroboration: CBD Article 8(j) requires wider application of community knowledge *"with the approval and involvement of the holders"*; the FPIC page quotes UNDRIP Article 10 and states *"mere consultation by itself is not a substitute for actual consent."*

## 3. What is explicitly not claimed

- Not a theory of everything and not a final governance form. The arrangement is a declared `gap` (Fig 9 :32, Fig 13 :134).
- Not that local communities are always benefactors, and not that all top-down protection fails. Fig 11 :49-51 refuses both standing lists.
- Not that participation scores are useless. They are useful as conventions about shared meaning; they stop being conventions when they decide an outcome (Fig 10 :47).
- Not that the two knowledge frames must be combined into one report to be respected. That spends the independence that makes the agreement informative (Fig 8 :48).
- Not that livelihood benefits are compensation after exclusion. They are contact-changing levers (Fig 11 :40).
- Not `grounded`, and not a site measurement.

## 5. What would settle it

A ground reading, not agreement. In order of cheapness:

1. An independent biodiversity reading at each site that does not update on the program's report.
2. An independent rights judgment with a refusal seat the program did not author.
3. Provenance separating which predictions came from which knowledge frame.
4. A source-discriminating reading that separates consent from co-option, elite capture and a program-owned report.
5. A matched-site comparison of prior consent versus post-hoc consultation on both grounds.

The full six-condition refuter list is in `01_figure_grounded_audit.md` section 4 and was written before the conclusion.

## 6. What an expert can check in twenty minutes

1. Read Fig 11 :44, :49, :66, :68-69 and Fig 8 :45, :48, :52. Six short passages.
2. Pick one site you know. Write four lines: who is in contact with the residual, what a fence would move rather than remove, which two frames could disagree, and where refusal sits relative to the decision.
3. Name one instrument that would distinguish consent from co-option at that site, or say why none is available.
4. Send the refutation. A failure is more useful than an endorsement.

**Cost if wrong:** bounded and locatable, because the packet states mode, sub-claims and refuters before the conclusion, so an error can be found rather than absorbed. The larger cost is spending an outside reader's independence by sending them the whole framework before their stage-1 verdict; Fig 2 :42 says *"Buy independence in proportion to the cost of being wrong."*

**Undoable:** yes. The three sub-claims can be corrected without withdrawing the union structure, and a site verdict can be superseded by a measured one. No primitive has to be invented or removed.

**Reversed by:** a ground reading. A matched-site measurement showing that prior and post-hoc consent are equivalent on both grounds would break C3. A program whose exclusion leaves no unselected actors and no displaced pressure would break C1. Two overlapping frames returning the same surplus would break C2. Agreement alone reverses nothing.
