# Outreach draft - check the output first, then decide about the framework

**Use:** one to one, or as a cover letter attached to a submission through the public venues in `03_public_contact_points.csv`. **Not** for bulk send.

**Hard precondition:** do not send until the shape claim and the refuter below both survive the reader's own check. If the stage-1 output does not survive, there is nothing else to look at.

**Standing:** this is a simulated derivation checked for internal consistency against the figures. It is not a ground reading and cannot settle the question it reports on.

**Subject:** Check one claim about conservation and local communities before you decide whether the framework is worth reading

> Hi [name],
>
> I have one specific output I would like you to check against what you already know from conservation and livelihoods work. I am not asking you to read the framework first, and I am not asking you to agree with me. If the output does not survive, there is nothing else to look at.
>
> **The output (`specified`, not `grounded`).** A conservation program faces two conditions with different grounds, not one dial. The biodiversity condition is settled by an outcome that does not move when the program changes its own report. The rights and knowledge condition is legitimately settled by human judgment, because that half of the question is about people. These form a union compound: a lever that clears a species count while displacing people has cleared one condition and left the intersection standing.
>
> The shape claim has five parts. (1) The habitat must be enumerated with people, conditions and second-order contacts in it, not treated as a species list plus a stakeholder row. (2) Local actors have no standing valence: the same community is a benefactor for one residual and a malfactor for another, so no universal friend or enemy list survives. (3) Excluding people does not remove them from the habitat; it substitutes another habitat and inherits actors you did not choose, with two silent failures, what the goal needed that is no longer reproduced and what you brought in that you did not choose. (4) Formal science and local knowledge are two frames whose surplus exists only where their evidence pools differ; merging them into one project report loses that surplus. (5) Consent is a refusal term that must be admitted before the prescription, not a participation score recorded afterwards.
>
> **What I am not claiming.** I am not claiming that community management always works, that all protected areas are invalid, that any one governance arrangement is the answer, or that a figure line names a percentage, a boundary or a benefit split. The framework returns a shape, not a name. Local knowledge is kept independent precisely so it can disagree. Livelihood benefits are contact-changing levers, not compensation paid after exclusion.
>
> **What would refute this, written down before I sent it:**
> 1. a single lever that clears both conditions in an overlapping case, with each ground registered independently;
> 2. an exclusion program that reproduces everything the goal required, inherits no unchosen actors, leaves no displaced pressure and settles the rights claim at a human anchor it did not author;
> 3. two frames drawing on substantially the same evidence that produce the same surplus, or a program's own report about community views that counts as corroboration at the ground;
> 4. a post-authorisation consultation with the same refusal power as prior consent, with no program interest entering the reading;
> 5. a figure line that names a specific governance arrangement or site allocation.
>
> If any one holds, I withdraw that part of the claim. A one-line "this fails because..." is more useful to me than agreement. If you would rather not engage, no reply is needed.
>
> **If it survives, and only then:** the sources are below. The figures, not my summary of them, are the authority.
> - Local figure text with line numbers: `FRAMEWORK/reference/03-FIGURES_TEXT.md` in this project, cited as `Fig N :line`.
> - Berkes, F. 2007, "Community-based conservation in a globalized world", PNAS 104(39):15188-15193, open access: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2000555/
> - Convention on Biological Diversity, Article 8(j) in-situ conservation text: https://www.cbd.int/convention/articles/?a=cbd-08
> - Ecology and Society, open-access journal for resilience and sustainability, submissions: https://ecologyandsociety.org/submissions/
>
> **If you want to use the machinery:** the formula is in the framework's figure folder. Run it on a problem you know well and can check against a ground. Write down what would refute your result *before* you run it, and state the mode of what you get. A reported failure is more useful to me than a reported success.
