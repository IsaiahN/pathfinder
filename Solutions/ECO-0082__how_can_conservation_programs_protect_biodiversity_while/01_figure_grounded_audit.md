## 0. The residual, stated in effect terms

The question asks how a conservation program can bring about two outcomes at once. Stated as effects rather than as a slogan:

- **Effect A (biodiversity ground).** The program acts so that a state of the living world improves in a way that an instrument or a counted outcome can register: populations, interactions, habitat continuity, viability. If the program's own report is the only thing that registers, the channel has failed (Fig 10 :24, *"the ground does not decay, the channel does"*).
- **Effect B (rights and knowledge ground).** The program acts so that the people already in contact with that place are not displaced, overridden, or used as data sources without standing. Because this half of the question is constitutively about people, human judgment is a legitimate anchor for it (Fig 2 :36, *"An anchor is legitimate when the question is constitutively about it."*).

The residual is the gap between these effects and what the program's current library can represent. Two options are compared:

| option | what it does with people | what it leads to |
|---|---|---|
| **A. Exclusion / fortress** | treats local presence as a variable to remove from the protected area | substitutes a habitat, inherits actors it did not choose, severs the local knowledge frame, and moves the rights question out of the program's reach |
| **B. Triangulation / maintained habitat** | enumerates people as actors in the habitat, keeps formal science and local knowledge as two low-overlap frames, and installs a refusal seat before prescribing | returns a checkable shape with per-domain verdicts and written refuters; returns no site-specific allocation or named institution |

R by arity, symmetry and scale:

- **Arity.** Two conditions, not one dial. The question is a **union compound** (Fig 9 :54, *"And when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again."*).
- **Symmetry.** Not symmetric. Effect A is registered by instruments that do not update on what a community thinks. Effect B is registered by human judgment, which does update on reasons and consent. Swapping the two anchors is a type error, not a different policy.
- **Scale.** The site is local; biodiversity is also a regional and global commons. The same program sits at several levels at once, each with its own currency. World corroboration: Berkes 2007 (*PNAS*, opened this run) writes that *"Biodiversity is a global commons important for humanity as a whole, a regional commons important for ecotourism and other benefits, and a local commons that produces ecosystem services for human well-being at the community level."*

The answer would look like: a set of conditions a governance arrangement must satisfy so that both grounds stay reachable and both knowledge frames keep their independence. That is an ordinary term we can bind and let accrue a residual, rather than a preference the derivation cannot see (Formula, "Direction").

## 3. Findings

### F1 - The question is a union compound, not a partition (`specified`)

Fig 9 :4-5: *"First: is there a fact here? Disagreements that do not shrink with effort, where each rule keeps working well on a different subset, are not one hard question but several well-formed ones. Split it rather than search."*
Fig 9 :55: *"A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones. A single lever clears the region where only one condition holds and leaves the intersections standing, because the second condition still binds."*
Fig 9 :57: *"a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either"*

Biodiversity protection and respect for rights are two conditions with different anchors. A program that maximises a biodiversity count while displacing people has cleared one condition and left the intersection standing. A program that secures consent while the species list declines has done the mirror image. There is no single lever that settles both, and an averaged "conservation with safeguards" score hides the intersection.

**Mode:** `general` for the union structure, `specified` for the claim that these two conditions form it. **Status: triangulated** across Fig 9 :54-57 and Fig 2 :36-38 (two figures, low overlap: one governs problem splitting, the other governs anchor legitimacy). **Refuter:** exhibit a single lever that clears both conditions in a case where they currently overlap, with both grounds registered independently and no residual left in the intersection.

### F2 - The habitat must be enumerated with people in it (`specified`)

Fig 11 :43: *"The habitat is enumerated, never composed."*
Fig 11 :44: *"List everything in contact with the residual, then what is in contact with those, and outward until the cascade stops mattering."*
Fig 11 :47: *"relations: pollination, predation, competition, dependency"*
Fig 11 :48: *"You do not invent the list. You read it off the world, and what you cannot perceive or measure yet is the residual."*

A conservation site is not a list of species plus a stakeholder row. It is the cascade of actors, conditions and relations in contact with the residual. Local people are actors in that list: harvesters, burners, monitors, neighbours, rights holders. The enumeration comes off the world, not off a categories template. World corroboration: Berkes 2007 reports successful cases with *"10-15 partners"* and *"four or five levels of organization"*, and says *"the 'correct' governance mechanism depends on the level at which one chooses to examine the social-ecological system."*

**Mode:** `specified`. **Status: triangulated** across Fig 11 :43-48 and Fig 1 :19-22 (habitat enumeration and per-slot residual are different figures with low overlap). **Refuter:** produce a program design whose habitat list omits any human actor or condition in contact with the site and still leaves both grounds reachable after implementation.

### F3 - Actor valence is relative to the residual, so no standing friend or enemy list survives (`specified`)

Fig 11 :49: *"An actor's valence is relative to the residual, not a property it carries."*
Fig 11 :51: *"No standing list of good actors survives a change of residual."*

A community is not intrinsically a threat to biodiversity or intrinsically its guardian. The same actor is a benefactor for one residual and a malfactor for another. This is why a universal "local communities are the solution" claim and a universal "local communities are the pressure" claim are the same error with opposite signs. The question has to be asked per slot, per residual.

World corroboration is mixed and therefore informative. The Wikipedia article *Community-based conservation* (opened 2026-11-23) states *"CBC tends to be most effective where traditional ecological knowledge is recognized, governance is inclusive, and conservation goals align with the everyday needs of communities"*, while the same article records that in Madagascar *"outcomes have included localized reductions in forest loss, but also instances where environmental pressure was displaced to surrounding areas."* Berkes 2007 warns that *"community-based conservation as a blueprint solution threatened to become a panacea itself"* and quotes Barrett et al. that *"the current fashion for community-based natural resource management overemphasizes the place of local communities in tropical conservation efforts, much as the previous top-down model underemphasized [it]."*

**Mode:** `specified`. **Status: triangulated** across Fig 11 :49-51 and the Fig 1 per-slot rule. **Refuter:** exhibit a fixed actor classification that keeps its valence across two changed residuals without a new measurement.

### F4 - Exclusion is a habitat substitution, not a subtraction (decisive against option A)

Fig 11 :61: *"The seat's power is asymmetric. Introduction is available; exclusion is not."*
Fig 11 :62: *"You can add an actor and you cannot subtract the habitat, because contact is a fact about the world and does not consult you."*
Fig 11 :66: *"Isolation is not removal of the habitat. It is substitution of one habitat for another."*
Fig 11 :67: *"Move the residual and you must reproduce everything its goal required, and you inherit the new habitat's actors, which you did not choose."*
Fig 11 :68: *"what you failed to reproduce is invisible until the goal fails"*
Fig 11 :69: *"what you unintentionally introduced is invisible until it acts"*

A fortress program does not delete people from the ecology. It moves the residual into a substituted habitat: a resettlement, a wage relation, an extractive employer, an urban periphery. Two silent failures follow, exactly as the figure names them. What the conservation goal required from the original habitat (harvest regimes, burning, monitoring, local legitimacy) is not reproduced, and what the new habitat brings (poverty, dependence, enforcement incentives) was not chosen.

World corroboration: the Wikipedia article *Fortress conservation* (opened 2026-11-23) defines the model as *"based on the belief that protection of biodiversity and historical sites is best achieved by creating protected areas in isolation from human disturbance"* and records *"Estimates suggest that more than 20 million people have been displaced globally under exclusionary conservation regimes."* The same article reports the reverse direction too: *"Granting title of the land has shown to have less clearing than state run parks, notably in the Brazilian Amazon"* and *"areas with community concessions have significantly less deforestation as communities are incentivized to manage the land sustainably, even reducing poverty."*

**Mode:** `specified`. **Status: triangulated** across Fig 11 :61-69 and Fig 4 :25 (*"A recording carried upward looks like knowledge and is a description of one occasion"*, the blueprint version of the same failure). **Refuter:** show an exclusion program that reproduces everything the goal required, inherits no unchosen actors, and leaves no displaced pressure or rights claim, verified against both grounds.

### F5 - Contact cascades, so first-order scoping misses the material effect (`specified`)

Fig 11 :52: *"And contact cascades. Scoping the habitat to direct contact is how DDT reached the eagles: first-order enumeration, second-order effect, and the residual that mattered was three steps out."*
Fig 11 :40: *"An improvement that does not change contact changes nothing, however much it improves."*

A program that fences the boundary and counts what is inside has scoped to first-order contact. The material residual may be three steps out: where displaced harvesters go, who employs them, what pressure moves to the neighbouring forest. Livelihood provision is not a compensation payment after the fact; it is a contact-changing lever, and by Fig 11 :40 a lever that does not change contact has not changed capability.

**Mode:** `specified`. **Status: triangulated** across Fig 11 :52-54 and Fig 3 :20-23 (cascade and dependency order are different figures). **Refuter:** show that a program can improve the biodiversity reading while every contact relation relevant to the residual remains unchanged.

### F6 - Two anchors, one per condition (`specified`)

Fig 2 :29: *"An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks."*
Fig 2 :36: *"An anchor is legitimate when the question is constitutively about it."*
Fig 2 :37: *"Human judgment is the right anchor for is this a good answer for a person, because the question is about people."*

The biodiversity half is settled by an outcome that does not move when the program changes its report. The rights half is legitimately settled by human judgment because that half of the question is about people. Holding both anchors is not a contradiction; it is what a union compound requires. What is forbidden is promoting one to the place of the other: a species count cannot settle a consent claim, and a satisfaction survey cannot settle a population decline.

Fig 2 :34-35 is the failure mode when the anchor is missing: *"everything drifts together, and the drift is coherent, which is why nobody notices"* and *"Alignment is triangulation, not negotiation. A mirror cannot correct you."* A program that asks communities to endorse its own monitoring report has built panel 1 of Figure 2, not panel 3.

World corroboration: Convention on Biological Diversity Article 8(j) (opened 2026-11-23) requires parties to *"respect, preserve and maintain knowledge, innovations and practices of indigenous and local communities embodying traditional lifestyles relevant for the conservation and sustainable use of biological diversity"*, with wider application *"with the approval and involvement of the holders"*. The Wikipedia article *Free, prior and informed consent* (opened 2026-11-23) quotes UNDRIP Article 10: *"Indigenous Peoples shall not be forcibly removed from their lands or territories. No relocation shall take place without the free, prior and informed consent of the Indigenous Peoples concerned"*, and notes *"mere consultation by itself is not a substitute for actual consent."*

**Mode:** `specified`. **Status: triangulated** across Fig 2 :29/:36-38 and Fig 10 :47 (*"a convention that decides an outcome has stopped being one"*). **Refuter:** show that the same instrument legitimately settles both a biodiversity claim and a rights claim without one of them being an instrument on an axis it does not measure.

### F7 - Triangulation requires two low-overlap frames, and this is where respecting knowledge becomes checkable (`specified`)

Fig 8 :45: *"Two frames together reach what neither reaches alone."*
Fig 8 :48: *"How much two frames draw on the same evidence decides both whether their agreement means anything and whether their union has any surplus."*
Fig 8 :52: *"Convergent derivation and adopted import look identical in the contents. Only the record of where each came from separates them"*
Fig 8 :53: *"Residual first, frame second."*
Fig 2 :31: *"Independence, between two frames, is how little they draw on the same evidence."*
Fig 2 :32: *"Independence is what makes agreement mean something."*

Formal conservation science and local ecological knowledge are two frames. The surplus exists only where their closures differ. If the program elicits knowledge into its own report and then scores its own report, the two frames have become *"one frame wearing two names"* (Fig 8 :48), and the agreement is the pool talking. The practical requirement is provenance: which predictions came from which frame, which ground each was ranged against, and what each would have predicted if the other were absent.

Respecting knowledge therefore has a checkable meaning here: keep the knowledge frame independent enough to disagree, and let the agreement, when it comes, be a triangulation. Berkes 2007 states the same condition in world terms: *"The global lens of biodiversity conservation (that it is a global commons) is therefore different from the local lens on biodiversity (local commons for livelihoods). This difference does not mean one perspective is right and the other wrong; it means they can both be correct from different points of view."* He adds that *"local knowledge may often appear at odds with science. But in many cases, the differences in knowledge and understanding of a resource system have to do with differences in the level at which information is obtained."*

**Mode:** `specified`. **Status: triangulated** across Fig 8 :45-53 and Fig 2 :31-35 (surplus and alignment are different figures, low overlap). **Refuter:** show that two frames with substantial evidence overlap produce the same surplus, or that agreement between a report and its own source registers as corroboration at the ground.

### F8 - A refusal seat must be installed before the prescription (`specified`)

Operators :58: *"a refutation is a negation, which is why a vocabulary without it can only accumulate"*
Fig 10 :47: *"a convention that decides an outcome has stopped being one"*
Fig 10 :49: *"Declaring a meaning does not ground it."*
Fig 10 :59: *"an interest in the outcome is unavoidable, an interest in the reading is disqualifying"*
Fig 10 :60: *"Where a stake cannot be removed, declare it."*

Consent is a negation term: it can refuse a proposed action. It is not a satisfaction score in a project log, which is a convention promoted into a verdict. By Formula step 2 a refusal is owed before a mint; building the management prescription first and soliciting consent afterwards aims the refusal at the outcome the program did not want rather than at the term it holds. The program's stake in the biodiversity outcome is unavoidable and is declared; its interest in the reading of the rights ground is disqualifying and must be removed by giving the community its own seat.

**Mode:** `specified`. **Status: triangulated** across Fig 10 :47-60 and the Operators table negation entry (two low-overlap authorities: the seat rule and the operator arity rule). **Refuter:** show a program in which a post-hoc consultation has the same refusal power as prior consent at the ground, with no channel by which the program's own stake enters the reading.

### F9 - Verdicts stay per slot; a programme average destroys the signal (`general`)

Fig 1 :19: *"The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated."*
Fig 1 :22: *"Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average."*
Fig 9 :60: *"Use filters for the budget and witnesses for the verdict. Never let a filter hand you a verdict."*

A regional programme is a set of site slots, each with its own habitat enumeration, anchors and frames. Reporting one compliance percentage merges live local residuals into a global number near zero, which Fig 1 names as a legal state, not a clean one. Ranking sites by budget cost or by ease of consent is a filter for where to try first; it is never a witness for whether the program protected biodiversity or respected rights.

**Mode:** `general` structural rule, `specified` for the programme application. **Status: triangulated** across Fig 1 :19-22 and Fig 9 :60. **Refuter:** exhibit a program-level average that preserves the site where both conditions are failing.

### F10 - Blueprints are recordings; the transferable object is the method (`specified`)

Fig 4 :22: *"Only methods cross up. Only head starts come down."*
Fig 4 :25: *"A recording carried upward looks like knowledge and is a description of one occasion."*
Fig 4 :30: *"So take the round trip and measure the gap. Without it you are navigating by dead reckoning: confident about a position nobody checked."*

A governance recipe copied from one park into another is a recording of one occasion. What can cross is the method: enumerate the habitat, keep the two frames separate, install refusal before mint, register both grounds per slot, and measure the round-trip gap when the method is carried to a new site. World corroboration: Berkes 2007 says *"a solution package developed from one case cannot readily be transferred to others"*, cites the Philippine community marine protected-area case where replication *"with little attention to context, resulted in a high rate of failure"*, and concludes that *"using diagnostics within a pluralistic framework rather than a blueprint approach"* is the alternative.

**Mode:** `specified`. **Status: triangulated** across Fig 4 :22-30 and Fig 7 :35-36 (*"A room's agreement is variation, not a verdict. Selection between rooms is where the ground votes."*). **Refuter:** show a copied governance recipe that transfers without a diagnostic re-census and still leaves both grounds reachable.

### F11 - The framework returns a shape, not a named arrangement (gap, honestly reported)

Fig 9 :32: *"you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself"*
Fig 13 :134: *"Unreachable is a verdict here, not a silence"*
Fig 13 :135: *"It cannot say which primitive is missing, because naming it would mean already holding it."*
Fig 8 :39: *"So a search that finds nothing is never a proof of absence."*

No figure line selects co-management over an Indigenous and community conserved area, an FPIC protocol over a co-management board, or one benefit-sharing percentage over another. Those are bindings from the world frame, provenanced as such, not derivations and not imports that move the wall. The figure-derived object is the **shape**: non-updating anchors for both conditions, an enumerated habitat that includes people, two low-overlap knowledge frames, a refusal seat before prescription, per-slot verdicts, and a method rather than a recording.

This is a `gap` of the shape-not-a-name kind, reported as unreached rather than as silence. It is not a warrant to invent a number or a universal institution.

**Mode:** `general`. **Status: single-source** for the limit (Fig 9 and Fig 13 agree but share the same framework evidence pool; canonical README confirms, so this is internal consistency rather than independent corroboration). **Refuter:** exhibit a figure line that names a specific governance arrangement or allocates a specific share of land or benefits.

## 4. Pre-registered refuter

Written before the conclusion, per the standing instruction that a falsifier written before the run is a design instrument. The shape claim is refuted if any of the following holds:

1. **Union refuter.** A single lever is shown to clear both conditions in an overlapping case, with each ground registered independently and no residual left in the intersection (breaks F1).
2. **Isolation refuter.** An exclusion program is shown to reproduce everything the conservation goal required, inherit no unchosen actors, leave no displaced pressure, and settle the rights claim at a human anchor it did not author (breaks F4 and F5).
3. **Independence refuter.** Two frames drawing on substantially the same evidence are shown to produce the knowledge surplus, or a program's own report about community views is shown to count as corroboration at the ground (breaks F7).
4. **Refusal refuter.** A post-authorisation consultation is shown to have the same refusal power as prior consent, with no program interest entering the reading (breaks F8).
5. **Anchor refuter.** A biodiversity instrument is shown to settle a rights claim, or a human judgment is shown to settle a population-viability claim, on the same axis (breaks F6).
6. **Naming refuter.** A figure line is found that names a specific governance arrangement or site allocation (breaks the declared gap in F11).

If any fires, the affected finding is withdrawn and the census is rerun. Until one is run, no part of this package may be labelled `grounded`.

## 5. Recommendation

1. Begin with the habitat census, including people, conditions and second-order contacts. Do not begin by choosing a protected-area category (F2, F5).
2. Keep two independent knowledge frames and record which prediction came from which. Treat agreement from a shared pool as one witness (F7).
3. Install the refusal seat before the prescription. Consent is a negation term, not a participation metric (F8).
4. Treat livelihood effects as contact-changing levers, not as compensation after exclusion (F4, F5).
5. Keep verdicts per site slot and never average the two conditions into one score (F9).
6. Send the method and the refuter, not a governance recipe (F10).
7. Report the `gap` on the specific arrangement plainly; name bindings as bindings with provenance, never as figure derivations (F11).

### SEARCHED, INWARD, OUTWARD kept distinct

## 7. Strains, gaps and honest stop

**Live strains.**

- **S1, anchor pluralism.** Fig 2 :37 licenses human judgment as an anchor for a question about people, while the biodiversity half needs an instrument that does not update on judgment. Re-census shows two constitutive anchors in a union compound, not one anchor that moves. The strain is dissolved but kept visible because it is the point most likely to be misread.
- **S2, introduction versus subtraction.** Fig 11 :61 says exclusion is unavailable although states legally exclude people. Dissolved by re-census: contact is a fact about the world, so the legal act substitutes a habitat. The word "exclusion" in option A always means the substitution, never a literal deletion of contact.
- **S3, two currencies.** Fig 12 :125 says a composition costs in bits and in actions and the two do not add. A program's description cost (how simply it states its rules) and its action cost (what it takes to check both grounds at a site) are held apart. No combined "effectiveness" figure is computed.

**Declared gap.** No figure selects the arrangement, the percentage, the boundary shape or the benefit split. F11 records it as `unreachable` at this budget, not underivable in principle and not silence. A site-level discriminator would be needed to close it (Fig 13 :134-135, Fig 8 :39).

**Honest stop.** This audit is internal consistency, not corroboration. The simulated second seat in `11_audit.md` shares this evidence pool; by Fig 2 :40 its agreement is one pool talking. No measurement at any site was taken. The verdict stays `candidate` until a ground pays for it.
