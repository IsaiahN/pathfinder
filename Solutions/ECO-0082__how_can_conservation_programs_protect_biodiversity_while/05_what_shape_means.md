## 1. A shape is a constraint, not a filler

The canonical usage rule states the limit: the framework returns *"what a missing thing would have to look like (a shape, not a name)."* Fig 9 :32: *"you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself"*. Fig 13 :135: *"It cannot say which primitive is missing, because naming it would mean already holding it."*

From *The Library of Babel*: *"This means that you can cut your search short if the set of books returned is small and descriptive enough that it provides a general shape of the answer that satisfies your question."*

So a shape is the narrowed form of an answer you can state before you can name the particular arrangement. It constrains; it does not fill. For this question the shape is: two non-updating anchors for two conditions, an enumerated habitat that includes people, two low-overlap knowledge frames, a refusal term admitted before the prescription, per-slot verdicts, and a transferable method rather than a site recipe. The name of the arrangement (co-management, an ICCA, an FPIC protocol, a particular benefit split) is not part of the shape.

## 2. How a shape is obtained

- *Library of Babel*: use the vocabulary already closest to the thing, then bootstrap. *"With each new term gained, you are able to narrow the space by definition and deepen your vocabulary."*
- Fig 9 :27-28: *"describe it before you go looking, or any frame you pick will seem to fit"*. Fig 6 :30: *"The description travels. Fails by being sharpened into terms only we can read. Cast it in effect terms."* Fig 9 :30-31: *"your words are borrowed too, so describe the effect rather than the cause"*.
- From *Decoding Tacit Knowledge*: *"When contact isn't possible, the best you can send is a plain description of what you'd expect to see, because anyone can check that against the world."* That is exactly the situation of an outside reader checking a conservation claim: they cannot inherit the site history, but they can check an effect description.
- Fig 4 :30: *"So take the round trip and measure the gap. Without it you are navigating by dead reckoning: confident about a position nobody checked."* The shape carried up from a site must be brought back down and compared, and the gap recorded.

Applied here: the residual was described in effect terms (two grounds, two conditions) before any frame was chosen. Only then were the habitat figure, the ground figure, the union figure and the membrane figure brought in. Choosing "community-based conservation" or "fortress conservation" first would have been apophenia: any frame would have seemed to fit.

## 3. What licenses a name

Only two things, both outside the shape itself:

1. **The ground settles it.** Formula step 5: *"the ground settles it. Verified only by what pays. A gate is not the ground."* Here, a particular governance arrangement is named only after a site measurement shows both grounds reachable with it.
2. **A provenanced import from a frame whose closure differs** (Fig 6 :23, Fig 8 :52), with a source gate and a shadow test. Berkes 2007, the CBD Article 8(j) text and Ostrom's design principles are such imports. They enter with author, venue and year recorded in `10_source_ledger.csv`.

From *The Hard Problem is About Source*: a colour instrument reads green and *"cannot comment on the source"*; the reading does not determine whether the source was one green light, yellow plus blue, RGB or CMYK. *"If several paths create the same behavior, then behavior is a projection with lossy information."* The essay's conclusion is that *"The answer is unreached until a better instrument exists"*, and that it was the spectrometer, not an argument, that resolved the case.

Applied here: a "participation score" reads green. It cannot comment on whether the source was genuine consent, co-option, elite capture or a program-authored report. Several sources produce the same reading. What is owed is a discriminating instrument (an independent refusal seat), not a better narrative about participation.

## 4. Why a single universal institution is the specific forbidden error

*They're Scanning Books When They Should* (carried from the exemplar's canonical mapping, not re-opened this run) states: *"The real import is not a new term in the existing space. It is a new dimension of the space."* It also says: *"Declaring the primitives does not make them neutral. It makes the choice visible."*

The conservation literature makes the same point independently. Berkes 2007 warns that *"community-based conservation as a blueprint solution threatened to become a panacea itself"* and that *"a solution package developed from one case cannot readily be transferred to others."* The Wikipedia article *Community-based conservation* records that benefits can be *"concentrated among local elites"* and that *"community participation has been limited to carrying out decisions made elsewhere, weakening local engagement and trust."* Naming one institutional form as the answer turns a visible choice into an invisible one.

So the deliverable is a shape plus a falsifier, not a universal governance template.

## 5. Q: Does "shape, not a name" forbid naming co-management or FPIC?

**Answer: no, and the earlier overstatement is corrected here.**

The canonical phrase is *"what a **missing** thing would have to look like (a shape, not a name)."* Its subject is a primitive not held in the library. Co-management, consent procedures, benefit sharing and community tenure are not missing primitives in this question: they are available bindings from the world frame (Berkes 2007, the CBD text, the FPIC page). Naming one is provenance work, not the invention of a seventh primitive.

What remains forbidden:

- claiming the binding is figure-derived when it is an import;
- claiming the binding moves the wall, when Fig 6 :22 says *"IMPORT adds an atom, and the added atom is what expands the closure"* and this binding adds no atom;
- claiming a site result for a binding before the ground pays for it.

Both claims are now recorded so neither is quietly lost. The result is an honest `gap` on which arrangement to use at a given site (see F11 of `01_figure_grounded_audit.md`), not a ban on discussing real institutions.

## 6. Prestige is not action

From *The Recursive Self-Improvement Trap*: *"Prestige must never be allowed to validate an action directly."* And: *"Prestige can fund an action. It can give an action a budget to be tried. But it cannot settle an action. Only the ground can settle it. The Ground Maintainer must be blind to prestige."*

In this question, prestige appears as the well-known conservation NGO, the famous reserve, the high-impact journal, or the community leader with the loudest voice. Any of these can give a program a budget to be tried. None can settle whether the biodiversity ground improved or whether rights were respected. Fig 8 :48 warns that *"Two frames that overlap almost completely are one frame wearing two names"*; a room assembled from famous names that all read the same report is one frame, not a check.

## 7. My own audit is a simulation, not a ground reading

From the same essay: *"the output is only as good as the validator. The agent, or the tool, or the frame, or the theory, cannot validate itself."* And: *"LLMs can quickly simulate an approximate answer/output with the formula... However, the truly testable output would need to be derived from the real architecture or working the formula yourself."*

So `01_figure_grounded_audit.md`, `06_isomers_and_order.md`, `07_the_tether_answer.md`, `08_expert_verification_packet.md`, `09_watson_answer.md` and `11_audit.md` are simulated derivations checked for internal consistency against the figures. They are not site measurements and they cannot settle the question they report on. The canonical README's own limit applies: the framework *"stops at the limit of what the available instruments can distinguish, and records the lineage so the stop is honest"*, and *"Indiscernibility is not identity."*

## 8. One-paragraph version

> A shape is what an answer must look like before you are allowed to name the particular institution. Here it says: enumerate the habitat with people in it, keep two grounds that do not update on the program's own reports, keep formal science and local knowledge as two independent frames, give consent the power to refuse before the prescription is minted, judge each site separately, and transfer a method rather than a recipe. It does not say which governance form wins. That is owed to a ground reading, and until one arrives the honest report is `specified`, `candidate`, with a written refuter.
