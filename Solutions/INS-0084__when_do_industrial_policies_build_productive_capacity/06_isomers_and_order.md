# Conditional versus unconditional support: structure, order, and analogues

**Mode: `specified`.** This is a simulated derivation checked against the figures, not a ground reading (see `05_what_shape_means.md` §6).

## RESIDUAL

The question asks when support builds capacity and when it protects connected firms. The same budget line can produce either outcome. That means the difference is not in the ingredients. The second derivation owed here is: what is the structure that carries the difference, in what order do its links run, and what known analogues license the reading?

## DOMAIN CENSUS (supporting derivation)

Atoms held: a target sector, a support instrument (subsidy, credit, tariff, procurement), a metric, a withdrawal clause, an evaluator. Bonds held: the seven, including ⋛ comparison, → sequence, ⇒ production, − subtraction, and the unary ¬ negation. No new primitive is needed. Order by dependency: describe residual → state metric → bond support to metric → verify → refuse or continue.

## DERIVATION

### Q1 - What is the structure that distinguishes the two regimes?

- Fig 12 :95 *"Two entries with an identical ingredient set are not variants of one thing. They are different things whose notation cannot tell them apart, and the difference is carried entirely by the bond."*
- Fig 12 :94 *"The arrangement is the substance."* Fig 12 :99 *"A bond costs what it could have been, and nothing more."*
- A conditional regime bonds support to metric with ⋛ (comparison of target against actual) and adds ¬ (withdrawal when the residual persists and does not trend down). An unconditional regime bonds support to political allocation with no comparison and no refusal. Same ingredient set, different bond, different substance.
- Fig 12 :111 *"A vocabulary without bonds can name a state and cannot name a route to one. A plan is a sequence. A goal is a comparison. Progress is a subtraction."* Operators :51 *"a goal is a comparison, which is why a vocabulary without it cannot state one"*; Operators :45 *"progress is a subtraction, which is why a vocabulary without it cannot say how far along it is"*. The unconditional vocabulary can name "we support steel" and cannot state the goal or the progress.
- Operators :74 *"A vocabulary with − and without ¬ can say what is missing from an arrangement and cannot say that an arrangement is wrong, so it can only ever accumulate."* Without ¬ the program can only accumulate supported firms.
- **Verdict:** the bond is the difference. `general`, triangulated (Fig 12 bond pricing, Fig 12 route vocabulary, Operators comparison/subtraction/negation).

### Q2 - What order do the links run?

- Fig 3 :20 *"The chain is ordered by dependency, not by importance. Each link consumes what the one above produces, so effort spent above the break arrives at a step that cannot use it."*
- Ordered links: (1) perception: describe the market-failure residual with an instrument already returning something (Fig 6 :32 *"An instrument is not built from a description. It is improved from a worse instrument already returning something."*); (2) vocabulary/objective: state what counts as progress, state-representable and ground-checkable (Fig 3 :10, Fig 10 :47); (3) planning: choose the instrument under that objective; (4) verify against the ground; (5) refuse before minting a new support (formula step 2).
- Fig 3 :19 *"Optimising a link that already works cannot rescue one that does not."* Spending more budget on link 3 (bigger subsidies) cannot rescue a broken link 2 (no metric).
- Fig 3 :30 *"Most improvement effort in practice lands above the break, because that is where the measurements already exist."* This is the structural reason political pressure arrives as more subsidy rather than a better metric.
- **Verdict:** order is fixed by dependency. `general`, single-source for the specific chain (Fig 3), triangulated for the refusal step (formula + Operators + Fig 5).

### Q3 - Is the difference an isomer, a functional group, or something else?

- Fig 12 :117 *"isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."*
- Fig 12 :123 *"functional group. A substructure whose presence fixes the outcome. The group says what kind; the qualifier says which member."*
- As usually described the two regimes share the support instrument but differ in whether a metric and a withdrawal clause are present. They do **not** share the same atom set, so under Fig 12's definition they are **not isomers**. They are two members of the functional group "targeted support" (Fig 12 :123: *"the group says what kind; the qualifier says which member"*), one qualified "conditional" and one "unconditional".
- The stronger form: if both regimes are written over the full shared atom set {target sector, support instrument, metric, withdrawal clause, evaluator}, differing only in which bond connects them, then they **are** isomers. That is the checkable version worth testing.
- **Falsifier, written before the run:** if a regime lacking either the metric or the withdrawal clause can be shown to produce the same surviving capacity as one with both, across matched cases, the bond claim fails and the difference is in the atoms after all.
- **Mode:** `general` for the definition-based reading, `specified` for the falsifiable claim. Triangulated for "different bond, different substance" (Fig 12 :94, :95, :117), narrowed on isomerism as recorded in `01` §6.

### Q4 - What analogues license the habitat reading?

- **Substituted habitat / test harness.** Fig 11 :66 *"Isolation is not removal of the habitat. It is substitution of one habitat for another."* Fig 11 :70 *"A test harness is a substituted habitat. Which is why a synthetic solve proves wiring and never capability."* A protected domestic market is a test harness: it proves the firm can produce for that market and says nothing about world-market survival.
- **Catalyst.** Fig 12 :119 *"catalyst. A part that survives its own use. Spent and regenerating parts behave differently across uses, and nothing records which a term is."* Monitoring capacity is a catalyst if it survives the first allocation and can be reused to refuse the next; it is spent if the first allocation consumes the evaluator's independence.
- **Mirror.** Fig 2 :35 *"Alignment is triangulation, not negotiation. A mirror cannot correct you."* An evaluator drawn from the protected firms is a mirror.
- **Goodhart / Campbell (world, secondary only).** A metric used for allocation stops being a good measure (Goodhart's law, Strathern's formulation, read this session). A social indicator used for decision-making becomes subject to corruption pressures (Campbell 1979, read this session). These corroborate Fig 10 :47 *"a convention that decides an outcome has stopped being one"* but do not decide the verdict.
- **Verdict:** `general` for the structural analogues, `specified` for their application to a given program. Triangulated (Fig 11 plus Fig 2 plus two low-overlap world fields, with overlap declared in the ledger).

### Q5 - What order is the union of capacity conditions?

- Fig 9 :54 *"When the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again."* Fig 9 :58 *"a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either. It stops being a mixture and becomes a specific compound."*
- The compound is: (A) a described residual that support can act on, AND (B) a discipline channel that keeps the support aimed at it. A lever that improves A alone (pick a better sector) leaves B binding; a lever that improves B alone (install a metric with no described failure) leaves an empty target. Fig 9 :56 *"A single lever clears the region where only one condition holds and leaves the intersections standing, because the second condition still binds."*
- Union, not partition. Two loops, two verdicts, kept separate.

## CONCLUSION

The difference between capacity and capture is carried by the bond between support and the ground, not by the size or type of the support. The links run in dependency order: describe the residual, state a checkable metric, bond support to it, verify, refuse. Conditional and unconditional support are a functional group under the usual description and a real isomer claim under the full shared atom set. A protected habitat proves wiring and never capability. The falsifier is written above and was written before the verdict.
