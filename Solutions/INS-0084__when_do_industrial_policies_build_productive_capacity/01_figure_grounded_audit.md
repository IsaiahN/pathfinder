## 0. The residual in effect terms

A state targets a sector with subsidies, credit, tariffs, or procurement. Two outcomes are observed in the literature and in policy debate:

- **Capacity:** the supported sector becomes more productive in ways that persist after support is withdrawn.
- **Capture:** support becomes a durable transfer to firms whose main asset is political connection, with no such surviving gain.

The residual R is the gap between the promise (capacity) and the observed distribution of outcomes (capacity in some cases, capture in others). The question asks what distinguishes the two regimes. Stated as a bound ordinary term (formula step "Direction"):

`CAPACITY := (described residual) AND (support changes contact) AND (grounded metric with refusal) AND (low-overlap evaluation)`

An answer is worth having only if each conjunct can fail separately and be checked separately. A single "good governance" score would average the domains and lose the signal, which is the error named in Fig 1 :19 and :22.

**R by arity, symmetry, scale.**
- Arity: two conditions, so a **union compound**, not one hard question. Fig 9 :54 *"When the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again."* Fig 9 :58 *"a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either. It stops being a mixture and becomes a specific compound."*
- Symmetry: not symmetric. Capacity and capture differ by the bond between support and the ground, not by an amount of support. Fig 12 :94 *"The arrangement is the substance."*
- Scale: first order at the firm or sector slot, second order at the evaluating institution, with a lossy round trip between the measurement and the policy (Fig 4).

3. Planning: choose the instrument, with a bond to the objective (Fig 12 :111-112).
4. Learn and carry: verify against the ground and carry the method (Fig 3 :15).
5. Refuse before mint: withdraw from the persistent non-performer (formula step 2; Fig 5 :58-59).

## 3. Findings

### F1 - The two outcomes are a union compound, not a point on one scale (general)

Fig 9 :54 *"When the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again."* Fig 9 :58 *"a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either. It stops being a mixture and becomes a specific compound."*

So "capacity versus capture" is not one question about how much policy is good. It is a compound of at least two conditions: a real productive residual is described, and support is bonded to a discipline channel. A lever that clears the region where only one condition holds leaves the intersection standing (Fig 9 :56 *"A single lever clears the region where only one condition holds and leaves the intersections standing, because the second condition still binds."*). The second-order value is that the residue becomes describable (Fig 9 :59 *"not that the pile is smaller, but that the residue is finally describable"*).

**Mode:** `general` (structural). **Refuter:** a controlled comparison showing a single scalar (for example total subsidy per firm) separates the two regimes with no interaction term. Verdict: triangulated (Fig 9 union/partition plus Fig 12 bond structure).

### F2 - An anchor that updates is not an anchor; capture is the mutual-update collapse (specified)

Fig 2 :29 *"An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks."* Fig 2 :34 *"If it does, panel 3 turns into panel 1: the anchor becomes another frame, everything drifts together, and the drift is coherent, which is why nobody notices."*

In the capture regime the metric that allocates support (jobs promised, investment pledged, loans disbursed) is authored by the same actors who receive the support and moves when they lobby. The would-be ground has become another frame. Fig 2 :14 *"they converge, and what they converge on is whoever had the most influence"* names the result. Fig 2 :35 *"Alignment is triangulation, not negotiation. A mirror cannot correct you."* A monitoring board dominated by the firms it monitors is a mirror.

Fig 2 :40 *"agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool"* and Fig 2 :42 *"Buy independence in proportion to the cost of being wrong."* Political connection is correlation with the allocation decision. It is disqualifying for a verdict about productive capacity (Fig 2 :39 *"Correlation has valence."*).

**Mode:** `specified`, falsifiable by a metric that is shown to be invariant to lobbying pressure. **Refuter:** demonstrate that the allocation metric does not move when political pressure is applied, and that capacity outcomes are unchanged by its removal. Verdict: triangulated (Fig 2 collapses plus Fig 7 room selection plus Fig 10 stake rules).

### F3 - Capability is agent-and-habitat; protection substitutes a habitat (specified)

Fig 11 :39 *"capability is a property of agent-and-habitat, never of the agent alone."* Fig 11 :40 *"An improvement that does not change contact changes nothing, however much it improves."*

Giving a firm capital, tax relief, or protection without changing its contact with a competitive ground improves the agent in isolation. By the figure that changes nothing about capability. Fig 11 :66 *"Isolation is not removal of the habitat. It is substitution of one habitat for another."* A tariff wall or a guaranteed domestic buyer substitutes a protected habitat for the competitive one. Fig 11 :70 *"A test harness is a substituted habitat. Which is why a synthetic solve proves wiring and never capability."*

Fig 11 :61 *"Introduction is available; exclusion is not. You can add an actor and you cannot subtract the habitat, because contact is a fact about the world and does not consult you."* The state can add support and can introduce a competitor or an export test; it cannot repeal the fact of contact. Fig 11 :68 *"what you failed to reproduce is invisible until the goal fails"* and Fig 11 :69 *"what you unintentionally introduced is invisible until it acts"* name the two silent failure modes of the substituted habitat.

Fig 11 :49 *"An actor's valence is relative to the residual, not a property it carries."* A connected firm is not a benefactor or a malfactor by kind; its valence is relative to the residual (capacity). Fig 11 :51 *"No standing list of good actors survives a change of residual."*

**Mode:** `specified` at the firm slot. **Refuter:** a supported firm sustaining world-market sales and productivity growth for several years after all support is withdrawn. Verdict: triangulated (Fig 11 contact rules plus Fig 6 :23 *"No frame escapes its own closure. The bound moves only on contact with a frame whose closure differs."* plus Fig 8 :45 *"Two frames together reach what neither reaches alone."*).

### F4 - The middle link is where industrial policy breaks: having terms for progress (general structure, specified prediction)

Fig 3 :24 *"having terms for what a situation offers and being able to say what would count as progress, has neither, so it attracts the least attention and the fewest instruments."* Fig 3 :27 *"A link with no instrument is not a link that works. It is a link nobody has looked at."* Fig 3 :29 *"Before asking whether a system is good, ask which link it currently stops at, and whether the answer was measured or assumed."*

A state can perceive a sector (perception has obvious tests) and can design an instrument (planning has obvious tests) yet break at the link between them: saying what would count as progress in its own terms. Most effort lands above that break because that is where the measurements already exist (Fig 3 :30 *"Most improvement effort in practice lands above the break, because that is where the measurements already exist."*). Fig 3 :19 *"Optimising a link that already works cannot rescue one that does not."*

Fig 12 :111 *"A vocabulary without bonds can name a state and cannot name a route to one. A plan is a sequence. A goal is a comparison. Progress is a subtraction."* Operators :51 *"a goal is a comparison, which is why a vocabulary without it cannot state one"* and Operators :45 *"progress is a subtraction, which is why a vocabulary without it cannot say how far along it is"*. A policy vocabulary with "we support steel" but no target versus actual and no baseline versus now can name the state and cannot name the route.

**Mode:** `general` for the dependency structure, `specified` for the prediction that this is the common break. **Refuter:** a policy regime with no explicit progress metric whose outcomes are statistically indistinguishable from one with a metric and withdrawal clause. Verdict: `single-source` for the specific "middle link breaks" claim (Fig 3), triangulated for "needs comparison and subtraction" (Fig 12 plus Operators table).

### F5 - Refusal is load-bearing: a vocabulary without negation can only accumulate (general requirement, specified prediction)

Operators :58 *"a refutation is a negation, which is why a vocabulary without it can only accumulate"*. Operators :74 *"A vocabulary with − and without ¬ can say what is missing from an arrangement and cannot say that an arrangement is wrong, so it can only ever accumulate."*

A state that can add subsidies but cannot refuse a persistent non-performer can only accumulate supported firms. Fig 5 :59 *"a term whose residual PERSISTS AND DOES NOT TREND DOWN is the one to point the refusal at"* gives the selection rule: point withdrawal at the supported firm whose residual keeps being wrong and is not improving. Fig 12 :119 *"catalyst. A part that survives its own use."* Monitoring capacity is a catalyst only if it survives its own use rather than being consumed in the first allocation.

Fig 5 :41 *"Any one of them at zero stops the mint entirely. Which is why a system that is minting nothing may have nothing wrong with its minting."* A program that produces no new capacity may be correctly inert, not badly run. Fig 5 :44 *"A term that explains everything by saying nothing fails this, and so does a term that explains one occasion perfectly."* A policy that cites one national champion as proof is the one-occasion failure.

**Mode:** `general` for the operator requirement, `specified` for the prediction. **Refuter:** show that withdrawal clauses have no incremental effect on outcomes once selection is controlled. Verdict: triangulated (Fig 5 amendment plus Operators negation plus Fig 12 catalyst).

### F6 - The same instrument under a different bond is a different substance (general)

Fig 12 :95 *"Two entries with an identical ingredient set are not variants of one thing. They are different things whose notation cannot tell them apart, and the difference is carried entirely by the bond."* Fig 12 :99 *"A bond costs what it could have been, and nothing more."*

"Subsidy" names an ingredient. A conditional, contestable, time-limited subsidy with an export or productivity test and a withdrawal clause is a different substance from an unconditional, exclusive, permanent one, even when the budget lines are identical. Fig 12 :117 *"isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."* Fig 12 :123 *"functional group. A substructure whose presence fixes the outcome. The group says what kind; the qualifier says which member."*

This is the direct answer to "when": the bond, not the amount. It is also why asking "does industrial policy work?" is a malformed question. Fig 12 :94 *"The arrangement is the substance."*

**Mode:** `general`. **Refuter:** a case where two support regimes with identical bonds produce systematically different outcomes with all observables matched, which would mean the bond is not the whole difference. Verdict: triangulated (Fig 12 isomer, functional group, priced bond). **Retraction recorded in §5:** the strong isomer form is narrowed to a functional-group claim after re-census.

### F7 - The residual is per slot; aggregate success hides the captured firm (general)

Fig 1 :19 *"The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated."* Fig 1 :22 *"Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average."* Fig 1 :23 *"The ground enters as the only metric."*

A sector can show rising total output while a particular supported firm has a large live residual. Fig 1 :13 *"a global R near zero with one live slot residual is a legal state"*. Sector GDP, jobs created, and disbursement totals are frame-internal quantities the policy also produces: Fig 1 :27 *"They are frame-internal. A frame cannot score itself with a quantity it also produces."* Fig 5 :48 *"Its own output is never a measure of success. A system can be enthusiastically curious and produce nothing."*

**Mode:** `general`. **Refuter:** demonstrate that an aggregate sector metric has higher out-of-sample prediction of surviving capacity than per-firm residuals. Verdict: triangulated (Fig 1 plus Fig 5 :48).

### F8 - Evaluation needs low-overlap frames, not a larger room (general)

Fig 8 :45 *"Two frames together reach what neither reaches alone."* Fig 6 :53 *"searchers drawing on the same evidence are one searcher wearing many names."* Fig 2 :40 *"agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool"* (already quoted in F2; the overload here is on independence, a distinct quantity). Fig 10 :93 *"A ground with a reputation to protect is a ground someone benefits from, and its keeper is thereby disqualified."*

A ministry that both allocates support and grades the supported firm, using data supplied by the firm, is one frame with three labels. Fig 10 :50 *"Looking inside the frame it grades is how the seat stops being outside."* Fig 7 :35 *"A room's agreement is variation, not a verdict. Selection between rooms is where the ground votes."* The independent frame is a foreign competitor's export data, an audit with access to firm-level records, or a matched counterfactual, not another sympathetic reviewer.

**Mode:** `general`. **Refuter:** show that two evaluators drawing on disjoint evidence produce no more accurate capacity prediction than one evaluator using both. Verdict: triangulated (Fig 8 plus Fig 6 plus Fig 10).

### F9 - Describe the residual before picking the instrument (general)

Fig 9 :28 *"describe it before you go looking, or any frame you pick will seem to fit"*. Fig 8 :53 *"Residual first, frame second."* Fig 6 :32 *"An instrument is not built from a description. It is improved from a worse instrument already returning something."*

Naming a strategic sector before describing the market failure it corrects is the inversion the figure forbids. Any industry can be made to look strategic after the fact. What is owed is the specific coordination problem, information externality, or learning spillover, plus an instrument already returning a weak signal (export orders, pilot yields, patent citations) that can be sharpened. Fig 9 :32 *"you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself"* limits what the framework returns: a shape for the instrument, not the name of the winning sector.

**Mode:** `general`. **Refuter:** a case where starting from the sector rather than the residual produced equally good instrument choices out of sample. Verdict: triangulated (Fig 9 plus Fig 8 plus Fig 6).

## 4. Pre-registered refuter (written before the conclusion)

Required before any outreach: *"a falsifier written before the run is a design instrument, not only an honesty device"* (Seat Map, failure genera). The shape claim `CAPACITY := (described residual) AND (support changes contact) AND (grounded metric with refusal) AND (low-overlap evaluation)` is **refuted** if any of the following holds:

1. A credible counterfactual (matched firms, a natural experiment, or a randomized rollout) shows no productivity or export gain that survives withdrawal of support, after selection into support is controlled. This breaks F3 and fires against Fig 1 :19 (no live per-slot residual) and Fig 5 :44 (one-occasion explanation).
2. A regime with no progress metric and no withdrawal clause produces outcomes statistically indistinguishable from one with both. This breaks F4 and F5 and removes the load-bearing bond in F6.
3. The allocation metric is shown to move systematically with political contributions, lobby visits, or connected-board membership, and capacity outcomes track that movement rather than the stated target. This breaks F2: the anchor updates.
4. Supported firms' world-market performance collapses as soon as protection is removed and never recovers, with no spillover to unrelated firms. This confirms the substituted-habitat reading of F3 rather than refuting it, so it **confirms** rather than refutes; it is recorded here as the discrimination test that separates the two regimes, not as a refuter. (Kept visible as a written-before-run expectation; see §5 for the correction.)

If 1, 2, or 3 fires, the shape is wrong and the packet is withdrawn. Until one is run, no part of this may be labelled `grounded`.

### Strains and re-census

- **S1: Fig 11 :61 *"Introduction is available; exclusion is not"* versus F5's withdrawal of support.** Re-census: Fig 11 governs the habitat (contact is a fact about the world and cannot be subtracted); F5's ¬ governs the state's own introduced term (a promise of support can be refused). Different quantities. Resolved, not smoothed.
- **S2: Fig 3 "in its own terms" versus Fig 10 :47 *"a convention that decides an outcome has stopped being one"*.** Re-census: Fig 3 requires the state to represent progress; Fig 10 forbids the state from authoring a metric that cannot be violated. A metric can be both state-representable and ground-checkable. Resolved.
- **S3 (live, named): the identity of the anchor is figure-silent.** The figures govern the anchor's role (Fig 2 :29; Symbols :104 *"Whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks."*), not whether the right measure is total factor productivity, export survival, or patent quality. Choosing among them is a binding from the economics frame, provenanced as such. This is a shape, not a name. Reported as a live strain, not hidden.

### Updated verdict

The shape survives. Capacity requires a described residual, a change in the firm's contact with a competitive ground, an external metric with a real refusal clause, and independent low-overlap evaluation. The specific sector, the specific metric, and the specific subsidy amount are not given by the figures. The two outcomes are a union compound under different bonds, not two points on a scale. Strains after re-census: S1 and S2 resolved, S3 live and named.

**Mode of this verdict: `specified`, ungrounded, candidate.** No measurement settles it. A credible counterfactual, a demonstrated invariance of the metric to political pressure, or a demonstration that withdrawal clauses are inert would move it; agreement or prestige would not.
