# Expert verification packet - the Tether on industrial policy and connected firms

**What this is:** a claim in a form an industrial policy researcher can check. Mode, derivation and refuters are stated before the conclusion, so a reader knows what to look for and what would settle it.

**Mode: `specified` - not `grounded`.** No measurement is claimed. This is a shape and a route, not a result.

**Status:** a simulated derivation checked for internal consistency against the figures, not a ground reading. It cannot settle the question it reports on.

## 1. The claim, in one paragraph

The split between capacity-building and capture is a union of two conditions, not one scale of policy quality. Targeted support builds capacity when a market-failure residual is described before the instrument is chosen, when support changes the firm's contact with a competitive ground, when the allocation metric is an anchor that does not move with political pressure, when the program can refuse a persistent non-performer, and when evaluation uses independent low-overlap evidence. It protects connected firms when those conditions are absent and selection runs by influence. The support instrument is an ingredient; the bond between support and the ground is the substance. The framework names neither the winning sector nor the correct performance measure. What it offers is the shape any answer must have, three refuters written in advance, and the instruments that would close it.

## 2. Three checkable sub-claims

### C1 - framework-internal and economics-facing; checkable by reading Fig 11 and the policy evidence

- **Statement:** A subsidy that does not change a firm's contact with a competitive ground cannot produce the capability at issue. Protection substitutes a habitat and proves wiring, not world-market capability.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 11 :39 *"capability is a property of agent-and-habitat, never of the agent alone."* Fig 11 :40 *"An improvement that does not change contact changes nothing, however much it improves."* Fig 11 :70 *"A test harness is a substituted habitat. Which is why a synthetic solve proves wiring and never capability."*
- **What to check:** Is the characterization faithful to the cases you know? Did supported firms that later became competitive face an external test (export discipline, hard budget constraint, foreign entry) during the support period, or only a protected domestic market?
- **Refuter:** A supported firm sustaining world-market sales and productivity growth for several years after all support is withdrawn, with no continuing external test. That breaks the substituted-habitat reading.

### C2 - framework-internal; checkable by reading Fig 2, Fig 3 and the Operators table

- **Statement:** An allocation metric that moves with political pressure is not an anchor; the program also needs a comparison (target versus actual) and a negation (withdrawal) or it can only accumulate supported firms.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 2 :29 *"An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks."* Fig 2 :34 *"If it does, panel 3 turns into panel 1: the anchor becomes another frame, everything drifts together, and the drift is coherent, which is why nobody notices."* Operators :51 *"a goal is a comparison, which is why a vocabulary without it cannot state one"* and Operators :74 *"A vocabulary with − and without ¬ can say what is missing from an arrangement and cannot say that an arrangement is wrong, so it can only ever accumulate."*
- **What to check:** Can you name a program whose target metric stayed invariant under observed lobbying or electoral pressure? Does its withdrawal clause have a recorded use, or is it dead text?
- **Refuter:** A metric demonstrated invariant to political pressure and a program with no withdrawal clause performing indistinguishably from one with a used clause, across matched cases. That breaks C2.

### C3 - framework-internal; checkable by reading Fig 1, Fig 6, Fig 8 and Fig 12

- **Statement:** The difference between the two regimes is carried by the bond, and the evaluation must use frames that do not share an evidence pool. Aggregate sector outcomes cannot score the policy because the policy also produces them.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 12 :95 *"Two entries with an identical ingredient set are not variants of one thing. They are different things whose notation cannot tell them apart, and the difference is carried entirely by the bond."* Fig 6 :53 *"searchers drawing on the same evidence are one searcher wearing many names."* Fig 1 :19 *"The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated."*
- **What to check:** In a case you know, did two programs with the same instrument and budget but different conditions produce different surviving outcomes? Did the evaluation use data the implementing agency did not author?
- **Refuter:** A case where identical bonds produce systematically different outcomes with all observables matched, or where a single aggregate metric out-predicts per-firm residuals out of sample. That breaks C3.

## 3. What is explicitly not claimed

- Not that industrial policy works or fails in general, and not a ranking of sectors or instruments.
- Not the winning sector, the correct metric (TFP, export survival, patents), or the subsidy amount. The slot stays marked `???`; those are bindings from the economics frame, provenanced as such.
- Not that protection never produces output. It can produce output for the protected market; that is a test harness, not capability in the sense at issue.
- Not a derivation of economics from the six-term substrate, and no new primitive.
- Not `grounded`. Nothing here is asserted as settled by experiment.
- Not corroboration by agreement. The world sources in `09_watson_answer.md` are secondary; the simulated second seat in `11_audit.md` shares the evidence pool and is not a witness.

## 5. What would settle it

A source-discriminating instrument, not a further argument. From *The Hard Problem is About Source*: an instrument that reads green *"cannot comment on the source"*; the spectrometer resolved it. Here:

1. **Counterfactual test:** matched firms or a natural experiment. Does productivity survive withdrawal, and does it hold once selection into support is controlled?
2. **Anchor-invariance test:** does the allocation metric move with political contributions, lobby contacts, or connected-board membership?
3. **Withdrawal test:** do programs with a used metric and a real refusal clause outperform those without, after selection is controlled?

## 6. What an expert can check in twenty minutes

1. Read Fig 11 :39, :40, :70 and Fig 2 :29, :34. Six short passages.
2. Check C1 against one case you know: was there an external test during support, and did the firm survive without support?
3. Name one instrument that would distinguish genuine capacity from subsidy-driven entry with firm-level data, or say why none is possible.
4. Send the refutation. A failure is more useful than an endorsement.
