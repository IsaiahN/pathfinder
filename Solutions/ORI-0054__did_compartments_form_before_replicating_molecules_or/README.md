## The question restated as an effect

Two options are offered. Option A, compartment first: a physically bounded volume exists, then a replicating molecule is enclosed inside it. Option B, replicator first: a self-copying molecule exists in solution, then it becomes enclosed afterward. What each leads to: A gives a container that concentrates molecules and couples their fates before heredity exists; B gives a replicator lineage exposed to parasitic collapse until a boundary appears. The thing being decided is not a chemical name but an **ordering between two conditions** that both had to hold before Darwinian evolution could begin. An answer would look like a licensed bond between the two conditions, with a ground that can date them.

## What is deliberately not here

- No personal emails of named individuals.
- No ranking of researchers by how likely they are to agree with the compartment-first or replicator-first reading.
- No bulk-send plan.

Three reasons, in the figures' own words:

1. **Agreement is not an anchor.** Fig 2 :35: *"Alignment is triangulation, not negotiation. A mirror cannot correct you."* A list of people selected for agreement manufactures the correlation that disqualifies a verdict (Fig 2 :40: *"disqualifying for a verdict: agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool"*).
2. **The first contact carries the residual, not the framework.** Fig 10 :52: *"Bring a characterised residual, never the source."* A reader who has absorbed the derivation before being asked for a verdict has joined the frame being checked.
3. **The field's own disagreement is evidence, not an obstacle to be smoothed.** Fig 9 :4-5: *"Disagreements that do not shrink with effort, where each rule keeps working well on a different subset, are not one hard question but several well-formed ones. Split it rather than search."*
