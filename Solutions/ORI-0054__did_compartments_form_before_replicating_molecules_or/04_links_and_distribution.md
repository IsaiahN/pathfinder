## What the compartment-first versus replicator-first literature actually shows

These are secondary world sources only. They characterise the field; they do not decide the figure verdict.

1. **Compartments-early case.** Monnard and Walde (2015) call the view that life emerged as dynamic compartmentalized chemical systems *"inescapable"* and say compartment formation very likely occurred in early prebiotic times. They also warn it would be *"too easy and likely wrong"* to limit compartments to lipid vesicles, citing coacervates, mineral surfaces and mixed systems.
2. **Replicators-early case, with a stated gap.** Fine and Pearlman (2023) support an RNA core before coded proteins and DNA, but concede that how RNA-like polymers stayed intact over generations until encapsulated *"remains unclear"*, and that a complete demonstration of RNA self-replication coupled to a self-replicating protocell has not been achieved.
3. **The coupling view.** Schrum, Zhu and Szostak (2010) and Joyce and Szostak (2018) both define the protocell as a compartment plus an informational polymer and locate the hard part in replication and compatibility, not in either component alone. Joyce and Szostak call compartmentalization *"an essential step on the path to biology"* and list multiple non-vesicular alternatives.
4. **A genuinely different frame.** Robinson (2005) reports Russell and Martin's hydrothermal-chamber model in which mineral compartments precede lipid membranes, and records that neither genes-first nor metabolism-first had emerged as a clear favorite. This is the lowest-overlap witness in the set, because it draws on fossil chimneys and bacterial physiology rather than vesicle kinetics.
5. **No dated sequence.** None of the sources read supplies a temporally resolved order. The squared-off either/or question is therefore not answered by the world literature either; it is split by Fig 9 :4-5 and left as a gap on the literal order by Fig 6 :40.
