**Subject:** One checkable split for the compartments-versus-replicators question

> Hi [name],
>
> I have one specific output to check against what you already know. I am not asking you to read a theory first, and I am not asking you to agree with either the compartment-first or the replicator-first camp. If the output does not survive, there is nothing else to look at.
>
> **The output (`general` on the shape, `specified` on the order claim, not `grounded`):** the question "did compartments form before replicating molecules, or were molecules enclosed afterward?" is not one question. It is a union of two conditions that both had to hold before Darwinian evolution could start: a maintained boundary that keeps related molecules together, and a copying cycle whose products re-enter as input. The figure-derived shape says (1) a boundary is a filter with a direction and a space relation (contained), (2) a replicator is a closed cycle, not a chain, and (3) the capability belongs to the coupled pair, not to either component alone. It does **not** say which came first. It returns a shape, not a date.
>
> **Why I am asking you:** the literature splits cleanly. Some work argues compartments were likely present very early; other work argues RNA preceded coded proteins and that encapsulation remains unclear; a third line puts mineral chambers before lipid membranes. Each result works on a different subset. I want to know whether you think those are several well-formed questions, or whether one mechanism actually clears both conditions at once.
>
> **What would refute the shape claim:**
> 1. A single mechanism, under one instrument, that produces boundary and heredity together in a dated sequence (the union collapses to a partition).
> 2. A dated ground measurement that licenses the sequence or production bond between the two components.
> 3. Sustained Darwinian evolution in a replicator population with no boundary at any scale.
> 4. A self-copying system whose copying is a chain rather than a closed cycle across generations.
> 5. A figure line that returns a temporal order between a boundary term and a copying term without an import.
>
> I wrote these down before sending this. If any holds, I retract the claim. A one-line "this fails because..." is more useful to me than agreement. If you would rather not engage, no reply is needed.
>
> **If it survives, and only then:** the sources are below. The figures are the authority, not my summary of them.
> - Figures text with line numbers (local): `FRAMEWORK/reference/03-FIGURES_TEXT.md`
> - Canonical figures README (local): `FRAMEWORK/reference/FIGURES_README_canonical.md`
> - Fine & Pearlman 2023, "On the origin of life: an RNA-focused synthesis and narrative", RNA 29(8):1085-1098: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10351881
> - Joyce & Szostak 2018, "Protocells and RNA Self-Replication", Cold Spring Harbor Perspectives in Biology 10(9):a034801: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6120706
>

Write down what would refute your result *before* you run it: a frame cannot check the direction of its own question, and outputs can be contrived to fit the formula. A reported failure is more useful to me than a reported success.
