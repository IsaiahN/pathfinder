## 0. The residual in effect terms

The question asks which of two sequences leads to the first Darwinian entity:

- **A, compartment first:** a bounded volume (vesicle, coacervate, mineral pore) forms and concentrates molecules; a replicator later arises or is enclosed inside it.
- **B, replicator first:** a self-copying molecule arises in solution; it is enclosed afterward.

Stated as an effect, the residual is the **absence of a unit of selection**: heritable variation must be tied to something that stays together long enough for selection to act on it. Both options try to supply that unit. An answer would be a licensed bond between boundary and replicator, plus a ground able to date them.

**R by arity, symmetry, scale.**

- **Arity:** two conditions. The literature's failure to converge (Robinson 2005: *"neither has emerged as a robust and obvious favorite"*) is the signature Fig 9 :4-5 names, not one hard question but several well-formed ones.
- **Symmetry:** enclosure is asymmetric (a container contains a content). The figure system separates ordered from symmetric bonds: Operators :61 *"Under an ordered bond, different orders are different terms, and charging for the sequence is correct."* A symmetric bond would erase the question's order entirely (Operators :59 *"Under a symmetric bond, k operands have k! spellings and one term"*). Which bond holds is itself the question.
- **Scale:** both conditions are pre-cellular; the composed entity (a protocell capable of Darwinian evolution) is the next stratum. Fig 12 :103: *"Composition is recursive, and the level decides what is irreducible."*

## 3. Findings F1..F9

### F1: The question is a union compound and must be split (decisive, triangulated)

Fig 9 :4-5:

> **"Disagreements that do not shrink with effort, where each rule keeps working well on a different subset, are not one hard question but several well-formed ones. Split it rather than search."**

Fig 9 :54:

> **"And when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again."**

The world literature already shows the pattern. Monnard and Walde (2015) argue compartments are near-universal and likely prebiological; Fine and Pearlman (2023) argue RNA preceded coded proteins while conceding encapsulation remains unclear; Robinson (2005) reports genes-first and metabolism-first models with neither favored. Each rule works on a different subset. Under Fig 9 the correct move is to split the question into D1 (can a boundary form and persist prebiotically?), D2 (can a replicator arise and copy with sufficient fidelity?), D3 (what couples them into a Darwinian unit?), and then examine the intersection. Asking the original either/or question before the split is asking a single lever to clear two conditions; Fig 9 :55-56 says the second condition still binds.

This is triangulated with Fig 11 :43 (*"The habitat is enumerated, never composed"*) because the two conditions are read off different habitats, and with Fig 1 :19 (*"The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated"*) because averaging the two literatures into a single "early life" story is exactly how the live signal disappears.

**Refuter:** show that one and the same mechanism, under one instrument, simultaneously produces the boundary and the heredity in a dated sequence. Then the union collapses to a partition and the split is wrong.

### F2: No figure line licenses a historical order (gap, triangulated on the limit)

Searched first: Fig 9 :44 *"Only a sealed room can be searched to the end, and no frame is a sealed room. So searching and finding nothing proves nothing."* The figure set contains no line that returns a date or a temporal order for chemical events. Searched inward: Fig 6 :29 asks whether the representation can hold the residual, and :40 answers the instrument question directly:

> **"What is unavailable is a sensor for something that has never registered anywhere, at any resolution."**

Searched outward: Fig 8 :39 *"So a search that finds nothing is never a proof of absence."* The external frames (geochemistry, paleontology, prebiotic chemistry) hold candidate mechanisms but not a dated sequence for the transition, as the world sources record.

Fig 13 :134 states the correct verdict: **"Unreachable is a verdict here, not a silence"**. This is a `gap` on the literal "which came first"; it is not a silence and not a refusal to answer. The answer is that the figures govern the shape of any candidate answer and leave the temporal order to a ground that does not yet exist.

**Refuter:** a temporally resolved ground reading (a dated prebiotic sequence from the rock record or a laboratory reconstruction with a demonstrated order) that makes one order observable. Until then this finding stands.

### F3: A boundary is a space state and a filter, not a "before" (general)

Fig 13 :10 lists space as *"near, far, between, contained"*. Containment is a spatial relation, one of the six substrate states. Fig 4 :21:

> **"The membrane is the boundary between the sizes, and the rule about what may cross it. Not a wall: a filter with a direction."**

A compartment in the figures is a maintained boundary that filters exchange and makes an inside possible. Nothing in these lines orders it before or after a replicator. Joyce and Szostak (2018) report that vesicle assembly appears *"straightforward and perhaps inevitable"* while *"The real problem is not protocell formation, but protocell replication."* That is a statement about difficulty, not a figure-derived order; it is recorded as world-corroboration only.

**Refuter:** derive a figure line in which a boundary term has a temporal index relative to a copying term. None exists in the current set after re-census.

### F4: A replicator is a cycle that must close, not a chain (specified, triangulated)

Fig 12 :121:

> **"The output re-enters as input. The sign of the loop decides whether it closes, and a chain cannot close by construction."**

Fig 12 :119:

> **"A part that survives its own use. Spent and regenerating parts behave differently across uses, and nothing records which a term is."**

A replicating molecule is Fig 12's cycle; a catalyst that survives its own use is the functional-group member that makes copying cumulative. A mere chain of monomers cannot close, so "replication" is not an extra molecule but a bond structure over the same atoms. Fig 12 :111: *"A vocabulary without bonds can name a state and cannot name a route to one."* This is the figure-grounded reason the question cannot be answered by listing ingredients (lipids, RNA, clay) alone.

Triangulated with Fig 12 :119 (catalyst) and the world source Fine and Pearlman (2023) on ribozymes and autocatalytic feedback. The figure definition governs; the chemistry is a low-overlap corroboration.

**Refuter:** exhibit a self-copying chemical system whose copying is a chain rather than a closed cycle across successive generations. Then the cycle reading fails for that system.

### F5: "Before" asks a bond-type question, and the bond is not decided (specified)

The Operators table distinguishes **sequence** (Operators :30: *"A then B; B needs A to have happened"*) from **production** (Operators :36: *"A produces B; B did not exist before"*). The question's word "before" invites sequence; the world literature often actually asserts production or mutual dependence (a boundary concentrates monomers, which then polymerize; a replicator's products recruit membrane material).

My first reading tried to write the D1/D2 relation as production (boundary produces replicator). **Retracted after the strain test.** Production requires the second operand to be absent until the first fires, and both world models hold the other component present or co-emerging (Monnard and Walde 2015; Robinson 2005). A symmetric reading (conjunction, both hold at once) fits the coupling data better, and under a symmetric bond order is not a term (Operators :59). Strain resolved by re-census: the order claim belongs to domain D3, where the figure answer is a split, not a bond. This retraction is kept visible.

**Refuter:** show that one of the two operands is genuinely absent until the other fires, in a dated ground measurement. Then the production bond is licensed and the symmetric reading fails.

### F6: The capability is in the coupling, not in either component alone (general, triangulated)

Fig 11 :39:

> **"capability is a property of agent-and-habitat, never of the agent alone."**

Fig 11 :40: *"A system cannot be made more capable by improving the agent in isolation. An improvement that does not change contact changes nothing, however much it improves."*

Applied to this residual: a better membrane alone does not produce a lineage, and a faster replicator alone does not produce a unit of selection. The capability (Darwinian evolution) belongs to the coupled protocell-and-habitat. This is why the compartment-first versus replicator-first framing is a category error at D3: it asks which component carries a property that only the compound has.

World corroboration is strong and low overlap: Schrum, Zhu and Szostak (2010) write that *"The integration of a dynamic fatty-acid compartment with robust, generalized genetic polymer replication would yield a laboratory model of a protocell with the potential for classical Darwinian biological evolution"*; Joyce and Szostak (2018) write that *"compartmentalization is an essential step on the path to biology."*

**Refuter:** observe sustained, open-ended Darwinian evolution of a replicator population with no boundary at any scale. That would move capability back to the agent alone and break this finding.

### F7: The slot is a shape, not a chemical name (general)

The framework returns *"a shape, not a name"* (figures README, canonical local copy) and Fig 9 :32 *"you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself"*. What the figures license here is: a maintained boundary that filters (Fig 4 :21), a cycle that closes (Fig 12 :121), and a coupling that makes variation heritable (Fig 11 :39). Which chemical instantiation fills each slot (fatty acid vesicle, coacervate, mineral pore; RNA, pre-RNA, PNA) is a binding from the world frame, not a derivation, and carries no import that moves the wall (Fig 6 :21: *"MINT composes inside the closure and can never add an atom."*).

**Correction to my own first pass.** I initially wrote that the framework forbids naming any component at all. That overstates the rule. The README's phrase governs a **missing** primitive; here the named items are world-frame chemicals, not members of the six-term substrate. What is forbidden is claiming the derivation selects one chemical, or that the selection adds a primitive. Both claims are now withdrawn; the binding remains recorded as provenance. Compare `05_what_shape_means.md` §5b and the exemplar's correction in `06_isomers_and_order.md` Q1.

**Refuter:** a figure derivation that selects fatty acids over coacervates, or RNA over pre-RNA, from the six-term set alone. It cannot be done without an import, and an import needs a source gate and shadow test (Fig 8).

## 4. Pre-registered refuter (written before the conclusion)

Required before outreach: a falsifier written before the run is a design instrument. The shape claim is refuted if any of the following holds.

1. A figure line is exhibited that returns a temporal order between a boundary term and a copying term, with no import. (Breaks F2.)
2. A dated ground measurement shows one component present and the other absent across the transition in a way that licenses the sequence or production bond. (Breaks F2 and F5.)
3. Sustained Darwinian evolution is observed in a replicator population with no boundary at any scale. (Breaks F6.)
4. A single mechanism, under one instrument, is shown to produce both components simultaneously, collapsing the union into a partition. (Breaks F1.)
5. A self-copying system is exhibited whose copying is a chain rather than a closed cycle across generations. (Breaks F4.)

If any fires, the shape claim is withdrawn or narrowed. Until one is run, nothing here is labelled `grounded`.

### Seven-question recursive derivation (summary)

1. **Is there one fact here?** No. Fig 9 :4-5 splits it; the two models work on different subsets.
2. **What is the residual?** Absence of a unit of selection, per slot (Fig 1 :19), not an average over "early life".
3. **What is a compartment in the figures?** A maintained filter and a space state (contained), Fig 4 :21 and Fig 13 :10. No temporal index.
4. **What is a replicator in the figures?** A closed cycle with a surviving catalyst, Fig 12 :121 and :119. A chain cannot close.
5. **Which bond connects them?** The question's "before" invites sequence; the data fit mutual dependence (symmetric conjunction). Production strained and was retracted (F5). Under a symmetric bond, order is not a term.
6. **Can the order be imported?** No sensor exists for a sequence that never registered at any resolution (Fig 6 :40). Imports need a source gate and shadow test (Fig 8); none supplies a dated order.
7. **What is the stop?** `gap` on the literal order, `general` on the split-and-coupling shape. Fig 13 :134: *"Unreachable is a verdict here, not a silence."*

Every specific temporal claim (compartment first or replicator first as a figure verdict) is refused.
