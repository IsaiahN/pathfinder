### Q2: Cycle versus chain: what makes a replicator a replicator

Fig 12 :121:

> **"cycle. The output re-enters as input. The sign of the loop decides whether it closes, and a chain cannot close by construction."**

Fig 12 :119:

> **"catalyst. A part that survives its own use. Spent and regenerating parts behave differently across uses, and nothing records which a term is."**

A chain of nucleotides is a degenerate molecule: Fig 12 :48 says *"A chain is the degenerate case: one bond type, no branching. Nothing about a chain is a different kind of object."* Replication is not an extra ingredient; it is the cycle bond closing over the same atoms. A catalyst that survives its own use is what makes successive cycles cumulative rather than a single event.

Applied to the two candidate components:

- A compartment that grows and divides is a cycle (output membrane becomes input material for daughters), documented experimentally by Schrum, Zhu and Szostak (2010) and Joyce and Szostak (2018).
- A replicator that copies is a cycle, with the template re-entering as input. Fine and Pearlman (2023) report the ribozyme and autocatalytic-feedback evidence, while noting the complete coupled demonstration has not been achieved.
- So both components are cycles. Neither is "before" in the bond sense; they are two cycles whose closures must become compatible.

**Falsifier:** a self-copying chemical system whose copying remains a chain across generations, with no re-entry of output as input. That breaks the replicator-is-a-cycle finding.

### Q3: Are compartment and replicator isomers?

Fig 12 :117: **"Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."**

Fig 12 :124: **"functional group. A substructure whose presence fixes the outcome. The group says what kind; the qualifier says which member."**

In the figures' own atom set, both components decompose to the same six terms: a boundary is space (contained) bonded to a structure (network or gradient), and a replicator is energy and time bonded through a cycle over a structure. They are not different substances in the isomer sense, because they are not built from a shared closed atom set in the way two molecules are; they are different **arrangements of the same substrate kinds**. The stronger isomer claim would require both to be written over the full shared six-term set with only the mediating structure differing. That is a testable structural claim, but it is not figure-derivable from the essay set alone, and no instrument here settles it. Mark `specified`, unmeasured.

**Falsifier:** exhibit a compartment arrangement requiring an atom the replicator arrangement does not, or vice versa. That kills the isomer reading and leaves a functional-group reading instead.

### Q4: What about first-order and second-order composition?

Fig 12 :104: **"A settled molecule becomes an atom for whatever composes over it. So irreducible is a fact about a level and never about the world"**

Each component is a first-order composition (state, structure, state, with a closing cycle). Once a protocell is settled against the ground, it becomes an atom for whatever composes over it: populations, competition, Darwinian evolution. That is the recursion Fig 12 names, and it is why the capability appears at the second order rather than in either first-order component (compare Fig 11 :39-40). The union of the two first-order cycles is a second-order object, which is exactly what Fig 9 :55-58 says a compound becomes: *"A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones."*

**Type caveat, kept open:** Fig 12 :121's cycle sign decides whether the loop closes. At second order the two cycles must close compatibly (membrane growth coupled to copying rate). Fine and Pearlman (2023) report that this coupling has not been demonstrated in a prebiotically plausible system. This is a strain in the world data, not a figure conflict; it is named, not hidden.

## CONCLUSION

The word "before" is not licensed as a sequence or production bond over the six-term substrate. Within the closure, compartment and replicator are co-present cycles, so the bond that fits is symmetric conjunction, under which order is not a term (Operators :59). A genuine temporal order appears only in world-frame geological scenarios (mineral chambers before lipids), which must be imported with provenance and a shadow test and cannot be derived here. Both components are cycles over the same six-term kinds; the isomer question is a `specified` structural claim with a stated falsifier, not a result. The second-order union is where Darwinian capability lives.

This framing rescues what is defensible in both camps without pretending the figures date the transition. The chemical instantiations remain bindings from the world frame, and the literal order remains a gap (Fig 13 :134: *"Unreachable is a verdict here, not a silence"*).
