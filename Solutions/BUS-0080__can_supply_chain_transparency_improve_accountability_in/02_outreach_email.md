# Outreach draft — check the discriminator before you decide about the framework

**Use:** one-to-one, or as a cover note attached to a submission through a public venue in `03_public_contact_points.csv`. **Not** for bulk send.

**Hard precondition:** do not send until the mode labels and the gap on "often" in `01_figure_grounded_audit.md` are intact in the text below. The offer collapses on contact if the frequency claim is smuggled in as a result.

---

**Subject:** One checkable claim about disclosure and consequences, before any theory

> Dear [name],
>
> I have one specific claim I would like you to check against work you already know. I am not asking you to read a framework first, and I am not asking you to agree with anything. If the claim does not survive, there is nothing else to look at.
>
> **The claim (`specified`, not `grounded`):** supply-chain transparency and accountability are two conditions, not one. Disclosure, audits and certificates are quantities the reporting system produces about itself. They are useful as a shared convention and as a way to aim an investigation. They cannot be the measure of accountability, because a measure produced by the thing being measured cannot settle the question. Accountability needs a consequence that does not move when the firm disputes the report: a penalty actually levied, back pay actually paid, an injury rate actually reduced, a contract actually terminated.
>
> The claim predicts a discrimination, not a slogan. Where published information reaches a frame with consequences that can be violated, disclosure can change what happens. Where it stays inside the reporting frame, it changes only the report. The framework returns that shape of the discriminator, not a list of tools and not a frequency.
>
> **What I am not claiming:** that transparency never works, that certification is always harmful, or that this derivation shows the phenomenon is common. The word "often" in the question is a frequency claim. I have not measured it, and I mark it as a gap. A literature that leans one way is not an anchor.
>
> **What would refute the claim, written down before this message:**
> 1. A disclosure measure is in ordinary use whose value is set by the anchor and which moves when the anchor moves but not when the report changes.
> 2. Publication alone, with no enforcement, reliably produces remediation across multiple suppliers and multiple slots.
> 3. Audit scores predict worker outcomes out of sample, measured by a source that does not draw on the auditor's own evidence.
> 4. A disclosure vocabulary carries a route, a threshold, a per-slot progress subtraction and a standing refusal, and itself delivers consequences.
>
> A one-line "this fails because..." is far more useful to me than agreement. If you would rather not engage, no reply is needed.
>
> **If it survives, and only then:** here are the sources. The verbatim figures are the authority, not my summary of them.
>
> - Verbatim figure text with line numbers (local extract): `FRAMEWORK/reference/03-FIGURES_TEXT.md`
> - The full audit with the census and the retraction log: `01_figure_grounded_audit.md`
> - The independent second-seat check: `11_audit.md`
>
> **If you want to reuse the machinery:** the eight-step loop is in `THE_FORMULA.md` inside the framework. Run it on a problem you know well and can check against a ground. Write down what would refute your result before you run it, and label the mode of what you get. A reported failure is more useful to me than a reported success.
>
> — [name]

## Stage order (do not compress)

1. **Stage 1:** send the claim, its mode and its refuter. Ask what the recipient would count as a failure. Record the verdict before anything else is shown.
2. **Stage 2, opt-in only:** if they are curious, offer the figure text and the audit. Their later agreement is never pooled as corroboration (Fig 2 :40).
3. **Stage 3:** offer the formula as a method to reuse, with the instruction that each user writes their own refuter and states their own mode.

A stage-1 verdict that arrives after the recipient has read the framework is not a stage-1 verdict. Keep the order.
