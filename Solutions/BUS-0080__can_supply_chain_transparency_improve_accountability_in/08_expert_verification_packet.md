# Expert verification packet — the Tether on supply-chain transparency and accountability

**What this is:** a claim in a form a researcher in supply chain transparency or business ethics can check. Mode, derivation and refuters are stated before the conclusion, so a reader knows what to look for and what would settle it.

**Mode: `general` on the shape, `specified` on the conditional verdict, `gap` on the word "often". Not `grounded`.** No frequency or outcome effect is claimed as measured by this derivation.

**Status:** simulated derivation checked for internal consistency against the verbatim figures, not a ground reading. It cannot settle the question it reports on.

## 1. The claim, in one paragraph

Disclosure, audits and certificates are quantities the reporting frame produces about itself. They are efficient as conventions and as filters for where to investigate, but they cannot be the verdict on accountability, because a frame cannot score itself with a quantity it also produces. Accountability requires an anchor from a different closure: a consequence that does not change when the disclosed party disputes the report. The two conditions form a union compound, so publishing more clears disclosure-only cases and leaves the intersection standing. Reporting vocabulary names states but lacks a route, a threshold, a progress subtraction and a refusal. The conditional verdict is that transparency changes what happens only when it crosses contact with a violable anchor. How often that happens is a `gap`, not a result.

## 2. Three checkable sub-claims

### C1 — Frame-internal quantities cannot be the verdict (checkable by reading Figure 1 and Figure 2)

- **Statement:** certificates, audit scores and disclosure counts are outputs of the reporting frame. They cannot settle accountability. A convention that decides an outcome has stopped being a convention.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 1 :27 — *"They are frame-internal. A frame cannot score itself with a quantity it also produces."* Fig 2 :29-30 — *"An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks."* Fig 2 :43 — *"a convention that decides an outcome has stopped being one."*
- **Refuter:** exhibit a disclosure measure in ordinary use whose value is set by the anchor, which moves when the anchor moves and not when the report changes; or show that the convention in question does not decide any outcome. Note the second route is real: a certificate used only for coordination is not refuted by this sub-claim.

### C2 — Disclosure and consequence form a union compound (checkable by reading Figure 9 and Figure 6)

- **Statement:** the question bundles two conditions. A single lever acting on disclosure cannot close the intersection where consequence also binds. More disclosure inside the same closure cannot compose an atom from another closure.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 9 :56 — *"A single lever clears the region where only one condition holds and leaves the intersections standing, because the second condition still binds."* Fig 6 :23 — *"No frame escapes its own closure. The bound moves only on contact with a frame whose closure differs."* Fig 9 :60 — *"Use filters for the budget and witnesses for the verdict. Never let a filter hand you a verdict."*
- **Refuter:** show that consequence follows necessarily from disclosure in the same operation with no external rule adding a spendable difference; or show a case where iterating disclosure inside the frame produced a consequence not previously composable. If either holds, the union census is wrong and C2 fails.

### C3 — The reporting vocabulary cannot carry its own correction (checkable by reading Figure 12)

- **Statement:** a report names a state. Accountability needs an ordered route, a threshold comparison, a per-slot progress subtraction and a standing refusal. An accumulating vocabulary can be corrected and cannot carry the correction.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 12 :111 — *"A vocabulary without bonds can name a state and cannot name a route to one."* Fig 12 :112 — *"A plan is a sequence. A goal is a comparison. Progress is a subtraction. A refutation is a negation, and that one is not a bond."*
- **Refuter:** exhibit a disclosure system whose own vocabulary carries all four and which itself delivers consequences, while still being correctly described as only reporting and certification. If it delivers consequences, C3 is false as worded even if C1 and C2 stand.

## 3. What is explicitly not claimed

- Not that transparency never improves accountability. Import from a different closure is permitted (Fig 6 :14: *"the only operator that moves the wall"*).
- Not that certification is always harmful. Fig 2 :41: *"agreement about a shared meaning is the product, not a corruption of it."*
- Not that this happens "often". No frequency was measured. This is a `gap` in F9, not a hedged result.
- Not that a particular standard, platform or law is the answer. The framework returns a shape, not a name.
- Not `grounded`. Nothing here was settled by an outcome study.
- Not that the chosen anchor (worker outcomes, enforcement, remediation) is nature's own list. It is a declared choice for this domain and its stake is declared.

## 5. What would settle it

An outcome measurement with an anchor that does not move when the firm disputes it. Specific tests:

1. Does the measure track the anchor rather than the report? (C1)
2. Does publication alone, with no enforcement, remediate across multiple suppliers and slots? (C2)
3. Do audit scores predict worker outcomes out of sample, from a source that does not share the auditor's evidence pool? (C1, C2)
4. Does the reporting vocabulary carry route, threshold, progress and refusal, and does it deliver consequences? (C3)
5. With a stated sampling frame and instrument, how often does disclosure actually cross into consequence? (closes the gap)

Fig 10 :18: *"a declared meaning is not a grounded one"*; Fig 10 :19: *"install what can be violated"*.

## 6. What an expert can check in twenty minutes

1. Read Fig 1 :27, Fig 2 :29-30 and :43, Fig 9 :56 and :60, Fig 6 :23, Fig 12 :111-112. Seven short passages.
2. Check C1 against one disclosure measure you know: who sets its value, the reporting system or the anchor?
3. Check C3 against one reporting standard you know: can it say "this certificate is false" with the same standing as "this certificate is valid"?
4. Name one instrument that would close the frequency gap, or say why none is possible.
5. Send the refutation. A failure is more useful than an endorsement.
