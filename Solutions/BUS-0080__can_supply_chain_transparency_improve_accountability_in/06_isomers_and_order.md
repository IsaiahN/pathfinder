# Union, order and the missing operators — supporting derivation

**Mode: `general` on the structure, `specified` on the application to supply chains.** Simulated derivation checked against the figures, not a ground reading (see `05_what_shape_means.md` §7).

## RESIDUAL

The question bundles at least two conditions: visibility/disclosure and consequence/enforcement. Is this one problem or a compound, and what follows about the order in which a remedy can be attempted? A secondary question: is disclosure a state term or does it carry a route to a remedy?

## DOMAIN CENSUS

- **R:** `report(s) - ground(s)` per slot `s`. Arity two conditions, union not partition (Fig 9 :55-59). Symmetry: ordered, consequence is an import from a different closure (Fig 6 :14). Scale: first order inside the reporting frame, second order at the governance frame.
- **D1 disclosure:** actors firm, auditor, standard setter; currency documents and scores; no nameable anchor inside the frame.
- **D2 consequence:** actors inspector, worker, buyer, court; currency penalties, back pay, remediation, termination; anchor outcomes that do not update on the firm's sayings.
- **D3 observation:** actors researchers and journalists; currency measured outcomes; anchor the ground, never agreement.
- **Order by dependency:** perception to vocabulary to objective to planning to learn and carry (Fig 3).

## DERIVATION

### Q1 — Union or partition?

Fig 9 :55: **"A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones."**
Fig 9 :56: **"A single lever clears the region where only one condition holds and leaves the intersections standing, because the second condition still binds."**
Fig 9 :59: **"it stops being a mixture and becomes a specific compound."**

Let `D` = disclosure present, `C` = consequence able to act on what is disclosed.

- `D and not C`: reporting and certification only. The disclosure frame is self-scoring (Fig 1 :27).
- `C and not D`: enforcement on a chain nobody mapped. Consequences exist but cannot be aimed at the specific slot.
- `D and C`: a specific compound with its own ground. This is the only region in which the question's Option A can be true.
- `neither`: invisible and unenforced.

A single lever (publish more) manipulates `D` only. It clears the first region and leaves the intersection unchanged, because `C` still binds. This is exactly the figure's union warning. The practical corollary: asking "does transparency improve accountability?" is asking about the compound, while most transparency interventions act on `D` only.

**Falsifier, written before the run:** if `C` follows necessarily and immediately from `D` in the same operation, the two conditions are one and the union reading fails. Locally, enforcement sometimes does follow publication automatically (a mandatory registry that triggers an inspection). That is a case where the two conditions are coupled by a rule. The falsifier is not fired by such a coupling, because the coupling is itself the imported atom `C`, authored in a different closure. It would fire only if `C` appeared without any such external rule.

### Q2 — Where does the chain stop?

Fig 3 :20: **"The chain is ordered by dependency, not by importance."**
Fig 3 :24: **"Why the middle link is where chains usually break."**
Fig 3 :27: **"A link with no instrument is not a link that works. It is a link nobody has looked at."**
Fig 3 :19: **"Optimising a link that already works cannot rescue one that does not."**

Transparency instruments are strong at links 1 and 2 (perception, vocabulary) because those links have obvious tests: a supplier list can be counted, a form can be checked. Link 3 (objective: what counts as progress here) has no obvious instrument. It is the one link where "we published the list" is silently substituted for "the injury rate fell". Effort spent improving link 1 cannot rescue link 3. Fig 3 :29: **"Before asking whether a system is good, ask which link it currently stops at, and whether the answer was measured or assumed."**

**Falsifier:** an independent instrument that measures link 3 directly and shows it working in the same systems where link 1 works. If such an instrument exists and is in ordinary use, the "usually breaks at the middle link" application is wrong for this domain, even though the dependency order in Fig 3 stands.

### Q3 — Is a report a state or a route?

Fig 12 :111: **"A vocabulary without bonds can name a state and cannot name a route to one."**
Fig 12 :112: **"A plan is a sequence. A goal is a comparison. Progress is a subtraction. A refutation is a negation, and that one is not a bond."**
Operators table, `→`: *"A then B; B needs A to have happened."* `⇒`: *"A produces B; B did not exist before."* `⋛`: *"a threshold relation between two quantities."* `¬`: *"this does not hold."*

A supplier map names states: who is present, what they declared. It does not express:

- the ordered route from finding to remedy (`→`),
- the production of a consequence that did not exist before (`⇒`), which is what an enforced penalty is,
- the threshold comparison that makes "acceptable" more than a label (`⋛`),
- the subtraction between two readings at the same slot that would make progress sayable (`−`),
- the standing to refuse a claim (`¬`), which is why a vocabulary that only accumulates can correct nothing.

The last point is decisive. A certificate registry is an accumulating vocabulary. It can add statements and can revise a status, but it cannot carry *"this certificate is false"* as a term with the same standing as the certificate. Formula step 3: *"a refusing term pays the same bargain. What it removes is not a term but a term's standing to be composed over."* Without `¬`, the report cannot carry its own correction.

**Falsifier:** a disclosure system whose vocabulary contains all five operators and which itself delivers consequences. If such a system exists and is still correctly described as "only reporting and certification", Q3 is false. If it delivers consequences, it has crossed into D2 and the F2 verdict stands regardless.

### Q4 — What order do the two conditions compose in?

Fig 13 :24-25: **network**, *"that entities relate, and influence travels"*.
Fig 13 :22-23: **gradient**, *"a difference that can be spent"*.
Fig 13 :74: **"Everything else is a composition across the two kinds, never within one."**

The supply chain is a network (a structure). A disclosure is also a structure in the sense that it establishes relations and lets influence travel along them: once published, information moves. But information moving is not a gradient, because nothing about the movement is spendable. Gradient becomes available only when there is a difference that can be spent: a buyer can walk away, a regulator can fine, a worker can withhold labour. Those are consequences, and they live in D2.

So the order is: network (the chain and the disclosure flow) crosses a state (a fact about conditions at a slot) into a gradient (a spendable difference at the governance level). Publishing a network is not the same as composing a gradient over it. The disclosure frame can name the network and the state; only the governance frame can supply the gradient.

This is why more visibility does not automatically become more pressure. Fig 13 :33: *"Neither kind generates anything alone. Neither denotes anything alone."*

**Falsifier:** a case where publication alone creates a spendable difference with no actor able to spend it. That is a contradiction in terms, so the standing falsifier is different and empirical: show a disclosed fact that changed behaviour without any actor acquiring a spendable consequence. If that is common, the gradient requirement is wrong for this domain.

### Q5 — Is success measurable within the disclosure frame?

Fig 1 :27: **"They are frame-internal. A frame cannot score itself with a quantity it also produces."**
Fig 5 :49: **"Machinery firing is not the milestone. A running system can mint and still return nothing new, which is exactly what the third guard exists to record."**
Fig 12 :125: **"A composition costs in two currencies, and they do not add."**

Counting certificates and counting remediated injuries are not two readings of one quantity. They are different currencies, and one of them is produced by the frame that is being scored. Summing them into a "transparency and accountability index" is the error Fig 12 :130 names: *"Summing them into one figure is the error this names, not the fix for it."*

**Falsifier:** a single measure whose value is invariant to changes in the reporting system and sensitive to changes in the ground. That measure could be composed over. None was found in the sources read, and the studies read explicitly separate compliance from effectiveness (Vandenbroucke 2023) and monitoring from remediation (Locke et al. 2007).

## CONCLUSION

The question is a union compound whose intersection is the only region where Option A can hold. Disclosure tools act on the first condition, usually stop at the middle link of the dependency chain, and speak a vocabulary that names states but cannot carry a route, a production, a threshold, a progress subtraction or a refusal. The spendable difference that would turn visibility into pressure belongs to a different closure and arrives by import. On the frequency word "often", this derivation says nothing: that is a `gap` to be closed by measurement, not by more composition.
