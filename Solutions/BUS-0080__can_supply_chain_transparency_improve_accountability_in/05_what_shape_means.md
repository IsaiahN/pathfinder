## 1. A shape is a constraint, not a filler

Fig 9 :32: *"you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself."* The figures README's phrase is *"a shape, not a name."*

From *The Library of Babel*: *"You can cut your search short if the set of books returned is small and descriptive enough that it provides a general shape of the answer that satisfies your question."*

For BUS-0080, the shape is not a transparency platform, a standard, a certification scheme or a disclosure metric. Those are names. The shape is:

> A discriminator with two conditions. Disclosure reaches perception and vocabulary inside the reporting frame. Consequence lives in a different closure and arrives only as an import with a source gate, a shadow test and an anchor that does not move when the reported party pushes on it. Where the import is present, disclosure can change what happens. Where it is absent, disclosure changes only the report.

That constrains any answer. It does not fill in which standard, which law or which platform works.

## 2. How the shape was obtained

- *Library of Babel*: use the vocabulary closest to the thing and recurse on recurring terms. *"With each new term gained, you narrow the space and deepen your vocabulary."* Here the closest terms were "disclosure", "audit", "certification", "compliance" and "outcome". Their proximity immediately surfaced the split the literature itself makes between visibility and disclosure (Kraft, Yu and Zheng, 2023) and between compliance and effectiveness (Vandenbroucke, 2023).
- Fig 6 :30: *"The description travels. Fails by being sharpened into terms only we can read. Cast it in effect terms."* Fig 9 :30-31: *"your words are borrowed too, so describe the effect rather than the cause."* The residual was therefore stated as `report(s) - ground(s)` per slot, not as "lack of transparency".
- *Decoding Tacit Knowledge*: *"When contact isn't possible, the best you can send is a plain description of what you'd expect to see, because anyone can check that against the world."* That is the outreach order in `02_outreach_email.md`.
- *Library of Babel* on stopping: the finish line is where the answer was composed during the search. Here the search composed a discriminator. It did not compose a frequency.

## 3. What licenses a name

Two things, both outside the shape itself (`THE_FORMULA.md`, step 5, and `01_figure_grounded_audit.md` §3):

1. **The ground settles it.** A measured frequency, a demonstrated consequence, an instrument that discriminates reporting from remediation. Formula step 5: *"the ground settles it. Verified only by what pays. A gate is not the ground."*
2. **A provenanced import from a frame whose closure differs** (Fig 6 :23, Fig 8 :50-52), with a source gate and a shadow test.

*They're Scanning Books When They Should* sharpens this: *"The real import is not a new term in the existing space. It is a new dimension of the space."* It also warns: *"A minimum viable set of six terms is a human's careful guess at reality's compression axes; another sensorium's minimum set need not be those six."* The choice of "worker outcome" as the anchor is a visible choice, not nature's own list. Declaring it does not make it neutral, and it must be declared.

## 4. Why a report cannot name its own source

*The Hard Problem is About Source* gives the mechanism directly. An instrument that detects green *"is speaking only about what it measured, it cannot comment on the source."* Several underlying causes can give the same reading: *"If several paths create the same behavior, then behavior is a projection with lossy information."*

Applied here: an audit score reads green. The source might be genuine remediation, a factory prepared for the visit, a coached worker, a selective excerpt of records or an auditor paid by the party being audited. The reading does not determine the source. The essay's resolution was instrumental, not argumentative: *"It was proven wrong not by argument, but by the arrival of a better instrument."* This is the load-bearing reason F1 and F2 in `01` refuse to treat a certificate as an anchor.

*How Intelligence Actually Works* adds the video-frame example: the frame and the photograph "are indistinguishable as artifacts, and they carry different warrants", and nothing in the image records which. A disclosure artifact carries no warrant for how it was produced. That is provenance loss, and the lineage has to be recorded.

## 5. Q2: what licenses a particular transparency tool?

**Nothing in the framework.** The framework returns the condition the tool must meet: it must cross contact with an anchor that can be violated. A particular standard or platform is licensed by the ground (a measured consequence) or by a provenanced import with a shadow test. Until one of those, the choice is the searcher's contribution and must be recorded as such.

This is not the same error as the QMGR exemplar's naming of Gradient. There the named object was already one of the six held atoms, so the issue was a binding from another frame. Here "worker outcome" is not a primitive at all; it is the anchor chosen for this domain. The framework never selects it. The selection must be declared and its stake named.

## 5b. Correction to my own first reading

I initially wrote that the framework's "shape, not a name" rule forbids naming any anchor. That overstates it. The README's phrase governs *"what a missing thing would have to look like (a shape, not a name)"*, i.e. a primitive not held in the library. An anchor is not a primitive to be composed; it is the ground for this domain, and a domain without a nameable anchor is not a domain yet (Fig 2 :29-30, master plan §8). So naming the anchor is required by the census, not forbidden by the shape rule. What remains forbidden is naming a *tool* as if the framework had selected it. Both statements are kept here so neither is quietly lost.

## 6. Prestige is not action

*The Recursive Self-Improvement Trap*: *"Prestige must never be allowed to validate an action directly... Someone let prestige stand in for proof. Someone let reputation substitute for result. Someone let the trusted name validate the untested claim."* And the operative sentence: *"Prestige can fund an action. It can give an action a budget to be tried. But it cannot settle an action. Only the ground can settle it. The Ground Maintainer must be blind to prestige."*

For this question, a well known certifier or a widely adopted disclosure standard gives the claim a budget to be tried. It does not make the claim true. A campaign that gathers famous signatories to a transparency pledge is the named collapse, not corroboration. The legitimate ask is for a refutation against outcomes.

## 7. My own audit is a simulation

Same essay: *"LLMs can quickly simulate an approximate answer/output... However, the truly testable output would need to be derived from the real architecture or working the formula yourself."* The article's demo caveat is blunter: outputs generated this way *"must be seen as simulacra"* and *"could never be verified if the solutions presented were not contrived to fit the formula."*

So `01_figure_grounded_audit.md`, `06_isomers_and_order.md`, `07_the_tether_answer.md` and `09_watson_answer.md` are simulated derivations checked for internal consistency against the figures. They cannot settle the question. This is stated on every substantive file, not just once.

## 8. The society and the frontier, applied

*How a Society of Agents Would Work*: *"capability is a property of agent-and-habitat, never of the agent alone."* A disclosure tool dropped into a supply chain without enforcement is a better reader without more contact. The essay's own falsifier applies: an agent-only improvement that moved the ground would refute the capability claim.

*They're Scanning Books* closes the loop: the generator is *"discovery under consequence"*, a random walk plus a model that can be surprised. A reporting system has the walk (more data) but no model that consequences can violate. That is Fig 10 :19 (*"install what can be violated"*) stated in the language of the essays.
