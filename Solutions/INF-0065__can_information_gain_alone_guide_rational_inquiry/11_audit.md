## Round trip gap R_T

The Symbols table defines R_T as `gap(x, (T_E ∘ T_A)(x))`, the gap between what was sent up and what came back down. Fig 4 :30 requires it to be measured rather than assumed. I ran the round trip on the condensation the folder actually uses.

1. **Sent up (x):** the verbatim figure set, chiefly Fig 1 :19, :23, :27, :28; Fig 5 :42-48; Fig 12 :125-130; Fig 2 :29, :42; Fig 9 :55, :60; Fig 11 :40.
2. **Abstraction (T_A):** the shorthand carried in `07_the_tether_answer.md`: gain is a filter and a gradient generator, not a guide; relevance, cost, and consequences must be combined; the combination is a union.
3. **Concretisation (T_E):** a field-level objective, for example expected information gain minus a cost term, with a relevance filter and a decision-weighted utility.

**R_T is nonzero.** Four things the shorthand holds do not survive the trip back down:

- the **slot index** (Fig 1 :19, :28), which a field objective very easily aggregates away;
- the **non-additivity of the two currencies** (Fig 12 :125-130), which a single scalar objective necessarily reintroduces;
- the **union structure** (Fig 9 :55, :57), which a weighted sum silently converts back into a partition-like optimization;
- the **ground range** (Fig 2 :29, :42), which becomes a scoring-rule choice inside the frame rather than a settlement outside it.

Conversely, one thing survives cleanly: a frame cannot score itself with the quantity it also produces, so a gain number cannot be the verdict. That is the residue worth sending. R_T was **not** measured numerically, because no ground reading exists; it is recorded qualitatively as the enumerated set of dropped constraints. Treating "the formula is the condensed version of the metalaws" as identity would set R_T to zero and would be the dead-reckoning error Fig 4 :30 names.
