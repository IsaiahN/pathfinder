## 1. A shape is a constraint, not a filler

The canonical figures README states the limit: the framework returns "what a missing thing would have to look like (**a shape, not a name**)." Fig 9 :32 "you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself". The README's stopping rule: it "stops at the limit of what the available instruments can distinguish, and records the lineage so the stop is honest. Indiscernibility is not identity."

From *The Library of Babel*: "you can cut your search short if the set of books returned is small and descriptive enough that it provides a general shape of the answer that satisfies your question." And: "The problem you have determines what makes a book relevant to you or not."

So for this question a **shape** is the narrowed form of an inquiry policy that can be stated before any particular objective function is named. It constrains; it does not fill.

| not a shape (a name) | the shape |
|---|---|
| "maximize expected KL divergence minus λ times cost" | a move is admitted only after a per-slot residual, a two-currency bargain, and a ground-tied verdict |
| "use information bottleneck with β = 4" | the relevance variable must be fixed by the residual, not by the amount of entropy reduced |
| "gain alone is the correct objective" | gain is a filter and a gradient generator, not the verdict |

The third row is the one this question decides. The first two are bindings from the applied fields, provenanced as such, and are not produced by the framework.

## 2. How the shape is obtained

- *Library of Babel*: start with the vocabulary you already have that is closest to the answer, then "With each new term gained, you are able to narrow the space by definition and deepen your vocabulary." Each pass narrows by proximity, not by counting.
- *Library of Babel* on steering: "The books that you are returning in your searches need to be tested or ranged against the problem you want answered in order to steer the direction of the next search." This is the operational version of "relevance": a candidate is ranged against the residual, not merely scored for size.
- Fig 6 :30 "The description travels. Fails by being sharpened into terms only we can read. Cast it in effect terms." Fig 9 :30-31 "your words are borrowed too, so describe the effect rather than the cause".
- *Decoding Tacit Knowledge*: "When contact isn't possible, the best you can send is a plain description of what you'd expect to see, because anyone can check that against the world." This is why `02_outreach_email.md` sends the residual and the refuter before the theory.
- *Library of Babel* on stopping: "You will know that you are done because the finish line will also be the starting place" - and the paradox that "you have already composed the answer through the process of locating it." For this solution the stop is not a finished objective function; it is the figure set refusing further arrangement.

## 3. What licenses a name in this question

Only two things, both outside the shape itself:

1. **The ground settles it.** `THE_FORMULA.md` step 5: "the ground settles it. Verified only by what pays. A gate is not the ground." A measured performance comparison between a gain-only policy and a four-leg policy would license a name. None is claimed here.
2. **A provenanced import from a frame whose closure differs** (Fig 6 :23, Fig 8 :50-52), with a source gate and a shadow test. The economics and animal-behaviour sources in `09_watson_answer.md` are such imports: each is cited with author, venue, year, and the field it comes from. They corroborate the shape; they do not name the framework's own term.

*They're Scanning Books* sharpens the second route: "The real import is not a new term in the existing space. It is a new dimension of the space." And: "A minimum viable set of six terms is a human's careful guess at reality's compression axes; another sensorium's minimum set need not be those six." Also: "Declaring the primitives does not make them neutral. It makes the choice visible." So stating the four legs does not make them a neutral, exhaustive account of rational inquiry; it makes the constraints this derivation uses visible so they can be revised.

## 4. The specific shape this solution returns

The shape is a four-condition inquiry policy:

1. a residual indexed per slot, with the live slot named before any score is computed (Fig 1 :19, :28);
2. an acceptance bargain that charges both the term and what remains unexplained (Fig 5 :42-44);
3. two prices held apart, bits for the description and actions for finding out (Fig 12 :125-130);
4. a verdict that ranges against a ground the frames cannot move, with independence bought against the cost of being wrong (Fig 2 :29, :42; Fig 9 :60).

Fig 12 :94 "The arrangement is the substance." The four legs are not a checklist whose items can be satisfied one at a time; they are an arrangement, and the bond between them carries information the list does not. Fig 9 :55 says a bundled problem is "a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones." This is why the answer to the question is "must be combined" and not "would be nicer to combine."

## 5. Two consequences for outreach

**Prestige is not action.** From *The Recursive Self-Improvement Trap*: "Prestige can fund an action. It can give an action a budget to be tried. But it cannot settle an action. Only the ground can settle it. The Ground Maintainer must be blind to prestige." The ask in `02_outreach_email.md` gives the claim a budget to be tried; it does not ask for endorsement.

**This audit is a simulation, not a ground reading.** Same essay: "LLMs can quickly simulate an approximate answer/output with the formula found in the architecture post." and "the truly testable output would need to be derived from the real architecture or working the formula yourself." From *Arrival of the Fittest*: the outputs "could never be verified if the solutions presented were not contrived to fit the formula." So `01_figure_grounded_audit.md` is a simulated derivation checked for internal consistency against the figures. It is **not** a test of the claim and cannot settle it.

## 6. Correction to my own §3 and §5

I first wrote that "shape, not a name" forbids naming the four legs, and that the whole answer must stay as `GAIN(???)CONSEQUENCE`. That overstates the rule. The README's phrase governs a **missing** thing, one not held in Γ. Relevance, cost, and consequence are not missing primitives here: they are quantities the figures already hold and govern. Naming them as constraints is not inventing a seventh term. What remains genuinely unnameable is the **weighting** among them and the **specific objective** a field would use. Both require a ground or a provenanced import. The narrower reading is now in force; the earlier one is preserved here rather than erased.

A second correction from *The Hard Problem is About Source*: "If several paths create the same behavior, then behavior is a projection with lossy information (it cannot define consciousness in full)." A reported information-gain score is such a projection: several different inquire-and-act policies can produce the same bits. The instrument that would discriminate them is not a larger gain number but a source-discriminating ground. The essay's conclusion applies directly: "An instrument that can't distinguish these cases demonstrates its own inadequacy." That was the figure-grounded reason for demanding consequences as a separate leg rather than folding them into gain.
