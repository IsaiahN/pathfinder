# Outreach draft: check one claim about inquiry before you decide about the framework

**Use:** one-to-one, or as a cover letter attached to a submission through the public venues in `03_public_contact_points.csv`. **Not** for bulk send.

**Hard precondition:** do not send until the shape claim and its refuter in `08_expert_verification_packet.md` are checked against the verbatim figure text. The offer collapses on contact if the stage-1 output does not survive.

---

**Subject:** A residual about information gain and inquiry, with a refuter written in advance

> Hi [name],
>
> I have one specific claim I would like you to check against what you already know. I am not asking you to read the theory first, and I am not asking you to agree with anything. If the claim does not survive, there is nothing else to look at.
>
> **The residual, in effect terms.** An inquirer must choose a next move: which probe, which term, which frame, which experiment. One answer is to maximize information gain (expected entropy reduction or mutual information) and let that scalar pick the move. The other is to treat gain as a filter for where signal may be, and choose the move only after relevance (which slot, which effect), cost (bits and actions), and consequences (what changes at the ground, and the cost if wrong) are also checked.
>
> **The shape claim (`general`, not `grounded`).** Under the framework's figures, gain alone cannot be the guide. Four independent reasons: a frame cannot score itself with a quantity it also produces; the residual is indexed by slot and a global average throws away the only signal that says where to look; the acceptance rule is a two-sided bargain containing both the cost of the term and the leftover residual; and bits price the description while actions price finding out whether it holds, with the two explicitly not adding. Gain remains useful as a filter and as a curiosity gradient when nothing is scoring. It is not sufficient as a guide. This is a shape, not a measurement.
>
> **What I am not claiming:** that maximizing information gain is never useful, that relevance or cost can be read off a single number, that this resolves any live debate in experimental design or information theory, or that the framework is settled by experiment. "Gain is necessary but not sufficient" is a structural claim at this stage.
>
> **What would refute the shape claim** (written before this message):
> 1. a figure line that makes a self-scored quantity the ground, or gain alone the acceptance test;
> 2. a derivation in which bits and actions are the same currency, or summing them loses nothing;
> 3. a frame that selects a move with no per-slot residual, no cost term, and no ground range, and still settles the residual;
> 4. a re-census in which the four legs form a partition, so gain alone clears everything;
> 5. an agent-only increase in gain that moves the ground while contact stays fixed.
>
> If any one holds, I will retract the claim. A one-line "this fails because..." is more useful to me than agreement. If you would rather not engage, no reply is needed.
>
> **Stage 2, only after you have recorded a verdict on the above:** if you are curious, I can send three short sources: the verbatim figures, the eight-step formula, and the canonical usage note. The figures, not my summary, are the authority. Your agreement after seeing the theory is not corroboration and I will not count it as such.
>
> **Stage 3, opt-in only:** the formula is offered as a method to reuse. Run it on a problem you know well and can check against a ground, write down what would refute your result *before* you run it, and state the mode of what you get. A reported failure is more useful to me than a reported success.
>
> - [your name]
