## Residual in effect terms

An inquirer must choose a next move: which probe, which term to mint, which frame to consult, which experiment to run. Option A says maximize information gain alone (expected entropy reduction, mutual information, compression achieved) and let that scalar pick the move. Option B says information gain is a filter for where signal may exist, but the move is chosen only after relevance (which slot and which effect), cost (bits and actions), and consequences (what the move changes at the ground, and the cost if wrong) are also checked. A sends a self-consistent policy that can aim at the wrong slot, cannot compare currencies, and has no ground-tied stop. B sends a checkable policy with a per-slot residual, a two-currency bargain, and a pre-registered refuter.

## What is deliberately not here

- No personal emails of named individuals.
- No ranking of people by how likely they are to agree with this answer.
- No bulk-send plan.

Three reasons, in the figures' own words:

1. **Agreement is not a verdict.** Fig 2 :35 "Alignment is triangulation, not negotiation. A mirror cannot correct you." Selecting recipients for receptiveness manufactures the correlation that makes their agreement worthless (Seat Map :163).
2. **A verifier must not hold the interiors.** Fig 10 :42 "A verifier that can reconstruct the claim cannot verify it." The first contact carries only the residual and its refuter.
3. **Information gain is not a self-scoring credential.** Fig 1 :27 "They are frame-internal. A frame cannot score itself with a quantity it also produces." A campaign that reports how much attention the claim received is reporting the frame's own output, not the ground's verdict.
