**Subject:** Check one prediction about codified knowledge before you decide whether the framework is worth reading

> Hi [name],
>
> I have one specific output I would like you to check against your own work on tacit knowledge and codification. I am not asking you to read the framework first, and I am not asking you to agree with it. If the output does not survive, there is nothing else to look at.
>
> **The output (`specified`, not `grounded`):** the loss when knowledge moves from local practice into a textbook, database, or policy document is not one defect in the document. It is a union of conditions at four places, each with its own anchor. The move is a directed, lossy transform: what crosses well is a method or a head start, and what usually crosses instead is a recording of one occasion that looks like knowledge. A flat description records the ingredients but not the typed relations between them, and nothing in the notation signals that anything was lost. The reader's habitat is substituted, with two failures that stay invisible until the goal fails: what was not reproduced and what was unintentionally introduced. Lived history does not travel in text, so only a description of effects can cross without contact. The document is a closure with no internal certificate of completeness, and a declared meaning is not a grounded one.
>
> The two-part consequence: sharing a definition is efficient for a convention, where agreement is the product; the same agreement is disqualifying as a verdict about what a practitioner can actually do. So codification helps where the goal is a shared meaning and harms where the goal is competent action with no channel back to the ground.
>
> **What I am not claiming:** that a universal percentage of knowledge is lost, or that any figure line names one. I am also not claiming that documents are useless or that tacitness is mystical. The claim is about which relational information a notation can carry, and what has to be rebuilt through contact.
>
> **What would refute it:**
> 1. a round trip on a named document that returns exactly what was sent, with `R_T = 0` on the relevant slot, against a ground that pays;
> 2. a reader who, from the document alone, reliably produces the competent act with no practitioner contact and no residual;
> 3. a document whose atom list plus explicit bond labels fully determines the competent act, with no residual;
> 4. an instrument that yields a stable universal loss percentage across domains.
>
> I wrote these down before sending this. If any one holds, the corresponding finding is withdrawn. A one-line "this fails because..." is more useful to me than agreement. If you would rather not engage, no reply is needed.
>
> **If it survives, and only then:** the sources are below. The figures are the authority, not my summary of them.
> - The governing essay, on why context and subtext travel but pretext does not: https://adventuresinml.substack.com/p/decoding-tacit-knowledge-why-context
> - Tacit knowledge, the Polanyi and Collins background, and the Bessemer case: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tacit_knowledge
> - Grounding in communication, the repair loop that a document lacks: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grounding_in_communication
>
> **If you want to use the machinery:** the figures and the formula are public in the links in `04_links_and_distribution.md`. Run the round trip on a document you know well. Write down what would refute your result before you run it, and state the mode of what you get. A reported failure is more useful to me than a reported success.
>
> Best,
> [name]
