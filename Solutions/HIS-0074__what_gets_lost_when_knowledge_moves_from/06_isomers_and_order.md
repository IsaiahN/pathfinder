# Structure, order and analogues: which bonds fail to cross

**Mode: `specified` on the structural diagnosis, `general` on the bond-table consequence, not `grounded`.** This is a simulated derivation checked against the figures, not a ground reading (see `05_what_shape_means.md` §6).

## RESIDUAL

`01` F2 and F3 claim that what fails to cross the codification membrane is the typed relation, especially the ordered bonds and negation. Two questions are owed: (1) precisely which bond classes a flat document can carry, and (2) whether the document and the practice are isomers over a shared atom set, so that one lever cannot clear both.

## DOMAIN CENSUS

Atoms held: the facts, ingredients and states that a document can list. Bonds held: the seven typed relations plus unary negation (Operators table). No new primitive is available without import. The anchor for the structural claim is the Operators table itself; the anchor for the analogy is the ground in each case study. These are different anchors, so the claim stays split.

## DERIVATION

### Q1 — Which bond classes can a flat document carry?

- Operators table :16: *"the only bond in a flat formula, which is why a formula records ingredients and not arrangement"*. A flat list can write `+` (conjunction: both hold at once).
- Fig 12 :55 to :69 define three symmetric bonds: `+` conjunction, `∥` disjunction, `≡` identity. Their tests are swaps or removals or substitutions that change nothing. These are the ones a document can approximate, because order is irrelevant to them.
- Fig 12 :70 to :89 define four ordered bonds: `→` sequence, `⇒` production, `−` subtraction, `⋛` comparison. Their tests all depend on order, absence, or which quantity is larger. A list that only records ingredients cannot state any of them.
- Operators :53 to :58 define `¬` negation, the unary refusal: *"a refutation is a negation, which is why a vocabulary without it can only accumulate"*. It is not a bond (Fig 12 :134 to :136).
- Fig 12 :112: **"A plan is a sequence. A goal is a comparison. Progress is a subtraction. A refutation is a negation, and that one is not a bond."**
- ⇒ The lost classes are not "detail." They are **route, threshold, direction, absence and refusal**. A document can say what a situation contains; it cannot say what would count as getting somewhere from it (Fig 12 :113).
- **Falsifier, written before the run:** a flat document that lets a reader correctly order steps, state a threshold, and refuse an out-of-date rule at the ground, with no extra contact. If that holds, the ordered-bond claim is narrowed to the classes that actually fail. Status: triangulated (Fig 12 plus Operators table).

### Q2 — Are the document and the practice isomers?

- Fig 12 :117: **"isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."**
- Fig 12 :94: **"The arrangement is the substance."** Fig 12 :95 to :96: *"Two entries with an identical ingredient set are not variants of one thing. They are different things whose notation cannot tell them apart."*
- Write the practice and the document over the same atom list. The practice bonds those atoms with `→`, `⇒`, `−`, `⋛` and `¬` as the situation demands. The document can only assert their co-presence. Under Fig 12 they are **isomers**: same atoms, different bond, different substance, and the difference is invisible in the ingredient list.
- This is exactly the Melt / Freeze / Boil case (Fig 12 :9 to :17): three different changes, one written formula, *"nothing signals that anything was lost."*
- **Live qualifier, not hidden:** the isomer claim holds only if both sides share the atom set. If the document imports a genuinely new atom (a frame the practice never held), the relation is composition plus import, not isomerism. The qualifier is named in `08` and `11` and left open.
- **Falsifier:** a document that explicitly carries the same ordered bonds and the same refusal as the practice, and whose reader still fails at the ground. That would move the loss from bond to habitat (F4), not refute the bond analysis.

### Q3 — What is the order of the membrane?

- Fig 4 :21: **"The membrane is the boundary between the sizes, and the rule about what may cross it. Not a wall: a filter with a direction."**
- Fig 4 :22: **"Only methods cross up. Only head starts come down."**
- Fig 4 :23: *"upward: a method that can be reapplied. Never the recording of a particular success."*
- Fig 4 :24: *"downward: something that makes the next search cheaper. Never a replay of what worked once."*
- Fig 4 :25: **"A recording carried upward looks like knowledge and is a description of one occasion."** Replayed downward, *"it produces a system that repeats a past success and cannot produce a new one"* (Fig 4 :25 to :26).
- ⇒ The direction is **not** symmetric. A textbook is an upward artifact. Used by a reader as a replay, it is a downward recording. The legal downward object is a head start that makes the reader's own search cheaper, which is a different thing. The transform loses information (Fig 4 :27) and is not reversible (Fig 4 :28 to :29).
- **Round trip:** Fig 4 :30 requires measuring the gap rather than assuming zero. Fig 4 :31 to :32: *"The gap is not an error to eliminate. It is the measurement of what the coarser description cannot hold, and it is the only honest report of how much was given up."* `R_T` is therefore the quantity to report, not the document's word count or coverage.
- **Falsifier:** a round trip that returns exactly what was sent for a named slot against a ground that pays.

### Q4 — Analogues from lower-overlap fields

These corroborate the shape; they do not decide it. The figures decide.

1. **Bessemer process (history of technology, opened this session).** A patented, published method failed in the hands of four licensees; the missing element was supplied later by another practitioner (Mushet's reintroduction of carbon and manganese), and the decline was attributed partly to lack of trained personnel. Same atoms (iron, air, converter, patent), different bond (when to stop the blow, what to add back), different substance. An isomer and a habitat case in one.
2. **Collins' sapphire-Q replication (sociology of science, via the opened Wikipedia entry).** A published experimental result could not be reproduced until Russian and Western scientists worked together; *"laboratory visits enhance the possibility for the transfer of tacit knowledge."* The repair loop needed co-presence, not a better protocol.
3. **Bartlett's War of the Ghosts (cognitive psychology, opened this session).** Elements that did not fit the listener's schema were *"omitted from the recollection, or transformed into more familiar forms."* Silent substitution in a transmission chain, the cognitive version of Fig 12 :15.
4. **Clark and Brennan's grounding in communication (psycholinguistics, opened this session).** A contribution is complete only when the partner shows understanding; a puzzled look is the correction. A document has no acceptance phase, so it cannot detect its own failure. This is Fig 4 :30 at the conversational scale.
5. **Polanyi's face recognition (philosophy of science, via the opened tacit-knowledge entry).** One can pick a friend out of a million photographs and cannot say how. Somatic pretext, not a missing fact.

### Q5 — Functional group: what the three document forms share

Textbook, database and policy document are not three different losses. They share a functional group (Fig 12 :123 to :124): *"functional group. A substructure whose presence fixes the outcome. The group says what kind; the qualifier says which member."* Their common structure is "explicit, codified, shareable without the knowing subject" (the Wikipedia tacit knowledge page names exactly this property of explicit knowledge). The qualifier decides which member: a textbook fails mainly at the bond, a database mainly at closure and completeness, a policy document mainly at the anchor (its definitions decide an outcome and stop being conventions, Fig 10 :47). Verdicts stay per member; the functional group is not an average.

## CONCLUSION

The specific content that fails to cross is not detail but grammar: the four ordered bonds and the unary refusal that turn a state description into a route, a goal, progress, a threshold and a correction. The document and the practice are isomers over a shared atom set (same ingredients, different bond, different substance), which is why an ingredient list cannot predict whether competence transferred. The membrane is directed and its legal downward cargo is a head start, not a replay. `R_T` is the honest measure of what was given up. One live qualifier is recorded: the isomer claim needs a shared atom set; a genuinely new import breaks it.
