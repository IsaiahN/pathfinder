## 0. The question in effect terms

> How should conservation planning account for species that move across administrative and national borders?

Restated as an effect, with the options separated:

- A species is a **slot** tracked by a planning frame. Its **habitat** is the field of contact that sustains that slot: where it feeds, breeds, rests, disperses, and what acts on it there.
- An **administrative border** is a line drawn by a jurisdiction. It fixes legal authority, budget, and reporting, not contact.
- **Option A, plan per jurisdiction.** Each agency enumerates the habitat inside its own line, sets its own objective, and reports its own compliance. What this leads to: a live population split across the union can be invisible in every per-jurisdiction report, because each report sees a fragment and the fragments are averaged into a national score. Scope to direct contact misses the cross-border cascade.
- **Option B, plan the habitat as one enumerated contact field across the union.** What this leads to: the residual stays indexed per species, the corridor and the range are treated as the object, jurisdiction becomes a delivery mechanism rather than the unit of ecology, and coordination is checked against an independent ground rather than against agreement.

What an answer would look like, bound as an ordinary term before the search (Formula "Direction"): *a rule that says which unit to enumerate over, which anchor settles a cross-border verdict, and what would refute the rule.* This is the sought-for shape. Binding it makes it refusable.

## 1. Domain census, before any figure line is chosen

### R, the residual, by arity, symmetry and scale

- **Arity.** One species slot is held in part by several jurisdictions. Arity is k (the number of range units), not one. The same structure repeats over many species, so R is indexed per slot. Fig 1 :19: "The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated." Fig 1 :22: "Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average." A single national biodiversity index is exactly the aggregation the figure warns about.
- **Symmetry.** A border is symmetric as a spatial relation (A adjoins B as B adjoins A), but the species' use of the corridor is ordered. Fig 12 :71: "A then B; B needs A to have happened" (sequence, →). Seasonal migration and source to sink flow are ordered. A symmetric adjacency count cannot choose the arrow, so a cross-border plan that only counts "neighbouring jurisdictions" has recorded the wrong bond.
- **Scale.** Local site, region, nation, flyway or range. Fig 4 :20: "Only the actors, the currency and the anchor change." The kernel is the same loop at each size. The species lives at the range scale while the mandate usually lives at the national or subnational scale, so the membrane between scales is where the failure occurs.

### One line per domain: actors, currency, anchor

**D1. The ecological and habitat frame.**
- Actors: the focal species, prey, predators, pollinators, competitors, vegetation, water, fire, season, terrain.
- Currency: contact, survival, reproduction, gene flow, population viability.
- Anchor: the ground, meaning what the population actually does and where it persists, read by telemetry, genetics, counts, or a marking and recapture programme.
- Nameable anchor: yes, the measured population outcome. D1 is a domain.

**D2. The administrative and planning frame.**
- Actors: agencies, legislatures, landowners, Indigenous nations, boundary lines, mandates, budgets.
- Currency: legal authority, budget, compliance, votes, hectares designated.
- Anchor: the law and the mandate are a convention, not a ground. Fig 10 :18: "a declared meaning is not a grounded one." The jurisdiction's genuine anchor is the same species outcome as D1, but its reported metric can update on its own reports.
- Nameable anchor: yes, the species outcome if independently read; the mandate alone is not one. D2 is a domain, with the anchor distinction as its live question.

**D3. The coordination and verification frame.**
- Actors: range states, treaty bodies, scientific councils, local communities, independent monitors.
- Currency: agreements, data sharing, coordinated action, refusals.
- Anchor: the ground, the species outcome, never agreement between agencies.
- Nameable anchor: yes. D3 is a domain.

### Partition or union

**Union, explicitly.** The species' requirements are a bundle of overlapping conditions spread across jurisdictions: breeding in one, wintering in another, a stopover in a third. Fig 9 :54: "And when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again." Fig 9 :55 says a single lever "clears the region where only one condition holds and leaves the intersections standing, because the second condition still binds." A protected area in one country that ignores the wintering ground clears only the region where the breeding condition holds. The intersections are where the population is lost. Verdicts for D1, D2 and D3 are therefore kept separate and never averaged into one "conservation score."

### Dependency order (Fig 3)

1. **Perception.** Can the cross-border movement be detected at all?
2. **Vocabulary.** Are there terms for the cross-border contact: corridor, flyway, metapopulation, source to sink, range state, ecological connectivity?
3. **Objective.** What counts as progress: hectares designated per jurisdiction, or viability over the full range?
4. **Planning.** Coordinate action across the union.
5. **Learn and carry.** Carry the method to the next species or the next range, not the recording of one success.

Fig 3 :20: "The chain is ordered by dependency, not by importance. Each link consumes what the one above produces, so effort spent above the break arrives at a step that cannot use it." The usual break in this question is between links 2 and 3: agencies have a vocabulary for habitat inside their line but no objective defined over the cross-border population. Fig 3 :27: "A link with no instrument is not a link that works. It is a link nobody has looked at."

## 3. Findings

### F1 — The border is a convention, not a ground (decisive for D2)

Fig 10 :18: "a declared meaning is not a grounded one."
Fig 2 :29: "An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks. A measurement, an experiment, a counter, a market, a patient outcome. If it moves when you push on it, it is not an anchor."
Fig 2 :33: "The anchor must not update."

A border is real as law and as budget authority, but it is not a fact about the species. Treating the border as the unit of ecology promotes a convention into a verdict. Fig 10 :47: "a convention that decides an outcome has stopped being one." A quota or a designation target that ignores the cross-border population has stopped being a coordination convention and has become an outcome-fixing verdict.

**Refuter:** show a case where the boundary line itself is the thing the species tracks, such that changing the line changes the animal's survival with no change in habitat, movement or demography. Then the border is a ground for that residual and this finding fails.

### F2 — Enumerate the habitat by contact, not by jurisdiction (decisive for D1)

Fig 11 :43: "The habitat is enumerated, never composed."
Fig 11 :44: "List everything in contact with the residual, then what is in contact with those, and outward until the cascade stops mattering."
Fig 11 :48: "You do not invent the list. You read it off the world, and what you cannot perceive or measure yet is the residual."

The enumeration unit is the species' contact field. Actors, conditions and relations are read off the world. Fig 11 :45-47: "actors: what acts on it, and what it acts on"; "conditions: light, water, temperature, season, terrain"; "relations: pollination, predation, competition, dependency." None of these lists has an administrative line in it. The jurisdiction enters later, as a delivery frame, not as the census boundary. "Enumerate the habitat inside the district" is a category error: it substitutes the frame's closure for the world's contact field.

**Refuter:** exhibit a species whose critical contact relations are all and only inside one jurisdiction across all seasons and life stages, with an independently measured demonstration that nothing two steps out matters. Then jurisdiction-bounded enumeration is sufficient for that slot. This is possible for a strict endemic; it is not a general rule.

### F3 — Scoping to direct contact misses the third-order effect

Fig 11 :52-53: "And contact cascades. Scoping the habitat to direct contact is how DDT reached the eagles: first-order enumeration, second-order effect, and the residual that mattered was three steps out."
Fig 11 :54: "Carson, Silent Spring, 1962. The mechanism, biomagnification through the food chain to eggshell thinning, was established over the following decade."

The figure's own example is a cross-boundary cascade. The same structure governs a migratory population: the factor that matters may be a stopover wetland three links away from the breeding site, in another country. World corroboration from ecotoxicology is explicit: the biomagnification review records that "A well known example of the harmful effects of DDT biomagnification is the significant decline in North American populations of predatory birds such as bald eagles and peregrine falcons due to DDT causing eggshell thinning in the 1950s" (Wikipedia, Biomagnification, 2026). That source is a genuinely low-overlap witness, ecotoxicology rather than conservation policy.

**Refuter:** a measured case where the population's variance is fully accounted for by first-order contacts inside the jurisdiction, with a demonstrated absence of a cascade at two or more steps. Mode `specified`: the figure asserts the cascade structure, not that every species has a three-step one.

### F4 — A corridor is a bond, not an ingredient list

Fig 12 :5: "A set of ingredients is not one. The bond is the rest of the information, and it is the half nothing was recording."
Fig 12 :117: "Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."
Fig 13 :24: "that entities relate, and influence travels."

Two jurisdictions can hold the same list of habitats, patches and species, and produce different substances because the connection between them is a different bond. Counting protected hectares in each country records the ingredients. It does not record whether the flyway is connected. World corroboration from metapopulation ecology: "The development of metapopulation theory, in conjunction with the development of source–sink dynamics, emphasised the importance of connectivity between seemingly isolated populations" (Wikipedia, Metapopulation, 2026). And "A wildlife corridor, also known as a habitat corridor or green corridor, is an area that connects wildlife populations that have been separated by human activities or structures" (Wikipedia, Wildlife corridor, 2026). Connectivity is a relation, not an extra patch.

**Refuter:** show that the same atoms under two different arrangements yield the same population trajectory and the same gene flow, so the bond carries no information. Then the isomer reading fails for this case.

### F5 — There is no standing list of good cross-border interventions

Fig 11 :49: "An actor's valence is relative to the residual, not a property it carries."
Fig 11 :50: "The same actor is a benefactor to one residual and a malfactor to another, so mutualism and parasitism are relations rather than kinds."
Fig 11 :51: "No standing list of good actors survives a change of residual."

A corridor, a fence, a hunting ban, or a dam removal has no valence by itself. It is a benefactor to one residual and a malfactor to another. This is why a universal checklist of "best practices for transboundary conservation" is structurally the wrong object. The checklist can be carried as a **method**, with its provenance, and then ranged against the actual residual. It cannot be carried as a standing verdict.

**Refuter:** a demonstrated intervention whose valence is invariant across at least two independent residuals with different anchors. That would break the relativity claim.

### F6 — The question is a union, not a partition

Fig 9 :4-5: "Disagreements that do not shrink with effort, where each rule keeps working well on a different subset, are not one hard question but several well-formed ones. Split it rather than search."
Fig 9 :54: "And when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again."
Fig 9 :57: "What remains is smaller and it is also categorically different: a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either."

Breeding habitat, stopover habitat, wintering habitat and the legal mandate to act are overlapping conditions, not alternatives. A single lever, such as expanding one protected area, clears the region where only one condition holds and leaves the intersections standing. World corroboration: the flyway literature defines the object over the whole span. "A flyway is a flight path used by large numbers of birds while migrating between their breeding grounds and their overwintering quarters. Flyways generally span continents and often pass over oceans" (Wikipedia, Flyway, 2026). The CMS source puts it as a range problem: the convention "is an international agreement that aims to conserve migratory species throughout their ranges" (Wikipedia, Convention on Migratory Species, 2026).

**Refuter:** a species for which the cross-border conditions form a clean partition, each with its own disjoint ground and no intersection. Then a single lever per block works and the union finding does not apply.

### F7 — One jurisdiction's closure cannot derive its neighbour's atom

Fig 6 :21: "MINT composes inside the closure and can never add an atom."
Fig 6 :23: "No frame escapes its own closure. The bound moves only on contact with a frame whose closure differs."
Fig 8 :32: "no search, however long, adds an atom."
Fig 8 :45: "Two frames together reach what neither reaches alone."

If the monitoring data, the species record and the instrument all live in one country, more searching inside that country will not produce the fact that the population wintering in the next country has collapsed. That fact is an import from a frame whose closure differs. Imports need provenance and a shadow test: Fig 6 :12-13 names "source gate: provenance" and "shadow test: explains a gap." A neighbouring agency's dataset shared without provenance is an adopted import, and Fig 8 :50-52 warns that "Convergent derivation and adopted import look identical in the contents. Only the record of where each came from separates them."

**Refuter:** a case where the missing fact is demonstrably derivable from the first jurisdiction's own data at the same closure, so no import was owed. Then the import finding is not triggered.

### F8 — Coordination is triangulation, not negotiation

Fig 2 :31: "Independence, between two frames, is how little they draw on the same evidence. Two people reading the same report are not two frames; two instruments pointed at the same thing are."
Fig 2 :35: "Alignment is triangulation, not negotiation. A mirror cannot correct you."
Fig 2 :40: "disqualifying for a verdict: agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool."
Fig 2 :42: "Buy independence in proportion to the cost of being wrong."

Two agencies that both cite the same national population report are one frame wearing two names. Two independent monitoring programmes pointed at the same population are two frames. The surplus of a union is a function of the same independence parameter (Fig 8 :47: "Independence is one quantity read twice"), so low overlap is the asset to protect, not the problem to smooth away. World corroboration from the transboundary governance literature is mixed and is recorded honestly: a transboundary protected area "is an ecological protected area that spans boundaries of more than one country or sub-national entity" (Wikipedia, Transboundary protected area, 2026), but the same source records that "a number of authors criticize that peace parks have a very limited impact on formal relations between states, but can accelerate conflicts on the local level." That is not a conflict with the figure verdict. It is evidence that agreement between states is not the ground for the species, exactly as Fig 2 :40 predicts.

**Refuter:** a case where inter-agency agreement itself changes the measured population with no change in habitat or contact. Then agreement is a ground for this residual and the finding fails.

### F9 — The residual stays indexed per species, not per jurisdiction

Fig 1 :19: "The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated."
Fig 1 :23: "The ground enters as the only metric."
Fig 1 :27: "They are frame-internal. A frame cannot score itself with a quantity it also produces."

A national index of "percentage of migratory species with a management plan" is produced by the same frames it is used to score. It is frame-internal. The metric that matters is the per-species residual, the gap between predicted and observed persistence for each slot. Fig 1 :24-27 disqualifies "coverage, and compression achieved" and "predicates minted, and credibility accrued." Hectares designated are the same genus of quantity.

**Refuter:** a demonstrated case where a pooled national score predicts a specific population's collapse before any per-species reading does. Then the aggregation carries information the slot reading lacks.

### F10 — The chain breaks between vocabulary and objective

Fig 3 :20: "The chain is ordered by dependency, not by importance. Each link consumes what the one above produces, so effort spent above the break arrives at a step that cannot use it."
Fig 3 :25-26: "The link between them, having terms for what a situation offers and being able to say what would count as progress, has neither, so it attracts the least attention and the fewest instruments."
Fig 3 :29-30: "Before asking whether a system is good, ask which link it currently stops at, and whether the answer was measured or assumed."

Conservation planning usually has the vocabulary (corridor, connectivity, flyway, range state) and the perception (tracking data). What it often lacks is an objective defined over the full range: a stated comparison of what counts as a viable cross-border population. Effort spent on more tracking data lands above the break. An objective is a comparison, and a vocabulary without comparisons cannot state one (Fig 12 :112: "A plan is a sequence. A goal is a comparison. Progress is a subtraction.").

**Refuter:** a jurisdiction with a measured, operational, cross-border objective that is demonstrably not the limiting link. Then the diagnosis does not apply to that frame.

### F11 — Moving a population substitutes a habitat; it does not remove one

Fig 11 :66: "Isolation is not removal of the habitat. It is substitution of one habitat for another."
Fig 11 :67: "Move the residual and you must reproduce everything its goal required, and you inherit the new habitat's actors, which you did not choose."
Fig 11 :68: "what you failed to reproduce is invisible until the goal fails"
Fig 11 :69: "what you unintentionally introduced is invisible until it acts"

Translocation, captive breeding and reintroduction are the tempting cross-border instruments. The figure says they are substitutions with two silent failure modes. This matters for the outreach: offering "move the animals to a safer country" as a solution is an import of an instrument without enumerating the new habitat. Fig 11 :61: "The seat's power is asymmetric. Introduction is available; exclusion is not. You can add an actor and you cannot subtract the habitat, because contact is a fact about the world and does not consult you."

**Refuter:** a demonstrated substitution in which all required contact was reproduced and no unchosen actor acted, verified over a full generation. Rare, but not impossible in principle; the finding is `specified`, not a ban.

### F12 — Managed connectivity is permanent work, not a solved state

Fig 11 :63: "Which is why a managed ecosystem is permanent work rather than a solved state."
Fig 7 :6: "the limit: no last room"
Fig 7 :30: "every room closes some of the gap; no finite number of them ever arrives"

A signed treaty is a room, not a final closure. The next room must differ from the last and match the residual it surfaced (Fig 7 :36: "A chain of mirrors is one long room. The next room must differ from the last, and match the residual it surfaced."). The right institutional form is a schedule of different rooms selected against the residual, not one permanent global authority.

**Refuter:** a demonstrated institutional arrangement that is final, closes the residual, and needs no further handoff. That would break "no last room" and the framework with it.

### F13 — Carry methods across scales, not recordings

Fig 4 :20: "Only the actors, the currency and the anchor change."
Fig 4 :22: "Only methods cross up. Only head starts come down."
Fig 4 :25: "A recording carried upward looks like knowledge and is a description of one occasion."
Fig 4 :34-35: "A seed carries no small tree: it carries what will grow one, and the conditions it lands in decide what it becomes."

A corridor design that worked for one flyway crosses to another as a **method** (how to enumerate contact, how to test independence, how to write a refuter), not as a copied map. Copied maps are recordings of one occasion. The round trip carries a gap that must be measured: Fig 4 :30: "So take the round trip and measure the gap. Without it you are navigating by dead reckoning: confident about a position nobody checked." This is recorded in `11_audit.md` as R_T.

**Refuter:** a case where a copied design transfers with no loss and needs no re-enumeration against the new habitat. Then the round-trip gap is zero for that case and the method requirement is relaxed.

### F14 — The framework returns a shape, not a recipe

Fig 9 :32: "you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself"
Fig 13 :134: "So the system can report that it lacks something rather than producing an answer anyway. Unreachable is a verdict here, not a silence,"
Fig 8 :39: "So a search that finds nothing is never a proof of absence."

No figure line returns a corridor width, a patch count, a fraction of range to protect, or the number of range states that must sign. The canonical usage rule states the limit directly: the framework gives "what a missing thing would have to look like (a shape, not a name)" and then "stops at the limit of what the available instruments can distinguish, and records the lineage so the stop is honest." These numbers are a declared `gap`, of the unreached-at-this-budget kind, not an invitation to invent a default.

**Refuter:** a figure line that determines a numerical corridor width or a required number of signatories. Until one is quoted verbatim, the gap stands.

## 4. Pre-registered refuter

Written before the conclusion, per Seat Map :287: "And a falsifier written before the run is a design instrument, not only an honesty device." The shape claim is refuted if any of the following holds:

1. **Border is a ground.** A measured case where changing only the line, with habitat, movement and demography held fixed, changes the population's persistence. (Fails F1.)
2. **Contact is a list.** A case where the same atoms under two different arrangements give the same persistence and the same gene flow, so the bond carries no information. (Fails F4.)
3. **No cascade.** A species whose population variance is fully accounted for by first-order contacts with a demonstrated absence of any effect two or more steps out. (Fails F3.)
4. **Partition.** A species whose cross-border conditions form a clean partition with disjoint grounds and no intersections. (Fails F6.)
5. **Pooled score wins.** A national aggregate that predicts a specific population's collapse before any per-species reading does. (Fails F9.)
6. **Standing list survives.** An intervention whose valence is invariant across two independent residuals with different anchors. (Fails F5.)
7. **Last room.** An institutional closure that closes the cross-border residual and needs no further handoff. (Fails F12.)
8. **Recipe found.** A verbatim figure line that determines a numerical corridor width or signatory count. (Closes F14.)

If any fires, the claim is withdrawn or narrowed in this file with the retraction left visible. Until one is run, nothing here is `grounded`.

## 6. Recommendation

1. Replace the planning unit. Enumerate the species' contact field across the full range first; map jurisdictions onto it second (F2, F3).
2. Define the objective over the full range before buying more monitoring. Write down what would count as a viable cross-border population, then bind it as an ordinary term so it can accrue a residual (F10, Formula "Direction").
3. Keep per-species residuals. Delete the national aggregate from the verdict path; keep it only as a budget filter (F9, Fig 9 :60: "Use filters for the budget and witnesses for the verdict. Never let a filter hand you a verdict.").
4. Protect independence between range states' readings. Two agencies reading one report count as one frame. Buy a second instrument pointed at the same population (F8, Fig 2 :42).
5. Treat the treaty as a room in a schedule, not a final closure. The next room is selected against the residual the last one surfaced (F12).
6. Send the generator, not the recording. Fig 4 :22: "Only methods cross up. Only head starts come down."

## 7. Strains, re-census and retraction log

### S1, retracted and preserved — "the framework forbids administrative borders"

First pass read Fig 11 :43 ("The habitat is enumerated, never composed") as a ban on drawing jurisdiction lines. That was a strain: it made the figure contradict the ordinary practice of conservation law. Re-census: Fig 11 governs the **habitat enumeration**, Fig 10 :18 governs the **status of the convention**, and Fig 4 :20 governs the **scale change**. Different objects. The framework does not forbid borders; it forbids promoting a border into the unit of ecology. The original overstatement is kept here rather than erased.

### S2, retracted and preserved — "connectivity must be a seventh substrate member"

First pass tried to make connectivity a new primitive. Re-census: Fig 13 :24 already holds "network" as a structure, and Fig 13 :75 says "Everything else is a composition across the two kinds, never within one." Connectivity composes from network, space and gradient. It adds no atom, so no import is owed. Retracted.

### S3, narrowed and preserved — "shape, not a name"

The canonical phrase governs a **missing** primitive. Naming "range state", "flyway" or "corridor" is a binding from the ecology and policy frames onto atoms already held (network, space, sequence). It is provenance, not the invention of a new primitive. What remains forbidden is claiming the framework derived those names, or that naming them moves the wall (Fig 6 :21: "MINT composes inside the closure and can never add an atom."). This correction is repeated in `05_what_shape_means.md` so it is not quietly lost.

### S4, live and named — two currencies in the objective

Fig 12 :125: "A composition costs in two currencies, and they do not add." A corridor plan is priced in bits (the description of the arrangement) and in actions (finding out whether it holds on the ground). A national score that adds hectares to monitoring effort mixes units. The figure gives no conversion. Named as a live type question, not smoothed into one index.

### S5, live and narrowed — emergence and amenability to management

The transboundary governance source records criticism that peace parks can accelerate local conflict. Read carefully this is not a conflict with the figure verdict: it is evidence for F8, that agreement between states is not the species ground. But it does mean a coordination room can be selected for the wrong residual (state relations rather than population persistence). Recorded as a design constraint: the room must be selected against the species residual, not against diplomatic comfort (Fig 7 :36).

### S6, no strain — cross-kind composition

Fig 13 :75 "Two states never bond directly. It is not space-time but space, a structure, time; and two structures join only through a state." governs composition of the six substrate terms. The border question is about the scope of an enumeration and the status of a convention, not about bonding substrate states. Different objects, so no strain after re-census.
