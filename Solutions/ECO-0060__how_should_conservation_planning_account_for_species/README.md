## Key verdict in one line

Plan the species' habitat as an enumerated contact field, not as the intersection of jurisdiction boxes: cross-border planning is a union of overlapping conditions requiring independent grounds and selection between rooms, and the administrative border is a convention that makes a disagreement statable but does not fix the ecology.

## What is deliberately not here

- No personal emails of named individuals.
- No ranking of people by how likely they are to agree.
- No bulk-send plan.
- No universal corridor width, patch count, or fraction of a range to protect. No figure line returns those, so they are a declared `gap`, not a hidden assumption.

Three reasons in the figures' own words:

1. **Agreement is not an anchor.** Fig 2 :35: "Alignment is triangulation, not negotiation. A mirror cannot correct you." Selecting correspondents for receptiveness manufactures the correlation that makes their agreement worthless.
2. **The ground is not updated by a report.** Fig 2 :29: "An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks." The anchor here is what the population actually does, not an agency's compliance score.
3. **A verifier who has absorbed the frame cannot verify it.** Fig 10 :42: "A verifier that can reconstruct the claim cannot verify it." The first contact carries the residual and its refuter, never the architecture.

Legally: scraping personal emails for unsolicited bulk outreach breaches GDPR and CAN-SPAM style rules in many jurisdictions. That is a practical failure on top of the structural one.
