# Outreach draft — check the output first, then decide about the framing

**Use:** one to one, or as a cover note attached to a submission through a public venue in `03_public_contact_points.csv`. **Not** for bulk send.

**Hard precondition:** do not send until the claim in `08_expert_verification_packet.md` survives the reader's own check. If the stage-1 output does not survive, the two-stage offer collapses on contact.

---

**Subject:** One checkable claim about cross-border conservation planning, before any theory

> Hi [name],
>
> I have one specific output I would like you to check against what you already know. I am not asking you to read a framework first, and I am not asking you to agree with anything. If the output does not survive, there is nothing else to look at.
>
> **The output (`specified`, not `grounded`).** A species that crosses a border is tracked per species, not per jurisdiction. The unit to enumerate is its contact field: where it breeds, feeds, rests, disperses, and what acts on it there, outward until the cascade stops mattering. An administrative line fixes authority and budget; it does not fix contact. So the live residual is the gap between the jurisdiction's closure and the species' closure, and it shows up first as a population that is failing across the union while every jurisdiction's own report looks healthy.
>
> This is a **shape**, not a measurement. It does not say which corridor, how wide, or how many states must sign. Those are open, and I am recording them as open rather than filling them in.
>
> **What would refute it:**
> 1. a measured case where changing only the boundary line, with habitat, movement and demography held fixed, changes the population's persistence;
> 2. a case where the same habitat atoms under two different arrangements give the same persistence and the same gene flow, so the connection carries no information;
> 3. a species whose cross-border conditions form a clean partition with no intersections, so one lever per jurisdiction clears the whole problem;
> 4. a national aggregate that predicts a specific population's collapse before any per-species reading does.
>
> I wrote these down before sending this. If any one holds, I retract the claim. A one-line "this fails because..." is more useful to me than agreement. If you would rather not engage, no reply is needed.
>
> **If it survives, and only then:** I can send three short public sources, with the note that the figure text, not my summary of it, is the authority. I can also send a one-page verification packet with the exact lines and the refuters.
>
> **If you want to use the method:** the rule is to describe the residual in effect terms first, then go looking for a frame, because any frame you pick first will seem to fit. Each use needs its own ground and its own refuter written before the run.
>
> Thanks,
> [name]

## Stage order

1. **Stage 1.** Send the residual, the shape claim, and the four refuters. Ask what the reader would count as a failure. Record the verdict before showing any theory.
2. **Stage 2, opt in only.** If they are curious, offer the three source links and the verification packet. Note that the verbatim figure text is the authority.
3. **Stage 3.** Offer the method as a reusable generator, with the instruction that each user writes their own refuter and states their own mode.

No agreement, no trust request, no bulk send, no ranking of recipients by likely receptiveness. A failure is more useful than an endorsement.
