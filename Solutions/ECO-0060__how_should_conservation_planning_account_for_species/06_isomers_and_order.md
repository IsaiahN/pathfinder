# Isomers, bonds and order: what a cross-border plan is made of

**Mode: `general` on the structural claims, `specified` on the ecological application.** This is a simulated derivation checked against the figures, not a ground reading (see `05_what_shape_means.md` §7).

## RESIDUAL

Two jurisdictions can hold the same list of habitats, species, budgets and legal instruments and still produce a working corridor or a broken one. The ingredient list does not decide the outcome. Two questions are owed:

1. Are two cross-border plans **isomers** under Fig 12's definition, that is, the same atoms under a different bond?
2. What **order** governs the union: a symmetric adjacency of neighbours, or an ordered route, and where does the union sit in the dependency chain?

## DOMAIN CENSUS

- Atoms held for this derivation: space, time, energy (states); spectrum, gradient, network (structures), plus the boundary as a jurisdictional convention and the species as the tracked slot.
- Bonds held: the seven, including → sequence (ordered), ⋛ comparison (ordered), + conjunction (symmetric) and ≡ identity (symmetric).
- Currency: persistence of the population and the cost of settling whether a given arrangement persists.
- Anchor: an independent reading of the population, not a designation count.
- No new primitive is available without import. Connectivity composes from the held network term and does not add an atom (Fig 13 :24: "that entities relate, and influence travels").

## DERIVATION

### Q1 — Is a corridor a bond or an ingredient?

Fig 12 :5: "A set of ingredients is not one. The bond is the rest of the information, and it is the half nothing was recording."
Fig 12 :40: "same two atoms, three substances"
Fig 12 :111: "A vocabulary without bonds can name a state and cannot name a route to one."

A map of protected areas names a state: what is present where. It does not name a route: what connects to what, in which order, and whether influence can travel. Fig 12 :112 continues: "A plan is a sequence. A goal is a comparison. Progress is a subtraction." A cross-border plan is therefore a molecule, not a list. The bond is the connection that lets an individual, a gene or a pollen grain get from one patch to another.

**Falsifier, written before the run:** if two jurisdictions holding identical atom sets but different connections produce statistically indistinguishable persistence and gene flow across multiple independent species, the bond carries no information and the isomer reading fails for this case.

**World check (secondary only).** "The development of metapopulation theory, in conjunction with the development of source–sink dynamics, emphasised the importance of connectivity between seemingly isolated populations" (Wikipedia, Metapopulation, 2026). And: "Wildlife corridors improve habitat connectivity, which can be defined as the extent to which flora and fauna, or plants and animals, can move between patches of habitat" (Wikipedia, Wildlife corridor, 2026). Both corroborate the bond reading. They do not outrank it.

### Q2 — Are two plans isomers?

Fig 12 :117: "Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."

Take two plans over the same atom set:

- **Plan A.** Three protected patches in three countries, connected by a continuous riparian corridor under one management agreement. Bond: connection with a shared objective.
- **Plan B.** The same three patches, the same area, the same budgets, but fenced and managed as three separate units with no shared objective. Bond: conjunction, +, "both hold at once".

Same atoms, different bond, different substance. In Fig 12's terms they are isomers. Counting hectares, species or signatories cannot tell them apart, which is the definition of invisibility in the formula.

This is not an ornament. It is the precise reason a national score is the wrong verdict object. A score reads the formula (the ingredients) and not the arrangement.

**Falsifier:** if the two plans' population trajectories are the same under a well-powered independent measurement, the arrangement is not decisive and the substances are not different. The isomer claim is then retracted for that atom set.

### Q3 — What is the order of the route?

Fig 12 :71: "A then B; B needs A to have happened" (→, sequence, symmetric = no).
Fig 12 :87: "is it about which is larger, rather than both being present? Yes." (⋛, comparison).

A border is symmetric as a spatial relation: A adjoins B as B adjoins A. A migration route is ordered: the bird must reach the stopover before it can cross the next leg, and the wintering ground is not interchangeable with the breeding ground. So the map of adjacent jurisdictions uses a symmetric relation, while the species uses →. Treating adjacency as if it were the route is a bond type error: it records "both hold at once" where the ecology needs "A then B".

This matters for the union in `01` F6. Fig 9 :54 says "And when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again." The union is over ordered conditions, not over a symmetric set of neighbours. Unbundling means separating breeding, stopover, wintering and the legal mandate, then locating the intersections.

**World check (secondary only).** "A flyway is a flight path used by large numbers of birds while migrating between their breeding grounds and their overwintering quarters. Flyways generally span continents and often pass over oceans" (Wikipedia, Flyway, 2026). The CMS definition quoted there manages populations "over their entire migration space." That is → over a range, not a symmetric adjacency count.

### Q4 — Where does the union sit in the dependency chain?

Fig 3 :20: "The chain is ordered by dependency, not by importance. Each link consumes what the one above produces, so effort spent above the break arrives at a step that cannot use it."
Fig 3 :27: "A link with no instrument is not a link that works. It is a link nobody has looked at."

The chain for a cross-border plan is: perception of movement → vocabulary for cross-border contact → objective over the full range → coordinated plan → carried method. The union of conditions sits at the third link, the objective, because that is where the comparison "viable across the whole range or not" is stated. If that link is missing, more tracking data (link 1) arrives at a step that cannot use it. The chain must be read before the plan is judged (Fig 3 :29: "Before asking whether a system is good, ask which link it currently stops at, and whether the answer was measured or assumed.").

### Q5 — Does the cross-scale move carry a method or a map?

Fig 4 :22: "Only methods cross up. Only head starts come down."
Fig 4 :25: "A recording carried upward looks like knowledge and is a description of one occasion."

A corridor design transferred from one flyway to another is a method only if it carries the enumeration rule, the independence test and the refuter. If it carries only the map, it is a recording of one occasion and the new habitat remakes it (Fig 4 :34-35: "A seed carries no small tree: it carries what will grow one, and the conditions it lands in decide what it becomes."). The round trip gap R_T is measured in `11_audit.md`.

### Q6 — Are there analogues, and what do they license?

Three analogues, each with a stated limit:

1. **Metapopulation.** A set of spatially separated populations that interact. Licenses: per-patch extinction and recolonisation reasoning, and the rescue effect. Limit: the administrative border is not a habitat patch boundary; importing the patch concept must not smuggle the border back in.
2. **Flyway.** An ordered route over a whole migration space. Licenses: treating the range as the planning unit. Limit: mostly waterfowl and shorebirds in the source literature, so it is a shape analogue for other taxa, not a measured fact about them.
3. **Percolation threshold.** The metapopulation source notes that "At a given percolation threshold, habitat fragmentation effects take place in these configurations predicting more drastic extinction thresholds." Licenses: connectivity can be a threshold, so a small loss can flip the system. Limit: no figure line or source here supplies the numerical threshold for a given species. That number is the declared `gap`.

Analogue discipline, from Fig 9 :27-28: "describe it before you go looking, or any frame you pick will seem to fit." The residual was described in `01` §0 before these three frames were chosen.

## CONCLUSION

The cross-border plan is a molecule. Two plans over the same atom set but different bonds are isomers, and the difference is carried entirely by the connection, which a hectare count cannot see. The route is ordered (→), not a symmetric adjacency of neighbours (+), and the union of conditions sits at the objective link of the dependency chain. Connectivity adds no seventh primitive; it composes from the held network term. What crosses between scales is the method, not the map.

The falsifier remains live: if identical atoms under two different arrangements yield indistinguishable persistence and gene flow, the isomer reading fails and the simpler ingredient-list account stands instead.
