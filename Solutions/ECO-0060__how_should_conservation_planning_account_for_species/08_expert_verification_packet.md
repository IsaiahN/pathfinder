# Expert verification packet — cross-border conservation planning

**What this is:** a claim in a form a conservation planner or landscape ecologist can check. Mode, derivation and refuters are stated before the conclusion, so the reader knows what to look for and what would settle it.

**Mode: `specified` on the per-domain claims, `general` on the structural shape, `gap` on the numerical recipe. Not `grounded`.** No population measurement is claimed here. This is a simulated derivation checked for internal consistency against the figures, not a ground reading.

## 1. The claim, in one paragraph

Enumerate the species' habitat as a contact field across its full range, not as the intersection of jurisdiction boxes; cascade outward until the effect stops mattering. The administrative border is a convention that fixes authority and budget, not a grounded fact about the animal. The conditions that sustain a migratory population form a union of overlapping conditions, so a single lever leaves the intersections standing. Connectivity is a bond between habitat atoms, not an extra atom or a hectare count, so two plans with the same patches under different connections are isomers. Coordination is triangulation between independent readings of the population, not negotiation between agencies, and the treaty is a room in a schedule rather than a final closure. The framework returns that shape and a set of pre-registered refuters, not a corridor width or a signatory count.

## 2. Three checkable sub-claims

### C1 — Ecological frame; checkable by a landscape ecologist

- **Statement:** the enumeration unit is the species' contact field, read off the world and cascaded outward; the border is not in the enumeration. Scoping to direct contact misses third-order effects.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 11 :43 — "The habitat is enumerated, never composed." Fig 11 :44 — "List everything in contact with the residual, then what is in contact with those, and outward until the cascade stops mattering." Fig 11 :52-53 — "And contact cascades. Scoping the habitat to direct contact is how DDT reached the eagles: first-order enumeration, second-order effect, and the residual that mattered was three steps out."
- **What to check:** for a species you know, is the thing that actually limits the population inside the jurisdiction, or two or more links out in another country? Is a per-jurisdiction habitat list the right census unit, or does it omit the relation?
- **Refuter:** a species whose population variance is fully accounted for by first-order contacts inside one jurisdiction, with a demonstrated absence of any effect two or more steps out. Also: a measured case where changing only the line, with habitat, movement and demography fixed, changes persistence.

### C2 — Structural claim; checkable by reading Figure 12 against two real plans

- **Statement:** two plans over the same habitat atoms but with different connections are isomers; the difference is carried by the bond and is invisible in an ingredient count. The migration route is ordered (→), not a symmetric adjacency of neighbours.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 12 :5 — "A set of ingredients is not one. The bond is the rest of the information, and it is the half nothing was recording." Fig 12 :117 — "Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour." Fig 12 :71 — "A then B; B needs A to have happened".
- **What to check:** take two transboundary plans with the same protected areas and the same budgets. Does the connection (shared objective, continuous corridor, timing of access) change persistence and gene flow? If yes, a hectare count is the wrong verdict object.
- **Refuter:** identical atoms under two different arrangements giving statistically indistinguishable persistence and gene flow across multiple independent species. Then the bond carries no information and the isomer claim fails.

### C3 — Verification claim; checkable by anyone who has run independent monitoring

- **Statement:** coordination is triangulation between independent grounds, not agreement between agencies. Two bodies reading the same report are one frame; two instruments pointed at the same population are two frames. The treaty is a room in a schedule, not a final closure.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 2 :35 — "Alignment is triangulation, not negotiation. A mirror cannot correct you." Fig 2 :31 — "Two people reading the same report are not two frames; two instruments pointed at the same thing are." Fig 7 :36 — "A chain of mirrors is one long room. The next room must differ from the last, and match the residual it surfaced."
- **What to check:** do the two range states' population estimates come from independent instruments, or from the same national database? If the latter, their agreement is one evidence pool talking, not corroboration. Is the coordination mechanism scheduled to change when the residual changes?
- **Refuter:** a case where inter-agency agreement itself changes the measured population with no change in habitat or contact, or an institutional closure that closes the residual and needs no further handoff.

## 3. What is explicitly not claimed

- Not that borders are unreal. They are real as law and budget, and are used as the delivery mechanism.
- Not that every species needs a corridor. A strict endemic may have no cross-border contact.
- Not that translocation solves the problem. Fig 11 :66-69 gives two silent failure modes for a substituted habitat.
- Not a corridor width, stepping-stone spacing, patch count, signatory count, or fraction of range. That is a declared `gap`.
- Not a final institutional design. Fig 7 :6: "the limit: no last room."
- Not `grounded`. No measurement in this packet settles the claim.
- Not that any world source outranks a figure line. The ecology and governance sources are corroboration only.

## 5. What would settle it

A source-discriminating instrument. From *The Hard Problem is About Source*: "It was proven wrong not by argument, but by the arrival of a better instrument." For this claim:

1. **Boundary test.** Change only the line, hold habitat, movement and demography fixed. Does persistence change?
2. **Bond test.** Compare same-atom plans with different connections. Do persistence and gene flow differ?
3. **Cascade test.** Is there a measured effect two or more steps out from the breeding site, in another jurisdiction?
4. **Discriminator.** Can an independent reading distinguish a real population signal from a pooled reading of several declines?

## 6. What an expert can check in twenty minutes

1. Read Fig 11 :43-44, :52-53 and Fig 12 :5, :117, :71. Five short passages.
2. Check C1 against one species you know: where is the limiting contact, and is it across a line?
3. Check C3 against one real coordination body: are the two estimates independent, or the same report twice?
4. Name one instrument that would distinguish a connected range from three fenced fragments, or say why none is possible.
5. Send the refutation. A failure is more useful than an endorsement.
