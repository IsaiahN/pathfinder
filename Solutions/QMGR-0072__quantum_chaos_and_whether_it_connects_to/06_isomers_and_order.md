# Order and structure: why the two chaos diagnoses are not isomers

**Mode: `specified`.** This is a simulated derivation checked against the figures, not a ground reading (see `05_what_shape_means.md` §6).

## RESIDUAL

Given that chaos is a derived remainder (Fig 13 :31-32) and that the question splits into a spectral and a dynamical condition (Fig 9), two structural questions are owed:

1. What is the order of the composition that leaves the chaotic remainder?
2. Are the spectral and dynamical diagnoses **isomers** under Fig 12's definition, or something else?

## DOMAIN CENSUS

Atoms held: Space, Time, Energy (states) and Gradient, Network, Spectrum (structures). Bonds held: the seven, including `→` sequence, `⋛` comparison and the cycle structure described at Fig 12 :121. Negation is available as the eighth operator. No new primitive is available without import.

## DERIVATION

### Q1 - What is the order of the amplifying chain?

- Fig 13 :74: **"Everything else is a composition across the two kinds, never within one."**
- Fig 13 :75: **"Two states never bond directly. It is not space-time but space, a structure, time; and two structures join only through a state."**
- Fig 13 :28-29: **"They alternate, and the alternation is where both the recursion and the randomness come from. A state with no structure has no magnitude"**.
- Fig 13 :31-32: **"because each turn leaves a remainder (the residual) that re-enters the next, it is also where the randomness comes from: a remainder compounding through a chain that amplifies is chaos"**.

A first-order cross-kind molecule is `state → structure → state`, one crossing. The remainder of that turn re-enters the next. Amplification requires that the re-entry have the sign that grows the remainder rather than damping it. Fig 12 :121 names the structure: **"cycle. The output re-enters as input. The sign of the loop decides whether it closes, and a chain cannot close by construction."**

So the order is not an ordering of the six kinds (the figure writes them with commas, not arrows). The order is an ordering of **turns**: first-order cross, remainder, re-entry, amplification. Writing `Energy → Spectrum → Time → (remainder) → ...` is legitimate only if the parenthesised remainder is treated as a derived term that has re-entered, not as a seventh atom. This is the same distinction the exemplar needed for `(Space → Gradient → Time) ⋛ (Energy → Spectrum → Time)`: the order belongs to the composition, not to the primitives.

- **Falsifier, written before the run:** exhibit a chain of cross-kind turns in which the remainder re-enters with the amplifying sign but no cycle structure is present, so Fig 12 :121 does not govern it. If that is possible, the order derivation fails.

### Q2 - Are the spectral and dynamical diagnoses isomers?

- Fig 12 :117: **"isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."**
- Fig 12 :124: **"functional group. A substructure whose presence fixes the outcome. The group says what kind; the qualifier says which member."**

Compare the two conditions as the world sources describe them:

| condition | atoms or objects it actually uses | diagnostic | instrument |
|---|---|---|---|
| spectral quantum chaos | Hamiltonian, energy levels, spacings, spectral form factor | level repulsion, ramp and plateau | spectrum measurement |
| dynamical scrambling | operators `V`, `W`, commutator `[W(t),V]`, temperature | early exponential growth rate `λ_L` | OTOC measurement |

They share almost no atoms. The spectral condition has no operator commutator and no temperature-dependent growth rate; the dynamical condition has no level-spacing statistic. Under Fig 12's definition they are **not isomers**: they are not the same atoms under a different bond. They are two different structures that happen to carry the same inherited name "chaos."

The stronger version would require both to be written over the full shared set of six terms, differing only in which structure mediates which state pair. That reading is not available here: the two diagnoses do not share an atom set to rearrange. So the isomer claim fails at the weaker level too.

What they do share is a **functional group**: both are derived leftovers of cross-kind composition, and in both a remainder re-enters a chain (Fig 13 :31-32). Fig 12 :124: *"the group says what kind; the qualifier says which member."* The kind is "derived remainder"; the qualifiers are "spectral statistics" and "OTOC growth." That is the honest structural relation, and it is weaker than isomorphism.

- **Falsifier, written before the run:** exhibit one atom that is necessary to both diagnoses and absent from neither, and show that the two differ only in the bond joining a fixed atom set. That would make them isomers and overturn this finding.

### Q3 - Is the chaos bound a second-order composition?

- Fig 12 :104: **"A settled molecule becomes an atom for whatever composes over it. So irreducible is a fact about a level and never about the world."**
- The OTOC growth rate is a settled derived quantity in the physics frame (measured in trapped ions, NMR and spin systems; see the Scholarpedia review). It can serve as an atom at the next level.
- The comparison with temperature then has the **shape** of a second-order threshold: `(settled growth rate) ⋛ (temperature)`. This is the recursion Fig 12 names, not a new primitive and not an import of a new atom.
- **Strain, still live:** the Operators table defines ⋛ as *"a threshold relation between two quantities"*. At second order the operands are a rate-now-atom and a state. Fig 12 :104 permits the demotion to atom, but whether a rate counts as a quantity at that level needs an instrument, not an argument. This is the strain recorded in `01` F3 and `11_audit.md`. It is named, not hidden.

- **Falsifier, written before the run:** show that ⋛'s operands must be bare quantities at every level, so the rate-now-atom cannot occupy one. That kills the second-order reading and leaves only the shape (a threshold on a derived rate) without a bond.

### Q4 - What about the cycle bond and the sign of amplification?

Fig 12 :121 says the sign of the loop decides whether it closes. Translated to the dynamical condition: a positive Lyapunov exponent is the amplifying sign; a negative one is the damping sign. Trunin's refined indicator distinguishes the two in systems where the plain OTOC cannot (a LOTOC grows logarithmically in an integrable system, linearly in a chaotic one). This is a world-source analogue of the figure's sign rule, not a figure line, and it is recorded as corroboration only.

In the spectral condition there is no exponential loop at all: level repulsion is a static ordering statistic. So the cycle structure governs Q1 for the dynamical condition and is **silent** for the spectral condition. That silence is another reason the union must be split.

## CONCLUSION

The amplifying chain has the order `cross-kind turn, remainder, re-entry, amplification`, with the cycle structure of Fig 12 :121 governing the re-entry. The two chaos diagnoses are **not isomers**: they share almost no atoms, so they are two members of a functional group (derived remainders of cross-kind composition) with different qualifiers. The chaos bound has the *shape* of a second-order threshold over a settled growth rate, and that reading needs no new primitive; the ⋛ operand type is a live strain. The isomer claim and the identity claim are both refused without touching the shape.
