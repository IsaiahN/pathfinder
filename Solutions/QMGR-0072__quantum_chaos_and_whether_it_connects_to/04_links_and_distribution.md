## Wider literature that bears on this question

The physics literature is not one pool. Three distinct closures are visible in the material actually read:

1. **Spectral / statistical** (Scholarpedia's first half): Wigner's compound-nucleus level statistics, random-matrix ensembles, the spectral form factor, ETH. No `2π` bound here.
2. **Dynamical / holographic** (MSS, Shenker-Stanford, Sekino-Susskind, SYK via Trunin): OTOCs, scrambling time, the butterfly effect, saturation. This is where the bound lives.
3. **Diagnostic critique** (Xu et al., Trunin): explicitly argues that the diagnostic used in closure 2 can fire without chaos. This is the low-overlap check that forces the union split in `01` F2.

These three do not share a single evidence pool: (1) is spectral statistics, (2) is holography and large-N field theory, (3) is semiclassical integrability and Keldysh replicas. Their agreement that OTOCs are *useful* does not make one witness into three, and the explicit disagreement between (2) and (3) on what an OTOC growth rate means is recorded as a conflict, not smoothed away.
