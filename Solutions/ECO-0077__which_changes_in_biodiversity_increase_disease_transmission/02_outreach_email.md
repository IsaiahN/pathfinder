# Outreach draft: check the output first, then decide about the framework

**Use:** one-to-one, or as a cover note attached to a submission through the public venues in
`03_public_contact_points.csv`. **Not** for bulk send.

**Hard precondition:** do not send until the shape claim and its refuter in
`01_figure_grounded_audit.md` survive the reader's own check. If the output does not survive, there
is nothing else to offer.

---

**Subject:** One claim about biodiversity and disease that you can check in twenty minutes

> Hi [name],
>
> I have one specific output I would like you to check against what you already know. I am not asking
> you to read the framework first and I am not asking you to agree with it. If the output does not
> survive, there is nothing else to look at.
>
> **The output (`general` on the shape, `specified` on the mechanism conditions, not `grounded`):**
> a change in biodiversity does not carry its own sign for disease transmission. The sign belongs to
> the relation between the change and a specific residual, meaning a specific pathogen, host, place
> and scale. The same actor can lower transmission for one pathogen and raise it for another. So the
> question "which changes increase transmission" cannot be answered with a context-free list. What
> can be answered is the shape of the answer: for a named residual, enumerate everything in contact
> with it (hosts, vectors, competitors, predators, conditions and relations), follow the cascade
> outward until it stops mattering, and read the sign at the link that changed. Changes that raise
> competent-host density, contact rate or transmissibility amplify. Changes that lower them, or add
> non-competent hosts or predators that suppress a reservoir, dilute.
>
> This is a prediction of a **shape**, not a measurement and not a universal rule. I am not claiming
> that biodiversity always protects health, that any named species is always good or bad, or that a
> review verdict counts as evidence for a particular place.
>
> **What would refute it, written down before this message:**
> 1. one aggregate diversity metric predicts the sign of transmission change across independently
>    chosen pathogen systems, out of sample, with no interaction terms;
> 2. the same actor keeps the same transmission valence for two different pathogens in the same
>    habitat and scale;
> 3. a habitat contact can be removed outright, with no substitution and no continuing work, and
>    disease risk falls;
> 4. a net effect sign identifies its component mechanism without any component measurement and
>    survives replication.
>
> If any of these holds, I will retract the claim. A one-line "this fails because..." is more useful
> to me than agreement. If you would rather not engage, no reply is needed.
>
> **If it survives, and only then:** here are the public sources, with the note that the figures,
> not my summary of them, are the authority.
> - Figure text extract with line numbers (local copy in this project, path on request)
> - Keesing et al., 2010, Nature, https://www.nature.com/articles/nature09575
> - Luis et al., 2018, PNAS, https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6077700/
> - Chen and McFarlane, 2025, Ecology and Evolution,
>   https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12343334/
>
> **If you want to reuse the method:** the loop is public. Run it on a pathogen you know well and can
> check against measured infection. Write down what would refute your result *before* you run it and
> state the mode of what you get. The source warns that outputs can be simulated rather than tested,
> so a reported failure is more useful to me than a reported success.
>
>: [your name]

## Why this version is ordered this way

| the move | figure-grounded reason |
|---|---|
| Output first, theory second | Fig 10 :52: *"Bring a characterised residual, never the source."* The recipient checks the claim without holding the framework's interior. |
| Do not ask for agreement | Fig 2 :35: *"Alignment is triangulation, not negotiation. A mirror cannot correct you."* |
| Refuter before conclusion | A falsifier written before the run is a design instrument, not an honesty device. |
| Mode stated on the claim | `THE_FORMULA.md`: *"Mode. general · specified · grounded. State which."* |
| Links only after the stage-1 verdict | A reader who has absorbed the source before answering stops being an outside frame; later agreement is never pooled as corroboration (Fig 2 :40). |
| No "biodiversity always wins" framing | Fig 11 :49-51: valence is relative to the residual and no standing list of good actors survives a change of residual. |
| Management advice avoided in stage 1 | Fig 11 :61-62: introduction is available, exclusion is not; the lever depends on the enumerated habitat. |
| Offer the method, not an answer | Fig 4 :22: *"Only methods cross up. Only head starts come down."* |
| Ask the reader to state their own ground | Fig 2 :29: an anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks. |
