## Wider literature that bears on the residual

- Ostfeld and Keesing's dilution-effect framing, as restated on the Cary Institute page, is the
  positive case: high host diversity lowers the probability that a tick feeds on a competent mouse.
- Salkeld et al. 2013 and Chen and McFarlane 2025 are the critical case: the relationship is weak,
  heterogeneous and hard to falsify at the net level.
- Luis et al. 2018 is the reconciliation case: opposing mechanisms run at once, and the net sign is
  whichever is stronger.
- Keeling and Eames 2005 and Silk et al. 2019 supply the mathematics: structure of contact, not just
  count of individuals, governs spread.
- Hopkins et al. 2022 supplies the policy case: win-win interventions exist but are context
  dependent and often data deficient.

Overlap warning: Keesing 2010, Luis 2018, Salkeld 2013, the Cary page and Hopkins 2022 cite one
another and draw on the same disease-ecology evidence pool. They are one witness with several names
(Fig 2 :40). Keeling and Eames (mathematics) and Chen and McFarlane (philosophy of science) are the
low-overlap witnesses.

## Distribution: offer, do not push

Figure-grounded reasons:

- **Fig 4 :22-25**: *"Only methods cross up. Only head starts come down."* Sending the full
  architecture before anyone has described a residual sends a recording, not a generator. Lead with
  the shape claim and its refuter and offer the links after.
- **Fig 10 :42**: *"A verifier that can reconstruct the claim cannot verify it."* Seat 4 must not
  hold the interiors. A recipient who has absorbed the framework before answering has joined the
  frame being checked.
- **Fig 9 :27-28**: *"describe it before you go looking, or any frame you pick will seem to fit."*
  Ask what they would expect to see if the shape claim were true before giving them the sources.
- **Fig 2 :40**: shared-pool agreement is disqualifying for a verdict. Never pool later agreement
  as corroboration.
- **Fig 7 :13**: *"agreement compounds, evidence does not."* A wider audience is not a better test.

**Recommended order in a reply:** (1) the shape claim and its four refuters; (2) ask what they would
count as a failure and which contact link they would measure first; (3) only then the links, with
the note that the figure text, not this summary, is the authority.

## The two-stage offer (with the optional third stage)

1. **Stage 1.** Send the residual and the refuter. Ask for a verdict on that alone and record it
   before showing any theory. This is Fig 10 :52, *"Bring a characterised residual, never the
   source,"* and Fig 2 :45, *"Frames range against the ground. They do not negotiate with it."*
2. **Stage 2, opt-in only.** If they are curious, offer the figure text and the eight world links.
   Once a recipient has absorbed the theory, their agreement stops being evidence, so the stage-1
   verdict must already be on record (Fig 2 :40).
3. **Stage 3, also opt-in.** Offer the formula as a reusable method. Fig 4 :22 permits sending a
   generator. Each user must pick their own ground, write their own refuter before running it and
   state the mode of what they get, because a loop cannot check the direction of its own question.

**Hard precondition:** stages 1 to 3 only work if the stage-1 output survives. If a checker fires one
of the four refuters, the claim is withdrawn or narrowed before anything else is sent.
