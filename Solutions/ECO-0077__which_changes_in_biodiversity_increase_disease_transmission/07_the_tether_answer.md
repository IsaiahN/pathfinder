# The Tether's answer: biodiversity change and disease transmission

**Mode: `general` on the shape, `specified` on the mechanism conditions, not `grounded`.** Simulated
derivation checked against the figures (`05_what_shape_means.md` §6), not a ground reading.

## 1. The residual it answers

A change in the living community is proposed, and someone needs the sign and size of the resulting
change in transmission of a named pathogen to a named host. The residual is that the sign does not
resolve from the aggregate "biodiversity went up or down." It resolves only when the host, vector,
contact structure and scale are named. Fig 1 :19: *"The residual is indexed by object slot, not
aggregated."* This is a union compound of component mechanisms, not one hard question (Fig 9 :54).
It has its own loop and its own ground.

## 2. The shape, per domain, kept separate

Fig 11 :43: *"The habitat is enumerated, never composed."* This is the rule that opens the first domain.

### D1, the pathogen's habitat

1. **Valence is a relation, not a property.** Fig 11 :49-51: *"An actor's valence is relative to the
   residual, not a property it carries."* *"The same actor is a benefactor to one residual and a
   malfactor to another, so mutualism and parasitism are relations rather than kinds."* *"No standing
   list of good actors survives a change of residual."* So no universal list of transmission-
   increasing or transmission-reducing biodiversity changes is figure-licensed. The framework
   refuses the list as written.
2. **The habitat is enumerated, not composed.** Fig 11 :43-44: *"The habitat is enumerated, never
   composed."* *"List everything in contact with the residual, then what is in contact with those,
   and outward until the cascade stops mattering."* The list is read off the world (Fig 11 :48), not
   derived from species counts.
3. **Contact cascades.** Fig 11 :52-53: the DDT example makes a third-order effect decisive. A
   first-order habitat description can be right and still miss the residual that matters.
4. **Mechanism-level sign.** The changed contact link is read as host density, contact rate,
   transmissibility per contact and host competence. Changes that raise competent-host density,
   contact rate or transmissibility amplify; changes that lower them, or add non-competent hosts or
   predators that suppress reservoirs, dilute. This is the `specified` part, corroborated by Luis et
   al. 2018 (opened), Keeling and Eames 2005 (opened) and the Cary Institute page (opened).

### D2, the management seat

5. **Introduction is available, exclusion is not.** Fig 11 :61-62: *"The seat's power is asymmetric.
   Introduction is available; exclusion is not. You can add an actor and you cannot subtract the
   habitat, because contact is a fact about the world and does not consult you."* "Reduce biodiversity
   to reduce disease" is therefore not generally an available lever. Available levers add actors
   (predators, non-competent hosts, vaccination, vector control) or substitute habitats.
6. **Isolation is substitution.** Fig 11 :66-69: *"Isolation is not removal of the habitat. It is
   substitution of one habitat for another."* You inherit the new habitat's actors and you cannot see
   what you failed to reproduce until the goal fails. Managed ecosystems are permanent work.
7. **Unenumerated introduction is the named failure.** Fig 11 :65: cane toads and kudzu are the same
   move with the cascade unenumerated. Any biodiversity-based intervention that skips step 2 of the
   order in `06_isomers_and_order.md` is that failure.

### D3, the verification frame

8. **A net pattern is a filter, a component measurement is a witness.** Fig 9 :60: *"Use filters for
   the budget and witnesses for the verdict. Never let a filter hand you a verdict."* A review
   verdict, a meta-analytic mean and a policy label decide where to spend effort, not what is true in
   a system. Chen and McFarlane 2025 (opened) make the same methodological point from philosophy of
   science.
9. **Agreement is not an anchor.** Fig 2 :35: *"Alignment is triangulation, not negotiation. A mirror
   cannot correct you."* Fig 2 :40: *"disqualifying for a verdict: agreement about the world is
   worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool."* Most ecology-facing sources read here
   share one pool, so their convergence is one witness.
10. **The verdict is a shape, not a name.** Fig 9 :32: *"you get a kind of thing, never the answer
    itself."* The answer tells you what any acceptable answer must contain; it does not fill the slot
    with a species or a policy.

## 3. What it explicitly does not say

- Not that biodiversity always reduces disease, and not that it always increases it. Both net signs
  occur in the literature and the figure verdict explains why neither is a law.
- Not that any named species, predator, forest or wetland is universally good or bad. That is exactly
  the "standing list" the figures forbid (Fig 11 :51).
- Not a universal list of which biodiversity changes increase transmission. The question as asked is
  the wrong slot; the framework returns a shape (Fig 9 :32). This is a refusal, not a silence.
- Not that a single index (richness, evenness, Shannon) is adequate. It averages the slots (Fig 1 :22).
- Not management advice without an enumerated habitat. Fig 11 :61-69 applies.
- Not `grounded`. No measurement here settles any real system.

## 4. What would settle it: the ground

A source-discriminating measurement for the specific residual, not a further argument.

1. Does the sign predicted by the enumerated contact cascade match measured infection in the target
   host after the change? That is the anchor (Fig 2 :29-30: *"An anchor is whatever settles the
   question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks."*).
2. Does one aggregate diversity metric predict the sign out of sample across independently chosen
   pathogens? If yes, F1 fails and the universal list becomes thinkable.
3. Does the same actor hold the same valence for two pathogens in the same habitat and scale? If yes,
   Fig 11 :50-51 fails for this domain.
4. Can a habitat contact be subtracted outright with no substitution and no ongoing work? If yes,
   Fig 11 :61-69 fails for this domain.
5. Does first-order enumeration suffice where a third-order cascade was expected? If yes, the order
   claim in `06_isomers_and_order.md` loses a step.

## 5. The honest stop

**The universal list is refused, not unreachable.** Fig 13 :134 says *"Unreachable is a verdict here,
not a silence"*, but that line governs a term out of reach with the library as it stands. Here the
term is not out of reach; it is the wrong shape. The stop is at the limit of what the figures can
license: a rule for constructing a system-specific answer and four written refuters. Beyond that,
only a measured ground for a named pathogen, host and place can close the residual. This derivation
records the route so the stop is checkable.

## 6. One-paragraph version for outreach

> The figures do not say that biodiversity is good or bad for disease. They say that valence belongs
> to the relation between a community change and a specific pathogen, host and place, not to the
> change itself. So there is no universal list of transmission-increasing or reducing biodiversity
> changes; there is a shape. For a named residual, enumerate everything in contact with it, follow
> the cascade outward until it stops mattering, read the sign at the link that changed (host density,
> contact rate, transmissibility, competence), and let measured infection in the target host decide.
> Managers can add actors or substitute habitats but cannot subtract a habitat, so a proposed
> intervention with an unenumerated cascade is the failure mode the figures name. Four refuters are
> written in advance. This is a `specified` shape, not a `grounded` ecological result, and the
> simulated derivation cannot settle any particular system.
