**Subject:** Check one output about prebiotic settings before you decide whether the shape is worth arguing with

> Hi [name],
>
> I have one specific output I would like you to check against what you already know about early-Earth settings. I am not asking you to read the framework first, and I am not asking you to agree with it. If the output does not survive, there is nothing else to look at.
>
> **The output (`specified` on the conditions, `general` on the union, a declared `gap` on the named list):**
>
> The question "which settings lasted long enough for complicated chemistry to build up" is not one question. It is a union of four conditions, and a setting has to clear all four:
>
> 1. **Contact and persistence.** The conditions have to stay in touch with the chemistry long enough for products to compound. A setting that severs contact early fails regardless of how good its chemistry is.
> 2. **Concentration.** Building blocks have to be crowded together faster than dilution removes them. A well-mixed open setting fails this even if it supplies energy.
> 3. **A spendable gradient.** A difference that can actually drive uphill synthesis is needed, not merely a uniform warm environment.
> 4. **Re-entrant cycling.** Wet/dry, freeze/thaw, diurnal, or seasonal loops must feed their output back as input. A one-pass flow cannot close.
>
> That is a shape, not a ranking. The framework returns a kind of thing, never the answer itself, and it does not name which geological setting occupies the shape. I am explicitly not giving you a winner among vents, ponds, ice, hot springs, and crust. Which of those actually lasted long enough is owed to your ground: rock, dating, concentration assays, and lab reconstruction, not to agreement.
>
> **What would refute the shape claim:**
> 1. multi-step accumulation in a setting with no spendable gradient and no imported gradient;
> 2. accumulation compounding through a single pass with no output re-entering as input;
> 3. concentration and products increasing in a well-mixed setting where dilution exceeds input;
> 4. accumulation completing after contact with the setting has been severed;
> 5. the four conditions collapsing to one measured lever, making the union a partition.
>
> I wrote these down before sending this. If any one holds, the shape is wrong and I will retract it. A one-line "this fails because..." is more useful to me than agreement. If you would rather not engage, no reply is needed.
>

## Contact discipline

- One recipient at a time. No bulk send, no automation, no misleading subject line.
- Record the stage-1 verdict **before** any framework material is shown. A later agreement is never pooled as corroboration (Fig 2 :40).
- If a recipient returns a genuine refutation, copy it into `11_audit.md` with provenance and re-evaluate it as if it came from any other source (Fig 10 :62-64, rulings carry provenance).
- The venue list in `03_public_contact_points.csv` is institutional and opt-in. Use the public form or submission route, never a personal address found on a page.
