## VERDICT (from the figures)

The shape survives; the list does not. A qualifying setting is an enumerated habitat whose conditions stay in contact with the chemistry long enough (Fig 13 :12, Fig 11 :40) for a spendable gradient (Fig 13 :22) and re-entrant cycling with the right sign (Fig 12 :121) to concentrate building blocks faster than degradation removes them (Fig 6 :21). Over the fixed condition set, settings are isomers (Fig 12 :117) or at minimum members of one functional group (Fig 12 :123). The question is a union, so the four verdicts are kept separate and never averaged (Fig 9 :54). Which named setting actually qualified is a declared `gap` owed to rock, dating, concentration assays and experiment (Fig 2 :29, Fig 9 :32, Fig 13 :134); it is `unreached`, not silence and not a guess. This is a `specified`, ungrounded result. Simulated derivation, not a ground reading.

- **cost if wrong:** bounded and locatable. The shape is mode-labeled, lists five refuters written in advance, and records its lineage, so an error is findable rather than invisible. The real cost is spending the independence of outside readers by sending a named winner instead of a shape and refuter (Fig 2 :42: "Buy independence in proportion to the cost of being wrong."). That cost is avoidable by leading with the refuter.
- **undoable:** yes. Any binding of the four conditions to a named setting can be removed without touching the shape. A ground result can supersede the gap; no primitive has to be withdrawn. The isomer claim can fall back to the functional-group claim with no loss to the rest.
- **reversed by:** a ground reading, not agreement. A duration or concentration discriminator that selects a setting; a direct measurement of accumulation after contact is severed; a demonstration of accumulation with no spendable gradient or no re-entrant cycle; a re-census that dissolves the four-condition union into one measured lever; or a figure re-census that names a setting. Until one of these fires, the verdict stands as `candidate`.
