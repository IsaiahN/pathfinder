## The question restated as an effect

What is being decided is not *which named lever is best* but *what an intervention must be made of to keep driving a residual down while its operands and its habitat move*. In effect terms: a designer inserts an intervention I into a library Gamma so that the prediction Gamma(b,a) tracks the observation o-prime and R = |Gamma(b,a) - o-prime| shrinks on each object slot s. R is indexed per slot, never aggregated (Fig 1 :19, Fig 1 :22). A global R near zero with one live slot is a legal state (Fig 1 :13).

- Option A, "the right lever is a fixed rule applied once": leads to the prediction that success is a property of the lever considered alone.
- Option B, "a working intervention is a maintained relation between a designer, adaptive agents, and a changing habitat": leads to the prediction that success is a property of designer, agents and habitat together, and fails when the target becomes movable, the channel decays, or the habitat is substituted (Fig 11 :39, Fig 11 :40, Fig 11 :66).
- What an answer would look like, written down and bound as an ordinary term before the run (Formula, "Direction"): a *shape* naming the conditions that must all hold for a slot's R to keep trending down under strategic adaptation and endogenous rewiring, plus an instrument that would discriminate a failure of one condition from a failure of another. That shape is the sought-for term; until it is bound it predicts nothing and no check in the loop can see it.

The verdict recorded in `09_watson_answer.md` is Option B in `specified` mode, kept per domain and never averaged. Which named strategy (pricing, mandate, nudge, seeding) is best is a declared `gap`, not a hidden answer.

## What is deliberately not here

- No personal emails of named individuals.
- No ranking of people by how likely they are to agree.
- No bulk-send plan.
- No claim that this derivation is `grounded`, no claim that it settles the intervention question, and no claim that the framework is the only true account of intervention in adaptive systems.

Three figure-grounded reasons, plus one practical one:

1. A room is not an anchor. *"A room's agreement is variation, not a verdict. Selection between rooms is where the ground votes."* (Fig 7 :35) and *"Alignment is triangulation, not negotiation. A mirror cannot correct you."* (Fig 2 :35). Gathering agreeable readers manufactures the correlation that disqualifies a verdict.
2. Correlation has valence. *"disqualifying for a verdict: agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool"* (Fig 2 :40). A list selected for receptiveness is one witness wearing many names.
3. A verifier must be outside the interior. *"A verifier that can reconstruct the claim cannot verify it."* (Fig 10 :42). The first contact therefore carries the residual and its refuter, not the framework.
4. Legally, unsolicited bulk email to scraped addresses breaches GDPR and CAN-SPAM style rules in many jurisdictions and gets the sending domain blacklisted. Public opt-in routes avoid that failure as well as the structural one.
