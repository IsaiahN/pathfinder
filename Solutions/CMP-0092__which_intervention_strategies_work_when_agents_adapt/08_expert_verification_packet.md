# Expert verification packet - intervention under adaptation and network change

**What this is:** a claim in a form an adaptive-interventions researcher can check. Mode, derivation and refuters are stated before the conclusion, so a reader knows what to look for and what would settle it.

**Mode: `specified` - not `grounded`.** No domain measurement is claimed. This is a shape and a route, not a result. Simulated derivation checked for internal consistency against the figures, not a ground reading.

## 1. The claim, in one paragraph

An intervention keeps driving a per-slot residual down under strategic adaptation and endogenous network change only if it has five properties: its target does not move when the agents push on it; it is maintained contact, not a one-shot setting; it is evaluated per slot against a ground, not by aggregate compliance; it treats strategic response and network rewiring as a union of two conditions needing more than one lever; and it is selected against the residual the previous intervention surfaced, from frames that stay independent. The framework does not name a winning instrument. It returns the shape any working candidate must have, with falsifiers written in advance.

## 2. Three checkable sub-claims

### C1 - framework-internal; checkable by reading Figures 2 and 10

- **Statement:** a control target that agents can move is not an anchor. A compliance metric that decides an outcome has stopped being a metric of the outcome.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 2 :30 - *"you push on it, it is not an anchor."* Fig 2 :33 - *"The anchor must not update."* Fig 10 :47 - *"a convention that decides an outcome has stopped being one"*.
- **Refuter:** an intervention that keeps the per-slot residual trending down while its stated target is demonstrably movable by the agents it targets breaks this sub-claim. In world terms, a measure that survives being made a target without degradation would also break it.

### C2 - domains 2 and 3; checkable by an adaptive-interventions or network researcher

- **Statement:** intervention valence is relative to the residual, and the network that rewires is a habitat update, not a ground update. There is no standing list of good interventions; the next one must be selected against the residual the last one surfaced.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 11 :51 - *"No standing list of good actors survives a change of residual."* Fig 11 :73 - *"The habitat is not the ground. The habitat is where actors come from and it updates; the ground is what settles, and it does not."* Fig 11 :63 - *"Which is why a managed ecosystem is permanent work rather than a solved state."*
- **What to check:** in your own system, does an instrument that worked before adaptation still work after the agents learned it? Does the connection structure change in a way that re-enters the same residual? Does the intervention need to be maintained? The adaptive-resistance literature (Cremers and Nguyen 2019, Cancer Drug Resistance) and the adaptive-rewiring literature (Weng and Lee 2025, arXiv) are relevant comparisons, but they are corroboration only.
- **Refuter:** an intervention whose sign stays fixed across a documented change of residual, or a one-shot intervention with no maintained contact that survives a habitat substitution.

### C3 - the union and measurement; checkable by a policy or programme evaluator

- **Statement:** strategic adaptation and endogenous rewiring are a union of two conditions, so one lever clears only the single-condition region and leaves the intersection. Aggregate success metrics hide the live slot.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 9 :56 - *"A single lever clears the region where only one condition holds and leaves the intersections standing, because the second condition still binds."* Fig 1 :22 - *"Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average."*
- **Refuter:** one lever that restores both conditions with no categorically different residue; or a programme whose aggregate and per-slot residuals diverge and whose divergence is reported in advance.

## 3. What is explicitly not claimed

- Not that any named intervention (pricing, mandate, nudge, norm, network seeding) is the winner. That is a declared `gap` (Fig 9 :32).
- Not that the intervention question is one problem. It is a union compound with its own loop.
- Not that adaptation or network change moves the ground. The ground does not move; the target selected was never the ground.
- Not a one-shot fix. Managed ecosystems are permanent work, and introduction is available while exclusion is not.
- Not `grounded`. Nothing here is asserted as settled by experiment.
- Not that a reply agreeing with this packet is corroboration. Agreement from a shared evidence pool is one witness (Fig 2 :40).

## 5. What would settle it

A ground reading, not agreement. The discriminators:

1. Does the target move when agents push on it?
2. Does the intervention change the per-slot success rate, or only the aggregate?
3. Does the connection structure rewire after the intervention, and does that re-enter as input to the same residual?
4. Is the intervention spent or regenerated across uses?
5. Does one lever clear both union conditions, leaving no categorically different residue?

## 6. What an expert can check in twenty minutes

1. Read Fig 2 :29-34, Fig 11 :51-73 and Fig 9 :54-57. Three short passages.
2. Check C1 against one measure in your field: can the agents being measured move it?
3. Check C2 against one intervention you know: did its sign change after adaptation, and was the habitat substituted?
4. Name one instrument that would distinguish a real improvement from adaptation to the metric, or say why none is possible.
5. Send the refutation. A failure is more useful than an endorsement.
