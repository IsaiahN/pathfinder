# The Tether's answer on intervention under adaptation and network change

**Mode: `specified` - not `grounded`.** This is a shape and a route, not a result. Simulated derivation checked against the figures (`05_what_shape_means.md` section 6), not a ground reading.

## 1. The residual it answers

The question asks which intervention strategies work when agents adapt strategically and the network changes in response. Restated as an effect: an intervention is meant to drive R = |Gamma(b,a) - o-prime| toward zero per slot s, but the agents being intervened on update their own b, w and Gamma after it is applied, and the coupling structure (the habitat) updates at the same time. R persists and does not trend down (Fig 5 :59). The two changes are a union compound, not one hard problem: *"What remains is smaller and it is also categorically different: a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either."* (Fig 9 :57). It has its own loop and its own ground.

## 2. The shape

1. **A movable target is not an anchor.** Fig 2 :29-30: *"An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks. A measurement, an experiment, a counter, a market, a patient outcome."* And Fig 2 :30: *"you push on it, it is not an anchor."* Strategic adaptation is exactly the case where the controlled quantity updates on what the controlled frames think. Fig 10 :47: *"a convention that decides an outcome has stopped being one."* An intervention whose success is measured by a score the agents can move has no anchor.

2. **Intervention is agent-and-habitat, not agent alone.** Fig 11 :39: *"So the answerable question is not what intelligence is, but what makes a system capable. And capability is a property of agent-and-habitat, never of the agent alone."* Fig 11 :40: *"A system cannot be made more capable by improving the agent in isolation. An improvement that does not change contact changes nothing, however much it improves."* The network that rewires is the habitat updating; the ground does not update (Fig 11 :73).

3. **Valence is relative to the residual.** Fig 11 :51: *"No standing list of good actors survives a change of residual."* An intervention that was a benefactor can become a malfactor (Fig 11 :50). So there is no permanent "best strategy" list; the next intervention is selected against the residual the last one surfaced, from a room that differs from the last (Fig 7 :36).

4. **It is maintained work, introduction not exclusion.** Fig 11 :61: *"The seat's power is asymmetric. Introduction is available; exclusion is not. You can add an actor and you cannot subtract the habitat, because contact is a fact about the world and does not consult you."* Fig 11 :63: *"Which is why a managed ecosystem is permanent work rather than a solved state."* A sandbox proves wiring, not capability (Fig 11 :70).

5. **A single lever leaves the union intersection standing.** Fig 9 :56: *"A single lever clears the region where only one condition holds and leaves the intersections standing, because the second condition still binds."* Strategic adaptation and network rewiring are two conditions, so a lever aimed at one leaves the compound intact.

6. **Measure per slot and by round trip, not by aggregate.** Fig 1 :22: *"Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average."* Fig 1 :27: *"They are frame-internal. A frame cannot score itself with a quantity it also produces."* Fig 4 :30: *"So take the round trip and measure the gap. Without it you are navigating by dead reckoning: confident about a position nobody checked."* Compliance counts, coverage and number of interventions shipped are frame-internal; the live slot residual and R_T are the honest report.

7. **An intervention is a composition, not a scalar.** Fig 12 :112: *"A plan is a sequence. A goal is a comparison. Progress is a subtraction."* Fig 12 :121: *"cycle. The output re-enters as input. The sign of the loop decides whether it closes, and a chain cannot close by construction."* The intervention must be a catalyst that survives its own use (Fig 12 :119), because the adaptive network is a cycle that can close against it.

8. **Keep the frames independent.** Fig 2 :42: *"Buy independence in proportion to the cost of being wrong."* Fig 8 :47: *"Independence is one quantity read twice."* A panel of adapters who all learned the same response is one frame wearing many names.

9. **Which named strategy wins is a `gap`.** Fig 9 :32: *"you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself."* No figure line selects among pricing, mandate, nudge, norm or seeding after a genuine re-census. The missing thing is a source-discriminating instrument per domain, characterizable in effect terms but not held. *"Unreachable is a verdict here, not a silence,"* (Fig 13 :134).

## 3. What it explicitly does not say

- Not that any named strategy is the best one, in any domain.
- Not that the intervention question is one problem. It is a union of two conditions with its own compound.
- Not that adaptation or network change moves the ground. The ground does not move; the target selected was never the ground.
- Not that an intervention can be applied once and left. Managed ecosystems are permanent work.
- Not `grounded`. No domain measurement settles any of this here.
- Not a claim that the figures are the only true account of intervention. The world sources corroborate at three pools, not eight independent witnesses.

## 4. What would settle it - the ground

A source-discriminating instrument, not a further argument. From *The Hard Problem is About Source*: an instrument that reads green *"cannot comment on the source"*; the spectrometer resolved it. Here:

- Does the proposed target move when agents push on it? An instrument that discriminates a real improvement from adaptation to the metric would anchor the intervention.
- Does the intervention change the per-slot success rate, or only the aggregate? A per-slot instrument would reveal the live case hidden by averaging.
- Does the connection structure rewire after the intervention, and does that re-enter as input to the same residual? A network-contact instrument would close or refuse the cycle.
- Six standing falsifiers from `01` section 4, including a lever that clears both union conditions with no categorically different residue, and a chain of agreeing interventions that keeps the per-slot residual down.

## 5. The honest stop

**Unreached, not unreachable.** The six-term substrate is *"a human's careful guess at reality's compression axes"*; declaring it makes the choice visible, not neutral (see *They're Scanning Books*). The answer stops at the limit of the available instruments and records the lineage so the stop is checkable. *"Indiscernibility is not identity."* The name of the winning intervention needs a ground reading in a specific field; this folder does not have one.

## 6. One-paragraph version for outreach

> The Tether does not name a winning intervention strategy, because the framework returns a shape, not a name. It says that a target which moves when agents push on it is not an anchor; that intervention is a property of agents and habitat together, so a network that rewires is the habitat updating rather than the ground moving; that no standing list of good interventions survives a change of residual; that managed intervention is permanent work in which you can introduce but not subtract; that strategic adaptation and endogenous rewiring form a union, so a single lever clears one condition and leaves the intersection; and that progress must be measured per slot and by a round-trip gap, not by aggregate compliance. An intervention is a bonded composition of plan, goal and subtraction, maintained as a catalyst against a cycle that can close against it. What is offered is the shape any working candidate must have, the six falsifiers written in advance, and the instruments that would close it. Which named strategy wins stays open until a ground settles it.
