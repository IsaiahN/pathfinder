## What the wider literature bears on the question

Three evidence pools, not ten independent witnesses.

1. **Metrics, incentives and policy (Goodhart, Campbell, Lucas).** A measure used as a target degrades; a quantitative social indicator used for decisions gets corrupted; policy changes alter the decision rules of the agents being modelled. All three predict that a fixed lever tuned on historical behaviour fails after the agents learn the lever. This corroborates F1 and F2 at the world level. Overlap between the three is high (shared "gaming the measure" literature), so they count as one witness.
2. **Systems and resilience (Meadows, adaptive management, complex adaptive systems).** Leverage is not in parameters but in rules, goals, self-organization and the ability to transcend paradigms. Adaptive management treats policy as experiments, needs repeated decisions to learn, and warns that information feedback frequently fails to reach decision makers. CAS work says outcomes *"may be influenced but not precisely controlled."* Corroborates F3, F4, F5 and F6. One pool, high internal overlap.
3. **Adaptive networks (Weng and Lee, Cremers and Nguyen).** In social networks, agents who can sever and form ties reshape cooperation outcomes, and constraint level changes the regime. In oncology, targeted therapy is followed by rapid signalling-network rewiring that circumvents the drug, and combination strategies are the response. Both corroborate the union claim in F5: rewiring is a second condition that a lever aimed only at behaviour leaves standing. Two fields with low overlap with each other, but both are simulation or mechanistic case studies, not measurements of the general intervention question.

No world source was read that contradicts the figure verdict. The conflict rule was not triggered. Agreement across the three pools is expected and does not make the framework true; the figures decide, the world corroborates.

## Distribution: offer, do not push

- **Fig 4 :22** - *"Only methods cross up. Only head starts come down."* Sending the full architecture before anyone has described a residual sends a recording, not a generator. Lead with the shape claim and its refuter; offer links after.
- **Fig 10 :42** - *"A verifier that can reconstruct the claim cannot verify it."* A recipient who has absorbed the framework before being asked for a verdict has joined the frame being checked.
- **Fig 9 :27-28** - *"describe it before you go looking, or any frame you pick will seem to fit"* - ask what they would expect to see if the shape claim were true before giving them the literature.
- **Fig 2 :40** - *"disqualifying for a verdict: agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool"* - do not select recipients for receptiveness and do not pool later agreement as corroboration.
- **Fig 2 :42** - *"Buy independence in proportion to the cost of being wrong."* - prefer readers whose ground is in a different closure from the framework's, and keep their first verdict separate from any later discussion.

**Recommended order in a reply:** (1) the shape claim and its five refuters; (2) ask what they would count as a failure in their own domain; (3) record that verdict; (4) only then, the links, with the note that the figures, not this summary, are the authority.

## The two-stage offer

1. **Stage 1:** validate the stage-1 output against the ground. Fig 10 :52 - *"Bring a characterised residual, never the source."*
2. **Stage 2, opt-in only:** if the recipient is curious after recording a verdict, offer the three local framework files. Their later agreement is never pooled as corroboration.
3. **Stage 3:** offer the formula as a method to reuse, with the instruction that each user state their own mode and write their own refuter before running it. `THE_FORMULA.md` "Direction" gives the reason: a loop *"cannot check the direction of its own question."*

**Hard precondition:** stages 1 to 3 only work if the stage-1 output survives. That is what the five refuters are for. A failure is more useful than an endorsement.
