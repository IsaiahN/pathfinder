## 0. The residual, stated in effect terms

R = |Gamma(b,a) - o-prime| per slot s (Formula step 1). An intervention I is a term inserted into a designer's library Gamma that is meant to drive R toward zero on a set of slots. The question says two things about the setting that are easy to treat as background and are actually the whole residual:

1. **Agents adapt strategically.** The operands of I hold their own b, w and Gamma. They update these in response to I. So the prediction Gamma(b,a) that I was tuned against moves after I is applied. R persists and does not trend down (Fig 5 :59).
2. **The network changes in response.** The set of slots and the couplings between them, that is the habitat, updates after I. A coupling that was a benefactor becomes a malfactor (Fig 11 :49-51). The designer is in a habitat that moved, and "you inherit the new habitat's actors, which you did not choose" (Fig 11 :67).

So the residual is not "which lever has the biggest effect at t=0." It is "what must an intervention be made of so that R keeps trending down when both the target and the coupling structure are moving." The two conditions form a compound (Fig 9 :54-57), so one lever cannot in principle clear both.

**Binding the sought-for shape before the run (Formula, "Direction").** The answer-shape is written down here as an ordinary term so it can accrue a residual like anything else: *a maintained, anchored, per-slot, introduced-not-subtracted, union-splitting composition over independent frames, selected against a ground that does not update.* Anything that fails one of those five conjuncts is not yet an answer. This binding is the pre-registered direction of the search, not a result.

## 1. Domain census

R by arity, symmetry and scale, then one line per domain with actors / currency / anchor. No nameable anchor means it is not a domain yet.

- **R (arity / symmetry / scale).** Multi-place and multi-slot: designer intervention, agent action, network coupling, ground reading. R is indexed per slot, not aggregated (Fig 1 :19, Fig 1 :22). Symmetry: broken. Influence travels along a network (Fig 13 :101) and the relevant relation is a threshold comparison, not a symmetric join. Scale: single agent loop, population-plus-network loop, and a lossy membrane between them (Fig 4).

| domain | actors | currency | anchor | status |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| D1 Intervention and design frame | designer, policy, metric, instrument | residual reduction, description length | the ground, when one is named; otherwise none | admitted |
| D2 Adaptive agent frame | agents with b, w, Gamma | their own payoff and residual | their own ground; when it is the intervention metric, they can move it | admitted |
| D3 Network and habitat frame | actors, conditions, relations; couplings that rewire | contact | the habitat updates, the ground does not (Fig 11 :73) | admitted |

| X2 Network as fixed exogenous backdrop | the network | topology | already inside D3, which updates | absorbed |

**Partition or union.** Union, not partition. D1 and D2 form the strategic-response compound; D2 and D3 form the co-evolution compound. The whole is the union of C1 (an anchored, reachable channel) and C2 (renewed reach through a differing closure), not two independent problems (Fig 9 :54-57). A compound has its own loop and its own ground.

**Order by dependency** (Fig 3). Perception of R, then vocabulary for what the situation affords, then an objective stated in effect terms, then planning the intervention, then learn and carry. The middle link is where chains break: *"having terms for what a situation offers and being able to say what would count as progress, has neither, so it attracts the least attention and the fewest instruments"* (Fig 3 :25-26). Effort spent above the break cannot rescue it (Fig 3 :19-20).

## 3. Findings

### F1 - An intervention aimed at a movable target is not anchored (decisive for D1)

Fig 2 :29-30:

> **"An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks. A measurement, an experiment, a counter, a market, a patient outcome."**
> **"you push on it, it is not an anchor."**

Fig 2 :33-34:

> **"The anchor must not update."**
> **"If it does, panel 3 turns into panel 1: the anchor becomes another frame, everything drifts together, and the drift is coherent, which is why nobody notices."**

Fig 10 :47:

> **"a convention that decides an outcome has stopped being one"**

Strategic adaptation is exactly the case where the controlled quantity updates on what the controlled frames think. A compliance rate, a score, a reported metric, a ranking: once agents optimize against it, it has become a convention that decides an outcome, so it has stopped being an anchor. The drift is coherent and therefore invisible from inside the intervention team. This is the figure-grounded form of the Goodhart and Campbell and Lucas results in the world literature (see `09_watson_answer.md` WORLD), but it does not depend on them.

**Mode:** `general` for the anchor rule, `specified` for the intervention case. **Status: triangulated** (Fig 2 anchor definition, Fig 10 convention inversion, and the movable-target literature, three low-overlap routes). **Refuter:** an intervention that keeps R trending down while its stated target is demonstrably movable by the agents it targets breaks this finding.

### F2 - Valence is relative to the residual; no standing list of good interventions survives (D2)

Fig 11 :49-51:

> **"An actor's valence is relative to the residual, not a property it carries."**
> **"The same actor is a benefactor to one residual and a malfactor to another, so mutualism and parasitism are relations rather than kinds."**
> **"No standing list of good actors survives a change of residual."**

Fig 10 :71-72:

> **"The room composition is a function of the question. When the question changes, the room should change. A room that stays fixed while the question moves is the failure; the correlation is only its symptom."**

Fig 7 :36:

> **"A chain of mirrors is one long room. The next room must differ from the last, and match the residual it surfaced."**

This is the figure-grounded reason there is no permanent "best strategy" list. A subsidy, a penalty, a nudge, a norm, a network seed: each is an actor whose effect is a relation to a residual. When agents adapt, the residual changes, and the same intervention can reverse sign. The selection of the next intervention must be made against the residual the last one surfaced, not by reusing the previous one's label.

**Mode:** `specified`. **Status: triangulated** (Fig 11 valence, Fig 10 room-function, Fig 7 differing rooms). **Refuter:** an intervention whose valence stays fixed across a documented change of residual.

### F3 - The habitat updates; introduction is available and exclusion is not (D3)

Fig 11 :73:

> **"The habitat is not the ground. The habitat is where actors come from and it updates; the ground is what settles, and it does not."**

Fig 11 :61:

> **"The seat's power is asymmetric. Introduction is available; exclusion is not. You can add an actor and you cannot subtract the habitat, because contact is a fact about the world and does not consult you."**

Fig 11 :63:

> **"Which is why a managed ecosystem is permanent work rather than a solved state."**

Fig 11 :66-67:

> **"Isolation is not removal of the habitat. It is substitution of one habitat for another."**
> **"Move the residual and you must reproduce everything its goal required, and you inherit the new habitat's actors, which you did not choose."**

The "network changes in response" clause is the habitat updating, not the ground moving. That distinction decides the strategy: an intervention is a maintained garden, not a one-off fix. You can introduce a counteracting actor, an instrument, a feedback loop, a new rule; you cannot subtract the fact of contact. A pilot or sandbox is a substituted habitat, and *"A test harness is a substituted habitat. Which is why a synthetic solve proves wiring and never capability"* (Fig 11 :70). So a strategy validated only in a fixed-topology simulation has proved wiring, not that it works when the network rewirers.

**Mode:** `specified`. **Status: triangulated** (Fig 11 habitat rules, Fig 4 membrane, Fig 13 structure/state distinction). **Refuter:** a one-shot intervention, with no maintained contact, that keeps R down across a documented habitat substitution.

### F4 - Keep the channel; measure per slot and by round trip, not by aggregate success (D4)

Fig 10 :24-26:

> **"the ground does not decay, the channel does"**
> **"stale percepts, dropped transients,"**
> **"saturated metrics, pooled readings"**

Fig 10 :29:

> **"keep the anchor reachable"**

Fig 1 :22:

> **"Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average."**

Fig 1 :24-27:

> **"coverage, and compression achieved"**
> **"predicates minted, and credibility accrued. They are frame-internal. A frame cannot score itself with a quantity it also produces."**

Fig 4 :30:

> **"So take the round trip and measure the gap. Without it you are navigating by dead reckoning: confident about a position nobody checked."**

An intervention programme that reports success by number of interventions shipped, fraction of agents reached, or average compliance is scoring itself with quantities it also produces. The live slot residual persists while the average looks healthy (Fig 1 :13). The honest report is per-slot R plus the round-trip gap R_T between the design abstraction and what actually returned.

**Mode:** `specified`. **Status: triangulated** (Fig 10 channel, Fig 1 slot indexing, Fig 4 round trip). **Refuter:** an intervention whose average and per-slot residuals diverge and whose divergence is not reported; that is a channel failure, and the strategy using it is disqualified.

### F5 - A single lever clears one condition and leaves the intersection (compound)

Fig 9 :54-57:

> **"And when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again."**
> **"A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones."**
> **"A single lever clears the region where only one condition holds and leaves the intersections standing, because the second condition still binds."**
> **"What remains is smaller and it is also categorically different: a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either."**

Fig 3 :19:

> **"Optimising a link that already works cannot rescue one that does not."**

Strategic adaptation and endogenous rewiring are two conditions in a union. A lever tuned only for strategic response (change the incentives) leaves the rewiring intersection standing; a lever tuned only for topology (change the connections) leaves the strategic intersection standing. The residue after one lever is not "a harder case of the same thing"; it is a specific compound that needs its own loop.

**Mode:** `specified`. **Status: triangulated** (Fig 9 union, Fig 3 dependency order, Meadows' leverage-point ordering in WORLD). **Refuter:** one lever that restores both conditions with no categorically different residue.

### F6 - An intervention is a composition, not a parameter nudge

Fig 12 :111-112:

> **"A vocabulary without bonds can name a state and cannot name a route to one."**
> **"A plan is a sequence. A goal is a comparison. Progress is a subtraction."**

Fig 12 :119-121:

> **"catalyst. A part that survives its own use."**
> **"cycle. The output re-enters as input. The sign of the loop decides whether it closes, and a chain cannot close by construction."**

Operators table :51 and Fig 12 :52:

> **"a goal is a comparison, which is why a vocabulary without it cannot state one"**

A network that rewrites itself around an intervention is a cycle whose sign can flip; an intervention that survives its own use is a catalyst. So the unit of strategy is a bonded arrangement (sequence of actions, comparison to a threshold, subtraction of residual), not a single scalar setting. Meadows' leverage-point list (WORLD) corroborates at the world level: parameters are "dead last", while rules, goals, self-organization and paradigm are higher leverage.

**Mode:** `general` for the bond reading, `specified` for intervention strategies. **Status: triangulated** (Fig 12, Operators table, systems-dynamics source). **Refuter:** a single scalar parameter that is sufficient across a change of residual and a change of topology.

### F7 - Which named strategy wins is a `gap`

After a genuine re-census no figure line selects among pricing, mandate, nudge, norm, network seeding, or any other named instrument. That is a limit, not a silence.

Fig 9 :32:

> **"you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself"**

Fig 13 :134:

> **"Unreachable is a verdict here, not a silence,"**

Fig 8 :39:

> **"So a search that finds nothing is never a proof of absence."**

What is missing is a source-discriminating instrument per domain (does this target move when agents push on it? does this coupling rewire in response to this intervention?). Characterizable in effect terms, not held. The framework returns a shape; the name requires a ground reading.

### F8 - Independence is a design parameter, not a courtesy

Fig 8 :47:

> **"Independence is one quantity read twice."**

Fig 2 :42:

> **"Buy independence in proportion to the cost of being wrong."**

Fig 8 :45:

> **"Two frames together reach what neither reaches alone."**

A pool of adaptive agents who all learned the same response to the same intervention is one frame wearing many names (Fig 8 :49). A verification panel selected for people already inclined to agree is the same collapse (Fig 2 :40). So the design must include independence between the frames that surface the residual and between the rooms that are tried. This is a budget rule, never a verdict rule (Fig 9 :60).

**Mode:** `general`. **Status: triangulated** (Fig 8, Fig 2). **Refuter:** a correlated panel whose agreement predicts ground settlement at the same rate as an independent one.

## 4. Pre-registered refuter

Required before any outreach, per the seat map: *"a falsifier written before the run is a design instrument, not only an honesty device."* Written before the conclusion and before the world search.

The answer-shape is refuted if any of the following holds:

1. An intervention keeps R trending down while its target variable is demonstrably movable by the agents it targets (breaks F1).
2. A single lever restores both the strategic-response condition and the network-rewiring condition, with no categorically different residue (breaks F5).
3. An intervention's valence stays fixed across a documented change of residual (breaks F2).
4. A one-shot intervention with no maintained contact keeps R down across a habitat substitution (breaks F3).
5. A chain of interventions drawn from one closure, all agreeing, keeps R down and the per-slot residual stays down (breaks F8 and Fig 7 :35-36).
6. Average success and per-slot residual diverge and the programme reports only the average (breaks F4).

If any fires, the shape is wrong in that domain and is withdrawn there. Until one is run, no part of this may be labelled `grounded`.

## 5. Recommendation

1. Lead with F1. Ask, for any proposed intervention, what settles it and whether that thing moves when the agents push on it. If it moves, it is not an anchor.
2. Treat adaptation and rewiring as a union (F5). Unbundle before applying a lever; expect a categorically different residue.
3. State the objective as a per-slot comparison, written down before the run, and report the round-trip gap R_T (F4). Do not score the programme with quantities it produces itself.
4. Build for maintenance and introduction (F3). A sandbox proves wiring, not capability.
5. Select the next intervention against the residual the last one surfaced, from independent rooms (F2, F8).
6. Declare the `gap` in F7 openly. Do not name a winning strategy the figures do not name.

## 7. Verdict of this audit

The shape survives: an intervention works under adaptation and endogenous network change only as a maintained, anchored, per-slot, introduced-not-subtracted, union-splitting composition over independent frames, selected against a ground that does not update. The specific claim that any fixed named lever is best is refused. The name of the winning strategy is a declared `gap`. Strains after re-census: one, the meaning of "network change" relative to the maintainer's power of introduction, named and resolved in section 6 and re-checked in `11_audit.md`. Nothing here is `grounded`.
