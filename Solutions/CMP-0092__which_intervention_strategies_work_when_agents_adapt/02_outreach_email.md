# Outreach draft - validate the output first, then decide about the framework

**Use:** one to one, or as a cover letter attached to a submission through the public venues in `03_public_contact_points.csv`. **Not** for bulk send.

**Hard precondition:** do not send until the stage-1 shape and its refuter are stated cleanly. The output must survive validation or the two-stage offer collapses on contact.

---

**Subject:** Check one claim about interventions under adaptation before you decide whether the framework is worth reading

> Hi [name],
>
> I am working on a narrow question in adaptive interventions research: which intervention strategies keep working when the agents being intervened on adapt strategically and the network they sit in rewires in response? I have one specific output I would like you to check against what you already know. I am not asking you to read the theory first, and I am not asking you to agree with it. If the output does not survive, there is nothing else to look at.
>
> **The output (`specified`, not `grounded`):** a fixed lever applied once is not the right unit of analysis. An intervention keeps driving a residual down only if all of the following hold. Its target does not move when the agents push on it, because a quantity that updates on what the frames think is not an anchor. It is maintained, because you can introduce a counteracting actor but you cannot subtract the habitat. It is measured per case, because an average hides the one live case that matters. It is chosen against the residual the previous intervention surfaced, because an actor that was a benefactor can become a malfactor when the residual changes. And it treats strategic adaptation and network rewiring as a union of two conditions, because a single lever clears the region where only one condition holds and leaves the intersections standing.
>
> This is a shape, not a list of winning instruments. The framework returns a kind of thing, not the answer itself, so I am not claiming that pricing, mandates, nudges, norms or network seeding is the winner. Which one wins is a declared gap that needs a ground reading in your domain.
>
> **What would refute the shape:**
> 1. an intervention that keeps the per-case residual trending down while its stated target is demonstrably movable by the agents it targets;
> 2. one lever that restores both the strategic-response condition and the rewiring condition, with no categorically different residue left over;
> 3. an intervention whose sign stays fixed across a documented change of the residual;
> 4. a one-shot intervention with no maintained contact that survives a substitution of the habitat.
>
> I wrote these down before running the derivation. If any holds, the shape is wrong in that domain and I will retract it. A one-line "this fails because..." is more useful to me than agreement. If you would rather not engage, no reply is needed.
>
> **If it survives, and only then:** the sources are below. The figures, not my summary of them, are the authority.
> - Canonical figures and formula (local extract): `FRAMEWORK/reference/03-FIGURES_TEXT.md` and `FRAMEWORK/figures/THE_FORMULA.md`
> - Canonical usage rule (local): `FRAMEWORK/reference/FIGURES_README_canonical.md`
> - The full derivation and the verification packet are in the same folder as this note.
>
> **If you want to use the machinery:** the formula is in the figures. Run it on an intervention problem you know well and can check against a ground. Write down what would refute your result before you run it, and state the mode of what you get. A reported failure is more useful to me than a reported success.
>
> Thanks,
> [name]
