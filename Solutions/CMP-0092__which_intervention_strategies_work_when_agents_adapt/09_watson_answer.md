# WATSON answer, CMP-0092

**Mode: `specified`, not `grounded`.** Figures are primary and decide. World sources are secondary, corroborate but never overrule, and a conflict returns the derivation to the domain census. This is a simulated derivation checked for internal consistency against the figures, not a ground reading.

## QUESTION

Which intervention strategies work when agents adapt strategically and the network changes in response?

Restated as an effect: why does an intervention that drives R = |Gamma(b,a) - o-prime| down at t=0 stop trending down when the operands update their own Gamma and the coupling structure updates with them? R is indexed per slot, never aggregated (Fig 1 :19 "The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated."). Anchor: the ground, which does not update; the question's own success metric is not the ground unless it cannot be moved.

## DOMAIN CENSUS

Arity: multi-slot and coupled, designer intervention plus agent action plus network coupling plus ground reading. Carries sequence, comparison, subtraction and cycle bonds, not symmetric ones. Symmetry: broken; influence travels and the success relation is a threshold comparison. Scale: single agent loop, population-plus-network loop, and a lossy membrane between them.

## WORLD (secondary, each source read; field, author/venue/year, quote, prediction, corroborates|conflicts)

**Pool A, metrics and incentives (one witness, not four).**

- **Economics / monetary policy.** Charles Goodhart, "Problems of Monetary Management: The UK Experience", 1975, read via Wikipedia (entry and references) 2026-11-22. Quote: "Any observed statistical regularity will tend to collapse once pressure is placed upon it for control purposes." Prediction: a lever tuned on historical behaviour degrades when used for control. **Corroborates** D1. Later Strathern variant, "When a measure becomes a target, it ceases to be a good measure", makes the same point.
- **Social science / evaluation.** Donald T. Campbell, "Assessing the Impact of Planned Social Change", Evaluation and Program Planning 2(1), 1979, read via Wikipedia (entry and references) 2026-11-22. Quote: "The more any quantitative social indicator is used for social decision-making, the more subject it will be to corruption pressures and the more apt it will be to distort and corrupt the social processes it is intended to monitor." Prediction: the measured indicator is corrupted by the act of using it. **Corroborates** D1. Same pool as Goodhart.
- **Macroeconomics.** Robert Lucas, "Econometric Policy Evaluation: A Critique", 1976, read via Wikipedia (entry and references) 2026-11-22. Quote: "any change in policy will systematically alter the structure of econometric models." Prediction: agents' decision rules change with the policy, so the lever cannot be evaluated on past data. **Corroborates** D1 and D2. Same "gaming the measure" pool.

**Pool B, systems and resilience (one witness).**

- **Systems dynamics.** Donella Meadows, "Leverage Points: Places to Intervene in a System", The Donella Meadows Project (original 1999), read 2026-11-22. Quote: "Leverage points are not intuitive. Or if they are, we intuitively use them backward, systematically worsening whatever problems we are trying to solve." The list ranks "The power to add, change, evolve, or self-organize system structure" and "The power to transcend paradigms" above parameters. Prediction: parameter tweaks fail; rules, goals and self-organization are higher leverage. **Corroborates** the composition finding in 06 Q1 and the maintenance finding in D3.
- **Ecology / resource management.** Adaptive management (Holling and Walters tradition), Wikipedia entry read 2026-11-22. Quote: "is a structured, iterative process of robust decision making in the face of uncertainty, with an aim to reducing uncertainty over time via system monitoring." Also: "Learning about a managed system is only useful in cases where management decisions are repeated." Prediction: intervention must be iterative and monitored, and one-shot programmes cannot learn. **Corroborates** D3 and D4. Same resilience pool as Meadows.
- **Complex systems theory.** "Complex adaptive system", Wikipedia entry read 2026-11-22. Quote: "outcomes emerge from interactions among adaptive agents, and may be influenced but not precisely controlled." Also: "Agents in the system are adaptive. They update their strategies in response to input from other agents, and the system." Prediction: no fixed lever controls the outcome; agents update. **Corroborates** D2 and the union. Same broad systems pool.

**Pool C, adaptive networks (two low-overlap fields).**

- **Network science / multi-agent systems.** Yi-Ning Weng and Hsuan-Wei Lee, "Q-Learning-Driven Adaptive Rewiring for Cooperative Control in Heterogeneous Networks", arXiv:2509.01057, 2025, abstract read 2026-11-22. Quote: "This method combines temporal difference learning with network restructuring so that agents can optimize strategies and social connections based on interaction histories." The paper reports three regimes depending on rewiring constraint. Prediction: agents optimize behaviour and ties together, so the network is an operand of the intervention. **Corroborates** the union and D3.
- **Systems biology / oncology.** C. G. Cremers and L. K. Nguyen, "Network rewiring, adaptive resistance and combating strategies in breast cancer", Cancer Drug Resistance 2(4), 2019, DOI landing page and abstract read 2026-11-22. Quote: "Such network rewiring allows tumour cells to adapt to the drug treatment, circumvent the initial drug challenge and continue to survive in the presence of the drug." The review discusses combination treatment strategies. Prediction: a single targeted agent is circumvented by network rewiring; combinations are the response. **Corroborates** the union and the failure of the single lever. Different evidence pool from Weng and Lee.

## VERDICT (from the figures)

The figure-derived verdict: an intervention works under adaptation and endogenous network change only as a maintained, anchored, per-slot, introduced-not-subtracted, union-splitting composition over independent frames, selected against a ground that does not update and against the residual the previous intervention surfaced. It is not a named lever. Valence is relative to residual, so no standing strategy list survives; a rewiring network is a habitat update and a cycle with a sign, not a movable ground. The three world pools corroborate the failure of fixed levers and the need for combinations, but the verdict comes from the figures. Mode `specified`, ungrounded. Which named strategy wins is a declared `gap`.

- **Cost if wrong:** a lever tuned to one condition of a union is applied to both, the intersection survives, and the adaptive response is invisible until it acts. Meanwhile frame-internal metrics (compliance, coverage, interventions shipped) report success while the live slot residual persists (Fig 1 :13). The cost is real, bounded and locatable if per-slot residuals and R_T are kept.
- **Undoable:** yes. No term is deleted if the claim fails; a refutation is added, and the reach falls because *"something was added rather than because anything was deleted"* (Fig 6 :43). A failed intervention can be superseded and the lineage kept: Fig 10 :65 "Provenance shows a ruling was reached legitimately. It never shows it was right: the ground does that, and a wrong ruling stays wrong however clean its record."
- **Reversed by:** a ground reading, not agreement. Any of the six pre-registered refuters in `01` section 4 would reverse it, in particular (1) R keeps trending down with a movable target, (2) one lever clears both union conditions with no categorically different residue, or (3) an intervention's valence stays fixed across a documented residual change. A source with genuinely different evidence pool showing that per-slot persistence is predicted by a single fixed lever would also reverse it. Until one fires, the verdict stands as `candidate`.
