## 1. Independent domain census

I redid the census from the residual line alone, before relying on the primary's conclusions, using the WATSON order: R by arity, symmetry and scale; one line per domain with actors / currency / anchor; partition or union; order by dependency.

- **R (arity / symmetry / scale).** The residual is the gap between an evaluation score and a field outcome under three coupled conditions. Arity: three, so a **union compound** (Fig 9 :54, :57-58), not one hard question. Symmetry: the score is an instrument and the outcome an anchor; they are not interchangeable (Fig 2 :36). Scale: score inside one frame at one scale, outcome after the habitat changes at another (Fig 4, Fig 11 :52-53).
- **Domain 1, evaluation frame.** Actors: benchmark builders, metrics, leaderboards. Currency: scores, coverage, credibility. Anchor: none legitimate for the real-environment question. Verdict: not a verdict domain; a head start and a habitat actor.
- **Domain 2, deployment habitat.** Actors: users, institutions, affected parties, the system as introduced actor. Currency: real outcomes. Anchor: the outcome the question is constitutively about. Verdict: union member, delay loop.
- **Domain 3, verification frame.** Actors: external auditors, independent instruments. Currency: residual and independence. Anchor: the same ground, never agreement. Verdict: union member, ambiguity loop.
- **Domain 4, delayed-outcome channel.** Actors: time, drift, pooled readings, saturated metrics. Currency: contact. Anchor: the ground, which does not decay. Verdict: dependent domain, a channel failure, not a ground failure.

**Order by dependency:** anchor, habitat vocabulary, objective per slot, instruments, carry. This follows Fig 3 :20-21. My independent starting figures were Fig 9 (union split), Fig 3 (dependency) and Fig 2 (anchor), whereas the primary census led with Fig 1 (per-slot residual) and Fig 11 (habitat). ## 4. Round trip gap R_T

The Symbols table defines `R_T` as the gap between what was sent up and what came back down, `gap(x, (T_E ∘ T_A)(x))`, measuring what the coarser description cannot hold. Fig 4 :30 requires it to be measured rather than assumed zero. I ran the round trip on the condensation this folder actually uses.

1. **Sent up (x):** the verbatim figure set, chiefly Fig 1 :19, :27 (per-slot residual, frame-internal quantities), Fig 2 :33, :36 (anchor), Fig 10 :24 (channel), Fig 11 :70 (harness).
2. **Abstraction (T_A):** the shorthand in `07_the_tether_answer.md`: a score cannot be the ground; measure per slot; enumerate the habitat; triangulate; record the gap.
3. **Concretisation (T_E):** a concrete evaluation policy for a named deployment: a specific outcome instrument, a specific slot table, two named independent audits, a field follow-up after the delay.

**R_T is nonzero.** Three things the shorthand holds do not survive the trip back down:

- **which outcome is legitimate** for the named deployment. The figures say "constitutively about it"; they do not choose among candidate outcomes. That is a binding from the deployment frame and needs provenance.
- **how overlap is measured** between two instruments. Fig 2 :31-32 defines independence but supplies no estimator; the field must provide one.
- **what counts as a slot boundary** in a given setting. Fig 1 says per object slot; it does not say whether a slot is a population, a site, a time window or a person.

One thing survives cleanly: a single score cannot be its own ground, and residual must be kept per slot. That is the residue worth sending. R_T was **not** measured numerically, because no field reading exists; it is recorded qualitatively as the enumerated set of dropped constraints. Treating "the formula is the condensed version of the metalaws" as identity would set R_T to zero and commit the dead-reckoning error Fig 4 :30 names. The honest form is a lossy condensation with a recorded gap, which is what `09_watson_answer.md` states.
