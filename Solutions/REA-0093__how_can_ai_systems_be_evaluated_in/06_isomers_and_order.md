# Structure, order and analogues for real-world evaluation

**Mode: `specified`.** This is a simulated derivation checked against the figures, not a ground reading (see `05_what_shape_means.md` section 6).

## RESIDUAL

The question names three conditions, delay, ambiguity and human response. Two things are owed: (1) what is the structure that binds them, and (2) in what order must an evaluation protocol address them. A third question follows: are two protocols that use the same evaluation atoms but bond them differently the same thing or different things?

## DOMAIN CENSUS

Atoms held: a score `S`, a field outcome `O`, time `t`, people `P` (as habitat actors), an anchor `A`, and a residual `R` indexed per slot. Bonds held: the seven (including `→` sequence, `⋛` comparison, `−` subtraction) plus negation `¬`. No new primitive is available without import. Conditions: delay is a channel property (Fig 10 :24), ambiguity is a per-slot property (Fig 1 :19), human response is a habitat relation (Fig 11 :39).

## DERIVATION

### Q1 - What structure binds the three conditions? UNION, not a list

- Fig 9 :54: **"And when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again."**
- Fig 9 :57-58: **"What remains is smaller and it is also categorically different: a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either. It stops being a mixture and becomes a specific compound."**
- Delay alone needs a time-aligned outcome instrument. Ambiguity alone needs per-slot residuals. Human response alone needs habitat enumeration. One lever for all three leaves a residue, so the structure is a union of overlapping conditions, and the overlap is a compound with its own loop.
- Verdict: **union compound**. Three loops plus one compound loop. Verdicts are kept separate and never averaged (Fig 1 :22).
- **Falsifier (written before the run):** exhibit one measurement change that removes all three conditions with no categorical residue. If it works, the union reading fails and this derivation is rewritten as a partition.

### Q2 - What order must the protocol follow? DEPENDENCY, not importance

- Fig 3 :20-21: **"The chain is ordered by dependency, not by importance. Each link consumes what the one above produces, so effort spent above the break arrives at a step that cannot use it."**
- Fig 3 :19: **"Optimising a link that already works cannot rescue one that does not."**
- The ordered chain for `E*`:
  1. Anchor: name `A`, test that it does not update and is legitimate (Fig 2 :33, :36).
  2. Vocabulary: enumerate the habitat, including `P` and the system as introduced actor (Fig 11 :43, :61).
  3. Objective: say what would count as progress in `A`'s terms, per slot (Fig 3 :9-11, Fig 1 :19).
  4. Instruments: choose two low-overlap readings of the same `A` (Fig 2 :31-32).
  5. Carry: record the round trip gap and the lineage (Fig 4 :30, Fig 12 :103-104).
- Fig 3 :25-27 warns that the middle link (having terms for what a situation affords and being able to say what counts as progress) has neither obvious failures nor obvious tests, so effort lands above the break where measurements already exist. A benchmark-first protocol is effort above the break.
- **Falsifier:** show a protocol that reaches a valid per-slot field residual without first naming a non-updating legitimate anchor. Under Fig 3 that would be a reading of nothing, not a shorter route.

### Q3 - Are two protocols with the same atoms isomers?

- Fig 12 :117: **"isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."**
- Protocol 1: `S ⋛ O` (score compared with field outcome, then deployment decision). Protocol 2: `O → S` (field outcome fed back to adjust the score, then deployment decision). Same atoms `{S, O, A, t, P}`, different bonds, different substances: one is an audit, the other is a training loop that changes the thing being measured.
- They are isomers under Fig 12's definition, and the difference is invisible if a report lists only its metrics and not its bonds. This is the flat-formula failure: Fig 12 :14-16 notes that *"the ingredients are recorded and the arrangement is not"* and *"nothing downstream can tell them apart."*
- Fig 12 :111-112: **"A vocabulary without bonds can name a state and cannot name a route to one."** **"A plan is a sequence. A goal is a comparison. Progress is a subtraction. A refutation is a negation, and that one is not a bond."** A metric suite names states. Only a protocol with `→`, `⋛`, `−` and `¬` can name a route, a goal, progress and a refusal.
- **Falsifier:** show that two protocols with identical atoms but different bonds produce the same deployment decision under the same anchor. Then the bond carries no information here and the isomer claim fails.

### Q4 - What does the protocol cost, and in what currencies?

- Fig 12 :125-126: **"A composition costs in two currencies, and they do not add. Bits price the description. Actions price finding out whether it holds."**
- Describing a five-part protocol is cheap. Running it is expensive: longitudinal outcomes take time (the delay), human studies take participants, independent audits take budget. A system holding only the descriptive price will propose protocols it cannot afford to execute, and the budget decides how much of the space is reached.
- Fig 2 :42: **"Buy independence in proportion to the cost of being wrong."** The two-instrument requirement is therefore not a luxury; its price is set by the harm of a wrong deployment.
- **Falsifier:** show a field where the two currencies are interchangeable, so that a richer description reduces the actions needed to settle the outcome without any import or instrument change. Fig 12 says they are in different units and do not add.

### Q5 - Which analogue governs which condition?

| condition | analogue (low overlap with figures) | what it contributes | where it strains |
|---|---|---|---|
| delay | CAST surrogate endpoints, Echt et al. 1991 via StatsAlly explainer | marker can improve while patient outcome worsens; the anchor is the later outcome | a machine deployment has no mortality endpoint; the analogue is structural, not literal |
| ambiguity | HELM multi-metric evaluation | seven metrics over core scenarios, trade-offs exposed rather than averaged | still benchmark-internal; it is a head start, not the ground |
| human response | Lucas critique (Ericsson and Irons quoting Lucas 1976) | agents change decision rules when the rule changes, so parameters drift | optimal-agents assumption is stronger than real user behavior; use as warning, not proof |
| all three | ecological validity (Holleman et al. 2020) | specify the context rather than appeal to "the real world" | the essay critiques the term without offering an evaluation protocol |

No analogue outranks a figure line. Agreement among analogues from different fields is corroboration; conflict would send us back to the census (WATSON rule).

## CONCLUSION

The three conditions form a **union compound**, addressed in dependency order: anchor, vocabulary, objective, instruments, carry. Two protocols using the same evaluation atoms but different bonds are **isomers** in Fig 12's sense, which is why a report must record the arrangement and not just its metric list. The protocol is priced in two non-additive currencies, description and actions, and independence is bought in proportion to the cost of being wrong. Each sub-derivation carries its own falsifier above.
