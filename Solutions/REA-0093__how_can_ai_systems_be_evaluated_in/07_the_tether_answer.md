# The Tether's answer: evaluating AI systems in real environments

**Mode: `specified` on the substantive claims, `general` on the structural shape. Not `grounded`.** This is a shape and a route, not a measured result. Simulated derivation checked for internal consistency against the figures (`05_what_shape_means.md` section 6), not a ground reading.

## 1. The residual it answers

A decision maker must decide whether to keep, change or stop an AI system when the outcome that matters arrives late, is multi-valued, and is partly produced by how people respond to the system. The evaluation score is the prediction `Γ(b,a)`; the real outcome is `o'`; the gap is `R = |Γ(b,a) − o'|` per slot. The three conditions (delay, ambiguity, human response) form a union compound with its own loop, not one hard question (Fig 9 :54, :57-58). They must be unbundled, given separate instruments, and only then recombined.

## 2. The shape

1. **A benchmark score cannot be the anchor.** Fig 1 :27: **"They are frame-internal. A frame cannot score itself with a quantity it also produces."** Fig 1 :23: **"The ground enters as the only metric."** Scores, coverage and credibility are useful head starts, but they are produced by the frame that reports them.

2. **The legitimate anchor is the outcome the question is constitutively about.** Fig 2 :36: **"An anchor is legitimate when the question is constitutively about it."** Fig 2 :37-38: **"Human judgment is the right anchor for is this a good answer for a person, because the question is about people. It is only an instrument for is this code correct, and instruments can be wrong on axes they do not measure."** For a deployment question about people, a human or institutional outcome is the anchor and the score is an instrument.

3. **The anchor must not update when the score moves.** Fig 2 :33: **"The anchor must not update."** If optimising the metric drags the metric's own target along with it, the arrangement has become the coherent-drift collapse (Fig 2 :34). This is the structural form of Goodhart's law, corroborated from measurement science and the Lucas critique.

4. **Measure residual per slot, never as an aggregate.** Fig 1 :19: **"The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated."** Fig 1 :22: **"Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average."** A slot is a use case, a population, a site, a time window or an affected party. A global score near zero with one live slot is a legal state.

5. **A test harness is a substituted habitat.** Fig 11 :70: **"A test harness is a substituted habitat. Which is why a synthetic solve proves wiring and never capability."** Fig 11 :39: **"capability is a property of agent-and-habitat, never of the agent alone."** Human behavior is part of that habitat, and the system is itself an introduced actor. Effects two or three steps out matter (Fig 11 :52-53, DDT cascade).

6. **Delay is a dead channel to a live ground.** Fig 10 :24: **"the ground does not decay, the channel does"** The remedy is to restore the reading from an instrument already returning something (Fig 6 :32), not to argue about what the outcome would have been.

7. **Triangulate; do not convene a room.** Fig 2 :31-32: **"Independence, between two frames, is how little they draw on the same evidence. Two people reading the same report are not two frames; two instruments pointed at the same thing are."** Fig 2 :35: **"Alignment is triangulation, not negotiation. A mirror cannot correct you."** Two low-overlap instruments range against the same anchor; their overlap must be measured, and agreement from a shared pool carries no verdict weight.

8. **Record the round trip gap and stop at the instrument limit.** Fig 4 :30: **"So take the round trip and measure the gap. Without it you are navigating by dead reckoning: confident about a position nobody checked."** Fig 7 :6: **"the limit: no last room"** No evaluation suite is final. The output is a shape, a lineage and a list of refuters, not a certificate.

## 3. What it explicitly does not say

- Not that benchmarks are useless. They are head starts (Fig 4 :22: *"Only methods cross up. Only head starts come down."*).
- Not that one metric suite, one venue or one panel is the answer. Naming one would be a name where the framework returns a shape.
- Not that any specific deployment has been evaluated. No field reading was taken.
- Not that human judgment is always the anchor. It is the right anchor when the question is constitutively about people; otherwise it is an instrument with axes it does not measure (Fig 2 :37-38).
- Not `grounded`. Nothing here is asserted as settled by experiment.

## 4. What would settle it: the ground

A field reading that pays for the shape, not a further argument:

1. **Anchor test.** Does the named outcome stay fixed when the system's score is pushed up, and is the question about that outcome? If not, the anchor is an instrument.
2. **Per-slot test.** Does any slot carry positive residual while the global score looks healthy? Publish the per-slot table, not only the mean.
3. **Habitat test.** Does a score improvement that leaves contact unchanged leave real outcomes unchanged? If it moves outcomes without changing contact, Fig 11 :40 is refuted and the shape must be rewritten.
4. **Independence test.** Measure the overlap between the two instruments. If it is high, their agreement is one pool talking.
5. **Round trip test.** Measure `R_T` between the score abstraction and the field concretisation. Report the dropped constraints: operand type, order of refusal, missed slots. A zero gap assumed rather than measured is dead reckoning.
6. **Standing refuters.** A single score that is both a legitimate non-updating anchor and retains the per-slot signal; one lever that clears all three conditions; a sealed final evaluation room; a metric that never needs channel maintenance. Any of these fires against this answer.

## 5. What it would cost if wrong, and whether that is undoable

**Cost if wrong:** a deployment could be approved on a healthy score while a live slot residual harms a population that was averaged away. Fig 2 :42 prices the remedy: *"Buy independence in proportion to the cost of being wrong."* The avoidable part is spending readers' independence by sending a recording instead of a refuter.

**Undoable:** yes, at the protocol level. Evaluations are conventions and instruments, not primitives. Keep every cut ranked and reversible (Fig 9 :40-41). A wrong anchor can be replaced without withdrawing the shape.

**Reversed by:** a ground reading, not agreement. The six tests above are the reversal routes. Prestige, consensus and a larger panel are not.

## 6. The honest stop

**Unreached, not unreachable.** The five-part protocol is a visible design choice, not nature's own list. Another field's evaluation axes need not be these five. The framework stops where the available instruments stop discriminating and records the lineage so the stop is checkable. *"Indiscernibility is not identity."* This answer reports the shape any defensible evaluation of delayed, ambiguous, human-mediated outcomes must have. It does not report a measurement.

## 7. One-paragraph version for outreach

> The Tether does not offer a better benchmark score. It says that a score cannot be the ground it is scored against: for a question about people in real settings, the legitimate anchor is a human or institutional outcome that does not move when the score moves. It says to measure the gap per slot (use case, population, site, time window, affected party) because a global average hides the only signal that says where to look; to treat a test harness as a substituted habitat that proves wiring and never capability; to keep at least two low-overlap instruments ranging against the same anchor rather than convening a room of agreeing reviewers; and to record the round trip gap between the score and the field outcome. Delay is a stale channel to a live ground, not an unanswerable question. What is offered is a shape with six written refuters and a list of field tests that would close it, not a certificate and not a final room.
