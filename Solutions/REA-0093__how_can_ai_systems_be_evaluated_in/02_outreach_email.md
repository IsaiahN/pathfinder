# Outreach draft: check the evaluation output before deciding about the theory

**Use:** one-to-one, or as a cover letter attached to a submission through the public venues in `03_public_contact_points.csv`. **Not** for bulk send.

**Hard precondition:** send only after you have stated your own ground and your own refuter for the deployment you have in mind. An evaluation claim without a named failure condition collapses on contact (Seat Map :287).

---

**Subject:** One evaluation claim to check before you decide whether the method is worth reading

> Hi [name],
>
> I have one specific output I would like you to check against what you already know. I am not asking you to read the framework first. I am not asking you to agree with it. If the output does not survive, there is nothing else to look at.
>
> **The output (`specified`, not `grounded`):** when an AI system is deployed where outcomes arrive late, are multi-valued, and depend on how people respond to the system, a single evaluation score cannot settle whether the system is good. What survives is a shape with five parts:
>
> 1. Name the outcome that is constitutively at issue and check that it does not move when the score moves. For a question about people, a human or institutional outcome is the right anchor; a metric is only an instrument, and instruments can be wrong on axes they do not measure.
> 2. Report the gap between prediction and outcome per slot, where a slot is a use case, a population, a site, a time window or an affected party. A global average can be near zero while one live slot still carries the only signal that says where to look.
> 3. Enumerate the habitat, including the system itself as a new actor and the effects two or three steps out. A test harness is a substituted habitat, so a synthetic solve proves wiring and never capability.
> 4. Keep at least two low-overlap instruments ranging against the same anchor. Two teams reading the same benchmark are one frame wearing two names.
> 5. Record the round trip gap between the score and the field outcome. Late outcomes mean the channel to the ground has gone stale; the ground has not decayed, the channel has.
>
> **What I am not claiming:** that benchmarks are useless, that this is a final evaluation protocol, or that any particular metric suite is the anchor. Benchmarks are head starts. I am not claiming the framework returns a name for the anchor either; it returns a shape, and the binding to a real outcome is yours to prove and to date.
>
> **What would refute this shape claim (written before this message):**
> 1. A single score shown to be a non-updating, legitimate anchor for the deployment question **and** to retain the per-slot where-to-look signal.
> 2. An agent-only improvement that changes real outcomes without changing contact with the habitat.
> 3. Two evaluation frames with measured near-zero overlap whose agreement is shown to be independent convergence rather than a shared evidence pool.
> 4. One lever that clears delay, ambiguity and human response with no categorical residue.
> 5. A metric that stays a live channel to the field outcome indefinitely with no maintenance.
>
> If any one holds, I will retract the claim. A one-line "this fails because..." is more useful to me than agreement. If you would rather not engage, no reply is needed.
>
> **What I would like from you (five minutes, stage 1):** what would you count as a failure in your own setting? Please answer before reading anything I have written. I will record your verdict before showing any source.
>
> **If it survives, and only then:** I will send three public sources. The figures, not my summary of them, are the authority:
> - The Figures text extract, local canonical file: `FRAMEWORK/reference/03-FIGURES_TEXT.md`
> - The eight essays and the canonical figures README, listed in `05_what_shape_means.md`
> - Verified public venues and wider literature in `04_links_and_distribution.md`
>
> **If you want to reuse the method:** the formula is public. Run it on a problem you know well and can check against a ground. Write down what would refute your result *before* you run it, and state the mode of what you get. A reported failure is more useful to me than a reported success.
>
> Thanks,
> [name]

## Stage 2 and stage 3, held back until invited

**Stage 2, opt-in only.** If the recipient returns a stage-1 verdict, ask whether they want the sources. Send at most the three listed above. Note that they share an author with the framework, so their agreement with the figures is one evidence pool, not independent corroboration.

**Stage 3, method only.** Offer the formula as a generator. Fig 4 :22 permits methods to cross. Each user must state their own ground and write their own refuter before running it, because a frame *"cannot check the direction of its own question"* (Formula "Direction"). The Arrival essay warns that LLM-assisted outputs *"could never be verified if the solutions presented were not contrived to fit the formula,"* which is why this packet labels itself a simulation.

**Stage 1 precondition remains.** If the stage-1 output fails, stages 2 and 3 do not happen.
