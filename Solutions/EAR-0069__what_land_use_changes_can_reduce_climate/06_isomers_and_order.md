# Union, order and isomers in the land-use question

**Mode: `specified`.** This is a simulated derivation checked against the figures, not a ground reading (see `05_what_shape_means.md` §6).

## RESIDUAL

The question appears to ask for one list of land-use changes. Two prior questions are owed before any list is attempted: (1) is the question a partition or a union, and (2) are the two famous answers, land sparing and land sharing, isomers under Fig 12, or only members of one functional group? A wrong answer to (1) makes the search aim at a scalar that does not exist; a wrong answer to (2) makes a context-dependent strategy look like a universal substance.

## DERIVATION

### Q1 - Union or partition?

- Fig 9 :54: **"When the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again."**
- Fig 9 :55: **"A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones."**
- Fig 9 :57: **"What remains is smaller and it is also categorically different: a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either."**
- The three axes overlap on the same parcel. A parcel that is mitigating is also producing food and hosting relations. Their intersection is not a harder mitigation case; it is a specific compound. ⇒ **Union.** Each axis keeps its own loop and its own ground. Any answer that treats the three as a list of separate optimizations has misidentified the object.
- **Falsifier, written before the result:** if the three axes are shown to share one currency and one instrument, so that improving one necessarily improves the others in every slot, the union claim fails and the partition reading stands.

### Q2 - Where is the dependency break?

- Fig 3 :20: **"The chain is ordered by dependency, not by importance."**
- Fig 3 :19: **"Optimising a link that already works cannot rescue one that does not."**
- Fig 3 :27: **"A link with no instrument is not a link that works. It is a link nobody has looked at."**
- Fig 3 :29-30: **"Most improvement effort in practice lands above the break, because that is where the measurements already exist."**
- Link 1 (perception) and link 2 (vocabulary) have instruments in land-use science: flux towers, land-cover maps, species surveys. Link 3 (the objective across three currencies) has no comparable instrument. Effort spent on better carbon accounting lands above the break. ⇒ The bottleneck is the definition of progress, not the precision of the carbon number. This is a `specified` structural claim; it needs a survey of actual funding and effort before it is `grounded`.
- **Falsifier:** exhibit an instrument that states progress across all three currencies simultaneously and is used at the same effort level as carbon accounting.

### Q3 - Are land sparing and land sharing isomers?

- Fig 12 :117: **"Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."**
- Fig 12 :123: **"functional group. A substructure whose presence fixes the outcome. The group says what kind; the qualifier says which member."**
- As the literature usually writes them, they do not share an atom set. Land sparing uses {high-yield farmland, discrete spared habitat}; land sharing uses {lower-yield farmland, wildlife-friendly matrix across the farmed land}. Yield level and spatial arrangement are different atoms. ⇒ As written, they are **not isomers**; they share a **functional group**, the reconciliation of production with biodiversity, with different members.
- The stronger form: over the shared atom set {production, biodiversity, land area, yield, habitat quality, matrix}, both can be written as arrangements of the same atoms under different bonds. Then they are isomers: same atoms, different arrangement, different substance, and the difference is decisive in behaviour. This is the claim worth testing, and it is `specified`, unmeasured.
- World check, secondary and conflicting: Phalan et al. (Science, 2011) report *"land sparing is a more promising strategy for minimizing negative impacts of food production"* in Ghana and India; Kremen (2015) argues that *"Both large, protected regions and favorable surrounding matrices are needed"* and that the dichotomy is limiting; PNAS Nexus (2025) finds that evidence supports neither as the main strategy. The conflict is expected under the union finding: each study answers a different residual at a different scale. It does not flip the figure verdict.
- **Falsifier:** if either strategy can be written without an atom the other requires, the isomer claim fails and the functional-group reading stands.

### Q4 - What is the order of the union?

- Fig 12 :112: **"A plan is a sequence. A goal is a comparison. Progress is a subtraction."**
- Fig 12 :111: **"A vocabulary without bonds can name a state and cannot name a route to one."**
- Fig 9 :43: **"The verdict tells you to leave. The lookup tells you where to go. Elimination is what you do after you arrive."**
- A land-use list is a plan, hence a sequence, and a plan needs bonds that name a route. The six substrate terms alone give a state vocabulary, not a route. Since neither sparing nor sharing is settled universally, neither can be promoted to an atom at the next level by Fig 12 :104 (*"A settled molecule becomes an atom for whatever composes over it"*). They are candidates, not atoms. The union is therefore a **search with elimination after arrival**, not a composition.
- **Falsifier:** exhibit a context in which both strategies are settled at the same level and can be composed over, so the union becomes a composition rather than a search.

### Q5 - Is management a chain or a cycle?

- Fig 12 :121: **"cycle. The output re-enters as input. The sign of the loop decides whether it closes, and a chain cannot close by construction."**
- Fig 11 :63-64: **"Which is why a managed ecosystem is permanent work rather than a solved state."**
- A managed parcel is a cycle: monitoring output re-enters as the next decision's input. A one-off intervention written as a chain cannot close by construction, so it cannot represent maintenance. ⇒ The shape must be written as a cycle, with the sign of the loop (does monitoring correct the intervention or merely repeat it?) left to the ground.
- **Falsifier:** exhibit a land-use intervention whose output does not re-enter as input and which nevertheless remains effective without further work across the full cascade.

## CONCLUSION

The question is a union compound, not a partition; its dependency break is at the objective link; land sparing and land sharing are a functional group as usually written and a plausible isomer pair over a shared atom set; the union is a search, not a composition; and a managed parcel is a cycle, not a chain. These are structural results. They rescue the part of the land-use debate that is checkable (the shape of a valid answer) without naming a winner between strategies that the world literature itself contests.
