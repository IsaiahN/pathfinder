## 1. A shape is a constraint, not a filler

The canonical figures README states the limit: *"what a missing thing would have to look like (a shape, not a name)"*. Fig 9 :32: **"you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself"**. Fig 13 reports the same limit from inside a closure: *"It can say the answer is out of reach with the library as it stands... It cannot say which primitive is missing, because naming it would mean already holding it."*

From *The Library of Babel*: *"You can cut your search short if the set of books returned is small and descriptive enough that it provides a general shape of the answer that satisfies your question."*

For EAR-0069 a **shape** is therefore the five-part class written down in `01` section 0: the slot, the currency, the habitat as enumerated actors, conditions and relations, the cascade two or three steps out, and the instrument that would show payment. A named intervention such as "afforest this watershed" is a name, not a shape. A shape constrains; it does not fill.

## 2. How a shape is obtained

- *The Library of Babel*: use the vocabulary already held that is *closest* to the thing, then look for recurring terms that overlap or correlate with it. *"With each new term gained, you narrow the space and deepen your vocabulary."* This is bootstrapping by proximity, not by a list of preselected interventions.
- *The Library of Babel* on a bundled question, directly relevant here: *"Perhaps your problem was a bundle or conflation of several problems masquerading as one? You will need to search all of those problems individually to find their answers and then combine them in a satisfactory way."* This is the essay-level corroboration of F1.
- Fig 6 :30: **"outward: does another frame already hold it? The description travels. Fails by being sharpened into terms only we can read. Cast it in effect terms."** Fig 9 :30-31: **"your words are borrowed too, so describe the effect rather than the cause"**.
- *Decoding Tacit Knowledge*: *"When contact isn't possible, the best you can send is a plain description of what you'd expect to see, because anyone can check that against the world."* This is why the outreach email in `02` sends an effect description first.
- *The Library of Babel* on stopping: *"You will know that you are done because the finish line will also be the starting place"* - the answer is composed through the process of locating it. In this solution the "answer" is the shape, not a prescription.

## 3. What licenses a name

Only two things, both outside the shape itself:

1. **The ground settles it.** Formula step 5: *"SETTLE the ground settles it. Verified only by what pays. A gate is not the ground."* For land use, that means measured emissions, household-level food access and biodiversity state in the named place, not a modelled co-benefit.
2. **A provenanced import from a frame whose closure differs** (Fig 6 :23, Fig 8 :45-46), with a source gate and a shadow test. Agronomy, ecology, remote sensing and local knowledge are imported; they are not derived from a carbon-accounting closure.

*The Hard Problem is About Source* gives the mechanism for why a single reading cannot license a name. An instrument reads green but *"it cannot comment on the source"*, and *"If several paths create the same behavior, then behavior is a projection with lossy information"*. The spectrometer, not an argument, resolved it. Applied here: a hectare of increased carbon stock is a reading; it cannot comment on whether food access or pollination was preserved. Several land-use histories can give the same carbon reading. What is owed is a better instrument, not a name.

## 4. Why this shape cannot be a single scalar

Fig 1 :19: **"The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated."** Fig 9 :60: **"Use filters for the budget and witnesses for the verdict. Never let a filter hand you a verdict."** From *The Recursive Self-Improvement Trap*: *"The Tether ties alignment to the ground. Not to human preferences. Not to a reward model trained on human feedback. To the thing that settles a question regardless of what any frame thinks."*

A single "net benefit" score over carbon, food and biodiversity would be a preference made to look like a measurement. The figures do not supply weights for adding the three currencies; that gap is recorded in `01` section 4 rather than filled here.

## 5. Prestige is not action

From *The Recursive Self-Improvement Trap*: *"Prestige must never be allowed to validate an action directly."* Also: *"Prestige can fund an action. It can give an action a budget to be tried. But it cannot settle an action. Only the ground can settle it. The Ground Maintainer must be blind to prestige."*

For this question, a well-known review or a high-impact journal is a budget that lets a land-use claim be tried. It is not evidence that the claim worked. The contact venues in `03` are chosen as public, ground-visible routes, not as sources of endorsement.

## 6. My own audit is a simulation, not a ground reading

From *The Recursive Self-Improvement Trap*: *"LLMs can quickly simulate an approximate answer/output with the formula found in the architecture post."* The truly testable output *"would need to be derived from the real architecture or working the formula yourself."* So `01`, `06`, `07`, `08`, `09` and `11` are simulated derivations, checked for internal consistency against the figures. They are **not** tests of any land-use intervention and must be labelled as such. They cannot settle the question they report on.

## 7. Correction to my own §1 (kept visible)

An earlier draft treated Fig 9 :32 (*"you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself"*) as forbidding any named land use whatsoever. The canonical README narrows this: the phrase governs a **missing** thing. A specific intervention is not a missing primitive of the framework; it is a binding from the land-management frame onto arrangements of the six held terms. Naming one is therefore not forbidden by the shape rule. What remains wrong is naming it as *the* answer without a ground reading or a provenanced import. Both the narrowed rule and the original overstatement are kept here so the correction is not silently lost. This matches the exemplar's own §5b correction pattern.
