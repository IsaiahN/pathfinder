## 0. The residual, stated in effect terms

R is the gap, in a named place and season, between what a land-use intervention was predicted to do on three axes and what the instruments returned on those axes. The three axes are not one quantity:

- **Climate risk:** radiative forcing and the biophysical state of the land surface (albedo, evapotranspiration, carbon stock and flux).
- **Food security:** availability, access, utilisation and stability, all four at once, at household level.
- **Ecosystems:** the abundance of species, the state of habitats, and the relations (pollination, predation, competition, dependency) that carry effects across the land.

**Arity:** three conditions, each with its own slot set. **Symmetry:** not symmetric. Climate is a global accumulating stock with local fluxes; food security is distributional and household-level; ecosystem integrity is local and relational. They do not share a currency, so they do not add. **Scale:** field or farm, landscape or region, and global; the transform between scales is lossy (Fig 4).

**The sought-for shape, bound as an ordinary term before the run** (formula, Direction): an answer is a *class* of land-use interventions specified by (i) the slot where it acts, (ii) the currency it reduces, (iii) the habitat it touches, enumerated as actors, conditions and relations, (iv) the cascade two or three steps out, and (v) the instrument that would show whether it paid. Any candidate that lacks one of these five is not yet an answer. This binding is written down here so it can accrue a residual and be refused by the same test as everything else, rather than remaining an invisible preference.

## 1. Domain census

R by arity, symmetry and scale, then one line per domain with actors / currency / anchor. No line is admitted as a domain without a nameable anchor.

- **R (the residual).** Arity three conditions, so a **union compound**, not one hard question. Symmetry: the three currencies do not commute or add. Scale: nested, with a lossy transform between field, landscape and globe. Fig 9 governs the compound: *"a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either. It stops being a mixture and becomes a specific compound."*
- **Domain 1, biophysical mitigation.** Actors: atmospheric carbon, albedo, evapotranspiration, soil carbon, sinks and fluxes. Currency: tCO2e and land-surface state. Anchor: flux towers, atmospheric concentration records, satellite land-surface measurements. A nameable anchor exists, so this is a domain.
- **Domain 2, food and agriculture.** Actors: crops, livestock, soils, water, pollinators, pests, households, markets. Currency: yield, access, dietary diversity, stability over time. Anchor: harvest measurement and household food-security surveys. A nameable anchor exists.
- **Domain 3, ecosystem integrity.** Actors: species, habitats, and the relations among them; conditions of light, water, temperature, season and terrain. Currency: abundance and habitat state. Anchor: field survey and long-term monitoring plots. A nameable anchor exists.
- **Domain 4, the decision and verification frame.** Actors: planners, landholders, markets, subsidies and reviewers. Currency: agreement, cost and policy. Anchor: measured outcomes in domains 1 to 3, never agreement itself. This domain can author conventions but cannot author the ground.
- **Partition or union:** a **union**. Domains 1 to 3 overlap on the same parcel. An intervention that clears only the region where one condition holds leaves the intersections standing (Fig 9 :55-56). The overlap is a compound with its own loop and its own ground, not a harder instance of any one domain.
- **Order by dependency (Fig 3):** (1) perception: can the residual be read in this slot at all; (2) vocabulary: terms for what the land affords; (3) objective: what counts as progress across the three currencies; (4) planning: a route under incomplete information; (5) learn and carry: name what worked, verify, and carry it to the next parcel. The chain is *"ordered by dependency, not by importance"* (Fig 3 :20).

Strain test applied before any conclusion: Fig 11 says a seat can introduce but cannot exclude, while land policy plainly can set land aside. Re-census resolves this. The figure's "exclusion" concerns the habitat, meaning contact, conditions and relations, not the removal of one actor from a parcel. Different objects, so no strain survives re-census (recorded in section 4).

## 3. Findings

Each finding states its mode and its triangulation status. Verdicts are kept separate and are never averaged.

### F1 - The question is a union compound, so there is no single lever (`specified`, triangulated)

Fig 9 :55: **"A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones."**
Fig 9 :55-56: **"A single lever clears the region where only one condition holds and leaves the intersections standing, because the second condition still binds."**
Fig 9 :57-58: **"a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either. It stops being a mixture and becomes a specific compound."**

Fig 11 operates on a different object, actor valence, and gives the same structure:
Fig 11 :49: **"An actor's valence is relative to the residual, not a property it carries."**
Fig 11 :51: **"No standing list of good actors survives a change of residual."**

A forest, a crop, a wetland or a pollinator is a benefactor to one residual and a malfactor to another. Therefore "which land use is good" has no context-free answer. The two figures have low overlap (search/unbundling versus habitat/actor valence), so the verdict is **triangulated**, not a double count of one pool. World corroboration, secondary: the IPCC AR6 WGIII Summary for Policymakers states that *"Barriers to implementation and trade-offs may result from the impacts of climate change, competing demands on land, conflicts with food security and livelihoods, the complexity of land ownership and management systems, and cultural aspects."*

**Refuter:** exhibit one intervention that clears all three conditions, in every season and region where it is applied, leaving no positive residual in any slot, and show that the three conditions share one currency so they are in fact one condition.

### F2 - The residual must be read per slot, not as a global average (`specified`, single-source)

Fig 1 :19: **"The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated."**
Fig 1 :22: **"Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average."**
Fig 1 :13: **"a global R near zero with one live slot residual is a legal state"**

A national net-zero land sector can coexist with a live food-access slot in one district and a live biodiversity slot in one watershed. The global average is not evidence that those slots are clear. This finding is **single-source** within the figures (Fig 1); the other figure lines that mention scale concern a different quantity. World corroboration, secondary and low-overlap: FAO's basic-concepts guide says *"An adequate supply of food at the national or international level does not in itself guarantee household level food security."*

**Refuter:** show a scalar formed from a global carbon total, a national food-availability mean and a species-richness index that predicts all three slot outcomes without losing the live slots.

### F3 - Enumerate the habitat; do not compose a prescription from the six terms (`general`, triangulated)

Fig 11 :43: **"The habitat is enumerated, never composed."**
Fig 11 :44: **"List everything in contact with the residual, then what is in contact with those, and outward until the cascade stops mattering."**
Fig 11 :46: **"conditions: light, water, temperature, season, terrain"**
Fig 11 :47: **"relations: pollination, predation, competition, dependency"**
Fig 11 :48: **"You do not invent the list. You read it off the world, and what you cannot perceive or measure yet is the residual."**

Fig 9 gives the same instruction from the frame-lookup direction, low overlap:
Fig 9 :27-28: **"describe it before you go looking, or any frame you pick will seem to fit"**

So the correct first move is a list of actors, conditions and relations for the actual parcel, not a choice between "afforestation", "intensification" or "rewilding" made before the list exists. **Triangulated** (Fig 11 plus Fig 9).

**Refuter:** produce a land-use prescription derived purely by combining the six substrate terms with no local enumeration, and show it pays against the ground in a named place.

### F4 - Management is introduction, not exclusion; it is permanent work (`specified`, single-source)

Fig 11 :61-62: **"The seat's power is asymmetric. Introduction is available; exclusion is not. You can add an actor and you cannot subtract the habitat, because contact is a fact about the world and does not consult you."**
Fig 11 :63-64: **"Which is why a managed ecosystem is permanent work rather than a solved state."**

A land manager can plant, restore, diversify or introduce; the manager cannot subtract the light, water, season, terrain or the relations already in contact. Therefore an intervention is a continuing obligation, not a one-off credit. **Single-source** within the figures; the strain about protected areas was resolved by re-census in section 1. World corroboration, secondary: Jones, Lawton and Shachak (Oikos, 1994) define engineers as organisms that *"modify, maintain and create habitats"*, which is the same continuing work in ecological vocabulary.

**Refuter:** show a land manager subtracting the habitat itself, not merely one actor from a parcel, so that the intervention reaches a solved state needing no further maintenance.

### F5 - Displacement is substitution of habitat, not removal of the residual (`specified`, triangulated)

Fig 11 :66: **"Isolation is not removal of the habitat. It is substitution of one habitat for another."**
Fig 11 :67: **"Move the residual and you must reproduce everything its goal required, and you inherit the new habitat's actors, which you did not choose."**
Fig 11 :68: **"what you failed to reproduce is invisible until the goal fails"**
Fig 11 :69: **"what you unintentionally introduced is invisible until it acts"**

Shifting production to another region to spare land here does not remove the food or biodiversity residual; it moves it into a habitat whose actors were not chosen. Fig 4 gives the same structure from the cross-scale direction, low overlap:
Fig 4 :34-35: **"The level a method lands on remakes it. A seed carries no small tree: it carries what will grow one, and the conditions it lands in decide what it becomes."**

**Triangulated** (Fig 11 plus Fig 4). World corroboration, secondary: Meyfroidt and colleagues describe *"the displacement of land-uses to near or remote sites, often described as either a spillover effect or leakage"*; Eakin and colleagues note that in telecoupled systems *"the critical outcomes revealed in the telecoupling process tend to be indirect, emergent, and of a second or third order, such that they are difficult to anticipate or measure."*

**Refuter:** demonstrate an intervention that displaces production to another region and still leaves all three slot residuals non-positive there over a full cascade, with the receiving habitat's unchosen actors explicitly accounted for.

### F6 - Effects cascade beyond first order (`specified`, single-source)

Fig 11 :52-53: **"Scoping the habitat to direct contact is how DDT reached the eagles: first-order enumeration, second-order effect, and the residual that mattered was three steps out."**
Fig 11 :54: **"Carson, Silent Spring, 1962. The mechanism, biomagnification through the food chain to eggshell thinning, was established over the following decade."**

A land-use change is owed an enumeration outward until the cascade stops mattering, not a first-order inventory of its immediate neighbours. **Single-source** within the figures. World corroboration, secondary: Jones, Lawton and Shachak (1994) note that ecosystem engineers *"modulate the availability of resources to other species"* and that the biggest effects come from actors with large per-capita impact acting over large areas and long times.

**Refuter:** exhibit a land-use change whose cascade was enumerated only to first order and whose third-order residual was then measured at zero.

### F7 - The bottleneck is the objective link, not a shortage of carbon data (`specified`, single-source)

Fig 3 :20: **"The chain is ordered by dependency, not by importance."**
Fig 3 :19: **"Optimising a link that already works cannot rescue one that does not."**
Fig 3 :27: **"A link with no instrument is not a link that works. It is a link nobody has looked at."**
Fig 3 :29-30: **"Most improvement effort in practice lands above the break, because that is where the measurements already exist."**

Carbon accounting is a working link with instruments. Stating what counts as progress across three currencies at household and landscape level is the middle link, and it has no comparable instrument. More carbon measurement lands above the break and cannot rescue it. **Single-source** (Fig 3). World corroboration, secondary: the IPCC summary's C.9 says the opportunities are *"country-specific"* and depend on *"competing demands on land, conflicts with food security and livelihoods"*, which is an objective statement, not a missing carbon number.

**Refuter:** exhibit an instrument that states progress across all three currencies at the same time and is actually used at the same effort and funding level as carbon accounting.

### F8 - The ground, not agreement, settles the compound (`specified`, triangulated)

Fig 2 :29: **"An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks."**
Fig 2 :35: **"Alignment is triangulation, not negotiation. A mirror cannot correct you."**
Fig 2 :40: **"agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool"**
Fig 2 :42: **"Buy independence in proportion to the cost of being wrong."**
Fig 2 :45: **"Frames range against the ground. They do not negotiate with it."**

Fig 9 adds the budget/verdict separation from a different direction, low overlap:
Fig 9 :60: **"Use filters for the budget and witnesses for the verdict. Never let a filter hand you a verdict."**

Two land-use models using the same emission factors, the same land-cover dataset and the same yield assumptions are one witness wearing two names. A cost per tonne is a filter for which option gets tried, not a witness for whether it worked. **Triangulated** (Fig 2 plus Fig 9).

**Refuter:** show two reviewer frames with genuinely separate instruments and separate evidence pools counting as two witnesses, and name what each measured that the other could not move.

### F9 - Improvement that does not change contact changes nothing (`general`, triangulated)

Fig 11 :40: **"A system cannot be made more capable by improving the agent in isolation. An improvement that does not change contact changes nothing, however much it improves."**
Fig 6 :32: **"An instrument is not built from a description. It is improved from a worse instrument already returning something."**
Fig 6 :33: **"Nobody specified a telescope from an account of Mars. A wandering point in the sky was already a reading, and every sharper instrument was built on the residual the last one produced."**

Better models of the same satellite products do not add contact; a new household survey or a new biodiversity transect does. The two figures have low overlap (capability/habitat versus instrument construction), so **triangulated**.

**Refuter:** name a monitoring instrument for a land-use residual that was specified from a description alone, with no worse instrument already returning something that failed to resolve.

### F10 - The framework returns a shape, not a named land use (`general`, single-source plus canonical rule)

Fig 9 :32: **"you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself"**
Fig 9 :30-31: **"your words are borrowed too, so describe the effect rather than the cause"**

The canonical figures README states the limit as *"what a missing thing would have to look like (a shape, not a name)"*. So the deliverable is the five-part class described in section 0, with the slot left open. Naming one intervention as *the* answer is a binding from the land-management frame, provenanced as such, not a derivation. **Single-source** in the figures (Fig 9), governed additionally by the canonical usage rule.

**Refuter:** exhibit a figure line that selects a named intervention, crop or land use for this residual without a ground reading or a provenanced import.

### F11 - When closure fails, import, with a source gate and a shadow test (`specified`, triangulated)

Fig 6 :23: **"No frame escapes its own closure. The bound moves only on contact with a frame whose closure differs."**
Fig 6 :14: **"the only operator that moves the wall"**
Fig 8 :45: **"Two frames together reach what neither reaches alone."**
Fig 8 :53: **"Residual first, frame second."**
Fig 8 :39: **"A search that finds nothing is never a proof of absence."**

A carbon-accounting frame cannot derive a household food-access measure or a pollination relation from its own atoms. It must import from agronomy, ecology, remote sensing or local knowledge, with provenance and a shadow test (does the imported term explain a gap the frame could not). Describe the residual first (section 0), then pick the frame. **Triangulated** (Fig 6 plus Fig 8, low overlap between closure geometry and union surplus).

**Refuter:** derive a locally valid food-security and biodiversity outcome measure entirely inside the carbon-accounting closure, with no imported atom and no provenance record.

## 5. Pre-registered refuter

Written before the recommendation and before the verdict, per the seat-map rule that *"a falsifier written before the run is a design instrument, not only an honesty device."*

The shape claim in this folder is refuted if any of the following holds:

1. A single land-use intervention clears all three conditions in every slot and season, with no positive residual left in any one of them (refutes F1).
2. A global carbon total plus a national food-availability mean plus a biodiversity index form a scalar that predicts all three slot outcomes without losing a live slot (refutes F2).
3. A land manager can subtract the habitat itself, so an intervention reaches a solved state with no further maintenance (refutes F4).
4. A displacement of production to another region leaves all three slot residuals non-positive there over a full cascade, with the receiving habitat's unchosen actors accounted for (refutes F5).
5. A figure line is exhibited that selects a named land use for this residual without a ground reading or a provenanced import (refutes F10).
6. A derived scalar is produced that legitimately adds the three currencies, with a ground reading that settles it (refutes the open gap in section 4).

If any fires, the relevant finding is withdrawn and the recommendation is rewritten. Until one is run, no claim here may be labelled `grounded`.

## 6. Recommendation

1. Lead with the union finding (F1) and the per-slot finding (F2). Ask for the residual in a named place before naming any intervention.
2. Enumerate the habitat (F3) and the cascade (F6) before comparing options. A first-order inventory is not an assessment.
3. State the objective across three currencies as an ordinary term and mark it as the unmeasured middle link (F7). Do not spend more effort above the break.
4. Report displacement explicitly as substitution (F5), and report maintenance as continuing work (F4).
5. Import across low-overlap frames with source gate and shadow test (F11). Do not derive food security from carbon accounting.
6. Keep cost curves as filters and measurements as witnesses (F8). Never let a cost per tonne hand a verdict.
7. Offer the generator, the shape claim and the refuter, not the conclusion. Fig 4 :22: **"Only methods cross up. Only head starts come down."**
