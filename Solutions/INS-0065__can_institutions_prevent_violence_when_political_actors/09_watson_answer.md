# WATSON answer: INS-0065, can institutions prevent violence under expected exclusion or retaliation?

**Mode: `specified` on the substantive claims, `general` on the structural shape, not `grounded`.** Figures are primary and decide. World sources are secondary, corroborate but never overrule, and a conflict returns the derivation to the domain census. This is a simulated derivation checked for internal consistency against the figures, not a ground reading. No violence event data were analysed.

## QUESTION

Can institutions prevent violence when political actors expect future exclusion or retaliation?

Restated as an effect: why does `R = |Γ(b,a) - o'|` stay small for some actor-slots and grow in others, where b is the actor's expectation of future harm, a is compliance or preemption, and o' is observed treatment? R is indexed per actor and per time, never as a national peace score (Fig 1 :19). Anchor: observed violence and observed treatment of losers, named as a counter in Fig 2 :29.

Option A: assert that institutions prevent violence by writing and enforcing sufficient rules. Option B: keep only the figure-derived shape, namely two conditions with their own instruments, and leave the specific mechanism as a `gap`. A sends a `general`-mode verdict without a measurement; B sends a checkable shape, written refuters and an open slot.

## DOMAIN CENSUS

R by arity, symmetry and scale: a multi-place relation among institution, actor belief, action and observed treatment, indexed by actor-slot and ordered by time. It carries an ordered bond (sequence, because the expectation is future-directed), a comparison bond (threshold, because expected loss is weighed against gain from striking first), and negation (the actor's "will NOT be protected"). It is not symmetric: the assurance must not move when the actor pushes.

## VERDICT (from the figures)

The figure-derived verdict: institutions can prevent violence under expected exclusion or retaliation only when C1 (an assurance that does not update under pressure from the parties) and C2 (a channel that keeps that assurance reachable at each actor-slot) both hold and are kept separate, in that order. Prevention is a property of the institution and its habitat; renewal needs contact with a differing closure; a mechanism that once produced peace can change sign when the residual changes; and the actor's expectation is a negation that more of the same assurance cannot repair. Which specific mechanism fills the credibility slot is a declared `gap`. Mode `specified`, ungrounded, candidate. No world source flipped a verdict; the apparent external-support point was returned to the census and re-mapped to F1 and F6.

- **cost if wrong:** misdirected diagnosis and a spent outside check. An institution failing C2 would be sold more rules, or one failing C1 told to improve monitoring, and one lever would be applied to a union that needs two. Sending the framework before the residual also spends the recipient's independence (Fig 10 :42). The cost is bounded and largely reversible if the shape and refuter lead.
- **undoable:** yes. A refutation adds a term and removes nothing, so the reach falls because *"something was added rather than because anything was deleted"* (Fig 6 amendment :42-43). The lineage is recorded so a later reader can locate the error (Fig 10 :62-64).
- **reversed by:** a ground reading, not agreement. The binding test and the channel test applied per actor-slot, or any of the eight falsifiers in `01` section 4, would move a domain verdict. A source-discriminating instrument that names the operative mechanism would close the `gap`. A figure re-census that derives one of the six physical terms from five, or fails idempotence, would break the framework itself. Until one of these fires, the verdict stands as `candidate`.
