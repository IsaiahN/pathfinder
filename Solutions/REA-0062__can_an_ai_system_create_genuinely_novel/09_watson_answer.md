The agent, or the tool, or the frame, or the theory, cannot validate itself.") it cannot settle the question it reports on. No measurement was taken.

## QUESTION

Decide, in effect terms, what the Tether says about the record's question: can an AI system create genuinely novel ideas, or can novelty only be judged relative to human knowledge? Option A asserts a world-absolute novelty a generator can produce. Option B makes human knowledge the sole standard for every novelty claim. What each leads to: A attributes to a generator what only an import to a frame can do, and sets the anchor inside the generating frame; B gets the anchor right for one sub-question and overgeneralizes it into a universal rule that would forbid any other outside frame. A third reading, derived below, splits the union and answers each part separately.

## DOMAIN CENSUS

R by arity, symmetry and scale, then one line per domain with actors / currency / anchor, partition or union, and order by dependency.

- **R (the residual).** The mismatch between a generator's output (a term its library does not hold) and an observer's standard for "never seen before". Arity: two conditions, generator-side and verdict-side, overlapping, so a **union compound** (Fig 9 :55 "A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones"), not one hard question. Symmetry: NOVEL is a membership test φ not in atoms(Γ), not symmetric between frames; new to Γ_A and new to Γ_B are different claims. Scale: first order inside one closure, second order at the frame membrane, third at the ground. Per slot: Fig 1 :19 "The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated." An aggregate novelty score hides the live slot (Fig 1 :22).
- **Domain 1, the generator frame.** Actors: Γ, atoms(Γ), closure(Γ), mint, import, instruments. Currency: φ, cost, R_s. Anchor: the ground for settlement; library membership for the novelty guard. Question type: partition boundary (what composes inside the closure versus what crosses).
- **Domain 2, the human-knowledge frame.** Actors: the public record, prior art, editors, reviewers, the field. Currency: membership in the documented corpus. Anchor: the human record, legitimate only for the human-relative question. Question type: one frame among many.
- **Domain 3, the ground.** Actors: instruments, experiments, markets, outcomes. Currency: contact; whether a new primitive entered the world. Anchor: the immovable ground. Question type: not a frame, the reference for D1 and D2.
- **Domain 4, the verification frame.** Actors: outside frames, seats, expert readers. Currency: agreement and residual. Anchor: the ground, never agreement. Question type: union with D1/D2 where evidence pools overlap; in this simulated run all domains share one pool, so independence is zero.
- **Order by dependency.** Describe R before choosing a frame (Fig 9 :28 "any frame you pick will seem to fit"); settle generator-side membership before judging; refuse before mint (formula step 2); settle first-order molecules before comparison; range against the ground last (Fig 2 :45).

Strain test: "Genuinely novel" is not a figure term; it is a held preference about the sought-for shape. Re-census resolution: bind it as the ordinary term `world-atom-added` (formula "Direction"), mode `specified`, and declare the binding. Human knowledge is anchor for one sub-question and instrument for another; Fig 2 :36-38 makes this a scope distinction, not a contradiction. No figure line was forced.

## VERDICT (from the figures)

The offered dichotomy is false because the question is a union. Split, the figures give two separate `specified` verdicts, never averaged. **Generator side:** a system can mint a term new to its own atoms, which is real novelty in the only sense the figures define; no frame, human or AI, adds a primitive to the world, so world-absolute novelty is a category error, not a harder task. **Verdict side:** human knowledge is the legitimate anchor for the question that is constitutively about human knowledge, and only an instrument for world questions; judging novelty needs an outside frame with different closure, and no search over a non-sealed record proves absence. The agency condition on "create" is an open item, declared rather than decided. This is `specified` and ungrounded.

- **cost if wrong:** bounded and locatable. Both verdicts carry modes, verbatim lines and refuters written in advance, so an error is findable. The real cost is spending the independence of outside readers by sending a conclusion instead of a generator (Fig 2 :42 "Buy independence in proportion to the cost of being wrong."). That cost is avoidable by leading with the split and its refuter. A second cost is mis-anchoring: treating human agreement as a world verdict when the question was about the world, which Fig 2 :34 says drifts coherently so nobody notices.
- **undoable:** yes. Both verdicts are structural and no primitive is withdrawn. A correction is a re-census of which anchor governs which sub-question, or a retraction of one refuter. An already sent packet can be superseded by the corrected one. The open item on agency can be closed by a derived criterion without touching either verdict.
- **reversed by:** a ground reading, not agreement. A generator whose output increases the world's primitive count would break Fig 6 :26; an instrument that certifies absence from a non-sealed frame would break Fig 8 :39 and Fig 9 :44; a figure re-census showing that mint adds an atom would break Fig 6 :21; a derivation of an agency criterion within the figure set would close the open item; a proof that the two sub-questions form a partition would replace the split with a single loop. Prestige or consensus is not such an instrument.
