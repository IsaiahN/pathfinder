# Outreach draft: check the output first, then decide about the theory

**Use:** one to one, or as a cover letter attached to a submission through the public venues in `03_public_contact_points.csv`. **Not** for bulk send.

**Hard precondition:** do not send until the agency open item in `01_figure_grounded_audit.md` §5 is declared in the covering note. The output must survive validation or the two-stage offer collapses on contact.

---

**Subject:** Check one claim about machine novelty before you decide whether the framework is worth reading

> Hi [name],
>
> I have one specific output I would like you to check against what you already know. I am not asking you to read the theory first, and I am not asking you to agree with it. If the output does not survive, there is nothing else to look at.
>
> **The question, stated as an effect.** "Can an AI create genuinely novel ideas, or can novelty only be judged relative to human knowledge?" I found that this bundles two different questions. One is about what a generator can produce. The other is about what settles a claim of novelty. They do not share an anchor.
>
> **The output (`specified`, not `grounded`):**
> 1. A system can mint a term that is new relative to its own library. This is the framework's third guard, stated as φ not in atoms(Γ). It is a real event.
> 2. No frame, human or artificial, adds a primitive to the world by composing or importing. Import adds an atom to a frame and gives that frame access. So "novelty relative to the world" does not describe any generator's output.
> 3. Human knowledge is the right standard for the question "is this new to people?" It is only an instrument for the question "is this new to the world?", and an instrument can be wrong on the axes it does not measure.
>
> This is a shape claim, not a measurement. I am not asserting that any particular generated idea is or is not novel.
>
> **What I am not claiming:** that the framework names what novelty is in the philosophical sense, that it settles whether an AI has agency, that a prior-art search proves an idea is new, or that a panel of experts agreeing constitutes a verdict. On agency I explicitly do not have a result. The figures do not test it.
>
> **What would refute the output, written before I sent this:**
> 1. show a mint inside a frame's closure that adds an atom to that frame's primitives;
> 2. show an import that adds a primitive to the world rather than access for a frame;
> 3. show a standard for novelty that changes its answer when a frame pushes on it;
> 4. show a frame certifying its own completeness, or a search over a non-sealed record proving absence;
> 5. show that the two sub-questions form a single problem rather than an overlap of two conditions.
>
> If any one holds, I will retract the corresponding claim. A one-line "this fails because..." is more useful to me than agreement. If you would rather not engage, no reply is needed.
>
> **If it survives, and only then:** the sources are below. The figures are the authority, not my summary of them.
> - Local framework figure text, with line numbers: `FRAMEWORK/reference/03-FIGURES_TEXT.md`
> - Canonical figures README: https://github.com/IsaiahN/tether/blob/general-agent/figures/README.md
> - How Intelligence Actually Works: https://adventuresinml.substack.com/p/how-intelligence-actually-works
>
> **If you want to use the machinery:** the formula is in the figures link. Run it on a problem you know well and can check against a ground. Write down what would refute your result *before* you run it, and state the mode of what you get. A reported failure is more useful to me than a reported success.
>
> Thanks for reading,
> [name]
