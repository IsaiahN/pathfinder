# Expert verification packet  -  the Tether on AI and genuine novelty

**What this is:** a claim in a form a researcher can check. Mode, derivation and refuters are stated before the conclusion, so a reader knows what to look for and what would settle it.

**Mode: `specified` on the two verdicts, `general` on the split. Not `grounded`.** No instrument was run. This is a shape and a route, not a result.

**Status:** a simulated derivation checked for internal consistency against the figures, not a ground reading. It cannot settle the question it reports on.

## 1. The claim, in one paragraph

"Can an AI create genuinely novel ideas, or can novelty only be judged relative to human knowledge?" is a union of two questions, not one. On the generator side, a system can mint a term new to its own library, which is real frame-relative novelty; but no frame, human or artificial, adds a primitive to the world, so world-absolute novelty is a category error. On the verdict side, human knowledge is the legitimate anchor for the question that is constitutively about human knowledge, and only an instrument for the question that is about the world. Judging novelty requires an outside frame with a different closure, and no search over a non-sealed record proves absence. The framework offers this shape and its refuters, not a name for any particular novel idea, and it declares as open the question of whether an AI has agency in the philosophical sense.

## 2. Three checkable sub-claims

### C1  -  framework-internal; checkable by reading Figures 5, 6 and 8

- **Statement:** NOVEL is a membership test relative to a frame's atoms. Mint cannot add an atom; import adds an atom to a frame, not to the world.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 6 :22  -  "NOVEL means novel relative to atoms, not to the world. IMPORT adds an atom, and the added atom is what expands the closure." Fig 6 :21  -  "MINT composes inside the closure and can never add an atom." Fig 6 :25-26  -  "An import adds no atom to the world." and "The world does not gain primitives; a frame gains access." Fig 8 :32  -  "Closure is idempotent. Composing inside a closed set never leaves it, so no search, however long, adds an atom. This one is definitional: it is what closure means."
- **Refuter:** show a mint that adds an atom to atoms(Γ), or an import that adds a primitive to the world, or a composition that leaves its own closure.

### C2  -  question-relative anchor; checkable by reading Figure 2 and the anchor definition

- **Statement:** an anchor is legitimate only when the question is constitutively about it. Human knowledge is the right anchor for "new to people" and an instrument for "new to the world".
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 2 :36  -  "An anchor is legitimate when the question is constitutively about it." Fig 2 :37  -  "Human judgment is the right anchor for is this a good answer for a person, because the question is about people." Fig 2 :29  -  "An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks." Fig 2 :33  -  "The anchor must not update."
- **Refuter:** show an anchor for world-novelty that does not move when the human record changes, or show that a question about the world is constitutively about people.

### C3  -  framework-internal plus world-facing; checkable by reading Figures 8, 9 and the patent-law source

- **Statement:** no judge can certify world-absolute novelty, because a non-sealed frame cannot prove absence. A prior-art search reports non-detection in one record.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 8 :39  -  "So a search that finds nothing is never a proof of absence." Fig 9 :44  -  "Only a sealed room can be searched to the end, and no frame is a sealed room. So searching and finding nothing proves nothing." Fig 13 :134  -  "Unreachable is a verdict here, not a silence,"
- **World-facing check:** Wikipedia Novelty (patent), opened 2026-11-23: "No search can possibly cover every single publication or use on earth, and therefore cannot prove that an invention is 'new'." Predicts the same limit from law; corroborates, does not decide. Overlap with the figure source: none in author or evidence pool.
- **Refuter:** exhibit a sealed frame, or an instrument whose negative result is a proof of absence rather than a report of non-detection.

## 3. What is explicitly not claimed

- Not that AI novelty is impossible. Minting a term new to a library is definitional and real.
- Not that human judgment is useless, or that there are no legitimate human-relative novelty claims.
- Not that novelty is subjective in the sense of "anything goes". The frame must be named and the membership test must be checkable.
- Not that a prior-art search proves newness.
- Not that a panel's agreement is a verdict (Fig 7 :35: "A room’s agreement is variation, not a verdict. Selection between rooms is where the ground votes.").
- Not that the framework decides whether an AI has agency, intentionality or a "soul". That is an open item, declared here.
- Not `grounded`. No measurement was taken.

## 5. Pre-registered refuter (full list)

Written in `01_figure_grounded_audit.md` §4 before the conclusion. Any one firing withdraws the corresponding claim:

1. a mint inside closure(Γ) adds an atom to atoms(Γ);
2. an import adds a primitive to the world rather than access for a frame;
3. an anchor for novelty changes its answer when a frame pushes on it;
4. a frame certifies its own completeness, or a search over a non-sealed record proves absence;
5. a search over a complete record proves a thing is new;
6. the two sub-questions form a partition rather than a union.

## 6. What an expert can check in twenty minutes

1. Read Fig 6 :21-22 and :25-26, Fig 2 :36-37, and Fig 8 :39  -  four short passages.
2. Check C2 against your own field: for a question about that field, is expert judgment the anchor, or an instrument ranged against something else? Name the thing that cannot move.
3. Check C3 against your field's version of a novelty search. Does a negative result prove absence, or report non-detection?
4. Check the open item: is there a figure-governed criterion for agency, or is the declared open item the right response? A short "this fails because..." is enough.
5. Send the refutation. A failure is more useful than an endorsement, and agreement after seeing this packet is never pooled as corroboration (Fig 2 :40: "disqualifying for a verdict: agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool").
