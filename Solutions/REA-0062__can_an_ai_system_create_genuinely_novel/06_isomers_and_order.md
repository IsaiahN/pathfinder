# Novelty scopes as isomers, and the order of judgment

**Mode: `specified`.** This is a simulated derivation checked against the figures, not a ground reading (see `05_what_shape_means.md` §8).

## RESIDUAL

The word "novelty" is used for at least two different things in one sentence. Are they variants of one phenomenon, or different substances whose notation hides the difference? A second question follows: in what order must the checks run, so that a verdict about novelty is not a verdict about the wrong link in the chain?

## DOMAIN CENSUS

Atoms held: the frame Γ, its atoms, its closure, the mint and import operators, the ground, and the comparison bond ⋛. Bonds held: all seven plus negation. No new primitive is available without import. The residual has arity two (generator side and verdict side) and the two overlap, so it is a union (Fig 9 :55).

## DERIVATION

### Q1  -  Are P-creativity and H-creativity isomers?

- Fig 12 :117: **"isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."**
- Paul and Stokes (SEP, 2024), read 2026-11-23: "Newton's calculus and the student's compatibilism were not new in all of history, but they were new to their respective creators, and that is enough for them to count as creative." In Boden's terminology from the same source, these are P-creative (psychological) and not H-creative (historical). "creativity always exhibits psychological novelty, though it doesn't always exhibit historical novelty."
- Wikipedia Creativity (read 2026-11-23): "Margaret Boden distinguished between h-creativity (historical) and p-creativity (personal)."
- Both judgments use the same atoms: an output, a library, and a membership test. They differ in which library is the reference. That is a different bond (new to Γ versus new to the human record), and by Fig 12 it is a different substance whose formula looks identical.
- **Conclusion:** yes, P-novelty and H-novelty are isomers under the figure's definition. Same atoms, different reference bond, different verdict. The notation "novel" collapses them, which is exactly the flat-formula failure Fig 12 names.
- **Falsifier, written before the run:** if a single reference frame can serve both judgments without loss, or if P-novelty and H-novelty differ only in degree along one scale rather than in which frame is referenced, the isomer claim fails and they are one quantity at two resolutions.

### Q2  -  What is the order of the checks?

Fig 3 :19-21: "Optimising a link that already works cannot rescue one that does not. The chain is ordered by dependency, not by importance. Each link consumes what the one above produces."

The correct order for a novelty claim:

1. **Describe R in effect terms** (Fig 9 :28: "any frame you pick will seem to fit"). Name what failed to resolve and in which slot (Fig 1 :19).
2. **Settle the generator-side link:** is φ in closure(Γ), and is φ in atoms(Γ)? (formula step 3). Without this link, the verdict-side link has nothing to judge.
3. **Refuse before mint** where a held term is false (formula step 2). A novelty claim built on a wrong term is a refusal owed, not a new term owed.
4. **Settle the first-order molecule** before comparing it with another frame's molecule (Fig 12 :104: "A settled molecule becomes an atom for whatever composes over it. So irreducible is a fact about a level and never about the world.").
5. **Choose the outside frame** only after the residual is described (Fig 9 :53: "Residual first, frame second.").
6. **Judge against the ground** last. Fig 2 :45: "Frames range against the ground. They do not negotiate with it."

A link with no instrument is not a link that works (Fig 3 :27). The middle link here is step 2, which is a comparison. Operators table :52: "a goal is a comparison, which is why a vocabulary without it cannot state one." A vocabulary that can say "generated" and "recorded" but cannot say "new to which frame?" has a dictionary, not a grammar (Fig 12 :115).

**Falsifier:** if a novelty verdict can be delivered without first checking membership in a named frame, or if choosing the judging frame before describing the residual gives the same verdict across all frames, the dependency order is wrong and this derivation fails.

### Q3  -  Is "new to the world" a functional group or a seventh member?

- Fig 13 :114-115: "the array is unbounded and the reach is not" and "The set is closed. The arrangements are not. That gap is why import exists."
- Fig 13 :116: "Six members, and no amount of arranging produces a seventh: a new member would have to be a new kind, and composition only ever rearranges the kinds already held."
- "New to the world" is not a seventh primitive of novelty. It is a **functional group** in Fig 12 :124's sense: "the group says what kind; the qualifier says which member." The group is `new relative to a frame`; the qualifier is which frame. When the qualifier is "the world", the term has no referent that a frame can settle, because no frame holds the world's atom list.
- *They're Scanning Books* (read 2026-11-23) supplies the cross-domain check: "The real import is not a new term in the existing space. It is a new dimension of the space." World-absolute novelty would require a new dimension of the space, which is an import into a frame, not an addition to the world.
- **Falsifier:** exhibit a frame that holds the complete atom list of the world and can test membership in it. Then "new to the world" becomes an ordinary member of the same group and this claim is withdrawn.

### Q4  -  Does the animal-behaviour analogue hold?

- Wikipedia g factor in non-humans (read 2026-11-23): "Innovation has often been observed in animals, typically involving novel solutions to everyday problems or the use of tools to achieve a goal." The underlying source is a meta-analysis of 4,000 primate behaviour papers (Reader, Hager and Laland 2011).
- So a novelty standard can be non-human and still be a standard: the animal's own behavioural repertoire is the Γ. This corroborates Fig 6 :22 (novel relative to atoms, not to the world) in a field with no overlap with the framework's author.
- It does **not** corroborate the claim that animals judge their own novelty. The observation is made by human researchers. That distinction is the same one as Fig 8 :50: "Two frames can hold the same atom by convergence, reaching it separately, which is corroboration at maximum independence." Here the atom (a novel solution) is observed by an outside frame.
- **Falsifier:** show a case where the animal's repertoire and the observer's catalogue are demonstrably the same frame, in which case this is one frame wearing two names (Fig 8 :49).

### Q5  -  Does the patent analogue hold?

- Wikipedia Novelty (patent) (read 2026-11-23): "No search can possibly cover every single publication or use on earth, and therefore cannot prove that an invention is 'new'."
- Wikipedia Prior art (read 2026-11-23): patent offices use a novelty search to determine whether a claim meets novelty and non-obviousness criteria.
- So a real institution treats novelty as a searchable, frame-relative membership test and explicitly refuses the absolute version. This is Fig 8 :39 in legal clothing: "So a search that finds nothing is never a proof of absence."
- **Falsifier:** show a patent system whose negative search result is treated as proof of world-absolute newness rather than as a report of non-detection within prior art.

## CONCLUSION

P-novelty and H-novelty are isomers: same atoms, different reference frame, different substance, invisible in the unqualified word "novel". "New to the world" is a functional group with an empty qualifier, not a seventh kind of novelty. The checks must run in dependency order: describe the residual, settle generator-side membership, refuse before mint, settle first-order molecules, choose the outside frame, then range against the ground. The animal-behaviour and patent-law analogues corroborate the frame-relative reading from independent fields but decide nothing. Each claim above has its own falsifier; none was written after the result.
