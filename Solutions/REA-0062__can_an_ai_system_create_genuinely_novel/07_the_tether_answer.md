# The Tether's answer on AI and genuine novelty

**Mode: `specified` on the two verdicts, `general` on the split. Not `grounded`.** This is a shape and a route, not a measurement. Simulated derivation checked against the figures (`05_what_shape_means.md` §8), not a ground reading.

## 1. The residual it answers

The question bundles two conditions. One: can a generator produce something that has never been produced before? Two: what settles a claim that something is novel? Fig 9 :55: "A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones." They overlap because the answer to the first changes what the second must check, but they are not the same question and do not share an anchor.

## 2. The shape, per domain

**D1, the generator frame.** A system can mint a term new to its own library. Fig 6 :22: "NOVEL means novel relative to atoms, not to the world. IMPORT adds an atom, and the added atom is what expands the closure." The third guard checks φ not already in the library (Fig 5 :25). It is a real event, and it is the whole of the defensible reading of "AI can create novel ideas". But Fig 5 :49 warns: "Machinery firing is not the milestone. A running system can mint and still return nothing new, which is exactly what the third guard exists to record." So activity is not novelty; the guard decides.

**D1b, world-absolute novelty.** No frame adds an atom to the world. Fig 6 :25-26: "An import adds no atom to the world. The world does not gain primitives; a frame gains access." Fig 8 :32: "Closure is idempotent. Composing inside a closed set never leaves it, so no search, however long, adds an atom. This one is definitional: it is what closure means." So the "genuinely" in the question does not name a harder case of novelty. It names a category that no generator, human or artificial, can occupy. The error is in the adjective, not in the AI.

**D2, the human-knowledge frame.** Human knowledge is the right anchor for the question that is constitutively about human knowledge. Fig 2 :36: "An anchor is legitimate when the question is constitutively about it." Fig 2 :37: "Human judgment is the right anchor for is this a good answer for a person, because the question is about people." The same lines add that it is "only an instrument" for a question like whether code is correct, and instruments can be wrong on axes they do not measure. So Option B is correct when restricted to "new to people" and incorrect as a universal claim about novelty. Its word "only" is unsupported.

**D3, the ground.** Judging novelty requires a frame whose closure differs from the generating frame. Fig 6 :23: "No frame escapes its own closure. The bound moves only on contact with a frame whose closure differs." Fig 8 :45: "Two frames together reach what neither reaches alone." But no judge, human or machine, can certify world-absolute novelty, because Fig 8 :39 says "So a search that finds nothing is never a proof of absence", and Fig 9 :44 says "Only a sealed room can be searched to the end, and no frame is a sealed room. So searching and finding nothing proves nothing." Fig 13 :134: "Unreachable is a verdict here, not a silence."

**D4, the verification frame.** A room of judges does not supply the missing anchor. Fig 7 :14: "blending: novelty swamped to the mean". Fig 7 :35: "A room’s agreement is variation, not a verdict. Selection between rooms is where the ground votes." Fig 2 :31: independence is how little two frames draw on the same evidence. Agreement among people who share a corpus is one witness, not many.

## 3. What it explicitly does not say

- Not that AI novelty is impossible. Minting a term new to a library is definitional and real.
- Not that human judgment is useless. It is the right standard for the human-relative question and a legitimate outside frame for many generator-side questions.
- Not that novelty is purely subjective. It is frame-relative, which is a structural claim, not a claim that anything goes. The reference frame must be named and the membership test must be checkable.
- Not that a prior-art search proves anything is new. It reports non-detection in one record.
- Not that a panel's agreement settles the claim.
- Not that the framework settles whether an AI has agency or "truly creates" in the philosophical sense. The figures do not test that. It is an **open item**, declared in `01` §5, and Operators table :68 is the reason: "A relation that fails all seven is not an operator you are missing; it is a claim that has not been made yet."
- Not `grounded`. No instrument returned a value.

## 4. What would settle it

A ground reading, not a further argument.

- **An instrument that discriminates sources.** *The Hard Problem is About Source*: a colour reading "cannot comment on the source"; it was the spectrometer, not an argument, that resolved it. Here: an instrument that distinguishes "new to the training corpus", "new to the published record" and "new primitive in the world" would name the slots the current vocabulary blends.
- **A generator that adds a primitive to the world.** If an output increased the world's atom count rather than a frame's access, Fig 6 :26 would be broken and the world-absolute claim would be back on the table.
- **A frame that certifies its own completeness**, or a sealed room whose exhaustive search returns a definite absence. Either breaks Fig 8 :37 and Fig 9 :44.
- **An agency criterion that the figures can range over.** If one is derived rather than imported, the open item in §3 closes. Until then, declaring it is the honest move (Seat Map :171: "Where a stake cannot be removed, declare it.").
- **Standing falsifiers from `01`:** a mint that adds an atom, an anchor that moves when frames push on it, or a proof that the two sub-questions form a partition.

## 5. The honest stop

**Unreached, not unreachable.** *They're Scanning Books*: "A minimum viable set of six terms is a human's careful guess at reality's compression axes; another sensorium's minimum set need not be those six." The standard of novelty we hold is similarly a visible choice, not a neutral discovery. *How Intelligence Actually Works*: "The recursion converges toward indiscernibility, and indiscernibility is not identity." An output that looks novel to every available judge is still an output until a ground settles it. The answer stops at the limit of the available instruments and records the lineage so the stop is checkable.

## 6. One-paragraph version for outreach

> The question bundles two things, and the figures refuse to average them. On the generator side, an AI can produce a term new to its own library: that is the framework's novelty guard, and it is a real event. But no frame, human or artificial, adds a primitive to the world, so "genuinely novel" in the world-absolute sense is not a harder case of novelty; it is a category mistake. On the verdict side, human knowledge is the right standard for the question "is this new to people?", because that question is about people; it is only an instrument for the question "is this new to the world?", and an instrument can be wrong on the axes it does not measure. Judging novelty needs an outside frame with a different closure, and no search over a non-sealed record proves absence. So the offered either/or fails, and what is offered instead is a shape: novelty is a frame-relative membership test, declared from outside the frame, with the anchor named for the question actually asked, plus falsifiers written in advance and the open item about agency declared rather than hidden.
