# Expert verification packet: the Tether on technology diffusion

**What this is:** a claim in a form a technology-diffusion researcher can check. Mode, derivation and refuters are stated before the conclusion, so a reader knows what to look for and what would settle it.

**Mode: `specified` on the application, `general` on the structural rules, not `grounded`.** No measurement was taken by this run. This is a shape and a route, not a result. It is a simulated derivation checked for internal consistency against the figures.

## 1. The claim, in one paragraph

The question is a union of two conditions, not one hard problem: uptake speed by a using unit, and the lag before the effect becomes visible in economy-wide productivity. A case in both is categorically different from either alone. The residual is indexed per technology-in-context slot, never as a global adoption curve. A slot spreads quickly when the technology composes inside what the adopter already holds, the per-slot advantage has positive and discriminable mass, and the habitat and channel to the ground are intact. A slot takes decades when a complement must be imported from a frame the adopter does not have, or when the channel to the ground has decayed. The framework returns the shape, not the name of the complement or the number of years.

## 2. Three checkable sub-claims

### C1: framework-internal; checkable by reading Figure 9 and Figure 1

- **Statement:** the two conditions form a union, and the residual must be read per slot. A single lever applied to the union leaves a categorically different residue.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 9 :55 "A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones." Fig 9 :57 "What remains is smaller and it is also categorically different: a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either." Fig 1 :19 "The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated."
- **What to check:** are the two conditions really different objects with different grounds, or one phenomenon measured twice? Does an averaged adoption statistic hide the per-slot signal?
- **Refuter:** one lever clears both conditions with no categorically different residue, or an averaged statistic predicts which slot will lag better than a per-slot residual measured on the same ground.

### C2: economics-facing and network-facing; checkable against the diffusion literature

- **Statement:** speed is a property of adopter-and-habitat, not of the artifact alone, and a missing complement outside the adopter's closure is the long pole. A technology that composes inside existing reach is a cheap search; one that needs an import is out of reach until contact with a differing frame occurs.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 11 :39 "So the answerable question is not what intelligence is, but what makes a system capable. And capability is a property of agent-and-habitat, never of the agent alone." Fig 6 :21 "MINT composes inside the closure and can never add an atom." Fig 13 :115 "The set is closed. The arrangements are not. That gap is why import exists."
- **What to check:** against Griliches 1957 (profitability by region), Comin and Hobijn 2006 (education and trade openness), Centola and Macy 2007 (multiple-source reinforcement), and Beck et al. 2023 (learning rule changes adoption order). Is the complement distinction doing work the profitability and habitat variables do not already do, or is it a restatement of them in other words?
- **Refuter:** isolated adopters adopt at the same speed as embedded adopters, or two technologies with the same reachable atom set, advantage and habitat differ by decades because of a term already in the adopter's closure.

### C3: measurement-facing; checkable against productivity data

- **Statement:** part of the long lag is a channel failure, separate from the missing-complement condition. The economy-wide series is a lossy reading of a ground that has not decayed.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 10 :24 "the ground does not decay, the channel does". Fig 10 :26 "saturated metrics, pooled readings". Fig 4 :30 "So take the round trip and measure the gap. Without it you are navigating by dead reckoning: confident about a position nobody checked."
- **What to check:** against Triplett's Brookings review of the seven Solow-paradox explanations and the Comin, Hobijn and Rovito finding that technologies "do not diffuse in a logistic way" once the intensive margin is measured. Can a per-slot, source-discriminating instrument separate a decayed channel from a genuinely absent complement?
- **Refuter:** the economy-wide ground moves while channel instruments are unchanged and no complement was imported, so the lag cannot be attributed to either condition.

## 3. What is explicitly not claimed

- Not that any named technology is fast or slow.
- Not that profitability, education, trade openness, network ties or organisational capital is the single cause.
- Not that the Rogers S-curve is figure-derived. It is a world empirical shape and is used only as a budget filter.
- Not that all slow productivity visibility is a measurement artifact. Channel decay and a missing complement are different conditions and are never averaged.
- Not that the framework can name the missing complement or derive the 45-year lag. That is a declared `gap`.
- Not `grounded`. Nothing here was settled by a measurement in this run.
- Not a claim that this document proves the framework correct. A metatheory cannot validate itself.

## 6. What an expert can check in twenty minutes

1. Read Fig 9 :55 and :57, Fig 1 :19, Fig 10 :24, and Fig 11 :39. Four short passages.
2. Check C2 against one diffusion dataset you know: does the complement distinction change any prediction, or is it a rename of the usual covariates?
3. Name one instrument that would separate a decayed measurement channel from a genuinely absent complement at the sector level, or say why none is possible.
4. Send the refutation. A failure is more useful than an endorsement.
