# The Tether's answer: why some technologies spread quickly and others take decades

**Mode: `specified` on the application, `general` on the structural rules, not `grounded`.** This is a shape and a route, not a result. Simulated derivation checked for internal consistency against the figures (`05_what_shape_means.md` §7), not a ground reading. No measurement was taken by this run.

## 1. The residual it answers

Some technologies are taken up quickly and raise measured output quickly; others are taken up slowly or are taken up quickly and still take decades to register in economy-wide productivity. The per-slot gap R = |Γ(b,a) − o′| stays live for a long time in some technology-in-context slots and closes fast in others. World data gives the scale: a mean adoption lag near 45 years across a large technology sample (Comin and Hobijn, AER 2010, opened this session) and a long-running aggregate visibility puzzle (Solow via Triplett, Brookings 1999). Those are measurements from world sources, not figure verdicts.

## 2. The shape

1. **This is two conditions of a union, not one hard problem.** Fig 9 :55: "A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones." Fig 9 :57: "What remains is smaller and it is also categorically different: a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either." Condition A is uptake speed by a using unit; condition B is when the effect becomes visible in economy-wide productivity. A case in both is a compound with its own loop.
2. **The residual is per slot, never an average.** Fig 1 :19: "The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated." Fig 1 :22: "Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average." A global adoption curve is a budget filter, not a witness.
3. **Quick spread when the technology composes inside existing reach.** Fig 8 :32: "Closure is idempotent. Composing inside a closed set never leaves it, so no search, however long, adds an atom. This one is definitional: it is what closure means." A technology that needs no new atom is a cheap search inside the adopter's closure. A technology that needs a complement is out of reach until an import arrives from a frame whose closure differs.
4. **Capability is adopter-and-habitat, not artifact alone.** Fig 11 :39: "So the answerable question is not what intelligence is, but what makes a system capable. And capability is a property of agent-and-habitat, never of the agent alone." Fig 11 :40: "A system cannot be made more capable by improving the agent in isolation. An improvement that does not change contact changes nothing, however much it improves." Skills, suppliers, standards, peers, education and trade openness are habitat actors, not decoration.
5. **Part of the long lag is a channel failure.** Fig 10 :24: "the ground does not decay, the channel does". Fig 10 :26: "saturated metrics, pooled readings". An aggregate productivity series can lose contact with a ground that is still moving. That is a report of lost contact, not a verdict about the technology.
6. **The missing thing is characterized in effect terms, not named.** Fig 9 :32: "you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself." The framework cannot say which complement or how many years. It can say what an answer must look like and what would refute it.

## 3. Verdicts per domain, kept separate and never averaged

- **D1 Adopter loop, `triangulated` (Fig 1 and Fig 5, per-slot indexing and the support gate).** Adoption fires when the per-slot live mass is positive and discriminable. Griliches' profitability result and the Rogers attributes corroborate from different fields, with no overlap with the framework. Refuter: a technology with no measured positive per-slot advantage spreading at the same rate under matched habitat.
- **D2 Habitat, `triangulated` (Fig 11 and world network evidence, habitat enumeration versus complex contagion).** Speed belongs to adopter-and-habitat. Comin and Hobijn's education and trade-openness result, Centola and Macy's multiple-source reinforcement, and Beck et al.'s learning-rule result are three low-overlap witnesses to the same shape. Refuter: isolated adopters adopting at the same speed as embedded adopters.
- **D3 Technology reach, `triangulated` (Fig 6, Fig 8 and Fig 13, import moves the wall).** A complement outside the adopter's closure is the long pole; no amount of same-frame search adds it. Refuter: two technologies with the same reachable atom set, the same advantage and the same habitat differing by decades because of a term already in the closure.
- **D4 Measurement channel, `triangulated` (Fig 10 and Fig 4, channel decay and lossy cross-scale transform).** Aggregate visibility is a separate condition. Triplett's seven explanations of the Solow paradox corroborate that the reading can fail in several distinct ways. Refuter: ground moves while channel instruments are unchanged and no complement was imported.
- **Which complement, which technology, which number, `gap`.** Fig 13 :134: "Unreachable is a verdict here, not a silence," and Fig 8 :39: "So a search that finds nothing is never a proof of absence." The missing instrument is characterized in effect terms but not held. Refuter: a figure line that names the complement or derives the lag.

## 4. What it explicitly does not say

- Not that any particular technology is fast or slow. The framework returns a shape, not a technology name.
- Not that profitability, education, trade openness, network ties, or organisational capital is *the* cause. Each is a world finding from a particular evidence pool; the figures decide only the two-condition shape.
- Not that the Rogers S-curve is figure-derived. Comin, Hobijn and Rovito report that technologies "do not diffuse in a logistic way" once the intensive margin is measured. The S-curve is a filter for the budget.
- Not that all slow productivity visibility is a measurement artifact. Channel decay (D4) and a genuinely absent complement (D3) are different conditions of the union and are never averaged.
- Not that more studies inside one frame add evidence. Fig 7 :13: "agreement compounds, evidence does not".
- Not `grounded`. Nothing here was settled by a measurement in this run.

## 5. What would settle it: the ground

A source-discriminating instrument, not a further argument. From *The Hard Problem is About Source*: a color instrument "cannot comment on the source"; the spectrometer resolved it. Here the corresponding tests are:

1. **Per-slot residual test.** Measure R for a technology in a using context against that context's own payoff, and check whether it trends down when the technology composes inside existing reach and stays flat when a complement is absent. A flat residual that does not respond to an imported complement would break the shape.
2. **Union test.** Apply one lever to the spread condition and a different lever to the visibility condition. If the residue after both is not categorically different from either alone, F2 fails.
3. **Habitat substitution test.** Remove peers, supplier contact and complementary skills while holding the artifact fixed. If speed is unchanged, D1/D2 fail.
4. **Channel test.** Change the sector boundary or the measurement instrument while holding adoption fixed. If the productivity reading tracks the instrument rather than the ground, D4 is confirmed; if it tracks neither, the channel story is in doubt.
5. **Standing falsifiers** from `01_figure_grounded_audit.md` §4 all remain live.

## 6. The honest stop

**Unreached, not unreachable.** The six-term substrate is "a human's careful guess at reality's compression axes" (*They're Scanning Books*, read this session); declaring it makes the choice visible, not neutral. The framework stops where the instruments stop distinguishing sources, and records the lineage so the stop is checkable. Indiscernibility is not identity. Both the missing complement and the numeric lag remain open. Agreement from a room of diffusion researchers is variation, not a verdict.

## 7. One-paragraph version for outreach

> The Tether does not name the technology that will spread fast, and it does not give a single cause. It says the question is two conditions of a union: how fast a technology is taken up, and when its effect becomes visible in economy-wide productivity, with a case in both being categorically different from either. It reads the residual per technology-in-context slot, not as one global adoption curve. A slot spreads quickly when the technology composes inside what the adopter already holds, the per-slot advantage has positive, discriminable mass, and the habitat and the channel to the ground are intact. A slot takes decades when a complement must be imported from a frame the adopter does not have, or when the channel to the ground has decayed. It does not name the complement or the number of years, because it returns a shape, not a name. What it offers is the shape any answer must have, five refuters written in advance, and the instruments that would close it. The slot stays open until the ground settles it.
