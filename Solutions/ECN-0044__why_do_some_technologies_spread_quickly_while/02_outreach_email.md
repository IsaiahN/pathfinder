**Subject:** One checkable split on why technologies diffuse at different speeds

> Hi [name],
>
> I have one specific output I would like you to check against work you already know. I am not asking you to read the theory first, and I am not asking you to agree with it. If the output does not survive, there is nothing else to look at.
>
> **The output (`specified`, not `grounded`):** the question is a union of two conditions, not one hard problem. Condition A is how fast a technology is taken up by a using unit. Condition B is when its effect becomes visible in economy-wide productivity. A case in both is categorically different from either alone: a technology can spread fast and register slowly, or spread slowly and pay quickly where it is used. The residual has to be read per technology-in-context slot, not as one global adoption curve, because an average throws away the signal that says where to look.
>
> The proposed rule is this. A slot spreads quickly when (i) the technology composes from what the adopter already holds, with no new complement required, (ii) the per-slot advantage has positive, discriminable mass, and (iii) the habitat and the channel to the ground are intact. A slot takes decades when a complement must be imported from a frame the adopter does not have, or when the channel to the ground has decayed. This is a shape and a route, not a technology name and not a number. I do not claim the framework can tell you which complement or how many years.
>
> **What is deliberately not claimed:** that the Rogers S-curve is figure-derived, that any one variable (profitability, education, trade openness, network ties) is the whole cause, that all slow diffusion is a measurement artifact, or that this derivation is `grounded`. It is a simulated derivation checked for internal consistency against the figures. The 45-year mean adoption lag in the literature is a world measurement, not something this framework produced.
>
> **What would refute it, written before this email:**
> 1. An averaged adoption statistic predicts which specific technology-context slot will lag better than a per-slot residual measured on the same ground.
> 2. One lever clears both the spread condition and the productivity-visibility condition with no categorically different residue left.
> 3. Two technologies with the same reachable atom set, the same per-slot advantage and the same habitat differ by decades, and the difference traces to a term already in the adopter's closure.
> 4. Isolated adopters adopt at the same speed as embedded adopters, or an artifact-only improvement moves the ground with the habitat held fixed.
> 5. The economy-wide ground moves while channel instruments are unchanged and no complement was imported.
>
> If any one holds, the corresponding part is withdrawn. A one-line "this fails because..." is more useful to me than agreement. If you would rather not engage, no reply is needed.
>
> **If it survives, and only then:** the sources are below. The figures are the authority, not my summary of them.
> - Verbatim figure text with line numbers (local framework extract): `FRAMEWORK/reference/03-FIGURES_TEXT.md` in the Project Pathfinder workspace
> - WATSON synthesis with per-domain quotes and verdict fields: `09_watson_answer.md` in the same solution folder
> - Empirical anchor: Comin and Hobijn, "An Exploration of Technology Diffusion", American Economic Review 100(5), 2010: https://www.aeaweb.org/articles?id=10.1257/aer.100.5.2031
> - Habitat and measurement: Griliches 1957 (Econometric Society abstract): https://www.econometricsociety.org/publications/econometrica/1957/10/01/hybrid-corn-exploration-economics-technological-change and Triplett, Brookings 1999: https://www.brookings.edu/articles/the-solow-productivity-paradox-what-do-computers-do-to-productivity/
>
> **If you want to use the machinery:** ask for the formula and write down what would refute your own result *before* you run it. A frame cannot check the direction of its own question, so the refuter has to be an ordinary written term. A reported failure is more useful to me than a reported success.
>
> — [name]

## Why this version is ordered this way

| the move | figure-grounded reason |
|---|---|
| Output first, theory second | Fig 10 :52: "Bring a characterised residual, never the source." A reader ranges the classification against their own ground without holding the framework interior. |
| Union split before mechanism | Fig 9 :55: "A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones." Naming a single cause before the split produces a partition error. |
| Per slot, never averaged | Fig 1 :19: "The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated." Fig 1 :22: "Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average." |
| Do not ask for agreement | Fig 2 :35: "Alignment is triangulation, not negotiation. A mirror cannot correct you." |
| Pre-registered refuter | A falsifier written before the run is a design instrument, not only an honesty device. |
| Mode stated on every claim | `THE_FORMULA.md` mode rule: general, specified, grounded; state which. |
| S-curve withheld as a filter | Comin, Hobijn and Rovito report that technologies "do not diffuse in a logistic way" once the intensive margin is measured. A filter must not hand us a verdict (Fig 9 :60). |
| Shape, not a name | Fig 9 :32: "you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself." The email never names the missing complement. |
| No request for trust, prestige or belief | Prestige can fund an action but cannot settle one (*The Recursive Self-Improvement Trap*, read this session). The ask gives the claim a budget to be tried. |
| Offer the formula as a method to reuse | Fig 4 :22: "Only methods cross up. Only head starts come down." Sending a generator is permitted; sending a recording is not. |
