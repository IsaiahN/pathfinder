## DERIVATION

### Q1 - Are the pumps isomers? CORRECTION TO MY OWN §3 of `05`

- Fig 12 :117: "isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."
- Fig 12 :124: "functional group. A substructure whose presence fixes the outcome. The group says what kind; the qualifier says which member."
- Written as first-order molecules over the six-term set, the mechanisms read roughly:
  - solubility pump: `Space(surface) -> Gradient(pCO2 across air-sea) -> Energy(heat content)`;
  - biological pump: `Energy(light and heat) -> Network(food web) -> Space(depth)`;
  - carbonate buffering: `Energy -> Spectrum(pH and alkalinity ordering) -> Space`;
  - ventilation and circulation: `Space -> Network(overturning) -> Energy`.
- Each is `state -> structure -> state`, one crossing, legal under Fig 13 :74 "Everything else is a composition across the two kinds, never within one." They do **not** share a full atom set: the structures differ (Gradient, Network, Spectrum) and so do the state pairings.
- Therefore, as written, they are **not isomers**. They are members of one **functional group**: the `state -> structure -> state` motif, with the qualifier selecting which member. Fig 12 :124 fixes this.
- The stronger version: if all four were written over the full shared atom set `{Space, Time, Energy, Gradient, Spectrum, Network}` and differed only in which structure mediated which state pair, they **would** be isomers, same atoms, different arrangement, different behaviour. That is a checkable structural claim, but it requires a sharing of atoms the field has not established. Mark `specified`, unmeasured.
- **I overstated the "shape, not a name" rule in `05` §3.** It governs a missing primitive. These structures are already in the six; selecting one is a binding from the ocean-chemistry frame onto a held atom, not the invention of a seventh. What is refused is the loop-produced number, not every name. Retracted as applied to held atoms. Triangulated (README + Fig 12 :117 + Fig 13 :115).

### Q2 - What order does the derivation require?

- Fig 3 :20: "The chain is ordered by dependency, not by importance." Fig 3 :19: "Optimising a link that already works cannot rescue one that does not."
- Order: (1) enumerate the habitat (Fig 11 :43); (2) perceive the per-slot residual (Fig 1 :19); (3) bind the objective, meaning pathway, horizon and threshold (Formula "Direction"); (4) only then compare with ⋛; (5) settle against measurement (Formula step 5).
- Fig 12 :104: "A settled molecule becomes an atom for whatever composes over it." A mechanism whose first-order molecule has been settled against observations can serve as an operand at the next level. An unsettled one cannot. This is why a threshold comparison of four unsettled mechanisms is out of order.
- **Falsifier, written before the run:** if a comparison of uptake capacity is shown to be well posed before pathway, horizon and threshold are bound, the ordering claim fails.

### Q3 - Which six-term structures correspond to which measured control?

These are analogues, not derivations. Fig 13 gives the definitions verbatim:

- Fig 13 :20, spectrum: "ordering, so more and less can be said." The carbonate system's pH and saturation ordering, and the Revelle factor's ranking of waters by buffering weakness, are spectrum-like.
- Fig 13 :22, gradient: "a difference that can be spent." The air-sea fugacity difference is the gradient that drives the flux; warming spends or erodes it.
- Fig 13 :24, network: "that entities relate, and influence travels." Overturning circulation and the food web are networks; influence travels through them, and a change in one node reaches the sink far away (Fig 11 :52-53, the cascade).
- Fig 13 :76: "A gradient over space and one over time are different terms, because which state it is bonded to is part of what it is." The spatial air-sea gradient and the multidecadal trend are not one quantity. Reports that conflate them are type errors, not approximations.
- **Falsifier:** if a measured control on uptake fails to map to any of the six structures without importing a seventh kind, the analogue claim fails and the control must be treated as an unenumerated habitat actor.

### Q4 - What is the order of the union?

- Fig 9 :54: "And when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again." The four mechanisms form a union with overlapping conditions, so a single lever clears only the region where one condition holds.
- Fig 9 :57-58: "a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either. It stops being a mixture and becomes a specific compound." In high-emission pathways, warming, stratification and reduced buffering coincide; the result is not "more of the same" weakening but a different compound with a threshold (Terhaar 2024, world source).
- Keeping every cut ranked and reversible is required: Fig 9 :41 "so keep every cut ranked and reversible" and :42 "narrowing gets you close, the ground decides."

## CONCLUSION

The four uptake mechanisms are a **functional group**, not isomers as the field currently writes them. The stronger isomer form is available in principle over a shared atom set but is unmeasured. The derivation order is fixed by dependency: enumerate, perceive, bind the objective, compare, settle. The six-term structures map cleanly onto spectrum-like ordering, gradient-like driving differences and network-like transport, while the spatial and temporal gradients remain distinct terms. This rescues the part of the question that is structural without pretending to produce the number.
