# Expert verification packet: the Tether on remaining ocean carbon uptake

**What this is:** a claim in a form an ocean carbon researcher can check. Mode, derivation and refuters are stated before the conclusion.

**Mode: `specified` on the substantive claims, `general` on the structural shape, not `grounded`.** No capacity value is claimed. This is a shape and a route, not a result.

## 1. The claim, in one paragraph

The question "how much carbon can the ocean keep absorbing as it warms and its chemistry changes?" is asked in the wrong form. The residual between projected and observed uptake is indexed by slot (basin, biome, mechanism), so one global scalar loses the regional sign reversals that carry the live signal. Uptake is a union compound of solubility, carbonate buffering, ventilation and biology, not one lever. The flux is a gradient-driven flow whose spatial and temporal gradient terms are distinct. Any admissible answer must name pathway, horizon and threshold before calling anything a capacity, must treat measurement as the anchor and model agreement as a budget filter, must report the measured gap between projection and observation, and must enumerate second-order cascades. The framework returns the shape; the instruments own the number.

## 2. Three checkable sub-claims

### C1 - framework-internal; checkable by reading Figures 1 and 9

- **Statement:** the residual is slot-indexed and the mechanisms form a union compound, so a single global capacity scalar is not an admissible answer form.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 1 :19 "The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated." Fig 1 :22 "Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average." Fig 9 :54 "And when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again."
- **Refuter:** show a single global scalar that predicts per-basin and per-mechanism residuals without losing sign or rank; or show the four mechanisms reduce to one variable under one monotonic law across all pathways.

### C2 - framework-internal derivation; checkable by reading Figures 13 and 4

- **Statement:** the air-sea flux is a gradient-driven flow; the spatial gradient and the long-term trend are different terms; the projection-to-observation round trip carries a measurable gap.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 13 :76 "A gradient over space and one over time are different terms, because which state it is bonded to is part of what it is." Fig 13 :75 "Two states never bond directly. It is not space-time but space, a structure, time; and two structures join only through a state." Fig 4 :30 "So take the round trip and measure the gap. Without it you are navigating by dead reckoning: confident about a position nobody checked."
- **What to check:** the published flux decomposition is delta fCO2 times gas transfer velocity times solubility (Muller et al. 2025, Methods), which is a gradient and a transfer coefficient, consistent with the figure's flow. Is any published treatment conflating the spatial gradient with the multidecadal trend?
- **Refuter:** show the flux is not composable as a flow from a gradient, or that the round-trip gap is zero (that every regional residual is recoverable exactly from the global scalar), or that spatial and temporal gradients are one term under the six-term set.

### C3 - the anchor and the objective; checkable by reading Figures 2, 3, 9 and 11

- **Statement:** the anchor is measurement, model agreement is a budget filter, the objective is bound before the capacity, and the habitat is enumerated including second-order cascades.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 2 :29 "An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks." Fig 9 :60 "Use filters for the budget and witnesses for the verdict. Never let a filter hand you a verdict." Fig 11 :52-53 "first-order enumeration, second-order effect, and the residual that mattered was three steps out."
- **What to check:** the literature reports fCO2 products around 3.1 +/- 0.3 and GOBMs around 2.6 +/- 0.4 GtC per year over 2014 to 2023, with a roughly 10% model underestimate against interior constraints. Does a reviewer treat that spread as a measured gap to be explained, or average it into a number? The 2023 record-SST year: did a first-order thermal model predict the sign of the regional anomaly?
- **Refuter:** exhibit a model ensemble that moves the measurement rather than ranging against it; or produce a pathway-independent capacity with a stated objective and instrument; or show a direct-contact-only contact list predicts the 2023 regional sign reversal.

## 3. What is explicitly not claimed

- Not a capacity value in GtC per year. No figure line produces one.
- Not that the global sink has flipped sign. The world literature says the fraction has stayed near 25% while the absolute sink has grown; weakening is a projected tendency and a regional anomaly.
- Not that any model ensemble is the anchor.
- Not that 4.5 PgC per year is a universal wall. It is one pathway-conditioned model threshold.
- Not `grounded`. Nothing here is asserted as settled by experiment.
- Not that the pumps are isomers as currently written. They are a functional group; the isomer form is `specified` and unmeasured.

## 5. What would settle it

A source-discriminating instrument, not a further argument. From the essay *The Hard Problem is About Source*: an instrument that reads green "cannot comment on the source"; the spectrometer resolved it.

1. **Slot test:** do per-basin, per-mechanism reports predict the next year's regional anomalies better than a global scalar, using fCO2 observations and repeat hydrography?
2. **Threshold test:** under a stated pathway, does the proportional relation break at a definable sink strength, reproduced by an independent instrument rather than the same model family?
3. **Cascade test:** can an enumerated contact web including DIC depletion and stratification predict the sign reversal a first-order thermal model missed in 2023?
