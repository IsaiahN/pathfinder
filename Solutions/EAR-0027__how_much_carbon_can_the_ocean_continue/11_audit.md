## Round trip gap R_T

The Symbols table defines R_T as the gap between what was sent up and what came back down, `gap(x, (T_E o T_A)(x))`, measuring what the coarser description cannot hold. Fig 4 :30 requires it to be measured rather than assumed. I ran the round trip on the condensation this folder actually uses.

1. **Sent up (x):** the verbatim figure set, chiefly Fig 1 :19 and :22 (slot indexing), Fig 9 :54 (union), Fig 13 :75 and :76 (cross-kind flow), Fig 2 :29 (anchor), Fig 4 :30 (gap).
2. **Abstraction (T_A):** the shorthand carried in `07_the_tether_answer.md`: refuse the scalar, report a per-slot pathway-conditional residual, treat measurement as anchor, report the gap and the cascades.
3. **Concretisation (T_E):** the concrete literature claim: the ocean sink is about a quarter of emissions, the 2023 sink fell about 10% below expectation, and a threshold appears near 4.5 PgC per year in high-emission models.

**R_T is nonzero.** Four things the shorthand holds do not survive the trip back down:

- the **opposite regional signs** in 2023 (tropics stronger, North Atlantic weaker), which a global scalar cannot hold;
- the **second-order DIC compensation**, which a first-order thermal reading misses;
- the **pathway dependence of the threshold**, which a universal "capacity" would erase;
- the **product versus model bias**, which a mean sinks into uncertainty.

One thing survives cleanly: measurement is the anchor and agreement is a filter. That is the residue worth sending. R_T was **not** measured numerically, because no single ground reading defines the whole capacity; it is recorded qualitatively as the enumerated set of dropped constraints. Treating "the ocean absorbs about a quarter of emissions" as identical to the assessments that produce it would set R_T to zero and would be the dead-reckoning error Fig 4 :30 names. The honest form is a lossy condensation with a measured gap, which `09_watson_answer.md` now states.
