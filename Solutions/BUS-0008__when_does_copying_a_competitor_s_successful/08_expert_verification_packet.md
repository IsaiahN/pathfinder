# Expert verification packet — the Tether on copying a competitor's strategy

**What this is:** a boundary in a form a strategy scholar or practitioner can check. Mode, derivation and refuters are stated before the conclusion, so a reader knows what to look for and what would settle it.

**Mode: `specified` on the boundary, `general` on the structural shape, not `grounded`.** No market measurement is claimed. This is a shape and a route, not a result.

**Status:** simulated derivation checked for internal consistency against the figures, not a ground reading. It cannot settle the question it reports on.

## 1. The claim, in one paragraph

Copying a competitor's successful strategy works when what crosses is a generator, a re-applicable method with its binding arrangement intact, and when the habitat the success depended on is reproduced or the generator is remade by the receiving level, with the receiver still a differing room relative to the competitor. It destroys the paid-for distinctiveness when what crosses is the recording of one occasion, the bonds are lost, the habitat is substituted through a degraded channel, or the receiver converges to the competitor's closure. Copying is an import from a live peer and needs a source gate (provenance) and a shadow test (an explained gap); without them the receiver imports a wrong atom at a cost above starting from primitives. The chain usually breaks at the middle link: the firm can name the ingredients and has no term for the bonds or for its own progress. Which concrete elements are method and which are recording in a named firm is a declared gap.

## 2. Three checkable sub-claims

### C1 — framework-internal plus practice-facing; checkable by reading Fig 4 and Fig 12

- **Statement:** the visible artifact of a success is a recording of one occasion. The arrangement that made it pay is invisible to the artifact, so copying ingredients without bonds produces a different substance. A method (a generator) is what may cross.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 4 :23, "upward: a method that can be reapplied. Never the recording of a particular success."; Fig 4 :25-26, "Replayed downward, it produces a system that repeats a past success and cannot produce a new one."; Fig 12 :5, "The bond is the rest of the information, and it is the half nothing was recording."; Fig 12 :117, "Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."
- **What to check:** is the artifact/method distinction drawn at the right place for the case you know? Does the artifact omit a binding arrangement that the success actually required?
- **Refuter:** copying only the visible artifact, with no method transfer and no rebinding, reproduces the market return repeatedly in a new habitat. If that holds, C1 fails.

### C2 — habitat and anchor; checkable against firm and market evidence

- **Statement:** transplanting a strategy substitutes a habitat, and the two failure modes are silent; the market is the anchor, so convergence removes the difference it pays for.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 11 :66, "Isolation is not removal of the habitat. It is substitution of one habitat for another."; Fig 11 :67, "Move the residual and you must reproduce everything its goal required, and you inherit the new habitat's actors, which you did not choose."; Fig 11 :68-69, "what you failed to reproduce is invisible until the goal fails" and "what you unintentionally introduced is invisible until it acts"; Fig 2 :29, "An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks. A measurement, an experiment, a counter, a market, a patient outcome."; Fig 7 :36, "A chain of mirrors is one long room. The next room must differ from the last, and match the residual it surfaced."
- **What to check:** which conditions of the original success were not reproduced in the transplant? Did the market pay a difference before the copy and stop paying it after?
- **Refuter:** a market that pays two near-identical firms above-average returns indefinitely with no residual difference at the anchor, or a transplant into a genuinely different habitat that pays the same return with no reproduction and with the channel measured as degraded. Either would break C2.

### C3 — framework-internal derivation about order and cost

- **Statement:** the transplant chain runs perception, vocabulary, objective, planning, learn and carry, and breaks between vocabulary and objective, where a firm can name ingredients but has no term for bonds or progress. A one-occasion replay fails the bargain; where the arrangement is not recorded, every lineage pays again.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 12 :111, "A vocabulary without bonds can name a state and cannot name a route to one."; Fig 3 :27, "A link with no instrument is not a link that works. It is a link nobody has looked at."; Fig 5 :43-44, "A term that explains everything by saying nothing fails this, and so does a term that explains one occasion perfectly."; Fig 12 :108, "Where it is not, the result carries forward and the deriving does not, so every lineage pays again."
- **What to check:** in a case you know, which link actually stopped the chain? Was there a progress measure in the receiving firm's own terms, or only a list of practices?
- **Refuter:** a firm with an ingredient-only vocabulary and no stated progress measure reproduces the return repeatedly in a different habitat, or a recorded artifact copy pays for a fresh occasion with no re-derivation and with cost measured in actions. Either would break C3.

## 3. What is explicitly not claimed

- Not that copying never works. Head starts are permitted to come down (Fig 4 :24).
- Not a list of copyable practices. The framework returns a shape, not a name (Fig 9 :32).
- Not that the framework can identify which element of a named strategy is the generator. F6 is a declared gap.
- Not that a famous competitor's practice is a better model. From *The Recursive Self-Improvement Trap*: "Prestige can fund an action. It can give an action a budget to be tried. But it cannot settle an action. Only the ground can settle it."
- Not grounded. No market measurement is asserted. No filed case is claimed as verified.
- Not that a similarity proves a copy. Convergent derivation and adopted import look identical in the contents; only the record separates them (Fig 8 :50-52).

## 5. What would settle it

1. **Artifact-only test:** copy only the visible artifact into a new habitat, no method transfer, measure return across repeated occasions.
2. **Habitat-substitution test:** transplant into a genuinely different habitat, measure return with the channel independently checked for degradation.
3. **Anchor test:** measure whether a market pays two near-identical firms above-average returns indefinitely with no residual difference.
4. **Provenance test:** show a same-closure copy moving the wall with no source gate and no shadow test.
5. **Cost test:** show a recorded artifact copy paying for a fresh occasion without re-derivation, cost measured in actions.
6. **Discriminator:** an instrument that separates method from recording in a named firm, described in effect terms.

## 6. What an expert can check in twenty minutes

1. Read Fig 4 :22-26, Fig 12 :5 and :117, Fig 11 :66-69 and Fig 2 :29 (six short passages).
2. Take one firm transplant you know. Say which link stopped it: artifact versus method, habitat, anchor, provenance, or the missing progress term.
3. Name one observation that would distinguish the generator from the recording in that case, or say why none is available.
4. Send the refutation. A failure is more useful than an endorsement.
