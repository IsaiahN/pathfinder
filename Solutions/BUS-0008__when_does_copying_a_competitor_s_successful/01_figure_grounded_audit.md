## 0. The residual, stated in effect terms

A firm observes that a competitor's strategy returns a market reward. The firm copies it. The residual R is the gap between what the copying firm predicted the market would pay and what the market actually paid, indexed **per firm and per time**, never averaged (Fig 1 :19, "The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated."; Fig 1 :22, "Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average.").

The question asks for a boundary, not a preference. Restated: under which conditions does the transplant reduce R, and under which conditions does it increase R by removing the difference the market was paying for? "Distinctiveness" is a comparison relative to competitors, not a property carried by the strategy (Fig 12 :87, "a threshold relation between two quantities"; Fig 11 :49, "An actor's valence is relative to the residual, not a property it carries.").

**Bind the sought-for shape as an ordinary term before the run**, per the formula step "Direction": a frame cannot check the direction of its own question, because the shape it seeks is a preference, not a held term. The bound term is:

> `COPY-SHAPE`: a transplant reduces R iff a generator crosses with its arrangement intact into a reproduced habitat through a maintained channel, and the receiving firm remains a differing room relative to the model. A transplant increases R when the visible recording crosses instead, the binding arrangement is lost, the habitat is substituted, the channel is saturated, or the receiver converges to the model's closure.

This term is written down here so it can accrue a residual and be refused by the same test as any other claim.

## 2. Domain census (derive first, one loop per domain)

**R by arity, symmetry and scale.** Arity: a three-place relation, (firm library Γ, act of copying, market return), indexed per firm and per time (Fig 1 :19). Symmetry: distinctiveness is a threshold comparison, not a symmetric join; the copy is an ordered transplant, `source → receiver`, not a swap (Fig 12 :87, Fig 12 :74). Scale: single-firm loop, population ranking, and the time channel across market changes, with a lossy membrane between the levels (Fig 4 :20, "Only the actors, the currency and the anchor change.").

| domain | actors | currency | anchor | status |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| D1 Imitator's firm loop | decision makers, routines, Γ | actions and residual reduction | realized market return | admitted |
| D2 Market habitat | firms, customers, entrants, the modelled competitor, regulators | survival, share, profit | customer choice and willingness to pay | admitted |
| D3 The competitor's strategy as a composition | atoms (visible ingredients) and bonds (arrangement) | reach, cost of arrangement | settlement against the market | admitted |
| D4 The channel and memory of the original success | instruments, metrics, percepts | contact with the ground | the ground, which does not decay | admitted |
| X1 "The act of copying is itself a capability" | the copy event | none nameable | none nameable | not a domain yet |
| X2 "Industry attractiveness is the sole cause" | the industry | structural rents | already inside D2 | absorbed |

**Partition or union.** Union, not partition. D1 and D3 form the method-versus-recording compound; D2 and D4 form the habitat-versus-channel compound. Their overlap is itself a compound with its own loop (Fig 9 :55, "A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones."; Fig 9 :54, "And when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again."). Verdicts are kept separate per domain and never averaged.

**Order by dependency (Fig 3).** Perception: perceive the competitor's residual-sealing move. Vocabulary: have terms for both ingredients and bonds (Fig 3 :7, "it has terms for what things afford: what can be moved, opened, joined, blocked"). Objective: say what counts as progress in the receiving firm's own habitat. Planning: route the import under incomplete information. Learn and carry: verify against the market and carry a method, not a recording (Fig 3 :15, "name what worked, verify it, and carry it to the next problem"). The chain usually breaks between vocabulary and objective, the link with neither an obvious failure nor an obvious instrument (Fig 3 :27, "A link with no instrument is not a link that works. It is a link nobody has looked at."). Fig 3 :19: "Optimising a link that already works cannot rescue one that does not."

## 3. Findings, with verbatim lines before conclusions

### F1 — D3, method versus recording (decisive, triangulated)

Fig 4 :22: **"Only methods cross up. Only head starts come down."**
Fig 4 :23: **"upward: a method that can be reapplied. Never the recording of a particular success."**
Fig 4 :24: **"downward: something that makes the next search cheaper. Never a replay of what worked once."**
Fig 4 :25: **"A recording carried upward looks like knowledge and is a description of one occasion."**
Fig 4 :25-26: **"Replayed downward, it produces a system that repeats a past success and cannot produce a new one."**
Fig 4 :34-35: **"A seed carries no small tree: it carries what will grow one, and the conditions it lands in decide what it becomes."**

Fig 12 :5: **"The bond is the rest of the information, and it is the half nothing was recording."**
Fig 12 :47: **"A recipe that names only its ingredients has recorded half of itself."**
Fig 12 :94: **"The arrangement is the substance."**
Fig 12 :117: **"Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."**

**Reading (mode `specified`).** The observable artifact of a competitor's success is a recording of one occasion. What the market rewarded was a generator, a re-applicable method whose binding arrangement is precisely what the artifact does not show. A firm that copies the ingredient list and not the bond has recorded half of the thing, and under Fig 12 :117 it has produced a different substance. A head start (a method the receiver can reapply) is permitted to come down; a replay is not. So copying works when the generator crosses and fails when the recording crosses.

**Triangulation.** Fig 4 (the membrane, what crosses between scales) and Fig 12 (the bond, what a composition carries) are two figures with low overlap. They converge on the same boundary from different quantities. Marked `triangulated`.

**Refuter.** A documented case in which copying only the visible artifact, with no transfer of the generative method and no rebinding of the arrangement, reproduces the competitor's market return in a new firm and on repeated occasions. If that holds, the method/recording boundary is wrong and F1 is withdrawn.

### F2 — D2 and D4, habitat substitution and channel loss (triangulated)

Fig 11 :39: **"capability is a property of agent-and-habitat, never of the agent alone."**
Fig 11 :40: **"A system cannot be made more capable by improving the agent in isolation. An improvement that does not change contact changes nothing, however much it improves."**
Fig 11 :50: **"The same actor is a benefactor to one residual and a malfactor to another, so mutualism and parasitism are relations rather than kinds."**
Fig 11 :51: **"No standing list of good actors survives a change of residual."**
Fig 11 :66: **"Isolation is not removal of the habitat. It is substitution of one habitat for another."**
Fig 11 :67: **"Move the residual and you must reproduce everything its goal required, and you inherit the new habitat's actors, which you did not choose."**
Fig 11 :68: **"what you failed to reproduce is invisible until the goal fails"**
Fig 11 :69: **"what you unintentionally introduced is invisible until it acts"**

Fig 10 :24: **"the ground does not decay, the channel does"**
Fig 10 :26: **"stale percepts, dropped transients, saturated metrics, pooled readings"**
Fig 10 :27-28: **"the output is not a verdict, it is you have lost touch"**
Fig 10 :68: **"a generator crosses, an instance does not, and the level it lands on remakes it."**
Fig 10 :71-72: **"The room composition is a function of the question. When the question changes, the room should change. A room that stays fixed while the question moves is the failure; the correlation is only its symptom."**

**Reading (mode `specified`).** A strategy that worked in the competitor's habitat is an instance, not a generator. Transplanting it into the copier's habitat substitutes one habitat for another. The copier inherits actors it did not choose, and the two failure modes are silent: what it failed to reproduce shows up only when the goal fails, and what it unintentionally introduced shows up only when it acts. The channel by which the original success was read degrades even though the market ground does not. Copying therefore works when the habitat is reproduced or the generator is remade by the new level, and destroys distinctiveness when the artifact is transplanted into a substituted habitat with a stale channel.

**Triangulation.** Fig 11 (habitat and isolation) and Fig 10 (ground maintainer and crossing) are low-overlap figures. Marked `triangulated`.

**Refuter.** A strategy transplanted into a genuinely different habitat that produces the same market return without habitat reproduction or re-derivation, on repeated occasions and with the channel independently measured as degraded. If that holds, F2 fails.

### F3 — D1 and D2, the market anchor and the loss of distinctiveness (triangulated)

Fig 2 :29: **"An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks. A measurement, an experiment, a counter, a market, a patient outcome."**
Fig 2 :35: **"Alignment is triangulation, not negotiation. A mirror cannot correct you."**
Fig 2 :40: **"agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool"**
Fig 8 :49: **"Two frames that overlap almost completely are one frame wearing two names."**
Fig 8 :12: **"high overlap"** and Fig 8 :14: **"their union adds nothing"**
Fig 7 :12-13: **"same closure, handed along;"** **"agreement compounds, evidence does not"**
Fig 7 :35: **"A room’s agreement is variation, not a verdict. Selection between rooms is where the ground votes."**
Fig 7 :36: **"A chain of mirrors is one long room. The next room must differ from the last, and match the residual it surfaced."**
Fig 7 :42: **"booking a room like the last one is how a chain stops travelling at all."**

**Reading (mode `specified`).** Distinctiveness is the difference the market, the anchor, pays for. The market "does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks." A copier that converges to the competitor's closure becomes one frame wearing two names; the union adds nothing, the chain books a room like the last one, and travel stops. This is why copying can destroy the very distinctiveness it targeted: the successful strategy's reward depended on being a differing room, and the copy removes the difference. Internal agreement with the competitor cannot substitute for the anchor, because a mirror cannot correct you.

**Triangulation.** Fig 2 (anchor), Fig 8 (overlap and surplus) and Fig 7 (room chain) are three low-overlap routes. Marked `triangulated`.

**Refuter.** A market that pays two near-identical firms above-average returns indefinitely with no residual difference between them, measured at the anchor. If the anchor pays for sameness, the distinctiveness claim fails.

### F4 — D3 and D1, copying as import from a live peer (triangulated with a named strain)

Fig 6 :14: **"the only operator that moves the wall"**
Fig 6 :23: **"No frame escapes its own closure. The bound moves only on contact with a frame whose closure differs."**
Fig 6 :12: **"source gate: provenance"**; Fig 6 :13: **"shadow test: explains a gap"**
Fig 6 :22: **"IMPORT adds an atom, and the added atom is what expands the closure."**
Fig 6 :24: **"Import from a live peer is debited against that frame's independence. A wrong atom costs more than starting from primitives."**
Fig 8 :50-52: **"Convergent derivation and adopted import look identical in the contents. Only the record of where each came from separates them"**
Fig 8 :32: **"Closure is idempotent. Composing inside a closed set never leaves it, so no search, however long, adds an atom."**

**Reading (mode `specified`).** Copying is an import from a live peer, and import is the only operator that moves the wall. But it moves the wall only through a source gate (provenance) and a shadow test (explains a gap). A copy from a high-overlap peer has little surplus (Fig 8 :14), and the import is debited against the receiver's independence. So the figure-grounded question is not "is copying allowed" but "did the copy add an atom that the receiver's closure lacked, with a recorded source and a gap it explains?" Without that, the firm has imported a wrong atom at a cost above starting from primitives. Convergent derivation and adopted import look identical in the contents, so the record, not the artifact, decides which happened.

**Strain, named not hidden.** Fig 4 says only methods cross the membrane; Fig 6 says import moves the closure wall. Read as one line these would conflict. Re-census: Fig 4 governs the between-scale membrane (what may cross up or down), Fig 6 governs the closure boundary (what adds an atom). Different quantities, so no contradiction after re-census, in keeping with the canonical rule that a reading making figures contradict one another has misidentified its domain.

**Refuter.** A copy from a same-closure peer that demonstrably moves the wall with no source gate and no shadow test, or a case where a wrong imported atom costs less than starting from primitives.

### F5 — D1 and D3, the MDL bargain and the one-occasion copy (single-source plus triangulated substrate)

Fig 5 :42: **"The acceptance test is a bargain, not a threshold."**
Fig 5 :43-44: **"A term that explains everything by saying nothing fails this, and so does a term that explains one occasion perfectly."**
Fig 12 :107-108: **"where the arrangement is recorded, re-deriving it is a lookup and the actions were spent once. Where it is not, the result carries forward and the deriving does not, so every lineage pays again."**

**Reading (mode `general` for the bargain, `specified` for firms).** A copy that reproduces one occasion perfectly fails the bargain: it explains the competitor's success and cannot pay for the receiver's next occasion. Whether the receiver can skip the cost of establishing the arrangement depends on the substrate. If the arrangement is recorded as a method, re-deriving it is a lookup; if only the result carried forward, every lineage pays again. This is why a cheap-looking artifact copy is expensive over time.

**Status.** Single-source for the MDL bargain (Fig 5); triangulated with Fig 12 on the substrate question. Marked `single-source` for the bargain itself.

**Refuter.** A recorded artifact copy that pays for a fresh occasion in a new habitat without re-derivation or a method lookup, with the cost measured in actions rather than asserted.

### F6 — Naming which concrete elements are method and which are recording (`gap`)

No figure line governs this after a genuine re-census. Fig 9 :32: **"you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself."** Fig 13 :134: **"Unreachable is a verdict here, not a silence,"** Fig 8 :39: **"So a search that finds nothing is never a proof of absence."**

**Reading.** The figures give the boundary in effect terms but do not say which element of a named strategy is the generator and which is the artifact. That requires a source-discriminating instrument in the firm, not another figure line. This is `unreached`, not `unreachable`: Fig 6 :29 says of the inward instrument, "inward: is our representation adequate to hold it? The instrument extends access. Fails by refining forever on an axis that cannot carry it." What is owed is an instrument that separates method from recording in a specific firm, characterised in effect terms but not yet held.

## 4. Pre-registered refuter (written before the conclusion)

Per the seat rule, "a falsifier written before the run is a design instrument, not only an honesty device." The `COPY-SHAPE` term bound in §0 is refused if any of the following holds:

1. Copying only the visible artifact, with no method transfer and no rebinding, reproduces the market return repeatedly in a new habitat (breaks F1).
2. A strategy transplanted into a genuinely different habitat produces the same return without habitat reproduction or re-derivation, with the channel measured as degraded (breaks F2).
3. A market pays two near-identical firms above-average returns indefinitely with no residual difference at the anchor (breaks F3).
4. A copy from a same-closure peer moves the wall with no source gate and no shadow test, or a wrong imported atom costs less than primitives (breaks F4).
5. A recorded artifact copy pays for a fresh occasion without re-derivation, cost measured in actions (breaks F5).
6. An instrument discriminates method from recording in a named firm and shows the visible artifact is the generator, not its shadow (closes F6 against the gap).

If any fires, `COPY-SHAPE` is withdrawn or narrowed and the finding is recorded, not silently dropped. Until one is run, no part of this may be labelled `grounded`. Per Fig 11 :49, an actor's valence is relative to the residual, so a strategy that is a benefactor to one firm's residual can be a malfactor to another's; no standing list of "copyable practices" survives a change of residual.

## 5. Recommendation

1. Lead with the boundary in effect terms (method crosses, recording does not; habitat must be reproduced or the generator remade; the receiver must remain a differing room), never with a list of copyable practices (F1 to F3).
2. Ask for provenance and a shadow test before treating a copy as an import (F4). Fig 8 :50-52: convergent derivation and adopted import look identical in the contents.
3. Price the copy in both currencies. A cheap artifact is expensive in actions if every lineage pays again (Fig 12 :107-108); Fig 12 :125 says "A composition costs in two currencies, and they do not add."
4. Keep the verdicts per domain separate. Never average the method result with the habitat result with the anchor result.
5. Declare F6 as a gap. Do not name which practices are copyable; the figures return a shape, not a name (Fig 9 :32).
6. Send the generator, not the conclusion. Fig 4 :23: "upward: a method that can be reapplied. Never the recording of a particular success." This applies to the outreach packet itself.

## 7. Updated verdict

The boundary survives; any list of concrete copyable practices does not. Mode `specified`, ungrounded. Copying works when a generator crosses with its arrangement intact into a reproduced habitat through a maintained channel and the receiver remains a differing room. Copying destroys distinctiveness when a recording of one occasion crosses instead, the bonds are lost, the habitat is substituted, the channel is saturated, or the receiver converges to the model's closure. Strains after re-census: one named (Fig 4 versus Fig 6), none hidden. Gap: which concrete elements are method versus recording in a named firm. The market, not agreement with the competitor, settles any instance.
