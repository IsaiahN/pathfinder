# The Tether's answer on copying a competitor's successful strategy

**Mode: `specified` on the boundary, `general` on the structural shape, not `grounded`.** A shape and a route, not a market result. Simulated derivation checked against the figures (`05_what_shape_means.md` §6), not a ground reading.

## 1. The residual it answers

A firm observes a competitor's success, copies it, and wants to know whether the market will pay again. R is the gap between predicted and realized return, indexed per firm and per time (Fig 1 :19). Distinctiveness is not a property of the strategy; it is a threshold relative to other firms, and an actor's valence is relative to the residual (Fig 11 :49). The question is a union compound with its own loop, not one hard problem (Fig 9 :55).

## 2. The boundary, per domain

1. **The generator crosses; the recording does not.** Fig 4 :22: "Only methods cross up. Only head starts come down." Fig 4 :25-26: "Replayed downward, it produces a system that repeats a past success and cannot produce a new one." The visible product, price or practice is a description of one occasion. The bond that made it pay is invisible to a reader of the artifact (Fig 12 :5, "The bond is the rest of the information, and it is the half nothing was recording."). Copying works when a re-applicable method crosses; it fails when a replay crosses.
2. **The habitat must be reproduced, or the generator must be remade by the new level.** Fig 11 :66: "Isolation is not removal of the habitat. It is substitution of one habitat for another." Fig 11 :67: "Move the residual and you must reproduce everything its goal required, and you inherit the new habitat's actors, which you did not choose." The two failure modes are silent (Fig 11 :68-69). Fig 10 :68: "a generator crosses, an instance does not, and the level it lands on remakes it." A strategy is an instance of its original habitat.
3. **The market is the anchor; convergence removes what it pays for.** Fig 2 :29: "An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks. A measurement, an experiment, a counter, a market, a patient outcome." Fig 8 :49: "Two frames that overlap almost completely are one frame wearing two names." Fig 7 :36: "A chain of mirrors is one long room. The next room must differ from the last, and match the residual it surfaced." If the copier becomes the competitor, there is no difference left for the market to reward. Fig 2 :35: "Alignment is triangulation, not negotiation. A mirror cannot correct you."
4. **Copying is an import, and it needs a source gate and a shadow test.** Fig 6 :23: "No frame escapes its own closure. The bound moves only on contact with a frame whose closure differs." Fig 6 :24: "Import from a live peer is debited against that frame's independence. A wrong atom costs more than starting from primitives." Fig 8 :50-52: "Convergent derivation and adopted import look identical in the contents. Only the record of where each came from separates them." Without provenance and a gap the copy explains, a similarity is not evidence of a successful transfer.
5. **A one-occasion replay fails the bargain.** Fig 5 :43-44: "A term that explains everything by saying nothing fails this, and so does a term that explains one occasion perfectly." Fig 12 :108: "Where it is not, the result carries forward and the deriving does not, so every lineage pays again." Where the arrangement is not recorded as a method, each firm pays the cost again.
6. **The chain breaks at the middle link.** Fig 12 :111: "A vocabulary without bonds can name a state and cannot name a route to one." A firm can see the practice and name its ingredients and still have no term for its own progress. Fig 3 :27: "A link with no instrument is not a link that works. It is a link nobody has looked at."

**Verdict kept separate, never averaged:** the method-domain boundary (1, 4, 5) says what may cross; the habitat-domain boundary (2) says where it may land; the anchor-domain boundary (3) says what the market pays for. A single lever clearing one leaves the intersections standing (Fig 9 :57).

## 3. What it explicitly does not say

- Not that copying never works. A generator can cross, and a head start can make the next search cheaper (Fig 4 :24).
- Not a list of copyable practices. That is a name where the framework returns a shape (Fig 9 :32).
- Not that the framework can identify which element of a named strategy is the method. That is a declared `gap` (F6).
- Not that a famous competitor's practice is a better model. "Prestige can fund an action. It can give an action a budget to be tried. But it cannot settle an action. Only the ground can settle it." (*The Recursive Self-Improvement Trap*)
- Not grounded. No market measurement of a transplant is claimed. Mode `specified`.

## 4. What would settle it — the ground

A source-discriminating observation, not a further argument. From *The Hard Problem is About Source*: an instrument that reads green "cannot comment on the source"; "The answer is unreached until a better instrument exists." Here:

- **The artifact-only test:** copy only the visible artifact into a new habitat, with no method transfer, and measure the return across repeated occasions. If it pays repeatedly, F1 fails.
- **The habitat-substitution test:** transplant a strategy into a genuinely different habitat and measure the return with the channel independently checked for degradation. If it pays with no reproduction and no re-derivation, F2 fails.
- **The anchor test:** measure whether a market pays two near-identical firms above-average returns indefinitely with no residual difference. If it does, F3 fails.
- **The provenance test:** show a copy from a same-closure peer that moves the wall with no source gate and no shadow test. If it does, F4 fails.
- **The cost test:** show a recorded artifact copy paying for a fresh occasion with no re-derivation, with cost measured in actions. If it does, F5 fails.
- **The discriminator:** an instrument that separates method from recording in a named firm, characterised in effect terms. That closes F6.

## 5. The honest stop

**Unreached, not unreachable.** Fig 8 :39: "So a search that finds nothing is never a proof of absence." Fig 13 :134: "Unreachable is a verdict here, not a silence," Fig 6 :30: "The description travels. Fails by being sharpened into terms only we can read. Cast it in effect terms." The answer stops at the limit of the available instruments and records the lineage so the stop is checkable. "Indiscernibility is not identity."

## 6. One-paragraph version for outreach

> The Tether does not say that copying never works. It says that a competitor's visible success is a recording of one occasion, and the bond that made it pay is the half the recording does not carry. What can cross is a generator, a method that can be reapplied, and only into a habitat reproduced well enough to keep its original conditions, or when the new level remakes it. The market is the anchor, so a copy that converges to the competitor removes the difference the market was paying for; two frames that overlap almost completely are one frame wearing two names. Copying is an import and needs a source gate and a shadow test, otherwise it imports a wrong atom that costs more than starting from primitives. The firm usually fails at the middle link: it can name the ingredients and has no term for the bonds or for its own progress. What is offered is the boundary in effect terms, five refuters written in advance, and a declared gap: which concrete elements of a named strategy are method and which are recording needs an instrument, not another theory.
