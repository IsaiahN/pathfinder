## 0. The claim under audit

> Option A: unions raise wages and protect workers, but at the cost of employment.
> Option B: unions improve productivity and counterbalance employer power.
> Implied choice: A or B, as if one were the correct single answer.

The audit does not accept the either/or. It asks whether the question is one residual or several, then reads each slot separately. This is the derive-first move: state R in effect terms before choosing a figure line.

---

## 1. Residual and domain census

### 1.1 R by arity, symmetry and scale

- **Arity.** At least five outcome slots are named or implied: wage level, employment level, job protection and tenure, productivity, and distribution of income and bargaining power. The question tries to fold them into one scalar, "do unions help or hurt."
- **Symmetry.** The union/employer relation is not a symmetric conjunction of two equals. It is an ordered comparison of power, a threshold relation of the kind the Operators table defines for ⋛: "a threshold relation between two quantities" with the test "is it about which is larger, rather than both being present?" The two faces are not interchangeable: a wage gain can be a cost to capital and a gain to labor at the same time (Fig 12 :112, "A plan is a sequence. A goal is a comparison. Progress is a subtraction.").
- **Scale.** Slots are resolved at different scales: the bargaining unit and firm for wages and employment, the workplace for voice and productivity, the industry or economy for distribution and political power. A scalar that mixes them is a dead reckoning condensation, not a measurement (Fig 4 :30).

### 1.2 One line per domain: actors, currency, anchor

| domain | actors | currency | anchor | question type |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| D1 Wage setting and employment (monopoly face) | workers, union as seller of labor, employer, would-be entrants | wage rate, headcount, hours | market-clearing wage and employment data; quasi-experiments (election thresholds, right-to-work instruments) | one condition in a union compound; first-order in dependency, because a wage change precedes the employment response |
| D2 Voice and productivity (institutional face) | workers, grievance procedure, management, work organization | quit rate, tenure, value added per worker, information flow | firm productivity and turnover data; case studies of labor-management relations | second condition; its sign is conditional on relationship quality, not fixed by union presence alone |
| D3 Distribution and countervailing power | labor, capital, union, state and labor law | wage compression, income share, political voice | wage-share data, legal framework, meta-analysis | third condition; cross-cutting, because a gain here can be the mirror of a cost in D1 or to firm profits |
| D4 Measurement and verification | researchers, meta-analysts, datasets, reviewers | estimates and residuals | the empirical ground: data, natural experiments, replication | the seat that settles; in this simulated run all four domains draw on one literature pool, so their agreement is one pool talking |

**No nameable anchor means it is not a domain yet.** D1 to D3 each have a public anchor (data, an experiment, a legal record). D4 has the ground itself. A proposed "public opinion about unions" domain was dropped at census because its anchor moves with framing and it cannot settle any slot; it is a frame inside D4, not a separate domain. This is the anchor test from Fig 2 :29.

### 1.3 Partition or union

**Union, not partition.** The three effect domains co-exist and overlap in the same firms and workers. Fig 9 :54: "And when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again." Fig 9 :57: "a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either." Asking which single option is correct is asking a compound to behave like a simple case. The compound is categorically different, not merely larger (Fig 9 :58).

### 1.4 Dependency order (Fig 3)

1. The wage move (D1) is a prerequisite for the employment response within the same bargaining unit; optimising the employment estimate cannot rescue a wage estimate that never arrived (Fig 3 :19, :20-21).
2. The voice mechanism (D2) acts on quits and information flow, which can feed back into wage pressure and productivity; it is not downstream of D1 in the sense of being caused by it, so D1 and D2 are parallel first-order conditions, not a single chain.
3. Distribution (D3) composes over both; it is second-order in the sense that it reads the results of D1 and D2 as operands (Fig 12 :104).
4. Settlement (D4) runs under all three; "Frames range against the ground. They do not negotiate with it." (Fig 2 :45)

Dependency here is a reading, not a property of the chain: "The break is drawn between three and four as an illustration. Where a given chain stops is a reading, never a property of the chain." (Fig 3 :18)

## 3. Findings

### F1 : The either/or is a bundle presented as one question (decisive)

Fig 9 :54: "And when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again."
Fig 9 :57: "a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either."
Fig 1 :19: "The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated."
Fig 1 :22: "Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average."

The question asks for one answer across five slots. Fig 1 forbids the aggregate, and Fig 9 forbids treating a union compound as one hard case. A single "unions are net good" or "unions are net bad" is exactly the live signal disappearing into an average. **Verdict: `triangulated`** across Fig 1 (measurement) and Fig 9 (problem structure), two different figures with low overlap. **Mode: `specified`** on the structure of the question, not on any empirical direction.

**Refuter:** show that one outcome measure, applied consistently, dominates every other slot with the same sign in every institutional context and that no conditional reversal exists. Then the slots collapse to one and the union reading fails.

### F2 : The anchor for each slot is data, never agreement

Fig 2 :29: "An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks. A measurement, an experiment, a counter, a market, a patient outcome."
Fig 2 :35: "Alignment is triangulation, not negotiation. A mirror cannot correct you."
Fig 2 :45: "Frames range against the ground. They do not negotiate with it."

The neoclassical monopoly frame and the collective-voice frame are both published positions with public data anchors. The verdict on each slot is what the data pay, not which camp has more citations. Kochan and Kimball (2019) supply the exact case: "Neither Lewis (1962) nor Freeman and Medoff (1984) were able to adjudicate between these two hypotheses" for the source of the wage premium. That is an open empirical question, not a vote. **Verdict: `triangulated`** across Fig 2 :29, :35, :45. **Mode: `general`** for the rule, `specified` for its application here.

**Refuter:** produce a settlement of a slot by vote, prestige or citation count rather than by a measurement, and show the figures permit it.

### F3 : The two faces are one institution with different bonds, not two substances with a fixed ranking

Fig 12 :123: "functional group. A substructure whose presence fixes the outcome. The group says what kind; the qualifier says which member."
Fig 12 :117: "Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."
Operators table :51: "a goal is a comparison, which is why a vocabulary without it cannot state one"

Freeman and Medoff (1979) define the pair as two views of one institution: the "monopoly" view, in which unions "do little more than raise the wages of their members beyond what they would otherwise be," and the "collective voice/institutional response" view, in which unions provide a voice that "elicits institutional responses which dramatically change the nature of the employment relationship and, in so doing, increases the levels of productivity and equality in many settings." Under Fig 12, the shared substructure is "collective organization of workers applied to the employment relation"; the two faces differ in what is bonded to it (monopoly power versus voice). That is a functional group. Calling them **isomers** requires the stronger claim that both are written over the full same atom set and differ only in arrangement; that is a checkable structural claim, not yet a result (see `06_isomers_and_order.md`). The countervailing-power slot is a ⋛ comparison, not a wage magnitude, so it cannot be ranked on the wage axis. **Verdict: `single-source`** for the functional-group definition (Fig 12) with the world description corroborating; the isomer form is `single-source` plus falsifier. **Mode: `specified`**.

**Refuter:** show one face can be written without an atom the other requires, which kills the isomer form and leaves the functional group standing.

### F4 : Slot directions are real but conditional; none is a universal sign

Fig 2 :29 makes the anchor local to the question; Fig 13 :39 warns that overlapping members are fewer members wearing six names.

- **Wages, D1:** positive. Brändle (2024): "There is strong evidence for a union-non-union wage premium, even for individuals working similar jobs." Beauregard, Lemieux, Messacar and Saggio (2025), as summarized by NBER: "about 60 percent of the union wage premium comes from unions' ability to negotiate higher wages, while the balance is from unionized firms being more productive than their nonunionized counterparts." Mode: the authors' `grounded` finding; reported here as world evidence, not derived by this simulation.
- **Employment, D1:** negative on the growth margin. Brändle (2024): "The union effect on employment is theoretically more ambiguous, but empirically labelled as 'the one constant' among the effects of unions: employment growth is two to four percent lower in firms with union bargaining." Mode: the author's `grounded` synthesis; the same source notes positive effects on employment quality or duration, so the slot is not one scalar either.
- **Productivity, D2:** positive on average, conditional on relations. Kochan and Kimball (2019): "unions can and have had highly variable effects on managerial practices and on organizational performance, depending on the quality of the labor-management relationship." Their three pathways are "restricting labor supply, restrictive work rules, and 'voice'". Mode: the authors' `grounded` finding; the sign is a function of a condition, so no unconditional verdict is licensed.
- **Distribution and power, D3:** a shift from capital to labor. The US Department of Labor literature review (2024) reports of the 300-study meta-analysis by Doucouliagos, Freeman and Laroche (2017): "At the heart of trade union effects on the economy is a shift in income from capital to labor." Kochan and Kimball (2019) add the other side: "Most of the studies on this issue report negative effects" on firm profits. A gain in D3 and a cost to capital are the same event read from two slots (Fig 12 :112, "Progress is a subtraction."). Mode: `grounded` in the cited authors.

The four slot verdicts are kept separate and never averaged. **Verdict on the pattern: `single-source` with respect to evidence pool.** Most of these sources descend from the same Freeman-Medoff literature and cite one another; by Fig 2 :40 their agreement is one pool talking. The organizational-psychology source in F6 is the only genuinely low-overlap witness. **Mode: `specified` for the pattern; the individual directions are the world authors' `grounded` claims.**

**Refuter:** a fully independent evidence pool, not drawing on the Freeman-Medoff lineage, that finds all four signs reversed and survives re-analysis.

### F5 : Heterogeneity is a slot property, not noise

Fig 1 :19 again: "The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated." Dodini, Salvanes, Willén and Zhu (2023): "focusing on a restricted set of outcomes, such as wages and employment, generates a fractionalized understanding of the multidimensional career effect that union membership has on workers." They also find "the career effect of union membership differs greatly depending on the age at which workers enroll." Beauregard et al. (2025) report wage compression that "can create a wage floor, which helps low-skill workers, as well as a ceiling, which can hurt higher-skilled workers."

So even within the wage slot the sign is not one number: it depends on skill and career stage. A scalar wage premium is a lossy condensation of a slot that is itself compound. **Verdict: `triangulated`** between Fig 1 :19 and two independent empirical results (life-cycle evidence and matched employer-employee evidence) that do not share a dataset. **Mode: `specified` for the shape; the results are the authors' `grounded` findings.**

**Refuter:** show that age and skill interactions vanish under a correctly specified single model, so the slot genuinely has one sign.

### F6 : The voice mechanism is corroborated from a different field, but the corroboration is of mechanism, not of net welfare

OSHwiki / EU-OSHA, *Organisational Justice* (Mattila-Holappa, Joensuu, Cox and Winski; published 2012, updated 2017): "The effect or enhancement of this obtained process control, first presented by Folger, has been termed the 'voice effect'; referring to the ability to express one's views. 'Voice' has been found to have an important role in justice perceptions." It also reports a meta-analysis of 190 studies by Cohen-Charash and Spector in which procedural justice had "a stronger association with work performance."

This is a low-overlap witness: organizational psychology and occupational health, not labor economics. It predicts that a voice channel affects work attitudes and performance even holding pay fixed. It corroborates D2's mechanism. It does **not** corroborate a net welfare verdict for unions, because the same page says the evidence "is not strong enough to exclude the possibility that the association is non-causal or confounded." **Verdict: `triangulated` for the mechanism (Fig 12 functional group plus this source), `gap` for any net-welfare claim from this source. Mode: `specified`.**

**Refuter:** a low-overlap replication showing voice has no effect on performance once pay is held fixed.

### F7 : Provenance separates the derived from the imported

Fig 8 :51: "Convergent derivation and adopted import look identical in the contents. Only the record of where each came from separates them"
Fig 9 :27: "describe it before you go looking"

The two-face vocabulary is an **import** from labor economics into the figure frame. It is not derived from Fig 13's six terms and it moves no wall (Fig 6 :21, "MINT composes inside the closure and can never add an atom."). What the figures derive is the shape: a union compound, per-slot residuals, an anchor for each slot, a comparison bond for the power axis. The identification of "wage", "voice" and "income share" as the slots is chosen from the world literature and must be recorded as such. Freeman and Medoff themselves credit Hirschman's *Exit, Voice, and Loyalty*, which is a provenance chain, not a derivation. **Verdict: `triangulated`** between Fig 8 :51 and the actual citation chain in the world sources. **Mode: `general`** for the source-gate rule.

**Refuter:** derive the specific slot list from the six-term set alone with no import, then the provenance caution is unnecessary.

### F8 : A literature that only checks itself drifts together

Fig 2 :40: "agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool"
Fig 7 :13: "agreement compounds, evidence does not"
Fig 6 :32: "An instrument is not built from a description. It is improved from a worse instrument already returning something."

Most cited sources here are labor-economics papers sharing data traditions (PSID, NLS, administrative registers, right-to-work instruments). Their convergence is a feature of one pool. The remedy is not a larger consensus but a differently anchored instrument: an independent administrative dataset, a registration before the analysis, a low-overlap field such as the organizational-psychology source in F6, or a formal review that can refuse the claim. Fig 2 :42: "Buy independence in proportion to the cost of being wrong." **Verdict: `triangulated`** across Fig 2 :40, Fig 7 :13 and the citation pattern observed in the ledger. **Mode: `general`**.

**Refuter:** show the sources' effective sample size is not reduced by correlation, i.e. that the design effect is one (Fig 2 :44 names the standard correction).

## 4. Pre-registered refuter

Required before any outreach, per the discipline that "a falsifier written before the run is a design instrument, not only an honesty device."

The shape claim is: *this question is a union compound whose effects must be read per slot against separate data anchors, and no single net direction is composable from the figure frame.*

It is refuted if any of the following holds:

1. One outcome measure, consistently applied, dominates all other slots with the same sign across all institutional contexts, and no conditional reversal is found.
2. Institutional context (law, product market, labor-management relationship quality) makes no difference to the sign of any slot effect, so the compound is in fact one condition.
3. A fully independent evidence pool reproduces none of the four slot directions, and the original pattern does not survive re-analysis.
4. The figures' per-slot rule is shown to be inapplicable because R here is a genuine single scalar with no object slots.

If any fires, the shape claim is withdrawn. Until one is run, no part of this may be labelled `grounded` by this derivation. Clause 3 was written before the world search, so it could not be tuned to the sources found.

## 5. Recommendation

1. Refuse the either/or in the question as written. Send the per-slot shape, not a verdict (F1).
2. State the mode on every slot: the figures give the structure; the authors give the directions (F2, F4).
3. Label the two faces a functional group; keep isomer as a falsifiable stronger claim (F3).
4. Add the conditions that flip or scale each slot (skill, career stage, relationship quality, law) rather than averaging them away (F5).
5. Record the import: the slot names come from labor economics, not from Fig 13 (F7).
6. Seek one low-overlap witness and one new anchor before any consolidated claim; the organizational-psychology source is a start, not a finish (F6, F8).
7. Fig 4 :22-24: "Only methods cross up. Only head starts come down." Send the method of unbundling and the refuter, not a conclusion about unions.

### Seven-question recursive derivation (summary)

1. **One question or several?** Several, a union compound. Fig 9 :54, :57. `specified`.
2. **Which slots?** Wage, employment, protection, productivity, distribution. Chosen from world sources, imported with provenance, not derived from the six terms.
3. **What anchor per slot?** Data and experiments, not agreement. Fig 2 :29.
4. **Are the two faces isomers?** Not as written. Functional group (Fig 12 :123); stronger isomer form falsifiable in `06`. `single-source`.
5. **Can the figure frame give the net direction?** No. The figures give shape; the authors give directions. Fig 8 :51, Fig 9 :27.
6. **Is more search inside one literature the remedy?** No. Same atoms, same wall, more visited space (Fig 6 :49); need an independent anchor (Fig 2 :42).
7. **What is unreachable at this budget?** A net scalar valid across contexts. "Unreachable is a verdict here, not a silence" (Fig 13 :134). Stop there, record the lineage.

### Updated verdict

The shape survives: unbundle the question, read each slot against its own ground, keep the two faces as a functional group, treat power as a comparison, and refuse any averaged net verdict. The specific claim that either option A or option B is *the* answer does not survive. After re-census: **no hidden strains**; the one live limitation is evidence-pool correlation, which is named in F8 and F4. The empirical directions are the authors' `grounded` reports, not this simulation's results, and no settlement of the question as a whole is claimed.
