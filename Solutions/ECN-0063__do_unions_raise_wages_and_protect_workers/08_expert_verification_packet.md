# Expert verification packet : the Tether on unions, wages and worker protection

**What this is:** a claim in a form a labor economist or industrial-relations scholar can check. Mode, derivation and refuters are stated before the conclusion, so a reader knows what to look for and what would settle it.

**Mode: `specified` : not `grounded` by this derivation.** No new measurement is claimed. This is a shape and a route, not a result. The empirical directions quoted here are the published findings of the named authors and are attributed to them.

## 1. The claim, in one paragraph

The question "do unions raise wages and protect workers at the cost of employment, or improve productivity and counterbalance employer power?" is a union compound presented as a single choice. The figure frame returns a shape: unbundle the question into slots, give each slot its own data anchor, keep the monopoly and voice faces as a functional group, read "at the cost of employment" as a subtraction inside the wage slot, and read countervailing power as a comparison bond. It does not return a net verdict, because no such scalar is composable from the frame and the available evidence pool is correlated. This packet asks for a check of that shape, not an endorsement of a position on unions.

## 2. Three checkable sub-claims

### C1 : framework-internal plus empirical; checkable by reading Fig 1 and Fig 9

- **Statement:** the question is a union compound and the residual must be read per slot, never as a net scalar.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 9 :54 : "And when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again." Fig 9 :57 : "a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either." Fig 1 :19 : "The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated." Fig 1 :22 : "Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average."
- **What to check:** are wage level, employment, voice, productivity and power genuinely distinct measured outcomes with distinct anchors, or are they one outcome under different names? If they are one, the union reading fails.
- **Refuter:** one outcome measure, consistently applied, dominates all other slots with the same sign in every institutional context, with no conditional reversal.

### C2 : labor-economics-facing; checkable against the published record

- **Statement:** the two faces are a functional group, not demonstrated isomers; the slot signs are conditional; the subtraction in "at the cost of employment" is local to the wage and employment slots.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 12 :123 : "functional group. A substructure whose presence fixes the outcome. The group says what kind; the qualifier says which member." Fig 12 :117 : "Same atoms, different bond, different substance." Fig 12 :112 : "A plan is a sequence. A goal is a comparison. Progress is a subtraction."
- **What to check:** can either face be written without an atom the other requires? Does the sign of the productivity effect really depend on relationship quality as Kochan and Kimball (2019) report? Is the employment effect "the one constant" at two to four percent lower growth as Brändle (2024) reports? Does a single wage premium hide a floor for low-skill workers and a ceiling for high-skill workers as the NBER Digest (2025) reports?
- **Refuter:** either face can be written without an atom the other requires (kills the isomer form, leaves the functional group); or the conditional effects vanish under a correctly specified single model, so the slots have one sign.

### C3 : framework-internal; checkable by reading the Operators table and Fig 2

- **Statement:** countervailing power is a ⋛ comparison, not a wage magnitude; a settlement of any slot requires a data anchor, not agreement.
- **Source, verbatim:** Operators table :48 : "a threshold relation between two quantities"; Operators table :51 : "a goal is a comparison, which is why a vocabulary without it cannot state one." Fig 2 :29 : "An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks. A measurement, an experiment, a counter, a market, a patient outcome." Fig 2 :35 : "Alignment is triangulation, not negotiation. A mirror cannot correct you."
- **What to check:** are the operands of the power comparison quantities at the economy scale? Is the comparison well typed at the workplace scale, where "voice" is a mechanism rather than a quantity? This is the live type strain, named rather than hidden in `06_isomers_and_order.md`.
- **Refuter:** show the power slot is a version of the wage slot, so the comparison is redundant; or show a vote or prestige ranking settled a slot without a measurement and that the figures permit it.

## 3. What is explicitly not claimed

- Not that unions are net good or net bad. No net scalar is composable here.
- Not that this derivation ran a study. It is a simulated derivation checked for internal consistency against the figures.
- Not that the two faces are isomers. That is a falsifiable stronger claim, not a result.
- Not that the monopoly face is the only cost, or the voice face the only benefit.
- Not that agreement among economists, a citation count or institutional prestige settles any slot (Fig 2 :35; Fig 7 :35).
- Not that the six figure terms derived the slot list. The slot names are provenanced imports from labor economics and organizational psychology (Fig 8 :51).
- Not `grounded`. The net-scalar answer is unreachable from this frame at this budget.

## 5. What would settle it

A source-discriminating instrument or a new anchor, not a further review inside the same literature pool.

1. An instrument that discriminates rent extraction from productivity selection within the wage premium beyond the decomposition already published.
2. A formal test of the isomer form: write both faces over the same atom set and look for the missing atom the falsifier names.
3. A well-typed quantity for each operand of ⋛ at the workplace scale, or a withdrawal of the comparison there.
4. One independent replication of a slot with a new dataset and a pre-registered analysis.
5. Firing of any of the four refuter clauses in `01` section 4.

## 6. What an expert can check in twenty minutes

1. Read Fig 1 :19, :22; Fig 9 :54, :57; Fig 12 :112, :117, :123; Operators table :48, :51. Eight short passages.
2. Name one empirical outcome in this literature that is genuinely one slot with one anchor across all contexts, or say why none exists. That is C1.
