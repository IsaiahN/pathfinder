**Mode: `specified`, not `grounded` by this derivation.**

**Status: SIMULATED DERIVATION, not a ground reading.** This WATSON run synthesizes material already in `01_figure_grounded_audit.md`, `05_what_shape_means.md`, `06_isomers_and_order.md`, `07_the_tether_answer.md` and `08_expert_verification_packet.md`. No experiment or dataset was run here.

## QUESTION

Decide, in effect terms, what the Tether says about the question: "Do unions raise wages and protect workers at the cost of employment, or improve productivity and counterbalance employer power?" Option A: unions help on wages and protection but hurt employment. Option B: unions help productivity and counterbalance capital. What each leads to: A and B each select one half of a union compound and present it as the answer; the figure-grounded shape refuses both as scalars and returns a per-slot structure with separate anchors, a functional-group reading of the two faces, a local subtraction for the employment cost, a comparison bond for power, and a pre-registered refuter.

## DOMAIN CENSUS

R by arity, symmetry and scale, then one line per domain giving actors, currency and anchor; partition or union; order by dependency.

- **R (the residual).** The gap between the question's either/or framing and the four measured outcome slots. Arity: at least four conditions (wage and employment; voice and productivity; distribution and power; conditional modifiers). Symmetry: the union/employer relation is an ordered comparison, not a symmetric conjunction; the two faces are not interchangeable. Scale: bargaining unit and firm for D1 and D2, industry and economy for D3. Fig 9 names this a union compound, not one hard question: "a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either."
- **Domain 1, wage setting and employment.** Actors: workers, union as seller of labor, employer, would-be entrants. Currency: wage rate, headcount, hours. Anchor: wage and employment data with quasi-experimental designs. Compound member, first-order in dependency.
- **Domain 2, voice and productivity.** Actors: workers, grievance procedure, management, work organization. Currency: quits, tenure, value added per worker, information flow. Anchor: workplace and firm data. Compound member, first-order in dependency, parallel to D1 rather than caused by it.
- **Domain 3, distribution and countervailing power.** Actors: labor, capital, union, state and labor law. Currency: wage compression, income share, political voice. Anchor: wage-share data and legal records. Compound member, second-order, composing over D1 and D2.
- **Domain 4, measurement and verification.** Actors: researchers, meta-analysts, reviewers. Currency: estimates and residuals. Anchor: the empirical ground. In this simulated run all four domains draw on one literature pool, so their agreement is one pool talking, not independent corroboration.
- **Order by dependency.** Resolve D1 and D2 before D3 (Fig 3 :20); unbundle before searching (Fig 9 :54); describe each slot before choosing a frame (Fig 9 :27); bring a characterised residual, never the source (Fig 10 :52).

Strain test applied: Fig 9's union ruling governs the *question's structure*; Fig 13's closed-set ruling governs *what a six-term vocabulary can compose*. Different objects, so no strain after re-census (see `01` section 6). One live strain remains: ⋛ is defined for two quantities, and "voice" at the workplace scale is a mechanism, so the power comparison is well typed only at the economy scale (see `06` Q4).

## VERDICT (from the figures)

The shape survives; the either/or does not. The question is a union compound and must be answered per slot. The two faces are a functional group, with the isomer form left as a falsifiable stronger claim. "At the cost of employment" is a subtraction inside the wage and employment slot, with a negative growth effect reported by Brändle (2024). Countervailing power is a ⋛ comparison between shares, not a point on the wage scale, and its workplace-scale operand type is a live strain. Slot directions are conditional and come from the authors' data, not from the figures. No net scalar is composable from this frame, so both Option A and Option B are refused as written. This is a `specified`, ungrounded result; the empirical directions are the world authors' `grounded` reports, not this simulation's.

- **cost if wrong:** bounded and locatable. The packet states mode, slot definitions and refuters before the conclusion, so an error is findable rather than invisible. The real cost is spending the independence of outside readers by sending a verdict instead of a shape, which Fig 2 :42 prices ("Buy independence in proportion to the cost of being wrong."). That cost is avoidable by leading with the slots and the refuter. A second cost is political: a false net scalar could be used to license a policy that ignores the slot it hurts.
- **undoable:** yes. The net-scalar framing is removable without touching the figure derivation; `07_the_tether_answer.md` and `08_expert_verification_packet.md` already remove it. No primitive has to be withdrawn, and a sent shape can be superseded by a corrected one. The one thing not undoable is a reader's independence once they have absorbed the framework before giving a verdict (Fig 10 :42), which is why the ordering matters.
- **reversed by:** a ground reading, not agreement. A source-discriminating instrument that separates rent extraction from productivity selection would sharpen D1; an independent dataset with a pre-registered analysis could reverse a slot direction; a demonstration that one outcome dominates all slots with one sign in every context would fire refuter clause 1 and collapse the compound; a demonstration that the two faces share the full atom set would promote the functional group to isomers; a typed quantity for both ⋛ operands at the workplace scale would clear the strain. Until one of these fires, the verdict stands as `candidate`.
