## Three things in the literature that bear directly on the union question

1. **The two faces are a cataloguing device, not a settled model.** IZA Discussion Paper 892 (Hirsch, 2003) writes that by 1984 the "two faces of unions" dichotomy "had become a standard framework or cataloguing device" and calls the net productivity effect "an empirical question." The same paper notes the categories are "neither distinct nor without ambiguity." That is exactly the Fig 8 :51 provenance warning: the face labels are adopted imports, not derived primitives. Content read via search integration; direct PDF fetch was not attempted, so treat this as a lower-confidence citation.
2. **The decomposition of the wage premium splits the residual into slots.** The NBER Digest reports that 60 percent of the premium is rent extraction and 40 percent is firm productivity selection, and that wage compression "can create a wage floor, which helps low-skill workers, as well as a ceiling, which can hurt higher-skilled workers." This is Fig 1 :19 in empirical clothing: the aggregate premium hides opposing slot movements.
3. **The voice effect is real but conditional, and it is attested in another field.** Kochan and Kimball (2019) find wide variation by labor-management relationship quality; the OSHwiki article reports the "voice effect" from organizational psychology and adds that the causal evidence is not strong enough to exclude confounding. The low-overlap witness corroborates the mechanism, not the net verdict.

## The two-stage offer (and its optional third stage)

1. **Stage 1:** validate the unbundling against the reader's own knowledge of labor markets. No theory shown. The probe is: do the four slots have different anchors, and can one sign cover all four?
2. **Stage 2, opt-in:** the sources. The figures are the authority. The reader's stage-1 verdict is recorded before this stage, and any later agreement is never pooled as corroboration (Fig 2 :40).
3. **Stage 3:** offer the unbundling method for another problem, with the instruction to name a ground and write a refuter before running it. The framework's own demo caveat says outputs "could never be verified if the solutions presented were not contrived to fit the formula," so each user must check the result against something that can refuse it.

**Hard precondition:** stages 1 to 3 only work if the stage-1 artifact survives. It does so only as a shape claim. If the email is tightened into "unions are net good" or "unions are net bad," the refuter no longer covers it and the offer should not be sent.
