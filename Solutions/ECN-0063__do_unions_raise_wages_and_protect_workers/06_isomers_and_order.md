### Q2 : Functional group or isomer?

- Fig 12 :123: "functional group. A substructure whose presence fixes the outcome. The group says what kind; the qualifier says which member."
- Fig 12 :117: "isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."
- Fig 12 :97 to :98 gives the chemistry case: "silver fulminate and silver cyanate have the same composition and different properties, which is what forced structure into the notation" (Liebig and Wöhler, 1824 to 1826; named isomerism by Berzelius, 1830).
- The shared substructure of the two faces is: collective organization of workers applied to the employment relation. Freeman and Medoff (1979) write that the "monopoly" view holds unions raise wages beyond what they otherwise would be, while the "collective voice/institutional response" view holds that voice "elicits institutional responses which dramatically change the nature of the employment relationship."
- As the faces are normally written, they differ in the **bond** to that substructure: monopoly power (a threshold comparison of bargaining strength) versus voice (a channel that changes information flow and quits). Same institution, different bond, different outcome. That satisfies the functional-group definition and the *form* of the isomer definition.
- The stronger isomer claim requires the **same full atom set** on both sides. The monopoly face needs wage level, employment and employer rent; the voice face needs quits, tenure, information flow and relationship quality. These sets are not identical. So as currently written they are **not isomers** under Fig 12 :117; they are two members of a functional group.
- **The checkable stronger form:** if both faces are rewritten over the full shared atom set {wage, employment, quits, tenure, productivity, income share, legal context} and differ only in which bond mediates which relation, then they are isomers. That is the claim worth testing. **Mode: `specified`.**

**Falsifier, written before the run:** if either face can be written without an atom the other requires, for example a voice face with no wage or employment term, or a monopoly face with no information-flow term, the isomer claim fails and the functional-group reading stands. This was written before the world search and could not be tuned to the sources found.

### Q3 : What is the order of the compound?

- Fig 3 :20: "The chain is ordered by dependency, not by importance. Each link consumes what the one above produces, so effort spent above the break arrives at a step that cannot use it."
- Fig 3 :18: "Where a given chain stops is a reading, never a property of the chain."
- The four slots are **not** a chain. Wage and employment are first-order and within-unit; voice and productivity are first-order and within-workplace; distribution and power are second-order in the sense that they compose over the first-order results. Fig 12 :104: "A settled molecule becomes an atom for whatever composes over it. So irreducible is a fact about a level and never about the world, and there is no bottom"
- So the dependency order is: (1) wage and employment resolved together against the same data anchor; (2) voice and quits resolved against workplace data; (3) distribution and power read over both. This is a **union with internal dependency**, not a sequence of four causal links. Writing `wage → employment → voice → productivity → power` would import a → sequence (Fig 12 :70, "A then B; B needs A to have happened") that the figure does not license.
- The one genuine ordered bond inside a slot is the subtraction: "at the cost of employment" is wage gain minus employment, with a witness in Brändle (2024) that "employment growth is two to four percent lower in firms with union bargaining." Fig 12 :112: "Progress is a subtraction." This subtraction is within D1, not across the whole compound.

### Q4 : What kind of bond is "counterbalance employer power"?

- Operators table :47 to :51: ⋛, comparison, "a threshold relation between two quantities," with the test "is it about which is larger, rather than both being present?"
- Operators table :51: "a goal is a comparison, which is why a vocabulary without it cannot state one"
- DOL (2024) reports the meta-analysis conclusion: "At the heart of trade union effects on the economy is a shift in income from capital to labor." Kochan and Kimball (2019) report that "Most of the studies on this issue report negative effects" on firm profits.
- So the power slot is a comparison between labor's and capital's shares, with a threshold (who gets what share), not a larger value on the wage axis. Placing it on the wage axis is a type error: it would treat a bond as a quantity.
- **Type caveat, still open:** ⋛ is defined for "two quantities." Income shares are quantities, so the bond is well typed at the economy scale. At the workplace scale, "voice" is a mechanism, not a quantity, so a ⋛ between voice and authority is not well typed without further specification. This needs an instrument, not an argument. Recorded as a strain, not hidden. **Verdict: `single-source`** for the bond definition (Operators table), with the world side supplying the quantities.

## CONCLUSION

The two faces are **not demonstrated isomers** as written; they are a functional group sharing the substructure of collective worker organization. The stronger isomer claim is real, checkable and carries a falsifier. The compound is a **union with internal dependency**, not a chain: wage and employment first, voice and productivity in parallel, distribution and power second-order over both. The only licensed ordered bond is the within-slot subtraction that makes "at the cost of employment" meaningful. Countervailing power is a ⋛ comparison, not a wage magnitude; at the workplace scale its operand type is a live strain.

This framing keeps what is defensible in the two-faces literature without turning it into a figure-derived verdict. No new primitive is owed and no import is claimed to move the wall (Fig 6 :21, "MINT composes inside the closure and can never add an atom.").
