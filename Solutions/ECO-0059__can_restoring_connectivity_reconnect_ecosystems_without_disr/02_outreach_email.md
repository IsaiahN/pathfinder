# Outreach draft : check the shape before you decide about the framework

**Use:** one to one, or as a cover note attached to a submission through the public venues in `03_public_contact_points.csv`. **Not** for bulk send.

**Hard precondition:** do not send until the mode labels and refuters in `01_figure_grounded_audit.md` and `08_expert_verification_packet.md` are intact. The output must survive a twenty-minute check or the two-stage offer collapses on contact.

**Subject:** One checkable claim about corridor restoration, before any theory

> Hi [name],
>
> I have one specific output I would like you to check against what you already know about connectivity restoration. I am not asking you to read the framework first, and I am not asking you to agree with it. If the output does not survive, there is nothing else to look at.
>
> **The residual, in effect terms (`specified`, not `grounded`).** A manager adds a link to a fragmented habitat. Two things are being asked of the same link: it should restore movement, gene flow and persistence for the target biota, and it should leave the established local communities intact. Those are two conditions on one habitat, not one hard question. The same link that carries a target species out also carries whatever else is in the matrix in.
>
> **The shape claim.** Three things follow from the figures before any species or site is named. First, the two conditions are a union, so they need separate measurements and a single landscape score will not decide them. Second, the manager can add a connector but cannot subtract the resident community, so this is permanent work, not a solved state. Third, the sign of the connector is relative to the residual: the same corridor is a benefactor to the target and a possible malfactor to the residents, and the effect that decides the outcome can be two or three steps out.
>
> **What I am not claiming.** I am not claiming that corridors always harm residents, and not claiming they never do. I am not naming a species, a corridor width or a site. The figures return a shape, not a name. Which connector works where is a declared `gap`, and I record it as such rather than filling it with a favourite example.
>
> **What would refute the shape claim (written before this run):**
> 1. one lever demonstrably closes both residuals at the same sites with no categorically different residue;
> 2. an operator that removes an incumbent actor from a habitat without substituting another habitat;
> 3. a connector that keeps the same sign for every residual across documented changes of species and community;
> 4. restoration cases where the decisive non-disruption effect is always first-order and no second- or third-order effect changes a verdict;
> 5. a measurement that settles a corridor outcome once and stays valid while habitat, climate and community measurably change.
>
> These are written down before the run. If any holds, I withdraw the corresponding claim. A one-line "this fails because..." is more useful to me than agreement. If you would rather not engage, no reply is needed.
>
> **What I would count as a ground test:** paired before/after measurements of target movement and resident community structure at the same sites, with detection of predators, pathogens and invaders along the link, and a stated threshold at which the design would be reversed. If that already exists for your system, tell me where it fails.
>
> **If it survives, and only then:** the sources are below. The figures, not my summary, are the authority.
> - Verbatim figure text with line numbers (local extract): `FRAMEWORK/reference/03-FIGURES_TEXT.md`
> - Habitat and cascade figure, Fig 11, lines 43 to 70 in that extract.
> - The formula, including the habitat paragraph: `FRAMEWORK/figures/THE_FORMULA.md`
>
> **If you want to use the machinery:** run it on a problem you know well and can check against a ground. Write down what would refute your result *before* you run it, and state whether what you got is `general`, `specified` or `grounded`. A reported failure is more useful to me than a reported success.
>
> : [name]

## Why this order

| the move | figure-grounded reason |
|---|---|
| Shape claim and refuter first, theory withheld | Fig 10 :52 *"Bring a characterised residual, never the source."* The recipient checks without holding the framework's interior. |
| No request for agreement | Fig 2 :35 *"Alignment is triangulation, not negotiation. A mirror cannot correct you."* |
| Two conditions stated separately | Fig 9 :54 *"And when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again."* |
| Pre-registered refuter | A falsifier written before the run is a design instrument, not an honesty device. |
| Mode stated on every substantive claim | *"Mode. general · specified · grounded. State which."* (`THE_FORMULA.md`). |
| No species or site named | Fig 9 :32 *"you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself"*; the framework returns a shape, not a name. |
| Links only after a verdict | Fig 2 :40 *"disqualifying for a verdict: agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool"*; a later agreement is never pooled as corroboration. |
| Ask for a ground test, not trust | Fig 2 :45 *"Frames range against the ground. They do not negotiate with it."* |
| Stake declared | Fig 10 :60 *"Where a stake cannot be removed, declare it. An undeclared interest is modelled anyway and an unnamed model runs unchallenged."* A manager or author has an interest in the outcome; only the reading must be disinterested. |
| Offer the formula as a method | Fig 4 :22-25: only methods cross up, only head starts come down. Giving away the generator does not give away an answer. |
