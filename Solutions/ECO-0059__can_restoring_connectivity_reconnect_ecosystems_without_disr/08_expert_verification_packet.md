# Expert verification packet : the Tether on connectivity restoration

**What this is:** a claim in a form a restoration ecologist, landscape geneticist or disease ecologist can check. Mode, derivation and refuters are stated before the conclusion, so a reader knows what to look for and what would settle it.

**Mode: `specified` for the substantive claims, `general` for the structural shape. Not `grounded`.** No site measurement is claimed. This is a shape and a route, not a result. It is a simulated derivation checked for internal consistency against the figures, not a ground reading.

## 1. The claim, in one paragraph

Reconnecting a fragmented habitat while leaving established local communities intact is a union of two slot residuals on one habitat, not one hard question. Restoration is an introduction into a habitat the manager did not author: a link can be added and the residents cannot be subtracted, so this is permanent work rather than a solved state. The same link has valence relative to the residual, so it can be a benefactor to the target and a malfactor to the residents, and the decisive effect can be two or three steps out. The ground, not agreement, settles each residual, and "without disruption" must be bound as a measurable term before it can be checked. Which connector works where is a declared `gap`.

## 2. Three checkable sub-claims

### C1 : Framework-internal; checkable by reading Figure 9 and Figure 1

- **Statement:** the two conditions form a union and must be measured per slot, never as one landscape score.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 9 :54 *"And when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again."* Fig 9 :57 *"What remains is smaller and it is also categorically different: a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either."* Fig 1 :19 *"The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated."* Fig 1 :22 *"Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average."*
- **Refuter:** one intervention demonstrably closes both residuals at the same sites, with the same actors, and leaves a residue that is not categorically different from either condition alone.

### C2 : Framework-internal with an ecology-facing check; Figure 11

- **Statement:** restoration is introduction under asymmetric power, with residual-relative valence and a cascade that reaches beyond first order.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 11 :59 *"The seat's power is asymmetric. Introduction is available; exclusion is not. You can add an actor and you cannot subtract the habitat, because contact is a fact about the world and does not consult you."* Fig 11 :49 *"An actor's valence is relative to the residual, not a property it carries."* Fig 11 :52 *"And contact cascades. Scoping the habitat to direct contact is how DDT reached the eagles: first-order enumeration, second-order effect, and the residual that mattered was three steps out."* Fig 11 :60 *"Which is why a managed ecosystem is permanent work rather than a solved state."*
- **What to check against the world:** is there a documented restoration where the resident residual was settled once by a first-order measurement and no second- or third-order effect ever changed a verdict? The Elwha dam removal case reported by Leeds 2025 is a candidate counterexample to a first-only reading: native fish recovered and invasive plants increased through the same new connection.
- **Refuter:** any one of (a) an operator that removes an incumbent actor without substituting a habitat, (b) a connector with invariant sign across documented changes of residual, (c) consistent first-order-only determination across restoration cases, or (d) an outcome settled once and still valid after habitat, climate and community measurably change.

## 3. What is explicitly not claimed

- Not that corridors always harm residents, and not that they never do. The sign is relative to the residual.
- Not a species, a corridor width, a site or a monitoring threshold.
- Not a single landscape connectivity score, and not an average across species or years.
- Not that the resident community can be removed or excluded to make room for a target.
- Not a claim that human communities and ecological communities are the same residual. The human reading (D5) changes the anchor and is kept separate.
- Not `grounded`. Nothing here is asserted as settled by experiment.

## 5. What would settle it

A paired-site, source-discriminating measurement, not a further argument.

1. Do target movement and resident community structure change together or independently at the same sites, with pathogen and invader detection along the link?
2. Which second- or third-order residual actually changes a management verdict, and how far from the link does it sit?
3. Was the non-disruption threshold stated before construction, and was the design actually reversed when it fired?

## 6. What an expert can check in twenty minutes

1. Read Fig 9 :54, :57 and Fig 11 :49, :52, :59, :60. Six short passages.
2. Check C2 against one restoration case you know: was the decisive residual first, second or third order?
3. Name one instrument that would discriminate target movement from antagonist spread at the same site, or say why none is currently possible.
4. Send the refutation. A failure is more useful than an endorsement.
