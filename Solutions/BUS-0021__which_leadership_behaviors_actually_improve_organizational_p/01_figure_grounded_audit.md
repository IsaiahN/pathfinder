## 0. The claim under audit and the residual in effect terms

> Which leadership behaviors actually improve organizational performance, and which merely correlate with it?

The question as posed asks for a standing list: behaviors on the "improves" side and behaviors on the "correlates" side. The audit first states what is being decided in effect terms, before any figure line is chosen.

Let s be an organizational slot (a team, a store, a business unit, a firm at a time). Let Γ be the leadership research frame's library: its constructs, its questionnaires, its rater expectations, its accumulated correlations. Let b be what the frame believes before acting and a the action or behavior it attributes. Γ(b,a) is the predicted performance; o' is what the world returns. R = |Γ(b,a) - o'| is a residual indexed by slot (Fig 1 :19), not a global average (Fig 1 :22).

- The "improves" side asserts an ordered relation: intervening on a changes o'. In the bond vocabulary this is production (⇒) or sequence (→), both ordered (Fig 12 :112-113: *"A plan is a sequence. A goal is a comparison. Progress is a subtraction."*).
- The "merely correlates" side asserts a symmetric co-variation: r(a, o') = r(o', a). A symmetric relation cannot choose which side is the cause. Fig 2 :39 calls out the valence explicitly: *"Correlation has valence."*
- Scale: the comparison must be made per slot. Averaging across firms is exactly how the signal that says where to look disappears (Fig 1 :22).
- Symmetry: this is not a symmetric question. Correlation cannot be promoted to improvement by accumulating more of itself; that is Fig 2's common-cause collapse, not a second witness.

What an answer would look like, written down and bound before the run (Formula step, "Direction"): a shape with two conditions, (i) the behavior reading and the outcome reading come from independent sources, and (ii) the outcome is an anchor that does not move when the research frame changes its mind. Plus an instrument that returns a residual when those conditions fail. The shape is bound as an ordinary term so it can accrue a residual and be refused like anything else.

## 1. Domain census, before any figure line is chosen

Describe R by arity, symmetry and scale, then one line per domain with actors, currency and anchor. A domain with no nameable anchor is not a domain yet.

**R (arity / symmetry / scale).** Arity: two conditions, because leadership behavior and organizational performance are joint products of a habitat, not two independent variables (a case in two conditions, Fig 9). Symmetry: the observed relation is symmetric (correlation), the asserted relation is ordered (improvement), so the residual is the unchosen direction. Scale: per slot and per time, never pooled across organizations (Fig 1 :19).

| domain | actors | currency | anchor | partition / union | status |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| D1, leadership research frame | researchers, scale designers, leaders, subordinate raters, editors | correlations, effect sizes, publications, citations | none internal; an anchor must be imported | this is the frame that mints the "behaviors" list; it is frame-internal | domain, anchor imported only |
| D2, organizational habitat | leader, followers, task, incentives, market, institutions, time | performance outcomes, survival, resources | hard outcomes that do not update on what researchers think | union with D1 and D3; the behavior's valence is relative to this slot's residual | domain, anchor present |
| D3, identification / causal inference frame | experiments, natural experiments, instruments, panel designs, auditors | counterfactual contrast P(y\|do(x)) against P(y\|x) | the ground, that is manipulability of the behavior | low overlap with D1 when it uses administrative or randomized data rather than the same rating scale | domain, anchor present |

**Partition or union.** Union compound, not partition. The question bundles three conditions: (i) does changing the behavior change the outcome, (ii) are the two readings independent, and (iii) which residual in which habitat. A single lever, one more correlational study, clears only the region where one condition holds and leaves the intersections standing (Fig 9 :54). It is not a list of separate questions. Keep it as one compound with its own loop.

**Order by dependency (Fig 3).** Describe the residual before choosing a frame (Fig 9 :27-28). Settle the measurement link (are behavior and outcome read independently?) before asking which behavior is best; optimizing a link that already works cannot rescue one that does not (Fig 3 :19). A link with no instrument is not a link that works (Fig 3 :27).

## 3. Findings

### F1 - The question asks for a standing list, and the habitat figure forbids it (decisive for the literal wording)

Fig 11 :49-51:

> **"An actor's valence is relative to the residual, not a property it carries."**
> **"The same actor is a benefactor to one residual and a malfactor to another, so mutualism and parasitism are relations rather than kinds."**
> **"No standing list of good actors survives a change of residual."**

A leadership behavior is an actor in the habitat. Its valence (helps, hurts, does nothing) is relative to the residual, meaning the performance problem in that slot at that time. A behavior that clears a coordination failure in one unit can be a malfactor in a unit whose binding constraint is something else. The question's phrase "which leadership behaviors actually improve organizational performance" presupposes a context-free list. That presupposition is exactly what Fig 11 :51 refuses.

This is not an analogy imported from ecology. Fig 11 :52-54 gives the reason with a named case: *"Scoping the habitat to direct contact is how DDT reached the eagles: first-order enumeration, second-order effect, and the residual that mattered was three steps out."* A behavior rated helpful at first order can fail at third order. Mode: `specified`, habitat domain. Refuter: a behavior with a stable positive effect on a hard outcome across habitats whose residuals genuinely differ, measured independently. If one is found, Fig 11 :51 is narrowed and the standing list is back on the table. Triangulated with the animal-behaviour frame below (low overlap with management research): in mammalian societies, *"Leadership styles and, by extension, who is most likely to occupy a leadership role within a society, are often context dependent and variable over time"* (Leadership and Status in Mammalian Societies, 2020), and in pigeon flocks *"dominance and leadership hierarchies are completely independent of each other"* (Context-dependent hierarchies in pigeons, PNAS 2013). Two frames that share no management evidence pool reach the same context-dependence shape.

### F2 - Correlation cannot choose direction, and a shared-source correlation is disqualified as a verdict

Fig 2 :39-40, :35, :31:

> **"Correlation has valence."**
> **"disqualifying for a verdict: agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool"**
> **"Alignment is triangulation, not negotiation. A mirror cannot correct you."**
> **"Independence, between two frames, is how little they draw on the same evidence."**

Most leadership-performance correlations read the behavior and the performance from the same rater or the same survey at the same moment. That is Fig 2's panel 2 (one evidence pool) or panel 1 (mutual update), not panel 3 (triangulation against a pinned anchor). Agreement inside the pool is the pool talking. It cannot settle "improves" any more than a mirror can correct a face.

World corroboration, low overlap with the figures: *"the inability to legitimately deduce a cause-and-effect relationship between two events or variables solely on the basis of an observed association or correlation between them"* (Correlation does not imply causation, Wikipedia, 2026), and Antonakis, Bendahan, Jacquart and Lalive (2010, The Leadership Quarterly) on common-method variance and endogeneity: *"researchers fail to address at least 66% and up to 90% of design and estimation conditions that make causal claims invalid."* Mode: `specified`, research-frame domain. Refuter: two frames with genuinely independent outcome sources and independent behavior measurements converging on the same behavior in the same slot, with the provenance of each recorded. Triangulated (Fig 2 + statistics frame + management methods review).

### F3 - The metrics that usually rank the behaviors are frame-internal

Fig 1 :19, :22, :23, :27:

> **"The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated."**
> **"Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average."**
> **"The ground enters as the only metric."**
> **"They are frame-internal. A frame cannot score itself with a quantity it also produces."**

Fig 1 :24-26 names the disqualified quantities: coverage, compression achieved, predicates minted, credibility accrued. In this field the analogues are the number of studies, the pooled effect size, the number of citations, and the rater's belief that the leader is effective. Judge and Piccolo (2004, Journal of Applied Psychology, 89(5):755-768) report that across 626 correlations from 87 sources transformational leadership had an overall validity of .44, yet *"transformational leadership failed to predict leader job performance"*; the same meta-analysis finds *"transformational leadership was strongly correlated with contingent reward (.80)"*, so the two scales are partly the same reading. A 2022 evidence-based primer reports transformational leadership behaviours *"accounted for 8.4% of the variance in leader effectiveness"* and about 7 percent of variance in employee job performance. These numbers are useful as a budget filter (which behaviors to test first) and useless as a verdict (which behaviors work). Mode: `general` on the disqualification, `specified` on the specific numbers, research-frame domain. Refuter: an effect-size metric that the research frame does not itself produce, for example a hard outcome registered before the behavior was measured. Triangulated.

### F4 - The broken link is the measurement link, and most of the literature has no instrument on it

Fig 3 :19, :27, :29:

> **"Optimising a link that already works cannot rescue one that does not."**
> **"A link with no instrument is not a link that works. It is a link nobody has looked at."**
> **"Before asking whether a system is good, ask which link it currently stops at, and whether the answer was measured or assumed."**

Fig 3's chain runs perception, vocabulary, objective, planning, learn and carry. This field has excellent instruments for perception (leadership scales, rater surveys) and for reporting (meta-analysis). The objective link, being able to say what would count as progress in its own terms with a reading independent of the perception link, is the one that usually stops the chain. The 2025 cross-cultural meta-analysis of 519 studies and 121,385 employees states plainly: *"the majority of articles in this meta-analysis (i.e. 83%) used a cross-sectional design"* and *"we were only able to locate a single experimental study among working populations"*. Adding more cross-sectional studies improves a link that already works. Mode: `specified`, identification domain. Refuter: a representative sample in which the objective link is independently instrumented and the pooled estimate changes materially. Triangulated (Fig 3 + methods review + the 2025 meta-analysis's own admission).

### F5 - The anchor must not move when the frame thinks

Fig 2 :29-30 and Fig 10 :24, :93:

> **"An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks."**
> **"the ground does not decay, the channel does"**
> **"A ground with a reputation to protect is a ground someone benefits from, and its keeper is thereby disqualified."**

If the "performance" in the correlation is a subordinate's rating of the leader, the rating moves when the subordinate knows the leader's reputation, when the survey framing changes, and when the organization wants a flattering result. That is not an anchor; it is a channel that has drifted. Antonakis et al. (2010) list *"Common-method variance"* among the threats that render estimates causally uninterpretable, and call the randomized experiment the gold standard. Fig 10 :59 adds the honest constraint on any expert consulted here: *"an interest in the outcome is unavoidable, an interest in the reading is disqualifying."* A leadership researcher has an unavoidable interest in whether leadership matters; that interest must be declared and must not be allowed to author the reading. Mode: `specified`, habitat and identification domains. Refuter: a rating source that is demonstrably invariant to knowledge of the hypothesis and to the leader's reputation, cross-checked against a hard outcome. Triangulated.

### F6 - Reaching an instrument is an import with a source gate, not more search inside the same frame

Fig 6 :23, :34 and Fig 8 :32, :39, :53:

> **"No frame escapes its own closure. The bound moves only on contact with a frame whose closure differs."**
> **"The question is not whether a sensor could exist. It is whether anything, at any resolution, is already returning something that fails to resolve."**
> **"Closure is idempotent. Composing inside a closed set never leaves it, so no search, however long, adds an atom."**
> **"So a search that finds nothing is never a proof of absence."**
> **"Residual first, frame second."**

The leadership frame's closure contains questionnaires, correlations and effect sizes. No amount of composition inside that closure yields an exogenous variation. The import comes from the identification frame (experiments, instrumental variables, regression discontinuity, difference in differences), which already returns something at this problem: Antonakis et al. (2010) review six such methods explicitly for leadership research. The import must carry a source gate (provenance) and a shadow test (it must explain the shared-source residual). It is debited against independence, so the identification frame must not simply adopt the leadership frame's outcome measure. World corroboration from experimental design: a randomized controlled trial uses *"random allocation of participants to one or more comparison groups"* and reduces selection bias and confounding (Randomized controlled trial, Wikipedia, 2026); confounding *"is a causal concept rather than a purely statistical one, and therefore cannot be fully described by correlations or associations alone"* (Confounding, Wikipedia, 2026). Mode: `general` on the import rule, `specified` on the methods, identification domain. Refuter: a derivation of a valid counterfactual contrast from the leadership frame's own instruments alone. Triangulated.

### F7 - A literature of agreeing correlations is one long room

Fig 7 :13, :35, :36:

> **"same closure, handed along; agreement compounds, evidence does not"**
> **"A room's agreement is variation, not a verdict. Selection between rooms is where the ground votes."**
> **"A chain of mirrors is one long room. The next room must differ from the last, and match the residual it surfaced."**

A meta-analysis that pools 519 cross-sectional studies of the same questionnaire has added vocabulary and not an anchor. This is not an objection to meta-analysis as a filter; it is the reason a pooled r cannot be promoted to a causal verdict by pooling more of the same. Fig 8 :50-52 supplies the provenance test: *"Convergent derivation and adopted import look identical in the contents. Only the record of where each came from separates them, and the surplus lives only where the closures differ."* Mode: `general`, research-frame domain. Refuter: a pool in which the next study was selected against the residual the last one surfaced and used a different outcome source, with the gap measured. Triangulated.

### F8 - The framework can return the separator, not the names

Fig 9 :32 and Fig 13 :134:

> **"you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself"**
> **"Unreachable is a verdict here, not a silence"**

What the figures license here is a shape: (i) read the behavior and the outcome from different sources, (ii) fix an anchor that does not update on the frame's opinion, (iii) introduce exogenous variation in the behavior per slot, (iv) state and pre-register what would refute the result. What no figure line licenses is the sentence "transformational leadership improves organizational performance" or "instruction fails." Fig 11 :51 already refused the context-free list. The correct output is the criterion plus an open slot per habitat. Mode: `general` on the limit, `specified` on the criterion. Refuter: a figure line that names a specific leadership behavior as a primitive or as a settled molecule. None exists after the census. Single-source for the figure limit (Fig 9) but triangulated with the canonical README (*"a shape, not a name"*) and with the habitat finding F1.

## 4. Pre-registered refuter, written before the conclusion

Required before any outreach (Seat Map :287: *"a falsifier written before the run is a design instrument, not only an honesty device"*).

The shape claim is refuted, or narrowed, if any of the following holds:

1. A behavior is found with a stable positive effect on a hard outcome across habitats whose binding residuals genuinely differ, with independent outcome measurement and exogenous variation. This breaks Fig 11 :51 and reinstates a standing list.
2. Two frames with genuinely low evidence overlap (different instruments, different outcome sources, different habitats) converge on the same behavior in the same slot, with each route's provenance recorded. This breaks F2 and F7.
3. An instrument shows that a leadership rating does not move when the rater's knowledge of outcomes or the hypothesis changes, and exogenous manipulation of the behavior moves the hard outcome in the predicted direction in that slot. This settles that slot at the ground and moves the claim from `candidate` toward `grounded`.
4. A figure census produces a line that names a specific leadership behavior as a primitive or settled molecule. This breaks F8.

If any fires, the shape is revised and the change is recorded here, not silently erased. Until one is run, no part of this may be labelled `grounded`.

## 5. Recommendation

1. Retire the standing-list framing. Ask instead which residual in which slot, and whether the reading of behavior and outcome are independent (F1, F2).
2. Treat published effect sizes as a budget filter, never a witness (F3; Fig 9 :60: *"Use filters for the budget and witnesses for the verdict. Never let a filter hand you a verdict."*). Ask which behavior to test first, not which behavior works.
3. Put the measurement link instrument in front of the causal question (F4). Pre-register the outcome source before the behavior is measured.
4. Import identification methods with provenance and a shadow test, and keep them independent of the leadership frame's rating scales (F5, F6).
5. Ask outside readers what they would count as a failure, not whether they agree (Fig 10 :52: *"Bring a characterised residual, never the source."*).
6. Keep the two verdicts separate: the separator survives, the list is a gap. Do not average them into "leadership matters somewhat."

### Seven-question recursive derivation (summary)

1. **Is the question a list?** No. It is a union compound (Fig 9 :54) plus a habitat-relative valence claim (Fig 11 :49-51).
2. **What is the residual?** The unchosen direction: a symmetric correlation read as an ordered improvement, per slot (Fig 1 :19; Fig 2 :39).
3. **Which link is broken?** The objective/measurement link; perception and reporting already have instruments (Fig 3 :19, :27).
4. **Can more studies close it?** No. Idempotent closure and one long room (Fig 8 :32; Fig 7 :13).
5. **What can cross?** An import from the identification frame, with source gate and shadow test, debited against independence (Fig 6 :23; Fig 8 :50-52).
6. **Can the behavior be named?** No. The framework returns a separator shape and an open slot; naming is a binding from the habitat with provenance, not a derivation (Fig 9 :32).
7. **Is anything settled?** No measurement was taken. Status `candidate` per domain; the literal list is a `gap` (Fig 13 :134).

### Updated verdict

The separator survives: independent readings, a non-updating anchor, exogenous variation per slot, and a pre-registered refuter. The standing list does not: it is a `gap` after a genuine census, and the habitat figure structurally refuses it. Strains after re-census: **none hidden**. The one open item is that "organizational performance" as usually measured (a rating) is not an anchor; that is named in F5 and carried into the verification packet. This remains a simulated derivation checked for internal consistency against the figures, not a ground reading.
