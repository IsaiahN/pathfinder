## 1. A shape is a constraint, not a filler

The canonical usage rule states the limit: the framework returns *"what a missing thing would have to look like (a shape, not a name)."* Fig 9 :32: *"you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself."* Fig 13 :134: *"Unreachable is a verdict here, not a silence"* - and the framework cannot name the missing primitive, because naming it would mean already holding it.

From *The Library of Babel*: *"you can cut your search short if the set of books returned is small and descriptive enough that it provides a general shape of the answer that satisfies your question."*

For this question a **shape** is the form of a checkable career for a leadership claim. It has four constraints: two independent readings, an anchor that does not update, exogenous variation, and a failure condition written in advance. It does not contain the name of a behavior. "Transformational leadership improves performance" is a name; "a behavior improves performance in this slot only if the outcome moved when the behavior was varied for reasons outside the unit's own success and the outcome was read by someone who could not see the assignment" is a shape.

The question as posed asks for a list of names. That is why the answer is a `gap` at the level of the list and a `specified` result at the level of the shape.

## 2. How a shape is obtained

- *Library of Babel*: use the vocabulary already held that is closest to the thing, look for recurring new terms that overlap or correlate with it, recurse. *"With each new term gained, you are able to narrow the space by definition and deepen your vocabulary."*
- Fig 6 :30: *"The description travels. Fails by being sharpened into terms only we can read. Cast it in effect terms."* Fig 9 :30-31: *"your words are borrowed too, so describe the effect rather than the cause."* So describe "a reading that does not move when the rater learns the hypothesis," not "a better leadership scale."
- *Decoding Tacit Knowledge*: *"When contact isn't possible, the best you can send is a plain description of what you'd expect to see, because anyone can check that against the world."* This is exactly what the first outreach stage sends.
- *Library of Babel* on bundling: *"Perhaps your problem was a bundle or conflation of several problems masquerading as one? You will need to search all of those problems individually..."* This is the census result F1: the question is a union compound, not a list.
- Fig 9 :27-28: *"describe it before you go looking, or any frame you pick will seem to fit."* The first draft asked which leadership theory to apply and then looked for evidence. The audit reverses that: describe the residual (a symmetric reading promoted to an ordered claim) and only then pick the identification frame.

## 3. What licenses a name

Only two things, both outside the shape itself:

1. **The ground settles it.** Formula step 5: *"the ground settles it. Verified only by what pays. A gate is not the ground."* Here that means an actual exogenous variation in a named behavior with an independently read hard outcome in a particular slot. Until then the name is unlicensed.
2. **A provenanced import from a frame whose closure differs** (Fig 6 :23, Fig 8 :50-52), with a source gate and a shadow test. A name imported from the management frame into this derivation is a binding with provenance, not a derivation.

*They're Scanning Books When They Should Be Studying Nature* sharpens the second point: *"The real import is not a new term in the existing space. It is a new dimension of the space."* For this question the needed import is not another leadership construct; it is the dimension of exogenous variation, which the questionnaire frame does not contain. The same essay adds: *"A minimum viable set of six terms is a human's careful guess at reality's compression axes; another sensorium's minimum set need not be those six"* and *"Declaring the primitives does not make them neutral. It makes the choice visible."* Applied here: naming "performance" as a rating is a visible choice, not a neutral measurement.

## 4. The ground and the source problem

*The Hard Problem is About Source* gives the mechanism. An instrument reads green and *"cannot comment on the source"*; one green light, or yellow plus blue, or RGB, or CMYK, or an undiscovered scheme can all produce the same reading. *"If several paths create the same behavior, then behavior is a projection with lossy information."* The reading does not determine the source. *"An instrument that can't distinguish these cases demonstrates its own inadequacy"* and the case was resolved *"not by argument, but by the arrival of a better instrument."*

Here: a high leadership score plus high performance is a projection. The source could be behavior causing performance, performance causing the rating, a third variable (task, market, selection into leadership), or a shared response bias. The rating instrument cannot comment on which. What is owed is a discriminator (independent outcome source plus exogenous variation), not a more refined leadership questionnaire.

*How Intelligence Actually Works* makes the same point with provenance: *"The projection that no instrument distinguishes from the thing is still a projection, which is why the lineage has to be kept even when the copy is perfect."* A pooled effect size that survives every robustness check inside the same measurement pool is still a projection of that pool.

## 5. Answer to the shape question

**What licenses a particular leadership behavior as "improving performance"?** Nothing in the framework. The framework licenses the separator: independent readings, a non-updating anchor, exogenous variation, and a pre-registered failure condition, applied per slot. A particular behavior is licensed only by a ground reading that returns a positive effect in that slot with its provenance recorded.

**Does the field commit the error the essays forbid?** Yes, in the common case. A rater's leadership score and the rater's performance rating are a projection read twice; naming the behavior as the source is the metamerism error. The correct form is an open slot per habitat until an instrument discriminates sources. This is not a claim that no leadership behavior works. It is a claim that the usual reading cannot say which one does.

**Correction to my own preliminary reading.** I first treated the shape limit as a ban on naming any behavior at all. On re-census against the canonical README it is narrower: the phrase governs a *missing* thing. A behavior is not a missing primitive; it is a candidate binding from the habitat onto an already-held construct. So naming is not forbidden, but it carries provenance and remains a `candidate` until the ground pays. This correction is kept here rather than erased, and it is applied in `06_isomers_and_order.md` and `07_the_tether_answer.md`.

## 6. Why this derivation is a simulation, not a ground reading

*The Recursive Self-Improvement Trap*: *"the output is only as good as the validator. The agent, or the tool, or the frame, or the theory, cannot validate itself. An external validator, or ground, is always needed to tether the veracity of the output to reality."* And: *"LLMs can quickly simulate an approximate answer/output with the formula found in the architecture post... However, the truly testable output would need to be derived from the real architecture or working the formula yourself."* LLM output is *"simulacra."*

So `01_figure_grounded_audit.md` is a simulated derivation checked for internal consistency against the figures. It is not an experiment, not a meta-analysis of new data, and not a reading of the ground. It cannot settle which leadership behaviors work. The world sources in `10_source_ledger.csv` are secondary corroboration, and all of them were opened for this solution; the conflict rule was not triggered.

## 7. Two consequences for outreach

**Prestige is not action.** From *The Recursive Self-Improvement Trap*: *"Prestige must never be allowed to validate an action directly"* and *"Prestige can fund an action. It can give an action a budget to be tried. But it cannot settle an action. Only the ground can settle it. The Ground Maintainer must be blind to prestige."* For this question: a famous leadership scholar's endorsement is a budget to try the separator, not evidence that the separator is right. A list of agreeable readers is the named collapse.

**Declare the interest.** Fig 10 :59: *"an interest in the outcome is unavoidable, an interest in the reading is disqualifying."* Leadership researchers, including this derivation's author, have an unavoidable interest in whether leadership matters. The correct handling is to declare it, write the failure condition first, and let an outside reader refuse the shape. A reported failure is worth more than an endorsement.
