**Subject:** One check on "which leadership behaviors work" before anyone ranks them

> Hi [name],
>
> I am writing about a specific distinction, not asking you to read anything first and not asking you to agree. If the distinction fails, there is nothing else to look at.
>
> **The output (`specified` on the criterion, `general` on the shape, not `grounded`):** a correlation between a leadership behavior and organizational performance is symmetric, while "improves" is ordered. A number that says the two move together cannot say which side moves the other. If the behavior rating and the performance rating come from the same person at the same moment, the two are not two measurements of a relation; they are one reading wearing two names. Before calling a behavior a cause, four things have to hold for that unit at that time: the behavior and the outcome were read from different sources, the outcome does not change when the researchers change their opinion of the leader, the behavior was varied for reasons outside the unit's own success, and a failure condition was written down in advance.
>
> What I am **not** claiming: that any named behavior, transformational, transactional, servant, directive or otherwise, has been shown by this derivation to work. The derivation does not name behaviors. It says a behavior's value is relative to the performance problem in that unit, so a behavior that helps one unit can hurt another. The published effect sizes are useful for deciding what to test first, not for deciding what works.
>
> **What would refute the shape claim:**
> 1. a behavior with a stable positive effect on a hard outcome across units whose binding problems genuinely differ, with the outcome measured independently;
> 2. two genuinely independent research groups, using different instruments and different outcome sources, converging on the same behavior in the same unit, with each route recorded;
> 3. an instrument showing that a leadership rating does not shift when the rater knows the outcome or the hypothesis, together with exogenous variation in the behavior that moves the hard outcome.
>
> I wrote these down before sending this. If one holds, the shape is wrong or needs narrowing, and I will retract or amend it. A one-line "this fails because..." is more useful to me than agreement. If you would rather not engage, no reply is needed.
>
> **If it survives, and only then:** the sources are below. The figures, not my summary of them, are the authority.
> - Figures and formula, branch `general-agent`: https://github.com/IsaiahN/tether/tree/general-agent/figures
> - Canonical usage rule: https://github.com/IsaiahN/tether/blob/general-agent/figures/README.md
> - The leadership-methods paper this question was checked against: https://people.unil.ch/johnantonakis/files/2024/01/Causal_Claims.pdf
>
> **If you want to use the machinery:** the formula is in the figures link. Run it on a leadership question you know well and can check against an actual outcome. Write down what would refute your result *before* you run it. The framework's own rule is that a loop cannot check the direction of its own question, so a result produced without a pre-registered failure condition is not checkable. A reported failure is more useful to me than a reported success.
