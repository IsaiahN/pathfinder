## QUESTION

Decide, in effect terms, what the Tether says about a standing list that separates leadership behaviors which improve organizational performance from those which merely correlate with it. Option A: rank behaviors by pooled correlations and publish the winners as causes. Option B: refuse the standing list, keep the residual per organizational slot, and require an instrument that breaks the shared source before "improves" can be said. What each leads to: A produces a convention scored by frame-internal quantities and spends the independence of the people asked to check it; B produces a checkable separator shape, a per-slot verdict and an open slot until a ground settles it. The effect restatement and the bound shape term are written before the run in `README.md` and `01_figure_grounded_audit.md` section 0.

## DOMAIN CENSUS

R by arity, symmetry and scale, then one line per domain with actors / currency / anchor, partition or union, and order by dependency.

- **R (the residual).** A symmetric reading (a correlation between a leadership behavior and performance) is being used to license an ordered claim ("improves"). Arity: two conditions at minimum, since behavior and performance are joint products of a habitat; the compound is a union of at least four overlapping conditions, not a partition. Symmetry: correlation is symmetric, improvement is ordered. Scale: per organizational slot, never pooled across firms.
- **Domain 1, leadership research frame.** Actors: researchers, scale designers, leaders, subordinate raters, editors. Currency: correlations, effect sizes, publications, citations. Anchor: none internal; an anchor must be imported. Question type: frame-internal scoring, so it cannot certify its own verdict.
- **Domain 2, organizational habitat.** Actors: leader, followers, task, incentives, market, institutions, time. Currency: performance outcomes, survival, resources. Anchor: hard outcomes that do not update on what researchers think. Question type: valence relative to this slot's residual, so no context-free list.
- **Domain 3, identification / causal inference frame.** Actors: experiments, natural experiments, instruments, panel designs, auditors. Currency: the counterfactual contrast P(y|do(x)) against P(y|x). Anchor: the manipulability of the behavior, which is the ground for this question. Question type: import with a source gate and shadow test, low overlap with Domain 1 when it uses administrative or randomized data rather than the same rating scale.
- **Partition or union.** Union compound, not partition. A single lever (another correlational study) clears only the region where one condition holds and leaves the intersections standing (Fig 9 :54). Keep it as one compound with its own loop.
- **Order by dependency.** Describe the residual before choosing a frame; instrument the objective link before asking which behavior is best; refuse the symmetric-to-ordered promotion before minting any causal term; settle per slot at the ground (Fig 3 :20, Fig 9 :27-28).

Strain test applied: the apparent conflict between Fig 11 :51 (no standing list of good actors) and Fig 12 :123 (a functional group "says what kind") dissolves on re-census. Fig 11 governs actors and valence relative to a residual; Fig 12 :123 governs arrangement kinds. Different objects, so no strain. All three domains are simulated and share this solution's evidence pool, so their agreement is internal consistency, not corroboration.

## VERDICT (from the figures)

The separator survives; the standing list is a `gap`. A correlation is symmetric and "improves" is ordered, so a pooled effect size cannot choose the arrow. Independent readings, a non-updating anchor, exogenous variation and a pre-registered failure condition are the four conditions that would let a slot be settled; an outcome assessor blind to the assignment is required. A behavior's value is relative to that slot's binding problem, so no context-free ranking follows (Fig 11 :49, :51). Published effect sizes are filters for the budget, never witnesses for the verdict (Fig 9 :60). This result is `specified` and ungrounded; the list is unreachable as posed, which is a verdict and not silence (Fig 13 :134).

- **cost if wrong:** bounded and locatable. The packet states its mode, writes refuters before the conclusion and records the lineage, so an error is findable rather than invisible. The real cost is spending the independence of outside readers by sending them a ranking instead of a separator, which Fig 2 :42 prices: "Buy independence in proportion to the cost of being wrong." That cost is avoidable by leading with the shape claim and its refuter. A second cost is practical: a firm that acts on a standing list will apply a behavior where its valence is negative, and the failure will be attributed to the behavior rather than to the habitat mismatch.
- **undoable:** yes. The list framing is removable without touching the separator. `07_the_tether_answer.md` and `08_expert_verification_packet.md` already remove it. No primitive has to be withdrawn, and an earlier ranking can be superseded by a per-slot verdict with provenance (Fig 10 :62-66: a ruling carries provenance so a later reader can locate the error).
- **reversed by:** a ground reading, not agreement. A behavior with a stable positive effect on a hard outcome across habitats whose binding residuals genuinely differ, under exogenous variation with an independent blind outcome assessor, would break Fig 11 :51 and restore the list. Two genuinely low-overlap frames converging with recorded provenance would break the shared-source finding. An instrument showing that a leadership rating is invariant to knowledge of the hypothesis and reputation would move a slot toward `grounded`. Until one of these fires, the verdict stands as `candidate` in each domain, with the list marked `gap`.
