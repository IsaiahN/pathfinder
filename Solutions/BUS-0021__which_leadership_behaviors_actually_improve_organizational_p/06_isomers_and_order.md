# Supporting derivation: bonds, order, and analogues for cause versus correlate

**Mode: `specified` on the bond typing and the union reading, `general` on the dependency order.** This is a simulated derivation checked against the figures, not a ground reading (see `05_what_shape_means.md` section 6).

## RESIDUAL

The question asks which behaviors *improve* performance and which merely *correlate* with it. In the bond vocabulary of Fig 12, "improves" and "correlates" are not two scores on one scale; they are two different bond types between the same two operands. The residual is a type error: a symmetric reading is being used to license an ordered claim. Three questions are owed:

1. Which bond does "correlates with" use, and which does "improves" use?
2. Is the question a partition (two lists) or a union compound (overlapping conditions)?
3. What is the dependency order, and what analogues corroborate it from a different closure?

## DOMAIN CENSUS

Atoms held: a behavior reading B_s and an outcome reading O_s per slot s; the habitat conditions (task, incentives, market, selection into the role); the identification instruments (randomization, instruments, panels). Bonds held: the seven, of which `+ conjunction`, `∥ disjunction` and `≡ identity` are symmetric, and `→ sequence`, `⇒ production`, `− subtraction` and `⋛ comparison` are ordered; plus `¬ negation` (refusal), which is not a bond.

Anchor: the hard outcome per slot, which does not move when the research frame changes its mind (Fig 2 :29). No new primitive is available without an import with a source gate.

## DERIVATION

### Q1 - "Correlates with" and "improves" are different bonds

Operators table :14-15: conjunction is symmetric, and the identifying test is *"swap the operands. Does the meaning change? No."* Fig 12 :23: *"this bond is symmetric, so the four are one term"* - the order of operands carries nothing. A correlation coefficient has exactly this property: r(B,O) = r(O,B). Under a symmetric bond, swapping the operands produces one term, not a new claim.

By contrast Operators table :31-33: sequence is ordered, and the test is *"swap them. Does it become a different thing? Yes."* Production (⇒) is stronger: *"A produces B; B did not exist before"* (Operators table :37). "Improves" asserts one of these ordered bonds. Fig 12 :112-113: *"A plan is a sequence. A goal is a comparison. Progress is a subtraction."* Improvement is a comparison against a prior state plus a sequence from an intervention; the symmetric correlation can express neither.

- **Falsifier, written before the run:** if a correlation could determine the direction of its own arrow, the bond distinction would collapse. The existence of reverse causation and common-cause cases (Correlation does not imply causation, Wikipedia, 2026) is the standing refutation of that collapse. Status: `specified` on the typing, triangulated across the Operators table, Fig 12 and the statistics frame.

### Q2 - The question is a union, not a partition

Fig 9 :54: *"And when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again."* Fig 9 :57-58: *"a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either. It stops being a mixture and becomes a specific compound."*

The two lists in the question overlap. A behavior can correlate because it improves performance (causal), because performance enables the behavior (reverse), because both are driven by task structure (common cause), or because both are responses to the same rater (measurement). These are not two piles; they are four conditions, and a unit can sit in several at once. A single lever, one more correlational study, clears only the region where one condition holds and leaves the intersections standing.

- **Falsifier:** if each observed leadership-performance association could be assigned to exactly one condition with no overlap, the partition reading would be restored. The 2010 review's list of simultaneous threats (omitted variables, selection, simultaneity, measurement error, common-method variance) is evidence against that. Status: `specified`, triangulated (Fig 9 + Antonakis et al. 2010).

### Q3 - Dependency order: describe, then separate readings, then vary, then settle

Fig 3 :20: *"The chain is ordered by dependency, not by importance. Each link consumes what the one above produces, so effort spent above the break arrives at a step that cannot use it."* Fig 3 :19: *"Optimising a link that already works cannot rescue one that does not."*

Order for this question:

1. **Describe the residual in effect terms** (Fig 9 :27-28: *"describe it before you go looking, or any frame you pick will seem to fit"*). The residual is a symmetric reading promoted to an ordered claim, per slot.
2. **Vocabulary / objective link:** state what would count as an independent outcome reading in this unit's own terms. This is the link that usually has no instrument (Fig 3 :27: *"A link with no instrument is not a link that works. It is a link nobody has looked at."*).
3. **Planning:** choose the identification design that can deliver exogenous variation given this habitat, not the design most fashionable in the field.
4. **Learn and carry:** name what worked *and* what failed, verify against the hard outcome, and record the lineage so the next slot pays less (Fig 4 :22: *"Only methods cross up. Only head starts come down."*). A recorded effect size carried across habitats without the lineage is a replay, not a method.

The 2025 cross-cultural meta-analysis's own numbers locate the break: 83 percent cross-sectional and one experimental study among working populations. Most effort lands above the break, where the questionnaires already work.

### Q4 - Isomer or functional group?

Fig 12 :117: *"Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."* Fig 12 :123: *"A substructure whose presence fixes the outcome. The group says what kind; the qualifier says which member."*

Take two behaviors measured in the same units: "inspirational motivation" and "contingent reward." As the literature records them they share a large part of their atom set (the same rater, the same questionnaire family, often the same subordinates) but differ in the bond asserted between the behavior and the outcome. Judge and Piccolo (2004) find transformational leadership correlated .80 with contingent reward, so the two are near the same reading (Fig 2 :48-49: two frames that overlap almost completely are one frame wearing two names). Under Fig 12 they are not isomers; they are two members of the same **functional group** (a rated behavior paired with a rated outcome) whose difference is carried by a qualifier, not by a new substance.

The stronger claim worth testing: hold the habitat atoms fixed (task, incentives, market, role selection) and vary only the arrangement, that is, which behavior is varied exogenously and which independent outcome is read. Then the two are genuine isomers: same atoms, different bond, different substance. This is a checkable structural claim and it needs an instrument, not an argument.

- **Falsifier:** if the two behaviors cannot be written over the same habitat atom set (one requires an atom the other does not, for example a market that the second does not touch), the isomer claim fails and the functional-group reading stands. Status: `specified` for the functional-group reading (triangulated: Fig 12 + Judge and Piccolo 2004); `specified`, unmeasured for the isomer reading.

### Q5 - Analogues from a different closure

The animal-behaviour frame supplies a low-overlap check on the habitat claim (it draws on tracking data and field observation, not leadership questionnaires):

- *"Leadership styles and, by extension, who is most likely to occupy a leadership role within a society, are often context dependent and variable over time"* (Leadership and Status in Mammalian Societies: Context Matters, Trends in Cognitive Sciences, 2020). Hungry fish and thirsty zebra lead collective movements until the need is met, without changing their standing in the group. This is Fig 11 :49-51 at the world level: valence is relative to the current residual.
- *"dominance and leadership hierarchies are completely independent of each other"* (Context-dependent hierarchies in pigeons, PNAS, 2013). Two hierarchical readings of the same group, different atoms, no conflation. This is Fig 2 :31 independence in a different substrate.
- *"who is leading depends on the time- and length-scale of the movements considered"* (Anatomy of leadership in collective behaviour, Chaos, 2018). This is the scale clause of the residual census: the same group has different leaders at different scales, so "who leads" is not a context-free name.

These analogues corroborate; they do not decide. The figure governs (Fig 11 :51). Agreement from a shared evidence pool would be one witness; here the pools are genuinely different, so the agreement has some weight.

## CONCLUSION

"Improves" uses an ordered bond; "correlates" uses a symmetric one. The question is a union compound of at least four conditions, not two lists. The dependency order runs describe-residual, instrument the objective link, choose the identification design, then settle and carry the method. The two most-discussed behaviors are, as currently measured, members of one functional group rather than isomers; they become isomers only when the habitat atoms are held fixed and the arrangement varies. Animal-behaviour analogues from a different evidence pool corroborate the habitat-relative valence claim.

Strain after re-census: none hidden. The apparent tension between Fig 12 :123 (a functional group "says what kind") and Fig 11 :51 (no standing list) dissolves because the former fixes a kind of arrangement while the latter governs which actor fills the slot. Different objects.

## FALSIFIER

The whole supporting derivation fails if a single behavior is shown to have a stable positive effect on a hard outcome across habitats with genuinely different binding residuals, under exogenous variation and independent outcome measurement. That would restore a standing list, collapse the union into a partition, and make the functional-group/isomer distinction moot. It has not been run here.
