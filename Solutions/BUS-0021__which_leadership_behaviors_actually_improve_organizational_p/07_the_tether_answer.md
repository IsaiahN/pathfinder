# The Tether's answer: which leadership behaviors actually improve performance

**Mode: `specified` on the separator and the per-domain verdicts, `general` on the shape, `gap` on the literal standing list. Not `grounded`.** This is a shape and a route, not a result. Simulated derivation checked against the figures (`05_what_shape_means.md` section 6), not a ground reading.

## 1. The residual it answers

A leadership-performance correlation is symmetric; "improves" is ordered. The residual is the unchosen direction, measured per slot. It persists because the behavior and the outcome are usually read from the same source at the same moment, so the two are one reading wearing two names. Fig 2 :40: *"disqualifying for a verdict: agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool"* and Fig 2 :35: *"Alignment is triangulation, not negotiation. A mirror cannot correct you."*

Fig 1 :19: *"The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated."* An average across firms hides the only signal that says which unit needs which behavior. Fig 1 :22: *"Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average."*

The question is a union compound (Fig 9 :54: *"And when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again"*), not a two-list partition. Its verdicts are kept per domain and never averaged.

## 2. The shape

1. **A correlation is a budget filter, never a verdict.** Fig 9 :60: *"Use filters for the budget and witnesses for the verdict. Never let a filter hand you a verdict."* Published effect sizes say which behavior to test first in a given habitat. They do not say which behavior works. Fig 1 :27: *"They are frame-internal. A frame cannot score itself with a quantity it also produces."*
2. **The two readings must be independent.** Fig 2 :31: *"Independence, between two frames, is how little they draw on the same evidence."* A leader's score and a subordinate's performance rating from the same questionnaire at the same moment are not two frames. Fig 7 :13: *"same closure, handed along; agreement compounds, evidence does not"* - a literature of 519 such studies is one long room (Fig 7 :35: *"A room's agreement is variation, not a verdict"*).
3. **The outcome must be an anchor.** Fig 2 :29: *"An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks."* A rating that moves when the rater knows the leader's reputation is not an anchor. Fig 10 :24: *"the ground does not decay, the channel does"* - the drift is in the channel, and it looks like a working measurement until it fails.
4. **Variation must come from outside the unit's own success.** Otherwise behavior and performance are jointly caused and the arrow is unchosen. Fig 6 :23: *"No frame escapes its own closure. The bound moves only on contact with a frame whose closure differs."* The import is the identification frame, with a source gate and a shadow test, debited against independence (Fig 8 :53: *"Residual first, frame second."*).
5. **A behavior's value is relative to the slot's residual.** Fig 11 :49: *"An actor's valence is relative to the residual, not a property it carries."* Fig 11 :51: *"No standing list of good actors survives a change of residual."* A behavior that clears a coordination failure can be a malfactor where the binding constraint is different. This is why the question's standing list is a `gap`, not merely an unmeasured list.
6. **The framework returns the separator, not the names.** Fig 9 :32: *"you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself."* What it gives is four conditions and an open slot. Naming a behavior is a binding from the habitat with provenance, a `candidate` until the ground pays. Fig 13 :134: *"Unreachable is a verdict here, not a silence"* - the list is unreachable as posed; the shape is not silent.

## 3. What it explicitly does not say

- Not that leadership is irrelevant. The correlations are real; their valence is undecided by the reading that produces them.
- Not that any named behavior, transformational, transactional, servant, directive or otherwise, has been shown to work here. No behavior is named by a figure line.
- Not that effect sizes are useless. They are filters for the budget, not witnesses for the verdict (Fig 9 :60).
- Not that this is a meta-analysis or an experiment. No new data were collected.
- Not `grounded`. Nothing here was settled by an instrument.
- Not that the question is silly. It is a union compound that must be unbundled before it is searched again.

## 4. What would settle it - the ground

An instrument that breaks the shared source, per slot:

1. **Independent outcome source.** The behavior reading and the outcome reading come from different instruments, and the outcome assessor cannot see the behavior assignment. This is Fig 10 :42 in practice: *"A verifier that can reconstruct the claim cannot verify it."*
2. **Exogenous variation.** The behavior is varied for reasons outside the unit's own success (randomized assignment where feasible; otherwise an instrument, a regression discontinuity, a difference-in-differences design). Antonakis et al. (2010) supply the menu and the assumptions.
3. **Hard outcome.** Survival, measured output, margin or another counter that does not update on the researcher's opinion of the leader. Fig 2 :29 must hold.
4. **Pre-registered failure condition.** Written before the run; the same shrinking test used everywhere else in the loop, read with the opposite sign. Seat Map :287: *"a falsifier written before the run is a design instrument, not only an honesty device."*

A result that clears these four in a slot settles that slot, not the standing list. A behavior that clears them across habitats with genuinely different residuals would refute Fig 11 :51 and would require a retraction of the list-as-gap verdict. The 2025 cross-cultural meta-analysis reports that 83 percent of its studies were cross-sectional and that only one experimental study among working populations could be located, so the instrument is the scarce thing, not the correlation.

## 5. The honest stop

**Reached for the separator, unreached for the list.** The figures license the four conditions and the habitat-relative valence claim; they do not license a name. Fig 9 :32 again: *"you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself."* The 2026-11-22 census found no figure line naming a leadership behavior. That is a `gap` after a genuine search, and "a search that finds nothing is never a proof of absence" (Fig 8 :39). Another habitat, another sensorium and another instrument may return the name. The lineage is recorded so the stop is checkable; indiscernibility is not identity.

## 6. One-paragraph version for outreach

> The Tether does not produce a ranking of leadership behaviors. It says why the ranking cannot be produced from the usual evidence: a correlation between a behavior and performance is symmetric, while "improves" is ordered, and when both readings come from the same rater they are one measurement wearing two names. What it offers instead is a shape, stated in advance: read the behavior and the outcome from independent sources, use an outcome that does not move when the researcher changes their mind about the leader, vary the behavior for reasons outside the unit's own success, and write down what would refute the result before running it. A behavior's value is then relative to the performance problem in that unit, so the standing list the question asks for is a gap, not a hidden answer. Published effect sizes are useful for choosing what to test first and carry no verdict weight. The framework returns a separator, not a name; a genuine cross-habitat effect under those four conditions would be a ground reading, and would settle the slot rather than the list.
