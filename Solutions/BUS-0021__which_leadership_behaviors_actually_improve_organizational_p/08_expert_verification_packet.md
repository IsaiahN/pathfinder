# Expert verification packet: separating cause from correlate in leadership research

**What this is:** a claim in a form a researcher in leadership or organizational behavior can check. Mode, derivation and refuters are stated before the conclusion, so a reader knows what to look for and what would settle it.

**Mode: `specified` on the three sub-claims, `general` on the shape, `gap` on the standing list. Not `grounded`.** No new measurement is claimed. This is a shape and a route, not a result.

## 1. The claim, in one paragraph

A leadership-performance correlation cannot tell you which leadership behaviors improve performance, because the observed relation is symmetric while "improves" is ordered, and in the usual design the behavior and the outcome are read from the same source at the same moment. The checkable output is a separator with four conditions, applied per organizational slot: independent readings of behavior and outcome, an outcome that does not change when the research frame changes its mind, exogenous variation in the behavior, and a failure condition written down before the run. A behavior's value is relative to that slot's binding performance problem, so no context-free list of good behaviors follows. Published effect sizes are filters for what to test first, not witnesses for what works. The framework returns the separator, not the names.

## 2. Three checkable sub-claims

### C1 - Same-source readings are one witness, not two (checkable by reading Fig 1 and Fig 2)

- **Statement:** a behavior score and a performance score from the same rater at the same moment are not two independent frames ranging against an anchor. Their agreement cannot support a causal verdict.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 2 :40 - *"disqualifying for a verdict: agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool"*; Fig 2 :31 - *"Independence, between two frames, is how little they draw on the same evidence."* Fig 1 :27 - *"They are frame-internal. A frame cannot score itself with a quantity it also produces."*
- **What to check:** in a representative sample of leadership studies, how often are the predictor and the criterion collected from the same source at the same time? Antonakis, Bendahan, Jacquart and Lalive (The Leadership Quarterly, 2010) coded 110 articles and report that at least 66 percent and up to 90 percent of the design and estimation conditions for valid causal claims were unaddressed, with common-method variance among the named threats. Judge and Piccolo (2004) report a .80 correlation between transformational and contingent reward scales, which is two names for a partly shared reading.
- **Refuter:** two frames with genuinely independent outcome sources and independent behavior measurements converging on the same behavior in the same unit, each route's provenance recorded. Or a demonstration that a rating is invariant to the rater's knowledge of the hypothesis and the leader's reputation. If either holds, this sub-claim is narrowed.

### C2 - The broken link is the objective measurement link (checkable against the methods literature)

- **Statement:** the field has good instruments for perceiving and rating leadership and for pooling results, but the link that states an outcome independent of the perception instrument is the one that usually has no instrument. More of the same study cannot rescue it.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 3 :27 - *"A link with no instrument is not a link that works. It is a link nobody has looked at."* Fig 3 :19 - *"Optimising a link that already works cannot rescue one that does not."*
- **What to check:** the 2025 cross-cultural meta-analysis (519 studies, 121,385 employees, 39 nations) reports that 83 percent of its studies were cross-sectional and that only one experimental study among working populations could be located. It names experiments and instrumental variables as the route to causal identification.
- **Refuter:** a representative sample in which the objective link is independently instrumented and the pooled estimate changes materially, or a valid counterfactual contrast derived from the rating frame's own instruments alone. If either holds, this sub-claim fails.

### C3 - No standing list follows (checkable by reading Fig 11 and the animal-behaviour analogues)

- **Statement:** a behavior's value is relative to the residual in that slot, so the question's context-free list is a `gap`. The framework returns a separator shape, not a name.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 11 :49 - *"An actor's valence is relative to the residual, not a property it carries."* Fig 11 :51 - *"No standing list of good actors survives a change of residual."* Fig 9 :32 - *"you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself."*
- **What to check:** outside the management evidence pool, leadership roles in mammalian societies are *"often context dependent and variable over time"* (Trends in Cognitive Sciences, 2020); in pigeon flocks *"dominance and leadership hierarchies are completely independent of each other"* (PNAS, 2013); in migrating caribou *"who is leading depends on the time- and length-scale of the movements considered"* (Chaos, 2018). These are corroboration from a different closure, not the authority.
- **Refuter:** a behavior with a stable positive effect on a hard outcome across habitats whose binding residuals genuinely differ, measured under exogenous variation with an independent outcome source. If one is found, Fig 11 :51 is narrowed and the standing list is back on the table.

## 3. What is explicitly not claimed

- Not that leadership is irrelevant, and not that the published correlations are false.
- Not that any named behavior has been shown here to work. The packet names none.
- Not that effect sizes have no use. They are budget filters (Fig 9 :60: *"Use filters for the budget and witnesses for the verdict. Never let a filter hand you a verdict."*).
- Not that this is an experiment or a new meta-analysis. No data were collected.
- Not `grounded`. Every substantive claim is `specified`, the shape is `general`, and the list is a `gap`.
- Not a claim that the framework is the only account of leadership and performance. It makes no uniqueness claim here.

## 5. What would settle it

A ground reading per slot: an independent outcome assessor blind to the behavior assignment, exogenous variation in the behavior, a hard outcome, and a failure condition registered before the run. Fig 10 :42: *"A verifier that can reconstruct the claim cannot verify it."* A result that clears these settles the slot. A positive effect across habitats with different residuals would refute C3 and move the list from `gap` to a real claim. Prestige or agreement is not such an instrument: from *The Recursive Self-Improvement Trap*, *"Prestige can fund an action. It can give an action a budget to be tried. But it cannot settle an action. Only the ground can settle it."*

## 6. What an expert can check in twenty minutes

1. Read Fig 2 :31, :40, Fig 3 :27 and Fig 11 :49, :51 - five short passages.
2. Check C1 against a sample of studies you know: were predictor and criterion collected from the same source at the same time?
3. Check C2 against the 2025 meta-analysis's own design counts. Is the objective link instrumented?
4. Name one instrument that would break the shared source in a habitat you know, or say why none is possible.
5. Send the refutation. A failure is more useful than an endorsement. Record your verdict before looking at the framework; later agreement is never pooled as corroboration.
