## 1. Independent domain census

I redid the census before reading the primary's conclusions, using the WATSON order: R by arity, symmetry and scale; one line per domain with actors / currency / anchor; partition or union; order by dependency.

- **R (arity / symmetry / scale).** The residual is a symmetric reading used to license an ordered claim. Arity: at least two conditions, and plausibly a four-way union (direct effect, reverse direction, common cause, shared measurement), so a union compound and not a two-list partition. Symmetry: r(B,O) = r(O,B) while "improves" is ordered; the residual is the unchosen direction. Scale: per organizational slot per time, never pooled across firms. I reached this before looking at F1 or F2 of the primary audit.
- **Domain 1, research frame.** Actors: researchers, scale designers, raters, editors. Currency: correlations, effect sizes, citations. Anchor: none internal. It is the frame that proposes the "behaviors", so it cannot certify them.
- **Domain 2, habitat.** Actors: leader, followers, task, incentives, market, institutions. Currency: hard outcomes and survival. Anchor: an outcome that does not update on what the frame thinks. The question of which behavior helps is relative to this slot's binding problem.
- **Domain 3, identification.** Actors: randomized or quasi-experimental designs, instruments, auditors. Currency: counterfactual contrast. Anchor: the manipulability of the behavior. Low overlap with Domain 1 only if the outcome source is different.
- **Order by dependency.** Describe the residual before picking a frame; instrument the objective link before asking which behavior is best; refuse the symmetric-to-ordered promotion before minting any causal term; settle per slot.

My census matches the primary's three domains and its union reading. Where the primary derived from Fig 1 and Fig 2, I derived from Fig 9's union/partition split and Fig 12's bond symmetry (Fig 12 :23: *"this bond is symmetric, so the four are one term"*). Different starting figures, same domain boundaries: low overlap between routes, so the agreement is weak but not circular within the framework. It is still one evidence pool.

## 4. Round trip gap R_T

The Symbols table defines R_T as the gap between what was sent up and what came back down, `gap(x, (T_E ∘ T_A)(x))`, measuring what the coarser description cannot hold. Fig 4 :30 requires it to be measured rather than assumed. I ran the round trip on the condensation this folder actually uses.

2. **Abstraction (T_A):** the shorthand carried in `07_the_tether_answer.md` and `09_watson_answer.md`: independent readings, non-updating anchor, exogenous variation, pre-registered failure; the list is a gap.
3. **Concretisation (T_E):** a concrete leadership claim, for example "transformational leadership improves performance, so train leaders in it."

**R_T is nonzero.** Three things the shorthand holds do not survive the trip back down:

- the **per-slot indexing** of the residual, which a firm-wide training recommendation discards by averaging (Fig 1 :22);
- the **independence debit** on the identification import, which a recommendation that reuses the same rating scales ignores (Fig 6 :24);
- the **non-updating anchor** requirement, which a before-and-after rating by the same raters breaches.

Conversely, one thing survives cleanly: a symmetric correlation cannot license an ordered improvement claim without an instrument. That is the residue worth sending. R_T was **not** measured numerically, because no ground reading exists; it is recorded qualitatively as the enumerated set of dropped constraints. Treating "the formula is the condensed version of the metalaws" as identity would set R_T to zero and commit the dead-reckoning error Fig 4 :30 names. The honest form is a lossy condensation with a recorded gap, which `09_watson_answer.md` now states.
