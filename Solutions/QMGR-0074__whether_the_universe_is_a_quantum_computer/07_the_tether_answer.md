# The Tether's answer: is the universe a quantum computer

**Mode: `specified` on the shape claims, `general` on the barriers, not `grounded`.** This is a shape and a route, not a result. Simulated derivation checked against the figures (`05_what_shape_means.md` §7), not a ground reading.

## 1. The residual it answers

The question is a union, not one question (Fig 9 :55: "A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones"). Three conditions with separate verdicts:

- **C1, literal identity:** the universe *is* a computer.
- **C2, computability:** its behaviour *can be computed* or simulated by a quantum computer.
- **C3, consequence:** what follows for physical law.

Each has its own ground and its own loop. They are never averaged.

## 2. The shape

1. **The identity question dissolves; the contact question survives.** Fig 11 :41: "Which is the same move as asking what generates lift rather than whether a thing is a bird. The substrate question dissolves; the contact question does not." What survives is: *what generates the observed regularity, and can an instrument discriminate among the candidate sources?*

2. **"Computer" is not a primitive.** Fig 13 :115: "The set is closed. The arrangements are not. That gap is why import exists." Fig 12 :103: "Composition is recursive, and the level decides what is irreducible." A computational description is a molecule at a level, not a seventh kind. Naming it as fundamental would violate Fig 13 :43-44: "A generating set must not contain what its own members produce."

3. **The literal claim is unreached, not refuted.** Fig 8 :39: "So a search that finds nothing is never a proof of absence." Fig 13 :134: "Unreachable is a verdict here, not a silence,". Copeland, Shagrir and Sprevak (2017) call it "wide open"; the essay *How Intelligence Actually Works* says it is "below the union horizon" and that "'No trace exists' can never be confirmed by better instruments". No instrument currently returns a discriminating reading, so there is no ground on which to say yes or no.

4. **The unqualified "everything is computation" claim has no anchor.** Fig 2 :29: "An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks." Floridi (2017): pancomputationalism "is simply not vulnerable to a refutation". A claim with no possible counterexample is a convention, and Fig 10 :46 says "A convention makes no claim about the world; a verdict and an atom both do."

5. **"Physical law is the source code, finished" is refused.** Fig 7 :6: "the limit: no last room". Fig 8 :37: "A frame cannot certify its own limit." A terminal computational law would be the last room and a frame certifying itself. What survives is a generator with an open center and a recorded lineage.

6. **The name is a binding, not a derivation.** Fig 9 :30-31: "your words are borrowed too, so describe the effect rather than the cause." Several sources (literal computation, mere computability, computational metaphor) give the same behavioral reading. This is the metamerism case from *The Hard Problem is About Source*: "If several paths create the same behavior, then behavior is a projection with lossy information." An instrument, not an argument, would be needed to pick the source.

7. **Which structure, if any, fills the slot is a `gap`.** No figure line governs it after a genuine re-census. Fig 9 :32: "you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself." Fig 8 :39 and Fig 13 :134 forbid turning silence into an answer.

## 4. What it explicitly does not say

- Not "yes, the universe is a quantum computer". No ground settles that.
- Not "no, the universe is not a computer". A search that finds nothing proves nothing (Fig 9 :44).
- Not a simulation claim, and not a claim about a programmer or external hardware. That domain has no nameable anchor.
- Not a theory of everything, not a final source code, and not self-validating.
- Not "gravity is computation" or any specific physics mapping. Those are bindings from other frames, provenanced as such.
- Not `grounded`. The derivation is a simulation checked for internal consistency, not a measurement.

## 5. What would settle it - the ground

A source-discriminating instrument, not a further argument. From *The Hard Problem is About Source*: an instrument that reads green "cannot comment on the source"; the spectrometer resolved it. Here:

- **Discriminator.** An instrument or proven bound that distinguishes a universe computing its own evolution from a merely computable universe and from neither, returning a determinate answer. Copeland et al. describe the Planck-scale interferometer class of experiment; they also note the evidence is currently absent and hard to obtain.
- **Operational definition.** A definition of literal cosmic computation at a stated level with a possible failure observation. That would give the claim an anchor (Fig 2 :29) and repair Fig 3's broken middle link.
- **New primitive.** A demonstration that computation adds a seventh primitive with a source gate and a shadow test, moving the closure wall (Fig 6 :14).
- **Standing falsifiers** from `01` §5: a positive discriminator result; a new primitive with a source gate; a terminal self-validating law; an operational failure-capable definition; or a derivation showing no computational description composes from the six.

## 6. The honest stop

**Unreached, not unreachable.** "Computer" is a word borrowed from finite machines and mapped onto the whole. Its declared primitives are "a human's careful guess", and declaring them makes the choice visible, not neutral. The answer stops at the limit of the available instruments and records the lineage so the stop is checkable. Fig 4 :30: "So take the round trip and measure the gap. Without it you are navigating by dead reckoning: confident about a position nobody checked." The gap here is the unmeasured difference between "regarded as" (Lloyd) and "is" (Zuse). *Indiscernibility is not identity.* Until an instrument fires, the verdict stands as `candidate`.

## 7. One-paragraph version for outreach

> The Tether does not answer yes or no to "is the universe a quantum computer", because that question is a bundle. It splits into literal identity, mere computability, and consequences for law, and those have different grounds. Under the six-term substrate, "computer" is not a primitive: it is an arrangement at a level, so calling the universe one adds no atom and cannot settle anything by itself. The literal claim is unreached rather than refuted, because no instrument currently distinguishes a universe that computes from one that is merely computable. The unqualified "everything computes" version has no possible counterexample, so it is a convention, not a verdict. And if "yes" were taken to mean that physical law is a finished source code, that would be a last room, which the framework forbids. What the Tether offers is the shape any answer must have, five falsifiers written in advance, and the kind of instrument that would close it. The center stays open until the ground settles it.
