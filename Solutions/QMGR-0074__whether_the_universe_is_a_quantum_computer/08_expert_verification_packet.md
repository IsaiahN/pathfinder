# Expert verification packet - the Tether on "the universe is a quantum computer"

**What this is:** a claim in a form a physicist, philosopher of computation or philosopher of science can check. Mode, derivation and refuters are stated before the conclusion, so a reader knows what to look for and what would settle it.

**Mode: `specified` on the shape claims, `general` on the barriers, not `grounded`.** No Planck-scale measurement is claimed. This is a shape and a route, not a result.

**Status:** a simulated derivation checked for internal consistency against the figures, not a ground reading. It cannot settle the claim it reports on.

## 1. The claim, in one paragraph

"Is the universe a quantum computer?" is a union, not one question. Literal identity, computability and consequences for law have different grounds and must not be averaged. Under the six-term substrate (three states, three structures, cross-kind composition only), "computer" is not a primitive: it is a level-relative arrangement, so calling the universe one adds no atom and does not move a closure wall. The literal identity claim is unreached rather than refuted, because no instrument currently discriminates a universe that computes from one that is merely computable. The unqualified "everything computes" version has no possible counterexample and therefore no anchor. A terminal "law is source code" conclusion is refused as a last room. What is offered is the required shape, five falsifiers written in advance, and the kind of instrument that would close the question.

## 3. What is explicitly not claimed

- Not "yes, the universe is a quantum computer" and not "no, it is not". The first is unreached; the second is forbidden by Fig 9 :44.
- Not a simulation claim, and not a programmer or external hardware. That domain has no nameable anchor (X1 in `01` §2).
- Not a theory of everything, not a final source code, not self-validating.
- Not a derivation of physics from computation, and no new primitive.
- Not `grounded`. Nothing here is asserted as settled by experiment.
- Not a claim about which computational structure occupies the fundamental slot. That is a declared `gap`.
- Not that Lloyd's "regarded as" and Zuse's "is" are the same claim. Fig 8 :50: "Convergent derivation and adopted import look identical in the contents. Only the record of where each came from separates them".

## 5. What would settle it

A discriminator, not an argument. From *The Hard Problem is About Source*: an instrument that reads green "cannot comment on the source"; the spectrometer resolved it. Here:

1. **Discriminator:** a measurement or bound that distinguishes literal computation from mere computability and from neither, returning a determinate answer. Copeland et al. describe Planck-scale interferometry as the closest existing class of test and report that it has not produced evidence either way.
2. **Operational definition:** a definition of literal cosmic computation with a stated level of abstraction and a possible failure observation. This gives the claim an anchor.
3. **Recomposition:** a source-gated seventh primitive, or a demonstration that no computational description composes from the six.

## 6. What an expert can check in twenty minutes

1. Read Fig 13 :115 and :43-44, Fig 11 :41, Fig 8 :39 and :37. Five short passages.
2. Check the union claim against the literature: are literal identity (Zuse), computability (physical computability thesis) and "regarded as" (Lloyd) three claims, not one? Copeland et al. 2017 makes the split explicitly.
3. Name one instrument that would discriminate literal computation from mere computability, or say why none is possible. If you can name one, C2 is reversed on the world side.
4. Decide whether the "law is source code, finished" conclusion would be a terminal frame. If not, explain what outside it remains importable.
5. Send the refutation. A failure is more useful than an endorsement.
