## The residual, restated as an effect

The question asks whether the correct fundamental description of the universe is that it *computes* (specifically, that it is a quantum computer), and what follows for the form of physical law if so. Two options and their consequences:

- **Option A (literal identity):** the universe is a quantum computer. Physical law becomes the program or source code; the universe computes its own future state; the expected marks are discreteness, finite computational resource, and a terminal "theory of everything".
- **Option B (refuse the identity, keep the contact question):** "computer" is a description at a level of abstraction, not a primitive. What is checkable is whether the universe's behaviour is generated by a finite, discrete computational process with measurable bounds, and what a discriminator would look like.

An answer would look like a **shape**: a source-discriminating instrument or a proven barrier that distinguishes "the universe literally computes" from "the universe is merely computable" from "neither", plus a statement of what changes in physical law under each. That shape is bound as an ordinary term in `01` and carried through every file.

## What is deliberately not here

- No personal emails of named individuals.
- No ranking of people by how likely they are to agree that the universe computes.
- No bulk-send plan.
- No claim that a `specified` shape is a `grounded` result.

Three reasons in the figures' own words:

1. **Agreement is not an anchor.** Fig 2 :35: "Alignment is triangulation, not negotiation. A mirror cannot correct you." Selecting recipients for receptiveness manufactures the correlation that makes their agreement worthless.
2. **A verifier must not hold the interiors.** Fig 10 :42: "A verifier that can reconstruct the claim cannot verify it." The first contact carries the residual and its refuter, never the framework.
3. **Prestige cannot settle an action.** The Recursive Self-Improvement Trap essay: "Prestige can fund an action. It can give an action a budget to be tried. But it cannot settle an action. Only the ground can settle it."

Legally: scraping personal emails for unsolicited bulk outreach breaches GDPR/CAN-SPAM-style rules in many jurisdictions. Public opt-in venues avoid that failure as well as the structural one.
