## Three findings in the wider literature that bear directly on this question

1. **The "yes" camp's strongest form is a model, not an identity.** Lloyd 2013 says the universe "can be regarded as a giant quantum computer". That is the convergent-derivation case in Fig 8 :50, where "Convergent derivation and adopted import look identical in the contents. Only the record of where each came from separates them". Do not present it as an identity claim the framework produced.
2. **The "no" camp conflicts with the "yes" camp, and a conflict goes back to the census.** Wharton 2025 says we must drop the assumption that the universe is a computer. Fig 2 :35: "Alignment is triangulation, not negotiation. A mirror cannot correct you." A conflict between two frames with different closures is a reason to re-census, never a reason to flip the verdict to whichever voice is louder.
3. **The strongest neutral source calls it wide open.** Copeland, Shagrir and Sprevak 2017 give the three obstacles (emergence, evidence, implementation) and explicitly say the evidence question is unknown. That is the `unreached` verdict from an independent field. Floridi 2017 independently gives the reason the unqualified claim cannot be a verdict: it has no possible counterexample. Two fields, different evidence pools, same shape. They corroborate the figures; they do not overrule them.

## Distribution: offer, do not push

- **Fig 10 :52** - "Bring a characterised residual, never the source." Sending the framework before anyone has described a residual sends a recording, not a generator. Lead with the union split and the refuter; offer links after.
- **Fig 10 :42** - "A verifier that can reconstruct the claim cannot verify it." A recipient who has absorbed the whole architecture before being asked for a verdict has joined the frame being checked.
- **Fig 9 :27-28** - "describe it before you go looking, or any frame you pick will seem to fit." Ask what they would expect to see if literal computation were true before giving them Lloyd or Wharton.
- **Fig 2 :40** - "agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool." Do not pool later agreement as corroboration, especially with readers who came from the same framework material.

**Recommended order in a reply:** (1) the shape claim and its refuter; (2) ask what they would count as a failure; (3) record their verdict on stage 1; (4) only then the links, with the note that the figures, not the summary, are the authority.

## The two-stage offer

- **Stage 1 before stage 2** is Fig 10 :52 and Fig 2 :45 "Frames range against the ground. They do not negotiate with it." The recipient checks the output without holding the framework's interior.
- **Stage 2 is opt-in by construction.** Once a recipient has absorbed the theory, their agreement stops being evidence. Their verdict on stage 1 must be recorded *before* any theory is shown.
- **Stage 3 sends a method, which is permitted.** Fig 4 :22: "Only methods cross up. Only head starts come down." The formula is a generator, so giving it away does not give away an answer.
- **Stage 3 still needs a ground per problem.** `THE_FORMULA.md` "Direction" says a frame cannot check the direction of its own question. Each user must write down what would refute their result before running it and must label the mode of what they get.
