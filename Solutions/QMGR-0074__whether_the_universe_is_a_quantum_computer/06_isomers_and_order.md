# Isomers, bonds and order: the three computational conditions

**Mode: `specified`.** This is a simulated derivation checked against the figures, not a ground reading (see `05_what_shape_means.md` §7).

## RESIDUAL

The word "computation" is doing at least three jobs in the question: identity (the universe *is* a computer), computability (its behaviour *can be computed*), and consequence (what law would be if either held). Fig 9 :55: "A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones." Three questions are owed:

1. Which **bond** does each condition use, and can they be told apart by the operators table?
2. Are the two positive conditions **isomers** over the six-term set, or merely members of a functional group?
3. What is the **dependency order** of the union, and where does the chain break?

## DERIVATION

### Q1 - Which bond does each condition use?

- Operators table :23-27: `≡` identity means "two names, one referent", test: "substitute one for the other. Is anything lost? No." C1 (the universe *is* a computer) is `≡`: it asserts that the two names have one referent.
- Operators table :29-33: `→` sequence means "A then B; B needs A to have happened", test: "swap them. Does it become a different thing? Yes." C2 (the universe's state at t *produces* its state at t+1 by computation) is `→` or `⇒` (production: "A produces B; B did not exist before").
- Fig 12 :49: "The seven bonds, and whether the order of the operands matters. Negation is the eighth operator and is not a bond."
- So the "yes" answer quietly swaps an ordered bond for a symmetric one. That is a bond type error, not a synthesis. Fig 12 :40: "same two atoms, three substances", and :41 "the bond is charged for what it could have been".
- C3 is not a bond at all; it is a comparison `⋛` between two consequences, and the Operators table defines `⋛` as "a threshold relation between two quantities". Applying it to "physical law before" and "physical law after" needs an instrument to say what is being compared. Mark `specified`, unmeasured.

**Falsifier, written before the run:** show that C1 and C2 use the same bond under the operators table's six tests, or that `⋛` can compare two laws without an instrument. If so, Q1 fails and the union collapses toward one question.

### Q2 - Are the two positive conditions isomers?

- Fig 12 :117: "isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."
- Fig 12 :124: "functional group. A substructure whose presence fixes the outcome. The group says what kind; the qualifier says which member."
- C1 and C2 drawn over the full shared atom set `{Space, Time, Energy, Gradient, Network, Spectrum}` differ only in which bond and which structure mediates which state pair. Under Fig 12 :117 they are **isomers**: same atoms, different arrangement, different substance. This is a real structural claim.
- As the two camps actually write them, C1 is "universe = universal computer" (the whole set plus a converter) and C2 is "universal quantum computers possess decoherent histories in which complex adaptive systems evolve with high probability" (Lloyd 1997). These share only the broad label "computation", analogous to two molecules sharing only one atom. Under Fig 12 :124 they are members of the same **functional group**, not isomers.
- The stronger isomer claim is the one worth testing: both over the full shared set, arranged differently. The weaker one is a family resemblance.

**Falsifier, written before the run:** if either condition can be written without an atom the other requires, the isomer claim fails and the functional-group reading stands. Triangulated for the figure definition (Fig 12) with the physics characterizations; single-source for the physics.

### Q3 - Dependency order and the broken link

Fig 3 :4-17 gives the chain: perception → vocabulary → objective → planning → learn and carry. The claim's chain is:

1. **Perception.** What actually registers? Law-governed, mathematically describable evolution. Reached (Fig 3 :5).
2. **Vocabulary.** Do we have a term for what the situation affords? "Computer", "computable", "simulation" are borrowed from a finite-machine frame and applied to the whole cosmos. This is the disputed link. Fig 3 :7: "it has terms for what things afford: what can be moved, opened, joined, blocked". Fig 3 :8: "what the search can reach".
3. **Objective.** Can we say what would count as progress here in our own terms? Not until a level and a failure are named. Floridi 2017: pancomputationalism "is simply not vulnerable to a refutation". Anderson and Piccinini 2017: the sensational claims are unwarranted once computation is distinguished from ordinary dynamics.
4. **Planning and carrying.** Blocked by 2 and 3.

Fig 3 :27: "A link with no instrument is not a link that works. It is a link nobody has looked at." The middle link (vocabulary → objective) is where this chain stops. Fig 3 :25-26 explains why: "Perception has obvious failures and obvious tests. Planning has obvious failures and obvious tests. The link between them, having terms for what a situation offers and being able to say what would count as progress, has neither, so it attracts the least attention and the fewest instruments."

**Falsifier, written before the run:** produce an operational definition of literal cosmic computation at a stated level with a possible failure observation. Then the middle link works and the chain continues. Until then, effort on C3 (consequences for law) lands above the break.

### Q4 - Why the "regarded as" reading is a convergent derivation, not an adopted import

- Lloyd 2013: "the universe can be regarded as a giant quantum computer." Copeland et al. 2017: Zuse's thesis is the literal version.
- Fig 8 :50-52: "Convergent derivation and adopted import look identical in the contents. Only the record of where each came from separates them, and the surplus lives only where the closures differ."
- Lloyd's computational model is derived from physics (energy bounds, Hilbert-space dimension, computational universality). Zuse's CA is an imported primitive. In the contents they resemble each other. The record separates them.
- Fig 6 :25: "An import adds no atom to the world." So even if "computer" were imported, it would add no primitive; it would only give our frame access to an arrangement the six already span.
- **Falsifier:** show a source gate and shadow test for "computer" as a new primitive and demonstrate that a closure wall moved. Then it is a genuine import and Q4 fails.

## CONCLUSION

The two positive conditions use different bonds (`≡` versus `→`/`⇒`) and are not the same question. Over the full shared atom set they are a real **isomer** claim; as the camps actually write them they are a **functional group**. The chain breaks at vocabulary → objective, because "computer" has no operational definition with a possible failure at a stated level. The "regarded as" reading is a convergent derivation whose provenance must be recorded, not an import that adds a primitive.

This framing keeps what is defensible without the part that fails: the union split needs no new primitive, the isomer test is checkable, and the broken link can be repaired by a single instrument or operational definition. `metric tensor is a computed bit` and `the universe is literally a Turing machine` remain unproven bindings.
