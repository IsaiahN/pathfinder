## 0. The claim under audit

> "Whether the universe is a quantum computer and what that would mean for physical law."
> (QM & GR Questions.txt line 96)

The claim is not one proposition. In effect terms it is a **union of three conditions** (Fig 9 :55):

- **C1, literal identity.** The universe *is* a computer (Zuse's thesis as reported by Copeland, Shagrir and Sprevak 2017; the "regarded as" claim in Lloyd 2013 is weaker).
- **C2, computability.** The behaviour of the universe *can be computed*, or can be simulated by a quantum computer (the physical computability thesis; Lloyd 1997, 1999, 2013).
- **C3, consequence.** If C1 or C2 holds, something follows for the form of physical law: law as program, discreteness, finite resource, a terminal theory.

**What an answer would look like (bound as an ordinary term, per the Formula's "Direction"):** a shape, namely a source-discriminating instrument or a proven barrier that would distinguish C1 from C2 from neither, together with a statement of which commitments in C3 actually change. The shape is held as a term with a residual so it can be refused; it is not a preference smuggled in. This binding is the refusable object of §3.

## 1. Residual

**R (the residual).** The question "is the universe a quantum computer" is asked from within a physics frame that already computes predictions. Its effect is: a reading (the universe evolves in a law-governed, mathematically describable way) is being used to name a source (computation). Several sources can give the same reading (Copeland et al. 2017 on Zuse versus the physical computability thesis; Floridi 2017 on levels of abstraction; Anderson and Piccinini 2017 on literal versus metaphorical pancomputationalism).

- **Arity:** three conditions, C1, C2, C3, over one observation. Not one hard question.
- **Symmetry:** C1 is an identity claim (`≡`, symmetric); C2 is a production or sequence claim (`→` / `⇒`, ordered). Mixing them is a bond type error, not a deep insight.
- **Scale:** first order inside physics (does a dynamics admit a finite computational description?); second order at the metaframe (is the whole universe a computer?), which is where Godel, Tarski and Chaitin bite (Fig 8 :34, :37).

R is indexed by slot, not averaged (Fig 1 :19: "The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated."). The three conditions keep separate verdicts.

## 4. Findings F1 to F8

### F1 — The question is a substrate question, and the figures dissolve it (decisive for the form of the answer)

Fig 11 :39 and :41:

> **"So the answerable question is not what intelligence is, but what makes a system capable. And capability is a property of agent-and-habitat, never of the agent alone."**
> **"Which is the same move as asking what generates lift rather than whether a thing is a bird. The substrate question dissolves; the contact question does not."**

"Calls the universe a computer" is "calls the thing a bird". The checkable question is "what generates the observed regularity", and it is answered by contact with a ground, never by a label. Fig 13 :43-44 adds the barrier half:

> **"A continuum was the seventh and was removed for failing exactly this"** ... **"A generating set must not contain what its own members produce."**

"Computer" is not a seventh irreducible kind. It is a composition already reachable from the six (states, structures and their bonds), so minting it as a primitive would violate the generating-set rule. **Verdict: the literal identity claim is not a domain question; the contact question is.** Mode `general` for the dissolution, `specified` for its application here. Refuter: exhibit a measurement under which "computer" and "not a computer" universes differ while the six-term substrate is held fixed.

### F2 — The question is a union, not one question

Fig 9 :55 and :57:

> **"A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones."**
> **"What remains is smaller and it is also categorically different: a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either."**

The world sources make the split precisely. Copeland, Shagrir and Sprevak (2017) distinguish Zuse's thesis (the universe literally is a cellular automaton) from "is the whole physical universe computable?" (the physical computability thesis). Lloyd (2013) says the universe "can be regarded as a giant quantum computer", which is a model, not an identity. Anderson and Piccinini (2017) distinguish literal from metaphorical pancomputationalism. Three conditions, three bonds. Operators table :73-74 warns that a vocabulary with subtraction but without negation can only accumulate: saying "not literally a computer" requires `¬`, not just removing the word. **Verdict: unbundle.** Mode `general`. Refuter: a single operational definition under which C1 and C2 are the same test.

### F3 — The literal claim is unreached, not refuted

Fig 8 :39, Fig 9 :44, Fig 6 :34-35, Fig 13 :134-135:

> **"So a search that finds nothing is never a proof of absence."**
> **"Only a sealed room can be searched to the end, and no frame is a sealed room. So searching and finding nothing proves nothing."**
> **"Where nothing is, there is no edge to extend from, and the search for a proto-instrument is a search for one that is already there."**
> **"Unreachable is a verdict here, not a silence,"** **"It cannot say which primitive is missing, because naming it would mean already holding it."**

Copeland et al. report "no experimental evidence at all" for Zuse's thesis, that "evidence either way would be hard to find", and that the answer "is currently unknown". The essay "How Intelligence Actually Works" says the simulation question "is below the union horizon" and that "'No trace exists' can never be confirmed by better instruments. A bigger and better instrument finding nothing is indistinguishable from a trace it still can't reach. You can only ever say nothing has been found yet." That is the figure's rule applied to this exact question. **Verdict: unreached. Never "false", never "true".** Mode `general` for the barrier, `specified` for the field. Refuter: a Planck-scale measurement (for example the Hogan interferometer class described by Copeland et al.) returning a positive for discreteness.

### F4 — As stated, the claim has no anchor and therefore no verdict

Fig 2 :29, :35; Fig 9 :60; Fig 10 :46:

> **"An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks."**
> **"Alignment is triangulation, not negotiation. A mirror cannot correct you."**
> **"Use filters for the budget and witnesses for the verdict. Never let a filter hand you a verdict."**
> **"A convention makes no claim about the world; a verdict and an atom both do."**

Floridi (2017): "pancomputationalism is simply not vulnerable to a refutation. By failing to look at computation at a specified level, all things become computational in some way." A claim for which every observation is evidence and none could refute it is a convention doing the work of a verdict, the exact failure Fig 10 :46 names. Anderson and Piccinini (2017) make the same move from the other side: once computation is distinguished from ordinary dynamics, "the most sensational pancomputationalist claims are unwarranted". **Verdict: no anchor, so no verdict until a level and a possible failure are stated.** Refuter: produce an observation that would falsify "the universe is a quantum computer" at a named level.

### F5 — "What it would mean for physical law" cannot be a final theory

Fig 7 :6, :31; Fig 13 :90; Fig 8 :34, :37:

> **"the limit: no last room"**
> **"no finite number of them ever arrives"**
> **"One chain is one route through a space that has no last route."**
> **"A frame cannot reach its own metaframe."**
> **"A frame cannot certify its own limit."**

If C1 were true in the strong sense, physical law would be the program of the universe, a terminal room after which nothing further can be asked. The framework forbids both the last room and self-certification. So the honest consequence is not "law is source code, finished"; it is "any computational law is a generator whose center stays open and whose lineage is recorded". This is the canonical "not a Theory of Everything" point (figures README, section titled "This is not a Theory of Everything - and that is the point"). **Verdict: C3, read as a terminal law, is refused.** Mode `general`. Refuter: a complete, self-validating computational law that settles every sub-question and whose frame can certify its own limit.

### F6 — Naming "computer" from the reading is the metamerism error

Fig 9 :30-31; Fig 11 :48:

> **"your words are borrowed too, so describe the effect rather than the cause"**
> **"You do not invent the list. You read it off the world, and what you cannot perceive or measure yet is the residual."**

The Hard Problem is About Source essay (framework-internal, maximal overlap, used for the canonical reading only): "If several paths create the same behavior, then behavior is a projection with lossy information." A universe that literally computes, a universe that is computable, and a universe merely described computationally produce the same reading. Naming "computer" from that reading confuses projection with source. Polak and Krzanowski (2019) add the borrowed-words version: the Turing model's "limitations" are imported onto the Universe. **Verdict: the name is a binding from the observer's frame, provenanced as such, not a derivation.** Mode `specified`. Refuter: an instrument that discriminates among those three sources at the same behavioral reading.

### F7 — "Computer" is a level-relative molecule, not a primitive and not an import that moves the wall

Fig 12 :103-104; Fig 13 :115; Fig 4 :33; Fig 8 :50:

> **"Composition is recursive, and the level decides what is irreducible."**
> **"A settled molecule becomes an atom for whatever composes over it. So irreducible is a fact about a level and never about the world."**
> **"The set is closed. The arrangements are not. That gap is why import exists."**
> **"Which is why there is no top and no infinite regress."**
> **"Convergent derivation and adopted import look identical in the contents. Only the record of where each came from separates them"**

A computational description is an arrangement of the six-term set at some level. It becomes an atom only when settled at that level. It adds no atom to the world (Fig 6 :25: "An import adds no atom to the world."). **Verdict: the "computer" description is a binding onto held atoms.** Mode `general` for the level rule, `specified` for the physics binding. Refuter: show that the description introduces a seventh primitive with a source gate and a shadow test, and that it moves the wall.

## 5. Pre-registered refuter (written before the conclusion)

Required before any outreach. A falsifier written before the run is a design instrument. The shape claim is refuted if any of the following fires:

1. **Discriminating instrument.** A measurement or proven bound distinguishes "the universe literally computes its evolution" from "the universe is merely computable" and from "neither", and returns a positive for literal computation.
2. **New primitive with a source gate.** A demonstration that "computer" adds a seventh primitive to the six-term set, with provenance and a shadow test, and that it moves the closure wall.
3. **Terminal law.** A closed, complete computational law of physics that settles all sub-questions and certifies its own limit (breaks Fig 7 :6 and Fig 8 :37).
4. **Operational definition.** A definition of literal cosmic computation at a stated level of abstraction with a possible failure observation, satisfying the anchor test (breaks F4).
5. **Recomposition failure.** A derivation showing that no computational description composes from the six-term set at any level (breaks F7).

If any fires, the verdict is withdrawn or rewritten. Until one is run, no part of this may be labelled `grounded`.

## 6. Recommendation

1. Unbundle C1, C2 and C3 before any further search; do not answer "the universe is a quantum computer" as one yes/no (F2).
2. State the level of abstraction and a possible failure in the same sentence as any identity claim (F4).
3. Ask for a discriminator, not for agreement. The right first contact is "what would you count as a failure of this shape claim?" (Fig 9 :27-28: "describe it before you go looking, or any frame you pick will seem to fit").
4. Refuse the "source code / final law" framing. Offer a generator with an open center and a recorded lineage (F5, Fig 4 :22-25: "Only methods cross up. Only head starts come down.").
5. Mark the specific structure as `gap`, not as a placeholder to be filled by whoever is most enthusiastic.

### Seven-question recursive derivation (summary)

1. **Is this one question?** No. Fig 9 :55 union. Three conditions with separate grounds.
2. **Is "computer" a primitive?** No. Fig 13 :43-44 and :115. It is a composition; the set of kinds is closed.
3. **Is the literal claim settled?** No. Fig 8 :39, Fig 9 :44. No instrument returns a discriminating reading.
4. **Does "regarded as a quantum computer" (Lloyd) assert identity?** No. It is a model, a convergent derivation from a differing closure; Fig 8 :50 requires the record to separate it from an adopted import.
5. **Does the "no" camp (Wharton, Floridi) settle it?** No. Wharton conflicts with Lloyd, and a conflict returns to the census; it does not flip a verdict. Fig 2 :35.
6. **Does the "it would mean law is source code" step close?** No. Fig 7 :6, Fig 8 :37. No last room, no self-certification.
7. **Route.** Fig 9 :30-31: describe the effect before choosing the frame. The residual is the metamerism of "computation"; the frames were named first in the literature, so the mapping is an import until provenanced.

### Updated verdict

The shape survives: the identity question dissolves into a contact question; the literal version is unreached rather than refuted; an unfalsifiable version has no anchor and therefore no verdict; and a terminal computational law is forbidden. Everything specific in the strong "yes" reading fails: it adds no primitive, it names a source from a projection, it is below the union horizon, and it would close the last room if taken as final. **Strains after re-census: none hidden.** One live item remains external to the figures: whether any instrument can be built that discriminates literal computation from mere computability. That is the `gap`, stated in effect terms.
