**Subject:** One checkable output on "the universe is a quantum computer"

> Hi [name],
>
> I have one specific output I would like you to check against what you already know. I am not asking you to read a theory first, and I am not asking you to agree with it. If the output does not survive, there is nothing else to look at.
>
> **The output (`specified`, not `grounded`):** the question "is the universe a quantum computer?" is not one question. It is at least three: (1) does the universe literally *compute*, (2) is its behaviour merely *computable*, and (3) what would follow for physical law if either held. Those have different grounds and must not be averaged.
>
> Under the six-term substrate (space, time, energy as states; spectrum, gradient, network as structures; composition across the two kinds and never within one), "computer" is not a primitive. It is an arrangement at some level, and the set of kinds is closed while the arrangements are not. So a literal identity claim cannot be settled by pointing at the arrangement. It needs a source-discriminating instrument: something that would distinguish a universe that computes its own evolution from one that is merely computable and from one that is neither. No such instrument is on the table. The claim is therefore **unreached, not refuted**.
>
> Two further limits follow from the same framework. First, a description for which every observation counts as evidence and no observation could refute it is a convention, not a verdict. So the version that says "everything is computation at some level" has no anchor. Second, if the answer were "physical law is the program, finished", that would be a terminal explanation after which nothing further can be asked. The framework's rule is that there is no last room, so any computational law stays a generator with an open center.
>
> **What I am not claiming:** that the universe is a simulation, that there is a programmer or external hardware, that the metric tensor or any other object *is* a computed bit, or that this framework settles the question. "Computer" here is a binding from our frame, and convergent derivation and adopted import look identical in the contents; only the record separates them. I am also not claiming a theory of everything.
>
> **What would refute the shape claim (written before this message):**
> 1. an instrument or proven bound that distinguishes literal computation from mere computability and returns a positive for literal computation;
> 2. a demonstration that "computer" adds a seventh primitive to the six-term set and moves the closure wall;
> 3. a complete, self-validating computational law that settles every sub-question;
> 4. an operational definition of literal cosmic computation with a stated level and a possible failure observation;
> 5. a derivation showing no computational description composes from the six-term set at any level.
>
> If any one holds, I withdraw the claim. A one-line "this fails because..." is more useful to me than agreement. If you would rather not engage, no reply is needed.
>
