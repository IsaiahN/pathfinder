## 0. The question stated as a residual in effect terms

The question asks whether the symbol ψ (the quantum state assigned by a preparation) is (A) a real physical state of the world, or (B) only a representation of a knower's knowledge.

Stated as an effect, not a label:

- A preparation is an action `a`. The theory attaches a state ψ to it. The library predicts an outcome through `Γ(b,a)`, and the measurement returns `o′`. The loop's job is to reduce `R = |Γ(b,a) − o′|` per slot.
- The dispute is **not** about the prediction. Both readings reproduce the same statistics in their domains. The dispute is about **which term ψ binds to**: a state in the world, or the belief `b` of the frame doing the predicting. This is a **binding residual**, not a prediction residual.
- Option A leads to: ψ is world furniture; measurement update must be a physical mechanism (a collapse, a branching, a hidden-variable dynamics).
- Option B leads to: ψ is a state of knowledge; measurement update is a belief update, and no physical collapse is owed.

Binding an answer as an ordinary term before deriving (formula step "Direction"): *"make the sought-for shape an ORDINARY TERM - write down what an answer would look like, bind it, and let it accrue a residual like anything else."* The bound shape here is:

> **SHAPE:** ψ is a term in Γ that predicts `o′` but is not itself `o′`; the ontic and epistemic readings are two bindings of that one term, and what is owed between them is a structure (a source-discriminating instrument), not a further argument.

This shape is written down before the census so it can be refused by the same test as everything else.

## 3. Findings F1..F8

### F1: The framework's own symbols put ψ on the b-side of the b/o′ divide (D1, `specified`, triangulated)

Symbols table :19-21 binds `b` as:

> **"What the system takes to be the case before it acts. Formed from its own history and from what it inherited, mixed by w. Not the observation."**

Symbols table :36-38 binds `Γ(b,a)` as:

> **"What the library says should happen, given belief b and action a."**

Symbols table :31-32 binds `o′` as:

> **"What actually happened. Read o-prime."**

The wavefunction is attached at preparation time and is used to compute the prediction. It is never a single observed outcome. In the loop's own vocabulary it therefore occupies the `b` slot: what the frame takes to be the case before it acts. Symbols table :23-25 adds that the weighting `w` is *"How far the belief leans on private history versus inherited prior"* and is learnable from past outcomes. An epistemic reading maps directly onto this machinery. An ontic reading maps onto a state in the world, which the Symbols table does not provide a slot for at first order.

**This is a functional claim, not an ontological one.** Verdict: ψ *functions* as a belief-side term in Γ. It does not follow that ψ *is* knowledge. Metamerism blocks that step (F3). Triangulated across the b, Γ(b,a) and o′ entries; the three have low overlap in what they assert (role in prediction, composition of prediction, what actually happened).

### F2: A structure must sit between the two readings (D1, `general`, triangulated)

Fig 13 :74-75:

> **"Everything else is a composition across the two kinds, never within one."**
> **"Two states never bond directly. It is not space-time but space, a structure, time; and two structures join only through a state."**

The ontic reading (ψ is a world-state) and the epistemic reading (ψ is a knowledge-state) are both state-like. By the substrate's own composition rule they cannot be bonded directly by an argument. A structure must sit between them. That structure is the instrument, the measurement procedure that discriminates the source. This is the figure-licensed **shape** of an answer: not "ontic wins" and not "epistemic wins", but "an instrument is owed in the slot".

Fig 9 :32: *"you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself."* The figures return a kind of thing, a source-discriminating structure, not the name of the winning interpretation. General mode, triangulated across Fig 13 :74-75 and Fig 9 :32 (one governs composition, one governs what a frame can return).

### F3: Identical predictions do not identify the binding (D2/D3, `specified`, triangulated)

Fig 8 :50-52:

> **"Convergent derivation and adopted import look identical in the contents. Only the record of where each came from separates them, and the surplus lives only where the closures differ."**

The ontic and epistemic readings are designed to agree on measurement statistics. From the contents (the predictions) they are indiscernible. *The Hard Problem is About Source*, read 2026-11-23: *"An instrument that can't distinguish these cases demonstrates its own inadequacy, it cannot claim anything substantial about what is being measured or what causes it."* And: *"If several paths create the same behavior, then behavior is a projection with lossy information (it cannot define consciousness in full)."*

This is the metamerism error applied to quantum states: reading the source (world or knowledge) off a prediction that several sources can produce. What is owed is a better instrument, not a name. Triangulated across Fig 8 and the essay, but the essay shares the framework author and evidence pool, so it is weak corroboration, not an independent witness.

### F4: The world's own no-go results are conditional, hence not yet an anchor (D2, `specified`, single-source per result)

World sources read 2026-11-23:

- Pusey, Barrett and Rudolph, *On the reality of the quantum state*, Nature Phys. 8, 475 (2012), arXiv:1111.3328: *"any model in which a quantum state represents mere information about an underlying physical state of the system, and in which systems that are prepared independently have independent physical states, must make predictions which contradict those of quantum theory."* This pushes toward ontic **given preparation independence**.
- Spekkens, *In defense of the epistemic view of quantum states: a toy theory*, Phys. Rev. A 75, 032110 (2007), arXiv:quant-ph/0401052: *"A wide variety of quantum phenomena are found to have analogues within this toy theory... The diversity and quality of these analogies is taken as evidence for the view that quantum states are states of incomplete knowledge rather than states of reality."* This keeps epistemic readings viable **under weaker assumptions**, and names what the toy model fails to reproduce (Bell violations, Kochen-Specker).
- Harrigan and Spekkens, *Einstein, incompleteness, and the epistemic view of quantum states*, Found. Phys. 40, 125 (2010), arXiv:0706.2661: *"Does the quantum state represent reality or our knowledge of reality? In making this distinction precise, we are led to a novel classification of hidden variable models of quantum theory."* This supplies the classification the question needs.

Fig 2 :29-30: an anchor *"does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks"* and *"If it moves when you push on it, it is not an anchor."* The PBR result moves when the preparation-independence assumption is pushed; the Spekkens result moves when the toy model's knowledge-balance principle is pushed. Both are instruments that sharpen the closure; neither is the ground that settles the reference question. This domain is a **`gap`** for the verdict, not a silence: *"Unreachable is a verdict here, not a silence"* (Fig 13 :134). Mark each world result single-source (its own theorem and assumptions); do not average them into a consensus.

Filters versus witnesses, Fig 9 :60: *"Use filters for the budget and witnesses for the verdict. Never let a filter hand you a verdict."* The no-go theorems are filters on which model classes survive; they are not witnesses for the verdict.

### F5: The ground is the only thing that can settle reference, and agreement is not it (D3, `specified`, triangulated)

Fig 2 :35: *"Alignment is triangulation, not negotiation. A mirror cannot correct you."*
Fig 2 :40: *"disqualifying for a verdict: agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool"*
Fig 2 :36: *"An anchor is legitimate when the question is constitutively about it."*

The question is constitutively about the ground (what the world does, what an instrument returns), so agreement among interpretations is the wrong instrument. Hüllermeier and Waegeman, *Aleatoric and Epistemic Uncertainty in Machine Learning*, Machine Learning (2021), arXiv:1910.09457, distinguish two uncertainty types in a low-overlap field: *"this includes the importance of distinguishing between (at least) two different types of uncertainty, often referred to as aleatoric and epistemic."* That is a corroboration of the *shape* (the world/knowledge distinction is a reusable compression axis), not a verdict on ψ. In this simulated run D3 draws on one evidence pool, so its agreement carries no independent weight.

### F6: "ψ is real" is a binding held as a preference, and must be made an ordinary term before it can be refused (D1/D3, `specified`, triangulated)

Operators table :57-58: *"a refutation is a negation, which is why a vocabulary without it can only accumulate."*

### F7: The instrument must be improved from one already returning something (D2, `specified`, single-source)

Fig 6 :32-34: *"An instrument is not built from a description. It is improved from a worse instrument already returning something."* And: *"Nobody specified a telescope from an account of Mars."*

Here the worse instruments already exist: preparation procedures and measurement statistics return ψ-dependent readings that fail to resolve the source. PBR and Spekkens sharpen the assumptions those readings rest on. That is an **INWARD** track (sharpen in our own terms). An **OUTWARD** track would import a frame whose closure predicts a source-discriminating measurement, for example a source-discriminating protocol from quantum optics. Fig 6 :30-31 keeps them opposed: *"Neither answer tells you much about the other. Run them as two tracks, and never read one against the other."*

## 4. Strain test and re-census

**Strain 1, resolved by re-census.** One might try to bind the ontic reading to Fig 13's energy state: *"conserved, finite, directionless alone"* (Fig 13 :14). That strains immediately. ψ is not conserved under measurement update, is defined on configuration space for many-particle systems, and is not finite in the region sense the substrate uses. By the strain rule, the line governs a different quantity: it governs the energy state, not ψ. Re-census: ψ is **not one of the six primitives**. It is a b-side predictive term in Γ. The ontic reading is a binding from the physics frame onto a formal object, not a new member of the substrate, and not an import that moves the wall (Fig 6 :23: *"No frame escapes its own closure. The bound moves only on contact with a frame whose closure differs."*).

**Strain 2, named and left live.** Fig 2 :36 says an anchor is legitimate when the question is constitutively about it. Is the question "is ψ real or knowledge" constitutively about an instrument? The epistemic side says the question is constitutively about a knower, whose belief is `b`; the ontic side says it is constitutively about a world-state. The disagreement is about which anchor is legitimate, and that is itself a strain. It is not resolved by fiat. It is recorded, and it is one of the things a refuter must address.

After re-census: **two strains named, none hidden**, one resolved, one live.

## 5. Pre-registered refuter (written before the conclusion)

Required before any outreach: a falsifier written before the run is a design instrument, not only an honesty device. The shape claim in §0 is refuted if any of the following holds:

1. **Ground refutation.** An experiment discriminates the ontic from the epistemic binding under assumptions both sides accept (for example a loophole-free preparation-independence test, or a source-discriminating measurement that distinguishes a world-state from a knowledge-state), so that one binding settles the residual at the ground.
2. **Loop refutation.** ψ is shown to enter the loop as `o′`, directly read off a single measurement outcome, rather than as `b` in `Γ(b,a)`. That breaks F1.
3. **Substrate refutation.** ψ's ontic binding is shown to compose legally as a state term in the six-term set (conserved, finite, directionless alone, or a legal composition across the two kinds). That breaks F2 and the re-census of Strain 1.

If any fires, the shape claim is withdrawn. Until one is run, no part of this may be labelled `grounded`.

### Three-question route check (SEARCHED, INWARD, OUTWARD)

Per formula step 7, asked in order and kept distinct:

1. **SEARCHED first:** is the answer in the current closure and merely unfound? Partly. PBR and Spekkens are inside the physics frame's closure and give conditional answers; more search over interpretations would sweep a region already swept. 2. **INWARD second:** extend the instrument from one already returning something. Loophole-free tests and sharper preparation-independence protocols are the live inward track (F7).
3. **OUTWARD third:** import a frame whose closure predicts the shape of a source-discriminating measurement. A candidate imported protocol needs a source gate (provenance) and a shadow test (explains a gap), Fig 6 :12-13. No import is made in this simulation; it is named as the third instruction.
