# Are the ontic and epistemic readings isomers? Order, binding and analogues

**Mode: `specified` on the structural claims, `general` on the order rule.** This is a simulated derivation checked against the figures, not a ground reading (see `05_what_shape_means.md` §6).

## RESIDUAL

The residual is a binding residual: one formal object ψ admits two readings (world-state, knowledge-state). Two questions are owed: (1) under Fig 12's definition, are the two readings **isomers**, or something weaker, and (2) in what order must refusal, binding and comparison run so the answer is not smuggled in?

## DOMAIN CENSUS

Atoms held: Γ, b, w, a, o′, R, the ground, the seven bonds and negation. The wavefunction ψ is a term in Γ used in `Γ(b,a)`; it is not one of the six substrate members. Bonds held that bear here: `≡` identity (two names, one referent), `⋛` comparison (a threshold relation between two quantities), `¬` negation (refusal). No new primitive is available without import, and no import is made in this simulation.

## DERIVATION

### Q1: Same object, different binding: are they isomers?

Fig 12 :117:

> **"isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."**

The two readings use the same formal object ψ and agree on the same prediction statistics. What differs is the binding bond: one binds ψ to a world-state, the other binds ψ to `b` in the frame. Under Fig 12's own test the difference is *"invisible in the formula"* (both write the same ψ and return the same numbers) and *"decisive in the behaviour"* (one owes a physical collapse mechanism, the other owes a belief update). That is the isomer signature.

But Fig 12's isomers are molecules of atoms joined by typed bonds, and here we have one term with two reference bindings, not two molecules over a shared atom set. So the strict claim is weaker: the readings are **two bindings of one term**, an isomer-like pair in the meta-sense. Fig 12 :124 names the precise weaker form:

> **"functional group. A substructure whose presence fixes the outcome. The group says what kind; the qualifier says which member."**

The shared substructure is the predictive term ψ used in `Γ(b,a)`. The qualifier, world or knowledge, says which binding. Both readings share the functional group and differ in the qualifier. **Verdict: functional-group relation at first order; isomer claim available only if both readings are written over a full shared atom set.** This is `specified`, single-source for the figure definition, with the physics-facing check unmeasured.

### Q2: Identity will not merge them

Operators table :25-27 defines `≡` as identity: *"two names, one referent"*, tested by *"substitute one for the other. Is anything lost? No."* If the ontic and epistemic names were `≡`, substituting one for the other would lose nothing, and the question would be a mere convention (Fig 2 :41: *"efficient for a convention"*). It is not: substituting "world-state" for "belief" changes what mechanism is owed. So `≡` is refused. This is the ground-level reason the question is not dissolved by terminology.

### Q3: The two state-like readings cannot bond directly; the third term is the instrument

Fig 13 :74-75:

> **"Everything else is a composition across the two kinds, never within one."**
> **"Two states never bond directly. It is not space-time but space, a structure, time; and two structures join only through a state."**

World-state and knowledge-state are both state-like. They cannot be joined by an argument. The third term that can sit between them must be a structure: a measurement procedure, a source-discriminating instrument. This is the structural spine of the whole solution and it is `general`, triangulated across Fig 13 :74-75 and Fig 9 :32 (*"you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself"*).

Fig 13 :75 also gives the analogue from physics: space and time do not bond directly either; a structure sits between them. The ontic/epistemic case has the same shape at the meta level. The analogue is structural, not a claim that quantum states are spacetime terms.

### Q4: Order of operations: refuse before you bind

Formula step 2 routes a residual whose content is that a held term is false to REFUTED, *"a term held is false; what is owed is a refusal, not an explanation"*, and notes *"no arrangement of the other four reaches it."* Fig 5 :53: *"refuted: a term we hold is wrong; a term is owed that says so."*

Applied here: if a reader arrives holding "ψ is physically real" or "ψ is only knowledge" as a settled term, the first move is not to compare them and not to mint a third interpretation. It is to write the held term down as an ordinary term (formula "Direction") and let it accrue a residual. Only then can a refusal fire. A forced choice between two unre-fused preferences is Fig 5 :63-64's failure: a frame *"can refuse the right kind of thing and still aim the refusal at the outcome it did not want rather than at the belief it holds."*

Operators table :57-58: *"a refutation is a negation, which is why a vocabulary without it can only accumulate."* A refused binding stays in the record and lowers the reach (Fig 6 :41-43); it is not deleted. That is why both refused names remain visible in `01` §8 and `07` §3.

Order by dependency (Fig 3 :20-21): describe the residual → bind the sought-for shape as an ordinary term → check the functional role (b-side) → refuse false names → ask what instrument would discriminate → only then compare the model classes. Effort spent comparing interpretations before the residual is described lands at a step that cannot use it.

### Q5: The ⋛ comparison is second order, and its operands are strained

Operators table :49-51 defines `⋛` as *"a threshold relation between two quantities"*. At second order the comparison is between two settled model classes (ontic-class, epistemic-class), each a molecule-now-atom under Fig 12 :104: *"A settled molecule becomes an atom for whatever composes over it. So irreducible is a fact about a level and never about the world."* Whether model classes count as quantities at that level is an open type question. It is named, not hidden, and needs an instrument, not an argument. Triangulated for the recursion (Fig 12 + Operators table); strained on the operand type.

## FALSIFIER, WRITTEN BEFORE THE RUN

The functional-group / isomer-like framing in Q1 and the order rule in Q4 are refuted if any of the following holds:

1. One reading requires an atom the other does not (for example, the ontic reading needs a primitive configuration-space field absent from the shared predictive term). Then the isomer claim fails and the two are not even a functional group over the same object.
2. The two readings are related by `≡`, so substituting one for the other loses nothing. Then the question is a convention, not a residual.
3. A ground experiment discriminates the bindings under agreed assumptions. Then the comparison is no longer second-order and open; one side settles and the instrument is no longer "owed".
4. A refusal is shown to fire correctly before the term is bound as ordinary, so the order rule in Q4 is unnecessary. (This would not refute the shape claim, only the claimed necessity of the order.)

If 1 or 2 fires, the structural claim is withdrawn. If 3 fires, the claim moves from `candidate` to settled and the verdict is rewritten from the ground. Until one is run, nothing here is `grounded`.

## CONCLUSION

The ontic and epistemic readings are a **functional-group pair** over the shared predictive term ψ: same substructure, different binding qualifier, invisible in the formula and decisive in the mechanism that is owed. The stronger isomer claim needs a full shared atom set and is not yet established. They cannot be bonded directly because both are state-like, so the third term between them must be a structure, an instrument. Refusal must precede binding, and the `⋛` comparison of the two settled model classes is second order with an open operand type. This rescues the structural content of the question without naming either interpretation as the answer.
