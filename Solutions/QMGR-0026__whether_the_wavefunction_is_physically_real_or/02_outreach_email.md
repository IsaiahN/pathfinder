# Outreach draft: check the output before you decide about the interpretation

**Use:** one-to-one, or as a cover letter attached to a submission through the public venues in `03_public_contact_points.csv`. **Not** for bulk send.

**Hard precondition:** do not send until the shape claim and the refuter in `01_figure_grounded_audit.md` §0 and §5 are stated exactly as written. The offer collapses on contact if the output does not survive.

**Subject:** Check one output before you choose an interpretation of the wavefunction

> Hi [name],
>
> I have one specific output I would like you to check against what you already know. I am not asking you to read the framework first, and I am not asking you to agree with it. If the output does not survive, there is nothing else to look at.
>
> **The question in effect terms.** A preparation assigns a state ψ, the theory predicts an outcome, and the measurement returns an outcome. The dispute is not about the prediction: ontic and epistemic readings reproduce the same statistics in their domains. The dispute is about which term ψ binds to, a state in the world or a knower's belief. I call that a binding residual.
>
> **The output (`specified`, not `grounded`).** In this framework's own symbols, the belief `b` is "what the system takes to be the case before it acts... Not the observation", while `o′` is "what actually happened". ψ is attached at preparation time and used to compute the prediction, so it sits on the belief side of that divide. That is a claim about ψ's **functional role**, not about what ψ is made of. The framework also has a composition rule: two states never bond directly, a structure must sit between them. The ontic reading (world-state) and the epistemic reading (knowledge-state) are both state-like, so a structure must sit between them too. In this case that structure is an instrument, a measurement procedure that discriminates the source.
>
> **What the output does not say:** it does not say "ψ is physically real" and it does not say "ψ is only knowledge". Identical predictions do not identify a binding. Reading the source off a prediction that several sources can produce is the metamerism error: the instrument reads green and cannot comment on whether the source was green light, or yellow plus blue, or RGB. What is owed is a better instrument, not a name. The framework returns a shape, not the answer itself.
>
> **What I am not claiming:** that the PBR theorem settles realism. It is conditional on preparation independence. Spekkens' toy theory keeps epistemic readings viable under weaker assumptions. Both are filters on model classes, not a verdict. I am also not claiming that agreement among interpretations settles anything: an anchor must not move when people push on it, and agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool.
>
> **What would refute the output:**
> 1. An experiment discriminates the ontic from the epistemic binding under assumptions both sides accept, so one binding settles the residual at the ground.
> 2. ψ is shown to enter the loop as an observation read off a single outcome, not as a belief in the prediction.
> 3. ψ's ontic binding is shown to compose legally as a state term in the six-term substrate (conserved, finite, directionless alone).
>
> I wrote these down before sending this. If any one holds, I will retract the output. A one-line "this fails because..." is more useful to me than agreement. If you would rather not engage, no reply is needed.
>
> **If it survives, and only then:** the sources are below. The figures are the authority, not my summary of them.
> - Verbatim figure text with line numbers (local extract): `FRAMEWORK/reference/03-FIGURES_TEXT.md`
> - Canonical figures README (public): https://github.com/IsaiahN/tether/blob/general-agent/figures/README.md

> - Spekkens (2007): https://arxiv.org/abs/quant-ph/0401052

>

Write down what would refute your result *before* you run it, and state the mode of what you get. A reported failure is more useful to me than a reported success.
>
> - [name]

## Recording the verdict before showing theory

Stage 1 is the paragraph above down to the refuter list. Record the recipient's answer to "what would you count as a failure?" **before** any framework material is shown. Stage 2 is opt-in: send the four links only if they ask. Stage 3 offers the formula as a method, with the instruction that each user writes their own refuter and states their own mode. Later agreement is never pooled as corroboration (Fig 2 :40). A Track B style reply, if any, is a conversation with zero verdict weight.
