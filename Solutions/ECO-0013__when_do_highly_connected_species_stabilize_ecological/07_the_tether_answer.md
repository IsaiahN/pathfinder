# The Tether's answer on highly connected species and network stability

**Mode: `specified` - not `grounded`.** This is a shape and a set of conditions, not a result. Simulated derivation checked against the figures (`05_what_shape_means.md` section 6), not a ground reading.

## 1. The residual it answers

A connectance story predicts that more links damp a perturbation, so high-degree species stabilize. Cascade cases show the opposite: disturbing one hub takes many partners with it. The residual is the gap between that prediction and what a web actually does, measured per species slot (Fig 1 :19: "The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated."). It is a **union compound**, not one hard problem: a hub sits in several relations at once, so a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either (Fig 9). It has its own loop and its own ground.

## 2. The shape, with verdicts per domain

1. **A hub has no fixed valence.** Fig 11 :49-51: "An actor's valence is relative to the residual, not a property it carries." "The same actor is a benefactor to one residual and a malfactor to another, so mutualism and parasitism are relations rather than kinds." "No standing list of good actors survives a change of residual." Stabilizing and destabilizing are relations, not species kinds. Verdict: **single-source** (Fig 11), `general` on the rule.
2. **The flip condition is overlap of responses.** When the partners a hub links respond independently, the union has a surplus: one failing is compensated by another that does not fail with it. When their responses are correlated, they are "one frame wearing two names" (Fig 8 :49), the union adds nothing, and the hub becomes a single point of failure. Fig 2 :31-32: "Independence, between two frames, is how little they draw on the same evidence." "Independence is what makes agreement mean something." Verdict: **triangulated** across Fig 2 and Fig 8 (low overlap between the anchoring law and the union-surplus law), `specified` in application.
3. **Links are typed, not interchangeable.** Fig 12 :46-47: "A bond is the typed relation between two operands. There are seven, they are not interchangeable, and which one holds is not recoverable from the operands." A degree count is the flat-formula reading: "A set of ingredients is not one. The bond is the rest of the information, and it is the half nothing was recording." Verdict: **single-source** (Fig 12), `general`.
4. **The decisive cascade is second or third order.** Fig 11 :52-54: "And contact cascades. Scoping the habitat to direct contact is how DDT reached the eagles: first-order enumeration, second-order effect, and the residual that mattered was three steps out." Degree is a first-order statistic; the sign of a hub's contribution is a higher-order result. Verdict: **single-source** (Fig 11), `specified`.
5. **Stability is per slot, not an average.** Fig 1 :22: "Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average." A global connectance near a mean is compatible with one live slot about to fail. Verdict: **single-source** (Fig 1), `general`.
6. **Managed and unmaintained webs differ.** Fig 11 :61-63: "The seat's power is asymmetric. Introduction is available; exclusion is not." "Which is why a managed ecosystem is permanent work rather than a solved state." Adding a generalist is an introduction with a cascade that can be unenumerated; it cannot be undone by subtracting the habitat. Verdict: **single-source** (Fig 11), `specified`.
7. **The ground decides, not agreement.** Fig 2 :35: "Alignment is triangulation, not negotiation. A mirror cannot correct you." Fig 7 :13: "agreement compounds, evidence does not". Verdict: **triangulated** (Fig 2 and Fig 7), `general`.

Verdicts are kept separate and never averaged. The only triangulated figure verdicts are the flip condition and the ground-over-agreement rule.

## 3. What it explicitly does not say

- Not a roster of stabilizing species. A named list that keeps its sign across a residual change would refute Fig 11 :49-51.
- Not that connectance alone predicts persistence. Connectance is a budget filter, not a witness (Fig 9 :60).
- Not that direct neighbours are sufficient. The cascade runs further out (Fig 11 :52-54).
- Not that high connectivity is always good or always bad. That is precisely the question the figures refuse to answer in the abstract.
- Not a new primitive. "Network" is already one of the six structures (Fig 13 :23-24). What is owed is instrumentation, not a seventh term.
- Not `grounded`. No perturbation experiment or interaction-strength measurement is claimed here.

## 4. What would settle it - the ground

A source-discriminating instrument, not a further argument. *The Hard Problem is About Source*: an instrument that reads green "cannot comment on the source"; the spectrometer resolved it. Here the corresponding questions are:

- **Overlap Test:** measure the correlation among the responses of a hub's partners under perturbation. Do low-overlap hubs buffer and high-overlap hubs concentrate failure? This is the load-bearing test; it bears directly on the triangulated verdict.
- **Cascade Test:** compare a direct-link-only model with a cascade-enumerating model against observed first extinctions. Does the third-order residual carry the sign?
- **Slot Test:** does a per-slot residual predict the first extinction where a global connectance does not?
- **Bond Test:** do two webs with the same species set but different typed arrangements behave differently under the same perturbation? If not, the isomer claim fails.
- **Management Test:** in a managed web, can an introduced hub be removed as cleanly as it was added? If yes, the asymmetry claim fails.

Standing refuters from `01` section 4 apply to all of the above. Any one firing narrows or withdraws the shape.

## 5. The honest stop

**Unreached, not unreachable.** The figures give the condition (overlap of responses) but no numeric threshold for how much asynchrony flips the sign. That is an instrument question, not an argument. Fig 13 :134: "Unreachable is a verdict here, not a silence". Fig 8 :39: "a search that finds nothing is never a proof of absence". The six-term set is "a human's careful guess at reality's compression axes" (essay, *They're Scanning Books*); declaring it makes the choice visible, not neutral. The answer stops at the limit of the available instruments and records the lineage so the stop is checkable.

## 6. One-paragraph version for outreach

> The Tether does not say that highly connected species are good or bad for ecological networks. It says that a hub's sign is a relation, not a property: the same species stabilizes one residual and destabilizes another. The condition is overlap. When a hub's partners respond independently, their union has a surplus and one failing is compensated by another, so the hub is a conduit for insurance. When their responses are correlated, they are one frame wearing many names, the apparent redundancy adds nothing, and the hub becomes a single point of failure. The links are typed, so a degree count records the ingredients and not the arrangement, and the residual that decides the sign is often two or three steps out, not at the hub's direct neighbours. Stability has to be read per species slot, and in a managed web an introduced hub cannot be subtracted as easily as it was added. What the answer offers is that shape, the conditions written in advance that would refute it, and the instruments that would close it. The threshold stays open until the ground settles it.
