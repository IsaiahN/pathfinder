# Tether outreach - figure-grounded package: ECO-0013

**Question:** When do highly connected species stabilize ecological networks, and when do they make those networks vulnerable to collapse?

**Source:** ecology.txt line 18

**Section:** 2. Species interactions and ecological networks

**Mode:** `specified` on the substantive valence rule, `general` on the structural shape. No single network measurement settles which regime a given web is in, so nothing here is `grounded`.

**Status:** done

**Registry status:** pending

## Restating the question as an effect

A "highly connected species" is a node with many interaction links (a generalist, a hub). The question asks for the sign of that node's contribution to a system outcome: does its degree raise the probability that the network persists after a perturbation, or raise the probability of a cascading collapse? Both options lead to different management decisions. If high degree is stabilizing, protecting hubs is leverage. If it is destabilizing, protecting hubs is a liability. The residual is the gap between what a connectance-based prediction says and what the web actually does under perturbation.

Following the formula's Direction step, the sought-for shape is bound as an ordinary term so it can accrue a residual and be refused: `CONNECTANCE_VALENCE(R)` is the sign of a high-degree node's contribution to persistence under residual R. It predicts a conditional sign, not a fixed one, so it can fail per slot.

## What an answer would look like

A shape with conditions, not a species list. High degree has no valence of its own; the valence is a relation fixed by (i) the overlap or correlation among the responses of the partners the hub links, (ii) the strength distribution and typing of those links, and (iii) how far the contact cascade reaches before it stops mattering. A named list of "good hubs" that survives a change of residual would refute this.

## What is deliberately not here

- No personal emails of named individuals.
- No ranking of people by how likely they are to agree with the shape claim.
- No bulk-send plan.

Three reasons, in the figures' own words:

1. **A room does not upgrade the label.** Fig 7 :13: "agreement compounds, evidence does not". Adding more readers of a connectance claim raises the vocabulary and does not add an anchor.
2. **Selecting for receptiveness manufactures the disqualifying correlation.** Fig 2 :40: "disqualifying for a verdict: agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool". A list ranked by likely agreement is a list selected to agree.
3. **Asking people to trust before they check spends their independence.** Fig 10 :42: "A verifier that can reconstruct the claim cannot verify it." The outside frame must not hold the interiors.

Legally: scraping personal emails for unsolicited bulk outreach breaches GDPR and CAN-SPAM style rules in many jurisdictions and will get a sending domain blacklisted. That is a practical failure on top of the structural one.
