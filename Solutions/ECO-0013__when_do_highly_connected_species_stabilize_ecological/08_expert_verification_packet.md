# Expert verification packet - the Tether on highly connected species and network stability

**What this is:** a claim in a form an ecologist or network scientist can check. Mode, derivation and refuters are stated before the conclusion, so a reader knows what to look for and what would settle it.

**Mode: `specified` - not `grounded`.** No perturbation measurement is claimed. This is a shape with conditions, not a result.

## 1. The claim, in one paragraph

A highly connected species has no fixed stabilizing or destabilizing valence. Its sign is a relation fixed by the residual. A hub stabilizes a network when the partners it links respond independently, so one failing is compensated by another that does not fail with it. It destabilizes when those responses are correlated, so the hub is one point of failure and its loss removes many functions at once. The links are typed, so a degree count records ingredients and not arrangement; the residual that decides the sign is often second or third order, not the hub's direct neighbours; and stability must be read per species slot, not as a network average. In a managed web, introducing a hub is not reversible by subtracting it. What is offered is that shape, its refuters written in advance, and the instruments that would close it.

## 2. Three checkable sub-claims

### C1 - ecological, framework-internal; checkable by reading Figure 11

- **Statement:** the valence of a hub is relative to the residual, not a property it carries. Mutualism and parasitism are relations, not kinds, and no standing roster of good hubs survives a change of residual.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 11 :49 - "An actor's valence is relative to the residual, not a property it carries." Fig 11 :50 - "The same actor is a benefactor to one residual and a malfactor to another, so mutualism and parasitism are relations rather than kinds." Fig 11 :51 - "No standing list of good actors survives a change of residual."
- **What to check:** does this match how the sign of a generalist or hub actually changes across perturbation types in your system? Allesina and Tang (2012, Nature) is a world-facing cross-check: they find the same complexity stabilizing for predator-prey interactions and destabilizing for mutualistic and competitive ones.
- **Refuter:** a fixed roster of stabilizing hubs that keeps its sign across a genuine change of residual. That breaks Fig 11 :49-51.

### C2 - the flip condition; checkable by a perturbation experiment or a response time series

- **Statement:** low overlap (asynchrony) among a hub's partners gives a resilience surplus; high overlap makes the hub a single point of failure.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 2 :31 - "Independence, between two frames, is how little they draw on the same evidence." Fig 8 :49 - "Two frames that overlap almost completely are one frame wearing two names." Fig 2 :40 - "disqualifying for a verdict: agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool".
- **What to check:** measure the correlation of partner responses under the same perturbation. Do low-overlap hubs buffer and high-overlap hubs concentrate failure? Yachi and Loreau (1999, PNAS) derived the same condition theoretically: with perfectly positively correlated responses, "the ecosystem behaves exactly as a single species."
- **Refuter:** a web whose hub partners have perfectly correlated responses buffers as well as one with independent responses. That breaks Fig 2 :31-32 and Fig 8 :48-49.
- **Triangulation status:** this is the one triangulated figure verdict in the packet. Fig 2 (anchor and correlation) and Fig 8 (union surplus) have low overlap and agree on the condition.

### C3 - ecological; checkable against cascade and per-slot data

- **Statement:** degree is a first-order statistic; the sign of a hub's contribution is a second- or third-order result, and must be read per species slot rather than as a network average. Links are typed, so a degree count is the flat formula.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 11 :52-54 - "And contact cascades. Scoping the habitat to direct contact is how DDT reached the eagles: first-order enumeration, second-order effect, and the residual that mattered was three steps out." Fig 1 :22 - "Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average." Fig 12 :46-47 - "A bond is the typed relation between two operands. There are seven, they are not interchangeable, and which one holds is not recoverable from the operands."
- **What to check:** does a direct-link-only model predict first extinctions as well as a cascade-enumerating model? Does a global connectance preserve the live-slot signal? Do two webs with the same species set but different typed arrangements behave differently under the same perturbation?
- **Refuter:** any one of those three checks coming back in the negative breaks the corresponding part of C3.

## 3. What is explicitly not claimed

- Not a roster of stabilizing species. A named list that keeps its sign across a residual change would refute C1.
- Not that connectance alone predicts persistence. It is a budget filter, not a witness (Fig 9 :60).
- Not that direct neighbours suffice, and not that the sign is readable at first order.
- Not a new primitive. "Network" is already one of the six structures (Fig 13 :23-24).
- Not that managed webs can subtract an introduced hub as cleanly as they add one. Fig 11 :61-62 says introduction is available and exclusion is not.
- Not `grounded`. Nothing here is asserted as settled by experiment.
- Not a claim that any particular named species is stabilizing in a particular web. That requires a ground reading.

## 5. What would settle it

An instrument, not agreement. From *The Hard Problem is About Source*: an instrument that reads green "cannot comment on the source"; the spectrometer resolved it. Here:

1. **Overlap Test:** correlation among partner responses under perturbation. This is the load-bearing test.
2. **Cascade Test:** direct-only versus cascade-enumerating prediction of first extinctions.
3. **Slot Test:** per-slot residual versus global connectance as a predictor of first extinction.
4. **Bond Test:** same species set, different typed arrangement, same perturbation, different outcome.
5. **Management Test:** can an introduced hub be removed as cleanly as it was added?

## 6. What an expert can check in twenty minutes

1. Read Fig 11 :49-51, Fig 2 :31-32 and Fig 8 :48-49, and Fig 11 :52-54. Four short passages.
2. Check C2 against your own system: do correlated partner responses remove the buffering you would otherwise expect?
3. Name one instrument that would measure response asynchrony at the per-slot level in your study system, or say why none is possible.
4. Send the refutation. A failure is more useful than an endorsement.
