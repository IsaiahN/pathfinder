## Independent domain census

- **R (arity / symmetry / scale).** The residual is the gap between a connectance prediction and observed persistence after perturbation. Arity: one node property mapped to a system outcome through incident relations, so the effective arity is the number of typed links on the slot. Symmetry: degree looks symmetric only after lumping; real links are typed and directed, so the mapping has the type problem Fig 12 names. Scale: first order is direct partners, second and third order are the cascade. Union compound by Fig 9, not one hard question.
- **Domain 1, ecological habitat.** Actors: species, conditions, relations (pollination, predation, competition, dependency). Currency: contact and persistence. Anchor: the ground, what the web does under perturbation. Union of overlapping conditions: a hub occupies several relations at once.
- **Domain 2, network structure.** Actors: nodes, typed links, per-slot residuals. Currency: influence travelling. Anchor: observed adjacency and interaction strengths. Union, so unbundle before search. This is where I derive the flip condition: Fig 2 :31 (independence) and Fig 8 :49 (high overlap is one frame wearing two names) give the condition from anchor and closure, not from habitat enumeration.

## Round trip gap R_T

The Symbols table defines R_T as `gap(x, (T_E ∘ T_A)(x))`, the gap between what was sent up and what came back down. Fig 4 :30 requires it to be measured rather than assumed. I ran the round trip on the condensation this folder actually uses.

1. **Sent up (x):** the verbatim figure set, chiefly Fig 11 :49-51 (valence relative to residual), Fig 11 :52-54 (cascade), Fig 2 :31-32 and Fig 8 :48-49 (overlap condition), Fig 12 :46-47 (typed bonds), Fig 1 :19 and :22 (per slot).
2. **Abstraction (T_A):** the shorthand in `07_the_tether_answer.md`, "a hub's sign is set by the overlap of its partners' responses; the cascade is second or third order; read per slot".
3. **Concretisation (T_E):** a management rule of the form "protect hubs with low-overlap partners, remove hubs with high-overlap partners".

**R_T is nonzero.** Four things the shorthand holds do not survive the trip back down:

- the **no-threshold limit**. The figures give the direction of the overlap condition but no number. A management rule needs a threshold the framework does not supply.
- the **residual relativity**. "Remove the hub" assumes a fixed sign. Fig 11 :51 refuses a standing roster, and Fig 11 :61-62 makes introduction available and exclusion unavailable, so a cull is not a clean inverse of an introduction.
- the **bond typing**. Degree-based shorthand is the flat formula; Fig 12 :46-47 requires the relation type to be carried.
- the **union unbundling**. A hub sits in several relations at once; a single lever clears one condition and leaves the intersections standing (Fig 9 :54-58).
- the **ground rule**. The shorthand can look like a verdict; Fig 2 :35 and Fig 8 :39 require an instrument and forbid treating an unfound stabilizing hub as proof of absence.

Conversely, one thing survives cleanly: low overlap buffers and high overlap concentrates failure, read per species slot with the cascade enumerated outward. That is the residue worth sending. R_T was **not** measured numerically, because no perturbation reading exists; it is recorded qualitatively as the enumerated set of dropped constraints. Treating "the formula is the condensed version of the metalaws" as identity would set R_T to zero and would be the dead-reckoning error Fig 4 :30 names.
