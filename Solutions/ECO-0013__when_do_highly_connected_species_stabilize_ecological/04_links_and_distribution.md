## Wider literature that bears on the flip condition

These are the world sources read for `09_watson_answer.md`. They are secondary corroboration only; the figures decide. Full provenance and exact quotes are in `10_source_ledger.csv`.

1. **May (1972), "Will a Large Complex System be Stable?", Nature 238:413 to 414.** Fetched at `https://www.nature.com/articles/238413a0`. The abstract says large systems assembled at random "may be expected to be stable up to a certain critical level of connectance, and then, as this increases, to suddenly become unstable." Predicts that degree alone cannot carry a fixed positive sign; corroborates the need for a condition. Field: theoretical ecology. Overlap: origin point of the complexity-stability problem; ecology-to-ecology overlap with McCann and Allesina is declared.
2. **McCann (2000), "The diversity-stability debate", Nature 405:228 to 233.** Fetched at `https://www.nature.com/articles/35012234` (an earlier URL guess, 35019022, returned 404 and is not used). The abstract states that "Both theory and empirical evidence agree that we should expect declines in diversity to accelerate the simplification of ecological communities." Predicts that the sign of complexity is conditional and mechanism-dependent; corroborates F1 and F5. Field: ecology review.
3. **Allesina and Tang (2012), "Stability criteria for complex ecosystems", Nature 483:205 to 208.** Fetched at `https://www.nature.com/articles/nature10832`. The abstract reports "remarkable differences between predator-prey interactions, which are stabilizing, and mutualistic and competitive interactions, which are destabilizing", and that "Stable predator-prey networks can be arbitrarily large and complex, provided that predator-prey pairs are tightly coupled." Directly corroborates Fig 11 :50 (mutualism and parasitism are relations, not kinds) and Fig 12 :46-47 (bonds are typed). Field: theoretical ecology. Overlap: different authors and method from the field experiments, but same ecological problem pool as May and McCann.
4. **Albert, Jeong and Barabasi (2000), "Error and attack tolerance of complex networks", Nature 406:378 to 382.** Fetched at `https://www.nature.com/articles/35019019` and the author manuscript at `https://arxiv.org/abs/cond-mat/0008064`. The abstract says "error tolerance is not shared by all redundant systems" and that these networks are "extremely vulnerable to attacks", that is "the selection and removal of a few nodes that play a vital role in maintaining the network's connectivity." This is the hub-verbatim result in a non-ecological field: redundant wiring stabilizes against random error but concentrates vulnerability in high-degree nodes. Corroborates F2 from a low-overlap field (network science and statistical physics).
5. **Yachi and Loreau (1999), "Biodiversity and ecosystem productivity in a fluctuating environment: the insurance hypothesis", PNAS 96:1463 to 1468.** Full text fetched at `https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC15485/`. The abstract: "The strength of these insurance effects is determined by three factors: (i) the way ecosystem productivity is determined by individual species responses to environmental fluctuations, (ii) the degree of asynchronicity of these responses, and (iii) the detailed form of these responses." The results section states that with perfectly positively correlated responses "the ecosystem behaves exactly as a single species." This is Fig 8 :49 ("one frame wearing two names") derived independently in population biology. Field: ecology. Overlap: ecology-to-ecology with sources 1 to 3, but a different model class (stochastic insurance, not random matrices).

## Distribution: offer, do not push

Figure-grounded reasons:

- **Fig 11 :52-54** - "And contact cascades. Scoping the habitat to direct contact is how DDT reached the eagles: first-order enumeration, second-order effect, and the residual that mattered was three steps out." Sending a conclusion before the recipient has enumerated the residual is a first-order reading of a third-order problem. Lead with the residual and the refuter.
- **Fig 10 :42** - "A verifier that can reconstruct the claim cannot verify it." A reader who has absorbed the framework before being asked for a verdict has joined the frame being checked.
- **Fig 9 :27-28** - "describe it before you go looking, or any frame you pick will seem to fit." Ask what they would expect to see if the overlap condition were true before giving them the literature list.
- **Fig 2 :40** - "disqualifying for a verdict: agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool." A send selected for people who already believe complexity stabilizes webs produces a convention, not corroboration.
- **Fig 2 :42** - "Buy independence in proportion to the cost of being wrong." The cost here is a wrong conservation or management decision about a hub species, so the independence is worth buying.

**Recommended order in a reply:** (1) the residual and the shape claim with its refuter; (2) ask what they would count as a failure; (3) record that verdict; (4) only then the figure text and the five literature links, with the note that the figures, not the summary, are the authority.

## The two-stage offer

- **Stage 1 before stage 2** is Fig 10 :52, "Bring a characterised residual, never the source", and Fig 2 :45, "Frames range against the ground. They do not negotiate with it." The recipient checks the output without holding the framework's interior.
- **Stage 2 is opt-in by construction.** Seat 4 must not hold the interiors. Once a recipient has absorbed the theory, their agreement stops being evidence, so the stage-1 verdict is recorded *before* any theory is shown and is never pooled as corroboration.
- **Stage 3 sends a method, which is permitted.** Fig 4 :22: "Only methods cross up. Only head starts come down." The formula is a generator, so giving it away does not give away an answer.
- **Stage 3 still needs a ground per problem.** `THE_FORMULA.md` Direction: a frame "cannot check the direction of its own question, because the shape a frame is searching for is not a term it holds." Each user must write what would refute their result before running it, and label the mode of what they get.

**Hard precondition:** stages 1 to 3 work only if the stage-1 output survives. The pre-registered refuter in `01_figure_grounded_audit.md` section 4 is what decides that. Nothing here is a ground reading; all of it is a simulated derivation checked for internal consistency against the figures.
