**Subject:** One conditional claim about hub species, and the failure condition I wrote first

> Hi [name],
>
> I have one specific output I would like you to check against what you already know about ecological networks. I am not asking you to read any framework first, and I am not asking you to agree. If the output does not survive, there is nothing else to look at.
>
> **The question in effect terms:** does a species with many interaction links raise the probability that a food web persists after a perturbation, or raise the probability of a cascade? A connectance story predicts the first. Cascade cases suggest the second. The residual is the gap between that prediction and what a web actually does, measured per species slot.
>
> **The output (`specified`, not `grounded`):** high degree carries no fixed valence. A hub stabilizes when the partners it links respond independently, so one failing is compensated by another that does not fail with it. It destabilizes when those responses are correlated, so the hub is one point of failure and its removal drops many functions at once. The relation flips with the overlap of responses, with the strength and typing of the links, and with how far the cascade reaches. This is a shape with conditions, not a list of good and bad species.
>
> **What I am not claiming:** that any particular named species is stabilizing, that connectance alone predicts the sign, that direct neighbours are enough to read the cascade, or that a single averaged network number preserves the first-extinction signal. The framework returns a shape, not a roster. A fixed roster of "good hubs" that kept its sign across a change of residual would refute the claim.
>
> **What would refute it (written before this message):**
> 1. a fixed list of stabilizing hubs keeps its sign across a genuine change of residual;
> 2. a web whose hub partners have perfectly correlated responses buffers as well as one with independent responses;
> 3. direct first-order links predict collapse as well as a cascade-enumerating model;
> 4. one global connectance number preserves the per-slot first-extinction signal.
>
> If any holds, I withdraw or narrow the claim. A one-line "this fails because..." is more useful to me than agreement. If you would rather not engage, no reply is needed.
>
> **If it survives, and only then:** here are the public sources. The verbatim figures, not my summary, are the authority.
> - The canonical figure text with line numbers (local copy in the solution folder): `FRAMEWORK/reference/03-FIGURES_TEXT.md`
> - The canonical usage rule: `FRAMEWORK/reference/FIGURES_README_canonical.md`
> - The ecology and network-science literature that bears on the flip condition is listed in `04_links_and_distribution.md` and `10_source_ledger.csv`.
>
> **If you want to use the machinery:** the formula is in the figures. Run it on a web you know well and can check against a ground. Write down what would refute your result *before* you run it. The framework's own rule is that a loop cannot check the direction of its own question, and LLM output of this kind is a simulation, not a ground reading. A reported failure is more useful to me than a reported success.
>
> Thanks,
> [name]
