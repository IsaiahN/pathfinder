## Round trip gap `R_T`

The Symbols table defines `R_T` as the gap between what was sent up and what came back down, `gap(x, (T_E composed with T_A)(x))`, measuring what the coarser description cannot hold. Fig 4 :30 requires it to be measured rather than assumed. I ran the round trip on the condensation the folder actually uses.

1. **Sent up (x):** the verbatim figure set, chiefly Fig 11 :70 and :66 (test harness, habitat substitution), Fig 4 :22 and :30 (method over recording, round trip), Fig 9 :55-57 (union).
2. **Abstraction (T_A):** the shorthand in `07_the_tether_answer.md`: a lab result is a wiring claim under a substituted habitat; carry a method; measure `R_T`; enumerate the field; the ground decides.
3. **Concretisation (T_E):** a concrete field claim, for example "montmorillonite cycling gives 55-mers in the field too, so the result scaled."

**`R_T` is nonzero.** Three things the shorthand holds do not survive the trip back down:

- the **identity of the new habitat's actors**, which must be enumerated from the field and cannot be derived from lab terms (Fig 11 :43-44, :48);
- the **arrangement**, sequence and surface, which are not in the ingredient list (Fig 12 :5);
- the **field-equivalence criterion**, which the figure set does not supply (F7).

One thing survives cleanly: a method crosses the membrane and a recording does not, and the gap must be reported. That is the residue worth sending. `R_T` was **not** measured numerically, because no field reading exists in this derivation. It is recorded qualitatively as the enumerated set of dropped constraints. Treating "the formula is the condensed version of the figures" as identity would set `R_T` to zero and would be the dead-reckoning error Fig 4 :30 names. The honest form is a lossy condensation with a stated gap, which is what `09_watson_answer.md` says.
