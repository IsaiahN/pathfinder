# Outreach draft: check the output before you decide about the framing

**Use:** one-to-one, or as a cover letter attached to a submission through the public venues in `03_public_contact_points.csv`. **Not** for bulk send.

**Hard precondition:** do not send until the two gaps in `01_figure_grounded_audit.md` (F7 field-equivalence instrument, F4 unmeasured `R_T`) are stated, not hidden. The offer collapses on contact if the output cannot survive a refuter.

**Subject:** One checkable claim about scaling prebiotic lab results to the field

> Hi [name],
>
> I have one specific output I would like you to check against what you already know. I am not asking you to read a theory first, and I am not asking you to agree with anything. If the output does not survive, there is nothing else to look at.
>
> **The output (`specified`, not `grounded`):** a result obtained in a tiny, controlled lab system does not scale by magnification. The lab system is a substituted habitat, a test harness. Moving the result to a geologically realistic setting is a habitat substitution: you must reproduce everything the result's goal required, and you inherit the new habitat's actors whether you chose them or not. There are two silent failure modes, what you failed to reproduce and what you brought with you. What can be carried across is the method, never the recording of the one success. The transform is lossy and not reversible, so the honest report is the round-trip gap `R_T`, not "it scales" or "it fails."
>
> The claim is a shape, not a protocol. It does not name which mineral, which cycling regime, which concentration mechanism or which duration matters. Those are bindings from your field, and I am not asserting them.
>
> **What I am not claiming:** that any particular prebiotic result has or has not scaled; that laboratory chemistry is irrelevant to early Earth; that the geological setting can be derived from lab terms. I am claiming only that the question is a habitat substitution plus a lossy transform, and that a single-lever answer is a union error.
>
> **What would refute the shape, written before this message:**
> 1. a lab result unchanged across a documented habitat substitution that removes a condition its goal required, and still paid by a field or rock measurement;
> 2. a recorded outcome replayed at field scale with no method carried, still paid by the ground;
> 3. a measured round trip with `R_T = 0` for every condition the result required;
> 4. one intervention that restores all four conditions at once and leaves no categorically different residue.
>
> If any of these holds, I withdraw the claim. A one-line "this fails because..." is more useful to me than agreement. If you would rather not engage, no reply is needed.
>
> **What I would value from you:** what would you count as a failure of this claim, and do you already use an instrument or a convention that states what "the same result happened in the field" would mean? I do not have one; I am recording that as a gap rather than filling it.
>
> **If it survives, and only then:** the sources are below. The figure text, not my summary, is the authority.
> - Verbatim figures with line numbers (local extract): `FRAMEWORK/reference/03-FIGURES_TEXT.md`
> - Figures README, branch `general-agent`: https://github.com/IsaiahN/tether/blob/general-agent/figures/README.md
> - The eight essays and the formula, index: https://medium.com/@IsaiahNwukor/arrival-of-the-fittest-the-tether-architecture-1de7edf641ec
>
> **If you want to use the machinery:** the formula is in the figures link. Run it on a problem you know well and can check against a ground. Write down what would refute your result *before* you run it. The framework's own rule is that a loop cannot check the direction of its own question, and the source article warns that outputs *"could never be verified if the solutions presented were not contrived to fit the formula."* A reported failure is more useful to me than a reported success.

## The two-stage offer, restated

1. **Stage 1.** Send the shape claim and its four refuters. Ask what the recipient would count as a failure, and whether they already have a field-equivalence instrument. Record their verdict **before** any theory is shown.
2. **Stage 2, opt-in only.** If they are curious, offer the three links. The figures, not the summary, are the authority. Their later agreement is never pooled as corroboration (Fig 2 :40).
3. **Stage 3, on request.** Offer the formula as a method to reuse, with the instruction that each user writes their own refuter and states their own mode (Fig 4 :22, methods cross).

**Stage 1 cannot work unless the shape claim is honest about its gaps.** It is. F7 is a declared gap and F4 is an unmeasured gap, both in `01_figure_grounded_audit.md`.
