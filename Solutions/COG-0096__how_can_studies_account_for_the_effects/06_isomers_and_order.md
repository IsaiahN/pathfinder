## Q1 - Are two sites with the same scores isomers?

> **"isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."** (Fig 12 :117)
> **"functional group. A substructure whose presence fixes the outcome. The group says what kind; the qualifier says which member."** (Fig 12 :123)
> **"Symmetry is what decides whether an arrangement is a new thing."** (Fig 12 :92)

Two sites whose respondents endorse the same items with the same frequencies hold the same atoms. If the bonds differ, that is, if one site's answers are composed through a schooled, test-familiar history and the other through a migration-shaped, locally grounded history, they are **isomers** under Fig 12 :117: same atoms, different bond, different behaviour, with the difference invisible in the score distribution. This is the figure-grounded version of the invariance problem, and it is stronger, because the arrangement is itself a substance and carries information the item list does not.

If the sites share only a pattern (both show a `history → response → score` sequence) while differing in which atoms occupy the slots, the weaker claim holds: they are members of the same **functional group** (Fig 12 :123), the qualifier saying which member. The presence of the pattern fixes the kind, not the outcome.

The distinction is testable. A score-distribution comparison that finds no difference between two sites cannot distinguish isomer from functional-group; the bonds have to be recorded. Fig 12 :5: *"A set of ingredients is not one. The bond is the rest of the information, and it is the half nothing was recording."*

Ritchie, Bates and Deary 2015 provide a within-population instance at a different scale: education's effects are *"direct effects on specific cognitive skills"* rather than on g, so two groups can share a total score while differing in which components produce it. The population-genetics source supplies a very low-overlap structural instance: population structure yields spurious associations even when every measured allele frequency looks comparable in aggregate (Wikipedia, read directly).

**Falsifier, written before the run:** if two sites with identical atoms and identical bonds nevertheless diverge in downstream comparison outcomes with no difference in ground contact or imported atoms, the isomer framing is insufficient and the difference must be habitat (D2), which would move the explanation out of D3.

**Status:** `specified`, triangulated for the legality of the reading (Fig 12 and Fig 6 :26, different figures), single-source for the cross-cultural application. The world corroboration is Ritchie et al. and the population-genetics article, which are low overlap with each other but not a local measurement.

## Q2 - What order do the conditions bind in?

> **"The chain is ordered by dependency, not by importance. Each link consumes what the one above produces, so effort spent above the break arrives at a step that cannot use it."** (Fig 3 :20-21)
> **"Optimising a link that already works cannot rescue one that does not."** (Fig 3 :19)

Order for a study facing a new comparison site:

1. **Perception.** Can the study read the local response at all? Failure here is visible and has obvious tests.
2. **Vocabulary.** Does its instrument have terms for what the local situation affords, and can it say what would count as progress there? This is the middle link. Fig 3 :26: *"The link between them, having terms for what a situation offers and being able to say what would count as progress, has neither, so it attracts the least attention and the fewest instruments."*
3. **Objective.** Can it state a comparison that would count as getting somewhere? Fig 12 :112: *"A plan is a sequence. A goal is a comparison. Progress is a subtraction."* An instrument with only items cannot state a local goal.
4. **Planning.** A recruitment, translation and analysis route under incomplete information.
5. **Learn and carry.** Name what worked, verify it, carry it to the next site. Without import from a differing closure the reach stops moving (Fig 6 :21).

The four conditions map onto this chain: C3 (channel) is mostly a failure of links 1 and 2; C4 (convention) is a failure at link 3, because the cut is chosen before the objective is stated; C1 (history/weighting) is a failure at link 2, because the instrument has no term for the history it must weight; C2 (habitat) is mostly a failure of link 5, because the site is never enumerated as a habitat. That mapping is why a single lever cannot clear all four (Fig 9 :55).

Exposure history adds an ordered bond. Migration before schooling is not schooling before migration. Symbols :24-26 defines `w` as learnable from the outcomes of past weightings, so the order of exposures changes the weighting that arrives at the task. `→` is the correct bond here; `+` is the flat-form error Fig 12 :5 names.

The order also decides which instrument to buy first. Fig 3 :27: *"A link with no instrument is not a link that works. It is a link nobody has looked at."* Links 1 and 4 have accounting, survey and statistical instruments; link 2 has almost none. Budget naturally piles up above the break while the break sits at 2 and 5.

**Falsifier:** a study whose chain demonstrably works at links 1, 2 and 3 and fails only at 4. That would falsify the "usually middle" claim of F10 in the primary audit, though not the dependency ordering.

## Q3 - Competing explanations, and what each can and cannot reach

| candidate explanation | what it names | figure-line bearing | what it cannot reach |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Confounder adjustment** (covariate lists for age, sex, income, schooling, migration) | the instrument's Γ, its library of recorded conditions | Γ is a real operand; Fig 6 :21 says minting cannot add an atom | Why the same condition changes valence across sites (Fig 11 :50). It has no contact variable and no per-slot indexing, so it conflicts with Fig 1 :19 until re-mapped as habitat enumeration |
| **Measurement invariance / differential item functioning** | the bond between an item and a construct | close to C3 and to Fig 12 :95-96 | It is a methods-pool binding, not a derivation from the figures. It does not by itself say which source produced the reading, and Sorokowski and Kowal 2024 warn that over-correction can distort genuine variability |
| **Response-style correction** | the scale-usage channel, acquiescence, extremity | matches Fig 10 :25-26 (stale percepts, pooled readings) | Munafò et al. and the 2024 reply both show that selection and over-correction can themselves bias the estimate. A correction is an import with provenance owed, not a verdict |
| **WEIRD sampling critique** (Henrich, Heine, Norenzayan 2010) | the reference group as an unrepresentative instrument | matches Fig 2 :29-30 and F8 | From the public abstract only, because direct fetch failed; reduced weight. It names the defect but does not supply the source-discriminating instrument |
| **Selection and collider bias** (Constant et al.; Munafò et al.) | selection into and out of the sample | low-overlap corroboration of F6 and Fig 11 :67 | The migration result is about health, not cognition; the collider result is about genetic associations. Both corroborate the mechanism shape at a different stratum, not the cognitive ground |
| **Population stratification** (population genetics) | history as a confounder of group comparison | very low-overlap corroboration of Fig 9 :27-28 and Fig 12 :95-96 | A genetics analogue; it cannot name the cognitive construct that occupies the slot |
| **Cultural dimension labels** (individualism, tightness, collectivism) | a name for the source of a reading | at best a binding onto an atom already in the habitat | Reading the source off a projection, which the source essay forbids. A dimension label is not a discriminator |
| **Habitat and urban-adaptation work** (Lee and Thornton 2021) | habitat as enumerated conditions and relations | low-overlap corroboration of F2 and Fig 11 :43-48 | Animal cognition, not a cross-cultural cognitive measurement. Fig 4 :20 requires the anchor to be re-named at the new stratum |
| **Luck / unexplained residual** | an unearned early reading that persists while unfuted | Fig 8 :40: *"Underivable-in-principle and unreachable-at-this-budget are different facts"* | Cannot be excluded from inside a frame; Fig 8 :39: *"So a search that finds nothing is never a proof of absence."* Honest `gap`, not a rival verdict |

No candidate is discarded by prestige or familiarity. Each is ranged against the same residual in the order Fig 8 :53 requires. The point is not to pick one; it is to notice that the first four are all in one methods evidence pool (Fig 2 :31) and that the low-overlap fields corroborate mechanism, not the local cognitive fact.

## Q4 - Does the shape generate the literature's answers?

Partly. The shape can generate:

- the covariate-adjustment emphasis on recording conditions, as habitat enumeration under a closure that has not yet met a differing closure (C2 and Fig 11 :43-48);
- the invariance emphasis on item equivalence, as C3 and Fig 12 :95-96;
- the response-style emphasis on channel failure, as Fig 10 :24-26, with the 2024 warning retained as the over-correction branch;
- the WEIRD emphasis on the reference group, as C4 and Fig 2 :29-30;
- the selection and stratification emphasis on history, as Symbols :24-26 and Fig 11 :67.

It cannot generate the *name* of the exposure that produced a specific gap. That is the `gap` recorded in F11 of `01`. It is a gap at the instrument boundary, not a silence: Fig 13 :134 (*"Unreachable is a verdict here, not a silence,"*) and Fig 8 :39 apply. The essay's source rule states why: an instrument that reads a result *"cannot comment on the source"*, and *"the answer is unreached until a better instrument exists"*.

## CONCLUSION

Two sites with the same atoms but different bonds are isomers under Fig 12 :117; two sites sharing only a `history → response → score` pattern are members of one functional group under Fig 12 :123. The conditions bind in dependency order, with the middle link (vocabulary and objective) and the carry link the usual breaks, which is why C1 to C4 need different instruments, and exposure history adds an ordered bond that a flat covariate list erases. The standard explanations are mostly bindings of one methods evidence pool and are generated by the shape only up to the point where a name would be required. The isomer claim has a written falsifier; the order claim has one; the naming gap is declared rather than filled.
