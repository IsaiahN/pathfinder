## 1. The claim, in one paragraph

Migration, urbanization, media and schooling are not nuisance variables that a rich covariate list makes harmless. They are the histories that change how a person weights their own experience against what they inherited, and they are conditions in a local habitat whose valence depends on the question being asked. A between-population score difference may be read as a construct difference only when four conditions hold: the change in that weighting is measured; each site's habitat is enumerated from the world rather than assumed; the measurement channel is shown to be reachable rather than stale, saturated or pooled; and the exposure cut and the reference group are declared before the outcome and kept falsifiable. These are four different failure modes with four different instruments, not four degrees of one problem. Two cohorts with identical scores can still be different, because the arrangement of identical answers carries information the answer list does not. The claim is a shape that any candidate answer must have; it does not name which exposure caused a given gap.

## 2. Three checkable sub-claims

### C1 - Framework-internal; checkable by reading the Symbols table and Figures 11, 10 and 2

- **Statement:** belief is formed from private history and inherited prior mixed by a learnable weighting, so two populations with identical architecture can diverge because their histories diverge; exposure valence is relative to the local residual; the reference group is an instrument, not an anchor.
- **Source, verbatim:**
  - Symbols :24-26 - *"How far the belief leans on private history versus inherited prior. w_A + w_B = 1, and w is learnable from the outcomes of past weightings. Two frames with identical architecture diverge because their histories and their w diverge."*
  - Fig 11 :50 - *"The same actor is a benefactor to one residual and a malfactor to another, so mutualism and parasitism are relations rather than kinds."*
  - Fig 10 :24 - *"the ground does not decay, the channel does"*; Fig 10 :26 - *"saturated metrics, pooled readings"*
  - Fig 2 :29 - *"An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks."* The same line names *"a measurement, an experiment, a counter, a market, a patient outcome."*
- **Refuter:** a documented exposure contrast that produces no change in the measured history-versus-prior weighting on a task known to be sensitive to it; or a between-site score difference independently shown to be a construct difference while every channel indicator measures stable and non-drifting. Either fires against C1.

### C2 - Framework-internal derivation plus two world-facing checks

- **Statement:** the habitat must be enumerated per site including cascades; sampling into the study is a selection that can induce spurious associations; the effect must be described in effect terms before a frame is named.
- **Source, verbatim:**
  - Fig 11 :43 - *"The habitat is enumerated, never composed."*; Fig 11 :48 - *"You do not invent the list. You read it off the world, and what you cannot perceive or measure yet is the residual."*
  - Fig 9 :27-28 - *"describe it before you go looking, or any frame you pick will seem to fit"*
  - Fig 6 :30 - *"outward: does another frame already hold it? The description travels. Fails by being sharpened into terms only we can read. Cast it in effect terms."*
  - Fig 8 :39 - *"So a search that finds nothing is never a proof of absence."*
- **What a researcher can check against their own knowledge:** Lee and Thornton 2021 write that *"Empirical studies are often based on generalisations about how urban and rural environments differ, and few studies attempt to quantify these differences directly"* (animal behaviour review, very low overlap with cross-cultural methods). Munafò et al. 2018 write that *"selection can induce collider bias... selection can lead to substantially biased estimates of associations"* (epidemiology). Is the site's habitat in your own study actually enumerated, and is participation in your sample demonstrably independent of the exposure and the outcome? A "yes" to both, with data, is a real refutation of the default priority of C2.
- **Refuter:** a site whose enumerated habitat contributes no residual beyond the first-order covariates, so the cascade never matters; or a demonstration that conditioning on participation leaves all associations in the intended population unchanged. Either breaks C2.

### C3 - Structure, order and competing explanations

- **Statement:** the four conditions form a union that must be unbundled; two sites with identical item endorsements but different composing histories are isomers, so a score inventory cannot predict equivalence; the conditions bind in dependency order with the middle link the usual break.
- **Source, verbatim:**
  - Fig 9 :57 - *"What remains is smaller and it is also categorically different: a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either."*
  - Fig 12 :117 - *"isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."*
  - Fig 12 :5 - *"A set of ingredients is not one. The bond is the rest of the information, and it is the half nothing was recording."*
  - Fig 3 :27 - *"A link with no instrument is not a link that works. It is a link nobody has looked at."*
- **What a researcher can check:** Ritchie, Bates and Deary 2015 report that *"the association of education with improved cognitive test scores is not mediated by g, but consists of direct effects on specific cognitive skills."* Do your own data show a common factor shift, or component-specific changes that a total score hides? If the former is robust across your sites, the isomer claim loses its grip on your comparison.
- **Refuter:** one intervention that restores all four conditions in all failure types and leaves no categorically different residue; or two sites with identical atoms and identical bonds whose comparison outcomes diverge with no measured difference in ground contact or imported atoms. Either breaks C3.

## 3. What is explicitly not claimed

- Not `grounded`. No local, source-discriminating measurement has paid for any part of this.
- Not a theory of everything about comparison, and no final or unique theory claim.
- Not an average. No claim about the percentage of variance attributable to culture, exposure, method or sampling.
- Not that migration, schooling, media or urbanization are irrelevant. They are the terms that set `w` and the habitat conditions; the claim is that they do not settle the comparison on their own.
- Not a ban on covariate adjustment, invariance testing or response-style correction. They are instruments and imports with provenance owed (Fig 6 :23), not verdicts. Sorokowski and Kowal 2024 warn that over-correction can itself distort, so the discriminator matters more than the default.
- Not that any specific exposure is the answer. That is a `gap` at the instrument boundary, not a hidden answer.
- Not that the methods literature independently corroborates this as several witnesses. The invariance, response-style and WEIRD sources share one evidence pool. The low-overlap sources from animal behaviour, epidemiology, population genetics and cultural evolution corroborate the mechanism shape, not the local cognitive fact.
- Not that this packet, produced with an LLM, is submittable or publishable as authored work. See the policy note in section 6.

## 5. What would settle it

A prospective multi-site study that measures C1 to C4 separately. Concretely:

1. **Weighting test:** does a documented migration or schooling contrast change the measured reliance on private history versus inherited prior on a task known to be sensitive to that weighting?
2. **Habitat test:** enumerate each site's actors, conditions and relations, then check whether second- and third-order contacts carry residual beyond first-order covariates (Fig 11 :44, :52).
3. **Channel test:** independently measure translation equivalence, response-scale use, recruitment and retention; watch both under-correction and over-correction (Fig 10 :25-26; Sorokowski and Kowal 2024).
4. **Convention test:** was the exposure cut and reference group declared before the outcome was seen, and does the answer survive plausible alternative cuts? (Fig 10 :47, :49)
5. **Independence test:** do sites with genuinely different evidence pools still disagree in the way the shape predicts, or is their agreement explained by shared media and sampling (Fig 2 :31, :40)?
6. **Source-discriminating instrument:** can the study distinguish a construct difference from an exposure-driven reading, a translation artifact, a response convention and sampling luck when all produce the same score difference? The source essay says a reading alone *"cannot comment on the source"*.

## 7. What an expert can check in twenty minutes

1. Read Symbols :24-26, Fig 11 :43, :48, :50, Fig 10 :24, :26, Fig 2 :29, Fig 9 :27-28, Fig 12 :117 and Fig 3 :27. Ten short passages, about eight minutes.
2. Check C2 against your own study: is the habitat actually enumerated, and is participation independent of the exposure and outcome? About five minutes.
3. Name one instrument that would discriminate a construct difference from an exposure-driven reading, a translation artifact, a response convention and sampling luck using observable data, or say why none exists. About five minutes.
4. Send the refutation. A failure is more useful than an endorsement.
