## 1. A shape is a constraint you can state before you can name the thing

The canonical figures README says the framework returns *"what a missing thing would have to look like (a shape, not a name)."* Fig 9 :32: *"you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself."* Fig 13 :133: *"what it returns is a shape rather than a name."* Fig 13 :134: *"Unreachable is a verdict here, not a silence."*

From *The Library of Babel* (2026-10-02): *"you can cut your search short if the set of books returned is small and descriptive enough that it provides a general shape of the answer that satisfies your question."*

So a **shape** here is the set of constraints any acceptable definition of alignment must satisfy, stated before the definition's content is available. It constrains and it refuses; it does not pick a utility function. In this question the shape is: non-updating anchor, per-slot residual preserved, convention separated from verdict, union unbundled, refusal included, stake declared. A particular answer, such as "maximise expected human welfare under risk tolerance R," is a name, not a shape.

## 2. How the shape is obtained

- *The Library of Babel*: use the vocabulary you already have that is closest to the thing, then connect it to *"commonly recurring new terms that you are unfamiliar with."* *"With each new term gained, you narrow the space by definition and deepen your vocabulary."*
- Fig 9 :27 to :28: *"describe it before you go looking, or any frame you pick will seem to fit."* Fig 9 :30 to :31: *"your words are borrowed too, so describe the effect rather than the cause."*
- *Decoding Tacit Knowledge* (2026-11-10): *"When contact isn't possible, the best you can send is a plain description of what you'd expect to see, because anyone can check that against the world."* This is why the outreach email in `02` sends a checkable shape rather than a theory.
- *The Library of Babel* on stopping: *"You will know that you are done because the finish line will also be the starting place,"* and the paradox that by then you have *"composed the answer through the process of locating it."*

## 3. What would license a name

Two things, both outside the shape itself:

1. **The ground settles it.** Formula step 5: *"the ground settles it. Verified only by what pays."* For a contested value there is no such settlement yet, so the name stays withheld.
2. **A provenanced import from a frame whose closure differs** (Fig 6 :23; Fig 8 :50 to :52), with a source gate and a shadow test. A survey result, a law, or a ratified standard can supply content, but the record must show where it came from and which gap it explains.

*The Hard Problem is About Source* (2026-08-28) gives the instrument discipline. An instrument that reads green *"cannot comment on the source,"* and *"An instrument that can't distinguish these cases demonstrates its own inadequacy."* The spectrometer, not an argument, resolved the case. For alignment, a rating that says "acceptable" cannot by itself distinguish the values behind it; that is a source question, and it needs a discriminator.

## 4. Why the framework's own alignment essay matters here

*The Recursive Self-Improvement Trap* (2026-11-19) contains the section "Alignment to What?", which is the framework author's own statement of this question:

- *"The alignment problem is a bill you cannot pay."*
- *"What does it mean to align an AI to human values when human values are a moving target? You cannot align to something that changes."*
- *"The Tether ties alignment to the ground. Not to human preferences. Not to a reward model trained on human feedback. To the thing that settles a question regardless of what any frame thinks. An outcome. A measurement. A board that pays or does not."*
- On agreement: *"You may all agree and be wrong. You may set your clock to someone else's, who was setting your clock to yours, and be off by many deviations over time. You need ground truth to define where you stand."*

This essay is secondary corroboration, not a figure line. It is by the framework author, so its agreement with the figures is expected and carries little independent weight (overlap declared in `10_source_ledger.csv`). It is used to show that the split answer is the framework's own reading of alignment, not an outside reading imposed on it.

## 5. Correction to my own earlier use of "shape, not a name"

I initially wrote this section as if the phrase forbade naming any component of an answer. That overstates it. The README's phrase is *"what a **missing** thing would have to look like (a shape, not a name)."* It governs a primitive not held in Γ. Naming something already in the library is a binding from another frame, which is provenance work, not the invention of a new primitive.

For **this** question the narrowed rule still bites, and in fact bites harder. The value set that would make a definition precise is not among the figures' primitives and is not derivable from them: Fig 6 :23 says the bound moves only on contact with a frame whose closure differs, and Fig 13 :135 says *"It cannot say which primitive is missing, because naming it would mean already holding it."* So the framework can deliver the shape of a definition and the constraints on its anchor, but not the name of the values. Both the original claim and this correction are kept visible.

## 6. Two consequences for this solution

**Precision is not the same as settlement.** *How a Society of Agents Would Work* (2026-10-22) restates the alignment rule in plain language: *"Alignment is triangulation, not negotiation. A mirror cannot correct you,"* and explains that two navigators find a position by each taking a bearing on a fixed landmark, not by persuading each other. A precise convention is a shared map; it is not the landmark.

**My own derivation is a simulation, not a ground reading.** From *The Recursive Self-Improvement Trap*: LLM output can *"simulate an approximate answer/output,"* while *"the truly testable output would need to be derived from the real architecture or working the formula yourself."* So `01_figure_grounded_audit.md` is a simulated derivation checked for internal consistency against the figures. It cannot settle anything it reports on, and no statement in this folder should be read as an experimental finding about real alignment practice.
