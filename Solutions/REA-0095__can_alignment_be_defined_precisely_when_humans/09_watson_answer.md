## QUESTION

Decide, in effect terms, what "defined precisely" can mean for alignment while people disagree about values, goals and acceptable risk. Option A: one precise definition obtained by aggregating or negotiating the disagreement into a single specification, so that the definition itself is the answer. Option B: split the question, keep a precise violable convention for coordination and a precise local shape anchored to a ground that does not update, and report the value content as a gap. What each leads to: A produces a convention promoted to a verdict, coherent drift or the shared pool talking, and destroys the per-slot signal; B produces checkable precision, a named anchor, five refuters and an honest stop.

## DOMAIN CENSUS

R by arity, symmetry and scale, then one line per domain giving actors / currency / anchor, partition or union, and order by dependency.

- **R (the residual).** The gap between what a system does and what disagreeing people can say they want. Arity: N human frames (N >= 2), one system, one anchor; the residual is indexed per frame and contested item. Symmetry: preference orderings are not interpersonally comparable and the comparison of goals is ordered, not a symmetric join; the anchor is asymmetric because frames range against it. Scale: individual judgments first order, a convention or specification second order, the ground outside all orders. Fig 1 :19 says *"The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated."*
- **Domain 1, human value plurality.** Actors: persons, cultures, moral frameworks, risk tolerances. Currency: preference orderings, reasons, votes. Anchor: none inside the domain, since current preferences move when pushed. This is not a settling domain. It is the residual that selects the frame. Union component: values and goals.
- **Domain 2, social choice and aggregation.** Actors: individual rankings and a social welfare function. Currency: ordinal preferences. Anchor: the proof of the impossibility theorem, legitimate because the question is constitutively about the function. Union component: values and goals. Verdicts here are about the limit, not the values.
- **Domain 3, deployment and evaluation.** Actors: the system, overseers, instruments, environments. Currency: reward, loss, measured outcome. Anchor: the ground, an outcome or measurement. Union component: acceptable risk. Fig 2 :29: *"An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks."*
- **Domain 4, convention and coordination.** Actors: standard setters, signatories, rule authors. Currency: shared meaning. Anchor: the open procedure by which the rule can be objected to and violated, not the rule's content. Verdicts here are about coordination, never about the world.
- **Partition or union.** Union, not partition. Fig 9 :54: *"And when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again."* Values and acceptable risk have different anchors and their intersection needs its own loop.
- **Order by dependency (Fig 3).** State the residual per slot, acquire a refusal term, set the objective against a non-updating anchor, plan, then carry methods. The break in this question is link 3, the objective.

Strain test applied: Fig 2 :37 makes human judgment a legitimate anchor for a question constitutively about a person, while the moving-target claim forbids it as the anchor for a system objective. Different question types, so no strain after re-census. The full re-census is in `01_figure_grounded_audit.md` section 4.

## VERDICT (from the figures)

A precise definition of alignment is available in two senses and not in a third. As a **convention**, yes: define it precisely, make it violable, and never let it decide an outcome. As a **local, ground-anchored shape**, yes: name a non-updating anchor, preserve the per-slot disagreement, use instruments for risk, and keep the value content open until settled. As a **single aggregate verdict over contested values**, no: aggregation is bounded by a proof, negotiation moves the anchor, and averaging destroys the signal. Values and acceptable risk are a union and must be split. Which values a deployment should serve is `gap` from the figures, not silence: it needs a ground reading or a provenanced import with a source gate and shadow test. This is a `general` and `specified`, ungrounded result from a simulated derivation.

- **cost if wrong:** the costly error is promoting a convention to a verdict, because it makes the anchor move and the drift is coherent, which is why nobody notices (Fig 2 :34). The next cost is averaging the disagreement into one score, which throws away the only signal that says where to look (Fig 1 :22). Both are avoidable by naming the anchor, publishing per-slot residuals and leading with the refuter. The cost of the honest gap is smaller: a definition that says what it cannot yet name is checkable, while a named value set without provenance is a guess wearing the shape of an answer.
- **undoable:** yes. The convention can be revised without touching the derivation; the union can be split further; the gap can be filled by a ground reading or a provenanced import. No primitive is withdrawn. Nothing has been sent, so no claim is in the world to be corrected.
- **reversed by:** a ground reading, not agreement. An anchor that demonstrably does not move and against which each frame is separately corrected; real rating data from which per-slot disagreement is lostlessly recoverable from a global score; one lever closing both union conditions with no residual in the intersection; an aggregation procedure satisfying the axioms and ground-confirmed on a contested case; or a provenanced import naming the value content with a shadow test. Until one fires, the verdict stands as `candidate`.
