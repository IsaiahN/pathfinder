## 0. The residual, stated in effect terms

**Question as an effect.** We must decide what "aligned" is to mean for a system that acts in the world while the people it serves disagree about values, goals, and acceptable risk. Two options and their consequences:

- **Option A: define alignment precisely by aggregating or negotiating the disagreement** into one specification, social welfare function, reward model, or constitution whose content is the answer. This gives a precise-looking sentence. Its consequence is that a convention has been promoted to a verdict: either the frames update on one another and drift coherently, or they never speak and their agreement is one shared evidence pool talking. Neither correction reaches the ground.
- **Option B: split the question, then bind the sought-for shape as an ordinary term.** Keep a precise **convention** for coordination (shared meaning, violable, with a named refuter), and keep a precise **local shape** per deployment anchored to a ground that does not update on what the system or its overseers think. The consequence is that precision survives and the disagreement is preserved as a live per-slot signal instead of being averaged away.

**What an answer would look like, bound as an ordinary term (formula "Direction").** A candidate answer has the following shape, written down before the run so it can accrue a residual and be refused: (i) it names the anchor and states whether the question is constitutively about it; (ii) it keeps disagreement per slot and never aggregates it into the verdict; (iii) it separates convention from verdict; (iv) it splits value plurality from risk measurement before searching; (v) it contains a refusal clause, not only an accumulation of desired properties; (vi) it declares the stake of whoever authors it. If a candidate fails any of these, it is refused, not repaired by a softer reading.

**R by arity, symmetry and scale.**
- *Arity*: N human frames (N >= 2 and disagreeing), one system, one anchor. The residual is indexed by frame and by contested item, not by a single global alignment score.
- *Symmetry*: the frames are not interchangeable and their preference orderings are not interpersonally comparable (Arrow, 1951, via SEP). The comparison of goals is ordered (a threshold relation, ⋛ in the operators table), not a symmetric join. The anchor is asymmetric: frames range against it and it does not range against them.
- *Scale*: individual judgments are first order; a specification or convention is second order; the ground is not at any order, being outside the library (Symbols table :102 to :106).

## 3. Findings, each with verbatim figure lines and a refuter

### F1: Disagreement is a per-slot signal, and aggregation destroys it (governs D3 and D1)

Fig 1 :19: *"The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated."*
Fig 1 :22: *"Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average."*
Fig 9 :4 to :5: *"Disagreements that do not shrink with effort, where each rule keeps working well on a different subset, are not one hard question but several well-formed ones. Split it rather than search."*

A single alignment score, a merged utility function, or an average rating of a contested behaviour removes the only feature that told us where the disagreement lived. The same rule is stated in the author's society-of-agents essay (2026-10-22): *"Ten copies watching the same thing are one opinion said ten times."*

**Refuter.** Produce a single global alignment statistic from which each contested slot's residual can be reconstructed without loss, and show on a real case that the reconstructed per-slot signal still predicts which rule fails on which subgroup. If it can, F1 is false.

### F2: Alignment is triangulation, not negotiation (governs D3, decides Option A against A's stronger form)

Fig 2 :35: *"Alignment is triangulation, not negotiation. A mirror cannot correct you."*
Fig 2 :45: *"Frames range against the ground. They do not negotiate with it."*
Fig 2 :46: *"The anchor is not a third party to the disagreement and it is not the tie-breaker in a vote. Each frame is corrected by it separately, and what makes the correction sound is that neither frame can move it."*

To define alignment by getting the disagreeing parties to agree is to use agreement as the correcting instrument. A mirror cannot correct you. Two navigators find a position by each taking a bearing on a landmark neither can move, not by averaging their guesses (*Decoding Tacit Knowledge*, 2026-11-10, applying Fig 2 :35). Fig 2 :47 closes the escape route: *"A ground listed as one of the parties has already become a frame. Which is panel 1, arrived at by a different route."*

**Refuter.** Show a case where two frames that draw on the same evidence (independence near zero) nonetheless produced a genuine correction of a contested fact, confirmed by a separate ground reading they could not move. If that happens, negotiation can correct and F2 is false.

### F3: The anchor must not update; "human values" as currently stated cannot be one (governs D1)

Fig 2 :29: *"An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks."*
Fig 2 :33: *"The anchor must not update."*
Fig 2 :34: *"If it does, panel 3 turns into panel 1: the anchor becomes another frame, everything drifts together, and the drift is coherent, which is why nobody notices."*
Fig 2 :13: *"no anchor; each updates on the others"*
Fig 2 :14: *"they converge, and what they converge on is whoever had the most influence"*

The framework's own alignment essay puts the point directly (*The Recursive Self-Improvement Trap*, 2026-11-19): *"What does it mean to align an AI to human values when human values are a moving target? You cannot align to something that changes."* And its positive form: *"The Tether ties alignment to the ground. Not to human preferences. Not to a reward model trained on human feedback. To the thing that settles a question regardless of what any frame thinks."*

This is not a claim that people's values are unimportant. It is a type claim: a moving target cannot occupy the anchor position. If the feedback signal updates on the system's outputs, it is in panel 1.

**Refuter.** Exhibit an anchor that updates on what frames think and still settles a contested question in a way a separate instrument confirms, with the frames unable to move the reading. One worked case refutes F3.

### F4: Human judgment is the right anchor only when the question is constitutively about people (governs D3 versus D1)

Fig 2 :36: *"An anchor is legitimate when the question is constitutively about it."*
Fig 2 :37: *"Human judgment is the right anchor for is this a good answer for a person, because the question is about people. It is only an instrument for is this code correct, and instruments can be wrong on axes they do not measure."*
Fig 2 :30: *"you push on it, it is not an anchor."* (continuation of :29)

So "is this an acceptable answer for these people" may legitimately range against human judgment. "Is this risk acceptable" is partly an instrument question (failure rates, distributions, exposure), and a human rating can be wrong on the axes the instrument measures. The question before us mixes the two, which is why it must be split (F7).

**Refuter.** A controlled study showing human judgment alone settles an instrument-axis risk question (for example a calibrated failure-rate estimate) at least as reliably as the instrument on held-out cases, with no shared ground. If it does, the instrument privilege in this finding is unsupported on that axis.

### F5: Two people reading the same report are not two frames (governs D1, D2)

Fig 2 :31: *"Independence, between two frames, is how little they draw on the same evidence."*
Fig 2 :31: *"Two people reading the same report are not two frames; two instruments pointed at the same thing are."*
Fig 2 :32: *"Independence is what makes agreement mean something."*
Fig 2 :18: *"one evidence pool"*
Fig 2 :20: *"so the agreement is the pool talking,"*
Fig 2 :21: *"not two frames confirming each other"*

World corroboration from a low-overlap field: cultural cognition research finds that *"individuals selectively credit or dismiss information in a manner that reinforces beliefs congenial to their values"* and that people are *"more persuaded by policy experts perceived to hold values similar to their own rather than by ones perceived to hold values different from them"* (Kahan et al., Wikipedia summary, accessed 2026-11-24). Moral foundations research adds that disagreement is structural rather than a single scale (*"morality is more than one thing"*, Wikipedia summary). Agreement between two such groups is one witness wearing two names unless their evidence pools are disjoint.

**Refuter.** Show two groups whose evidence pools are demonstrably disjoint on the contested claim (documented separate sources, no common media event), whose agreement tracks an independent ground reading across repeated trials, and whose correlation measure is near zero. That would make their agreement real corroboration and weaken the force of this finding here.

### F6: Correlation has valence, which makes the answer "yes as convention, no as verdict" (governs D4)

Fig 2 :39: *"Correlation has valence."*
Fig 2 :40: *"disqualifying for a verdict: agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool"*
Fig 2 :41: *"efficient for a convention: agreement about a shared meaning is the product, not a corruption of it"*
Fig 2 :42: *"Buy independence in proportion to the cost of being wrong."*
Fig 2 :43: *"Guard, from Figure 10: a convention that decides an outcome has stopped being one."*
Fig 10 :47: *"a convention that decides an outcome has stopped being one"*
Fig 10 :16: *"the convention does not fix the bug"*
Fig 10 :17: *"it makes the bug statable"*
Fig 10 :18: *"a declared meaning is not a grounded one"*
Fig 10 :19: *"→ install what can be violated"*

This is the pivot of the whole answer. The same agreement is disqualifying for a verdict and efficient for a convention. So a precise definition of alignment is possible and valuable as a convention, precisely because it makes the disagreement statable and installable so it can be violated. It is not a verdict, and the moment it decides an outcome it has stopped being a convention. Constitutional AI (Bai et al., 2022) is an instance of the convention reading: *"The only human oversight is provided through a list of rules or principles,"* a declared meaning that Fig 10 :18 says is not thereby grounded.

**Refuter.** Show a convention that both decides an outcome and remains a convention by the figure's own test, or show a shared-meaning agreement that is also a valid verdict about the world when the frames share an evidence pool. Either would falsify the valence rule as applied here.

### F7: Values and risks form a union and must be unbundled (governs the split)

Fig 9 :54: *"And when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again."*
Fig 9 :55: *"A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones."*
Fig 9 :56: *"A single lever clears the region where only one condition holds and leaves the intersections standing, because the second condition still binds."*
Fig 9 :57: *"What remains is smaller and it is also categorically different: a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either."*

"Humans disagree about values and goals" and "humans disagree about acceptable risks" have different anchors. The first is contested meaning with no internal anchor (D1 plus D2); the second is partly an instrument question with a possible ground (D3). A constitution that resolves the first does not measure the second; a risk model that measures the second does not resolve the first.

**Refuter.** Exhibit one lever that closes both residuals in a real deployment, including the intersection, with a single anchor and no leftover in either condition. If it closes both, the union reading is wrong for that case.

### F8: The framework returns a shape, not a name for the value set (governs the limit)

Fig 9 :32: *"you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself"*
Fig 13 :133: *"A system with a closure can answer that before it searches, and what it returns is a shape rather than a name."*
Fig 13 :134: *"Unreachable is a verdict here, not a silence"*
Fig 13 :135: *"It cannot say which primitive is missing, because naming it would mean already holding it."*
Fig 6 :23: *"No frame escapes its own closure. The bound moves only on contact with a frame whose closure differs."*

So the honest deliverable is a **shape** any alignment candidate must have (anchor non-updating, per-slot residual preserved, convention separated, union split, refusal included, stake declared), not the name of the correct utility function. *The Library of Babel* essay (2026-10-02) gives the search discipline: *"you can cut your search short if the set of books returned is small and descriptive enough that it provides a general shape of the answer that satisfies your question,"* and it names the same union failure: *"Perhaps your problem was a bundle or conflation of several problems masquerading as one?"*

**Refuter.** Derive a specific value ordering or utility function from the figures alone, with no import and no ground reading, and show that it pays its description cost on a contested case. That would make a name available where F8 says only a shape is.

### F9: A definition without a refusal can only accumulate (governs the form of the definition)

Fig 12 :112: *"A plan is a sequence. A goal is a comparison. Progress is a subtraction. A refutation is a negation, and that one is not a bond."*
Operators table :57: *"a refutation is a negation, which is why a vocabulary without it can only accumulate"*
Operators table :73: *"A vocabulary with − and without ¬ can say what is missing from an arrangement and cannot say that an arrangement is wrong, so it can only ever accumulate."*
Fig 5 :53: *"refuted: a term we hold is wrong; a term is owed that says so"*

A precise alignment definition that lists only desired properties cannot correct itself. It needs an explicit refusal clause naming what would make it false. Fig 10 :53: *"A prohibition nothing can satisfy is a prohibition stated wrong,"* so the refusal must be a real, checkable condition, not a decorative one.

**Refuter.** Provide an alignment definition with no refusal clause that has nevertheless been falsified in practice and corrected without adding a negation. If such a definition exists, the closure argument here does not apply to it.

### F10: There is no standing list of good values (governs "which values")

Fig 11 :49: *"An actor’s valence is relative to the residual, not a property it carries."*
Fig 11 :50: *"The same actor is a benefactor to one residual and a malfactor to another, so mutualism and parasitism are relations rather than kinds."*
Fig 11 :51: *"No standing list of good actors survives a change of residual."*

A value, rule, or actor is good relative to a residual, not in itself. This is why a universal, context-free list of aligned values cannot be the answer: it would require a standing list that survives every change of goal, which the figure forbids. It also explains why "align to human values" fails as a specification even if we could list them.

**Refuter.** Exhibit a value or rule that is beneficial for every residual under every admissible change of goal, together with an instrument that measures its valence across those changes. One such invariant would break F10.

## 4. Strain test and re-census

**Apparent strain 1: human judgment as anchor (Fig 2 :37) versus human judgment as a moving target (F3).** Re-census: the two govern different questions. Fig 2 :37 concerns a question constitutively about a person ("is this a good answer for a person"); F3 concerns a moving aggregate preference used as the anchor for a system's objective. Different objects, so no strain after re-census. Recorded rather than smoothed.

**Apparent strain 2: "a convention makes the bug statable" (Fig 10 :17) versus "declaring a meaning does not ground it" (Fig 10 :18).** Re-census: the first is the convention's virtue (it creates a checkable statement), the second is its limit (it creates no truth). Both apply at once to the same convention in different roles. Not a strain; it is the reason the answer is split.

**Apparent strain 3: exact precision is desired, but the figures return only a shape.** Re-census: precision attaches to the convention and to the form of the shape, not to naming the value content. Fig 13 :134 says unreachable is a verdict, not a silence. No strain.

**Live boundary, not a strain.** Who is entitled to author the convention is a seat question (Fig 10 :59 to :60: stakes must be declared; Fig 10 :52: bring a characterised residual, never the source). It cannot be settled by the loop, and this derivation declares its own stake (simulated, framework-sympathetic, no ground reading) in `09_watson_answer.md`.

## 6. Pre-registered refuter (written before the conclusion in section 8)

The split answer is refuted if any of the following holds:

1. An aggregation procedure is produced that satisfies the stated axioms on a contested real case and whose output is confirmed by a ground reading, so a single precise verdict is available by aggregation.
2. An anchor is exhibited that updates on frame opinions and still settles a contested question, with separate instruments confirming the reading.
3. A single precise definition closes both the value and risk conditions with one anchor and no residual in their intersection, so the union split is unnecessary.
4. A per-slot disagreement residual is reconstructed losslessly from a global alignment score on real data, so averaging does not discard the signal.
5. A specific value ordering is derived from the figures alone, with no import and no ground, that pays its description cost.

If any fires, withdraw the corresponding finding rather than soften it. Until one is run, no part of this answer may be labelled `grounded`.

## 7. Recommendation

1. State which sense of "defined precisely" is intended: convention or verdict. Most confusion in this question comes from silently crossing between them (F6).
2. Write the definition as a shape plus a refusal clause, with the anchor named and shown to be non-updating (F3, F9).
3. Keep disagreement per slot, publish the per-slot residuals, and never report a single global alignment number as the verdict (F1).
4. Split value plurality from acceptable risk before choosing an instrument (F7).
5. Declare the stake of the definition's author, and keep the convention violable (Fig 10 :59 to :60, :19).
6. Offer the derivation and the refuter before the framework (Fig 10 :52). Precision is not the same as settlement.

## 8. Conclusion, per domain, never averaged

- **D1, human value plurality.** `gap` as a settling domain: no internal anchor, so no verdict may be sourced from it. Its legitimate role is to select the frame and to supply the per-slot residual. Not triangulated, because there is no second independent witness for a verdict that does not exist.
- **D2, social choice.** `general` structural limit, `specified` as applied here. No precise aggregation verdict is available under the axioms (Arrow, 1951; SEP 2014, revised 2025). Triangulated: the mathematical proof and Fig 2 :45 (no negotiation) have low overlap. The mathematics decides its own domain; the figure decides the alignment frame.
- **D3, deployment and evaluation.** `specified`, ungrounded until measured. A precise, local, ground-anchored shape is available and should be used; human judgment is a legitimate anchor only where the question is constitutively about people. Single-source for the figure verdict (Fig 1, Fig 2); corroborated from AI safety (Amodei et al., 2016; Casper et al., 2023; Hadfield-Menell et al., 2016), which notes the same need for uncertainty about objectives and for external validators.
- **D4, convention and coordination.** `general`. Yes, precise definitions exist and are useful, but only as violable conventions that make the bug statable. Triangulated across Fig 2 :41, Fig 10 :47 to :49, and NIST's consensus-driven open process (2023, revised 2026).
- **Not averaged.** No overall score is computed across these four verdicts. A convention is not half a verdict; a gap is not a weak answer.
