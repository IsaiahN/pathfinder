## 1. A shape is a constraint, not a filler

The figures README states the limit: the framework returns "what a missing thing would have to look like (a shape, not a name)". Fig 9 :32 "you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself". Fig 13: "It can say the answer is out of reach with the library as it stands... It cannot say which primitive is missing, because naming it would mean already holding it."

From *The Library of Babel*, read 2026-11-22: "you can cut your search short if the set of books returned is small and descriptive enough that it provides a general shape of the answer that satisfies your question."

So a **shape** is the narrowed form of an answer you can state before you can measure it. It constrains; it does not fill. For this question:

- **Shape:** "the sink persists where hydrological contact is maintained, the loop sign stays on the accumulation side with all gases counted, and landscape support holds; the three are separate conditions with separate instruments, indexed per site and microform."
- **Not the shape:** "peatlands will remain sinks," "peatlands will become sources," "the threshold water table is 7.8 cm," or any single global verdict.

The first is checkable per site. The second set either aggregates across slots or names a source from a reading.

## 2. How a shape is obtained

- *Library of Babel*: use the vocabulary you already have closest to the thing, look for recurring new terms that overlap or correlate with it, recurse. "With each new term gained, you narrow the space and deepen your vocabulary." For this question the bootstrapped vocabulary is: water table, redox, respiration, production, combustion, hysteresis, bistability, net carbon balance. Each new term narrows what an answer could be.
- Fig 6 :30 "The description travels. Fails by being sharpened into terms only we can read. Cast it in effect terms." Fig 9 :30-31: "your words are borrowed too, so describe the effect rather than the cause."
- *Decoding Tacit Knowledge*: "When contact isn't possible, the best you can send is a plain description of what you'd expect to see, because anyone can check that against the world." For a consulting peatland ecologist that description is: if contact is failing, deeper water tables should be accompanied by rising CO2 despite unchanged vegetation; if the loop sign is failing, the site should show production no longer exceeding respiration plus combustion.
- *Library of Babel* on bundling: "Perhaps your problem was a bundle or conflation of several problems masquerading as one? You will need to search all of those problems individually to find their answers and then combine them in a satisfactory way." This is the essay-level warrant for the union census in `01` §1.3.
- *Library of Babel* on stopping: "the finish line will also be the starting place" and "you have already composed the answer through the process of locating it."

## 3. What licenses a name

Only two things, both outside the shape itself:

1. **The ground settles it.** THE_FORMULA.md step 5: "the ground settles it. Verified only by what pays. A gate is not the ground." The README: the framework "stops at the limit of what the available instruments can distinguish, and records the lineage so the stop is honest."
2. **A provenanced import from a frame whose closure differs** (Fig 6 :23, Fig 8 :50-52), with a source gate and a shadow test.

For EAR-0059 a name would be a universal flip depth or a universal temperature threshold. Nothing in this folder licenses one. Jayasekara et al. 2026 report a cross-site transition "at 7.8 cm below the peat surface" while explicitly finding "no evidence of a water table threshold" and explaining only 19% of NEE variance with water-table depth. That is a reading with limited discrimination, not a name.

*They're Scanning Books When They Should* sharpens the point: "The real import is not a new term in the existing space. It is a new dimension of the space." Also: "A minimum viable set of six terms is a human's careful guess at reality's compression axes; another sensorium's minimum set need not be those six." and "Declaring the primitives does not make them neutral. It makes the choice visible." Declaring "sink" as a condition of the peat rather than of the peat-and-habitat is exactly the invisible choice being made visible here.

## 4. The source error this question is prone to

*The Hard Problem is About Source* gives the mechanism. Metamerism: an instrument reads green and "it cannot comment on the source". The same reading can come from one green light, or yellow plus blue, or RGB, or CMYK, or a scheme not yet discovered. "If several paths create the same behavior, then behavior is a projection with lossy information". And: "An instrument that can't distinguish these cases demonstrates its own inadequacy". It was the spectrometer, not an argument, that resolved it. "The answer is unreached until a better instrument exists."

Here the analogous error is naming the source of a single reading:

- A site is green and uptake-like in a remotely sensed index. The source could be a healthy Sphagnum carpet, a drained site with a productive but rapidly decomposing shrub layer, or a flooded site with high CH4 that the index never sees. The reading does not determine the source.
- A site is classed "intact" and yet is a net CO2 source in 19% of cases in the Jayasekara et al. 2026 synthesis; 21% of "degraded" sites were net sinks. The category is a projection; the flux is the closer reading; the source of the flux still requires a per-slot instrument.
- A water-table number is read as "the cause" of sink status, while CO2 and CH4 respond in opposite directions to the same number (Koch et al. 2023; Zou et al. 2022). The metamerism is in the single-variable reading.

What is owed is a better instrument: per-slot, per-microform net carbon balance including CH4, combustion and lateral losses, held long enough to see the sign change. Not another argument about whether peatlands are "generally" sinks.

## 5. Q — What does "shape" mean for this question? ANSWERED

**The answer is a conjunction with named instruments, not a global verdict.** The shape is C1 and C2 and C3 from `01` §1.3: hydrological contact maintained; loop sign on the accumulation side with CH4 and combustion included; landscape support not pushing the site across a state threshold. Each has a distinct instrument, each can fail separately, and the union must be unbundled before a lever is chosen. Anything that returns one global yes or no has aggregated across slots and has "thrown away the only signal that says where to look" (Fig 1 :22).

**Does the world literature commit the forbidden error?** Yes, regularly, when a meta-analysis reports a mean NEE per climate zone or condition and the headline becomes "peatlands are sinks" or "peatlands are sources." Jayasekara et al. 2026 themselves note that temperate, tropical and polar zones were mean net CO2 sources while alpine and sub-polar zones were mean sinks, that the Tropics, Africa and Oceania are underrepresented, and that methods vary substantially. A mean is a budget filter (Fig 9 :60), not a per-slot prediction. The correct form is the shape with the per-slot residual attached.

## 6. Correction to my own §4

I initially wrote that "shape, not a name" forbids naming any threshold. Reading the README again: the phrase governs "what a **missing** thing would have to look like", that is, a primitive not held in Γ. A measured site-level flip point is not a missing primitive; it is a ground reading. The rule that applies is the one about instruments, not the one about primitives: until a source-discriminating instrument pays, the number is a candidate reading with admitted lossy discrimination. The narrower claim stands (no universal name is licensed here); the broader claim is retracted. Both forms are recorded so neither is quietly lost.

## 7. Two consequences

**Prestige is not action.** From *The Recursive Self-Improvement Trap*, read 2026-11-22: "Prestige must never be allowed to validate an action directly." And: "Prestige can fund an action. It can give an action a budget to be tried. But it cannot settle an action. Only the ground can settle it. The Ground Maintainer must be blind to prestige." A campaign that gathers well-known peatland scientists to agree that the shape is plausible is the named collapse, not corroboration. The legitimate ask is the opposite: give the shape a budget to be tried at named sites, and let the ground settle it.

**This audit is a simulation, not a ground reading.** Same essay: "LLMs can quickly simulate an approximate answer/output with the formula found in the architecture post. It will often be better than the generic outputs that you will usually get otherwise too... However, the truly testable output would need to be derived from the real architecture or working the formula yourself." So `01` is a simulated derivation checked for internal consistency against the figures. It is not a test of the claim and must be labelled as such. It cannot settle anything it reports on.
