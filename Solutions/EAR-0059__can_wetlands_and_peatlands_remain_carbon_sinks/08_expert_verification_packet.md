# Expert verification packet: the Tether on wetland and peatland carbon sinks

**What this is:** a claim in a form a wetland or peatland researcher can check. Mode, derivation and refuters are stated before the conclusion, so a reader knows what to look for and what would settle it.

**Mode: `specified` on the shape, `general` on structural rules, not `grounded`.** No site-level carbon measurement is claimed here. This is a shape, a union and a route, not a result.

## 1. The claim, in one paragraph

Sink status is a property of a peatland and its habitat, not of the peat alone. The answer is indexed by site and microform: hummocks and pools in the same bog can have opposite carbon signs. Three conditions must be kept separate and measured with separate instruments: C1 hydrological contact (water table near the surface at the microform that actually crosses), C2 loop sign (measured net carbon balance with CO2, CH4 and combustion counted over a declared horizon), and C3 landscape support (groundwater flux and neighbouring state not pushing the site across a bistable threshold). A high water table changes valence with the gas and the horizon, so there is no universal good depth. Quick loss of sink status is a channel failure, pooled readings, missing gases, a logger in the wrong microform, rather than decay of the ground. The universal flip point is a declared `gap`, not a hidden answer.

## 2. Three checkable sub-claims

### C1 — framework-internal plus field-facing; checkable by reading Fig 11 and the site record

- **Statement:** sink status is peatland-and-habitat, and the water table's valence is relative to the residual. There is no standing list of good water-table depths.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 11 :39 "So the answerable question is not what intelligence is, but what makes a system capable. And capability is a property of agent-and-habitat, never of the agent alone." Also Fig 11 :49 "An actor's valence is relative to the residual, not a property it carries." and Fig 12 :121 "cycle. The output re-enters as input. The sign of the loop decides whether it closes, and a chain cannot close by construction."
- **What to check against the field:** Koch et al. 2023 report that "a deeper water table favors CO2 emissions and a very shallow water table permits CH4 emissions"; Zou et al. 2022 find that "greenhouse gas exchange rates peak in flooded and drained conditions." Is the same water position beneficial for one gas and harmful for the other at the sites you know?
- **Refuter:** a peatland whose measured sink is unchanged across a habitat substitution that removes water-table contact, or a water-table position monotonically beneficial for CO2 and CH4 at all horizons.

### C2 — methodological; checkable against a flux record

- **Statement:** the residual is per slot and per microform; a global or pooled mean is a budget filter, not a witness for the verdict.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 1 :19 "The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated." Fig 1 :22 "Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average." Fig 10 :26 "stale percepts, dropped transients, saturated metrics, pooled readings"
- **What to check:** Harris, Roulet and Moore 2020 report that in one drained bog "net ecosystem production (NEP) remained positive (C sink) for these microforms at the drained bog. Dry pools with mostly bare peat at the drained bog had negative NEP (C source to atmosphere)". Does your site record show the same coexistence of signs, and would a site mean hide it?
- **Refuter:** a pooled average that predicts prospectively and per named site which microform crosses to source next, paid against measured net carbon balance.

### C3 — framework-internal plus landscape-facing; checkable by reading Fig 9 and the landscape record

- **Statement:** the question is a union of three overlapping conditions, not one hard question; persistence is a chain of differing rooms; the flip point is a `gap`.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 9 :57 "What remains is smaller and it is also categorically different: a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either." Fig 7 :36 "A chain of mirrors is one long room. The next room must differ from the last, and match the residual it surfaced." Fig 9 :32 "you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself"
- **What to check:** van der Velde et al. 2021 distinguish forward and reverse thresholds and define "restoration resistance" as "the range in groundwater flux where both forests and peatlands are stable states". Do your sites show hysteresis, and can a single lever restore both hydrology and loop sign with no different residue?
- **Refuter:** one intervention that restores all three conditions with no categorically different residue, or a ground measurement that names a universal flip point and pays prospectively across sites.

## 3. What is explicitly not claimed

- Not that all peatlands remain sinks, and not that all become sources.
- Not that wetlands and peatlands are interchangeable categories.
- Not that rewetting is a universal remedy; emissions peak in flooded as well as drained conditions in Zou et al. 2022.
- Not that 7.8 cm below the surface is a threshold. Jayasekara et al. 2026 report "no evidence of a water table threshold" and explain 19% of NEE variance with water table depth.
- Not that peatland regulatory feedbacks are weak everywhere. Béguin et al. 2019 find high pattern resilience under drought, on a different ground (pattern statistics, not carbon balance); the disagreement is preserved in `01` §4.
- Not `grounded`. Nothing here was settled by a measurement taken in this folder.

## 5. What would settle it

A per-slot instrument, not a further argument. From *The Hard Problem is About Source*, an instrument that reads green "cannot comment on the source"; the spectrometer resolved it. Here:

1. Does the site's water table stay near the surface at the microform that crosses, through dry seasons?
2. With CH4 and combustion counted, does measured net carbon balance stay negative over the declared horizon, and does production exceed respiration plus loss?
3. Does landscape groundwater flux remain in the peatland basin, and is the neighbour state still peatland rather than forest?
4. At what depth and temperature does the sign flip, and does the same value flip a neighbouring slot?

## 6. What an expert can check in twenty minutes

1. Read Fig 11 :39, :49; Fig 1 :19; Fig 9 :57; Fig 7 :36; Fig 9 :32. Five short passages.
2. Check C1 against the sites you know: does the same water position help one gas and hurt the other?
3. Check C2 against one flux record: do microforms within a site have opposite signs, and would a site mean hide them?
4. Name one instrument that would discriminate the three conditions per slot, or say why none is available.
5. Send the refutation. A failure is more useful than an endorsement.
