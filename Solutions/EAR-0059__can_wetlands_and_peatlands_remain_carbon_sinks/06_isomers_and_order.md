# Sign, isomerism and order in the wetland carbon question

**Mode: `specified`.** This is a simulated derivation checked against the figures, not a ground reading (see `05_what_shape_means.md` §7).

## RESIDUAL

A site is observed as a sink in one period and a source in another, or one microform is a sink while its neighbour is a source. The question asked of this supporting derivation is: are these **different substances** in the figures' sense, or different degrees of one substance? And what order governs the transitions? If they are only degrees, one lever should move them continuously; if they are different arrangements with a sign, a single lever will clear one region and leave a categorically different residue (Fig 9 :57).

## DERIVATION

### Q1 — Is the sink/source difference a difference of substance?

- Fig 12 :121 "cycle. The output re-enters as input. The sign of the loop decides whether it closes, and a chain cannot close by construction."
- Fig 12 :117 "Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."
- Fig 12 :87 "a threshold relation between two quantities": the comparison at zero that separates sink from source.

A peatland that accumulates fixes carbon that becomes more peat, which holds more water, which suppresses decomposition further. The output re-enters as input with a positive sign. Reverse the sign and the same loop mines the stock. These are not two readings of one quantity; Fig 12 :121 says the sign decides whether the loop closes. So the strict figure reading is that sink and source are **different substances**, and the comparison `⋛` at zero is the relation that names which one is present.

### Q2 — Are the sink and source microforms isomers?

- Fig 12 :124 "functional group. A substructure whose presence fixes the outcome. The group says what kind; the qualifier says which member."
- Harris, Roulet and Moore 2020 report that in one drained bog "net ecosystem production (NEP) remained positive (C sink) for these microforms at the drained bog. Dry pools with mostly bare peat at the drained bog had negative NEP (C source to atmosphere)".

Two readings, kept separate:

1. **As the field record writes them**, the positively and negatively signed microforms do not share the same atom set. The bare peat pool has lost the Sphagnum cap and the sedge layer that the pristine pool and the hummock still hold. By Fig 12 :117 they are therefore **not isomers**. They are members of the same functional group, a peatland microform with a particular water-table qualifier, where the presence of the living cap fixes the outcome (Fig 12 :124). The specific qualifier is the water-table position relative to the peat surface.
2. **The stronger checkable form**: hold the atom set fixed, that is, the same peat, the same water, the same plant functional types and the same microbes, and vary only the arrangement (water table above versus below the surface; cap continuous versus broken). The claim is that the two arrangements are isomers under Fig 12 :117 and flip the sign of NEP. This is a real figure-derived prediction, not a reading of the Harris data, and it needs a controlled comparison to pay.

**Falsifier, written before the run:** if two microforms with an identical atom set and only a different water-table arrangement keep the same NEP sign across the growing season, the isomer claim fails and the functional-group reading stands alone. Conversely, if a microform changes sign while its atom set is unchanged, the functional-group reading is insufficient and the isomer claim survives.

### Q3 — What order governs the transition, and is it reversible?

- Fig 12 :72 "A then B; B needs A to have happened": sequence is ordered, not symmetric.
- van der Velde et al. 2021 define "restoration resistance" as "the range in groundwater flux where both forests and peatlands are stable states" and distinguish the tipping point from peatland to forest from the one in the opposite direction.

The transition is **ordered and path dependent**. A peatland-to-forest shift and a forest-to-peatland restoration are not inverse operations: the water-table change needed to restore is larger than the change that caused the loss, because the peat stock and the water-holding structure have changed while the path ran. This is a sequence bond, not a symmetric one. Fig 12 :119 adds the catalyst term: "A part that survives its own use. Spent and regenerating parts behave differently across uses, and nothing records which a term is." The Sphagnum cap that regenerates across wet years is a catalyst; after severe drying and fire it may be spent, so the same nominal site no longer has the same term available.

**Falsifier:** a recorded transition whose forward and reverse thresholds coincide, with no hysteresis and no change in the retaining stock, would break the ordered-bond reading and reduce the transition to a symmetric comparison.

### Q4 — What does the microform-to-global round trip lose?

- Fig 4 :30 "So take the round trip and measure the gap. Without it you are navigating by dead reckoning: confident about a position nobody checked."
- Symbols table, `R_T`: the gap between what was sent up and what came back down, `gap(x, (T_E ∘ T_A)(x))`.

Going up from a site to a global budget throws away which microform crossed and why. That is the point of the abstraction, but it means the budget cannot say where to act. Jayasekara et al. 2026 illustrate the danger: mean NEE differs by climate zone, land use and condition, while 19% of "intact" sites were net CO2 sources and 21% of "degraded" sites were net sinks. A global total can be right as a total and still be useless as a diagnosis. R_T is nonzero and must be reported qualitatively as the set of dropped distinctions: microform identity, gas identity, horizon, combustion loss and hysteresis direction. It was not measured numerically because no ground reading was taken here.

## CONCLUSION

Sink and source are different substances under Fig 12 :121 when atoms are shared and only the bond differs; as the field records currently write them they are a functional group distinguished by the living cap and the water-table qualifier. The transition is ordered and hysteretic, not symmetric, and the catalyst term decides whether a site can regenerate after a burn. The round trip from microform to global budget loses the per-slot signal, so the gap R_T must be carried explicitly. What survives the derivation is the structure, the sign and the order, not a universal threshold.
