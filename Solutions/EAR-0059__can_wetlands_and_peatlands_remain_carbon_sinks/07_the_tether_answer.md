# The Tether's answer: can wetlands and peatlands remain carbon sinks?

**Mode: `specified` on the substantive shape, `general` on the structural rules, not `grounded`.** This is a shape, a union and a route, not a result. Simulated derivation checked for internal consistency against the figures (`05_what_shape_means.md` §7), not a ground reading.

## 1. The residual it answers

R = |Γ(b,a) − o′| per slot s. Here s is a wetland or a microform inside it, a is warming plus a hydrological change, and o′ is the measured net carbon balance with all gases and combustion counted. The observed phenomenon is that some slots keep the uptake sign across change while others cross to release, and that different studies report different global verdicts. The question as asked, "can they remain sinks", is a bundled union that has to be unbundled before it can be answered (Fig 9 :55): "A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones."

## 2. The shape

1. **Sink status is a property of peatland and habitat, not of peat alone.** Fig 11 :39 "So the answerable question is not what intelligence is, but what makes a system capable. And capability is a property of agent-and-habitat, never of the agent alone." A list of peat properties is Γ, a candidate library whose reach must be tested against the ground, not the answer.

2. **The answer is per slot and per microform, never a global average.** Fig 1 :19 "The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated." Fig 1 :22 "Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average." Within one drained bog, hummocks stayed sinks while dry pools became sources (Harris, Roulet and Moore 2020). A global yes or no has already thrown away the signal that says where to act.

3. **There are three conditions, kept separate.** C1: hydrological contact maintained, so the acrotelm stays saturated and decomposition is slow. C2: the loop sign stays on the accumulation side, with CH4 and combustion included, over a declared horizon. C3: landscape support (groundwater flux and the neighbour state, forest or peatland) does not push the site across a threshold. Fig 9 :57 "What remains is smaller and it is also categorically different: a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either." A site failing only C1 and one failing only C2 need different levers.

4. **The same water table changes valence with the residual and the horizon.** Fig 11 :49 "An actor's valence is relative to the residual, not a property it carries." A high water table suppresses CO2 yet permits CH4; a low one reduces CH4 yet accelerates oxidation and raises burn severity. Koch et al. 2023 report that "a deeper water table favors CO2 emissions and a very shallow water table permits CH4 emissions", and Zou et al. 2022 find that "greenhouse gas exchange rates peak in flooded and drained conditions." There is no standing list of good water tables.

5. **Quick loss of sink status is a channel failure, not a decay of the ground.** Fig 10 :24 "the ground does not decay, the channel does". Pooled readings, a missing CH4 term and a water-table logger in the hummock but not the pool are losses of contact (Fig 10 :26). The ground itself does not move when a frame stops reading it.

6. **Persistence is a chain of differing rooms.** Fig 7 :36 "A chain of mirrors is one long room. The next room must differ from the last, and match the residual it surfaced." A site that keeps returning the same category and the same measurement is not gaining evidence about its future. New predictive terms arrive only from frames whose closures differ (Fig 6 :23).

7. **The universal flip point is a `gap`.** Fig 9 :32 "you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself". Fig 13 :134 "Unreachable is a verdict here, not a silence". The figures return the conditions and the falsifiers, not the depth or temperature at which a given site flips. What is owed is a source-discriminating instrument, not another argument.

## 3. What it explicitly does not say

- Not that all peatlands remain sinks, and not that all become sources. Both are aggregates across slots.
- Not that wetlands and peatlands are interchangeable. They share a hydrological habitat but differ in substrate, vegetation and the size of the stock at risk.
- Not that rewetting is a universal remedy. Zou et al. 2022 find emissions peak in flooded as well as drained conditions; the correct target and its sign are site-specific.
- Not a universal threshold water-table depth or temperature. The 7.8 cm result in Jayasekara et al. 2026 is a cross-site reading with 19% explained variance and no asserted threshold, not a name.
- Not that the regulators are weak everywhere. Béguin et al. 2019 find high resilience of spatial pattern under drought, but their ground is pattern statistics, not carbon balance; the disagreement is recorded in `01` §4.
- Not `grounded`. No measurement in this folder settles the claim.

## 4. What would settle it, the ground

A per-slot instrument, not a further argument. From *The Hard Problem is About Source*: a colour instrument "cannot comment on the source"; the spectrometer resolved it. Here the corresponding questions are:

- **C1 test:** does the site's water table stay near the surface through the dry season, measured at the microform that actually crosses, not at the hummock?
- **C2 test:** with CH4 and combustion counted, does measured net carbon balance stay negative over the declared horizon, and does production still exceed respiration plus loss at the site?
- **C3 test:** does the landscape groundwater flux remain in the basin of the peatland state, and does the neighbour state stay peatland rather than forest? van der Velde et al. 2021 give "restoration resistance" as the figure of merit, so the forward and reverse thresholds must both be measured.
- **Threshold test:** with per-slot balances in hand, does a flip occur, at what water-table depth and temperature, and does the same value flip a neighbouring slot? Until this is run, F8 stays a `gap`.
- **Standing falsifiers:** the seven in `01` §5, in particular sink unchanged across removal of water-table contact, one lever restoring all three conditions, a pooled average predicting named slots, and a monotonic water table.

## 5. The honest stop

**Unreached, not unreachable.** The conditions and the falsifiers are in hand; the per-slot instrument that would name the flip is not. The framework stops at the limit of what the available instruments distinguish and records the lineage so the stop is checkable. "Indiscernibility is not identity." The fact that this derivation cannot name a threshold is a result about this frame, not a claim that no threshold exists in the world.

## 6. One-paragraph version for outreach

> The Tether does not answer yes or no to whether wetlands and peatlands will remain carbon sinks, because that bundles several conditions into one. It says that sink status is a property of peatland and habitat, not of peat alone; that the answer must be indexed by site and microform, since hummocks and pools in the same bog can have opposite signs; and that three conditions have to be kept separate, hydrological contact, the sign of the carbon loop with methane and fire counted, and landscape support against a state shift. The same water table changes sign with the gas and the horizon, so no universal "good" depth exists. Quick loss of sink status is a failure of the channel to the ground, that is pooled readings and missing gases, rather than a decay of the ground itself. What the framework offers is this shape, seven refuters written in advance, and a `gap` for the flip point that only a per-slot net carbon instrument can close.
