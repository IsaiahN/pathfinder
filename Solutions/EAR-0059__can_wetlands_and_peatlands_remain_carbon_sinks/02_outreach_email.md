**Use:** one to one, or as a cover letter attached to a submission through the public venues in

**Subject:** One checkable claim about peatland sink status, before any theory

> Hi [name],
>
> I have one specific output I would like you to check against what you already know about
> wetlands and peatlands. I am not asking you to read the framework first, and I am not asking
> you to agree with it. If the output does not survive, there is nothing else to look at.
>
> **The output (`specified`, not `grounded`):** the carbon-sink status of a peatland is a property
> of the peatland and its habitat, not of the peat alone, and it has to be indexed by site and by
> microform. Three conditions have to be kept separate because they have different instruments:
> (1) hydrological contact, the water table staying near the surface at the microform that
> actually crosses; (2) the sign of the carbon loop, with CO2, CH4 and combustion all counted over
> a declared horizon; (3) landscape support, the groundwater flux and the neighbouring state not
> pushing the site across a shift to forest or to a non-peatland state. The same water table
> suppresses CO2 yet permits CH4, so there is no single good depth.
>
> Quick loss of sink status is, on this reading, a failure of the channel to the ground, a pooled
> site mean, a missing methane term or a logger in the hummock rather than the pool, and not a
> decay of the ground itself. I am explicitly **not** claiming that all peatlands remain sinks or
> that all become sources. I am not claiming a universal flip depth. The 7.8 cm figure in a recent
> synthesis is a cross-site reading, not a threshold they assert.
>
> **What would refute the shape claim:**
> 1. a peatland whose measured net sink is unchanged across a habitat substitution that removes
>    its water-table contact;
> 2. one intervention that restores all three conditions with no categorically different residue;
> 3. a pooled global average that predicts, prospectively and per named site, which microform
>    crosses to source next;
> 4. a water-table position that is monotonically beneficial for CO2 and CH4 at every horizon and
>    in every peat type;
> 5. sink status lost while per-slot net carbon balance and water-table contact both measure high
>    and non-declining.
>
> I wrote these down before sending this. If any one holds, the shape is wrong and I will retract
> it. A one-line "this fails because..." is more useful to me than agreement. If you would rather
> not engage, no reply is needed.
>
> **If it survives, and only then:** the framework sources are below. The figures are the
> authority, not my summary of them.
> - Verbatim figure text with line numbers (local framework copy):
>   `FRAMEWORK/reference/03-FIGURES_TEXT.md`
> - Figures and formula, branch `general-agent`: https://github.com/IsaiahN/tether/tree/general-agent/figures

## Recording the verdict before the theory

Stage 1 verdict is whatever the recipient returns on the five refuters, recorded as: which fired, which did not, what instrument they would use, and the date. Only after that is recorded do we offer the three links. A later agreement with the framework is never pooled as corroboration (Fig 2 :40 "agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool"). The recipient's expertise counts because it can break the shape, not because it endorses it.
