# Expert verification packet - unequal treatment, inconsistent enforcement, and institutional legitimacy over time

**What this is:** a claim in a form a procedural justice researcher can check. Mode, derivation, and refuters are stated before the conclusion, so a reader knows what to look for and what would settle it.

**Mode: `specified` - not `grounded`.** No new measurement is claimed. This is a shape and a route, not a result.

**Status of this packet:** a simulated derivation checked for internal consistency against the figures, not a ground reading. It cannot settle the claim it reports on.

## 1. The claim, in one paragraph

Unequal treatment and inconsistent enforcement are not two doses of unfairness. They are overlapping conditions in a union with different instruments, and they stand to each other as isomers: the same rule-and-case atoms joined by a comparison bond across subjects versus a sequence bond across occasions. Legitimacy is not a stock an institution holds. It is a property of the people judging it and the habitat they judge it in. It can fall quickly when the channel that tells people about actual treatment decays, even if the institution keeps treating people fairly, and it persists when that channel stays reachable and people meet a differing closure, the same institution applying the rule neutrally even when the outcome is unwelcome. The same enforcement act changes sign with the group's residual, and cooperation re-enters as input to later encounters, so the loop can hold or collapse. The framework does not name the reform that fills the slot. It returns the shape any candidate answer must have, two anchors kept separate, and falsifiers written in advance.

## 2. Three checkable sub-claims

### C1 - framework-internal; checkable by reading Figures 9 and 12

- **Statement:** the question is a union of two overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones; unequal treatment and inconsistent enforcement are isomers under the figure's definition.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 9 :55 - **"A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones."** Fig 9 :57 - **"What remains is smaller and it is also categorically different: a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either."** Fig 12 :117 - **"isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."**
- **What to check:** is the comparison/sequence distinction faithful to how you use the two concepts? In particular, does a remedy aimed at outcome equality act on a different axis from one aimed at consistency of application?
- **Refuter:** one intervention that restores both conditions in both failure types, measured per group and per occasion, with no categorically different residue left over. Or a single scalar that represents both with no loss of predictive information. Either breaks the union and isomer claim.

### C2 - measurement-facing; checkable by a legitimacy researcher

- **Statement:** legitimacy must be indexed per subject and per group; the aggregate trust score is a pooled reading, and the institution's own legitimacy report is disqualified as a ground. Two anchors are required: per-subject judgment for the legitimacy question, an independent record of treatment for the causal question.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 1 :19 - **"The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated."** Fig 2 :36 - **"An anchor is legitimate when the question is constitutively about it."** Fig 10 :93 - **"A ground with a reputation to protect is a ground someone benefits from, and its keeper is thereby disqualified."**
- **What to check:** would your design distinguish a group whose legitimacy is falling from a national average that is stable? Does it treat the institution's self-description as evidence or as a claim to be refused? The world literature is invited as corroboration, not as authority: Tyler 2006 defines legitimacy through the belief of *"those connected to"* an authority; De Micheli and Taylor 2024 warn that *"confidence in institutions should not be interpreted as synonymous with institutional legitimacy"* and find that *"personal experiences shape the trustworthiness of the court."*
- **Refuter:** a single aggregate index that predicts prospectively, group by group, which group's legitimacy will fall next, with no group-level measurement used. Or a documented case where institutional self-report predicts compliance independently of any subject judgment and survives an independent treatment audit. Either weakens the two-anchor requirement.

### C3 - time-facing; checkable against longitudinal evidence

- **Statement:** quick erosion is channel failure rather than decay of fair treatment; legitimacy over time is a signed cycle in which cooperation re-enters as input.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 10 :24 - **"the ground does not decay, the channel does"**; Fig 10 :26 - **"saturated metrics, pooled readings"**; Fig 12 :121 - **"cycle. The output re-enters as input. The sign of the loop decides whether it closes, and a chain cannot close by construction."**
- **What to check:** Walters and Bolger 2019 report that the direct procedural-justice-to-compliance path *"could not be reliably established in longitudinal datasets"*, while the mediated paths survive. Menzner and Schweizer 2026 find *"self-reinforcing dynamics"* and only limited support for a buffering reservoir. Do those two results support a loop, or do they require a different time model? A failure here narrows the claim; it does not flip a figure verdict.
- **Refuter:** a group loses legitimacy while independent instruments show accurate perception of treatment and real neutral treatment, both high and non-declining; or a panel in which cooperation at t demonstrably has no bearing on the encounter at t+1 once prior judgment is controlled. Either breaks C3.

## 3. What is explicitly not claimed

- Not that unfair treatment mechanically destroys legitimacy, or that any specific reform is the answer.
- Not that unequal treatment and inconsistent enforcement are one condition under two names. They are a union.
- Not that a national trust score, an impact factor, or a well-cited author settles anything. *"Use filters for the budget and witnesses for the verdict. Never let a filter hand you a verdict."* (Fig 9 :60)
- Not that procedural justice theory is derived from the figures. It is a peer frame, and its four components are a binding with no source gate for a new institution.
- Not that the institution's own legitimacy rating is a ground. Fig 10 :93 disqualifies a ground with a reputation to protect.
- Not `grounded`. No per-subject panel and no independent treatment record were collected here.
- Not corroborated by this packet's two simulated seats. They share one evidence pool, so their agreement is internal consistency only.

## 5. What would settle it

Two anchors, kept separate: per-subject judgment and an independent record of treatment (Fig 2 :29, :36-37). The missing instrument would discriminate three sources of one survey reading: unequal outcomes, inconsistent application, and a degraded channel. From *The Hard Problem is About Source*, an instrument that reads green *"cannot comment on the source"*; the spectrometer resolved it. Here a per-slot panel with treatment coded independently per case would be the corresponding discriminator. Eight numbered falsifiers are in `01_figure_grounded_audit.md` section 4.

## 6. What an expert can check in twenty minutes

1. Read Fig 9 :55, :57 and Fig 12 :117, :121, four short passages, and say whether the union and cycle claims are faithful readings.
2. Check C2 against your own design practice: does your measure survive per-group indexing, and would you disqualify the institution's own score?
3. Check C3 against your own longitudinal result: does the direct path failure reported by Walters and Bolger support or weaken a mediated loop?
4. Name one instrument that would distinguish unequal treatment from inconsistent enforcement from a degraded channel in your institution, or say why none is available.
5. Send the refutation. A failure is more useful than an endorsement.
