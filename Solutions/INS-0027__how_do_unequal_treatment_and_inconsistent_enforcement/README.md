## What is deliberately not here

- No personal emails of named researchers.
- No ranking of people by how likely they are to agree that unequal treatment erodes legitimacy.
- No bulk-send plan.
- No claim that this derivation settles the question. It does not.

Three figure-grounded reasons, stated in the source's own words:

1. **Agreement is not an anchor.** *"Alignment is triangulation, not negotiation. A mirror cannot correct you."* (Fig 2 :35). A list selected for receptiveness manufactures the correlation that would make its agreement worthless.
2. **Receptiveness is a disqualifying selection criterion for a verdict.** Fig 9 :51 separates evidence bearing on the residual from *"a stake-inducing channel, and the verifier is denied it."* Fig 10 :59: *"an interest in the outcome is unavoidable, an interest in the reading is disqualifying."*
3. **The outside frame must not hold the interiors.** *"A verifier that can reconstruct the claim cannot verify it."* (Fig 10 :42). First contact carries the residual and its refuter, not the framework.

Legally: unsolicited bulk email to personal addresses breaches GDPR/CAN-SPAM-style rules in many jurisdictions. Public opt-in submission and conference routes avoid that failure as well as the structural one.
