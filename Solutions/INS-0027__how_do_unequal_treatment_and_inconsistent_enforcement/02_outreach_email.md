# Outreach draft - validate the output first, then decide about the framework

**Use:** one-to-one, or as a cover letter attached to a submission through a public venue in `03_public_contact_points.csv`. **Not** for bulk send.

**Hard precondition:** do not send until the shape claim in `01_figure_grounded_audit.md` survives the refuter listed in section 4. The output must survive validation or the two-stage offer collapses on contact.

---

**Subject:** Check one claim about unequal treatment and legitimacy before you decide whether the framework is worth reading

> Hi [name],
>
> I have one specific claim I would like you to check against what you already know. I am not asking you to read a theory first, and I am not asking you to agree with it. If the claim does not survive, there is nothing else to look at.
>
> **The claim (`specified`, not `grounded`):** unequal treatment and inconsistent enforcement are not two doses of the same thing. They are overlapping conditions in a union with different instruments. Legitimacy is not a stock an institution holds. It is a property of the people judging the institution and the habitat they judge it in. So an institution can keep treating people equally while its legitimacy falls quickly, if the channel that tells people about that treatment decays: isolated incidents stand for the whole, aggregate scores saturate, and group-level readings are pooled. Conversely, a single bad outcome need not destroy legitimacy if the procedure around it is neutral, gives voice, and treats the person with dignity.
>
> **What I am claiming about time:** legitimacy is a signed loop. Compliance and cooperation re-enter as inputs to later judgments. The same enforcement act can build legitimacy in one group and destroy it in another, depending on what that group expects. A chain of identical reviews or statements produces agreement, not evidence.
>
> **What I am not claiming:** that any particular reform restores legitimacy, that a single national trust score settles the question, that legitimacy is a property of the institution alone, or that this is a measured result. This is a shape, not an answer. I cannot name which reform fills the slot, and I am not asking you to supply a name.
>
> **What would refute the shape claim:**
> 1. legitimacy measured per person and per group is unchanged across a habitat shift that removes the institution's actual payment for fair treatment;
> 2. one aggregate trust score predicts, group by group and prospectively, which group will lose legitimacy next, with no group-level data used;
> 3. one intervention restores both conditions in both failure types with no categorically different residue left over;
> 4. legitimacy falls while independent instruments show both accurate perception of treatment and real neutral treatment, both high and non-declining;
> 5. the same enforcement act has invariant sign across a documented change in the affected group's expectations;
> 6. compliance and cooperation demonstrably do not feed back into later legitimacy judgments.
>
> I wrote these down before sending this. If any one holds, I will retract the claim. A one-line "this fails because..." is more useful to me than agreement. If you would rather not engage, no reply is needed.
>
> **If it survives, and only then:** I can send the source notes and the figure text. The figures are the authority, not my summary of them. None of this has been settled by measurement, and I will not call it settled.
>
> **If you want to reuse the method:** the eight-step loop is public. Run it on a problem you know well and can check against a ground. Write down what would refute your result *before* you run it, and state the mode of what you get. A reported failure is more useful to me than a reported success.
>
> - Isaiah (figure-grounded submission, one-to-one)

## Stage 1 before stage 2

Record the recipient's verdict on the claim before any framework material is shown. Once a reader has absorbed the framework, their agreement stops being evidence: *"A verifier that can reconstruct the claim cannot verify it."* (Fig 10 :42). Later agreement is never pooled as corroboration, because *"agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool"* (Fig 2 :40).
