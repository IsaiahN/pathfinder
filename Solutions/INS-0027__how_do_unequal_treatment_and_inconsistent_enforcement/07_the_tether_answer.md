# The Tether's answer on unequal treatment, inconsistent enforcement, and institutional legitimacy over time

**Mode: `specified` - not `grounded`.** This is a shape and a route, not a result. Simulated derivation checked against the figures (`05_what_shape_means.md` §6), not a ground reading.

## 1. The residual it answers

An institution's subjects expect the same rule to apply to like cases and to apply consistently over time. What they observe is differential treatment and uneven enforcement. R = |Γ(b,a) - o′| is indexed per subject and per group, never averaged into a national trust score.

> **"The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated."** (Fig 1 :19)

Legitimacy is the state of a slot whose residual stays small across successive handoffs: the subject still judges the institution appropriate and entitled to be obeyed even when a particular decision goes against them. Erosion is a residual that persists and does not trend down. The question is a union of two conditions, not one hard problem: a ground condition (treatment really is equal and enforcement really is consistent) and a channel condition (subjects keep contact with that treatment and meet disconfirming experience over time).

> **"What remains is smaller and it is also categorically different: a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either."** (Fig 9 :57)

## 2. The shape, by domain

**D1, the encounter loop.** Legitimacy is a property of subject-and-institution, not of the institution alone.

> **"And capability is a property of agent-and-habitat, never of the agent alone."** (Fig 11 :39)

A reform that changes internal rules without changing what subjects actually experience is an improvement that does not change contact (Fig 11 :40). The subject's own judgment of the encounter is an anchor, because the question is constitutively about people (Fig 2 :36-37).

**D2, the rule and enforcement frame.** Equal treatment and consistent enforcement are isomers: same rule-and-case atoms, a comparison bond across subjects versus a sequence bond across occasions (`06_isomers_and_order.md` Q1). They need different instruments and cannot be blended into one fairness score. The anchor for the causal question is an independent record of what was actually done, not the institution's own report.

> **"A ground with a reputation to protect is a ground someone benefits from, and its keeper is thereby disqualified."** (Fig 10 :93)

**D3, the social habitat.** The same enforcement act changes sign with the group's residual.

> **"An actor’s valence is relative to the residual, not a property it carries."** (Fig 11 :49)
> **"No standing list of good actors survives a change of residual."** (Fig 11 :51)

That is why a neutral procedure can raise legitimacy after an unwelcome outcome in one group and do nothing in a group whose history reads it as theatre. Jackson 2018 reports the cross-national version: procedural justice is *"the strongest predictor of police legitimacy in most of the countries under investigation"*, but fair-process judgments may be displaced by effectiveness concerns, corruption, crime scale, and an oppressive history. Culture is habitat, not ground (Fig 11 :73).

**D4, the temporal channel.** Quick erosion is a failure of the channel, not a decay of the ground.

> **"the ground does not decay, the channel does"** (Fig 10 :24)
> **"saturated metrics, pooled readings"** (Fig 10 :26)

A group can lose legitimacy while treatment is still fair, because memorable incidents stand for the whole and aggregate statistics hide the group's reading. Persistence, when it happens, is a chain of differing rooms selected against the surfaced residual.

> **"same closure, handed along;"** (Fig 7 :12) **"agreement compounds, evidence does not"** (Fig 7 :13)
> **"A chain of mirrors is one long room. The next room must differ from the last, and match the residual it surfaced."** (Fig 7 :36)

**The loop.** Legitimacy is not a one-way chain. Compliance and cooperation re-enter as inputs to later encounters.

> **"cycle. The output re-enters as input. The sign of the loop decides whether it closes, and a chain cannot close by construction."** (Fig 12 :121)

Menzner and Schweizer 2026 find *"self-reinforcing dynamics"* in 29 waves of panel data and only limited support for a buffering reservoir. That corroborates the cycle; it does not settle its sign for any institution.

**Renewal.** A group whose only experience is unfairness holds a closure that predictably returns the same verdict. No amount of internal reframing adds the disconfirming atom.

> **"No frame escapes its own closure. The bound moves only on contact with a frame whose closure differs."** (Fig 6 :23)

The disconfirming atom here is an actual experience of the same institution applying the rule neutrally, with voice and dignity, especially when the outcome is unwelcome. Tyler and Jackson 2014, citing Tyler and Fagan 2008, report exactly that shape.

## 3. What it explicitly does not say

- Not that unequal treatment and inconsistent enforcement are one dose of unfairness. They are a union.
- Not that legitimacy is a stock the institution owns, or that a national trust score settles the question.
- Not that any specific reform is the answer. The framework returns a shape, not a name (Fig 9 :32).
- Not that procedural justice theory is derived from the figures. It is a peer frame; its four components are a binding with no source gate for a new institution.
- Not that the institution's own legitimacy report is a ground. It has a reputation to protect (Fig 10 :93).
- Not `grounded`. No per-subject record and no independent treatment record were collected here.

## 4. What would settle it - the ground

Two anchors are needed, kept separate: per-subject judgment for the legitimacy question, and an independent record of treatment for the causal question (Fig 2 :29, :36-37). The discriminating instrument would have to separate three sources of one survey reading: unequal outcomes, inconsistent application, and a degraded channel. From *The Hard Problem is About Source*, an instrument that reads green *"cannot comment on the source"*; the spectrometer resolved it. Here:

- **Per-slot panel:** record the same subjects' judgments across repeated encounters, with treatment independently coded per case. Does R stay small, trend down, or persist?
- **Comparison test:** hold consistency fixed and vary equality across groups, then hold equality fixed and vary consistency across occasions. If one factor never moves legitimacy, the union claim narrows.
- **Channel test:** does accurate knowledge of actual treatment mediate the effect? If legitimacy falls while knowledge stays high and treatment stays fair, F4's mechanism is wrong.
- **Cycle test:** does cooperation at time t predict the character of the encounter at time t+1, with the subject's prior judgment controlled? If not, F8 fails.
- **Standing falsifiers:** the eight listed in `01_figure_grounded_audit.md` section 4, including derivation of one of the six physical terms from five, which would break the figures themselves.

## 5. The honest stop

**Unreached, not unreachable.** A minimum viable set of six physical terms is *"a human's careful guess at reality's compression axes"* ( essay *They're Scanning Books*), and declaring it makes the choice visible, not neutral. Here the stop is at the shape: two conditions, two anchors, a signed loop, and eight falsifiers. Which reform belongs in a given institution is a `gap`, not a hidden answer.

> **"you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself"** (Fig 9 :32)

The lineage is recorded so the stop is checkable. *"Indiscernibility is not identity"* (essay *How Intelligence Actually Works*): a shape that fits every available reading is still not the source of that reading.

## 6. One-paragraph version for outreach

> The Tether does not say that unfair treatment mechanically destroys institutional legitimacy, and it does not name a reform. It says that unequal treatment and inconsistent enforcement are two different arrangements of the same rule: one compares across people, the other across occasions, so one lever cannot clear their intersection. Legitimacy is not a stock an institution holds. It is a property of the people judging it and the habitat they judge it in, so it can fall quickly when the channel that tells people about actual treatment decays, even if the institution keeps treating people fairly. It persists when that channel stays reachable and when people meet a differing closure, the same institution applying the rule neutrally even when the outcome is unwelcome. The same enforcement act changes sign with the group's history, and compliance feeds back into the next encounter, so the loop can hold or collapse. What is offered is the shape any candidate answer must have, two anchors kept separate, and eight falsifiers written in advance. The slot for the specific reform stays open until the ground settles it.
