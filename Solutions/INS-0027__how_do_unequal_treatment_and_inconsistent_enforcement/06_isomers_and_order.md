# Are unequal treatment and inconsistent enforcement isomers? Structure, order, and the legitimacy loop

**Mode: `specified`.** This is a simulated derivation checked against the figures, not a ground reading (see `05_what_shape_means.md` §6).

## RESIDUAL

The primary audit leaves three structural questions open:

1. Are unequal treatment and inconsistent enforcement two arrangements of the same atoms, or one condition under two names?
2. What is the bond that makes legitimacy a process over time rather than a snapshot?
3. Does crossing from a subject to a group to an institution preserve what was measured, and how is the loss reported?

## DERIVATION

### Q1 - Are the two conditions isomers?

- Fig 12 :117: **"isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."**
- Fig 12 :94: **"The arrangement is the substance."**
- Unequal treatment: `{rule, case A, case B, subjects A and B, treatment}` under **`⋛` comparison** across subjects. Fig 12 :87: **"a threshold relation between two quantities"**; Operators table :51: *"a goal is a comparison, which is why a vocabulary without it cannot state one."*
- Inconsistent enforcement: `{rule, case A, time 1, time 2, treatment}` under **`→` sequence** across occasions. Fig 12 :72: **"A then B; B needs A to have happened"**; Operators table :34: *"the only bond a flat chain can express, so every term today is a sequence whether or not it should be."*
- Same atoms from the rule's point of view, different bond, different behaviour. Under Fig 12's definition they are **isomers**, not variants of one thing. The formula "unfairness" records the ingredients and loses the arrangement.
- The practical consequence: a remedy aimed at the comparison bond (standardise outcomes across people) does not act on the sequence bond (apply the same rule on Tuesday as on Monday), and the other way round. That is Fig 9 :57, *"a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either"*, stated at the level of bonds.
- **Falsifier, written before the run:** if a single scalar can represent both conditions with no loss of predictive information about legitimacy, per group and per occasion, the isomer claim fails and the two conditions are one dimension. The world literature's four-component decomposition (voice, neutrality, dignity, motive) would be evidence against the collapse, but it is not the test itself.

### Q2 - What bond makes legitimacy a process?

- Fig 12 :121: **"cycle. The output re-enters as input. The sign of the loop decides whether it closes, and a chain cannot close by construction."**
- A snapshot of legitimacy is a state. A persistence claim over time needs a cycle: judgment leads to compliance or cooperation, which becomes a further encounter, which updates judgment. A chain cannot close; only a loop can sustain a residual that persists and does not trend down.
- The sign is not determined by the bond. Fig 12 :121 says the sign decides whether it closes. In the world, Menzner and Schweizer 2026 find *"self-reinforcing dynamics"* and limited support for a buffering reservoir. That is the loop observed in survey time series, not a measurement of its sign for a particular institution.
- Fig 12 :119: **"catalyst. A part that survives its own use."** A procedurally fair encounter is a catalyst if its legitimacy effect regenerates across uses, and a spent part if the second identical encounter adds nothing. Nothing in the encounter's description records which. This is the figure-grounded form of "the same reform works once and then stops".
- **Falsifier:** a documented legitimacy trajectory where cooperation does not change the next encounter's reading, with the loop demonstrably open.

### Q3 - What is subtracted, and what is refused?

- Operators table :46: *"progress is a subtraction, which is why a vocabulary without it cannot say how far along it is"*; Fig 12 :82: **"A with B removed or absent"**.
- A legitimacy deficit is a subtraction: expected treatment minus received treatment, per slot. The question "how far along is the erosion?" is a subtraction, not a list of ingredients.
- Operators table :58: *"a refutation is a negation, which is why a vocabulary without it can only accumulate."* An institution's claim that it is legitimate because it says it is must be refused, not re-fitted. Formula step 2: REFUTED owes *"a refusal, not an explanation."* This is the structural warrant for Fig 10 :93, *"A ground with a reputation to protect is a ground someone benefits from, and its keeper is thereby disqualified."*
- Negation is the eighth operator, not a bond. It joins nothing (Fig 12 :134-136), so refusing the institution's self-report is not the same as subtracting it from a list. The record keeps the self-report and removes its standing to be composed over.

### Q4 - What order is the union, across scales?

- Fig 3 :20: **"The chain is ordered by dependency, not by importance."** The order is perception, vocabulary, objective, planning, learn and carry. For this question: can the subject perceive differential treatment, does the subject have voice/neutrality terms, can the subject say what fair treatment here would be, can the subject act on it, and does the institution carry the lesson?
- Fig 4 :20-22: the kernel is unchanged across sizes; *"Only methods cross up. Only head starts come down."* Going from one subject to a group *"throws away detail; that is what makes the result reapplicable"* (Fig 4 :27-28), so a group-level fairness score cannot be read back down to a subject.
- Fig 4 :30: **"So take the round trip and measure the gap. Without it you are navigating by dead reckoning: confident about a position nobody checked."** The round-trip gap R_T for this question is enumerated in `11_audit.md`.
- The ordered structure is therefore: encounter (`→` across occasions), comparison (`⋛` across subjects), subtraction of received from expected, judgment, cycle back into the next encounter, and a lossy cross-scale transform to the group and the institution. No step in this order may be replaced by an average across slots (Fig 1 :22).

### Q5 - A low-overlap analogue, with its caveat

- Brosnan and de Waal 2003 (Nature 425:297-299) report that brown capuchins *"refused to participate if they witnessed a conspecific obtain a more attractive reward for equal effort, an effect amplified if the partner received such a reward without any effort at all."* This is a comparison bond (`⋛`) operating without institutional vocabulary, in a different field from procedural justice research. It corroborates the shape "unequal treatment registers before it is named", not the institutional claim.
- Wynne 2004 (Nature 428:140) replies that this *"does not show that monkeys are averse to inequity, only that they reject a lesser reward when better rewards are available."* As the prompt's own figures require, a genuine conflict sends the derivation back to the census and never flips a verdict. Re-census: the capuchin case governs the comparison bond in a food-exchange habitat; INS-0027 governs legitimacy judgment in an institutional habitat. The analogue is corroboration of the bond's availability at a low stratum, not of its institutional effect. It carries no verdict weight either way.

## CONCLUSION

Unequal treatment and inconsistent enforcement are **isomers** under Fig 12: the same rule-and-case atoms joined by a comparison bond across subjects versus a sequence bond across occasions. That is why one lever cannot clear their intersection. Legitimacy over time requires a **cycle** whose sign decides whether it holds or collapses, a **subtraction** that measures the deficit, and a **negation** that refuses the institution's own account. The cross-scale transform is lossy, so a measured R_T is owed. The capuchin analogue supports the comparison bond at a low stratum and is explicitly caveated rather than stretched.
