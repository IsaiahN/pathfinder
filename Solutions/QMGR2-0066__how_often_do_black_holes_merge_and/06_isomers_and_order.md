## RESIDUAL

The rate half of the question and the source half are not the same object. Section 4 of `01` established that the rate is symmetric under exchange of events while the route is ordered, and that the route is recovered from lineage rather than from the artefact. Three supporting questions are owed:

1. Under what structure are two formation channels the same thing and under what structure are they different things?
2. What is the dependency order of the decomposition, and where does the chain actually break?
3. Does hierarchical remerger have a figure structure, and does it have ground contact?

## Q1: Are the channels isomers?

- Fig 12 :117 "isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."
- Fig 12 :124 "functional group. A substructure whose presence fixes the outcome. The group says what kind; the qualifier says which member."

Write the two stellar-origin channels over the shared atom set:

- Isolated: `(two massive stars) -> (common envelope) -> (two black holes in a bound orbit) -> (coalescence)`.
- Dynamical: `(two black holes captured in a dense cluster) -> (hardening by encounters) -> (coalescence)`.

Over the reduced atom set `{two black holes, coalescence}`, both have the same atoms and differ in the ordered bond before coalescence. The difference is invisible in the count of coalescences and decisive in spin orientation and eccentricity. Under Fig 12 :117 they are **isomers**: same atoms, different bond, different substance. This is a structural analogue, `specified`, not a claim about stellar dynamics derived from the figures.

Now the limit. A primordial origin does not contain the atom "two massive stars" or "common envelope". Its atom set differs. Under Fig 12 :117 it is **not** an isomer of the stellar channels; it is a different composition, and the relation between them is not "same atoms, different bond" but "different atoms, different molecule". An AGN-disc channel shares more atoms with dynamical capture (a dense environment, encounters) than with isolated evolution; it sits at the boundary and must be judged case by case.

Gas assisted capture in an AGN disc is a real third channel with a real published mechanism (Whitehead et al. 2024, MNRAS 531, 4656; arXiv:2309.11561, opened 2026-11-23): gas dissipation during close encounters "forming stably bound eccentric BBHs". It shares the "dense environment" and "encounter" atoms with the cluster channel, so it is closer to an isomer of dynamical capture than of isolated evolution. Its discriminator is eccentricity plus an environmental association, which is a new axis, not a new name (They're Scanning Books: "The real import is not a new term in the existing space. It is a new dimension of the space.").

- **Falsifier, written before the run:** if two candidate channels share all atoms and all bonds yet produce identical observable distributions on every instrument, they are not isomers in the figure's sense, because the "decisive in the behaviour" condition fails and the isomer claim is withdrawn. Conversely, if primordial origin is confirmed for an event, the isomer claim across all channels fails and is replaced by a split between shared-atom channels and a different composition. Recorded before any conclusion was drawn.
- **Status:** `specified`, single-source on the figure definition (Fig 12), with the physics-facing check still unmeasured. Triangulated weakly with `01` F4 at low overlap.

## Q2: What is the dependency order, and where does the chain break?

Fig 3 :20 "The chain is ordered by dependency, not by importance." The chain for this question:

1. **Perception:** an interferometer returns a strain transient. Instrument returns something.
2. **Vocabulary:** terms for mass, spin, eccentricity, redshift and rate exist. The search can reach a population description.
3. **Objective:** "how often" is a comparison against volume and time; "which channel" is a comparison among routes. Both are statable (the comparison bond ⋛ at Fig 12 :85-89; Operators table :51 "a goal is a comparison, which is why a vocabulary without it cannot state one").
4. **Planning:** choose discriminators. This is where the chain usually breaks. Fig 3 :25-27: the middle link, having terms for what a situation offers and being able to say what would count as progress, "has neither, so it attracts the least attention and the fewest instruments", and "A link with no instrument is not a link that works. It is a link nobody has looked at."
5. **Learn and carry:** name what worked, verify it, carry it to the next event.

The break is between objective and planning for the source half. We can say what would count as progress (a channel fraction with an error rate) but the instrument that pays for it is only partly built. The rate half does not break: detectors and population inference already pay for it.

Fig 3 :22 "A step whose input never arrives cannot be diagnosed, only its predecessor can." A channel attribution made before a settled event catalog and a settled rate is a reading of nothing. This is why the dependency order in `01` section 2.4 puts D1 before D2 before D3.

- **Falsifier:** exhibit a working channel discriminator that requires no prior event catalog or rate. Then step 3 does not depend on step 2 and the dependency chain is wrong.

## Q3: Is hierarchical remerger a cycle?

- Fig 12 :121 "cycle. The output re-enters as input. The sign of the loop decides whether it closes, and a chain cannot close by construction."
- A second-generation black hole is a prior merger's remnant re-entering as an input to a later coalescence. The structure is a cycle, not a chain. This is why a simple "progenitor star -> black hole -> merger" chain cannot represent it: the chain has no re-entry.
- Ground contact, partial. The LIGO newsroom on 2026-05-26 reports "evidence for the existence of second-generation black holes" in GWTC-5.0, and the 2025-10-28 release on GW241011 and GW241110 says the spin and mass asymmetries give "tantalizing evidence that these black holes were formed from previous black hole mergers". Both are "could be" and "tantalizing", not a route measurement. So the figure structure is firm and its instantiation is `specified`, unmeasured.
- The sign question is live. The cycle closes only if the remnant's spin is measurable and inconsistent with a first-generation natal spin. An alternative explanation by natal spin would break the closure without breaking the figure.

- **Falsifier:** a source-discriminating measurement showing the anomalous spins arise from natal spin rather than a previous coalescence. The cycle analogue survives; its instantiation is retracted.
- **Status:** `specified`, triangulated for the structure (Fig 12 :121 plus the LIGO releases), single-source for the ground reading (one collaboration, one detector network, one evidence pool).

## Q4: What order is the union of the two halves of the question?

- Fig 9 :54 "when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again." Fig 9 :57 "a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either".
- The frequency condition and the source condition overlap at the redshift trend: the rate's evolution with z is also a channel imprint, because it couples to star-formation history and delay-time distributions. So the union is genuine, with a non-empty intersection.
- Unbundling gives three searches: (a) rate per volume per time with its redshift trend, ground-owned; (b) channel fractions over the shared-atom set, requiring discriminators; (c) the intersection, which is the redshift-trend-as-imprint question. A single lever clears (a) and leaves (b) and (c) standing.
- Fig 9 :59: "the residue is finally describable." After unbundling, the residue is specifically "which observable separates which pair of channels at what misclassification rate", which is a far more checkable object than "what does the rate tell us about formation".

- **Falsifier:** show the redshift trend to be independent of channel, with no overlap. Then the union collapses to a partition and unbundling is unnecessary.

## Q5: Does the flat answer lose information?

Fig 12 :14-17 describes the flat formula: "the ingredients are recorded and the arrangement is not / nothing downstream can tell them apart, / and nothing signals that anything was lost". A one-line answer that reports a rate and names a channel without recording the bond between them is the flat case. It records `{rate, channel}` and not the typed relation. Nothing downstream can tell whether the channel was derived, imported, or guessed.

Fig 8 :51-52: "Convergent derivation and adopted import look identical in the contents." Only the record separates them. So the lineage is the term, not documentation of the term (Fig 13 :130 "That record is not documentation of the term. It is the term."). This folder's `10_source_ledger.csv` exists for that reason.

- **Falsifier:** exhibit a flat answer from which the bond type is recoverable without any record. Fig 12 says the bond is not recoverable from the operands, so such an answer would break the figure.

## CONCLUSION

Over the shared atom set, the isolated and dynamical channels are isomers: same atoms, different ordered bond, different and measurable behaviour. Primordial origin is not an isomer of them, because its atom set differs. Hierarchical remerger is a cycle, structurally firm and only partly grounded. The dependency order is detect, aggregate, infer, and it breaks at the discriminator step for the source half. The two halves of the question are a genuine union with a non-empty intersection at the redshift trend, so unbundling is required and a single lever cannot finish the job.

The strongest result of this supporting derivation is negative and cheap: a rate, however precise, cannot name a route. The positive result is a shape for what a route answer must contain, namely a typed bond, a lineage, and a discriminator that already returns something. No new primitive and no wall-moving import are needed for either result.
