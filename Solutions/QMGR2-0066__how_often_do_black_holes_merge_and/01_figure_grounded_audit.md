## 1. The residual stated in effect terms

The question arrives as one sentence with two verbs, "how often" and "what does that tell us". Treating them as one query is the first failure to avoid.

- **Effect 1, frequency.** Something is counted: coalescence events per comoving volume per unit time, and the trend of that count with redshift. The residual is the gap between the observed count and the count a population model predicts. This gap is indexed per redshift bin and per mass bin, not as one global number. The anchor is the strain data. It does not move when we change our formation story.
- **Effect 2, source.** Something is attributed: the observed distribution is read as evidence of a production route (isolated binary evolution, dynamical encounter in a dense cluster, gas assisted capture in an AGN disc, primordial origin, hierarchical remerger). The residual is the gap between the reading and the route. The crucial property is that several routes can produce the same reading. The rate, by itself, is a projection with lost information.

Fig 9 :55: "A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones." The overlap is real: the redshift trend of the rate is itself a discriminating observable, because it couples to star formation history and to delay-time distributions. So this is a union, not a partition, and it must be unbundled before search (Fig 9 :54). One lever, for example a more precise rate, clears the region where only effect 1 holds and leaves the intersection standing (Fig 9 :57).

What an answer would look like, written down and bound before the run, per the formula's Direction discipline: a rate with units and a credible interval, plus a map from a stated set of observables to a stated set of channels with a stated misclassification rate. Binding this makes it refusable. Neither half is asserted as settled by the figures.

## 2. Domain census, before choosing any figure line

### 2.1 R by arity, symmetry and scale

- **Arity:** two conditions. Frequency and source. Union (Fig 9 :54, :55), not one hard question (Fig 9 :57 "a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either").
- **Symmetry:** the merger rate is a symmetric aggregate. Exchanging the labels of any two events in the catalog changes no term in the count: Fig 12 :92 "Under a symmetric bond, k operands have k! spellings and one term, so a code that charges for the sequence overcharges by log₂(k!) while the bond term undercharges." The formation route is ordered. Progenitor, binary, coalescence, possible remerger is a sequence, not a set: Fig 12 :111 "A vocabulary without bonds can name a state and cannot name a route to one." This asymmetry is not a side comment. It is why the rate cannot contain the route.
- **Scale:** first order is a single detector event (one slot). Second order is the population distribution built from settled events. Third order is the channel, a lineage across cosmic history. Fig 12 :104 "A settled molecule becomes an atom for whatever composes over it." Each settled event becomes an atom for the population; the settled population becomes an atom for the channel inference.

### 2.2 One line per domain: actors, currency, anchor

| domain | actors | currency | anchor | nameable anchor? |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| D1, detector and event frame | LIGO, Virgo, KAGRA interferometers; individual coalescence slots s | strain, signal-to-noise ratio, probability of astrophysical origin; per-slot residual | the strain data stream and the event catalog | yes, the data |

No domain is admitted without a nameable anchor. D3 is admitted because its anchor is reachable, but the reach is conditional: without a discriminator the channel claim has no ground contact and is not yet a domain verdict.

### 2.3 Partition or union

Union. D1 and D2 are a dependency chain, effectively a partition boundary between per-event and population quantities. D2 and D3 overlap: rate observables are also channel observables. D3 and D4 overlap: an expert's verdict is only useful if it changes contact with the ground. Per Fig 9 :54, unbundle before searching. Per Fig 9 :60 "Use filters for the budget and witnesses for the verdict. Never let a filter hand you a verdict." A credible interval is a filter for effort; a discriminator is a witness.

### 2.4 Order by dependency (Fig 3)

1. Detect and settle individual events (D1). Perception and vocabulary.
2. Aggregate settled events into a population and a rate (D2). Objective: how many, with what trend.
3. Only then infer the source (D3). Plan: choose discriminators that separate channels.
4. Only then ask an outside reader (D4).

Fig 3 :20 "The chain is ordered by dependency, not by importance." Fig 3 :22 "A step whose input never arrives cannot be diagnosed, only its predecessor can." A formation claim made before a settled rate is a reading below the break, which is a reading of nothing (Fig 3 :27 "A link with no instrument is not a link that works. It is a link nobody has looked at.").

## 4. Findings, each with verbatim quote and refuter

### F1: the frequency half has an anchor; the source half does not get one for free

Fig 2 :29: "An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks." The detector strain is such an anchor for "how often": it does not change because a population model likes a different channel. Fig 2 :24-27 adds that the anchor "does not update on A or B" and "is not a third party to the disagreement".

For the source half, the anchor is not the rate. It is the population distribution read through an instrument that can separate channels. Without that instrument, the source claim is a frame ranging against a reading, not against the ground.

**Refuter:** show a case where the detector anchor moves when a formation hypothesis changes, or a case where two channels are separated by the rate value alone with no additional observable. Either would collapse the split.

### F2: the question is a union, so one lever leaves the intersection standing

Fig 9 :54: "when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again." Fig 9 :55: "A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones." Fig 9 :57: "a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either".

A more precise merger rate is the obvious big lever. It clears the region where only frequency is at issue. It leaves the source-condition intersection standing, because the same rate is compatible with more than one channel. This is the second-order value described at Fig 9 :59: the residue becomes describable, and that is the larger gain.

**Refuter:** demonstrate that frequency and source are independent conditions (a partition). Then a single rate lever would settle both and this finding fails.

### F3: the rate is symmetric, the route is ordered, and no amount of counting converts one into the other

Fig 12 :92 on the symmetric bond. Fig 12 :111: "A vocabulary without bonds can name a state and cannot name a route to one." The Operators table gives the ordered bonds `->` (sequence) and `=>` (production), and `+` (conjunction, symmetric). A merger count uses the symmetric side. A formation route uses the ordered side.

This is the structural heart of the answer. The rate is a name for a state of the population. The route is a path to that state. A state does not determine its path (Fig 13 :129, below).

**Refuter:** exhibit a typed bond under which a population count carries its production route as part of the term. If such a bond is admitted by the figures, counting would determine formation and this finding fails.

### F4: the route is recovered from the lineage, not from the artefact

Fig 13 :126: "Nothing about a composed term tells you, from looking at it, which route produced it or whether that route was available." Fig 13 :129: "It is recoverable from the lineage." The merger catalog is the artefact. The redshift trend, the spin alignment, the eccentricity, the mass-gap occupancy are parts of the lineage that survived into the artefact. They are what make the route checkable. This is triangulated with F3 at low overlap: one figure speaks about bond symmetry, the other about provenance of a composed term.

World corroboration, secondary: the GWTC-3 catalog release states (LIGO Caltech news, 2026-05-26) that "Certain characteristics of these mergers - in particular the spin of the black holes - indicate the objects involved could be 'second-generation' black holes". The route is read from spin, not from the count.

**Refuter:** show that the route can be read off the rate value with no lineage information, or show that the stated lineage observables are unrelated to production route.

### F5: average rate hides the per-event signal

Fig 1 :19: "The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated." Fig 1 :22: "Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average." The highest-mass or most rapidly spinning events are not noise around a rate; they are the slots with live formation residual. Collapsing them into a single rate per Gpc^-3 yr^-1 throws away exactly the signal that discriminates channels.

World corroboration: the LVK GWTC-3 population paper reports localized over- and underdensities in the mass distribution and peaks at chirp masses of about 8.3 and 27.9 solar masses. The structure is in the slots, not in the mean.

**Refuter:** show a formation discriminator that is a function of the mean rate only and is invariant under redistribution of events among slots. Under Fig 1 :22 such a discriminator cannot exist, so a demonstration would break the figure.

### F6: a discriminator must already be returning something

Fig 6 :32: "An instrument is not built from a description. It is improved from a worse instrument already returning something." Fig 6 :34: "Where nothing is, there is no edge to extend from, and the search for a proto-instrument is a search for one that is already there."

The source question is not answered by wishing for a perfect channel meter. It is answered by sharpening observables that already register: spin orientation (already measured), orbital eccentricity (already bounded), mass-gap occupancy (already counted), redshift evolution (already fitted). World corroboration, Zevin et al. 2021 (ApJL): "Orbital eccentricity is one of the most robust discriminators for distinguishing between dynamical and isolated formation scenarios of binary black hole mergers using gravitational-wave observatories such as LIGO and Virgo." That instrument already returns a value.

**Refuter:** identify a proposed discriminator that has never registered anything at any resolution and show the framework licensing it as an instrument rather than an import. That would break the Fig 6 rule.

### F7: same atoms, different bond, different substance

Fig 12 :117: "isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour." Two black holes in a coalescence are the same atom set under the isolated and dynamical channels; what differs is the production bond. The difference is invisible in the rate and decisive in spin and eccentricity. This is an analogue, marked `specified`, not an identity claim about stellar dynamics. It is single-source on the figure side, triangulated weakly with F4.

The limit matters. A primordial origin does not share the same atom set, because it needs no stellar progenitor. Under Fig 12 :117 that is not an isomer of the stellar channel; it is a different composition. The honest census keeps primordial and hierarchical channels in separate sub-verdicts rather than forcing all four into one isomer family.

**Refuter:** show that two candidate channels share all atoms and all bonds yet produce identical observable distributions. Then the isomer analogue has no decisive behaviour and is withdrawn.

### F8: hierarchical merger is a cycle, and the cycle is now partly settled at the ground

Fig 12 :121: "cycle. The output re-enters as input. The sign of the loop decides whether it closes, and a chain cannot close by construction." A second-generation black hole is exactly an output re-entering as input. The 2026-05-26 LIGO news release says the catalog includes "evidence for the existence of second-generation black holes", and the 2025-10-28 release on GW241011 and GW241110 says the spin and mass asymmetries provide "tantalizing evidence that these black holes were formed from previous black hole mergers". Status: `specified`, ground-touching but not settled, because "could be" and "tantalizing evidence" are not a measurement of the route.

**Refuter:** a source-discriminating measurement showing the spin signature arises from natal spin rather than a previous coalescence would remove the cycle's ground contact. The figure analogue survives; its instantiation does not.

### F9: the number itself is ground-owned, not figure-derived

No figure line governs a rate of 17.9 to 44 Gpc^-3 yr^-1. That number comes from the LVK GWTC-3 population paper (Physical Review X 13, 011048, 2023), which inferred "the binary black hole merger rate, allowing for evolution with redshift, to be between 17.9 and 44" at a fiducial redshift z = 0.2, in units of Gpc^-3 yr^-1. The LIGO news release of 2026-05-26 reports 390 confirmed events in GWTC-5.0. These are imported ground readings, with provenance, not results of this derivation. Declaring a figure line for the number would be a strain, so the verdict for the numeric half is `gap (ground-owned)`, which is a verdict and not a silence (Fig 13 :134 "Unreachable is a verdict here, not a silence").

**Refuter:** produce a derivation of the numerical rate from the six-term set with a stated code and a paid bargain. Until then the gap stands.

### F10: independence decides whether two channels are two witnesses or one pool

Fig 13 :38: "independence is how little two frames draw on the same evidence, and it is what makes their agreement mean anything." Two population studies that use the same GWTC catalog and the same waveform approximant are one witness wearing two names, even if one is labelled "dynamical" and the other "field". Fig 2 :40: "agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool." In this simulated run the primary seat and the second seat in `11_audit.md` share the whole evidence pool, so their agreement is internal consistency and carries no corroborating weight.

**Refuter:** show two channel analyses drawing on disjoint instruments or disjoint data, and their agreement becomes a real second witness. This finding is about weight, not about the channels being true or false.

### F11: the shape, not a name, limit applies to the channel claim

Fig 9 :32: "you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself." The canonical figures README says the framework gives "what a missing thing would have to look like (a shape, not a name)". For this question the shape is: a discriminator that changes value across the candidate routes, is already registering at some resolution, and ranges against a non-updating anchor. The framework does not select the correct channel for a given event. That is an instrument's job. Naming a channel from a rate reading is the metamerism error described in the Hard Problem is About Source essay: an instrument reading green "cannot comment on the source".

Correction kept visible: the "shape, not a name" rule governs a primitive not held in the library. Channel names such as "dynamical" are bindings from the astrophysics frame, not invented primitives. What is forbidden is presenting a binding as a figure-derived derivation. This correction mirrors the one recorded in the exemplar's `05` section 5b and is preserved here rather than silently adopted.

**Refuter:** exhibit a figure line that selects one formation channel over another for a given event. None was found after re-census.

### F12: outreach must not spend the reader's independence

Fig 10 :52: "Bring a characterised residual, never the source." Fig 10 :42: "A verifier that can reconstruct the claim cannot verify it." For this question the residual is short: the rate is symmetric and settled; the route is ordered and not settled by the rate; a discriminator is owed. That is what goes in the first contact. The framework interior and the figure links come only after a verdict is recorded. Fig 4 :22: "Only methods cross up. Only head starts come down." Sending this whole folder first sends a recording, not a generator.

**Refuter:** show a case where giving the full framework before the verdict increases the independence of the reader. By Fig 10 :42 it can only decrease it.

## 5. Pre-registered refuter

Written before the conclusion, per the seat rule that a falsifier written before the run is a design instrument.

The shape claim is refuted if any of the following holds:

1. A figure line is exhibited that directly yields a numerical merger rate with physical units, making F9's ground-owned gap false.
2. A source-discriminating instrument is shown to give identical values for all candidate channels over every accessible observable, so the route is genuinely indiscernible and F4's lineage requirement is vacuous.
3. Frequency and source are shown to be a partition, with no overlap, so F2's union verdict fails.
4. A typed bond is admitted under which a symmetric population count carries its ordered production route, so F3 fails.
5. The detector anchor is shown to move when a formation hypothesis changes, so F1 fails.

Until one of these is run, no part of this folder may be labelled `grounded` as a derivation. The cited collaborations' rate measurements are ground readings in their own right and are reported as such.

## 6. Recommendation

1. Keep the two effects separate. Report the rate as a ground-owned measurement and the channel as an inference with a stated error rate. Do not average them into one sentence (F1, F2).
2. Keep residuals per event and per bin. The outlier slots carry the formation signal (F5).
3. Ask for a discriminator, not for a better rate. The discriminator must already be returning something: spin, eccentricity, mass-gap occupancy, redshift trend (F6).
4. Keep primordial and hierarchical channels in separate sub-verdicts. Only shared-atom channels are isomers (F7, F8).
5. State the shape the answer must have and stop there. The framework does not name the channel for a given event (F11).
6. Send the residual and the refuter first, through public institutional venues, one to one (F12).
