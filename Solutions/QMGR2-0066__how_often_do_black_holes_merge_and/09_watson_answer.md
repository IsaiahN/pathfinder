## DOMAIN CENSUS

R by arity, symmetry and scale, then one line per domain with actors, currency and anchor, partition or union, and order by dependency.

- **R (arity / symmetry / scale).** Arity: two conditions, frequency and source. Symmetry: the rate is a symmetric aggregate (exchange two events, the count is unchanged); the route is ordered (progenitor, binary, coalescence, possible remerger). Scale: first order is a detector event, second order is the population distribution, third order is the channel lineage. Fig 9 :54-57 names a union with overlap, not a partition. Fig 12 :92 and :111 govern the symmetry-order split.
- **Domain 1, detector and event frame.** Actors: LIGO, Virgo and KAGRA interferometers; individual coalescence slots. Currency: strain, signal-to-noise ratio, probability of astrophysical origin, per-slot residual. Anchor: the strain data stream, which does not move when a formation story changes. Domain type: perception and vocabulary, the top of the dependency chain.
- **Domain 2, population and rate frame.** Actors: settled events as atoms, mass and redshift bins. Currency: posterior densities, credible intervals, rate per volume per time. Anchor: the catalog and its public data products. Domain type: objective, a comparison against volume and time. The rate is settled here by the detectors, not derived here.
- **Domain 3, formation and source frame.** Actors: progenitor stars, dense clusters, AGN discs, primordial fluctuations, candidate channels. Currency: discriminating observables, spin alignment, eccentricity, mass-gap occupancy, redshift trend. Anchor: the observed population read through simulations, reachable only through a discriminator. Domain type: planning, the link most likely to break.
- **Domain 4, verification and outreach frame.** Actors: expert readers. Currency: residual and agreement. Anchor: the ground, never agreement. In this simulated run all seats draw on one evidence pool, so agreement is internal consistency, not corroboration.
- **Order by dependency.** Detect and settle events, then aggregate into a rate, then infer the source, then ask an outside reader. Fig 3 :20 "The chain is ordered by dependency, not by importance." A channel claim made before a settled rate is a reading below the break.

## VERDICT (from the figures)

The question is a union of a ground-owned frequency condition and an inferred source condition. The rate is measured and symmetric; the route is ordered and is not carried by the count. The route is recoverable from the lineage that survives into the data, and the relevant observables already return values. Over a shared atom set the stellar-origin channels are isomers; primordial origin is a different composition; hierarchical remerger is a cycle with partial ground contact. The framework returns the shape a channel claim must satisfy and refuses to name a channel for an event. The numeric rate is a `gap` for this derivation and is reported only as an imported measurement with provenance. Mode `specified`, ungrounded, candidate.

- **cost if wrong:** bounded and locatable if the packet is sent as written. It carries mode, three checkable sub-claims, verbatim lines and five refuters, so an error is findable rather than invisible. The real avoidable cost is spending an outside reader's independence by sending the framework interior before their verdict (Fig 10 :42). Fig 2 :42 says "Buy independence in proportion to the cost of being wrong." That cost here is low, because the claim can be checked against public data in twenty minutes.
- **undoable:** yes. The binding of channel names to figure atoms is removable without touching the derivation. A refuted claim can be superseded and the refutation copied into `11_audit.md` with provenance. No primitive has to be withdrawn, and the measured rate is unaffected by the fate of the shape claim.
- **reversed by:** a ground reading, not agreement. A discriminator giving identical values for all channels on all accessible observables would make the route indiscernible and collapse the lineage verdict; a typed bond admitting a symmetric count that carries an ordered route would collapse the symmetry verdict; a demonstration that frequency and source are a partition would collapse the union verdict; a figure line yielding a numerical rate would close the declared gap.
