# Outreach draft: check the residual before you decide about the framework

**Use:** one to one, or as a cover note attached to a submission through a public venue in `03_public_contact_points.csv`. **Not** for bulk send.

**Hard precondition:** do not send until the shape claim in `08_expert_verification_packet.md` survives a first read by someone who knows merger-rate inference. If the stage-1 claim does not survive, there is nothing else to look at.

---

**Subject:** One checkable claim about merger rates and formation channels

> Hi [name],
>
> I am working on a figure-grounded derivation and have one specific output I would like you to check against what you already know. I am not asking you to read the framework first, and I am not asking you to agree with it. If the output does not survive, there is nothing else to look at.
>
> **The output (`specified`, not `grounded`):** the question "how often do black holes merge and what does that tell us about how they formed" is two conditions in one sentence, not one question. Fig 9 :55 says a bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones. The frequency half is a symmetric count over events, settled by the detectors. The source half is an ordered production route, and it is not carried by the count. Fig 12 :92 says under a symmetric bond k operands have k! spellings and one term. Fig 12 :111 says a vocabulary without bonds can name a state and cannot name a route to one. Fig 13 :126 says nothing about a composed term tells you, from looking at it, which route produced it; Fig 13 :129 says it is recoverable from the lineage.
>
> So the shape claim is: a merger rate alone cannot identify a formation channel. What is owed is a discriminator that changes value across candidate routes, already returns something at some resolution, and ranges against a detector anchor that does not move when our story changes. Spin orientation, eccentricity, mass-gap occupancy and redshift evolution are the partial discriminators already in hand.
>
> **What I am not claiming:** that the figures produce the number 17.9 to 44 per cubic gigaparsec per year. That number is a measurement by the LIGO, Virgo and KAGRA collaborations, imported with provenance. I am not claiming that the framework names a channel for a given event, or that it settles whether GW241011 and GW241110 were second-generation black holes. Those are instrument questions. I am not claiming the channels are all isomers: a primordial origin does not share the stellar progenitor atom set, so it is a different composition, not the same atoms under a different bond.
>
> **What would refute the shape claim (written before this message):**
> 1. a figure line that yields a numerical rate with physical units;
> 2. a discriminator that gives identical values for every candidate channel on every accessible observable, making the route genuinely indiscernible;
> 3. a demonstration that frequency and source are a partition, with no overlap;
> 4. a typed bond under which a symmetric population count carries its ordered production route;
> 5. a case where the detector anchor moves when a formation hypothesis changes.
>
> If any one holds, I withdraw the claim. A one-line "this fails because..." is more useful to me than agreement. If you would rather not engage, no reply is needed.
>
> **If it survives, and only then:** the sources are below. The figures are the authority, not my summary of them.
> - Figures and formula, branch `general-agent`: https://github.com/IsaiahN/tether/tree/general-agent/figures
> - Canonical figures README: https://github.com/IsaiahN/tether/blob/general-agent/figures/README.md
> - Gravitational Wave Open Science Center, public data and catalogs: https://gwosc.org/
>
> **If you want to use the machinery:** the formula is in the figures link. Run it on a problem you know well and can check against a ground. Write down what would refute your result before you run it, and label the mode of what you get. The framework's own rule is that a loop cannot check the direction of its own question, so an output can be contrived to fit. A reported failure is more useful to me than a reported success.
