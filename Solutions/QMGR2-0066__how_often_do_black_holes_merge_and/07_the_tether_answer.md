# The Tether answer: how often black holes merge, and what that tells us about formation

**Mode: `specified` on the structural shape and the per-domain verdicts, `general` on the union split, not `grounded`.** Simulated derivation checked for internal consistency against the figures, not a ground reading. The numerical rate is a ground reading made by the cited collaborations and imported with provenance; this folder derives no number.

## 1. The residual it answers

The question bundles two effects. "How often" is a frequency effect, settled by detectors. "What does that tell us about how they formed" is a source effect, not settled by a frequency reading. Fig 9 :55 "A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones." The overlap is the redshift trend of the rate, which is both a count and a channel imprint. The two must be unbundled before search (Fig 9 :54), and verdicts must be kept per domain, never averaged.

## 2. The shape

1. **The rate is ground-owned and symmetric.** Events are exchangeable in the count: Fig 12 :92 "Under a symmetric bond, k operands have k! spellings and one term, so a code that charges for the sequence overcharges by log₂(k!) while the bond term undercharges." The LVK GWTC-3 population paper inferred a binary black hole merger rate between 17.9 and 44 Gpc^-3 yr^-1 at z = 0.2, with a redshift trend proportional to (1+z)^kappa, kappa = 2.9 (+1.7, -1.8). The GWTC-5.0 release of 2026-05-26 reports 390 confirmed events. These are imports, not figure outputs. Fig 2 :29 "An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks." The strain data are that anchor.

2. **The route is ordered and is not carried by the count.** Fig 12 :111 "A vocabulary without bonds can name a state and cannot name a route to one." The rate names a state of the population. A formation channel is a path to that state. Under the ordered bonds `->` (sequence) and `=>` (production), the progenitor, the binary, the coalescence and any remerger form a route. A symmetric count has no bond that carries that order.

3. **The route is recovered from lineage, not from the artefact.** Fig 13 :126 "Nothing about a composed term tells you, from looking at it, which route produced it or whether that route was available." Fig 13 :129 "It is recoverable from the lineage." The catalog is the artefact. The spin orientations, eccentricities, mass-gap occupancy and redshift trend are the surviving parts of the lineage. The framework's own Hard Problem is About Source essay states the same limit in instrument terms: an instrument reading green "cannot comment on the source".

4. **Do not average away the live slots.** Fig 1 :19 "The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated." Fig 1 :22 "Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average." The unusual events are not scatter around a rate; they are the slots where the formation residual is live.

5. **Discriminators must already register something.** Fig 6 :32 "An instrument is not built from a description. It is improved from a worse instrument already returning something." Spin is measured, eccentricity is bounded, the redshift trend is fitted, mass-gap occupancy is counted. Zevin et al. 2021 call orbital eccentricity "one of the most robust discriminators for distinguishing between dynamical and isolated formation scenarios". The instrument exists; sharpen it.

6. **Channels are isomers only over a shared atom set.** Fig 12 :117 "isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour." Isolated and dynamical stellar-origin channels share the two-black-hole atoms and differ in the ordered bond, so they are isomers in this structural sense. A primordial origin needs no stellar progenitor and is a different composition, not an isomer. Hierarchical remerger is a cycle (Fig 12 :121 "The output re-enters as input"), currently with partial ground contact in the GWTC-5.0 second-generation candidate events.

7. **The shape stops before the name.** Fig 9 :32 "you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself." The framework returns the constraints a channel claim must satisfy, not the channel for a given event. Naming a channel from a rate reading is the metamerism error.

## 3. What it explicitly does not say

- Not a numerical derivation. No figure line yields Gpc^-3 yr^-1 (Fig 13 :134 "Unreachable is a verdict here, not a silence").
- Not a channel verdict for any named event. "Could be second-generation" is the collaborations' wording, not a settled route.
- Not that all channels are isomers. The primordial case fails the shared-atom condition.
- Not that the rate is useless. Its redshift trend is one of the discriminators; it is simply not sufficient on its own.
- Not `grounded` as a derivation. The whole folder is a simulation checked for internal consistency.

## 4. What would settle it, the ground

A source-discriminating instrument, not a further argument. The questions to put to the ground:

- Does the spin-orbit alignment distribution require a dynamical production bond, or is natal spin sufficient? The LVK GWTC-2 population paper's conditional inference ("Under the assumption that such systems can only be formed by dynamical interactions, we infer that between 25% and 93% of BBH" with non-vanishing effective inspiral spin) shows what happens when the assumption does the work: the number inherits the assumption, not the ground.
- Is measurable eccentricity present in enough events to bound the cluster fraction? Zevin et al. give concrete counts: after roughly 150 observations, seeing none would exclude clusters as the majority source at 95% credibility.
- Does the redshift trend follow star formation or show a delayed component? That separates prompt from long-delay channels.
- Are the mass-gap features stable as the catalog grows? The GWTC-3 population paper reports peaks at chirp masses of about 8.3 and 27.9 solar masses and localized over- and underdensities; their interpretation is still open.

A channel claim is settled only when a discriminator pays for it (THE_FORMULA.md step 5: "the ground settles it. Verified only by what pays. A gate is not the ground.").

## 5. Standing falsifiers

Written before the conclusion, repeated from `01` section 5:

1. a figure line that yields a numerical rate with physical units;
2. a discriminator giving identical values for every channel on every accessible observable;
3. frequency and source shown to be a partition;
4. a typed bond under which a symmetric count carries its ordered route;
5. the detector anchor shown to move when a formation hypothesis changes.

## 6. The honest stop

**Unreached, not unreachable.** The figure set returns a shape and a refusal, not a channel name. It stops at the limit of the current discriminators and records the lineage so the stop is checkable. "Indiscernibility is not identity." The catalog is growing: GWTC-5.0 added 161 events and the fourth observing run accounts for roughly 75% of all detections since 2015. That growth changes the instrument, not the framework. What the figures say now will still be sayable after the next release; which channel produced a given event will remain an instrument question.

## 7. One-paragraph version for outreach

> The question is really two conditions in one sentence. The first, how often black holes merge, is a count that detectors settle and that is symmetric under exchange of events: the current LVK inference gives roughly 18 to 44 mergers per cubic gigaparsec per year at redshift 0.2, with 390 confirmed events in the GWTC-5.0 catalog. The second, how they formed, is an ordered production route that the count cannot carry, because a symmetric aggregate names a state and not a path to it. The route is recovered from the lineage that survived into the data: spin alignment, eccentricity, mass-gap occupancy and the redshift trend. Over a shared atom set the isolated and dynamical channels behave like isomers, same atoms and different bond, with different observable behaviour; a primordial origin is a different composition, and hierarchical remerger is a cycle. The framework refuses to name a channel for a given event. It returns the shape a channel answer must have, five written refuters, and an honest stop at the limit of what the current instruments can separate.
