## What in the wider literature bears on this question

These are secondary corroboration only. None overrules a figure line. Their fields are deliberately different from one another so that agreement is not one evidence pool wearing several names (Fig 2 :31-32).

1. **Economics, information asymmetry.** The Wikipedia synthesis of Akerlof, Spence, Stiglitz and later work states that asymmetric information *"creates an imbalance of power in transactions"* and lists adverse selection, moral hazard, and monopolies of knowledge. This corroborates F3's power slot from a model class built on contracts, not on the Tether. It does not supply the figure line; it predicts that power tracks the information differential, which is what F3 says in effect terms.
2. **Communication and sociology, digital divide.** The same synthesis reports that the divide *"worsens inequality in access to information and resources"* and that focus shifted from binary access to differentiated use, with income and education the strongest explanatory variables. This corroborates F4's claim that the effect is a differential in capability and contact, not in mere device possession.
3. **Information science, the field itself.** Yu (2011) defines information inequality as *"multifaceted disparity between individuals, communities or nations in mobilizing society's information resources"* and calls for integrative theorizing. This corroborates F5's unbundling: the field's own definition is multifaceted, so a single averaged score is the wrong object.
4. **Network science and social epistemology, echo chambers and cascades.** The echo-chamber article distinguishes an epistemic bubble (sources excluded by omission) from an echo chamber (sources actively discredited), and the cascade article reports that outside information overrides private signals. These corroborate F3's mirror-chain failure from network interaction data, a different evidence pool from the contract models in item 1.
5. **Cognitive and organizational science, transactive memory.** Wegner's hypothesis says a group's shared, specialized knowing *"can provide the group members with more and better knowledge than any individual could access on their own"* and that strong systems reduce the need for explicit coordination. This corroborates F1 and F2 from laboratory and organizational data: differential access is a resource when frames are complementary and know who knows what, which is the figure's independence condition.
6. **No world source conflicts with a figure verdict.** The conflict rule was not triggered. Where a source seemed to strain (for example, echo chambers described as widespread versus weak), the disagreement is internal to that field's measurements, not a conflict with the figures, so it is recorded as an open empirical question rather than used to flip a verdict.

## The two-stage offer

- **Stage 1 before stage 2** is Fig 10 :52 — *"Bring a characterised residual, never the source"* — and Fig 2 :45 — *"Frames range against the ground. They do not negotiate with it."* The recipient checks the shape without holding the framework interior.
- **Stage 2 is opt-in by construction.** Once a recipient has absorbed the theory, their agreement stops being evidence (Fig 2 :40). Their stage-1 verdict must be recorded before any theory is shown, and later agreement is never pooled as corroboration.
- **Stage 3 sends a method**, which is permitted: Fig 4 :22 says only methods cross up. The formula is a generator, so giving it away does not give away an answer.
- **Stage 3 still needs a ground per problem.** `THE_FORMULA.md` "Direction": a frame *"cannot check the direction of its own question, because the shape a frame is searching for is not a term it holds."* Each user must write down what would refute their result before running it and state the mode of what they get.

**Hard precondition:** stages 1 to 3 only work if the stage-1 output survives. If a recipient fires one of the six refuters in `01` §3, stop and withdraw that slot before offering any theory.
