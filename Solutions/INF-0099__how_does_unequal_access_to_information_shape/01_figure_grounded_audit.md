## 0b. The residual in effect terms

Differential access is a comparison between at least two frames: what frame A can represent and reach versus what frame B can. Arity: at least two frames, and three effect slots (power, coordination, inequality). Symmetry: the comparison is ordered (who has more reach), so the natural operator is the comparison bond `⋛` (*"a threshold relation between two quantities"*, Operators table :48-49), not a symmetric conjunction. Scale: one frame, a population of frames, and a cross-scale cascade (Fig 11 :52-53). The residual is *not* "how much information exists"; it is the gap between differential reach and the three effects.

Binding of the sought-for shape (written before the run): *unequal access is differential reach; its effects run through independence, the anchor, and the habitat, not through the volume of information held.* This is an ordinary term now, so it can accrue a residual and be refused.

**Partition or union.** The three named effects are three slots of one residual (Fig 1 :19), so they are kept separate and never averaged (Fig 1 :22). So each slot gets its own loop, and the compound gets its own loop too.

## 2. Findings

### F1 — "Access" is a change in a frame's reach, not an addition to the world (`general`, figure-derived)

Fig 6 :25-26:

> **"An import adds no atom to the world."**
> **"The world does not gain primitives; a frame gains access."**

Fig 6 :23:

> **"No frame escapes its own closure. The bound moves only on contact with a frame whose closure differs."**

Fig 6 :21:

> **"MINT composes inside the closure and can never add an atom."**

So unequal access to information is, in effect terms, unequal reach: some frames have had contact with a differing closure and others have not. It is not that the world holds more information for one group. It is that one frame's `closure(Γ)` is larger. This is the single most direct figure statement of the question's subject. **Mode `general`** (the metalaw is domain-agnostic); **status triangulated** across Fig 6 (import mechanics) and Fig 8 :45 *"Two frames together reach what neither reaches alone"* (union mechanics), two figures with low overlap (different diagrams, different claims).

**Refuter:** show a frame adding a term to its reach by pure composition with no import from a closure that differs. That breaks Fig 6 :21 and :23. Or show the world gaining a primitive by a frame's import. That breaks Fig 6 :25.

### F2 — Whether differential access helps or harms depends on independence, not on volume (`general`)

Fig 8 :48-49:

> **"How much two frames draw on the same evidence decides both whether their agreement means anything and whether their union has any surplus."**

Fig 8 :13:

> **"their union adds nothing"**

Fig 8 :50:

> **"But shared atoms are not the signature."**

Fig 8 :51-52:

> **"Convergent derivation and adopted import look identical in the contents. Only the record of where each came from separates them"**

**Strain test and re-census.** F1's *"Two frames together reach what neither reaches alone"* (Fig 8 :45) appears to say differential access is always good, because two different closures create a surplus. That reading strains whenever the two frames actually draw on the same pool. The re-census is: Fig 8 :45 governs **independence (overlap)**, not the raw access differential. With low overlap the union has surplus; with high overlap *"their union adds nothing"* (Fig 8 :13). So the figure line governing this finding is :48-49, and F1 is not stretched to cover it. This is the prompt's rule applied: a line that only fits with a strain governs another quantity, so re-census. **After re-census: no hidden strain.** Status triangulated for the independence quantity (Fig 8 plus Fig 2 :31, see F3).

**Refuter:** two frames that overlap almost completely still produce a union surplus that survives an independent ground reading. That breaks Fig 8 :13 and :48-49.

### F3 — Power is influence over the pool, and coordination is triangulation (`general` on shape, `specified` on the social binding)

Fig 2 :14-15 (collapse 1, mutual update):

> **"they converge, and what they converge on is whoever had the most influence"**

Fig 2 :19-21 (collapse 2, common cause):

> **"they never talk, and still agree"**
> **"so the agreement is the pool talking, not two frames confirming each other"**

Fig 2 :35:

> **"Alignment is triangulation, not negotiation. A mirror cannot correct you."**

Fig 2 :40-41:

> **"disqualifying for a verdict: agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool"**
> **"efficient for a convention: agreement about a shared meaning is the product, not a corruption of it"**

Fig 7 :12-13:

> **"same closure, handed along; agreement compounds, evidence does not"**

Reading: when frames update on one another instead of ranging against an unmoved anchor, convergence follows influence. That is the figure-grounded shape of power: it is not possession of information, it is position in the update graph and control of what enters the shared pool. Coordination that works is triangulation against the ground by frames that do not update on each other (Fig 2 :45 *"Frames range against the ground. They do not negotiate with it."*). Coordination that fails is a mirror chain (Fig 7), which feels like agreement and adds no evidence. The valence split matters: high correlation is bad for a verdict and good for a convention (Fig 2 :39-41). A group that shares a meaning is producing a convention, not corroborating a fact. **Status triangulated** across Fig 2 (anchor/alignment) and Fig 7 (room chain), two figures with low overlap.

**Refuter:** a measurement in which two frames with a shared evidence pool produce agreement that survives an independent ground reading on the same axis. Or a demonstration that convergence follows something other than influence when no anchor is pinned. Either breaks Fig 2 :14-15 and :40.

### F4 — Social inequality is a differential in contact and habitat, not in agents (`general` on shape, `specified` on the social binding)

Fig 11 :39-40:

> **"capability is a property of agent-and-habitat, never of the agent alone"**
> **"An improvement that does not change contact changes nothing, however much it improves."**

Fig 11 :61-62:

> **"The seat's power is asymmetric. Introduction is available; exclusion is not."**
> **"You can add an actor and you cannot subtract the habitat, because contact is a fact about the world and does not consult you."**

Fig 4 :22:

> **"Only methods cross up. Only head starts come down."**

Reading: an "information inequality" that is fixed by improving the isolated agent (more devices, more lessons) is the named error. Capability lives in the agent-and-habitat pair. The seat can introduce an actor or a method (a head start sent down) but cannot subtract the habitat, so inequality management is permanent work, not a solved state. Contact cascades (Fig 11 :52-53: *"And contact cascades. Scoping the habitat to direct contact is how DDT reached the eagles: first-order enumeration, second-order effect, and the residual that mattered was three steps out."*), so the effect three steps out is the one that matters and first-order enumeration misses it. **Status triangulated** across Fig 11 (habitat/capability) and Fig 4 (membrane/head starts), two figures with low overlap.

**Refuter:** a controlled intervention that raises one group's access without changing its habitat or contact and that nevertheless moves the ground-settled inequality outcome. That breaks Fig 11 :40. Or a demonstration that a seat can remove an actor from a habitat. That breaks Fig 11 :61-62.

## 3. Pre-registered refuter

Required before outreach, Seat Map :287: *"a falsifier written before the run is a design instrument, not only an honesty device."* Written before the findings were closed.

The shape claim is refused if any of the following holds:

1. A frame adds a term to its reach by pure composition with no import from a differing closure, or the world gains a primitive by a frame's import (breaks Fig 6 :21, :23, :25).
2. Two frames whose evidence pools overlap almost completely still produce a union surplus that survives an independent ground reading on the same axis (breaks Fig 8 :13, :48-49).
3. Two frames sharing an evidence pool produce agreement that survives an independent ground reading (breaks Fig 2 :40), or convergence follows something other than influence with no anchor pinned (breaks Fig 2 :14-15).
4. An intervention that raises one group's access without changing its habitat or contact moves the ground-settled inequality outcome (breaks Fig 11 :40), or a seat removes an actor from a habitat (breaks Fig 11 :61-62).
5. A single scalar predicts all three effects better than separate slot readings on a held-out ground reading (breaks Fig 1 :19-22).
6. A derivation from the six terms and seven bonds names a unique institution for a given residual without an import (breaks Fig 9 :32 and the canonical limit).

If any fires, the corresponding slot is withdrawn. Until one is run, no part of this may be labelled `grounded`. **Reached, not unreachable** for the structural claims; **gap** for the institutional name.
