## 1. The claim, in one paragraph

Unequal access to information is differential reach, not a stock of atoms: a frame gains reach only through contact with a frame whose closure differs, because composition inside a closure never adds an atom (Fig 6 :21, :23, :26). From that single shape three separate effects follow, and they are never averaged. Power is influence over the shared evidence pool when the anchor is not pinned (Fig 2 :14-15, :40). Coordination is triangulation against an unmoved ground, and a chain of agreeing frames compounds agreement without adding evidence (Fig 2 :35, Fig 7 :13). Social inequality is differential capability in an agent-and-habitat pair, with asymmetric introduction power and contact cascading several steps out (Fig 11 :39-40, :61-62). The three effects are a functional group over different atom sets, not one scalar, because description bits and settling actions do not add (Fig 12 :125-126). The framework returns a kind of thing, not the institution that causes it (Fig 9 :32), so that name is a stated gap.

## 2. Three checkable sub-claims

### C1 — framework-internal; checkable by reading Figures 6 and 8

- **Statement:** access is reach won by contact with a differing closure; its value depends on overlap, not volume. High overlap yields no union surplus.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 6 :26 *"The world does not gain primitives; a frame gains access."* Fig 6 :21 *"MINT composes inside the closure and can never add an atom."* Fig 6 :23 *"No frame escapes its own closure. The bound moves only on contact with a frame whose closure differs."* Fig 8 :48-49 *"How much two frames draw on the same evidence decides both whether their agreement means anything and whether their union has any surplus."* Fig 8 :13 *"their union adds nothing"*.
- **What to check:** does the differential behave as a reach (a change in what a frame can represent) rather than as a quantity of atoms? Does overlap, not volume, predict whether two frames together reach more?
- **Refuter:** a frame gains a term by pure composition with no import from a differing closure; or the world gains a primitive by import; or two frames with almost complete overlap produce a union surplus that survives an independent ground reading.

### C2 — social/epistemic; checkable by a researcher in information inequality, communication, or network science

- **Statement:** power follows influence over the shared pool when no anchor is pinned; coordination that works is triangulation, while a chain of agreeing frames is a mirror. High correlation is disqualifying for a verdict and efficient only for a convention.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 2 :14-15 *"they converge, and what they converge on is whoever had the most influence"*. Fig 2 :19 *"they never talk, and still agree"*. Fig 2 :35 *"Alignment is triangulation, not negotiation. A mirror cannot correct you."* Fig 2 :40 *"disqualifying for a verdict: agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool"*. Fig 2 :41 *"efficient for a convention: agreement about a shared meaning is the product, not a corruption of it"*. Fig 7 :12-13 *"same closure, handed along; agreement compounds, evidence does not"*.
- **What to check:** in a real information ecosystem, does convergence track who controls the shared evidence pool rather than who has the most information? Does cross-checking against an independently measured anchor differ from mutual updating among platforms? Is agreement within a homogeneous community a convention (which can be a product) rather than corroboration of a fact about the world?
- **Refuter:** two frames sharing a pool produce agreement that survives an independent ground reading on the same axis; or convergence follows something other than influence with no anchor pinned.

### C3 — structural/social; checkable by a sociologist, information-systems researcher, or organizational scientist

- **Statement:** social inequality is differential capability in agent-and-habitat; an access improvement that leaves contact unchanged cannot move the outcome; a seat can introduce but not exclude, so inequality management is permanent work.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 11 :39 *"capability is a property of agent-and-habitat, never of the agent alone"*. Fig 11 :40 *"An improvement that does not change contact changes nothing, however much it improves."* Fig 11 :61 *"The seat's power is asymmetric. Introduction is available; exclusion is not."* Fig 4 :22 *"Only methods cross up. Only head starts come down."* Fig 11 :52-53 *"And contact cascades. Scoping the habitat to direct contact is how DDT reached the eagles: first-order enumeration, second-order effect, and the residual that mattered was three steps out."*
- **What to check:** does device-level access (link 1 of Fig 3's chain) fail to move ground-settled capability outcomes when habitat and contact are unchanged? Are the effects three steps out the ones that matter? Is the introduction/exclusion asymmetry visible in the institution being studied?
- **Refuter:** a controlled intervention that raises access without changing habitat or contact and moves the ground-settled inequality outcome; or a seat removes an actor from a habitat.

## 3. What is explicitly not claimed

- Not that more information reduces inequality (Fig 11 :40 says an improvement that does not change contact changes nothing).
- Not that any named institution, platform, law, or curriculum is *the* cause. That is a name where the framework returns a shape (Fig 9 :32). The institutional name is a stated **gap**, not a hidden conclusion.
- Not one inequality score. Three slots, held apart (Fig 1 :19-22); description bits and settling actions do not add (Fig 12 :125-126).
- Not that expert agreement settles anything (Fig 2 :35, :40).
- Not `grounded`. All three verdicts are `candidate`. No measurement was taken in this run.

## 5. What would settle it

A ground reading, not agreement. In order of leverage:

1. **Habitat test:** an intervention that raises access without changing habitat or contact, and a measurement of whether the ground-settled inequality outcome moves. This is the test most likely to reverse C3.
2. **Anchor test:** a convergent/disconnected design in which frames share an evidence pool or update on each other versus ranging against an independently measured anchor. Settles C2.
3. **Overlap test:** a design with low versus high frame overlap, checking whether the union surplus survives an independent ground reading. Settles C1.
4. **Reach test:** an instrument that distinguishes importing a term from a differing closure versus pure composition inside a closure. Settles C1 at the mechanism level.
5. **Name test:** a provenanced import with a source gate and shadow test naming a specific institution, then a ground reading. This is what would close the recorded gap.

The six pre-registered refuters are in `01` §3. Any one firing withdraws its slot before any theory is shown.
