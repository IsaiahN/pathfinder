# Isomers and order: two supporting derivations

**Mode: `specified`.** Simulated derivation checked against the figures, not a ground reading (see `05_what_shape_means.md` section 7).

## RESIDUAL

Two past episodes can present identically in an archive: same observation, same failed prediction, same duration without a fix. The historian nonetheless wants to call one a correctable mistake and the other an enduring limit. Two questions are owed:

1. Can two such entries be **isomers** under Fig 12, that is, same atoms, different bond, different substance?
2. What is the **dependency order** of the operations that would discriminate them, and which links have instruments?

## DOMAIN CENSUS

Atoms held in the historian's library: the residual `R` of the episode, the prediction Γ(b,a), the observation o', the ground of the past frame, the closure of the past frame, and the archive narrative. Bonds held: the seven plus negation (`¬`). No new primitive is available without import. The ground, not the narrative, is the anchor.

## DERIVATION

### Q1 - Are two identically presented errors isomers?

Fig 12 :117: "isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour." Fig 12 :96: "Two entries can share every ingredient and be inverses of one another."

Take two entries with the same ingredient set `{R, prediction, observation, ground reading}`:

- Entry A is routed as mis-attached: Fig 5 :13 "mis-attached: refit what you have, do not invent." The bond between prediction and object is wrong; the prediction term is kept and rebound.
- Entry B is routed as refuted: Fig 5 :53 "refuted: a term we hold is wrong; a term is owed that says so." The bond is negation, not rebinding. Fig 12 :112: "A refutation is a negation, and that one is not a bond." The Operators table lists negation as unary and states "a refutation is a negation, which is why a vocabulary without it can only accumulate."

Same atoms, different operator, different substance, and the difference is invisible in the archive's flat summary. That is Fig 12's isomer claim applied to error study, not an analogy imported from chemistry. The chemistry case is itself cited in the figure: Fig 12 :97-98 mentions silver fulminate and silver cyanate, "same composition and different properties".

The limit case is not a third isomer. It is not a bond between the archive's atoms at all: it is a statement about the reach, and it needs the atom "available source frame" or the instrument "something already registering", neither of which is in the ingredient set. Under Fig 12 :89 "A relation that fails all six is not a bond you are missing. It is a claim that has not been made yet." So the limit case is a different *kind* of claim, not another spelling of the same molecule.

**Falsifier, written before the run:** if two episodes with identical ingredients and different route bins behave identically under every ground-reading action (same fix, same instrument, same import), the isomer claim fails and the entries are one kind wearing two names (Fig 8 :49 "two names for one frame").

### Q2 - What is the dependency order?

Fig 3 :20-21: "The chain is ordered by dependency, not by importance. Each link consumes what the one above produces." The chain for this question has five links:

| link | what it consumes | instrument | status |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 state the residual | the episode's prediction and observation | the ground of that frame: a measurement, counter, outcome | exists in principle for an empirical episode; must be recovered from primary sources |
| 2 route the residual | link 1 | the five-bin boundary diff (Fig 5 :10-14, :53) | figure-given |
| 3 refuse before mint | link 2 | negation (`¬`), an operator, not a bond | figure-given (Operators table) |
| 4 run reachability questions separately | link 3 plus the closure of the past frame | inward instrument and outward import (Fig 6 :28-31) | inward needs something already returning something (Fig 6 :32); outward needs a source frame |
| 5 classify and record | links 1 to 4 | the ground settles; provenance recorded (Fig 10 :62) | ground required, not agreement |

Fig 3 :27: "A link with no instrument is not a link that works. It is a link nobody has looked at." Link 4 is the one most often skipped in historical narrative: a long-unfixed episode is simply called a limit, with no inward or outward instrument named. Effort spent at link 5 before link 4 "arrives at a step that cannot use it" (Fig 3 :20-21).

### Q3 - Is there a functional group?

Fig 12 :124: "functional group. A substructure whose presence fixes the outcome. The group says what kind; the qualifier says which member."

All correctable entries share the functional group "an action is owed inside the reach" (refit, mint, or refuse). All limit entries share the functional group "an import or a new instrument is owed". The two groups are different kinds, not different members of one kind. A historian who says "this was just another error" has collapsed a functional-group difference into a member difference, which is the flat-formula failure Fig 12 :14-16 names: "the ingredients are recorded and the arrangement is not ... nothing signals that anything was lost".

**Falsifier for Q3:** show one action that is both inside-the-reach and outside-the-reach for the same episode and the same ground. If that is possible, the two groups are one group and the limit distinction dissolves.

### Q4 - What order can never be reversed?

Three orderings are forced, and none is a matter of style:

1. **Residual before frame** (Fig 9 :27-28): "describe it before you go looking, or any frame you pick will seem to fit."
2. **Refusal before mint** (`THE_FORMULA.md` step 2): the fifth bin sorts by what is WRONG, and "no arrangement of the other four reaches it".
3. **Settlement before composition** (Formula step 5; Fig 12 :106: "Nothing composes over what was never admitted as a part."). A classification built on an unsettled binding cannot be composed over in the next historical argument.

Inward and outward (link 4) are **opposed** and "never read one against the other" (`THE_FORMULA.md` step 7; Fig 6 :31 "Neither answer tells you much about the other. Run them as two tracks"). Treating "we searched harder and found nothing" as evidence that a new frame is needed is the budget error Fig 8 :42-43 prices: "Importing against a budget limit adds an atom that was not needed".

## CONCLUSION

Two errors with the same archive ingredients but different route bonds are genuine isomers under Fig 12: the difference is invisible in the flat record and decisive in what action is owed. The limit case is not a third isomer; it is a different kind of claim about the reach and needs an instrument or a source frame that is not in the ingredient set. The dependency order is residual, route, refuse, reachability, classify, with settlement as ground rather than agreement. The middle links, route and reachability, are the ones with no instrument in ordinary error-study narratives, which is exactly where Fig 3 :26 says chains usually break.
