**Subject:** One checkable claim about learning from scientific errors

> Hello [name],
>
> I have one output I would like you to check against what you already know. I am not asking you to read a theory first, and I am not asking you to agree with anything. If the output fails, there is nothing else to look at.
>
> **The output (`general` on the shape, `specified` on applications, not `grounded`):**
>
> The question "can studying past scientific errors distinguish correctable mistakes from enduring limits?" is not one binary question. It is a compound. A past error is a residual of some earlier frame against its ground, and a residual can be routed in five ways: the model was right, the probe needs extending, the model is attached to the wrong object, the mechanism needs a new term, or a term that was held is false and owes a refusal. Only the last kind requires saying that something is wrong; the other four say what is missing. An enduring limit is a different object again: a wall of the reach, underivable in principle, unreachable at this budget, or a structure no instrument has ever registered.
>
> The claim is this: **the archive of past errors cannot, by itself, tell you which kind of case you have.** From inside the record, a term that was never found and an atom that was never available look the same. So the useful output of error study is not a label for each episode. It is a procedure that changes which ground-reading action you owe next, plus a written refuter.
>
> **What this does not claim:** that any particular famous episode was a mistake rather than a limit; that long-unresolved equals permanent; that a list of agreeable historians corroborates a classification. It also does not claim the framework is a theory of everything or the only true one.
>
> **What would refute it (written before this check):**
> 1. A rule applied to the archive alone, with no access to the earlier frame's ground or closure, that sorts episodes correctly on held-out cases.
> 2. A representative archive in which no episode ever changes route bin when its ground is consulted, so the five-bin sort does no work.
> 3. A case where the ground of the earlier frame changes with what the present frame thinks, and the classification stays stable.
>
> I wrote these down before sending this. A one-line "this fails because..." is more useful to me than agreement. If you would rather not engage, no reply is needed. I will record your verdict on this paragraph before showing you any source.
>
> **If it survives, and only then:** the sources are below. The figures are the authority, not my summary of them.
> - Verbatim figure text with line numbers (local framework file): `FRAMEWORK/reference/03-FIGURES_TEXT.md`
> - Figures and formula, branch `general-agent`: https://github.com/IsaiahN/tether/tree/general-agent/figures

## Stage sequence

1. **Stage 1:** send the residual, the shape claim, and the refuter. Ask what the recipient would count as a failure. Record the answer.
2. **Stage 2, opt-in only:** if they are curious, offer the three links. Their stage-1 verdict is never pooled with later agreement (Fig 2 :40: "agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool").
3. **Stage 3:** offer the formula as a method to reuse, with the instruction that each person writes their own refuter and states their own mode.

No stage asks for trust, endorsement, or a show of hands. A failure is the preferred outcome of the check.
