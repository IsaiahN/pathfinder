## 0. The residual, in effect terms

The question asks for a discriminator. A student or historian holds an archive of past scientific errors. Each entry is the residual `R` of some past frame against its ground: a prediction that failed, an observation that would not resolve, a term that did not pay. The archive also carries later narrations of those episodes. What is owed is a sort:

- **Correctable mistake.** A wrong or mis-attached term *inside* a closure, for which the ground could settle a fix: a refusal, a refit, a new term, or an import from a frame with a different closure.
- **Enduring limit.** A wall of the reach: underivable in principle, unreachable at this budget, or a structure for which no instrument has ever registered anything to extend from.

The residual is that **the archive does not carry either label on its face**. A failed prediction looks the same in the record whether it was a mis-attached term or a missing atom. The word "error" spans a per-slot wrong belief and a closure-level absence, which are different objects at different scales. Sorting them by how long they lasted, by how famous the people were, or by how the story is told today imports a preference that was never bound as a term.

Stated as an effect and bound as an ordinary term (Formula step "Direction"): **a study of past errors is useful iff it changes which ground-reading action is owed next.** If it leaves that action unchanged, it has produced a narrative, not a discriminator. This is the shape the answer must have, written down before the run.

## 1. Domain census (before choosing any figure line)

**R by arity, symmetry, scale.**

- *Arity:* the question is phrased as a two-way split (correctable / limit), but the figures route residuals into five bins and ask three further reachability questions. The binary is therefore a **union compound presented as a partition**, not one hard question. Fig 9 :54: "And when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again." The *Library of Babel* essay names the same failure: "Perhaps your problem was a bundle or conflation of several problems masquerading as one?"
- *Symmetry:* a past error is a directional residual, prediction against observation. Swapping prediction and observation does not leave the same thing, and the source of a reading is not recoverable from the reading. *The Hard Problem is About Source*: "If several paths create the same behavior, then behavior is a projection with lossy information".
- *Scale:* per-slot (one wrong belief in one object slot, Fig 1 :19 "The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated.") versus closure-level (a missing atom or a wall). The same word "error" covers both, and that is the first thing the census must separate.

**One line per domain: actors / currency / anchor.**

| domain | actors | currency | anchor | question type |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| D1, the past scientific frame | the researchers, their library Γ, the terms they held and predicted with | residuals against their own ground | the ground of that science: instrument, experiment, counter, patient outcome | partition-like sort into route bins, but only after the residual is stated |
| D2, the limit / closure frame | the closure, the atoms, the instruments, the source frames | reachability and import | the same ground, which does not update | union of three opposed questions (searched / inward / outward) |
| D3, the historian / student frame | readers of the archive, the handed-down narratives, the present frame | agreement and residual | the ground, never agreement | a compound: it inherits D1 and D2 and adds a shared-evidence-pool failure |

A domain with no nameable anchor is not a domain yet. All three have one: D1's experiment, D2's ground under the wall, D3's ground rather than the agreement of readers. So all three are domains.

**Partition or union.** D1 is a partition once the residual is stated (Fig 5's bins are mutually exclusive by what is owed). D2 is a union: the three questions are "DIFFERENT FACTS with different instructions" (`THE_FORMULA.md` step 7). D3 is a union compound, because it contains D1 and D2 plus the further condition that the reader's evidence pool may be shared with the narration. The overall question is therefore a union, and must be unbundled before a verdict is attempted (Fig 9 :54).

**Dependency order (Fig 3).** Fig 3 :20-21: "The chain is ordered by dependency, not by importance. Each link consumes what the one above produces." The order here is:

1. state the residual of the past episode in effect terms (perception, Fig 3 :4-5);
2. route it into the bins (vocabulary/objective, Fig 3 :6-11); refuse before mint where a held term is false;
3. ask reachability (Fig 6 / Fig 8) only for the bins that owe a new term;
4. let the ground settle; agreement is not a gate that can substitute (Formula step 5: "A gate is not the ground.");
5. only then classify. Fig 3 :19: "Optimising a link that already works cannot rescue one that does not."

Effort spent on step 5 before step 1 is effort above the break. It produces confident classification with no residual behind it.

## 3. Findings

### F1 - The split is a union masquerading as a partition (governing finding)

Fig 9 :54: "And when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again." Fig 9 :55-57 add that a bundled problem is "a union of overlapping conditions", so a single lever clears the region where only one condition holds and leaves the intersections standing.

The two asking pairs here overlap. A term can be wrong *and* the frame can lack the instrument that would settle the replacement. Calling that episode a "mistake" clears the wrong-term condition and leaves the instrument condition binding; calling it a "limit" does the reverse. Fig 9 :57: "a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either. It stops being a mixture and becomes a specific compound."

*Corroboration, low overlap:* Fig 5's five bins and Fig 9's union/partition rule are different figures with different subject matter. The *Library of Babel* essay independently names the bundle failure. **Triangulated** across Fig 5 + Fig 9 (two figures); the essay is a high-overlap witness from the same author.

*Refuter:* show that every entry in a representative archive falls into exactly one of the two bins and never changes bin when its ground is consulted. If the binary is a genuine partition, this finding is wrong and the rest of the sort is unnecessary.

### F2 - A past error is a residual, and four of the five bins are correctable in principle

Fig 5 :12: "new: extend perception, aim the probe here." Fig 5 :13: "mis-attached: refit what you have, do not invent." Fig 5 :14: "mechanism broken: a new term is owed." Fig 5 :53: "refuted: a term we hold is wrong; a term is owed that says so." Fig 5 :11: "held: the model was right, nothing owed."

`THE_FORMULA.md` step 2 uses the same five-way route: TRANSFERRED, NOVEL, BROKEN rebinding, BROKEN mechanism, REFUTED, and states that "the first four sort R by what is MISSING; the fifth by what is WRONG, and no arrangement of the other four reaches it."

Each of these owes an in-frame or importable action: refit, mint, refuse, or import with a source gate. That is what "correctable" means in effect terms. The fifth bin needs an operator the first four do not: Fig 12 :112: "A refutation is a negation, and that one is not a bond." The Operators table confirms negation is unary and that "a refutation is a negation, which is why a vocabulary without it can only accumulate."

*World corroboration, independent of the framework:* the *Problem of induction* Wikipedia entry reports that Popper held "what should be done is to look to find and correct errors." The *Philosophy of science* entry states "Popper argued that the central property of science is falsifiability." SEP's *Karl Popper* entry (Thornton) requires that "criteria of refutation have to be laid down beforehand: it must be agree which observable situations, if actually observed, mean that the theory is refuted."

**Triangulated** across Fig 5 and Fig 12 (low overlap: a routing figure and a composition figure), with independent world corroboration.

*Refuter:* find a past episode whose residual cannot be routed into any of the five bins even after its ground is known. If the bins are not exhaustive, the correctable side of the sort is incomplete.

### F3 - An enduring limit is a property of the reach, not an error of a term

Fig 6 :14 calls import "the only operator that moves the wall". Fig 6 :23: "No frame escapes its own closure. The bound moves only on contact with a frame whose closure differs." Fig 8 :32: "Closure is idempotent. Composing inside a closed set never leaves it, so no search, however long, adds an atom."

The figures then split "limit" into three different facts:

1. *Unreachable at this budget.* Fig 8 :41-42: "Underivable: go outside. Unreachable-at-budget: the term is" / "already in the closure and was not found, so the remedy is more search, not another atom."
2. *Underivable in principle.* Fig 8 :39: "So a search that finds nothing is never a proof of absence." Fig 8 :40: "Underivable-in-principle and unreachable-at-this-budget are different facts".
3. *No edge to extend from.* Fig 6 :32: "An instrument is not built from a description. It is improved from a worse instrument already returning something." Fig 6 :40: "What is unavailable is a sensor for something that has never registered anywhere, at any resolution."

None of these is a wrong term. Calling them "errors" is a category error: it applies the refusal bin to a wall.

*World corroboration:* SEP's *Scientific Revolutions* entry (Nickles) describes "anomalous experimental and theoretical results, some of which will long resist the best attempts to resolve them"; that is a limit-shaped record, but the entry does not say which kind of limit. *The Limits of Science* was checked and is a biography of Medawar in the fetched page, so it is not used as evidence here.

**Triangulated** across Fig 6 and Fig 8 (low overlap: instrument rule versus idempotence and budget rule).

*Refuter:* demonstrate that a wall can be removed by refusing a held term with no import and no new instrument. If the reach can fall up to a limit without an import, the wall is not what this finding says it is.

### F4 - The archive alone cannot label depth or absence

Fig 6 :27: "Which is why some unreachability is depth and some is genuine absence, and from inside the frame the two look the same." Fig 6 :28: "Which makes reachability two questions, not one. The instruments are opposed." Fig 8 :34: "A frame cannot reach its own metaframe."

This is the load-bearing reason the answer is conditional. A past episode in which the fix was never found is compatible with both an unfound term inside the closure and an absent atom outside it. Duration in the archive does not discriminate. "A search that finds nothing is never a proof of absence" (Fig 8 :39) is not a licence for despair; it is a statement about what the search can certify.

*The Hard Problem is About Source* sharpens this with metamerism: an instrument reads green "cannot comment on the source", and the source was resolved "not by argument, but by the arrival of a better instrument." What an error-archive records is the reading. What it usually fails to record is the discriminator that would have separated the sources.

**Triangulated** across Fig 6 and Fig 8.

*Refuter:* produce a rule applied to the archive alone, with no access to the past frame's closure or ground, that sorts every episode correctly and whose correctness can be checked on a held-out set. Until such a rule is shown, the archive supplies candidates and lineage, not the verdict.

### F5 - A historian who reads only handed-down narratives is a mirror chain

Fig 7 :13: "agreement compounds, evidence does not." Fig 7 :12 is the label for that panel: "same closure, handed along". Fig 2 :40: "agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool." Fig 2 :31: "Independence, between two frames, is how little they draw on the same evidence."

The habit of citing many historians who all read the same secondary literature raises the vocabulary and not the anchor. Fig 2 :42: "Buy independence in proportion to the cost of being wrong." Here the cost of being wrong is exactly the misclassification the question asks about, so independence is worth buying with primary sources and with instruments, not with more commentaries.

*World corroboration:* the *Historiography of science* Wikipedia entry warns of narratives "selecting only those events that seem important from the present point of view", which is the present-centred reading the figures forbid. SEP's *Thomas Kuhn* entry (Bird) notes that within normal science "anomalies are ignored or explained away if at all possible", so the archive's own labels are theory-laden before the historian arrives.

**Triangulated** across Fig 7 and Fig 2.

*Refuter:* show two frames whose classifications agree, drawn from disjoint primary evidence and disjoint instruments, with the agreement recorded before they read each other. That would be genuine corroboration at high independence, not one pool talking.

### F6 - Each past frame must be ranged against its own ground

Fig 2 :29: "An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks." Fig 2 :45: "Frames range against the ground. They do not negotiate with it." Fig 2 :46: "Each frame is corrected by it separately, and what makes the correction sound is that neither frame can move it." Fig 2 :33-34: if the anchor moves, "everything drifts together, and the drift is coherent, which is why nobody notices."

For error study this means the historian may not use the present verdict as the anchor. Doing so makes the past frame a party to a vote it cannot win. Fig 10 :62: "A ruling carries provenance like an atom." The provenance of a classification is the residual it answered, the reasoning, and the date, so a later reader can locate the error rather than only feel it.

**Triangulated** across Fig 2 and Fig 10 (low overlap: alignment figure versus the checking-seat figure).

*Refuter:* name a case where the ground of a past frame legitimately changes with the present frame's judgement, and show that the resulting classification is still stable. If such a case exists, the anchor is not immovable here.

### F7 - Describe the residual before selecting the frame

Fig 9 :27-28: "describe it before you go looking, or any frame you pick will seem to fit." Fig 9 :30-31: "your words are borrowed too, so describe the effect" / "rather than the cause." Fig 8 :53: "Residual first, frame second."

A survey of the history-of-errors literature that begins by choosing Kuhn, or Popper, or Lakatos, and then finds episodes to fit, is the named inversion. The *Decoding Tacit Knowledge* essay gives the checkable form: "When contact isn't possible, the best you can send is a plain description of what you'd expect to see, because anyone can check that against the world."

**Single-source for the figure line** (Fig 9), with independent world corroboration from the essay's ordinary-language criterion. The essay shares an author with the framework, so that corroboration is weak, not a second witness.

*Refuter:* show a classification procedure that starts from a named frame and still predicts which episodes will change bin under a new ground, with the prediction committed before the episodes are examined.

### F8 - What is returned is a shape and a procedure, never a list of names

Fig 9 :32: "you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself." Fig 13 :134: "Unreachable is a verdict here, not a silence". Fig 13 :135: "It cannot say which primitive is missing, because naming it would mean already holding it." The canonical figures README states the limit as "what a missing thing would have to look like (a shape, not a name)".

So the deliverable of error study, under these figures, is the shape of a discriminator: state the residual, route it, run the opposed reachability instruments separately, let the ground decide, keep the lineage. The name of the missing primitive, or the label "this episode was merely a mistake", is a contribution of the searcher and must be recorded as such.

**Triangulated** across Fig 9 and Fig 13.

*Refuter:* show the framework naming a missing primitive and settling it by the ground in the same move. That would break the shape/not-a-name limit.

## 4. Strain test and re-census

**Strain A: Fig 5 :14 "mechanism broken: a new term is owed" versus Fig 6 :14 "the only operator that moves the wall".** Read naively, a new term owed sounds like an enduring limit. Re-census resolves it: Fig 5 :23 defines the new term as one that "composes from what we already have", so the minted term is inside the closure. Fig 6's wall is about a term that cannot compose from what is held. Different objects. No strain after re-census.

**Strain B: the archive's long-unfixed anomalies (Nickles) versus Popperian immediate refutation (Thornton).** These two world sources pull in opposite directions about how scientists actually treated anomalies. The conflict rule sends us back to the census, not to a flipped verdict. Re-census: the disagreement is between two *world frames describing past behaviour*, not between the figures. Fig 5's bins accommodate both: the Kuhnian behaviour is the mis-attached bin (refit, do not mint), and the Popperian behaviour is the refuted bin (a refusal is owed). The figure verdict is unchanged; the conflict is a finding about the archive's theory-ladenness (F5).

**Strain C: Fig 8 :39 "a search that finds nothing is never a proof of absence" versus the intuition that a centuries-old unresolved error is a limit.** Re-census: the first is about underivability from inside, the second is a claim about depth, which needs the other instrument (Fig 6 :28 "The instruments are opposed"). Different instruments. No strain, but a reason to keep the two questions separate.

**After re-census: three strains examined, zero hidden, zero left standing.**

## 5. Pre-registered refuter

Written before the conclusion, per the standing rule that "a falsifier written before the run is a design instrument." The claim that studying past errors helps discriminate is refuted if any of the following holds:

1. **Archive-sufficiency.** A rule applied to the archive alone, with no access to the past frame's ground or closure, sorts every episode correctly and is checked on held-out episodes. This would make the ground-reading procedure unnecessary.
2. **Bin collapse.** In a representative archive, no episode ever changes route bin when its ground is consulted; the five bins always reduce to the same two labels. The sort would be doing no work.
3. **Moving anchor.** A case is found where the ground of a past frame changes with what the present frame thinks, and the classification remains stable. This breaks Fig 2 :25 "pinned: it does not update on A or B".
4. **Mode violation as evidence.** Someone labels a particular episode `grounded` on the strength of this simulated derivation. That is a verdict in the wrong mode, not a corroboration.

If any fires, the structural claim is withdrawn or narrowed. Until one is run, no part of this folder is `grounded`.

## 6. Recommendation

1. Lead with F1 and F4: the split is a compound, and the archive cannot certify absence. That is the honest answer to the question as asked.
2. Teach the five bins and the three reachability questions as a method, not as a vocabulary for labelling famous episodes (Fig 4 :22 "Only methods cross up. Only head starts come down.").
3. Require the residual and the refuter to be written before any classification is defended (Fig 5 :66 "Committing a prediction before a run is this, already working.").
4. Buy independence with primary sources and instruments, never with more agreeable commentaries (Fig 2 :42).
5. Keep the lineage: which residual was answered, by which ground, on what date (Fig 10 :62).

## 8. Verdict per domain (never averaged)

- **D1, the past scientific frame - `general`, triangulated (Fig 5 + Fig 12).** Its errors are residuals with five route bins, four of which owe in-reach actions and the fifth a refusal. Study of the residual is genuinely informative about what was owed. This is the part of the question that gets a "yes".
- **D2, the limit / closure frame - `general`, triangulated (Fig 6 + Fig 8).** A limit is a wall, not a wrong term. The three reachability questions must be run separately. Study of the residual supplies the candidate wall but not the verdict about which kind of wall. This is a "yes, but only as a shape".
- **D3, the historian / student frame - `specified`, triangulated (Fig 2 + Fig 7).** This frame can corrupt the whole exercise by reading a shared pool and calling the agreement evidence. Study helps only if it changes which ground-reading action is owed next. This is a "yes iff" condition, not a verdict of fact.

The three verdicts are kept separate. Averaging them into "yes, studying errors helps somewhat" would destroy the only useful content, which is *which* action each domain owes.
