## VERDICT (from the figures)

Studying past errors **can help, but only as a procedure and never as a classifier by inspection**. The archive supplies the residual and the lineage. It does not supply the label: a term never found and an atom never available look the same from inside the record (Fig 6 :27), and no amount of search proves absence (Fig 8 :39). The usable discriminator states the residual in effect terms, routes it into the five bins, refuses before minting, runs inward and outward as opposed tracks, ranges each past frame against its own ground, and records the refuter and the provenance. The result is a shape, not a list of famous episodes labelled right or wrong. This is `general` on the shape and `specified` on applications, with no `grounded` claim.

- **cost if wrong:** mislabelling in both directions is expensive and locatable. Calling a correctable mistake a limit freezes an in-reach refusal and treats a wall that is not there. Calling a limit a mistake spends budget searching inside a closure that cannot contain the answer; Fig 8 :42-43 warns that "Importing against a budget limit adds an atom that was not needed". The second-order cost is spending readers' independence by sending a conclusion instead of a refuter, which Fig 2 :42 prices: "Buy independence in proportion to the cost of being wrong." Both costs are avoidable by leading with the residual and the refuter.
- **undoable:** yes. No primitive is withdrawn and no episode is committed to a label. Each classification can be kept ranked and reversible (Fig 9 :41: "so keep every cut ranked and reversible"). A superseding packet can replace a defective one without touching the method.
- **reversed by:** a ground reading, not agreement. A bin-change test on primary sources in which episodes change route bin when their ground is consulted; an instrument showing that nothing at any resolution registers the resolving quantity; a framed import with a source gate and shadow test; or a demonstration that the archive-only refuter in `01` section 5 succeeds on held-out cases. Until one of these fires, the verdict stands as `candidate`.
