## Headline verdict

Studying past errors helps, but only as a supplier of residuals, failed terms, instrument states, and lineage. It does not arbitrate. The figures recognise the distinction the question asks for, but restate it as a difference of reachability, not of truth: a correctable mistake is unreachable at this budget, while an enduring limit is underivable relative to a closure. From inside a frame the two look the same. History can name candidates and record the route; only the ground settles the case. The binary itself is a union of two loops and must be unbundled before it is searched. Verdicts are kept separate and never averaged.

## What is deliberately not here

- No personal emails of named individuals.
- No ranking of people by how likely they are to agree with this answer.
- No bulk-send plan.

Three figure-grounded reasons:

1. Fig 2 :35: "Alignment is triangulation, not negotiation. A mirror cannot correct you." Selecting recipients for receptiveness manufactures the disqualifying correlation.
2. Fig 2 :31: "Independence, between two frames, is how little they draw on the same evidence." A list of readers drawn from one tradition is one frame wearing many names.
3. Fig 10 :42: "A verifier that can reconstruct the claim cannot verify it." The first contact carries the residual and its refuter, not the framework's interiors.

Legally: unsolicited bulk email engages GDPR and CAN-SPAM style rules in many jurisdictions. Public opt-in channels avoid that failure as well as the structural one.
