## Round trip gap R_T

The Symbols table defines `R_T` as the gap between what was sent up and what came back down, `gap(x, (T_E ∘ T_A)(x))`. Fig 4 :30 requires it to be measured rather than assumed. I ran the round trip on the condensation this folder actually uses.

1. **Sent up (x):** the verbatim figure set, chiefly Fig 9 :54 (union unbundling), Fig 5 :11-14 and :53 (route bins), Fig 6 :23 and :27 and Fig 8 :32 and :39 (reach and the no-absence-proof limit), Fig 2 :40 and Fig 7 :13 (shared pool).
2. **Abstraction (T_A):** the shorthand carried in `07_the_tether_answer.md`: "study helps iff it changes which ground-reading action is owed next"; errors are residuals; limits are walls; the archive gives a shape, not a label.
3. **Concretisation (T_E):** the tempting concrete claim: "this famous episode was a correctable mistake, that one an enduring limit, and here is the list."

**R_T is nonzero.** Four things the shorthand holds do not survive the trip back down:

- the **union condition**. A single episode can hold a wrong term and a missing atom at once; the shorthand's "either/or" loses the intersection (Fig 9 :55-57);
- the **opposition of instruments**. Inward and outward must not be read against each other (Fig 6 :31); the shorthand can quietly merge them into "more evidence";
- the **anchor requirement**. Each past frame must be corrected separately (Fig 2 :46); the shorthand "we now know better" silently uses the present frame as the anchor, which is panel 1 of Fig 2;
- the **provenance of the classification**. Fig 10 :62 requires the residual, reasoning, and date; a list of labels carries none of these.

Conversely, one thing survives cleanly: a past error is a residual that can be routed by what is owed, and the archive alone cannot certify absence. That is the residue worth sending. R_T was **not** measured numerically, because no ground reading exists; it is recorded qualitatively as the enumerated set of dropped constraints. Treating the formula as identical to the figure set would set R_T to zero and would be the dead-reckoning error Fig 4 :30 names.
