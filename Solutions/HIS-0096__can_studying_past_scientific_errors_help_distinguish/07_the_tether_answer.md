# The Tether's answer: can studying past errors sort mistakes from limits?

**Mode: `general` on the discriminator shape, `specified` on applications, not `grounded`.** Simulated derivation checked for internal consistency against the figures (`05_what_shape_means.md` section 7). It cannot settle the question it reports on.

## 1. The residual it answers

An archive of past scientific errors is a set of residuals of past frames against their grounds. The student wants it to sort into two kinds: correctable mistakes and enduring limits. The residual is that the archive carries the failed predictions but not the label, and from inside the record a term that was never found looks like an atom that was never available (Fig 6 :27: "Which is why some unreachability is depth and some is genuine absence, and from inside the frame the two look the same.").

## 2. The shape

1. **The binary is a compound, not a partition.** Fig 9 :54: "And when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again." A wrong held term and a reach that lacks the replacement can both be present in one episode. A single lever clears the region where only one holds.

2. **A past error is a residual with five route bins.** Fig 5 :12 "new: extend perception, aim the probe here"; :13 "mis-attached: refit what you have, do not invent"; :14 "mechanism broken: a new term is owed"; :53 "refuted: a term we hold is wrong; a term is owed that says so". Four bins say what is missing, one says what is wrong. "Correctable" means an action is owed inside the reach: refit, mint, refuse, or import from a source frame.

3. **An enduring limit is a wall, not a wrong term.** Fig 6 :14 calls import "the only operator that moves the wall"; Fig 8 :32 "Closure is idempotent. Composing inside a closed set never leaves it, so no search, however long, adds an atom." The limit splits into unreachable-at-budget, underivable-in-principle, and no proto-instrument registering anything (Fig 6 :32, :40). These have different instructions and must not be averaged.

4. **The archive cannot certify the label.** Fig 8 :39: "So a search that finds nothing is never a proof of absence." Duration is not a discriminator. What is owed is an instrument or a source frame, and the record of where it came from.

5. **The historian's frame must be ranged separately.** Fig 7 :13: "agreement compounds, evidence does not." Fig 2 :40: "agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool." Ten readers of the same survey are one reader wearing ten names.

6. **The usable output is a procedure, not a taxonomy.** Fig 9 :32: "you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself." State the residual, route it, refuse before mint, run inward and outward as opposed tracks, let the ground decide, keep the lineage and the refuter, stop where the instruments stop.

## 5. What would settle it - the ground

A source-discriminating check, not a further argument. From *The Hard Problem is About Source*, an instrument that reads green "cannot comment on the source"; the case closed "not by argument, but by the arrival of a better instrument". Here the corresponding checks are:

- **Bin-change test.** Take a sample of episodes, state each residual from primary sources, route it, then consult the ground. Does any episode change bin? If none ever does, the sort does no work and the refuter in `01` section 5 fires.
- **Inward test.** Is anything at any resolution already returning something that fails to resolve (Fig 6 :34)? If yes, the remedy is more search, not an import.
- **Outward test.** Is there a frame whose closure predicts the shape of the residual (Fig 8 :27-28)? If yes, an import with source gate and shadow test is owed.
- **Provenance test.** Does the narrative record which residual was answered, by which ground, on what date (Fig 10 :62)? If not, it is a recording, not a checkable classification.

## 6. The honest stop

**Unreached, not unreachable.** The discriminator can be stated and its refuters written, but its application to any episode is a ground reading this derivation does not perform. Fig 13 :134 gives the honest form: "Unreachable is a verdict here, not a silence". The lineage is recorded so the stop is checkable. *How Intelligence Actually Works*: "The recursion converges toward indiscernibility, and indiscernibility is not identity."

## 7. One-paragraph version for outreach

> No, not by itself, and yes as a method. A past scientific error is a residual of an earlier frame against its ground, and a residual can be routed five ways: nothing owed, extend the probe, refit what you have, mint a new term, or refuse a term that was false. An enduring limit is a different object entirely, a wall of the reach that needs an import or a new instrument, not a better argument. The problem is that the archive does not record which one it has: from inside the record, a term that was never found looks exactly like an atom that never existed, and a long search that finds nothing proves no absence. So studying errors helps only if it changes what you do next, by forcing you to state the residual in effect terms, keep the reachability questions separate, range each past frame against its own ground, and write down in advance what would make you retract the classification. What it returns is the shape of a discriminator, not a list of famous episodes labelled right or wrong. The historians who all read the same survey are one witness, not a jury.

## 8. Falsifiers (pre-registered in `01` section 5, repeated here)

1. An archive-only rule that sorts correctly on held-out cases, with no access to ground or closure.
2. A representative archive in which no episode ever changes bin when its ground is consulted.
3. A case where the past frame's ground moves with present judgement and the classification remains stable.
4. A `grounded` label assigned to a specific episode on the strength of this document alone.

Any one of these narrows or withdraws the claim. None has been run here.
