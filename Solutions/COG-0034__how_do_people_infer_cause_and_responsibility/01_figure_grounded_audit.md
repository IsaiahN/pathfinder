## 0. The residual, stated in effect terms

Let the outcome be `o'`, let the reasoner hold a model `Γ(b,a)`, and let there be `k` candidate factors indexed by slot `s`. The residual is `R_s = |Γ(b,a)_s - o'_s|`, **per slot**, never one global number.

The effect to be explained is not "what caused the outcome." It is: why does one candidate factor acquire positive residual mass while another does not, why does responsibility attach to an actor rather than to a condition, and why does changing the arrangement of the same factors change the attribution? A global answer ("the situation did it," "the system failed") explains the outcome once and throws away the only signal that says where to look.

**Binding the sought-for shape (formula step, "Direction").** I write down what an answer would look like before searching: an answer is a rule that assigns a residual share and a responsibility verdict to each factor `s`, such that it (a) survives a permutation-invariant test on factor order where order is not meaningful, (b) changes when the bond type between factors changes, and (c) can be checked in a vignette where factors are varied one at a time. Binding it as an ordinary term lets it accrue a residual and be refused, instead of remaining an invisible preference.

## 1. Domain census (before choosing any figure line)

**R by arity, symmetry, scale.** Arity: `k > 2`, so several operands, both symmetric bonds (`+` conjunction, `∥` disjunction) and ordered bonds (`→` sequence, `⇒` production, `⋛` comparison, `-` subtraction) are in play. Symmetry: the overall relation is ordered (cause precedes outcome; actor and observer are not interchangeable), so a permutation of operands is a different term where an ordered bond holds. Scale: first order (direct factor contact), second order (cascades, Fig 11 :52), and population scale (culture and institutions), with the same loop at each scale (Fig 4 :19-20).

One line per domain, giving actors / currency / anchor. A domain with no nameable anchor is not a domain yet.

| domain | actors | currency | anchor (nameable?) | question type |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| D1 Lay causal cognition | reasoners; candidate factors in a vignette | per-factor causal judgments | a controlled vignette with factors varied independently (yes) | how the empirical answer is sought |
| D2 Responsibility and blame | observer, actor, harmed party | blame / culpability judgments | an explicit norm or court decision (yes) | compound with D1, needs its own loop |
| D3 Scientific causal inference | researchers, variables, instruments | effect estimates | randomized experiment (yes) | holds the instrument that can close D1 |
| D4 Safety engineering and accident analysis | barriers, latent conditions, active failures | accident trajectories | incident record (yes) | low-overlap cross-check |
| D5 Comparative cognition | crows, hidden agents | causal-inference behaviour | controlled HCA/UCA test (yes) | low-overlap cross-check |

Culture is a scale change inside D1, not a sixth domain: the same loop run over groups and institutions.

**Partition or union: union compound.** "Cause inference" and "responsibility attribution" are two conditions, not one harder question. Fig 9 :55: "A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones." Within cause inference there is a second union: detecting a live factor and detecting the bond between factors. Both need separate loops; a single lever that clears one leaves the intersection standing (Fig 9 :55-58).

**Order by dependency (Fig 3).** Link 1: perceive which factors are present. Link 2: have terms for what each factor affords (Fig 3 :7, and Spelke & Kinzler 2007 / Gibson 1979 as borrowed machinery in the architecture post). Link 3: say what counts as progress, namely a per-slot residual that trends down. Link 4: infer the arrangement. Link 5: name what worked and carry it. Fig 3 :19: "Optimising a link that already works cannot rescue one that does not." Fig 3 :27: "A link with no instrument is not a link that works. It is a link nobody has looked at." The middle link, having terms for what factors afford and what counts as a cause, is where such chains usually break (Fig 3 :24-27).

## 3. Findings F1 to F9

### F1 - Attribution must be per factor, not a global verdict (framework-internal, `specified`)

Fig 1 :19: "The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated." Fig 1 :22: "Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average." Fig 1 :28: "The gap is measured per object, not per step. A system that averages across objects has thrown away the only signal that says where to look."

World source, social psychology: the covariation model asks which factor "covaries" with the effect, using consensus, distinctiveness and consistency (Kelley, 1973, as reported in the opened Wikipedia entry "Covariation model", 2026). The kinship is at the level of the question, not the mechanism: both ask which of several candidate factors carries the signal. The figures govern the shape; Kelley is a world binding. **Mode: `specified`, triangulated** across Fig 1 and Fig 11 :43 (low overlap: slot residual versus habitat enumeration).

**Refuter:** a controlled study in which a single global cause judgment predicts responsibility ratings as well as or better than any per-factor judgment, across several factor sets, would break Fig 1 :19-22 as governance for this domain.

### F2 - Responsibility is a relation to the residual, not a property of an actor (`specified`)

Fig 11 :49: "An actor's valence is relative to the residual, not a property it carries." Fig 11 :50: "The same actor is a benefactor to one residual and a malfactor to another, so mutualism and parasitism are relations rather than kinds." Fig 10 :59: "an interest in the outcome is unavoidable, an interest in the reading is disqualifying."

World source, experimental philosophy: the Knobe effect, in which the moral valence of a side effect changes whether the same act is judged intentional (82 percent versus 23 percent in the original vignette, as reported in the opened Wikipedia entry "Joshua Knobe", 2026). I initially read this as a conflict with Fig 11 :49. Re-census below (section 4) resolves it: the figure governs ecological valence relative to a residual; the Knobe effect governs a frame-internal instrument bias. The finding corroborates the need for an anchor, it does not flip the verdict. **Mode: `specified`, triangulated** across Fig 11 and Fig 2 :29 (habitat valence plus anchor rule).

**Refuter:** a vignette study showing responsibility tracks a fixed trait of the actor across different outcomes and residuals, with the anchor held constant, would break Fig 11 :49-50.

### F3 - Direct contact is not enough; the factor that matters can be three steps out (`specified`)

Fig 11 :52: "Scoping the habitat to direct contact is how DDT reached the eagles: first-order enumeration, second-order effect, and the residual that mattered was three steps out." Fig 11 :48: "You do not invent the list. You read it off the world, and what you cannot perceive or measure yet is the residual."

World source, safety engineering: the Swiss cheese model, where an accident occurs when holes in several defensive layers momentarily align, with "active failures" directly linked to the event and "latent failures" lying dormant for days, weeks or months (opened Wikipedia entry "Swiss cheese model", 2026). World source, statistical causal inference: in sufficiently complex systems "the common-cause fallacy" appears because the correct variable was not captured in the data (opened Wikipedia entry "Causal inference", 2026). This matches Fig 2 :6-7, "collapse 2: common cause (hidden shared source)". **Mode: `specified`, single-source for the specific cascade claim (Fig 11), triangulated for the general indirect-cause shape with Fig 2 :6-7 and two low-overlap world fields.**

**Refuter:** a study in which enumerating only immediate, directly visible factors predicts attribution as well as an enumeration extended to second- and third-order contacts would break Fig 11 :52.

### F4 - The same factors under a different bond are a different explanation (`specified`)

Fig 12 :5: "A set of ingredients is not one. The bond is the rest of the information, and it is the half nothing was recording." Fig 12 :117: "isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour." Fig 12 :119: "catalyst. A part that survives its own use. Spent and regenerating parts behave differently across uses, and nothing records which a term is."

World source, psychology of attribution: Kelley's three causal schemata distinguish multiple sufficient causes (either A or B suffices), multiple necessary causes (both are needed), and compensatory causes (either maximal or both moderate), as recorded in the opened Wikipedia entry "Covariation model" (2026). These are three different bonds over the same two factors, which is exactly Fig 12 :117's isomer point in the world literature. World source, safety engineering: latent versus active failures are the same distinction between a standing condition (catalyst) and a trigger. **Mode: `specified`, single-source for the bond taxonomy (Fig 12), corroborated by two low-overlap fields.**

**Refuter:** if people's causal and responsibility judgments are invariant when the same factor set is presented under a necessary reading versus a sufficient reading, the isomer claim fails for this domain.

### F5 - Two reasons agreeing may be one evidence pool (`general`)

Fig 2 :31: "Independence, between two frames, is how little they draw on the same evidence." Fig 2 :40: "agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool". Fig 2 :35: "Alignment is triangulation, not negotiation. A mirror cannot correct you." Fig 7 :13: "agreement compounds, evidence does not".

The lived version of this finding is in *Decoding Tacit Knowledge* (2026-11-10): two people who only check against each other "drift together, and they drift together, so neither notices", and "shared pretext is good for one thing and ruins another." For this question it means the classic actor-observer difference is not two independent witnesses when both participants read the same vignette and the same norms. **Mode: `general`, triangulated** across Fig 2 and Fig 7.

**Refuter:** a demonstration that agreement between two reasoners drawn from the same vignette and training set tracks ground-truth factor manipulation as well as a randomized-experiment benchmark would break Fig 2 :40 here.

### F6 - A missing factor is a `gap`, not a silence (`general`)

Fig 8 :32: "no search, however long, adds an atom". Fig 8 :39: "So a search that finds nothing is never a proof of absence." Fig 13 :134: "Unreachable is a verdict here, not a silence". *How Intelligence Actually Works* (2026-09-16) adds "Contact is Distinction Resolver": the missing factor is found by contact with a frame or instrument that already registers it, not by staring harder at the vignette.

This governs the empirical verdict: I did not run an experiment, so the psychological mechanism is unreached. It is not declared false, and it is not declared true. **Mode: `general`, triangulated** across Fig 8, Fig 13, and the essay.

**Refuter:** an instrumented study that closes the mechanism would move the verdict from `gap` to `grounded`; that is a settlement, not a refutation of the `gap` verdict at the time it was recorded.

### F7 - The middle link is the one without an instrument (`specified`)

Fig 3 :20: "The chain is ordered by dependency, not by importance." Fig 3 :27: "A link with no instrument is not a link that works. It is a link nobody has looked at." The architecture post records Gibson (1979) and Spelke & Kinzler (2007) as borrowed machinery for the affordance link. *How a Society of Agents Would Work* (2026-10-22) applies Fig 1 directly: "Keep the rate per kind of situation. A method that is brilliant in one setting must not be drowned by an average across all of them."

**Mode: `specified`, single-source for the chain reading (Fig 3), corroborated by the essays and the world attribution literature.**

**Refuter:** an experiment showing that people's failure on multi-factor attribution is equally well explained at the perception link and at the "what counts as a cause" link, with no dependency ordering, would break Fig 3 :20-27 as governance here.

### F8 - Cause and responsibility must be unbundled before search (`specified`)

## 4. Strain test and re-census

**Strain 1, Fig 12 bonds versus the act of attributing.** An early reading tried to make "responsibility is a bond" between actor and outcome. That strains: the figure's bonds compose factors in the world; an attribution is a term in the reasoner's library, minted against the ground. Re-census: two quantities, the structure attributed and the judgment about it. Fig 12 governs the first; no figure line governs the psychological mechanism of the second beyond the general loop. The empirical mechanism is therefore `gap`, not a strained figure fit.

**Strain 2, Fig 11 :49 versus the Knobe effect.** The figure says valence is relative to the residual; the world finding says the outcome's moral valence leaks into the judgment of intentionality. Re-census: Fig 11 governs an actor's valence relative to a residual in a habitat. The Knobe effect governs a measuring instrument, a person's judgment, under a hidden shared source (the moral valence of the outcome). It is the drift Fig 2 :6-7 and Fig 2 :33-34 name, not a contradiction of the ecological law. Recorded as an apparent conflict resolved by re-census; the verdict was not flipped to accommodate the world source.

**No figure line governs** which specific attribution rule people actually use. Genuine re-census done: D1's anchor is an experiment that was not run here. Verdict `gap`, plainly.

## 5. Pre-registered refuter (written before the conclusion)

The shape claim is refused if any of the following holds:

1. A controlled study shows that causal attributions are invariant when the same factor set is presented under different bond types (necessary versus sufficient versus threshold), which breaks Fig 12 :117.
2. A study shows a single global cause judgment predicts responsibility as well as or better than per-factor judgments across several factor sets, which breaks Fig 1 :19-22.
3. A vignette shows responsibility tracking a fixed property of the actor regardless of which residual is in view, with the anchor held constant, which breaks Fig 11 :49-50.
4. A manipulation shows the named cause is systematically the factor sharing an unobserved source with the outcome, and adding an independent anchor does not change the attribution, which breaks Fig 2 :29-30.

These were written before the verdict in `07_the_tether_answer.md`. If one fires, the shape is withdrawn or narrowed in the log, not silently repaired.
