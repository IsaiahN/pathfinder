# Expert verification packet: the Tether on multi-factor cause and responsibility

**What this is:** a claim in a form a causal-reasoning researcher can check. Mode, shape and refuters are stated before the conclusion.

**Mode: `specified` on the shape, `general` on the framing, `gap` on the empirical mechanism. Not `grounded`.** No study was run in this derivation.

## 1. The claim, in one paragraph

An adequate attribution of cause and responsibility under many interacting factors must be indexed per factor, not delivered as one global verdict; responsibility must be treated as a relation between an actor and the outcome being explained, not as a fixed property of the actor; the same factor set under a different bond must count as a different explanation; and the factor that matters can be several steps out. These are constraints on the shape of an answer, not a psychological mechanism. The mechanism is `gap` because no factor manipulation was performed here. Four refuters were written before this packet.

## 2. Three checkable sub-claims

### C1 - Framework-internal; checkable by reading Figure 1
- **Statement:** attribution is per factor; a global verdict averages away the live signal.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 1 :19 "The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated." Fig 1 :22 "Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average." Fig 1 :28 "The gap is measured per object, not per step. A system that averages across objects has thrown away the only signal that says where to look."
- **Refuter:** a controlled study in which a single global cause judgment predicts responsibility as well as or better than any per-factor judgment across several factor sets.

### C2 - Framework-internal; checkable by reading Figures 11 and 12
- **Statement:** responsibility is relative to the residual, and arrangement carries information the factor list does not.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 11 :49 "An actor's valence is relative to the residual, not a property it carries." Fig 11 :50 "The same actor is a benefactor to one residual and a malfactor to another, so mutualism and parasitism are relations rather than kinds." Fig 12 :117 "isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour." Fig 12 :119 "catalyst. A part that survives its own use. Spent and regenerating parts behave differently across uses, and nothing records which a term is."
- **Refuter:** responsibility tracks a fixed actor trait across different outcomes with the anchor held constant; or responsibility ratings are invariant when the same factor set is read as necessary versus sufficient.

### C3 - Framework-internal plus world-facing; checkable against the causal-inference literature
- **Statement:** first-order enumeration misses indirect causes, and an anchor that does not update is what separates a cause from a shared source.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 11 :52 "Scoping the habitat to direct contact is how DDT reached the eagles: first-order enumeration, second-order effect, and the residual that mattered was three steps out." Fig 2 :6-7 "collapse 2: common cause (hidden shared source)". Fig 2 :29 "An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks." Fig 2 :40 "agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool".
- **What to check against the world:** does randomized assignment, which holds other factors constant, remove the shared-source attribution in the way the figure predicts? The causal-inference literature records the common-cause fallacy as the failure that appears when the correct variable was not captured in the data.
- **Refuter:** a manipulation in which the named cause is systematically the factor sharing an unobserved source with the outcome, and giving an independent anchor does not change the attribution.

## 3. What is explicitly not claimed

- Not a psychological mechanism, and not a named attribution model derived from the figures.
- Not a verdict in any real case, and not a claim about which factors were actually present.
- Not a claim that two people agreeing makes an attribution correct.
- Not `grounded`. Nothing here was settled by measurement.
- Not that global attributions are useless. They are lossy summaries, and the loss is named in C1.

## 5. What would settle it

A factor manipulation, not a further argument. (1) Vary each candidate factor independently and score global versus per-factor attribution against the manipulation. (2) Hold the factor set fixed, change only the bond (necessary, sufficient, threshold), and look for a difference in responsibility ratings. (3) Give an independent anchor to half the participants and see whether the shared-source attribution moves. (4) Cross actor identity with the outcome explained. A finding that fires any refuter is more useful than an endorsement.
