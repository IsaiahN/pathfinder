**Subject:** One checkable shape claim about multi-factor causal attribution

> Hi [name],
>
> I have one specific output about how people infer cause and responsibility when an outcome has many interacting factors. I am not asking you to read the theory first, and I am not asking you to agree with anything. If the output does not survive, there is nothing else to look at.
>
> **The output (`specified` as a shape, `gap` as an empirical claim):** an adequate attribution has to be indexed per candidate factor, not delivered as one global verdict. A global "the situation did it" can explain the outcome and still throw away the only signal that says which factor was live. Responsibility is a relation between an actor and the outcome being explained, not a fixed property of the actor. The same factor set under a different bond (necessary versus sufficient, direct versus three steps out) is a different explanation. And a factor can matter because it is a standing condition rather than a trigger, which the factor list alone does not record.
>
> **What I am not claiming:** I am not claiming to know which attribution rule people actually use. I did not run a study, so the mechanism is `gap`, not `grounded`. I am not claiming that naming a factor settles responsibility, or that two people agreeing makes the attribution correct. I am not asking you to trust the source of this claim.
>
> **What would refute the shape claim (written before this message was sent):**
> 1. A controlled study showing that causal judgments are invariant when the same factor set is presented under necessary versus sufficient versus threshold readings.
> 2. A study showing a single global cause judgment predicts responsibility as well as or better than per-factor judgments across several factor sets.
> 3. A vignette showing responsibility tracks a fixed property of the actor regardless of which outcome is in view.
> 4. A manipulation showing the named cause is systematically the factor sharing an unobserved source with the outcome, and that giving people an independent anchor does not change the attribution.
>
> If any of these holds, I withdraw or narrow the claim. A one-line "this fails because..." is more useful to me than agreement. If you would rather not engage, no reply is needed.
>
> **If it survives, and only then:** I can send the sources behind it. The figures, not my summary of them, are the authority. I would also be glad to send the formula as a method to reuse, with the instruction that you write your own refuter and state your own mode before you run it.
>
> [name]
