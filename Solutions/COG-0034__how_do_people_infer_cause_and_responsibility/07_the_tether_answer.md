# The Tether's answer on cause and responsibility under many factors

**Mode: `specified` on the shape, `general` on the framing, `gap` on the empirical mechanism. Not `grounded`.** Simulated derivation checked against the figures (`05_what_shape_means.md` section 6), not a ground reading.

## 1. The residual it answers

An outcome occurs with `k` interacting candidate factors. The residual is `R_s = |Γ(b,a)_s - o'_s|` per factor slot, not one global number. The effect to explain is which factor carries live residual mass, why responsibility attaches to an actor rather than to a condition, and why the same factors arranged differently yield a different attribution. This is a **union compound**: cause inference and responsibility attribution are two conditions with different anchors (Fig 9 :55 "A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones."). Each has its own loop; verdicts below are kept separate and are never averaged.

## 2. The shape, per domain

**D1, lay causal cognition.** `gap` at the ground, shape `specified` and triangulated. Fig 1 :19: "The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated." Fig 1 :22: "Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average." An adequate attribution is indexed per candidate factor; a global "the situation did it" explains the outcome once and discards the signal that says where to look. The figures do not say which rule people actually use. That is owed from an experiment and is unreached here. Fig 8 :39: "So a search that finds nothing is never a proof of absence." Fig 13 :134: "Unreachable is a verdict here, not a silence".

**D2, responsibility attribution.** `specified`, triangulated. Fig 11 :49: "An actor's valence is relative to the residual, not a property it carries." Fig 11 :50: "The same actor is a benefactor to one residual and a malfactor to another, so mutualism and parasitism are relations rather than kinds." Blame is a relation between an actor and the outcome being explained, not a trait carried into every vignette. Fig 10 :59: "an interest in the outcome is unavoidable, an interest in the reading is disqualifying." The normative rule that selects the blameworthy actor is `gap`: no figure line decides it.

**D3, scientific causal inference.** `specified`, single-source for the figure reading. Fig 2 :29: "An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks." The randomized experiment is the anchor that varies one factor while holding others constant. Fig 6 :32: "An instrument is not built from a description. It is improved from a worse instrument already returning something." This frame holds the instrument D1 lacks. **cost if wrong:** bounded, since the per-factor shape is checkable without committing to a mechanism.

**D4, safety engineering.** `specified`, single-source for the figure line, low-overlap world corroboration. Fig 11 :52: "Scoping the habitat to direct contact is how DDT reached the eagles: first-order enumeration, second-order effect, and the residual that mattered was three steps out." A factor that matters may be two or three steps out; first-order enumeration misses it. The Swiss cheese model is the world frame's name for the same arrangement.

**D5, comparative cognition.** `specified`, single-source for the figure line, low-overlap world corroboration. Fig 11 :43: "The habitat is enumerated, never composed." Fig 11 :48: "You do not invent the list. You read it off the world, and what you cannot perceive or measure yet is the residual." Taylor et al. (2012) show a crow inferring an unseen causal agent, confirming that the capacity to infer hidden causes is not uniquely human. This is corroboration from a different evidence pool, not a second witness for the figure.

## 3. Three structural consequences

1. **The same factors under different bonds are a different explanation.** Fig 12 :117: "isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour." Fig 12 :5: "A set of ingredients is not one. The bond is the rest of the information, and it is the half nothing was recording." Necessary, sufficient and threshold readings are different bonds over the same factors (see `06_isomers_and_order.md`).
2. **Standing conditions differ from triggers across uses.** Fig 12 :119: "catalyst. A part that survives its own use. Spent and regenerating parts behave differently across uses, and nothing records which a term is." A single-episode factor list cannot tell them apart.
3. **Agreement between reasoners is not a verdict.** Fig 2 :40: "agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool". Fig 7 :13: "agreement compounds, evidence does not". Two people reading the same vignette and the same norms are one frame wearing two names.

## 4. What it explicitly does not say

- Not a psychological mechanism. The figures do not derive covariation, discounting, the Knobe effect or any named attribution model. Those are world bindings with provenance in `10_source_ledger.csv`.
- Not a verdict on who was responsible in any particular case. No norm, court or victim was consulted.
- Not a claim that any particular factor was present. The habitat is enumerated from the world, not composed (Fig 11 :43).
- Not `grounded`. No study was run, no participant was read, no vignette was scored.
- Not a claim that a global attribution is never useful. It is a lossy summary: Fig 1 :22 says what the loss is, not that the summary is forbidden.

## 5. What would settle it, the ground

1. **Per-factor test:** vary each candidate factor independently in a controlled vignette and score both a global and a per-factor attribution against the manipulation. Refuter 2 in section 6 is the one that fires if the global verdict wins.
2. **Arrangement test:** hold the factor set fixed and change only the bond (necessary, sufficient, threshold). If responsibility judgments do not move, Fig 12 :117 fails here.
3. **Anchor test:** give half the participants an independent anchor (an outcome record or an explicit norm) and see whether the shared-source attribution disappears. If it does not, Fig 2 :29 fails here.
4. **Valence test:** cross the actor's identity with the outcome being explained. If blame tracks a fixed actor trait rather than the residual, Fig 11 :49 fails here.

## 6. Pre-registered refuters (written before the conclusion)

The shape is refused if any of the following holds: (1) causal attributions are invariant across necessary, sufficient and threshold readings of the same factor set; (2) a single global cause judgment predicts responsibility as well as or better than per-factor judgments across several factor sets; (3) responsibility tracks a fixed property of the actor regardless of which residual is in view; (4) naming the shared-source factor is unaffected by giving an independent anchor. Full text and provenance in `01_figure_grounded_audit.md` section 5.

## 7. The honest stop

**Unreached, not unreachable.** Fig 8 :32: "no search, however long, adds an atom". The missing thing here is an instrument, a controlled factor manipulation, not more reading. Fig 6 :32 says instruments are improved from worse instruments already returning something, and the causal-inference world frame already has one: randomized assignment. Importing it is a binding with provenance, and it is charged against independence. The answer stops at the limit of what was checked in this derivation and records the lineage so the stop is checkable. "Indiscernibility is not identity."

## 8. One-paragraph version for outreach

> The figures do not say which attribution rule people use, and this derivation did not run a study to find out. They do say what an adequate answer must look like: cause must be assigned per interacting factor, because a global verdict averages away the only signal that says where to look; responsibility is a relation between an actor and the outcome being explained, not a trait an actor carries; the same factors under a different arrangement are a different explanation, and a standing condition behaves differently from a one-off trigger across uses; the factor that matters can be several steps out, and two people reading the same vignette are not two independent witnesses. Four refuters were written in advance. What is owed is a controlled manipulation of the factors, not more agreement about the story.
