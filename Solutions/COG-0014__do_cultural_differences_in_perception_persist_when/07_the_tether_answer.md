# The Tether's answer: do cultural differences in perception persist under the same task and conditions?

**Mode: `general` on the structural shape, `specified` on the per-domain claims, `gap` on the global empirical answer. Not `grounded`.** This is a simulated derivation checked for internal consistency against the figures (`05_what_shape_means.md` section 7), not a ground reading.

## 1. The residual it answers

The question asks whether an observed between-group difference in a measured percept survives an intervention that makes the task and the immediate conditions the same. The residual is per slot: `R_s = |Γ(b,a) - o'|` for each observer, compared across frames. The intervention touches the action `a` and part of the habitat. It does not touch the belief `b`, the weighting `w`, or the library Γ. Whether it removes the difference therefore depends on where the difference was carried, and that is not one question.

Fig 9 :54, "And when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again." The bundle has at least three parts: is there a difference, is it in the conditions or the carrier, and is the "same task" actually the same task.

## 2. The shape, per domain

**D1, the perceptual frame.** Symbols table :25-26, "Two frames with identical architecture diverge because their histories and their w diverge." With architecture and action squared, a difference in history or weighting persists. Fig 1 :19, "The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated." So there is no global answer: each slot and task gets its own verdict. The route by which history re-enters is a cycle (Fig 12 :121, "cycle. The output re-enters as input. The sign of the loop decides whether it closes, and a chain cannot close by construction."), and the sign of that loop decides persistence versus washout. The sign is a ground question. Verdict: `general` structural, `specified` per slot, triangulated across the Symbols table, Fig 1 and Fig 12.

**D2, the habitat.** Fig 11 :66, "Isolation is not removal of the habitat. It is substitution of one habitat for another." Fig 11 :70, "A test harness is a substituted habitat. Which is why a synthetic solve proves wiring and never capability." A controlled task does not subtract history; it inherits actors the experimenter did not choose. Convergence under the harness proves the wiring responds to the harness. Persistence under the harness shows the carrier is in the observer rather than in the immediate conditions. Verdict: `general`, triangulated across Fig 11 and the Symbols table.

**D3, the method and verification frame.** Fig 3 :7, "it has terms for what things afford: what can be moved, opened, joined, blocked", and Fig 3 :10, "it can say what would count as progress here, in its own terms". "Same task" and "same conditions" are vocabulary and objective claims that require instruments; in most studies they are assumed. Fig 2 :29, "An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks." Fig 2 :37, "Human judgment is the right anchor for is this a good answer for a person, because the question is about people." The measured percept anchors "what was perceived"; it does not anchor "what produced it". Fig 2 :31, "Independence, between two frames, is how little they draw on the same evidence." Verdict: `general`, triangulated across Fig 2, Fig 3 and Fig 8.

The three verdicts are kept separate and never averaged. Over a shared atom set the two observers are isomers, "Same atoms, different bond, different substance" (Fig 12 :117); when the atom sets differ, the weaker functional-group reading applies (Fig 12 :124).

## 3. What it explicitly does not say

- Not "yes, cultural differences always persist". The figures predict persistence only when the difference is carried in terms the intervention does not touch. A genuine habitat change can move the percept (Fig 11 :40, "An improvement that does not change contact changes nothing, however much it improves.").
- Not "no, perception is universal". Universality is an empirical claim about a slot, not a figure verdict. The world literature is split and the figures do not choose a side.
- Not that any named culture, stimulus or task differs in a named way.
- Not any percentage of perception attributable to culture. Fig 9 :32, "you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself".
- Not a causal arrow from "culture" to "percept". The flat formula can name the state and cannot name the route (Fig 12 :111).
- Not `grounded`. No measurement was taken and no instrument was run.

## 4. What would settle it: the ground

The four pre-registered refuters are written in `01` section 5 and repeated in `08`. The instruments that would fire them:

1. **Collapse:** a registered multi-site replication with independently validated task and stimulus equivalence in each habitat, following the APS Registered Replication Reports model ("Direct replications are necessary to estimate the true size of an effect"), with every pre-registered per-slot residual at zero and intervals excluding classical effect sizes.
2. **Carrier:** a source-discriminating instrument showing identical `Γ`, `b` and `w` across histories while the difference remains.
3. **Figure:** a re-census showing the weighting and habitat lines do not govern perception.
4. **Loop sign:** evidence that the re-entrant history loop has the same sign in every habitat and task, which would license a general answer.

World work already points in both directions on particular slots. Amir and Firestone 2025 argue the flagship case is "almost certainly false", reporting that the illusion appears in nonhuman animals, in other modalities, in congenitally blind subjects and under natural scene statistics. Segall, Campbell and Herskovits 1963 reported between-population differences from 1.4% to 20.3% on the same printed task. Both readings are about different parts of the union, and neither settles the shape claim.

## 5. The honest stop

**Unreached, not unreachable.** The answer stops where the instruments stop: no source-discriminating measurement was taken, no task-equivalence link was measured, and no registered multi-site protocol was run. The lineage is recorded so the stop is checkable. Fig 8 :39, "So a search that finds nothing is never a proof of absence." The canonical README: "Indiscernibility is not identity." The global answer is a declared `gap`, which is a verdict, not a silence.

## 6. One-paragraph version for outreach

> The Tether does not say that cultural differences in perception always persist, or that perception is universal. It says the question is bundled: "same task, same conditions" fixes the action and the immediate setting, but it does not fix the observer's history, the weighting between private history and inherited prior, or the library of learned priors. So a difference carried in those terms will persist, while a difference carried in the stimulus or the response measure should wash out. The answer has to be given per perceptual slot, never as a group mean, and "same task" itself needs measuring rather than asserting. The controlled setting is a substituted habitat, not a removal of history, so convergence in a harness proves only that the wiring responds to the harness. The framework returns this shape and four written refuters, not a named culture, a universal direction or a percentage. Which sign the re-entrant history loop has, and where the difference actually lives, are questions for a registered, independent, source-discriminating experiment, not for further argument.
