# Expert verification packet: the Tether on cultural differences in perception

**What this is:** a claim in a form a cross-cultural perception researcher can check. Mode, derivation and refuters are stated before the conclusion, so a reader knows what to look for and what would settle it.

**Mode: `general` on the structural shape, `specified` on the per-domain claims, `gap` on the global empirical answer. Not `grounded`.** No measurement at any task or in any culture is claimed. This is a shape and a route, not a result. It is a simulated derivation checked for internal consistency against the figures, not a ground reading.

## 1. The claim, in one paragraph

The question "do cultural differences in perception persist under the same task and the same conditions?" bundles a conditions question and a carrier question that have different anchors. The intervention fixes the action and the immediate habitat; it does not fix the observer's history, the weighting between private history and inherited prior, or the library of learned priors. A difference carried in those terms therefore persists; a difference carried in the stimulus or the response measure should wash out. Because the residual is indexed per object slot, the answer must be given per slot and per task, never as a between-group mean. "Same task" is itself a vocabulary and objective claim that most studies assume rather than measure, and the controlled setting is a substituted habitat rather than a removal of history. The framework returns this shape and four written refuters, not a named culture, a universal direction or a percentage.

## 2. Three checkable sub-claims

### C1 — framework-internal, checkable by reading the Symbols table and Figure 11

- **Statement:** "Same task, same conditions" does not touch the carrier of a history-borne difference, and isolation substitutes a habitat rather than removing one.
- **Source, verbatim:** Symbols table :25-26, "Two frames with identical architecture diverge because their histories and their w diverge." Fig 11 :66, "Isolation is not removal of the habitat. It is substitution of one habitat for another." Fig 11 :70, "A test harness is a substituted habitat. Which is why a synthetic solve proves wiring and never capability."
- **What to check:** is the weighting term in the framework genuinely untouched by fixing the action and the immediate conditions? If yes, persistence is the structural default for a history-borne difference.
- **Refuter:** an intervention that removes a history term rather than substituting a habitat, with the percept unchanged; or a demonstration that the weighting and habitat lines do not govern perception at all.

### C2 — method-facing, checkable by a cross-cultural researcher against their own protocols

- **Statement:** "same task" and "same conditions" are measured vocabulary and objective links, not properties of the printed instruction, and the measured percept is an anchor for "what" but not for "why".
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 3 :7, "it has terms for what things afford: what can be moved, opened, joined, blocked". Fig 3 :10, "it can say what would count as progress here, in its own terms". Fig 3 :27, "A link with no instrument is not a link that works. It is a link nobody has looked at." Fig 2 :37, "Human judgment is the right anchor for is this a good answer for a person, because the question is about people."
- **What to check:** in a study you know, was task equivalence measured in each group with an instrument independent of the experimenter's vocabulary? If not, the "same task" premise is an assumption. Was the perceptual report used as the anchor for a mechanism claim? If so, the anchor was pushed past what it measures.
- **Refuter:** a protocol that measures both links exactly and still yields a global answer with no per-slot variation; or a source-discriminating instrument that makes the "why" question directly measurable from the same reading.

### C3 — framework-internal and world-facing, checkable by reading Figure 1 and the two literatures

- **Statement:** the two literatures are ranging against different parts of a union; their results must not be averaged into a global "cultures differ somewhat in perception".
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 1 :19, "The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated." Fig 1 :22, "Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average." Fig 9 :54, "And when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again." Fig 8 :50, "Convergent derivation and adopted import look identical in the contents. Only the record of where each came from separates them".
- **What to check:** Amir and Firestone 2025 report that the flagship effect appears across species, modalities and congenital blindness and that "the statistics of natural scenes are sufficient to capture the illusion". Segall, Campbell and Herskovits 1963 reported "the mean fractional misperception of the length of the line segments varied from 1.4% to 20.3%" across populations on the same printed task. Do these bear on the same slot measured with the same anchor and independence? If not, they are two parts of the union.
- **Refuter:** a single registered protocol that settles both literatures on the same slots with the same anchor, so the union collapses to a partition.

## 3. What is explicitly not claimed

- Not that cultural differences always persist, nor that perception is universal. Both are empirical questions about particular slots.
- Not that any named culture, stimulus or task shows a named difference.
- Not that a percentage of perception can be assigned to culture. Fig 9 :32, "you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself".
- Not that "culture" is a seventh primitive. Fig 13 :115, "The set is closed. The arrangements are not. That gap is why import exists."
- Not a causal arrow from culture to percept. Fig 12 :111, "A vocabulary without bonds can name a state and cannot name a route to one."
- Not `grounded`. Nothing here was settled by an experiment.

## 5. What would settle it

1. **Collapse:** a registered, multi-site replication with independently validated task and stimulus equivalence in each habitat, based on a shared preregistered protocol, finds every pre-registered per-slot residual at zero with intervals excluding classical effect sizes.
2. **Carrier:** a source-discriminating instrument shows identical `Γ`, `b` and `w` across histories while the difference remains.
3. **Figure:** a re-census shows the weighting and habitat lines do not govern perception.
4. **Loop sign:** the re-entrant history loop is shown to have the same sign in every habitat and task.

The four refuters were written before the derivation concluded. If any fires, the shape claim is withdrawn or narrowed.
