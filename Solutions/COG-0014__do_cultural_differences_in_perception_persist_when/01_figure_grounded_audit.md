## 0. The claim under audit

The ordinary question bundles three claims that have different anchors:

1. **Difference claim:** observers from different developmental habitats produce different measured percepts on some task.
2. **Invariance claim:** holding the task and the immediate conditions fixed is an intervention that should remove such a difference if the difference is caused by the conditions.
3. **Persistence claim:** if the difference survives that intervention, it is carried by something in the observer, usually named "culture".

The audit asks what the figures say about the *sufficiency of the intervention*, not which culture differs on which stimulus. The latter is a ground question and no measurement was taken.

An answer would look like this, written down and bound before the run (Formula, "Direction"): a statement of which terms in `R_s = |Γ(b,a) - o'|` the intervention touches, which it leaves alone, and under which conditions the difference must persist, must vanish, or remains undecidable. A bare "yes, they persist" binds a term the derivation cannot produce.

## 1. The residual in effect terms

**R (arity, symmetry, scale).**

- **Arity:** two observer frames compared on one task with one stimulus. The stimulus is a shared operand, not a third frame. The comparison of the two residuals is binary.
- **Symmetry:** the identity constraints are symmetric, "same stimulus", "same instructions", "same apparatus" (identity, `≡`). The comparison of outcomes is ordered, "which group shows the larger residual" (comparison, `⋛`), and the perceptual judgment itself is ordered (sequence, `→`, a s). A between-group mean is a symmetric pooling that throws away the ordering. So a difference cannot be read off the atoms; it lives in the bond or arrangement.
- **Scale:** first order inside each observer (perception, belief, action, observation). Second order at the between-group comparison of two settled first-order percepts. Third order when the difference itself becomes an atom in the social frame, for example a stimulus selected because it is supposed to discriminate cultures.

**Dependency order (Fig 3).** Perception (1) before vocabulary (2) before objective (3) before planning (4) before learn and carry (5). Fig 3 :7, "it has terms for what things afford: what can be moved, opened, joined, blocked". Whether two groups have the same task is a vocabulary question. Fig 3 :10, "it can say what would count as progress here, in its own terms". Whether two groups have the same conditions is an objective question. Fig 3 :15, "name what worked, verify it, and carry it to the next problem". History is the fifth link, and it feeds back into the first. Fig 3 :19, "Optimising a link that already works cannot rescue one that does not." Fig 3 :27, "A link with no instrument is not a link that works. It is a link nobody has looked at." The vocabulary and objective links are the ones with no obvious instrument in this literature, which is the structural place where the question breaks.

## 4. Findings

### F1 — Same task and same conditions do not touch the carrier of the difference (decisive, D1)

Symbols table :25-26, the governing line of this audit:

> **"Two frames with identical architecture diverge because their histories and their w diverge."**

And the definition immediately above it, Symbols table :24-25:

> **"How far the belief leans on private history versus inherited prior. w_A + w_B = 1, and w is learnable from the outcomes of past weightings."**

The intervention in the question fixes the architecture and the action. It does not fix `b`, `w`, or Γ. Two observers with the same eyes, the same instructions and the same stimulus can therefore still diverge, because the terms that differed were not among the terms squared. Under the figures, persistence is the default prediction, not an anomaly needing a cultural explanation.

**Mode:** `general` (the structure of the loop), with the per-task application `specified`. **Status:** triangulated across the Symbols table and Fig 11 :39 (different figure, different quantity: capability of agent-and-habitat). **Refuter:** demonstrate a perceptual slot where `b`, `w` and Γ are proven identical across the two histories and a difference still remains at the ground. That would move the carrier out of the terms the figures name.

### F2 — The answer cannot be global; it is indexed per slot (D1)

Fig 1 :19, "The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated." Fig 1 :22, "Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average." Fig 1 :13, "a global R near zero with one live slot residual is a legal state".

A between-group mean over stimuli, tasks and response measures can do exactly what Fig 1 forbids: it can hide a live slot, or manufacture a difference from a few live slots. Therefore "do differences persist" has no single answer at the right level of description. It has one answer per slot, and those answers are not to be averaged into a cultural index.

**Mode:** `general`. **Status:** triangulated across Fig 1 and Fig 11 :43-44 (the habitat is enumerated, never composed), which independently require item-level enumeration. **Refuter:** show that for the proposed domain the residual is genuinely not sliceable, that every slot carries the same sign and magnitude under a fixed transform. Then a global answer is licensed.

### F3 — "Same conditions" is a substituted habitat, not a removal of history (D2)

Fig 11 :66, "Isolation is not removal of the habitat. It is substitution of one habitat for another." Fig 11 :70, "A test harness is a substituted habitat. Which is why a synthetic solve proves wiring and never capability." Fig 11 :67, "Move the residual and you must reproduce everything its goal required, and you inherit the new habitat's actors, which you did not choose."

A laboratory that holds conditions constant has not subtracted the observers' histories. It has placed them in a new habitat while they brought the old one with them in `b`, `w` and Γ. Two outcomes follow, and they are not symmetrical:

- Convergence under the harness shows only that the wiring responds to the harness. It does not show the difference is absent from the agent-and-habitat system.
- Persistence under the harness shows the difference is carried in the observer rather than in the immediate stimulus conditions.

Fig 11 :39, "capability is a property of agent-and-habitat, never of the agent alone", and Fig 11 :40, "An improvement that does not change contact changes nothing, however much it improves."

**Mode:** `general` on the substitution law, `specified` on its application to a lab task. **Status:** triangulated across Fig 11 and the Symbols table (two different figures, low overlap). **Refuter:** an intervention that genuinely removes a history term rather than substituting a habitat, with the percept still unchanged. By Fig 11 :66 that is not an available operation, so a claimed refuter of this kind must show the figure is wrong about isolation, not merely run another between-groups task.

### F4 — "Same task" is a vocabulary and objective question, and it is usually unmeasured (D3)

Fig 3 :7, "it has terms for what things afford: what can be moved, opened, joined, blocked". Fig 3 :10, "it can say what would count as progress here, in its own terms". Fig 3 :24, "Why the middle link is where chains usually break." Fig 3 :27, "A link with no instrument is not a link that works. It is a link nobody has looked at."

Two groups can be given the same printed instruction and be performing different tasks if the affordances and the objective are read differently. The experimenter's dictionary is an emic artifact presented as an etic instrument. World source: Wikipedia "Cross-cultural psychology" (opened 2026-11-22): "the emic approach studies behavior from within the culture, and mostly is based on one culture; the etic approach studies behavior from outside the culture system, and is based on many cultures." A pseudoetic task is an emic task wearing an etic label, so "same task" is an assumption, not a measurement.

**Mode:** `general`. **Status:** triangulated across Fig 3 and the cross-cultural method source (framework and world, low overlap). **Refuter:** measure task equivalence in both groups with an instrument independent of the experimenter's vocabulary, and show it is exact. Until then this is a named open link, not a defect in any particular study.

### F5 — The anchor is the measured percept, not the agreement between groups (D3)

Fig 2 :29, "An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks." Fig 2 :31, "Independence, between two frames, is how little they draw on the same evidence." Fig 2 :35, "Alignment is triangulation, not negotiation. A mirror cannot correct you." Fig 2 :36, "An anchor is legitimate when the question is constitutively about it." Fig 2 :37, "Human judgment is the right anchor for is this a good answer for a person, because the question is about people."

This split matters. For "what did this observer perceive", the observer's judgment is the right anchor, because the question is constitutively about a person. For "what produced that percept", the same judgment is only an instrument, and instruments can be wrong on axes they do not measure. A verbal report of line length is a legitimate answer to the first question and not a verdict about the second.

**Mode:** `general`. **Status:** triangulated across Fig 2 and Fig 8 :50 (convergent derivation versus adopted import look identical in the contents). **Refuter:** a source-discriminating measurement that reads the source of a percept directly and does not merely re-read the percept. Until such an instrument exists, mechanism claims stay `candidate`.

### F6 — The two groups are usually one evidence pool (D3)

Fig 2 :40, "agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool". Fig 8 :50, "Convergent derivation and adopted import look identical in the contents. Only the record of where each came from separates them". Fig 7 :35, "A room's agreement is variation, not a verdict. Selection between rooms is where the ground votes."

Cross-cultural perception studies often present a stimulus drawn from one habitat to all groups. The participants then range against one evidence pool, and the experimenter's "cultural difference" may be the pool talking. Many studies repeating the same stimulus are a room chain, not corroboration: "same closure, handed along; agreement compounds, evidence does not" (Fig 7 :12-13).

**Mode:** `general`. **Status:** triangulated across Fig 2, Fig 7 and Fig 8 (three figures, distinct mechanisms). **Refuter:** demonstrate low overlap between the frames, for example independently constructed stimuli for each habitat with provenance recorded, and show the residual is the same sign. That is convergence at maximum independence, not a shared pool.

### F7 — The question is a union, so one lever leaves the intersection standing

Fig 9 :54, "And when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again." Fig 9 :55, "A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones." Fig 9 :57, "What remains is smaller and it is also categorically different: a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either."

The three claims in section 0 overlap in the stimulus. A lever that holds conditions fixed can test the claim "the difference is caused by the conditions", and it cannot test the claim "the difference is caused by history" at the same time, because the history travels with the observer into the fixed condition. The question must be split before it is searched.

**Mode:** `general`. **Status:** triangulated across Fig 9 and Fig 11 (union law and substitution law). **Refuter:** exhibit a case where "same conditions" is a complete specification of the habitat, including history, language and prior, so the union degenerates to a partition. Fig 11 :66 says this cannot be done by isolation, so the burden of proof is on the claim.

### F8 — No figure line names the perceptual content or the culture (limit, not silence)

Fig 9 :32, "you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself". The canonical README: the framework says "what a missing thing would have to look like (a shape, not a name)". Fig 13 :134, "It cannot say which primitive is missing, because naming it would mean already holding it."

The figures return the shape: a per-slot residual, a weighting between history and prior, a habitat that is substituted rather than removed, and a source-discriminating instrument still owed. They do not return "the culture that differs is X" or "the structure in the slot is Y". Those are bindings from the psychology frame, provenanced as such, or they are names where only a shape is available. No numeric percentage of cultural contribution is derivable.

**Mode:** `general`. **Status:** triangulated across Fig 9 and the canonical README. **Verdict:** `gap` for the global answer and for the list of named differences, recorded as **unreachable at this budget and by this method**, not as silence. Fig 8 :39, "So a search that finds nothing is never a proof of absence."

### F9 — The world literature splits exactly along the union, and that is evidence for the union, not against it

World sources opened 2026-11-22:

- Amir and Firestone, *Is visual perception WEIRD? The Müller-Lyer illusion and the cultural byproduct hypothesis*, Psychological Review, 2025 (issue 2026): "the evidence for cultural variation and its correlation with key cultural variables is in fact highly inconsistent, beset by questionable research practices, and misreported by later discussions." Their abstract also reports that the illusion appears in nonhuman animals, in other sense modalities and in congenitally blind subjects, and that "the statistics of natural scenes are sufficient to capture the illusion".
- Segall, Campbell and Herskovits, *Cultural Differences in the Perception of Geometric Illusions*, Science, 1963, bibliographic record opened via Semantic Scholar; the reported result, read on the Wikipedia Müller-Lyer page, is that "the mean fractional misperception of the length of the line segments varied from 1.4% to 20.3%".
- Howe and Purves, *The Müller-Lyer illusion explained by the statistics of image-source relationships*, PNAS, 2005, opened in full: "the perceptual effects elicited by the Müller-Lyer stimulus and its major variants are correctly predicted by the probability distributions of the possible physical sources underlying the relevant retinal images."
- Wikipedia "Cultural psychology", opened 2026-11-22: "According to Richard Shweder, there has been repeated failure to replicate Western psychology laboratory findings in non-Western settings." Also: "In terms of cognition styles, Chinese tend to perceive image using a holistic view compared to American."

One literature reports large differences under the same printed task; another argues the flagship case is an artifact and the effect is universal. The figures do not choose between them. They say the two literatures are ranging against different objects: one against the stimulus-and-conditions part of the union, one against the history-and-w part, with different anchors and different independence. Averaging them into "cultures differ somewhat in perception" would commit the aggregation error of Fig 1 :22 at the level of whole literatures. Verdicts stay separate.

**Mode:** `specified` for the reading of each source, `general` for the union conclusion. **Status:** world corroboration of the structural verdict, not of either empirical side. **Refuter:** a single registered protocol that settles both literatures on the same slots with the same anchor; then the union would collapse to a partition and F7 would be refuted.

### F10 — The 2025 review is a re-description of the residual, which is the figure-approved move

Fig 9 :27-28, "describe it before you go looking, or any frame you pick will seem to fit". Fig 6 :30, "The description travels. Fails by being sharpened into terms only we can read. Cast it in effect terms."

The classical studies named "culture" first and then looked for a discriminating stimulus. Amir and Firestone re-described the effect in effect terms (does it appear in animals, in other modalities, in the blind, under natural scene statistics?) and got a different verdict. That is the order the figures require, whatever one thinks of the conclusion. Provenance, not agreement, is what separates a convergent derivation from an adopted import (Fig 8 :50).

**Mode:** `general`. **Status:** triangulated across Fig 9 and Fig 6. **Refuter:** show that the effect-term re-description changed the object rather than sharpening the same residual, in which case the two literatures are simply about different things and the union grows rather than collapses. This is recorded as a live design question, not hidden.

## 5. Pre-registered refuter

Written before the conclusion, per the standing instruction that a falsifier written before the run is a design instrument. The shape claim S is:

> S: holding task and conditions fixed is not sufficient to determine whether a between-group perceptual difference persists, because the difference may be carried in terms the intervention does not touch; the answer is therefore per slot, per task and per anchor, and a global yes or no is an ill-bound question.

S is refuted if any of the following fires:

1. **Collapsing refuter.** A registered, multi-site, shared-protocol replication, of the kind described by the Association for Psychological Science Registered Replication Reports ("Direct replications are necessary to estimate the true size of an effect"), independently validates task and stimulus equivalence in each habitat and finds every pre-registered per-slot residual indistinguishable from zero with intervals excluding the classical effect sizes, across all pre-registered tasks. That would show the conditions fully determine the percept and the union collapses.
2. **Carrier refuter.** A source-discriminating instrument shows identical `Γ`, `b` and `w` in the two groups despite different histories, while the percept difference remains. That removes the carrier the figures name.
3. **Figure refuter.** A re-census shows that the Symbols table `w` line and Fig 11's agent-and-habitat line do not govern perception at all, for example because perceptual slots are proven to have no belief term. Then the derivation has no governing figure and becomes a `gap`.
4. **Loop-sign refuter.** The re-entrant history loop of Fig 12 :121 is shown to have the same sign in every measured habitat and task, so that persistence versus washout is not conditional and a general answer is licensed.

If any fires, the shape claim is withdrawn or narrowed, and the earlier reading stays visible in section 8. Until one is run, nothing here may be labelled `grounded`.

## 6. Recommendation

1. Split the question into a conditions question and a carrier question before any search (F7). Do not answer the bundled question.
2. Report results per slot and per task, with the aggregation ban explicit (F2).
3. Measure the vocabulary and objective links, or state that they are unmeasured (F4). An unmeasured "same task" is a guess.
4. Use a measured percept as the anchor for "what", and refuse to treat the same measurement as the anchor for "why" (F5).
5. Buy independence with stimuli and instruments that do not share one evidence pool, and record provenance so convergence can be told from import (F6, F10).
6. Do not send a verdict on any specific culture or task. Send the shape and its refuter (F8, and Fig 4 :22, "Only methods cross up. Only head starts come down.").

### Seven-question recursive derivation (summary)

1. **What is squared by "same conditions"?** The action and the immediate habitat, not `b`, `w` or Γ (Symbols :25-26, Fig 11 :66).
2. **Where is the residual?** Per object slot, never as a group mean (Fig 1 :19, :22).
3. **Is the task the same task?** A vocabulary and objective question with no instrument in most studies (Fig 3 :7, :10, :27).
4. **Is the groups' agreement a witness?** No if they share a stimulus pool; only convergence at low overlap counts (Fig 2 :31, :40, Fig 8 :50).
5. **What is the anchor?** The measured percept for "what"; a source-discriminating instrument still owed for "why" (Fig 2 :36-37).
6. **What carries the difference if it persists?** A re-entrant history term in the cycle, whose sign decides persistence or washout (Fig 12 :121). The sign is a ground question.
7. **What is unreachable?** The named culture, the named slot, the percentage and the global yes or no (Fig 9 :32, Fig 13 :134). Owed: an instrument, not another argument.

## 8. Strains, re-census and updated verdict

- **S1, Fig 13 applied to perceptual content.** First pass tried to read a perceptual prior as one of the six substrate structures. Strain. Re-census: Fig 13 governs the primitives of the loop and their cross-kind composition; it does not name the content of a learned prior. Declined as a governing line, kept visible. The relation of "culture" to `w` is a binding from the social-science frame, marked `specified`, ungrounded, provenance required.
- **S2, persistence versus contact.** First pass read the Symbols table line as "differences always persist". Re-census: Fig 11 :40 says an improvement that does not change contact changes nothing, so a genuine habitat change can move the percept. No contradiction after the carrier is named; the two figures govern different quantities (weighting versus contact).
- **S3, the two literatures.** World conflict, returned to census. Re-census assigns Amir and Firestone to the conditions-and-measurement part of the union and the classical cross-cultural results to the history-and-prior part, with different anchors and different independence. The verdict was not flipped.
- **S4, "shape, not a name" overstatement.** Corrected in `05` section 6: the rule governs a missing primitive, not the naming of an already held atom. Naming a weighting or a habitat binding is provenance, not the invention of a seventh term.
- **After re-census: no hidden strain.** Four strains recorded, two dissolved by re-census, one declined as governing, one narrowed and kept visible.

**Updated verdict.** The intervention does not touch the carrier, so persistence is the default structural prediction when the difference is in `b`, `w` or Γ. Washout is the expected result when the difference was in the conditions or the measurement. Which of these is true for any given task is a ground question, and the figures refuse to answer it in advance. The honest output is a per-slot split of the union, a pre-registered refuter, and an instrument owed. Nothing here is `grounded`.
