# Outreach draft: check the residual split before you decide about the framework

**Use:** one to one, or as a cover letter attached to a submission through the public venues in `03_public_contact_points.csv`. **Not** for bulk send.

**Hard precondition:** do not send until the task-equivalence and anchor distinctions in F4 and F5 of `01_figure_grounded_audit.md` are stated plainly. If the residual is not correctly split, the two-stage offer collapses on contact.

---

**Subject:** One question about "same conditions" that I would like you to refute

> Hi [name],
>
> I am working on a small, checkable claim about cross-cultural perception research. I am not asking you to read a theory first, and I am not asking you to agree with anything. If the claim does not survive, there is nothing else to look at.
>
> **The residual (`general` on the shape, `specified` on the per-task application):** the question "do cultural differences in perception persist under the same task and the same conditions?" treats "same conditions" as if it squared the observer. It does not. There are at least three variables in the perceptual loop that the intervention leaves alone: the observer's private history, the weighting between private history and inherited prior, and the library of learned perceptual priors. A difference carried in any of those will survive a change of immediate conditions. A difference carried in the stimulus or the measurement will not. The figures also require the residual to be indexed per perceptual slot, so a group mean can hide a live difference or invent one.
>
> A second point: "same task" is not given by the printed instruction. It requires a measured vocabulary and objective link in each group. In most studies that link is assumed, not measured.
>
> **What would refute the shape claim, written down before I sent this:**
> 1. A registered multi-site replication with independently validated task and stimulus equivalence in every habitat finds every pre-registered per-slot residual at zero, with intervals excluding the classical effect sizes, across all pre-registered tasks.
> 2. A source-discriminating instrument shows identical beliefs, weightings and perceptual libraries in the two groups despite different histories, while the percept difference remains.
> 3. A re-census shows that the weighting and habitat lines of the framework do not govern perception at all, so the derivation has no governing figure.
> 4. The re-entrant history loop is shown to have the same sign in every habitat and task, so persistence versus washout is not conditional.
>
> If any holds, I withdraw the claim. A one-line "this fails because..." is more useful to me than agreement. If you would rather not engage, no reply is needed.
>
> **What I am not claiming:** that any particular culture perceives better, worse, or differently in a named way. That a specific illusion is or is not cultural. That a percentage of perception can be assigned to culture. That the framework settles an empirical question. None of those follow from the shape, and I will not assert them.
>
> **If it survives, and only then:** here are the sources. The figures are the authority, not my summary of them.
> - Verbatim figure text with line numbers (local framework extract): `FRAMEWORK/reference/03-FIGURES_TEXT.md`
> - Canonical usage rule: `FRAMEWORK/reference/FIGURES_README_canonical.md`
> - The eight-step loop: `FRAMEWORK/figures/THE_FORMULA.md`
> - The published debate this bears on: Amir and Firestone, "Is visual perception WEIRD? The Müller-Lyer illusion and the cultural byproduct hypothesis", Psychological Review, DOI 10.1037/rev0000549
>
> **If you want to use the machinery:** the formula is in the figures link. Run it on a problem you know well and can check against a ground. Write down what would refute your result *before* you run it, and state the mode of what you get. The framework's own rule is that a loop cannot check the direction of its own question, and its own essays warn that outputs can be contrived to fit the formula. A reported failure is more useful to me than a reported success.
>
> — [name]

## The three stages

1. **Stage 1:** send the residual split and the four refuters. Ask what the recipient would count as a failure. Record their verdict before showing any framework material.
2. **Stage 2, opt-in only:** if they are curious, offer the figure links and the published debate. Their later agreement is never pooled as corroboration, because by then they hold the interiors (Fig 2 :40).
3. **Stage 3:** offer the formula as a method to reuse, with the instruction that each user writes their own refuter, names their own ground and states their own mode.
