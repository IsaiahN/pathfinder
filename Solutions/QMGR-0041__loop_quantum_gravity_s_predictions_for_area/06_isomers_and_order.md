## DERIVATION

### Q1 - Are area and volume isomers?

- Fig 12 :117: **"isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."**
- Both slots are built from the same held atoms: Space (a finite region), Spectrum (an ordering so more and less can be said) and Network (the relations that make a region finite and bounded).
- They differ in arrangement. Area orders a two-dimensional boundary; volume orders a three-dimensional region. Same atoms, different bond structure, different measured substance. Under Fig 12's definition they are **isomers**, not two spellings of one term.
- This is not a chemistry analogy imported for colour. Fig 12 :95-98 records that isomerism was forced into chemical notation by Liebig and Wöhler's substances with identical composition and different properties, and named by Berzelius in 1830. The figure itself uses the case to define the term.
- **Falsifier, written before the run:** if either slot requires an atom the other does not (for example, if volume needs a seventh primitive, or if area can be written with no Spectrum), the isomer claim fails and the weaker functional-group reading below stands instead.

### Q3 - Is the minimum gap a kind or a specific member?

### Q4 - Which analogues are licensed, and which are declined?

- **Licensed with care: electron spin.** The Stanford Encyclopedia entry (opened 2026-11-23) says of LQG: *"Thus, like electron spins, they can only take certain values."* This transfers the structural relation (a bounded set of allowed values), not the physics. Mark `general` for the analogue, `specified` for the LQG claim it illustrates.
- **Licensed with care: atomic spectra.** The word "spectrum" in the framework is a structure defined as *"ordering, so more and less can be said"* (Fig 13 :19-20). Discrete operator eigenvalues are an ordered set with gaps. The mapping is structural and is recorded as a binding from the physics frame, not as an identity between two different senses of the word.
- **Declined: the regular pixel grid.** The figures license an ordering with a threshold. They do not license a uniform lattice with equal spacing. A grid is an extra commitment imported from an analogy, and Fig 8 :50-52 says convergent derivation and adopted import look identical in the contents. Unless a source pays for equal spacing, the grid is refused.
- **Declined: the discrete-versus-continuum dichotomy as a primitive choice.** Fig 13 :124 says discreteness is what a composition leaves behind. Asking which of the two is fundamental is a category error inside this framework. The continuum was removed as a seventh member (Fig 13 :43-44).

**Falsifier for the analogue section:** if a figure line is found that requires a uniform lattice rather than a threshold ordering, the declined pixel grid must be reinstated and this section corrected.

## CONCLUSION

Area and volume are **isomers** under Fig 12 :117: same held atoms, different arrangement, different substance. The derivation runs in the dependency order of Fig 3, with the threshold gap at step 4 and the coefficient still unreached at step 5. The gapped spectrum is a **functional group** (Fig 12 :124); the Immirzi-dependent coefficient is a qualifier from the physics frame. Electron spin and atomic spectra are licensed as structural analogues only; the regular pixel grid and the fundamental continuum-versus-discrete dichotomy are declined. The isomer claim, the order claim and the functional-group claim all have stated falsifiers and none has a ground reading.
