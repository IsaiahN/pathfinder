## Wider literature that bears on the question

1. **The kinematic versus physical observable disagreement** is the live ground question. Dittrich and Thiemann show in simplified models that a kinematic discrete spectrum need not survive gauge-invariant completion; Rovelli replies that the counterexample is a pathology not present in gravity. The Stanford Encyclopedia entry independently records the same open status: the result *"has been shown to hold only for operators on the kinematical Hilbert space, that is, for gauge-variant quantities."* This is one issue discussed from three angles, not three independent witnesses. Record the overlap.
2. **Black-hole entropy is a constraint, not an observation.** The Stanford entry says LQG gets the entropy right for generic black holes *"but only up to an overall constant"* and that the most notable test involves Hawking radiation, *"a phenomenon which has never been observed."* It also records Curiel's warning about treating a theorem as empirical evidence. Do not present entropy counting as a measured confirmation.
3. **Lorentz bounds constrain models, not the discreteness shape.** The Fermi result pushes a linear-in-energy speed variation beyond the Planck scale. The Stanford entry records the Rovelli-Speziale argument that a minimal length does not imply Lorentz violation. So a Lorentz bound is not a refutation of discreteness. Fig 2 :29: *"An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks."* The bound settles a different question.
4. **The continuum recovery is owed.** The Stanford entry calls the low-energy limit *"a major challenge of loop quantum gravity."* This is the D2/D3 bridge and the place where the shape claim can actually fail.
