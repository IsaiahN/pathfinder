# Outreach draft: check the shape before you decide about the prediction

**Use:** one-to-one, or as a cover letter attached to a submission through the public venues in `03_public_contact_points.csv`. **Not** for bulk send.

**Hard precondition:** do not send until a reader can state the refuter in their own words. The output must survive validation or the two-stage offer collapses on contact. The LQG coefficient is withheld at stage 1 by design.

---

**Subject:** One checkable claim about discrete area and volume, before the theory

> Hi [name],
>
> I have one specific output I would like you to check against what you already know. I am not asking you to read the framework first, and I am not asking you to agree with it. If the output does not survive, there is nothing else to look at.
>
> **The residual, in effect terms.** A finite region of space has two measured slots, area and volume. Classical general relativity treats both as continuum values on a smooth manifold. Loop quantum gravity derives operators whose spectra are discrete. The question is what kind of thing a gap in a geometry spectrum is: a new primitive, or a remainder left by a composition.
>
> **The output (`specified`, not `grounded`).** The framework's minimum set has six terms in two kinds, states (space, time, energy) and structures (spectrum, gradient, network), and composition runs across the two kinds, never within one. In that set the continuum is explicitly not a primitive: it was removed for failing the generating-set test, as a spectrum without gaps or smoothness over space and time. The same figure names discreteness as something a composition leaves behind, not a seventh member. So the shape any candidate answer must have is: a gapped ordering at one scale, with a smooth continuum that has to be derived as a limit rather than declared as a floor. This is a shape, not a measurement.
>
> **What I am not claiming at this stage.** I am not asking you to accept the numerical value of a minimum area or volume, or its dependence on the Immirzi parameter. Those come from the LQG frame, not from this derivation. Convergent derivation and adopted import look identical in the contents and only the record separates them, so I am holding the coefficient back until you have recorded a verdict on the shape.
>
> **What would refute the shape claim:**
> 1. deriving discreteness or the continuum as a seventh irreducible primitive from the six-term set;
> 2. showing a composition of two states or two structures that must be admitted with no intervening opposite-kind term;
> 3. a settled, gauge-invariant area or volume observable with a continuous spectrum in the relevant regime;
> 4. a direct Planck-scale geometry measurement returning a smooth continuum at every resolution where the shape predicts a gap.
>
> I wrote these down before sending this. If any one holds, I withdraw the affected claim. A one-line "this fails because..." is more useful to me than agreement. If you would rather not engage, no reply is needed.
>
> **If it survives, and only then:** I will send the published LQG derivation and the sources below. The figures are the authority, not my summary of them.
> - Rovelli and Smolin, "Discreteness of area and volume in quantum gravity", Nucl. Phys. B442 (1995) 593, https://arxiv.org/abs/gr-qc/9411005
> - Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy, "Quantum Gravity", section 3.2 and 4.1, https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/quantum-gravity/
> - Figures, formula and canonical usage rule, local framework copy at `FRAMEWORK/reference/03-FIGURES_TEXT.md`
>
> **If you want to use the machinery:** the formula is in the figures link. Run it on a problem you know well and can check against a ground. Write down what would refute your result *before* you run it, and state the mode of what you get. A reported failure is more useful to me than a reported success.
