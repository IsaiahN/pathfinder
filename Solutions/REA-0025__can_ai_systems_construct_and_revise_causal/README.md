## The residual in effect terms

An agent holds a library `Γ`, a belief `b`, a slot-indexed residual `R`, and an action `a`. Observation supplies `o'`, what happened. Intervention is an action that changes a variable in the world and therefore changes what `o'` can be. The question asks whether acting on the world is required to move causal-slot residuals to zero, or whether passive observation suffices. Two sub-skills are named. **Construct** means compose a new causal term, which in the figures is the ordered production bond. **Revise** means refuse a held causal term, which in the figures is negation, not reweighting. The honest answer must say what intervention buys that observation cannot, what revision costs, and where the system's own claim stops.

## The sought-for shape, bound as an ordinary term (formula step "Direction")

Before the run I write down what an answer would look like, so the preference can accrue a residual like any other term (Fig 5 :65). The shape I am looking for has four parts: (1) a loop in which acting is a first-class operator and the ground is the only metric; (2) a distinction between the ordered production bond and the sequence or association bond, so causation is not reducible to prediction; (3) a refusal operator, so a held causal claim can lose standing to be composed over; (4) a seat for the ground that does not update, with capability located in agent and habitat together rather than in the model alone. An answer that returns a named architecture, a specific accuracy number, or a claim that observation alone settles an open equivalence class fails this shape and is refused.

## What is deliberately not here

- No personal emails of named individuals, and no ranking of people by likelihood of agreement.
- No bulk-send plan, and no claim that agreement from a shared evidence pool is corroboration.
- No claim that this simulated derivation settles the question it reports on.

Three reasons, in the figures' own words. Fig 2 :35: "Alignment is triangulation, not negotiation. A mirror cannot correct you." Fig 2 :40: "disqualifying for a verdict: agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool". Fig 10 :42: "A verifier that can reconstruct the claim cannot verify it." Selecting recipients for receptiveness manufactures exactly the correlation that makes their agreement worthless. A reply to the first contact is a conversation, never corroboration.
