# Expert verification packet: intervention and causal model learning

**What this is:** a claim in a form a researcher in causal inference, causal discovery, or machine learning can check. Mode, derivation, and refuters are stated before the conclusion, so the reader knows what to look for and what would settle it.

**Mode: `specified` on the shape and per-domain verdicts, `general` on quoted structural rules. Not `grounded`.** No measurement is claimed and no AI system is named as settled.

**Status of this packet:** a simulated derivation checked for internal consistency against the figures, not a ground reading. It cannot settle the question it reports on.

## 1. The claim, in one paragraph

Acting is structurally required to settle a causal slot, not merely useful. An agent that only observes runs an incomplete loop, because the residual is indexed per object slot and an average prediction score can hide a live slot. Cause is represented by an ordered production bond that intervention discriminates from association. Revising a causal model is a refusal, not a reweight, so the reach falls when a term loses standing to be composed over. Capability belongs to the agent and the habitat it can act on together. The figures return this shape and stop; they do not name an architecture, a benchmark, or a system. What is offered is the shape, refuters written in advance, and the instruments that would close it.

## 2. Three checkable sub-claims

### C1. Framework-internal, checkable by reading Figure 1 and Figure 12
- **Statement:** acting is a first-class loop operator and cause is an ordered bond, so observation alone cannot discharge a causal slot.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 1 :9 "perceive · plan · act · predict"; Fig 1 :23 "The ground enters as the only metric."; Fig 12 :77 "A produces B; B did not exist before"; Fig 12 :111 "A vocabulary without bonds can name a state and cannot name a route to one."
- **What to check:** is the action operator in that loop world-directed rather than internal bookkeeping, and is the production bond ordered rather than symmetric? If the answer to either is no, the claim fails.
- **Refuter:** derive the ordered production relation from a symmetric relation over the same variables, without an intervention and without an imported assumption, or show that the action does not change what the ground returns.

### C2. Framework-internal, checkable by reading Figure 5 and the Operators table
- **Statement:** revision is negation, not subtraction or a parameter change. A refusal adds a term that removes the held claim's standing to be composed over, so the reach falls while the record grows.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 5 :53 "refuted: a term we hold is wrong; a term is owed that says so"; Operators table :58 "a refutation is a negation, which is why a vocabulary without it can only accumulate"; Fig 12 :82 "A with B removed or absent"; Fig 6 :42 "A refutation adds a term that refuses; it removes nothing, and the reach falls because something was added rather than because anything was deleted."
- **What to check:** is the distinction between subtraction and negation actually drawn in the source, and does a refusal change what may be composed over? If a score change can do the same work, the claim is too strong.
- **Refuter:** exhibit a system with no negation operator that withdraws a held causal claim so that the claim loses standing to be composed over, with no refusal term added.

### C3. World-facing, checkable against the published causal-learning literature
- **Statement:** the literature instantiates the discriminating role of intervention, but it does not test the full shape in an unauthored habitat.
- **What to check:** He and Geng (JMLR 2008) collect further information from experiments with external interventions; Hauser and Bühlmann (JMLR 2012) and Yang, Katcoff and Uhler (ICML 2018) define finer interventional equivalence classes; Méndez-Molina et al. (PMLR 2022) and Sauter et al. (AAMAS 2024) learn intervention policies; Sobel and Sommerville (Frontiers in Psychology 2010) find that acting first beats observing first for four-year-olds. Do those characterisations hold, and is any of them a test of capability rather than wiring?
- **Refuter:** a published experiment showing a system settling a causal slot from a single observational distribution with no functional-form assumption, no prior causal knowledge, and no import, or a deployment in an unauthored habitat that closes a pre-specified causal slot and keeps the ground paying.

## 3. What is explicitly not claimed

- Not a named architecture, algorithm, benchmark, or accuracy number. Fig 9 :32 "you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself".
- Not that observation is useless. It provides slots and the first residual; only its sufficiency is refused.
- Not that every structure update is a revision. A posterior shift without refusal is a correction.
- Not that observational identification results are wrong. Those that rely on functional-form assumptions import an atom, which Fig 6 permits at a cost.
- Not that a synthetic solve proves capability. Fig 11 :70 "A test harness is a substituted habitat. Which is why a synthetic solve proves wiring and never capability."
- Not grounded. No measurement was taken for this packet.
- Not corroborated by agreement. Six world sources share the Pearl or Markov-evidence pool; one psychology pair is low overlap. Their agreement is noted, never pooled as a second witness.

## 5. What would settle it

A ground reading, not a further argument. From *The Hard Problem is About Source*, an instrument that reads green "cannot comment on the source"; the spectrometer settled it. Here the equivalent questions are:

1. Does an agent acting on an unauthored habitat close a causal slot that observation left open, with the slot and residual written down before the run?
2. Can a held causal relation be shown to lose standing to be composed over through a refusal, rather than only changing weight?
3. Does an agent-only change ever move the ground without changing contact? If yes, Fig 11 :72 makes the capability claim refuted.

## 6. What an expert can check in twenty minutes

1. Read Fig 1 :9 and :23, Fig 12 :77 and :111, Fig 5 :53, Fig 12 :82, and Fig 11 :70. Six short passages.
2. Check C1 against what you know about the identification of causal structure: does interventional data do something observational data cannot, in the sense stated?
3. Check C2 against your own practice: when a model is revised, is a relation refused, or only reweighted?
4. Name one experiment in an unauthored habitat that would close a pre-specified causal slot, or say why none is possible.
5. Send the refutation. A failure is more useful than an endorsement.
