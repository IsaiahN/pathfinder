# Outreach draft: check the shape claim before you decide about the framework

**Use:** one-to-one, or as a cover note attached to a submission through the public venues in
`03_public_contact_points.csv`. **Not** for bulk send. Replace `[your name]` before use.

**Hard precondition:** do not send until the three sub-claims in `08_expert_verification_packet.md`
have been checked by someone outside this derivation. The offer collapses on contact if the

**Subject:** One checkable claim about learning from policy failure, before any theory

> Hi [name],
>
> I have one specific claim I would like you to check against what you already know. I am not
> asking you to read the framework first, and I am not asking you to agree with it. If the claim
> does not survive, there is nothing else to look at.
>
> **The claim (`specified`, not `grounded`):** an institution learns from a policy failure only if
> four things are present. First, the outcome measure must be able to change its answer when the
> institution pushes on it; if it cannot, it is not a measure of the failure, it is a mirror.
> Second, whoever holds the measurement must be outside the slice being graded, or must declare the
> stake that cannot be removed. Third, the failure must stay indexed to the policy, place and
> population that produced it; a programme average is how the live signal disappears. Fourth, the
> institution must have a way to declare a held belief false, and that refusal must come before it
> mints a new initiative. An institution that can only adjust, add and report has a vocabulary that
> can be corrected and cannot carry the correction.
>
> I am not claiming that transparency alone does this. A room's agreement is variation, not a
> verdict, and a mirror cannot correct you. The next room has to differ from the last one and match
> the residual it surfaced.
>
> **What would refute it.** I wrote these down before writing the claim:
> 1. an institution corrects its predictions against a measure that changes when it is pushed,
>    with its own credibility as the only witness;
> 2. an institution whose measurement keeper is the measured party, with a reputation to protect
>    in the outcome, produces ground-paid corrections with no independent seat anywhere;
> 3. an institution with no way to refuse a held premise prevents recurrence while keeping that
>    premise available as a base for new policy;
> 4. an institution that carries only conclusions between policy cycles, with no method and no
>    measured round-trip gap, produces a genuinely new correct prediction.
>
> If any holds, I withdraw the corresponding part. A one-line "this fails because..." is more
> useful to me than agreement. If you would rather not engage, no reply is needed.
>
> **If it survives, and only then:** the sources are below. The figures are the authority, not my
> summary of them.
> - Verbatim figure text with line numbers (local framework copy): `FRAMEWORK/reference/03-FIGURES_TEXT.md`
> - Canonical usage rule: `FRAMEWORK/reference/FIGURES_README_canonical.md`
> - The eight-step loop and mode rule: `FRAMEWORK/figures/THE_FORMULA.md`
>
> **If you want to use the machinery:** the formula is in the figures link. Run it on a problem you
> know well and can check against a ground. Write down what would refute your result *before* you
> run it, and label the mode of what you get. The framework's own rule is that a loop cannot check
> the direction of its own question, so a reported failure is more useful to me than a reported
> success.
>
> - [your name]

## Two-stage offer, stated plainly

1. **Stage 1.** Send the claim and its four refuters. Ask what the recipient would count as a
   failure. Record that verdict before showing any theory.
2. **Stage 2, opt-in only.** If they are curious, offer the three local links. Later agreement is
   never pooled as corroboration, because once they hold the framework they share its evidence
   pool (Fig 2 :40 - *"agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an
   evidence pool"*).
3. **Stage 3.** Offer the formula as a method to reuse, with the instruction that each user writes
   their own refuter and states their own mode. Fig 4 :22 permits sending a generator.

**Hard precondition repeated:** stage 1 must survive. As this package stands, stage 1 is a
simulated derivation checked for internal consistency, not a ground reading. It states a shape to
test. It is not a test.
