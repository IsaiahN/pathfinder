## DERIVATION

### Q1 - Are concealment and repetition isomers?

Fig 12 :117: **"isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."**

Write the two failures over the same six atoms `{Γ, b, R, seat, channel, method}`:

- **Concealment:** `R → (channel degraded) → seat never receives R`. The bond from `R` to `seat`
  is broken by subtraction of the channel. The library Γ is unchanged, the belief `b` is unchanged,
  and the seat never sees the residual.
- **Repetition:** `R → seat receives R → (method replaced by recording) → Γ replays past a`.
  Here the channel works. The bond from `R` to a changed library is replaced by a bond from the
  recording to the action, so the residual arrives and produces no change in the reach.

Same atoms, different bonds, different behaviour: one produces silence, the other produces
familiar action. Under Fig 12 :117 they are **isomers**. This is not a metaphor: the distinction is
invisible if you record only "the institution had a failure report and did not change" and decisive
in what you must repair. Repairing a channel does nothing for a replay failure, and installing a
refusal operator does nothing for a broken channel. This is the structural reason for F1's union
finding, reached by a different route (Fig 12 rather than Fig 9). Low overlap between routes, so
triangulated within the framework, though still one evidence pool.

**Falsifier, written before the run:** if the same set of repairs clears both failures in every
case, including the compound case where both conditions bind, the isomer claim fails and the two
are one condition. Fig 9 :55-56 predicts the compound case will survive a single lever.

### Q2 - What order are the mechanisms in, and what does the order buy?

Fig 3 :20-21: **"The chain is ordered by dependency, not by importance. Each link consumes what the one above produces, so effort spent above the break arrives at a step that cannot use it."**
Fig 3 :19: **"Optimising a link that already works cannot rescue one that does not."**

Dependency order from the residual:

1. **Perception (F2, F4).** Is `R` arriving at a seat, per slot? If the channel is dead, nothing
   downstream can be diagnosed. Fig 3 :22-23: *"A step whose input never arrives cannot be
   diagnosed, only its predecessor can."*
2. **Seat and refusal (F3, F5).** Can the seat range against a ground it does not own, and can it
   refuse a held term? Formula step 2 puts REFUTED before mint.
3. **Selection (F7).** Is the next room different from the last, with low overlap?
4. **Carry (F6, F8).** Does the correction cross as a method with provenance, and is `R_T`
   measured?
5. **Direction (F9).** Was the desired shape bound before the run? This one is a discipline that
   runs across all four steps, not a fifth link. Fig 5 :65: *"The remedy is a discipline rather than
   a branch: make the sought-for shape an ORDINARY TERM."*

What the order buys: it makes a failed installation locatable. An institution with step 4 but no
step 1 has excellent after-action templates fed by a dead channel. Fig 3 :29-30: *"Before asking
whether a system is good, ask which link it currently stops at, and whether the answer was
measured or assumed."* The order is a diagnostic, not a to-do list ranked by importance.

**Falsifier:** if installing carry (step 4) before perception (step 1) produced ground-paid
learning in a case where the channel was dead, the dependency order in Fig 3 would not govern this
domain, and the census would be wrong. Record, do smooth.

### Q3 - Which of Fig 12's four structures actually appears?

Fig 12 :116: **"Four structures a compositional system exhibits, and each names something the flat form cannot write."**

- **Isomer (Fig 12 :117).** Concealment and repetition, Q1. Present and load-bearing.
- **Catalyst (Fig 12 :119): *"catalyst. A part that survives its own use. Spent and regenerating parts behave differently across uses, and nothing records which a term is."*** Present. An oversight seat that survives its own use can become a ground with a reputation to protect (Fig 10 :93), which disqualifies its keeper. A seat that regenerates (low overlap, renewed independence, Fig 10 :80-81) keeps working. The flat record says only "there is an oversight body"; it does not record which kind. This is why the figure's warning applies directly here.
- **Cycle (Fig 12 :121): *"cycle. The output re-enters as input. The sign of the loop decides whether it closes, and a chain cannot close by construction."*** Present. A reporting system whose output (the incident report) re-enters as the input to the next assessment can close a learning loop or a blame loop. The sign is set by whether reporting is protected (Reason, BMJ 2000, world corroboration) or punished. A chain cannot close by construction, so an institution that only hands reports upward has a chain, not a loop.
- **Functional group (Fig 12 :124): *"functional group. A substructure whose presence fixes the outcome. The group says what kind; the qualifier says which member."*** Present, and it is the correct reading of the "keeper outside the graded slice" property. The group (a keeper outside the graded slice) fixes the kind; the qualifier (court, auditor, ombud, open data, peer frame) says which member. The framework gives the group, not the qualifier. This is the structural statement of the "shape, not a name" limit for this question.

All four appear. None is an unused analogy, but only isomer and functional group do derivational
work in the main verdict; catalyst and cycle are confirmations that explain why a named body can
rot and why a reporting chain can fail to close.

## CONCLUSION

Concealment and repetition are **isomers** over the same institutional atom set: same atoms,
different bonds, different behaviour, and a compound case that a single lever does not clear. The
mechanisms are ordered by dependency (perception, seat and refusal, selection, carry), with the
direction discipline running across all four; the order is a diagnostic for where an installation
stopped. All four Fig 12 structures appear. Functional group is the correct structural reading of
"a keeper outside the graded slice": the framework names the kind, and the real body that fills it
is a provenanced binding, never a derivation.
