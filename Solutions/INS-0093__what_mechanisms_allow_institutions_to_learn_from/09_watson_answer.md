## QUESTION

Decide, in effect terms, what the Tether says about the mechanisms that let an institution learn
from a policy failure instead of concealing or repeating it. Option A: answer with a single lever,
usually transparency or more reporting. Option B: split the question into its conditions, derive a
shape per domain, and state what would break each claim. What each leads to: A hands a filter to a
checker as if it were a witness, and a mirror cannot correct you (Fig 2 :35); B hands a checkable
shape, six written refuters, and ground tests that a reader outside the institution can run.

## DOMAIN CENSUS

R by arity, symmetry and scale, then one line per domain with actors / currency / anchor, partition
or union, and order by dependency.

- **R (the residual).** The gap between what an institution's library predicts and what the world
  returned, indexed per policy, place and population. Arity: two conditions, so a **union**
  compound. Symmetry: not symmetric across slots; a programme average throws away the signal
  (Fig 1 :22). Scale: first order inside one policy cycle, second order across cycles where a
  settled correction becomes a term, third order at the seat that checks the checker.
- **Domain 1, the policy frame.** Actors: officials, belief `b`, library Γ of policies. Currency:
  prediction against observation. Anchor: the outcome, if the frame ranges against it; its own
  credibility if it does not. Question type: partition at the boundary between frame and ground.
- **Domain 2, the ground.** Actors: affected population, counter, measurement, market, patient
  outcome, experiment. Currency: `o'`, what actually happened. Anchor: the outcome itself, which
  does not change because a frame thinks otherwise (Symbols :104). Question type: the anchor that
  settles all other domains.
- **Domain 3, the verification seat.** Actors: auditor, court, open data, peer frame, confidential
  reporting channel. Currency: residuals surfaced and conventions authored, never verdicts or
  atoms. Anchor: the reachability of the ground (Fig 10 :29). Question type: the seat that arranges
  refusal.
- **Domain 4, the transmission frame.** Actors: methods, head starts, recordings, the membrane.
  Currency: what crosses between cycles. Anchor: the round-trip gap `R_T` (Symbols :77). Question
  type: the crossing that decides learning versus repetition.
- **Order by dependency (Fig 3).** Perception, then seat and refusal, then selection between
  rooms, then carry. Formula step 2 puts REFUTED before mint. The direction discipline runs across
  all four steps rather than forming a fifth link.

Strain test applied: Fig 10 :24 (*"the ground does not decay, the channel does"*) versus Fig 11 :73
(*"The habitat is not the ground. The habitat is where actors come from and it updates; the ground
is what settles, and it does not."*) is not a strain after re-census. The ground is the outcome;
the habitat is the surrounding actors and conditions. Different objects, same rule. See
`01_figure_grounded_audit.md` §6.

## WORLD (secondary sources read)

World sources are corroboration only and never decide a verdict. Each is given with field, author
and venue and year, quote, what it predicts here, and its status.

- **Field, organizational learning theory. Author and venue and year:** Chris Argyris and Donald
  Schön, 1978, definition as quoted by Mohamed Amine Chatti, Matthias Jarke and Ulrik Schroeder,
  *Encyclopedia of the Sciences of Learning*, Springer, 2012. Quote: "learning that results in a
  change in the values of theory-in-use, as well as in its strategies and assumptions." Prediction:
  institutions that only change actions will repeat the governing premise; corroborates T3 on
  refusal. Status: **corroborates**. Overlap: medium with T3; the figure adds the unary operator and
  the refusal-before-mint ordering.
- **Field, human factors and patient safety. Author and venue and year:** James Reason, *BMJ*,
  2000;320:768-770. Quote: "Effective risk management depends crucially on establishing a
  reporting culture." Prediction: without a protected channel and a just culture, failures go
  unreported and the residual never arrives; corroborates T1 and T2. Status: **corroborates**.
  Overlap: medium.
- **Field, sociology of organizations. Author and venue and year:** Diane Vaughan, *The Challenger
  Launch Decision*, University of Chicago Press, 1996 (enlarged edition 2015). Quote: "NASA
  insiders, when repeatedly faced with evidence that something was wrong, normalized the deviance
  so that it became acceptable to them." Prediction: a frame can reinterpret its own anomalies
  until it cannot see them, which is Fig 2 :34 coherent drift; corroborates T1 and T2. Status:
  **corroborates**. Overlap: low, so this is the most useful witness.
- **Field, organizational psychology. Author and venue and year:** Karl E. Weick and Kathleen M.
  Sutcliffe, *Managing the Unexpected*, 2001, Ross School of Business summary page 2003. Quote:
  "Too often, success narrows perceptions, breeds overconfidence in current practices and
  squelches opposing viewpoints." Prediction: past success is itself a channel killer, which is
  Fig 4 :26; corroborates T3. Status: **corroborates**. Overlap: medium.
- **Field, cognitive psychology. Author and venue and year:** Gary Klein, *Harvard Business
  Review*, 2007 (method page at gary-klein.com). Quote: "team members assume that the project they
  are planning has just failed, and then generate plausible reasons". Prediction: binding the
  failure shape before the run makes it refutable; corroborates T3 on direction. Status:
  **corroborates**. Overlap: low.
- **Field, social psychology and organizational behaviour. Author and venue and year:** Dennis F.
  Gioia and Henry P. Sims Jr., *Journal of Applied Social Psychology*, 1985;15(6):547-563. Quote:
  "Self-serving attributional bias is the tendency for a subordinate to attribute personal
  (internal) cause for successful performance but to assign cause for failures to external
  factors." Prediction: external attribution lets an institution keep a failed premise composable;
  corroborates T3. Status: **corroborates**. Overlap: medium.
- **Field, business and management, empirical. Author and venue and year:** Euisin Kim and Mooweon
  Rhee, *Journal of Air Transport Management*, 2017;58:135-143. Quote: "the greater the proportion
  of internally attributed causes is, the more likely it is that an airline will learn from its
  accidents and will experience a lower subsequent accident rate." Prediction: internal
  attribution is the world-facing form of refusal; corroborates T3. Status: **corroborates**.
  Overlap: medium; independent NTSB data.
- **Field, organization science, empirical. Author and venue and year:** David W. Lehman and
  Rangaraj Ramanujam, *Organization Science*, 2022. Quote: "Research to date offers inconclusive
  and even conflicting evidence regarding whether organizations learn from failure." Prediction:
  attention to one failure class can raise another, which is Fig 1 :19 per-slot residual and Fig 12
  two currencies. Status: **conflicts** with a simple learning-is-good story. It was returned to
  the census and did not flip a verdict. Overlap: low.

## VERDICT (from the figures)

The question is a union, not a single mechanism. Concealment and repetition are isomers over the
same atom set: same atoms, different bonds, different behaviour, and a compound case that resists a
single lever. For the channel condition the figures require a non-updating anchor, a keeper outside
the graded slice with declared stakes, and a residual kept per slot. For the transmission condition
they require a refusal operator ordered before mint, methods rather than recordings crossing the
membrane, provenance on imports, and a sought-for shape bound as an ordinary term before the run.
Reviewers must differ from one another and match the residual the last one surfaced. No final
institutional design is licensed; the framework returns a shape. This is a `specified`, ungrounded
result. Its strongest figure point is Fig 10 :86 and :93 on the keeper, and its strongest
world corroboration is Vaughan's low-overlap archival case.

- **cost if wrong:** bounded and locatable. The packet states mode, three checkable sub-claims and
  six refuters before the conclusion, so an error is findable rather than invisible. The real cost
  is spending the independence of outside readers by sending a recording instead of a generator,
  which Fig 2 :42 prices as "Buy independence in proportion to the cost of being wrong." That
  cost is avoidable by leading with the shape claim and its refuter.
- **undoable:** yes. The specific claims are removable without touching the derivation;
  `07_the_tether_answer.md` and `08_expert_verification_packet.md` already state the non-claims.
  A sent claim can be superseded by a corrected packet, and no figure line has to be withdrawn to
  withdraw an institutional binding.
- **reversed by:** a ground reading, not agreement. A measure shown to be movable by what the
  institution thinks would break the anchor test; a measured party with a reputation to protect
  shown to produce ground-paid corrections with no independent seat would break Fig 10 :93; an
  institution with no refusal operator shown to prevent recurrence while keeping the premise
  composable would break Operators :74; a recording-only chain shown to generate a new correct
  prediction would break Fig 4 :26; a mirror chain shown to correct a prediction would break
  Fig 7 :35. Until one of these fires, the verdict stands as `candidate`.
