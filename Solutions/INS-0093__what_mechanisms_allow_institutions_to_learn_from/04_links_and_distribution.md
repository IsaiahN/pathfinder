## Wider literature that bears on this question

- **Reporting culture and just culture.** Reason's four components (reporting, just, flexible,
  learning) are the closest world analogue to Fig 10's reachable anchor plus Fig 10 :60's declared
  stake. A reporting system without a just culture is a channel with stale percepts: people file
  nothing. NASA ASRS is a public instance of the design.
- **Normalization of deviance.** Vaughan's archival finding is the closest world case of Fig 2
  collapse 1 (coherent drift) inside an institution: signals were reinterpreted as acceptable
  inside the frame, so the frame could not see them.
- **Attribution bias.** Gioia and Sims, and Kim and Rhee, give a mechanism for why a failure is
  assigned to the environment rather than to the held policy: external attribution lets the
  institution repeat the same action expecting a different result. This corroborates the need for
  a refusal operator (F5), it does not derive it.
- **Pre-mortem.** Klein's exercise is a practical instance of binding the sought-for shape before
  the run (F9). It is an analogue, not a figure line.
- **The conflicting source.** Lehman and Ramanujam find that attention to human-error failures can
  reduce those failures while increasing failures from other causes. Fig 12 :125-127 says bits and
  actions are two currencies that do not add; the world result is a reminder that a mechanism can
  move one slot and worsen another. It does not overturn any figure verdict; it sends us back to
  the census to keep slots separate (Fig 1 :19).

## Distribution: offer, do not push

Figure-grounded reasons:

- **Fig 10 :52** - *"Bring a characterised residual, never the source."* Send the claim and its
  refuter first. Sending this whole folder before a checker has stated a verdict converts a
  potential outside frame into a participant in the framework.
- **Fig 4 :22-26** - *"Only methods cross up. Only head starts come down."* What is worth sending
  is the generator (the residual, the figure lines, the refuter), not the conclusion.
- **Fig 9 :27-28** - *"describe it before you go looking, or any frame you pick will seem to fit."*
  Ask what the recipient would expect to see if the claim were true before offering the links.
- **Fig 2 :40** - *"agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an
  evidence pool"*. A list selected for receptiveness manufactures the disqualifying correlation.
- **Fig 7 :6** - *"the limit: no last room"*. Do not present any institutional design as final.

**Recommended order in a reply:** (1) the claim and its four refuters; (2) ask what they would
count as a failure; (3) record that verdict; (4) only then the three local links, with the note that
the figures, not this summary, are the authority.
