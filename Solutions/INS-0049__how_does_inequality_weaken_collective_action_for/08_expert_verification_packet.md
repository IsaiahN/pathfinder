# Expert verification packet - the Tether on inequality and collective reform

**What this is:** a claim in a form a researcher in inequality and collective action can check. Mode, derivation and refuters are stated before the conclusion, so a reader knows what to look for and what would settle it.

**Mode: `specified` - not `grounded`.** No institutional measurement is claimed. This is a shape and a route, not a result. It is a simulated derivation checked for internal consistency against the figures.

## 1. The claim, in one paragraph

Collective action for institutional reform is a population loop, not one agent, and its residual is indexed per participant, never averaged. Inequality weakens it through four separable mechanisms that form a union: the aggregate hides the live slot; a concentrated stake in the reading moves the metric that should settle the reform; classes draw on one evidence pool, so their agreement adds no new option; and the objective link has no instrument, so whoever is loudest authors what counts as progress. The action cost of testing a term is unequally payable, which is the enabling condition, and capability sits in the habitat rather than in the members alone. The framework stops at that shape and names no policy. Which specific reform works is a declared `gap`.

## 2. Three checkable sub-claims

### C1 - framework-internal; checkable by reading Figure 1 and Figure 2

- **Statement:** the harm is slot-indexed, and a metric that moves under a stakeholder's push is not an anchor.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 1 :19 - **"The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated."** Fig 1 :22 - **"Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average."** Fig 2 :29 - **"An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks. A measurement, an experiment, a counter, a market, a patient outcome."** Fig 2 :30 - **"you push on it, it is not an anchor."**
- **Field-facing check:** is there a documented case where a reform metric shifted with a transfer of resources to the actors it measures, while the underlying institutional outcome did not move? That is the anchor test.
- **Refuter:** a metric proven invariant under such transfers has no effect on coordination; or a coalition where the mean moves and the worst-off slot does not shows no loss of collective action.

### C2 - cross-field; checkable against the inequality and collective-action literature and the consensus literature

- **Statement:** agreement among frames that share an evidence pool carries no surplus; only contact with a differing closure imports a new term. Inequality concentrates that contact.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 6 :23 - **"No frame escapes its own closure. The bound moves only on contact with a frame whose closure differs."** Fig 8 :49 - **"Two frames that overlap almost completely are one frame wearing two names."** Fig 7 :13 - **"agreement compounds, evidence does not"**. Fig 2 :42 - **"Buy independence in proportion to the cost of being wrong."**
- **What to check:** do cross-class or cross-disciplinary collaborations actually import new institutional options, or do they mostly amplify the same dataset and the same framing? The low-overlap witness is Baronchelli (2018), which reports that *"status, influence and connectedness play an important role"* in consensus and that isolated echo chambers hinder it. Field: mathematics and complex systems. Predicts that unequal influence and shared evidence change the outcome distribution, not just the mean. Corroborates the shape only.
- **Refuter:** a coalition whose members all draw on one evidence source, with no import from a differing closure, produces a ground-verified new institution; or cross-class independence is uncorrelated with institutional change once the anchor is held fixed.

### C3 - framework-internal derivation with a field-facing check

- **Statement:** the objective link is the middle break, and the action currency is priced separately from the description.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 3 :27 - **"A link with no instrument is not a link that works. It is a link nobody has looked at."** Operators table :51: *"a goal is a comparison, which is why a vocabulary without it cannot state one"*. Fig 12 :125 - **"A composition costs in two currencies, and they do not add."** Fig 12 :126 - **"Bits price the description. Actions price finding out whether it holds."**
- **What to check:** in a case you know, was "what counts as reform" instrumented before mobilization, or was it authored by the most resource-rich participant? Was participation limited by the action cost (time, exposure, organisation) rather than by agreement with the proposal?
- **Refuter:** an instrumented objective link shows no dependence on who authors it; or proposals matched for action cost show no participation difference across slots.

## 3. What is explicitly not claimed

- Not a `grounded` result. No measurement of a real reform coalition is reported here.
- Not that inequality always reduces redistribution demand. The Meltzer-Richard and Milanovic result is a real conflict and is handled in `01` F7 by a re-census into a different domain, not by denial.
- Not that any named country, class, person or policy is the cause or the cure.
- Not a numeric inequality threshold. That is a `gap` (Fig 13 :134: **"Unreachable is a verdict here, not a silence"**).
- Not a claim that a larger or more prestigious coalition settles anything (Fig 7 :13: **"agreement compounds, evidence does not"**).
- Not a claim that this simulated derivation can validate itself. From *The Recursive Self-Improvement Trap*: *"the output is only as good as the validator. The agent, or the tool, or the frame, or the theory, cannot validate itself."*

## 5. What would settle it

A ground reading, not agreement:

1. **Anchor test:** does the reform metric stay fixed under a documented resource transfer to those it measures?
2. **Slot test:** does the worst-off slot's residual move while the mean is held fixed?
3. **Import test:** do different classes in the coalition draw on genuinely different evidence, and does a new rule appear that none could derive alone?
4. **Objective instrument:** can the coalition say what would count as progress in its own terms, and is there an instrument for it?
5. **Action-price test:** are proposals equally payable in actions across slots, not merely equal in description?
6. **Union test:** does one lever clear all four intersections, or are categorically different residues left?

## 6. What an expert can check in twenty minutes

1. Read Fig 1 :19 and :22, Fig 2 :29-30, Fig 6 :23 and Fig 12 :125-126. Six short passages.
2. Check C2 against one case you know: did the parties share an evidence pool, and did their agreement produce a new option?
3. Name one instrument that would distinguish a moved metric from a fixed one in that case, or say why none is possible.
4. Try the union test: name a lever that clears all four conditions at once. If you can, the union claim fails.
5. Send the refutation. A failure is more useful than an endorsement. Record the verdict before reading any framework material.
