# Are the four mechanisms one cause or four isomers? Structure, order and analogues

**Mode: `specified`.** This is a simulated derivation checked against the figures, not a ground reading (see `05_what_shape_means.md` section 6).

## RESIDUAL

`01` F1 to F5 name four mechanisms by which inequality weakens collective reform: hidden slot residual, moved anchor, lost cross-class imports, and an uninstrumented objective, with an unequal action price as the enabling condition. Two questions are owed. (1) Are these one cause written four ways, or different molecules over a shared atom set? (2) If they are a union, in what dependency order must they be treated?

## DERIVATION

### Q1 - One scalar or a union?

Fig 9 :4-5: **"First: is there a fact here? Disagreements that do not shrink with effort, where each rule keeps working well on a different subset, are not one hard question but several well-formed ones. Split it rather than search."** Fig 9 :55: **"A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones."** Fig 9 :57: **"What remains is smaller and it is also categorically different: a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either."**

*The Library of Babel* gives the same instruction in search vocabulary: *"Perhaps your problem was a bundle or conflation of several problems masquerading as one? You will need to search all of those problems individually."*

**Reading.** The four mechanisms are not four degrees of one variable. Each works on a different subset and each fails on a different axis, so a single scalar such as the Gini coefficient cannot carry them. They are a union with intersections. Mode `general` for the split rule, `specified` for this application.

### Q2 - Are they isomers under Fig 12?

Fig 12 :117: **"Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."** Fig 12 :124: **"functional group. A substructure whose presence fixes the outcome. The group says what kind; the qualifier says which member."** Fig 12 :112: **"A plan is a sequence. A goal is a comparison. Progress is a subtraction."**

Write each mechanism over the shared bound atom set `{R_s, anchor, closure, objective, action cost, contact}`:

| mechanism | bond pattern | figure vocabulary |
|---|---|---|
| M1 hidden slot residual | many `R_s` bonded, then aggregated | Fig 1 :22, aggregation loses the signal; subtraction − of the per-slot detail |
| M2 anchor capture | `contact` bonded to `anchor` through a stakeholder with a stake in the reading | ⋛ comparison applied to the metric; Fig 2 :30 |
| M3 objective capture | `objective` bonded to `influence` before any instrument exists | → sequence written in the wrong order; Fig 3 :27 |
| M4 unequal action price | `action cost` bonded to slot holdings | − subtraction of affordability; Fig 12 :125-126 |

**Reading.** As the world literatures currently write them, the four share few atoms and are a **functional group**: each is a "failure of collective action" with different members in the slots. Written over the full shared atom set, they are **isomers**: same atoms, different bonds, different substance. That is the checkable structural claim. The 2000 paper on social capital puts it in empirical terms: social capital is larger in *less polarized* societies, which is a system-level reading of the arrangement rather than of any one member. *How a Society of Agents Would Work* states the same figure rule directly: *"The arrangement mattered more than the members."*

**Falsifier, written before the run.** If any mechanism can be written without an atom another requires (for example anchor capture with no slot residual, or an objective failure with no action-cost distribution), the isomer claim fails and the functional-group reading stands instead. If one lever clears all four intersections at once, the union claim fails.

### Q3 - What dependency order do they run in?

Fig 3 :19: **"Optimising a link that already works cannot rescue one that does not."** Fig 3 :27: **"A link with no instrument is not a link that works. It is a link nobody has looked at."** Fig 3 :20-21: the chain is **"ordered by dependency, not by importance. Each link consumes what the one above produces, so effort spent above the break arrives at a step that cannot use it."**

| order | mechanism | why it is upstream or downstream |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | F1, slot-indexed perception | link 1: if the live slot cannot be read, nothing downstream has a valid input |
| 2 | F4, instrumented objective | link 3, the usual break: a goal is a comparison; without an instrument the coalition cannot say what counts as progress |
| 3 | F2, anchor integrity | a cross-cutting guard. A moved anchor corrupts every link, so it is checked at each step rather than once |
| 4 | F3, cross-class imports | supplies new terms to the population closure; requires independent frames to exist first |
| 5 | F5, action price | the budget decides how much of the reachable space is actually tested (Fig 12 :126) |

**Reading.** Effort spent on mobilization (downstream) cannot rescue an uninstrumented objective (upstream). This is why the recommendation in `01` section 4 orders the actions as it does. Mode `specified`.

**Falsifier.** If a reform succeeds while F4 remains uninstrumented and the other conditions are unchanged, the dependency order is wrong.

### Q4 - What is the analogue, and where does it strain?

Two low-overlap analogues were opened this session:

- **Ecology, rigidity trap.** MathisonSlee et al. (2022) describe a configuration that becomes *"highly connected, rigid, and inflexible"* with *"System capacity for novelty is diminished"*. This maps to M3 plus M2: a coalition whose metric is captured and whose objective is fixed by one voice loses novelty. Different field, same shape. The strain: ecological rigidity is a physical configuration with a measurable phase; the institutional analogue has no agreed phase variable.
- **Mathematics of consensus.** Baronchelli (2018) reports a committed-minority threshold near ten percent and that *"status, influence and connectedness play an important role"*, plus isolated echo chambers that hinder consensus. Maps to M2 and M4: unequal influence and one evidence pool. The strain: the threshold is a model result for naming games, not a measurement of reform coalitions.

Fig 4 :30: **"So take the round trip and measure the gap. Without it you are navigating by dead reckoning: confident about a position nobody checked."** The analogues are carried up as methods (how to look for rigidity and for echo chambers), not as recordings of a threshold that can be applied to institutions. R_T is nonzero and is recorded in `11_audit.md`.

The framework essay *How a Society of Agents Would Work* supplies a third, internal analogue: *"If every agent's 'w' drifts to the average, the spread is gone and the gradients are spent"*, which is coherent drift, Fig 2 :34. It shares the figure evidence pool, so it is a reading, not corroboration.

## CONCLUSION

The four mechanisms are a **union**, not one scalar. Written over the shared atom set they are **isomers**, same atoms with different bonds; as the literatures currently write them they are a **functional group**. Their dependency order starts at per-slot perception and the objective link, with anchor integrity as a cross-cutting guard, and ends at cross-class imports and action price. The isomer and union claims each have a stated falsifier. The analogues from ecology and consensus mathematics corroborate the shape, not the institutional fact, and both carry a declared strain.
