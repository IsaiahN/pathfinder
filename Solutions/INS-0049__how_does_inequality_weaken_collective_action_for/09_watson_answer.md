**Mode: `specified`, not `grounded`.** Figures are primary and decide. World sources are secondary, corroborate but never overrule, and a conflict returns the derivation to the domain census. This is a simulated derivation checked for internal consistency against the figures, not a ground reading. By the framework's own rule it cannot settle the question it reports on. ## QUESTION

How does inequality weaken collective action for institutional reform?

Restated as an effect: why does the residual R = |Γ(b,a) − o′| for a reform coalition fail to trend down in some participant slots while it does in others? R is indexed per slot, never aggregated (Fig 1 :19). Inequality is not one variable in that loop; it is a heterogeneous distribution over the slots (different Γ_s, different weighting w_s, different contact with differing closures, different stake in the metric, different action budget). Option A: treat inequality as one scalar cause of less collective action. Option B: keep four figure-derived mechanisms separate as a union and stop at the shape. A predicts a single lever and a threshold. B predicts categorically different residues after any one lever is pulled, and a `gap` on the name of the right reform.

## DOMAIN CENSUS

R by arity, symmetry and scale, then one line per domain with actors, currency and anchor; partition or union; order by dependency.

- **R, the residual.** Arity: at least three place (actor slot, action, ground return) and a k-ary composition over slots. Symmetry: broken, because one concentrated slot can move the reading while many dispersed slots cannot, so a comparison bond ⋛ is involved (Operators table :48). Scale: single reformer loop, population with a ranking, and the lossy membrane between them (Fig 4 :27).
- **D1 Reformer loop.** Actors: reformer with belief b, weighting w, library Γ. Currency: per-slot residual reduction, description cost, action cost. Anchor: the institution as it actually behaves, a ground of the Fig 2 :29 kind. Admitted.
- **D2 Population membrane and ranking.** Actors: many agents, a shared library, something that ranks what they produce (Fig 4 :5). Currency: methods up, head starts down. Anchor: the ground, never the ranking. Admitted.
- **D3 Distribution of weight and stake.** Actors: slots with differing w_s, holdings and stake in the reading. Currency: influence over the reading, ability to pay action costs. Anchor: the ground only if it does not move when pushed (Fig 2 :30); otherwise this is capture, not a ground domain. Admitted conditionally.
- **D4 Habitat and contact distribution.** Actors: everything in contact with the residual, conditions and relations (Fig 11 :45). Currency: contact. Anchor: the ground. Admitted.
- **D5 Verification and seat frame.** Actors: metric, monitor, outside checker. Currency: independence between frames, contact with the ground. Anchor: the ground, which does not update (Fig 10 :24). Admitted.
- **X1 Inequality as a single scalar.** Actors: an aggregate across slots. Currency: a distributional index. Anchor: none that settles the mechanism; an average throws away the live slot (Fig 1 :22). Not a domain yet.
- **Union, not partition.** Inequality enters D2 to D5 at once. A case in two conditions is not a harder case of either (Fig 9 :57); the bundle must be unbundled before it is searched again (Fig 9 :54).
- **Dependency order (Fig 3).** Perception, vocabulary and objective, planning, learn and carry. Refusal precedes minting (Formula step 2); residual before frame (Fig 9 :28).

Strain test: the Fig 9 split, the Fig 12 isomer reading and the Fig 3 dependency order govern different quantities. After re-census no figure line is strained. The strains live in the world evidence and are declared in WORLD and in `01` section 6.

Mode `specified`. Refuter: a metric invariant under documented transfers has no effect on coordination, or a metric shown to move under push has no effect either.

**D4 Habitat and contact distribution, status triangulated (Fig 6, Fig 8 and Fig 11, closure, overlap and habitat).**

- Fig 6 :23 "No frame escapes its own closure. The bound moves only on contact with a frame whose closure differs." -> reform needs an import, and inequality concentrates who has the contact.
- Fig 8 :49 "Two frames that overlap almost completely are one frame wearing two names." -> a coalition drawing on one pool has no surplus.
- Fig 11 :40 "A system cannot be made more capable by improving the agent in isolation. An improvement that does not change contact changes nothing, however much it improves." -> individual-only remedies do not build collective capability.
- Fig 11 :49 "An actor's valence is relative to the residual, not a property it carries." -> no permanent roster of good or bad groups.

Mode `general` for the capability and closure laws, `specified` for the reform application. Refuter: a coalition with one evidence pool produces a ground-verified new rule; or a member-only improvement with contact held constant moves the ground.

**D5 Verification and seat frame, status triangulated (Fig 3, Fig 9 and Fig 12, middle link, union split, two currencies).**

- Fig 3 :27 "A link with no instrument is not a link that works. It is a link nobody has looked at." -> the objective link is the unmeasured break.
- Fig 9 :57 "What remains is smaller and it is also categorically different: a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either." -> the four mechanisms are a union, not a scalar.
- Fig 12 :125 "A composition costs in two currencies, and they do not add." and Fig 12 :126 "Bits price the description. Actions price finding out whether it holds." -> participation is limited by the action currency, unequally distributed.

Mode `specified`. Refuter: an instrumented objective shows no author dependence; proposals matched on action cost show no participation difference; one lever clears all four intersections.

**Naming the specific reform that works, status `gap`.** No figure line governs the name. Fig 9 :32 "you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself"; Fig 13 :134 "Unreachable is a verdict here, not a silence"; Fig 8 :39 "So a search that finds nothing is never a proof of absence." ## WORLD (secondary, each source read; field, author/venue/year, quote, prediction, corroborates or conflicts)

**Evidence pool A, institutional political economy (one witness, not several).**

- **Public choice / collective action.** Mancur Olson, *The Logic of Collective Action*, Harvard University Press, 1965, via the Collective action overview, read 2026-11-23. Quote recorded there: the claim that "individuals with more resources will carry a higher burden in the provision of the public good than poorer ones" (the exploitation of the great by the small). Prediction: inequality makes contribution burdens asymmetric and invites free riding. **Corroborates** the per-slot and action-price readings, but shares a broad institutional pool with the next three sources, so it is one witness, not four.
- **Institutional economics / commons.** Elinor Ostrom, *Collective Action and the Evolution of Social Norms*, Journal of Economic Perspectives 14(3), 2000, hdl 10535/5683, read 2026-11-23. Quote: the zero-contribution thesis "contradicted observations of everyday life in mature democracies that many people voted, did not cheat on their taxes, and contributed effort to voluntary associations." Prediction: coordination failure is conditional, not universal. **Corroborates** the union reading and refuses a single-cause story.
- **Political economy / inequality.** Joseph Stiglitz, *The Price of Inequality* (2012), via the Economic inequality overview, read 2026-11-23. Quote: "the use of political power generated by wealth by certain groups to shape government policies financially beneficial to them." Prediction: concentrated wealth moves the rules and their measurement. **Corroborates** D3. Same broad pool as Olson.
- **Sociology / social capital.** Pierre Bourdieu and Francis Fukuyama via the Social capital overview, read 2026-11-23. Quotes: the wealthy use networks "to maintain advantages for themselves, their social class, and their children"; "one person's civic engagement is another's rent-seeking." Prediction: what looks like civic capacity can be capture. **Corroborates** D2 and D3. Same pool.

**Evidence pool B, voting and redistribution (the conflict).**

- **Political economy / social choice.** Allan Meltzer and Scott Richard, *A Rational Theory of the Size of Government*, Journal of Political Economy 89(5), 1981, RePEc record read 2026-11-23 (no abstract; full text restricted). Branko Milanovic (2000) via the Median voter theorem overview, read 2026-11-23. Quote: "the greater the inequality in a country's pre-tax income distribution, the more aggressive the redistributive policies of the winning government." Prediction: more inequality, more collective demand for redistribution. **Conflicts** with a scalar "inequality always weakens collective action" reading. Returned to the census: it governs demand inside an already-fixed frame, while the figures govern the capacity to change the frame. Verdict unchanged. ## VERDICT (from the figures)

Inequality weakens collective action for institutional reform through four separable mechanisms held as a union and never averaged: the aggregate hides the live slot; a concentrated stake moves the metric that should settle the reform, so the anchor becomes a frame; the classes share one evidence pool, so their agreement carries no import; and the objective link is uninstrumented, so power writes the benchmark. The action cost of testing a rule is unequally payable, and capability is a property of agent-and-habitat, so member-only remedies do not change contact and do nothing. The Meltzer-Richard and Milanovic conflict is a different quantity and was re-censused, not denied. Which specific reform restores collective action is a `gap`, and no numeric threshold is asserted. Mode `specified`, ungrounded, candidate.

- **cost if wrong:** bounded and locatable. The packet states refuters before the conclusion and records the lineage, so a wrong reading is findable rather than invisible. The real cost is spending an expert's twenty minutes refuting a shape that was never the right shape, or sending a recording instead of a generator and thereby consuming the independence of an outside reader (Fig 2 :42, "Buy independence in proportion to the cost of being wrong."). That cost is avoidable by leading with the shape and the refuter.
- **undoable:** yes. Nothing is deleted if the claim fails; a refusal is added and the reach falls because something was added (Fig 6 :42-43). The four mechanisms, the union claim, the conflict re-census and the gap are recorded with figure line citations and dates, so a later reader can locate the error (Fig 10 :62-64). No named policy has to be withdrawn because none was named.
- **reversed by:** a ground reading, not agreement. The anchor test, the slot test, the import test, an objective instrument and the action-price test would each settle a mechanism. One lever clearing all four intersections would kill the union claim. A single aggregate predicting failure after conditioning on cross-class contact and independence, with no live slot left over, would kill the slot-indexed claim. A primary source with a genuinely different evidence pool showing that redistribution demand and coordination failure are one quantity would collapse the F7 re-census. Until one of these fires, the verdict stands as `candidate`.
