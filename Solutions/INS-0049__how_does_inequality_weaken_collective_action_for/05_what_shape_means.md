## 1. A shape is a constraint, not a filler

The figures README states the limit: the framework reports *"what a missing thing would have to look like (a shape, not a name)."* Fig 9 :32: **"you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself."** Fig 13 :134: **"Unreachable is a verdict here, not a silence"**; the same passage says the framework *"cannot say which primitive is missing, because naming it would mean already holding it."*

From *The Library of Babel*: *"you can cut your search short if the set of books returned is small and descriptive enough that it provides a general shape of the answer that satisfies your question."*

So a **shape** is the narrowed form of an answer you can state before you can name it. It constrains; it does not fill. For this question the shape is the four conditions in `01` section 0, not a named reform. "Cross-class independence plus an ungamed anchor" is a shape; "campaign finance limits" is a name.

## 2. How the shape is obtained for this question

- *Library of Babel*: use the vocabulary you already have that is closest to the thing, then look for recurring new terms that overlap or correlate with it: *"With each new term gained, you narrow the space and deepen your vocabulary."* Applied here: start with the effect words (residual per participant, anchor, contact, objective) and let the figure lines narrow them.
- Fig 9 :28: **"describe it before you go looking, or any frame you pick will seem to fit"**. The residual was described in effect terms in `01` section 0 before the institutional literature was consulted. That ordering matters: had "inequality and collective action research" been named first, any finding would have seemed to fit.
- Fig 6 :30: **"The description travels. Fails by being sharpened into terms only we can read. Cast it in effect terms."**
- *Library of Babel* warns that the thing that looks like one problem may be several: *"Perhaps your problem was a bundle or conflation of several problems masquerading as one? You will need to search all of those problems individually."* This is the canonical warrant for the union reading in `01` and `06`, and for Fig 9 :55.
- *Library of Babel* on stopping: *"you have already composed the answer through the process of locating it."* The stopping point here is a shape with a refuter, not a final verdict.

## 3. What licenses a name

Only two things, both outside the shape itself:

1. **The ground settles it.** Formula step 5: *"the ground settles it. Verified only by what pays. A gate is not the ground."* The figures README: the framework *"stops at the limit of what the available instruments can distinguish, and records the lineage so the stop is honest."*
2. **A provenanced import from a frame whose closure differs** (Fig 6 :23, Fig 8 :50 to 52), with a source gate and a shadow test.

*The Hard Problem is About Source* gives the failure mode. An instrument that reads green *"cannot comment on the source"*; several paths can give the same reading: *"If several paths create the same behavior, then behavior is a projection with lossy information."* And: *"The answer is unreached until a better instrument exists."* So reading a low level of collective action off an aggregate statistic and naming inequality as its source is the metamerism error. The corresponding move here is to refuse the name and ask for an instrument that discriminates among the four mechanisms.

## 4. Why the question is a union, and why that is a shape claim

*How a Society of Agents Would Work* (essay 4) gives the canonical multi-agent warning in one paragraph: *"A group is only worth having while its members stay different. Figure 2 defines independence as 'how little they draw on the same evidence.' Ten copies watching the same thing are one opinion said ten times."* It names both collapses: agents updating on each other converge on *"whoever had the most influence"*, and agents who never talk but share one source produce *"the agreement is the pool talking."*

It also gives the slot rule an external echo: *"A method that is brilliant in one setting must not be drowned by an average across all of them."* And the inequality-specific observation, from an experiment cited in that essay: letting people see others' choices *"increased both inequality and unpredictability of success"* (Salganik, Dodds and Watts 2006, read here through the essay, second-hand, not as an independent witness). The essay's own summary is enough for the shape: visibility of others' choices changes the distribution of outcomes, not just the mean.

These are shape claims. They say what any answer must preserve: member difference, independent evidence, per-slot measurement. They do not name a policy.

## 5. Prestige is not action, and neither is membership

From *The Recursive Self-Improvement Trap*: *"Prestige can fund an action. It can give an action a budget to be tried. But it cannot settle an action. Only the ground can settle it."* And the concrete collapse: *"If prestige could buy actions, the system would collapse into a monoculture. The highest-prestige agent would monopolize compute and repeat its past success, and the system would stop exploring."* That is the structural form of elite capture, stated at the level of a system rather than an accusation against a person.

The same essay: *"the output is only as good as the validator. The agent, or the tool, or the frame, or the theory, cannot validate itself."*

From *How a Society of Agents Would Work*, on headcount: *"However many join, the group never counts as more than 1/ρ, and ten clones count as one."* And on the arrangement: *"The arrangement mattered more than the members."* So a larger coalition of similar people is not more collective action; it is more spelling of one frame (Fig 8 :49, **"Two frames that overlap almost completely are one frame wearing two names."**).

## 6. My own audit is a simulation, not a ground reading

Same essay, verbatim: LLMs *"can quickly simulate an approximate answer/output with the formula found in the architecture post"*, but *"the truly testable output would need to be derived from the real architecture or working the formula yourself."*

So `01_figure_grounded_audit.md`, `06_isomers_and_order.md`, `07_the_tether_answer.md`, `09_watson_answer.md` and `11_audit.md` are simulated derivations checked for internal consistency against the figures. They are **not** tests of the institutional claim and must be labelled as such. They cannot settle anything they report on. No measurement of a real reform coalition was taken in this session.

## 7. Consequences for this question

1. The deliverable is a shape plus a refuter, not a ranking of policies.
2. Any answer that names a specific reform as *the* figure-derived fix commits the name error the README forbids.
3. The honest stop is at the four conditions and the gap in `01` F8. The lineage is recorded so a later reader can locate the error. *"Indiscernibility is not identity."*
