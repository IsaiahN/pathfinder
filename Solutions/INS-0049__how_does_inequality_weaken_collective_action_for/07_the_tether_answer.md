# The Tether's answer on inequality and collective reform

**Mode: `specified` - not `grounded`.** This is a shape and a route, not a result. Simulated derivation checked against the figures (`05_what_shape_means.md` section 6), not a ground reading.

## 1. The residual it answers

A reform coalition can register collective agreement while the institution does not change. The gap is per participant, not an average (Fig 1 :19): **"The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated."** Inequality is a heterogeneous distribution over those slots: different holdings, different weight in the shared belief, different contact with frames whose closures differ, different stake in the metric, and different capacity to pay the actions needed to test a change. The question is which slot structures stop the population loop from registering against the ground. The answer keeps four verdicts separate; it never averages them.

## 2. The shape

1. **The aggregate hides the live slot.** Fig 1 :22: **"Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average."** A coalition can look satisfied while one concentrated slot carries the residual that blocks reform. What is owed is a per-slot instrument, not a better summary.

2. **A moved metric is not an anchor.** Fig 2 :29: **"An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks. A measurement, an experiment, a counter, a market, a patient outcome."** Fig 2 :30: **"you push on it, it is not an anchor."** Where inequality gives some slots a stake in the reading, the metric moves under push and the ground is replaced by a frame. Fig 10 :24: **"the ground does not decay, the channel does"**; Fig 10 :59: **"an interest in the outcome is unavoidable, an interest in the reading is disqualifying."**

3. **One evidence pool means no surplus.** Fig 6 :23: **"No frame escapes its own closure. The bound moves only on contact with a frame whose closure differs."** Fig 8 :49: **"Two frames that overlap almost completely are one frame wearing two names."** Fig 7 :13: **"agreement compounds, evidence does not"**. A reform needs a term from outside the coalition's closure; if the classes in the coalition draw on the same source, the agreement is the source talking.

4. **The objective link is the middle break.** Fig 3 :27: **"A link with no instrument is not a link that works. It is a link nobody has looked at."** A goal is a comparison (Operators table :51). Where no instrument says what would count as progress, whoever has the louder voice authors the objective, and mobilization above the break cannot rescue it.

5. **The action currency is unequally payable.** Fig 12 :125: **"A composition costs in two currencies, and they do not add."** Fig 12 :126: **"Bits price the description. Actions price finding out whether it holds."** A reform can be cheap to write and impossible to test for most slots. The mint goes inert, and the fault is not in the minting machinery (Fig 5 :41).

6. **Capability lives in the habitat.** Fig 11 :40: **"A system cannot be made more capable by improving the agent in isolation. An improvement that does not change contact changes nothing, however much it improves."** Raising the resources of disadvantaged members without changing contact with the institution does not build collective capability. Fig 11 :49: **"An actor's valence is relative to the residual, not a property it carries."**

7. **The mechanisms are a union, not a scalar.** Fig 9 :57: **"What remains is smaller and it is also categorically different: a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either."** One lever clears the single-condition regions and leaves the intersections standing. The dependency order runs from per-slot perception to the objective instrument, with anchor integrity as a guard, then cross-class imports and action price (`06_isomers_and_order.md` Q3).

## 3. What it explicitly does not say

- Not that inequality always reduces the demand for redistribution. The Meltzer-Richard and Milanovic line predicts the opposite and is handled by a re-census in `01` F7: demand inside an existing frame is a different quantity from the capacity to change the frame.
- Not that any named class, country or person is the cause. Fig 11 :51: **"No standing list of good actors survives a change of residual."**
- Not which specific institution to adopt. Fig 9 :32: **"you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself."** That is a `gap`, not an unfinished sentence.
- Not a numeric threshold. No figure line says at what Gini value coordination fails.
- Not `grounded`, not a measurement, not a verdict about any real polity.

## 4. What would settle it - the ground

A source-discriminating instrument, not a further argument. *The Hard Problem is About Source*: an instrument that reads green *"cannot comment on the source"*; *"The answer is unreached until a better instrument exists."* Here the corresponding tests are:

- **Anchor test:** does the reform metric stay fixed under a documented resource transfer to the actors it measures? If it moves, the anchor has become a frame.
- **Import test:** do the classes in a coalition draw on different evidence, and does a new rule appear that no class could derive alone? If all draw on one pool, there is no surplus to import.
- **Objective instrument:** can the coalition state what would count as progress in its own terms, and is there an instrument for it? If not, the chain stops at link 3.
- **Slot test:** does the worst-off slot's residual move when the reform is attempted, holding the mean fixed? If only the mean moves, the live signal is still hidden.
- **Action-price test:** are the proposals equally payable in actions across slots, not merely equal in description?
- Standing falsifiers from `01` section 3: a coalition with all four conditions failing while a matched coalition without them succeeds; a fully predictive single aggregate; a collapse of the F7 domain split.

## 5. The honest stop

**Unreached, not unreachable.** The four conditions are a shape, and the specific institution is a `gap`: Fig 13 :134: **"Unreachable is a verdict here, not a silence"**; Fig 8 :39: **"So a search that finds nothing is never a proof of absence."** The evidence pool here is one pool, so agreement between the figures, the essays and the second seat is internal consistency, not corroboration (Fig 2 :40: **"disqualifying for a verdict: agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool"**). The lineage is recorded so a later reader can locate the error.

## 6. One-paragraph version for outreach

> The Tether does not name a policy and does not claim a measured law. It says that collective action for reform is a population loop whose residual is indexed per participant, so an average can hide the one slot that blocks change. Inequality weakens that loop in four separable ways: it lets a concentrated stake move the metric that is supposed to settle the reform; it leaves the classes drawing on one evidence pool, so their agreement adds no new option; it leaves "what counts as progress" uninstrumented, so the loudest voice writes the objective; and it makes the action cost of testing a rule payable by some and not others. Capability sits in the habitat, not in the members alone, so improving people without changing their contact with the institution does nothing. These are a union, not one cause, and the framework stops at that shape. Which reform works is an open gap, and the falsifiers are written down in advance. The ground, not agreement, settles it.
