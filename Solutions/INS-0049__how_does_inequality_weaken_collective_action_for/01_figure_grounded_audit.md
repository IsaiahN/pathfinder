## 0. The claim under audit

> How does inequality weaken collective action for institutional reform?

**Effect form.** A reform is a bet by a population of actor slots s that action a (a coordinated rule change) will bring observation o′ (the institution as it actually behaves) close to prediction Γ(b,a). The residual is R = |Γ(b,a) − o′|, indexed per slot, never aggregated (Fig 1 :19). Inequality is not one variable in that loop. It is a heterogeneous distribution over the slots: different Γ_s, different weight w_s in the shared belief (Symbols table :24), different contact with frames whose closures differ, different stake in the metric used to settle the reform, and different capacity to pay the actions needed to test a change. The residual asks which of those slot-level structures make the population loop stop registering against the ground.

**The sought-for shape, bound before the run** (Formula, Direction): collective capability for reform survives when (i) the ground metric stays ungamed, (ii) classes keep independent contact with differing closures, (iii) the objective link is instrumented, and (iv) the action cost of testing a term is payable across slots. This is written as an ordinary term so it can accrue a residual like anything else and be refused by the same test. If a coalition satisfying all four still fails while a matched coalition without them succeeds, the shape is wrong.

**What an answer would not be.** Not a single scalar story in which more inequality always produces less collective action. Fig 9 :5 says to split rather than search when disagreements do not shrink with effort, and Fig 9 :55 says a bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions.

## 0b. Domain census before choosing any figure line

**R by arity, symmetry and scale.** Arity is at least three place: actor slot, action, ground return; and it is a k-ary composition over slots with typed bonds, not a binary symmetric relation. Symmetry is broken: one concentrated slot can move the reading while many dispersed slots cannot, so comparison (⋛, a threshold relation between two quantities, Operators table :48) is involved rather than a symmetric join. Scale is threefold: the single reformer loop, the population ranking, and the lossy membrane between them (Fig 4 :27).

One line per domain, with actors, currency and anchor. A domain without a nameable anchor is not admitted yet.

| domain | actors | currency | anchor | status |

| D2 Population membrane and ranking | many agents, a shared library, something that ranks what they produce (Fig 4 :5) | methods carried up, head starts down | the ground, never the ranking | admitted |

**Partition or union.** Union, not partition. The failure modes compound; *"a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either"* (Fig 9 :57). Inequality enters D2, D3, D4 and D5 at once, so one lever clears the region where only one condition holds and leaves the intersections standing (Fig 9 :55).

**Order by dependency (Fig 3).** Perception, then vocabulary and objective, then planning, then learn and carry. The break is drawn at the middle link for illustration; a link with no instrument is not a link that works (Fig 3 :27). Refusal precedes minting (Formula step 2), and the residual is described before a frame is chosen (Fig 9 :28).

## 2. Findings

### F1 - Inequality acts per slot; the aggregate hides the live residual (triangulated)

Fig 1 :19: **"The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated."** Fig 1 :22: **"Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average."** Fig 1 :13: **"a global R near zero with one live slot residual is a legal state"**.

Fig 10 :26 names the matching channel failure: **"stale percepts, dropped transients, saturated metrics, pooled readings"**.

**Reading.** A reform coalition can look collectively satisfied (a low average residual, a high average welfare, a large participating membership) while one concentrated slot carries the live residual that blocks the change. The mechanism is not that the poor are absent from the statistics; it is that the statistic is the wrong instrument. Inequality matters because it makes the distribution, not the mean, the object of measurement. Mode `general` for the slot rule, `specified` for the coalition application.

**Refuter.** An intervention that raises the mean while holding the worst-off slot's residual fixed changes nothing; or one that moves the worst-off slot while holding the mean fixed changes nothing. Either result kills the slot-indexed claim. Triangulated: Fig 1 (per-slot residual) and Fig 10 (pooled readings as channel decay), low overlap between an agent-loop figure and a ground-maintainer figure.

### F2 - Concentrated stake turns the anchor into a frame (triangulated)

Fig 2 :29: **"An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks. A measurement, an experiment, a counter, a market, a patient outcome."** Fig 2 :30: **"you push on it, it is not an anchor."**

Fig 10 :59: **"an interest in the outcome is unavoidable, an interest in the reading is disqualifying."** Fig 10 :93: **"A ground with a reputation to protect is a ground someone benefits from, and its keeper is thereby disqualified."** Fig 10 :24: **"the ground does not decay, the channel does"**.

**Reading.** Institutional reform needs an anchor: the rule as it actually operates, an outcome, a count. Where wealth or status gives some slots a stake in the reading of that anchor, the metric moves when they push on it and ceases to be an anchor. The ground has not changed; the channel has. This is the figure-level account of rent-seeking and metric capture, and it stays structural: it does not assert that any named person is corrupt. Mode `specified`.

**Refuter.** Show a reform metric invariant under a documented transfer of resources to the actors it measures, with collective action unchanged. Also refutable if a metric that demonstrably moves under push has no effect on coordination. Triangulated: Fig 2 (anchor test) and Fig 10 (stake disqualification), different figures with different offices.

### F3 - Unequal contact with differing closures removes the import that reform needs (triangulated)

Fig 6 :23: **"No frame escapes its own closure. The bound moves only on contact with a frame whose closure differs."** Fig 6 :24: **"Import from a live peer is debited against that frame's independence. A wrong atom costs more than starting from primitives."**

Fig 8 :29: **"low overlap: B does not draw on A's evidence"**. Fig 8 :48: **"How much two frames draw on the same evidence decides both whether their agreement means anything and whether their union has any surplus."** Fig 8 :49: **"Two frames that overlap almost completely are one frame wearing two names."**

Fig 7 :13: **"agreement compounds, evidence does not"**.

**Reading.** Reform requires a term that is not in the coalition's closure: a new rule, a new enforcement mechanism, a new way of counting. Only contact with a differing closure supplies it. Inequality concentrates that contact. When the classes in a coalition draw on one evidence pool, their agreement is the pool talking, and the union has no surplus even if everyone is in the room. Homogeneous consensus is motion, not a route to a new institution. Mode `general` for the closure law, `specified` for the class application.

**Refuter.** A coalition whose members all draw on the same evidence source, with no imports from a differing closure, produces a ground-verified new institution. Or: cross-class independence is shown to be uncorrelated with institutional change once the metric is held fixed. Triangulated: Fig 6 (import) and Fig 8 (overlap and surplus), with Fig 7 (room chain) as a third, lower-overlap route.

### F4 - The objective link is the middle break, and power writes it (single-source on the figure, `specified` on the field)

Fig 3 :19: **"Optimising a link that already works cannot rescue one that does not."** The middle link: **"The link between them, having terms for what a situation offers and being able to say what would count as progress, has neither, so it attracts the least attention and the fewest instruments."** Fig 3 :27: **"A link with no instrument is not a link that works. It is a link nobody has looked at."**

Fig 12 :112: **"A plan is a sequence. A goal is a comparison. Progress is a subtraction."** Operators table :51: *"a goal is a comparison, which is why a vocabulary without it cannot state one"*.

**Reading.** Collective action does not fail only at mobilization. It fails where the coalition cannot say what would count as progress in its own terms. That link has no instrument, so whoever has the louder voice defines the benchmark, and effort spent on mobilization above the break arrives at a step that cannot use it. Inequality enters because the capacity to author the objective is unequally distributed. Mode `specified`: the figure names the link; which objective is captured is a measurement.

**Refuter.** An instrumented objective link shows no dependence on who sets it, or two coalitions with different authors but the same instrument produce the same ground return. Single-source for the dependency-chain claim (Fig 3); Fig 12 supplies the vocabulary of goal and plan, which overlaps in the framework but is a different figure.

### F5 - Reform is priced in two currencies, and only one is equally distributed (triangulated)

Fig 12 :125: **"A composition costs in two currencies, and they do not add."** Fig 12 :126: **"Bits price the description. Actions price finding out whether it holds."** Fig 5 :42: **"The acceptance test is a bargain, not a threshold."** Fig 5 :41: **"Any one of them at zero stops the mint entirely. Which is why a system that is minting nothing may have nothing wrong with its minting."**

**Reading.** A reform proposal is cheap in bits and expensive in actions: describing a policy is easy, testing whether it holds against the ground takes time, money, organisation and exposure. Inequality is an unequal distribution of the action currency. A coalition can therefore mint nothing even when the description is excellent, and the minting machinery is not at fault. This is the figure-level reason why participation, not agreement, is the scarce resource. Mode `specified`.

**Refuter.** Reform proposals matched for action cost show no difference in participation across slots, or pros equal in action cost participate at equal rates. Triangulated: Fig 12 (two currencies) and Fig 5 (bargain and guards), low overlap between a composition figure and a pipeline figure.

### F6 - Capability is agent-and-habitat; improving the poor in isolation does nothing (triangulated)

Fig 11 :39: **"So the answerable question is not what intelligence is, but what makes a system capable. And capability is a property of agent-and-habitat, never of the agent alone."** Fig 11 :40: **"A system cannot be made more capable by improving the agent in isolation. An improvement that does not change contact changes nothing, however much it improves."**

Fig 11 :49: **"An actor's valence is relative to the residual, not a property it carries."** Fig 11 :51: **"No standing list of good actors survives a change of residual."** Fig 11 :43: **"The habitat is enumerated, never composed."** Fig 11 :61: **"The seat’s power is asymmetric. Introduction is available; exclusion is not."**

**Reading.** An intervention that raises the skills or income of disadvantaged members without changing their contact with the institution does not build collective capability. The same actor can be a benefactor or a malfactor depending on which residual is in view, so there is no permanent roster of good and bad groups. And because exclusion is not available, a coalition cannot simply remove the habitat it dislikes; introduction is the only move, and introductions have cascades that must be enumerated. Mode `general` for the capability law, `specified` for reform coalitions.

**Refuter.** A documented improvement in member capability that leaves contact unchanged and still moves a ground-verified institutional outcome. Triangulated: Fig 11 (habitat and valence) and Fig 4 (kernel changes only actors, currency, anchor), different scale figures.

### F7 - The median-voter conflict is a different domain, not a reversal (re-census, no verdict flip)

The world literature contains a real conflict. Milanovic (2000), as reported in the median voter theorem page read 2026-11-23: *"the greater the inequality in a country's pre-tax income distribution, the more aggressive the redistributive policies of the winning government."* Meltzer and Richard (1981) is the standard theoretical source for that direction. More inequality, on that reading, produces more collective demand for reform, not less.

**Strain test.** The result only fits the present question if "collective action for institutional reform" is treated as one scalar: amount of redistribution demanded. Under the figures that is a mis-census. Meltzer-Richard and Milanovic govern the direction of a median demand inside an already-fixed, one-dimensional, already-institutionalized frame. The figures govern whether the cross-scale channel and the independent frames that could change the institution exist at all. Fig 9 :5: **"First: is there a fact here? Disagreements that do not shrink with effort, where each rule keeps working well on a different subset, are not one hard question but several well-formed ones. Split it rather than search."** Fig 9 :54: **"And when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again."**

**Reading.** Both can be true at once. Inequality can raise the median demand for redistribution while simultaneously degrading the anchor, the cross-class independence and the objective instrument that would convert demand into institutional change. One is a preference inside a frame; the other is the frame's contact with its ground. No figure line was changed, and the verdict does not flip. Mode `specified` for the re-census, `general` for the split rule.

**Refuter.** A single model that predicts both the redistribution direction and the coordination failure with one parameter and no residual left over would collapse the two domains and refute the split.

### F8 - Which specific reform works is a `gap`, not a hidden answer (`gap`)

Fig 9 :32: **"you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself."** Fig 13 :134: **"Unreachable is a verdict here, not a silence"**. Fig 8 :39: **"So a search that finds nothing is never a proof of absence."** Fig 13 :114: **"infinite effort inside a finite reach."**

**Reading.** No figure line governs which named institution (campaign finance limits, proportional representation, union density, a particular ombuds office, a specific land reform) restores collective action. The figures return the conditions a candidate must satisfy, not the name. A specific policy is a binding from the world frame, admitted only with a source gate and a shadow test (Fig 6 :23, Fig 8 :50 to 52), and it is debited against independence if it is imported from a live peer. A numeric threshold is also a gap: no figure says at what Gini value coordination fails. This is a verdict, not a silence.

**Refuter.** Derive a specific institution from the six-term set, or produce a figure line that predicts a numeric inequality threshold. Until then, name no policy in the verdict.

## 3. Pre-registered refuter

Written before the conclusion, per Seat Map :287: *"a falsifier written before the run is a design instrument, not only an honesty device."*

The shape claim is refuted if any of the following holds:

1. A coalition satisfying all four conditions (ungamed anchor, independent cross-class frames, instrumented objective, affordable action cost) still fails to produce a ground-verified institutional change, while a matched coalition satisfying none of them succeeds.
2. A reform metric shown invariant under documented wealth transfers has no effect on collective action, or a metric shown to move under push has no effect either.
3. A single aggregate such as the Gini coefficient fully predicts coordination failure after conditioning on cross-class contact and independence, with no live slot left over.
4. A source with a genuinely different evidence pool shows that slot-indexed residuals do not enter coordination failure at all.
5. The two domains in F7 collapse into one with no residual, so the union reading was wrong.

If any fires, the shape is withdrawn or re-censused. Until one is run, no part of this may be labelled `grounded`.

## 4. Recommendation

1. Lead with the slot-indexed shape and the refuter, not with the framework (Fig 10 :52, bring a characterised residual, never the source).
2. Treat the metric as a seat question before treating it as a design question (Fig 10 :59, :93). Ask who benefits from the reading.
3. Buy independence across classes in proportion to the cost of being wrong (Fig 2 :42); a coalition that shares one evidence pool is one frame wearing two names.
4. Instrument the objective link before spending on mobilization (Fig 3 :27).
5. Price proposals in actions as well as bits (Fig 12 :125-126). Counting signatures is a description cost, not a settlement.
6. Stop at the gap in F8. Offer the shape and the falsifiers; do not name a policy as if the figures derived it.
7. Figure 4 :25: *"A recording carried upward looks like knowledge and is a description of one occasion."* Send the generator, which is the derivation and the refuter, not a conclusion.

### Strains still live

### Updated verdict

The shape survives: inequality weakens collective action through four separable mechanisms, slot-hidden residual, a stake that moves the anchor, lost cross-class imports, and an uninstrumented objective, with an unequal action price as the enabling condition. The mechanisms are a union, never averaged. Which named reform fixes them is a `gap`. After re-census, strained figure readings: **none**. Strains in the world evidence: **two, named above**.
