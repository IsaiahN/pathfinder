# Outreach draft: check the output first, then decide about the framework

**Use:** one-to-one, or as a cover letter attached to a submission through the public venues in `03_public_contact_points.csv`. **Not** for bulk send.

**Hard precondition:** do not send until the shape claim in `01_figure_grounded_audit.md` and the three sub-claims in `08_expert_verification_packet.md` survive your own check. If the stage-1 output fails, the two-stage offer collapses on contact.

**Subject:** One checkable claim about inequality and collective reform, before any theory

> Hi [name],
>
> I have one specific output I would like you to check against what you already know about inequality and collective action. I am not asking you to read the framework first, and I am not asking you to agree with it. If the output does not survive, there is nothing else to look at.
>
> **The output (`specified`, not `grounded`).** Collective action for institutional reform is a population loop, not one agent. It can look healthy in the aggregate while one concentrated participant carries the live failure that blocks the change. From that, four separate mechanisms are predicted to weaken reform:
>
> 1. The metric used to settle the reform moves when concentrated actors push on it, so it stops being an anchor;
> 2. classes in the coalition draw on one evidence pool, so their agreement adds no new option and no new term can be imported;
> 3. nobody instruments what would count as progress, so whoever is loudest authors the objective;
> 4. the action cost of testing a rule change is payable by some slots and not others, so proposals stay cheap to describe and impossible to settle.
>
> This is a shape, not a measurement, and it says nothing about which named reform works. Specifically, it does not say that more inequality always reduces the demand for redistribution. A body of work on majority voting predicts the opposite direction, and I take that seriously: the two may be governing different things, the demand inside an existing frame and the capacity to change the frame.
>
> **What would refute it:**
> 1. a coalition meeting all four conditions still fails to produce a ground-verified institutional change while a matched coalition without them succeeds;
> 2. a reform metric proven invariant under resource transfers shows no effect on coordination;
> 3. a single aggregate such as the Gini coefficient predicts failure fully after conditioning on cross-class contact and independence, with nothing left over.
>
> I wrote these down before sending this. If any holds, I withdraw the claim. A one-line "this fails because..." is more useful to me than agreement. If you would rather not engage, no reply is needed.
>
> **What I am not claiming:** that any named country, class, or person is the cause; that any specific policy follows; that a simulation can settle an institutional question; or that a list of prestigious endorsements would help. A room reaching a conclusion without a ground is agreement, and agreement is a weighted average of influence, not evidence.
>
> **If it survives, and only then:** the sources are below. The figures, not my summary of them, are the authority.
> - Local figure text with line numbers: `FRAMEWORK/reference/03-FIGURES_TEXT.md` in this project folder
> - Canonical usage rule: `FRAMEWORK/reference/FIGURES_README_canonical.md`
> - Open literature on the anchor and independence questions: [Collective action](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Collective_action), [Economic inequality](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Economic_inequality), [The emergence of consensus: a primer](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5830794/)
>
> **If you want to reuse the method:** the eight-step loop is in the local `FRAMEWORK/figures/THE_FORMULA.md`. Run it on a problem you know well and can check against a ground. Write down what would refute your result **before** you run it, and state the mode of what you get. A reported failure is more useful to me than a reported success.
>
> Thanks for reading,
> [name / project]
