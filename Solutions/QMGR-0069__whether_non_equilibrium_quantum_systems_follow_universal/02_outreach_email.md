# Outreach draft: check the shape claim first, then decide whether the framework is worth reading

**Use:** one-to-one, or as a cover note attached to a submission through a public venue in `03_public_contact_points.csv`. **Not** for bulk send.

**Hard precondition:** do not send the full architecture before the stage-1 verdict is recorded. A recipient who has absorbed the framework before being asked for a verdict has joined the frame being checked (Fig 10 :42: *"A verifier that can reconstruct the claim cannot verify it."*).

**Subject:** One checkable claim about universality in non-equilibrium quantum systems

> Hi [name],
>
> I have one specific output I would like you to check against what you already know. I am not asking you to read any framework first, and I am not asking you to agree with anything. If the output does not survive, there is nothing else to look at.
>
> **The output (`specified`, not `grounded`):** the question "do non-equilibrium quantum systems follow universal laws that mirror those of gravity" is two questions, not one. A universality claim within driven quantum dynamics and a mirroring claim between that dynamics and gravity have different anchors. Figure 9 says to unbundle a union before searching: *"And when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again."*
>
> The shape claim I get from the figures is this: if a law is universal in the relevant sense, it is the same cross-kind composition rule recurring at different scales, with only the actors, the currency and the anchor changing. Figure 4 puts it as *"The kernel is the part that does not change with size... Only the actors, the currency and the anchor change."* That is `general`, structural. It is not a claim that any named physical equation, fluctuation theorem, chaos bound or area law, has been derived.
>
> **What I am not claiming:** that any specific law in your field is a gravitational law in disguise; that a shared equation is evidence of a shared ground; or that the figures settle a physics question. The framework returns *"a kind of thing, never the answer itself."* Where the slot would hold a specific law, I leave it marked `???`. Any name is a binding from a physics frame and needs provenance and an instrument, not an analogy.
>
> **What would refute or revise the shape claim:**
> 1. A measurement in a non-equilibrium quantum system showing a regularity that cannot be recovered as a cross-kind composition of the six-term set (space, time, energy as states; spectrum, gradient, network as structures).
> 2. A provenance audit showing that the candidate "shared law" in the two domains rests on one evidence pool and one review community, with no independent instrument. That makes the mirroring a shared-source collapse, not corroboration. The converse also counts: a low-overlap convergent derivation would strengthen the claim, and I would report it as such.
> 3. A derivation of one of the six terms from the other five, or the admission of a continuum as a seventh primitive.
>
> I wrote these down before sending this. If any one holds, I retract or revise the corresponding claim. A one-line "this fails because..." is more useful to me than agreement. If you would rather not engage, no reply is needed.
>
> **If it survives, and only then:** here are the sources. The figures are the authority, not my summary of them.
> - Verbatim figure text with line numbers (local framework extract): `FRAMEWORK/reference/03-FIGURES_TEXT.md`
> - The eight essays and the canonical figures README, as listed in `05_what_shape_means.md`
> - Public physics sources relevant to the residual: the fluctuation theorem, the chaos bound, fluid dynamics from gravity, and thermodynamic derivations of the Einstein equation, all listed with URLs in `10_source_ledger.csv`
>
> **If you want to use the machinery:** run the eight-step formula on a problem you know well and can check against a ground. Write down what would refute your result *before* you run it. The framework's own limit is that a loop cannot check the direction of its own question, and its author warns that outputs *"could never be verified if the solutions presented were not contrived to fit the formula."* A reported failure is more useful to me than a reported success.

## The two-stage offer in one line each

1. **Stage 1:** send the residual, the bound shape term and the refuter. Ask what the reader would count as a failure. Record their verdict before showing any framework material.
2. **Stage 2, opt-in only:** if curious, supply the figure text and the essay list, with the note that the quotes are the authority. Later agreement is never pooled as corroboration (Fig 2 :40).
3. **Stage 3, opt-in only:** offer the formula as a reusable method, with the instruction that each user states their own ground, mode and pre-registered refuter.
