# The Tether's answer: do non-equilibrium quantum systems follow universal laws that mirror gravity?

**Mode: `specified` on the shape and the refusals, `general` on the kernel claim, `gap` on the empirical content. Not `grounded`.** Simulated derivation checked against the figures, not a ground reading (`05_what_shape_means.md` §7).

## 1. The residual it answers

The question bundles two conditions with different anchors: (U) a universal law holds across far-from-equilibrium quantum systems, and (M) that law mirrors gravitational law. Fig 9 :54: **"And when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again."** They must be answered separately and never averaged.

## 2. The answer, per condition

**Condition U, the universality claim: affirmed at the kernel level, `general`.**
Fig 4 :19-20: **"The kernel is the part that does not change with size: the same loop and the same rule for naming what worked, whether one agent is running it or a thousand. Only the actors, the currency and the anchor change."**

Universality in this framework is recurrence of the same cross-kind composition rule across scales and strata. It is a statement about form. It is not a statement that any named equation governs all non-equilibrium quantum systems. Whether a *specific* regularity (fluctuation relation, chaos bound, hydrodynamic attractor) is universal is `specified` and unmeasured, and in places contradicted: the arXiv listing fetched for this audit contains *"Molecular structure breaks universality in screening behavior of electrolyte solutions"*, a reminder that universality is system-dependent and must be shown, not assumed.

**Condition M, the mirroring claim: split, `specified` refusal plus `gap`.**
- If "mirror" means a shared evidence pool, it is the named collapse: Fig 7 :12-13 **"same closure, handed along; agreement compounds, evidence does not"**; Fig 2 :35 **"Alignment is triangulation, not negotiation. A mirror cannot correct you."** Shared mathematics and shared reviewers are one witness, not two (Fig 2 :31).
- If "mirror" means two frames converged from separate instruments, it is genuine corroboration at maximum independence (Fig 8 :50). Which case obtains is a provenance question, not a figure verdict.
- If "mirror" means the two cores are the same structure, the closest figure term is *isomer* over a shared atom set, or the weaker *functional group*; see `06_isomers_and_order.md`. The word itself names no bond.

**The specific law: slot stays open.** Fig 9 :32: **"you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself."** Candidate names are bindings from the physics frame, each with author, venue and year in `10_source_ledger.csv`. None is derived from the six-term set.

**The continuum is not shared as a primitive.** Fig 13 :43-44: **"A continuum was the seventh and was removed for failing exactly this: it is a spectrum without gaps, or smoothness over space and time."** It can recur as a derivative, which is how analogue-gravity models work, but not as an atom.

## 3. What it explicitly does not say

- Not that the fluctuation theorem, the bound on chaos, an area law or the Einstein equation is *the* universal law. Those are physics-frame bindings, unproven as figure derivations.
- Not that quantum non-equilibrium dynamics and gravity share a ground. They have separate anchors: measurement and simulation in one case, observation and experiment in the other.
- Not that a rhyming equation is corroboration. Agreement from a shared evidence pool is one witness.
- Not a theory of everything, and not a final room. The comparison sits in an open chain (Fig 7 :6: *"the limit: no last room"*).
- Not `grounded`. No measurement at the relevant scale settles `left(R,φ)`.

## 4. What would settle it (the ground)

A source-discriminating check, not another analogy. From *The Hard Problem is About Source*: an instrument that reads green *"cannot comment on the source"*; the spectrometer resolved it. Here:

1. **Provenance test:** for a candidate shared law, show whether the D1 and D2 derivations draw on separate evidence and separate instruments, or on one mathematical tradition. Low overlap yields triangulation; shared pool voids the corroboration claim (Fig 2 :40).
2. **Isomer test:** can both cores be written over the full shared atom set `{Space, Time, Energy, Gradient, Spectrum, Network}`, differing only in arrangement? If not, the functional-group reading stands (`06_isomers_and_order.md` Q2).
3. **Order test:** is each first-order molecule settled enough to serve as an atom for the second-order comparison? Fig 12 :104 requires settlement before composition. The chaos bound remains a conjecture, so that part of D1 is not yet an atom.
4. **Standing refuters from the audit:** a regularity not recoverable as cross-kind composition; a derivation of one of the six from the other five; the admission of the continuum as a seventh primitive.

## 6. One-paragraph version for outreach

> The Tether does not answer yes or no to the question as written, because the question is two questions with different anchors. At the level of form it says that a universal law, if there is one, is the same cross-kind composition rule recurring at different scales while actors, currency and anchor change. It refuses to name that law; candidate names are bindings from physics, not derivations. "Mirror" is not a bond and adds no evidence: if the two domains merely share a mathematical culture, their agreement is one pool talking, while separate instruments converging on the same form would be real corroboration. The two cores are best read as members of one functional group, possibly as isomers over a shared atom set, and their comparison is second-order only where both are settled. The specific empirical question is left open until a source-discriminating check settles it. What is offered is the shape, the refuters written in advance, and the instrument that would close it.
