## 1. The claim, in one paragraph

The question bundles two conditions with different anchors and should be split. Condition U: a universal law holds across far-from-equilibrium quantum systems. Condition M: that law mirrors gravitational law. The figure-derived answer is that universality, if it holds, is recurrence of the same cross-kind composition rule at different scales while actors, currency and anchor change (`general`); that "mirror" is not a bond and adds no evidence when the two domains share one mathematical pool, but can be genuine corroboration when separate instruments converge (`specified` refusal); and that no specific law is named by the framework, so the slot stays open (`gap`). The two cores are members of one functional group over the six-term set, possibly isomers over the full shared atom set, and their comparison is second-order only where both are settled.

### C2: evidence-facing; checkable by a physicist against the literature

- **Statement:** "mirror" is corroboration only at low overlap. A shared mathematical culture and review pool make it the common-cause collapse, not two witnesses.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 7 :12-13 **"same closure, handed along; agreement compounds, evidence does not"**; Fig 2 :31 **"Two people reading the same report are not two frames; two instruments pointed at the same thing are."**; Fig 8 :29 **"low overlap: B does not draw on A's evidence"**
- **What to check:** take a candidate shared law, for example an entropy-production symmetry or a chaos bound with a gravity dual. Did the D1 and D2 claims arise from separate experiments and separate instruments, or did one mathematics and one review community carry both? A citation trail is enough to check this in twenty minutes.
- **Refuter:** demonstrate low-overlap, independent derivations landing on the same law. This would strengthen the cross-domain claim and should be reported as such. Alternatively, demonstrate a shared pool with no independent instrument, which voids the corroboration claim.
