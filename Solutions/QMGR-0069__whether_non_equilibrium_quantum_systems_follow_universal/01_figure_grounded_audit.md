## 0. The claim under audit

> **Question (QM & GR Questions.txt line 88):** Whether non-equilibrium quantum systems follow universal laws that mirror those of gravity.

Restated as an effect: is there a regularity R that (a) holds across many far-from-equilibrium quantum systems despite their microscopic differences, and (b) stands in a relation of *mirroring* to gravitational law? The two parts are not the same claim and do not share an anchor.

**Option A, affirm as written:** assert a universal law and a gravity-like form. Leads to a "same law" verdict whose support is often one shared mathematical culture, which is Fig 2's common-cause collapse, and which attributes to the figures a specific law they do not derive.

**Option B, unbundle and keep the comparison open:** affirm the kernel-level universality as `general`, refuse the "mirror" relation where the evidence is shared, refuse to name a specific law without provenance, and route the empirical part to an instrument. Leads to a checkable shape with an open slot.

**Bound shape term S** (from README, bound before the run): a law is universal across the two domains iff it is the same cross-kind composition rule recurring at different scales, with only actors, currency and anchor changing; any specific shared equation is a physics-frame binding owed provenance and a source-discriminating instrument.

- **Scale:** non-equilibrium quantum systems range from single driven qubits to hydrodynamic and holographic regimes; gravity ranges from Newtonian limit to Planck scale. A universality claim across these ranges is a **cross-scale** claim, which is the membrane question of Fig 4, not a same-level question.

### F3: "Mirror" is the wrong relation where the evidence is shared (refusal)

Fig 7 :12-13: **"same closure, handed along; agreement compounds, evidence does not"**
Fig 2 :35: **"Alignment is triangulation, not negotiation. A mirror cannot correct you."**
Fig 2 :31: **"Two people reading the same report are not two frames; two instruments pointed at the same thing are."**
Fig 2 :40: **"disqualifying for a verdict: agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool"**
Fig 8 :29: **"low overlap: B does not draw on A's evidence"**

Re-census on the symmetry strain from §1.1: "mirror" in physics discourse means a formal analogy (same equation or symmetry in two systems). "Mirror" in Fig 7 means a chain of near-identical frames that compounds agreement without evidence. They are different objects. The question's word is ambiguous between them, and the ambiguity is load-bearing.

If the analogy is supported by separate instruments and separate anchors, the two frames may converge at maximum independence (Fig 8 :50), which is genuine corroboration. If it is supported by one shared mathematical culture, one review pool, and one set of authors, the agreement is the pool talking, and counting it as corroboration is the named collapse. The figure does not tell us which case obtains in the literature; that must be read off provenance, and this audit's world sources mostly share the same human physics enterprise, so their agreement is weak corroboration at best.

**Refuter:** demonstrate two genuinely low-overlap derivations, from D1 and D2 separately, landing on the same law with no adopted import. That would convert the mirror refusal into a triangulation verdict and should be reported as a strengthening of the cross-domain claim. Note the asymmetry: a shared-pool finding weakens the cross-domain claim, a low-overlap finding strengthens it. Both are honest outcomes.
