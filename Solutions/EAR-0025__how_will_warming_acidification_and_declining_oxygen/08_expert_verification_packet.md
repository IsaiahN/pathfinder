# Expert verification packet — the Tether on interacting ocean stressors

**What this is:** a claim in a form an ocean biogeochemist can check. Mode, derivation and refuters are stated before the conclusion, so a reader knows what to look for and what would settle it.

**Mode: `specified` — not `grounded`.** No regional projection is claimed. This is a shape and a route, not a result.

**Status:** simulated derivation checked for internal consistency against the figures, not a ground reading. It cannot settle the question it reports on.

## 1. The claim, in one paragraph

Warming, acidification and declining oxygen form a union compound rather than three independent trends. Regions where two or three conditions coincide are specific compounds, not amplified single-stressor cases, and a lever that clears a single-condition region leaves the intersections standing. The residual must be indexed by region and depth, because a global mean near zero is a legal state while one slot carries a live signal. Those slots are coupled by circulation, and contact cascades reach third order into nitrogen cycling, nitrous oxide and habitat volume. An actor's effect is relative to the residual and region, not intrinsic. A spatial gradient is a different term from a temporal trend. Settlement requires independent observational anchors, not agreement among models sharing an evidence pool.

## 2. Three checkable sub-claims

### C1 — union compound, checkable against the figure text and against regional data

- **Statement:** the three drivers are overlapping conditions whose intersections are categorically different compounds; a single-driver lever does not clear the intersections.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 9 :55-56 — *"A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones. A single lever clears the region where only one condition holds and leaves the intersections standing, because the second condition still binds."* Also Fig 9 :57-58 — *"a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either."*
- **What to check:** in the eastern boundary upwelling systems, oxygen minimum zones, Arabian Sea or Mediterranean, does the two- or three-condition region behave as a distinct compound, or as the sum of independent single-driver responses?
- **Refuter:** demonstrate a partition in which one lever clears all intersections, or show the intersections are well modelled as amplified single-condition cases.

### C2 — per-slot residual and coupled slots, checkable against observations

- **Statement:** the residual is indexed by region and depth; a near-zero global signal with one live slot is a legal state, and the slots are coupled by transport.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 1 :19 — *"The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated."* Fig 1 :22 — *"Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average."* Fig 1 :13 — *"a global R near zero with one live slot residual is a legal state."* Fig 11 :52-53 — *"Scoping the habitat to direct contact is how DDT reached the eagles: first-order enumeration, second-order effect, and the residual that mattered was three steps out."*
- **What to check:** do independent oxygen and pH products show live regional residuals when the global mean is small? Do cross-slot transport and ventilation make the second- and third-order effects material at the timescale of interest?
- **Refuter:** show a global aggregate preserves every live regional signal, or show cross-slot transport is negligible at that timescale.

### C3 — ground over agreement, checkable against the modelling literature

- **Statement:** model ensemble agreement is not settlement; independent observational anchors are the ground.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 2 :35 — *"Alignment is triangulation, not negotiation. A mirror cannot correct you."* Fig 2 :40 — *"agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool."* Fig 7 :13 — *"agreement compounds, evidence does not."*
- **What to check:** do CMIP-generation models share enough forcing and parameter heritage to count as one pool for subsurface oxygen? Do the reported model-data differences (about half the observed oxygen loss, per Oschlies et al. 2018) survive when individual models are ranged separately against observations?
- **Refuter:** exhibit two low-overlap observational frames that converge on the same regional interaction map with the ground paying. That moves the quantity toward `grounded`; it does not refute the shape.

## 3. What is explicitly not claimed

- No regional pH, oxygen or temperature numbers; Fig 9 :32 — *"you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself."*
- Not that synergistic interactions dominate. Hu et al. (2024) find additive 40 percent, antagonistic 44 percent, synergistic 16 percent, with region-dependent interaction type.
- Not that any one driver is globally dominant, or that global trends are useless.
- Not a derivation of one driver from another, and no new primitive.
- Not `grounded`. Nothing here is asserted as settled by experiment.
- Not that the three drivers are isomers; they are a functional group under Fig 12 :123.

## 5. What would settle it

1. Independent observational products resolving the same double- and triple-condition regions, with the ground paying (moves to `grounded`).
2. A source-discriminating measurement separating solubility-driven from circulation-driven oxygen loss in a named region (names the mechanism in that slot).
3. A direct test of whether a two-condition region behaves as a compound or as the sum of its parts (settles C1).
4. A figure re-census admitting a different domain structure (would change the shape claim itself).

## 6. What an expert can check in twenty minutes

1. Read Fig 9 :55-58, Fig 1 :13/:19/:22 and Fig 2 :35/:40. Six short passages.
2. Check C1 against one region you know well: eastern boundary upwelling, an OMZ, the Arabian Sea or the Mediterranean. Is the compound reading faithful?
3. Name one independent observational pair that could resolve a two-condition region, or say why none exists.
4. Send the refutation. A failure is more useful than an endorsement.
