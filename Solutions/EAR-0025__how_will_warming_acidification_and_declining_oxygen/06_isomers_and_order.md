## DERIVATION

### Q1 — Union or partition?

- Fig 9 :54: *"And when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again."*
- Fig 9 :55-56: *"A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones. A single lever clears the region where only one condition holds and leaves the intersections standing, because the second condition still binds."*
- Fig 9 :57-58: *"What remains is smaller and it is also categorically different: a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either."*
- ⇒ The three drivers are overlapping conditions. Their intersections are compounds with their own loop. A lever that acts on one driver clears only the single-condition regions.
- **Falsifier, written before the run:** a demonstrated partition in which each region carries exactly one driver and one lever clears all regions.

### Q2 — What kind of overlap is it: isomer or functional group?

- Fig 12 :117: *"isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."*
- Fig 12 :123: *"functional group. A substructure whose presence fixes the outcome. The group says what kind; the qualifier says which member."*
- The three drivers do not share the same atom set: warming is largely a state-and-gradient composition, acidification a carbonate-spectrum composition, deoxygenation a gradient-and-network composition with a biologicalTerm. By Fig 12's definition they are **not isomers** of one another. They share a **functional group**: a compound stressor arrangement in which the qualifier (which pair of drivers coincides, and in which region) decides the member and the outcome.
- The stronger isomer claim would require both regions to be written over one shared atom set and to differ only in arrangement. That is a checkable structural claim, not something this derivation asserts.
- **Falsifier:** show two regions with identical shared atoms and identical bonds but different behaviour (breaks the isomer reading), or show they share no substructure fixing the outcome (breaks the functional-group reading).

### Q3 — Slots are indices, not independent frames

- Fig 1 :19: *"The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated."*
- Fig 13 :24: *"network"* and :23 *"that entities relate, and influence travels"*. Circulation is a network that couples slots.
- Fig 11 :52-53: *"Scoping the habitat to direct contact is how DDT reached the eagles: first-order enumeration, second-order effect, and the residual that mattered was three steps out."*
- ⇒ Regions and depth layers are measurement slots. They are enumerated and indexed, but the residual in one slot can arrive from another through the network. Treating slots as independent frames would mistake an index for a separation.
- **Falsifier:** demonstrate zero cross-slot transport of the relevant properties on the timescale of the residual.

### Q4 — Gradient over space and gradient over time

- Fig 13 :76: *"A gradient over space and one over time are different terms, because which state it is bonded to is part of what it is."*
- Fig 13 :35: *"Space, time and energy are not three readings of one quantity."*
- ⇒ A latitudinal or vertical oxygen gradient is one term; a temporal oxygen decline is another. They may be composed, but neither reduces to the other. The question's phrase "in different ocean regions" is a space-gradient request; "how will ... declining" is a time-gradient request. Answering one with the other is the flat-formula error.
- **Falsifier:** a single measured quantity that carries both with no loss, and the ground paying for it.

### Q5 — Order by dependency

- Fig 3 :20-21: *"The chain is ordered by dependency, not by importance. Each link consumes what the one above produces, so effort spent above the break arrives at a step that cannot use it."*
- Fig 3 :27: *"A link with no instrument is not a link that works. It is a link nobody has looked at."*
- ⇒ Dependency order: physical forcing sets stratification and ventilation; those plus solubility set oxygen supply; carbonate chemistry and oxygen set the biological response; verification ranges all against observations. A biological impact claim that assumes the physical link has been measured is a reading below the break unless that measurement exists.
- **Falsifier:** show the dependency order can be reversed without loss, for example that the biological response determines the stratification term in the same composition.

### Q6 — Analogues as corroboration, not as witnesses

- Low-overlap clinical analogue: the 2013 comorbidity review finds interactions among conditions generate clinical complexity and that single-disease guidance does not transfer to multimorbidity. This is a structural rhyme with Q1, from a field sharing no evidence pool with ocean biogeochemistry.
- Field analogue: Tan et al. distinguish joint, causal and composite relationships among climatic impact-drivers. This is a same-field witness for the union reading, so it is corroboration from the primary pool, not an independent frame.
- Fig 8 :50-52: convergent derivation and adopted import look identical in the contents; only the record separates them. Both analogues are declared as analogues, not promoted to figure lines.

## CONCLUSION

The three drivers form a union compound, not a partition, so single-driver levers leave the intersections standing. Regions and depth layers are indexed slots coupled by the circulation network, not independent frames. A space gradient and a time trend are distinct bonded terms. The drivers are a functional group rather than isomers under Fig 12's definition, with a stronger isomer claim available if a shared atom set is supplied. Dependency order runs from physical to chemical to oxygen to biological, with verification ranging all against the ground. Every claim is `specified` and falsifiable; none is `grounded`.
