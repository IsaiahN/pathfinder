# The Tether's answer: how the three stressors interact by ocean region

**Mode: `specified` — not `grounded`.** This is a shape and a route, not a set of regional numbers. Simulated derivation checked against the figures (`05_what_shape_means.md` §7), not a ground reading.

## 1. The residual it answers

Warming, acidification and deoxygenation are changing together, and the question asks for their interaction across regions. The live residual is at the intersections and at the slot scale: what is true of a region where two or three conditions coincide, and what changes when the residual is read per region and depth instead of as a global mean. The field literature agrees on the individual drivers and disagrees about interaction type by region (Hu et al. 2024: additive 40 percent, antagonistic 44 percent, synergistic 16 percent), and reports that models simulate about half the observed oxygen loss (Oschlies et al. 2018). So the gap is both structural and instrumental.

## 2. The shape

1. **It is a union compound, not three trends.** Fig 9 :55-56: *"A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones. A single lever clears the region where only one condition holds and leaves the intersections standing, because the second condition still binds."* Two- and three-condition regions are specific compounds (Fig 9 :57-58: *"a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either"*), so they need their own loop.
2. **The residual is indexed by region and depth.** Fig 1 :19: *"The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated."* Fig 1 :22: *"Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average."* A global mean near zero with one live oxygen-minimum-zone signal is a legal state (Fig 1 :13: *"a global R near zero with one live slot residual is a legal state"*).
3. **Slots are coupled, not independent.** The circulation is a network and contact cascades reach three steps out (Fig 11 :52-53: *"Scoping the habitat to direct contact is how DDT reached the eagles: first-order enumeration, second-order effect, and the residual that mattered was three steps out."*). Warming reduces solubility (first order), weakens ventilation and expands oxygen minimum zones (second order), and alters nitrogen cycling, nitrous oxide and habitat volume (third order).
4. **Valence is relative to the residual and region.** Fig 11 :49: *"An actor's valence is relative to the residual, not a property it carries."* Stratification can favour high-latitude surface production while reducing deep oxygen supply. The same actor is not globally beneficial or harmful.
5. **Space gradients and time trends are different terms.** Fig 13 :76: *"A gradient over space and one over time are different terms, because which state it is bonded to is part of what it is."* A latitudinal or vertical gradient cannot be substituted for a temporal decline.
6. **Drivers form a functional group rather than isomers.** Fig 12 :123: *"functional group. A substructure whose presence fixes the outcome. The group says what kind; the qualifier says which member."* The qualifier is which pair of drivers coincides and where.
7. **Settlement needs independent anchors, not model agreement.** Fig 2 :35: *"Alignment is triangulation, not negotiation. A mirror cannot correct you."* Fig 2 :40: *"agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool."* Observational profiles, moorings and time series are the ground; ensembles sharing generation are one pool.

## 3. What it explicitly does not say

- No regional pH, oxygen or temperature numbers. Fig 9 :32: *"you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself."*
- Not that all interactions are synergistic. The measured literature finds antagonistic and additive interactions are more common overall.
- Not that any single driver is the dominant one globally. Valence is region-relative.
- Not that a global trend is useless, only that it cannot carry a live regional signal.
- Not `grounded`. No instrument reading was taken in this package.

## 4. What would settle it — the ground

A source-discriminating set of regional observations, not a further argument. From *The Hard Problem is About Source*, an instrument that reads green *"cannot comment on the source"*; the spectrometer resolved it. Here:

- Do independent observational products (profile data, moorings, time series, satellite products with different heritage) resolve the same two- and three-condition regions? If yes and the ground pays, the region map moves toward `grounded`.
- Does a source-discriminating measurement separate solubility-driven oxygen loss from circulation-driven loss in a given region? That would name the mechanism in that slot.
- Does a region where two conditions coincide behave as a specific compound, or as the sum of two single-condition cases? That is the direct test of F1.
- Standing refuters: a partition that clears all intersections; a global aggregate that preserves every live regional signal; intrinsic rather than residual-relative valence; a single term carrying both space and time gradients; a measured compound structure different from the one predicted.

## 6. One-paragraph version for outreach

> The Tether does not give regional pH or oxygen numbers for a warming, acidifying, deoxygenating ocean, and it does not rank the three stressors. It says the three form a union compound, so regions where two or three coincide are specific cases that a single-driver lever does not clear; that the residual must be read by region and depth, because a global mean near zero can coexist with one live slot; that circulation couples those slots and third-order cascades reach nitrogen cycling and habitat; that an actor's effect depends on the residual and the region rather than being intrinsic; and that a spatial gradient is not a time trend. The answer it returns is a shape with written refuters and named instruments. The regional quantities stay open until independent observations settle them.
