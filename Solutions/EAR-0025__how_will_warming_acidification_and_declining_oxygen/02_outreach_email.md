# Outreach draft — check the shape claim before deciding about the framework

**Use:** one-to-one, or as a cover note attached to a submission through a public venue in `03_public_contact_points.csv`. **Not** for bulk send.

**Hard precondition:** do not send until the three sub-claims in `08_expert_verification_packet.md` have been re-checked against the verbatim figure text. The offer collapses on contact if the stage-1 claim does not survive.

**Subject:** One checkable claim about interacting ocean stressors, before any theory

> Hi [name],
>
> I am writing about a narrow question in ocean biogeochemistry: how warming, acidification and declining oxygen interact across different ocean regions. I have one structural claim I would like you to check against what you already know. I am not asking you to read a theory first, and I am not asking you to agree with anything. If the claim fails, there is nothing else to look at.
>
> **The claim (`specified`, not `grounded`):** these three drivers are not three independent trends that can be ranked and added. They are a union of overlapping conditions, and the two- and three-condition regions are specific compounds, not worse versions of a single-stressor case. A lever that clears a region where only one condition holds leaves the intersections standing because the other conditions still bind. The residual must be indexed by region and depth: a global mean near zero is a legal state while one region carries a live signal. Depth and latitude gradients must be kept distinct from time trends, because a gradient over space and one over time are different terms.
>
> **What I am not claiming:** I am not giving regional pH, oxygen or temperature numbers. The framework I use returns a shape, not a quantity, and I will not manufacture one. I am not claiming that every interaction is synergistic. Published meta-analyses find additive and antagonistic interactions are common, and the mix changes by region and trophic level. I am not claiming model ensemble agreement settles anything: shared-generation models are one evidence pool.
>
> **What would refute the claim, written before this message:**
> 1. showing the three conditions form a partition such that one lever clears all intersections;
> 2. showing a global aggregate preserves every live regional signal and supports the same regional decisions;
> 3. showing an actor's effect is intrinsic rather than relative to the residual and the region;
> 4. a source-discriminating measurement of a two- or three-condition region that returns a compound structure different from the one above.
>
> A one-line "this fails because..." is more useful to me than agreement. If you would rather not engage, no reply is needed.
>
> **If it survives, and only then:** I can send three public links: the verbatim figure text with line numbers, the canonical usage rule, and a worked field review of compound ocean state changes. The figures, not my summary, are the authority.
>
> **If you want the method:** the eight-step loop is public. If you use it, write down what would refute your result *before* you run it and state the mode of what you get. A reported failure is more useful to me than a reported success.
>
> Thanks,
> [name]

## Two-stage rule for the sender

1. **Stage 1.** Send only the claim and its refuter. Record the recipient's verdict and their own failure criterion before any framework material is shown.
2. **Stage 2, opt-in only.** If they are curious, send the three links. Their later agreement is never pooled as corroboration (Fig 2 :40).
3. **Stage 3.** Offer the loop as a reusable method, with the instruction that each user writes their own refuter and states their own mode.

Nothing is sent by this agent. Hosting and sending are the user's to do.
