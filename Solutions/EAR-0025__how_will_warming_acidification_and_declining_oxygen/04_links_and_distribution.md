## Two things in the wider literature that bear directly on the answer

1. **The compound-structure finding is measured, not asserted.** Tan et al. define compound climatic impact-drivers and distinguish joint, causal and composite relationships, then report double and triple emergence concentrated in the subtropical and tropical Atlantic, the subtropical Pacific, the Arabian Sea and the Mediterranean, with depth-dependent percentages. This is a direct world witness for F1 and F2. It shares no evidence pool with the framework.
2. **Synergy is not the default.** Hu et al. find synergistic interactions are the minority (16 percent) and that interaction type changes by climate region, from synergistic in temperate regions to compensatory in subtropical regions and positive in tropical regions. This is a partial conflict with a naive "all three stressors amplify each other" reading. Per the WATSON rule the conflict sends us back to the census, not to a softer verdict: it strengthens F3 (valence is relative to the residual and region) and weakens no figure line, because no figure line asserts universal synergy.

Overlap check: Gruber, Tan, Oschlies, Hu and the IUCN chapter all draw on ocean observing and modelling programmes, so they are not five fully independent witnesses. They are read as one primary-field pool plus one low-overlap clinical analogue. The essays all share an author with the figures and carry high overlap with them.
