# Solution ECN-0060

**Question:** How should economists compare policies that increase total income with those that distribute it more evenly?

**Source:** economics questions.txt line 79

**Section:** 6. Inequality, poverty, and distribution

**Mode:** `specified` on the shape of the comparison procedure, `general` on the structural rules, `gap` on the ranking of the two policies. Nothing here is `grounded`. Every substantive claim in this folder is a simulated derivation checked for internal consistency against the figures, not a ground reading, so it cannot settle the question it reports on.

**Status:** done

**Registry status:** pending

**Key verdict in one line:** the two policies are two currencies that do not add, the comparison needs an objective with an anchor, and no such anchor exists among the standard welfare criteria, so the honest output is the shape of a comparison procedure (two readings held apart, per-slot reporting, unbundled compounds, a named threshold, recorded provenance), not a winner.

## Question restated as an effect

Two policy options are on the table. Option A raises total income and may leave the distribution more unequal. Option B leaves a more even distribution and may reduce total income. Economists are asked which to prefer. What is being decided is the **form of the comparison**: whether the two effects are commensurable, what number or rule would settle the preference, and what each option leads to if that rule is applied. An answer would look like a ranking procedure whose output does not depend on an ungrounded choice of welfare weights. That sought-for shape is bound as an ordinary term in `01` so it can accrue a residual like anything else (formula step "Direction").

## What is deliberately not here

- No personal emails of named individuals.
- No ranking of people by how likely they are to agree with this reading.
- No bulk-send plan.

Three reasons, in the figures' own words:

1. **A room does not upgrade the label.** Agreement among selected frames is a weighted average of influence, not an anchor (Seat Map, as recorded in the exemplar; Fig 2 :35, *"Alignment is triangulation, not negotiation. A mirror cannot correct you."*).
2. **Selecting for receptiveness manufactures the disqualifying correlation.** Fig 2 :40, *"agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool."*
3. **Asking people to trust before they check spends their independence.** Fig 10 :42, *"A verifier that can reconstruct the claim cannot verify it."*

Legally, scraping personal emails for unsolicited outreach breaches GDPR and CAN-SPAM style rules in many jurisdictions. That is a practical failure on top of the structural one.
