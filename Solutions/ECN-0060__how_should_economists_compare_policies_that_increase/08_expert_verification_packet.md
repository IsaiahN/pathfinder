# Expert verification packet: the Tether on comparing growth and redistribution policies

**What this is:** a claim in a form a policy evaluator can check. Mode, derivation and refuters are stated before the conclusion, so a reader knows what to look for and what would settle it.

**Mode: `specified` on the shape of the comparison procedure, `general` on the structural rules, `gap` on the ranking. Not `grounded`.** No measurement settles the objective. This is a shape and a route, not a result.

**Status of this packet:** a simulated derivation checked for internal consistency against the figures, not a ground reading. It cannot settle the question it reports on.

## 1. The claim, in one paragraph

A policy that raises total income and a policy that distributes income more evenly are recorded in two currencies that do not add. The comparison needs a third quantity, a social objective, and no available objective is an anchor, because every candidate changes its answer when its weights are pushed. The derivation therefore returns the shape a comparison procedure must have (two readings held apart, reported per group, with the union unbundled, the threshold named and the objective's provenance recorded) and declares the ranking a `gap`. Fig 2 :29 defines the anchor; Fig 12 :125 gives the no-add rule; Fig 1 :19 gives per-slot indexing; Fig 9 :54 gives unbundling; Operators table :52 identifies the comparison as a goal. Four refuters are pre-registered in `01` §3.

## 2. Three checkable sub-claims

### C1 - framework-internal plus policy-evaluation-facing; checkable by reading Fig 12 and the cost-benefit literature

- **Statement:** the two effects are currencies that cannot be added, so a single composite score is the named error.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 12 :125 *"A composition costs in two currencies, and they do not add."* Fig 12 :130 *"Summing them into one figure is the error this names, not the fix for it."* The figure's own currencies are bits and actions; Fig 4 :20 *"Only the actors, the currency and the anchor change."* licenses the substitution of income and distributional weight, so the application is `specified`.
- **What to check:** is the substitution faithful, or does it rely on a conversion that is itself a chosen welfare weight? The world literature records the same shortfall: cost-benefit analysis "relies on the Kaldor-Hicks criteria to make statements about efficiency without addressing issues of income distribution" (Wikipedia synthesis of Phaneuf and Requate, opened 2026-11-23).
- **Refuter:** exhibit a unit in which total income and the full distribution are commensurable without a lossy, convention-dependent conversion, and show the conversion is ground-settled. Equivalent variation and compensating variation do not qualify because both depend on a welfare weight.

### C2 - framework-internal; checkable by reading Fig 2 and Fig 10

- **Statement:** the ranking is a `gap`, not a silent omission. The objective required to rank the two policies is not an anchor.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 2 :29 *"An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks."* Fig 10 :49 *"Declaring a meaning does not ground it."* Fig 13 :134 *"Unreachable is a verdict here, not a silence"*.
- **What to check:** does any proposed objective survive the anchor test, that is, does its ranking stay fixed under admissible reweighting and alternative interpersonal-comparison assumptions? The social-welfare-function literature records that the objective packages value judgments (Bergson) and that interpersonal utility comparison "is not measurable by any empirical test" (Robbins, as recorded in the Wikipedia synthesis, opened 2026-11-23).
- **Refuter:** exhibit an objective whose ranking of A and B does not change under any admissible reweighting and which is not simply a measured quantity. If one is produced, C2 fails.

### C3 - framework-internal; checkable by reading Fig 9 and Fig 1

- **Statement:** the question is a union compound, and reporting must be per slot.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 9 :54 *"And when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again."* Fig 9 :57-58 *"a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either. It stops being a mixture and becomes a specific compound."* Fig 1 :19 *"The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated."* Fig 1 :22 *"Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average."*
- **What to check:** do real tax and transfer packages change both conditions? If yes, a single-axis comparison is a mixture, not a ranking. Does any single national summary preserve all decision-relevant group effects? The Atkinson index entry (opened 2026-11-23) records that only the generalized entropy family is additively subgroup decomposable and that the Gini index is not.
- **Refuter:** show that every policy in the comparison set affects only one of the two conditions (which would make it a partition), or that a single summary preserves all per-group effects across all policies. The first is empirically false for taxation; the second holds only in the degenerate case where all groups move identically.

## 3. What is explicitly not claimed

- Not a winner. Neither policy is asserted to be better.
- Not that a particular welfare function, index or compensation test is correct.
- Not that efficiency and equity can be summed after choosing a conversion.
- Not that better measurement would settle the objective. The break is at the objective link, not at measurement (Fig 3 :19).
- Not `grounded`, and not final. No last room; the shape stops where the instruments stop.
- Not that the imported welfare function adds a ground atom (Fig 6 :26). It is a provenanced convention.

## 5. What would settle it

A ground reading, not agreement. Four pre-registered tests:

1. **Anchor:** does a ranking survive admissible reweighting and alternative interpersonal comparisons?
2. **Currency:** is a ground-settled commensurable unit available?
3. **Instrument:** is there a procedure at the objective link that returns different, ground-checkable readings for A and B?
4. **Aggregate:** does any single summary preserve all decision-relevant per-group effects?

Until one fires, the shape is `specified` and the ranking is `gap`. Prestige or agreement is not such an instrument.

## 6. What an expert can check in twenty minutes

1. Read Fig 12 :125, :130 and Fig 4 :20 (three short passages). Is the no-add rule faithfully carried over to income and equity?
2. Read Fig 2 :29 and Fig 10 :49. Apply the push test to whichever objective you normally use. Does its ranking move?
3. Read Fig 9 :54-58 and Fig 1 :19, :22. Does your policy case belong to the pure-efficiency, pure-distribution or compound category, and would your reporting hide the live slot?
4. Name one instrument that would settle the objective link, or say why none exists.
5. Send the refutation. A failure is more useful than an endorsement.
