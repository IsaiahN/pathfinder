# The Tether's answer on income-increasing versus distribution-increasing policies

**Mode: `specified` on the shape of the comparison procedure, `general` on the structural rules, `gap` on which policy is better. Not `grounded`.** This is a shape and a route, not a result. Simulated derivation checked against the figures (`05_what_shape_means.md` §6), not a ground reading.

## 1. The residual it answers

Economists are asked to rank a policy that raises total income against one that spreads income more evenly. The two effects are recorded in different currencies: money income, anchored in markets and accounts, and the arrangement of income across persons, anchored in records and surveys. A ranking needs a third quantity, a social objective that trades one for the other. Every standard candidate changes its answer when its weights or comparison assumptions are pushed, so none is an anchor (Fig 2 :29, *"An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks."*). The residual is a **missing objective**, not a shortage of data. It is a union compound with its own loop, so it must be unbundled before it is searched (Fig 9 :54).

## 2. The shape

1. **Two currencies, held apart.** Fig 12 :125: *"A composition costs in two currencies, and they do not add."* Fig 12 :130: *"Summing them into one figure is the error this names, not the fix for it."* Report total income and the distribution as two readings. A single composite score repeats the named error. The figure's own currencies are bits and actions; the income and equity substitution is a domain mapping licensed by Fig 4 :20 (*"Only the actors, the currency and the anchor change."*), so it is `specified`, not `grounded`.

2. **Per slot, not per aggregate.** Fig 1 :19: *"The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated."* Fig 1 :22: *"Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average."* Report who gains and who loses at each group or percentile. A global mean with one live slot is a legal state.

3. **Unbundle the union.** Fig 9 :5 splits questions whose disagreements do not shrink with effort; here measurement does shrink and the objective does not, so split at the objective. Fig 9 :54: *"And when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again."* Separate pure-efficiency, pure-distribution and compound cases. Fig 9 :57-58: *"a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either. It stops being a mixture and becomes a specific compound."*

4. **The comparison is a goal with a threshold.** The Operators table defines ⋛ as *"a threshold relation between two quantities"* and :52 says *"a goal is a comparison, which is why a vocabulary without it cannot state one"*. State the two quantities and the cut-off before ranking. Without an anchored cut-off the comparison is unbound.

5. **Record the provenance of the objective.** Fig 6 :26: *"An import adds no atom to the world."* Fig 8 :51: *"Convergent derivation and adopted import look identical in the contents. Only the record of where each came from separates them"*. Importing a welfare function gives access to a convention, not a ground fact.

6. **The winner is not derivable.** Fig 9 :32: *"you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself"*. The framework returns the kind of comparison owed, not which policy wins. The ranking is a declared `gap` (Fig 13 :134, *"Unreachable is a verdict here, not a silence"*), not an omission.

## 3. What it explicitly does not say

- Not that Policy A or Policy B is better. No winner is asserted.
- Not that a particular welfare function, inequality index, or compensation test is correct.
- Not that efficiency and equity can be added after choosing the right conversion factor. The no-add rule is the point.
- Not that better data would settle it. Data lands above the dependency break (Fig 3 :19, *"Optimising a link that already works cannot rescue one that does not."*).
- Not `grounded`. No measurement in this derivation settles which objective is right.
- Not that this is a final answer. There is no last room; the shape stops at the limit of the available instruments and records the lineage.

## 4. What would settle it: the ground

A discriminating instrument, not a further argument. *The Hard Problem is About Source* (as quoted in `05` §4): a reading of green *"cannot comment on the source"*; the spectrometer resolved it. Here the corresponding questions are:

- **Anchor test:** does a proposed objective return a stable ranking under admissible reweighting and alternative interpersonal-comparison assumptions? An objective that flips is not an anchor (Fig 2 :29).
- **Instrument test:** is there a procedure that returns different, ground-checkable answers for A and B at the objective link, rather than merely restating a preference (Fig 3 :27)?
- **Compound test:** when a policy changes both total income and the split, do the per-group effects remain distinguishable after the change, or does the aggregate swallow them (Fig 1 :22)?
- **Falsifiers from `01` §3:** the anchor refuter, the currency refuter, the instrument refuter, and the aggregate refuter. Any one firing moves the claim.

## 5. The honest stop

**Unreachable at this budget, not proven impossible.** Fig 8 :39: *"So a search that finds nothing is never a proof of absence."* The six-term minimum set and the figure rules constrain the *shape* of any admissible comparison; they do not supply a social objective, and naming one would be inventing a primitive. The eight essays record that declaring primitives makes a choice visible, not neutral. The derivation stops where the instruments stop and records the route, so the stop is checkable. *"Indiscernibility is not identity."*

## 6. One-paragraph version for outreach

> The Tether does not tell economists which of the two policies is better. It tells them what a comparison would have to look like before it could mean anything. Total income and the distribution are two currencies in different units, so adding them into one score is the error, not the fix; report them separately, and report them per group, because an average hides the slot where the damage is. The question is a union of a pure-efficiency case, a pure-distribution case and a compound where one lever moves both, so unbundle it before comparing. The comparison itself is a threshold relation, a goal, so the two quantities and the cut-off must be stated. And the objective that sets the cut-off has no anchor: every welfare function changes its answer when its weights are pushed. That is why the ranking is declared a gap and the shape is offered instead, with four refuters written in advance. The ground, not agreement, would close it.
