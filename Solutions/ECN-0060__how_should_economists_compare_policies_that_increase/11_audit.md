## 4. Round trip gap R_T

The Symbols table defines R_T as the gap between what was sent up and what came back down, `gap(x, (T_E ∘ T_A)(x))`, measuring what the coarser description cannot hold. Fig 4 :30 requires it to be measured rather than assumed. I ran the trip on the condensation the folder actually uses.

1. **Sent up (x):** the verbatim rules, chiefly Fig 12 :125-130 (no add), Fig 1 :19 and :22 (per slot), Fig 9 :54-58 (union) and Fig 2 :29 (anchor).
2. **Abstraction (T_A):** the outreach shorthand, hold two currencies apart, report per group, unbundle, name the threshold, record provenance.
3. **Concretisation (T_E):** a ranking procedure an evaluator would actually run on policy data.

**R_T is nonzero.** Three constraints in the shorthand do not survive the trip back down:

- the **anchor condition**. Nothing in the shorthand forces the threshold to be ground-settled, so an implementation can silently reintroduce a chosen welfare weight;
- the **union intersection**. A flat checklist tends to treat the compound case as two simple cases and loses the both-conditions residue;
- the **per-slot requirement**. A routine implementation defaults to a national average and loses the live group.

One thing survives cleanly: the two currencies do not add and the objective has no anchor. That is the residue worth sending. R_T was **not** measured numerically because no ground reading exists; it is recorded qualitatively as the enumerated set of dropped constraints. Treating the formula as identical to the metalaws would set R_T to zero and would be the dead-reckoning error Fig 4 :30 names.
