## Distribution: offer, do not push

- **Fig 10 :52**, *"Bring a characterised residual, never the source."* Send the shape claim and its refuter before any framework material.
- **Fig 2 :35**, *"Alignment is triangulation, not negotiation. A mirror cannot correct you."* Do not ask for agreement.
- **Fig 2 :40**, *"agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool."* Record the recipient's verdict on stage 1 before showing any theory, and never pool later agreement as corroboration.
- **Fig 9 :27-28**, *"describe it before you go looking, or any frame you pick will seem to fit"* Ask what they would expect to see if the shape claim were true before giving them the figure text.
- **Fig 2 :42**, *"Buy independence in proportion to the cost of being wrong."* One-to-one contact through public venues, not a list selected for likely agreement.

**Recommended order in a reply:** (1) the shape claim and four refuters; (2) the question "what would you count as a failure here"; (3) record their answer; (4) only then the links, with the note that the figures, not the summary, are the authority.

## The two-stage offer

1. **Stage 1:** send the residual (two currencies, unanchored objective) and the four pre-registered refuters. Ask for a failure, not an endorsement.
2. **Stage 2, opt-in only:** if they are curious, offer the three links above. Their stage-1 verdict stays separate from any later view of the framework.
3. **Stage 3:** offer the formula as a method to reuse, with the instruction that each user writes their own refuter and states their own mode before running it.

Stage 2 is opt-in by construction because a reader who has absorbed the framework before being asked for a verdict has joined the frame being checked. Stage 3 is permitted because a method is a generator, not a recording of one result.
