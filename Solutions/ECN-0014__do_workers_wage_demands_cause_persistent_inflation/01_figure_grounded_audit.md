## 0. The claim under audit, stated in effect terms

The question as posed is a forced choice between two statements about the same observed co-movement:

- **A:** workers' wage demands cause persistent inflation (a wage-push reading, with an ordered link from wages to prices).
- **B:** wages mainly respond to prices and labor-market conditions (an ordered link from prices and slack to wages, with the wage-to-price link treated as weak).

**R, the residual, stated as an effect rather than a theory.** What fails to resolve is the *direction of an ordered link inside a feedback process* when the only thing read off is a *symmetric co-occurrence*: wages and prices rise together and the co-movement persists. Three things are actually in contact with R: a prices-and-conditions to wage-demands link, a wage-costs to prices link, and at least one common source (aggregate demand, inflation expectations, supply and import shocks) that drives both. The question compresses all three into one `A or B`, which discards the structure that would decide anything.

- **Arity:** three arms, not two. Two ordered links plus at least one common cause. A and B each name one arm.
- **Symmetry:** the observation is symmetric. Under the conjunction bond, "swap the operands. Does the meaning change? No." (Fig 12 :59). A symmetric observation cannot decide an ordered conclusion; ordered bonds require "A then B; B needs A to have happened" (Fig 12 :72) or "is there a B before A fires? No." (Fig 12 :75). Fig 12 :5: "A set of ingredients is not one. The bond is the rest of the information, and it is the half nothing was recording."
- **Scale:** micro (a wage bargain in one firm or occupation), meso (sector, bargaining regime, import exposure), macro (an aggregate index), and across time (a one-period response versus a multi-period persistent process). The question collapses all four into one aggregate verb.
- **Slot indexing:** Fig 1 :19: "The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated." Fig 1 :22: "Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average." An economy-wide wage growth number is a pooled reading; the directional information lives in which wages moved, where, and under what contract.

**What an answer would look like, bound as an ordinary term before the run (formula step "Direction").** A checkable answer must state: (i) which ordered link is being measured; (ii) which slot (which wage measure, which price measure, which bargaining regime); (iii) which common cause is conditioned on; (iv) at which horizon (impact versus persistence); and (v) which instrument would reverse the verdict. An answer that does not bind all five is a preference for a direction, not a claim. This is the pre-registered shape of section 5.

## 3. Findings

### F1: The question is a cycle misread as a chain (decisive for the framing)

Fig 12 :121: "cycle. The output re-enters as input. The sign of the loop decides whether it closes, and a chain cannot close by construction."

The wage-price process is exactly this: an output (the price level) re-enters as an input to wage demands, and a wage settlement re-enters as a cost input to prices. Both arms of the question posit a chain with one direction. A chain cannot close by construction. So option A and option B are each a partial description of a typed cycle, and the sign of the loop, not the choice of a single direction, decides whether the process closes as persistence. Fig 13 :88-89: "The residual of the turn just closed is what the next turn composes against, so no two passes begin from the same place." That is why the second round of a wage round is not the first round: each pass begins from a residual the previous pass left.

World corroboration (secondary, low overlap with the figures): the wage-price spiral entry describes wage increases causing price increases "in a positive feedback loop"; the systems literature defines positive feedback as "A produces more of B which in turn produces more of A"; Blanchard's NBER abstract describes adjustment as "attempts by workers to maintain or increase their real wage and by firms to maintain or increase their markups of prices over wages." Three independently sourced descriptions of the same closed loop, in economics and in systems theory.

- **Mode:** `general` on the structure, `specified` on the prediction that both arms must be measured.
- **Triangulation:** `triangulated` (Fig 12 and Fig 13 on the framework side; positive-feedback theory and Blanchard on the world side, low overlap).
- **Refuter:** a single regime in which only one ordered link is operative in every subset, with no feedback and no common cause, and in which the dispute shrinks with effort. That would make it a chain after all and F1 would be withdrawn.

### F2: A symmetric co-movement cannot decide causal direction

Fig 2 :39: "Correlation has valence." Fig 2 :40: "disqualifying for a verdict: agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool".

The observation that wages and prices rise together is a conjunction. Fig 12 :59 identifies the symmetric bond by the test "swap the operands. Does the meaning change? No." An ordered bond has the opposite test, "swap them. Does it become a different thing? Yes." (Fig 12 :74). Swapping wage and price in a co-movement statement changes nothing; swapping them in a causal statement changes everything. The observation is therefore typed with the wrong bond to carry the conclusion. Symbols :38-40: R is "The gap between prediction and observation. The central quantity: everything the system does is driven by it. It is the aim, not only the error signal, and it is always a slice: what cannot be perceived yet is residual and simply cannot be read."

World corroboration from statistics (the genuinely low-overlap witness): the Granger causality entry states that "the Granger causality tests whether X forecasts Y" and that "As its name implies, Granger causality is not necessarily true causality." More decisively: "If both X and Y are driven by a common third process with different lags, one might still fail to reject the alternative hypothesis of Granger causality. Yet, manipulation of one of the variables would not change the other." That is Fig 2's collapse 2 (common cause) in the language of econometrics.

- **Mode:** `general` (a type constraint on what counts as evidence for direction).
- **Triangulation:** `triangulated` (Fig 2, Fig 12, Symbols table, and an independent statistical source).
- **Refuter:** an instrument that measures the ordered link directly, for example an exogenous, pre-announced wage shock that shifts wage demands while holding demand, expectations and supply fixed, and that produces a price response without a common cause. If that exists and F1's cycle is absent, the correlation-ceiling reading is too strong.

### F3: The common cause is a hidden shared source, and the question omits it

Fig 2 names "collapse 2: common cause (hidden shared source)" and gives the pattern: "one evidence pool", "they never talk, and still agree", "so the agreement is the pool talking, not two frames confirming each other" (Fig 2 :17-21). Wages and prices do not have to talk to each other to co-move; both range against the same shared source (demand pressure, expectations, import and energy prices) and the co-movement is the source talking.

Fig 2 :29: "An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks." The anchor here is realized inflation and the realized bargain, not the agreement between two series. Fig 2 :33: "The anchor must not update." If the common source is measured by the very series it is supposed to explain, the anchor has become a frame and panel 3 turns into panel 1: "everything drifts together, and the drift is coherent, which is why nobody notices" (Fig 2 :34).

World corroboration (high quality, distinct institution): the Federal Reserve FEDS note of 2026-11-09 writes that "the post-pandemic surge in inflation was extraordinary and the result of a confluence of factors, including reopening demand, fiscal stimulus, labor-market tightness, supply-chain disruptions, and high energy prices" and that the post-pandemic interactions "may partly reflect the co-movement of oil prices with broader global demand pressures." It adds that "inflation expectations depend on past inflation outcomes" in its backward-looking augmented Phillips curve, which is the mechanism by which a common source becomes persistent without any wage push being the origin. The BIS Bulletin 55 of 2022 agrees on the expectations channel: "Household inflation expectations have risen alongside higher inflation. These expectations matter for central banks since they affect consumption decisions and wage demands, shaping the public debate on inflation."

- **Mode:** `specified` (the common-cause domain is nameable and measurable in principle), not `grounded` (no shock measure was run here).
- **Triangulation:** `triangulated` (Fig 2 plus two independent institutional world sources, low overlap).
- **Refuter:** a design that blocks or conditions on the common source, for example a do-intervention on wage demands with demand and expectations held fixed, in which the wage-price co-movement disappears entirely and the wage-to-price link alone accounts for persistence. Then D3 is empty and F3 fails.

### F4: Aggregate wage growth is a pooled reading, not a verdict

Fig 1 :19 and :22 kill the economy-wide average as a directional instrument. Fig 10 :24: "the ground does not decay, the channel does". Fig 10 :25-26 names the channel failures: "stale percepts, dropped transients, saturated metrics, pooled readings". Fig 10 :27-28: "the output is not a verdict, it is you have lost touch". An average wage growth rate is a pooled reading that has lost exactly the thing the question asks about: who demanded what, under which contract, in which sector.

Fig 1 :28: "The gap is measured per object, not per step. A system that averages across objects has thrown away the only signal that says where to look." The relevant objects are bargains, occupations, sectors and contract types, not quarters of an aggregate index.

- **Mode:** `specified`.
- **Triangulation:** `triangulated` (Fig 1 plus Fig 10, two different figures with different concerns).
- **Refuter:** a demonstration that an aggregate wage series carries exactly the directional information that the distribution of settlements carries, such that conditioning on sector, contract type and occupation adds no residual. Empirically unlikely, but it is the stated falsifier.

### F5: Verdicts per domain, kept separate and never averaged

| domain | what the figures license | mode | status |
|---|---|---|---|

Fig 6 :14 names import as "the only operator that moves the wall". Fig 6 :23: "No frame escapes its own closure. The bound moves only on contact with a frame whose closure differs." The exogenous variation is that contact. Fig 6 :30: "The description travels. Fails by being sharpened into terms only we can read. Cast it in effect terms." ### F6: If the wage-push regularity keeps failing flat, refuse it rather than extend it

Fig 5 names a fifth kind of gap: "refuted: a term we hold is wrong; a term is owed that says so" (Fig 5 :53). Fig 5 :59: "a term whose residual PERSISTS AND DOES NOT TREND DOWN is the one to point the refusal at". The wage-push regularity is a candidate for this test: if across subsets and episodes it keeps predicting persistence and failing, and its residual does not trend down, the term to refuse is "wage demands are the origin of persistence", not some auxiliary parameter. Fig 2 :42: "Buy independence in proportion to the cost of being wrong." The cost of wrongly blaming wages is a policy error applied to the wrong link, so independence is worth buying here.

- **Mode:** `specified` (a rule about which regularity to refuse), with no claim that it has fired.
- **Triangulation:** `single-source` (Fig 5) on the refusal rule; the question of whether it has fired is a `gap` owed to the ground.
- **Refuter:** a wage-push specification whose residual trends down as subsets are added, in which case it is being repaired, not refused.

### F7: "Wage demands" is a term at the instrument-poor middle link

Fig 3 :26-27: having terms for what a situation offers and being able to say what would count as progress "has neither, so it attracts the least attention and the fewest instruments", and "A link with no instrument is not a link that works. It is a link nobody has looked at." Most wage data are *settlements*, not *demands*. A demand is a claim formed before the bargain; a settlement is the outcome after it. Using the latter as a proxy for the former is a reading below the break, and Fig 3 :23 calls it "a reading of nothing."

World corroboration: BIS Bulletin 55 notes that households are "more influenced by the prices of frequently purchased items" and that "their expectations of future inflation exceed those of professional forecasters", which is why a survey of perceived prices is not a clean reading of inflation expectations. The measurement of the demand itself is the missing middle link.

- **Mode:** `general`.
- **Triangulation:** `triangulated` (Fig 3 plus an independent institutional source).
- **Refuter:** a validated instrument that measures pre-bargain wage claims directly and separately from settlements, with a stated error band. Then the middle link has an instrument and F7 is downgraded to a data-availability note.

### F8: Agreement among wage-inflation researchers is one evidence pool

Fig 7 :13: "agreement compounds, evidence does not". Fig 7 :35: "A room's agreement is variation, not a verdict. Selection between rooms is where the ground votes." Ten papers using the same national accounts wage series are not ten frames; they are one frame with ten names. Fig 8 :32: "Closure is idempotent. Composing inside a closed set never leaves it, so no search, however long, adds an atom." More regressions on the same series do not add an atom; an instrument from a different closure does. Fig 8 :50-52: "Two frames can hold the same atom by convergence, reaching it separately, which is corroboration at maximum independence" but "Convergent derivation and adopted import look identical in the contents. Only the record of where each came from separates them".

- **Mode:** `general`.
- **Triangulation:** `triangulated` (Fig 7 and Fig 8).
- **Refuter:** two studies deriving the same directional verdict from genuinely disjoint evidence pools (one from contract-level bargain data, one from firm-level price microdata) with recorded provenance. That is corroboration at maximum independence and would upgrade the verdict.

### F9: The magnitude is `gap`, and unreachable is a verdict

No figure line returns a coefficient, a share of inflation "caused" by wages, or a lag length. Fig 9 :32: "you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself". Fig 13 :134: "Unreachable is a verdict here, not a silence". Fig 8 :39: "So a search that finds nothing is never a proof of absence." The honest report is that the figures constrain the *shape* of any admissible answer (a typed cycle, split union, per-slot measurement, conditioned common cause, named instrument) and leave the *magnitude* to the ground. Fig 13 :136-139: the framework "can say the answer is out of reach with the library as it stands, so something has to be imported before it is asked again" and "you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself, and narrowing gets you close while the ground decides."

Formula step 7 separates three questions: SEARCHED (is it in here and unfound?), INWARD (extend the instrument), OUTWARD (import from a frame whose closure differs). Here SEARCHED has been run inside the figure closure: no line yields a magnitude. INWARD is owed at D4 (a demand instrument). OUTWARD is owed at D1/D2 (exogenous wage and price variation from a different closure). Importing against a budget limit would add an unneeded atom, so none is invented here.

- **Mode:** `gap`.
- **Triangulation:** not applicable; this is a declared absence after a genuine re-census.

### F10: The mechanism of "persistent" is a loop sign, not a longer lag

The word "persistent" in the question is doing structural work. Fig 12 :121 says "The sign of the loop decides whether it closes". A one-off wage push that raises the price level once is a sequence. A wage-price loop with a positive sign re-enters its own residual and is a cycle. A negative sign (a margin squeeze that breaks the loop) is a different loop and closes differently. Conflating them is what makes the question look like a choice about speed when it is a choice about structure. The FEDS note corroborates the distinctness of the two: it separates "persistence" (the sum of the lags of core inflation) from "pass-through" (the response to an external price shock), finding both increased after the pandemic but by different mechanisms and with different timing. That is two coefficients, not one "persistence" number, exactly as the figure distinguishes loop sign from loop gain.

- **Mode:** `specified`.
- **Triangulation:** `triangulated` (Fig 12 plus a distinct institutional world source).
- **Refuter:** a demonstration in which the same loop sign accounts for both the own-lag persistence and the external pass-through with one coefficient and no loss of fit. Then the distinction collapses and F10 is withdrawn.

## 5. Pre-registered refuter (written before the conclusion)

A falsifier written before the run is a design instrument. The derived shape is refuted if any of the following holds:

1. **Single-chain refuter.** One ordered link, in every subset and episode, accounts for wage-price co-movement with no feedback and no common cause, and the literature's disagreement shrinks with effort. This kills F1 and the union reading (Fig 9 :4-5, Fig 12 :121).
2. **Instrument refuter.** An exogenous shift of wage demands with demand, expectations and supply held fixed produces no price response (kills the wage-to-price arm), or an exogenous shift of prices produces no wage-demand response (kills the price-to-wage arm).
3. **Aggregation refuter.** An aggregate wage series carries exactly the per-slot directional information of the distribution of settlements, so conditioning on sector, occupation and contract type adds no residual (kills F4).
4. **Averaging refuter.** A verdict from D1 can be averaged with a verdict from D4 without loss, or one domain's anchor can be substituted for another's (kills the separation rule, Fig 2 :29).
5. **Loop-sign refuter.** Own-lag persistence and external pass-through are shown to be the same coefficient under one mechanism with no loss of fit (kills F10).
6. **Wage-push trend refuter.** The wage-push specification's residual trends down as subsets are added, so the regularity is being repaired rather than refused (qualifies F6).

If any fires, the shape is withdrawn or narrowed. Until one is run, no part of this may be labelled `grounded`.

## 6. Recommendation

1. Do not answer `A or B`. Answer: which link, in which slot, at which horizon, conditioned on which common cause, and with what instrument.
2. Split the union per subset and per slot before searching (Fig 9 :4-5, :54). Keep D1 to D4 verdicts separate and unaveraged.
3. Replace "wage growth" with the distribution of wage settlements and claims, indexed per slot (Fig 1 :19, :22, :28). Treat an average as a budget filter, never as a witness (Fig 9 :60).
4. Name the common cause before naming a frame (Fig 9 :27-28). Demand, expectations, import and energy prices must be conditioned on, otherwise any dataset will seem to fit.
5. Measure the round trip from micro bargain to aggregate index and back, and report R_T rather than assuming the two scales carry the same information (Fig 4 :30).
6. Report which regularity is being refused, with the persistent-and-flat test written down in advance (Fig 5 :59).
7. Send the generator, that is the residual, the shape and the refuter, not a recording of the conclusion (Fig 4 :22).
