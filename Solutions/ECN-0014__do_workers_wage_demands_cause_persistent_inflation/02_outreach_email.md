# Outreach: check the output first, then decide about the framework

**Use:** one-to-one, or as a cover letter attached to a submission through the public venues in `03_public_contact_points.csv`. **Not** for bulk send.

**Hard precondition:** do not send until the shape claim and its refuters in `01_figure_grounded_audit.md` have been checked by a second seat (see `11_audit.md`). The output must survive validation or the two-stage offer collapses on contact.

---

**Subject:** One checkable claim about wages and inflation, with its refuter written in advance

> Hi [name],
>
> I am working on a narrow question in wage and inflation research and have one specific output I would like you to check against what you already know. I am not asking you to read a theory first, and I am not asking you to agree with it. If the output does not survive, there is nothing else to look at.
>
> **The output (`specified` on the structure, `gap` on the size).** The question is usually put as: do workers' wage demands cause persistent inflation, or do wages mainly respond to prices and labor-market conditions? That either/or fails its own test. Wages and prices form a feedback loop: the price level re-enters wage demands, and wage settlements re-enter firms' costs. The framework I am using calls this a cycle, and says the sign of the loop decides whether it closes, while a chain cannot close by construction. It also says a disagreement that does not shrink with effort, where each rule keeps working well on a different subset, is several well-formed questions and must be split rather than searched. So the answer I am offering is not A or B. It is: name the link, the slot, the common cause, the horizon and the instrument that would reverse you.
>
> **What is specifically checkable:**
> 1. The observed co-movement of wages and prices is symmetric; swapping the two words changes nothing about the observation. A symmetric observation cannot carry an ordered causal conclusion. That is a type error, not a matter of degree.
> 2. Wages and prices can co-move without causing each other, because both respond to demand, expectations and supply or import shocks. In statistics this is the common-cause problem: a Granger test can fire even when manipulating one variable would not change the other.
> 3. An economy-wide wage growth average is a pooled reading. The directional signal lives in which wages moved, under which contract, in which sector. An average is a filter for the budget, not a witness for the verdict.
>
> **What would refute this, written down before I sent it:**
> 1. One ordered link, in every subset and episode, accounts for the co-movement with no feedback and no common cause, and the dispute shrinks with effort.
> 2. An exogenous shift of wage demands, with demand, expectations and supply held fixed, produces no price response, or an exogenous shift of prices produces no wage-demand response.
> 3. An aggregate wage series carries exactly the directional information of the distribution of settlements, so conditioning on sector, occupation and contract type adds no residual.
>
> If any of those holds, I withdraw the shape claim. A one-line "this fails because..." is more useful to me than agreement. If you would rather not engage, no reply is needed.
>
> **Where I honestly stop:** the figures I use constrain the shape an answer must have; they return no coefficient, no share of inflation caused by wages, and no lag length. Those are a `gap` in my derivation, owed to a measurement, not something I can assert. "Unreachable is a verdict here, not a silence" is the rule I am following. I am not claiming this derivation settles the empirical question, and I am not claiming wages never push prices. Both arms of the loop are real; which dominates, where, and for how long is for the ground to decide.
>
> **If it survives, and only then:** here are the sources. The figures are the authority, not my summary of them.
> - The verbatim figure text with line numbers (local extract): `FRAMEWORK/reference/03-FIGURES_TEXT.md`
> - The canonical usage rule (local): `FRAMEWORK/reference/FIGURES_README_canonical.md`
> - The eight-step loop and mode rule (local): `FRAMEWORK/figures/THE_FORMULA.md`
> - Public figures repository: https://github.com/IsaiahN/tether/tree/general-agent/figures
>
> **If you want to use the machinery:** the formula is in the figures link. Run it on a problem you know well and can check against a ground. Write down what would refute your result *before* you run it, because the framework's own rule is that a loop cannot check the direction of its own question. A reported failure is more useful to me than a reported success.

## The two-stage offer, made explicit

**Stage 1.** The recipient checks the three checkable claims and the three refuters against their own knowledge of wage setting, price setting and identification. Their verdict on stage 1 is recorded before anything else is shown.

**Stage 2.** Only if they ask, the framework and its sources are offered. Any later agreement is a conversation, never corroboration, because the recipient now holds the interiors (Fig 10 :42, Fig 2 :40).

The order is not a courtesy. It is the only order in which the recipient's independence is still intact at the moment they give the verdict.
