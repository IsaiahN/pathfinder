# Supporting derivation: structure, bond order and union splitting

**Mode: `specified` on the structural derivations, `general` on the bond-typing rule, `gap` on any numerical order or magnitude. This is a simulated derivation checked against the figures, not a ground reading (see `05_what_shape_means.md` §7).**

## RESIDUAL

The question's `A or B` treats wage-push and wage-response as alternative single mechanisms. Two structures are owed: (1) the bond type that can carry a causal direction, and (2) the order in which the residual must be split, measured and refused.

## Q1: Cycle or chain?

- Fig 12 :121: "cycle. The output re-enters as input. The sign of the loop decides whether it closes, and a chain cannot close by construction."
- In the wage-price process the price level re-enters wage demands through expected real wages and indexation, and the wage settlement re-enters prices through unit labor cost. That is a closed loop.
- Fig 13 :88-89: "The residual of the turn just closed is what the next turn composes against, so no two passes begin from the same place." Each round of bargaining starts from the residual the previous round left, so persistence is a property of the loop, not a longer first link.
- World corroboration: the positive-feedback entry defines the form as "A produces more of B which in turn produces more of A", and Blanchard's abstract describes the two defending moves (real wage and markup) explicitly.
- **Result:** cycle, not chain. Both arms are real and must be measured separately. Mode `general` on the structure, `specified` on the prediction.

## Q2: Which bond can carry the causal claim?

- The observed co-movement is a conjunction. Fig 12 :59 gives the symmetric test: "swap the operands. Does the meaning change? No." Swapping "wages rose with prices" changes nothing.
- The causal claims are ordered. Fig 12 :72: "A then B; B needs A to have happened". Fig 12 :75: "is there a B before A fires? No." Swapping these changes the claim entirely.
- Fig 12 :111: "A vocabulary without bonds can name a state and cannot name a route to one." A vocabulary with only `+` can say that wages and prices were both high; it cannot say how one got there.
- Fig 12 :112: "A plan is a sequence. A goal is a comparison. Progress is a subtraction. A refutation is a negation, and that one is not a bond." "Persistent" is a comparison over time; "caused" is a production or sequence; "refuted" is a negation. Three different operators are hidden in the one word "cause".
- Operators table :68: "A relation that fails all seven is not an operator you are missing; it is a claim that has not been made yet." The symmetric co-observation fails the ordered tests; it is not a missing ordered bond, it is a claim that has not been made.
- **Result:** an `A or B` verdict attempts to read an ordered conclusion off a symmetric bond. That is a type error, not a data shortage. Mode `general`.

## Q3: Is the question a union or a partition, and what is the analogue?

- Fig 9 :54: "And when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again."
- Fig 9 :55-56: "A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones. A single lever clears the region where only one condition holds and leaves the intersections standing, because the second condition still binds."
- Fig 9 :57-58: "What remains is smaller and it is also categorically different: a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either. It stops being a mixture and becomes a specific compound."
- Applied: an indexed contract with strong demand is in both conditions at once. Prices drive wages mechanically through the index and wages drive prices through cost; the intersection is a compound with its own loop, not a harder instance of either arm.
- The chemical analogue in Fig 12 :117 is useful but must not be over-read: "isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour." Two wage-price episodes over the same atom set (same contract types, same slack, same shock set) but with a different mediating bond are isomers in this sense; two episodes that differ in which atoms are present are instead a **functional group** case, Fig 12 :123: "functional group. A substructure whose presence fixes the outcome. The group says what kind; the qualifier says which member."
- **Result:** union, with a real intersection. The isomer reading is `specified` and conditional on the atom set being held fixed; the functional-group reading is the weaker fallback. Mode `specified`.
- **Falsifier for Q3:** if a subset exists in which one condition alone holds in every episode and the union collapses to a partition, the unbundling requirement is too strong for that subset and the verdict must be re-censused per subset.

## Q4: What is the order between micro bargaining and the aggregate index?

- Fig 4 :22: "Only methods cross up. Only head starts come down."
- Fig 4 :27: "The transform between scales loses information, and that is the point." Fig 4 :28-29: "But it means the trip is not reversible, and something carried up and brought back down will not match what was sent."
- Fig 4 :30: "So take the round trip and measure the gap. Without it you are navigating by dead reckoning: confident about a position nobody checked."
- Sent up (x): the distribution of wage claims and settlements across slots. Abstraction (T_A): an economy-wide wage growth number. Concretisation (T_E): using that number to say something about a particular bargain. The round trip does not return the original; the gap is R_T.
- The Symbols table defines R_T as "The gap between what was sent up and what came back down: gap(x, (T_E ∘ T_A)(x)). Measures what the coarser description cannot hold."
- **Result:** R_T is nonzero by construction and must be reported, not assumed zero. The figure gives no numerical value, so the magnitude is `gap`. Mode `general` on the existence of the gap, `gap` on its size.
- **Falsifier for Q4:** show that the aggregate index preserves every per-slot directional distinction relevant to the causal question, so that T_E after T_A returns the original up to the measurement error already declared. Then R_T is zero for this quantity and the round-trip warning does not apply.

## Q5: What is the order between refusing and minting?

- `THE_FORMULA.md` step 2 routes the fifth kind, REFUTED, before the four missing-term bins: "the fifth by what is WRONG, and no arrangement of the other four reaches it".
- Fig 5 :53: "refuted: a term we hold is wrong; a term is owed that says so". Fig 5 :59: "a term whose residual PERSISTS AND DOES NOT TREND DOWN is the one to point the refusal at".
- Applied to this question: the regularity to test first is the held one, "a wage push is the origin of persistent inflation". If it persists flat across subsets, the owed term refuses it. Only after that is a new mechanism owed. Minting an alternative mechanism before the refusal would be out of order.
- Operators table :58: "a refutation is a negation, which is why a vocabulary without it can only accumulate." The refusal is the unary operator, not a subtraction of the wage arm from a list.
- **Result:** refuse before mint. Mode `specified` on the order, with no claim that the refusal has in fact fired.

## Q6: Which frames are independent enough to witness the verdict?

- Fig 2 :31: "Independence, between two frames, is how little they draw on the same evidence. Two people reading the same report are not two frames; two instruments pointed at the same thing are."
- Fig 8 :51-52: "Two frames can hold the same atom by convergence, reaching it separately, which is corroboration at maximum independence" but "Convergent derivation and adopted import look identical in the contents. Only the record of where each came from separates them".
- Fig 9 :60: "Use filters for the budget and witnesses for the verdict. Never let a filter hand you a verdict."
- Applied: ten papers using the same national accounts series are a budget filter on how much reading has been done, not ten witnesses. Two studies using disjoint evidence (contract-level bargain data versus firm-level price microdata) with recorded provenance would be two witnesses.
- **Result:** separation is by evidence pool, not by author count. Mode `general`.

## CONCLUSION

The structure is a cycle with a signed loop (Q1), the evidence capable of deciding direction must use an ordered bond and cannot be a symmetric co-observation (Q2), the question is a union with a real intersection that must be unbundled before search (Q3), the micro-to-macro transform carries a nonzero R_T (Q4), refusal precedes minting (Q5), and witnesshood requires disjoint evidence pools (Q6). Every one of these is a shape constraint. None returns a coefficient. The numerical question remains `gap`, reported as a verdict and not as silence (Fig 8 :39, Fig 13 :134).

## Global falsifier, written before the run

If all of the following are demonstrated in one coherent body of evidence, the whole supporting derivation fails and must be withdrawn: a single chain mechanism, verified in every subset with no feedback and no common cause; an ordered measurement made directly on the aggregate series without any per-slot conditioning; a round trip from micro to macro with R_T equal to zero; and a refusal test in which the held wage-push regularity's residual trends steadily down as subsets are added. Until such a body of evidence exists, the derivation stands as `specified` and `gap`, not `grounded`.
