# The Tether's answer on wage demands and persistent inflation

**Mode: `general` on the structural shape, `specified` on the per-domain applications, `gap` on which link dominates and on any magnitude. Nothing here is `grounded`.** This is a simulated derivation checked for internal consistency against the verbatim figures, not a ground reading. It cannot settle the question it reports on.

## 1. The residual it answers

The question asks for a choice between "wage demands cause persistent inflation" and "wages mainly respond to prices and labor-market conditions". Stated as an effect, what fails to resolve is the direction of an ordered link inside a feedback process when only a symmetric co-movement is read. Three arms are in contact with R: a prices-and-conditions to wage-demands arm, a wage-costs to prices arm, and a common source (demand, expectations, supply and import shocks) that drives both. The question collapses all three into one verb.

## 2. The shape

1. **It is a cycle, not a chain.** Fig 12 :121: "cycle. The output re-enters as input. The sign of the loop decides whether it closes, and a chain cannot close by construction." Prices re-enter wage demands through expectations and indexation; wage settlements re-enter prices through unit labor costs. Both arms are real. Persistence is the sign of the closed loop, not a longer first link (Fig 13 :88-89).
2. **The question is a union, not a partition.** Fig 9 :4-5 says to split a disagreement "where each rule keeps working well on a different subset" and Fig 9 :54 says to "unbundle before you search again". An indexed contract under strong demand sits in both conditions at once, which is a specific compound with its own loop (Fig 9 :57-58), not a harder case of either arm.
3. **A co-movement cannot decide direction.** Fig 2 :39: "Correlation has valence." The observation is a symmetric conjunction; swapping wages and prices changes nothing about it (Fig 12 :59). A causal claim needs an ordered bond: "A then B; B needs A to have happened" (Fig 12 :72) or "is there a B before A fires? No." (Fig 12 :75). Fig 12 :5: "A set of ingredients is not one. The bond is the rest of the information, and it is the half nothing was recording."
4. **The common source is a hidden shared source, and it is the omitted third domain.** Fig 2 :20: "so the agreement is the pool talking, not two frames confirming each other". Wages and prices need not talk to each other to co-move. Blanchard's 1985 abstract places the adjustment after an increase in aggregate demand; the 2026 Federal Reserve note attributes the post-pandemic surge to a confluence of demand, fiscal, labor-market, supply-chain and energy factors. Both are world corroboration of Fig 2's collapse 2, not overrulers of it.
5. **The residual is per slot.** Fig 1 :19: "The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated." Fig 1 :22: "Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average." Fig 10 :25-26 names "pooled readings" as a channel failure. The directional information lives in which wages moved, under which contract, in which sector, not in an economy-wide average.
6. **A "wage demand" is not a "wage settlement".** Fig 3 :25-27 names the instrument-poor middle link: having terms for what a situation offers and being able to say what would count as progress "has neither, so it attracts the least attention and the fewest instruments", and "A link with no instrument is not a link that works." A claim formed before the bargain is a different object from the outcome after it. Using the settlement as a proxy for the demand is a reading below the break, "a reading of nothing" (Fig 3 :23).
7. **Persistence and pass-through are different objects.** Fig 12 :121 again: the sign of the loop and the gain of the loop are distinct. The Federal Reserve note estimates own-lag persistence and oil-to-core pass-through separately, with different timing, which is the empirical counterpart of that distinction.
8. **The magnitude is `gap`.** No figure line returns a coefficient, a share of inflation "caused" by wages, or a lag length. Fig 9 :32: "you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself". Fig 13 :134: "Unreachable is a verdict here, not a silence". Fig 8 :39: "So a search that finds nothing is never a proof of absence."

## 4. What it explicitly does not say

- Not that wages never push prices. The wage-to-price arm is real (D2). The refused claim is that wage demands are the *origin* of persistence in general.
- Not that prices alone determine wages. The price-to-wage arm is real (D1), but so is the common cause (D3).
- Not a coefficient, a share, a lag length or a dominant mechanism in any named country or episode. Those are `gap`.
- Not a monetarist or a Keynesian verdict. Friedman's "It's the external manifestation of inflation, but not its source" and Blanchard's markup-and-real-wage adjustment are both world descriptions that fit different arms of the cycle; the figure census does not choose between schools, it types the link.
- Not that inflation expectations are a magic variable. BIS Bulletin 55 shows they are measured with error and biased by frequently purchased items (F7).
- Not a policy recommendation. The framework's rule is that a frame cannot check the direction of its own question, so no policy lever is asserted here.

## 5. What would settle it, the ground

Fig 2 :29: "An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks." The anchor is the realized wage bargain and the realized price outcome, not agreement among readers. Fig 6 :14 calls import "the only operator that moves the wall"; Fig 6 :23 says "The bound moves only on contact with a frame whose closure differs."

Owed instruments, stated in effect terms:

1. **A demand instrument.** A direct reading of pre-bargain wage claims, separate from settlements, with a stated error band. This fills the middle link of F7.
2. **An exogenous wage shift.** A wage demand shifted while demand, expectations and supply are held fixed. If prices do not move through the markup channel, the wage-to-price arm is dead in that slot.
3. **An exogenous price shift.** A price level shifted while slack and expectations are held fixed. If wage demands do not move, the price-to-wage arm is dead in that slot.
4. **A common-source block.** A design that conditions on demand, expectations and import and energy prices, so the shared source is screened off before either arm is estimated.
5. **A per-slot distribution.** Settlements indexed by sector, occupation and contract type, with the aggregate index treated as a filter for the budget, not as a witness (Fig 9 :60).
6. **A measured R_T.** The round trip from micro bargain to aggregate index and back, with the dropped information enumerated rather than assumed away (Fig 4 :30).

## 6. The honest stop

**Unreached, not unreachable.** The figure set constrains the shape of any admissible answer and returns no number. *The Hard Problem is About Source* (re-opened) gives the reason: "An instrument that can't distinguish these cases demonstrates its own inadequacy", and the question was settled "not by argument, but by the arrival of a better instrument." Here the better instrument is the source-discriminating design above. It is not held, so the answer stops at the limit of the available instruments and records the lineage so the stop is checkable. "Indiscernibility is not identity."

## 7. Pre-registered falsifiers (written before the conclusion)

1. A single chain mechanism works in every subset with no feedback and no common cause, and the dispute shrinks with effort.
2. An exogenous wage shift with the common source held fixed produces no price response, or an exogenous price shift produces no wage-demand response.
3. An aggregate wage series carries exactly the per-slot directional information of the distribution of settlements.
4. A verdict from D1 can be averaged with D4 without loss.
5. Own-lag persistence and external pass-through are one coefficient with no loss of fit.

Any one firing narrows or withdraws the shape. See `08_expert_verification_packet.md` for the checkable form.

## 8. One-paragraph version for outreach

> The Tether does not choose between "wages cause inflation" and "wages follow prices", because that either/or asks a chain question of a cycle. Wages and prices form a loop: prices re-enter wage demands through expectations and indexation, and wage settlements re-enter prices through costs and markups. Their observed co-movement is a symmetric reading, and a symmetric reading cannot tell you which way the arrows point, especially when demand, expectations and supply shocks drive both at once. So the answer is a shape rather than a number: split the union before searching, measure each link per slot rather than in an aggregate average, name the common cause, distinguish persistence from pass-through, and write down in advance the exogenous shift that would reverse you. The framework returns no share of inflation caused by wages and no lag length. That is a declared `gap`, not a silence, and it stays open until a source-discriminating instrument settles it.
