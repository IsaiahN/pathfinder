# Expert verification packet: the Tether on wage demands and persistent inflation

**What this is:** a claim in a form a wage or inflation researcher can check. Mode, derivation and refuters are stated before the conclusion, so a reader knows what to look for and what would settle it.

**Mode: `general` on the structural shape, `specified` on the domain applications, `gap` on dominance and magnitude. Not `grounded`.** No measurement at any scale is claimed here. This packet is a simulated derivation checked for internal consistency against the verbatim figures, not a ground reading.

## 1. The claim, in one paragraph

The question "do workers' wage demands cause persistent inflation, or do wages mainly respond to prices and labor-market conditions?" is a union compound and a cycle misread as a chain. Wages and prices form a signed loop: the price level re-enters wage demands through expectations and indexation, and wage settlements re-enter prices through unit labor cost and markups. Both arms are real and must be measured separately. The observed co-movement is a symmetric reading that cannot by itself decide an ordered causal conclusion, particularly because demand, expectations and supply or import shocks are a hidden shared source driving both. The figure-licensed answer is a shape, not a number: split the union per subset, measure each link per slot rather than in an aggregate average, name the common cause, separate loop sign from loop gain, and bind the exogenous shift that would reverse the verdict. Which link dominates, where and by how much is a `gap` owed to the ground.

## 2. Three checkable sub-claims

### C1: The cycle structure, checkable by reading Figures 12 and 13

- **Statement:** the wage-price process is a typed cycle, not a chain; a verdict that picks one direction as the whole story is structurally incomplete, and persistence is the sign of the loop.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 12 :121: "cycle. The output re-enters as input. The sign of the loop decides whether it closes, and a chain cannot close by construction." Also Fig 13 :88-89: "The residual of the turn just closed is what the next turn composes against, so no two passes begin from the same place."
- **Refuter:** demonstrate a single ordered link that operates in every subset with no feedback and no common cause, such that the disagreement shrinks with effort. That makes it a chain and C1 fails.

### C2: The observation is the wrong bond type for a causal verdict, checkable by reading Figures 2 and 12

- **Statement:** a wage-price co-movement is a symmetric conjunction. Swapping the operands leaves it unchanged. Causal claims require ordered bonds. Therefore a correlation, a Granger test on two series, or an agreement among studies reading the same index cannot by itself decide the direction.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 2 :40: "disqualifying for a verdict: agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool". Fig 12 :59: "swap the operands. Does the meaning change? No." Fig 12 :72: "A then B; B needs A to have happened". Also Fig 2 :20: "so the agreement is the pool talking, not two frames confirming each other".
- **What to check:** is the common-source pattern (collapse 2) a correct description of how demand, expectations and supply shocks enter wage and price determination in the researcher's own framework? World corroboration is secondary and recorded in `09_watson_answer.md`.
- **Refuter:** an ordered measurement made directly on the co-movement, for example an exogenous wage shift with demand, expectations and supply held fixed that moves prices through the markup channel with no common cause. If that works, C2's ceiling is too strong.

### C3: Per-slot measurement and a nonzero round trip, checkable by reading Figures 1, 10 and 4

- **Statement:** an economy-wide wage growth average is a pooled reading, not a directional instrument; the signal lives in the distribution of settlements across slots, and the micro-to-macro transform loses information that must be reported as R_T.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 1 :19: "The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated." Fig 1 :22: "Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average." Fig 10 :25-26: "stale percepts, dropped transients, saturated metrics, pooled readings". Fig 4 :30: "So take the round trip and measure the gap. Without it you are navigating by dead reckoning: confident about a position nobody checked."
- **Refuter:** show that an aggregate wage series preserves every per-slot directional distinction relevant to the question, so that T_E after T_A returns the original up to declared error. Then R_T is zero for this quantity and C3 is withdrawn.

## 3. What is explicitly not claimed

- Not that wage demands never push prices. The wage-to-price arm is real. The refused claim is that wage demands are the origin of persistence in general.
- Not that prices determine wages alone. The price-to-wage arm and the common source are both real.
- Not a coefficient, a share of inflation caused by wages, a lag length, a country verdict or a policy recommendation.
- Not a settlement between schools of thought. Friedman's "It's the external manifestation of inflation, but not its source" and Blanchard's real-wage and markup adjustment are treated as descriptions of different arms, not as rivals to be adjudicated by the figures.
- Not that the pre-bargain wage demand has been measured here. That instrument is owed (F7 in `01_figure_grounded_audit.md`).
- Not `grounded`. This packet is a candidate, and a perfectly provenanced candidate is still a candidate.

## 5. What would settle it

The anchor is the realized wage bargain and the realized price outcome, which do not change because a frame thinks otherwise (Fig 2 :29). Four instruments are owed, described in effect terms:

1. A direct reading of pre-bargain wage claims, separate from settlements, with a stated error band.
2. An exogenous wage shift with demand, expectations and supply held fixed, testing the wage-to-price arm.
3. An exogenous price shift with slack and expectations held fixed, testing the price-to-wage arm.
4. A design screening off the common source (demand, expectations, import and energy prices) before either arm is estimated.

Plus a per-slot distribution of settlements and a measured R_T for the micro-to-macro round trip.

## 6. What an expert can check in twenty minutes

1. Read Fig 12 :121 and Fig 9 :4-5 and :54. Three short passages. Decide whether cycle plus union is a better description of the phenomenon than either arm alone.
2. Check C2 against your own knowledge of identification: is a symmetric co-movement plus a shared shock source enough to defeat a causal verdict in your framework? Name a case where it is not.
3. Name one instrument that would discriminate wage push from price response in a slot you know well, or say why none is possible.
4. Send the refutation. A failure is more useful than an endorsement. If you disagree with the shape, say which of C1, C2 or C3 fails and on what evidence.
