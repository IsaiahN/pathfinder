# The Tether's answer on legal predictability

**Mode: `specified` on the per-domain claims, `general` on the structural shape. Not `grounded`.** This is a shape and a route, not a result. It is a simulated derivation checked against the figures (`05_what_shape_means.md` section 7), not a ground reading.

## 1. The residual it answers

A litigant must predict an outcome before committing resources. The gap between the rule as stated and the outcome as experienced is `R_s = |Γ(b,a) − o'|`, and it is indexed per case. Fig 1 :19: **"The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated."** The question is why that gap closes systematically in some orders and stays open in others, where the outcome tracks personal connection and ungoverned discretion. The answer is a union of three conditions, each with its own loop; the verdicts are kept separate and are never averaged.

## 2. The shape

1. **The applicable rule must not move with influence.** Fig 2 :29-30: **"An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks."** Fig 2 :30: **"If it moves when you push on it, it is not an anchor."** Where there is no anchor, decision-makers converge on *"whoever had the most influence"* (Fig 2 :13-15), which is exactly what "personal connections" means in effect terms. The derived requirement is invariance under swapping party identity and bench identity with case facts held fixed. That requirement does not name a legal family.
2. **The reason is the bond that makes a judgment composable.** Fig 8 :50-52: **"Convergent derivation and adopted import look identical in the contents. Only the record of where each came from separates them, and the surplus lives only where the closures differ."** A rule-following judgment and a favor-trading judgment can share facts, parties and outcome. Only the recorded reason distinguishes them, so reasons are structural, not decorative (Fig 12 :6: **"A recipe that names only its ingredients has recorded half of itself."**).
3. **Capability belongs to the bench and its habitat together.** Fig 11 :39: **"capability is a property of agent-and-habitat, never of the agent alone"** and :40: **"An improvement that does not change contact changes nothing, however much it improves."** The instruments are published reports, open court, a recorded decision, enforceable conflict declarations and an appellate path that can actually fire.
4. **Review must select, not mirror.** Fig 7 :35: **"A room's agreement is variation, not a verdict. Selection between rooms is where the ground votes."** Fig 7 :36: **"A chain of mirrors is one long room. The next room must differ from the last, and match the residual it surfaced."** An appellate tier that agrees because it shares the trial tier's evidence adds motion, not progress.
5. **Agreement inside one pool is not a second witness.** Fig 2 :40: **"agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool"**; Fig 8 :48-49: **"Two frames that overlap almost completely are one frame wearing two names."** The test uses benches with demonstrably separate evidence pools, not a chorus of insiders.
6. **The framework returns a shape, not a name.** Fig 9 :32: **"you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself."** Common law, civil law, codification and "judicial independence" are bindings from the legal frame, not derivations. Fig 13 :134: **"Unreachable is a verdict here, not a silence"**; the honest report is a kind of thing, then a stop at the instrument limit.

## 3. Verdicts per domain, kept separate

| domain | verdict | mode | status |

| 1, adjudication frame | an anchor must not move with influence; identity must not enter the deciding bond | `specified`, falsifiable | `triangulated` (Fig 2 with Fig 10) |

These are not added together into a predictability score. A system can pass domain 1 and fail domain 2; that is a specific compound, not a harder case of either (Fig 9 :57-58).

## 4. What it explicitly does not say

- Not that any legal family is predictable and another is not. No such derivation exists in the figures.
- Not that a national rule-of-law index measures an individual litigant's prospects. Fig 9 :60: **"Use filters for the budget and witnesses for the verdict. Never let a filter hand you a verdict."**
- Not that a published reason is truthful. Legal realism's objection is recorded: a reason may be a post hoc rationalisation. The claim is about the recorded bond, not about motive.
- Not that predictability is justice. Fig 2 :37-38 makes human judgment the right anchor for a question about people and only an instrument for a question about code correctness. The two verdicts stay separate.
- Not `grounded`. No court dataset was read in this derivation.
- Not that the three conditions are nature's own list. They are a visible choice of where to look, per *They're Scanning Books*: declaring the primitives makes the choice visible, not neutral.

## 5. What would settle it: the ground

A source-discriminating instrument, not a further argument. From *The Hard Problem is About Source*: an instrument that reads green *"cannot comment on the source"*; the spectrometer resolved it. Here the corresponding tests are:

- **Swap test:** matched case facts, swap party identity and then bench identity. If outcomes move with either swap, F1 fires and the invariance claim fails on that case.
- **Reason test:** compare two judgments with the same facts and outcome but different recorded bonds. If later courts cite them interchangeably with no trace of the difference, F3 fails.
- **Selection test:** measure the correlation between trial-level error and appellate correction. If it is zero and trial outcomes remain predictable, F5 fails.
- **Habitat test:** introduce an agent-only improvement with no change to record, review, reasons or conflict rules. If matched-case outcomes move, F4 fails, as Fig 11 allows it to.
- **Index test:** on cases where an aggregate index and the per-slot test disagree, see which predicts individual outcomes. If the index wins, F2 fails.

The pre-registered refuter is in `01_figure_grounded_audit.md` section 4 and was written before this conclusion. Fig 8 :39: **"So a search that finds nothing is never a proof of absence."** A null result is a report about the instrument, not a verdict of impossible.

## 6. The honest stop

**Unreached, not unreachable.** The three domain conditions came from the figures; their status in any actual court came from no measurement here. The derivation stops at the limit of the instruments available in this run and records the lineage so the stop is checkable. Fig 13 :134: **"Unreachable is a verdict here, not a silence"** and :138-139: **"narrowing gets you close while the ground decides."** Indiscernibility is not identity. A second seat that shares this evidence pool is a mirror with a different name, not corroboration (Fig 2 :35).

## 7. One-paragraph version for outreach

> The Tether does not name a legal family and does not claim that a country score predicts your case. It says that predictability is a shape with three separate conditions: the applicable rule must not move when a party pushes on it, so outcomes must be invariant under swapping who the parties and the judge are; the written reason must be recorded as the bond that makes a judgment usable as precedent, because a rule-following decision and a favor-trading decision look identical in the outcome; and the bench must sit in a habitat with published reports, declared conflicts and an appeal that selects against trial error rather than mirroring it. Agreement among people who share one social or professional evidence pool carries no verdict weight. What the derivation offers is that shape, five refuters written in advance, and the tests that would close it. The verdict in each domain stays separate, and the question of justice is not answered here.
