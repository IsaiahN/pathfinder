## 0. The residual, stated in effect terms

A litigant must predict the incidence of public force through a court before committing money, time and reputation. Write the case as a slot `s`. The prediction is `Γ(b,a)` from the declared rules plus the recorded decisions; the observation is `o'`, the outcome that actually arrived. The residual is `R_s = |Γ(b,a) − o'|`. Fig 1 :19: **"The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated."** The question asks why `R_s` systematically closes in some legal orders and stays open in others, where the same case yields a different outcome depending on who the litigant knows and who decides.

In effect terms, here is what each side predicts before any theory is named:

| observation | predictable order | connection-dependent order |
|---|---|---|
| swap litigant identity, hold case facts fixed | outcome unchanged (symmetry) | outcome changes |
| swap decision-maker, hold case facts fixed | outcome unchanged | outcome changes |
| written rule is pushed by a party | rule does not move | rule moves with influence |
| reasons published and recorded | yes, checkable | absent or private |
| appeal | selects against trial error | mirrors the trial result |

**Binding the sought-for shape as an ordinary term (formula, "Direction").** Written down before the run: *an answer will name the condition that makes an outcome invariant to who decides and whom they know, while leaving room for reasoned judgment.* This makes the preference refusable by the same test as everything else. If the derivation can only produce a list of good legal families, that is a failure of the binding and is recorded in the retraction log.

The question is **not** "which legal family is best" and **not** "are judges corrupt." Both were considered first and both were re-censed away (section 6).

## 1. Domain census (before choosing any figure line)

### R by arity, symmetry, scale

- **Arity:** indexed per case slot `s`, not per system. Fig 1 :19 and :22: **"Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average."** A national predictability score is a filter for the budget, not a witness for the verdict (Fig 9 :60).
- **Symmetry:** a predictable outcome is invariant under swapping the identities of the parties and the decision-maker. That is the symmetric-bond test: *"swap the operands. Does the meaning change? No."* (Fig 12 :59). A connection-dependent outcome is ordered: which operand is first, the connected party, changes the term. Fig 12 :74: *"swap them. Does it become a different thing? Yes."*
- **Scale:** first order is one decision; second order is review and appeal; third is the legal order as a population with a membrane between the single bench and the court system (Fig 4 :19-20: **"Only the actors, the currency and the anchor change."**).

### One line per domain: actors / currency / anchor

**Domain 1, the adjudication frame (one decision).** Actors: judge or magistrate, parties, counsel. Currency: a decision, that is an outcome plus its stated reasons. Anchor: the enacted rule and the recorded precedent, which must not move when a party pushes on it. Fig 2 :29-30: **"An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks."** Fig 2 :30: **"If it moves when you push on it, it is not an anchor."** A predictable decision ranges against a pinned anchor (Fig 2 :24-27). A connection-dependent decision has no anchor: Fig 2 :13-15: **"no anchor; each updates on the others"**, **"they converge, and what they converge on is whoever had the most influence."** Personal connection is literally "the most influence" in the anchorless panel. Verdict: `triangulated` (Fig 2 panels 1 and 3 with Fig 10 :59-60).

**Domain 2, the institutional habitat (the conditions of contact).** Actors: registries, law reports, open court, the bar, appellate benches, conflict-of-interest rules, anti-corruption bodies, legal aid. Currency: trace, meaning published reasons, recorded cases, and review that can actually fire. Anchor: the ground, what settles a real case, which does not decay; the channel does (Fig 10 :24). Fig 11 :39: **"capability is a property of agent-and-habitat, never of the agent alone"** and :40: **"An improvement that does not change contact changes nothing, however much it improves."** Verdict: `triangulated` (Fig 11 with Fig 7 :35-36).

**Domain 3, the observer frame (litigant, researcher, market, journalist).** Actors: those trying to learn the rule from outcomes. Currency: evidence and residual. Anchor: outcomes, never agreement. Fig 8 :47-49: **"Independence is one quantity read twice."**, **"Two frames that overlap almost completely are one frame wearing two names."** A circle of connected insiders sharing one social pool is one frame with many names; their agreement is *"the pool talking"* (Fig 2 :20-21). Verdict: `triangulated` (Fig 8 with Fig 2 :31-32, :40).

### Partition or union

This is a **union of overlapping conditions**, not a partition. A predictable order needs an unmoving anchor AND a habitat that keeps it reachable AND observers who can tell separately. Fig 9 :54-55: **"And when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again."**, **"A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones."** Fig 9 :56-58 adds the consequence: a single lever clears the region where only one condition holds and *"leaves the intersections standing, because the second condition still binds."* This is why "appoint honest judges" and "write a better code" each fail in the intersections. Unbundled, the three domains get one loop each; their overlaps are compounds with their own loop.

### Order by dependency (Fig 3)

1. **Perception:** can the system tell cases apart and tell them apart from earlier ones? Fig 3 :4-5.
2. **Vocabulary:** does it have terms for what a case affords, what can be moved, opened, joined, blocked? Fig 3 :6-8.
3. **Objective:** can it say what would count as a correct decision in its own terms? Fig 3 :9-11.
4. **Planning:** a route to that objective under incomplete information. Fig 3 :12-13.
5. **Learn and carry:** name what worked, verify it, carry it to the next case. Fig 3 :14-15.

Fig 3 :19: **"Optimising a link that already works cannot rescue one that does not."** Connection-dependence typically breaks at the link between 2 and 3: the vocabulary of "what a case affords" becomes "who you know", and there is no instrument for "what would count as progress." Fig 3 :25-27: **"having terms for what a situation offers and being able to say what would count as progress, has neither, so it attracts the least attention and the fewest instruments."** Fig 3 :27: **"A link with no instrument is not a link that works. It is a link nobody has looked at."**

## 3. Findings

Every quote below is verbatim from `03-FIGURES_TEXT.md`. Mode is stated per finding. Each finding carries its own refuter.

### F1: An anchor that does not move, versus convergence on influence (domain 1)

Fig 2 :29-30: **"An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks. A measurement, an experiment, a counter, a market, a patient outcome."** Fig 2 :30: **"If it moves when you push on it, it is not an anchor."** Fig 2 :33: **"The anchor must not update."** Against this, the anchorless panel is Fig 2 :13-15: **"no anchor; each updates on the others"**, **"they converge, and what they converge on is whoever had the most influence."** Fig 2 :35: **"Alignment is triangulation, not negotiation. A mirror cannot correct you."**

A predictable legal order is one where the written rule plus the recorded decisions function as an anchor that the parties and the bench range against separately. A connection-dependent order is panel 1: the applicable rule is whatever the network converges on, and influence decides the convergence. Fig 10 :59: **"an interest in the outcome is unavoidable, an interest in the reading is disqualifying"** and :60: **"Where a stake cannot be removed, declare it."** Recusal and declared conflicts are not etiquette. They are the institutional form of declaring a stake before it becomes the reading.

**Mode:** `specified` (an invariance prediction; no case-level measurement). **Triangulation:** `triangulated`, Fig 2 panels 1 and 3 with Fig 10 :59-60 (low overlap: anchor geometry versus seat ethics).

**Refuter:** show a matched pair of cases where the applicable rule demonstrably changes with a party's influence while the outcome stays invariant across swapping connected and unconnected litigants. That breaks the anchor prediction. Or show a decision-maker whose declared private stake produces no shift in outcomes across matched pairs, which breaks the reading-disqualification claim.

### F2: The residual is per case, so predictability is not an average (domains 1 and 3)

Fig 1 :19: **"The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated."** Fig 1 :22: **"Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average."** Fig 9 :60: **"Use filters for the budget and witnesses for the verdict. Never let a filter hand you a verdict."**

A country-level rule-of-law index is a filter that decides which jurisdiction gets studied first. It is not a witness for whether a particular litigant can predict a particular outcome. The live signal is the per-case residual, that is the variance in outcomes for matched case facts across changes in party identity and bench identity.

**Mode:** `general` (structural), with a `specified` prediction per case. **Triangulation:** `triangulated`, Fig 1 with Fig 9 :60.

**Refuter:** produce an aggregate index that predicts individual matched-case outcomes as well as the per-slot test, on cases where the index and the slot disagree. If the average carries the live signal, the figure distinction is wrong here.

### F3: The bond is not recoverable from the outcome (domains 1 and 3)

Fig 8 :50-52: **"Convergent derivation and adopted import look identical in the contents. Only the record of where each came from separates them, and the surplus lives only where the closures differ."** Fig 10 :62-63: **"A ruling carries provenance like an atom. The residual it answered, the reasoning, the date, in a record the seats below can read."** Fig 12 :6: **"A recipe that names only its ingredients has recorded half of itself."** Fig 12 :111-113: **"A vocabulary without bonds can name a state and cannot name a route to one. A plan is a sequence. A goal is a comparison. Progress is a subtraction."**

A judgment that followed the rule and a judgment that rewarded a connection can have identical parties, identical facts and an identical outcome. The content does not separate them. Only the written reasons do. Published reasons are therefore not decoration added after the decision; they are the typed bond that makes the judgment composable as precedent. Without the bond, the next case cannot tell which molecule it is inheriting.

**Mode:** `specified`, ungrounded. **Triangulation:** `triangulated`, Fig 8 :50-52 with Fig 12 :6 and Fig 10 :62-63 (three distinct figures, low overlap: import provenance, notation, and seat record).

**Refuter:** find two judgments with identical facts and outcomes but different legal reasoning that are later cited interchangeably as precedent, with no trace in the record distinguishing them. That would show the bond carries nothing downstream.

### F4: Capability lives in the habitat, not in the hero (domain 2)

Fig 11 :39: **"capability is a property of agent-and-habitat, never of the agent alone"** and :40: **"An improvement that does not change contact changes nothing, however much it improves."** Fig 10 :24: **"the ground does not decay, the channel does"** and :27-28: **"the output is not a verdict, it is you have lost touch"**, :29: **"→ keep the anchor reachable"**.

A reform that trains better judges without changing the record, the reasons, the review path or the conflict rules has not changed contact. It improves the agent in isolation. The instruments that matter are the habitat: published reports, open court, recorded reasons, independent review, security of tenure and enforceable conflict rules. Fig 11 :48: **"You do not invent the list. You read it off the world, and what you cannot perceive or measure yet is the residual."**

**Mode:** `specified`. **Triangulation:** `triangulated`, Fig 11 with Fig 10 :24-29 (agent-and-habitat versus channel maintenance).

**Refuter:** Fig 11 :72 allows the direct one. Show an agent-only improvement that moves the ground, that is a change to a single decision-maker that changes outcomes on matched cases without any change to record, review, reasons or conflict rules. The capability claim would then be false.

### F5: Appeal is selection between rooms, or it is a mirror (domain 2)

Fig 7 :35: **"A room's agreement is variation, not a verdict. Selection between rooms is where the ground votes."** Fig 7 :36: **"A chain of mirrors is one long room. The next room must differ from the last, and match the residual it surfaced."** Fig 7 :13: **"agreement compounds, evidence does not"** and :32: **"→ one long room: motion, no progress"**.

A predictable order has an appellate tier whose cases differ from the trial tier and are selected against the residual the trial surfaced. A rubber-stamp appellate court is `ρ ≈ 1`: it agrees because it draws on the same evidence, and the chain is one long room. Fig 7 :37-39 names the two factors that must both be nonzero: bracket fidelity across the handoff and ground pressure on the room.

**Mode:** `specified`. **Triangulation:** `single-source` for the figure definition (Fig 7), with an empirical check still unrun.

**Refuter:** show an appellate tier whose reversals are uncorrelated with trial-level error (zero ground pressure) while outcomes on matched cases remain fully predictable. That breaks the selection-between-rooms requirement.

### F6: Insiders agreeing are not two witnesses (domain 3)

Fig 2 :31-32: **"Independence, between two frames, is how little they draw on the same evidence. Two people reading the same report are not two frames; two instruments pointed at the same thing are."** Fig 2 :40: **"agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool"** and :42: **"Buy independence in proportion to the cost of being wrong."** Fig 8 :47-49: **"Independence is one quantity read twice."**, **"Two frames that overlap almost completely are one frame wearing two names."**

Two judges drawn from the same social circle, or two experts who read the same law reports and the same gossip, are one frame wearing two names. Their agreement on hard cases is not corroboration of predictability. It is the pool talking. The cheap instrument here is a matched-pair outcome test using benches whose evidence pools are demonstrably separate.

**Mode:** `general` (structural) with a `specified` prediction about agreement. **Triangulation:** `triangulated`, Fig 2 with Fig 8.

**Refuter:** exhibit two decision-makers with zero shared evidence pool whose agreement rate on hard cases is no better than two members of the same pool. Then independence is not doing the work the figures assign it.

### F7: This is a union, so one lever cannot clear the intersections (cross-domain)

Fig 9 :54-58: **"And when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again."**, **"A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones. A single lever clears the region where only one condition holds and leaves the intersections standing, because the second condition still binds."** The result is *"a specific compound"* (Fig 9 :58), not a harder case of any one condition.

The three domains overlap. An unmoving rule with no published reasons is only partially predictable; published reasons with a mirror appellate tier are only partially predictable. Each overlap needs its own loop. This is why the outreach packet carries three checkable sub-claims rather than one grand claim.

**Mode:** `general`. **Triangulation:** `single-source` for the union rule (Fig 9), supported by the domain census itself.

**Refuter:** a single institutional intervention that removes connection-dependence even with the other two conditions demonstrably absent. That would show the conditions are a list, not a union.

### F8: The framework returns a shape, not the name of a legal family (limit)

Fig 9 :32: **"you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself"** and :30-31: **"your words are borrowed too, so describe the effect rather than the cause."** Fig 13 :134: **"Unreachable is a verdict here, not a silence"** and :138-139: **"you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself, and narrowing gets you close while the ground decides."**

The derived answer is a shape: invariance of outcome under identity swaps, a reason-bearing bond, a habitat that keeps the anchor reachable, and review that selects against surfaced residuals. It is not "common law", "civil law", "codification", or "judicial independence" as a name. Naming one of those as the answer is a binding from the legal frame onto a held term, not a derivation from the figures. Fig 8 :53: **"Residual first, frame second."**

**Mode:** `general`. **Triangulation:** `triangulated`, Fig 9 with Fig 13 and Fig 8 :53.

**Refuter:** derive from the figures which specific legal family must be predictable, without importing an empirical premise. If that derivation succeeds, the shape-not-a-name limit is wrong here.

### F9: The strain test: what "anchor" can and cannot mean here (honest limit)

Fig 2 :36: **"An anchor is legitimate when the question is constitutively about it."** Fig 2 :37-38: **"Human judgment is the right anchor for is this a good answer for a person, because the question is about people. It is only an instrument for is this code correct, and instruments can be wrong on axes they do not measure."**

A legal case is constitutively about people *and* about rules. If the written rule is made the sole anchor, the derivation answers predictability but silences the question of justice. If human judgment is made the anchor, the derivation answers a different question and cannot deliver per-case invariance. The figures require a re-census, not a smoother reading: the anchor here is a **composite** of enacted rule, recorded precedent and written reasons, and it is legitimate for the question "what does the law require in this case", not for "is this outcome good". This is a limit of the answer, not a strain in the figures. Whether a predictable system can be predictably unjust is decided by the ground, not by this run.

**Mode:** `general`. **Triangulation:** `single-source` for the anchor-legitimacy clause (Fig 2 :36-38); recorded as a limit.

**Refuter:** produce a figure line that makes a legal order's justice and its predictability the same quantity. Until then the two verdicts stay separate and are never averaged.

## 4. Pre-registered refuter

Written before the conclusion, per Seat Map :287: *"a falsifier written before the run is a design instrument, not only an honesty device."* The shape claim is refuted if any of the following holds:

1. A matched-pair study shows outcomes invariant to party identity and bench identity while the applicable rule demonstrably moves with influence (breaks F1).
2. A jurisdiction has no published reasons yet outcomes remain predictable under the matched-pair test (breaks F3).
3. An appellate tier shows no correction of trial error, `ρ ≈ 1`, yet trial outcomes remain predictable (breaks F5).
4. An agent-only improvement moves the ground (breaks F4 and the habitat claim, as Fig 11 :72 permits).
5. An aggregate index predicts individual case outcomes as well as the per-slot test on disagreeing cases (breaks F2).

If any fires, the corresponding finding is withdrawn or narrowed and the retraction log is updated. Until one is run, no part of this is labelled `grounded`. A search that finds no refuter is not proof that none exists: Fig 8 :39 **"So a search that finds nothing is never a proof of absence."**

## 5. Recommendation

1. Measure per case, not per country. Use matched pairs that swap party and bench identity while holding case facts fixed (F2).
2. Treat published reasons as the primary datum, not as commentary on the outcome. An outcome without a reason is an unlabelled artifact (F3).
3. Reform the habitat, not the hero: record, open court, independent review, declared conflicts (F4, F5).
4. Do not pool insider agreement as corroboration; buy independence in proportion to the cost of being wrong (F6).
5. Keep the three domains separate; do not average the verdicts (F7).
6. Use the public institutional submission routes in `03_public_contact_points.csv` and ask for a refutation rather than belief. Fig 4 :25: **"A recording carried upward looks like knowledge and is a description of one occasion."** Send the shape and the refuter, not the conclusion.

### Strain test after re-census

### Updated verdict

The shape survives across three domains: an unmoving composite anchor (domain 1), a habitat with recorded reasons and selecting review (domain 2), and observers who do not mistake shared-pool agreement for evidence (domain 3). The specific names, the single-lever story, and the index-as-verdict reading do not. Every verdict is `specified` or `general`; none is `grounded`. The strongest figure-grounded point for outreach is F3, because it is checkable from a single rule of law report without accepting any part of the framework.
