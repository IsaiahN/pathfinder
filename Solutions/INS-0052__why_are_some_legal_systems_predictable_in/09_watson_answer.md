## QUESTION

Decide, in effect terms, what makes the per-case residual close in some legal orders and stay open in others where outcomes depend on personal connection or ungoverned discretion. Option A treats predictability as a property of a legal family or of individual judges. Option B treats it as a shape with three separable conditions. What each leads to: A names a frame before describing the residual, and spends the independence of the people asked to check it; B offers a per-case invariance test, a recorded-bond requirement, a habitat requirement, refuters written in advance, and a clean separation between predictability and justice.

## DOMAIN CENSUS

R by arity, symmetry and scale, then one line per domain with actors / currency / anchor, question type, and dependency order.

- **R (the residual).** For case slot s, `R_s = |Γ(b,a) − o'|`: the gap between the rule as stated and the outcome as experienced. Arity: one case at a time, indexed per slot. Symmetry: a predictable outcome is invariant under swapping party identity and bench identity, which is the symmetric-bond test; a connection-dependent outcome is ordered, so which operand is first changes the term. Scale: first order is one decision, second order is review, third is the court system as a population. Fig 1 :19 "The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated." So an index is a filter for where to look, not a witness for a case.
- **Domain 1, adjudication frame.** Actors: judge, parties, counsel. Currency: a decision, outcome plus reasons. Anchor: enacted rule, recorded precedent and written reasons as one composite that must not move when a party pushes. Question type: partition boundary, which compositions are allowed into the deciding molecule. Verdict: `triangulated`.
- **Domain 2, institutional habitat.** Actors: registries, reports, open court, bar, appellate bench, conflict rules. Currency: trace, published reasons and review that fires. Anchor: the ground, which does not decay; the channel does. Question type: union with domain 1, because an unmoving rule with no record is only partially predictable. Verdict: `triangulated`.
- **Domain 3, observer frame.** Actors: litigants, researchers, businesses, journalists. Currency: evidence and residual. Anchor: outcomes, never agreement. Question type: independence of evidence pools. In this simulated run all seats draw on one evidence pool, so this domain's independence is zero. Verdict: `triangulated`.
- **Order by dependency.** Perceive and record cases (Fig 3 :4-5), acquire terms for what a case affords (Fig 3 :6-8), say what counts as a correct decision (Fig 3 :9-11), plan a route (Fig 3 :12-13), then verify and carry (Fig 3 :14-15). Connection-dependence typically breaks at the link between vocabulary and objective, where there is no instrument (Fig 3 :27: "A link with no instrument is not a link that works. It is a link nobody has looked at.").

Strain test: Fig 2 :36-38 makes an anchor legitimate only when the question is constitutively about it. A case is about people and about rules, so the composite anchor governs "what the law requires", not "is this good". That is a limit, not a strain, and the two verdicts are not averaged.

## VERDICT (from the figures)

The shape survives; the names do not. Per case, an applicable rule that moves with influence is not an anchor, so the outcome must be invariant under swapping party and bench identity. The recorded reason is the bond that makes a judgment composable as precedent, because a rule-following judgment and a favor-trading judgment are indistinguishable in the outcome. Capability belongs to bench and habitat together, and review must select against surfaced residual rather than mirror it. Insider agreement carries zero verdict weight. No legal family is named, a country index is a budget filter, and predictability is not justice. All per-domain verdicts are `specified` or `general`; none is `grounded`. Each domain was triangulated from two figures with low overlap; the appeal definition and the honest-stop line remain `single-source` inside the framework.

- **cost if wrong:** bounded and locatable. The packet states its mode, writes five refuters before the conclusion and records the lineage, so an error is findable rather than invisible. The real cost is spending the independence of outside readers by sending a recording instead of a generator. Fig 2 :42 "Buy independence in proportion to the cost of being wrong." That cost is avoidable by leading with the per-case shape and its refuter and by recording the verdict before showing any theory.
- **undoable:** yes. The three conditions are independent, so a refutation removes one without touching the others. A sent claim can be superseded by a corrected packet. No figure line and no primitive has to be withdrawn to undo a domain claim. The legal-realism conflict was already absorbed by narrowing C2 to a recorded-bond claim, with the motive question left open.
- **reversed by:** a ground reading, not agreement. A matched-pair dataset whose outcomes move with party or bench identity while the rule stays fixed would break the anchor claim; two same-outcome judgments with different bonds cited interchangeably would break the bond claim; an agent-only reform that moves matched-case outcomes would break the habitat claim, as Fig 11 permits; an appellate tier with zero correction of trial error yet predictable outcomes would break the selection claim; an aggregate index that beats the per-slot test on disagreeing cases would break the residual claim. Until one of these fires, the verdict stands as `candidate`.
