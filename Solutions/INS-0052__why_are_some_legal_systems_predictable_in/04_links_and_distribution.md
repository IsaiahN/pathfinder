## Wider literature that bears on the claim

- Holmes 1897 is the original prediction thesis in law. If "law" means "what courts will do in fact", then predicting courts is the business of law, and the per-case residual is the right unit of analysis. It corroborates F2 and is low overlap with the figures.
- Waldron's SEP entry supplies the independent vocabulary: predictability, publicity, stability, a right to reasons. It corroborates F1 and F3 without using the framework, so it is the strongest external check in the packet.
- Procedural justice (Leventhal, Tyler) supplies "bias suppression" and "correctability" as measured properties of a process. It corroborates F4 and F5 from social psychology rather than jurisprudence.
- Legal realism supplies the honest objection: if judges are strongly influenced by extra-legal factors, then a reason-giving requirement may record a post hoc justification rather than the cause. This is not treated as a refutation. It is an open question about what a published reason discriminates, and it is why F3 is stated as a bond claim, not a motive claim.
- The closure-operator page corroborates Fig 8 :32 from mathematics. One evidence pool note: the figure itself cites closure as definitional, so this is agreement by convergence from a low-overlap field, not a second witness to a contested empirical fact.

A failed fetch is not evidence of absence (Fig 8 :39: *"So a search that finds nothing is never a proof of absence."*). It is a gap in this run's reading, and it is recorded so a later reader can close it.

## Distribution: offer, do not push

Figure-grounded reasons:

- **Fig 4 :22-25**: *"Only methods cross up. Only head starts come down. A recording carried upward looks like knowledge and is a description of one occasion."* Sending the full architecture before a residual is described sends a recording, not a generator. Lead with the per-case shape and its refuter; offer the links after.
- **Fig 10 :42**: *"A verifier that can reconstruct the claim cannot verify it."* A recipient who has absorbed the framework before giving a verdict has joined the frame being checked.
- **Fig 9 :27-28**: *"describe it before you go looking, or any frame you pick will seem to fit."* Ask what they would expect to see if the shape claim were true before giving them the article.
- **Fig 2 :40**: *"agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool"*; and Seat Map :163, *"High correlation is disqualifying for a verdict and efficient for a convention."* A send selected for likely agreement produces a convention, not corroboration.

**Recommended order in a reply:** (1) the matched-pair shape claim and its five refuters; (2) ask what the recipient would count as a failure and record the answer; (3) only then the links, with the note that the figures, not the summary, are the authority; (4) only on request, the formula as a reusable method with a user-written refuter and mode.
