# Judgments as molecules: isomers, bonds and order in legal predictability

**Mode: `specified`.** This is a simulated derivation checked against the figures, not a ground reading (see `05_what_shape_means.md` section 7).

## RESIDUAL

The primary audit derived that the recorded reason is the bond that makes a judgment composable as precedent (F3). Two questions are owed: (1) under Fig 12, are two judgments with the same facts and the same outcome different substances, and (2) what order governs a legal procedure, and which operator lets a system refuse a precedent rather than merely accumulate it?

## DOMAIN CENSUS

Atoms held in the legal frame: case facts, parties, the enacted rule, the recorded precedent, the decision-maker, the outcome. Bonds held: the seven, plus negation. No new primitive is available without import. The residual is indexed per case, so the molecule in question is one judgment, not a court system.

## DERIVATION

### Q1: Is a judgment a molecule, or just a list of ingredients?

Fig 12 :5: **"A bond is the typed relation between two operands. There are seven, they are not interchangeable, and which one holds is not recoverable from the operands."** Fig 12 :6: **"A recipe that names only its ingredients has recorded half of itself."**

A bare case report lists parties, facts and outcome. That is the ingredient list. It cannot tell a later court which relation connected the rule to the outcome. The written reason supplies the typed relation: sequence (`→`), comparison (`⋛`), subtraction (`−`), or identity (`≡`). Without it the judgment is a state, not a route to one. Fig 12 :111: **"A vocabulary without bonds can name a state and cannot name a route to one."**

Fig 10 :62-63: **"A ruling carries provenance like an atom. The residual it answered, the reasoning, the date, in a record the seats below can read."** That sentence is the legal system's own version of the figure's requirement: the record is not documentation of the judgment, it is part of the judgment.

### Q2: Are two such judgments isomers?

Fig 12 :117: **"isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."**

Take two judgments with identical facts, identical parties and an identical outcome. In the first, the rule was applied through a recorded sequence (`→`): the rule was read, the facts were subsumed, the outcome followed. In the second, the outcome was reached through a comparison of personal relation (`⋛` between who the party is to the bench and what they hoped for), with a brief written reason that mentions the same rule. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. In the outcome formula the two are indistinguishable, and that is exactly why the bond has to be recorded.

But the two are isomers only if they are written over the **full shared atom set**: facts, parties, rule, precedent, decision-maker and outcome, differing only in which bond connects them. If one judgment omits an atom the other requires, the figure's definition fails. Fig 12 :124: **"functional group. A substructure whose presence fixes the outcome."** Sequence of citation, subsumption and recorded reason is the functional group that makes a judgment a precedent; the qualifier, that is the particular court and statute, says which member.

**Falsifier, written before the run:** if a legal system can use two judgments with the same recorded atoms and the same outcome interchangeably as precedent while their recorded bonds differ, the isomer claim fails and the functional-group reading stands instead.

### Q3: What order governs a legal procedure?

Fig 12 :74 defines `→` as **"A then B; B needs A to have happened"** and :75: **"swap them. Does it become a different thing? Yes."** A trial is an ordered bond: notice before hearing, hearing before judgment, reasons before review. Swapping those stages produces a different thing, so charging for the order is correct under an ordered bond. Operators table :34 warns that *"the only bond a flat chain can express"* is sequence, so a system that records only a chronology will mistake every legal process for a sequence, even where the operative relation is a comparison or a production.

Two further bonds matter here:

- `⇒` production, Fig 12 :77: **"A produces B; B did not exist before"**. A judgment produces a precedent that did not exist before. The precedent is not waiting in the code to be found.
- `⋛` comparison, Fig 12 :87: **"a threshold relation between two quantities"**. Appeal and sentencing thresholds are comparisons. A goal is a comparison (Fig 12 :112-113).

**Critical point for predictability:** party identity must **not** be an atom in the deciding molecule. If it is, swapping operands yields a different term, and the swap test in `01` F1 fires as connection-dependence. A predictable system treats identity as outside the bond. That is the symmetry violation test stated in Domains 1 and 3, now restated in the vocabulary of bonds: under a symmetric bond k operands have k! spellings and one term (Fig 12 :92), so a bench that reaches the same term for all spellings of the party slots is behaving symmetrically.

### Q4: Can the system refuse a precedent?

Operators table :57-58: negation is **"this does not hold"**, and **"a refutation is a negation, which is why a vocabulary without it can only accumulate."** Fig 12 :139-140: **"Without a unary operator a system can write what a situation contains, what follows what, and how far along it is, and it cannot write that any of those is false. A vocabulary that cannot refuse can only accumulate."**

Overruling, reversal, recusal and a recorded dissent are all `¬` operations. They do not join two operands; they refuse a claim. A court that can only distinguish and never overrule can correct nothing in its own vocabulary, because distinguishing adds a new bond without refusing the old one. That is an agent-only-looking fix that leaves the habitat untouched, and by F4 it changes no contact. The refusal has to be recorded so the reach falls for the right reason (Fig 6 :42-43: *"A refutation adds a term that refuses; it removes nothing, and the reach falls because something was added rather than because anything was deleted."*).

### Q5: Round trip between the derived shape and a concrete court

Fig 4 :30: **"So take the round trip and measure the gap. Without it you are navigating by dead reckoning: confident about a position nobody checked."**

1. **Sent up:** the verbatim figure set, chiefly Fig 8 :50-52 (provenance), Fig 12 :5-6 (bond), Fig 11 :39-40 (habitat), Fig 2 :29-33 (anchor).
2. **Abstraction:** the shorthand in `07_the_tether_answer.md`, invariance under swaps plus a recorded reason plus selecting review.
3. **Concretisation:** a concrete claim about a real court, for example "this commercial court is predictable because it publishes reasons and has an appellate tier."

**R_T is nonzero.** What the shorthand holds and the concrete claim drops: the per-slot residual (a court can publish reasons and still be unpredictable on a subset), the independence requirement (the appellate judges may share the trial judges' evidence pool), and the refusal operator (the court may have no recorded overrule path). R_T is not measured numerically here because no dataset was read. It is recorded qualitatively as the enumerated set of dropped constraints. Treating the shorthand as identical to the concrete claim would set R_T to zero and would be the dead-reckoning error Fig 4 names.

## CONCLUSION

A judgment is a molecule: facts are operands and the recorded reason is the typed bond. Two judgments with the same ingredients and different bonds are isomers, invisible in the outcome and decisive in their later use, but only when written over the full shared atom set; otherwise the functional-group reading applies. Legal procedure is an ordered bond plus a production and comparisons; party identity must stay outside the deciding bond for the symmetry test to pass. Refusal (`¬`) is necessary for a court to correct itself, and an unrecorded refusal is no refusal. The round-trip gap between the shape and any concrete court is nonzero and is the honest measure of what the shorthand cannot hold.
