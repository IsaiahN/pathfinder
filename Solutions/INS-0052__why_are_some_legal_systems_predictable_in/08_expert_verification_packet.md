# Expert verification packet: the Tether on legal predictability

**What this is:** a claim in a form an empirical legal scholar, a sociolegal researcher or a judge can check. Mode, derivation and refuters are stated before the conclusion, so a reader knows what to look for and what would settle it.

**Mode: `specified` on the three sub-claims, `general` on the shape. Not `grounded`.** No case data was read in this derivation. This packet is a simulated derivation checked for internal consistency against the figures, not a ground reading. It cannot settle the claim it reports on.

## 1. The claim, in one paragraph

Predictability should be measured per case, not per country. A legal order is predictable to the extent that swapping the identity of the parties, or of the decision-maker, leaves the outcome of a matched case unchanged. That invariance requires three separable conditions: an applicable rule that does not move with influence; a recorded reason that acts as the bond making the judgment usable as precedent; and a habitat, including published reports, declared conflicts and selecting appellate review, that keeps the anchor reachable. Insider agreement drawn from one shared evidence pool is not corroboration. The framework names no legal family and does not equate predictability with justice. What it offers is the shape, five refuters written in advance, and the instruments that would close it.

## 2. Three checkable sub-claims

### C1: Anchor and symmetry (framework-internal and case-checkable)

- **Statement:** an applicable rule that changes with who pushes on it is not an anchor. In a predictable order, party identity and bench identity are outside the deciding bond, so swapping either with case facts held fixed leaves the outcome unchanged.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 2 :29-30: *"An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks. A measurement, an experiment, a counter, a market, a patient outcome."* Fig 2 :30: *"If it moves when you push on it, it is not an anchor."* Fig 2 :33: *"The anchor must not update."* The anchorless case is Fig 2 :13-15: *"no anchor; each updates on the others"*, *"they converge, and what they converge on is whoever had the most influence."*
- **Refuter:** matched cases where outcomes are invariant to swapping connected and unconnected parties while the applicable rule demonstrably moves with influence. Also: a decision-maker whose declared private stake shifts nothing across matched pairs.
- **Mode:** `specified`, falsifiable. **Status:** `triangulated` (Fig 2 with Fig 10 :59-60).

### C2: The reason is the bond, not commentary (checkable by reading two judgments)

- **Statement:** a rule-following judgment and a favor-trading judgment can have identical facts, parties and outcome. What distinguishes them for later use is the recorded reason, which is the typed bond that makes the judgment composable as precedent. Over the full shared atom set the two are isomers under Fig 12.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 8 :50-52: *"Convergent derivation and adopted import look identical in the contents. Only the record of where each came from separates them, and the surplus lives only where the closures differ."* Fig 12 :6: *"A recipe that names only its ingredients has recorded half of itself."* Fig 12 :117: *"isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."*
- **What to check:** find two judgments with identical legally relevant facts and identical outcomes but different written reasoning. Are they later cited interchangeably, or does the later court treat them as different precedents? If the latter, the bond carries information the outcome does not.
- **Refuter:** two such judgments used interchangeably as precedent with no trace in the record distinguishing them. That shows the bond carries nothing downstream.
- **Mode:** `specified`, ungrounded. **Status:** `triangulated` (Fig 8, Fig 10 :62-63, Fig 12).

### C3: Habitat and selection (checkable against court administration data)

- **Statement:** capability is a property of the bench and its habitat together, and an appeal that shares its evidence pool with the trial tier is a mirror, not a selection.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 11 :39: *"capability is a property of agent-and-habitat, never of the agent alone"* and :40: *"An improvement that does not change contact changes nothing, however much it improves."* Fig 7 :35: *"A room's agreement is variation, not a verdict. Selection between rooms is where the ground votes."* Fig 7 :36: *"A chain of mirrors is one long room. The next room must differ from the last, and match the residual it surfaced."*
- **What to check:** does the appellate tier's reversal pattern track trial-level error, or only the trial tier's preferences? Does a reform that changes only the individuals, with no change to record, reasons, review or conflict rules, move matched-case outcomes?
- **Refuter:** an agent-only improvement that moves the ground (Fig 11 explicitly allows this as a refuter), or an appellate tier with zero correction of trial error while trial outcomes remain fully predictable.
- **Mode:** `specified`, unmeasured. **Status:** `triangulated` (Fig 11 with Fig 7).

## 3. What is explicitly not claimed

- Not that any legal family is more predictable. No figure line derives that.
- Not that a rule-of-law index measures an individual case. Fig 9 :60: *"Use filters for the budget and witnesses for the verdict. Never let a filter hand you a verdict."*
- Not that a published reason is truthful. Legal realism's objection is recorded; the claim is about the recorded bond, not about motive.
- Not that predictability is justice. Fig 2 :37-38 makes human judgment the right anchor for a question about people and only an instrument for a question about code correctness. The two verdicts are not averaged.
- Not `grounded`, and not a claim that any named judge, court or country is predictable.
- Not that insider consensus corroborates the claim. Fig 2 :40: *"agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool."*

## 5. What would settle it

A matched-pair dataset, not more agreement. The five tests are: swap test (party and bench identity), reason test (different bonds, same outcome), selection test (appellate correction versus trial error), habitat test (agent-only improvement), index test (aggregate versus per-slot prediction on disagreeing cases). Fig 8 :39: *"So a search that finds nothing is never a proof of absence."* A fired refuter is a result; an unfired one is a report about the instrument.

## 6. What an expert can check in twenty minutes

1. Read Fig 2 :29-33 and Fig 8 :50-52. Two short passages, C1 and C2.
2. Name one case pair where you would expect party identity to change the outcome, and say which of the five refuters that would fire.
3. Say whether you have ever seen two same-outcome judgments with different reasons cited interchangeably. One example either way settles C2 for you.
4. Say what your court's appellate tier would have to look like for C3 to be false.
5. Send the refutation. A failure is more useful than an endorsement. Record your verdict before reading the framework material in `04`.
