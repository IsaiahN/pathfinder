## Independent domain census

I redid the census before checking the primary's findings, using the WATSON order: R by arity, symmetry and scale; one line per domain with actors / currency / anchor; partition or union; order by dependency.

- **R (arity / symmetry / scale).** The unobserved generation of a negative claim about an institution. Arity: one claim, two generations, one observer, one institution, one or more grounds. Symmetry: the A/B comparison is an ordered threshold relation, not a symmetric join; the Operators table defines comparison as *"a threshold relation between two quantities"*, and swapping A and B changes the verdict. Scale: individual and public judgment, with a lossy transform between them. Union risk: a single document can hold both generations.
- **Domain 1, claim to ground.** Actors: critic, claim, institution, ground. Currency: contact with the ground. Anchor: a ground that does not update under a push. Question type: partition.
- **Domain 2, observer and frames.** Actors: observer, peer frames, evidence pools. Currency: independence and agreement. Anchor: the ground, never agreement. Question type: partition by overlap.
- **Domain 3, institution as seat and channel.** Actors: institution as metric keeper, its standing, its audience. Currency: conventions, reach, stake. Anchor: the ground kept reachable. Question type: seat test.
- **Domain 4, valence and context.** Actors: critics, claims, conditions. Currency: relation to this residual. Anchor: the specific residual. Question type: relation, not kind.

Order by dependency: describe the residual first; check the anchor; check frame independence; keep the channel reachable and declare the stake; only then sort; unbundle unions before scoring.

My census matches the primary's four domains and its union reading. ## Round trip gap R_T

The Symbols table defines `R_T` as `gap(x, (T_E ∘ T_A)(x))`, and Fig 4 :30 requires it to be measured rather than assumed. I ran the round trip on the condensation this folder actually uses.

1. **Sent up (x):** the verbatim figure set, chiefly Fig 2 :29-34, :40 (anchor and independence), Fig 10 :24, :59-60, :93 (channel and stake), Fig 11 :49, :51 (valence), and Fig 9 :27-28, :55, :57 (residual first, union).
2. **Abstraction (T_A):** the four-property shorthand carried in `07_the_tether_answer.md` and `08_expert_verification_packet.md`: non-updating anchor, low-overlap frames, reachable channel with declared stake, valence relative to the residual.
3. **Concretisation (T_E):** a live case, for example "this report is delegitimizing the agency because it questions its independence".

**R_T is nonzero.** Three things the shorthand holds do not survive the trip back down:

- the **union condition**. The shorthand says "unbundle mixed documents"; a live text rarely arrives segmented, and the segmentation rule itself has no figure-specified instrument. This is the operational gap already recorded as F7.
- the **push test**. "Non-updating anchor" is a short form for a measurement of how the ground responds when both frames push on it. Condensed to a label, it is easy to treat as a property of the standard rather than as a test.
- the **stake narrowing**. "Declared stake" compresses Fig 10 :59-60 and the case-conditioned Fig 10 :93 into one phrase, and the shorthand can be read as the false universal "all institutions are disqualified".

Conversely, one thing survives cleanly: a claim must range against something that does not move when either side thinks about it, and agreement cannot supply the anchor. That is the residue worth sending. `R_T` was **not** measured numerically, because no ground reading exists; it is recorded qualitatively as the enumerated set of dropped constraints. Treating the formula as identical to the figures would set `R_T` to zero and commit the dead-reckoning error Fig 4 :30 names.
