# The Tether's answer: what enables the distinction

**Mode: `general` on the shape of the discriminator, `specified` on the four per-domain predictions. Not `grounded`.** Simulated derivation checked for internal consistency against the figures (`05_what_shape_means.md` §6), not a ground reading. Nothing here settles a live case.

## 1. The residual it answers

A negative claim about an institution can be generated by contact with the institution's actual performance against a standard, or by an attempt to lower the institution's standing regardless of that performance. The text alone does not display which. The residual is the missing discriminator. The answer is a shape with four refusable properties, not a verdict on any critic and not a score for any person.

## 2. The shape, one loop per domain, verdicts kept separate

### Domain 1, claim to ground: the anchor test (`general`, triangulated)

Justified criticism ranges against a ground whose answer does not change when either side thinks about it: an outcome, a measurement, a standard the institution declared, a counter. Fig 2 :29-30: *"An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks."* Delegitimation tries to make that anchor update, converting "did we miss the standard?" into "should this body exist?" Once the anchor updates, *"everything drifts together, and the drift is coherent, which is why nobody notices"* (Fig 2 :34). Triangulated across Fig 2 and Fig 1 :23 (*"The ground enters as the only metric"*), low overlap.

### Domain 2, observer and frames: independence, not agreement (`general`, triangulated)

Agreement means something only when the frames draw on different evidence. Fig 2 :31: *"Independence, between two frames, is how little they draw on the same evidence."* Fig 2 :32: *"Independence is what makes agreement mean something."* Many voices citing one report are one witness with many names (Fig 7 :12-13, *"agreement compounds, evidence does not"*). Fig 2 :40: *"disqualifying for a verdict: agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool."* Triangulated across Fig 2, Fig 7, Fig 8, and Fig 13, with the overlap declared as moderate: these are different mechanisms in one law set, not independent witnesses.

### Domain 3, institution as seat and channel: the stake test (`specified`, triangulated)

Institutions often keep their own metrics. A criticism that locates a saturated metric, a pooled reading, or a keeper who benefits from the reading is doing the work the figures prescribe: *"the ground does not decay, the channel does"* (Fig 10 :24), *"keep the anchor reachable"* (Fig 10 :29). Delegitimation asserts corruption without locating a channel failure, or attacks standing as a substitute for showing the ground moved. Fig 10 :59-61: *"an interest in the outcome is unavoidable, an interest in the reading is disqualifying"* and *"Where a stake cannot be removed, declare it."* Note the bound: Fig 10 :93 disqualifies a ground keeper whose ground *"has a reputation to protect"*; it is not a blanket rule against institutions. Triangulated across Fig 10 and Fig 9.

### Domain 4, valence and context: no standing roster (`general`, triangulated)

The same critic and the same wording can be justified for one residual and delegitimizing for another. Fig 11 :49: *"An actor's valence is relative to the residual, not a property it carries."* Fig 11 :51: *"No standing list of good actors survives a change of residual."* So the observer must describe the specific alleged failure first (Fig 9 :27-28) and then sort the claim against it. Triangulated across Fig 11 and Fig 9, low overlap.

### The compound case: unbundle, never average

A document can contain both a grounded performance claim and a standing attack. Fig 9 :55: *"A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones."* Segment and score each condition on its own loop. Averaging the two destroys the signal exactly the way an aggregate residual destroys a per-slot signal (Fig 1 :22, *"Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average"*).

### The operational instrument: `gap`

The figures give the shape of the discriminator, not an instrument that measures a person's ability to apply it. Fig 6 :32: *"An instrument is not built from a description. It is improved from a worse instrument already returning something."* A candidate proto-instrument exists in cognitive science (source vigilance plus content vigilance), imported with provenance in `09_watson_answer.md`. Until it is tested against a ground, F7 remains an honest `gap`: unreached at this budget, not proven unreachable. *"Unreachable is a verdict here, not a silence"* (Fig 13 :134).

## 3. What it explicitly does not say

- Not a verdict on any live institution, critic, or controversy. No such case was examined.
- Not a claim that criticism is honest and delegitimation dishonest as properties of speakers. Valence is relative to the residual (Fig 11 :49).
- Not a test of character, intent, or motive. The discriminator tests contact with a ground.
- Not a single trust score. The four domain verdicts are never averaged.
- Not a standing roster of legitimate critics. That is the exact failure F4 refuses.
- Not `grounded`. No measurement in this derivation settles the shape or returns a reading on a case.
- Not a final theory. Fig 7 :6 names *"the limit: no last room"*; the framework is a generator of shapes, not a terminal answer.

## 4. What would settle it: the ground

A discriminating instrument, not more agreement. The figure-grounded refuters (written before the run, `01_figure_grounded_audit.md` §4) are:

1. an anchor that updates under a measured push still sorts correctly;
2. two high-overlap frames whose agreement is still counted as corroboration;
3. a fixed roster of critics that sorts correctly after the residual changes;
4. a saturated or pooled metric treated as the settling ground;
5. a discriminator that sorts correctly with no change in contact.

Any one firing retracts the corresponding property. What would move F7 from `gap` to `grounded` is a test: apply source-vigilance and content-vigilance measures to a set of real criticisms, pre-register which claims will be sorted A or B against an independently specified standard, and see whether the discriminator's predictions survive contact with an outcome the observers cannot move. The *Hard Problem is About Source* essay's lesson applies: it was *"the arrival of a better instrument"*, not a better argument, that resolved the analogous case.

## 5. The honest stop

The answer stops at the shape. It says what any reliable discriminator must have, and it records the one thing it lacks (an instrument for F7). It does not name the correct critic, does not settle a live case, and does not turn agreement into evidence. *"you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself"* (Fig 9 :32). The canonical figures README adds that indiscernibility is not identity and that the record of the route is what keeps a result checkable. The lineage is recorded so a later reader can locate the error, which is the most this simulated derivation can honestly offer.

## 6. One-paragraph version for outreach

> The distinguishing feature is not who speaks, how loudly, or how many people agree. It is whether the criticism ranges against a ground that does not move when either side thinks about it, such as an outcome or a standard the institution itself declared. Four properties make that check work: a non-updating anchor; independent frames rather than one shared evidence pool; a reachable channel with any conflict of interest declared; and a willingness to re-describe the specific alleged failure rather than rely on a standing list of good critics. Mixed documents must be unbundled and scored in two loops, never averaged. The figures give this shape and five concrete refuters written in advance. They do not give an instrument for measuring a person's ability to apply it, so that part is recorded as an honest gap, not filled with a verdict. No live institution or critic is judged here.
