## 0. The residual in effect terms

An observer O is presented with a claim C directed at an institution I. C admits two generations:

- **A, justified criticism:** C is generated by contact between I and a ground G that I is answerable to (a measurement, an outcome, a standard I declared, a counter, a patient outcome). C tracks a real residual on some slot s of I.
- **B, delegitimation:** C is generated to reduce I's standing or authority, independently of whether I missed G. Its currency is the standing of the frame, not contact with the ground.

**The difficulty:** C alone does not display its generation. Both A and B are negative, both can cite true facts, both can be repeated by many people, both can sound reasoned. "The ground enters as the only metric" (Fig 1 :23), but the observer does not get the ground's reading handed to them; they must find it.

**Residual R:** the missing discriminator D such that O can sort A from B. R is not "are the facts true" (both can be true) and not "how many people agree" (agreement carries no valence here). R is "does this claim range against a non-updating ground, or does it try to move the ground and substitute a frame-internal quantity?"

**Bound answer shape (Formula step Direction).** Write down what D would look like before searching: four ordinary terms, each refusable. (1) an anchor that does not move when frames push on it; (2) low-overlap independent frames rather than one shared pool; (3) a reachable channel with declared stake; (4) valence relative to the specific residual, not to a standing roster of critics. These are bound now so they accrue a residual like anything else.

## 1. Domain census (before choosing any figure line)

### R by arity, symmetry, scale

- **Arity:** one claim, two candidate generations, one observer, one institution, one or more grounds. The A/B comparison is a **threshold discrimination**, not a symmetric join. Operators table :48 defines comparison as *"a threshold relation between two quantities"*; Fig 12 :87: *"is it about which is larger, rather than both being present? Yes."* Swapping A and B changes the verdict, so the relation is ordered.
- **Symmetry:** not symmetric under exchange of the two generations. It is also not a simple negation: a refusal term is legitimate inside the framework (Fig 5 :53, *"refuted: a term we hold is wrong; a term is owed that says so"*), so "the criticism negates the institution" is not a structural definition of B.
- **Scale:** individual observer judgment and collective or public judgment. These are related by the membrane (Fig 4 :19, *"The kernel is the part that does not change with size"*): the same loop at two sizes, with a lossy transform between. A verdict that holds at one scale is a recording, not a generator, at the other.

### One line per domain: actors / currency / anchor

| domain | actors | currency | nameable anchor? | question type |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1. Claim to ground | critic, claim, institution, measurement / outcome / declared standard | contact with the ground (residual on a slot) | yes: the institution's own outputs and its declared function | partition (does this claim range against G?) |
| 2. Observer and frames | observer frame, other frames, shared evidence pools | independence and agreement | yes: the ground, never agreement (Fig 2 :45) | partition by overlap (is this two instruments or one pool?) |
| 3. Institution as seat and channel | the institution as maintainer of its own metrics and conventions, its standing | conventions, reach, stake | yes: the ground kept reachable (Fig 10 :29) | seat test (who grades whose reading?) |
| 4. Valence and context | critics, claims, conditions, relations in the habitat | relation to this specific residual | yes: the residual itself, re-described per case (Fig 11 :51) | relation, not kind (no standing roster) |

All four domains have a nameable anchor, so all four are domains rather than proto-domains. Where an anchor could not be named, the honest move would be to stop and call it not-a-domain yet (master plan section 8).

### Partition or union

The target is a **partition** in the clean case: a claim either ranges against G or it does not. But the real case is often a **union compound**. A single document can contain a grounded performance claim and a standing attack in the same breath. Fig 9 :55: *"A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones."* Fig 9 :57: *"a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either."* Rule: unbundle before sorting. Score the ground-tracking part and the standing-attacking part separately. Do not average them into one verdict.

### Dependency order (Fig 3)

1. Is there a nameable ground at all? (perception and vocabulary, links 1 to 2)
2. Does this claim range against it? (objective, link 3)
3. Are the frames corroborating independently, or sharing one pool? (planning input, link 4)
4. Is the channel reachable and the stake declared? (learn and carry, link 5)

Fig 3 :19: *"Optimising a link that already works cannot rescue one that does not."* A campaign that adds more agreeing voices (step 3) cannot rescue a missing anchor (step 1).

## 3. Findings F1 to F7

### F1  -  The discriminator is an anchor test (decisive, `general`)

Fig 2 :29-30:

> **"An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks. A measurement, an experiment, a counter, a market, a patient outcome. If it moves when you push on it, it is not an anchor."**

Fig 2 :33-34:

> **"The anchor must not update."**
> **"If it does, panel 3 turns into panel 1: the anchor becomes another frame, everything drifts together, and the drift is coherent, which is why nobody notices."**

Fig 1 :23: **"The ground enters as the only metric."**

**Reading.** Justified criticism is a claim that ranges against a ground whose answer does not depend on what the critic or the institution thinks. Delegitimation is the attempt to make the anchor update: to convert "did I miss the standard?" into "should this institution exist / be trusted / be abolished?" Once the anchor updates, every frame drifts together coherently and nobody notices (Fig 2 :34). That is the signature, not a rhetorical flourish.

Fig 2 :36-38 adds the legitimacy condition:

> **"An anchor is legitimate when the question is constitutively about it."**
> **"Human judgment is the right anchor for is this a good answer for a person, because the question is about people. It is only an instrument for is this code correct, and instruments can be wrong on axes they do not measure."**

So for institutions the right anchor is constitutively tied to the institution's function and its effects on people: outputs, outcomes, the standard it declared. It is not the institution's prestige, and not the critic's sincerity.

**Refuter.** Produce a case where the alleged delegitimation tracks a non-updating ground and the alleged criticism only circulates a frame-internal quantity, with the ground's response to a push measured on both sides. If the sort reverses, F1 fails.

**Mode:** `general` (structural). Triangulated across Fig 2 (anchor) and Fig 1 (ground-only metric), two figures with different subject matter and low overlap.

### F2  -  Independence, not agreement (`general`)

Fig 2 :31-32:

> **"Independence, between two frames, is how little they draw on the same evidence."**
> **"Independence is what makes agreement mean something."**

Fig 2 :40: **"disqualifying for a verdict: agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool"**

Fig 7 :12-13: **"same closure, handed along; agreement compounds, evidence does not"**

Fig 8 :29: **"low overlap: B does not draw on A's evidence"**

**Reading.** A claim repeated by many voices is not thereby better anchored. If they cite the same report, the same dataset, the same outrage, their agreement is the pool talking, not two frames confirming each other. Legitimate corroboration requires low overlap: different instruments pointed at the same thing (Fig 2 :31-32). Fig 7 :35: *"A room's agreement is variation, not a verdict. Selection between rooms is where the ground votes."* Fig 2 :42: *"Buy independence in proportion to the cost of being wrong."* Firing a critic is a high-cost error, so independence is worth buying here.

**Refuter.** Show two frames with demonstrably high evidence overlap whose agreement is still counted as corroboration for the sort, or show two low-overlap instruments that disagree on the same residual while the sort remains confidence-invariantly high. Either breaks F2.

**Mode:** `general`. Triangulated across Fig 2 (independence), Fig 7 (mirror chain), Fig 8 (low-overlap surplus), and Fig 13 :38-39 (*"independence is how little two frames draw on the same evidence, and it is what makes their agreement mean anything"*). Overlap between these figures is moderate (same author, different mechanisms), so this is internal triangulation, not external corroboration.

### F3  -  The seat and channel test (`specified`)

Fig 10 :24-26:

> **"the ground does not decay, the channel does"**
> **"stale percepts, dropped transients, saturated metrics, pooled readings"**

Fig 10 :27-28: **"the output is not a verdict, it is you have lost touch"**

Fig 10 :59-61:

> **"an interest in the outcome is unavoidable, an interest in the reading is disqualifying"**
> **"Where a stake cannot be removed, declare it."**
> **"standing is a stake that survives the outcome it was earned on"**

Fig 10 :93: **"A ground with a reputation to protect is a ground someone benefits from, and its keeper is thereby disqualified."**

**Reading.** Institutions frequently occupy the ground-maintainer seat: they keep their own metrics and conventions. When a criticism identifies a saturated metric, a pooled reading, or a keeper who benefits from a particular reading, it is doing exactly the work the figure prescribes: keeping the anchor reachable (Fig 10 :29, *"keep the anchor reachable"*). Delegitimation instead asserts that the whole frame is corrupt without locating a channel failure, or attacks standing as a substitute for showing the ground moved. Note the asymmetry carefully: Fig 10 :93 is **not** a blanket rule that all institutions are disqualified. It fires when the ground has a reputation to protect and its keeper benefits from the reading. Recorded as `specified`, not `general`, precisely to prevent that overreach (see strain S2 in §5b).

Fig 10 :47: **"a convention that decides an outcome has stopped being one"**  -  an institution's own procedure cannot be both the convention and the verdict. Fig 9 :51: evidence bearing on the residual is admissible; evidence bearing only on its maker is a stake-inducing channel.

**Refuter.** Produce a case where the criticism identifies no channel failure and no conflict of interest, and the institution's metric is independently shown unsaturated, yet the sort still calls it justified criticism; or produce a case where an attack correctly identifies a conflict of interest and is still sorted as delegitimation. Either breaks F3.

**Mode:** `specified`. Triangulated across Fig 10 (channel and seat) and Fig 9 (convention that decides), two distinct mechanisms.

### F4  -  Valence is relative to the residual; no standing roster (`general`)

Fig 11 :49-51:

> **"An actor's valence is relative to the residual, not a property it carries."**
> **"The same actor is a benefactor to one residual and a malfactor to another, so mutualism and parasitism are relations rather than kinds."**
> **"No standing list of good actors survives a change of residual."**

Fig 11 :40: **"An improvement that does not change contact changes nothing, however much it improves."**

**Reading.** The same critic, the same wording, even the same statistic can be justified criticism for one residual and delegitimation for another. So you cannot sort by identity, tone, affiliation, or a permanent list of "legitimate critics." You sort by whether this claim, here, makes contact with the institution's actual failure against its declared function. This is the strongest single reason the answer is a discriminator and not a checklist of approved speakers.

**Refuter.** Exhibit a fixed roster of critics that continues to sort correctly after the residual changes (new institution, new standard, new failure mode), without re-describing the residual. If it does, valence is a property after all and F4 fails.

**Mode:** `general`. Triangulated across Fig 11 (valence) and Fig 9 :27-28 (*"describe it before you go looking, or any frame you pick will seem to fit"*), low overlap between habitat enumeration and leave-arrive lookup.

### F5  -  Describe the residual before naming the frame (`general`)

Fig 9 :27-31:

> **"describe it before you go looking, or any frame you pick will seem to fit"**
> **"your words are borrowed too, so describe the effect rather than the cause"**

Fig 8 :53: **"Residual first, frame second."**

Fig 9 :32: **"you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself"**

**Reading.** If you begin with "which institution is being attacked," any institution will seem to fit. The observer must first describe the effect: what failure against what standard is alleged, on which slot, at what scale. Only then pick the frame that predicts that shape. And what returns is a kind of thing (a discriminator with four properties), not the name of a correct critic or a verdict on a live case. The Recursive Self-Improvement Trap's warning about outputs *"contrived to fit the formula"* applies here: a discriminator assembled around a preferred conclusion is worthless.

**Refuter.** Show a case where the frame was named first and the residual was still recovered without post-hoc fitting, checked against a pre-registered description of the alleged failure. If that works generally, the order constraint is not load-bearing.

**Mode:** `general`. Triangulated across Fig 9 and Fig 8.

### F6  -  The discrimination is an ordered threshold, and often a union compound (`general`)

Fig 12 :111-112:

> **"A vocabulary without bonds can name a state and cannot name a route to one."**
> **"A plan is a sequence. A goal is a comparison. Progress is a subtraction. A refutation is a negation"**

Operators table :48: **"a threshold relation between two quantities"**

Fig 9 :55-58: **"A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones."** and **"a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either."**

**Reading.** Sorting A from B is a comparison (a threshold relation), not a merger and not a negation of the institution. A vocabulary that only has accumulation cannot carry the sort: it can record that a claim was made and cannot record that the claim was refused as a standing attack. The framework's eighth operator, negation, is what makes refusal writeable (Operators table :58: *"a refutation is a negation, which is why a vocabulary without it can only accumulate"*). Where a document bundles a grounded claim and a standing attack, score the two conditions separately; a single lever clears only the region where one condition holds.

**Refuter.** Show that in the target domain the two generations are not separable in principle, that is, every claim is irreducibly both, so the union reading and the comparison operator are the wrong structure. Then F6 fails and the sort must be replaced by a single scalar.

**Mode:** `general`. Single-source for the operator definition (Operators table and Fig 12 are the same law set), with the union rule from Fig 9 as a separate figure. Marked single-source for the operator type, triangulated for the unbundling rule.

### F7  -  What the figures do not give: the operational instrument (`gap`, honestly stated)

Fig 6 :32-34:

> **"An instrument is not built from a description. It is improved from a worse instrument already returning something."**
> **"Nobody specified a telescope from an account of Mars. A wandering point in the sky was already a reading, and every sharper instrument was built on the residual the last one produced."**

Fig 9 :32: **"you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself"**

Fig 8 :39: **"a search that finds nothing is never a proof of absence."**

Fig 13 :134: **"Unreachable is a verdict here, not a silence,"**

**Reading.** The figures govern the *shape* of D (F1 to F6) but supply no instrument that measures a person's ability to apply D, and no reading of any live case. There is no figure line that says "score discrimination ability with instrument X." There is a proto-instrument in the world literature (source vigilance and content vigilance, see `09_watson_answer.md` WORLD), which is an import with a source gate and a shadow test, not a figure-derived atom (Fig 6 :23, *"No frame escapes its own closure. The bound moves only on contact with a frame whose closure differs"*). Until such an instrument is imported with provenance and a shadow test, the operational question is **unreached at this budget**, not answered and not proven unreachable. This is a `gap` for the operational instrument, while the shape verdicts in F1 to F6 stand.

**Refuter.** Point to a figure line that does supply an operational discriminator, or exhibit an import with a clean source gate and shadow test that returns a residual on discrimination ability. Either closes F7.

**Mode:** `general` for the gap claim. Single-source / gap, not triangulated.

## 4. Pre-registered refuter (written before the conclusion)

Per the seat map rule, *"a falsifier written before the run is a design instrument, not only an honesty device"* (Seat Map :287), the shape claim is refuted if any of the following holds:

1. **Anchor failure.** An anchor that updates when frames push on it is still used as the discriminator, with the push measured on both frames (breaks Fig 2 :29-30, :33-34).
2. **Pooled agreement counted.** Two frames with high evidence overlap have their agreement counted as corroboration for the sort (breaks Fig 2 :40).
3. **Standing roster works.** A fixed list of "legitimate critics" sorts correctly across a change of residual without re-describing the residual (breaks Fig 11 :51).
4. **Saturated channel treated as ground.** An institution's metric is shown saturated or pooled yet is treated as the settling ground (breaks Fig 10 :24-26).
5. **No-contact discrimination works.** A discriminator sorts correctly with no change in contact with the ground (breaks Fig 11 :40).
6. **Union denied.** The two generations are proven irreducibly one condition, so no threshold sort is possible (breaks F6).

If any fires, the corresponding finding is withdrawn and the shape is re-censused. Until one is run, no part of this may be labelled `grounded`. These were written before §7 and before the WATSON run.

## 5. Recommendation

1. Lead with the four-term discriminator and its refuter, not with a theory of institutions. Fig 10 :52: *"Bring a characterised residual, never the source."*
2. Keep the four domain verdicts separate. Never average an anchor result, an independence result, a channel result, and a valence result into one "trust score." Fig 1 :19: *"The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated"*; Fig 1 :22: *"Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average."*
3. Unbundle mixed cases before sorting (Fig 9 :55-58).
4. Ask the outside frame what they would count as a failure before showing any theory. Record that verdict first. Do not pool later agreement as corroboration.
5. Use the public submission and contact points in `03_public_contact_points.csv`. Fig 4 :22: *"Only methods cross up. Only head starts come down."* Send the discriminator and its refuter, not a recording of a conclusion.
