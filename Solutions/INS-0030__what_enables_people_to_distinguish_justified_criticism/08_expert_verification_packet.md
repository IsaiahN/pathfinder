# Expert verification packet: what enables the justified criticism / delegitimation distinction

**What this is:** a claim in a form a researcher on institutions, trust, or criticism can check. Mode, derivation, and refuters are stated before the conclusion, so a reader knows what to look for and what would settle it.

**Mode: `general` on the shape, `specified` on the four domain predictions. Not `grounded`.** No measurement is claimed to settle the shape, and no live case is judged.

**Status:** a simulated derivation checked for internal consistency against the figures, not a ground reading. It cannot settle the question it reports on.

## 1. The claim, in one paragraph

A negative claim about an institution can be generated by contact with the institution's real performance against a standard, or by an attempt to lower its standing regardless of that performance. The surface text does not reveal which. A reliable discriminator has four properties: a ground that does not update when either side pushes on it; frames that corroborate independently rather than sharing one evidence pool; a reachable channel with any conflict of interest in the reading declared; and valence fixed to the specific alleged failure rather than to a standing roster of acceptable critics. Mixed documents are unbundled and scored in separate loops, never averaged. The figures supply this shape and five refuters written in advance; they do not supply an instrument for measuring a person's ability to apply it, which is recorded as an open gap.

## 2. Three checkable sub-claims

### C1  -  Anchor: justified criticism ranges against a non-updating ground
- **Statement:** a claim counts as grounded criticism only if the standard it invokes keeps its answer when the critic or the institution pushes on it. A claim that succeeds by turning the question into the institution's right to exist has moved the anchor.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 2 :29-30  -  *"An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks. A measurement, an experiment, a counter, a market, a patient outcome. If it moves when you push on it, it is not an anchor."* Fig 2 :34  -  *"If it does, panel 3 turns into panel 1: the anchor becomes another frame, everything drifts together, and the drift is coherent, which is why nobody notices."*
- **Refuter:** show a case where the alleged standing attack tracks a fixed ground and the alleged criticism only circulates a frame-internal quantity, with the ground's response to a push measured on both sides.

### C2  -  Independence: agreement is not corroboration when the frames share a pool
- **Statement:** many voices repeating one report do not make a claim better anchored. Corroboration requires low overlap between the frames' evidence.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 2 :31  -  *"Independence, between two frames, is how little they draw on the same evidence."* Fig 2 :32  -  *"Independence is what makes agreement mean something."* Fig 2 :40  -  *"disqualifying for a verdict: agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool."*
- **Refuter:** show two frames with demonstrably high evidence overlap whose agreement is still counted as corroboration for the sort, or two low-overlap instruments that disagree while the sort is held at the same confidence.

### C3  -  Valence and stake: sort by the specific residual, never by a roster, and declare the interest in the reading
- **Statement:** the same critic and the same wording can be justified in one case and delegitimizing in another; and an institution that keeps its own metrics must have its interest in the reading declared, not treated as absent.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 11 :49 and :51  -  *"An actor's valence is relative to the residual, not a property it carries."* and *"No standing list of good actors survives a change of residual."* Fig 10 :59-60  -  *"an interest in the outcome is unavoidable, an interest in the reading is disqualifying"* and *"Where a stake cannot be removed, declare it."*
- **Refuter:** exhibit a fixed roster of critics that sorts correctly across a change of residual without re-describing the residual; or show a saturated or pooled metric treated as the settling ground with the sort still holding.

### The compound case
A document that both cites a real failure and attacks the institution's standing is a union compound. Fig 9 :55  -  *"A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones."* Score the two conditions in separate loops. Refuter: show that in the target domain the two conditions are irreducibly one, so no threshold sort is possible.

## 3. What is explicitly not claimed

- Not a verdict on any live institution, critic, or controversy. None was examined.
- Not a claim that criticism is honest and delegitimation dishonest as properties of speakers.
- Not a test of motive, intent, or character. The test is contact with a ground.
- Not a single trust score. The four domain verdicts are never averaged (Fig 1 :22: *"Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average"*).
- Not a roster of legitimate critics. That is the failure C3 refuses.
- Not `grounded`. No measurement settles the shape or returns a reading on a case.
- Not a claim that the discriminator has been validated in people. That is the open gap.

## 5. What would settle it

1. **The instrument test.** Apply source-vigilance and content-vigilance measures (imported from cognitive science with provenance) to a set of real criticisms, pre-register the A/B sort against an independently specified standard, and see whether predictions survive contact with an outcome neither observers nor institution can move.
2. **The five refuters.** Any one firing in section 4 of `01_figure_grounded_audit.md` retracts the corresponding property.
3. **A source-discriminating observation.** Something that distinguishes "the claim tracked the standard" from "the claim tracked standing" where the surface text is identical. As in *The Hard Problem is About Source*, this is an instrument result, not an argument.

## 6. What an expert can check in twenty minutes

1. Read Fig 2 :29-34, :40, Fig 11 :49-51, and Fig 10 :59-60. Six short passages.
2. Check C1 against one case you know: did the criticism name a standard that stayed fixed when both sides pushed?
3. Check C2: were the corroborating voices actually independent, or one evidence pool?
4. Check C3: does your own field use a standing roster of "acceptable critics"? Would it survive a change of residual?
5. Name one instrument that would distinguish ground-tracking from standing-attacking where the text is identical, or say why none is possible.
6. Send the refutation. A failure is more useful than an endorsement, and later agreement will not be pooled as corroboration.
