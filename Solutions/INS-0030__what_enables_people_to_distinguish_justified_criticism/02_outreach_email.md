# Outreach draft: check the discriminator before deciding about the theory

**Use:** one-to-one, or as a cover note attached to a submission through the public venues in `03_public_contact_points.csv`. **Not** for bulk send.

**Hard precondition:** do not send until the packet in `08_expert_verification_packet.md` has been checked against the verbatim figure lines. The first contact must survive validation or the two-stage offer collapses on contact.

**Mode of the claim being offered:** `general` on the shape of the discriminator, `specified` on the four domain predictions. Not `grounded`. No measurement settles it in this derivation.

---

**Subject:** One checkable discriminator for criticism that tracks a standard versus criticism that attacks standing

> Hello,
>
> I am working on a narrow question in institutional criticism research and have one specific output I would like you to check against your own field. I am not asking you to read a theory first, and I am not asking you to agree with anything. If the output fails, there is nothing else to look at.
>
> **The question, in effect terms.** A person sees a negative claim about an institution. The claim could be tracking a real failure against a standard the institution is answerable to, or it could be aimed at reducing the institution's standing whether or not the standard was missed. The two look alike on the surface. What lets a person tell them apart?
>
> **The output (`general` shape, `specified` domain predictions).** The discriminator has four properties, each independently refusable:
>
> 1. **A non-updating anchor.** Justified criticism ranges against a ground whose answer does not change when the critic or the institution thinks about it: an outcome, a measurement, a standard the institution declared, a counter. If the claim succeeds only by making the question about the institution's right to exist rather than its performance, it has moved the anchor instead of ranging against it.
> 2. **Low-overlap frames, not a crowd.** Agreement means something only when the agreeing frames draw on different evidence. Many voices citing the same report are one witness wearing many names.
> 3. **A reachable channel with a declared stake.** Institutions often keep their own metrics. A criticism that locates a saturated metric, a pooled reading, or a keeper who benefits from the reading is doing real work. A criticism that asserts corruption without locating a channel failure is not. Both sides have a stake in the outcome; the question is whether the stake in the reading is declared.
> 4. **Valence relative to this residual, not to a roster.** The same critic and the same wording can be justified in one case and delegitimizing in another. There is no standing list of good critics. You have to describe the specific alleged failure first, then sort the claim against it.
>
> Mixed documents are common: a single text can contain a grounded performance claim and a standing attack in the same paragraph. Score the two parts separately; a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either.
>
> **What would refute this, written down before I sent it:**
>
> 1. An anchor that updates when frames push on it still sorts correctly, with the push measured on both sides.
> 2. Two frames with high evidence overlap have their agreement counted as corroboration and the sort still holds.
> 3. A fixed list of "legitimate critics" keeps sorting correctly after the residual changes and the list is not re-described.
> 4. A saturated or pooled metric is shown to be the settling ground and the sort still holds.
>
> If any of these lands, I withdraw the corresponding property. A one-line "this fails because ..." is more useful to me than agreement. If you would rather not engage, no reply is needed.
>
> **What I am not claiming.** Not a verdict on any live institution or critic. Not a test of a person's character. Not a score that can be averaged across cases. Not `grounded`: the framework returns the shape a discriminator must have, not a measurement of anyone's ability to apply it. I am also not claiming that criticism is honest and delegitimation is dishonest as a property of speakers; the same speaker can produce either depending on the case.
>
> **If it survives, and only then:** sources are below. The figures, not my summary of them, are the authority.
> - Local figure text with line numbers: `FRAMEWORK/reference/03-FIGURES_TEXT.md` in the Project Pathfinder workspace.
> - Public figures repository (branch `general-agent`): https://github.com/IsaiahN/tether/tree/general-agent/figures
> - Canonical figures README: https://github.com/IsaiahN/tether/blob/general-agent/figures/README.md
>
> **If you want to use the method:** write down what would refute your own result before you run it, state the mode of what you get (`general` / `specified` / `grounded`), and keep the verdicts for different domains separate. A reported failure is more useful to me than a reported success.
>
> Thank you for your time.

## Two-stage offer, restated

1. **Stage 1:** send the residual, the four-property discriminator, and the four refuters. Ask what the recipient would count as a failure. Record their verdict **before** showing any theory.
2. **Stage 2, opt-in only:** if they are curious, offer the three links. Later agreement is never pooled as corroboration.
3. **Stage 3:** offer the formula as a method to reuse, with the instruction that each user writes their own refuter and states their own mode.

**Stop condition:** if stage 1 fails on any refuter, stages 2 and 3 do not happen for that recipient. The failure goes into the log with provenance and is re-evaluated by the auditor seat as if it came from any other source.
