# Structure, order, and analogues: a supporting derivation

**Mode: `specified` on the structural claims, `general` on the operator reading. Simulated derivation checked against the figures, not a ground reading (see `05_what_shape_means.md` §6).**

## RESIDUAL

The main audit sorts a claim into A (justified criticism) and B (delegitimation). Two structural questions are owed before that sort is trusted:

1. What is the **bond** between the claim and the institution in each case? If only the atoms differ, the two are not the same kind of thing and the sort needs two vocabularies. If the atoms are the same and the bond differs, the sort is an isomer test.
2. What is the **order** of operations? In particular, must the residual be described before the frame is chosen, and must a mixed document be unbundled before it is scored?

## DOMAIN CENSUS

Atoms held for a claim about an institution: a claim C, an institution I, a ground G (standard or outcome), an audience O, and a standing S of I in O. Bonds available: the seven figure bonds plus negation (Operators table). No new primitive is available without import. The two figures that govern this derivation are Fig 12 (bond and isomer) and Fig 9 (union versus partition), with Fig 3 giving the dependency order.

## DERIVATION

### Q1  -  What bond holds in each generation?

Fig 12 :46: **"A bond is the typed relation between two operands. There are seven, they are not interchangeable, and which one holds is not recoverable from the operands."**

- **A, justified criticism:** C is bonded to G by a **comparison** (⋛): the institution's performance is set against its standard. Operators table :48: *"a threshold relation between two quantities"*; :51: *"a goal is a comparison, which is why a vocabulary without it cannot state one."* The claim says, in effect, "the output is below the standard."
- **B, delegitimation:** C is bonded to S, not to G. Its operation is to change the standing of I in O. Structurally this is an attempt to make the anchor update, which Fig 2 :33 forbids for a legitimate discriminator. It is **not** the negation operator (¬): Fig 5 :53 makes refusal a legitimate term, and a unary operator (Operators :70-74) cannot join the institution to an audience. The S3 mapping in `01` §5b is retracted for this reason.

So the two generations have the **same atoms** (C, I, G, O, S) but a **different bond**: comparison to the ground versus comparison to the standing. That is a candidate isomer.

### Q2  -  Are they isomers?

Fig 12 :117: **"isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."**

If both generations are written over the full atom set `{C, I, G, O, S}`, differing only in which operand C is bonded to (G for A, S for B), they meet Fig 12's definition: same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the surface text (both are negative claims) and decisive in behaviour (one can be settled by G, the other cannot).

The weaker reading: many real attacks **drop G entirely**. If the ground atom is absent from B's molecule, the two do not share all atoms and are not isomers. They then share a **functional group**, Fig 12 :124: *"functional group. A substructure whose presence fixes the outcome. The group says what kind; the qualifier says which member."* Both are "negative public claim about an institution"; the qualifier (which operand is bonded) decides the member.

**Which reading applies is an empirical question about the actual text, not a definitional one.** The honest output is: *isomers where the full atom set is present; functional-group variants where G is dropped.* Both are checkable by inspecting whether the claim names a standard and ranges against it.

### Q3  -  What is the order of operations?

Fig 3 :20-21: **"The chain is ordered by dependency, not by importance. Each link consumes what the one above produces, so effort spent above the break arrives at a step that cannot use it."**

Ordered steps for this question:

1. **Describe the residual in effect terms.** Which alleged failure, against which standard, on which slot, at which scale? Fig 9 :27-28: *"describe it before you go looking, or any frame you pick will seem to fit."*
2. **Check the anchor.** Does the named standard stay fixed when either side pushes on it? Fig 2 :29-30.
3. **Check the frames.** Do the corroborating sources draw on different evidence? Fig 2 :31-32.
4. **Check the channel and stake.** Is an institution grading its own reading, and is the stake in the reading declared? Fig 10 :24, :59-60.
5. **Only then sort the claim.** Steps 2 to 4 are the inputs to the comparison; a claim sorted before them has been classified by surface features.

Fig 3 :27: **"A link with no instrument is not a link that works. It is a link nobody has looked at."** Step 1 has an instrument in the literature (effect description, source/content vigilance); step 4 has partial instruments (conflict-of-interest disclosure, psychological safety surveys). F7 in `01` records that no single instrument returns a residual on the whole sort.

### Q4  -  Partition or union?

Fig 9 :54: **"And when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again."**

A document that both cites a real failure and attacks the institution's right to exist is a **union compound**, not one hard claim. Fig 9 :55: *"A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones."* Fig 9 :57: *"a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either."*

Operation: segment the text into ground-ranging assertions and standing-attacking assertions; score each separately on its own loop; recombine with the union recorded, never averaged. This is the direct analogue of unbundling before search: a single lever clears only the region where one condition holds, leaving the intersection standing.

### Q5  -  Analogues from low-overlap fields

These corroborate structure only; they do not decide the figure verdict.

1. **Political science.** Easton's distinction between *specific support* (performance satisfaction) and *diffuse support* (the reservoir of goodwill for the institution itself) is the same two-operand structure: outputs versus the object. Driscoll and Nelson record that specific support is *"a quid pro quo for the fulfillment of demands"* while diffuse support is longer-term and *"sticky"*. A criticism of outputs and an attack on the reservoir share the institution but bond to different operands. This corroborates Q1 and Q4.
2. **Animal behaviour.** Searcy and Nowicki define reliability as a correlation between a signal characteristic and an attribute of the world that the receiver benefits from knowing, and deceit as the breaking of that correlation with benefit to the sender. That is the bond difference in Q1, stated in a field with no contact with the framework. Penn and Számadó complicate it by refusing the simple "costly equals honest" mapping, which is the same refusal the figures make about standing rosters (F4).
3. **Cognitive science.** Sperber and colleagues split epistemic vigilance into *vigilance towards the source* (competence and honesty) and *vigilance towards the content* (believability). That is a two-operand check, and it corroborates the refusal to average domain verdicts into one trust score.

Overlap warning: the three analogues come from three genuinely different literature pools, so their convergence is weaker evidence than figure-to-figure triangulation but broader in field coverage. They are still secondary.

## FALSIFIER, WRITTEN BEFORE THIS RUN

The isomer/functional-group reading in Q2 and the order in Q3 are refuted if any of the following holds:

1. A claim can be shown to bond to G and to S simultaneously with no union structure, so unbundling (Q4) is impossible even in principle.
2. The bond in B is shown to be a genuine negation of a claim I holds, making ¬ the correct operator and the S3 retraction wrong.
3. A correct sort is produced reliably when the residual is described *after* the frame is chosen, checked against a pre-registered description, so the dependency order in Q3 is not load-bearing.
4. The two generations are shown to use disjoint atom sets in every case, so the functional-group reading also fails and there is no shared structure to compare.

If any fires, this supporting derivation is withdrawn and `07_the_tether_answer.md` is re-scripted. None was fired in this run.
