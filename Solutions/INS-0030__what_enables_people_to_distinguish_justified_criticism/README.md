## The question as an effect

An observer sees a claim directed at an institution. Two generations are possible. One tracks a real failure of the institution against a standard it is answerable to (justified criticism). The other aims to reduce the institution's standing whether or not the standard was missed (delegitimation). The two look alike on the surface: both are negative, both cite facts, both can be loud. The residual is the missing discriminator: something that lets the observer sort the two without reading the critic's motives directly. An answer is a shape with four properties, stated below and bound as ordinary terms so they can accrue a residual (Formula step "Direction").

## What is deliberately not here

- No personal emails of named individuals.
- No ranking of people by how likely they are to agree with this answer.
- No bulk-send plan, no purchased list, no scraping.

Three reasons in the figures' own words:

1. **Agreement is not a verdict.** Fig 2 :40: *"disqualifying for a verdict: agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool"* (Fig 2 :40). A roster selected for receptiveness manufactures the disqualifying correlation.
2. **A room does not upgrade the label.** Fig 7 :35: *"A room's agreement is variation, not a verdict. Selection between rooms is where the ground votes."* Many agreeing readers add vocabulary, not an anchor.
3. **Do not hand the recipient the interiors.** Fig 10 :42: *"A verifier that can reconstruct the claim cannot verify it."* The first contact carries the residual and its refuter, not the framework.

Legally: unsolicited bulk email breaches GDPR and CAN-SPAM style rules in many jurisdictions and gets the sending domain blacklisted. That is a practical failure on top of the structural one.
