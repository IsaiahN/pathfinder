## Round trip gap R_T

The Symbols table defines R_T as the gap between what was sent up and what came back down, `gap(x, (T_E ∘ T_A)(x))`, measuring what the coarser description cannot hold. Fig 4 :30 requires it to be measured rather than assumed. I ran the round trip on the condensation this folder actually uses.

1. **Sent up (x):** the verbatim figure set, chiefly Fig 4 :23-25 (method versus recording), Fig 12 :117 (isomer), Fig 1 :19 (per-slot residual), Fig 2 :31 (independence).
2. **Abstraction (T_A):** the shorthand carried in `07` §2: measure a round trip gap, report per context, run an isomer check, triangulate with two low-overlap instruments, write a refuter first.
3. **Concretisation (T_E):** a concrete proposal such as "run ARC or SCAN with a per-item residual vector and a second probe," or the stronger but unsupported "this benchmark proves flexible use."

**R_T is nonzero** in the stronger direction and small in the weaker one. What survives the trip back down: per-context reporting, the ordered nature of transfer, the need for a second low-overlap instrument, and the requirement that a failure condition be written first. What drops: the prohibition on naming any test (corrected in `05` §6 and `06` Q1), and the idea that a figure line selects a particular benchmark. R_T was **not** measured numerically, because no ground reading exists; it is recorded qualitatively as the enumerated set of dropped constraints. Treating "the formula is the condensed version of the metalaws" as identity would set R_T to zero and would be the dead-reckoning error Fig 4 :30 names.
