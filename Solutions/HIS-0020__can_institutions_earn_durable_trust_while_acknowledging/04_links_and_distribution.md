## Three things in the wider literature that bear on the claim

1. **The reviews disagree, and the disagreement is informative.** Dries et al. 2026 find no overall trust effect of uncertainty communication across 28 experiments; Schuster et al. 2026 find a generally positive effect across 24 articles; Guo et al. 2025 find different signs for different uncertainty objects. These are not measurements of the same quantity. Trust in a source, trust in a field, and response to consensus uncertainty are different slots (Fig 1 :19). Report the divergence; do not average it.
2. **Trust in the number and trust in the messenger separate.** van der Bles et al. 2019 and the 2023 COVID-statistics study both report that numeric uncertainty can reduce trust in the estimate while leaving trust in the source intact. That is a direct reason to keep the audience half and the ground half of the residual separate in any report.
3. **Durable reliability has an institutional shape, and it is not absence of error.** The high-reliability literature says the signature is that errors do not disable the organisation, and that reliability is persistent mindfulness rather than a static property. That is the engineering-language instance of Fig 10 :19 (*"install what can be violated"*) and Fig 10 :93. It comes from a field with no shared evidence pool with the figures.

## Distribution: offer, do not push

Figure-grounded reasons:

- **Fig 4 :22-25** - *"Only methods cross up. Only head starts come down."* Sending the full framework before anyone has described a residual sends a recording, not a generator. Lead with the shape and its refuter; offer the links after.
- **Fig 10 :42** - *"A verifier that can reconstruct the claim cannot verify it."* A recipient who has absorbed the whole architecture before being asked for a verdict has joined the frame being checked.
- **Fig 9 :27-28** - *"describe it before you go looking, or any frame you pick will seem to fit"* (line breaks normalised). Ask what they would expect to see if the shape claim were true before showing them anything.
- **Fig 2 :40** - *"agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool."* A send selected for people likely to agree produces a convention, not corroboration.
- **Seat Map :171** - *"an interest in the reading is disqualifying."* Declare any stake before the check, never after.

**Recommended order in a reply:** (1) the shape claim and its three conditions; (2) the pre-registered refuter and the question "what would you count as a failure?"; (3) record their verdict; (4) only if they are curious, the three links, with the note that the figures, not the summary, are the authority.

## The two-stage offer

**Stage 1.** Send the residual, the shape claim and the refuter. Ask what they would count as a failure. Record the verdict before any theory is shown. This is Fig 10 :52 (*"Bring a characterised residual, never the source"*) and Fig 2 :45 (*"Frames range against the ground. They do not negotiate with it."*).

**Stage 2, opt-in only.** If they are curious, offer the figures, the formula and the canonical article. Their later agreement is never pooled as corroboration (Fig 2 :40). A stage-1 verdict and a stage-2 endorsement are different objects.

**Stage 3, method reuse.** The formula is a generator, so giving it away does not give away an answer (Fig 4 :22). Each user still needs a ground per problem and writes their own refuter and mode, because a frame *"cannot check the direction of its own question"* (`THE_FORMULA.md`, Direction).

**Hard precondition:** stages 1 to 3 only work if the stage-1 shape survives. It is currently a `specified`, ungrounded shape with a declared `gap`; it is not a measurement of any institution.
