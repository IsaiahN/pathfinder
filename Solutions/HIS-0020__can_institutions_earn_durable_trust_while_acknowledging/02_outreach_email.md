# Outreach draft: check the shape before you decide about the framework

**Use:** one to one, or as a cover note attached to a submission through the public venues in `03_public_contact_points.csv`. **Not** for bulk send.

**Hard precondition:** do not send until the three sub-claims in `08_expert_verification_packet.md` survive the reader's own check. If the stage-1 shape fails, the two-stage offer collapses on contact.

---

**Subject:** One checkable claim about institutional trust, before any theory

> Hi [name],
>
> I work on institutional trust and uncertainty research. I have one specific claim I would like you to check against what you already know. I am not asking you to read the framework first, I am not asking you to agree with it, and I am not asking you to trust anything. If the claim does not survive, there is nothing else to look at.
>
> **The claim (`specified`, not `grounded`):** an institution keeps trust over decades not by projecting certainty but by staying disqualifiable. Three conditions have to hold, and they are different conditions with different instruments:
>
> 1. It keeps a ground reachable that does not change its answer because of what any reader thinks. The framework's line is that alignment is triangulation, not negotiation, and that a mirror cannot correct you.
> 2. The disagreement it hosts comes from frames drawing on different evidence, ranging against that ground separately. Agreement from one shared evidence pool is one witness speaking twice, and the framework treats it as disqualifying for a verdict.
> 3. A wrong ruling stays correctable. The record carries the residual answered, the reasoning and the date, so a later reader can locate the error rather than only feel it. Provenance shows a ruling was reached legitimately, never that it was right.
>
> What falls out of this is that acknowledging uncertainty and disagreement is not a tax on trust. It is the input to the mechanism that makes correction possible. The framework's own words for an unstatable disagreement are that it is "not yet a defect"; what is owed is a convention that makes the bug statable and installs what can be violated.
>
> **What I am explicitly not claiming:** that any named institution has or lacks durable trust; that a particular governance design is the trustworthy one; that publishing a trust score helps; or that admitting uncertainty always raises trust in an audience. On that last point the empirical literature itself disagrees, with one 2026 meta-analysis of 28 experiments finding no overall effect and another 2026 review of 24 articles finding a generally positive effect. I am not averaging them.
>
> **What would refute the shape claim:**
> 1. audience trust persists across a measured loss of ground contact, with no new independent witness and no shared-pool explanation;
> 2. demonstrably independent disagreement against a pinned ground gives no better ground accuracy, over many claims, than suppressed disagreement;
> 3. a keeper's reputation improves settlement rates without changing who may refuse a ruling, or complete provenance systematically delays correction.
>
> I wrote these down before sending this. If any one holds, I retract the claim. A one-line "this fails because..." is more useful to me than agreement. If you would rather not engage, no reply is needed.
>
> **If it survives, and only then:** the sources are below. The figures are the authority, not my summary of them.
> - Canonical figures text with line numbers (local extract): `FRAMEWORK/reference/03-FIGURES_TEXT.md`
> - Figures and formula, branch `general-agent`: https://github.com/IsaiahN/tether/tree/general-agent/figures
> - Full architecture: https://medium.com/@IsaiahNwukor/arrival-of-the-fittest-the-tether-architecture-1de7edf641ec
>
> **If you want to use the machinery:** the formula is in the figures link. Run it on a problem you can check against a ground. Write down what would refute your result *before* you run it, and state the mode of what you get. A reported failure is more useful to me than a reported success.

## Why this version is ordered this way

| the move | figure-grounded reason |
|---|---|
| Claim and refuter first, theory second | Fig 10 :52, *"Bring a characterised residual, never the source."* The recipient checks the shape without holding the framework's interior. |
| Do not ask for trust or agreement | Fig 2 :35, *"Alignment is triangulation, not negotiation. A mirror cannot correct you."* Asking for trust manufactures the correlation that disqualifies a verdict. |
| Three conditions kept separate | Fig 9 :57, *"a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either."* Here there are three; a blended trust score is a pooled reading (Fig 10 :26). |
| Pre-registered refuter | Seat Map :287, *"a falsifier written before the run is a design instrument, not only an honesty device."* |
| Mode on every substantive claim | `THE_FORMULA.md`, *"Mode. general · specified · grounded. State which."* |
| No claim about a named institution or a winning policy | Fig 9 :32, *"you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself."* |
| The two empirical reviews are not averaged | Fig 2 :40 and Fig 9 :60, *"Use filters for the budget and witnesses for the verdict. Never let a filter hand you a verdict."* |
| No request for prestige | Seat Map :171, *"an interest in the reading is disqualifying."* Also *The Recursive Self-Improvement Trap*: "Prestige can fund an action... but it cannot settle an action. Only the ground can settle it." |
| Offer the formula as a method to reuse | Fig 4 :22, *"Only methods cross up. Only head starts come down."* |
| Each user writes their own refuter | `THE_FORMULA.md` "Direction": a frame cannot check the direction of its own question because the sought shape is not a term it holds. |

## What this email does not do

It does not contain a personal address, a receptiveness rank, a bulk-send plan, a claim that the framework is the only true one, or a request for endorsement. Fig 7 :13: *"agreement compounds, evidence does not."* A reply that agrees is recorded as a conversation, not as corroboration.
