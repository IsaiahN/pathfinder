# Expert verification packet: the Tether on institutional trust under uncertainty and disagreement

**What this is:** a claim in a form a checker can examine. Mode, derivation and refuters are stated before the conclusion, so a reader knows what to look for and what would settle it.

**Mode: `specified` on the institutional claims, `general` on the structural shape. Not `grounded`.** No institution was measured. This is a shape and a route, not a result. It is a simulated derivation checked for internal consistency against the figures, not a ground reading.

## 1. The claim, in one paragraph

Durable institutional trust is a relation to a ground, not a credential an institution can issue to itself. It holds when three conditions are met and they are different conditions with different instruments: (C1) a ground is kept reachable that does not change its answer because of what any reader thinks; (C2) disagreement comes from frames drawing on different evidence and ranging against that ground separately; (C3) a wrong ruling stays correctable because the record carries what was answered, the reasoning and the date. The institution must declare the stake it cannot remove and must not let its standing decide what the ground returned. Acknowledging uncertainty and disagreement is not a tax on this mechanism; statable uncertainty and independent disagreement are its inputs. The framework does not name which institution or policy wins.

## 2. Three checkable sub-claims

### C1: framework-internal; checkable by reading Figures 1 and 2

- **Statement:** trust cannot be scored by the institution being scored; the ground, not agreement, settles a reading, and the ground must not be made a party to the disagreement.
- **Source, verbatim:**
  - Fig 1 :26 *"predicates minted, and credibility accrued"* and Fig 1 :27 *"They are frame-internal. A frame cannot score itself with a quantity it also produces."*
  - Fig 2 :29 *"An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks."*
  - Fig 2 :35 *"Alignment is triangulation, not negotiation. A mirror cannot correct you."*
  - Fig 2 :46 *"The anchor is not a third party to the disagreement and it is not the tie-breaker in a vote. Each frame is corrected by it separately, and what makes the correction sound is that neither frame can move it."*
- **Refuter:** an institution whose audience trust persists across a measured loss of ground contact, with no new independent witness and no shared-evidence-pool explanation. Note the scope: audience judgment is an admissible anchor when the question is constitutively about people (Fig 2 :36-38), but it is an instrument only for the ground half of this question. If those halves are pooled, the check is invalid.

### C2: framework-internal plus world-facing; checkable by reading Figures 2, 8 and 10 and by inspecting the verifier pool

- **Statement:** disagreement improves correction only when frames are independent and range against a pinned ground; agreement from a shared pool is one witness, and an institution-appointed panel is an authored room.
- **Source, verbatim:**
  - Fig 2 :31 *"Independence, between two frames, is how little they draw on the same evidence."* and Fig 2 :32 *"is what makes agreement mean something."*
  - Fig 2 :40 *"disqualifying for a verdict: agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool"*
  - Fig 8 :45 *"Two frames together reach what neither reaches alone."*
  - Fig 10 :14 *"a disagreement nothing can state"*, :15 *"is not yet a defect"*, :19 *"→ install what can be violated"*
  - Fig 10 :73 *"A candidate set chosen by a participant is an authored room however well the selection is reasoned"*
- **What to check:** who appointed the verifiers, what evidence do they draw on, and is there a ground none of them can move? Then check the world half: Oreskes 2019 argues that reliability comes from *"the collective or social process of critical scrutiny"*, while the HRO literature reports that reliable organisations show *"preoccupation with failure"* and *"deference to frontline expertise"*. These are corroborations from different fields, not figure lines, and they do not decide the verdict.
- **Refuter:** demonstrably independent disagreement against a pinned ground yields no better ground-accuracy, over many claims and years, than suppressed disagreement. A second refuter: an institution-selected, low-independence panel reliably produces ground-settled corrections at the same rate as unauthored panels.

### C3: framework-internal; checkable by reading Figure 10

- **Statement:** the checker must be unable to produce what it checks, must not own the reading by which it is graded, must declare its stake, and must keep a record that makes errors correctable rather than merely legitimated.
- **Source, verbatim:**
  - Fig 10 :42 *"A verifier that can reconstruct the claim cannot verify it."*
  - Fig 10 :43 *"Soundness requires the checker to be unable to produce what it is checking."*
  - Fig 10 :59 *"an interest in the outcome is unavoidable, an interest in the reading is disqualifying."*
  - Fig 10 :61 *"And standing is a stake that survives the outcome it was earned on."*
  - Fig 10 :65 *"Provenance shows a ruling was reached legitimately. It never shows it was right: the ground does that, and a wrong ruling stays wrong however clean its record."*
  - Fig 10 :93 *"A ground with a reputation to protect is a ground someone benefits from, and its keeper is thereby disqualified."*
- **Refuter:** a keeper's reputation improves ground-settlement rates without any change in who may refuse a ruling; or institutions with complete provenance systematically delay correction relative to matched institutions without it, with settlement measured.

## 3. What is explicitly not claimed

- Not that any named institution has or lacks durable trust. Nothing was measured.
- Not that publishing an uncertainty statement, a trust score or a reputation ranking earns trust. Fig 1 :27 makes self-produced credibility frame-internal.
- Not that admitting uncertainty always raises audience trust. The empirical reviews disagree by object; that divergence is reported, not averaged (see `01` F7).
- Not that disagreement is good in itself. Without independence it is drift or a common cause (Fig 2 :13-21).
- Not a theory of everything, not a final room (Fig 7 :6 names *"the limit: no last room"*).
- Not a `grounded` result. No per-claim record for any institution was opened.
- Not a name for the winning governance design. Fig 9 :32: *"you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself."*

## 5. What would settle it

A source-discriminating instrument, not a further argument. From *The Hard Problem is About Source*: a reading *"cannot comment on the source"*, and *"The answer is unreached until a better instrument exists."* Here that means a per-claim record that separates C1, C2 and C3 failures:

1. a ground-contact record: what was issued per claim and what the ground returned, readable by someone who held no interior;
2. an independence audit: what each disagreeing frame draws on, and whether any of them can move the ground;
3. a correction record: time from error found to error corrected, with and without provenance.

None of these is a trust survey. A trust survey reads the projection; the instrument must discriminate the source.
