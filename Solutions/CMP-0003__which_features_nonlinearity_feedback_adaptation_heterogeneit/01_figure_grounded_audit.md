## 0. The question under audit

> Which features, nonlinearity, feedback, adaptation, heterogeneity, or network structure, are necessary for complex behavior to emerge?
>
> Source: `Questions/complexity science.txt` line 6. Section 1, Foundations, definitions, and explanation.

**Effect restatement.** The word "necessary" is a counterfactual: remove feature F and complex behavior fails to emerge. The question therefore asks for a membership test over a five-element candidate set. It does not ask which features are common, correlated, or useful, and that distinction is the whole residual.

**Bind the sought-for shape as an ordinary term (Formula, "Direction").** Write down what an answer would look like before searching. Call it S: *a constraint that any candidate complex system must satisfy, stated independently of the five named features, such that each named feature can be checked against S and refused or admitted on the same test.* Without this binding, the search will pick whichever of the five features it happens to like and then find evidence for it.

## 1. The residual in effect terms

- **R (arity).** Five candidates, plus an unstated sixth possibility, that none of them is necessary. Arity is therefore six, not five.
- **R (symmetry).** Are the candidates interchangeable members of a list, or do they sit in different roles? If interchangeable, the question is a partition over one kind. If not, it is a union of overlapping conditions with an order.
- **R (scale).** Some candidates name a property of composition (nonlinearity, feedback), some name a property of the loop over time (adaptation), some name a property of the components (heterogeneity), and one names a member of the structural vocabulary (network). They are not on one scale.
- **R (anchor).** What could settle "necessary"? A counterfactual experiment that removes a feature and observes whether complex behavior fails, across all systems. No such anchor is available here. By Fig 2 :29, *"An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks."* With no anchor, any verdict is `specified`, not `grounded`.
- **R (slot).** What cannot be perceived yet is residual (Symbols table: R is *"always a slice: what cannot be perceived yet is residual and simply cannot be read"*). The missing discriminator among the five candidates is itself the residual.

## 4. Findings

### F1: The question is a union presented as a list (decisive)

Fig 9 :54: *"And when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again."*
Fig 9 :55: *"A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones."*
Fig 9 :57: *"What remains is smaller and it is also categorically different: a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either."*

The five candidates are mutually presupposing. A feedback loop is a cycle (Fig 12 :121); a cycle amplifies a remainder (Fig 13 :32); a remainder that re-enters requires a loop that carries (Formula step 8); a loop that carries requires something to carry (components, hence heterogeneity of roles if not of stuff); and "influence travels" is the network structure itself (Fig 13 :24). Asking which one is necessary is asking which intersection of a union to delete while keeping the others. The figures' instruction is to unbundle first. Verdict for D1 question type: **union, not partition**. Status: `general`, triangulated across Fig 9 and Fig 12 (two figures, low overlap: one governs problem splitting, the other governs bond types).

### F2: Only network structure is a primitive member of the minimum set

Fig 13 :3: *"No system functions without any of them, none is built from the others, and everything else is a composition across the two kinds."*
Fig 13 :41: *"Each is indivisible: no arrangement of the other five yields it."*
Fig 13 :48: *"And the set is minimal: remove one and something stops."*
Fig 13 :59: *"no relation between systems, and no configurations to count"* (the "without it" column for network).
Fig 13 :24: *"that entities relate, and influence travels"* (what network decides).

Network is one of the six structures. Remove it and, by the figure's own minimality table, there is no relation between systems and no configurations to count. Of the five candidates in the question, **network structure alone is a member of the minimum viable set**. Nonlinearity, feedback, adaptation and heterogeneity are not members. They are governed by other lines, below. Status: `specified` for the mapping from the phrase "network structure" onto the figure's network, triangulated across Fig 13 :3 and Fig 13 :41 and :59 (substrate statement plus minimality test). The mapping itself is ungrounded: no experiment here shows that the figure's six are nature's six.

### F3: Nonlinearity is what a composition leaves behind, not a member

Fig 13 :28: *"They alternate, and the alternation is where both the recursion and the randomness come from."*
Fig 13 :32: *"a remainder compounding through a chain that amplifies is chaos"*
Fig 13 :124-125: *"Smoothness, symmetry, chaos and discreteness are each what a particular composition leaves behind, and none is a seventh member."*
Fig 13 :71: *"no direction to drive the flow, and no remainder left after each step"* (the "without it" column for gradient).

Chaos and the directedness that can be spent are *left behind* by composition, or are listed as what is lost when a member is removed. They are not primitives and a fortiori not necessary features in the same sense as network. A direct dynamical-systems reading of "nonlinearity" (failure of superposition) is a **strain**, because Fig 13 :32 governs the origin of randomness in a composition chain, not the algebraic property of an equation. Under the strain test, that line governs another quantity, so the mapping is re-censused rather than forced. What survives is the shape statement: if complex behavior includes an amplifying remainder, a composition that can leave one is required, and the figure locates the source in alternation, not in a feature that can be added to a list. Status: `general`, single-source for the figure reading; the empirical nonlinearity claim is not decided here.

### F4: Feedback is the loop, not an optional feature

Fig 12 :121: *"cycle. The output re-enters as input. The sign of the loop decides whether it closes, and a chain cannot close by construction."*
Fig 6 :21: *"MINT composes inside the closure and can never add an atom."*
`THE_FORMULA.md` "Closure": *"A feedback loop: the ground is the reference, R is the error signal, Γ is the controller."*

Feedback in these figures is not a component you install. It is what makes a chain a loop, and the whole loop is the minimal machine. A chain *cannot* close by construction, so a system without re-entry is not a system that has simply omitted feedback; it is a different kind of object. This is why the question's list form misleads: feedback is at a different level of description from a candidate primitive. Status: `general`, triangulated across Fig 12 :121 (the cycle bond) and the formula's closure definition (two different statements, one about bonds and one about the loop).

### F5: Adaptation is the loop changing its own vocabulary, not a standing feature

`THE_FORMULA.md` step 3 to 4 and "Closure": steps 3 and 4 *"change the controller's vocabulary, not its parameters."*
Fig 11 :40: *"A system cannot be made more capable by improving the agent in isolation. An improvement that does not change contact changes nothing, however much it improves."*
Fig 3 :15: *"name what worked, verify it, and carry it to the next problem"* (link 5, learn and carry).

Adaptation in the framework is the loop's own operation: mint, settle, carry, and the refusal recorded so reach can fall. It is not a property a component carries. Fig 11 :40 adds the anchor condition: an improvement that does not change contact changes nothing. So "is adaptation necessary?" is not answerable as a feature question; it is the loop asking whether it is running. A strain is noted: evolutionary adaptation in biology and perfect adaptation in control theory are different quantities from the figure's mint-and-carry. Those fields are heard in WATSON as corroboration on structure, not as instances of the figure term. Status: `general`, single-source for the figure reading.

### F6: Heterogeneity is independence and relative valence, not a property of stuff

Fig 2 :31: *"Independence, between two frames, is how little they draw on the same evidence."*
Fig 13 :38: *"independence is how little two frames draw on the same evidence"*
Fig 13 :39: *"Six members that overlapped would be fewer members wearing six names."*
Fig 13 :37: *"their agreement would carry no information, and one of them would be redundant rather than minimal."*
Fig 11 :49: *"An actor's valence is relative to the residual, not a property it carries."*
Fig 11 :51: *"No standing list of good actors survives a change of residual."*

Heterogeneity is not "the components are different." It is the condition that frames or members draw on different evidence, so that their agreement carries information and the set is not one member wearing six names. Fig 11 :49 makes valence relative to the residual: the same actor is benefactor to one residual and malfactor to another. That kills any standing answer of the form "diversity is necessary", because diversity is relative to a question. Status: `general`, triangulated across Fig 2 :31 and Fig 13 :38-39 (the anchor/independence rule applied to the substrate itself).

### F7: The framework returns a shape, not a name

Fig 9 :32: *"you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself"*
Fig 13 :134: *"Unreachable is a verdict here, not a silence"*
Fig 13 :138: *"so something has to be imported before it is asked again"*
`FIGURES_README_canonical.md`: the framework says *"what a missing thing would have to look like (**a shape, not a name**)"* and stops *"at the limit of what the available instruments can distinguish, and records the lineage so the stop is honest."*

So S (§0) is exactly what is available: the narrowed form of the answer, not a filled slot. The figures can say that the five candidates are not one kind of thing and that the union has an order. They cannot say which empirical feature is necessary in all complex systems, because that would require a ground that removes features one at a time. Status: `general`, triangulated across Fig 9 and the canonical README.

### F8: The five candidates are filters for the budget, never witnesses for the verdict

Fig 9 :60: *"Use filters for the budget and witnesses for the verdict. Never let a filter hand you a verdict."*
Fig 2 :42: *"Buy independence in proportion to the cost of being wrong."*

A feature list is useful for deciding what to investigate first. It is not evidence that the feature is necessary. A campaign that gathers agreement that "feedback is the key" has produced a convention, not a finding. Status: `general`, single-source for the figure line; the outreach consequence is developed in `02` and `04`.

## 5. Pre-registered refuter

Written before the conclusion, per the standing rule that a falsifier written before the run is a design instrument. The shape claim S is refused if any of the following holds:

1. A single empirical feature among the five is shown to be necessary and sufficient for complex behavior across domains by a counterfactual removal experiment, with a named anchor that does not move when frames disagree. This would break the union claim at the ground.
2. One of the six terms is derived from the other five, or a seventh term is derived that is not a composition of them. This breaks Fig 13 :41 and :48.
3. The figures are shown to admit a feature list as a verdict rather than as a filter for the budget, breaking Fig 9 :60.
4. A frame is found whose closure predicts the shape of this residual at lower overlap than the figures do, and whose import passes a source gate and shadow test (Fig 6 :23). That would move the wall and change the verdict.

If any fires, the shape claim is withdrawn. Until one is run, no part of this may be labelled `grounded`.

## 6. Recommendation

1. Stop asking "which one is necessary" as a list question. Re-ask it as: which conditions are primitive, which are derivatives of composition, which are the loop itself, and which are frame-relative? (F1 to F6).
2. State the mode on every sentence. The only empirical claim, network as a primitive, remains `specified` until a ground settles the six-term set itself (F2).
3. Keep the verdict a shape. Do not name a winner among the five (F7).
4. Use the feature list to decide what to investigate first, never to decide what is true (F8).
5. Fig 4 :22: *"Only methods cross up. Only head starts come down."* Send the union split and the refuter, not the conclusion.
