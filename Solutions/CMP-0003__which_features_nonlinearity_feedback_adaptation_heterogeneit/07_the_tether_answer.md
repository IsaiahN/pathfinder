# The Tether's answer on necessary features of complex behavior

**Mode: `general` on the shape, `specified` on the structural classification. Not `grounded`.** This is a shape and a route, not an empirical result. Simulated derivation checked against the figures (`05_what_shape_means.md` §6), not a ground reading.

## 1. The residual it answers

The question asks which of five features are necessary for complex behavior to emerge. Read in effect terms, it asks for a counterfactual membership test: remove a feature, watch complex behavior fail, conclude necessity. No instrument here performs that removal across domains. What is available is a structural re-census of the five candidates against the figure vocabulary. The result is a shape: the candidates are not one kind of thing (Fig 9 :54, *"And when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again."*).

## 2. The shape

1. **The question is a union presented as a list.** The five candidates presuppose one another: a cycle amplifies a remainder, the loop carries it, the components differ in role, and influence travels through a network. Asking which one is necessary asks which intersection to delete while keeping the rest (Fig 9 :55-57).
2. **Network structure alone is a primitive member of the minimum set.** Fig 13 :3 says *"No system functions without any of them, none is built from the others, and everything else is a composition across the two kinds."* Network is one of the six structures (Fig 13 :24); without it there is *"no relation between systems, and no configurations to count"* (Fig 13 :59). This mapping is `specified`, not settled against nature.
3. **Nonlinearity is a derivative, not a member.** Fig 13 :32: *"a remainder compounding through a chain that amplifies is chaos"*; Fig 13 :124-125: *"Smoothness, symmetry, chaos and discreteness are each what a particular composition leaves behind, and none is a seventh member."* The figure's chaos is not the same quantity as failure of superposition, and the strain is named.
4. **Feedback is the loop, not an optional feature.** Fig 12 :121: *"cycle. The output re-enters as input. The sign of the loop decides whether it closes, and a chain cannot close by construction."* A system without re-entry is a different object, not one missing a part.
5. **Adaptation is an operation of the loop.** Mint, settle, carry (Formula steps 3 to 8). Fig 11 :40: *"An improvement that does not change contact changes nothing, however much it improves."* The figure governs contact, not biological adaptation, and that strain is named too.
6. **Heterogeneity is a relation, not a property of stuff.** Fig 11 :49: *"An actor's valence is relative to the residual, not a property it carries."* Fig 13 :39: *"Six members that overlapped would be fewer members wearing six names."* No standing list of diverse or good components survives a change of question.
7. **The framework returns a shape, not a name.** Fig 9 :32: *"you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself."* Fig 13 :134: *"Unreachable is a verdict here, not a silence."* So no empirical winner is named.

## 3. What it explicitly does not say

- Not that feedback, or any other single feature, *is* the necessary feature of complex systems.
- Not that the figure's six-term set is nature's own list. Fig 13 :134 and the canonical README both make the stop visible.
- Not that chaos (a compounded remainder) is the same thing as dynamical nonlinearity (failure of superposition). The strain is recorded in `01` §8.
- Not that adaptation in control theory or evolution is the figure's mint-and-carry. World sources in `09` are heard on structure only.
- Not that a feature list is a verdict. Fig 9 :60: *"Use filters for the budget and witnesses for the verdict. Never let a filter hand you a verdict."*
- Not `grounded`. No counterfactual removal experiment settles any of this.

## 4. What would settle it: the ground

A counterfactual instrument, not a further argument. The corresponding questions:

- **Removal Test:** can one feature be removed while complex behavior persists, across domains, with an anchor that does not move when frames disagree? If yes for all five, the necessity claim is empty.
- **Primitive Test:** derive one of the six terms from the other five, or derive a seventh that is not a composition. Either breaks Fig 13 :41 and :48.
- **Union Test:** show that the five candidates are independent conditions (a partition) rather than overlapping ones. That would reverse F1.
- **Import Test:** find a frame whose closure predicts this residual at lower overlap and whose import passes a source gate and shadow test (Fig 6 :23). That would move the wall.

## 5. The honest stop

**Unreached, not unreachable.** The six-term set is a visible choice, not a discovery of nature's list. The answer stops at the limit of the available instruments and records the lineage so the stop is checkable. *"Indiscernibility is not identity"* is the canonical README's rule. What is delivered is the shape any candidate answer must have, the four refuters written in advance, and the instruments that would close it. The empirical question stays open.

## 6. One-paragraph version for outreach

> The Tether does not name a single necessary feature of complex systems. It says that the question is a union masquerading as a list, and that the five candidates are not one kind of thing: network structure is the only one that is a primitive member of the minimum set the framework requires; nonlinearity, read as an amplifying remainder, is something a composition leaves behind; feedback is the loop itself, since a chain cannot close by construction; adaptation is that loop changing its own vocabulary; and heterogeneity is an independence between frames that is relative to the question, not a standing property of components. The framework returns that shape, not a name. It offers the dependency order among those classes, four falsifiers written in advance, and the removal, primitive, union and import tests that would close the question. The empirical winner stays open until the ground settles it.
