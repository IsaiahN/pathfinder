# Structure, order and analogues for the five candidates

**Mode: `specified` on the structural classification, `general` on the dependency order. Simulated derivation checked against the figures (`05_what_shape_means.md` §6), not a ground reading.**

## RESIDUAL

The five candidates are given as a flat list. Two questions are owed: (1) what *structure* does each candidate have under the figure vocabulary, and (2) in what *order* do they depend on one another? A third, analogue question follows: do the figure's own structural types (isomer, catalyst, cycle, functional group) classify these candidates, or do they govern something else?

## DOMAIN CENSUS

Atoms held: space, time, energy (states); spectrum, gradient, network (structures). Bonds held: the seven plus negation. Operators: eight. No new primitive is available without import. The candidates are not atoms in this library; most are names for arrangements or operations over atoms.

## DERIVATION

### Q1: Structural classification of the five candidates

- Fig 13 :3: *"No system functions without any of them, none is built from the others, and everything else is a composition across the two kinds."*
- Fig 12 :48: *"A molecule is a bonded arrangement. A chain is the degenerate case: one bond type, no branching."*

| candidate | figure structural type | member of the six? | mode |
|---|---|---|---|
| network structure | a **structure**: *"that entities relate, and influence travels"* (Fig 13 :24) | **yes** | `specified` mapping |
| nonlinearity | a **remainder of composition**: *"a remainder compounding through a chain that amplifies is chaos"* (Fig 13 :32) | no | `general` |
| feedback | the **loop / cycle bond**: *"The output re-enters as input. The sign of the loop decides whether it closes, and a chain cannot close by construction."* (Fig 12 :121) | no | `general` |

The five do not share a structural type. A list that mixes a primitive, a derivative, a bond, an operation and a relation is not a well-formed candidate set. This is the same point as F1 in `01`, reached from the structure side rather than the union side. Low overlap between routes, so the agreement is weak but not circular within the framework.

### Q2: Dependency order

Fig 3 :20-21: *"The chain is ordered by dependency, not by importance. Each link consumes what the one above produces."*

The dependency order is:

1. **Substrate**, the six terms and the rule that composition crosses kinds (Fig 13 :74: *"Everything else is a composition across the two kinds, never within one."*).
2. **Network**, the structure that lets entities relate, so that there is something for a loop to run through. Remove it: *"no relation between systems, and no configurations to count"* (Fig 13 :59).
3. **Feedback**, the cycle that makes the arrangement a loop rather than a chain (Fig 12 :121).
4. **Nonlinearity**, the amplifying remainder that the loop leaves behind when it compounds (Fig 13 :32).
5. **Adaptation**, the loop's own vocabulary change and carry once it has a residual to act on (Formula steps 3 to 8).
6. **Heterogeneity**, the independence condition that makes more than one frame or member non-redundant (Fig 13 :39: *"Six members that overlapped would be fewer members wearing six names."*).

Optimising a later link cannot rescue an earlier one (Fig 3 :19: *"Optimising a link that already works cannot rescue one that does not."*). So adding heterogeneity to a system with no relation between components buys nothing. This order is a dependency order, not an importance ranking.

### Q3: Do the figure's structural types classify the candidates?

- Fig 12 :117: *"isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."*
- Fig 12 :119: *"catalyst. A part that survives its own use."*
- Fig 12 :121: the cycle line above.
- Fig 12 :123: *"functional group. A substructure whose presence fixes the outcome. The group says what kind; the qualifier says which member."*

**Cycle** applies directly to feedback: the figure names it. **Isomer** does not apply to the five candidates, because they are not molecules sharing an atom set. It applies to *systems* built from the six: two complex systems can share all six terms and differ in which structure mediates which state pair, exactly as two isomers share composition and differ in substance. That is a claim about systems, not about features. **Catalyst** does not apply either, unless a part is shown to survive its own use (Fig 12 :119 records that "nothing records which a term is", so this needs an instrument). **Functional group** applies to the *class* "state, structure, state": it says what kind of composition a complex behavior is, and the qualifier says which member. So the analogues govern systems and compositions, not the feature list. Forcing them onto the five candidates would be a strain, so they are re-censused onto the right objects.

### Q4: What order is the union?

Fig 9 :57: *"What remains is smaller and it is also categorically different: a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either."*

The five candidates are a union, and unbundling leaves four categorically different residues (primitive, derivative, loop, relation). The union is not ordered by time of appearance in a system. It is ordered by dependency (Q2). A system can have network without adaptation; it cannot have a feedback loop without something relating (Fig 13 :59). So the order is structural, not historical.

## CONCLUSION

The five candidates are not one kind of thing and cannot be ranked as a single list. One (network) is a primitive member of the six-term set. One (nonlinearity as chaos) is a remainder a composition leaves behind. One (feedback) is the loop itself. One (adaptation) is an operation of that loop. One (heterogeneity) is a residual-relative relation between frames. The dependency order is substrate, network, feedback, nonlinearity, adaptation, heterogeneity. The figure analogues (cycle, isomer, catalyst, functional group) govern systems and compositions, not the feature list, and are re-censused accordingly.

**Falsifier, written before the run:** if a single figure structural type is found that covers all five candidates without strain, or if the dependency order is shown to be reversible (adaptation without a loop, a loop without related entities), the classification fails and the flat-list reading must be reconsidered.
