**Use:** one-to-one, or as a cover letter attached to a submission through the public venues in `03_public_contact_points.csv`. **Not** for bulk send.

**Hard precondition:** do not send until the shape claim in `01_figure_grounded_audit.md` §6 is stated exactly as written there. The output must survive validation or the two-stage offer collapses on contact.

**Subject:** Would you count this as a failure? One shape claim about necessary features in complex systems

> Hi [name],
>
> I am working on a small checkable claim about a question in complexity science: which of nonlinearity, feedback, adaptation, heterogeneity and network structure are *necessary* for complex behavior to emerge. I am not asking you to read a theory first, and I am not asking you to agree with me. If the claim does not survive, there is nothing else to look at.
>
> **The claim (`general` on the shape, `specified` on the mapping, not `grounded`):** the five features are not one kind of thing, so the question as a list has no single answer. Under the framework I am testing, four things follow.
>
> 1. Network structure is the only one of the five that is a primitive member of the minimum set the framework says any composing system needs. The others are not members.
> 2. Nonlinearity, read as an amplifying remainder, is something a composition *leaves behind*. It is a derivative, not a primitive. I am deliberately not claiming that this is the same as failure of superposition in a dynamical equation.
> 3. Feedback is the loop itself, the cycle in which output re-enters as input. A chain cannot close by construction, so a system without re-entry is a different kind of object, not one that merely forgot to include feedback.
> 4. Heterogeneity is not "the parts are different." It is independence between frames and a valence that is relative to the question, so no standing list of good or diverse components survives a change of question.
>
> This is a **shape and a route, not a measurement.** It says what any candidate answer must contain before you can name an empirical winner. It does not pick one of the five as the necessary feature, because I do not have an instrument that removes features one at a time and watches complex behavior fail.
>
> **What would refute this, written down before I sent it:**
> 1. A counterfactual removal experiment that shows one feature is necessary and sufficient across domains, with an anchor that does not move when people disagree.
> 2. A derivation of one of the framework's six terms from the other five, or a seventh term that is not a composition of them.
> 3. A demonstration that this framework admits a plain feature list as a verdict.
> 4. A different frame whose closure predicts the shape of this residual at lower overlap, and whose import passes a provenance and shadow test.
>
> If any of those holds, I withdraw the claim. A one-line "this fails because..." is far more useful to me than agreement. If you would rather not engage, no reply is needed.
>
> **If it survives, and only then:** the sources are below. The figures are the authority, not my summary of them.
> - Figures and the eight-step formula, public repository: https://github.com/IsaiahN/tether/tree/general-agent/figures
> - Canonical figures README: https://github.com/IsaiahN/tether/blob/general-agent/figures/README.md
> - Worked architecture essay: https://medium.com/@IsaiahNwukor/arrival-of-the-fittest-the-tether-architecture-1de7edf641ec
>
> **If you want to use the machinery:** it is in the figures link. Run it on a problem you know well and can check against a ground. Write down what would refute your result *before* you run it, and state the mode of what you get. A reported failure is more useful to me than a reported success.
>
> Thanks for reading this far.
