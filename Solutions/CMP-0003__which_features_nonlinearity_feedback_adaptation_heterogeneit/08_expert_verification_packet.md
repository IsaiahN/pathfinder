# Expert verification packet: the Tether on necessary features of complex behavior

**What this is:** a claim in a form a complex-systems researcher can check. Mode, derivation and refuters are stated before the conclusion, so a reader knows what to look for and what would settle it.

**Mode: `general` on the shape, `specified` on the structural classification. Not `grounded`.** No counterfactual removal experiment is claimed. This is a shape and a route, not an empirical result.

## 1. The claim, in one paragraph

The question "which features are necessary for complex behavior?" is a union presented as a list. Under the framework, the five candidates fall into four different categories: network structure is the only one that is a primitive member of the minimum set; nonlinearity, read as an amplifying remainder, is a derivative a composition leaves behind; feedback is the loop itself; and heterogeneity is a residual-relative relation, not a property of components. Adaptation is an operation of that loop rather than a standing feature. No empirical winner among the five is named, because the framework returns a shape, not a name, and no removal instrument settles necessity.

## 2. Three checkable sub-claims

### C1: framework-internal; checkable by reading Figures 9 and 13

- **Statement:** the five candidates are not one kind of thing, so no single feature can be a necessary condition in the list sense. Network is the only primitive member; nonlinearity as chaos is a remainder.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 9 :54 *"And when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again."* Fig 13 :3 *"No system functions without any of them, none is built from the others, and everything else is a composition across the two kinds."* Fig 13 :32 *"a remainder compounding through a chain that amplifies is chaos"* and Fig 13 :124-125 *"Smoothness, symmetry, chaos and discreteness are each what a particular composition leaves behind, and none is a seventh member."*
- **Refuter:** derive one of the six terms from the other five; derive a seventh that is not a composition; show the five candidates are a partition of independent conditions; or show by counterfactual removal that one feature is necessary and sufficient across domains.

### C2: domain-facing; checkable by a complex-systems researcher against the field's own literature

- **Statement:** the field has not established a universal necessary-feature list, and treats the candidates as a shared weak denominator or a set of generators whose combination varies by system type. Feedback and topology are repeatedly structural, while nonlinearity is explicitly contested as necessary.
- **What to check:** is that a fair reading of Mazzocchi 2025, Newman 2011 and Rickles, Hawe and Shiell 2007? Does the SERC teaching page's list count as evidence for necessity, or only as a pedagogical summary? Does the PLOS adaptation paper's proof of a necessary structural condition for adaptation generalise beyond that functionality?
- **Refuter:** a peer-reviewed result establishing a single feature as necessary and sufficient for complex behavior across domains, with a non-moving anchor, would break this sub-claim. Note the strain: a proof about biological perfect adaptation is not a proof about the figure's mint-and-carry.

### C3: framework-internal derivation of the dependency order

- **Statement:** the candidates sit in a dependency order, not an importance order: substrate, network, feedback, nonlinearity, adaptation, heterogeneity. Optimising a later link cannot rescue an earlier one.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 3 :20 *"The chain is ordered by dependency, not by importance. Each link consumes what the one above produces."* Fig 13 :59 *"no relation between systems, and no configurations to count"*. Fig 11 :49 *"An actor's valence is relative to the residual, not a property it carries."*
- **Refuter:** show that adaptation can run without a loop, or a loop without related entities; or show that heterogeneity is a fixed property of components independent of the question. Any of those reverses the order.

## 3. What is explicitly not claimed

- Not that feedback, or any other single feature, is the necessary feature of complex systems.
- Not that the figure's six terms are nature's own list. That would need a ground reading.
- Not that chaos as a compounded remainder is the same as dynamical nonlinearity. The strain is named in `01` §8.
- Not that control-theoretic or evolutionary adaptation is the framework's mint-and-carry.
- Not a derivation of any world theory from the figures. World sources are secondary corroboration only.
- Not `grounded`. Nothing here is asserted as settled by experiment.

## 5. What would settle it

The ground, not agreement. Concretely:

1. **Removal Test:** remove each feature in turn and observe whether complex behavior fails, across domains, with an anchor that does not move when frames disagree.
2. **Primitive Test:** derive one of the six from the other five, or derive a seventh that is not a composition.
3. **Union Test:** show that the candidates are independent conditions rather than overlapping ones.
4. **Import Test:** find a frame whose closure predicts this residual at lower overlap, with provenance and a shadow test.

## 6. What an expert can check in twenty minutes

1. Read Fig 9 :54-57, Fig 13 :3 and Fig 13 :32 (three short passages). They decide C1.
2. Check C2 against the field's own literature. Is a universal feature list actually established anywhere, or only a weak common denominator?
3. Name one instrument that could remove a feature and watch complex behavior fail across domains, or say why none is possible.
4. Send the refutation. A failure is more useful than an endorsement.
