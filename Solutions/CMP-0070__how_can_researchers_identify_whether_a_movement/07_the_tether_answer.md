# The Tether's answer: how to identify the producer of a movement's apparent turning point

**Mode: `specified` on the method shape, `general` on the structural limits, `gap` on the name of the instrument for a particular movement. Not `grounded`.** This is a simulated derivation checked for internal consistency against the figures, not a ground reading (`05_what_shape_means.md` section 6). No movement, dataset, or measurement was examined.

## 1. The residual it answers

An apparent turning point is an observation R(s,T) in slot s at time T. A change-point detector can locate T; it cannot locate the producer. Two producers are compatible with the same observation: the coordination ground crossed a threshold, or the visibility/reporting channel changed. A mixed case is possible and is a union compound, not a harder version of either (Fig 9 :57: "a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either").

## 2. The shape and the route

1. **Do not trust the break alone; the channel can decay while the ground does not.** Fig 10 :24: "the ground does not decay, the channel does". Fig 10 :25: "stale percepts, dropped transients, saturated metrics, pooled readings". A reporting series can tip because the metric saturated or the reading was pooled, with no ground change at all.

2. **Require a non-updating anchor.** Fig 2 :29: "An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks." Fig 2 :33: "The anchor must not update." An anchor produced by the reporting system is another frame, and by Fig 2 :34 "everything drifts together, and the drift is coherent, which is why nobody notices."

3. **Require a second frame with a different evidence pool.** Fig 2 :31: "Two people reading the same report are not two frames; two instruments pointed at the same thing are." Fig 2 :40: "disqualifying for a verdict: agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool." Ten news archives are one witness. The second frame must be an instrument on a different closure, whose evidence pool is declared.

4. **Keep the residual per slot.** Fig 1 :19: "The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated." Fig 1 :22: "Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average." A real threshold is expected to be local and ordered; a channel change is expected to be simultaneous across slots in the channel. This empirical expectation is a `specified` prediction to be checked at the ground, not a figure law.

5. **Enumerate the habitat outward.** Fig 11 :43: "The habitat is enumerated, never composed." Fig 11 :44: "List everything in contact with the residual, then what is in contact with those, and outward until the cascade stops mattering." Include platform rules, dataset versions, coding changes, and the feedback from coverage to participation, which can be several steps out (Fig 11 :52-53).

6. **Refit before minting.** A channel-only break belongs to Fig 5 :13 "mis-attached: refit what you have, do not invent". The owed move is to refit the observation model and refuse the "tip" reading, not to mint a new mobilisation mechanism. Fig 5 :65-66: "Write down what an answer would look like, bind it, and let it accrue a residual like anything else."

7. **Use an instrument already returning something.** Fig 6 :32: "An instrument is not built from a description. It is improved from a worse instrument already returning something." Fig 6 :34-35: "Where nothing is, there is no edge to extend from, and the search for a proto-instrument is a search for one that is already there."

8. **Stop at the shape and record the lineage.** Fig 9 :32: "you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself". The figures fix the properties of the discriminator and do not name the record for a given movement.

The resulting verdict procedure returns, per slot, one of {ground crossed, channel changed, both, undecidable at this instrument}, each with its anchor and its second-frame provenance. Verdicts are kept separate and never averaged.

## 3. What it explicitly does not say

- Not which producer operated in any actual movement. No measurement was taken.
- Not that a coordination threshold is unreal. The threshold-model literature treats it as a formal object; the figures only say a break cannot identify one by itself.
- Not that reporting is always the culprit. The channel may be sound and the ground may genuinely have crossed.
- Not that the figures name the anchor, the dataset, the lag, or the number of frames. Those are a `gap` and a budget choice respectively (Fig 9 :60: "Use filters for the budget and witnesses for the verdict. Never let a filter hand you a verdict.").
- Not a new theory of social movements. It is a discrimination method, `specified` and ungrounded.
- Not corroboration from a second seat that read the same files. Fig 2 :31 applies to audits as well as to fields.

## 4. What would settle it, and what would move a claim to grounded

A ground reading, not further agreement. Fig 9 :42: "narrowing gets you close, the ground decides". Concretely:

1. A per-slot record that does not update on reporting, already returning a reading, that shows a sustained change at the break while the instrument-change log is held fixed.
2. A second frame with declared low overlap that shows the same change against the same non-updating ground, reached by a different route (Fig 8 :51-52: "Convergent derivation and adopted import look identical in the contents. Only the record of where each came from separates them").
3. A documented instrument-change timestamp. If the break disappears when it is entered, the producer was the channel. This is the mis-attached case and the "tip" term is refused.
4. A re-run of the pre-registered refuter in `01_figure_grounded_audit.md` section 4. If any failure condition fires, the corresponding claim is withdrawn.

Until such readings exist, every verdict about a particular movement remains `candidate`.

## 5. The honest stop

**Shape reached, instrument name unreached.** Fig 8 :39: "So a search that finds nothing is never a proof of absence." Fig 13 :134: "Unreachable is a verdict here, not a silence,". The gap is of the shape-not-a-name kind: the method is derivable, the particular record is owed to the habitat and must be imported with provenance (Fig 6 :23: "No frame escapes its own closure. The bound moves only on contact with a frame whose closure differs."). The lineage of this stop is recorded in `01` and `11`, including the two live strains, so a later reader can locate the error rather than only feel it. What cannot be perceived yet is residual and, in the words of the Symbols table, "simply cannot be read."

## 6. One-paragraph version for outreach

> A turning point in a movement's visible activity is not, by itself, evidence that the movement tipped. The figures say that the ground does not decay while the channel does, so the same break can be produced by real coordination or by a change in what is being seen and recorded. To discriminate, a researcher needs three things: an anchor that does not update on the reporting it is used to judge, a second instrument whose evidence pool does not overlap the first, and a residual kept per slot rather than averaged into one movement-wide story. The break must then be checked against an instrument-change log; if it disappears when the log is entered, the correct move is to refit the observation model and refuse the tipping claim, not to invent a new mechanism. Coverage and participation can also feed each other, so the anchor must sit outside the loop. What the figures give is this shape and its refuter, not the name of the right record for any particular movement. That record has to be found in the case and declared as a choice. Until a ground reading exists, the verdict stays a candidate.
