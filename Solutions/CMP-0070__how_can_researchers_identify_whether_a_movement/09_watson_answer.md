## QUESTION

Decide, in effect terms, what the Tether says about a movement's apparent turning point. Option A: read the break as a real coordination threshold, a change in the ground. Option B: read it as a change in visibility and reporting, a change in the channel. What each leads to: A risks minting a mobilisation theory for a saturated or repooled metric and spends the independence of the people checking it; B risks refusing a real threshold and refitting the observation model when the ground actually moved. A third option is the union compound, in which both moved, and it has its own loop. The question asks for a discriminator, not a verdict about any actual movement, and no dataset was examined.

## DOMAIN CENSUS

R by arity, symmetry and scale, then one line per domain with actors, currency and anchor, partition or union, and order by dependency.

- **R (the residual).** An observed break R(s,T) in a movement's apparent activity in slot s at time T. Arity: at least two producing conditions (ground change, channel change) plus a mixed compound, so a **union**, not one dial with an optimum (Fig 9 :54-57). Symmetry: asymmetric under the available instruments, because the visible series is a product of ground and channel and exchanging them changes what is observable; the comparison of two readings is ordered, not a symmetric join. Scale: first order inside one series, second order at the union of two channels, and potentially third order when coverage and participation feed each other.
- **Domain 1, the coordination ground.** Actors: participants, local groups, organisations. Currency: actual coordinated acts, commitments, maintained roles. Anchor: a record of presence or commitment that does not move when media attention moves, for example rosters, meeting records, on-site counts, participant logs. Nameable, so admitted.
- **Domain 2, the visibility and reporting channel.** Actors: outlets, platform ranking systems, dataset builders, coders. Currency: mentions, coverage volume, coding categories, sampling rules. Anchor: a process log of instrument changes, an algorithm-change notice, a dataset version, a coder-retraining record. This anchors *when the instrument changed*, not what the movement did. Nameable, so admitted.
- **Domain 3, the inference and verification frame.** Actors: researchers, reviewers, outside frames. Currency: residuals and agreement. Anchor: the ground, never agreement (Fig 2 :45). In this simulated run all seats draw on one evidence pool, so its independence is zero.
- **Domain 4, the mixed compound.** Actors: both of the above with their lag or feedback. Currency: the residual that survives one lever. Anchor: a slot-level record of which condition still binds after the other is removed. Nameable in principle, so admitted as a union compound.
- **Domains refused.** Public sentiment inferred from the same posts, news-derived event counts, and expert consensus that the movement tipped. Each updates on the process under test and therefore has no nameable non-updating anchor.
- **Order by dependency (Fig 3).** Establish a non-reporting ground reading (perception) before vocabulary, objective, design or carry; describe the residual before choosing a frame (Fig 9 :27-28); refuse the held term that reads every break as a tip before minting a new mechanism (Fig 5 :53).

Strain test applied: Fig 10 (channel decay) and Fig 2 (anchor must not update) govern different objects, the behaviour of a channel and the required property of an anchor, so there is no contradiction after re-census.

## VERDICT (from the figures)

The figures govern this question and return a **method shape, not a finding about any movement**. A break in a reporting series cannot identify its producer. A discriminator must have a non-updating anchor, a second frame with a declared low-overlap evidence pool, a per-slot residual, an outward habitat enumeration, and an instrument already returning a reading. A channel-only break is a mis-attachment: refit the observation model and refuse the tipping term before minting any new mechanism. Coverage and participation can form a loop, so the anchor must sit outside it. The mixed case is a union compound with its own test. Which specific record serves as the anchor for a given movement is a declared `gap`, owed to the habitat and provenanced as a binding, not derivable from the six-term set. Everything here is `specified` or `general`, nothing is `grounded`, and verdicts were kept separate per domain and never averaged.

- **cost if wrong:** bounded and locatable. The packet states the refuter before the conclusion, so an error is findable rather than invisible. The real cost is empirical: treating a saturated or repooled metric as a threshold mints a false mobilisation theory and can redirect resources, while refusing a real threshold leaves a genuine coordination change unrecognised. Fig 2 :42 "Buy independence in proportion to the cost of being wrong." The avoidance is to buy the second frame before publishing the verdict.
- **undoable:** yes. The discriminator is a method, not a withdrawn primitive. A published "tip" reading can be refused when the instrument-change log arrives; by Fig 6 :42-43 "A refutation adds a term that refuses; it removes nothing, and the reach falls because something was added rather than because anything was deleted." No term in the figures has to be struck out.
- **reversed by:** a ground reading, not agreement. A per-slot non-updating record that changes at the break while the instrument-change log is held fixed would move the ground claim toward `grounded`; a documented instrument-change timestamp that makes the break vanish would confirm the channel reading and refuse the tipping term; a re-census admitting an anchor that updates on reporting would break Fig 2 :29 and reverse the independence requirement; a census showing thresholds are global and simultaneous would make the per-slot rule unnecessary for that domain; any of the six pre-registered conditions in `01` section 4 firing withdraws the corresponding finding. Until then the verdict remains `candidate`.
