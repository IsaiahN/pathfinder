## Wider literature that bears on the question

The sources fall into four low-overlap fields:

1. **Science and technology studies / history of science.** Wynne's Cumbrian sheep study is the historical case most directly on point: local knowledge that the experts did not hold exposed a failure in the experimental model, so engagement changed what was being modelled, not just whether farmers accepted it. Recorded via the opened Public awareness of science page, with Wynne 1996 as the underlying citation.
2. **Science communication and participation models.** The Bodmer report's deficit model, its later discrediting, and the dialogue, participation and civic science models map onto the acceptance-versus-questions distinction. The European Commission's Green Paper phrase "raise new questions" is the field's own wording for effect B.
3. **Participatory research and environmental governance.** Post-normal science's extended peer community and Norström et al. 2020 on "Principles for knowledge co-production in sustainability research" (recorded via the opened Post-normal science page) supply the institutional form in which publics share quality assurance rather than merely receive explanations.
4. **Experimental social science and complex systems.** Lorenz et al. 2011 is the strongest low-overlap corroborator for the collapse reading: making estimates visible narrowed diversity without improving accuracy, and boosted confidence. The Society of Agents essay also cites Fisher's fundamental theorem ("with no spread, it cannot adapt") and the Keller-Segel chemotactic collapse as mathematical analogues of a room whose members become too alike. These were read through the framework essay, are recorded with that overlap declared, and are not counted as independent witnesses to the historical claim.

**Overlap warning.** The Wikipedia pages and the framework essays partly share the same science-and-technology-studies evidence pool, and the essays share the framework author with the figures. Agreement among them is one pool talking, not several witnesses. The Lorenz experiment is the only source here from a genuinely different field that was opened in full. No world source conflicted with the figure verdict; the conflict rule was not triggered.

## Distribution: offer, do not push

- **Fig 10 :52** — *"Bring a characterised residual, never the source."* Lead with the two-condition residual and its refuters; offer links only after a verdict is recorded.
- **Fig 2 :35** — *"Alignment is triangulation, not negotiation. A mirror cannot correct you."* A recipient who has absorbed the whole framework before being asked for a verdict has joined the frame being checked.
- **Fig 9 :27-28** — *"describe it before you go looking, or any frame you pick will seem to fit."* Ask what they would expect to see if engagement had genuinely changed a research question before giving them the literature.
- **Fig 2 :40** — *"disqualifying for a verdict: agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool"*; later agreement is never pooled as corroboration.
- **Fig 4 :22** — *"Only methods cross up. Only head starts come down."* Stage 3 sends a method, which is permitted; it does not send an answer.

**Recommended order in a reply:** (1) the residual and the three refuters; (2) ask what they would count as a failure and record it; (3) only then the four links, with the note that the figures, not the summary, are the authority.
