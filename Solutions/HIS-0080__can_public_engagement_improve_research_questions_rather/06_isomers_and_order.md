# Structure and order: are acceptance and question-improvement the same thing?

**Mode: `specified` on the structural claims, `general` on the bond mapping, not `grounded`.** Simulated derivation checked against the figures, not a ground reading (see `05_what_shape_means.md` §7).

## RESIDUAL

The question treats "improve public acceptance" and "improve research questions" as two points on one scale. Two derivations are owed: (1) are they the same atoms under different bonds, or different substances; (2) what is the order of a successful engagement loop, and which bond carries each step.

## DOMAIN CENSUS (supporting)

Atoms held in the engagement habitat: a researcher, a research frame Γ, a public actor, a residual, a meeting, an observation, a record of origin, a ground. Bonds held: the seven, plus negation. No new primitive is available without import.

## DERIVATION

### Q1 — Are the two effects isomers or degrees of one thing?

- Fig 12 :5: **"A set of ingredients is not one. The bond is the rest of the information, and it is the half nothing was recording."**
- Fig 12 :46: **"A bond is the typed relation between two operands. There are seven, they are not interchangeable, and which one holds is not recoverable from the operands."**
- Fig 12 :117: **"isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."**

Write the same atom set under two bonds:

1. **Acceptance molecule:** `researcher + public` under `+` (conjunction, symmetric: both hold at once, a correlation of attitudes). The test in the Operators table :15 is *"swap the operands. Does the meaning change? No."* A satisfaction survey records this. It cannot record a route.
2. **Question-improvement molecule:** `described residual → differing closure → imported atom ⋛ gap it explains` under ordered bonds (`→` sequence, `⋛` comparison). The Operators table :33 test for `→` is *"swap them. Does it become a different thing? Yes."* The record of origin is part of the term.

Same ingredients, different bonds, different substances. Fig 12 :94: **"The arrangement is the substance."** So the question's either/or is not a choice between more or less of one quantity. It is a choice between two molecules.

**But the mode must be honest.** This is `single-source` on the figure side (Fig 12). The world participation typologies (Haklay 2013 in the opened Citizen science page: contributory data collection, co-developing research questions, defining research focus or leading research initiatives) corroborate the distinction, but they share the science-communication evidence pool and are not an independent witness. I do not upgrade it to triangulated.

**Falsifier, written before the run:** if acceptance and question-improvement move together under every intervention, with no case where one moves without the other, the isomer claim fails and the degree claim stands. The figures predict independent movement because the bonds differ; a single dissociation in either direction is enough to keep the isomer reading.

### Q2 — What is the order of a successful engagement loop?

- Fig 9 :27-28: **"describe it before you go looking, or any frame you pick will seem to fit"** / **"your words are borrowed too, so describe the effect rather than the cause"**. So step 1 is the residual as an effect description, not a list of public groups.
- Fig 11 :43-44: **"The habitat is enumerated, never composed."** / **"List everything in contact with the residual, then what is in contact with those, and outward until the cascade stops mattering."** So step 2 enumerates actors outward until the cascade stops.
- Formula step 2: **"REFUTED — a term held is false; what is owed is a refusal, not an explanation."** So step 3 refuses "acceptance improvement is question improvement." Fig 5 :53: **"refuted: a term we hold is wrong; a term is owed that says so."** The refusal is a negation, one of the eight operators but not a bond (Operators :70-74: *"Negation joins nothing, so its symmetry entry is empty rather than no"*). Nothing is deleted; the reach falls because a term was added (Fig 6 :41-43).
- Fig 8 :52: **"Convergent derivation and adopted import look identical in the contents. Only the record of where each came from separates them, and the surplus lives only where the closures differ."** So step 4 records provenance and checks that the input explains a pre-described gap.
- Formula step 5: **"the ground settles it. Verified only by what pays. A gate is not the ground."** So step 5 tests the changed question against a ground.

The order is: **describe, enumerate, refuse, import, settle.** The steps are ordered by dependency (Fig 3 :20: *"The chain is ordered by dependency, not by importance"*). Running the import before the description is apophenia; running settlement before the refusal is building to pass your own test.

**Falsifier:** a case where settlement pays before a residual is described, or where a contribution explains a gap that was never stated beforehand, and the result is still a figure-licensed change of question. Under Fig 9 :27-28 that is projection, so it would break the order claim.

### Q3 — What analogue carries "improve"?

Fig 12 :111-112:

> **"A vocabulary without bonds can name a state and cannot name a route to one."**
> **"A plan is a sequence. A goal is a comparison. Progress is a subtraction. A refutation is a negation, and that one is not a bond."**

"Improve" is not a state; it is a comparison plus a subtraction. Under the Operators table, `⋛` is **"a threshold relation between two quantities"** with the test *"is it about which is larger, rather than both being present? Yes."* `−` is **"A with B removed or absent,"** and progress is a subtraction. So a claim "engagement improved the research question" must name:

- the two quantities compared (before and after, on which slot);
- what was removed or absent (the term that is now refused);
- the ground against which the comparison pays.

An acceptance survey provides a rating, not this structure. Fig 12 :125: **"A composition costs in two currencies, and they do not add."** The currency of acceptance (attention, legitimacy) and the currency of a settled question (a term that pays at the ground) are different units, so a sum of the two is not a single improvement score. Fig 9 :60: **"Use filters for the budget and witnesses for the verdict. Never let a filter hand you a verdict."**

**Falsifier:** show that engagement outcome can be expressed as one additive utility in which acceptance points and settled-question points have the same unit. If that holds, the two-currency claim fails.

### Q4 — Functional group, not a standing category

Fig 12 :123: **"functional group. A substructure whose presence fixes the outcome. The group says what kind; the qualifier says which member."**

Across all the public actors and engagement formats, the substructure that fixes whether a question can change is `differing closure + described residual + provenance + ground`. The actor type is the qualifier. This is why `01` F6 refuses a standing list of helpful public actors while still allowing a general shape: the group is fixed, the members are per residual (Fig 11 :51: *"No standing list of good actors survives a change of residual."*).

**Falsifier:** a public actor type that fixes the outcome regardless of the residual and the described gap. That would make the qualifier do the group's work and break the functional-group reading.

## CONCLUSION

Acceptance and question-improvement share atoms but not bonds, so they are isomers in the figure's sense and different substances in behaviour. The order of a successful engagement loop is describe, enumerate, refuse, import, settle, and the order is dependency-ordered rather than preference-ordered. "Improve" is a comparison and a subtraction, not a state, so it needs a ground and a named before/after per slot. The general shape is a functional group (differing closure plus described residual plus provenance plus ground); the specific actor type is a qualifier that cannot be fixed in advance. No numerical effect size, participant list, or engagement format is returned. That is the declared gap, not silence (Fig 9 :32; Fig 13 :133).
