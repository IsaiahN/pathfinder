## QUESTION

Decide, in effect terms, what the Tether says about the claim that public engagement can improve research questions rather than only improve public acceptance of research. Option A: engagement improves questions only by building trust and legitimacy. Option B: engagement can change the questions themselves through contact with an outside frame. Option C: these are two conditions with different anchors and the either/or is a union compound. What each leads to: A collapses a convention into a verdict and cannot answer the question; B is true only under stated structural conditions; C unbundles the question and returns a conditional per-domain verdict with a declared gap on which specific input works.

## DOMAIN CENSUS

R by arity, symmetry and scale, then one line per domain with actors / currency / anchor, partition or union, and order by dependency.

- **R (the residual).** The two candidate effects of engagement. Arity: two conditions, so a union compound (Fig 9 :54-57), not one question with opposite answers. Symmetry: "improve" is ordered while acceptance is symmetric, so an acceptance correlation cannot choose the arrow. Scale: indexed per research slot (Fig 1 :19), never aggregated.
- **Domain 1, the research frame.** Actors: researchers, the library Γ, instruments, peer reviewers. Currency: residuals, settled terms, credibility. Anchor: the ground, an experiment, dataset, observation, or calculation that does not update on what the frame thinks. Question type: which compositions are reachable, and what is the question's direction.
- **Domain 2, the public as outside frames.** Actors: affected communities, local practitioners, amateur observers, patients, citizens, artists. Currency: lived experience, local observation, values, tacit knowledge, new questions. Anchor: the ground when the claim is about the world, human judgment when the question is constitutively about people. Question type: can a differing closure supply an atom D1 cannot derive.
- **Domain 3, the engagement interface.** Actors: venues, journals, funders, museums, platforms, facilitators. Currency: legitimacy, agreement, access. Anchor: the ground, never agreement. Question type: does the interface triangulate or negotiate.
- **Order by dependency.** Describe the residual before naming a frame (Fig 9 :27-28); enumerate the habitat (Fig 11 :43-44); refuse the false term before minting (formula step 2); import with provenance and a shadow test (Fig 8 :52); settle at the ground (formula step 5).

Strain test applied: the apparent conflict between a non-updating anchor (Fig 2) and public judgment as an anchor (Fig 11 domain reading) is resolved by Fig 2 :36-38, which makes anchor legitimacy a question-type condition. ## VERDICT (from the figures)

The either/or is a union, so there is no single averaged answer. Per domain: D1, a research frame cannot see its own question's direction, `specified` and triangulated. D2, an outside closure can supply a residual the research frame could not derive, `specified` and triangulated, but only with provenance, a described gap, and a ground. D3, acceptance alone cannot improve research questions; it is a convention, not a verdict, `specified` and triangulated. Cross-cutting, public judgment is the anchor only when the question is constitutively about people, and the design must triangulate rather than negotiate or it produces coherent drift and loses the surplus. Which specific public input changes which specific research question is a `gap`, reported as unreachable at this budget, not silence. The whole is `specified`/`general`, ungrounded, candidate, and a simulated derivation checked for internal consistency against the figures.

- **cost if wrong:** bounded and locatable. The packet states mode, three checkable sub-claims and their refuters before the conclusion, so an error is findable rather than invisible. The real cost is spending the independence of outside readers by sending a success story instead of a residual and a refuter, which Fig 2 :42 prices: "Buy independence in proportion to the cost of being wrong." That cost is avoidable by leading with the two conditions and the failure conditions. A second cost is mistaking a convention for a verdict and declaring a question improved because people feel consulted; that error is a category mistake and corrupts the record.
- **undoable:** yes. The refused term is removed from the reach by adding a refutation, not by deleting anything from the record (Fig 6 :41-43). No primitive has to be withdrawn. A sent claim can be superseded by a corrected packet with its refuters intact, and the per-domain verdicts can be revised independently as each ground is reached.
- **reversed by:** a ground reading, not agreement. A research term absent before engagement that is bound afterwards and later pays at an experiment, dataset, or patient outcome would move effect B toward grounded; a case where acceptance alone, with no new contact, settled such a term would break the refusal of effect A; an anchor that moves when researchers push on it would break the people-question condition; two frames made more alike by engagement producing a larger later-paying surplus than independent frames would break the independence claim; any of the eight pre-registered failure conditions in `01_figure_grounded_audit.md` §5 would withdraw its corresponding claim. Until one of these fires, the verdict stands as candidate.
