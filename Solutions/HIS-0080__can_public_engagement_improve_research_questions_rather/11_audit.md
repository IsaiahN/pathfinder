## Round trip gap R_T

The Symbols table defines R_T as `gap(x, (T_E ∘ T_A)(x))`, and Fig 4 :30 requires it to be measured rather than assumed. I ran the round trip on the condensation the folder actually uses.

1. **Sent up (x):** the verbatim figure set, chiefly Fig 1 :24-27 (frame-internal credibility), Fig 2 :36-41 (anchor legitimacy and correlation valence), Fig 6 :23 (contact with a differing closure), Fig 8 :52 (provenance), Fig 11 :40 (contact).
2. **Abstraction (T_A):** the shorthand carried in `07_the_tether_answer.md`, that engagement can bring outside knowledge into research but acceptance is not a verdict and public judgment is a conditional anchor.
3. **Concretisation (T_E):** the ordinary claim "public engagement improves research questions."

**R_T is nonzero.** Four things the shorthand holds do not survive the trip back down into the ordinary claim:

- the union structure and per-domain verdicts (the ordinary claim averages them into one yes or no);
- the convention/verdict distinction, so acceptance is silently promoted to a verdict;
- the anchor-type condition, so public judgment is treated as a universal ground rather than a people-question anchor;
- the provenance and shadow test on any import, so any public input is treated as a valid atom.

Conversely, one thing survives cleanly: a research frame cannot see its own direction, so contact with a differing closure is the only route to a genuinely new question. That is the residue worth sending. R_T was **not** measured numerically, because no ground reading exists; it is recorded qualitatively as the enumerated set of dropped constraints. Treating the shorthand as identical to the figure set would set R_T to zero and would be the dead-reckoning error Fig 4 :30 names. `09_watson_answer.md` states this honestly.
