# The Tether's answer: can public engagement improve research questions?

**Mode: `specified` on the per-domain claims, `general` on the structural shape, not `grounded`.** Simulated derivation checked for internal consistency against the figures, not a ground reading (`05_what_shape_means.md` §7). No study of public engagement was run.

## 1. The residual it answers

The question offers two effects of engagement under one verb, "improve." Arity: two conditions, so a **union compound**, not one question with opposite answers (Fig 9 :54-57). Symmetry: "improve" is ordered while acceptance is a symmetric correlation, so an acceptance score cannot choose the arrow. Scale: the residual is indexed per research slot, not pooled (Fig 1 :19: *"The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated."*).

## 2. The answer, per domain, never averaged

**D1, the research frame.** A research frame cannot see the direction of its own question from inside (Fig 5 :61-64). What it holds as a sought-for shape is a preference, not a term, and accrues no residual, so nothing inside can test it. This is a structural limit, not a lack of effort.

**D2, the public as outside frames.** Because the direction is invisible from inside, a frame whose closure differs can supply an atom the research frame could not derive: "No frame escapes its own closure. The bound moves only on contact with a frame whose closure differs" (Fig 6 :23), and "underivable is not unknowable" (Fig 8 :54). So **yes in principle**: public engagement can improve research questions by supplying a description of a residual the research frame did not hold. But the input must have provenance and must explain a gap described in advance (Fig 8 :52), and the kind of public is enumerated per residual rather than assigned a standing role (Fig 11 :49-51).

**D3, the engagement interface.** Acceptance is a different quantity. It is "credibility accrued," frame-internal (Fig 1 :24-27), efficient for a convention and disqualifying for a verdict (Fig 2 :40-41), and "An improvement that does not change contact changes nothing, however much it improves" (Fig 11 :40). So **no on effect A alone**: an engagement that only raises acceptance cannot improve research questions, and calling it a success is a category error, not a partial answer.

**The anchor condition, which cuts across all three.** "An anchor is legitimate when the question is constitutively about it" (Fig 2 :36). Where the research question is about people, public judgment is the anchor and engagement can be constitutive. Where the question is about the non-human world, public judgment is an instrument and "instruments can be wrong on axes they do not measure" (Fig 2 :38). The blanket yes and the blanket no both fail this condition.

**The design condition.** Engagement must triangulate, not negotiate: "Alignment is triangulation, not negotiation. A mirror cannot correct you" (Fig 2 :35). If the process makes researcher and public frames converge on an agreement neither ranges against a ground, it produces coherent drift (Fig 2 :33-34) and a mirror chain (Fig 7 :12-13, :35-36), and it loses the very surplus it sought, because "Two frames that overlap almost completely are one frame wearing two names" (Fig 8 :48-49).

**One-line verdict.** Yes, conditionally: engagement improves research questions when it is triangulation with a differing closure, a described residual, a provenanced import, and an anchor legitimate for the question; it does not do so merely by improving acceptance, and no figure line names which specific input will work.

## 3. What it explicitly does not say

- Not that all engagement improves research. Most engagement that only builds goodwill is a convention, which is legitimate but answers a different question.
- Not that public agreement settles a scientific fact. Public judgment is an anchor only where the question is constitutively about people.
- Not that consensus with the public is evidence. It is "efficient for a convention" (Fig 2 :41) and disqualifying for a verdict (Fig 2 :40).
- Not a list of participant types, formats, or an effect size. "you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself" (Fig 9 :32).
- Not a standing category of helpful publics. "No standing list of good actors survives a change of residual" (Fig 11 :51).
- Not `grounded`. No measurement was taken and no research question was actually changed in this derivation.
- Not that this document settles the historical question. It is a simulated derivation checked for internal consistency against the figures.

## 4. What would settle it — the ground

Settlement requires an instrument or a result, not a further argument.

- **A changed term that pays.** A research term or object slot that was absent before engagement, bound afterwards, and later settled against an experiment, dataset, or patient outcome. Formula step 5: "the ground settles it. Verified only by what pays. A gate is not the ground."
- **A before/after per slot.** The residual indexed per research question, with the input's origin and the gap it explained recorded. Aggregated satisfaction scores are filters for the budget, never witnesses for the verdict (Fig 9 :60).
- **An anchor test.** For a question about people, does public judgment stay unmoved when researchers push on it? If it moves, the anchor became another frame (Fig 2 :33-34).
- **An independence test.** Do the researcher and public frames still draw on separate evidence after engagement, or have they become one pool? Fig 8 :47-49 and the Lorenz 2011 experiment supply the criterion.
- **Standing falsifiers.** Any of the eight conditions in `01_figure_grounded_audit.md` §5 would fire and withdraw the corresponding claim.

## 6. One-paragraph version for outreach

> Public engagement can improve research questions, but not by making the public like research better. Acceptance is a legitimacy measure produced inside the research frame, useful as a convention and worthless as a verdict about what researchers ask; an improvement that does not change contact changes nothing. The real route is structural: a research frame cannot see the direction of its own question, so an outside frame whose closure differs can supply a residual the researchers could not derive from inside, provided that input has a recorded origin, explains a gap described in advance, and is checked against a ground. When the question is about people, public judgment is that ground; otherwise it is an instrument with limited axes. The design has to be triangulation, not negotiation, because frames that converge on agreement lose the independence that made their difference useful. That gives a checkable shape and a set of failure conditions written in advance; it does not give a list of techniques, a participant type, or a number, because the figures return a kind of thing and never the answer itself.
