## 1. A shape is a constraint, not a filler

The canonical rule: the framework returns *"what a missing thing would have to look like (a shape, not a name)."* Fig 9 :32: *"you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself."* Figures README: the framework *"stops at the limit of what the available instruments can distinguish, and records the lineage so the stop is honest."* Fig 13 :133: *"Unreachable is a verdict here, not a silence,"*.

From *The Library of Babel*: *"You can cut your search short if the set of books returned is small and descriptive enough that it provides a general shape of the answer that satisfies your question."*

For this question a **shape** is the structural form a successful answer must have, stated before anyone names a participant, a programme, or an effect size:

- The two effects have different anchors and cannot be averaged (constraint).
- A direction invisible from inside the research frame must be supplied through contact with a differing closure (constraint).
- Public judgment is an anchor only where the question is about people (constraint).
- Any imported input needs provenance and a shadow test (constraint).
- Which specific input helps which specific question is not nameable from the figures (gap).

A shape is not a list of engagement techniques. "Public engagement improves questions" is a claim about a bond, not a name of a method.

## 2. How a shape is obtained

- *Library of Babel*: use the vocabulary closest to the thing, look for recurring new terms that overlap with it, recurse. *"With each new term gained, you narrow the space and deepen your vocabulary."*
- Fig 6 :30: *"The description travels. Fails by being sharpened into terms only we can read. Cast it in effect terms."* Fig 9 :30-31: *"your words are borrowed too, so describe the effect rather than the cause."* This is why §0 of `01_figure_grounded_audit.md` writes the candidate answer shapes as observables, not mechanisms.
- *Decoding Tacit Knowledge* (opened): "When contact isn't possible, the best you can send is a plain description of what you'd expect to see, because anyone can check that against the world." This is the canonical outreach rule in `02_outreach_email.md`.
- *Library of Babel* on stopping: the answer is reached when *"the finish line will also be the starting place"* and the searcher has *"composed the answer through the process of locating it."*

## 3. What licenses a name

Only two things, both outside the shape itself:

1. **The ground settles it.** Formula step 5: *"the ground settles it. Verified only by what pays. A gate is not the ground."* For this question, that means a changed research term that later pays at an experiment, dataset, or patient outcome.
2. **A provenanced import from a frame whose closure differs** (Fig 6 :23; Fig 8 :50-52), with a source gate and a shadow test.

*They're Scanning Books*: *"The real import is not a new term in the existing space. It is a new dimension of the space."* Also: *"A minimum viable set of six terms is a human's careful guess at reality's compression axes; another sensorium's minimum set need not be those six."* And: *"Declaring the primitives does not make them neutral. It makes the choice visible."*

So a named public actor or a named engagement method is always a binding from a world frame, recorded as such. It is not a derivation from the figures.

## 4. The tacit-knowledge case is the mechanism, not a metaphor

*Decoding Tacit Knowledge* (opened, 2026-11-23) is the essay that bears most directly on this question. Its claims:

- "Writing carries the context well and the subtext partly, but it almost never carries the pretext, the 'you had to be there' part." So a public's local knowledge will not travel as a written report alone; the transfer requires contact.
- "The teacher's understanding grows too. The learner's attempt shows the teacher where their own explanation was too thin." This is the cleanest statement in the corpus of **effect B**: engagement changes the researcher's frame, not only the public's attitude. It is a figure-consistent reason the research questions themselves can change.
- "No frame escapes its own closure. The bound moves only on contact with a frame whose closure differs." and "underivable is not unknowable." These are Fig 6 :23 and Fig 8 :54 quoted in the essay, and they are the positive licence for public input as import.
- "An improvement that does not change contact changes nothing, however much it improves." (Fig 11 :40) This is the refusal of effect A as a way to improve questions.
- "High contact builds exactly the shared understanding that makes communication work, but that same factor makes the learner's agreement with the teacher worthless as a check." This is Fig 8 :47-49 applied to engagement design, and it is why the two-stage offer records a verdict before showing the theory.
- "Frames range against the ground. They do not negotiate with it." and "Alignment is triangulation, not negotiation. A mirror cannot correct you." (Fig 2 :45, :35)

## 5. Why "the public" is not an anchor by default

*The Hard Problem is About Source* (carried via exemplar) gives the instrument-discrimination picture: a meter reads green and *"cannot comment on the source"*; several paths can give the same reading; *"If several paths create the same behavior, then behavior is a projection with lossy information."* It was the spectrometer, not an argument, that resolved it. Applied here, a high acceptance score is a reading of a projection. It cannot comment on whether a research question changed, and treating it as such is the source-naming error the essay warns against.

Fig 2 :36-38 resolves when public judgment is more than a reading: *"Human judgment is the right anchor for is this a good answer for a person, because the question is about people. It is only an instrument for is this code correct, and instruments can be wrong on axes they do not measure."* This is a question-type condition, not a compliment to publics.

## 6. Correction to my own reading of "shape, not a name"

**I overstated the rule in an earlier pass.** I had written that naming any public actor or method is forbidden because the framework "returns a shape, not a name." That is wrong as stated. The canonical phrase is *"what a **missing** thing would have to look like (a shape, not a name)."* Its subject is a primitive not held in Γ. Public actors, meetings, datasets and publications are already in the research habitat; they are enumerated, not invented (Fig 11 :43-44). Naming them is a **binding** from the world frame, recorded with provenance. It is not the invention of a seventh term and it does not move the wall (Fig 6 :25: *"An import adds no atom to the world."*).

What remains forbidden is claiming the binding is a figure derivation, or that public agreement settles a non-human question. Both claims are refused in `01_figure_grounded_audit.md` F3 and F8. The earlier overstatement is kept here rather than silently erased, so a later reader can see the correction.

## 7. Two consequences for outreach

**Prestige is not action.** *The Recursive Self-Improvement Trap* (carried via exemplar): *"Prestige must never be allowed to validate an action directly... Prestige can fund an action. It can give an action a budget to be tried. But it cannot settle an action. Only the ground can settle it. The Ground Maintainer must be blind to prestige."* A campaign that gathers respected names to agree that engagement works is the named collapse, not corroboration. The legitimate ask is a budget to try the claim against a ground.

**This audit is a simulation, not a ground reading.** Same essay: *"LLMs can quickly simulate an approximate answer/output... However, the truly testable output would need to be derived from the real architecture or working the formula yourself."* LLM output is *"simulacra."* So `01` is a simulated derivation checked for internal consistency against the figures. It is **not** a study of public engagement and it must be labelled as such. It cannot settle the question it reports on.
