# Expert verification packet — the Tether on public engagement and research questions

**What this is:** a claim in a form a researcher or engagement practitioner can check. Mode, derivation and refuters are stated before the conclusion, so a reader knows what to look for and what would settle it.

**Mode: `specified` on the per-domain claims, `general` on the structural shape, not `grounded`.** No measurement of an engagement programme is claimed. This is a shape and a route, not a result.

**Status: simulated derivation checked for internal consistency against the figures, not a ground reading.** It cannot settle the question it reports on.

## 1. The claim, in one paragraph

Public engagement can improve research questions, but only under structural conditions, and not by improving acceptance. Acceptance is frame-internal credibility, efficient for a convention and disqualifying for a verdict; an improvement that does not change contact changes nothing. A research frame cannot see the direction of its own question, so an outside frame with a differing closure can supply a residual the research frame could not derive, provided the input has provenance, explains a gap described in advance, and is checked against a ground. Public judgment is that anchor only when the question is constitutively about people; otherwise it is an instrument with limited axes. The design must triangulate rather than negotiate, because frames that converge on agreement lose the independence that made their difference useful. The result is a checkable shape with failure conditions written in advance, not a list of techniques or an effect size.

## 2. Three checkable sub-claims

### C1 — Acceptance and question-improvement are different quantities (framework-internal, checkable by reading Figures 1, 2 and 11)

- **Statement:** a change in public acceptance is not a change in research questions. It is a different currency with a different anchor, and cannot be averaged with question-improvement.
- **Source, verbatim.** Fig 1 :24-27: "These do not count:" / "predicates minted, and credibility accrued" / "They are frame-internal. A frame cannot score itself with a quantity it also produces." Fig 2 :40 "disqualifying for a verdict: agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool" and Fig 2 :41 "efficient for a convention: agreement about a shared meaning is the product, not a corruption of it". Fig 11 :40 "An improvement that does not change contact changes nothing, however much it improves."
- **Refuter:** show a case where acceptance alone, with no new contact and no imported atom, settled a research term that later paid at a ground the research frame could not previously reach.

### C2 — An outside closure can supply a direction the research frame cannot see (framework-internal, checkable by reading Figures 5, 6 and 8)

- **Statement:** the direction of a question is invisible to its frame; an outside frame can supply an atom only through contact with a differing closure, with a recorded origin and a shadow test.
- **Source, verbatim.** Fig 5 :62 "A frame searching for an answer of a particular shape holds that shape as a PREFERENCE rather than as a term." Fig 6 :23 "No frame escapes its own closure. The bound moves only on contact with a frame whose closure differs." Fig 8 :54 "The atom cannot be derived from inside A. But the union is searchable from contact, so underivable is not unknowable." Fig 8 :52 "Convergent derivation and adopted import look identical in the contents. Only the record of where each came from separates them, and the surplus lives only where the closures differ."
- **What to check:** is there a described residual that the research frame could not derive, and does the public input explain that specific gap? If the "new" question was already in the frame, this is SEARCHED, not import.
- **Refuter:** show the new question was already derivable inside the research frame, or that no differing closure was actually in contact, or that the input had no provenance and explained no stated gap.

### C3 — Public judgment is an anchor only when the question is about people (framework-internal, checkable by reading Figure 2, with a world cross-check)

- **Statement:** on a question constitutively about people, public judgment is the anchor; on a non-human question it is an instrument and can be wrong on axes it does not measure.
- **Source, verbatim.** Fig 2 :36 "An anchor is legitimate when the question is constitutively about it." Fig 2 :37 "Human judgment is the right anchor for is this a good answer for a person, because the question is about people." Fig 2 :38 "It is only an instrument for is this code correct, and instruments can be wrong on axes they do not measure."
- **What to check:** is the research question about people, or about a non-human world? The answer is not a judgement of public competence; it is a question-type condition.
- **Refuter:** a question about people settled by an instrument that moved when researchers pushed on it, or a non-human question settled by public agreement with no instrument.

## 3. What is explicitly not claimed

- Not that all engagement improves research. Acceptance-only engagement is a convention, which is legitimate but answers a different question.
- Not that public agreement settles a scientific fact, or that consensus is evidence. Fig 2 :40 makes agreement about the world disqualifying as a verdict when frames share a pool.
- Not a list of participant types, engagement formats, or an effect size. Fig 9 :32 "you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself".
- Not a standing category of helpful publics. Fig 11 :51 "No standing list of good actors survives a change of residual."
- Not `grounded`, and not a study. This is a simulated derivation, internally consistent with the figures.
- Not neutral on the outcome. The author benefits from a positive answer; declared under Fig 10 :59-60, and handled with the refuters, not asserted away.

## 5. What would settle it

1. A research term that was absent before engagement, bound afterwards, and later settled against an experiment, dataset, or patient outcome, with the input's origin and target gap recorded.
2. A per-slot before/after comparison, not an aggregate satisfaction score (Fig 1 :19, :22).
3. An anchor test: for a question about people, does public judgment stay unmoved when researchers push on it (Fig 2 :33-34)?
4. An independence test: do the two frames still draw on separate evidence after engagement (Fig 8 :47-49)?
5. Any of the eight pre-registered failure conditions in `01_figure_grounded_audit.md` §5 firing, which withdraws the corresponding claim.

## 6. What an expert can check in twenty minutes

1. Read Fig 1 :24-27, Fig 2 :36-41, Fig 11 :40 and Fig 6 :23. Four short passages.
2. Decide whether C1 describes a real category distinction in your field, or whether acceptance and question-change are in fact one currency.
3. Name one case where an outside actor supplied a research question the researchers could not have derived from inside their frame, and state what ground later settled it. Or say why no such case exists.
4. For C3, name one research question that is genuinely about people and one that is not, and say what the anchor is in each.
5. Send the refutation. A failure is more useful than an endorsement.
