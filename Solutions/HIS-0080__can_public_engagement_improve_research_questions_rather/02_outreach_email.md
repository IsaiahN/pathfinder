# Outreach draft — check the output first, then decide about the framework

**Use:** one-to-one, or as a cover letter attached to a submission through the public venues in `03_public_contact_points.csv`. **Not** for bulk send.

**Hard precondition:** do not send until the refuter in `01_figure_grounded_audit.md` §5 has been read and the sender is willing to have it fired. The two-stage offer collapses on contact if the first stage cannot survive.

---

**Subject:** One checkable claim about public engagement and research questions

> Hi [name],
>
> I have one specific output I would like you to check against what you already know. I am not asking you to read the framework first, and I am not asking you to agree with me. If the output does not survive, there is nothing else to look at.
>
> **The question I am working on:** can public engagement improve research questions, rather than only improve public acceptance of research?
>
> **The output, stated before any theory (`specified`, not `grounded`):** these are two conditions with different anchors, not two answers to one question. Acceptance is a change in trust, legitimacy, or the sense of being consulted. Question-improvement is a change in what researchers actually ask, where a research term or object slot that was invisible from inside the research frame becomes visible through contact with someone living in a different situation.
>
> Three things follow, and each has a failure condition:
>
> 1. Acceptance alone does not change research questions. It is a frame-internal credibility measure, useful as a convention, disqualifying as a verdict, and an improvement that does not change contact changes nothing. **Refuter:** show a case where acceptance alone, with no new contact and no imported observation, settled a research term that later paid at a ground the researchers could not previously reach.
> 2. A research frame cannot see the direction of its own question from inside. An outside frame can supply an atom the research frame could not derive, but only through contact with a frame whose closure differs, with the origin of that input recorded and a stated gap it explains. **Refuter:** show the new question was already derivable inside the research frame, or that no differing closure was in contact.
> 3. Public judgment settles a question only when the question is constitutively about people. On a question about the non-human world it is an instrument, and instruments can be wrong on axes they do not measure. **Refuter:** a question about people settled by an instrument that moved when the researchers pushed on it, or a non-human question settled by public agreement with no instrument.
>
> I wrote these failure conditions down before sending this. If any one holds, I withdraw the corresponding claim. A one-line "this fails because..." is more useful to me than agreement. If you would rather not engage, no reply is needed.
>
> **What I am not claiming:** that public engagement always improves research; that any public input is automatically a valid import; that consensus with the public is evidence of a better question; that a hand-picked public panel is an independent check. I am also not claiming this derivation settles the question. It is a simulated derivation checked for internal consistency against a set of figures, not a measurement.
>
> **My stake:** I benefit from a positive answer to this question, so I declare it. The figures' own rule is that an interest in the outcome is unavoidable and an interest in the reading is disqualifying. The refuter above is how I keep the second out.
>
> **If it survives, and only then:** the public sources are below. The figures, not my summary of them, are the authority.
> - Public awareness of science, including the contextualist model and the Wynne sheep-farmer case: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Public_awareness_of_science
> - Post-normal science and the extended peer community: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Post-normal_science
> - Information deficit model and the "food for thought" finding: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Information_deficit_model
> - Citizen science typologies, including co-developing research questions: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Citizen_science
>
> **If you want to use the machinery:** the formula is public. Run it on a problem you know well and can check against a ground. Write down what would refute your result *before* you run it, and state the mode of what you get. A reported failure is more useful to me than a reported success.
>
> — [name]

## The two-stage offer, explicitly

**Stage 1 (this email).** Send the residual, the three conditions and their refuters. Ask what the recipient would count as a failure. Record their verdict **before** showing any framework material.

**Stage 2 (opt-in only).** If they are curious, offer the four public links. Their later agreement is never pooled as corroboration: "agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool" (Fig 2 :40).

**Stage 3 (method reuse, optional).** Offer the formula, with the instruction that each user writes their own refuter and states their own mode. Fig 4 :22 permits sending a generator; it does not permit replaying a recording of one success.
