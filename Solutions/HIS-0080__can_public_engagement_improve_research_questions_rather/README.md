## The residual, in effect terms

The question offers two effects of "public engagement" and asks which one is real:

- **Effect A, public acceptance.** Engagement changes how much the public trusts, accepts, or feels consulted about research. The currency is legitimacy, credibility, agreement.
- **Effect B, research questions.** Engagement changes what researchers ask, which affects the direction of the inquiry and the terms held in the research frame. The currency is a residual that was invisible from inside the frame and becomes visible through contact.

These are not opposite answers to one question. They are two conditions with different actors, different currencies, and different anchors, so the question is a **union compound** (Fig 9 :54-57), not a two-way split. The figures give a conditional verdict per condition, kept separate and never averaged:

- Effect A alone **cannot** improve research questions. Acceptance is frame-internal credibility (Fig 1 :24-27), it is efficient for a convention and disqualifying for a verdict (Fig 2 :40-41), and an improvement that does not change contact changes nothing (Fig 11 :40).
- Effect B **can** improve research questions, but only under stated structural conditions: the engagement must be triangulation rather than negotiation (Fig 2 :35), must reach a frame whose closure differs (Fig 6 :23), must carry an import with a source gate and a shadow test (Fig 8 :52), and must leave the sought-for shape refusable (Fig 5 :65-66). When the question is constitutively about people, public judgment is the anchor; otherwise it is an instrument and can be wrong on axes it does not measure (Fig 2 :36-38).

No figure line names which public input improves which research question. That is a declared `gap`, of the shape-not-a-name kind, not silence (Fig 13 :134-138).

## What is deliberately not here

- No personal emails of named individuals.
- No ranking of people by how likely they are to agree that engagement improves research questions.
- No bulk-send plan.

Three reasons, in the figures' own words:

1. **Agreement is not a verdict.** "disqualifying for a verdict: agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool" (Fig 2 :40). Selecting recipients for receptiveness manufactures exactly the correlation that makes their agreement worthless.
2. **A room does not upgrade the label.** "A room's agreement is variation, not a verdict. Selection between rooms is where the ground votes." (Fig 7 :35). Adding more people who already agree raises the vocabulary and does not add an anchor.
3. **Asking for trust before checking spends independence.** "A verifier that can reconstruct the claim cannot verify it." (Fig 10 :42). The first contact carries the residual and its refuter, never the framework's interiors.

Legally: scraping personal emails for unsolicited bulk outreach breaches GDPR and CAN-SPAM style rules in many jurisdictions. Public opt-in channels avoid that practical failure as well as the structural one. "Where a stake cannot be removed, declare it." (Fig 10 :60). This author has an interest in the outcome of this question, so it is declared here and handled with a pre-registered refuter.
