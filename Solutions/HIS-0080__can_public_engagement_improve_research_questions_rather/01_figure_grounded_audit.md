## 0. The question stated as an effect, and what an answer would look like

The ordinary sentence "public engagement can improve research" hides two different effects. Following formula step "Direction," the sought-for shape must be made an ordinary term before the run, so write down what an answer would look like and let it accrue a residual:

- **Candidate answer shape S1:** engagement changes only the public's attitude toward research. Observable: trust, acceptance, perceived legitimacy, sense of being consulted. The research questions after engagement are the same questions as before.
- **Candidate answer shape S2:** engagement changes the research questions themselves. Observable: at least one research term, object slot, comparison, or goal that was not in the research frame before engagement is now bound and predicting, and it accrues a residual against a ground that the public input helped make reachable.
- **Candidate answer shape S3:** engagement does both, or neither, depending on the condition. This is the union reading.

Direction rule (formula "Direction"): *"make the sought-for shape an ORDINARY TERM - write down what an answer would look like, bind it, and let it accrue a residual like anything else."* S1, S2 and S3 are now bound and can be refused. The preferred answer is not allowed to hide as an unbound preference (Fig 5 :61-64: *"A frame searching for an answer of a particular shape holds that shape as a PREFERENCE rather than as a term."*).

**What each option leads to.** If S1 alone is true, engagement is public relations, and the question "can it improve research questions" must be answered no. If S2 is true under conditions, the answer is a conditional yes with the conditions stated. If S3 is true, the either/or in the question is a false dichotomy and must be unbundled before it is searched (Fig 9 :54-57).

## 1. Residual R, described before any frame is chosen

Per Fig 9 :27-28: *"describe it before you go looking, or any frame you pick will seem to fit"* and Fig 9 :30-31: *"your words are borrowed too, so describe the effect rather than the cause."*

- **Arity.** The residual has at least two conditions: a legitimacy/acceptance condition and a question-direction condition. Fig 9 :54: *"And when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again."* This is a **union compound**, not one hard question with two opposite answers. Fig 9 :55-57: *"A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones. A single lever clears the region where only one condition holds and leaves the intersections standing, because the second condition still binds."* A single engagement intervention can clear acceptance without clearing direction; the intersection (both) needs both conditions.
- **Symmetry.** "Improve" is ordered; a correlation between engagement and acceptance is symmetric. An ordered claim cannot be settled by a symmetric statistic (Operators table :59-62: under a symmetric bond swapping operands does not change the term, under an ordered bond it does). So a before/after acceptance score does not by itself license the arrow from engagement to question improvement.
- **Scale.** The residual must stay indexed per research slot, not pooled into a single institutional "public engagement score." Fig 1 :19: *"The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated."* Fig 1 :22: *"Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average."*

**Strain test before census.** "Public engagement" could be read as a single actor, but Fig 11 :49 refuses that: *"An actor's valence is relative to the residual, not a property it carries."* That is a strain on the ordinary noun, so the census below enumerates public actors rather than treating "the public" as one frame. No figure line was forced to fit.

## 4. Findings F1 to F10

Each finding carries its mode, verbatim figure lines, and a refuter. Verdicts are per domain and are never averaged.

### F1 — Acceptance and question-improvement are different quantities with different anchors (decisive for effect A)

**Mode: `specified`, triangulated across Fig 1, Fig 2 and Fig 11.**

Fig 1 :24-27:

> **"These do not count:"**
> **"coverage, and compression achieved"**
> **"predicates minted, and credibility accrued"**
> **"They are frame-internal. A frame cannot score itself with a quantity it also produces."**

Public acceptance is "credibility accrued." It is produced by the outreach frame and cannot be used by that frame to score itself. Fig 2 :39-41:

> **"Correlation has valence."**
> **"disqualifying for a verdict: agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool"**
> **"efficient for a convention: agreement about a shared meaning is the product, not a corruption of it"**

So building acceptance is efficient for a convention (a shared meaning, a legitimacy settlement), which is a legitimate product, but it is disqualifying as a verdict about whether the research questions improved. Fig 11 :40: **"An improvement that does not change contact changes nothing, however much it improves."** An acceptance campaign that leaves the research frame's contact with the ground unchanged changes nothing about the questions.

**Domain verdict D3 (interface):** effect A alone is a `convention`, not a `grounding`. It can be worth having, but it cannot answer the question asked. It is not "partially right" about research questions; it is about a different object.

**Refuter:** show a case where public acceptance changes a research term or object slot that is then settled against a ground the research frame could not reach before. If that happens, the case was effect B wearing effect A's clothes; re-census it as contact-changing, not as evidence that acceptance itself grounds a question.

### F2 — The direction of a research question is invisible to the research frame, and an outside frame can supply it only through contact

**Mode: `specified`, triangulated across Fig 5, Fig 6 and Fig 8.**

Fig 5 :61-64:

> **"BUT THE TERM MOST WORTH REFUSING MAY NOT BE IN THE LIBRARY AT ALL."**
> **"A frame searching for an answer of a particular shape holds that shape as a PREFERENCE rather than as a term. It is not bound, it predicts nothing, and it accrues no residual, so nothing here can see it."**

Fig 6 :23: **"No frame escapes its own closure. The bound moves only on contact with a frame whose closure differs."** Fig 8 :54: **"The atom cannot be derived from inside A. But the union is searchable from contact, so underivable is not unknowable."**

This is the positive mechanism by which engagement can improve research questions. A research frame cannot see the direction of its own question. A public frame that lives in a different closure can register a residual the research frame does not hold (a pattern, a hazard, a question, a value at stake), and can hand it over as an import. Formula "Direction" gives the discipline that makes it checkable: **"make the sought-for shape an ORDINARY TERM - write down what an answer would look like, bind it, and let it accrue a residual like anything else."**

**Domain verdict D2 (outside frames):** effect B is reachable in principle. It is `underivable`, not `unknowable` (Fig 8 :54). The public is not a source of votes; it is a possible source of an atom the research frame lacks.

**Refuter:** show that the alleged new question was already derivable inside the research frame (in which case it was SEARCHED, not imported; Fig 6 :49-50: *"more search at the same reach: is it in here and unfound? The closure does not change."*), or that no differing closure was actually in contact. If either holds, the improvement did not come from engagement.

### F3 — Public judgment is an anchor only when the question is constitutively about people

**Mode: `specified`/`general`, triangulated across Fig 2 :29, :36-38 and the formula mode rule.**

Fig 2 :29: **"An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks. A measurement, an experiment, a counter, a market, a patient outcome. If it moves when you push on it, it is not an anchor."**

Fig 2 :36-38:

> **"An anchor is legitimate when the question is constitutively about it."**
> **"Human judgment is the right anchor for is this a good answer for a person, because the question is about people. It is only an instrument for is this code correct, and instruments can be wrong on axes they do not measure."**

This is the sharpest conditional in the whole answer. If the research question is about people (what matters to a community, what counts as a harm, which risk is acceptable, whether an answer is useful to those who live with it), then public judgment is not a PR input, it is the anchor: the question is about people, and a frame cannot move it. If the research question is about a non-human world (the mass of a particle, the structure of a protein), public judgment is an instrument and "instruments can be wrong on axes they do not measure." Engagement can still help such questions via F2 (a new observation or a new question from a differing closure), but public agreement does not settle them.

**Domain verdict D1/D2 split:** per domain, not averaged. Where the question is about people, engagement can be constitutive. Where it is not, engagement is auxiliary contact. A blanket "yes, engagement improves research" or blanket "no, it only improves acceptance" both fail this split.

**Refuter:** exhibit a question about people where public judgment was moved by the researchers' own framing and still settled the case (anchor updated on a frame, Fig 2 :33-34: *"the anchor becomes another frame, everything drifts together, and the drift is coherent, which is why nobody notices"*), or a non-human question where public agreement, not an instrument, settled a term. Either would break the conditional.

### F4 — Engagement must triangulate, not negotiate, or it produces coherent drift and mirror chains

**Mode: `general`, triangulated across Fig 2, Fig 7 and Fig 8.**

Fig 2 :35: **"Alignment is triangulation, not negotiation. A mirror cannot correct you."** Fig 2 :45-46:

> **"Frames range against the ground. They do not negotiate with it."**
> **"Each frame is corrected by it separately, and what makes the correction sound is that neither frame can move it."**

Fig 7 :12-13: **"same closure, handed along; agreement compounds, evidence does not"**. Fig 7 :35: **"A room's agreement is variation, not a verdict. Selection between rooms is where the ground votes."**

An engagement process whose success measure is "we reached consensus with the public" is panel 1 of Fig 2 (mutual update, coherent drift) presented as a success. The Decoding Tacit Knowledge essay names the failure in plain words: "Two people who only check against each other drift, and they drift together, so neither notices," and "Shared pretext is good for one thing and ruins another. Agreement is the whole point when you're settling what words mean. It's worthless for settling what's true when both sides learned from the same sources." (Isaiah N, 2026, opened 2026-11-23.)

**Domain verdict D3:** the design of the interface is a **convention**, not a verdict (Fig 10 :47: *"a convention that decides an outcome has stopped being one"*). Its job is to keep D1 and D2 ranging separately against the ground, not to manufacture agreement.

**Refuter:** a case where a genuine convergence of researcher and public judgments, with neither ranging against anything outside the pair, produced a new settled research term that later paid at the ground. Under the figures that would be a genuine surprise, and the triangulation claim would need re-census.

### F5 — Homogenising engagement destroys the surplus it seeks

**Mode: `specified`, triangulated across Fig 8 :47-49 and world corroboration (Lorenz et al. 2011; Decoding Tacit Knowledge).**

Fig 8 :47-49:

> **"Independence is one quantity read twice."**
> **"How much two frames draw on the same evidence decides both whether their agreement means anything and whether their union has any surplus. Two frames that overlap almost completely are one frame wearing two names."**

Fig 10 :80: **"Frames whose independence is renewed by separate experience can be maintained; frames whose correlation is fixed at origin can only be rotated, because no amount of use makes them less alike and replacement is the only refresh."**

The Decoding Tacit Knowledge essay states the design consequence directly: "Long, close contact pulls the frames together, so the process that makes communication easy slowly uses up that extra reach." So there is a trade-off, and it is one quantity read twice: the same closeness that makes engagement pleasant and easy reduces the independence that makes engagement informative. This is not a reason to avoid engagement; it is a reason to design for maintained difference, with a ground neither side controls and separate experience that renews independence.

World corroboration (low overlap): Lorenz, Rauhut, Schweitzer and Helbing 2011 (PNAS, opened 2026-11-23) found experimentally that "even mild social influence can undermine the wisdom of crowd effect in simple estimation tasks" and that "social influence diminishes diversity in groups without improving its accuracy," while "the confidence effect boosts individuals' confidence after convergence of their estimates despite lack of improved accuracy." That is Fig 2's collapse read in a laboratory, from outside the framework's evidence pool. It corroborates the mechanism, not the historical claim.

**Refuter:** show that two frames made more alike by prolonged engagement produced a larger, later-paying surplus than comparably funded frames kept separate. That would require re-census of the independence parameter.

### F6 — There is no standing list of helpful public actors; valence is per residual

**Mode: `general` with `specified` enumeration owed, triangulated across Fig 11 :43-51 and Fig 10 :72-79.**

Fig 11 :43-44:

> **"The habitat is enumerated, never composed."**
> **"List everything in contact with the residual, then what is in contact with those, and outward until the cascade stops mattering."**

Fig 11 :49-51:

> **"An actor's valence is relative to the residual, not a property it carries."**
> **"The same actor is a benefactor to one residual and a malfactor to another, so mutualism and parasitism are relations rather than kinds."**
> **"No standing list of good actors survives a change of residual."**

So "the public" cannot be assigned a fixed epistemic role. For a contamination question, the people downstream of the site are in contact with the residual and may hold observations the researchers lack. For a question about the meaning of a medical outcome, patients are the anchor by Fig 2 :37. For a question about a particle mass, the same people are instruments with limited axes. The set must be enumerated for each question, outward until the cascade stops mattering; Fig 11 :52-54 records the cost of stopping at first order (the DDT-to-eagles case).

Fig 10 :72-79 adds the selection rule: *"The room composition is a function of the question. When the question changes, the room should change."* and *"A candidate set chosen by a participant is an authored room however well the selection is reasoned, so the pool must be unauthored first and examined second."* A convened "public panel" hand-picked by the research team is an authored room even if the picking was conscientious.

**Domain verdict D2:** `specified` per enumerated actor; `gap` for a universal ranking of public actors. That gap is structural, not a failure of data collection.

**Refuter:** exhibit a standing list of public actor types whose valence survives every change of research residual. Fig 11 :51 predicts none exists, so a single counterexample with a maintained valence would break the claim.

### F7 — Engagement changes questions when it changes the room to match a surfaced residual

**Mode: `specified`, triangulated across Fig 7 :35-42 and Fig 10 :71-72.**

Fig 7 :36: **"A chain of mirrors is one long room. The next room must differ from the last, and match the residual it surfaced."** Fig 7 :40: **"The room is summoned by the residual rather than composed."** Fig 7 :42: **"The chain approaches the truth only as the world runs out of ways to refuse it. Which means the next room you book is the direction you travel, and booking a room like the last one is how a chain stops travelling at all."**

An engagement exercise that adds more of the same kind of participant to the same framing is "booking a room like the last one": motion, not progress. An engagement exercise that changes who is in the checking arrangement because a residual surfaced that the old room could not see is progress, provided the new room differs in closure and still ranges against a ground. Fig 10 :72 supplies the test: *"A room that stays fixed while the question moves is the failure; the correlation is only its symptom."*

**Refuter:** show a room that stayed fixed while the question moved and still produced a settled, later-paying new research term. Under the figures that is the named failure, so it would need a re-census of what "changed room" means.

### F8 — Import needs a source gate and a shadow test; a wrong atom costs more than starting from primitives

**Mode: `specified`, triangulated across Fig 6 :23-26 and Fig 8 :50-52.**

Fig 8 :50-52:

> **"Convergent derivation and adopted import look identical in the contents. Only the record of where each came from separates them, and the surplus lives only where the closures differ."**

Fig 6 :23-24:

> **"No frame escapes its own closure. The bound moves only on contact with a frame whose closure differs."**
> **"Import from a live peer is debited against that frame's independence. A wrong atom costs more than starting from primitives."**

A public contribution is not automatically a valid import. It carries an origin and a sequence (formula step 4: *"Γ ← Γ ∪ {φ}, stamped with origin and seq"*). If the contribution is a value or an observation that explains a gap the research frame could not close, it passes a shadow test and expands the reach. If it is a preference the researchers already held, or a claim whose source is the researchers' own prior communication, it is a wrong atom or no atom, and it is debited against independence. Fig 6 :25: *"An import adds no atom to the world."* The world did not gain a new primitive; only the research frame gained access.

**Refuter:** a public contribution that relocated the research wall but had no provenance and explained no pre-existing gap. That would mean the source gate is not load-bearing, against Fig 6 and Fig 8.

### F9 — Acceptance and question-improvement are isomers over the same engagement atoms, not degrees of one thing

**Mode: `specified`, single-source on the figure definition with a stated analogue, not upgraded to triangulated.**

Fig 12 :5: **"A set of ingredients is not one. The bond is the rest of the information, and it is the half nothing was recording."** Fig 12 :117:

> **"isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."**

Take the same atoms: a researcher, a community, a meeting, a dataset, a publication, a grant. Under a bond of **negotiation** (who agrees with whom, a symmetric correlation of attitudes) the molecule is acceptance. Under a bond of **import with provenance and shadow test** (an ordered relation in which a residual is described, then a differing closure is found) the molecule is a changed research question. Same ingredients, different bond, different substance. Fig 12 :94: **"The arrangement is the substance."**

Fig 12 :111-112 adds the chain boundary: *"A vocabulary without bonds can name a state and cannot name a route to one."* / *"A plan is a sequence. A goal is a comparison. Progress is a subtraction. A refutation is a negation, and that one is not a bond."* Counting attendance, satisfaction, and media mentions names states; it cannot name a route from engagement to a changed question. The ordered bond, and the refusal of the false term in F1, do that work.

This finding is `single-source` on the figure side (Fig 12 only). The world literature on co-production and the citizen-science participation typologies corroborate the distinction, but they share the SSK and science-communication evidence pool and do not count as independent witnesses. See `06_isomers_and_order.md`.

**Refuter:** show that acceptance and question-improvement are the same observable at different levels, so that raising one necessarily raises the other by the same amount. The figure definition predicts they are different substances and can move independently.

## 5. Pre-registered refuter (written before the conclusion)

Required by seat reasoning: a falsifier written before the run is a design instrument. These are written before the recommendation and before the WATSON synthesis. The answer shape is refuted if any of the following holds:

1. **Acceptance grounds a question.** A case in which public acceptance, with no change in contact and no imported atom, settles a research term that later pays at a ground the research frame could not previously reach. This would break F1 and Fig 11 :40.
2. **Direction is visible from inside.** A research frame that derives its own sought-for shape from its held terms with no outside contact. This would break F2 and Fig 5 :61-64.
3. **Anchor illegality.** A question about people settled by an instrument that moved when the researchers pushed on it, or a non-human question settled by public agreement with no instrument. This would break F3 and Fig 2 :36-38.
4. **Drift that grounds.** Two frames that only checked against each other, with no common non-updating ground, produce a new settled research term that later pays. This would break F4 and Fig 2 :35.
5. **Homogenised surplus.** Frames made more alike by engagement produce a larger later-paying surplus than comparable frames kept independent. This would break F5 and Fig 8 :47-49.
6. **Standing actor list.** A public actor type whose valence survives every change of research residual. This would break F6 and Fig 11 :51.
7. **Unprovenanced wall move.** A contribution that moves the research wall with no provenance and no shadow test. This would break F8 and Fig 6 :23-24.
8. **Bond collapse.** Acceptance and question-improvement shown to be one quantity at different levels. This would break F9 and Fig 12 :117.

If any fires, the corresponding claim is withdrawn and the census is redone. Until one is run, nothing here is labelled `grounded`.

## 6. Recommendation

1. **Stop treating the either/or as a partition.** It is a union of two conditions with different anchors (Fig 9 :54-57). Ask separately: did trust change, and did a research term change and pay at a ground?
2. **Keep the residual per slot.** Do not report a single engagement satisfaction score as evidence about questions (Fig 1 :19, :22).
3. **Design for triangulation.** Keep researchers and publics ranging separately against a ground neither can move; publish the residual before the engagement, and record which input explained which gap (Fig 2 :45; Fig 8 :52).
4. **Make the sought-for shape an ordinary term.** Write down what a changed research question would look like before engagement, bind it, and let it accrue a residual (formula "Direction"; Fig 5 :65-66).
5. **Enumerate the habitat, do not hand-pick the room.** List everyone in contact with the residual outward until the cascade stops mattering; prefer a lottery, everyone who qualifies, or a set assembled before the question existed (Fig 11 :43-44; Fig 10 :72-79).
6. **Refuse first.** The term "engagement that only improves acceptance also improves research" is refused, not re-explained (formula step 2; Operators :58: *"a refutation is a negation, which is why a vocabulary without it can only accumulate"*). Nothing is deleted; the reach falls because a refusal was added (Fig 6 :41-43).
7. **Send the generator, not the recording.** Fig 4 :25: *"A recording carried upward looks like knowledge and is a description of one occasion."* Offer the residual, the conditions and the refuter; offer links only after a verdict is recorded.
8. **Declare the stake.** This author benefits from a positive answer about engagement; that interest in the outcome is unavoidable, but an interest in the reading is disqualifying (Fig 10 :59-60).
