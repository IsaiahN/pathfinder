**Use:** one-to-one, or as a cover letter attached to a submission through the public venues in `03_public_contact_points.csv`. **Not** for bulk send.

**Hard precondition:** do not send until the bond assignment strain in `01_figure_grounded_audit.md` section 4 is labelled as `specified` in the text you send. The output must carry its own mode or the two-stage offer collapses on contact.

**Subject:** One claim about local norm learning that I would like you to try to break

> Hi [name],
>
> I am working on a figure-grounded derivation of how children learn local norms of sharing, reciprocity, fairness and obligation. I have one specific output I would like you to check against what you already know. I am not asking you to read the theory first, and I am not asking you to agree with it. If the output does not survive, there is nothing else to look at.
>
> **The output (`specified`, not `grounded`):** the capacity to acquire a norm is a property of the child and the child's contact field together, not of the child alone. What passes from the community to the child is a head start that makes the child's own search cheaper, not a recording of a rule that fixes behaviour. That is why the content comes out local: the same general learning capacity, landed in different conditions, grows different norms. The four named norms are not one thing. They are a union of differently bonded relations over a small shared set (a resource, a self, a partner, a threshold), and the ingroup case is a compound with its own loop. The specific psychological mechanism is left open. The framework returns a shape, not a name.
>
> **What would refute it, written before I sent this:**
> 1. a child with unchanged internal machinery but changed contact acquiring identical norm content, or norm acquisition shown to be independent of the habitat;
> 2. a norm transmitted as a verbatim specification that fully fixes behaviour, with no re-derivation and no sensitivity to local conditions;
> 2b. a round trip from local norm to compressed rule and back with zero loss, that is, no sensitivity to the conditions it landed in;
> 3. the four norms collapsing into one condition with one bond, such that one lever clears all cases including the ingroup convention;
> 4. the question turning out not to be constitutively about people, so that behaviour is not the right anchor.
>
> If any holds, I withdraw the corresponding claim. A one-line "this fails because..." is more useful to me than agreement. If you would rather not engage, no reply is needed.
>
> **What I am not claiming:** that the mechanism is natural pedagogy, shared intentionality, reinforcement or any named theory; that all four norms have the same bond; that children merely copy rules; or that this is grounded. No measurement here settles it.
>
> **If it survives, and only then:** the sources are below. The figures are the authority, not my summary of them.
> - Verbatim figure text with line numbers (local extract): `FRAMEWORK/reference/03-FIGURES_TEXT.md`
> - Canonical usage rule (local): `FRAMEWORK/reference/FIGURES_README_canonical.md`
> - Figures as metalaws (local): `FRAMEWORK/reference/60_FIGURES_AS_METALAWS.md`
>
> **If you want to use the machinery:** the formula is in the framework material. Run it on a problem you know well and can check against a ground. Write down what would refute your result *before* you run it, and state the mode of what you get. A reported failure is more useful to me than a reported success.

## Two-stage rule

Stage 1: send only the output, its mode and its refuter. Ask what the reader would count as a failure. Record that verdict before showing any theory. Stage 2, opt-in only: if they are curious, offer the three local source paths. Their later agreement is never pooled as corroboration. Stage 3, optional: offer the formula as a method to reuse, with each user writing their own refuter and mode. A Track B reply, if any, is a conversation, never evidence.
