## Wider literature that bears, and how

1. **Developmental psychology and early normativity (W2, W3, W4).** Children do not merely copy rules: by about three they protest a mistake, use generic normative language, and apply the rule selectively by group. This is the strongest corroboration of F2 (head start, not recording) and F3 (union with a compound). It also gives the instrument for the Fig 3 objective link.
2. **Behavioural economics and development (W1).** Inequality aversion emerges between 3 and 8 and is parochial. This corroborates F1 and F5: the signal lives in the ingroup/outgroup distinction, which a pooled average would erase.
3. **Cross-cultural psychology (W5).** Fairness and cooperation vary across populations, and undergraduates are outliers. This is the ground-level reason "local" is not decorative and supports enumerating the habitat rather than assuming a universal.
4. **Animal behaviour (W6).** Reciprocity in vampire bats depends on opportunity for reciprocation and kinship, and evolves more easily in kin groups. This is a low-overlap field: it corroborates the cycle bond and the contact dependence without sharing the developmental-psychology evidence pool.

Overlap warning: W1 and W5 share the behavioural-economics/cross-cultural pool; W2, W3 and W4 share the Tomasello laboratory lineage and partly the same paradigm. These are not fully independent witnesses. W6 is the only genuinely low-overlap world source. The verdict comes from the figures, not from a vote over these six.
