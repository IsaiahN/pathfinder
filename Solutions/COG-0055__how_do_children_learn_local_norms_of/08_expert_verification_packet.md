# Expert verification packet: the Tether on local norm learning in children

**What this is:** a claim in a form a researcher in social development or norm acquisition can check. Mode, derivation and refuters are stated before the conclusion, so a reader knows what to look for and what would settle it. Send this, not the whole folder.

**Mode: `specified` on the shape, `general` on the structural reading, `gap` on the mechanism. Not `grounded`.** This is a shape and a route, not a result.

**Status:** simulated derivation checked for internal consistency against the figures, not a ground reading. It cannot settle the question it reports on.

## 1. The claim, in one paragraph

The capacity to acquire a norm is a property of the child and the child's contact field together, not of the child alone. What passes from the community to the child is a head start that makes the child's own search cheaper, not a recording of a rule that fixes behaviour; the child re-derives the norm under local conditions, which is why the content is local. Sharing, reciprocity, fairness and obligation are a union of differently bonded relations over one enumerated atom set, with the ingroup convention as a compound that needs its own loop. The thing to instrument first is what the child counts as doing it right. The framework returns a shape, not a mechanism name, and the mechanism is left `gap`.

## 2. Three checkable sub-claims

### C1 Child-in-habitat; checkable by reading Figure 11
- **Statement:** norm-learning capability is not a property of the child in isolation. Changing contact changes the content even with machinery held constant.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 11 :39 "capability is a property of agent-and-habitat, never of the agent alone." Also Fig 11 :40 "An improvement that does not change contact changes nothing, however much it improves."
- **Refuter:** show a child with unchanged internal machinery but changed contact acquiring identical norm content, or show norm acquisition fully determined without reference to the habitat. If that holds, C1 is withdrawn.

### C2 Head start, not recording; checkable by a transfer design
- **Statement:** what crosses from community to child is a method that makes subsequent search cheaper and is re-derived under local conditions, not a verbatim specification that fully fixes behaviour.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 4 :22 "Only methods cross up. Only head starts come down." Also Fig 4 :11 "a head start, never a recording" and Fig 4 :34-35 "A seed carries no small tree: it carries what will grow one, and the conditions it lands in decide what it becomes."
- **What to check:** does the child's behaviour track the local conditions into which the practice landed, rather than only the literal form of what was transmitted? Would a transfer to a different contact field change the content? Does the child correct and teach the rule, as a re-derivation predicts? The developmental record (W2, W4) says yes at about three years, but that is a witness from the field, not from the figures.
- **Refuter:** a verbatim rule that produces identical behaviour across two different contact fields with no re-derivation and no sensitivity to conditions. Also the round trip: carry a norm up to a compressed rule and back down to behaviour; if the gap `R_T` is zero, C2 fails (Fig 4 :30 "So take the round trip and measure the gap.").

### C3 Union with a compound; checkable by the bond analysis
- **Statement:** the four norms are not one condition. Over a shared atom set they are differently bonded molecules; the ingroup convention is a compound with its own loop.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 9 :54 "And when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again." Also Fig 12 :117 "Same atoms, different bond, different substance." and Fig 12 :121 "cycle. The output re-enters as input."
- **What to check:** the bond assignment in `06_isomers_and_order.md` (sharing and fairness as ⋛ comparisons with different operand scope; reciprocity as a returning sequence, that is, the cycle structure; obligation as status designation plus sequence plus subtraction). Is that faithful to what these four norms do? In particular, does moral-norm enforcement fail to show the ingroup gate that conventional game norms show (W3, W4)?
- **Refuter:** show that the four collapse to one condition with one bond, so one lever clears all cases including the intersection; or show the ingroup gate vanishing for every norm at every age. One live strain is declared rather than hidden: whether self-involved and third-party comparisons count as different bonded terms under Fig 13's operand rule is unmeasured.

## 3. What is explicitly not claimed

- Not a psychology of norm learning, and no named mechanism (natural pedagogy, shared intentionality, reinforcement, theory of mind are candidate imports, not figure outputs). The mechanism is `gap`.
- Not that children copy rules verbatim, or that adults install norms directly.
- Not that sharing, reciprocity, fairness and obligation are one module or one score.
- Not that moral and conventional norms have the same scope.
- Not `grounded`. Nothing here is asserted as settled by experiment.
- Not a claim that human behaviour as anchor means any single informant is authoritative. A researcher or parent is a frame that can be wrong (Fig 2 :36-37 and the Fig 10 seat rules).

## 5. What would settle it

A discriminating design, not further agreement. Specifically: (1) cross-contact or cross-community comparison with internal machinery held constant; (2) a transfer test separating a verbatim rule from a re-derived method; (3) the round trip with a measured `R_T`; (4) moral versus conventional and ingroup versus outgroup conditions within the same child, with an instrument for what the child counts as doing it right.

## 6. What an expert can check in twenty minutes

1. Read Fig 11 :39-40, Fig 4 :11 and :22, Fig 9 :54, and Fig 12 :117 and :121. Six short passages.
2. Try to break C1 with a case where contact changes but content does not.
3. Try to break C2 with a case where a literal rule fixes behaviour across habitats.
4. Check the bond assignment in `06` against your own knowledge of the four norms, and say which bond you would change.
5. Send the refutation. A failure is more useful than an endorsement. If you would rather not engage, no reply is needed.
