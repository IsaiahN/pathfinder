## 0. The residual in effect terms

The question asks how a child acquires norms that are *local*: sharing, reciprocity, fairness and obligation in one community often differ from those in another, yet each child reliably ends up with the local form.

- **What is being decided:** the route by which a general learning capacity acquires locally specific normative content, and what that content is made of.
- **R:** the gap between a generic-learning prediction of what the child will do and the child's observed local behaviour. It is a live residual, not a global score: one person or one resource can carry the whole signal.
- **Options and what each leads to.** Option A, treat the four norms as one universal module installed in the child: this predicts identical content across habitats and is contradicted by the cross-cultural record (see WORLD). Option B, treat the capacity as child-in-habitat and the four norms as a union of differently bonded relations: this predicts local variation, early sensitivity to context and group, and a re-derivation by the child rather than a passive copy.
- **What an answer would look like (Formula "Direction"):** it would name (1) the unit of learning, (2) the currency crossing the community/child boundary, (3) the structure of the four norms, and (4) the ground that settles whether learning happened. Write that description down and let it accrue a residual, so the sought-for shape is refusable like any other term instead of being an invisible preference.

## 1. Domain census (done before choosing any figure line)

### R by arity, symmetry, scale

- **Arity:** four contents are bundled in one question (sharing, reciprocity, fairness, obligation). Four is a reason to test for a union, not a reason to average.
- **Symmetry:** the four are not interchangeable. Reciprocity is a cycle (output re-enters as input); fairness is a threshold comparison; obligation is a directed sequence or production; sharing is a conjunction or disjunction over a resource. Different bonds, same small atom set (Fig 12).
- **Scale:** two scales are present. The child is one agent running a step-by-step loop; the community is a population with a shared library and something that ranks what it produces. A membrane lies between them.

### One line per domain: actors / currency / anchor

A domain is admitted only if it has a nameable anchor. All four below do.

| domain | actors | currency | anchor (nameable, does not move with a frame's opinion) | partition or union |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| D1 Child as learner, one scale | child, caregivers, peers, contested resource | per-slot residual R, action, observation | the child's observed choices and corrections in interaction | loop at one scale (Fig 4 "one agent") |
| D2 Local community / practice, population scale | community members, conventions, sanctions | transmitted methods, shared library, ranked output | what members of that community actually do and enforce | population (Fig 4 "the population") |
| D3 Membrane / transmission channel | what crosses: method vs recording; head start | compressed, verified method sent down | whether the transferred item makes the child's next search cheaper and can be re-derived | filter with direction, not a wall (Fig 4 "the membrane") |
| D4 Verification frame (us and a possible outside checker) | primary seat, second seat, sources | agreement and residual | the ground, never agreement; in this run independence is zero because all seats share one evidence pool | checking arrangement, not a content domain |

### Partition or union

By Fig 9 :54, "And when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again." A bundled problem is "a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones" (same passage). Here the four norms overlap (all involve a resource and a partner) but are not one condition: a child can share without reciprocating, enforce a game convention without applying it to an outgroup, and treat harm differently from a rule violation (world sources W2, W3, W4). So this is a **union** with at least one compound (conventional obligation x ingroup). It is unbundled in `06_isomers_and_order.md`, each part keeping its own loop and verdict.

### Dependency order (Fig 3)

Fig 3 :20: "The chain is ordered by dependency, not by importance." Order: perception (the child tells who gave and what happened) -> vocabulary (terms for what actions afford, e.g. "that counts as daxing") -> objective (what would count as doing it right, the norm's goal) -> planning -> learn and carry. The likely break is the middle link. Fig 3 :24: "Why the middle link is where chains usually break." Fig 3 :27: "A link with no instrument is not a link that works. It is a link nobody has looked at." The checkable question is therefore: can the child say, in its own terms, what would count as getting the local norm right? World source W2 answers yes by about three years, with normative protest.

## 3. Findings

### F1 Capability lives in the child-in-habitat, not in the child (governing, triangulated)

Fig 11 :39: "capability is a property of agent-and-habitat, never of the agent alone."
Fig 11 :40: "An improvement that does not change contact changes nothing, however much it improves."

The question's word "local" is not an incidental setting. Under Fig 11 the capacity to acquire the norm is a property of the pair. A child with identical internal machinery placed in a different contact field acquires different content; that is the prediction, and it is corroborated by W1, W3 and W5 (the ingroup gate, the game-norm selectivity, the cross-cultural variation in fairness). Domain: D1+D2. Mode `specified`, not `grounded`: the claim is structural, the magnitude of the contact effect is for the ground. Triangulated across Fig 11 :39-40 (habitat) and Fig 4 :19-20 "Only the actors, the currency and the anchor change." (different populations, same loop), low overlap between the habitat and membrane passages.

**Refuter:** show a child with unchanged contact acquiring the same norm content across different communities, or show norm acquisition fully determined by internal machinery with contact held irrelevant. That breaks Fig 11 :39-40 and the finding is withdrawn.

### F2 What crosses is a head start, never a recording (governing, triangulated)

Fig 4 :22: "Only methods cross up. Only head starts come down."
Fig 4 :11: "a head start, never a recording"
Fig 4 :34-35: "A seed carries no small tree: it carries what will grow one, and the conditions it lands in decide what it becomes."

A local norm is not copied into the child as a verbatim rule that fully determines behaviour. What crosses is a compressed method that makes the child's own search cheaper; the local conditions decide what grows. This is why the learning is local even though the learning capacity is general: same seed, different conditions, different norm. It also predicts the child's re-derivation and correction behaviour (W2, W4: children protest, teach and apply the rule to new cases) rather than mere parroting. Domain: D3. Mode `specified`. Triangulated across Fig 4 and Fig 12 :104 "A settled molecule becomes an atom for whatever composes over it." (a settled practice can become an operand for the child), different figures, low overlap.

**Refuter:** demonstrate transmission as a recording that fully fixes behaviour with no re-derivation and no sensitivity to local conditions (for example, a verbatim rule that produces identical behaviour across two different contact fields). Also the round-trip test in section 5, item 2b.

### F3 The four norms are a union, not one thing (governing, triangulated)

Fig 9 :54: "And when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again."
Fig 9 :4-5: "are not one hard question but several well-formed ones. Split it rather than search."
Fig 12 :117: "Same atoms, different bond, different substance."

Four names in one question are not four instances of one mechanism. Sharing, reciprocity, fairness and obligation share a small atom set (a resource, a self, a partner, a threshold) but differ in bond; see `06_isomers_and_order.md`. The world record agrees: moral harm norms are enforced universally by three-year-olds while conventional game norms are enforced only on ingroup members (W3), and sharing is strongly ingroup-gated (W1). So the union includes a compound, conventional obligation x ingroup, with its own loop and its own ground. Domain: D1+D3. Mode `general` for the union claim, `specified` for which bond goes with which norm. Triangulated across Fig 9 and Fig 12.

**Refuter:** show that the four are one condition with one bond, such that a single lever clears all four cases including the intersections, or show the ingroup gate disappearing for every norm at every age. That breaks Fig 9 :54 and Fig 12 :117.

### F4 The ground for this question is human behaviour, and the mechanism name is a gap (triangulated + explicit gap)

Fig 2 :29: "An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks."
Fig 2 :36: "An anchor is legitimate when the question is constitutively about it."
Fig 2 :37: "Human judgment is the right anchor for is this a good answer for a person, because the question is about people."
Fig 9 :32: "you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself"

Because the question is constitutively about people, the legitimate anchor is what children actually do and judge, not a physical instrument. But an anchor being human is not the same as any particular human being an anchor: a researcher, a parent or a single informant is a frame and can be wrong on axes it does not measure (same Fig 2 passage). The specific psychological mechanism (natural pedagogy, shared intentionality, reinforcement, theory of mind) is **not** nameable from the figures. Fig 9 :32 returns a kind of thing, never the answer. So the mechanism is recorded as `gap`, and the ground, developmental experiments, is what would fill it. Saying `gap` here is a verdict, not a silence (WATSON 60; Fig 8 :39 "a search that finds nothing is never a proof of absence"). Domain: D2+D4. Mode `general`.

**Refuter:** show the question is not constitutively about people (then Fig 2 :37 stops governing), or produce a figure derivation that names the mechanism. Until one of those, the mechanism stays `gap`.

### F5 R is per slot, and averaging erases the local signal (single-source for the figure, corroborated)

Fig 1 :19: "The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated."
Fig 1 :22: "Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average."

The child's error signal is about this person, this resource, this game, not a global "how well am I doing at fairness" score. This is the key instrument-level consequence of locality: a pooled measure hides exactly the variation the question asks about. W1 and W3 both show the effect living in the ingroup/outgroup distinction, which a pooled average would wash out. Domain: D1. Marked single-source at the figure level (Fig 1 only) but corroborated by two independent world fields (developmental psychology and behavioural economics) that did not draw on the figure text; overlap between those two fields is moderate, so this is not two fully independent witnesses.

**Refuter:** a demonstration that the acquisition signal is global across partners and resources and that per-partner residuals carry no predictive mass. Fig 1 :19 would then be the wrong domain for this residual.

### F6 Learning a norm is closure expansion on contact with a differing frame, with gates (triangulated)

Fig 6 :23: "No frame escapes its own closure. The bound moves only on contact with a frame whose closure differs."

The child's closure expands on contact with peers and adults whose closure differs. That is an import, and imports carry a source gate (provenance) and a shadow test (explains a gap, Fig 8). In this setting the gate is checkable: where did this practice come from, and does it explain a residual the child already had? Without the gate, a "norm" may just be a habit picked up from a correlated source, and convergent derivation and adopted import look identical in the contents. Domain: D2+D3. Mode `specified`. Triangulated across Fig 6 and Fig 8's import passage.

**Refuter:** show a norm entering the child's repertoire with no contact with a frame whose closure differs, with provenance and shadow both absent and the behaviour still locally stable. That breaks Fig 6 :23.

### F7 The objective link is the one to instrument (single-source figure, corroborated)

Fig 3 :24: "Why the middle link is where chains usually break."
Fig 3 :27: "A link with no instrument is not a link that works. It is a link nobody has looked at."

Between perceiving what happens and planning what to do lies the capacity to say what would count as progress in the child's own terms, that is, what the norm *is* for this context. W2 supplies the instrument: three-year-olds protest a puppet's mistake using normative language, and do so context-relatively. So the middle link is present early and is measurable; the chain does not break there in the studied ages. Domain: D1. Mode `specified`.

**Refuter:** a replicated result showing children at the relevant age cannot distinguish a mistake from the same act outside the game context (no normative protest above control). That would put the break at the objective link and change the answer's route.

### F8 Our two seats are one evidence pool (governing the audit itself, single-source)

Fig 2 :40: "agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool"
Fig 10 :42: "A verifier that can reconstruct the claim cannot verify it."
Fig 7 :35: "A room's agreement is variation, not a verdict."

The independent audit in `11_audit.md` reads the same figure text, the same exemplar, the same world sources. Its agreement is internal consistency, not corroboration. A real outside checker must see the residual and the refuter first, not the interiors (Fig 10 :52 "Bring a characterised residual, never the source."). Domain: D4. Mode `general`. Single-source within the framework, and declared as such.

**Refuter:** an outside frame in a different closure, given only the residual and refuter, returning a failure we did not anticipate. That is the outcome we want, and it is not a refutation of this paragraph.

## 4. Strain test and re-census

Two apparent strains were tested; neither survived re-census.

1. **Fig 11 (capability is agent-and-habitat) versus Fig 4 (only head starts cross down).** They look like competing accounts of the same transfer. Re-census: Fig 11 governs *where capability lives* (contact), Fig 4 governs *what may cross a scale boundary* (method versus recording). Different quantities, so no strain. Both are kept.
2. **Fig 2 :37 (human judgment is the right anchor) versus Fig 10 (a ground with a reputation to protect is disqualified).** Re-census: Fig 2 :37 makes human judgment the anchor for a question constitutively about people; Fig 10 disqualifies a particular seat whose reading serves its holder. Aggregate settled practice can be an anchor while each informant remains a frame that can be wrong. Different objects, no strain.

One genuine strain is recorded and not hidden: the bond assignment in `06` (which of →, ⋛, cycle, ∥ fits which norm) is `specified` and unmeasured. The figures license the bond inventory but do not select the bond for a given norm; that selection is a binding from the developmental frame, provenance recorded as such. After re-census: one named strain, none smoothed.

## 5. Pre-registered refuter (written before the recommendation and conclusion)

Per the exemplar's Seat Map rule, "a falsifier written before the run is a design instrument, not only an honesty device." The shape claim is refuted if any of the following holds:

1. **Contact-independence:** a child with unchanged internal machinery but changed contact acquires identical norm content, or norm acquisition is fully determined without reference to the habitat. Breaks Fig 11 :39-40 (F1).
2. **Recording transmission:** a norm crosses from community to child as a verbatim specification that fully fixes behaviour, with no re-derivation and no sensitivity to local conditions. Breaks Fig 4 :11, :22, :34-35 (F2).
   2b. **Round trip:** carry a local norm up to a compressed rule and back down to behaviour; if the gap `R_T` is zero (nothing lost, no sensitivity to conditions), the head-start claim fails. Fig 4 :30 "So take the round trip and measure the gap."
3. **One condition:** the four norms are shown to be one condition with one bond, so a single lever clears all cases including the ingroup/conventional intersection. Breaks Fig 9 :54 and Fig 12 :117 (F3).
4. **Wrong anchor:** the question is shown not to be constitutively about people, so human behaviour is not the legitimate anchor. Breaks Fig 2 :36-37 (F4).
5. **A figure-named mechanism:** a genuine derivation names the psychological mechanism from the figure set alone. That would close F4's `gap` in the framework's own terms.

If any fires, the corresponding finding is withdrawn. Until one is run, no part of this is `grounded`.

## 6. Recommendation

1. Lead with F1 and F2: locality is structural (child-in-habitat), and the transmitted item is a method, not a copy. These are the two most defensible figure-derived claims.
2. Unbundle the four norms before searching (F3). Ask about each bond separately, and keep the ingroup/conventional compound as its own loop.
3. Instrument the objective link (F7). Ask what the child counts as "doing it right," not only how often it shares.
4. Report the mechanism as `gap` (F4). Do not smuggle in a favourite mechanism under the vocabulary of the framework.
5. Keep verdicts per domain. Do not average the four norms into a single "norm acquisition" score; Fig 1 :22 says that is how the live signal disappears.
6. Outreach: send the residual and the refuter first (Fig 10 :52), use the public venues in `03_public_contact_points.csv`, and never pool later agreement as corroboration (Fig 2 :40).
