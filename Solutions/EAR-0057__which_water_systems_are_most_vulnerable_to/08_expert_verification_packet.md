# Expert verification packet - compound water vulnerability (EAR-0057)

**What this is:** a claim in a form a water researcher can check. Mode, derivation and refuters are stated before the conclusion, so a reader knows what to look for and what would settle it.

**Mode: `specified` on the shape and per-domain verdicts, `general` on the structural split, `gap` on a named ranking, not `grounded`.** No basin was measured. This is a shape and a route, not a result. Simulated derivation checked for internal consistency against the figures, not a ground reading.

## 1. The claim, in one paragraph

The four pressures in the question form a union compound, not an additive stress score. The vulnerable water system is the one where all four bind the same slot, with no pathway that exports or flushes the pressure, a storage gradient spent or effectively non-renewable on the management timescale, a flow network coupled tightly enough that a local failure travels outward, a return-flow cycle that accumulates rather than clears, and a quality state the supply process does not restore. Quantity and quality are two currencies and do not trade in one number. The residual is indexed per system, never averaged. The framework returns this shape and refuses to name which basins occupy it; that list is owed from measurements.

## 2. Three checkable sub-claims

### C1 - framing; checkable by reading Figures 1 and 9

- **Statement:** the four conditions are a union with an unassignable intersection; the ranking object is the per-system residual, not a composite scalar.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 9 :55 - *"A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones."* Fig 9 :56 - *"A single lever clears the region where only one condition holds and leaves the intersections standing, because the second condition still binds."* Fig 1 :19 - *"The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated."*
- **What to check:** is there a water system in which removing one pressure removes the whole compound residual, while in another system the same removal changes nothing because the other three still bind? That is the union signature. A composite index that ranks both systems identically fails the check.
- **Refuter:** exhibit a scalar index that preserves the per-system signal under a change in one member while all others are held fixed; or show the four conditions partition the set with assignable non-overlapping shares.

### C2 - substrate and structure; checkable by a hydrologist against the six-term instantiation

- **Statement:** the vulnerable shape is a spent gradient, a coupled network, an accumulating return-flow cycle and two incommensurable currencies. This is a binding onto held terms, not a new primitive.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 13 :74 - *"Everything else is a composition across the two kinds, never within one."* Fig 13 :22 - *"a difference that can be spent"* (gradient) and Fig 13 :24 - *"that entities relate, and influence travels"* (network). Fig 12 :121 - *"cycle. The output re-enters as input. The sign of the loop decides whether it closes, and a chain cannot close by construction."* Fig 12 :125 - *"A composition costs in two currencies, and they do not add."*
- **What to check:** does a closed or terminal basin with no outlet actually concentrate return-flow salts, and does a coupled aquifer-river network carry a local pumping failure to a third-order effect? Does an extractive fix that improves quantity worsen quality? If the two currencies trade at a measured exchange rate, the structure claim narrows.
- **Refuter:** show that quantity and quality losses trade in one unit with a stable measured rate; show that return-flow salinity and renewability are fully determined by instantaneous state with no history term; or derive a seventh primitive needed for hydrology. **Live type question S5:** whether basin-scale head is the spendable gradient in Fig 13 :22's sense needs an instrument, and this packet says so before the check rather than after.

### C3 - measurement and the honest stop; checkable by anyone who reads Figures 2, 7, 8 and 9

- **Statement:** independent instruments must settle the ranking; agreement among pressure communities that share a dataset is one witness; and the named ranking is a gap, not a silence.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 2 :29 - *"An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks."* Fig 2 :31 - *"Independence, between two frames, is how little they draw on the same evidence."* Fig 7 :13 - *"agreement compounds, evidence does not"*; Fig 9 :32 - *"you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself"*; Fig 8 :39 - *"So a search that finds nothing is never a proof of absence."*
- **What to check:** are the data behind a proposed ranking independent at the instrument level (gravity, gauges, quality sensors), or are they four readings of one assessment? A fifth review citing the same report adds no evidence.
- **Refuter:** show that the four communities' errors are independent and cancel in the ranking, or exhibit a figure line that returns a named ranking from the substrate alone.

## 3. What is explicitly not claimed

- Not a leaderboard. No river basin, aquifer or country is named as the figure-derived winner.
- Not an additive or composite water-stress score; aggregation destroys the per-system signal.
- Not that any one pressure is always harmful. Valence is residual-relative (Fig 11 :49).
- Not that a declared water-quality or allocation standard is an anchor. A convention that decides an outcome has stopped being one (Fig 10 :47).
- Not a provenanced import presented as a derivation. The water literature quoted in `09` is corroboration only and its overlap is declared.
- Not `grounded`. Nothing here was settled by measurement.

## 5. What would settle it

1. A union test: does the compound residual vanish when any one of the four conditions is removed in a measured system?
2. An export test: is there a flushing or export pathway restoring quantity and quality on the management timescale?
3. An instrument test: do gravimetric storage, in-situ quality and gauges produce a low-overlap, convergent per-system ranking?
4. The S5 test: is basin-scale head spendable at the relevant timescale?
5. A shape test: do closed/terminal and slow-recharge systems actually occupy the predicted configuration more often than open, fast-flushing systems?

## 6. What an expert can check in twenty minutes

1. Read Fig 9 :55-57 and Fig 1 :19. Three short passages. Decide whether the union framing or an additive score better describes the four pressures in a basin you know.
2. Read Fig 13 :22, :24 and Fig 12 :121, :125. Check C2 against one system you know: is there an accumulating cycle, a coupled network, and a quantity/quality split that will not sum?
3. Name one instrument that would distinguish a compound residual from a single-pressure residual at that system, or say why none exists.
4. Send the refutation. A failure is more useful than an endorsement.
