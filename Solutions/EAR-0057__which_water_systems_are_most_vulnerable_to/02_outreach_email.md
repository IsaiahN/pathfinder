# Outreach draft - check the shape first, then decide about the framework

**Use:** one-to-one, or as a cover letter attached to a submission through the public venues in `03_public_contact_points.csv`. **Not** for bulk send.

**Hard precondition:** do not send until the type strain S5 in `01_figure_grounded_audit.md` section 8 is either resolved or stated openly in the cover note. The shape must survive contact with a water researcher before the framework is offered.

**Subject:** Check one claim about compound water vulnerability before you decide whether the framework is worth reading

> Hi [name],
>
> I have one specific output about compound water vulnerability that I would like you to check against what you already know. I am not asking you to read the theory first, and I am not asking you to agree with it. If the output does not survive, there is nothing else to look at.
>
> **The question in effect terms:** which water systems are most vulnerable to warming, extraction, pollution, and population growth acting at the same time? The four are not four separate studies to average. They are conditions that can land on the same system. A lever that fixes one of them clears the region where only that one holds and leaves the intersection standing, because the other conditions still bind.
>
> **The output (`specified`, not `grounded`):** the vulnerable system is not whichever basin scores highest on a composite stress index. It is the system with a particular configuration, namely all four conditions binding the same slot, no pathway that exports or flushes the pressure, a storage gradient that is spent or effectively non-renewable on the management timescale, a flow network tightly coupled so a local failure travels outward, and a quality state that the supply process does not restore. Quantity and quality are two currencies here and do not trade against each other in one number. The ranking has to be reported per system, not as a global average.
>
> This is a **shape**, not a list of named basins. The framework returns a kind of thing, never the answer itself. Which specific systems occupy that shape is a measurement question, and I am not asserting a leaderboard.
>
> **What I am not claiming:** that I have measured any basin, that a named river or aquifer is officially the most vulnerable, that the six-term substrate of the framework is nature's own list, or that a convention such as a water-quality standard settles anything by itself. No measurement was taken for this derivation. It is a structure with a falsifier attached.
>
> **What would refute the shape claim:**
> 1. A global or composite scalar that ranks the vulnerable systems correctly under a change in one member while all others are held fixed.
> 2. A demonstration that the four conditions partition the set of systems with assignable, non-overlapping shares, so that summing them is legitimate.
> 3. A measured system carrying all four conditions that shows no compound residual because a flushing or export pathway restores quantity and quality on the relevant timescale.
> 4. Independent instruments, for example gravimetric storage and in-situ quality, converging on a ranking that contradicts the shape.
>
> I wrote these down before running the derivation. If any one holds, I withdraw the claim or the affected part of it. A one-line *this fails because...* is more useful to me than agreement. If you would rather not engage, no reply is needed.
>
> **If it survives, and only then:** the sources are below. The figures are the authority, not my summary of them.
> - Figures text extract with line numbers (local framework file): `FRAMEWORK/reference/03-FIGURES_TEXT.md`
> - Canonical usage rule (local framework file): `FRAMEWORK/reference/FIGURES_README_canonical.md`
> - The eight-step loop (local framework file): `FRAMEWORK/figures/THE_FORMULA.md`
>
> **If you want to use the machinery:** the figures and formula are in those files. Run them on a water problem you know well and can check against a ground. Write down what would refute your result *before* you run it, because a frame cannot check the direction of its own question, and state the mode of what you get. A reported failure is more useful to me than a reported success.
>
> - [name]

## The two-stage offer, in short

1. **Stage 1:** send the shape claim, its mode, and the four refuters. Ask what the recipient would count as a failure. Record their verdict before showing any theory.
2. **Stage 2, opt-in only:** if they are curious, offer the figure and formula links. Their later agreement is never pooled as corroboration, because after reading the framework they hold its interiors (Fig 2 :40).
3. **Stage 3:** offer the loop as a method to reuse, with the instruction that each user writes their own refuter and states their own mode. Methods are permitted to cross (Fig 4 :22).

No stage asks for trust. No stage asks for a show of hands.
