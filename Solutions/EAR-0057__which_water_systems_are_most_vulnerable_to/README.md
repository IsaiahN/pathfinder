# Solution EAR-0057 - figure-grounded package

**Question:** Which water systems are most vulnerable to simultaneous pressure from warming, extraction, pollution, and population growth?

**Source:** `Questions/earth systems.txt` line 76.

**Section:** 6. Soils, water, and land systems.

**Mode:** `specified` on the per-domain verdicts and the shape, `general` on the structural split, `gap` on the literal ranking of named basins. Nothing here is `grounded`: this is a simulated derivation checked for internal consistency against the figures, not a ground reading, so it cannot settle the question it reports on. Per `THE_FORMULA.md`: *"Mode. general · specified · grounded. State which. Accepted means the ground settled it; otherwise candidate."*

**Status:** done.

**Registry status:** pending.

**Boundary.** Work was confined to this folder. Every figure quote is taken verbatim from `FRAMEWORK/reference/03-FIGURES_TEXT.md` (cited as `Fig N :line`), never from a summary. World sources are real pages opened on 2026-11-22 and recorded in `10_source_ledger.csv`. The field supplied for public contact points is water resources research; `03_public_contact_points.csv` contains only public, opt-in institutional venues in that field, with no personal emails and no bulk-send plan.

**Residual in effect terms.** The question asks for a ranking. In effect it asks which system slot carries the largest positive gap between what the system can supply and absorb and what four concurrent conditions demand, where the four conditions are warming (erodes the buffering storage and the reliability of supply), extraction (removes stored water faster than recharge), pollution (degrades the usable quality state), and population growth (raises demand and the pollution load on the same slot). The residual is indexed per water system, never averaged into one global water-stress scalar (Fig 1 :19, Fig 1 :22). The four conditions form a union compound, not a partition with assignable shares (Fig 9 :55, Fig 9 :57), so the answer is the intersection where all four bind at once, with its own loop and its own ground.

## What is deliberately not here

- No personal emails of named researchers.
- No ranking of people by how likely they are to agree with the shape claim.
- No bulk-send plan.
- No claimed ranking of named river basins. The framework returns a shape, not a name (Fig 9 :32). Naming a specific basin is a ground reading or a provenanced import from hydrology, not a figure derivation.

Three figure-grounded reasons for the outreach refusals:

1. *"Alignment is triangulation, not negotiation. A mirror cannot correct you."* (Fig 2 :35). Agreement selected for receptiveness is a mirror, not a witness.
2. *"High correlation is disqualifying for a verdict"* is the framework's own rule (Fig 2 :40), so selecting recipients for likely agreement manufactures the correlation that voids their agreement.
3. *"A verifier that can reconstruct the claim cannot verify it."* (Fig 10 :42). The first contact carries the residual and its refuter, never the source.
