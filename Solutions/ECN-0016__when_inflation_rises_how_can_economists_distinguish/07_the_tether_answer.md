# The Tether's answer on demand-driven versus supply-driven inflation

**Mode: `specified` : not `grounded`.** This is a shape and a route, not a result. Simulated derivation checked against the figures (`05_what_shape_means.md` §6), not a ground reading.

## 1. The residual it answers

A rise in the price level is one reading with at least two generating conditions behind it: aggregate demand outrunning supply, or supply contracting and unit costs rising. The reading itself does not say which. It is a union compound (Fig 9: *"a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either"*), because both can hold at once. The question asks for the discriminator, not for a verdict about any named year.

## 2. The shape

1. **Do not read the cause off the aggregate.** Fig 1 :19: *"The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated."* Fig 1 :22: *"Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average."* A headline number is the wrong object to interrogate about its source. Read the components.
2. **Describe the effect, not the cause.** Fig 9 :30-31: *"your words are borrowed too, so describe the effect rather than the cause"*. The effect is the joint movement of prices, output, unemployment, components and expectations. "Demand-pull" and "cost-push" are names bound later, from a model, with provenance recorded.
3. **Triangulate with a low-overlap second reading.** Fig 2 :35: *"Alignment is triangulation, not negotiation. A mirror cannot correct you."* Fig 8 :45: *"Two frames together reach what neither reaches alone."* The world record supplies the asymmetry: demand-pull involves *"inflation rising as real gross domestic product rises and unemployment falls"* (Wikipedia, Demand-pull inflation), while an adverse supply shock gives *"high inflation, stagnant economic growth, and elevated unemployment"* (Wikipedia, Stagflation). These are two instruments, not two opinions.
4. **Enumerate the habitat.** Fig 11 :43: *"The habitat is enumerated, never composed."* The published statistical system already lists price indices, producer prices, import prices, employment cost and productivity. Read the list off the world rather than composing a favourite variable set.
5. **Keep mixed episodes unbundled.** Fig 9 :54: *"And when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again."* Keep each cut ranked and reversible (Fig 9 :40-41).
6. **The framework does not settle the episode.** It returns the shape any discriminator must have. The episode verdict belongs to the ground: published outturn data plus an instrument that discriminates sources. Fig 9 :32: *"you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself"*.

## 3. What it explicitly does not say

- Not a classification of any particular inflation episode. No data series was analysed and no instrument was run.
- Not "demand and supply are mutually exclusive". They form a union and can co-occur.
- Not a derivation of macroeconomics from the six-term substrate. Fig 13 governs substrate composition, not shock identification; using it here was a strain and was retracted (`01_figure_grounded_audit.md` §4).
- Not a claim that the ⋛ comparison bond decides core versus headline. That reading is a weak analogue and strained.
- Not `grounded`. `specified` means a checkable shape with a stated refuter.

## 4. Pre-registered refuter

Written before this conclusion (`01_figure_grounded_audit.md` §5). The shape claim is refuted if:

1. one aggregate number plus a fixed rule, with no components and no second series, classifies episodes with zero error;
2. the second series moves in the same direction under both causes, so it carries no separating information;
3. an episode fails to separate under all available series, which is reported as `unreachable-at-this-budget`, not smoothed into a verdict;
4. a figure re-census shows Fig 9 :30-31 and the shape-not-a-name limit do not govern source attribution.

## 5. What would settle it : the ground

A source-discriminating instrument, not more agreement. From *The Hard Problem is About Source* (carried in `05_what_shape_means.md` §4): a spectrometer resolved green metamerism; an argument did not. Here:

- **Component dispersion test:** does the rise sit in a few components (energy, food) or is it broad? The BLS September 2026 table (all items 3.7 percent, core 2.6 percent, energy 20.4 percent) is a public instance of the reading, not a verdict.
- **Direction test:** do output and unemployment move in the directions the two hypotheses predict? A price rise with falling output and rising unemployment points to supply; a price rise with rising output and falling unemployment points to demand.
- **Expectations and wage test:** did expected inflation and unit labour costs lead or follow? This is the second-order contact where a supply shock becomes persistent (Fig 11 :52-53).
- **Union test:** after the single-condition regions are cleared, does an intersection of demand and supply still bind? If yes, keep both cuts.

## 6. The honest stop

**Unreached, not unreachable.** The shape is statable now; the episode verdict needs data and an instrument, and this derivation supplies neither. The six-term set is, in the exemplar's phrase, *"a human's careful guess at reality's compression axes"*, and declaring it makes a choice visible rather than neutral. This framework does not replace macroeconometric identification; it says what any answer must look like and records where it stopped. *"Indiscernibility is not identity."*

## 7. One-paragraph version for outreach

> The Tether does not name the cause of an inflation episode, and it does not claim a new test. It says that a headline rate cannot carry its own source, because the same rise can come from demand outrunning supply or from supply contracting; that the discriminator must read the price index per component, add a second observable whose response differs between the two (output and unemployment are the standard pair), and range both against outturn data; that a mixed episode is a union to be unbundled rather than an either-or; and that the verdict belongs to a source-discriminating instrument, not to a vote among forecasters. What it offers is the shape any answer must have, three refuters written in advance, and the point at which the shape stops short of a name.
