# Expert verification packet : the Tether on demand-driven versus supply-driven inflation

**What this is:** a claim in a form an inflation researcher can check. Mode, derivation and refuters are stated before the conclusion, so a reader knows what to look for and what would settle it.

**Mode: `specified` : not `grounded`.** No episode is claimed as diagnosed. This is a shape and a route, not a result.

## 1. The claim, in one paragraph

A single headline inflation rate cannot tell an observer whether the episode is demand-driven or supply-driven, because both produce the same reading. Any discriminator must (i) read the residual per component rather than through the aggregate, (ii) add at least one second observable whose response differs between the two candidate sources, and (iii) range both against outturn data rather than against agreement among forecasters. An episode can be both demand and supply, so the two cuts are kept separate and reversible. The framework does not name the source of any particular episode; it gives the shape any answer must have, with refuters written in advance. The specific economic variables come from the economics frame and are imports with provenance, not figure-derived primitives.

## 2. Three checkable sub-claims

### C1 : framework-internal; checkable by reading Figures 1 and 9

- **Statement:** an aggregate reading cannot carry the source; the residual must be read per slot, and the cause must be described as an effect.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 1 :19 : *"The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated."* Fig 1 :22 : *"Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average."* Fig 9 :30-31 : *"your words are borrowed too, so describe the effect rather than the cause"*.
- **Refuter:** show that the aggregate rate plus a fixed rule, with no component detail and no other series, classifies dated demand and supply episodes with zero error. Or show that Fig 9 :30-31 governs only missing primitives and not source attribution from a lossy reading.

### C2 : measurement-facing; checkable against public price and labour data

- **Statement:** the component split and the second series exist in the public data and move as the two hypotheses predict. Demand-pull is described as *"inflation rising as real gross domestic product rises and unemployment falls"* (Wikipedia, Demand-pull inflation, fetched 2026-11-23). Stagflation, the adverse supply case, is *"the combination of high inflation, stagnant economic growth, and elevated unemployment"* (Wikipedia, Stagflation, fetched 2026-11-23). The BLS CPI page publishes headline and component series side by side (September 2026: All items 3.7%, All items less food and energy 2.6%, Energy 20.4%).
- **Figure side:** Fig 11 :43 : *"The habitat is enumerated, never composed."* Fig 11 :52-53 : *"And contact cascades. Scoping the habitat to direct contact is how DDT reached the eagles: first-order enumeration, second-order effect, and the residual that mattered was three steps out."*
- **What to check:** is the output and unemployment asymmetry real in the episodes you know, or does a supply shock sometimes coincide with falling unemployment? Does the component split carry information the headline does not?
- **Refuter:** if output and unemployment move in the same direction under both causes, the triangulation has no surplus (Fig 8 :45). Or if a named second-order contact can change the diagnosis and was omitted, the enumeration failed.

### C3 : decision-facing; checkable against the checking arrangement

- **Statement:** agreement among forecasters is not the anchor for an episode verdict; independent readings ranged against outturn data are.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 2 :35 : *"Alignment is triangulation, not negotiation. A mirror cannot correct you."* Fig 2 :40 : *"disqualifying for a verdict: agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool"*. Fig 2 :42 : *"Buy independence in proportion to the cost of being wrong."*
- **Refuter:** show a case where consensus among analysts, with no independent range against outturn data, reliably settled the source. Or show that the anchor moved in response to what analysts thought, which by Fig 2 :33 (*"The anchor must not update."*) makes it a frame, not an anchor.
- **Status:** triangulated across Fig 2 and Fig 8, which have low overlap (anchor definition versus union surplus). This simulated second seat shares the primary's evidence pool, so its agreement is internal consistency, not corroboration.

## 5. What would settle it

1. A component dispersion test on a named episode, showing whether the rise is broad or concentrated.
2. A direction test: do output and unemployment move as the two hypotheses predict?
3. An expectations and wage test: did expected inflation and unit labour costs lead or follow?
4. A union test on the intersection after single-condition regions are cleared.
5. The pre-registered refuters in §2, any one of which withdraws the shape claim.

## 6. What an expert can check in twenty minutes

1. Read Fig 1 :19, :22, Fig 9 :30-31, Fig 2 :35, :40 and Fig 11 :43. Six short passages.
2. Check C2 against one episode you know well. Did the component split and the output/unemployment pair separate the sources?
3. Name one observation that would make you abandon the shape. If you cannot, say so; that is useful too.
4. Send the refutation. A failure is more useful than an endorsement.
