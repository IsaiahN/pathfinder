# Outreach draft : check the diagnostic shape before you decide about the framework

**Use:** one-to-one, or as a cover note attached to a submission through the public venues in `03_public_contact_points.csv`. **Not** for bulk send.

**Hard precondition:** do not send until a concrete episode and its component data are attached to the shape claim. An ungrounded shape sent as a verdict collapses the two-stage offer on contact.

---

**Subject:** One claim about inflation diagnosis that you can refute in two minutes

> Hi [name],
>
> I am working on a method-independent question and would like your verdict on one output before I show you where it came from. I am not asking you to agree with it, and I am not asking you to trust a framework.
>
> **The output (`specified`, not `grounded`):** a single headline inflation rate cannot tell you whether an episode is demand-driven or supply-driven. The rate is one reading; the candidate causes are at least two conditions that produce the same reading. What discriminates them is a second observable whose response differs between the two. Broad demand pressure lifts output and lowers unemployment; an adverse supply shock raises prices while output falls and unemployment rises. Component detail matters: a broad rise and a rise concentrated in energy and food are different shapes of the same aggregate number.
>
> I am not claiming the framework settles any particular episode. I am claiming only that any answer must have that shape: per-component reading, a low-overlap second observable, and a check against outturn data. An episode can be both demand and supply at once, so I keep the two cuts separate and reversible rather than forcing an either-or.
>
> **What would refute it:**
> 1. a single aggregate number plus a fixed rule that classifies demand versus supply with zero error across dated episodes, with no component detail and no other series;
> 2. a candidate second series that moves in the same direction under both causes, so it carries no separating information;
> 3. a well-documented episode where prices, output, unemployment, components and expectations all fail to separate the two sources even with the data that exists.
>
> I wrote these down before assembling anything else. If any one holds, I withdraw the claim. A one-line "this fails because..." is more useful to me than agreement. If you would rather not engage, no reply is needed.
>
> **If it survives, and only then:** here are the sources. The figures, not my summary of them, are the authority.
> - Canonical figure text with line numbers (local extract): `FRAMEWORK/reference/03-FIGURES_TEXT.md`
> - Canonical usage rule: `FRAMEWORK/reference/FIGURES_README_canonical.md`
> - Public CPI data and component tables: https://www.bls.gov/cpi/
>
> **If you want to use the machinery:** the formula is in the framework files. Run it on an episode you know well and can check against a ground. Write down what would refute your result *before* you run it, and state the mode of what you get. A reported failure is more useful to me than a reported success.

## Two-stage rule for the reply

1. **Stage 1.** Send only the shape claim and the three refuters. Ask what the recipient would count as a failure. Record their verdict before any theory is shown.
2. **Stage 2, opt-in only.** If they are curious, send the three links, with the note that the figures are the authority. Their later agreement is never pooled as corroboration, because Fig 2 :40 says *"disqualifying for a verdict: agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool"*.
3. **Stage 3.** Offer the formula as a method, with the instruction that each user writes a fresh refuter and states a mode.

**Hard precondition restated:** stages 1 and 2 work only if the stage-1 output survives. A shape with no attached episode and no component split is not yet a checkable output.
