# Isomers, source separation and order in an inflation diagnosis

**Mode: `specified`.** This is a simulated derivation checked against the figures, not a ground reading (see `05_what_shape_means.md` §6).

## RESIDUAL

The price reading is many-to-one: at least two sources produce it. Three questions are owed: (1) is this a source-separation problem in the sense a low-overlap field already solves, (2) are two episodes with the same headline rise **isomers** under Fig 12's definition, and (3) what is the order of the diagnostic, and what does that order constrain?

## DERIVATION

### Q1 : Is this a source-separation problem? LOW-OVERLAP ANALOGUE, single-source as an analogue

- The engineering field of blind source separation defines the task as *"the separation of a set of source signals from a set of mixed signals"* (Wikipedia *Signal separation*, fetched 2026-11-23). It states: *"This problem is in general highly underdetermined, but useful solutions can be derived under a surprising variety of conditions."*
- The figure-side statement of the same shape is Fig 1 :22, *"Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average."* and Fig 9 :30-31, *"your words are borrowed too, so describe the effect rather than the cause"*.
- The analogue is genuinely low overlap: signal processing does not draw on the Tether figures or on macroeconomic theory, so its agreement with the figure shape is not a shared evidence pool. It corroborates the structure, never the verdict.
- The analogue also marks its own limit: separation needs at least as many independent mixes as sources, or an independence assumption. In economics the "mixes" are the published series. If only one series exists, the source is underdetermined in exactly the engineering sense.
- **Falsifier, written before the run:** if a single scalar observation with no independence assumption and no second series separates the sources exactly, the analogue fails and the underdetermination claim with it.

### Q2 : Are two episodes with the same headline rise isomers? WEAK ANALOGUE, strained

- Fig 12 :117: **"isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."**
- Fig 12 :94: **"The arrangement is the substance."**
- Two months can share the same atom set (a headline rise, a core rise, an energy rise, an output reading, an unemployment reading) and differ in arrangement: in one, the broad component rises lead and output is strong; in the other, energy leads and output is weak. Those are different substances under the figure's definition even though a flat headline formula cannot tell them apart.
- **But** the "atoms" here are published measurements, not substrate primitives, and the bonds are causal relations in a model, not Fig 12's seven typed bonds. The isomer reading is therefore an **analogue with a strain**, not a derivation. It is recorded, not promoted to the verdict. `01_figure_grounded_audit.md` §4 reaches the same conclusion from the strain test.
- **Falsifier:** if two episodes diagnosed as different sources have identical component detail, output, unemployment and expectations at every available date, the isomer claim fails and the episodes are one substance under the figure's definition.

### Q3 : What is the order of the diagnostic?

- Fig 3 :20-21: *"The chain is ordered by dependency, not by importance. Each link consumes what the one above produces, so effort spent above the break arrives at a step that cannot use it."*
- Ordered diagnostic chain:
  1. **Perception.** Are the headline, core and component series published and comparable? (BLS CPI page, yes.)
  2. **Vocabulary.** Do we have terms for what each series affords, that is what it would look like under demand and under supply? (Wikipedia demand-pull and stagflation pages, yes, with the cost-push category explicitly disputed.)
  3. **Objective.** What counts as progress? A classification whose confidence survives a check against outturn data, not a story that fits the price rise.
  4. **Planning.** Which observables to triangulate, and how to keep them independent (Fig 8 :45, :48-49).
  5. **Learn and carry.** Record the lineage of each classification so a later reader can locate the error (Fig 10 :62-63).
- Fig 3 :22-23: *"A step whose input never arrives cannot be diagnosed, only its predecessor can. Which is why a reading taken below the break is not a weak reading. It is a reading of nothing."* Attempting step 4 on an economy with no component data is a reading of nothing.

### Q4 : What does the union order change?

- Fig 9 :55-57: *"A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones. A single lever clears the region where only one condition holds and leaves the intersections standing, because the second condition still binds."*
- Order therefore matters twice: the time order of the series (did component prices lead the aggregate, and did output turn before unemployment?) and the logical order of the cuts (test demand alone, then supply alone, then their intersection).
- Fig 9 :40-41: *"a wrong cut removes the answer and speeds up so keep every cut ranked and reversible"*.
- **Falsifier for the union order:** a recorded episode in which the two conditions are provably mutually exclusive in the data, so the intersection cut never binds.

## CONCLUSION

The underdetermination shape is corroborated by a genuinely low-overlap engineering field, with a stated falsifier. The isomer reading is a weak analogue with a visible strain, not a derivation. The diagnostic chain is ordered by dependency, so a diagnosis attempted before component data exist is a reading of nothing. Mixed episodes require the union order with ranked, reversible cuts. Nothing here names the source of any particular episode.
