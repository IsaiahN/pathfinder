# Outreach draft: check the residual before you decide about the framework

**Use:** one-to-one, or as a cover letter attached to a submission through the public venues in `03_public_contact_points.csv`. **Not** for bulk send.

**Hard precondition:** do not send until the stage-1 claim has survived the refuter below. The output must be checkable on contact or the two-stage offer collapses.

---

**Subject:** One checkable claim about benchmark scores and context change

> Hi [name],
>
> I have one specific claim I would like you to check against your own work. I am not asking you to read a theory first, and I am not asking you to agree with anything. If the claim does not survive, there is nothing else to look at.
>
> **The residual, in effect terms (`general` shape, `specified` transfer prediction):** a system scores well on a familiar benchmark in context C0. The context changes slightly to C1 (a new distribution, a changed cue, a perturbed input, a shifted population). The score on C0 stays reportable, but task success in C1 collapses. The claim is that the benchmark is a **substituted habitat**: a high score on it proves that the wiring works in that habitat, and does not establish capability in a different one. Capability is a property of the system and its habitat together, not of the system alone.
>
> **The mechanism I would like refuted or confirmed:** when the habitat is substituted, two things are silent until the goal fails. What the test harness failed to reproduce stays invisible until the task breaks, and what the new habitat introduced stays invisible until it acts. So the pre-change score could not have carried the information about the post-change failure. The benchmark score is a filter for allocating effort, not a witness for the verdict about transfer.
>
> **What would refute the claim (written down before this message):**
> 1. a benchmark score that predicts performance under arbitrary small context changes as well as a direct measurement of contact with the changed habitat;
> 2. a system made more capable in a changed habitat by an improvement that did not change its contact, habitat, or instruments;
> 3. a demonstration that the benchmark is the ground for the generalization question itself, so that an unchanged score means there was no residual;
> 4. a case where continuing to compose inside the original training frame reaches the term that predicts the new context, with no new habitat and no new instrument;
> 5. a round trip from score to deployment and back that loses nothing for the capability of interest, that is, a zero round-trip gap.
>
> If any one holds, I will withdraw the corresponding part. A one-line "this fails because..." is more useful to me than agreement. If you would rather not engage, no reply is needed.
>
> **If it survives, and only then:** here is why I think the claim is shaped the way it is. The figures say a test harness is a substituted habitat, that capability belongs to agent and habitat together, and that when the question changes the evaluation room should change with it, otherwise the high score is only a symptom of the fixed room. They also say the ground does not decay but the channel to it does, and that a saturated metric is a channel failure. These are the authority; any summary I send is not.
>
> **What I am not claiming:** I am not saying which specific cue, artifact, or correlation caused any particular collapse. I am not saying more data or harder benchmarks never help; they help when the needed term is already inside the frame and was merely unfound. I am not claiming a measured size for the effect. This is a structural claim about what a score can and cannot license, checked for internal consistency against the figures, not a ground reading.
>
> **If you want to use the method:** the formula is public. Run it on a problem you know well and can check against a ground. Write down what would refute your result *before* you run it, and state the mode of what you get. A reported failure is more useful to me than a reported success.

## The two-stage offer, explicitly

1. **Stage 1:** send the residual, the shape claim and the five refuters. Ask what the recipient would count as a failure. Record their verdict **before** any framework is shown.
2. **Stage 2, opt-in only:** if they are curious, offer the sources in `04_links_and_distribution.md`. The figures, not this summary, are the authority. Their later agreement is never pooled as corroboration (Fig 2 :40: "agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool").
3. **Stage 3:** offer the formula as a method to reuse, with the instruction that each user writes their own refuter and states their own mode.

**Hard precondition:** stages 1 to 3 only work if stage 1 survives. As drafted here the stage-1 claim is a shape with refuters, not a measured effect. It is falsifiable, not asserted as `grounded`. Do not upgrade its mode when sending.

## One-to-one rule

Send to a single person at a time, with a real subject line and an opt-out line. Do not send a bulk mailing and do not rank recipients by likely agreement. Selecting for receptiveness manufactures the disqualifying correlation (Seat Map :163).
