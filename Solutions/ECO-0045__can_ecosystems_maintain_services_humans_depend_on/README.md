# Solution ECO-0045

**Question:** Can ecosystems maintain services humans depend on while losing much of their biodiversity?

**Source:** ecology.txt line 56

**Section:** 5. Ecosystem functions and services

**Mode:** `specified` on the substantive claims, `general` on the structural shape, not `grounded`.

**Status:** done

**Registry status:** pending

## Restatement as an effect

For each service s (pollination, soil formation, nutrient cycling, water regulation, carbon storage),
can the flow humans depend on stay above the level the ground pays for while the species count falls
substantially? Option A says yes, because species are buffered by redundancy. Option B says no,
because services depend on particular actors, relations and cascades. The answer-shape is bound before
the run: services stay up if and only if the functional groups, bonds and contact cascades that
generate each service survive, and each service is settled separately against a measured ground.
A diversity count alone does not decide any service.

## Key verdict

Conditional. A species count cannot decide a service. Interchangeability is a functional-group and
bond question; failure is third-order and delayed; maintenance is permanent work with addition
available and exclusion unavailable; each service is settled by its own ground. The safe loss
percentage is a declared `gap`, not a hidden answer. The framework's "habitat" is not the ecological
niche, and the canonical article explicitly declines that mapping.
