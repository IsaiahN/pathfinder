# Outreach draft: check the shape first, then decide about the framework

**Use:** one-to-one, or as a cover letter attached to a submission through the public venues in
`03_public_contact_points.csv`. **Not** for bulk send.

**Hard precondition:** do not send until the strains in `01_figure_grounded_audit.md` §4 are
acknowledged in the covering note. The output must survive a refuter-first reading or the two-stage
offer collapses on contact.

---

**Subject:** One checkable shape about biodiversity and ecosystem services

> Hi [name],
>
> I have one specific output I would like you to check against what you already know. I am not
> asking you to read the framework first, and I am not asking you to agree with it. If the output
> does not survive, there is nothing else to look at.
>
> **The question in effect terms.** For each service separately: pollination, soil formation,
> nutrient cycling, water regulation, carbon storage. Can the flow humans depend on stay above the
> level the ground pays for while the count of species falls substantially?
>
> **The shape (`specified`, not `grounded`).** A species count cannot decide any single service.
> Services stay up if and only if the functional groups, the bonds between them, and the contact
> cascades that generate each service survive, and each service is settled separately against a
> measured ground. Redundancy is real only inside a functional group with the same bond; same atoms
> under a different bond give a different service. Maintenance is permanent work: a manager can
> introduce an actor and cannot subtract the habitat, so a service that is "maintained" is a standing
> obligation, not a solved state. This is a shape, not a number and not a name.
>
> **What I am not claiming.** I am not claiming that a particular percentage of species loss is
> safe. I am not claiming which service fails first. I am not claiming that any assessment consensus
> or ecosystem-service index settles the question: an index is a filter for attention and budget,
> not a witness for a verdict. And I am not claiming that the framework returns a species name. It
> returns a kind of thing, never the answer itself.
>
> **What would refute the shape (written before this message):**
> 1. a scalar species count predicting every measured service with no per-service residual, across
>    several habitats and three or more independent cascades, with no management;
> 2. two arrangements with the same functional groups and bonds but different species producing the
>    same measured service flows, or a service surviving the loss of its whole functional group;
> 3. a service crossing its threshold while every enumerated actor, condition, relation and cascade
>    in its habitat is intact;
> 4. one intervention restoring every service with no categorically different residue left behind.
>
> If any one holds, I will retract the shape. A one-line "this fails because..." is more useful to me
> than agreement. If you would rather not engage, no reply is needed.
>
> **If it survives, and only then:** the sources are below. The figures are the authority, not my
> summary of them.
> - Figures and formula for direct download (SVG): local framework copy, path supplied on request.
> - Canonical figures README: local framework copy, path supplied on request.
> - Public ecological evidence on the relation between species loss and service flows: the
>   Cardinale 2012 and Hooper 2012 Nature papers, the IPBES 2019 Global Assessment, and the
>   Millennium Ecosystem Assessment 2005. Full links are in `04_links_and_distribution.md`.
>
> **If you want to use the machinery:** run it on a service you know well and can measure. Write
> down what would refute your result *before* you run it, and state the mode of what you get. A
> reported failure is more useful to me than a reported success.
>
> — [name]

**Two-stage discipline.** Stage 1 is the shape and its refuter, checked against the recipient's own
ground. Record their verdict **before** showing any framework material. Stage 2 is opt-in: the links
and the figures, with the note that the figures, not the summary, are the authority. A later
agreement is never pooled as corroboration (Fig 2 :40: "agreement about the world is worthless when
the agreeing frames share an evidence pool"). Stage 3 offers the method for reuse, with each user
writing their own refuter and stating their own mode.
