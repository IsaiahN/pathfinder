# Expert verification packet: the Tether on services under biodiversity loss

**What this is:** a claim in a form an ecosystem-services researcher can check. Mode, derivation and
refuters are stated before the conclusion, so a reader knows what to look for and what would settle
it.

**Mode: `specified` on the substantive claims, `general` on the structural shape, not `grounded`.**
No service flow is asserted as measured. This is a shape and a route, not a result.

## 1. The claim, in one paragraph

A species count cannot decide whether a service is maintained. Services stay up only when the
functional groups, the bonds between them and the contact cascades that generate each service
survive, and when each service is settled separately against a measured ground. Species are
interchangeable only within one functional group under the same bond; same atoms under a different
bond give a different service. Failure can be third-order and delayed, so a service can fail with its
counted species still present. Maintenance by humans is permanent work: a seat can introduce actors
and cannot subtract the habitat. The framework returns that shape, the falsifiers written in
advance, and the instruments that would close it. It returns no threshold and no species name.

## 2. Three checkable sub-claims

### C1 — framework-internal plus ecology-facing; checkable by reading Figures 11 and 12
- **Statement:** a species count is not the governing quantity; the functional groups, bonds and
  contact cascades are.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 11 :40: "A system cannot be made more capable by improving the agent in
  isolation. An improvement that does not change contact changes nothing, however much it improves."
  Fig 12 :123: "functional group. A substructure whose presence fixes the outcome. The group says
  what kind; the qualifier says which member." Fig 12 :117: "isomer. Same atoms, different bond,
  different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."
- **What to check:** is a service flow ever fully predicted by a species count with no per-service
  residual, across habitats with different contact structures? Are two species interchangeable for a
  service only when they occupy the same functional group under the same bond?
- **Refuter:** a scalar count predicting every measured service across several habitats and three or
  more independent cascades with no management; or a service surviving loss of its whole functional
  group with the bond intact.

### C2 — ecology-facing; checkable against cascades and delayed failure
- **Statement:** failure is third-order and delayed, so presence of the counted species does not
  imply presence of the service.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 11 :52-53: "And contact cascades. Scoping the habitat to direct contact
  is how DDT reached the eagles: first-order enumeration, second-order effect, and the residual that
  mattered was three steps out." Fig 11 :68: "what you failed to reproduce is invisible until the
  goal fails" and Fig 11 :69: "what you unintentionally introduced is invisible until it acts".
- **What to check:** find a documented service failure where the directly counted species were still
  present and the cause was two or three links out. Find a substituted or simplified system that
  appeared to work until a delayed failure. The figure's own case is Carson, Silent Spring, 1962
  (Fig 11 :54).
- **Refuter:** a service crossing its ground threshold while every enumerated actor, condition,
  relation and cascade remains intact and unchanged.

### C3 — framework-internal plus management-facing; checkable by reading Figure 11
- **Statement:** maintenance is permanent work with addition available and exclusion unavailable.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 11 :61: "The seat's power is asymmetric. Introduction is available;
  exclusion is not. You can add an actor and you cannot subtract the habitat, because contact is a
  fact about the world and does not consult you." Fig 11 :63: "Which is why a managed ecosystem is
  permanent work rather than a solved state."
- **What to check:** name a maintained service where exclusion of an unwanted actor was achieved, or
  where maintenance ended and the service stayed up without further work. Check whether the figure's
  historical examples (cane toads, kudzu, Fig 11 :65) are cases of an unenumerated cascade.
- **Refuter:** demonstrated exclusion of an unwanted actor from a habitat, making maintenance a
  solved state; or a maintained service with no seat and no declared stake.

## 3. What is explicitly not claimed

- Not a threshold. No percentage of species loss is declared safe.
- Not a species name or a service name selected by the framework. It returns a shape.
- Not that biodiversity is irrelevant. It is a filter for the budget, never a witness for the verdict
  (Fig 9 :60).
- Not that "habitat" here means the ecological niche. The canonical article declines that mapping
  (`05_what_shape_means.md` §5).
- Not a valuation of human benefit versus ecological value, and not a policy recommendation.
- Not `grounded`. Nothing here was settled by an instrument.

## 5. What would settle it

A source-discriminating instrument, not a further argument. From *The Hard Problem is About Source*:
an instrument that reads green "cannot comment on the source" - the spectrometer resolved it. Here:

1. **Count-invariance:** measure all five services across habitats with differing contact structures.
2. **Bond-invariance:** compare same-group/same-bond arrangements with different species identities.
3. **Intact-habitat failure:** watch for a threshold crossing with every enumerated contact intact.
4. **Exclusion:** attempt to subtract an unwanted actor.
5. **Single lever:** apply one intervention and measure all five services for a categorically
   different residue.

## 6. What an expert can check in twenty minutes

1. Read Fig 11 :40, :52-53, :61, :63 and Fig 12 :117, :123 - six short passages.
2. Check C1 against one service you know: is a species count sufficient, or do functional group and
   bond decide the outcome?
3. Name one documented third-order service failure, or say why none is known.
4. Name one case where exclusion of an unwanted actor succeeded, or say why exclusion is unavailable.
5. Send the refutation. A failure is more useful than an endorsement.
