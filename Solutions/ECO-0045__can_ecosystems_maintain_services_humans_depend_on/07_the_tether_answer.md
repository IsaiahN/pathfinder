# The Tether's answer on services under biodiversity loss

**Mode: `specified` on the substantive claims, `general` on the structural shape, not `grounded`.**
This is a simulated derivation checked against the figures (`05_what_shape_means.md` §6), not a ground
reading. No service flow was measured and no ecosystem was visited.

## 1. The residual it answers

The question is a vector, not a scalar. For each service s, can the flow humans depend on stay above
the level the ground pays for while species counts fall? Fig 1 :19: "The residual is indexed by
object slot, not aggregated." The bundle is a **union compound**, not one hard problem (Fig 9 :55:
"A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones"). It
has one loop and one ground per service. Fig 11 :43: "The habitat is enumerated, never composed."

## 2. The shape

1. **A species count cannot decide a service.** Fig 11 :40: "A system cannot be made more capable by
   improving the agent in isolation. An improvement that does not change contact changes nothing,
   however much it improves." What governs is the contact structure: actors, conditions, relations
   and their cascades, not the headcount. Caveat from `05` §5: the framework's "habitat" is not the
   ecological niche, and the article declines that mapping.
2. **Redundancy is a bond question.** Under Fig 12 :123, species are interchangeable only within one
   functional group and under the same bond. Under Fig 12 :117, same atoms with a different bond give
   a different substance, so two species lists that look identical can yield different services.
3. **Failure is third-order and delayed.** Fig 11 :52: "And contact cascades. Scoping the habitat to
   direct contact is how DDT reached the eagles: first-order enumeration, second-order effect, and the
   residual that mattered was three steps out." The figure itself names the ground that settled it:
   "Carson, Silent Spring, 1962" (Fig 11 :54). A service can fail with its counted species still
   present.
4. **Maintenance is permanent work with asymmetric power.** Fig 11 :61: "Introduction is available;
   exclusion is not." Fig 11 :63: "Which is why a managed ecosystem is permanent work rather than a
   solved state." So the affirmative answer is reachable only as ongoing, grounded work with a
   declared seat, never as a property of a species-poor system.
5. **Each service is settled separately by measurement.** Fig 1 :23: "The ground enters as the only
   metric." A count, an index or an assessment consensus is a filter for the budget, not a witness
   for the verdict (Fig 9 :60).

The one-sentence answer: **services can be maintained while biodiversity falls only conditionally,
when the functional groups, bonds and contact cascades that generate each service survive and each
service keeps paying at its own ground; a diversity count alone cannot decide it, and the
maintenance is permanent work rather than a solved state.**

## 3. What it explicitly does not say

- Not a threshold. No percentage of species loss is declared safe or unsafe. That is a `gap`, not a
  hidden answer.
- Not a species list. The framework returns a shape, not a name (Fig 9 :32).
- Not "biodiversity does not matter." It matters as a filter on where to look, and species identity
  changes outcomes (Hooper et al. 2012), but it is not the witness for a service verdict.
- Not "ecosystems are interchangeable with habitats." The ecological-niche mapping is explicitly
  declined by the canonical article (`05` §5).
- Not a policy ranking, and not a valuation of ecological versus human benefits. That bundle belongs
  to a different question and its own loop.
- Not `grounded`. Nothing here was settled by an instrument.

## 4. What would settle it: the ground

A per-service measurement, not a further argument. From *The Hard Problem is About Source*, an
instrument that reads green "cannot comment on the source"; the spectrometer resolved it. Here:

- **Count-invariance test:** measure every service across habitats with differing contact structures
  and three or more independent cascades. If a scalar count predicts all flows with no per-service
  residual, F1 and F6 fail.
- **Bond-invariance test:** compare arrangements with the same functional groups and bonds but
  different species identities. If every measured flow is identical, and if a service survives the
  loss of its whole functional group, F3 fails.
- **Intact-habitat failure test:** watch for a service crossing its threshold while every enumerated
  actor, condition, relation and cascade is intact. If found, F4 fails.
- **Exclusion test:** try to subtract an unwanted actor from a habitat. If exclusion is available,
  F5 fails and maintenance becomes a solved state.
- **Single-lever test:** apply one intervention and measure all services. If every service is restored
  with no categorically different residue, F7 fails.

## 5. The honest stop

**Unreached, not unreachable.** *They're Scanning Books When They Should*: "Declaring the primitives
does not make them neutral. It makes the choice visible." The six-term set is "a human's careful
guess at reality's compression axes; another sensorium's minimum set need not be those six." So this
answer stops at the limit of what the figures can constrain and records the lineage so the stop is
checkable. "Indiscernibility is not identity." A search that finds no threshold is not proof that no
threshold exists (Fig 8 :39: "So a search that finds nothing is never a proof of absence").

## 6. One-paragraph version for outreach

> The Tether does not answer the biodiversity question with a number. It says that a species count
> cannot decide any single service, because capability lives in agent and habitat, not in the agent
> alone. What decides a service is the functional groups present, the bonds between them and the
> contact cascades three steps out; species are interchangeable only inside a functional group under
> the same bond, and same atoms under a different bond give a different service. A service can fail
> while its counted species are still there. If humans are to maintain services while diversity
> falls, that is permanent work by a seat that can introduce actors and cannot subtract the habitat,
> with a declared stake and a measured ground for each service. The framework returns that shape, the
> five falsifiers written in advance, and the instruments that would close it. It does not name a
> threshold or a species, and the centre stays open until the ground settles it.
