## 0. The question as a residual, in effect terms

> Can ecosystems maintain services humans depend on while losing much of their biodiversity?

Stated as an effect: for each service s (pollination, soil formation, nutrient cycling, water
regulation, carbon storage), can the flow that humans depend on stay above the level the ground
pays for while the count of species in the system falls substantially?

- **Option A (yes).** Service output is buffered by redundancy. Many species are interchangeable, so
  losing some leaves the flow unchanged. Leads to: diversity count is a poor target; manage for
  service delivery with fewer species.
- **Option B (no).** Service output depends on particular actors, relations and cascades, so
  substantial loss degrades it, sometimes across a threshold, often with a delay. Leads to: count is
  a poor proxy in a different way, because which species and which relations matter, and the loss can
  be irreversible without permanent work.

**The answer-shape, written down and bound before running the census** (Formula "Direction": make
the sought-for shape an ordinary term so it can accrue a residual):

> SHAPE(ECO-0045) := services stay up if and only if the functional groups, the bonds between them,
> and the contact cascades that generate each service survive, and each service is settled
> separately against a measured ground; a diversity count alone does not decide any service.

This is a shape, not a name. It does not say which species, which threshold percentage, or which
service fails first.

## 1. Domain census, before choosing any figure line

**R by arity, symmetry and scale.** Arity: multi-place and indexed. The residual is a vector
`R_s` over services and over species slots, not one scalar (Fig 1 :19: "The residual is indexed by
object slot, not aggregated."). Symmetry: the services are **not** interchangeable. They stand to
one another as molecules under different bonds, so permuting actors or relations changes the
service (Fig 12 :117: "isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is
invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."). Scale: from the local habitat of actors,
conditions and relations, out to a cascade three steps beyond direct contact, and across time to a
delayed failure.

**One line per domain (actors / currency / anchor).** A domain without a nameable anchor is not a
domain yet.

| domain | actors | currency | anchor | status |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| D1 Habitat and ecological contact | species as actors; conditions (light, water, temperature, season, terrain); relations (pollination, predation, competition, dependency) | contact and the flows it sustains | what the ground kept in the unmaintained mode | admitted |
| D2 Service and function | functional groups, catalysts, cycles, substructures | service output: production, decomposition, nutrient flux, water regulation, carbon storage | measured service flow per service | admitted |
| D3 Management and assessment seat | managers, societies, assessment bodies, instruments | introduced actors, conventions, evidence | the same measured ground, which does not update | admitted |
| X1 "Biodiversity" as a free-floating service guarantee | a single count | none distinct from D1 and D2 | none nameable | not a domain yet, absorbed into D1/D2 |

**Partition or union.** Union, not partition. The services are overlapping conditions, not a list
of separate things (Fig 9 :55: "A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not
a list of separate ones."). One lever clears the region where a single condition holds and leaves
the intersections (Fig 9 :56). So the services must be unbundled before searching again.

**Order by dependency (Fig 3).** (1) describe `R_s` per service and per slot; (2) enumerate the
habitat of each service, actors, conditions, relations, then the cascade outward (Fig 11 :43-44);
(3) settle each service against its own ground before aggregating anything; (4) locate the seat and
declare its stake, because introduction is available and exclusion is not; (5) only then consider
trade-offs and valuation across services. Optimising a link above the break cannot rescue one below
it (Fig 3 :19).

## 3. Findings F1 to F8 (quotes before conclusions)

### F1 — The count is the wrong quantity; capability lives in agent and habitat

Fig 11 :39: "So the answerable question is not what intelligence is, but what makes a system
capable. And capability is a property of agent-and-habitat, never of the agent alone."
Fig 11 :40: "A system cannot be made more capable by improving the agent in isolation. An
improvement that does not change contact changes nothing, however much it improves."
Fig 1 :19: "The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated."
Fig 1 :22: "Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average."

**Reading.** "Biodiversity" as a single count is an agent-only metric. What maintains a service is
the contact structure: which actors, conditions and relations are present. A count that stays high
while contacts are severed is a live signal averaged away. This is the structural answer to the
question's own framing: the question asks about a count, but the figure says the count is not the
quantity that governs service delivery. **Mode `general`. Triangulated** across Fig 11 (habitat and
capability) and Fig 1 (slot-indexed residual), which have low overlap. **Refuter:** a measured case
where a scalar count predicts every measured service with no per-service residual across several
habitats, with contact structure held irrelevant.

### F2 — Actor valence is relative to the service, not carried by the species

Fig 11 :49: "An actor's valence is relative to the residual, not a property it carries."
Fig 11 :50: "The same actor is a benefactor to one residual and a malfactor to another, so mutualism
and parasitism are relations rather than kinds."
Fig 11 :51: "No standing list of good actors survives a change of residual."

**Reading.** A species is not a uniform "good" for services. The same actor can help pollination and
harm water regulation, or be a beneficial pollinator in one habitat and a pest in another. So
"losing X percent of species" cannot by itself predict a service, because the valence of the lost
species differs per service. World corroboration: Hooper et al. 2012 (Nature) report that "The
identity of species lost also had a large effect on changes in productivity and decomposition,
generating a wide range of plausible outcomes for extinction." **Mode `specified`. Single-source**
at figure level (Fig 11); the world source is secondary and shares the ecological evidence pool.
**Refuter:** an invariant, service-independent valence assigned to each species that survives a
change of residual and a change of service.

### F3 — Interchangeability is a bond question: functional group versus isomer

Fig 12 :123: "functional group. A substructure whose presence fixes the outcome. The group says
what kind; the qualifier says which member."
Fig 12 :117: "isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible
in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."
Fig 12 :119: "catalyst. A part that survives its own use. Spent and regenerating parts behave
differently across uses, and nothing records which a term is."
Fig 12 :121: "cycle. The output re-enters as input. The sign of the loop decides whether it closes,
and a chain cannot close by construction."
Fig 13 :121: "Different routes can arrive at the same substance."

**Reading.** Species are redundant for a service only when they occupy the same functional group
under the same bond. If the atoms are the same but the bond differs, they are isomers: the service,
like the substance, changes even though the ingredient list is unchanged. Catalysts (parts that
survive their own use, for example ecosystem engineers) and cycles (nutrient cycling) are further
structures that a species count cannot see. This is the figure-grounded content behind the
neighbouring ecology question about apparent redundancy. It yields a conditional answer: services
can be maintained under species loss when the functional groups, bonds and cycles are preserved;
"apparent redundancy" is the isomer case where they are not. **Mode `specified`, ungrounded.
Triangulated** across Fig 12 (bond structures) and Fig 13 (different routes to one substance), low
overlap. **Refuter:** two arrangements with the same functional groups and bonds but different
species identities that nevertheless produce different measured service flows; or a service that
survives the removal of its entire functional group with its bond intact.

### F4 — Contact cascades make failure third-order and delayed

Fig 11 :52-53: "And contact cascades. Scoping the habitat to direct contact is how DDT reached the
eagles: first-order enumeration, second-order effect, and the residual that mattered was three steps
out."
Fig 11 :54: "Carson, Silent Spring, 1962. The mechanism, biomagnification through the food chain to
eggshell thinning, was established over the following decade."
Fig 11 :68: "what you failed to reproduce is invisible until the goal fails"
Fig 11 :69: "what you unintentionally introduced is invisible until it acts"
Fig 3 :27: "A link with no instrument is not a link that works. It is a link nobody has looked at."

**Reading.** A system can retain its counted species and still lose a service through a path three
steps out. Conversely, a simplified or substituted system can appear to work until the missing
thing becomes visible at failure, and an introduced actor can act long after introduction. The
question "can services be maintained while diversity falls" cannot be answered at the level of
direct species-service pairs. World corroboration: the "Trophic cascade" entry describes cascades
as "powerful indirect interactions that can control entire ecosystems," and records hysteresis in
the Yellowstone case where "Such lack of return to a prior state is referred to as hysteresis."
**Mode `specified`. Triangulated** across Fig 11 and Fig 3. **Refuter:** a measured service that
fails with every enumerated contact and cascade in its habitat intact and unchanged.

### F5 — Maintenance is a seat's permanent work; addition is available, exclusion is not

Fig 11 :61: "The seat's power is asymmetric. Introduction is available; exclusion is not. You can
add an actor and you cannot subtract the habitat, because contact is a fact about the world and does
not consult you."
Fig 11 :63-64: "Which is why a managed ecosystem is permanent work rather than a solved state. And
in neither mode is the habitat composed: unmaintained nothing composes it, maintained the seat adds
to a habitat it still did not author."
Fig 11 :65: "Introducing an actor for its cascade is ecosystem engineering, after Jones, Lawton and
Shachak, 1994. Cane toads and kudzu are the same move with the cascade unenumerated."
Fig 10 :59: "an interest in the outcome is unavoidable, an interest in the reading is disqualifying."

**Reading.** The affirmative answer is reachable only as ongoing, grounded work by a seat, not as a
property of a species-poor system. Because the seat cannot subtract the habitat, because introduced
actors have cascades that were unenumerated in the historical examples (cane toads, kudzu), and
because the seat has a stake in the reading, any claim of maintenance must declare who is doing the
introducing and what ground is being read. World corroboration: Cardinale et al. 2012 conclude that
"together with management strategies such as payment for ecosystem services, the next generation of
biodiversity science has the potential to bring the modern era of biodiversity loss to an end and to
maintain a satisfactory level of ecosystem services." **Mode `specified`. Triangulated** across Fig
11 (habitat asymmetry) and Fig 10 (ground-maintainer stake), different offices. **Refuter:** a
demonstrated ability to subtract an unwanted actor from a habitat, making maintenance a solved state
rather than permanent work; or a maintained service with no seat and no declaration of stake.

### F6 — The ground settles each service; the count is a filter, not a witness

Fig 1 :23: "The ground enters as the only metric."
Fig 1 :27: "They are frame-internal. A frame cannot score itself with a quantity it also produces."
Fig 2 :29: "An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of
what any frame thinks."
Fig 2 :35: "Alignment is triangulation, not negotiation. A mirror cannot correct you."
Fig 9 :60: "Use filters for the budget and witnesses for the verdict. Never let a filter hand you a
verdict."
Fig 11 :73: "The habitat is not the ground. The habitat is where actors come from and it updates;
the ground is what settles, and it does not."

**Reading.** Whether a service is maintained is a question about measured flows (yields, water
quality, carbon flux, nutrient retention), each against its own anchor. A species count, an
ecosystem-service index or an assessment consensus is a filter that allocates attention and budget.
It is not a witness. In this simulated run the assessment bodies draw on one evidence pool, so their
agreement is the pool talking (Fig 2 :40). **Mode `general`. Triangulated** across Fig 1, Fig 2 and
Fig 9, three low-overlap figures. **Refuter:** an assessment agreement that predicts measured
service flows as well as direct measurement, across services and across habitats.

### F7 — The question is a union, so a single lever will leave a residue

Fig 9 :54: "And when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search
again."
Fig 9 :56: "A single lever clears the region where only one condition holds and leaves the
intersections standing, because the second condition still binds."
Fig 9 :32: "you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself"

**Reading.** Pollination, soil formation, nutrient cycling, water regulation and carbon storage are
overlapping conditions. A lever that preserves one (for example planting a single pollinator
resource) can leave the intersections failing. So a yes/no answer to the bundled question is not one
verdict but five verdicts kept separate, never averaged. **Mode `general`. Triangulated** across Fig
9 and Fig 1. **Refuter:** a single intervention that restores all measured services with no
categorically different residue left behind.

### F8 — What survives, and what does not

The defensible residue, with mode attached and verdicts kept separate:

| claim | mode | figure |
|---|---|---|
| A species count cannot decide a service outcome | `general` (structural) | Fig 11 :39-40, Fig 1 :19 |
| Actor valence is relative to the service | `specified` | Fig 11 :49-51 |
| Redundancy is real only within a functional group with the same bond | `specified` | Fig 12 :123, :117 |
| Cascades make failure third-order and delayed | `specified` | Fig 11 :52-53, :68-69 |

verdict, not a silence (Fig 13 :134; Fig 8 :39: "So a search that finds nothing is never a proof of
absence.").

## 4. Strains and re-census

- **Fig 11 is both the framework's habitat abstraction and the source domain of the metaphor, and
  the canonical article explicitly declines the ecological-niche mapping.** *Arrival of the Fittest:
  The Tether Architecture* has a section "Three mappings offered and declined," including: "The
  habitat as an ecological niche. A niche is defined by an organism's relation to its environment. A
  habitat here is defined by contact with a residual, and the residual moves so the same actors
  constitute a different habitat when the question changes." The article adds that the resemblance
  "is real and the definitions do not align, which is why the figure says enumerated, never composed
  rather than borrowing the ecological term outright." So applying Fig 11 to an ecology question is
  **not** a free borrowing of the ecology word. The figure's habitat is residual-relative and updates
  when the question changes; an ecological niche is not. Earlier reading ("low strain because it is
  the figure's home ground") is **narrowed and partly retracted**: the examples are ecological, but
  the mapping to the ecological term is one the framework refuses. The figure remains an internal
  structural constraint, not an independent ecological witness, and not a substitute for measuring
  an actual habitat. Recorded, not hidden.
- **Fig 12 bonds applied to species are a binding, not a derivation.** Calling a set of species a
  functional group or an isomer imports the ecology frame's vocabulary onto framework atoms. It
  needs a source gate and a shadow test (Fig 8 :50-51: "Convergent derivation and adopted import look
  identical in the contents. Only the record of where each came from separates them"). The world
  sources supply that corroboration secondarily; they do not decide.
- **Cardinale et al.'s "maintain a satisfactory level of ecosystem services" could be read as an
  unqualified yes.** Under Fig 1 :23 and the mode rule that is a `specified` claim awaiting its
  ground, not a verdict. The conflict was returned to the census and did not flip the verdict.
- **After re-census: three strains named (home-domain mapping, niche mapping, bond vocabulary),
  none hidden, no figure contradiction found.**

## 5. Pre-registered refuter (written before the conclusion)

A falsifier written before the run is a design instrument (Seat Map :287). SHAPE(ECO-0045) is
refuted if any of the following holds:

1. **Count invariance.** Across several habitats and three or more independent cascades, a scalar
   species count predicts every measured service with no per-service residual, with no introduction
   by any seat. Breaks F1, F2 and F6.
2. **Bond invariance.** Two arrangements with the same functional groups and bonds but different
   species identities produce the same measured service flows across all services, or a service
   survives the removal of its whole functional group with the bond intact. Breaks F3.
3. **Intact-habitat failure.** A service crosses its ground threshold while every enumerated actor,
   condition, relation and cascade in its habitat remains intact. Breaks F4.
4. **Solvable maintenance.** An unwanted actor can be subtracted from a habitat so that maintenance
   becomes a solved state, or a service is maintained with no seat and no declared stake. Breaks F5.
5. **Single lever.** One intervention restores every measured service with no categorically
   different residue left behind. Breaks F7.

If any fires, the shape is withdrawn. Until one is run, no part of this may be labelled `grounded`.

## 6. Recommendation

1. Restate the question in effect terms, per service and per slot, not as a biodiversity count.
2. Unbundle the five services before searching (Fig 9 :54). Keep their verdicts separate.
3. For each service, enumerate actors, conditions, relations and the cascade three steps out before
   measuring anything (Fig 11 :43-44, :52).
4. Test functional group versus isomer for each service, and record which binding came from which
   frame (Fig 12 :123, :117; Fig 8 :50-51).
5. Settle each service against its own anchor. Treat count, index and consensus as filters only.
6. Declare the seat, its stake and the fact that exclusion is unavailable. Budget for permanent work.

### Round-trip gap R_T (qualitative)

Sent up: the verbatim figure set (Fig 11 habitat, Fig 12 bonds, Fig 1 slot indexing). Abstracted:
"services depend on habitat structure, not count". Concretised: a claim that a particular service
will survive a particular percentage loss. **R_T is nonzero.** Dropped on the trip: which bonds,
which cascade depth, which seat, which ground per service. The shorthand is a lossy condensation,
not an identity (Fig 4 :30: "So take the round trip and measure the gap. Without it you are
navigating by dead reckoning: confident about a position nobody checked."). R_T is not measured
numerically, because no ground reading was taken; it is recorded as the enumerated set of dropped
constraints.
