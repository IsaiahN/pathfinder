# The Tether's answer: data and human judgment in company decisions

**Mode: `general` on the structural shape, `specified` on the per-domain predictions. Not `grounded`.** This is a shape and a route, not a result. Simulated derivation checked against the figures (`05_what_shape_means.md` §7), not a ground reading.

## 1. The residual it answers

A company commits an action `a` on a decision slot `s`. It predicts with a model, then observes what happened. `R_s = |Γ(b,a) − o'|`. When data are incomplete or biased, the model's residual on some slot persists and does not trend down. The question asks what should settle that gap: the model, the manager, or some balance of the two.

The framework refuses the framing. "Data versus judgment" is a union of conditions, not one ratio (Fig 9 :54: *"And when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again."*). Fig 9 :4-5: *"First: is there a fact here? Disagreements that do not shrink with effort, where each rule keeps working well on a different subset, are not one hard question but several well-formed ones. Split it rather than search."*

## 2. The shape

1. **Name the ground first.** Fig 2 :29-30: *"An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks. A measurement, an experiment, a counter, a market, a patient outcome. If it moves when you push on it, it is not an anchor."* Neither the model score nor the manager's override is the ground. What happened is the ground.

2. **Two separate ranges, not a blend.** Fig 2 :45: *"Frames range against the ground. They do not negotiate with it."* Fig 2 :35: *"Alignment is triangulation, not negotiation. A mirror cannot correct you."* Make the model and the human each read the same outcome separately and record both. A weighted mean is a convention that has decided an outcome, which the seat map forbids; it is not a verdict.

3. **Split by question type, and scope the human role.** Fig 2 :37-38: *"Human judgment is the right anchor for is this a good answer for a person, because the question is about people. It is only an instrument for is this code correct, and instruments can be wrong on axes they do not measure."* For a constitutively people-facing part of the decision, the affected people and those who know them are the legitimate anchor, but that judgment must be elicited before the model's framing is shown or it becomes a moving anchor (live strain S1 in `01` §5). For a factual prediction, human judgment is an instrument with blind spots, not a sovereign override.

4. **Keep the residual per slot.** Fig 1 :19: *"The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated."* Fig 1 :22: *"Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average."* A global accuracy number can be high while one segment is being systematically harmed. Aim review at slots with persistent, non-declining residual.

5. **Treat bad data as a channel fault, not as a verdict on either frame.** Fig 10 :24: *"the ground does not decay, the channel does"*. Fig 6 :32: *"An instrument is not built from a description. It is improved from a worse instrument already returning something."* Repair the instrument (a holdout, a field audit, a complaint log, an affected person's account) and change the habitat if needed. Replacing the model with intuition does not restore contact; it swaps one lossy channel for another. Fig 11 :40: *"An improvement that does not change contact changes nothing, however much it improves."*

6. **Use the human frame where it actually differs, with provenance.** Fig 8 :45: *"Two frames together reach what neither reaches alone."* Fig 2 :40: *"agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool"*. A manager who reads the same dashboard as the model is not a second frame. Buy independence in proportion to the cost of being wrong (Fig 2 :42: *"Buy independence in proportion to the cost of being wrong."*), and record which atom came from which frame (Fig 8 :50-52).

7. **Diagnose the chain before choosing a frame.** Fig 3 :19: *"Optimising a link that already works cannot rescue one that does not."* If perception is broken, a better model and a second reviewer both work above the break and cannot rescue it.

8. **Carry a method up, not a decision recording.** Fig 4 :22: *"Only methods cross up. Only head starts come down."* What scales from one decision to company policy is the checking method, with its round trip gap measured rather than assumed (Fig 4 :30).

## 3. What it explicitly does not say

- Not a percentage. No 80/20, no optimal weight, no universal split. A ratio is a budget filter, and Fig 9 :60 says *"Use filters for the budget and witnesses for the verdict. Never let a filter hand you a verdict."*
- Not "humans are better than models" and not "trust the data." Both frames are instruments on factual questions; both can be biased.
- Not that a committee vote is a verdict. Fig 7 :35: *"A room's agreement is variation, not a verdict. Selection between rooms is where the ground votes."*
- Not `grounded`. No decision outcome was measured in this run.

## 4. What would settle it - the ground

A source-discriminating record at the decision slot, not a better argument. The questions are:

- Did the outcome actually improve on the slot where the residual persisted, and by how much?
- Can the manager's contribution be separated from the model's in the record, so the surplus is attributable rather than assumed?
- Does an experimental holdout or field audit close the slot residual that the dashboard hides?
- Is the people-facing anchor elicited independently of the model's framing? If not, that slot has no legitimate anchor and the honest verdict is `gap`.

Standing falsifiers from `01` §6: a movable ground that still settles correctly; necessarily correlated frames; a global ratio that closes every slot residual; a model-only improvement that changes contact in an unchanged habitat; a non-human constitutive ground for a people-facing value question.

## 5. The honest stop

**Unreached, not unreachable.** The framework returns a shape, not a decision (Fig 9 :32: *"you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself"*). The company's metric set is a visible guess about what counts, not nature's own list; another habitat's minimum set need not be these metrics. The answer stops where the available instruments stop discriminating, and the lineage is recorded so the stop is checkable. Where human judgment is asked to be a moving anchor, the correct verdict is `gap`, not a softer balance.

## 6. One-paragraph version for outreach

> The Tether does not give a percentage split between data and judgment. It says that a company decision is several questions bundled into one: a factual prediction, a value question about people, and a legitimacy question. Each needs its own anchor, and the only legitimate anchor is what actually happened, which neither the model score nor the manager's override can move. The two frames should range against that ground separately, not be averaged, and the residual should be kept per decision rather than hidden in a dashboard average. Human judgment is the right anchor only where the question is constitutively about people, and is otherwise a fallible instrument whose value lies in being a genuinely different frame; if it reads the same data as the model, its agreement is worth nothing. When data are incomplete or biased, the fault is in the channel to the ground, so sharpen an instrument that is already returning something and change the habitat if needed rather than swapping the model for intuition. What this offers is the shape any defensible balance must have, the refuters written in advance, and the instruments that would close it. The final decision stays open until the ground settles it.
