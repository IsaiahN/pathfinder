## QUESTION

Decide, in effect terms, what the Tether says about the choice between specializing by comparative advantage and maintaining domestic capacity in strategically important industries. Option A: treat it as one scalar trade-off between cheapness and security and rank the two options. Option B: treat it as a union of conditions with different anchors, unbundle, and give a verdict per domain. What each leads to: A delivers a weighted convention with no instrument that can be violated, mislabels a scenario-relative designation as an intrinsic property, and spends the independence of the people asked to check it; B delivers a shape, separate verdicts, five written refuters and a named instrument that would close it.

## DOMAIN CENSUS

R by arity, symmetry and scale, then one line per domain with actors / currency / anchor, partition or union, and order by dependency.

- **R (arity / symmetry / scale).** The residual is a national allocation of scarce productive capacity under two descriptions: cheap access through exchange, and continuity when exchange is cut. Arity: at least two predicates that can both hold for the same industry, so a **union compound** (Fig 9 :57). Symmetry: comparative advantage is relational (a ratio of opportunity costs between producers), domestic capacity is absolute (a stock held here), so they cannot be placed on one axis without a lossy transform (Fig 12 :5). Scale: plant / firm, national industrial base, cross-border supply network, geopolitical habitat. Fig 1 :19: "The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated." It must be read per industry.
- **Domain 1, allocation.** Actors: countries, industries, firms, consumers. Currency: relative opportunity cost, prices, gains from trade. Anchor: a market, nameable. Question type: partition with a ground.
- **Domain 2, continuity.** Actors: designated strategic industries, supplier networks, potential adversaries, shocks. Currency: continuity under severance. Anchor: a named cut with a measurable cost, nameable only when a scenario is stated; otherwise this is not a domain yet. Question type: union compound (exposure to a cut AND exposure to other failures).
- **Domain 3, capability / habitat.** Actors: industrial base, skills, research, infrastructure, suppliers. Currency: what the base can compose and produce. Anchor: actual productive output when called on. Question type: habitat enumeration, not composition.
- **Domain 4, verification / policy frame.** Actors: analysts, modellers, readers. Currency: agreement and residuals. Anchor: the ground for D1 to D3, never agreement. In this simulated run all four domains draw on one evidence pool, so this domain's independence is zero.
- **Order by dependency (Fig 3).** Anchor "strategic" before using it; describe the residual before choosing a frame; refuse the blanket comparative-advantage term before minting (Formula step 2); keep the working market link; then price independence on the bond map (Fig 3 :19 "Optimising a link that already works cannot rescue one that does not.").

Strain test: Fig 13's six physical terms were considered as a mapping onto stocks and structures of an economy and **dropped as a strain**; its substrate is physical, so it does not govern this policy residual. The one live strain is the missing instrument for what counts as an independent supplier under a shared upstream source. It is recorded in `11_audit.md`, not hidden.

## VERDICT (from the figures)

The either/or framing is refused and the shape survives. The question is a union compound with two anchors. Comparative advantage governs the allocation domain as a budget filter with a market anchor. Continuity in the named intersection is bought as independence in proportion to the cost of a stated cut, where independence means low shared failure source. "Strategic" is a designation relative to the cut, not a property carried by an industry. Domestic capacity is an imported, settled, permanently maintained substitute habitat, and what must be measured is the supplier bond map under a cut because the same industries differently arranged are isomers with different behaviour. Verdicts are kept per domain and never averaged. Which industry, which cut and what cost remain a `gap` owed to a ground, not to more search. This is a `specified`, ungrounded, candidate result.

- **cost if wrong:** bounded and locatable. The packet states mode, keeps verdicts separate, and carries five pre-registered refuters, so an error is findable rather than invisible. The real cost is spending the independence of outside readers by sending a recording or a weighted score instead of a generator, which Fig 2 :42 prices as buying independence in proportion to the cost of being wrong, and which Fig 4 :30 names as dead reckoning when the round-trip gap is not measured.
- **undoable:** yes. No policy, primitive or industry list has to be withdrawn. The defective framing is removable: `07_the_tether_answer.md` and `08_expert_verification_packet.md` already replace the scalar ranking with per-domain verdicts, the intrinsic "strategic" label with a cut-relative designation, and the industry inventory with the bond map. A sent claim can be superseded by the corrected packet.
- **reversed by:** a ground reading, not agreement. A supplier graph enumerated past direct suppliers, combined with a named cut and a measured cost, would settle the continuity question; a demonstration that dependence was removed rather than substituted, that capability rose with unchanged habitat contact, or that identical atoms and bonds behaved differently under the same cut, would fire the corresponding pre-registered refuter; a proof that the predicates are mutually exclusive for every industry would restore a partition and a single ranking. A panel consensus, a weighted score, or a prestigious endorsement is not such an instrument. Until one of these fires, the verdict stands as `candidate`.
