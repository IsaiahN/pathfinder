# The Tether's answer on comparative advantage versus domestic capacity

**Mode: `specified` on the domain verdicts, `general` on the unbundling shape. Not `grounded`.** This is a shape and a route, not a policy result. Simulated derivation checked against the figures (`05_what_shape_means.md` section 7), not a ground reading. No country, industry or supplier graph was measured.

## 1. The residual it answers

A nation must commit scarce productive capacity under two descriptions: cheap access through exchange, and continuity when exchange is cut. The two predicates overlap on the same industries. The question as phrased bundles them into one either/or, which is why it has no single answer. Fig 9 :57: "What remains is smaller and it is also categorically different: a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either."

## 2. The shape, one loop per domain, verdicts kept separate

**Domain 1, allocation (the bulk of industries).**
The anchor is a market: prices, relative opportunity costs, observed trade flows. Fig 2 :29: "An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks. A measurement, an experiment, a counter, a market, a patient outcome." Comparative advantage is a legitimate **budget filter** for this domain: it says where the next unit of capital buys the most. But Fig 9 :60: "Use filters for the budget and witnesses for the verdict. Never let a filter hand you a verdict." **Verdict D1: specialize by comparative advantage for the ordinary set, as a filter, not as a universal doctrine.** `specified`, with a market anchor.

**Domain 2, continuity (the named intersection).**
The anchor is conditional: a specific cut scenario with a cost. Without one, this half is not yet a domain. "Strategic" is not a property carried by an industry; Fig 11 :49: "An actor's valence is relative to the residual, not a property it carries." Where a cut is named, the figure's pricing rule applies: Fig 2 :42 "Buy independence in proportion to the cost of being wrong." Independence means low shared failure source, so two suppliers on one upstream dependency are one supplier (Fig 2 :40). **Verdict D2: for the named intersection, buy independent capacity in proportion to the cost of a named cut, and refuse to buy it for everything.** `specified`, conditional on a named anchor.

**Domain 3, capability (what is actually being built).**
"Maintain domestic capacity" is not removing reliance on the world. Fig 11 :66: "Isolation is not removal of the habitat. It is substitution of one habitat for another." Fig 11 :67: "Move the residual and you must reproduce everything its goal required, and you inherit the new habitat's actors, which you did not choose." And Fig 11 :63: "Which is why a managed ecosystem is permanent work rather than a solved state." Capability is a property of agent-and-habitat, so counting plants is not measuring capacity (Fig 11 :39-40). A missing atom enters only by import from a differing closure (Fig 6 :21, :23). **Verdict D3: domestic capacity is an imported, settled, permanently maintained second habitat, not a stock of domestic firms.** `specified`.

**Cross-cutting measurement object (not a fifth domain).**
The bond map, not the industry list. Fig 12 :117: "Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour." **Verdict D4: evaluate the arrangement under a cut, not the inventory.** `specified`.

No averaging. The four verdicts are not weights in one score. A single "national interest" number would be a convention with no instrument that can be violated, which is exactly what Fig 1 :27 disqualifies: "A frame cannot score itself with a quantity it also produces."

## 3. What it explicitly does not say

- Not that free trade is always right, and not that protectionism is ever endorsed as a default.
- Not that any particular industry is strategic: that designation requires a named cut and a cost.
- Not that a country can subtract its way out of global supply chains. Exclusion is not available (Fig 11 :61).
- Not that a domestic champion firm equals domestic capability (Fig 11 :39-40).
- Not a warning that all specialization is dangerous. The market anchor is real and the allocation link already works (Fig 3 :19).
- Not a theory of everything about trade. No final room: the instrument is a supplier graph under a cut, and better instruments will move the answer.
- Not `grounded`. Nothing here has been measured.

## 4. What would settle it, and how to move it to grounded

A source-discriminating instrument, not a further argument:

1. **A supplier graph for the named industry**, with every tier, not just direct suppliers. Contact cascades; first-order enumeration misses the third-order dependency (Fig 11 :52-53).
2. **A named cut scenario with a cost.** An export ban, a blockade, a supplier failure, with the loss stated in advance. Without it, D2 has no anchor and the question is incomplete.
3. **An independence test.** Do the supposedly alternative suppliers share an upstream source, a port, a currency, a jurisdiction or a single point of failure? If they do, the independence was never bought.
4. **The bond-map comparison.** Two arrangements of the same atoms, run against the same cut, with measured continuity. This is the isomer test from `06_isomers_and_order.md`.
5. **The five refuters** in `01` section 4, any one of which withdraws the corresponding claim.

Standing falsifiers from the pre-registered set: prove a partition rather than a union; produce an intrinsic strategic industry; show dependence removed rather than substituted; show capability rising with unchanged habitat contact; show a missing atom added with no import; show identical atoms and bonds with different continuity.

## 5. The honest stop

**Unreached, not unreachable.** The continuity instrument is an ordinary supplier graph and a named cut, both of which already return readings, so this is unreachable-at-this-budget rather than underivable in principle (Fig 6 :27, Fig 8 :40-43). The remedy is INWARD, sharpening an instrument that already returns something, plus OUTWARD imports from ecology, finance and engineering that characterize the shape (Fig 9 :45 "Describing the residual is the expensive part"). The answer stops at the limit of what this simulated run can distinguish and records the lineage so the stop is checkable. "Indiscernibility is not identity."

## 6. One-paragraph version for outreach

> The Tether does not pick a side in the trade-versus-reshoring argument, because the question bundles two conditions that have different anchors. For the bulk of industries, comparative advantage is a real budget filter with a market behind it; it says where scarce capital buys the most, but it is not a witness for the continuity question. For the smaller set exposed to a named supply cut, the figure's rule is to buy independence in proportion to the cost of being wrong, and independence means genuinely separate failure sources, not a longer list of suppliers on the same upstream dependency. "Strategic" is a designation relative to the cut, not a property an industry carries. Maintaining domestic capacity is not subtracting reliance on the world: it substitutes a domestic habitat, requires importing whatever atoms the country does not hold, inherits actors you did not choose, and is permanent work rather than a solved state. What should be measured is the supplier bond map under a stated cut, because the same industries arranged differently behave differently. The framework returns that shape, five written refuters and the instrument that would close it. It does not name a winning country or industry, and nothing here is a measurement.
