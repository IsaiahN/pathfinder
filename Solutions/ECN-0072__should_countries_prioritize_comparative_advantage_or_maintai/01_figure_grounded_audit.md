## 0. The claim under audit

> Should countries prioritize comparative advantage or maintain domestic capacity in strategically important industries?

Restated as an effect, with each option tied to what it leads to:

- **Option A, specialize by comparative advantage.** Devote domestic resources to the activities with the lowest relative opportunity cost; obtain everything else through trade. Leads to lower immediate cost and higher observed consumption possibilities, and to dependence on continued access to foreign suppliers.
- **Option B, retain domestic capacity in strategic industries.** Keep a domestic productive base in named industries even where local cost exceeds the world price. Leads to a capability that survives severance of external supply, at a standing cost and with exposure to domestic supply failures.

**What an answer would look like, written down before the run as an ordinary term:** it would name the anchor that settles each option; it would say whether the two options are a partition (one scalar trade-off) or a union of two conditions with different grounds; it would give a verdict per domain and refuse to average them; it would state a refuter whose firing withdraws the claim. This is the `Direction` discipline in `THE_FORMULA.md`: the shape a frame seeks is not a term it holds, so it must be bound explicitly before the run or no check can touch it.

**Not claimed at the outset:** that either option is always right, that any named industry is strategic, or that a single scalar objective exists over which the two can be traded. Those are results to be derived or refused, not premises.

## 1. Residual and domain census

### 1.1 R by arity, symmetry and scale

- **Residual R (in effect terms).** A national allocation of scarce productive capacity is being decided under two descriptions of what the capacity is for: cheap access through exchange, and continuity of supply when exchange is cut. `R` is the gap between what a policy frame predicts each commitment buys and what the ground returns when a shock arrives. Per Fig 1 :19, "The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated." It must be read per industry, not as one national average: a country can be globally efficient and still have one live slot, its semiconductor or pharmaceutical or energy slot, that carries the whole residual.
- **Arity.** At least two conditions: an allocation condition (where should capacity go?) and a continuity condition (what survives a cut?). These are not two values of one variable; they are two predicates that can both hold at once for the same industry. Fig 9 :57: "What remains is smaller and it is also categorically different: a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either."
- **Symmetry.** Comparative advantage is a **relational** quantity: it is a ratio of opportunity costs between two producers, and it has no value for a single closed economy. Domestic capacity is an **absolute possession**: a stock held here. The two are not the same kind of quantity and cannot be placed on one axis without a lossy transform. Fig 12 :5: "A set of ingredients is not one. The bond is the rest of the information, and it is the half nothing was recording."
- **Scale.** The individual plant or firm; the national industrial base (a population of firms, with a membrane between the one and the many, Fig 4); the cross-border supply network; and the geopolitical habitat that can sever it. A verdict at one scale is not a verdict at the next.

### 1.2 One line per domain: actors, currency, anchor

A domain is admitted only if it has a nameable anchor. Following the rule that no nameable anchor means it is not a domain yet, the second candidate is admitted **conditionally** on naming a severance scenario.

### 1.3 Partition or union

The question as written is a **union compound**, not a partition. Efficiency and continuity are two predicates that overlap on the same industry. A single lever, for example "always specialize" or "always reshore", clears the region where only one condition holds and leaves the intersection standing. Fig 9 :54: "And when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again." A scalar "efficiency versus security" slider is the blend that Fig 8 warns against: it produces vocabulary, not an atom.

### 1.4 Order by dependency (Fig 3)

1. **Can "strategic" be anchored?** Before anything else, name the cut scenario and its cost. Fig 3 :27: "A link with no instrument is not a link that works. It is a link nobody has looked at." If no cut can be named, D2 is not yet a domain and Option B is not yet an answer.
2. **Describe the residual before choosing a frame.** Fig 9 :27-28: "describe it before you go looking, or any frame you pick will seem to fit." Otherwise any industrial policy will seem to fit.
3. **Refuse before mint (Formula step 2).** The blanket proposition "comparative advantage always wins" is a held term whose residual persists whenever a real cut occurs. It owes a refusal, not a new theory.
4. **Settle D1's allocation before pricing D2's insurance.** Fig 3 :19: "Optimising a link that already works cannot rescue one that does not." The market anchor is the working link; use it, do not rebuild it.
5. **Only then buy independence.** Fig 2 :42: "Buy independence in proportion to the cost of being wrong." The price is paid against a named cost, not against a mood.

## 3. Findings

### F1: The question is a union compound and must be unbundled (decisive for framing)

Fig 9 :54: "And when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again."

Fig 9 :55-56, on what a single lever does: "A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones."

Fig 9 :57: "What remains is smaller and it is also categorically different: a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either."

The two options overlap on the same industry. An industry can be both comparatively advantageous to import and strategically exposed to a cut. Answering "which one" as if they were mutually exclusive options throws away the intersection, which is exactly the part that matters. A country does not choose between cheapness and continuity in the abstract; it runs an allocation policy over the ordinary set and a continuity policy over the intersection, and the second policy has its own ground.

**Refuter:** show that the two predicates are mutually exclusive for every industry, that is, whenever the opportunity-cost case for importing holds, no severance scenario with a nonzero cost can be named. Then it is a partition and one scalar ranking is legitimate. This refuter was written before the conclusion.

### F2: The two halves have different anchors, so they cannot be averaged

Fig 2 :29: "An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks. A measurement, an experiment, a counter, a market, a patient outcome."

D1 has a nameable anchor: the market. Price and observed trade flows do not move because a policy advocate thinks differently. D2 has a nameable anchor only when a specific cut is stated: an export ban, a blockade, a war, a supplier failure with a measurable cost. If "strategic" is asserted without such a scenario, it fails the anchor test and is not yet a domain. Fig 2 :33 adds: "The anchor must not update." A security rating that is revised upward whenever protection is politically convenient is a frame, not an anchor.

Consequence: averaging the two into a single "national interest" score is a category error. Averaging two quantities with different anchors is how a live signal disappears into a mean (Fig 1 :22: "Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average.").

**Refuter:** produce one anchor that genuinely settles both the allocation and the continuity question without reducing one to the other, such that a single well-defined scalar verdict exists. Until then, verdicts stay separate.

### F3: "Strategic" is valence relative to a residual, not a property of an industry

Fig 11 :49: "An actor's valence is relative to the residual, not a property it carries."

Fig 11 :51: "No standing list of good actors survives a change of residual."

An industry is not strategic in itself. It is strategic relative to a named cut scenario and the cost that follows. Change the scenario, from a naval blockade to a pandemic to a domestic grid failure, and the list of strategic industries changes. This is why a permanent statutory list tends to outlive the reason for it: it freezes one residual's valence into a standing property. The Operators table's comparison bond is a threshold relation between two quantities, which is what a designation like "strategic" is: a threshold crossed under a stated condition, not a natural kind of industry.

**Refuter:** exhibit an industry that is strategic under every possible cut scenario and every change of residual, independent of any frame's designation. That would make it an intrinsic property and break this finding.

### F4: Domestic capacity is not removal of dependence; it substitutes a habitat

Fig 11 :66: "Isolation is not removal of the habitat. It is substitution of one habitat for another."

Fig 11 :67: "Move the residual and you must reproduce everything its goal required, and you inherit the new habitat's actors, which you did not choose."

This is the strongest figure-grounded claim in the answer. "Maintain domestic capacity" sounds like subtraction: stop relying on foreign suppliers. The figures say exclusion is not available. You cannot subtract the global habitat; contact is a fact about the world. What policy can do is introduce a second, domestic habitat and hope the cascade reproduces what the goal required. Two failures follow, both named in the figure at Fig 11 :29-30: "what you failed to reproduce, and what you brought with you." A domestic chip industry that lacks the upstream chemical suppliers, skilled labour and equipment ecology is a substituted habitat with the required actors missing. The domestic version also inherits actors that were not chosen, including domestic monopolies and political capture.

Fig 11 :61: "The seat's power is asymmetric. Introduction is available; exclusion is not."

Fig 11 :63: "Which is why a managed ecosystem is permanent work rather than a solved state."

So domestic capacity is permanent work, not a solved state, and the relevant cost is the standing cost of maintaining a substituted habitat, not just the one-off construction cost.

**Refuter:** show a case where dependence on the external habitat was genuinely removed rather than substituted, with no unchosen actors inherited and no required actor left unreproduced, and where the situation then stayed solved without continuing maintenance. That would break Fig 11's asymmetry and isolation claims.

### F5: Capability is agent-and-habitat, so a list of strategic firms is not capacity

Fig 11 :39: "So the answerable question is not what intelligence is, but what makes a system capable. And capability is a property of agent-and-habitat, never of the agent alone."

Fig 11 :40: "A system cannot be made more capable by improving the agent in isolation. An improvement that does not change contact changes nothing, however much it improves."

A policy that counts domestic capacity by counting plants, or by counting a national champion firm, grades the agent and ignores the habitat. If the firm's contacts with suppliers, skills, research and logistics do not change, the improvement does not change capability, however much it improves the firm's balance sheet. This also explains why a subsidy that produces a flagship plant without a supplier ecology can fail the goal while succeeding on every counted metric. The measurement is the problem, not the effort.

**Refuter:** exhibit a case where national capability in a named industry rose, and the ground paid for it, while the firm's or plant's pattern of contact with its industrial habitat was unchanged. That would refute the capability claim directly.

### F6: A capability absent from the national closure cannot be composed into existence

Fig 6 :21: "MINT composes inside the closure and can never add an atom."

Fig 6 :23: "No frame escapes its own closure. The bound moves only on contact with a frame whose closure differs."

If the skills, equipment or process knowledge for an industry are not atoms in the national library, domestic policy can rearrange what exists and cannot manufacture the missing atom by decree. The only operator that moves the wall is import from a frame whose closure differs: foreign machinery, foreign engineers, licensed process knowledge, migration of skilled people. This is a direct figure-level reason why the pure either/or framing fails. The coherent form of Option B is not isolation; it is a deliberate import of the missing atoms followed by their settlement in a domestic habitat. Fig 6 :22: "NOVEL means novel relative to atoms, not to the world."

**Refuter:** show a national capability that was added to the closure with no import, no contact with a differing closure, and no new atom, purely by recombination of atoms already held. If such a case pays at the ground, this finding fails.

### F7: Efficiency is a filter for the budget, never a witness for the verdict

Fig 9 :60: "Use filters for the budget and witnesses for the verdict. Never let a filter hand you a verdict."

Comparative advantage is an excellent filter. It says where the next unit of scarce capital buys the most consumption per unit of opportunity cost. It is not a witness for the continuity question, because the price that makes it a good filter is precisely a reading of the current, uncut world. Using it to decide the strategic question is letting a filter hand you a verdict.

Fig 2 :42: "Buy independence in proportion to the cost of being wrong."

That is the figure's pricing rule for the continuity domain. Independence, meaning suppliers that do not draw on the same failure source, is bought in proportion to the cost of being wrong. It is not bought for everything, and it is not free. Crucially, the quantity being bought is independence, so two suppliers on the same submarine cable, or two domestic plants drawing on the same single-source upstream chemical, are one supplier wearing two names. Fig 2 :40: "agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool."

**Refuter:** show a case where a policy maximized measured opportunity-cost efficiency and also maximized measured resilience against a named cut, with the two readings derived from independent instruments, so that no filter/witness distinction is needed. That would collapse the finding.

### F8: The arrangement, not the list of industries, is the object of policy

Fig 12 :5: "A set of ingredients is not one. The bond is the rest of the information, and it is the half nothing was recording."

Fig 12 :94: "The arrangement is the substance."

Fig 12 :117: "Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."

A national inventory listing "we have a chip plant, a fab, a packaging line" records ingredients and not arrangement. Two countries with the same list of industries can have entirely different continuity properties depending on which suppliers bond to which, whether the bonds are single-source or parallel, and whether a failure in one propagates. That is Fig 12's isomer: same atoms, different bond, different substance. Policy that targets a list rather than the bond map optimizes the formula and misses the substance.

**Refuter:** exhibit two national industry sets with identical ingredients and identical bond topology whose behaviour under the same named cut is decisively different for reasons not captured by any further bond. That would mean the bond does not carry the information Fig 12 says it carries.

### F9: Consensus and prestige are not instruments here

Fig 7 :35: "A room's agreement is variation, not a verdict. Selection between rooms is where the ground votes."

Fig 2 :35: "Alignment is triangulation, not negotiation. A mirror cannot correct you."

A panel of trade-policy experts who agree that a country "should" reshore has produced a convention, not a measurement. The ground here is a supplier map and a cut scenario, or an actual disruption. Fig 2 :42 prices the correct response: buy independence in proportion to the cost of being wrong, which means buy independent witnesses with low overlap, not a large agreeing room.

Fig 1 :27: "A frame cannot score itself with a quantity it also produces." An industrial-policy frame that grades itself by the number of domestic firms it created is scoring itself with a quantity it also produces. The measurement must come from outside that frame: continuity under a named cut.

**Refuter:** show an expert consensus that predicted continuity under an actual cut better than the supplier map and shock instrument did, with independent evidence. Then the room would have been a witness rather than a convention.

## 4. Pre-registered refuter

Required before any outreach: a falsifier written before the run is a design instrument. The shape claim is refuted if any of the following holds:

1. A partition is proved: the two predicates are mutually exclusive for every industry, so one scalar ranking is well defined (breaks F1 and F2).
2. A single anchor is produced that genuinely settles allocation and continuity without reducing one to the other (breaks F2).
3. An industry is shown to be strategic intrinsically, under every cut scenario and every change of residual (breaks F3).
4. A case is shown where external dependence was removed rather than substituted, with no unchosen actors inherited, no required actor missing, and no continuing maintenance needed (breaks F4).
5. National capability rose while the relevant agents' contact with the industrial habitat stayed unchanged and the ground paid for it (breaks F5).
6. A capability absent from the national closure was added with no import and no contact with a differing closure (breaks F6).
7. Two countries with identical ingredient lists and identical bond topology behaved decisively differently under the same cut for reasons no bond captures (breaks F8).

If any fires, the corresponding finding is withdrawn and the synthesis in `07` and `09` must be rewritten. Until one is run, nothing may be labelled `grounded`.

## 5. Recommendation

1. **Reject the either/or form as written** (F1, F2). Send the unbundling, not a ranking.
2. **Bind "strategic" to a named cut and a cost before using it** (F3, F4). If a proponent cannot name the cut, the domestic-capacity half has no anchor and the question is incomplete.
3. **Price independence, do not demand autarky** (F6, F7). The coherent policy is comparative advantage for the ordinary set plus import-enabled, habitat-aware capacity for the named intersection. Efficiency is the budget filter; continuity is a separate witness with its own ground.
4. **Measure the bond map, not the industry list** (F8). The verifiable object is a supplier graph under a stated cut.
5. **Lead outreach with the residual and its refuter** (F9, Fig 10 :52 "Bring a characterised residual, never the source"). Links only after a verdict on stage 1 is recorded.
6. **State mode on every substantive claim** (THE_FORMULA mode rule). `specified` here means "this is what would have to be measured", not "this has been measured".

### SEARCHED / INWARD / OUTWARD, kept distinct

- **SEARCHED.** Is a figure line already in the closure that closes the residual? Yes for the framing, the anchor test, the habitat laws and the filter/witness rule (F1, F2, F4, F5, F7). This was asked first and is cheapest.
- **INWARD.** Extend the instrument from one already returning something. Here: a supplier graph and a named cut scenario already return a reading of continuity; sharpen those, do not invent a new "national interest" sensor. Unreachable-at-budget, not underivable.
- **OUTWARD.** Import a frame whose closure differs and can predict the residual's shape: ecology for resilience, finance for correlated failure, engineering for redundancy and common-cause failure. These are characterizations in effect terms, with a source gate and a shadow test, recorded in `09_watson_answer.md` and `10_source_ledger.csv`. They corroborate; they do not decide.

### Updated verdict

The shape survives and the either/or framing does not. The question is a union compound with two anchors. Comparative advantage governs the allocation domain as a budget filter with a market anchor. Continuity in the intersection is bought as independence in proportion to the cost of a named cut, requires import of any missing atoms, is a substituted habitat under permanent maintenance, and must be measured on the bond map rather than an industry list. Verdicts are kept per domain and never averaged. Strains after re-census: one (Fig 13), named and dropped; one live type question (what exactly counts as an independent supplier under a shared upstream source), recorded in `11_audit.md`, not hidden.

No part of this is `grounded`. The instrument that would close it is a supplier graph plus a named cut with a measured cost, not another argument.
