# Expert verification packet: the Tether on trade and strategic capacity

**What this is:** a claim in a form a trade-policy researcher can check. Mode, derivation and refuters are stated before the conclusion, so a reader knows what to look for and what would settle it.

**Mode: `specified` on the sub-claims, `general` on the unbundling shape. Not `grounded`.** No supplier graph, cut scenario or cost was measured in this run.

## 1. The claim, in one paragraph

The question is a union compound, not a single trade-off. The allocation condition has a market anchor and is governed by comparative advantage as a budget filter. The continuity condition has an anchor only when a supply cut is named with a cost; "strategic" is a designation relative to that cut, not a property carried by an industry. For the named intersection, independence is bought in proportion to the cost of being wrong, and independence means low shared failure source. Maintaining domestic capacity is not removal of dependence but substitution of a domestic habitat, which must reproduce what the goal required, inherits unchosen actors, needs imported atoms for anything not already held, and is permanent work. The measurable object is the supplier bond map, because the same industries arranged differently behave differently under the same cut. Verdicts are kept per domain and never averaged.

## 2. Three checkable sub-claims

### C1: the union / partition and the anchor test (framework-internal and conceptual)

- **Statement:** the two predicates overlap on the same industry, so a single scalar ranking is illegitimate; the continuity predicate has no ground until a cut and a cost are named.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 9 :54 "And when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again." Fig 9 :57 "What remains is smaller and it is also categorically different: a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either." Fig 2 :29 "An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks. A measurement, an experiment, a counter, a market, a patient outcome."
- **What to check:** can the reader exhibit an industry for which a strong comparative-advantage case for importing coexists with a named, costly supply cut? If yes, the predicates overlap and the union reading stands. If no for every industry, it is a partition and a single ranking is legitimate.
- **Refuter:** a proof that the two predicates are mutually exclusive for every industry, or a single anchor that settles both without reducing one to the other.

### C2: domestic capacity is habitat substitution under asymmetry (framework-internal, checkable against industrial policy cases)

- **Statement:** isolation does not remove a habitat, it substitutes one; the domestic version must reproduce what the goal required, inherits actors not chosen, and is never a solved state. A firm improved in isolation does not improve national capability if its contacts are unchanged.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 11 :66 "Isolation is not removal of the habitat. It is substitution of one habitat for another." Fig 11 :67 "Move the residual and you must reproduce everything its goal required, and you inherit the new habitat's actors, which you did not choose." Fig 11 :61 "The seat's power is asymmetric. Introduction is available; exclusion is not." Fig 11 :39 "And capability is a property of agent-and-habitat, never of the agent alone."
- **What to check:** pick a reshoring or domestic-capacity episode the reader knows. Was external dependence removed or substituted? What required actor was left unreproduced? Which unchosen domestic actors were inherited? Did the plant's pattern of contact with suppliers, skills and research actually change, or only its output?
- **Refuter:** a case where dependence was removed rather than substituted, with nothing required left unreproduced, no unchosen actors inherited, no continuing maintenance needed, and capability rising while contacts stayed unchanged, with the ground paying for it.

### C3: the bond map and the price of independence (framework-internal plus a cross-field check)

- **Statement:** the object of measurement is the arrangement of capabilities, not the inventory; independence is a function of shared failure sources, so more suppliers are not automatically more independence.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 12 :117 "Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour." Fig 12 :5 "A set of ingredients is not one. The bond is the rest of the information, and it is the half nothing was recording." Fig 2 :42 "Buy independence in proportion to the cost of being wrong." Fig 2 :40 "disqualifying for a verdict: agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool."
- **What to check:** for a named industry, draw two supplier graphs with the same set of suppliers but different bonds. Do they have different continuity under the same cut? Then check whether the supposedly alternative suppliers share an upstream source, a port or a jurisdiction. Engineering calls this common-mode failure; finance calls it correlation rising under stress (both sources read and recorded in `10_source_ledger.csv`). These are secondary corroborations, not the authority.
- **Refuter:** two arrangements with identical atoms and identical bond topology whose continuity differs decisively for reasons no bond captures; or a case where counting suppliers with a shared upstream source genuinely purchased independence under a cut.

## 3. What is explicitly not claimed

- Not that free trade is always right, and not that protectionism is ever the default.
- Not that any named industry is strategic, or that a permanent statutory list tracks the current residual.
- Not that a country can subtract itself out of global supply chains.
- Not that a domestic champion firm is domestic capability.
- Not grounded. No supplier graph, cut, cost, or country was measured here.
- Not a single ranking, a weighted national-interest score, or a claim that the two verdicts can be averaged.
- Not that the analogues decide anything. They corroborate a shape from low-overlap fields and are recorded with overlap and field in the source ledger.

## 5. What would settle it

1. Name the industry and the cut. State the cost of the cut in advance.
2. Enumerate the supplier graph outward until the cascade stops mattering, not just direct suppliers (contact cascades, Fig 11 :52-53).
3. Apply the independence test: which alternatives share a failure source?
4. Draw the two isomer graphs and run the same cut on each.
5. Fire any of the five refuters in `01` section 4. A failure is more useful than an endorsement.

## 6. What an expert can check in twenty minutes

1. Read Fig 9 :54, :57, Fig 2 :29, :42 and Fig 11 :66, :67, :39. Six short passages.
2. Answer C1 for one industry they know: do the two predicates coexist, and can the cut be named with a cost?
3. Answer C2 for one policy episode they know: was it substitution or removal, and what was left unreproduced?
4. Name one instrument that would distinguish a real independent supplier from a shared-source supplier, or say why none is possible.
5. Send the refutation. A failure is more useful than an endorsement.
