## QUESTION

Decide, in effect terms, what the Tether says about how institutions such as schools, markets, states and religious organizations reshape habitual ways of thinking. Option A, the agent-only reading: institutions work by changing beliefs and discipline inside the individual. Option B, the contact-and-habitat reading: institutions work by changing the field of contact, supplying atoms from a closure that differs and sending head starts down a membrane. What each leads to: A predicts that improving the person in isolation changes the habit and that agreement inside an institution is evidence; B predicts that the habitat and the crossing are the lever, that agreement about shared meaning is the product, and that the institution-to-habit mapping stays open until a ground settles it.

## DOMAIN CENSUS

R by arity, symmetry and scale, then one line per domain with actors / currency / anchor, partition or union, and order by dependency.

- **R (the residual).** The gap between habitual thinking without sustained institutional contact and habitual thinking after it. Arity: a union of four institution types and three mechanisms, so a compound, not one question (Fig 9 :57: *"a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either"*). Symmetry: asymmetric, since introduction into the habitat is available and exclusion is not (Fig 11 :61). Scale: individual loop, population membrane, intergenerational head start.
- **Domain 1, individual cognitive frame.** Actors: beliefs, weighting `w`, object slots `s`, candidate terms φ. Currency: prediction against observation, residual R per slot. Anchor: the ground, which does not update. Partition boundary: which compositions cross the kinds inside the frame.
- **Domain 2, institution as maintained habitat.** Actors: the seat, rules, curriculum, prices, sanctions, ritual. Currency: compliance, grades, money, legitimation. Anchor: partial. Price can clear a market transaction, but most institutional rules move when pushed, so they are conventions or instruments, not grounds. Fig 10 :47: *"a convention that decides an outcome has stopped being one"*.
- **Domain 3, membrane between scales.** Actors: methods carried up, head starts sent down. Currency: compression and cost of re-derivation. Anchor: the round-trip gap `R_T`, which is measured, not assumed zero.
- **Domain 4, verification and research frame.** Actors: researchers, instruments, natural experiments. Currency: evidence versus agreement. Anchor: the ground, never agreement. In this simulated run all four domains draw on one evidence pool, so independence is zero and any agreement is internal consistency, not corroboration.
- **Order by dependency.** Perception first, then vocabulary, then objective, then planning and carry (Fig 3). Refute before mint (Formula step 2). Describe the residual before choosing a frame (Fig 8 :53: *"Residual first, frame second."*).

## VERDICT (from the figures)

The shape survives; the named mappings do not. An institution is a maintained habitat with a seat, so it works by changing contact. A person's frame moves when it meets a closure that differs, since internal composition cannot add an atom. What crosses the membrane is a method and a head start, never a recording, so each recipient remakes it under their own conditions. Institutional agreement about shared meaning is the product, not a verdict about the world. Effects are per person and per habit. A habit reads as a catalyst, with an installed rule as a functional group. Which institution produces which habit is an honest `gap`: the framework returns a shape, and a name would require a discriminating instrument and a provenanced import. Verdict status: `general` on the shape, `specified` on the mappings, `candidate` and ungrounded overall.

- **cost if wrong:** if the contact claim is wrong, effort spent changing habitats, curricula and incentives is aimed at the wrong lever, and the real mechanism is inside the agent. If the agreement valence is swapped, a convention is mislabelled as a verdict and the disqualifying correlation is manufactured (Fig 2 :40). The cost is bounded because the claim is a shape with written refuters, not a policy prescription. Fig 2 :42: *"Buy independence in proportion to the cost of being wrong."*
- **undoable:** yes. The four domains have separate loops, so a finding in one can be withdrawn without touching the others. No primitive has to be withdrawn, and no named binding is asserted that would need retracting.
- **reversed by:** a ground reading, not agreement. A contact-held-constant experiment that still changes the habit breaks the habitat claim; a closure expansion with no import breaks Fig 6 :23; a zero round-trip gap breaks Fig 4 :34; an average that beats a per-slot reading breaks Fig 1 :19; contrasting rooms ranged against an independent ground would close the mapping gap. Prestige or consensus is not such an instrument.
