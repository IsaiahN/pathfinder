## DERIVATION

### Q1 — Union or partition?

- Fig 9 :57: *"a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either"*.
- Fig 9 :54: *"when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again"*.
- Fig 13 :136: *"the question is not one question and must be split before it is searched again"*.
- The four institution types have different currencies: grades, money, law, ritual. The mechanisms differ: contact change, import, restabilization.
- ⇒ **Union**, with overlapping conditions. A single lever that clears the region where only one condition holds leaves the intersections standing, because the second condition still binds.
- *Library of Babel*: *"Perhaps your problem was a bundle or conflation of several problems masquerading as one?"* Triangulated, Fig 9 with Fig 13 and the essay. Low overlap between the structural figure and the search essay.

**Falsifier, written before the run:** if one mechanism, measured with one instrument, accounts for the habit change in all four institution types and in their intersections, the union reading fails and the partition reading stands instead.

### Q2 — What is the dependency order, and where is the break likely?

- Fig 3 :20: *"The chain is ordered by dependency, not by importance. Each link consumes what the one above produces, so effort spent above the break arrives at a step that cannot use it."*
- Fig 3 :19: *"Optimising a link that already works cannot rescue one that does not."*
- Fig 3 :27: *"A link with no instrument is not a link that works. It is a link nobody has looked at."*
- Fig 3 :25: *"The link between them, having terms for what a situation offers and being able to say what would count as progress, has neither, so it attracts the least attention and the fewest instruments."*
- The order for this question: perception (what the institution makes perceivable), vocabulary (what a situation affords), objective (what counts as progress), planning, carry.
- ⇒ The probable break is at link 2 or 3, vocabulary or objective, because that is where instruments are thinnest. Effort spent on teaching technique (link 4) cannot rescue a missing objective (link 3).
- **Falsifier:** if a link below the suspected break returns a clean, grounded reading while the vocabulary and objective links remain unmeasured, the break is not where this derivation places it.

### Q3 — Is a habit a catalyst, a functional group, or an isomer?

- Fig 12 :119: *"catalyst. A part that survives its own use. Spent and regenerating parts behave differently across uses, and nothing records which a term is."*
- Fig 12 :123: *"functional group. A substructure whose presence fixes the outcome. The group says what kind; the qualifier says which member."*
- Fig 12 :117: *"isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."*
- A habit is read as a **catalyst**: a part the frame composes over repeatedly without paying to re-derive it. The habit survives its own use.
- An institutional rule installed in the habitat is read as a **functional group**: a substructure whose presence fixes which member of a class of behaviours gets produced. This is what allows rule-following without adopting the underlying assumption, the topic of source question COG-0087. The qualifier fixes the member; the group fixes the kind.
- Different institutions over the same held atoms can be **isomers**: same atoms, different arrangement, different substance. This is a shape, not a claim about which structure belongs to which institution.
- **Mode:** `specified`. These are bindings from institutional and psychological vocabulary onto figure terms. They are not derived from the six-term set. **Status:** `single-source` for the catalyst and functional-group mapping (Fig 12 only).

**Falsifier:** show that a habit changes while the part that survives its own use is unchanged, or that an institution's installed substructure can be removed while the habit persists unaltered. Either fires against F6 in `01_figure_grounded_audit.md`.

### Q4 — What is the order of the crossing?

- Fig 4 :22: *"Only methods cross up. Only head starts come down."*
- Fig 4 :34: *"A seed carries no small tree: it carries what will grow one, and the conditions it lands in decide what it becomes."*
- Fig 12 :104: *"A settled molecule becomes an atom for whatever composes over it."*
- Fig 6 :23: *"No frame escapes its own closure. The bound moves only on contact with a frame whose closure differs."*
- Order: the institution's method is compressed and carried up (abstraction), it travels as a head start, it comes down into a specific person who remakes it (concretisation), and the person's settled result can become an atom for the next crossing.
- Fig 4 :30: *"So take the round trip and measure the gap. Without it you are navigating by dead reckoning: confident about a position nobody checked."*
- This is not a symmetric exchange. Introduction is available and exclusion is not (Fig 11 :61). The person can accept a head start and cannot subtract the habitat they are in.

**Falsifier:** exhibit a round trip in which what comes down matches what went up exactly, across changed landing conditions, so `R_T` is zero.

## CONCLUSION

The question is a **union** of institution types and mechanisms, not one problem. Its dependency order puts vocabulary and objective before planning, and those are the uninstrumented links. The word "habitual" receives a `specified` reading as a catalyst with an installed functional group, and the institutions remain potential isomers over a shared atom set rather than named mappings. None of this requires a seventh primitive, an import that moves the wall, or a claim about which institution produces which habit.
