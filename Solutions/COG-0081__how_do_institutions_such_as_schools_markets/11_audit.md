## Round trip gap `R_T`

The Symbols table defines `R_T` as the gap between what was sent up and what came back down, `gap(x, (T_E ∘ T_A)(x))`. Fig 4 :30 requires it to be measured rather than assumed zero.

1. **Sent up (x):** the verbatim figure set, chiefly Fig 11 :39-40 (contact and habitat), Fig 6 :21-23 (closure and import), Fig 4 :22 and :34 (head start and remaking), Fig 2 :40-41 (valence), Fig 1 :19 (per slot).
2. **Abstraction (T_A):** the shorthand carried in `07_the_tether_answer.md`, "institutions change contact, import atoms, send head starts, agreement is a convention, effects are per slot".
3. **Concretisation (T_E):** a concrete institutional claim, for example "schooling changes a child's sense of what a question affords by supplying the teacher and the assessment as habitat actors".

**`R_T` is nonzero.** Three things the shorthand holds do not survive the trip back down:

- the **instrument that discriminates sources**. The shorthand says contact changes the habit; it does not say how to tell a habitat effect from a selected prior that yields the same reading. The *Hard Problem* essay's metamerism point is exactly this.
- the **valenced use of agreement**. The shorthand holds "agreement is the product"; a concrete study can easily slide from shared meaning into a world verdict, and Fig 2 :36 is needed to keep them apart.
- the **asymmetry of introduction**. The shorthand says the crossing is directional; a concrete design must specify what the seat can add and what it cannot subtract (Fig 11 :61), and Most institutional studies do not record this.

One thing survives cleanly: a structure of contact that can import a method and a head start, with the recipient remaking it. That is the residue worth sending. `R_T` was **not** measured numerically because no ground reading exists; it is recorded qualitatively as the enumerated set of dropped constraints. Treating "the formula is the condensed version of the metalaws" as identity would set `R_T` to zero and would be the dead-reckoning error Fig 4 :30 names.
