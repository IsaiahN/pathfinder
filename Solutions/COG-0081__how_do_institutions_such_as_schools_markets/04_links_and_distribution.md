## Distribution: offer, do not push

- **Fig 10 :52** - *"Bring a characterised residual, never the source."* Send the shape and its refuter first. A recipient who has absorbed the whole framework before being asked for a verdict has joined the frame being checked.
- **Fig 2 :35** - *"Alignment is triangulation, not negotiation. A mirror cannot correct you."* Do not ask for agreement.
- **Fig 9 :27** - *"describe it before you go looking, or any frame you pick will seem to fit"* Ask what they would expect to see if the shape claim were true before offering any theory.
- **Fig 2 :41** - *"agreement about a shared meaning is the product, not a corruption of it"* A professional society's listserv creates shared meaning. That is fine. It is not a verdict about the world.
- **Seat Map :163** - *"High correlation is disqualifying for a verdict and efficient for a convention."* A send selected for people likely to agree produces a convention, not corroboration.
- **Fig 4 :22** - *"Only methods cross up. Only head starts come down."* The formula is a generator, so offering it later does not give away an answer.

## The two-stage offer

1. **Stage 1.** Residual, five refuters, per-domain verdicts. Ask what the recipient would count as a failure. Record their verdict before any theory is shown.
2. **Stage 2, opt-in only.** If they are curious, offer the three public framework links. The figures, not the summary, are the authority.
3. **Stage 3, optional.** Offer the formula as a method to reuse, with the instruction that each person writes their own refuter and states their own mode.

A stage-1 reply is a conversation, never corroboration. Later agreement is never pooled as a second witness. Fig 2 :40: *"agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool"*.
