## 0. The residual in effect terms

R = the gap between the habitual thinking a person would compose with without sustained institutional contact, and the habitual thinking they actually compose with after it.

"Habitual thinking" is not a vague label here. It names the cheaply retrievable molecules in a frame's library Γ that the frame composes over without re-deriving them: what a situation affords, what counts as progress, what is taken as given before deliberation starts. The effect to be explained is a change in those defaults.

Stated as options:

- **Option A, the agent-only reading.** Institutions reshape thinking by changing the individual's beliefs and discipline in place. What each person carries inside is the lever.
- **Option B, the contact-and-habitat reading.** Institutions reshape thinking by changing the field of contact the person is in, supplying atoms from a closure that differs and sending head starts down a membrane. What the habitat does to the agent is the lever.

What an answer would be: a shaped account of the mechanism that must be present in any true answer, with the specific mappings withheld until a ground settles them. Bound as an ordinary term so it can accrue a residual: **an answer must name what crosses from the institution to the person, in what direction, and what would count as a failure of the crossing.**

The residual is a **union compound**, not one hard question. "Institutions" is a disjunction of schools, markets, states and religious organizations with different actors, currencies and anchors. "Reshape habitual thinking" spans at least three mechanisms: contact change, import of new composeable atoms, and restabilization of a habit as a part that survives its own use. Fig 13 :136: *"the question is not one question and must be split before it is searched again"*. Verdicts are therefore kept per domain and never averaged.

## 1. Domain census

### R by arity, symmetry, scale

- **Arity.** Binary contact between an institution (a population-scale maintained frame) and an individual cognitive frame, plus a third term at population scale (the convention that is carried up). So a two-scale relation, not a single event.
- **Symmetry.** Asymmetric. The institution can introduce actors into the habitat and cannot subtract the habitat; Fig 11 :61: *"The seat's power is asymmetric. Introduction is available; exclusion is not."* This is not a symmetric bond between two equal peers.
- **Scale.** Individual (the step-by-step loop), population (the membrane), intergenerational (head starts sent down). Fig 4 :19-20: *"The kernel is the part that does not change with size: the same loop and the same rule for naming what worked, whether one agent is running it or a thousand. Only the actors, the currency and the anchor change."*

### One line per domain: actors / currency / anchor

| domain | actors | currency | anchor | nameable anchor? |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| D1, individual cognitive frame | beliefs, weighting `w`, object slots `s`, candidate terms φ | prediction against observation, residual R per slot | the ground (reality); it does not update | yes, the ground |
| D2, institution as maintained habitat | the seat, rules, curriculum, prices, sanctions, ritual | compliance, grades, money, legitimation | partly: price clears, a test scores; but most institutional rules are conventions, not grounds | partial, and must be tested |
| D3, membrane between scales | methods carried up, head starts sent down | compression, cost of re-derivation | the round-trip gap `R_T` | yes, the measured gap |
| D4, verification / research frame | researchers, instruments, natural experiments | evidence versus agreement | the ground, never agreement | yes, the ground |

D2 needs the anchor test applied before it is admitted as a domain. Fig 2 :29: *"An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks."* A market price can settle a transaction, but a school's grading rubric or a state's rule moves when the institution is pushed on it. Fig 10 :47: *"a convention that decides an outcome has stopped being one"*. So D2 is admitted as a **maintained habitat with a seat that authors conventions**, not as a ground. Its measures are instruments at best. Calling them anchors was my first reading and is retracted in §6.

### Partition or union

Union, with overlapping conditions. D2 and D3 overlap wherever an institution transmits a method; D1 and D4 overlap wherever a person is measured. By Fig 9 :54: *"when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again"*. The compound is *"a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either"* (Fig 9 :57). Each domain gets its own loop and its own verdict.

### Order by dependency (Fig 3)

1. Perception: does the institution change what the person can perceive and measure? Fig 3 :5: *"the system can tell what is in front of it, and tell it apart from what was there before"*.
2. Vocabulary: does it supply terms for what a situation affords? Fig 3 :7: *"it has terms for what things afford: what can be moved, opened, joined, blocked"*.
3. Objective: does it change what counts as progress? Fig 3 :10: *"it can say what would count as progress here, in its own terms"*.
4. Planning and carry: the route chosen under incomplete information, then naming what worked.

Fig 3 :27: *"A link with no instrument is not a link that works. It is a link nobody has looked at."* The middle links, vocabulary and objective, are exactly the ones with the fewest instruments in institutional research. Fig 3 :19: *"Optimising a link that already works cannot rescue one that does not."* So a policy that improves teaching technique (link 4) cannot rescue a missing objective (link 3).

## 3. Findings, one loop per domain

### F1 (D2, institutional frame): institutions act on contact, not on an isolated agent

Fig 11 :36: *"Where nothing maintains anything, intelligence is a label applied afterwards to a lineage that met its ground."*

Fig 11 :39: *"capability is a property of agent-and-habitat, never of the agent alone."*

Fig 11 :40: *"An improvement that does not change contact changes nothing, however much it improves."*

Fig 11 :58: *"Maintained. A seat exists and can act: choose the instruments, distil the impactful actors, introduce what was not there."*

A school, market, state or religious organization is not a belief injected into a head. It is a maintained habitat with a seat that chooses instruments and introduces actors. The lever is contact. A rule that leaves the person's contact with the ground unchanged changes the convention, not the thinking. This is the direct figure-grounded refusal of Option A.

**Mode:** `general`, structural. **Status:** `triangulated`, Fig 11 (contact and maintenance) with Fig 4 :27 *"The transform between scales loses information, and that is the point"* (a different figure, different route). Low overlap.

**Refuter:** exhibit a case where an institutional rule reliably changes habitual thinking while contact is held constant: same instruments, same affordances, same residuals arriving from the ground. Or exhibit an agent-only improvement made in isolation that moves the ground. The capsule in *Cultural Evolution* (Richerson and Boyd, via Wikipedia 2026) that culture is *"information capable of affecting individuals' behavior that they acquire from other members of their species through teaching, imitation and other forms of social transmission"* predicts the same thing from evolutionary anthropology, which is corroboration from a low-overlap field, not a figure line.

### F2 (D1 and D2 contact): the mechanism is import from a closure that differs

Fig 6 :23: *"No frame escapes its own closure. The bound moves only on contact with a frame whose closure differs."*

Fig 6 :21: *"MINT composes inside the closure and can never add an atom."*

Fig 6 :14: *"the only operator that moves the wall"*

Fig 8 :45: *"Two frames together reach what neither reaches alone."*

The institution is a peer frame whose closure differs from the individual's. Sustained contact imports atoms, so the individual can compose terms they could not previously reach. Pure internal reflection cannot do this: composition is idempotent inside a closure. *Decoding Tacit Knowledge* (Isaiah N, 2026) puts the same point in the human-development case: *"No frame escapes its own closure"* is used there to explain why an apprentice needs time at the bench, and *"underivable is not unknowable"* through contact. This is a framework-author source, so it is high-overlap corroboration only.

**Mode:** `general` for the mechanism, `specified` for the claim that this is what a school or a market actually does. **Status:** `triangulated`, Fig 6 (wall and import) with Fig 8 (surplus from differing closures). Low overlap between the two figures.

**Refuter:** show a frame whose closure expands by internal search alone with no import from a differing closure, or show sustained contact with a differing closure that produces no new composeable atom and no change in what can be composed.

### F3 (D3, membrane): what crosses is a head start, never a recording

Fig 4 :22: *"Only methods cross up. Only head starts come down."*

Fig 4 :23: *"upward: a method that can be reapplied. Never the recording of a particular success."*

Fig 4 :24: *"downward: something that makes the next search cheaper. Never a replay of what worked once."*

Fig 4 :34: *"A seed carries no small tree: it carries what will grow one, and the conditions it lands in decide what it becomes."*

Fig 12 :104: *"A settled molecule becomes an atom for whatever composes over it."*

An institution does not hand a person a finished habit. It hands a method and a cheaper search. The recipient remakes it under the conditions they land in, which is why the same institution produces different habitual thinking in different people and why a habit can be both stable and variable. The round trip is lossy, so Fig 4 :30 applies: *"So take the round trip and measure the gap. Without it you are navigating by dead reckoning: confident about a position nobody checked."*

**Mode:** `general`. **Status:** `triangulated`, Fig 4 (membrane) with Fig 12 :104 (settled molecule becomes an atom). Different figures, different objects.

**Refuter:** exhibit a case where an institution transmits the exact recording of a past success and the recipient reproduces it identically across changed conditions with no remaking. Equivalently, a transmitted habit whose round-trip gap `R_T` is zero.

### F4 (D2 and D4, valence): institutional agreement is the product, not a verdict

Fig 2 :40: *"agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool"*

Fig 2 :41: *"agreement about a shared meaning is the product, not a corruption of it"*

Fig 2 :36: *"An anchor is legitimate when the question is constitutively about it."*

Fig 10 :47: *"a convention that decides an outcome has stopped being one"*

Fig 7 :35: *"A room's agreement is variation, not a verdict. Selection between rooms is where the ground votes."*

A school in which everyone thinks the same way has produced a convention. That is a legitimate product when the question is about shared meaning, which many institutional questions are. It is disqualifying as evidence that the thinking is correct or that the institution caused it. The valence depends on what the question is constitutively about, and the two must not be swapped. The *Causal inference* entry (Pearl, Holland and others, via Wikipedia 2026) states the world-facing half from statistics: *"There is no inherent causality in phenomena that correlate."*

**Mode:** `general`. **Status:** `triangulated`, Fig 2 (correlation valence) with Fig 7 (selection between rooms) and Fig 10 :47 (convention that decides an outcome).

**Refuter:** exhibit a case where agreement inside one institution about a shared meaning legitimately settles a question about the world without a ground reading outside that institution.

### F5 (D4, measurement): per slot, never averaged

Fig 1 :19: *"The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated."*

Fig 1 :22: *"Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average."*

Fig 1 :13: *"a global R near zero with one live slot residual is a legal state"*

Fig 3 :27: *"A link with no instrument is not a link that works. It is a link nobody has looked at."*

The effect of an institution is per person and per habit. A population average near zero with one live slot is a legal state, not evidence of no effect. This is why a question of the form "do people in school X think differently" can return a null average while a specific habit in a specific subgroup has moved. The uninstrumented middle link is where the live slot usually sits.

**Mode:** `general`. **Status:** `triangulated`, Fig 1 (slot indexing) with Fig 3 :27 (uninstrumented link). Low overlap.

**Refuter:** show a population-level average that locates which habit changed, for whom, and through which link, as well as a per-slot reading does.

### F6 (D1, the habit itself): a habit is a catalyst and a functional group

Fig 12 :119: *"catalyst. A part that survives its own use. Spent and regenerating parts behave differently across uses, and nothing records which a term is."*

Fig 12 :123: *"functional group. A substructure whose presence fixes the outcome. The group says what kind; the qualifier says which member."*

Fig 12 :117: *"isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."*

This is the one place where the word "habitual" gets a figure-internal reading. A habit is a part that survives its own use and is composed over without re-derivation. An institution can install a functional group, a substructure whose presence fixes the outcome, which is why a person can follow a rule without adopting its underlying assumptions: the installed substructure does the fixing and the spent part keeps running. Different institutions over the same cognitive atoms can be isomers, same atoms arranged differently, which is a shape claim, not a claim that this or that institution maps to this or that structure.

**Mode:** `specified`. The mapping of "habit" onto catalyst and functional group is a binding from the psychology frame onto figure terms, not a derivation. **Status:** `single-source` for the catalyst mapping (Fig 12 only), with Fig 4 :25 *"A recording carried upward looks like knowledge and is a description of one occasion"* as partial, related corroboration.

**Refuter:** show that a habit changes while the part that survives its own use is unchanged, or that an installed substructure can be removed while the habit persists unaltered.

### F7 (D4, causal identification): a separate loop that the question drags in

Fig 9 :27: *"describe it before you go looking, or any frame you pick will seem to fit"*

Fig 8 :39: *"So a search that finds nothing is never a proof of absence."*

Fig 7 :42: *"the next room you book is the direction you travel, and booking a room like the last one is how a chain stops travelling at all."*

Fig 2 :42: *"Buy independence in proportion to the cost of being wrong."*

The question "how do institutions cause a cognitive change" is the twin of the next question in the source file (COG-0082). It is a separate domain with a separate ground. It cannot be settled by the same derivation as the mechanism: the shape says what must be present, not which institution produced which observed change in which person. Identification needs contrasting rooms selected against the residual and a value the ground returns independently.

**Mode:** `general`. **Status:** `triangulated`, Fig 9 (describe first) with Fig 7 (selection between rooms) and Fig 2 :42 (independence priced).

**Refuter:** exhibit a design that identifies the causal effect of an institution on a specific habit with no contrast between closures and no ground reading independent of the institution.

### F8, the honest gap: no named mapping is derivable

Fig 9 :32: *"you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself"*

Fig 13 :134: *"Unreachable is a verdict here, not a silence"*

Fig 8 :53: *"Residual first, frame second."*

Fig 6 :25: *"An import adds no atom to the world."*

After a genuine re-census there is no figure line that licenses "schools produce habit X, markets produce habit Y". That is **not** a silence to fill with a plausible story. It is a `gap`: the framework returns the shape any answer must have. A specific mapping would be a provenanced import with a source gate and a shadow test (Fig 6, Fig 8), and no such import was supplied. The *Library of Babel* essay (Isaiah N, 2026) states the stop: *"You can cut your search short if the set of books returned is small and descriptive enough that it provides a general shape of the answer that satisfies your question."* And it names the union failure directly: *"Perhaps your problem was a bundle or conflation of several problems masquerading as one?"*

**Mode:** `general`. **Status:** `gap` for the name, triangulated for the shape (Fig 9 :32 with the canonical figures README: *"a shape, not a name"*).

## 4. Strain test and re-census

1. **Fig 11 :40 versus Vygotsky's zone of proximal development.** The world source says a learner can perform *"only with support from a teacher or a peer with more knowledge or expertise"*. That looks like an agent-only gain. Re-census resolves it: the more knowledgeable other is part of the habitat, so the gain is a change of contact, exactly what Fig 11 :40 requires. Not strained.
2. **Fig 2 :40 versus Fig 2 :41.** One says agreement about the world is disqualified by a shared pool, the other says agreement about shared meaning is the product. Re-census: the two clauses govern different questions. Fig 2 :36 supplies the rule, *"An anchor is legitimate when the question is constitutively about it."* Not strained.
3. **Fig 4 :22 "never a recording" versus situated learning's claim that newcomers assimilate norms and values.** Re-census: situated learning describes the change of contact by which the head start is remade. It does not claim a recording is transmitted. Corroborating, not strained.
4. **Henrich's cultural-evolution claim that institutions shaped *"our brains and psychology"* is broader than the figure's structural claim.** Recorded as a scope caveat, not a conflict. The figure constrains the shape of the mechanism; the world source asserts a historical reach the figure does not measure.

After re-census: no strains hidden, one scope caveat recorded, one gap declared.

## 5. Pre-registered refuter

Written before the WATSON run and before the conclusion. A falsifier written before the run is a design instrument (Seat Map :287). The shape claim is refuted if any of the following holds:

1. An improvement in the individual agent in isolation, with contact held constant, reliably changes habitual thinking. Breaks Fig 11 :40.
2. A frame's closure expands by internal search alone, with no import from a frame whose closure differs. Breaks Fig 6 :21 and :23.
3. A population average locates which habit changed, for whom and through which link, as well as a per-slot reading. Breaks Fig 1 :19 and :22.
4. Agreement inside an institution about a shared meaning settles a question about the world without an independent ground. Breaks Fig 2 :40 and :41.
5. A transmitted habit has a round-trip gap `R_T` of zero across changed conditions. Breaks Fig 4 :30 and :34.

If any fires, the corresponding finding is withdrawn. Until one is run, no part of this folder may be labelled `grounded`.

## 7. Recommendation

1. Lead with the shape and its five refuters, not with a story about this or that institution. The framework returns a kind of thing, never the answer itself (Fig 9 :32).
2. Keep the four domains separate. Do not average a school result with a market result (Fig 1 :19, Fig 9 :57).

4. Buy contrasting rooms, not more agreeing respondents. Fig 2 :42 and Fig 7 :42.
5. Withhold the specific mappings until a ground pays for them. *"Declaring a meaning does not ground it"* (Fig 10 :49).
6. Send the generator, not the recording. Fig 4 :22: *"Only methods cross up. Only head starts come down."*
