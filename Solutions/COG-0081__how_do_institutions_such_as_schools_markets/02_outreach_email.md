**Subject:** Check one shape claim about institutions and habitual thinking

> Hi [name],
>
> I have one specific output I would like you to check against what you already know. I am not asking you to read the framework first, and I am not asking you to agree with it. If the output does not survive, there is nothing else to look at.
>
> **The residual, in effect terms.** Institutions such as schools, markets, states and religious organizations change the habitual defaults people compose with: what a situation affords, what counts as progress, what goes without saying. The question is what crosses from the institution to the person, in what direction, and what would count as a failure of the crossing.
>
> **The output (`general` on the shape, `specified` on the mechanisms, not `grounded`).** Five claims, kept separate:
> 1. An institution is a maintained habitat with a seat that can introduce actors and instruments. It changes contact. A rule that does not change contact changes the convention, not the thinking.
> 2. The person's frame is changed by contact with a frame whose closure differs. Internal reflection alone cannot expand what the frame can compose.
> 3. What crosses the individual-to-population boundary is a method and a head start, never a recording of a particular past success. Each person remakes what they receive under the conditions they land in.
> 4. Agreement inside an institution about shared meaning is the product, not a verdict about the world. Those two valences must not be swapped.
> 5. Effects are per person and per habit. A population average near zero with one live slot is a legal state.
>
> **What I am explicitly not claiming.** I am not saying that a particular institution produces a particular cognitive change. The framework returns a shape, not a name. I am also not claiming that a shared mindset is evidence of anything beyond shared meaning.
>
> **What would refute it.** I wrote these down before sending this:
> 1. An agent-only improvement, with contact held constant, reliably changes habitual thinking.
> 2. A frame's closure expands by internal search alone, with no import from a differing closure.
> 3. A population average locates which habit changed, for whom and through which link, as well as a per-slot reading.
> 4. Agreement inside an institution about shared meaning settles a question about the world without an independent ground.
> 5. A transmitted habit shows a round-trip gap of zero across changed conditions.
>
> If any one holds, I withdraw the corresponding claim. A one-line "this fails because..." is more useful to me than agreement. If you would rather not engage, no reply is needed.
>
> **If it survives, and only then:** here are the sources. The figures are the authority, not my summary of them.
> - Verbatim figure text with line numbers: `FRAMEWORK/reference/03-FIGURES_TEXT.md` in the local package.
> - Canonical usage rule: https://github.com/IsaiahN/tether/blob/general-agent/figures/README.md
> - Figures and formula for direct download: https://github.com/IsaiahN/tether/tree/general-agent/figures
>
> **If you want to use the machinery:** run it on a problem you know well and can check against a ground. Write down what would refute your result *before* you run it, and state whether your claim is general, specified or grounded. A reported failure is more useful to me than a reported success.
