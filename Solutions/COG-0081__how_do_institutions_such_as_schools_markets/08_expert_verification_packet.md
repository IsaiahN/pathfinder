# Expert verification packet: the Tether on institutions and habitual thinking

**What this is:** a claim in a form a researcher in institutional and cognitive development can check. Mode, derivation and refuters are stated before the conclusion, so a reader knows what to look for and what would settle it.

**Mode: `general` on the shape, `specified` on the mappings, not `grounded`.** No measurement is claimed to settle the institution-to-habit mapping. This is a shape and a route, not a result.

## 1. The claim, in one paragraph

An institution such as a school, market, state or religious organization is a maintained habitat with a seat, not a belief injected into a head. It reshapes habitual thinking by changing contact. A person's frame moves when it meets a frame whose closure differs, because composition inside a closure cannot add a new atom. What crosses the individual-to-population boundary is a method and a head start, never a recording of a past success, so each person remakes what they receive. Agreement about shared meaning is the product of an institution and is not a verdict about the world. The effect is per person and per habit, never a population average. A habit behaves like a part that survives its own use. The framework does not name which institution produces which habit; that mapping is an open gap requiring a discriminating instrument.

## 2. Three checkable sub-claims

### C1 - contact, not agent-only improvement (framework-internal plus psychology-facing)

- **Statement:** an institution works by changing the field of contact. A rule that leaves contact with the ground unchanged changes the convention, not the thinking.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 11 :40 - *"An improvement that does not change contact changes nothing, however much it improves."* Also Fig 11 :39 - *"capability is a property of agent-and-habitat, never of the agent alone."*
- **What to check:** in a case you know well, did the habitual change track a change in instruments, affordances or corrections, or did it track only a change in stated rule? Vygotsky's zone of proximal development is the friendly test case: the gain appears with a more knowledgeable other in the habitat, not in the isolated learner.
- **Refuter:** an agent-only improvement with contact held constant reliably changes the habit. That breaks Fig 11 :40.

### C2 - a head start, never a recording (framework-internal plus development-facing)

- **Statement:** what is transmitted across generations is a re-applicable method that makes the next search cheaper, not a replay of a particular success. Each recipient remakes it under their own conditions.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 4 :22 - *"Only methods cross up. Only head starts come down."* Fig 4 :34 - *"A seed carries no small tree: it carries what will grow one, and the conditions it lands in decide what it becomes."*
- **What to check:** does the institution transmit a criterion and a practice that recipients adapt, or a fixed behavioural script that recipients copy? Situated learning predicts the former: newcomers become part of a community of practice rather than downloading propositions.
- **Refuter:** a transmitted habit whose round-trip gap `R_T` is zero across changed landing conditions, meaning what came down matched what went up exactly. That breaks Fig 4 :30 and :34.

### C3 - per slot, and the right valence for agreement (measurement-facing)

- **Statement:** effects must be read per person and per habit. Institutional agreement about shared meaning is the product; using it as evidence about the world is a category error.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 1 :19 - *"The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated."* Fig 1 :13 - *"a global R near zero with one live slot residual is a legal state"*. Fig 2 :41 - *"agreement about a shared meaning is the product, not a corruption of it"*; Fig 2 :40 - *"agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool"*.
- **What to check:** does the design report per-person, per-habit change with a pre-registered effect description, or only a group mean? Does it treat within-institution consensus as a result, or only as a convention?
- **Refuter:** a population average that locates which habit changed, for whom and through which link as well as a per-slot reading; or a within-institution agreement that settles a world question without an independent ground.

## 3. What is explicitly not claimed

- Not a named institution-to-habit mapping. That is an honest `gap`: Fig 9 :32 - *"you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself"*.
- Not one mechanism for all four institution types. The question is a union compound (Fig 9 :57).
- Not a causal-identification result. That is the next question in the source file, COG-0082.
- Not that institutional consensus is worthless. It is the product when the question is about shared meaning.
- Not that an institution is a ground. Fig 10 :47 - *"a convention that decides an outcome has stopped being one"*.
- Not `grounded`. Nothing here is asserted as settled by experiment.

## 5. What would settle it

A discriminating instrument, not more agreement. From *The Hard Problem is About Source*: the colour instrument reads a value but *"cannot comment on the source"*, and an instrument that cannot distinguish cases *"demonstrates its own inadequacy."*

1. **Contact test:** hold contact constant while varying the rule. Does the habit still change?
2. **Closure test:** look for closure expansion with no import from a differing frame.
3. **Round-trip test:** measure `R_T` for a habit carried across generations under changed conditions.
4. **Per-slot test:** compare a group mean with a pre-registered per-person, per-habit reading.
5. **Contrast test:** select contrasting rooms against the residual and range each separately against a ground value. This closes the mapping gap.

## 6. What an expert can check in twenty minutes

1. Read Fig 11 :39-40, Fig 4 :22 and :34, Fig 1 :19, and Fig 2 :40-41. Six short passages.
2. Check C1 and C2 against one institution you know: did the habit track contact, and was the transmission a head start rather than a script?
3. Name one design that would discriminate the institution's contribution from a shared prior. If none is possible, say why.
4. Send the refutation. A failure is more useful than an endorsement.
