# The Tether's answer on small shocks and large recessions

**Mode: `general` on the structural shape, `specified` on the mechanism predictions, not `grounded`.** A shape and a route, not a result. Simulated derivation checked against the figures (`05_what_shape_means.md` section 6), not a ground reading.

## 1. The residual it answers

A proportional model predicts a response of the same order as the shock. On some occasions the response is much larger. The residual is that excess, indexed by the slot where it appears, not by the aggregate. The question asks whether the excess is explained by tipping points and feedback loops. Claim E (the effect happens) and claim M (those mechanisms explain it) are a union and are kept separate (Fig 9 :54-57).

## 2. The shape

1. **A small remainder becomes large only through an amplifying chain.** Fig 13 :31-32: "a remainder compounding through a chain that amplifies is chaos, and the detail a crossing discards reappears above it as noise". If the chain damps, the response stays proportional. `general`.
2. **A feedback loop is a cycle, and its sign decides closure.** Fig 12 :121: "The output re-enters as input. The sign of the loop decides whether it closes, and a chain cannot close by construction." A positive loop is the candidate push; a negative loop damps. `specified`.
3. **A tipping point is a threshold comparison, not a new kind of thing.** Fig 12 :86-89: "a threshold relation between two quantities". It names the crossing, not what pushed the system there. `specified`.
4. **Contact cascades reach several steps out.** Fig 11 :52-53: "And contact cascades. Scoping the habitat to direct contact is how DDT reached the eagles: first-order enumeration, second-order effect, and the residual that mattered was three steps out." A first-order model truncates before the residual that matters. `general`.
5. **Aggregation erases the live slot.** Fig 1 :22: "Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average". An aggregate model can be nearly right while one sector carries the signal. This is the most checkable part of the shape. `general`.
6. **The micro to macro step is lossy.** Fig 4 :30: "So take the round trip and measure the gap. Without it you are navigating by dead reckoning: confident about a position nobody checked." The round-trip gap R_T is the honest report of what the aggregate cannot hold. `general`.

## 3. What it explicitly does not say

- Not that this derivation establishes that actual economies have those thresholds or loop gains. That is a ground reading and none was taken.
- Not that "small shocks sometimes trigger large recessions" is settled here. Answering "sometimes" requires data, and the figures do not supply data.
- Not that the financial accelerator *is* the loop. That is a binding from the macro frame onto a held bond, provenanced as such, and unmeasured by this package.
- Not that all small shocks are amplified. Most chains damp. The shape states the condition for amplification, not its frequency.
- Not that a butterfly metaphor is evidence, and not that a consensus of economists is an anchor (Fig 7 :35).
- Not `grounded`.

## 4. What would settle it, the ground

A discriminator, not a further argument. *The Hard Problem is About Source* says an instrument that reads green "cannot comment on the source"; the spectrometer resolved it. Here:

- **Loop-gain test:** measure whether the relevant feedback loop has gain above one in the episode. If it does not, F3 is not the explanation.
- **Threshold test:** name the state variable and its critical value **before** the episode, then check whether a crossing occurred. Naming after the fact is projection (Fig 9 :27-28).
- **Slot test:** find an episode where the aggregate was near-right and one sector carried a live residual, then check whether averaging removed the signal (Fig 1 :22).
- **Cascade test:** enumerate contacts one step out, then two, then three, and see where the residual that matters sits (Fig 11 :44).
- **Round trip:** carry the micro account up to the aggregate and back and report R_T, rather than treating the aggregate as the micro story in fewer words.
- **Standing falsifiers** from `01_figure_grounded_audit.md` section 5: no amplifying chain; lossless aggregation; feedback without re-entry; tipping without comparison; large shock rather than small.

## 5. The honest stop

**Unreached, not unreachable.** The ground exists here: business-cycle data, balance sheets, network topology, impulse responses. It is simply not read by this derivation. Fig 8 :39: "So a search that finds nothing is never a proof of absence." So an absent measurement is not evidence that economies lack the mechanism. The answer stops at the limit of what this agent did, and records the lineage so the stop is checkable. "Indiscernibility is not identity."

## 6. One-paragraph version for outreach

> The figures do not answer yes or no to whether small shocks sometimes trigger large recessions, because that is a question for data. They give the shape an answer must have if tipping points and feedback loops are the explanation: an amplifying chain that compounds a remainder, a loop whose output re-enters as input with a sign that decides whether it grows or damps, a threshold comparison that marks the regime change, and a cascade that carries the effect several steps out. They also give the reason such effects are missed: a live residual sits in one slot, and averaging across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average. What this offers is the shape, the falsifiers written in advance, and the measurements that would close it, not a verdict and not a named economic mechanism. The empirical question stays open until the ground settles it.
