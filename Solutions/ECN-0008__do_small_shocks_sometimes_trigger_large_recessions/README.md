## Boundary and standing of this derivation

This is a simulated derivation checked for internal consistency against the canonical figures. It is not a ground reading and it cannot settle the question it reports on. From *The Recursive Self-Improvement Trap* (Isaiah N, 2026-11-19): "the truly testable output would need to be derived from the real architecture or working the formula yourself." See `05_what_shape_means.md` section 6. No recession data, impulse response, or network measurement was taken by this agent.

Unlike a physics question about a unique microscopic event, this question has a nameable ground: business-cycle data, national accounts, measured balance sheets and network topology. So the empirical half of the question is decidable in principle. This package does not perform that measurement. It reports the shape the figures license and hands the measurement to the reader.

## What an answer would look like, bound as an ordinary term

An answer here is a shape with three parts: (1) a chain in which a small residual is not damped but compounded, (2) a threshold relation that changes the regime when a quantity crosses it, and (3) a loop whose output re-enters as input with a sign that decides whether it amplifies or damps. Binding that shape as a term before the run makes the preference for "tipping points and feedback loops" refusable by the same test as everything else (Formula, "Direction"; Fig 5 :62-66). The pre-registered refuter in `01_figure_grounded_audit.md` is the instrument for that.

## Verdict in one line

The figures give a legal and structurally expected shape for amplification, but they do not establish that actual economies contain the particular thresholds and loop gains that would make it fire, and they do not establish that it fires "sometimes" in the data. That is a ground question. `general` / `specified`, candidate, ungrounded.

- No personal emails of named individuals.
- No ranking of people by how likely they are to agree with the claim.

Three reasons, in the figures' own words:

1. **Agreement is not an anchor.** Fig 2 :35: "Alignment is triangulation, not negotiation. A mirror cannot correct you."
2. **Correlation disqualifies a verdict.** Fig 2 :40: "disqualifying for a verdict: agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool." Selecting recipients for receptiveness manufactures exactly the correlation that makes their agreement worthless.
3. **The first contact carries the residual, not the source.** Fig 10 :52: "Bring a characterised residual, never the source." Fig 10 :42: "A verifier that can reconstruct the claim cannot verify it."

Legally: scraping personal emails for unsolicited bulk outreach breaches GDPR and CAN-SPAM style rules in many jurisdictions. That is a practical failure on top of the structural one.
