# Order, bond type and isomers in the amplification claim

## RESIDUAL

The question names two mechanisms in one breath: tipping points and feedback loops. Two derivations are owed: (1) are they the same structure at different scales, or different bonds, and (2) if two economies hold the same agents and the same thresholds, can a different arrangement alone produce a different response? The second is the isomer question (Fig 12 :117).

## DERIVATION

### Q1 - Is a tipping point a feedback loop? No, different bond type

- Fig 12 :86-89: **"a threshold relation between two quantities"** and the test **"is it about which is larger, rather than both being present? Yes."** This is `⋛`, comparison. It states a relation between a quantity and a critical value at a moment. It does not by itself move the quantity.
- Fig 12 :121: **"The output re-enters as input. The sign of the loop decides whether it closes, and a chain cannot close by construction."** This is the cycle. It moves the quantity over time because the output returns.
- Operators :51: **"a goal is a comparison, which is why a vocabulary without it cannot state one"**. A threshold states a target or a boundary; it does not supply the dynamics that reach it.
- ⇒ A tipping point and a feedback loop are **different bonds**. A tip names the crossing; a loop supplies the push. Conflating them is a notation error, not a deeper insight. `specified`, triangulated across Fig 12 and the Operators table for the distinction. **Single-source** for the claim that no new primitive is required, since both bonds are in the same figure set.

**Falsifier:** show a "tipping point" that is itself a closed loop with re-entering output, or a "feedback loop" that is a static comparison with no re-entry. Either collapses the distinction and this finding fails.

### Q2 - What order does the amplification chain have?

- Fig 12 :121 again: a chain cannot close by construction. So a linear chain of exposures cannot be a feedback loop; it terminates.
- Fig 13 :31-32: **"a remainder compounding through a chain that amplifies is chaos, and the detail a crossing discards reappears above it as noise"**. Compounding requires a remainder re-entering, which is the cycle, not the open chain.
- Fig 11 :52-53: **"And contact cascades. Scoping the habitat to direct contact is how DDT reached the eagles: first-order enumeration, second-order effect, and the residual that mattered was three steps out."** The cascade is first order, then second, then third. A model that stops at direct contact truncates the chain before the residual that matters.
- ⇒ The order is: shock lands in a slot, propagates along contacts, a loop returns output to input, gain above one amplifies, a threshold is crossed, the regime changes, and the aggregate reads a large recession. `specified`. Single-source for the order (Fig 11 plus Fig 12, low overlap between cascade and bond definition, so triangulated for the shape of re-entry).

**Falsifier:** a documented case where the large response arrives in one step with no re-entry and no intermediate contact. Then the order claim fails and the cascade is a spike, not a loop.

### Q3 - Are two economies with the same atoms isomers?

- Fig 12 :117: **"isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."**
- Consider economy A and economy B with the same firms, the same balance sheets, the same threshold values, and the same agents. A is arranged as a densely interconnected credit network; B as a sparse one with the same total credit. The ingredient lists are identical. Under Fig 12 :117 they are **isomers**: same atoms, different bond, different substance. Their impulse responses can differ even though their ingredient lists match.
- World corroboration, low overlap: "Cascading failure" (Wikipedia, 2026) states that "systemic risk is not due to individual institutions per se, but due to the interconnections", and that a failure of a small fraction of nodes in one network can trigger an iterative cascade across interdependent networks. "Agent-based model" (Wikipedia, 2026) reports that ABMs replace representative agents with heterogeneous, interdependent agents and can generate crashes and booms from nonlinear responses. These are secondary and do not set the verdict.
- **Falsifier, written before the run:** if two economies with identical agent sets, identical threshold values and different network arrangements show statistically indistinguishable impulse responses to the same small shock, the isomer claim fails and the two economies are merely two spellings of one term (Fig 12 :92-93: "Under a symmetric bond, k operands have k! spellings and one term").

### Q4 - Do the two mechanisms form a functional group?

- Fig 12 :123: **"functional group. A substructure whose presence fixes the outcome. The group says what kind; the qualifier says which member."**
- Tipping point and feedback loop both belong to the functional group "small cause, large effect". The qualifier says which member is doing the work in a given episode. This is the weaker claim that survives even if the isomer test fails.
- Fig 12 :104: **"A settled molecule becomes an atom for whatever composes over it. So irreducible is a fact about a level and never about the world"**. Each mechanism, once settled against the ground in a particular episode, can become an atom for a higher-level account of the recession. That higher-level account is a composition, not a new cause.

### Q5 - What does the comparison cost in two currencies?

- Fig 12 :125: **"A composition costs in two currencies, and they do not add."** Fig 12 :126: **"Bits price the description. Actions price finding out whether it holds."**
- A model with every sector and every credit link is expensive in bits and may take one action to refute. A single aggregate threshold model is cheap to state and may take a whole research budget to settle against data. Neither figure predicts the other; both must be read (Fig 12 :128-129).
- Fig 9 :60: **"Use filters for the budget and witnesses for the verdict. Never let a filter hand you a verdict."** This is a budget rule only.

## CONCLUSION

A tipping point and a feedback loop are **different bonds**: `⋛` comparison versus cycle. The tip names the crossing; the loop supplies the push. A shock-to-recession chain must close into a loop for a remainder to compound, and contact cascades carry the effect several steps beyond direct links. Two economies with the same atoms and different arrangements are isomers under Fig 12 :117, with a stated falsifier; if that fails, the weaker functional-group reading (Fig 12 :123) stands. Nothing here is `grounded`: the loop gain, the threshold value and the impulse response are still to be measured.
