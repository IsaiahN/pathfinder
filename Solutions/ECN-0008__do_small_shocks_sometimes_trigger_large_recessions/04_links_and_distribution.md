## What the wider literature adds, and what it does not

1. **The empirical "yes" is supported, but by secondary sources here.** The financial accelerator and amplification literature reports exactly the effect the question asks about: adverse shocks amplified by credit-market conditions. These are world sources. Under WATSON they corroborate; they do not set the verdict.
2. **The mechanism is multiple, not one.** Cascading-failure work attributes systemic risk to interconnections, ABM work attributes large swings to heterogeneous agents and nonlinear responses, and tipping-point work traces the idea to thresholds (Grodzins, Schelling, Granovetter). This is low overlap across fields, so the agreement is worth something. But all of these pages are tertiary summaries in this package, which raises overlap among the Wikipedia set; the ledger records that.
3. **No source read here settles a particular economy.** None of the pages gives a measured loop gain, a dated threshold crossing, or a slot-level residual for a named recession episode. That measurement is the unpaid part of this question.
4. **The butterfly analogy is not evidence.** It is included as a mathematics analogue because it is genuinely low overlap with macroeconomics. It is not a figure line and is never cited as one. A resemblance between a metaphor and a figure is not corroboration.

## Distribution rules: offer, do not push

Figure-grounded reasons:

- **Fig 10 :52** - "Bring a characterised residual, never the source." Sending the full audit before anyone has described what they would count as a failure sends the frame, not the output.
- **Fig 10 :42** - "A verifier that can reconstruct the claim cannot verify it." A recipient who has absorbed the framework before being asked for a verdict has joined the frame being checked.
- **Fig 9 :27-28** - "describe it before you go looking, or any frame you pick will seem to fit." Ask what they would expect to see if the shape were true before giving them the packet.
- **Fig 2 :40** - "disqualifying for a verdict: agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool." A list selected for people who already agree produces a convention, not corroboration.
- **Fig 7 :35** - "A room's agreement is variation, not a verdict. Selection between rooms is where the ground votes."
- **Fig 4 :22** - "Only methods cross up. Only head starts come down." The formula may be given away as a generator; the conclusion may not be passed off as a result.

**Recommended order in a reply:** (1) the shape claim, its mode and its refuter; (2) ask what they would count as a failure and record the answer; (3) only then the three figure lines and the audit, with the note that the figures, not this summary, are the authority; (4) the formula as a reusable method, with the instruction that each user names their own ground and writes their own refuter.

## The two-stage offer

- **Stage 1 before stage 2** is Fig 10 :52 and Fig 2 :45: frames range against the ground separately. The recipient checks the output without holding the framework's interior.
- **Stage 2 is opt-in by construction.** Once a recipient has absorbed the theory, their agreement stops being evidence. Their verdict on stage 1 must be recorded first and never pooled later (Fig 2 :40).
- **Stage 3 sends a method, which is permitted.** The formula is a generator, so giving it away does not give away an answer.
- **Stage 3 still needs a ground per problem.** A user who runs the loop on a macro question must write down what would refute their result before running it, because a frame cannot check the direction of its own question.

**Hard precondition:** the offer only works if the stage-1 output survives. The refuter in `01_figure_grounded_audit.md` section 5 is the test. Keep the shape and the refuter in front of the links.
