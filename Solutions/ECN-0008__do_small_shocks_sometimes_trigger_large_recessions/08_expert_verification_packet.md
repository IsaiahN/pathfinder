# Expert verification packet: the Tether on small shocks and large recessions

**What this is:** a claim in a form a macroeconomist or complexity researcher can check. Mode, derivation and refuters are stated before the conclusion, so a reader knows what to look for and what would settle it.

**Mode: `general` structurally, `specified` as a mechanism, not `grounded`.** No measured loop gain, threshold value or impulse response is claimed. This is a shape and a route, not a result. It is a simulated derivation checked for internal consistency against the figures, not a ground reading.

## 1. The claim, in one paragraph

The question bundles two conditions that must be kept separate. A feedback loop is a cycle whose output re-enters as input, and the sign of the loop decides whether it amplifies or damps. A tipping point is a threshold comparison between two quantities; it names the crossing, not what pushed the system there. An amplifying chain compounds a small remainder into a large effect, and contact cascades carry that effect several steps beyond direct links. A live residual sits in a slot, and averaging across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average, so an aggregate model can be nearly right while one sector carries the signal. The figures give this shape and its falsifiers. They do not establish that any actual economy has the required loop gains or thresholds, and they do not settle whether the effect happens "sometimes". That is owed to the ground.

## 2. Three checkable sub-claims

### C1 - framework-internal; checkable by reading Figures 12 and the Operators table

- **Statement:** tipping point and feedback loop are different bonds, not the same mechanism at two scales. The tip is a threshold comparison; the loop is a cycle with re-entering output and a sign.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 12 :86-89: "a threshold relation between two quantities" and "is it about which is larger, rather than both being present? Yes." Fig 12 :121: "The output re-enters as input. The sign of the loop decides whether it closes, and a chain cannot close by construction." Operators :51: "a goal is a comparison, which is why a vocabulary without it cannot state one".
- **Refuter:** show a tipping point that is itself a closed loop with re-entering output, or a feedback loop that is a static comparison with no re-entry. Either collapses the distinction and C1 fails.

### C2 - economics-facing; checkable against sectoral data and an aggregated series

- **Statement:** a live residual is indexed by slot, and aggregation erases it. Thus a well-fitting aggregate model can coexist with a large unmodelled signal in one sector, and the aggregate near-zero residual is not evidence that nothing is happening.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 1 :19: "The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated." Fig 1 :13: "a global R near zero with one live slot residual is a legal state". Fig 1 :22: "Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average."
- **What to check:** take a recession episode with a near-right aggregate forecast. Disaggregate to sectors. Is there a sector with a large, persistent, unmodelled residual that the average concealed? Then test whether that sector's loop gain, for example credit or inventory feedback, exceeded one.
- **Refuter:** a measured case where averaging across agents preserved the slot-level signal exactly, so the aggregate carried all the information about where the shock acted. C2 then fails.

### C3 - framework-internal derivation with a world-facing consequence

- **Statement:** a chain cannot close, so amplification requires re-entry, and contact cascades carry the residual several steps beyond direct links. A first-order exposure map truncates before the effect that matters.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 12 :121: "The output re-enters as input. The sign of the loop decides whether it closes, and a chain cannot close by construction." Fig 11 :52-53: "And contact cascades. Scoping the habitat to direct contact is how DDT reached the eagles: first-order enumeration, second-order effect, and the residual that mattered was three steps out."
- **Refuter:** a documented case where the large response arrives in one step, with no re-entry and no intermediate contact, or where enumerating second- and third-order contacts adds nothing to the explanation. C3 then fails.

## 3. What is explicitly not claimed

- Not a yes or no on the empirical question. The figures supply shape, not data.
- Not that the financial accelerator *is* the loop, or that a leverage ratio *is* the threshold. These are bindings from the economics frame onto held bonds, provenanced as such, and unmeasured here.
- Not that all small shocks are amplified. Most chains damp; the shape states the condition for amplification.
- Not that a butterfly metaphor or an economics consensus is evidence. Fig 7 :35: "A room's agreement is variation, not a verdict. Selection between rooms is where the ground votes."
- Not that this agent read recession data. It did not.
- Not `grounded`.

## 5. What would settle it

A discriminator, not a further argument. From *The Hard Problem is About Source*: an instrument that reads green "cannot comment on the source"; the spectrometer resolved it.

1. **Loop-gain test:** is the relevant loop gain above one in the episode? If not, the loop is not the push.
2. **Threshold test:** was a state variable and its critical value named before the episode, and was a crossing observed? Naming after the fact is projection (Fig 9 :27-28).
3. **Slot test:** did a sector carry a large live residual while the aggregate looked near-right (Fig 1 :22)?
4. **Cascade test:** enumerate contacts at one, two and three steps out. Where does the residual that matters sit (Fig 11 :52-53)?
5. **Round trip:** carry the micro account up and back and report R_T (Fig 4 :30).

## 6. What an expert can check in twenty minutes
