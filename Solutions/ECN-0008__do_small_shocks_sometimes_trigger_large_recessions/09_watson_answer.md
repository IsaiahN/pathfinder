## QUESTION

Decide, in effect terms, what the Tether says about the claim that small shocks sometimes trigger large recessions because economies contain tipping points and feedback loops. Option A: assert the claim as written, including that the mechanisms are present and causal in actual economies. Option B: keep the figure-derived shape, split the bundled mechanisms, and leave the empirical frequency and the particular mechanism to the ground. What each leads to: A states a `general` verdict about data without measuring anything, conflates two different bonds, and spends the independence of the people asked to check it; B sends a checkable shape, refuters written in advance, and an explicit list of the measurements that would close it.

## DOMAIN CENSUS

R by arity, symmetry and scale, then one line per domain with actors / currency / anchor, partition or union, and order by dependency.

- **R (the residual).** The excess of an observed output response over the proportional prediction for a small shock. Arity: the explanation names two conditions, tipping point and feedback loop, so this is a **union compound**, not one hard question (Fig 9 :54-57). Symmetry: the response is ordered and threshold-like, not a symmetric join. Scale: micro or sectoral input, macro output, so the map crosses a membrane (Fig 4).
- **Domain 1, threshold / tipping.** Actors: a state variable, its critical value, the regime on each side. Currency: comparison over an ordering. Anchor: a measured threshold variable and a dated regime change, nameable, so this is a domain. Question type: a threshold relation, ordered, not symmetric.
- **Domain 2, loop and cascade.** Actors: agents, counterparties, credit links, the re-entering output. Currency: influence and credit flow. Anchor: a measured loop gain and network adjacency, nameable. Question type: cycle plus contact cascade.
- **Domain 3, aggregation and scale.** Actors: sectors, statistical agencies, models. Currency: the slot-level residual versus the aggregate. Anchor: sectoral and aggregate data, nameable. Question type: lossy transform and round trip.
- **Domain 4, verification / outreach.** Actors: expert readers and their instruments. Currency: agreement and residual. Anchor: the ground, never agreement. In this simulated run all domains draw on one evidence pool, so this domain's independence is zero (Fig 2 :40).
- **Order by dependency.** Describe the residual before naming a frame (Fig 9 :27-28); keep the two bonds separate (Fig 9 :54-57); settle the slot-level signal before the aggregate (Fig 1 :19); measure the round trip rather than assuming it (Fig 4 :30).

Strain test applied: Fig 13's amplifying chain governs the structure of any chain that compounds a remainder, not a claim that a named economy is chaotic; Fig 4 :19-20 allows the actors, currency and anchor to change across strata. A tipping point is the `⋛` comparison bond, while the Fig 4 membrane is a scale boundary. Different quantities, so no strain after re-census.

## VERDICT (from the figures)

The shape is legal and structurally expected; the empirical claim is not settled here. An amplifying chain compounds a small remainder, a loop supplies the push with a sign that decides amplification, a threshold comparison marks the regime change, and contact cascades carry the effect several steps out. Aggregation erases the live slot, which is why the effect is easy to miss in aggregate data. Tipping point and feedback loop are different bonds and keep separate verdicts. The particular loop gains, threshold values and episode frequencies are ground questions. This is `general` / `specified`, candidate, ungrounded.

- **cost if wrong:** bounded and locatable. The packet states mode, refuters and provenance before the conclusion, so an error is findable rather than invisible. The real cost is spending the independence of outside readers by sending a verdict-shaped claim before they have recorded their own reading of the output. Fig 2 :42 "Buy independence in proportion to the cost of being wrong." That cost is avoidable by leading with the shape claim and its refuter.
- **undoable:** yes. The empirical gaps are filled by measurement, not by rewriting the shape, and the bindings from macroeconomics are removable without touching the derivation. A sent claim can be superseded by a corrected packet. No primitive has to be withdrawn.
- **reversed by:** a ground reading, not agreement. A measured loop gain at or below one in a documented large-response episode, a case where aggregation preserved the slot-level signal exactly, a mechanism called a feedback loop without re-entry, a tipping transition that is not a threshold comparison, or a derivation of one of the held bonds from the others would each fire a pre-registered refuter. A source-discriminating instrument that names the threshold variable and the loop gain in an episode would move the empirical half toward `grounded`. Until one of these fires, the verdict stands as `candidate`.
