# The Tether's answer: culture and emotion

**Mode: `specified` on the substantive claims, `general` on the structural shape. Not `grounded`.** This is a shape and a route, not a result. Simulated derivation checked against the figures (`05_what_shape_means.md` §6), not a ground reading.

## 1. The residual it answers

R = |Γ(b,a) − o′| per episode s. A person's emotion library predicts a reading for a bodily and social situation; what arrives is a bodily change plus a habitat full of other people. The observed residual has two contrasts: emotion varies between cultures and between people inside one culture, yet the variation is neither arbitrary nor total. The question is why. The contact is a **union compound**, not one hard problem (Fig 9 :57: "a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either"). It has its own loop and its own ground.

## 2. The shape

1. **Emotion is a property of person-and-habitat.** Fig 11 :39: "capability is a property of agent-and-habitat, never of the agent alone." The loop is the same kernel at every scale; what changes with culture is "the actors, the currency and the anchor" (Fig 4 :20). So culture can shape the contents of the loop without changing the loop.
2. **Culture enters through the weighting, not through a switch.** Symbols table: b is "formed from its own history and from what it inherited, mixed by w", and "Two frames with identical architecture diverge because their histories and their w diverge." Determination would require the private-history term to be zero and w to be fixed at the inherited pole. The loop forbids both. This is the framework's direct, structural answer to "without determining them entirely."
3. **The habitat is enumerated, never composed.** Fig 11 :43: "The habitat is enumerated, never composed." People, caregivers, language, roles, norms, practices and their second-order effects are read off the world, not derived from a trait list. And "An actor’s valence is relative to the residual, not a property it carries" (Fig 11 :49): no standing list of good or bad cultural emotions survives a change of situation.
4. **Cultures compose isomers from shared atoms.** Fig 12 :117: "Same atoms, different bond, different substance." Core affect, an emotion-concept system and controlled attention are the shared atoms (binding from Barrett 2009); the cultural arrangement of concept and situation is the bond. Where a culture fuses sadness and anger into one category, or reads anger as relational proximity rather than distance, it is the same atoms under a different bond. Where two cultures reach functionally equivalent grief by different routes, it is convergent derivation (Fig 13 :121), not a shared pool (Fig 8 :50).
5. **Misinterpretation is a channel failure.** Fig 10 :24: "the ground does not decay, the channel does." A report of anger is a reading, and "it cannot comment on the source" (*The Hard Problem is About Source*). The person's report is the legitimate anchor for felt quality because the question is about people (Fig 2 :37), while the category applied is an instrument that can be wrong on an axis it does not measure.
6. **Keep the two conditions separate.** C1 (culture supplies categories, weighting and habitat) and C2 (shared atoms, private history and learnable w resist fixation) have different instruments (Fig 9 :54-57). A single lever for both is a pooled reading and destroys the diagnostic (Fig 10 :26).

## 3. What it explicitly does not say

- Not that culture determines emotion. The private-history term in b is never zero (Symbols table).
- Not that emotion is a hard-wired universal module. Fig 11 :40 forbids the agent-only reading.
- Not a list of which emotions are universal or culture-specific. "you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself" (Fig 9 :32).
- Not that cross-cultural averages are evidence. "Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average" (Fig 1 :22). They are filters for the budget, not witnesses for the verdict (Fig 9 :60).
- Not a last theory of emotion. There is no final room (Fig 7 :6) and no last route (Fig 13 :90).
- Not grounded. No person-level measurement settles `left(R,φ)` here.

## 4. What would settle it, the ground

A person-level ground, not a further argument. From *The Hard Problem is About Source*: an instrument that reads green "cannot comment on the source"; the spectrometer resolved it. Here:

- **Weighting test:** can an instrument separate the inherited-prior contribution to a category choice from the private-history contribution, prospectively and per person? If yes, w is measured and F2 moves toward `grounded`.
- **Source-discrimination test:** for a report of "anger" or "shame", can an instrument discriminate among the bodily and cultural sources that produced the same reading? If yes, F8 stops being a `gap`.
- **Habitat substitution:** does an emotion capability survive a documented change of habitat that removes the culture's payment for that emotion? If it does, F1 is refuted.
- **Channel test:** can a category-supply change produce a felt-quality change with the body measured unchanged and no channel degradation? If yes, F5 is refuted.
- **Standing falsifiers from `01`:** any of the seven pre-registered conditions, including an invariant cultural-practice valence, an emotion with no shared atomic ingredient, or a single lever that restores both union conditions with no different residue.

## 5. The honest stop

**Unreached, not unreachable.** The three atoms borrowed from affective science are "a human's careful guess" at the axes of emotion, not nature's own list; declaring them makes the choice visible, not neutral. The person-level instrument that would measure w or discriminate sources is not held here. The answer stops at the limit of what the available instruments can distinguish and records the lineage so the stop is checkable. "Indiscernibility is not identity."

## 6. One-paragraph version for outreach

> The Tether does not give a list of universal emotions and does not say culture writes emotion into a person. It says that the emotion loop is the same at every scale, so culture changes the actors, the currency and the anchor, not the loop; that a person's belief mixes inherited prior with private history through a learnable weighting, so two people in one culture diverge; that culture supplies an enumerable habitat of people and practices rather than a trait list; and that different cultures bond the same shared bodily atoms into different emotion substances, the way the same ingredients make different molecules. Misnaming a feeling is a failure of the channel to one's own report, not a change in what was felt. What the framework offers is that shape, five falsifiers written in advance, and the instruments that would close it. The specific inventory of emotions stays open until a source-discriminating instrument, not an argument or an average, settles it.
