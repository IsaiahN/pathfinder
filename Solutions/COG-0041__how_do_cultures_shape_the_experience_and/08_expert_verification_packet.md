# Expert verification packet: the Tether on culture and emotion

**What this is:** a claim in a form a specialist in emotion research or cultural psychology can check. Mode, derivation and refuters are stated before the conclusion, so a reader knows what to look for and what would settle it.

**Mode: `specified` on the substantive claims, `general` on the structural shape. Not `grounded`.** No person-level measurement is claimed. This is a shape and a route, not a result.

## 1. The claim, in one paragraph

A culture shapes emotion by supplying the categories available for reading a bodily change, the weighting between inherited prior and private history, and an enumerable habitat of people and practices that the person's loop ranges against. It does not determine emotion, because the loop is the same at every scale, the bodily atoms are shared, the belief always carries a private-history term, the weighting is learnable, and differently-history'd people in one culture diverge. Culturally specific emotions are isomers over a shared affective atom set: same atoms, different bond, different substance. Misnaming a feeling is a channel failure, not a change in what was felt. The framework returns this shape, five falsifiers written in advance, and the instruments that would close it. It does not say which specific emotions are universal.

## 2. Three checkable sub-claims

### C1 - framework-internal and culture-facing; checkable by reading Fig 11 and the Symbols table

- **Statement:** capability is person-and-habitat; culture supplies the category library and the weighting, but b always carries a private-history term and w is learnable, so culture cannot be a total cause.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 11 :39, "So the answerable question is not what intelligence is, but what makes a system capable. And capability is a property of agent-and-habitat, never of the agent alone." Fig 11 :40, "A system cannot be made more capable by improving the agent in isolation. An improvement that does not change contact changes nothing, however much it improves." Symbols table :20-26, "What the system takes to be the case before it acts. Formed from its own history and from what it inherited, mixed by w" and "Two frames with identical architecture diverge because their histories and their w diverge."
- **Refuter:** an emotion capability invariant across a habitat substitution that removes the culture's payment for it; or a report fully predicted by inherited prior with a measured zero private-history contribution.

### C2 - emotion-science facing; checkable by a researcher against the cross-cultural emotion literature

- **Statement:** shared bodily atoms under different cultural bonds yield different emotion substances (isomers); the weaker claim is a shared functional group with cultural qualifiers.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 12 :117, "isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour." Fig 12 :123, "functional group. A substructure whose presence fixes the outcome. The group says what kind; the qualifier says which member." Fig 13 :121, "Different routes can arrive at the same substance."
- **What to check:** is the atomic layer (core affect, concept system, controlled attention) really shared, as Barrett 2009 claims: "At the psychological level, the ingredients that make up the recipes might be universal"? Are the cases of kizginlik, Russian sadness and Japanese anger genuinely bond differences rather than intensity or expression differences?
- **Refuter:** an emotion with no shared atomic ingredient across cultures, or identical atoms and identical bonds with different felt quality under a fixed self-report anchor. That kills the isomer claim and leaves the functional-group claim standing.

### C3 - framework-internal derivation; checkable by reading Fig 2 and Fig 10

- **Statement:** felt quality is anchored by the person's own report because the question is constitutively about people; the category applied is an instrument and can be wrong on axes it does not measure, so misinterpretation is a channel failure.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 2 :37, "Human judgment is the right anchor for is this a good answer for a person, because the question is about people"; Fig 2 :37-38, "instruments can be wrong on axes they do not measure." Fig 10 :24, "the ground does not decay, the channel does"; Fig 10 :26, "stale percepts, dropped transients, saturated metrics, pooled readings." Fig 10 :28, "the output is not a verdict, it is you have lost touch."
- **Refuter:** a felt-quality change with the bodily ground measured unchanged, the category supply held constant, and no channel degradation demonstrable.

## 3. What is explicitly not claimed

- Not that culture determines emotion, and not that emotion is a hard-wired universal module. Both deletions are refused in `01` F9.
- Not a list of universal or culture-specific emotion categories. That is a `gap`: Fig 9 :32, "you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself."
- Not that cross-cultural averages are evidence. Fig 1 :22, "Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average."
- Not that the borrowing of core affect and emotion concepts from affective science is an import that moves the wall. It is a binding from a differing frame, provenance recorded, and adds no framework primitive (Fig 6 :21).
- Not grounded. Nothing here is asserted as settled by a person-level measurement.
- Not a theory of everything. Fig 7 :6 names "the limit: no last room."

## 5. What would settle it

A person-level ground, not a further argument. From *The Hard Problem is About Source*: an instrument that reads green "it cannot comment on the source"; "The answer is unreached until a better instrument exists." Here:

1. **Weighting test:** an instrument that separates the inherited-prior contribution from the private-history contribution to a category choice, prospectively and per person.
2. **Source-discrimination test:** an instrument that discriminates among bodily and cultural sources producing the same emotion report.
3. **Habitat substitution:** a documented change of habitat that removes the culture's payment for an emotion, with the capability measured before and after.
4. **Channel test:** a category-supply change producing a felt-quality change with the body measured unchanged.
5. Any of the seven falsifiers in `01` §4 firing against the claim.

## 6. What an expert can check in twenty minutes

1. Read Fig 11 :39-40, Symbols table :20-26, Fig 12 :117 and :123, and Fig 2 :37-38. Six short passages.
2. Check C2 against your own knowledge: are the cross-cultural cases bond differences, or only differences of intensity and display? Name one that breaks the isomer claim if you can.
3. Name one instrument that would separate inherited prior from private history in a category choice, or say why none is possible.
4. Send the refutation. A failure is more useful than an endorsement.
