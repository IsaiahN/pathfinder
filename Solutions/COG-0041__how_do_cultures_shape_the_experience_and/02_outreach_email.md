**Subject:** Check one output about culture and emotion before you decide whether the framework is worth reading

> Hi [name],
>
> I have one specific output I would like you to check against what you already know about emotion and culture. I am not asking you to read the framework first, and I am not asking you to agree with it. If the output does not survive, there is nothing else to look at.
>
> **The output (`specified`, not `grounded`):** a culture does not write an emotion into a person. It supplies three things to the same loop every person runs: the categories available for reading a bodily change, the weighting between inherited prior and private history, and a habitat of people and practices the loop ranges against. Because the weighting always carries a private-history term and is learnable from outcomes, and because the bodily ingredients are shared while the categories are arranged differently, two people in one culture can feel the same situation differently, and people in different cultures can arrive at functionally equivalent feelings by different routes. Misnaming a feeling is a failure of the channel to the person's own report, not a change in what was felt. This is a prediction of a **shape**, not a measurement, and it does not say which specific emotions are universal.
>
> **What I am not claiming:** that any particular culture determines what its members feel; that emotion categories are hard-wired modules; that a cross-cultural average tells you anything about an individual; or that the framework names which categories are universal. It returns a shape, not a name. A lexical reading of a culture's emotion words is a reading, not a source, and several sources can produce the same reading.
>
> **What would refute the shape claim:**
> 1. an emotion capability that is unchanged when the culture's payment for that emotion is removed;
> 2. an emotion report fully predicted by the inherited prior with a measured zero private-history contribution;
> 3. a cultural practice whose valence never changes when the person's situation changes;
> 4. an emotion with no shared bodily ingredient across cultures, or one with identical ingredients and identical categories yet different felt quality under a fixed self-report;
> 5. a felt-quality change with the body measured unchanged, the category supply held constant, and no channel degradation anywhere.
>
> I wrote these down before sending this. If any one holds, I will retract the claim. A one-line "this fails because..." is more useful to me than agreement. If you'd rather not engage, no reply is needed.
>
