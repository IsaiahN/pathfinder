## 0. The residual in effect terms

The loop defines R = |Γ(b,a) − o′| per slot s (Formula step 1; Symbols table, R). Map the question onto those symbols before naming any theory:

- **b**, the belief. Symbols table: "What the system takes to be the case before it acts. Formed from its own history and from what it inherited, mixed by w." A person's prior about what this bodily change means.
- **w**, the weighting. Symbols table: "How far the belief leans on private history versus inherited prior. w_A + w_B = 1, and w is learnable from the outcomes of past weightings. Two frames with identical architecture diverge because their histories and their w diverge." This single line carries the whole "without determining them entirely" half of the question.
- **a**, the action: expressing, concealing, naming, regulating, or reappraising the feeling.
- **Γ**, the library: core affect plus the learned emotion concepts of the person's culture. Fig 1 :4, "Γ: typed DSL + library", "holds structure".
- **o′**, the observation: what the situation and the body actually deliver.
- **s**, the slot: one emotion episode in one person on one occasion.

> **"The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated."** (Fig 1 :19)
> **"Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average."** (Fig 1 :22)

The phenomenon to explain is not the level of an emotion but the shape of R across two contrasts: between cultures and between people inside one culture. Observed: emotions vary in which categories exist, in what a category includes, in what elicits it, and in how it is expressed; yet the variation is neither arbitrary nor total, because people recognise one another's emotions across cultures, share a bodily affective substrate, and differ from their neighbours in the same culture.

State the residual as: *why does the gap between what a person's emotion library predicts and what the situation and body return take a culturally variable shape that is nonetheless not fixed by culture, and why does it vary again between people who share that culture?* This names no theory, no "display rule", no "self-construal", and no "construction". Those are candidate names and are withheld until the census.

**Bind the sought-for shape as an ordinary term** (Formula, "Direction"), before the run: the answer would be (C1) an account of how culture supplies categories, weighting and habitat to the loop, and (C2) an account of what in the same loop resists cultural determination, plus an instrument that discriminates failure of one from failure of the other. Until bound, that shape predicts nothing and no loop check can see it. It is now bound, so the refuter in section 4 can fire on it.

**Anchor test.** Fig 2 :29: "An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks. A measurement, an experiment, a counter, a market, a patient outcome." Fig 2 :30: "If it moves when you push on it, it is not an anchor." For this question the anchor is constrained by Fig 2 :36: "An anchor is legitimate when the question is constitutively about it." The question is constitutively about people's experience, so the person's own report is the legitimate anchor. Fig 2 :37 qualifies it: "Human judgment is the right anchor for is this a good answer for a person, because the question is about people." The same line warns that "instruments can be wrong on axes they do not measure" (Fig 2 :37-38): physiology, facial coding, and observer ratings are instruments here, not anchors, because they do not measure the felt quality the question asks about. Fig 2 :35: "Alignment is triangulation, not negotiation. A mirror cannot correct you."

This anchor choice is itself a finding with a cost: it means no third-person measurement can override a first-person report about what was felt, though several instruments may bear on which category was applied. That asymmetry is used in F5.

## 1. Domain census (before choosing any figure line)

### 1.1 Arity, symmetry, scale of R

- **Arity.** R is a four-place relation: person Γ, cultural habitat H, action a, situation-and-body o′, indexed by episode s. It carries an **ordered** bond (Fig 12 :72, "A then B; B needs A to have happened": learning the categories precedes using them in an episode) and a **comparison** bond for "shaped but not determined" (Fig 12 :87, "a threshold relation between two quantities"). It is not symmetric: person and culture are not interchangeable.
- **Symmetry.** "Culturally shaped" is a threshold relation between the inherited-prior term and the private-history term of w, not a symmetric join. The question explicitly blocks the two degenerate readings (fully determined, fully free), so the residual is a threshold question, not a presence/absence question.
- **Scale.** Three scales are in view: the single person's step-by-step emotion loop (Fig 4 one-agent panel), the culture as a population with a shared library and "something that ranks what they produce" (Fig 4 :5), and the developmental time channel across a life. The membrane is "Not a wall: a filter with a direction" (Fig 4 :21), and "Only methods cross up. Only head starts come down." (Fig 4 :22). The transform is lossy, so R_T must be measured (Fig 4 :30).

### 1.2 One line per domain: actors, currency, anchor

A domain is admitted only if an anchor can be named. Where none can be named, it is not a domain yet.

|---|---|---|---|---|

X1 fails the anchor test in the form stated. This is a structural exclusion, not a claim that biology is irrelevant: the shared affective atoms are admitted inside D3. X2 is not admitted because it deletes terms that are already present in the loop (b's private history, w's learnability, the shared atoms). Both exclusions are made before figure lines are applied to them.

### 1.3 Partition or union

D1 and D3 overlap: Γ is inside the person's loop, so their overlap is a compound with its own loop. D2 and D4 overlap: interoception is calibrated inside a habitat, so that overlap is a second compound. The whole question is a **union**, not a partition.

> **"And when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again."** (Fig 9 :54)
> **"A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones."** (Fig 9 :55)
> **"What remains is smaller and it is also categorically different: a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either."** (Fig 9 :57)

The unbundled conditions are: (C1) a culture supplies the categories, weighting and habitat that constrain emotion, and (C2) the same loop, shared atoms, and private history keep it from being culturally fixed. A person failing C1 (no shared category to read a feeling with) and a person failing C2 (a feeling that will not be regulated by any cultural label) are not different degrees of one failure; they are different compounds and need different instruments. The essay *The Library of Babel* states the same stop condition: "Perhaps your problem was a bundle or conflation of several problems masquerading as one? You will need to search all of those problems individually to find their answers and then combine them in a satisfactory way."

### 1.4 Dependency order (Fig 3)

> **"The chain is ordered by dependency, not by importance."** (Fig 3 :20)
> **"Why the middle link is where chains usually break."** (Fig 3 :24)
> **"A link with no instrument is not a link that works. It is a link nobody has looked at."** (Fig 3 :27)

Order for this question: 1 perception (can the person read their bodily state?) → 2 vocabulary (do they have a category for what the state affords?) → 3 objective (can they say what a good regulation of it would count as?) → 4 planning (expression, concealment, reappraisal) → 5 learn and carry. The middle link, having a category for what the state affords and saying what would count as progress, is the one with no instrument in the classical literature. Fig 12 :115 says that having terms for what a situation affords "was taken for a dictionary and it is a grammar." Most research effort lands above the break, where the measurements already exist (Fig 3 :29-30).

### 1.5 Strain test

An early attempt to apply Fig 13's six physical terms directly to emotion only fit by calling core affect a kind of energy and an emotion category a kind of spectrum. That is a strain. By the canonical rule (a reading that makes figures contradict one another has misidentified its domain), Fig 13 does **not** govern the contents of an emotion library. It is re-censused to the honest-stop role only: the set is closed, the arrangements are not, and "Unreachable is a verdict here, not a silence" (Fig 13 :134). This strain (S1) is recorded in section 6. No other figure needed a strain after re-census.

## 3. Findings

Mode tag on each: `general` if structural, `specified` if a falsifiable prediction about emotion and culture, `grounded` only if settled by a measurement. None is `grounded`.

### F1: Emotion is a property of person-and-culture, not of the person alone

**Mode: `specified`. Domains D1, D2. Status: triangulated (Fig 11 and Fig 4, low overlap: habitat vs membrane).**

> **"So the answerable question is not what intelligence is, but what makes a system capable. And capability is a property of agent-and-habitat, never of the agent alone."** (Fig 11 :39)
> **"A system cannot be made more capable by improving the agent in isolation. An improvement that does not change contact changes nothing, however much it improves."** (Fig 11 :40)
> **"Only the actors, the currency and the anchor change."** (Fig 4 :20)

The emotion-generating loop is the kernel that does not change with size (Fig 4 :19-20); what changes with culture is the cast, the currency, and what settles an episode. So a culture can shape the contents of the loop without changing the loop. The habit of answering with a list of person-internal properties ("this person is an angry person", "this culture is an emotional culture") is an agent-only improvement that may not change contact.

The world corroborates at two strata. Markus and Kitayama 1991 write that "both the expression and the experience of emotions and motives may be significantly shaped and governed by a consideration of the reactions of others." Mesquita 2023 puts the same point in effect terms: "culture is not an independent variable that somehow predicts or causes emotions. One way to understand culture is as patterns of emotions and emotional practices." These are two fields (cultural psychology and affective science) with partially different evidence pools, so the corroboration is stronger than a single pool, but still not independent of the framework author's reading.

**Refuter:** a measured emotion capability that is invariant across a habitat substitution which demonstrably removes the culture's payment for that emotion. If it survives, F1 is false.

### F2: Culture enters through the weighting w, but private history keeps divergence

**Mode: `specified` for the person-level claim, `general` for the structural form. Domain D1. Status: triangulated (Symbols table w and b, plus Fig 2 :31-32 on independence, low overlap).**

> Symbols table :24-26: "How far the belief leans on private history versus inherited prior. w_A + w_B = 1, and w is learnable from the outcomes of past weightings. Two frames with identical architecture diverge because their histories and their w diverge."
> Symbols table :20-21: "What the system takes to be the case before it acts. Formed from its own history and from what it inherited, mixed by w."
> **"Independence, between two frames, is how little they draw on the same evidence. Two people reading the same report are not two frames; two instruments pointed at the same thing are."** (Fig 2 :31-32)

This is the figure-system's direct answer to "without determining them entirely." Culture supplies the inherited prior and part of w, but the private-history term is always in the mix, w is learnable from outcomes, and two frames with identical architecture diverge because their histories and their w diverge. Determination would require w to be fixed at the inherited pole and the private-history term to be zero. The loop does not allow that: b is defined as a mixture.

The world corroborates with unusual precision. Markus and Kitayama 1991: "Within a given culture, however, individuals will vary in the extent to which they are good cultural representatives and construe the self in the mandated way." Barrett 2009: "Variability exists across cultures, across individuals within the same culture, and within an individual across instances." Mesquita 2023: "There is quite a lot of malleability both between cultures and within any single person." Three sources, two distinct pools (the culture-and-self literature and the constructionist emotion literature), but they agree on the shape the figures already give.

**Refuter:** a case in which a person's emotion report is fully predicted by the inherited prior with a measured zero private-history contribution, and in which two people with identical architecture and different histories converge on the identical emotion for the identical stimulus. That would make culture determining and falsify the w term as used here.

### F3: The habitat is enumerated, never composed, and valence is relative to the residual

**Mode: `specified`. Domain D2. Status: triangulated (Fig 11 and Fig 12 :119, low overlap: field of contact vs composition structures).**

> **"The habitat is enumerated, never composed."** (Fig 11 :43)
> **"List everything in contact with the residual, then what is in contact with those, and outward until the cascade stops mattering."** (Fig 11 :44)
> **"You do not invent the list. You read it off the world, and what you cannot perceive or measure yet is the residual."** (Fig 11 :48)
> **"An actor’s valence is relative to the residual, not a property it carries."** (Fig 11 :49)
> **"The same actor is a benefactor to one residual and a malfactor to another, so mutualism and parasitism are relations rather than kinds."** (Fig 11 :50)

A culture cannot be read off a list of national traits. The habitat of an emotion episode is enumerated: caregivers, peers, language, roles, the physical setting, norms about who may feel what, and the second-order effects of those, outward until the cascade stops mattering. A practice that helps one person regulate grief can harm another in a different residual, so there is no standing list of "good" cultural emotions. Fig 11 :61 adds the asymmetry: "The seat’s power is asymmetric. Introduction is available; exclusion is not." A person can be introduced to a new category or practice; they cannot step out of the habitat that formed them by an act of will.

Mesquita's OURS model is the world's name for this habitat dependence: emotions are "OUtside the person, Relational, and Situated" (New Yorker, quoting Mesquita). Her Public Books interview is more specific: "In instances of emotions, there is always a part that is relational." And the counter-voice in the New Yorker is itself figure-consistent: "the OURS model of emotion turns out to be a common human inheritance." That is convergent derivation at maximum independence (Fig 8 :50), a critic arriving at the habitat claim from philosophy of language.

**Refuter:** a cultural practice whose measured valence is invariant across a documented change of the person's residual. If no such invariance ever fails, F3 weakens to `general`.

### F4: Shared atoms, different bonds: cultures compose isomers of emotion

**Mode: `specified`. Domains D2, D3. Status: triangulated (Fig 12 and Fig 13 :121-125, low overlap: bond structures vs convergent routes).**

> **"isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."** (Fig 12 :117)
> **"functional group. A substructure whose presence fixes the outcome. The group says what kind; the qualifier says which member."** (Fig 12 :123)
> **"Different routes can arrive at the same substance."** (Fig 13 :121)
> **"the same ingredients under a different bond are a different substance"** (Fig 13 :124-125)

Barrett 2009 supplies the shared atoms from a different field: "At the psychological level, the ingredients that make up the recipes might be universal". Her three primitives (core affect, an emotion-concept system, controlled attention) are the atoms; the "recipe" is the bond arrangement. The world then supplies the isomer cases: in the USA sadness and anger are separate categories, while in Turkey "sadness and anger are properties of a single emotion category called 'kizginlik'" (Barrett 2009, reporting Mesquita 1993); sadness is "more akin to physical agony in Russian but the experience of loss in the USA"; anger involves "psychological distance from others in the USA but increasing proximity and closeness in Japan."

Under Fig 12 these are not different amounts of one substance. They are the same atoms under a different bond, hence different substances. Where two cultures arrive at functionally equivalent emotions by different routes, Fig 13 :121 makes that convergent derivation, which is corroboration at maximum independence, not a shared pool (Fig 8 :50). Where no shared atom can be found for some culturally specific category, the isomer claim fails for that category and it becomes a genuine import question (F8).

**Refuter:** an emotion whose atomic ingredients differ across cultures with no shared substrate, or two episodes with identical atoms and identical bonds yet different experienced quality with the report anchor held fixed. Either fires F4.

### F5: Misinterpretation is a channel failure, not a change of the ground

**Mode: `specified`. Domain D4. Status: triangulated (Fig 10 and Fig 2 :36-38, low overlap: ground maintainer vs anchor legitimacy).**

> **"the ground does not decay, the channel does"** (Fig 10 :24)
> **"stale percepts, dropped transients, saturated metrics, pooled readings"** (Fig 10 :25-26)
> **"the output is not a verdict, it is you have lost touch"** (Fig 10 :28)
> **"→ keep the anchor reachable"** (Fig 10 :29)

When a person says "I thought I was scared, but I was actually excited", the figure reading is that the channel degraded, not that the body changed after the fact. The body delivered core affect; the category applied to it was wrong. Cultures supply the categories that calibrate or distort that channel, and the same machinery that lets a person name a feeling lets them misname it. The source essay makes the same point in instrument terms: an instrument that reads green "cannot comment on the source", and "The answer is unreached until a better instrument exists." Applied here, a report of "anger" is a reading, not a source; several sources can produce it.

This is also where the anchor decision of section 0 pays. The person's report is the legitimate anchor for a question about their experience, so a third-party observer cannot correct it. But the *category* applied is an instrument and can be wrong on an axis it does not measure. F5 keeps those two apart: felt quality is anchored by the person, category assignment is corrected by better discrimination.

**Refuter:** a case in which the person's report of felt quality changes while the bodily ground is independently measured as unchanged and the available category supply is held constant, with no channel degradation demonstrable. That would break the channel reading.

### F6: Never explain this by averaging across people or cultures

**Mode: `general` with a `specified` methodological consequence. Domains all. Status: triangulated (Fig 1 and Fig 9, low overlap).**

> **"The ground enters as the only metric."** (Fig 1 :23)
> **"predicates minted, and credibility accrued"** (Fig 1 :26)
> **"They are frame-internal. A frame cannot score itself with a quantity it also produces."** (Fig 1 :27)
> **"Use filters for the budget and witnesses for the verdict. Never let a filter hand you a verdict."** (Fig 9 :60)

A cross-cultural mean of "emotion intensity" or a national ranking on "emotional expressiveness" scores the frame with a quantity the frame produces. It is a filter for where to look and a budget instrument, not a witness. The specified consequence: any claimed answer to COG-0041 that is an average across cultures has already thrown away the individual variation that is exactly the "not determined entirely" phenomenon (Fig 1 :22). The New Yorker critic makes the same methodological point from outside the framework: "the sense in which emotions are culturally specific isn't a terribly exciting one. In the real world, differences are commonplace but don't defy understanding." Averages hide the commonplace differences.

**Refuter:** an averaged cross-cultural statistic that predicts, prospectively and per named individual, which category that person will apply to a novel bodily state. If one exists, F6's methodological consequence is false.

### F7: The question is a union, so experience and interpretation must be unbundled

**Mode: `specified`. Domains all. Status: triangulated (Fig 9 and the Library of Babel essay, but the essay is framework-internal so the external witness count is one).**

Fig 9 :54, :55 and :57 are quoted in section 1.3. The two conditions have different instruments:

- A C1 failure shows up as a missing or misapplied category, a saturated or pooled reading of one's own bodily state, and a closure that keeps handing itself the same room (Fig 10 :26, Fig 7 :12).
- A C2 failure shows up as a report that the inherited prior overwhelms private history, or as a reaching failure where no new category can enter from a differing closure (Fig 6 :21, Symbols table w).

A single lever, for example "teach the culture's emotion words", clears the region where only one condition holds and leaves the intersection standing (Fig 9 :55). F7 is the reason verdicts in `09_watson_answer.md` are kept separate per domain and never averaged.

**Refuter:** a single intervention that restores both conditions in both failure types with no categorically different residue left over. That would collapse the union into a partition.

### F8: Which specific categories are universal or culture-specific is a gap, not a hidden answer

**Mode: `general`. Status: figure-governed.**

> **"you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself"** (Fig 9 :32)
> **"Unreachable is a verdict here, not a silence,"** (Fig 13 :134)
> **"So a search that finds nothing is never a proof of absence."** (Fig 8 :39)

After a genuine re-census no figure line governs the specific inventory of universal emotion categories, nor which category a given culture holds at a given historical moment. The framework returns the shape: person-and-habitat, mixed b, learnable w, shared atoms with different bonds, an enumerable habitat, and a channel that can degrade. It cannot name the items. The New Yorker review shows exactly why naming is a ground question: Luganda "use the same word, okusunguwala, for 'anger' and 'sadness'", yet "Luganda speakers dispute her account, and note that the language readily marks the distinction between the two." A lexical reading does not discriminate the source. What is owed is a better instrument, not a verdict.

**Refuter:** a source-discriminating instrument that names the slot, or a figure re-census that admits a seventh primitive governing emotion categories. Until one fires, this row stays `gap`.

### F9: Conflict between the culture literature and the figures, and how it was resolved

**Mode: `general`. Status: figure-governed.**

Mesquita's strong constructionist formulation appears to say that culture fully makes the emotion. Mesquita in the New Yorker: "people's emotional lives are different, but emotions themselves are the same" was her earlier view, which she abandoned; her later view treats emotions as "instilled in us by our parents and other cultural agents." Read as a total-cause claim, that conflicts with the Symbols table's definition of b as a mixture and with Fig 11 :39-40. A conflict between world and figures sends the derivation back to the census; it never flips the verdict. Re-census R2 in section 6 resolves it: the constructionist claim is re-mapped to D2 and D3 (the habitat supplies the category library and the weighting), not to the deletion of the private-history term or the shared atoms. Under that mapping the strong constructionist literature corroborates C1 and carries zero evidentiary weight for the deletion of C2, because the deletion is not a claim the figures allow to be bound.

The universalist literature supplies the other pole. Barrett 2009 describes the basic-emotion assumption that "observed variability in emotional responding is the result of epiphenomenal social factors, like display rules". The New Yorker's critic presses the same point: "if Mesquita is right that people, wherever they come from, can reliably make their emotions intelligible to others, how culturally specific can those emotions really be?" That corroborates C2 (the not-entirely half) from a genuinely different evidence pool. Both poles are needed; neither is allowed to flip the verdict.

## 4. Pre-registered refuter (written before the conclusion)

Per the design-instrument rule: "a falsifier written before the run is a design instrument, not only an honesty device." The shape claim is refuted if any of the following is observed:

1. An emotion capability is invariant across a habitat substitution that removes the culture's payment for that emotion (breaks F1, Fig 11 :39-40).
2. A person's emotion report is fully predicted by the inherited prior with a measured zero private-history contribution, and two differently-history'd frames converge on the identical emotion (breaks F2, Symbols table w).
3. A cultural practice's valence is invariant across a documented change of the person's residual (breaks F3, Fig 11 :49-51).
4. An emotion is found with no shared atomic ingredient across cultures, or with identical atoms and bonds yet different felt quality under a fixed report anchor (breaks F4, Fig 12 :117).
5. Felt quality changes while the bodily ground is measured unchanged, the category supply is constant, and no channel degradation is demonstrable (breaks F5, Fig 10 :24).
6. A single lever restores both conditions with no categorically different residue (breaks F7, Fig 9 :57).
7. Fig 13's six terms are derived from five of them, or Fig 6's idempotence fails inside a closed set (breaks the figures themselves, not just this reading).

No part of this solution may be upgraded to `grounded` until at least one of these has been run and has failed to fire, and until a person-level measurement has paid for the shape.

## 5. Recommendation

1. Lead with the residual and the refuter, not with a theory of emotion or a catalogue of cultural differences (Fig 10 :52, "Bring a characterised residual, never the source").
2. Keep the two conditions separate in every report. A blended "cultural emotion score" is a pooled reading (Fig 10 :26) and destroys the diagnostic.
3. Ask, at each handoff, which link the chain stops at and whether that was measured (Fig 3 :29). Do not fund above the break.
4. Treat any list of emotion categories as Γ, that is, as a candidate library whose reach must be tested against the ground, never as the answer (Fig 1 :27).
5. Use the emotion literature as a budget filter and as corroborating witnesses, not as the verdict. The culture-and-self pool and the constructionist pool overlap partly (Fig 2 :31), and the New Yorker critic is the low-overlap witness (Fig 8 :50).
6. Offer the formula as a method after a verdict is recorded (Fig 4 :22). Each user writes their own refuter and states their own mode.
