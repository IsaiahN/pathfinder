## What in the wider literature bears on this question

1. **The weighting term is the whole "without determining" answer.** Barrett 2009 opens with "Variability exists across cultures, across individuals within the same culture, and within an individual across instances." Markus and Kitayama 1991 add that within one culture "individuals will vary in the extent to which they are good cultural representatives." These are the world's versions of b and w. They are the strongest corroboration for F2.
2. **The culture pool conflicts with itself, which is a witness to the union, not a defect.** Mesquita's strong social-constructionist position and the New Yorker's philosophical critique pull in opposite directions. The figures do not choose between them; they unbundle the question into C1 (habitat and categories) and C2 (shared atoms and private history) and let both poles be right about a different condition (F9).
3. **The lexical fallacy is a source-discrimination failure.** The New Yorker reports that Luganda speakers "use the same word, okusunguwala, for 'anger' and 'sadness'", and then that "Luganda speakers dispute her account, and note that the language readily marks the distinction between the two." One reading, several possible sources. This is the essay's metamerism case in a new field and it is why F8 is a `gap`, not a name.
4. **The recipe is the bond, not the ingredients.** Barrett 2009: "At the psychological level, the ingredients that make up the recipes might be universal". Also: "the products of the various recipes are not universal (although they are not infinitely variable or arbitrary)." That is Fig 12 :117 and Fig 12 :123 written in the vocabulary of affective science.
5. **The OURS/MINE contrast is a binding, not a universal inventory.** Mesquita's distinction is a useful name for the habitat half of the union, but the New Yorker critic's line "the OURS model of emotion turns out to be a common human inheritance" shows convergent derivation from a different pool (Fig 8 :50). Neither source licenses a claim about which specific categories are universal.

## The two-stage offer

Stage 1 is the residual, the shape claim, and the pre-registered refuter. Record the verdict before showing any theory (Fig 10 :52, "Bring a characterised residual, never the source"). Stage 2 is opt-in: if the reader is curious, offer the three links. Their later agreement is never pooled as corroboration, because by then they hold the framework's interior (Fig 2 :40, "agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool"). Stage 3 offers the formula as a generator, not an answer (Fig 4 :22), with the instruction that each user writes their own refuter and states their own mode.

**Hard precondition:** stages 1 to 3 only work if the stage-1 output survives. As drafted, it is a shape and not a measurement, and the five refuters are live. Repair any finding the checker fires before the text leaves your machine.
