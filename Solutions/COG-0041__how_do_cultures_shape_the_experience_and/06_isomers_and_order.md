# Supporting derivation: shared atoms, cultural bonds, and order

**Mode: `specified`.** This is a simulated derivation checked against the figures, not a ground reading (see `05_what_shape_means.md` §6).

## RESIDUAL

The question asks how cultures shape emotion without determining it. Two supporting questions are owed: (1) under what composition rule can the same bodily stuff yield different emotions across cultures and the same emotion by different cultural routes, and (2) in what order do culture, category, and bodily state enter an episode, so that a failure can be located rather than merely named.

## DERIVATION

### Q1 - Is a culturally specific emotion an isomer of a shared one?

- Fig 12 :117: "isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."
- Barrett 2009 supplies the atomic layer: "At the psychological level, the ingredients that make up the recipes might be universal" and the primitives are core affect, an emotion-concept system, and controlled attention.
- The world supplies the bond differences. In the USA sadness and anger are separate categories; in Turkey "sadness and anger are properties of a single emotion category called 'kizginlik'". Sadness is "more akin to physical agony in Russian but the experience of loss in the USA"; anger involves "psychological distance from others in the USA but increasing proximity and closeness in Japan" (Barrett 2009).
- ⇒ Under Fig 12 these are **isomers**: the same affective atoms under a different bonding of concept and situation, producing a different substance. The difference is invisible in a formula that lists only "valence, arousal, label", which is the flat-form failure Fig 12 names.
- **Falsifier, written before the run:** if a category can be shown to have no shared atomic ingredient with any category in another culture, or if two episodes with identical atoms and identical bonds yield different felt quality under a fixed self-report anchor, the isomer claim fails.

### Q2 - When is it only a functional group instead?

- Fig 12 :123: "functional group. A substructure whose presence fixes the outcome. The group says what kind; the qualifier says which member."
- Fig 13 :121: "Different routes can arrive at the same substance." Fig 8 :50: "Two frames can hold the same atom by convergence, reaching it separately, which is corroboration at maximum independence."
- If the claim is weakened to "every culture has something like grief at a loss", the shared substructure is the functional group (bodily perturbation plus a relational category), and the cultural qualifier says which member. Two cultures arriving at functionally equivalent grief by different routes are a convergent derivation, not an import (Fig 8 :51-52).
- ⇒ The strong claim (isomer over a shared atom set) and the weak claim (functional group with cultural qualifiers) are different claims with different instruments. Keep them separate; never average them.

### Q3 - What is the order of entry?

- Fig 12 :72 defines → : "A then B; B needs A to have happened."
- Fig 3 :20: "The chain is ordered by dependency, not by importance."
- Order for an emotion episode under this derivation: 1 bodily change and core affect (perception) → 2 availability of a category (vocabulary) → 3 what would count as a good regulation of the state (objective) → 4 expression, concealment, or reappraisal (planning) → 5 learn and carry (settle and compose at the next level).
- The middle link, category and objective, is where Fig 3 :24-27 says chains usually break and where no instrument exists. Markus and Kitayama 1991 give the world version: "both the expression and the experience of emotions and motives may be significantly shaped and governed by a consideration of the reactions of others" - the category is socially supplied before the episode is complete.
- **Falsifier:** a documented episode in which a person applies a category to a bodily change before any category was available to them, or in which planning succeeds while perception and vocabulary are measured absent. That breaks the dependency order.

### Q4 - What is ⋛ doing here, and is there a type strain?

- Fig 12 :87: "a threshold relation between two quantities." Operators table :48-51: "a threshold relation between two quantities."
- The "without determining entirely" half of the question is a threshold between the inherited-prior term and the private-history term of w: shaping is real when the prior term is large, determination would require the private term to be zero.
- Symbols table: "Two frames with identical architecture diverge because their histories and their w diverge." So the threshold is not a static property of a person but a learnable parameter.
- **Type caveat, still open:** w is a scalar in the framework, but the world sources do not supply a measurement of it. Calling the cultural contribution "the prior term of w" is a binding from this derivation onto the framework symbol, not a grounded identification. Record it as `specified`, unmeasured. Triangulated for the structural role (Symbols table plus Fig 2 :31 on independence); single-source for the numeric identification.

## CONCLUSION

The two supporting readings are: culturally specific emotions are **isomers over a shared affective atom set**, with the weaker **functional-group** reading available when only a shared substructure is claimed; and the episode has a **dependency order** whose middle link (category and objective) is the one most likely to be broken and least likely to be measured. The comparator that makes "shaped but not determined" precise is the weighting w, a learnable threshold between inherited prior and private history, bound here as an ordinary term and still unmeasured. No new primitive was needed, no import moves the wall, and both claims carry falsifiers.
