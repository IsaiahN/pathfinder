# Expert verification packet - the Tether on adaptation evidence

**What this is:** a claim in a form an ecologist, evolutionary geneticist, or animal-behavior researcher can check. Mode, derivation and refuters are stated before the conclusion, so a reader knows what to look for and what would settle it.

**Mode: `specified` - not `grounded`.** No common-garden, transplant, or sequence result is claimed. This is a shape and a route, not a result. It is a simulated derivation checked for internal consistency against the figures, not a ground reading (`05_what_shape_means.md` section 7).

**Boundary of the check.** The first three sections are the whole stage-1 ask. Provenance, links and the method offer come later and only if a verdict on stage 1 is recorded first.

---

## 1. The claim, in one paragraph

Persistence after environmental change is a reading, not a source. A heritable change, a within-lifetime behavioral or physiological adjustment, and a move to a different habitat can all produce the same low residual, so the evidence that distinguishes them must change one of three state variables and hold the other two fixed. For genetic adaptation, the discriminating evidence is heritable-state change across generations paid by a fitness assay, because selection alone narrows what is already in the library and cannot add a heritable atom. For behavioral flexibility, it is a reversible reaction norm or expression and behavior change with genotype and habitat held, the same atoms under a different bond. For movement, it is a substituted field of contact, enumerated as conditions, actors and relations, with genomic and controller continuity. The three are a union with overlapping compounds, so the verdicts stay separate and are never averaged. The anchor is a fitness or survival count under controlled conditions, not agreement about mechanism. The claim is a shape that any candidate answer must have; it does not name which allele, behavior, or habitat acted in any particular case.

- **Refuter:** a single reading of persistence that uniquely determines the source with no controlled comparison; or a mechanism attribution that changes when the examining community is told a different story about the same fitness data. Either fires against C1.

### C2 - Framework-internal derivation plus two field-facing checks

- **Statement:** only genetic change adds heritable atoms; flexibility keeps the atoms and changes the bond, in the same habitat; therefore the two need different instruments.
- **Source, verbatim:**
  - Fig 6 :21 - *"MINT composes inside the closure and can never add an atom."* Fig 6 :14 - *"the only operator that moves the wall"* (import).
  - Fig 12 :117 - *"isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."* Fig 12 :5 - *"A set of ingredients is not one. The bond is the rest of the information, and it is the half nothing was recording."*
  - Fig 11 :50 - *"The same actor is a benefactor to one residual and a malfactor to another, so mutualism and parasitism are relations rather than kinds."*
- **What a researcher can check against their own knowledge:** Schlötterer et al. 2015 write that E&R *"can identify selected variants in controlled and replicable experimental settings"*. Sommer 2020 writes that *"Phenotypic plasticity is defined as the property of organisms to produce distinct phenotypes in response to environmental variation."* Do those two sentences not already name the two different instruments, and is a trait slope alone sufficient to choose between them? A "yes" to the second question is a real refutation of the mapping.
- **Refuter:** a documented genetic adaptation with no heritable state change across generations while the trait persists in a common garden; or a plastic response with the same genotype, same measured environment and same history but no controller-state difference. Either breaks C2.

### C3 - Habitat, union and method

- **Statement:** movement substitutes a habitat rather than removing the organism's library; the three conditions form a union with overlapping compounds; the habitat is enumerated before it is used as an explanation; averages are filters, not witnesses.
- **Source, verbatim:**
  - Fig 11 :66 - *"Isolation is not removal of the habitat. It is substitution of one habitat for another."* Fig 11 :67 - *"Move the residual and you must reproduce everything its goal required, and you inherit the new habitat’s actors, which you did not choose."*
  - Fig 11 :43 - *"The habitat is enumerated, never composed."*
  - Fig 9 :57 - *"What remains is smaller and it is also categorically different: a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either."*
  - Fig 9 :60 - *"Use filters for the budget and witnesses for the verdict. Never let a filter hand you a verdict."*
- **What a researcher can check:** Donelson et al. 2019 state that a species *"can move to new areas that become available within their thermal niche"* and that the three responses *"are not mutually exclusive"*. Luqman et al. 2023 show that post-glacial range shifts and adaptive allele change were coupled in *Dianthus sylvestris*. Is there a case where movement and heritable change were separated cleanly, and what instrument did it? A documented clean separation is consistent with C3; a demonstration that one lever clears every overlap would refute it.
- **Refuter:** a habitat substitution with no change in the enumerated actor and condition list or occupancy coordinates; or one intervention that separates all three sources in every overlap and leaves no categorically different residue; or an averaged statistic that prospectively identifies the source per slot and survives transplant. Any fires against C3.

## 3. What is explicitly not claimed

- Not `grounded`. No fitness measurement has paid for any part of this.
- Not a theory of everything about ecology, and no final or unique theory claim.
- Not an average. No claim about the percentage of variance attributable to genes, plasticity, or range shift.
- Not that genes, learning, or habitat are irrelevant. They are the three state variables; the claim is that no one of them settles source on its own.
- Not that plasticity leads adaptation or slows it. Ho and Zhang 2018 and Botero 2026 report different effects at different strata; both are recorded, neither is used as the verdict.
- Not that the habitat is the ground. Fig 11 :73 - *"The habitat is not the ground. The habitat is where actors come from and it updates; the ground is what settles, and it does not."*
- Not that any specific allele, behavior, or habitat is the answer. That is a `gap` at the instrument boundary, not a hidden answer.
- Not that the ecology reviews independently corroborate the framework. They corroborate the instrument shape from different fields; the framework essays share one evidence pool with the figures.
- Not that this packet, produced with an LLM, is submittable or publishable as authored work. See the constraint in `04_links_and_distribution.md`.

## 5. What would settle it

A prospective, per-population study that measures C1, C2 and C3 separately, records bonds as well as atoms, enumerates the habitat, and reports which condition failed before the response. Concretely:

1. **Heritable test:** did allele frequencies or heritable regulatory state change between ancestral and descendant samples, and did a common garden or fitness assay pay for it? E&R and resurrection genomics are candidate instruments (Fig 6 :14, :21).
2. **Controller test:** with genotype and habitat held, did the phenotype change reversibly? Reaction norms and expression time courses are candidate instruments (Fig 12 :117).
3. **Contact test:** did the enumerated conditions, actors and relations change, with occupancy records and genomic continuity? Mark-recapture, telemetry and transplant survival are candidate instruments (Fig 11 :43, :66).
4. **Source-discriminating instrument:** can any single reading distinguish the three without a controlled comparison? The source essay says a reading alone *"cannot comment on the source"*. If yes, the shape fails.

## 6. What an expert can check in twenty minutes

1. Read Fig 1 :23, Fig 2 :29 and :35, Fig 11 :36 and :66, Fig 6 :21, Fig 12 :117, Fig 9 :57 and :60. Nine short passages, about nine minutes.
2. Check C2 against your own knowledge: is a trait slope ever sufficient to distinguish allele-frequency change from a reaction norm? About four minutes.
3. Name one instrument that would separate movement from in-situ adaptation when both leave the same phenotype reading, or say why none exists. About five minutes.
4. Send the refutation. A failure is more useful than an endorsement.
