## 0. The question under audit, in effect terms

> Are cross-functional teams more innovative, or do they create conflict and slow decision-making?

- **Option A:** cross-functional composition increases the production of new, settled, reusable terms (innovation).
- **Option B:** the same composition increases the cost of reaching a settling verdict (conflict, slow decisions).
- **What an answer would look like (bound as an ordinary term, formula step "Direction"):** a relation between team-composition diversity (independence of the members' closures) and two separate outputs, surplus terms on one side and settling cost on the other, each with a stated ground and a stated refuter. Committing to this shape before the run makes the preference refusable.

The residual is not "which slogan is true". It is: what does mixing functions do to the reach of the team, and what does it do to the cost of settling a decision? Those are two questions with two anchors.

## 1. Residual as effect

**R (arity, symmetry, scale).** Arity: two outcomes over one composition variable, so a **union compound**, not one hard question. Fig 9 :4-5: "are not one hard question but several well-formed ones. Split it rather than search." Fig 9 :55: "A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones." Fig 9 :57: "a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either." Symmetry: the innovation path is asymmetric and directional (a surplus that appears only at the boundary between closures); the conflict path is a drift with no anchor; decision is ordered in time. Scale: innovation lives at the population/membrane scale (Fig 4), decision at the single-room scale (Fig 7).

The two outcomes are **not averaged**. Fig 1 :19: "The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated." Fig 1 :22: "Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average." A single "teams are net positive" score would throw away the only signal that says where to look.

## 4. Findings F1 to F8

### F1 — The question is a bundled union and must be split (decisive, structural)

Fig 9 :4-5: "are not one hard question but several well-formed ones. Split it rather than search." Fig 9 :54: "And when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again." Fig 9 :55: "A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones." Fig 9 :57: "a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either."

"More innovative" and "more conflictful/slower" are not opposite ends of one scale. They are outputs of two different loops with two different anchors. Asking which one wins before splitting is asking for a verdict from an unbundled problem. Fig 9 :60: "Use filters for the budget and witnesses for the verdict. Never let a filter hand you a verdict."

**Refuter:** show that innovation output and decision cost are one quantity in one currency, so that a single average is a sufficient statistic. Fig 12 :125 says otherwise: "A composition costs in two currencies, and they do not add."

### F2 — Low overlap is necessary for surplus, but an undirected union is mute (innovation domain)

Fig 8 :45: "Two frames together reach what neither reaches alone." Fig 8 :46: "Composition crosses the boundary between them, and that surplus is where imports live." Fig 8 :19-21: "closures differ, so a surplus exists / but no residual is described / nothing was aimed; the union is mute." Fig 8 :53: "Residual first, frame second."

A cross-functional team has differing closures, which is a **precondition** for surplus. It is not a sufficient condition. If no residual is described and nothing is aimed, the union is mute: the team has more vocabulary and no mint. This refuses the folk claim that cross-functional composition is automatically more innovative. Fig 6 :23: "No frame escapes its own closure. The bound moves only on contact with a frame whose closure differs." Fig 8 :32 (closure is idempotent): "no search, however long, adds an atom."

**Refuter:** exhibit a cross-functional team with no described residual and no external ground that reliably produces novel settled terms. That would break the "nothing was aimed; the union is mute" clause.

### F3 — Independence is one quantity read twice: the trade-off is structural, not empirical noise

Fig 8 :47: "Independence is one quantity read twice." Fig 8 :48-49: "How much two frames draw on the same evidence decides both whether their agreement means anything and whether their union has any surplus. Two frames that overlap almost completely are one frame wearing two names." Fig 2 :39: "Correlation has valence." Fig 2 :40: "disqualifying for a verdict: agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool." Fig 2 :41: "efficient for a convention: agreement about a shared meaning is the product, not a corruption of it."

The same low overlap that creates the innovation surplus makes agreement expensive, and also makes any agreement that does converge carry information. High overlap (a functionally homogeneous team) agrees cheaply, which is "efficient for a convention", but that agreement is "worthless" as a verdict about the world. So the two halves of the user's question are coupled: the parameter that buys reach also buys deliberation cost. "More innovative OR more conflict" is not an empirical accident; it is one parameter seen from two sides.

**Refuter:** show that low overlap increases surplus while also decreasing settling cost (or the reverse), i.e. that independence is two independent parameters. That breaks Fig 8 :47.

### F4 — Conflict is the signature of an unanchored room, not of difference itself

Fig 2 :35: "Alignment is triangulation, not negotiation. A mirror cannot correct you." Fig 2 :13-15, the mutual-update collapse: "no anchor; each updates on the others", "they converge, and what they converge on", "is whoever had the most influence." Fig 2 :45: "Frames range against the ground. They do not negotiate with it." Fig 2 :29: "A measurement, an experiment, a counter, a market, a patient outcome."

When a cross-functional room has no external ground, its members update on one another and converge on whoever had the most influence. That is precisely the reported symptom: conflict plus slow, influence-driven decisions. The figure does not blame difference; it blames the missing anchor. With an external ground, low overlap becomes triangulation: each member ranges against the ground separately, and agreement becomes meaningful (Fig 2 :31-32: "Two people reading the same report are not two frames; two instruments pointed at the same thing are."). Homogeneous teams avoid the symptom partly because they share an anchor implicitly, but then their agreement cannot settle a verdict about the world.

**Refuter:** show a cross-functional room with no external ground whose decisions settle by evidence rather than influence, reliably, across tasks. That breaks the mutual-update collapse as applied here.

### F5 — Two currencies: description cost and action cost

Fig 12 :125: "A composition costs in two currencies, and they do not add." Fig 12 :126-127: "Bits price the description. Actions price finding out whether it holds. They are in different units, so a system holding one accounting cannot compare them and will not notice the shortfall." Fig 12 :111: "A vocabulary without bonds can name a state and cannot name a route to one."

"Innovation" is mostly a bits cost (how much description the new arrangement saves). "Conflict and slow decision-making" is mostly an actions cost (how many rounds of checking before the room settles). A management dashboard that reports only one is "holding one accounting" and "will not notice the shortfall". Fig 9 :60 again forbids letting the filter hand the verdict.

**Refuter:** give a single unit in which both the information value of a novel term and the deliberation time to settle it are commensurable, with an operational conversion. Until then the two are held apart.

### F6 — Which function helps depends on the residual, not on the function

Fig 11 :49: "An actor's valence is relative to the residual, not a property it carries." Fig 11 :50: "The same actor is a benefactor to one residual and a malfactor to another, so mutualism and parasitism are relations rather than kinds." Fig 11 :39: "capability is a property of agent-and-habitat, never of the agent alone." Fig 11 :40: "An improvement that does not change contact changes nothing, however much it improves."

There is no standing list of "innovative functions" or "slowing functions". A legal review may be a benefactor to a regulatory residual and a malfactor to a speed residual. Adding a function changes contact only if it touches the residual; otherwise it adds coordination cost without adding reach (Fig 13 :115: "The set is closed. The arrangements are not."). This also explains why the empirical literature keeps reporting moderators rather than a main effect.

**Refuter:** produce a function that is beneficial to every residual in the habitat, with a named ground on which it never becomes a cost.

### F7 — More members and more search are filters, not witnesses

Fig 6 :49-50: "more search at the same reach: is it in here and unfound? The closure does not change. Same atoms, same wall, more of the space actually visited. Fails by spending budget on a region already swept. Distribute the search, and keep the searchers independent." Fig 7 :12-13: "same closure, handed along; agreement compounds, evidence does not." Fig 7 :35: "A room's agreement is variation, not a verdict. Selection between rooms is where the ground votes."

Scaling a team of similar frames adds spelling, not search. The remedy is independence and selection between differing rooms against the residual, not a bigger meeting. Fig 2 :42: "Buy independence in proportion to the cost of being wrong."

**Refuter:** show that adding functionally identical members increases the rate of settled novel terms per unit budget, with the closure held fixed.

## 5. Pre-registered refuter (written before the conclusion)

Per the standing instruction: "A falsifier written before the run is a design instrument." The shape claim is refuted if any of the following holds:

1. Innovation output and decision cost are shown to be one quantity in one currency, so averaging them is legitimate (breaks Fig 12 :125-127).
2. A cross-functional team with no described residual and no external ground reliably produces novel settled terms (breaks Fig 8 :19-21).
3. Agreement among high-overlap (functionally homogeneous) frames is shown to carry verdict weight about the world (breaks Fig 2 :40).
4. Independence is shown to affect surplus and settling cost in the same direction rather than as one parameter read twice (breaks Fig 8 :47, Fig 2 :39-42).
5. A named function is shown to be a benefactor to every residual in the habitat (breaks Fig 11 :49-51).

If any fires, the shape is withdrawn or narrowed. Until one is run, no part of this may be labelled `grounded`.

## 6. Recommendation

1. Split the question into two loops before seeking an answer (F1). Report two verdicts, never one average.
2. Describe the residual first. Ask what the team is for before ranking team compositions (F2, F6).
3. Install an external ground for the decision loop: a customer outcome, a market test, an experiment, a dataset. Without one, the predicted symptom is influence-driven drift (F4).
4. Keep independence as the design parameter. Buy it in proportion to the cost of being wrong (F3, F7).
5. Report two accounting columns, bits of new description and actions spent settling. Do not sum them (F5).
6. Use filters (tier, size, budget) only to decide what gets tried, never as witnesses for the verdict (F1, F7).

Figure 4 :22: "Only methods cross up. Only head starts come down." Send the split, the residual description and the refuters, not a conclusion.

### Updated verdict

The shape survives and is sharper than the question it answers. The question is a bundled union. Split it: differing closures are necessary but not sufficient for innovation (an undirected union is mute), while the same independence raises the cost or raises the information value of agreement, and an unanchored room drifts to influence, which is the reported conflict and slowness. There is no general "yes" or "no"; there is a set of conditions under which each outcome appears, each with its own ground and its own refuter. Mode `specified`, ungrounded. Strained after re-census: none hidden; one open magnitude question recorded above.
