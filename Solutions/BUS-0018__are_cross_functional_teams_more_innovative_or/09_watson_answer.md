## QUESTION

Decide, in effect terms, what the Tether says about the claim that cross-functional teams are more innovative OR that they create conflict and slow decision-making. Option A: answer the question as posed with one verdict. Option B: split the bundled union into its two loops and derive each separately. What each leads to: A averages two currencies and produces a slogan that cannot be checked against a ground; B produces two verdicts, each with its own anchor, its own refuter, and a stated stop where the instruments run out.

## DOMAIN CENSUS

R by arity, symmetry and scale, then one line per domain with actors / currency / anchor, partition or union, and order by dependency.

- **R (the residual).** One composition variable (functional diversity of a team) with two outcomes: a surplus of new settled terms, and the cost of settling a verdict. Arity: two conditions, so a **union compound** (Fig 9 :4-5, :54-58), not one hard question. Symmetry: the surplus is directional (it appears only at a boundary between differing closures); the conflict path is an unanchored drift; decision is ordered in time. Scale: surplus at the population/membrane scale (Fig 4), settling at the single-room scale (Fig 7).
- **Domain 1, union of frames (innovation).** Actors: members as frames with differing closures. Currency: surplus atoms, minted terms, description length in bits. Anchor: the ground that settles a mint. Union: the surplus lives where closures differ (Fig 8 :45-46).
- **Domain 2, the room (conflict and decision).** Actors: the same members as frames that mutually update or range against an anchor. Currency: residuals, agreement, influence, actions. Anchor: an external measurement, market, customer outcome or experiment, never internal consensus. Partition if anchored; mutual-update collapse if not (Fig 2 panels 1 and 3).
- **Domain 3, cost of settling.** Actors: contributions from the composition. Currency: bits and actions, which do not add. Anchor: the budget. Two currencies held apart (Fig 12 :125-127).
- **Domain 4, valence of functions.** Actors: roles and disciplines. Currency: valence relative to the residual. Anchor: the specific residual. Enumerated from the world, not assigned by a standing list (Fig 11 :49-51).
- **Order by dependency.** Split the union before searching; describe each residual before choosing a frame; refuse a false held belief before minting a new process; settle a first-order contribution before composing over it; select between rooms against the residual, not by averaging within one (Fig 3 :20, Fig 9 :27-28, Fig 12 :104, Fig 7 :35).

Strain test applied: the union-surplus reading (Fig 8) and the agreement-weight reading (Fig 2) appear to pull in opposite directions on overlap, but they govern different quantities (reachable surplus vs evidentiary weight of agreement) through the same independence parameter. Re-census resolves the apparent contradiction, so no strain survives (see `01` section 7).

## WORLD (secondary, each source: field, author/venue/year, quote, prediction, corroborates or conflicts)

World sources corroborate but never overrule the figures. Fields were chosen for low overlap with each other.

- **Mathematics / computational social science.** Lu Hong and Scott E. Page, "Groups of diverse problem solvers can outperform groups of high-ability problem solvers", PNAS 2004. Quote: "a team of randomly selected agents outperforms a team comprised of the best-performing agents" and "diversity trumps ability." Prediction: functional diversity enlarges the reachable solution space, so differing closures are necessary for surplus. Corroborates the union-surplus verdict. The same paper records the cost: "identity-diverse groups often have more conflict, more problems with communication, and less mutual respect and trust among members" and "problem solvers with diverse perspectives may have trouble understanding solutions identified by other agents." Corroborates the two-currency trade-off. Overlap: distinct field; some conceptual overlap with independence, declared.
- **Organizational psychology.** Karen A. Jehn, "A Multimethod Examination of the Benefits and Detriments of Intragroup Conflict", Administrative Science Quarterly 1995. Quote: "whether conflict was beneficial depended on the type of conflict and the structure of the group in terms of task type, task interdependence, and group norms." Prediction: conflict valence depends on the task/residual, not on the diversity itself. Corroborates Fig 11 :49. Overlap: shares Jehn with the 2012 meta-analysis below, so those two are one pool for that claim.
- **Meta-science / organizational psychology.** De Wit, Greer and Jehn, "Task versus relationship conflict, team performance, and team member satisfaction: A meta-analysis", Journal of Applied Psychology 2012. Quote: "conflict had stronger negative relations with team performance in highly complex (decision making, project, mixed) than in less complex (production) tasks." Prediction: the actions cost of disagreement scales with task complexity and novelty. Corroborates Fig 12 :125-127. Overlap: medium, shares Jehn.
- **Organizational psychology.** Wang and colleagues, "Cognitive diversity and innovative work behaviour: The mediating roles of task reflexivity and relationship conflict and the moderating role of perceived support", Journal of Occupational and Organizational Psychology 2019. Quote: "Cognitive diversity encouraged IWB via the task reflexivity pathway, but impeded IWB via the relationship conflict pathway." Prediction: one input produces opposite-signed outcomes through two mechanisms, so no single average suffices; context (support) decides which path dominates. Corroborates the split and the refusal to average. Overlap: low, distinct author group.
- **Meta-science.** Wallrich and colleagues, "The Relationship Between Team Diversity and Team Performance: Reconciling Promise and Reality Through a Comprehensive Meta-Analysis Registered Report", Journal of Business and Psychology 2024. Quote: "the average linear relationships between demographic, job-related and cognitive diversity, and team performance are significant and positive, but insubstantial (|r| < .1)" and "Correlations were more positive when tasks were higher in complexity or required creativity and innovation." Prediction: no general main effect; context and task moderate. Corroborates the `specified`, not `general`, mode and the absence of a winner. Overlap: low.
- **Decision-making / organizational psychology.** Loyd, Wang, Phillips and Lount, "Social Category Diversity Promotes Premeeting Elaboration", Organization Science 2012. Quote: "disagreeing dyads with social category diversity elaborate more prior to meeting and, as a result, perform better on a decision-making task than those with social category homogeneity." Prediction: disagreement in low-overlap dyads improves decision quality through elaboration when a task ground exists. Corroborates Fig 2 :35 (triangulation). Overlap: medium, shares two authors with the 2019 JOOP paper.
- **Team cognition / organizational psychology.** Aggarwal and Woolley, "Team Creativity, Cognition, and Cognitive Style Diversity", Management Science 2019. Quote: "team cognitive diversity both enhances and inhibits team cognition". Prediction: a dual effect from one input. Corroborates the isomer/dual-path shape. Overlap: low.

No world source conflicts with the figure verdict, so the conflict rule (return to the census) was not triggered. Agreement among sources sharing an author or an evidence pool is one witness, declared above and not counted twice. The world pool is not fully independent of the question's own field, so its corroboration is weaker than a low-overlap physical or mathematical witness would be; the Hong and Page paper is the one genuinely low-overlap corroborator.

## VERDICT (from the figures)

The question as posed is a false dichotomy. The figures return a split and a set of conditions, not a winner. Differing closures are necessary but not sufficient for a surplus (an undirected union is mute); the same independence that can create the surplus also raises the cost or the information value of agreement, so the two halves of the question are one parameter read twice; and the reported conflict and slowness are the signature of a room with no external ground, where members converge on whoever has the most influence. Functions have valence only relative to a named residual. There is no general verdict on cross-functional teams, and averaging the two currencies is the specific error the figures forbid. This is a `specified`, ungrounded shape. The strongest single figure point for outreach is the split itself (Fig 9 :4-5), because it converts a slogan into two checkable questions.

- **cost if wrong:** bounded and locatable. Every claim is mode-labelled, carries a verbatim line and a written refuter, and the net outcome is explicitly left open, so an error is findable rather than invisible. The real cost is spending an outside reader's independence by sending a conclusion instead of a split and a refuter, which Fig 2 :42 prices ("Buy independence in proportion to the cost of being wrong."). That cost is avoidable by leading with the refuters.
- **undoable:** yes. If one of the five refuters fires, the affected claim is narrowed without disturbing the rest of the derivation, and no primitive has to be withdrawn. A circulated packet can be superseded by a corrected one. The split itself is the most durable part; the specific one-parameter reading is the part most likely to need narrowing if a ground reading arrives.
- **reversed by:** a ground reading, not agreement. A team-level instrument that measures a settled new term separately from new vocabulary, and actions-to-settle separately from reported satisfaction, with an external anchor present or absent, would test C2 and C3. Evidence that the two quantities are commensurable, that a mute union still produces settled novelty, that homogeneous agreement carries verdict weight, that independence moves both outputs together, or that some function helps every residual would each break the corresponding finding. Until one fires, the verdict stands as `candidate`.
