# The Tether's answer on cross-functional teams

**Mode: `specified` on the substantive claims, `general` on the structural split; not `grounded`.** This is a shape and a route, not a result. Simulated derivation checked against the figures (`05_what_shape_means.md` section 6), not a ground reading.

## 1. The residual it answers

We are asked to choose one of two outcomes for a team-composition decision. The two outcomes are in different currencies and have different anchors. Fig 9 :4-5: "are not one hard question but several well-formed ones. Split it rather than search." Fig 9 :57: "a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either." The residual is therefore a **union compound**, and the answer must be two verdicts, never one average (Fig 1 :19: "The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated.").

## 2. The shape

1. **Split the question.** Innovation (a surplus of new, settled, reusable terms) and decision cost (the actions spent settling a verdict) are governed by different figure lines. Fig 12 :125: "A composition costs in two currencies, and they do not add."
2. **On innovation: differing closures are necessary but not sufficient.** Fig 8 :45: "Two frames together reach what neither reaches alone." But Fig 8 :19-21: "closures differ, so a surplus exists / but no residual is described / nothing was aimed; the union is mute." A cross-functional team only pays off if a residual is described first and an external ground settles what is minted. Fig 8 :53: "Residual first, frame second."
3. **On the trade-off: it is one parameter, not two effects.** Fig 8 :47: "Independence is one quantity read twice." Low overlap buys union surplus, and also makes agreement costlier and more informative. High overlap buys cheap agreement that cannot settle a claim about the world (Fig 2 :40). So the either/or framing the question usually assumes is a false dichotomy: the same feature produces both outputs, and what remains is to say which ground you are optimizing against.
4. **On conflict and speed: the cause is a missing anchor, not difference.** Fig 2 :13-15: an unanchored room has "no anchor; each updates on the others" and "they converge, and what they converge on / is whoever had the most influence". Fig 2 :35: "Alignment is triangulation, not negotiation. A mirror cannot correct you." With an external ground (a customer outcome, a market, a dataset, an experiment), the same difference becomes triangulation. Fig 2 :29: "A measurement, an experiment, a counter, a market, a patient outcome."
5. **On which functions help: no standing list.** Fig 11 :49: "An actor's valence is relative to the residual, not a property it carries." Functions are enumerated per residual, not ranked in the abstract.
6. **On more people and more meetings: a filter, not a witness.** Fig 6 :49-50: "more search at the same reach: is it in here and unfound? The closure does not change. Same atoms, same wall, more of the space actually visited." Fig 9 :60: "Use filters for the budget and witnesses for the verdict. Never let a filter hand you a verdict."

## 3. What it explicitly does not say

- Not that cross-functional teams are generally more innovative. The undirected union is mute without a described residual and a ground.
- Not that difference itself causes conflict. The figure puts the cause in the missing anchor (the mutual-update collapse), not in low overlap.
- Not that homogeneous teams are better. Their agreement is cheap and "efficient for a convention" (Fig 2 :41), but it cannot settle a claim about the world.
- Not that there is a single net score. The two currencies do not add.
- Not a name for a winning team design. The framework returns a shape and conditions, not a name.
- Not `grounded`. No team was measured here. World sources corroborate the shape; they do not settle it.

## 4. What would settle it, the ground

A source-discriminating instrument, not a further argument. *The Hard Problem is About Source* (carried in `05`): an instrument that reads green "cannot comment on the source"; the spectrometer resolved it. Here the corresponding questions are:

- **Surplus test:** did the team produce a new, settled, reusable term, or only new vocabulary? A term is settled only when the ground pays (formula step 5: "the ground settles it. Verified only by what pays.").
- **Anchor test:** is there something outside the room that can settle a disagreement and does not move when people push on it (Fig 2 :29-30)? Without one, the predicted failure mode is influence-driven drift.
- **Two-column accounting:** report bits of new description and actions spent settling, separately (Fig 12 :130: "Summing them into one figure is the error this names, not the fix for it.").
- Standing falsifiers from `01`: commensurable currencies; a mute union that still produces settled novelty; homogeneous agreement with verdict weight; independence affecting both outputs in the same direction; a universally beneficial function.

## 5. The honest stop

**Unreached, not unreachable.** The figures give the direction of each output from independence and the conditions for each, but they do not give the empirical magnitude or the net objective in a given organization. That is a ground reading, not a derivation from these atoms. Fig 13 :134: "Unreachable is a verdict here, not a silence." Fig 8 :39: "so a search that finds nothing is never a proof of absence." Another habitat (a different industry, task, or measurement) may have different load-bearing functions. The answer stops where the available instruments can no longer distinguish surplus from vocabulary or triangulation from drift, and records the lineage so the stop is checkable.

## 6. One-paragraph version for outreach

> The Tether does not say that cross-functional teams are better or worse. It says the question is two questions in one coat. Innovation is a surplus of new settled terms; conflict and slow decisions are the cost of settling a verdict, and the two are in different currencies, so they cannot be averaged. Differing functional closures are necessary for a surplus but not sufficient: an undirected union with no described residual is mute. The same low overlap that can create the surplus also makes agreement costly and more informative, so the trade-off is one parameter read twice, not two accidents. The reported conflict and slowness are what a room does when it has no external ground: members update on one another and converge on whoever has the most influence. Give the decision loop an anchor, describe the residual before choosing functions, and report the two costs in two columns. What it offers is the shape any answer must have, the falsifiers written in advance, and the instruments that would close it. The net outcome stays open until the ground settles it.
