# Expert verification packet: the Tether on cross-functional teams

**What this is:** a claim in a form a team researcher or organization scholar can check. Mode, derivation and refuters are stated before the conclusion, so a reader knows what to look for and what would settle it.

**Mode: `specified` on the substantive claims, `general` on the structural split; not `grounded`.** No team measurement is claimed. This is a shape and a route, not a result.

**Status:** a simulated derivation checked for internal consistency against the figures, not a ground reading. It cannot settle the claim it reports on.

## 1. The claim, in one paragraph

The question "are cross-functional teams more innovative, or do they create conflict and slow decision-making?" bundles two outcomes in different currencies: innovation is a surplus of new, settled, reusable terms, and conflict plus slow decisions are a cost of settling a verdict. Differing functional closures are necessary but not sufficient for the surplus, because an undirected union with no described residual is mute. The same independence that can create the surplus also raises the cost or raises the information value of agreement, so the trade-off is one parameter read twice. Conflict and influence-driven slowness are the signature of a room with no external ground, not of difference itself. The claim is the shape and the conditions; the net outcome in any organization is left to the ground.

## 2. Three checkable sub-claims

### C1 — framework-internal; checkable by reading Figures 8, 9 and 12

- **Statement:** the two outcomes must be split and reported separately, and the two currencies must not be summed.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 9 :4-5 "are not one hard question but several well-formed ones. Split it rather than search." Fig 9 :54 "And when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again." Fig 12 :125 "A composition costs in two currencies, and they do not add." Fig 12 :126-127 "Bits price the description. Actions price finding out whether it holds."
- **Refuter:** give a single unit in which the information value of a new term and the deliberation cost of settling it are commensurable, with an operational conversion and a ground on which the conversion is settled.

### C2 — team-research-facing; checkable against team data

- **Statement:** differing closures are necessary but not sufficient for a surplus, and the same independence raises the cost or the value of agreement. A homogeneous team's agreement is cheap but cannot settle a verdict about the world.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 8 :45 "Two frames together reach what neither reaches alone." Fig 8 :19-21 "closures differ, so a surplus exists / but no residual is described / nothing was aimed; the union is mute." Fig 8 :47 "Independence is one quantity read twice." Fig 2 :40 "disqualifying for a verdict: agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool." Fig 2 :41 "efficient for a convention: agreement about a shared meaning is the product, not a corruption of it."
- **What to check:** in the data, is innovation output measured as a settled new term (not new vocabulary), and is decision cost measured as actions to settle (not satisfaction)? Does the apparent diversity effect disappear when those two are separated and each given its own ground? Does a "diversity main effect" survive once task/residual complexity is modelled?
- **Refuter:** exhibit a cross-functional team with no described residual and no external ground that reliably produces novel settled terms; or show independence affecting surplus and settling cost in the same direction; or show homogeneous agreement carrying verdict weight.

### C3 — team-research-facing; checkable against a conflict and decision dataset

- **Statement:** conflict plus slow, influence-driven decisions are the signature of a room with no external anchor. With an anchor, the same difference is triangulation.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 2 :13-15 "no anchor; each updates on the others", "they converge, and what they converge on", "is whoever had the most influence". Fig 2 :35 "Alignment is triangulation, not negotiation. A mirror cannot correct you." Fig 2 :29 "A measurement, an experiment, a counter, a market, a patient outcome."
- **What to check:** in nonroutine tasks with an external outcome measure, does task disagreement predict better decisions (elaboration/triangulation) rather than merely slower ones? In routine tasks with no such anchor, does the same disagreement become pure cost? Is the "conflict" effect actually a missing-anchor effect in disguise?
- **Refuter:** show a cross-functional room with no external ground whose decisions settle by evidence rather than influence, reliably across tasks.

## 3. What is explicitly not claimed

- Not that cross-functional teams are generally more innovative.
- Not that difference causes conflict; the cause is assigned to the missing anchor.
- Not that homogeneous teams are better; their agreement is cheap but evidentiary-empty.
- Not a single net score, not a name for a winning design, and not a new primitive.
- Not `grounded`. No team measurement here.
- Not that a named function is "the innovative one". Valence is relative to the residual (Fig 11 :49).
- World sources are corroboration only and are not used to overrule a figure line.

## 5. What would settle it

A source-discriminating instrument, not a further argument. From *The Hard Problem is About Source* (carried in `05`): an instrument that reads green "cannot comment on the source"; the spectrometer resolved it. Here:

1. **Surplus test:** a new, settled, reusable term versus new vocabulary. A term counts only when the ground pays.
2. **Anchor test:** something outside the room that settles disagreement and does not move when pushed.
3. **Two-column accounting:** bits of new description and actions spent settling, reported apart.
4. **Standing falsifiers:** commensurable currencies; a mute union that still produces settled novelty; homogeneous agreement with verdict weight; independence moving both outputs together; a universally beneficial function.

## 6. What an expert can check in twenty minutes

1. Read Fig 8 :45, :19-21, :47 and Fig 2 :35, :40-41, Fig 12 :125-127. Seven short passages.
2. Check C2 and C3 against data you know: are the two outcomes separable, and does the anchor condition flip the conflict effect?
3. Name one instrument that would distinguish a settled new term from new vocabulary, or say why none is possible.
4. Send the refutation. A failure is more useful than an endorsement.
