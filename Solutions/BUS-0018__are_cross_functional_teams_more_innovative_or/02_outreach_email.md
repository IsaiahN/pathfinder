# Outreach draft: check the split before you decide about the teams

**Use:** one-to-one, or as a cover note attached to a submission through a public venue in `03_public_contact_points.csv`. **Not** for bulk send.

**Hard precondition:** do not send until the two-stage order is respected. Send the residual and the refuter first. Record the recipient's verdict before showing any framework material.

---

**Subject:** One checkable claim about cross-functional teams, before any theory

> Hi [name],
>
> I have one specific output I would like you to check against what you already know from team data. I am not asking you to read a theory first, and I am not asking you to agree with me. If the output does not survive, there is nothing else to look at.
>
> **The output (`specified`, not `grounded`):** the question "are cross-functional teams more innovative, or do they create conflict and slow decision-making?" bundles two outcomes that are in different currencies. Innovation is a surplus of new, reusable terms. Conflict and slow decisions are a cost of settling a verdict. They have different anchors and must be reported separately, never averaged.
>
> From that split, three things follow as a shape, not a measurement:
>
> 1. Differing functional closures are **necessary but not sufficient** for a surplus. If no residual is described and nothing is aimed at, the union is mute: more vocabulary, no new settled term.
> 2. The same low overlap that can create the surplus also raises the cost of agreement, and makes any agreement that does converge more informative. Homogeneous teams agree cheaply, but that agreement cannot settle a claim about the world. The two halves of the question are one parameter read twice, not two unrelated effects.
> 3. The reported symptom, conflict plus slow influence-driven decisions, is the signature of a room with **no external ground**. Members update on one another and converge on whoever had the most influence. With an external ground (a customer outcome, a dataset, an experiment) the same difference becomes triangulation rather than negotiation.
>
> **What I am not claiming:** that cross-functional teams are generally more innovative; that difference itself causes conflict; that any particular function is "the innovative one"; or that one net score can decide the trade-off. The framework returns a shape and the conditions that flip each outcome, not a number for your organization. Whether the surplus exceeds the cost is exactly what your ground would have to settle.
>
> **What would refute the shape claim:**
> 1. showing that the information value of a new term and the deliberation cost of settling it are one quantity in one currency, so averaging is legitimate;
> 2. a cross-functional team with no described residual and no external ground that reliably produces novel settled terms;
> 3. evidence that agreement among functionally homogeneous frames carries verdict weight about the world;
> 4. a named function that is beneficial to every residual in the habitat.
>
> I wrote these down before sending this. If any holds, I will narrow or retract the claim. A one-line "this fails because..." is more useful to me than agreement. If you would rather not engage, no reply is needed.
>
> **If it survives, and only then:** the sources are below. The figures are the authority, not my summary of them.
> - Verbatim figure text with line numbers (local extract): `FRAMEWORK/reference/03-FIGURES_TEXT.md`
> - Figures and formula for direct download: https://github.com/IsaiahN/tether/tree/general-agent/figures
> - Canonical figures README (branch `general-agent`): https://github.com/IsaiahN/tether/blob/general-agent/figures/README.md
>
> **If you want to use the machinery:** the formula is in the figures link. Run it on a team question you can check against a ground. Write down what would refute your result *before* you run it, and state the mode of what you get. A reported failure is more useful to me than a reported success.
