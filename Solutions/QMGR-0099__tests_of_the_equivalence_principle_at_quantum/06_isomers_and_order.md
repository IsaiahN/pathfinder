# Isomers, order and a scaling analogue for the quantum EP

**Mode: `specified`.** This is a simulated derivation checked against the figures, not a ground reading (see `05_what_shape_means.md` §7).

## RESIDUAL

`01` leaves one structural question open. If the classical equivalence principle and a candidate quantum version are "the same principle", what is the composition relation between them? Does the quantum version require a seventh primitive, is it an isomer (same atoms, different bond, different substance), or is it a member of the same functional group (same bond pattern, different atoms in the slots)? A second question follows: in what order must the three sub-residuals A (statement), B (verdict) and C (interpretation) be searched?

## DOMAIN CENSUS

Atoms held: Space, Time, Energy (states) plus Gradient, Network, Spectrum (structures). Bonds held: the seven, of which `≡` (identity) and `⋛` (comparison) are symmetric, plus the unary negation `¬`. No new primitive is available without import. The classical EP is a settled molecule at first order. Candidate QEP formulations are bindings from the physics frame onto atoms already held, not new atoms. D3's instruments supply residuals, not kinds.

## DERIVATION

### Q1 — Is the quantum EP a seventh primitive? NO

- Fig 13 :41: *"Each is indivisible: no arrangement of the other five yields it."*
- Fig 13 :115: *"The set is closed. The arrangements are not. That gap is why import exists."*
- Fig 13 :133: *"what it returns is a shape rather than a name"*; Fig 13 :135: *"It cannot say which primitive is missing, because naming it would mean already holding it."*
- ⇒ A QEP formulation is an arrangement over held atoms. Calling it a new primitive would move the wall (Fig 6 :14: *"the only operator that moves the wall"*, import), which requires a source gate and a shadow test and is not available here.
- **Falsifier, written before the run:** a candidate formulation that cannot be written over the six without a genuinely new kind. If one is demonstrated with provenance and a shadow test, the wall moves and this finding is withdrawn.

### Q2 — Isomer or functional group?

- Fig 12 :117: **"Same atoms, different bond, different substance."**
- Fig 12 :124: **"functional group. A substructure whose presence fixes the outcome. The group says what kind; the qualifier says which member."**
- Over the full shared atom set `{Space, Time, Energy, Gradient, Spectrum, Network}`, writing the classical EP as a comparison `⋛` of two masses and a candidate QEP as an identity `≡` of mass operators, or as an equivalence of quantum reference frames composed over Spectrum and Network, gives **same atoms, different bond, different substance**. That is the stronger isomer claim.
- Under the weaker reading, both keep the comparison pattern and differ only in what fills the operand slots (point mass versus delocalized wave packet). They then share a **functional group** (the comparison structure) with different members in the slots.
- ⇒ The isomer claim is real and checkable, but only over the full shared atom set. As the classical EP and a QEP are normally written, the weaker functional-group claim is the safe one. This mirrors the exemplar's GR/QM result without importing its physics.
- **Falsifier:** if a candidate QEP requires an atom the classical EP does not (for example a primitive "quantum frame" irreducible to Network and Spectrum), both the isomer and functional-group claims fail and a genuine import is owed. Triangulated for the isomer definition (Fig 12 :117 plus the Operators table distinction between `≡` and `⋛`); single-source for its application here, unmeasured.

### Q3 — The order is A, then B, then C

- Fig 3 :20: **"The chain is ordered by dependency, not by importance. Each link consumes what the one above produces, so effort spent above the break arrives at a step that cannot use it."**
- Fig 3 :22: **"A step whose input never arrives cannot be diagnosed, only its predecessor can."**
- Fig 9 :27-28: **"describe it before you go looking, or any frame you pick will seem to fit"**.
- Sub-residual B (does the EP hold?) presupposes a statement of what is being compared (A). Sub-residual C (what does a signal mean?) presupposes both a statement and a signal. So the dependency order is A → B → C. Measuring η before stating the quantum principle is a step whose input never arrives.
- Fig 3 :19: **"Optimising a link that already works cannot rescue one that does not."** Making the interferometer more precise while A is open optimises a link above the break.
- **Falsifier:** a definition-free violation detector that returns a signal discriminated from fifth forces and systematics with no prior statement of the principle. If such a device exists, B can precede A and the order claim is wrong. I did not find one in the opened sources; Rosi et al. explicitly introduce a quantum formulation of EEP *before* interpreting their measurement.

### Q4 — A low-overlap analogue: scaling laws change with range

- Kolokotrones, Savage, Deeds and Fontana, *"Curvature in metabolic scaling"*, Nature 464:753-756, 2010. https://www.nature.com/articles/nature08920: **"we show that the relationship between mass and metabolic rate has convex curvature on a logarithmic scale, and is therefore not a pure power law, even after accounting for body temperature."**
- Biology, not physics. A scaling principle stable in one mass range turns out to have curvature when the range changes, and the apparent exponent depends on which organisms dominate the sample. This is an **analogue** for the habit of re-testing a principle at a new scale, not evidence about the equivalence principle. The figures govern; the analogue only corroborates the methodological move (Fig 4 :30: *"So take the round trip and measure the gap. Without it you are navigating by dead reckoning: confident about a position nobody checked."*).
- **Falsifier of the analogue:** a ground reading showing the EP passes into the quantum domain with zero residual, so that the passage is a lookup rather than a re-test. Then the scaling analogue is a false friend and is dropped. Recorded now, before the run.

### Q5 — The operand type of `⋛` at second order is still strained

- Operators table :48: **"a threshold relation between two quantities"**.
- Fig 12 :104: **"A settled molecule becomes an atom for whatever composes over it. So irreducible is a fact about a level and never about the world."**
- At the contact, the operands are settled first-order molecules now serving as atoms. Whether an atom counts as a quantity at that level cannot be decided by argument; it needs an instrument. This is a strain, named rather than hidden, and it is the same shape of strain the exemplar records for its ⋛ at second order. It does not flip any verdict in `01`.

## CONCLUSION

The classical EP and a candidate quantum EP are **isomers over the full shared atom set** under the stronger reading, differing in which symmetric bond (`⋛` versus `≡` or a Network/Spectrum composition) carries the relation; under the weaker, safer reading they share a **functional group**. No seventh primitive is needed, and naming one without a provenanced import would move the wall illegitimately. The search order is **A → B → C**, because B and C consume what A produces. The biology scaling analogue supports re-testing across scales and says nothing about the physics verdict. The `⋛` operand type at second order remains a live strain. All four claims carry falsifiers written before the run and remain `candidate` until a ground reading pays for them.
