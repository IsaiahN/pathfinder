# The Tether's answer on tests of the equivalence principle at quantum scales

**Mode: `specified` on the structural claims, `general` on the shape, `candidate` throughout. Nothing here is `grounded`.** This is a simulated derivation checked against the figures (`05_what_shape_means.md` §7), not a ground reading.

## 1. The residual it answers

We hold two theories that each work in their own domain. GR says all freely falling bodies follow the same geodesics regardless of mass or composition, so a local freely falling frame eliminates gravity. QM says the test body is a delocalized wave packet with internal energy operators and no single classical worldline. At their contact, three conditions bundle together: there is no agreed statement of a quantum equivalence principle (A), whether the principle holds is an instrument question (B), and a positive signal has several possible sources (C). Fig 9 :55-56: *"a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either"*. This is a union compound with its own loop and its own ground.

## 3. What it explicitly does not say

- Not a yes or no on whether the equivalence principle holds at quantum scales. That is an empirical verdict and this package has no instrument reading.
- Not a selection among mass-operator equality, quantum reference frame equivalence, or a violation model. Those are bindings from the physics frame and need provenance and a shadow test.
- Not a new primitive, not a seventh member of the substrate, and not an import that moves the wall.
- Not a claim that current null results prove the EP exact, and not a claim that a violation has been seen.
- Not a theory of everything, and no final room. Fig 7 :13: *"agreement compounds, evidence does not"*.
- Not `grounded`. No measurement was taken.

## 4. What would settle it: the ground

A source-discriminating instrument, not a further argument. *The Hard Problem is About Source*: an instrument that reads green *"cannot comment on the source"*; the spectrometer resolved it. Here the corresponding questions are:

1. Does the metric behave as **ordering** (spectrum-like) or **directedness** (gradient-like) when the test body is a superposition? An instrument that discriminates these would name the slot, but only with provenance (Fig 8 :50-52).
2. Does the ⋛ comparison hold between the two settled molecules at the relevant scale, or demonstrably not? The operand type must be settled at that level, not argued about here.
3. Does a differential-acceleration signal survive separation from a new force, from systematics and from a mis-stated principle? Two instruments with different closures are better than one more precise one (Fig 2 :31-32).
4. Standing refuters from `01` §4: a missed figure line that closes the residual without unbundling; a derivation from the six that names one formulation; a direct first-order composition that settles the verdict; or proof that no instrument returns anything, which would change the status toward unreachable-at-this-budget.

## 6. One-paragraph version for outreach

> The Tether does not say whether the equivalence principle holds at quantum scales. It says that the question is three conditions bundled into one: no agreed statement of the principle, an instrument question about whether it holds, and an interpretation question for any positive signal. Any admissible answer must compare two already-settled descriptions at second order, must express the relation at the level of operators or reference frames rather than classical worldlines, and must leave the physical verdict to an instrument. The framework returns a shape, not a name, so it does not pick among the formulations now in the literature. Atom interferometers already return phase residuals that fail to resolve, so this is a question that has been reached but not settled, not one beyond any instrument. Null results bound the principle; they do not prove it exact. What is offered is the shape, the order of the three conditions, the falsifiers written in advance, and the instruments that would close it. The slot stays open until a ground reading pays for a name.
