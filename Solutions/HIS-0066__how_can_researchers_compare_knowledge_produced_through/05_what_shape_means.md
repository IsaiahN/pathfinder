## 1. A shape is a constraint, not a filler

The canonical rule: the framework returns *"what a missing thing would have to look like (a shape, not a name)"* (figures README). Fig 9 :32 says *"you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself"*. Fig 13 says the framework *"cannot say which primitive is missing, because naming it would mean already holding it."*

From *The Library of Babel*: *"you can cut your search short if the set of books returned is small and descriptive enough that it provides a general shape of the answer that satisfies your question."*

So a **shape** is the narrowed form of an answer you can state before you can name the specific method that fills it. It constrains; it does not fill.

For this question:

- A **shape**: a comparison keeps two frames separate, ranges them against a shared anchor, reports the round-trip gap, declares the substituted habitat, and splits into domains of validity where each rule wins on a different subset.
- A **name**: "use randomised controlled trials", "use ethnography", "use a hierarchical evidence grading scale". Each is a binding from a particular methods frame onto the shape.

The shape is checkable before anyone agrees on a method. The name is a preference that must be provenanced.

## 2. How a shape is obtained

- *Library of Babel*: use the vocabulary you already have that is closest to the thing, then look for new terms that overlap or correlate with it. *"With each new term gained, you narrow the space and deepen your vocabulary."*
- Fig 6 :30: *"The description travels. Fails by being sharpened into terms only we can read. Cast it in effect terms."* Fig 9 :30-31: *"your words are borrowed too, so describe the effect rather than the cause"*.
- The search is steered by the question: *Library of Babel* says *"the finish line will also be the starting place"*, and the paradox is that by then you have *"already composed the answer through the process of locating it."* In this solution the residual was named first, then the figures were grepped, then the world fields were searched. The order is part of the method.

For this question the effect terms are: intervenes, controls, subtracts, observes over time, transmits skill by contact, loses something in transport, settles against an outcome. The names "experiment" and "practice" arrive after those effects, not before.

## 3. What licenses a name

Only two things, both outside the shape itself:

1. **The ground settles it.** Formula step 5: *"the ground settles it. Verified only by what pays."* The figures README says the framework *"stops at the limit of what the available instruments can distinguish, and records the lineage so the stop is honest."*
2. **A provenanced import from a frame whose closure differs** (Fig 6 :23, Fig 8 :50), with a source gate and a shadow test. Fig 6 :24 debits the import against that frame's independence.

*The Hard Problem is About Source* sharpens the first condition. Metamerism: an instrument reads green and *"cannot comment on the source"*. *"If several paths create the same behavior, then behavior is a projection with lossy information."* And: *"An instrument that can't distinguish these cases demonstrates its own inadequacy."* The spectrometer, not an argument, resolved it. The essay's own conclusion: *"The answer is unreached until a better instrument exists."*

Applied here: an effect size measured in a controlled setting is a reading, not a source. Naming field capability from that reading is a metamerism error. What is owed is an instrument that discriminates sources, which is another way of stating the round-trip gap in `01_figure_grounded_audit.md` F4.

## 4. Why "shape" governs this question rather than a method ranking

An evidence hierarchy is a name with a ranking attached. It answers "which method is higher" rather than "what would a valid comparison look like". Three figure lines rule it out:

- Fig 1 :27: *"A frame cannot score itself with a quantity it also produces."*
- Fig 2 :29: *"An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks."*
- Fig 9 :60: *"Use filters for the budget and witnesses for the verdict. Never let a filter hand you a verdict."*

Prestige, tradition and apparent rigour are filters for the budget: they decide which venue to try and how much effort to spend. They are not witnesses for the verdict. The shape is what remains after the filters are removed.

## 5. Correction to my own §3

**Overstatement, now narrowed.** I first read "shape, not a name" as forbidding the selection of any existing method, so that even naming "controlled experiment" or "long-term observation" as domains would be a violation.

That is wrong. The canonical phrase governs a **missing** primitive, one not held in the library. Experiment and observation are not missing; they are existing frames with nameable anchors, admitted as domains in the census. Naming them is a binding from the methods literature onto an existing frame, provenanced as such. Fig 13 requires a structure between two states but does not select among the structures it holds; analogously, the figures require a comparison with an anchor but do not select which published method occupies the slot.

What remains forbidden: inventing a new "kind of evidence" that is just a renamed version of an existing frame, and claiming that such a renaming moves the wall (Fig 6 :23: *"No frame escapes its own closure. The bound moves only on contact with a frame whose closure differs."*).

Both the original overstatement and this correction are kept visible.

## 6. Consequence for verification

The expert packet (`08_expert_verification_packet.md`) asks a reader to check the **shape**, not to endorse a method. The question put to them is: would you expect to see this pattern if the comparison claim were true, and what would count as a failure?

That ordering follows Fig 10 :52: *"Bring a characterised residual, never the source."* It also follows *The Hard Problem is About Source*: a reader who already holds the full theory has no instrument left to discriminate the source from the reading.
