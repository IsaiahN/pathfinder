# Expert verification packet: comparing experimental knowledge with observational or practice knowledge

**What this is:** a claim in a form a methodologist, historian of science or field researcher can check. Mode, derivation and refuters are stated before the conclusion, so a reader knows what to look for and what would settle it.

**Mode: `specified`, not `grounded`.** No measurement is claimed. This is a shape and a route, not a result. It is a simulated derivation checked for internal consistency against the figures, not a ground reading.

## 1. The claim, in one paragraph

A valid comparison of knowledge from controlled experiments with knowledge from long-term observation or practice is not a pooled score and not a prestige ranking. It is triangulation of two frames that draw on different evidence against one anchor neither frame can move. The residual must be described before a method is named. Transport from one frame to the other loses information, and the loss must be reported as a round-trip gap R_T. A controlled experiment is a substituted habitat: it proves wiring, never field capability. Where each rule wins on a different subset, the comparison resolves into domains of validity rather than a single verdict. The figures return that shape and five refuters written in advance; they do not name which published method occupies each slot.

## 2. Three checkable sub-claims

### C1: framework-internal; checkable by reading Figures 1, 2 and 7

- **Statement:** a comparison must range two independent frames against a shared anchor. Averaging and self-scoring are disqualified.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 2 :29: *"An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks."* Fig 1 :27: *"A frame cannot score itself with a quantity it also produces."* Fig 2 :35: *"Alignment is triangulation, not negotiation. A mirror cannot correct you."* Fig 7 :35: *"A room's agreement is variation, not a verdict. Selection between rooms is where the ground votes."*
- **Independence condition:** Fig 2 :31: *"Independence, between two frames, is how little they draw on the same evidence."* Two readings of the same dataset are one witness.
- **Refuter:** a pooled estimate that preserves the live experiment/field difference signal and is settled by an anchor neither frame authored; or a demonstration that frames sharing an evidence pool give genuine independent corroboration.

### C2: methods-facing; checkable by a researcher against transport and habitat practice

- **Statement:** carrying a result from a controlled setting into the field is a lossy round trip, and a controlled experiment is a substituted habitat that proves wiring and not capability.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 4 :30: *"So take the round trip and measure the gap. Without it you are navigating by dead reckoning: confident about a position nobody checked."* Fig 11 :70: *"A test harness is a substituted habitat. Which is why a synthetic solve proves wiring and never capability."* Fig 11 :66: *"Isolation is not removal of the habitat. It is substitution of one habitat for another."*
- **What to check:** is there a reported round-trip gap between the controlled result and the field outcome? Is the substituted habitat enumerated, including what was not reproduced and what was unintentionally introduced? Do field experiments or pragmatic trials in your field actually close the gap, or only shorten it?
- **Refuter:** a finding transported with zero gap and unchanged meaning, confirmed at the ground; or an experiment reproducing the whole field habitat that still fails to preserve capability; or an agent-only improvement that changes contact.

### C3: framework-internal derivation with a live type strain

- **Statement:** each body of knowledge is settled first and becomes an atom for a second-order comparison. Over a declared shared atom set the two bodies are isomers; as normally practised they are a functional group.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 12 :104: *"A settled molecule becomes an atom for whatever composes over it. So irreducible is a fact about a level and never about the world"*. Fig 12 :117: *"isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."* Fig 12 :124: *"functional group. A substructure whose presence fixes the outcome. The group says what kind; the qualifier says which member."*
- **What to check:** does a declared atom set make the isomer claim hold, and is the declaration visible? If no atom set is declared, the claim is the weaker functional-group one.
- **Refuter:** either body requires an atom the other does not, which kills the isomer claim; or a settled body cannot serve as an atom at the next level, which kills the order claim.
- **Strain, named:** the Operators table defines `⋛` as *"a threshold relation between two quantities"*, but at second order the operands are settled bodies now serving as atoms. Whether an atom is a quantity at that level needs an instrument. This is not resolved in the packet.

## 3. What is explicitly not claimed

- Not that experiments are higher knowledge than observation or practice.
- Not that one body can be translated into the other without loss.
- Not that a single method wins everywhere. Unions split into domains of validity.
- Not a new kind of evidence, not a primitive, not a theory of everything.
- Not `grounded`. No measurement was taken and this is a simulated derivation.
- Not a claim that the framework names a method. It returns a shape; method selection is a provenanced binding.
- Not that the whole question is a `gap`. No shared anchor was found to be absent; `gap` is available per claim after SEARCHED, INWARD and OUTWARD.

## 5. What would settle it

A source-discriminating comparison at the ground, not further agreement:

1. **Blend test:** a pooled estimate preserving the live difference signal and settled by an anchor neither frame authored.
2. **Transport test:** a round-trip gap measured rather than assumed.
3. **Habitat test:** full habitat reproduction preserving capability, or an agent-only improvement that changes contact.
4. **Single-rule test:** one method dominating at the ground across all subsets.
5. **Codification test:** a novice reproducing expert practice from a written record with no residual.
6. **Seven standing refuters** in `01_figure_grounded_audit.md` §3.

## 6. What an expert can check in twenty minutes

1. Read Fig 2 :29 and :31, Fig 1 :27, Fig 7 :35, Fig 4 :30, Fig 11 :66 and :70, Fig 12 :104 and :117. Nine short passages.
2. Check C2 against your own field: does anyone report a round-trip gap between controlled result and field outcome? If not, is that because it is absent or because nobody looks?
3. Name one instrument that would distinguish a transported experimental finding from a field capability, or say why none is possible.
4. Send the refutation. A failure is more useful than an endorsement.
