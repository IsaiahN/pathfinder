## 0. The claim under audit

> Researchers can compare knowledge from controlled experiments with knowledge from long-term observation or practice by finding a common measure, converting one into the other, or weighting them into a single ranked evidence hierarchy.

This is the working claim in effect terms, not a quotation from any single source. Three candidate decisions sit behind it:

- **Option A (blend).** Pool both into one estimate and rank one above the other. Each frame is then scored by a quantity the pool itself produces.
- **Option B (import).** Treat one frame as primary and translate the other into its vocabulary, as if the translation moved the wall.
- **Option C (triangulate and split).** Keep the two frames separate, range each against a shared ground, measure the loss in transport, and split the question where each rule works on a different subset.

The audit asks which option the figures license. The pre-registered refuter is in section 3 and was written before the findings were drawn up.

## 1. Residual and domain census

### 1.1 R in effect terms

A researcher holds two settled bodies of knowledge. E (experiment) intervenes, controls, randomises, and isolates. O (observation or practice) records what happens unmanipulated over long time and transmits skill through doing. Both are internally coherent. The residual R is the comparison itself: what does a claim from E mean for O, and what does a claim from O mean for E, without averaging the difference away, without ranking by prestige, and without stopping at "incommensurable"?

- **Arity.** Binary relation among two frames and a ground. Comparison is a bond between frames, not a single scalar score.
- **Symmetry.** Ordered, not symmetric. The Operators table defines comparison `⋛` as *"a threshold relation between two quantities"* with the test *"is it about which is larger, rather than both being present?"* A symmetric pool is a collapse, not a comparison.
- **Scale.** E and O are each a settled first-order body. Their comparison is a second-order composition over two settled molecules. Fig 12 :104 says *"A settled molecule becomes an atom for whatever composes over it. So irreducible is a fact about a level and never about the world"*.

Fig 1 :19 says *"The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated."* Fig 1 :22 says *"Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average."* The live slots here are the places where E and O disagree. Pooling them is the named failure, not the remedy.

### 1.2 One line per domain: actors, currency, anchor

| domain | actors | currency | anchor | nameable anchor? |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| D1 Experimental frame (E) | experimenter, treatment, control, random assignment, instrument, protocol | controlled subtraction (bond `−`), effect size, replicability, intervention | the outcome under intervention, re-runnable | yes, the re-run |
| D2 Observation / practice frame (O) | observer, practitioner, field site, long time series, cases, community of practice | temporal succession (`→`), co-occurrence (`+`), pattern recognition, tacit skill | the long-run unmanipulated record | yes, the field record |
| D3 Comparison / verification frame (C) | meta-researcher, methodologist, replication, registered-report reviewer | residuals, cross-checks, round-trip gap R_T, transportability | the ground: the outcome both frames aim to predict, never agreement between them | yes, the ground |

No domain is admitted without a nameable anchor. The popular phrase "the evidence hierarchy" is not a domain: its anchor is prestige, which moves when people push on it, and Fig 2 :29 defines an anchor as *"An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks."*

### 1.3 Partition or union

This is a **union**, not a partition. E and O are two frames whose closures differ, so a surplus is possible. Fig 8 :45 says *"Two frames together reach what neither reaches alone."* But the surplus is conditional. Fig 8 :29 says the useful case is *"low overlap: B does not draw on A's evidence"*, and Fig 8 :20 names the failure when there is no aim: *"nothing was aimed; the union is mute"*. Fig 2 :40 says *"disqualifying for a verdict: agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool."* Two researchers reading the same review are one witness wearing two names.

A second split is owed where each rule keeps working on a different subset. Fig 9 :4 says *"Disagreements that do not shrink with effort, where each rule keeps working well on a different subset, are not one hard question but several well-formed ones. Split it rather than search."*

### 1.4 Dependency order (Fig 3)

1. Describe R in effect terms before choosing a frame (Fig 8 :53, Fig 9 :27).
2. Refuse the symmetric blend and the self-scoring hierarchy before minting a comparison (Formula step 2, REFUTED; negation is not a bond).
3. Settle each first-order body before composing the second-order comparison (Fig 12 :104).
4. Measure the round trip before transporting any finding (Fig 4 :30).
5. Let the ground decide, never the room (Fig 7 :35).

Fig 3 :19 says *"Optimising a link that already works cannot rescue one that does not."* Fig 3 :27 says *"A link with no instrument is not a link that works. It is a link nobody has looked at."* The link most often assumed rather than measured here is step 3, the comparison's own anchor.

## 2. Findings

### F1: Comparison is triangulation against a shared anchor, not negotiation or averaging (decisive)

Fig 2 :29 defines the anchor: *"An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks."* Fig 1 :23 says *"The ground enters as the only metric."* Fig 1 :27 says *"A frame cannot score itself with a quantity it also produces."*

So an evidence hierarchy that ranks E above O by a score the experimental frame itself produces is the named output-layer defect. It is not a comparison; it is the experimental frame scoring itself. Fig 2 :35 says *"Alignment is triangulation, not negotiation. A mirror cannot correct you."* Fig 7 :35 says *"A room's agreement is variation, not a verdict. Selection between rooms is where the ground votes."*

Independence is the price of admission. Fig 2 :31 says *"Independence, between two frames, is how little they draw on the same evidence."* Fig 2 :42 says *"Buy independence in proportion to the cost of being wrong."* Two observations of the same dataset, or an experiment and an observational study built on the same cohort and the same outcome definition, are one frame wearing two names.

- **Status: `triangulated`.** Low overlap between Fig 1 (per-slot residual, ground as only metric), Fig 2 (anchor and independence), and Fig 7 (ground votes between rooms). Three different figures reach the same verdict by different routes.
- **Mode: `general`** on the structural claim, `specified` on the prediction that a self-scoring hierarchy will fail at the ground.
- **Refuter:** a pooled estimate that preserves the live E/O difference signal *and* is settled by an anchor neither frame authored; or a demonstration that two frames drawing on the same evidence give genuine independent corroboration. Either breaks F1.

### F2: Residual first, frame second

Fig 8 :53 says *"Residual first, frame second."* Fig 9 :27 says *"describe it before you go looking, or any frame you pick will seem to fit"*, and Fig 9 :32 says *"you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself"*.

The common research practice is the reverse: pick the method first ("randomised trials are the gold standard", or "twenty years in the field is the only real evidence"), then read every result as confirming the choice. That is echo without shadow. The residual has to be described in effect terms first: which claim failed to resolve, for which object slot, at which scale, against which anchor.

- **Status: `triangulated`** (Fig 8 and Fig 9, different figures, different diagrams).
- **Mode: `general`** on the order of operations.
- **Refuter:** show that naming the frame first reaches the same ground verdict without importing the frame's preference. If it does, the order claim is cosmetic.

### F3: An undirected union is mute

Two frames with different closures produce a surplus only when a residual is aimed. Fig 8 :20 says *"nothing was aimed; the union is mute"*. A paper that reports "the trial found X, the ethnography found Y, each has strengths" has described a union without a residual. It has done no comparison.

The surplus itself is often a bond the other frame holds. Fig 12 :133 says the surplus can be *"a bond the other frame holds and you do not."* Experiment holds subtraction (`−`: the control is the point), sequence (`→`: randomise, then treat), and threshold comparison (`⋛`: treatment against control). Practice holds long temporal succession (`→` across seasons), co-occurrence (`+`), and the functional group of skilled judgement. Neither can write the other's bond in its own notation without import.

- **Status: `single-source`** on the figure side for the mute-union claim (Fig 8), with the bond-reading corroborated separately by Fig 12 :133. Two figures, but Fig 12 is used again in F5 and F6, so overlap is moderate rather than low. Marked single-source to be conservative.
- **Mode: `specified`**.
- **Refuter:** a side-by-side listing with no described residual that nevertheless settles a claim at the ground. If one exists, the aim requirement is too strong.

### F4: Transport is a lossy round trip; measure R_T

Fig 4 :22 says *"Only methods cross up. Only head starts come down."* Fig 4 :25 says *"A recording carried upward looks like knowledge and is a description of one occasion."* A result carried from the experiment into the field is a recording of one occasion, not a method that travels.

Fig 4 :30 says *"So take the round trip and measure the gap. Without it you are navigating by dead reckoning: confident about a position nobody checked."* Fig 4 :31 says *"The gap is not an error to eliminate. It is the measurement of what the coarser description cannot hold"*. So the comparison must report the round-trip gap R_T between what was abstracted from one frame and what came back when it was concretised in the other. An effect size transported into a practice setting will not match; the size of the mismatch is the result.

World corroboration, low overlap with the figures: research-methods literature calls this external validity and transportability, defines the *scope* of a study, and notes that Pearl and Bareinboim derived conditions under which findings can be re-calibrated for a second population (*External validity*, Wikipedia, accessed 2026-11-23). Medicine separates trial evidence from real-world evidence and adds "fitness for purpose" (*Real world evidence*, Wikipedia, accessed 2026-11-23).

- **Status: `triangulated`** across Fig 4 and two world fields (statistics, medicine). The figures decide; the world sources corroborate.
- **Mode: `general`** on the lossy-transform claim, `specified` on any particular R_T value (none measured here).
- **Refuter:** a finding transported from experiment to field with zero gap and full preserved meaning, confirmed at the ground. That would falsify the lossy-transform claim.

### F5: A controlled experiment is a substituted habitat; it proves wiring and never field capability

This is the sharpest limit in the whole question. Fig 11 :66 says *"Isolation is not removal of the habitat. It is substitution of one habitat for another."* Fig 11 :70 says *"A test harness is a substituted habitat. Which is why a synthetic solve proves wiring and never capability."* Fig 11 :68 and :69 name the two invisible failure modes: *"what you failed to reproduce is invisible until the goal fails"* and *"what you unintentionally introduced is invisible until it acts"*.

Fig 11 :39 says *"capability is a property of agent-and-habitat, never of the agent alone"*, and Fig 11 :40 says *"An improvement that does not change contact changes nothing, however much it improves."* So a practice skill demonstrated in a lab is a demonstration about the lab habitat. It is not nothing: it is evidence about wiring. But it is not evidence that the same capability survives in the field, because the field habitat was substituted, not removed.

This does not make experiments worthless. World sources give the partial bridges: field experiments are *"experiments carried out outside of laboratory settings"* and can act as *"benchmarks for comparing observational data to experimental results"* (*Field experiment*, Wikipedia, 2026-11-23). Registered reports let the method be peer-reviewed before results are known (*Registered report*, Wikipedia, and COS, both 2026-11-23). Naturalistic observation collects data *"as they occur in nature, without any manipulation by the observer"* but admits that *"it is difficult to replicate findings and demonstrate their reliability"* (*Naturalistic observation*, Wikipedia, 2026-11-23). Each bridge is partial, which is what the figure predicts.

- **Status: `single-source` on the figure side (Fig 11 isolation law), with independent low-overlap world corroboration from experimental economics, ecology field methods, and medicine.** The verdict comes from Fig 11; the world sources are secondary.
- **Mode: `general`** on the isolation law, `specified` on any claim about a particular experiment.
- **Refuter:** an experiment that reproduces the whole field habitat (all actors, conditions and relations) and still fails to preserve capability; or an agent-only improvement that moves the ground, which Fig 11 :72 says would refute the capability claim. A field experiment that generalises without a stated habitat comparison also weakens F5.

### F6: Where each rule works on a different subset, split into domains of validity

Fig 9 :4 says to *"Split it rather than search"* when disagreements do not shrink with effort and each rule keeps working on a different subset. Fig 10 :11-13 describes two frames with *"two frames, one field, no shared reading"*, both locally correct. Fig 10 :18 says *"a declared meaning is not a grounded one"* and :19 says *"→ install what can be violated"*.

So the honest comparison often ends in a map of scopes rather than a single verdict: experiment is the authority for controlled, short-horizon, counterfactual questions where the habitat was reproduced; observation and practice are the authority for long-horizon, unmanipulated, high-ecological-validity questions and for the tacit skill of noticing what to measure. The map is not a compromise. It is the union split into its conditions, each with its own anchor.

World corroboration (low overlap): *External validity* (Wikipedia, 2026-11-23) says researchers *"tend to identify the 'scope' of the study"* and that *"field studies are not by their nature high in external validity and laboratory studies are not by their nature low in external validity"*. It also notes that qualitative research replaces external validity with transferability. That is the same split, reached in research methods rather than in the figures.

- **Status: `triangulated`** (Fig 9 plus Fig 10, and an independent world field).
- **Mode: `specified`**.
- **Refuter:** a single rule that dominates at the ground across every subset where each frame currently works. That would collapse the union to a partition and falsify the split verdict.

### F7: The comparison must be checkable by a seat that cannot reconstruct the claim

Fig 10 :42 says *"A verifier that can reconstruct the claim cannot verify it."* Fig 10 :52 says *"Bring a characterised residual, never the source."* Fig 10 :59 says *"an interest in the outcome is unavoidable, an interest in the reading is disqualifying"*.

So a comparison offered to a reviewer must be a characterised residual plus a refuter, not the full framework plus a conclusion. If the reviewer absorbs the theory first, their later agreement is the pool talking. Fig 2 :40 already prices that. Fig 9 :60 says *"Use filters for the budget and witnesses for the verdict. Never let a filter hand you a verdict."* A method's prestige is a filter for which venue to try; it is not a witness for whether the comparison holds.

- **Status: `single-source` on the figure side (Fig 10), corroborated by the checking-arrangement practice of anonymous review and registered reports (PSA submissions page; COS Registered Reports, both 2026-11-23).**
- **Mode: `general`**.
- **Refuter:** a check whose criterion is itself produced by the frame being checked. If such a check is sound, the verifier-non-access rule is wrong.

### F8: Practice knowledge is carried by contact, not by a record

The "practice" half of the question is not just long observation. It is skill. Fig 4 :25 says *"A recording carried upward looks like knowledge and is a description of one occasion."* The experimenter's regress makes the same point from the opposite side: Collins writes that *"since experimentation is a matter of skilful practice, it can never be clear whether a second experiment has been done sufficiently well to count as a check on the results of a first"* (*Experimenter's regress*, Wikipedia summarising H. M. Collins, accessed 2026-11-23). The published protocol omits *"tacit knowledge (i.e. unarticulated knowledge) that was not included in the published experimental methods"*, and that knowledge *"can never be fully articulated or translated into a set of rules"* (same source).

Both frames therefore hold unrecorded skill. Polanyi's *"we can know more than we can tell"* and the Wikipedia observation that tacit knowledge *"is personal and contextual; it is distributed across knowing subjects, and cannot easily be aggregated"* (*Tacit knowledge*, Wikipedia, 2026-11-23) corroborate Fig 1 :22: aggregation throws away the only signal that says where to look. The Bessemer example in that source is the clean case: a patent that could not transfer the skill, so the inventor had to run the steel company himself.

- **Status: `single-source` on the figure side (Fig 4), with independent low-overlap world corroboration from sociology of science and knowledge management.**
- **Mode: `specified`**.
- **Refuter:** a complete codification of practitioner or experimenter skill with no residual, such that a novice following only the record reproduces the capability at the ground. That would falsify the contact claim.

## 3. Pre-registered refuter

Written before the findings above were drawn up, per the standing rule that a falsifier written before the run is a design instrument.

The shape claim is refuted if any of the following holds:

1. **Blend fires.** A pooled or averaged comparison preserves the live E/O difference signal and is settled by an anchor neither frame authored. (Breaks F1, Fig 1 :22 and Fig 2 :29.)
2. **Self-scoring fires.** A frame scores the comparison with a quantity that frame also produces, and the result survives an independent ground check. (Breaks Fig 1 :27.)
3. **Zero-gap transport fires.** A finding moves from experiment to field with no round-trip gap and unchanged meaning, confirmed at the ground. (Breaks Fig 4 :30.)
4. **Full reproduction fires.** An experiment reproduces the entire field habitat and still fails to preserve capability, or an agent-only improvement changes contact. (Breaks Fig 11 :70 and :40.)
5. **Single-rule fires.** One method dominates at the ground across all the subsets where the other currently works. (Breaks the split verdict, Fig 9 :4.)
6. **Complete codification fires.** A novice following only a written protocol reproduces expert practice with no residual. (Breaks F8.)
7. **Ground absent.** After SEARCHED, INWARD and OUTWARD are run, no shared anchor can be named for a given claim. Then that claim is a `gap`, and comparison is impossible for it by verdict, not by silence.

If any of 1 to 6 fires, the shape claim is withdrawn or narrowed. Until one is run, nothing here is `grounded`.

## 4. Recommendation

1. **State R per slot.** Name the claim, the object slot, the scale, and the anchor before naming a method. Fig 1 :19 and Fig 8 :53.
2. **Buy independence.** Choose two frames with genuinely different evidence pools, not two readings of the same report. Fig 2 :42. Report overlap explicitly.
3. **Declare what each seat may not author.** The comparison frame may not write the verdict for either source frame. Fig 10 :42, :59.
4. **Run the round trip and report R_T.** Never present a transported result without the loss figure. Fig 4 :30.
5. **Declare the habitat.** List what the experiment substituted and what it failed to reproduce. Fig 11 :66-70.
6. **Split the union.** Where each rule wins on a different subset, publish a scope map rather than a winner. Fig 9 :4.
7. **Pre-register the refuter before the comparison.** Use a public registered-report route. Site 1 and the two-stage offer in `02_outreach_email.md` are built for this.
8. **Use filters for budget, witnesses for verdict.** Venue prestige chooses where to try, never whether the comparison holds. Fig 9 :60.
9. **Send the generator, not the conclusion.** Fig 4 :22: *"Only methods cross up. Only head starts come down."*

A one-paragraph version of the practical answer lives in `07_the_tether_answer.md` §6.

## 6. Strains, unsolved items, and the honest stop

- **`⋛` operand type at second order (live strain).** The Operators table restricts comparison to *"a threshold relation between two quantities"*. At second order the operands are two settled bodies of knowledge now serving as atoms. Fig 12 :104 permits the demotion to atom; whether an atom counts as a quantity at that level needs an instrument, not an argument. Named, not hidden.
- **Fig 11 versus field experiments (not strained after re-census).** Fig 11 governs habitat substitution and isolation. Field-experiment literature governs partial restoration of the natural setting. Different objects, so no contradiction. The figure still predicts that a residual remains when the habitat is not fully reproduced.
- **Fig 1 per-slot residual versus pooled meta-analysis (not strained after re-census).** Fig 1 governs the live signal that tells a searcher where to look. Meta-analysis governs average effect estimation. Both are legitimate for different quantities. The error is using the average to tell you where to look, which is exactly Fig 1 :22.
- **Unreachable versus underivable.** No claim here is `grounded`, so none is settled. Per Fig 8 :39, *"a search that finds nothing is never a proof of absence."* For a specific comparison where no anchor can be found after the three questions in Formula step 7, the honest output is `gap`, which is a verdict.

The answer stops where the available instruments can no longer discriminate between a transported experimental finding and a field capability, and records the lineage so the stop is checkable.
