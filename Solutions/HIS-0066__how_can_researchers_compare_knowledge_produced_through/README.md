**Question:** How can researchers compare knowledge produced through controlled experiments with knowledge gained through long-term observation or practice?

**Source:** `Questions/history of science and knowledge.txt` line 81

**Mode:** `specified` on the substantive shape, `general` on the structural claim that comparison is triangulation rather than averaging. Nothing here is `grounded`.

**Status:** done

**Registry status:** pending

## Restating the question as an effect

Two settled bodies of knowledge are in hand. One comes from controlled experiments (intervention, control, random assignment, isolation of a variable). The other comes from long-term observation or practice (unmanipulated field records, temporal sequences, tacit skill). Each is internally coherent and each works in its own setting. The decision to be made is what a claim from one body means for the other. Three ineffective outcomes are available: pool them into one average and lose the live difference; decree by prestige that one is the only real knowledge; or stop at "incommensurable" and compare nothing. The sought-for shape is a comparison that (a) keeps the two frames separate, (b) ranges both against a shared ground, (c) records what is lost in transport, and (d) states in advance what would refute the comparison. Bind that shape as an ordinary term and let it accrue a residual, per `THE_FORMULA.md` "Direction".

## What is deliberately not here

- No personal emails of named individuals.
- No ranking of people by how likely they are to agree.
- No bulk-send plan.

Three reasons in the figures' own words:

1. **An anchor is not a vote.** Fig 2 :35 says *"Alignment is triangulation, not negotiation. A mirror cannot correct you."* Fig 7 :35 adds *"A room's agreement is variation, not a verdict. Selection between rooms is where the ground votes."* A list selected for receptiveness manufactures the agreement it would then cite.
2. **Correlation disqualifies a verdict.** Fig 2 :40 says agreement is *"disqualifying for a verdict: agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool."* Two researchers reading the same review are one witness wearing two names.
3. **A verifier must not hold the interiors.** Fig 10 :42 says *"A verifier that can reconstruct the claim cannot verify it."* So the first contact carries the residual and its refuter, not the framework.

Legally: unsolicited bulk email to harvested personal addresses breaches GDPR and CAN-SPAM style rules in many jurisdictions. Public opt-in venues avoid that practical failure as well as the structural one.
