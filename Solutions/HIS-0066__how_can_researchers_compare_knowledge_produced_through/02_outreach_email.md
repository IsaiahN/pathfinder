# Outreach draft: validate the comparison output first, then decide about the theory

**Use:** one-to-one, or as a cover letter attached to a submission through the public venues in `03_public_contact_points.csv`. **Not** for bulk send.

**Hard precondition:** do not send until the live strain in `01_figure_grounded_audit.md` §6 is named in any version that claims `grounded` status. The output must survive checking or the two-stage offer collapses on contact.

---

**Subject:** Check one comparison output before you decide whether the framework is worth reading

> Hi [name],
>
> I am working on a narrow question in research methods: how to compare knowledge from controlled experiments with knowledge from long-term observation or practice. I have one specific output I would like you to check against what you already know. I am not asking you to read the theory first, and I am not asking you to agree with it. If the output does not survive, there is nothing else to look at.
>
> **The output (`specified`, not `grounded`):** a comparison of two methods must not be a single pooled score. It has four parts.
>
> 1. State the residual in effect terms before naming a method: which claim failed to resolve, for which object, at which scale, against which anchor. An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because the two methods think differently about it.
> 2. Keep the two bodies separate and range each against that anchor separately. Agreement between them counts only if they drew on different evidence. Two readings of the same dataset are one witness wearing two names.
> 3. Report the round-trip gap. When a result is carried from a controlled setting into a field setting, something is lost in the transport. Report the size of the loss rather than pretending the result arrived intact.
> 4. Declare the habitat. A controlled experiment replaces the field habitat with a substituted one. That is a legitimate way to prove wiring, but it does not by itself prove that the capability survives in the field.
>
> **What I am not claiming:** that experiments are higher knowledge than practice, that observation is merely anecdote, that one can be translated into the other without loss, or that a prestige-weighted evidence hierarchy settles anything. Each of those scores a frame with a quantity the frame itself produces.
>
> **What would refute the output:**
> 1. a pooled estimate that preserves the live experiment/field difference signal and is settled by an anchor neither frame authored;
> 2. a finding transported from experiment to field with zero gap and unchanged meaning, confirmed at the ground;
> 3. an experiment that reproduces the whole field habitat and still fails to preserve capability, or an agent-only improvement that changes contact;
> 4. a single method that dominates at the ground across every subset where the other currently works;
> 5. a novice who reproduces expert practice from the written record alone, with no residual.
>
> I wrote these down before asking anyone. If any one holds, I will narrow or retract the output. A one-line "this fails because..." is more useful to me than agreement. If you would rather not engage, no reply is needed.
>
> **If it survives, and only then:** the sources are below. The figures are the authority, not my summary of them.
> - Canonical figures and formula (local project copy): `FRAMEWORK/reference/03-FIGURES_TEXT.md` and `FRAMEWORK/figures/THE_FORMULA.md`
> - Figures README, canonical usage rule: `FRAMEWORK/reference/FIGURES_README_canonical.md`
> - Registered Reports, peer review before results are known: https://www.cos.io/initiatives/registered-reports
> - Experimenter's regress and the role of skilful practice: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Experimenter%27s_regress
>
> **If you want to use the machinery:** the formula is in the figures link. Run it on a comparison you already know and can check against a ground. Write down what would refute your result *before* you run it. A reported failure is more useful to me than a reported success.
>
[name]
