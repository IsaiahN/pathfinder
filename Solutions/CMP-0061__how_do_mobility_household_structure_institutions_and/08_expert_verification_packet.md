# Expert verification packet: the Tether on four conditions and epidemic spread

**What this is:** a claim in a form a social-networks or epidemic-dynamics researcher can check. Mode, derivation and refuters are stated before the conclusion, so a reader knows what to look for and what would settle it.

**Mode: `specified` on the shape, `general` on the composition rule, not `grounded`.** No coefficient or reproduction number is claimed. This is a shape and a route, not a result.

## 1. The claim, in one paragraph

The four conditions named in the question are not additive contributions to one joint effect; they form a union compound whose overlaps are categorically different cases. Any answer must keep the residual per condition in context, enumerate the contact habitat out to second-order cascades (online information changes behavior, behavior changes contact, contact changes infection, and cases feed back into information), and treat the information link as a cycle rather than a chain. Written over a shared set of persons, places, contacts, information and rules, the four conditions are isomers, same atoms arranged differently. The ground is measured outcomes and a single epidemic curve cannot name its own source. The framework supplies the shape and the falsifiers written in advance, not the coefficients.

## 2. Three checkable sub-claims

### C1 - framework-internal; checkable by reading Figures 9 and 1

- **Statement:** "jointly" is a union compound, and the residual must be indexed per slot, never averaged.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 9 :55 "A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones." Also Fig 9 :54 "And when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again." And Fig 1 :19 "The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated."
- **Refuter:** show that the four effects on a measured outcome are separable and additive with no interaction terms, and that one scalar summary predicts held-out outcomes as well as per-slot residuals with no hidden live slot (R1, R2).

### C2 - structure-facing; checkable by a network or epidemic modeler

- **Statement:** the information-to-contact link is a cycle, and the four conditions are isomers over a shared atom set.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 12 :121 "cycle. The output re-enters as input. The sign of the loop decides whether it closes, and a chain cannot close by construction." Also Fig 12 :117 "isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."
- **What to check:** is the feedback from cases to behavior measurable in real data (mobility change after case surges, advisory timing)? Do the four conditions share a common atom set, or must one be written with an atom the others do not use? World sources to compare: Granell et al. (2013, 2014) on coupled awareness and epidemics; Wang et al. (2015) on coupled disease-behavior dynamics.
- **Refuter:** demonstrate a one-way information effect with no feedback from cases to behavior (R3), or write one condition without an atom another requires and still get the same outcome class (kills the isomer claim, leaves the functional-group claim).

### C3 - ground-facing; checkable against data

- **Statement:** a single epidemic curve cannot attribute itself to one condition, and the ground is discriminating measurement.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 2 :35 "Alignment is triangulation, not negotiation. A mirror cannot correct you." Also Fig 2 :45 "Frames range against the ground. They do not negotiate with it." The supporting essay *The Hard Problem is About Source* states: "If several paths create the same behavior, then behavior is a projection with lossy information."
- **What to check:** can stratified contact data, mobility traces, serology by subgroup, and policy/information timing separate candidate mixtures that produce the same peak? If not, the source-attribution claim is unmeasured, not refuted.
- **Refuter:** show a discriminating instrument exists in principle and that agreement between co-calibrated models is being used as settlement (R5), or show random mixing within conditions with no second-order information effect (R4).

## 3. What is explicitly not claimed

- Not one additive "joint effect" number, and not a ranked importance list.
- Not a reproduction ratio, coefficient, or forecast. No measurement was taken.
- Not that any one condition is *the* cause. Attribution needs a source-discriminating instrument.
- Not that online connections are a physical contact layer. They act through behavior.
- Not a physical six-term mapping of the framework onto epidemics. Only the network structure and cross-kind rule generalize.
- Not grounded. Every substantive claim is `specified` or `general`.

## 5. What would settle it

A discriminating instrument, not a further argument. From *The Hard Problem is About Source*: an instrument "cannot comment on the source," and "An instrument that can't distinguish these cases demonstrates its own inadequacy." Here:

1. Are there non-additive interaction terms between mobility and household, or information and contact, on a measured outcome? (R1)
2. Does a per-slot model beat one pooled score on held-out data? (R2)
3. Do rising cases measurably change information and behavior, closing the cycle? (R3)
4. Are within-condition contacts clustered, with a second-order information effect? (R4)

## 6. What an expert can check in twenty minutes

1. Read Fig 9 :54-55, Fig 1 :19, Fig 12 :121 and :117, Fig 2 :35 and :45. Six short passages.
2. Check C2 against your own model: is the case-to-information feedback real in your data, or are you fitting a one-way chain?
3. Name one discriminator that would separate two mixtures producing the same peak, or say why none exists.
4. Send the refutation. A failure is more useful than an endorsement.
