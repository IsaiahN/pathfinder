## QUESTION

Decide, in effect terms, what the Tether says about the claim that mobility, household structure, institutions, and online connections jointly shape epidemic spread. Option A: read "jointly" as four additive contributions to one effect. Option B: read it as a union compound whose intersections are categorically different, with a per-slot residual, an enumerated habitat, a cyclic information link, and coefficients owed from measurement. What each leads to: A produces one pooled number that hides the live slot, attributes a projection to a single source, and cannot represent case-to-information feedback; B produces a falsifiable shape, written refuters, and an open slot until a discriminating instrument settles the coefficients. The figures decide B.

## DOMAIN CENSUS

R by arity, symmetry, scale, then one line per domain with actors / currency / anchor, partition or union, and order by dependency.

- **R (the residual).** The gap between what a model using the four conditions predicts and what the ground returns (case data, serology, arrival times). Arity: four conditions, so at least four-dimensional, never one scalar. Symmetry: not interchangeable. Mobility moves persons between places; household structure fixes persistent cliques; institutions add or remove edges by rule; online connections carry information. Scale: mobility is spatial and between-place, household is small-group, institutions are meso to macro, online is an information layer overlaid on the physical one.
- **Domain 1, physical transmission habitat.** Actors: people, households, schools, workplaces, transport nodes, pathogen. Currency: physical contacts and infection events. Anchor: case counts, seroprevalence, arrival times. This is a **union** (mobility and household overlap), not a partition.
- **Domain 2, information and behavior.** Actors: people as information receivers, online platforms, institutions as broadcasters. Currency: awareness, beliefs, behavior change. Anchor: measured mobility change after advisories, compliance surveys. This couples back into Domain 1 as a **cycle**.
- **Domain 3, institutional / policy.** Actors: institutions, regulators, policies. Currency: rules, closures, mandates. Anchor: the dated policy decision plus its measured effect on Domain 1 outcomes. It modifies Domains 1 and 2.
- **Order by dependency.** State the residual per slot, enumerate the habitat, pin the ground, identify the couplings, then fit coefficients. Refuse the one-number reading before minting any coefficient (formula step 2); describe the residual before choosing a frame (Fig 9 :27-28).

## WORLD (secondary sources read)

World sources are corroboration only. Each gives field, author / venue / year, a verbatim quote, its prediction here, and its status. They are genuine low-overlap sources from epidemic dynamics and network science, not framework-author essays.

- **Field: network epidemiology.** Pastor-Satorras, Castellano, Van Mieghem, Vespignani, Rev. Mod. Phys. 87, 925 (2015). Quote: "Physicists, mathematicians, epidemiologists, computer, and social scientists share a common interest in studying epidemic spreading and rely on similar models for the description of the diffusion of pathogens, knowledge, and innovation." Predicts that social contagion and pathogen spread share models, supporting a shared atom set; corroborates the isomer reading.
- **Field: multiplex contagion.** Granell, Gomez, Arenas, Phys. Rev. Lett. 111, 128701 (2013). Quote: "a cyclic information awareness process diffusing in the network of virtual social contacts between the same individuals." Predicts a cycle coupling awareness to epidemic onset; corroborates the cycle claim in domain 2.
- **Field: multiplex contagion.** Granell, Gomez, Arenas, Phys. Rev. E 90, 012808 (2014). Quote: "the mass media makes the metacritical point to disappear." Predicts that institutions broadcasting information change the epidemic onset, so institutions act across layers; corroborates domain 3 coupling.
- **Field: mobility and contagion.** Brockmann and Helbing, Science 342, 1337 (2013). Quote: "complex spatiotemporal patterns can be reduced to surprisingly simple, homogeneous wave propagation patterns, if conventional geographic distance is replaced by a probabilistically motivated effective distance." Predicts mobility reshapes the topology that spread traverses; corroborates the mobility slot as a structural actor, not a rate.
- **Field: household contact structure.** Goeyvaerts et al., Proc. R. Soc. B 285, 20182201 (2018). Quote: "Epidemic models, used to gain insight into infectious disease spread and control, typically rely on the assumption of random mixing within households. Until now, there has been no direct empirical evidence to support this assumption." Predicts household structure is a real, clustered slot, refuting the random-mixing baseline; corroborates the habitat enumeration.
- **Field: demographic household structure.** Molgelmose et al., J. R. Soc. Interface (2023). Quote: "a strong link between age and household structures and the implications thereof for the risk of infection for different population subgroups during an epidemic." Predicts subgroup-specific residuals, corroborating the per-slot rule.
- **Field: coupled disease-behavior dynamics.** Wang, Andrews, Wu, Wang, Bauch, Physics of Life Reviews 15, 1-29 (2015). Quote: "Human behavior such as contact precautions and social distancing clearly influence disease prevalence, but disease prevalence can in turn alter human behavior, forming a coupled, nonlinear system." Predicts a closed feedback loop; corroborates domain 2 cycle.

No world source conflicts with the figure verdict, so the conflict rule was not triggered. The sources draw on overlapping literature (several cite the same multiplex and coupled-behavior studies), so their agreement is one evidence pool, not seven independent witnesses.

## VERDICT (from the figures)

The shape survives; the coefficients do not exist yet in this derivation. Mobility, household structure, institutions and online connections form a union compound, so "jointly" must be unbundled into intersections rather than averaged. The residual stays per slot; the habitat is enumerated to second-order cascades; the information-to-contact link is a cycle whose sign can damp or amplify; the four conditions are isomers over a shared atom set (or a functional group if written disjunctly); and the ground is measured outcomes, not model agreement. A single epidemic curve cannot name its source. The numeric coefficients are a `gap` owed from a discriminating instrument, not from more search inside the figure set. Result: `specified` and ungrounded; candidate, not grounded.

- **cost if wrong:** bounded and locatable. The packet states its mode, lists refuters before the conclusion and records the lineage, so an error is findable. The real cost is spending an outside reader's independence by sending a recording (a coefficient table and a theory) instead of a generator (the shape and its refuter). Fig 2 :42 prices this: "Buy independence in proportion to the cost of being wrong." That cost is avoidable by leading with the shape claim and R1 to R6.
- **undoable:** yes. The partition reading, the pooled score, the one-way chain and the physical six-term mapping are removable without touching the shape. `07_the_tether_answer.md` and `08_expert_verification_packet.md` already remove them. A sent claim can be superseded by the corrected packet; no primitive has to be withdrawn.
- **reversed by:** a ground reading, not agreement. Non-additive interaction terms and a per-slot model beating a pooled score would settle the union/per-slot shape; a measurable case-to-information feedback would confirm the cycle; a demonstrated one-way effect would break it; clustered contacts with a second-order information effect would confirm the habitat claim; and a source-discriminating instrument would name which mixture produced a curve. A figure re-census that derives the coefficients without measurement would be required to move the numeric verdict off `gap`. Until one of these fires, the verdict stands as `candidate`.
