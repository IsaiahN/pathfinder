## Wider literature that bears on the four conditions

- **Mobility:** Brockmann and Helbing (2013) show that geographic distance is the wrong axis; mobility flows define an effective distance that predicts arrival. This is the mobility slot: the actor changes the topology, not just a rate.
- **Household structure:** Goeyvaerts et al. (2018) and Molgelmose et al. (2023) show that within-household contact is clustered and demographic structure changes subgroup risk. This is the household slot, and it is the strongest empirical refuter of the random-mixing baseline.
- **Institutions:** the workplace-closures modeling study (https://www.nature.com/articles/s41598-024-66741-3, Sci. Rep. 2024) reports that clustering within multi-layer networks dampens cumulative and peak infections and delays the peak, with stronger effects in larger households. Institutions act by adding or removing edges across layers.
- **Online connections:** Granell et al. (2013, 2014) put awareness on a virtual layer coupled to physical contacts and show the epidemic onset depends on the awareness process and virtual topology. This is the online slot and the cycle of F4.
- **Joint composition:** Pastor-Satorras et al. (2015) and Wang et al. (2015) place these in one literature on coupled, coevolving and time-varying networks. Their agreement with the figure shape is expected and is one evidence pool, not independent corroboration.

None of these world sources conflicts with the figure verdict, so the conflict rule was not triggered. They corroborate the shape; they do not supply the coefficients.
