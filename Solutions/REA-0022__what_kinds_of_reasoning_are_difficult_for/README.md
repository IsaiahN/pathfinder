## What the answer is, in effect terms

A reasoning failure in a system with a fixed library can have three different causes. The figures separate them: the needed term is not reachable from what the system holds (algorithmic closure limit); the term is reachable but was not found at this search budget (computing power); no instrument anywhere is returning a signal for it (data or instrument gap). Only the first is an algorithmic limit. Fig 8 :40 says *"Underivable-in-principle and unreachable-at-this-budget are different facts, and they issue different instructions."* Fig 6 :27 adds that *"some unreachability is depth and some is genuine absence, and from inside the frame the two look the same."*

The defensible answer is a taxonomy of five kinds of reasoning that are limited by the frame's structure rather than by data or compute, plus an honest diagnostic that tells you which cause you are facing before you spend a budget on the wrong remedy.

## What is deliberately not here

- No personal emails of named individuals.
- No ranking of people by how likely they are to agree with the taxonomy.
- No bulk-send plan.
- No claim that a named current system (a particular LLM, a particular architecture) has been diagnosed. The figures settle the shape of the limits; a ground reading of a system would be needed to assign a cause to one of its failures.

Three figure-grounded reasons for the omissions:

1. Fig 10 :42: *"A verifier that can reconstruct the claim cannot verify it."* Showing a recipient the whole framework before asking for a verdict spends the independence that makes their check worth having.
2. Fig 2 :40: *"agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool."* A list selected for receptiveness manufactures the correlation that disqualifies a verdict.
3. Fig 9 :60: *"Use filters for the budget and witnesses for the verdict. Never let a filter hand you a verdict."*
