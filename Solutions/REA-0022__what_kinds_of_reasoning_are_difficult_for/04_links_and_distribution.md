## Wider literature that bears on the question

The figures' own names for the barriers are Godel 1931, Tarski 1936, Chaitin 1974 (Fig 8 :44), Rissanen 1978 (Fig 5 :45), Cousot and Cousot 1977 (Fig 4 :36), Liebig and Wöhler 1824 to 1826 (Fig 12 :97-98), and Wolpert's-style no-free-lunch reasoning in the optimization sense. The world sources above are outside the framework's authors, which is what gives them whatever independent weight they have. Fig 2 :40 (*"agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool"*) is the reason the ledger records overlap explicitly: the SEP and Wikipedia sources are one broad mathematical pool in places (both report Gödel and Chaitin), so their agreement is corroboration of the proved results, not a second discovery of them.

Two cautions when using this literature:

1. **Computational irreducibility is not the same as an algorithmic limit here.** A claim that you must run the computation to know the result is a statement about time (cause B), not about an atom missing from a closure (cause A). Do not cite it as if it settled this question. Fig 8 :40-43 separates the two.
2. **No-free-lunch is about averaged performance over all functions.** It does not say a particular practical problem is hard. The Wikipedia article itself notes this. Cite it for the structural point about assumptions, not as evidence about a named system.

## Distribution: offer, do not push

Figure-grounded reasons:

- **Fig 4 :22** — *"Only methods cross up. Only head starts come down."* Sending the full taxonomy before someone has described a residual sends a recording, not a generator. Lead with the three-way split and the refuter; offer links after.
- **Fig 10 :42** — *"A verifier that can reconstruct the claim cannot verify it."* A recipient who has absorbed the whole framework before being asked for a verdict has joined the frame being checked.
- **Fig 9 :27-28** — *"describe it before you go looking, or any frame you pick will seem to fit."* Ask what they would count as evidence for a closure limit before giving them the source list.
- **Fig 9 :60** — *"Use filters for the budget and witnesses for the verdict. Never let a filter hand you a verdict."* A venue's prestige or a recipient's receptiveness is a contact filter, never evidence.
- **Fig 7 :35** — *"A room's agreement is variation, not a verdict."* A pile of agreements from a mailing list cannot corroborate the taxonomy.

**Recommended order in a reply:** (1) the three-way split and the one ground-facing refuter; (2) ask what they would count as a closure failure; (3) record their verdict; (4) only then the links, with the note that the figures, not the summary, are the authority.

## The two-stage offer

1. **Stage 1:** send the residual (which cause is active in a failure you both know) and the pre-registered refuter. Ask what they would count as a failure. Record the verdict before any theory is shown. This is Fig 10 :52, *"Bring a characterised residual, never the source."*
2. **Stage 2, opt-in only:** if they are curious, offer the figure extract and the world links. Their later agreement is never pooled as corroboration (Fig 2 :40).
3. **Stage 3, if asked:** offer the eight-step formula as a method to reuse, with the instruction that each user writes a refuter and states a mode before running it. The framework's own "Direction" section says a loop cannot check the direction of its own question unless the sought-for shape is bound as an ordinary term.

**Hard precondition:** stages 1 to 3 only work if stage 1 has an instance. A taxonomy with no failure to check is not ground-visible. Find the failure first.
