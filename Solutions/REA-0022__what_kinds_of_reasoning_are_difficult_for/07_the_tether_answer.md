# The Tether's answer: algorithmic limits on reasoning

**Mode: `specified` on the taxonomy and the diagnostic order, `general` on the structural shape. Not `grounded`.** Simulated derivation checked for internal consistency against the figures (`05_what_shape_means.md` §6), not a ground reading. No current system was measured.

## 1. The residual it answers

A system with library Γ fails at a reasoning task. The question asks which failures are due to the frame's structure rather than to data or compute. The figures make this a three-way union, not one hard problem: the term is not reachable from what is held (algorithmic), is reachable but unfound at this budget (compute), or has never registered on any instrument (data). Fig 8 :40: *"Underivable-in-principle and unreachable-at-this-budget are different facts, and they issue different instructions."* Fig 6 :27: *"some unreachability is depth and some is genuine absence, and from inside the frame the two look the same."*

## 2. The five kinds on the algorithmic side

1. **Reasoning about your own consistency, limit or complexity.** Fig 8 :34: *"A frame cannot reach its own metaframe. Godel’s second theorem: a consistent system cannot prove its own consistency. Tarski: truth for a language is not definable within that language."* Fig 8 :37: *"A frame cannot certify its own limit. Chaitin: beyond a fixed constant, no system proves that any object exceeds its own complexity."* Proved barriers, not empirical findings. `triangulated` at the level of two distinct quantities (consistency and complexity), with a shared-method caveat.

2. **Refusing a held claim.** Fig 12 :140: *"A vocabulary that cannot refuse can only accumulate."* Operators table :58: *"a refutation is a negation, which is why a vocabulary without it can only accumulate"*. More data increases the pile; refusal needs the unary operator ¬.

3. **Reaching outside your own closure.** Fig 8 :32: *"Closure is idempotent. Composing inside a closed set never leaves it, so no search, however long, adds an atom."* The phrase *"however long"* is the algorithmic limit in one line. Remedy: import, in effect terms (Fig 9 :27-28), never a longer run against the same wall.

4. **Recovering arrangement from ingredients.** Fig 12 :5: *"A set of ingredients is not one. The bond is the rest of the information, and it is the half nothing was recording."* Fig 12 :111: *"A vocabulary without bonds can name a state and cannot name a route to one."* If the recording dropped the bond, the information is not in the data at any volume.

5. **Inverting a lossy cross-scale transform, and checking your own question's direction.** Fig 4 :27: *"The transform between scales loses information, and that is the point."* Fig 4 :28-29: *"Going up throws away detail; that is what makes the result reapplicable. But it means the trip is not reversible, and something carried up and brought back down will not match what was sent."* Fig 5 :63: *"Every check above runs on the MECHANISM and none runs on the DIRECTION OF THE QUESTION"*. Remedy: measure `R_T` and bind the sought-for shape as an ordinary term (Fig 5 :65-66).

## 3. The diagnostic order (this is the usable part)

The order is dependency, not importance (Fig 3 :20). `THE_FORMULA.md` step 7 asks SEARCHED first because it is cheapest and the other two assume its answer:

```
SEARCHED  is the term already in closure and merely unfound?  -> more independent search
INWARD    can an existing instrument be sharpened to hold it?  -> a better instrument
OUTWARD   does another frame already hold it?                  -> a provenanced import
```

Do not skip to import when the case is a budget case: Fig 8 :42 says that adds an atom that was not needed. Do not treat a never-registered signal as a closure barrier: Fig 6 :34 says *"The question is not whether a sensor could exist. It is whether anything, at any resolution, is already returning something that fails to resolve."*

## 4. What it explicitly does not say

- Not a claim that any named current system (a particular LLM, a particular architecture) has been diagnosed. That needs a ground reading of that system.
- Not a claim that compute and data are irrelevant. Most practical failures may be budget or instrument failures. The claim is only that they are different causes with different remedies.
- Not a single hard-problem list. The three causes are a union compound (Fig 9 :54) and must be unbundled before searching.
- Not a complete taxonomy. Fig 7 :6 names *"the limit: no last room"*; Fig 9 :44: *"Only a sealed room can be searched to the end, and no frame is a sealed room."*
- Not `grounded`. No measurement settles any of this here.
- Not a claim that the three self-reference results are three independent witnesses. They share the diagonal method (Fig 2 :40). They are structural isomers, one corroborating pool.

## 5. What would settle it — the ground

1. **Ground-facing refuter (the one that matters):** take a failure the taxonomy marks as algorithmic for a real system, change only data or compute (no change of frame, operator or instrument), and see if it disappears. If it does, the application to current systems fails.
2. **F1:** a consistent system proving its own consistency, or certifying complexity above the fixed bound in its own terms.
3. **F2:** a system with no negation operator refusing a held claim from more of the same data.
4. **F3:** composition inside `closure(Γ)` adding an atom without import, or an import-free speedup fixing an underivable case.
5. **F4:** bond and arrangement fully recoverable from operands alone, with identical behaviour under every rearrangement.
6. **F5:** `R_T = 0` for every input, or a loop checking its own question's direction without binding the shape.
7. **Instrument test:** for a case that looks like a data limit, find something already returning a failing reading at some resolution. Fig 6 :35: *"Where nothing is, there is no edge to extend from."*

## 6. The honest stop

**Unreached, not unreachable.** The figures return a kind of thing, never an instance (Fig 9 :32). *The Recursive Self-Improvement Trap* is explicit that a model can *"simulate an approximate answer/output"* but the testable output *"would need to be derived from the real architecture or working the formula yourself."* This answer stops at the limit of what a simulated derivation can distinguish and records the lineage so the stop is checkable. *"Indiscernibility is not identity."* Fig 13 :134: *"Unreachable is a verdict here, not a silence"* applies to each cause separately.

## 7. One-paragraph version for outreach

> The Tether does not rank reasoning tasks by difficulty, and it does not claim to have measured any current model. It says that a reasoning failure has three possible causes, and that the figures treat them as different facts with different remedies: the needed term is not reachable from what the system holds (an algorithmic closure limit), it is reachable but unfound at this budget (compute), or nothing anywhere is returning a signal for it (data or instrument). Five kinds of reasoning sit on the algorithmic side: reasoning about your own consistency or complexity, refusing a held claim, reaching outside your closure, recovering an arrangement from a list of ingredients, and inverting a lossy abstraction back to an instance. The usable part is the order of diagnosis, which is dependency rather than importance: search first, then extend an instrument, then import from a frame whose closure differs. What would refute the taxonomy is a failure it marks as algorithmic that more data or more compute alone removes. Until someone runs that test against a real system, this is a shape and a route, not a result.
