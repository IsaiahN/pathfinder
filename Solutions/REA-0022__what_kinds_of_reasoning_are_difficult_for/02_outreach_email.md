# Outreach draft — check the residual before you decide about the theory

**Use:** one-to-one, or as a cover note attached to a submission through the public venues in `03_public_contact_points.csv`. **Not** for bulk send.

**Hard precondition:** do not send until you have a specific failure to check. A taxonomy with no instance is a talking point, not a ground-visible claim.

---

**Subject:** A three-way split for reasoning failures, and the one refuter that would break it

> Hi [name],
>
> I am working on a narrow question: when a reasoning system fails, how do you tell an algorithmic limit from insufficient data or insufficient compute? I have one specific claim I would like you to check against what you already know. I am not asking you to read a theory first, and I am not asking you to agree with it. If the claim does not survive, there is nothing else to look at.
>
> **The claim (`specified`, not `grounded`):** a reasoning failure can have three different causes, and the figures treat them as different facts with different instructions. The needed term is not reachable from what the system holds (an algorithmic closure limit); it is reachable but was not found at this budget (compute); or nothing anywhere is returning a signal for it (data or instrument). The source puts it plainly: *"Underivable-in-principle and unreachable-at-this-budget are different facts, and they issue different instructions."* And: *"Closure is idempotent. Composing inside a closed set never leaves it, so no search, however long, adds an atom."*
>
> From that split, five kinds of reasoning fall on the algorithmic side rather than the data or compute side:
>
> 1. reasoning about your own consistency, your own limit or your own complexity (Godel, Tarski, Chaitin),
> 2. refusing a held claim, which needs a unary negation operator rather than more data,
> 3. reaching outside your own closure, which needs an import rather than a longer search,
> 4. recovering an arrangement from a list of ingredients, where the bond was never recorded,
> 5. inverting a lossy abstraction back to an instance, or checking the direction of your own question.
>
> **What I am not claiming:** that I have measured a named current system and found it limited in one of these ways. That would need a ground reading of that system. I am also not claiming that more compute never helps. Most hard failures are probably budget failures. The claim is only that the three causes are different and that you cannot spend your way out of all of them.
>
> **What would refute the claim:** a concrete failure that the split marks as algorithmic, removed by more data or more compute alone, with no change of frame, operator or instrument. More narrowly: a consistent system proving its own consistency; a system with no negation operator refusing a held claim from more of the same data; composition inside a closed set adding a new atom; bond and arrangement recoverable from operands alone; a lossless round trip with zero gap.
>
> I wrote these down before sending this. If any of them holds, I will retract the taxonomy. A one-line "this fails because..." is more useful to me than agreement. If you would rather not engage, no reply is needed.
>
> **Before you answer, one question:** when you see a reasoning failure in a system you know, what would you count as evidence that it is a closure limit rather than a search-budget limit? I would like your verdict on that before I show you any framework material.
>
> **If it survives, and only then:** the sources are below. The figures, not my summary of them, are the authority.
> - Verbatim figure text with line numbers (local framework extract): `FRAMEWORK/reference/03-FIGURES_TEXT.md`
> - Canonical usage rule: https://github.com/IsaiahN/tether/blob/general-agent/figures/README.md
> - Figures and formula for direct download (branch `general-agent`): https://github.com/IsaiahN/tether/tree/general-agent/figures
> - Gödel's Incompleteness Theorems (Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy): https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/goedel-incompleteness/
> - Negation (Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy): https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/negation/
>
> **If you want to use the machinery:** the eight-step formula is in the figures link. Run it on a problem where you have a ground and can check the answer. Write down what would refute your result *before* you run it, and label the mode of what you get. A reported failure is more useful to me than a reported success.
