## QUESTION

Decide, in effect terms, which kinds of reasoning are difficult for current systems because of algorithmic limits rather than insufficient data or computing power. Option A: treat "hard reasoning" as one pile and look for a single lever. Option B: split the failure into three causes (not reachable from what is held; reachable but unfound at this budget; never registered on any instrument), then name the kinds that sit on the first cause and give a diagnostic order that tells the three apart. What each leads to: A spends compute or imports atoms against the wrong cause and mislabels budget failures as algorithmic ones; B returns a checkable taxonomy, a dependency-ordered diagnostic and a refuter, at the cost of refusing to name a cause in any specific system until a ground reading exists.

## VERDICT (from the figures)

The answer is a taxonomy of causes, not a list of hard tasks. Five kinds of reasoning are limited by the frame's structure rather than by data or compute: self-reference about one's own consistency or complexity (Domain 1, proved and triangulated); refusal, closure escape and arrangement recovery (Domain 2, triangulated at the boundary and single-source per sub-kind); and inverting a lossy cross-scale transform or checking one's own question's direction (Domain 3, single-source with external corroboration). The diagnostic order searched, inward, outward is itself triangulated across Figures 6, 8, 9 and the formula. The three causes form a union compound and cannot be averaged. No named current system is diagnosed: that would need a ground reading, so the verdict stands as `specified`, ungrounded and candidate.

- **cost if wrong:** bounded and locatable. The packet states modes, refuters and provenance before the conclusion, so an error is findable rather than invisible. The real cost is applying the wrong remedy: importing against a budget limit adds an atom that was not needed (Fig 8 :42), and treating a never-registered signal as a closure barrier sends a search for a proto-instrument where there is no edge to extend from (Fig 6 :35). Both costs are avoidable by running the diagnostic in dependency order. Fig 2 :42 says *"Buy independence in proportion to the cost of being wrong."*
- **undoable:** yes. The taxonomy can be reduced from five kinds to fewer without changing the figures: the falsifier in `06_isomers_and_order.md` Q4 says that if adding one operator (for example negation) removes the closure, bond and round-trip limits, the union was a partition and the taxonomy an overcount. A sent claim can be superseded by the corrected packet; no primitive has to be withdrawn.
- **reversed by:** a ground reading, not agreement. The decisive test is a failure marked algorithmic that disappears when only data or compute change, with no change of frame, operator or instrument. Narrower reversers: a consistent system proving its own consistency; a system without negation refusing from more data; composition adding an atom without import; bond fully recoverable from operands; `R_T = 0` for all inputs; or an instrument already returning a failing reading where the taxonomy predicted none. Until one of these fires, the verdict remains `candidate`.
