# The Tether's answer: what determines whether an introduced species becomes invasive and transforms an ecosystem?

**Mode: `specified` : not `grounded`.** This is a shape and a route, not a field result. Simulated
derivation checked against the figures (`05_what_shape_means.md` section 6), not a ground reading.

## 1. The residual it answers

The question arrives as an agent-only question: which species are invasive, and what traits do they
have? Stated as a residual, the contact failure is that we can list traits and still not predict
whether a given introduction will transform a given receiving system. Fig 11 :39: *"So the
answerable question is not what intelligence is, but what makes a system capable. And capability is
a property of agent-and-habitat, never of the agent alone."* The residual is a **union compound** of
establishment, cascade reach and impact sign (Fig 9 :55-56), not one hard question.
The governing enumeration rule is Fig 11 :43 "The habitat is enumerated, never composed."

## 2. The shape

1. **Invasiveness is a relation, not a property of the species.** Fig 11 :39-40: capability belongs
   to agent-and-habitat, and an improvement that does not change contact changes nothing. The
   answerable question is which (actor, habitat, residual) triples transform, not which species is
   "invasive" in the abstract. **Triangulated** with Fig 4 :34-35, *"A seed carries no small tree: it
   carries what will grow one, and the conditions it lands in decide what it becomes"* (a different
   figure about a different subject, so low overlap).

2. **Name the residual before using the word.** Fig 11 :49-51: valence is relative to the residual;
   the same actor is a benefactor to one residual and a malfactor to another; mutualism and
   parasitism are relations rather than kinds. There is no standing list of invasive species that
   survives a change of residual. **Single-source** in the figures, with `THE_FORMULA.md` "Habitat"
   restating the same rule (same framework, so not an independent witness).

3. **The transformation is a contact cascade out to third order.** Fig 11 :44: *"List everything in
   contact with the residual, then what is in contact with those, and outward until the cascade
   stops mattering."* Fig 11 :52: *"And contact cascades. Scoping the habitat to direct contact is
   how DDT reached the eagles: first-order enumeration, second-order effect, and the residual that
   mattered was three steps out."* Enumeration classes are actors, conditions and relations
   (Fig 11 :45-47). **Single-source** in the figures.

4. **Introduction is an asymmetric seat move.** Fig 11 :61-62: *"The seat’s power is asymmetric.
   Introduction is available; exclusion is not."* Fig 11 :65: *"Introducing an actor for its
   cascade is ecosystem engineering, after Jones, Lawton and Shachak, 1994. Cane toads and kudzu are
   the same move with the cascade unenumerated."* Engineering and disaster are the same move; the
   difference is whether the cascade was enumerated. **Single-source** in the figures.

5. **Keep the slots separate.** Fig 1 :19: *"The residual is indexed by object slot, not
   aggregated."* A single ecosystem impact score is the forbidden average. **Triangulated** with
   Fig 2 :37-38 (an instrument is right on the axes it measures and can be wrong on axes it does
   not), i.e. measurement method and residual indexing are two different figures.

6. **Isolation is substitution, not removal.** Fig 11 :66-69: moving the residual requires
   reproducing what the goal needed and inherits unchosen actors; what you failed to reproduce and
   what you brought with you are silent until they act. **Single-source** in the figures.

## 3. What it explicitly does not say

- Not a list of invasive species, and not a trait syndrome. The framework returns a shape, not a name.
- Not that the Tether habitat *is* the ecological niche. That mapping is declined in the canonical
  article; see `05_what_shape_means.md` section 7. The similarity is noted, the definitions are not
  aligned.
- Not that cane toads or kudzu are derived cases. Fig 11 :65 names them as the same move with the
  cascade unenumerated, which is an instance or retrodiction, not a forward prediction (Fig 8 :56).
- Not that ecology sources decide the verdict. They corroborate only; the figures decide.
- Not that consensus among specialists settles a case. Fig 2 :35: *"Alignment is triangulation, not
  negotiation. A mirror cannot correct you."*
- Not `grounded`. No field measurement was taken in this derivation.

## 4. What would settle it : the ground

A measurement of the enumerated contact graph and its cascade, per slot, against a named residual.
Not a further argument, and not a pooled impact index.

- **The enumeration test:** list actors, conditions and relations around the residual and follow the
  cascade until it stops mattering. Did the effect that mattered arrive at first, second or third
  order? (Fig 11 :44, :52).
- **The invariance test:** hold the actor and propagule pressure constant across habitats. If the
  cascade is invariant, Fig 11 :39 is refuted.
- **The residual test:** state which effect you are tracking. Reverse it. If the "invasive" label
  flips with the residual and nothing physical changed, Fig 11 :49-51 is confirmed; if a
  context-free ordering exists, it is refuted.
- **The exclusion test:** attempt a one-off removal. If the system restores permanently without
  continuing work, Fig 11 :61-64 is refuted.
- Fig 6 :32: *"An instrument is not built from a description. It is improved from a worse instrument
  already returning something."* The enumeration grows from whatever the field already measures,
  not from an abstract specification.

## 5. The honest stop

**Unreached, not unreachable.** The canonical article records that the capability claim and the
isolation law *"fit cases already known and have not been tested forward, so they can still come
back wrong."* The shape is held as `candidate`: a contact-relative account of invasiveness with a
three-order cascade and an asymmetric introduction. It stops where the instruments stop
discriminating, and records the lineage so the stop is checkable. Fig 8 :39: *"So a search that
finds nothing is never a proof of absence."* Indiscernibility is not identity.

## 6. One-paragraph version for outreach

> The Tether does not produce a list of invasive species. It says the question is asked in the wrong
> form: invasiveness is not carried by an actor but is a property of the actor together with the
> habitat it enters and the effect you are tracking. The answerable task is to name that effect,
> enumerate the actors, conditions and relations in contact with it, and follow the cascade outward
> until it stops mattering, because the change that transformed the system was three steps out in
> the DDT and eagles case. It also says the human introduction is an asymmetric move: you can add an
> actor and you cannot subtract the habitat, so managed ecosystems are permanent work. Cane toads
> and kudzu are not derived examples; they are the same move with the cascade left unenumerated.
> What the framework refuses to do is name the species or the trait. It offers the shape any answer
> must have, refuters written in advance, and the measurement that would close it. The stop stays
> open until the ground settles it.
