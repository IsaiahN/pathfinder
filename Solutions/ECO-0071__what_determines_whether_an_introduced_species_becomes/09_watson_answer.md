## QUESTION

Decide, in effect terms, what the Tether says about the question "what determines whether an
introduced species becomes invasive and transforms an ecosystem?". Option A: treat invasiveness as a
property carried by the species and answer with a trait list. Option B: keep the figure-derived
shape and refuse the agent-only framing. What each leads to: A answers a question the figures say is
not answerable, averages away the live slots, and mistakes the habitat for an anchor; B sends a
checkable shape, an enumerated habitat method, refuters written in advance, and an open slot until a
field measurement settles it.

## DOMAIN CENSUS

R by arity, symmetry and scale, then one line per domain with actors, currency and anchor;
partition or union; order by dependency.

- **R (arity / symmetry / scale).** The residual is the gap between trait-based prediction and the
  observed transformation after an introduction. Arity: three-place (actor, habitat, residual), so a
  **union compound** of establishment, cascade reach and impact sign, not one hard question (Fig 9
  :55). Symmetry: not symmetric under a change of residual, because the same actor flips sign.
  Scale: first-order contact, second- and third-order cascade (Fig 11 :52).
- **Domain 1, the introduced actor.** Actors: the organism, its traits, its propagule number.
  Currency: establishment and growth. Anchor: the ground, but only inside domain 2; removed from
  contact, no independent anchor is nameable, so this is a sub-loop, not a self-sufficient domain.
- **Domain 2, the habitat (field of contact).** Actors: residents, conditions, relations. Currency:
  the contact cascade. Anchor: the ground, measured change in the enumerated actors and relations.
  This is the governing domain and it is enumerated, never composed (Fig 11 :43).
- **Domain 3, the introducing seat.** Actors: the agency that moves the actor. Currency:
  introduction only; exclusion is not available. Anchor: the ground. In unmaintained mode there is
  no seat and what survives is what the ground kept.
- **Domain 4, the verifier.** Actors: specialist readers. Currency: agreement and residual. Anchor:
  the ground, never agreement. In this simulated run all four domains draw on one evidence pool, so
  its independence is zero.
- **Order by dependency.** Name the residual before judging sign; enumerate the habitat before
  assessing the actor; settle establishment before tracing cascade; keep slots per object; refuse
  before minting where a held term is wrong (Formula step 2).

Strain test: an apparent conflict between "the habitat updates" and "the anchor must not update"
was resolved by re-census. Fig 11 :73 states that the habitat is where actors come from and updates,
while the ground is what settles and does not. Different objects, no strain after re-census. A second
apparent conflict, between the ecological niche and the Tether habitat, was resolved as a retraction
of the mapping, not a figure conflict (see `05_what_shape_means.md` section 7).

## VERDICT (from the figures)

The shape survives; the agent-only framing does not. Invasiveness is a relation among an
introduced actor, an enumerated habitat and a named residual. The transformation is a contact
cascade whose decisive effect can be three steps out, so first-order scoping misses it. The human
introduction is an asymmetric seat move: addition is available, subtraction is not, and the disaster
form differs from ecosystem engineering only by an unenumerated cascade. Valence is relative to the
residual, so there is no standing list of invasive species and no single global impact score. The
Tether habitat is not the ecological niche; that mapping is refused. The species-only framing, the
trait-syndrome answer, and any claim that consensus or a pooled index settles the case are refused.
This is a `specified`, ungrounded, candidate result, and the canonical article records that the
capability and isolation claims have not been tested forward.

- **cost if wrong:** bounded and locatable. Each finding has a refuter written in advance and a named
  figure line, so an error can be found rather than invisible. The larger cost is spending the
  independence of outside readers by sending a finished theory before the shape claim has been
  checked. Fig 2 :42 says "Buy independence in proportion to the cost of being wrong." That cost is
  avoidable by leading with the shape claim and its refuter.
- **undoable:** yes. The agent-only framing, the niche mapping and the global impact score are
  removable without touching the derivation. `07_the_tether_answer.md` and
  `08_expert_verification_packet.md` already remove them. Nothing has to be withdrawn beyond the
  refuted bindings, and no new primitive was introduced.
- **reversed by:** a ground reading, not agreement. A field measurement holding actor and propagule
  pressure constant across habitats would test Fig 11 :39; a context-free harm ordering would break
  Fig 11 :49-51; a transformation with no detectable higher-order effects would break Fig 11 :52; a
  one-off exclusion that permanently restores a habitat would break Fig 11 :61-64. A figure
  re-census that aligns the Tether habitat with the ecological niche would clear the F5 retraction.
  Until one of these fires, the verdict stands as candidate.
