# Outreach draft: validate the output first, then decide about the theory

**Use:** one-to-one, or as a cover note attached to a submission through the public venues in
`03_public_contact_points.csv`. **Not** for bulk send.

**Hard precondition:** do not send until the shape claim and its refuter have been checked internally
against `01_figure_grounded_audit.md`. The output must survive validation or the two-stage offer
collapses on contact.

---

**Subject:** Check one output on invasion outcomes before you decide whether the theory is worth reading

> Hi [name],
>
> I have one specific output I would like you to check against what you already know about invasion
> ecology. I am not asking you to read the theory first, and I am not asking you to agree with it. If
> the output does not survive, there is nothing else to look at.
>
> **The output (`specified`, not `grounded`):** invasiveness is not a property carried by an
> introduced species. It is a property of the actor together with the habitat it enters and the
> effect you are tracking. The consequence is that the answerable question is not "which species are
> invasive?" but "which (actor, habitat, effect) triples produce a transforming contact cascade?"
> The transformation is itself a cascade: the effect that mattered was three steps out in the DDT
> and eagles case, so scoping to direct contact misses it. A second consequence is asymmetric: an
> introducing seat can add an actor and cannot subtract the habitat, which is why cane toads and
> kudzu are the same move as ecosystem engineering with the cascade left unenumerated.
>
> **What I am not claiming:** that this produces a list of invasive species or a trait syndrome. The
> framework returns a shape, not a name. I am also not equating its "habitat" with the ecological
> niche. That mapping is declined in the source article, which says a habitat here is defined by
> contact with a residual that moves, and is enumerated rather than composed. I am not claiming the
> ecology literature decides the verdict; it is a corroborating frame only.
>
> **What would refute the shape claim:**
> 1. an actor whose establishment and cascade are invariant across habitats at equal propagule
>    pressure;
> 2. a context-free, residual-independent harm ordering of species;
> 3. a transformation fully captured at first-order contact with no detectable second- or
>    third-order effects;
> 4. a one-off exclusion that permanently restores the habitat without continuing work.
>
> I wrote these down before sending this. If any one holds, the corresponding part of the claim is
> withdrawn. A one-line "this fails because..." is more useful to me than agreement. If you would
> rather not engage, no reply is needed.
>
> **If it survives, and only then:** the sources are below. The figures are the authority, not my
> summary of them.
> - Canonical article with all figures and the mapping refusals: https://adventuresinml.substack.com/p/arrival-of-the-fittest-the-tether
> - Verbatim figure text with line numbers (local copy in this solution's framework): `FRAMEWORK/reference/03-FIGURES_TEXT.md`
> - Two independent ecology sources that bear on the same shape: Hui, Pyšek and Richardson 2023, https://www.nature.com/articles/s44185-023-00019-1 ; Cassey et al. 2018, https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5933808
>
> **If you want to use the machinery:** the formula is in the canonical article. Run it on a problem
> you know well and can check against a ground. Write down what would refute your result before you
> run it, and state the mode of what you get. A reported failure is more useful to me than a
> reported success.
>
> [name]
