## 3. What the wider literature bears on the question

1. **Efficiency need not appear as a saving.** Jevons's coal observation and the modern productivity paradox are different strata of the same warning: aggregate accounts can miss where a gain went. They corroborate the per-slot shape but they do not measure it for any particular firm.
2. **Work can expand into the freed time.** Parkinson's organizational version, "Officials make work for each other", is the workplace form of the loop-sign problem. It predicts that the local saving and the whole-system load can move in the same direction.
3. **A metric that becomes a target degrades.** Goodhart and the architecture's own citation list agree that a measure used for control stops being a good measure. This is the one world source that shares the framework's evidence pool, so it is one witness, not two.
4. **Capability is not demonstrated impact.** METR's time-horizon work and Brynjolfsson, Li and Raymond's per-hour field result point in the same direction: capability rises fast, while impact must be measured in the deployment, per worker and per slot. The NBER result's 14 percent is a local reading with large heterogeneity, not a net firm-level verdict.
5. **A null reading is not an absence.** None of the sources above supplies an instrument that discriminates capacity released from work shifted. That is the gap the protocol names and leaves open.

No source conflicts with the figure verdict. The conflict rule was therefore not triggered, and no source was used to overrule a figure.
