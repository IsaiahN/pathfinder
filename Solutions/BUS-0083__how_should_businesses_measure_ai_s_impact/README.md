## The question restated as an effect

What is being decided is not whether AI saves time on a task, but **where the residual moves after the time is saved**. In effect terms, the loop defines `R = |Γ(b,a) - o′|` per slot s (Formula step 1; Symbols table, `R`):

- **b** is the business's belief about what the AI will do, for example "this assistant will cut draft time by 30 percent."
- **a** is the action: deploy the tool on task X.
- **Γ(b,a)** is the prediction: time saved on task X, local throughput, the seat-local before/after number.
- **o′** is what the whole system actually returns: total work, new review load, changed quality, customer outcome, realized value.
- **s** is the slot. The whole question is a mis-indexing problem: the measurement is taken on the task-X slot, while the live residual can appear on other slots, several steps out.

- Option A, "measure the time saved and net it against any new work": leads to one headline number, a single currency, and a live signal that disappears into an average (Fig 1 :22).
- Option B, "measure the residual per slot across the enumerated habitat and pin the verdict to a ground": leads to a protocol that can say where impact actually landed, and that can report `unreachable` instead of `zero` when no instrument registers it.

What an answer would look like, written down and bound as an ordinary term before the run (Formula, "Direction"): **a measurement protocol with four properties**, (i) per-slot indexing with no aggregate verdict, (ii) habitat enumeration out to the cascade distance where the residual stops mattering, (iii) the ground as the only metric, with time saved demoted to an instrument, and (iv) an explicit round-trip gap and mode label. Until bound, that shape predicts nothing and no loop check can see it. It is now bound, so the pre-registered refuter in `01` section 4 can fire on it.

The verdict in `09_watson_answer.md` is Option B in `specified` mode, kept per domain and never averaged.

## What is deliberately not here

- No personal emails of named individuals.
- No ranking of people by how likely they are to agree.
- No bulk-send plan.
- No claim that this derivation is `grounded`, no claim that it settles the business question, and no claim that the framework is the only true account of technology impact.

Four reasons, three from the figures and one practical:

1. A room is not an anchor. *"A room's agreement is variation, not a verdict. Selection between rooms is where the ground votes."* (Fig 7 :35) and *"Alignment is triangulation, not negotiation. A mirror cannot correct you."* (Fig 2 :35). Gathering agreeable readers manufactures the correlation that disqualifies a verdict.
2. Correlation has valence. *"disqualifying for a verdict: agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool"* (Fig 2 :40). A list selected for receptiveness is one witness wearing many names.
3. A verifier must be outside the interior. *"A verifier that can reconstruct the claim cannot verify it."* (Fig 10 :42). The first contact therefore carries the residual and its refuter, not the framework.
4. Legally, unsolicited bulk email to scraped addresses breaches GDPR and CAN-SPAM style rules in many jurisdictions and gets the sending domain blacklisted. Public opt-in routes avoid that failure as well as the structural one.
