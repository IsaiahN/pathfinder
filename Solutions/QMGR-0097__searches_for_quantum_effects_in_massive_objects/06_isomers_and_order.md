# Union, threshold and two currencies: the supporting derivation

**Mode: `specified`.** A simulated derivation checked against the figures, not a ground reading (see `05_what_shape_means.md` section 6).

## RESIDUAL

The phrase "the quantum to classical transition with mass" bundles at least two conditions and one ordering. Before choosing any frame, the structure of the bundle has to be derived: is it a partition or a union, what kind of ordering is mass, and in which currencies is a proposed answer paid for? An answer that prices only one currency will propose arrangements it cannot afford to test.

## DOMAIN CENSUS

Atoms held: mass as an ordering (spectrum), coherence and localisation as states of the measured system, visibility and likelihood ratio as certificates, an instrument lineage, and the collapse parameters lambda, r_C, R_0 as bindings from the physics frame. Bonds held: the seven, especially comparison (⋛) and sequence (→). No seventh substrate kind is available.

- R: arity three plus a threshold; symmetry broken (coherence is lost, not regained within a run); scale from trial to mass regime to instrument lineage.
- D1 instrument reach, D2 theory closure, D3 certification, each with the anchor named in `01_figure_grounded_audit.md` section 1b.
- Union of D1 and D2 at the mass boundary; D2 and D3 overlap where parameters are bounded by experiment.
- Order by dependency: perception, vocabulary, objective, planning, learn and carry (Fig 3 :20).

## DERIVATION

### Q1 - Is the bundle a partition or a union?

Fig 9 :54: **"And when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again."**

Fig 9 :55-56: **"A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones. A single lever clears the region where only one condition holds and leaves the intersections standing, because the second condition still binds."**

The two conditions are (a) can the instrument prepare and read the state at this mass, and (b) does the dynamics contain a collapse term. Adding mass or improving vacuum clears condition (a) in some region; it does nothing to condition (b). The residue is *"categorically different: a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either"* (Fig 9 :57).

World corroboration from a different field: Carlesso et al. (2022) split tests into interferometric (requires preparing a superposition) and non-interferometric (monitors side effects without preparing one) and state that the latter *"are relatively easier as they do not require one to prepare the system in a quantum superposition."* Two conditions, two instruments. Triangulated (Fig 9 plus the review).

**Falsifier:** if one lever clears both conditions, so the split is a partition, the union claim fails.

### Q2 - What kind of ordering is mass?

Fig 13 :20: **"ordering, so more and less can be said."**

Fig 12 :87 (bond table): **"a threshold relation between two quantities"**, with the identification test *"is it about which is larger, rather than both being present? Yes."*

Mass is a spectrum (an ordering), not a conjunction. The relevant claim about a boundary is a threshold: beyond some mass, the predicted and measured distributions separate. ⋛ is the right bond for that claim, and it is *"no"* in the symmetry column of Fig 12 :88, so reversing the operands is a different statement. It is not the symmetric *"both hold at once"* bond (Fig 12 :57). Operators table :52 adds the limit: **"a goal is a comparison, which is why a vocabulary without it cannot state one."** Stating the threshold is stating an objective for the search, not a result.

**Falsifier:** show that the mass dependence is symmetric or conjunctive rather than an ordered threshold, so ⋛ is the wrong bond.

### Q3 - Are the programs isomers, or members of a functional group?

Fig 12 :117: **"isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."**

Fig 12 :124: **"functional group. A substructure whose presence fixes the outcome. The group says what kind; the qualifier says which member."**

The levitated-interferometry program and the non-interferometric collapse program do not share all atoms: one prepares a superposition and reads fringes, the other monitors heating or X-ray emission without a superposition. As written they are not isomers; they are two members of a functional group, namely "an instrument ranged against the same ground through a declared certificate", with different atoms in the slots. The stronger isomer claim would require writing both over a full shared atom set (particle, trap, environment, certificate, mass ordering) and differing only in arrangement. That is a checkable structural claim, not yet settled here.

**Falsifier:** if either program can be written without an atom the other requires (for example, non-interferometric tests with no mass ordering, or interferometry with no certificate), the isomer claim fails and the functional-group reading stands.

### Q4 - In which currencies is the answer paid?

Fig 12 :125: **"A composition costs in two currencies, and they do not add."**

Fig 12 :126: **"Bits price the description. Actions price finding out whether it holds."**

Fig 12 :128: **"The two readings come apart in both directions."**

A collapse model over two phenomenological parameters is cheap to state in bits and extremely expensive in actions: the exclusion plots show how many experiments (cantilevers, cold atoms, LISA Pathfinder, X-ray emission, Neptune, neutron stars) were needed merely to bound it. A single visibility number is cheap in actions and loses most of the distribution, which the certification paper shows costs orders of magnitude more trials to reach the same confidence. Neither reading predicts the other; summing them into one "figure of merit" is the error Fig 12 names.

This is also why mass is a filter. Fig 9 :60: **"Use filters for the budget and witnesses for the verdict. Never let a filter hand you a verdict."** The witness is the distribution against the null.

**Falsifier:** exhibit a single currency in which both the description cost and the experimental cost are correctly ordered for these programs. Fig 12 :130 says this is *"the error this names, not the fix for it."*

### Q5 - How do rooms select, and what does agreement do?

Fig 7 :35: **"A room's agreement is variation, not a verdict. Selection between rooms is where the ground votes."**

Fig 2 :40: **"disqualifying for a verdict: agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool"**

Different instruments (rooms) are selected by the residual they surface. Two reviews quoting the same LISA Pathfinder bound are one room wearing two names. The next room must *"differ from the last, and match the residual it surfaced"* (Fig 7 :36). For this question that means the next experiment should differ in its coupling to mass or to the environment, not simply repeat the same trap at a slightly larger nanoparticle.

**Falsifier:** show that two rooms with low overlap agree by convergent derivation, with separate records of where each came from. Fig 8 :50-52 permits that as *"corroboration at maximum independence"*, and it is distinguishable from adopted import only by the record.

## CONCLUSION

The residual is a union, not a partition; mass is a spectrum on which a threshold claim is made with ⋛, not a conjunction; the two experimental programs are a functional group unless written over a full shared atom set; and the answer is paid in two currencies that cannot be added. The framework supplies the structure of the question and the gates a genuine answer must pass. It does not supply a mass. One strain remains live and is named in `11_audit.md`: whether the threshold's operands count as quantities at the level where settled molecules are reused as atoms is an instrument question, not an argument.
