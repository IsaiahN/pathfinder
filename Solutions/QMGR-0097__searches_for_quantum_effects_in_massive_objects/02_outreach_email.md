**Use:** one to one, or as a cover letter attached to a submission through a public venue in `03_public_contact_points.csv`. **Not** for bulk send.

**Subject:** One shape claim about mass-scale quantum tests, and what would refute it

> Hi [name],
>
> I am working on a figure-grounded derivation of how to read searches for quantum effects in massive objects, including levitated nanoparticles. I have one specific output I would like you to check against what you already know. I am not asking you to read a theory first, and I am not asking you to agree with anything. If the output does not survive, there is nothing else to look at.
>
> **The residual, in effect terms (`specified`):** a thinned channel between a predicted quantum position distribution and a measured one at increasing mass. Three readings are currently bundled together: the instrument has not reached the regime (budget), the environment decoheres faster than the protocol can outrun it (contact), or the superposition principle genuinely breaks down and the dynamics needs a new term (absence). The output says these must be kept separate. A null result at a given mass is a bound, not a proof of absence.
>
> **The shape claim (`general`):** an answer to this question has three parts. First, the search is an inward instrument extension from something already returning readings, not a search for a proto-instrument. Second, the certificate that decides classical versus quantum must range against the measured distribution per run, not against a single averaged visibility number. Third, a genuine mass boundary must arrive as a sourced import with provenance and a shadow test, not as a name assigned to a shape. Until such an import is gated, the mass slot stays open.
>
> **What would refute it:**
> 1. A figure line that, after re-census, names a mass or selects a collapse mechanism.
> 2. The residual turns out to be a partition, so one lever clears it and the three readings do not need separating.
> 3. Two genuinely independent instruments with disjoint evidence pools return the same threshold, and a sourced import predicts its shape. Then it is settled at the ground and the open slot is withdrawn.
> 4. Visibility is shown to be the only possible certificate, so the budget filter has become the witness.
>
> I wrote these down before running the derivation. If any holds, I retract the corresponding part. A one-line "this fails because..." is more useful to me than agreement. If you would rather not engage, no reply is needed.
>

Write down what would refute your result before you run it, and state the mode of what you get. A reported failure is more useful to me than a reported success.

## Two-stage offer, stated explicitly

**Stage 1.** Send the residual, the shape claim and the four refuters. Ask what the recipient would count as a failure. Record that verdict before showing any theory. This is the only stage that produces a checkable outside reading.

**Stage 2, opt-in only.** If the recipient is curious, offer the three public links. Note that the figures, not this summary, are the authority. Their later agreement is never pooled as corroboration (Fig 2 :40: *"disqualifying for a verdict: agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool"*).

**Stage 3, opt-in only.** Offer the formula as a method to reuse, with the instruction that each user writes their own refuter and states their own mode. This sends a generator, which Fig 4 :22 permits, not a recording.

No stage asks for trust, agreement or a show of support. A failure is more useful than an endorsement.
