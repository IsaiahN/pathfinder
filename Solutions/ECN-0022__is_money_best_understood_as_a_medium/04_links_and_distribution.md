## Two things in the world sources that bear directly on this question

1. **The competing theories are regime-indexed, not merely opposed.** The *History of money* page states there are at least two theories of what money is, the commodity theory (money of exchange, "a natural outgrowth of market activity") and the credit theory (money of account, with a possible key role for the state). That matches Fig 9 :4-5: each rule works on a different subset, so split rather than search. Do not present one theory as the single winner.
2. **The barter-origin story is not an established ground.** The anthropological material reports no described society using barter as its main mode of exchange (Humphrey, as reported; Graeber's *Debt: The First 5,000 Years*). This undercuts the strongest intuitive version of the pure-commodity account without establishing the pure-credit account by default. It is a reason to keep the union open, not a reason to close it the other way.

## Distribution: offer, do not push

Figure-grounded reasons:

- **Fig 4 :22** — "Only methods cross up. Only head starts come down." Sending a finished doctrine before anyone has described a residual sends a recording, not a generator. Lead with the shape claim and its four refuters; offer the links after.
- **Fig 10 :42** — "A verifier that can reconstruct the claim cannot verify it." A recipient who has absorbed the whole framework before being asked for a verdict has joined the frame being checked.
- **Fig 9 :27-28** — "describe it before you go looking, or any frame you pick will seem to fit." Ask what they would expect to see if the shape claim were true before giving them the essays.
- **Fig 2 :39-41** — "Correlation has valence." Do not select recipients for likely agreement. A list ranked by receptiveness manufactures the very correlation that makes agreement worthless as a verdict.
- **Fig 2 :40** — "disqualifying for a verdict: agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool." A reply from a reader who has read the same essays is a conversation, not corroboration.

**Recommended order in a reply:** (1) the union shape claim and its four refuters; (2) ask what they would count as a failure; (3) record their verdict before showing any theory; (4) only then the links, with the note that the figures, not the summary, are the authority.

## Two-stage offer, as adapted for this question

1. **Stage 1:** send the residual and the shape (split, union, anchored convention, source undetermined by acceptance) plus the pre-registered refuter. Ask what they would count as a failure. Record the answer before any theory is shown.
2. **Stage 2, opt-in only:** if they are curious, offer the figure text, the canonical README, and the two essays. Their later agreement is never pooled as corroboration (Fig 2 :40).
3. **Stage 3:** offer the formula as a method to reuse, with the instruction that each user writes their own refuter and states their own mode.

**Hard precondition:** stages 1 to 3 only work if the stage-1 output survives. As written in `01_figure_grounded_audit.md`, the substrate identity claim in F7 was refused and the union reading was substituted. Send the corrected shape, not the first draft.
