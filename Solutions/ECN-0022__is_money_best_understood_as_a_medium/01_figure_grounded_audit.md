**Mode of this document: `specified`, not `grounded`.** It states what would have to be settled and where. Per `THE_FORMULA.md`: "Mode. general · specified · grounded. State which. Accepted means the ground settled it; otherwise candidate."

**Status of this derivation: a simulation, not a ground reading.** From *The Recursive Self-Improvement Trap* (essay read 2026-11-23): "the output is only as good as the validator. ## 0. The residual stated in effect terms

The observable reading is invariant across the two candidate accounts: a thing is accepted in exchange, discharges a debt, and holds value. The question asks which account is "best." The disagreement does not shrink with effort because each account keeps working well on a different subset. That is the signature Fig 9 names for a multiple-question situation, not one hard question.

Stated as R: **what settles the standing of a thing as money, and is that source unique?** Arity: three offered options (institution, commodity, both), but structurally two conditions plus their intersection. Symmetry: the accounts are asymmetric, one is a legal and institutional fact, the other a physical and intrinsic property. Scale: a single transaction (first order) against the monetary system as a whole (second order).

## 1. Domain census (before choosing any figure line)

R by arity, symmetry and scale, then one line per domain with actors / currency / anchor. A domain without a nameable anchor is not a domain yet.

- **R (the residual).** Arity two conditions plus their union; symmetry asymmetric; scale transaction to system. It is a **union compound**, not a single hard problem. Fig 9 :54 governs: "And when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again."
- **Domain 1, the ground of monetary value.** Actors: buyers, sellers, banks, clearing houses, tax offices. Currency: acceptance and settlement. Anchor: a market, a clearing system, a tax demand. Something that does not move with belief (Fig 2 :29-30). This is the domain that settles what counts as money at all.
- **Domain 2, the source of that standing.** Actors: gold or cigarettes (commodity), the state (legal tender, taxation), commercial banks (credit creation), the accepting network. Currency: the same reading in every case, "accepted as money." Anchor: a source-discriminating observation; absent one, the source stays open. This is the metamerism domain.
- **Domain 3, the kind of thing money is.** Actors: the six substrate terms, chiefly network (a structure), energy-like states. Currency: composition inside `closure(Γ)`. Anchor: the figures. A "medium of exchange" reads as the network structure by which influence travels; a "commodity-like asset" reads as the operand that structure carries. This is a `specified` structural reading, not a derivation of a new primitive.
- **Domain 4, the institutional and credit frame.** Actors: banks, central banks, treasuries. Currency: credit, debt, legal tender. Anchor: the payment and clearing system, the tax office.
- **Domain 5, the commodity frame.** Actors: gold, silver, grain, cigarettes, beaver pelts. Currency: commodity market price. Anchor: the market for that commodity.
- **Domain 6, the checking / outreach frame.** Actors: monetary theorists and historians. Currency: agreement and residuals. Anchor: the ground, never agreement. In this simulated run all seats draw on one evidence pool, so this domain's independence is zero.

**Partition or union.** The offered third option "both" is decisive. Gold coin was simultaneously a commodity and an institutionally declared legal tender; a bank deposit is simultaneously institution-created and held as an asset. The two conditions overlap, so this is a **union**, not a partition. Per Fig 9 :54, unbundle: ask each condition in its own domain, then treat the intersection as a compound with its own loop. Fig 9 :44: "Only a sealed room can be searched to the end, and no frame is a sealed room."

**Order by dependency (Fig 3).** Settle the ground (Domain 1: what counts as money, what anchor settles value) before asking the source (Domain 2). Describe the residual before choosing a frame (Fig 9 :27-28). Unbundle the union before searching (Fig 9 :54). Optimising a link above the break cannot rescue one below it (Fig 3 :19).

## 3. Findings F1..F8 (verbatim quotes first, then the conclusion)

### F1 — This is not one question with three answers (governing)

Fig 9 :4-5:

> **"Disagreements that do not shrink with effort, where each rule keeps working well on a different subset, are not one hard question but several well-formed ones. Split it rather than search."**

The commodity account works well on gold, silver, grain, cigarettes, prisoner-of-war camps, and the pre-modern anchor function. The institutional and credit account works well on modern fiat, commercial bank deposit creation, legal tender, and tax-driven money. Each rule keeps working on a different subset. By the figure, the honest move is to split, not to keep searching for a single winner.

**Refuter:** show one account that governs every monetary regime and leaves no positive residual in any subset. If that is demonstrated, the split verdict fails.

**Status: triangulated** across Fig 9 and the world history of money (which records regime-dependence), with low overlap between the figure's decision rule and the historical record.

### F2 — "Both" is a union, not an average

Fig 9 :54:

> **"And when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again."**

Fig 1 :19 and :22:

> **"The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated."**
> **"Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average."**

The tempting answer to a three-option question is a centrist blend: money is "both, in different proportions." That is aggregation across slots. It throws away the signal that says where each account governs. The figure requires unbundling: Domain 4 and Domain 5 keep separate verdicts, and the intersection is named as a compound.

**Refuter:** show that the two conditions never co-occur in any actual monetary instrument, so the union is empty. Then "both" collapses and the partition reading stands.

**Status: triangulated** (Fig 9 and Fig 1, low overlap).

### F3 — Agreement is the product here, but the convention must be anchored

Fig 2 :39-41:

> **"Correlation has valence."**
> **"disqualifying for a verdict: agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool"**
> **"efficient for a convention: agreement about a shared meaning is the product, not a corruption of it"**

Money is the textbook case where agreement is the product. A banknote works because others accept it; that collective acceptance is not a defect to be explained away. So the institutional account is not refuted by pointing out that money rests on agreement. Fig 2 :41 explicitly licenses that.

But licensing a convention is not licensing a self-sustaining one. Fig 10 :47 and :49:

> **"a convention that decides an outcome has stopped being one"**
> **"Declaring a meaning does not ground it."**

A legal-tender declaration ("this is money") declares a meaning. It does not ground the value. The moment the convention decides the outcome by fiat, it has stopped being a convention and become an authored constant. So the institutional account is right about the mechanism (convention) only if the convention is ranged against something it cannot move.

**Refuter:** exhibit a monetary regime whose standing is settled entirely by mutual belief, with no external anchor, and that does not drift. That would show the anchor requirement is not load-bearing.

**Status: triangulated** (Fig 2 and Fig 10, two figures with low overlap; the correction to my first reading is logged in §5).

### F4 — The anchor test: what settles value must not update on frames

Fig 2 :29-30:

> **"An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks. A measurement, an experiment, a counter, a market, a patient outcome. If it moves when you push on it, it is not an anchor."**

Applied to Domain 1, the anchor is the market clearing, the payment system, the tax demand. It is not the pronouncement of any participant. This is where each candidate account supplies half the answer:

- The **commodity** account supplies an anchor that is genuinely outside the monetary convention. Gold has non-monetary demand, so its value does not depend solely on what money-holders believe. This is the commodity insight, and it survives.
- The **institutional** account supplies the tax and clearing anchor. A tax obligation is externally imposed and does not update on what any frame thinks about the currency's worth (within the regime). This is the chartalist insight, and it survives.

Fig 2 :35:

> **"Alignment is triangulation, not negotiation. A mirror cannot correct you."**

A monetary system whose only "ground" is the agreement of its own participants is a mirror chain. The two surviving accounts are two different anchors for the same residual.

**Refuter:** show an anchor for monetary value that does move when the relevant frames push on it and still settles the question soundly. By the figure that is a contradiction, so a real case would break the figure's anchor law.

**Status: triangulated** (Fig 2 plus the world sources on tax-driven money and commodity demand).

### F5 — The reading does not determine the source (central derivation)

*The Hard Problem is About Source* (essay read 2026-11-23): "If several paths create the same behavior, then behavior is a projection with lossy information." The essay's instrument reads green and "cannot comment on the source": green light, or yellow plus blue, or RGB, or CMYK, or a scheme not yet discovered. It was the spectrometer, not an argument, that resolved the source.

Money has the same shape. Acceptance is the reading. Commodity intrinsic value, state legal tender plus taxation, bank credit creation, and pure network effect are four different sources that produce that same reading. No observation of acceptance alone discriminates among them. Fig 9 :32:

> **"you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself"**

So the framework can return the *shape* money must have (an anchored convention, a network medium) but not the unique *name* of its source. Fig 2 :40 applies to this derivation itself: the second-seat audit shares this evidence pool, so its agreement is not corroboration.

**Refuter:** exhibit a source-discriminating observation in which acceptance uniquely determines one source, e.g. a regime where every accepted token is, and must be, a commodity with no institutional or credit component. That would break the metamerism claim.

**Status: triangulated** between Fig 9 and the essay, but with declared high overlap (the essay is by the framework author). The structure (many sources, one reading) is independently corroborated by the world sources on competing theories of money, so the *structural* claim is low-overlap; the specific framing is high-overlap.

### F6 — "Medium" is a structure, "asset" is a state

Fig 13 :24 lists network among the structures:

> **"that entities relate, and influence travels"**

A medium of exchange is precisely that: the structure by which influence (claims, value, purchasing power) travels between entities. Fig 13 :74:

> **"Everything else is a composition across the two kinds, never within one."**

So "medium created by banks and public institutions" names the *maintenance of a network structure*, while "commodity-like asset" names an *operand* (a state-like, conserved, finite thing) that the network carries. These are different kinds in the framework's sense, not two rival names for one substance. That is why asking "which is money really?" is a category error at this level: the question mixes a structure with its operand.

Fig 13 :75:

> **"Two states never bond directly. It is not space-time but space, a structure, time; and two structures join only through a state."**

Money as a medium cannot be a bare state; it needs a structure between transactors. This is the one place the figures bear directly on the word "medium" in the question.

**Refuter:** a re-census showing that money is, in the framework's sense, a state with no network term required, so that exchange can be described without any structure relating entities. That would break the structure reading.

**Status: single-source** for the figure definition (Fig 13), with the binding to economics terms marked `specified` and ungrounded.

### F7 — The substrate binding is a binding, not a derivation (strain named)

A natural over-reading of F6 is: commodity money *is* the substrate term energy (conserved, finite), and credit money *is* the network or gradient structure. I **retract** that as a derivation. Money is not one of the six terms; it is a composed term in the economics frame bound onto held atoms. Fig 6 :23:

> **"No frame escapes its own closure. The bound moves only on contact with a frame whose closure differs."**

Binding "commodity" to an energy-like state adds no atom to `Γ`, so it does not move the wall. It is a useful analogy (an economics-frame composition mapped onto framework atoms), and analogies are bindings with provenance, not derivations. This strain is named rather than hidden.

**Refuter:** derive money from the six terms alone, without importing any economics-frame content. If that succeeds, the binding was unnecessary.

**Status: single-source**, recorded as a strain, not a correction to the verdict.

### F8 — Keep verdicts per domain, never averaged

Fig 1 :19, :22 again, applied to the policy cost. If you force "commodity only," you misread fiat and the credit-creation mechanism (the Bank of England source shows the majority of modern money is created by bank lending). If you force "institutional only," you miss the anchor function a commodity can supply when trust in institutions collapses (cigarettes in POW camps and postwar Europe). The two failures are different and locatable. Aggregating them into a single "best" answer hides both.

**Refuter:** show that one forced account misreads no regime at all. That would remove the cost and with it the need to keep verdicts separate.

**Status: triangulated** (Fig 1 plus the world record of commodity money in collapsed-trust settings).

## 6. Pre-registered refuter (written before the conclusion)

Per the seat rule that "a falsifier written before the run is a design instrument, not only an honesty device." The shape claim is refuted if any of the following holds:

1. A single account is demonstrated to govern every monetary regime with no positive residual left in any subset (breaks F1 and F2).
2. A monetary regime is exhibited whose standing is settled purely by mutual belief, with no external market, clearing, or tax anchor, and that does not drift (breaks F3 and F4).
3. A source-discriminating observation shows that acceptance uniquely determines one source, commodity, state, or credit (breaks F5).
4. A re-census shows money is a state in the framework's sense with no network structure required, so a medium is not a structure (breaks F6).

If any fires, the shape is withdrawn. Until one is run, no part of this may be labelled `grounded`.
