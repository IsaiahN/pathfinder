# Expert verification packet — the Tether on the nature of money

**What this is:** a claim in a form a monetary theorist or historian of money can check. Mode, derivation and refuters are stated before the conclusion, so a reader knows what to look for and what would settle it.

**Mode: `specified` — not `grounded`.** No source-discriminating monetary observation is claimed. This is a shape and a route, not a result.

## 1. The claim, in one paragraph

The question "is money a medium created by banks and public institutions, a commodity-like asset, or both?" is a bundle, not one question with three answers. Each account governs a different subset, so split rather than search; and "both" is a union of overlapping conditions, not an average. The shape any correct answer must have is: money is an anchored convention and a network medium. Agreement about its value is the product for a convention, but a declaration of legal tender does not ground value, so the standing must be ranged against an anchor that does not update on what people think, a market clearing, a payment and clearing system, or a tax demand. The commodity account and the institutional account each supply one such anchor. Acceptance does not discriminate among commodity value, state taxation, bank credit creation and network effect, so no source name is licensed by the reading alone.

## 2. Three checkable sub-claims

### C1 — framework-internal; checkable by reading Figures 9 and 1
- **Statement:** the question must be split, and the two conditions form a union whose intersection is its own compound, not an average of two essences.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 9 :4-5 — *"are not one hard question but several well-formed ones. Split it rather than search."* Fig 9 :54 — *"And when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again."* Fig 1 :22 — *"Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average."*
- **Refuter:** exhibit a monetary universe in which no instrument is both institution-created and commodity-like, so the both-cell is empty; or show one account governing every regime with no residual left in any subset.

### C2 — convention and anchor; checkable by a monetary theorist against the historical and institutional record
- **Statement:** money's value is a convention where agreement is the product, but the convention must be ranged against an anchor that does not move with belief. Legal tender declares a meaning; it does not ground value.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 2 :41 — *"efficient for a convention: agreement about a shared meaning is the product, not a corruption of it."* Fig 10 :47 — *"a convention that decides an outcome has stopped being one."* Fig 10 :49 — *"Declaring a meaning does not ground it."* Fig 2 :29-30 — *"A measurement, an experiment, a counter, a market, a patient outcome. If it moves when you push on it, it is not an anchor."*
- **What to check:** is there a working monetary regime whose standing is settled entirely by mutual belief, with no external market, clearing or tax anchor, and that does not drift? If yes, the anchor requirement fails. Do legal-tender declarations ever ground value on their own? If yes, the declaration/ground distinction fails.
- **Refuter:** the pure-belief non-drifting regime; or a demonstration that a declaration alone reliably settles value.

### C3 — source discrimination; checkable against the theory literature
- **Statement:** acceptance is a reading, not a source discriminator. Commodity value, state taxation, bank credit creation and network effect all produce "accepted as money," so no source name is licensed by acceptance alone.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 9 :32 — *"you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself."* The structural claim is corroborated by a secondary source: Isaiah N, *The Hard Problem is About Source* (adventuresinml, 2026-08-28), *"If several paths create the same behavior, then behavior is a projection with lossy information."* That essay is by the framework author, so it is high overlap and not an independent witness; the competing-theories literature is the low-overlap corroboration.
- **What to check:** find an observation in which acceptance uniquely determines one source. If one exists, the metamerism claim fails.
- **Refuter:** any source-discriminating observation tying acceptance to a single source across regimes.

### Supporting structural claim (not one of the three primary checks)
- **Statement:** "medium" reads as a network structure and "asset" as an operand, so the question mixes two kinds.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 13 :24 — *"that entities relate, and influence travels"* Fig 13 :74 — *"Everything else is a composition across the two kinds, never within one."*
- **Refuter:** a re-census showing money is a state with no network term required. Note: the further mapping "commodity equals energy, credit equals network" was **retracted as a derivation** (`01` F7); it is a binding onto held atoms and moves no wall (Fig 6 :23).

## 3. What is explicitly not claimed

- Not a single "best" account, and not a 50/50 blend of the two.
- Not that collective agreement about value is a corruption. For a convention it is the product (Fig 2 :41).
- Not that a legal-tender declaration grounds value. Fig 10 :49 says it does not.
- Not that the framework names the source of money's standing. Fig 9 :32 says it returns a kind of thing, not the answer.
- Not that commodity money is the framework's energy term or credit money its network term. That binding was retracted (`01` F7).
- Not `grounded`. Nothing here was settled by a monetary measurement.
- Not a theory of money. It is a shape any theory of money must satisfy, with refuters written in advance.

## 5. What would settle it

A source-discriminating observation, not a further argument. From *The Hard Problem is About Source*: an instrument that reads green *"cannot comment on the source"*; the spectrometer resolved it. Here:

1. **One-account test:** does one account govern every regime with no residual in any subset?
2. **Anchor test:** is there a non-drifting regime sustained by mutual belief alone, with no market, clearing or tax anchor?
3. **Source test:** does any observation tie acceptance uniquely to commodity, state or credit as source?
4. **Structure test:** can money be fully described as a state with no network structure between transactors?

## 6. What an expert can check in twenty minutes

1. Read Fig 9 :4-5, :54 and Fig 1 :22 — three short passages. Decide whether the question is a bundle and whether the both-cell is a real union compound.
2. Read Fig 2 :41, Fig 10 :47, :49 and Fig 2 :29-30. Name the anchor in a fiat regime and in a commodity regime. Does either depend solely on belief?
3. Check C3 against the theory literature: do acceptance-based observations discriminate among commodity, chartalist and credit accounts? If they do, send the observation.
4. Send the refutation. A failure is more useful than an endorsement.
