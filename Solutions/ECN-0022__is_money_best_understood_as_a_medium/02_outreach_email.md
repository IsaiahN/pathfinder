**Subject:** Check one output on the nature of money before you decide whether the framing is worth reading

> Hi [name],
>
> I have one specific output I would like you to check against what you already know about monetary theory. I am not asking you to read a theory first, and I am not asking you to agree. If the output does not survive, there is nothing else to look at.
>
> **The output (`specified`, not `grounded`):** the question "medium created by banks and states, commodity-like asset, or both?" is not one question with three answers. Each account keeps working well on a different subset, so the figures' rule is to split rather than search. The two conditions also overlap: gold coin was both a commodity and a legal-tender instrument; a bank deposit is both institution-created and held as an asset. So "both" is a **union of overlapping conditions**, not a centrist average of two essences. A system that averages across slots throws away the signal that says where each account governs.
>
> The shape any correct account must have: money is an **anchored convention** and a **network medium**. Agreement about its value is the product here, not a corruption; that is what a convention is. But a convention that decides an outcome by declaration has stopped being one, and declaring a meaning does not ground it. So its standing must be ranged against something that does not move with what people think: a market clearing, a payment and clearing system, a tax demand. That is where the commodity account and the institutional account each supply half the answer. Gold has non-monetary demand, so it can serve as an anchor outside the convention; a tax obligation is externally imposed, so it can serve as an anchor for fiat.
>
> **What I am not claiming:** that acceptance tells you the source of money's standing. It does not. Commodity value, state taxation, bank credit creation, and network effect all produce the same reading, "accepted as money." Several paths to one reading is a projection with lossy information. A source-discriminating observation is needed to name the source, and none is supplied here. I am also not claiming that commodity money *is* the framework's energy term or that credit money *is* its network term. That is a binding of economics vocabulary onto held atoms, not a derivation, and it adds no new primitive.
>
> **What would refute the shape:**
> 1. one account shown to govern every monetary regime with no residual left in any subset;
> 2. a regime whose standing is settled purely by mutual belief, with no external market, clearing, or tax anchor, and that does not drift;
> 3. an observation in which acceptance uniquely determines one source;
> 4. a re-census showing money is a state with no network structure required.
>
> I wrote these down before sending this. If any holds, the shape is withdrawn. A one-line "this fails because..." is more useful to me than agreement. If you would rather not engage, no reply is needed.
>
> **If it survives, and only then:** the sources are below. The figures are the authority, not my summary of them.
> - The figures, verbatim with line numbers (local canonical extract): `FRAMEWORK/reference/03-FIGURES_TEXT.md` in the Project Pathfinder workspace.

>

Write down what would refute your result *before* you run it, and state the mode of what you get. A reported failure is more useful to me than a reported success.
