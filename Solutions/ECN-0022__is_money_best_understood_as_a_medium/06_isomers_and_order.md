## RESIDUAL

The question offers three answers: a medium created by banks and public institutions, a commodity-like asset, or both. Two derivations are owed. (1) Is the third option a **union** of overlapping conditions or a **partition** into separate cases? (2) What is the **order** in which the two accounts must be assessed, and are they **isomers** or members of one **functional group** under Fig 12?

## DERIVATION

### Q1 — Union or partition?

- Fig 9 :54: **"And when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again."**
- Fig 9 :55: **"A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones."**
- Fig 9 :57-58: **"What remains is smaller and it is also categorically different: a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either."**

The two conditions are "institutionally created" (C1) and "commodity-like asset" (C2). Four cases exist: C1 only (pure fiat deposit), C2 only (a commodity not declared legal tender), both (a gold coin that is legal tender; a banknote that is also held as an asset), and neither (a worthless object). Because the both-cell is populated and is not just a harder version of either single cell, the split lands on a **union**. Unbundle: assess C1 in Domain 4, C2 in Domain 5, and the both-cell as a compound with its own loop. A single lever (say "value comes from the state") clears the C1-only region and leaves the intersection standing, because C2 still binds there.

- **Falsifier, written before the run:** exhibit a monetary universe in which the both-cell is empty, so no instrument is both institution-created and commodity-like. The union collapses to a partition and the compound loop is withdrawn.

### Q2 — The order is dependency, not importance

- Fig 3 :20: **"The chain is ordered by dependency, not by importance."**
- Fig 3 :19: **"Optimising a link that already works cannot rescue one that does not."**

Dependency order for this question:

1. **Perception / ground:** what actually counts as money in the regime? (What settles and clears?) Without this, both accounts are unassessable.
2. **Vocabulary:** what does the regime afford? Credit, debt, legal tender, intrinsic value, clearing.
3. **Objective:** what would count as progress in answering the question? A shape with per-domain verdicts, not a single essence.
4. **Planning:** describe the residual in effect terms before picking a frame (Fig 9 :27-28).
5. **Learn and carry:** keep the union and its refuter so the next case starts from a head start, not a recording.

"A which account is best?" asks step 3 before step 1. That is the mistake the question invites. Fig 3 :22: *"A step whose input never arrives cannot be diagnosed, only its predecessor can."*

### Q3 — Are the two accounts isomers or a functional group?

- Fig 12 :117: **"isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."**
- Fig 12 :124: **"functional group. A substructure whose presence fixes the outcome. The group says what kind; the qualifier says which member."**

As the accounts are normally written, the institutional account's atoms are {credit, debt, legal tender, clearing} and the commodity account's are {scarcity, durability, intrinsic demand, market price}. They share almost nothing, so they are **not isomers** under Fig 12's definition. Both do share a substructure: *something is accepted in settlement and held across time*. That is the functional group. Fig 12 :124: the group says what kind (a settlement medium), the qualifier says which member (credit-created or commodity-backed).

The stronger form: if both accounts are written over a shared atom set in the framework's terms, for example `state → structure → state` with different structures mediating, they become a real isomer claim. But the specific atoms differ, so the stronger form needs a provenanced binding and is not established here.

- **Falsifier, written before the run:** if either account can be fully stated without the shared settlement substructure (institutional money with no acceptance, commodity money with no holding across time), the functional-group reading fails.

### Q4 — What order does the composition have?

- Fig 13 :24: **"that entities relate, and influence travels"** Network is a structure.
- Fig 13 :74: **"Everything else is a composition across the two kinds, never within one."**

At first order, a medium is a structure: it relates transactors and lets influence travel. A commodity-like asset is the operand that the structure carries. These are different kinds, and Fig 13 :74 forbids composing them directly within one kind. So "the medium is the asset" is not a legal first-order composition; it is a flat-formula error, the same mistake Fig 12 names when only ingredients are recorded.

At second order, the settled institutional arrangement and the settled commodity arrangement can each become an atom: Fig 12 :104, **"A settled molecule becomes an atom for whatever composes over it."** Their comparison is then a second-order composition, legal only because both first-order molecules settled against the ground (a market, a clearing system, a tax office).

- **Falsifier:** show that a monetary arrangement cannot settle and therefore cannot serve as an atom for a second-order comparison. Then the union cannot be composed at all and the question must stay split.

### Q5 — Why "both" is a catalyst-like survivor, not a blend

- Fig 12 :119: **"catalyst. A part that survives its own use."**

The overlap cell (both conditions hold) is the part that survives across regimes: a gold coin remains a commodity after the legal-tender declaration, and a bank deposit remains an asset after being created by lending. It is not a blend that averages the two accounts; it is a distinct compound that persists through use. Fig 12 :121: **"cycle. The output re-enters as input."** In credit money the output of lending becomes the input to further lending, so the institutional account contains a cycle, not a one-shot creation. That is a further reason a single static "essence" cannot describe it.

## CONCLUSION

The third option is a **union**, not a partition and not an average. The two accounts are **not isomers** as normally written; they share a **functional group** (a settlement medium held across time), with the qualifying difference being credit-created versus commodity-backed. Their composition order is: first-order structure (medium) plus operand (asset), then a second-order comparison legal only after each settles against its own ground. The overlap cell is a catalyst-like compound that survives use, and the credit account contains a cycle. Each claim here carries a falsifier written before the run.
