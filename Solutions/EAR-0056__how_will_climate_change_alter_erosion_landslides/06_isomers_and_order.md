# Supporting derivation: structure, order, and analogues

**Mode: `specified` on the structural reading, `general` on the figure definitions. Simulated derivation checked against the figures, not a ground reading.**

## RESIDUAL

The primary audit leaves two structural questions open: (1) are erosion, landslides and sediment flows **isomers** under Fig 12's definition, or merely members of one **functional group**, and (2) what is the **order** of the union, given that the processes feed each other? Answering them decides whether one loop or three is owed.

Atoms available (framework-internal, no import): states space, time, energy; structures spectrum, gradient, network; habitat conditions water, temperature, season, terrain; actors sediment, rock strength, roots, channel morphology. Bonds available: the seven, with negation as the unary operator.

## DERIVATION

### Q1 - Functional group or isomers?

Fig 12 :124: *"functional group. A substructure whose presence fixes the outcome. The group says what kind; the qualifier says which member."*
Fig 12 :117: *"isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."*

Written as the literature writes them, the three processes do not share the same atom set:

- **Landslide:** water (pore pressure) and terrain bond to a threshold comparison of driving against resisting stress. Atoms: water, terrain, energy/gradient; bond: ⋛ comparison, then → sequence into delivery.
- **Erosion:** a gradient over space and a spendable flow bond to rock/sediment; atoms: gradient, water, rock; bond: ⇒ production of detached sediment.
- **Sediment flow:** a gradient over space carries produced sediment downstream; atoms: gradient, energy, sediment, time; bond: → sequence and storage.

They share the **substructure** `condition -> structure -> response` (Fig 13 :76: *"Energy and a gradient compose a flow."*), so they are a functional group: same kind, different members. They are **not** strict isomers as written, because the atom sets differ. The stronger claim would require rewriting all three over the full shared atom set `{water, terrain, sediment, gradient, time, energy}` and differing only in which bond mediates which pair. That is a checkable structural hypothesis, not a finding. This mirrors the QMGR exemplar's correction: the weaker functional-group reading is what the record as written supports; the isomer claim needs a rewrite.

**Falsifier, written before the run:** if any one of the three processes can be expressed without an atom the others require (for example a landslide with no water or no sediment delivery, or erosion with no gradient), the union-compound claim in F1 weakens toward a partition and the functional-group reading stands alone.

### Q2 - What order is the union?

Fig 3 :20: *"The chain is ordered by dependency, not by importance."*
Fig 12 :104: *"A settled molecule becomes an atom for whatever composes over it."*

Dependency order is not the order in a headline. It is:

1. **Perception:** can the slot be measured at all? (gauge, inventory, repeat topography)
2. **Vocabulary:** what does the slope or reach afford: what can be moved, blocked, stored?
3. **Objective:** what counts as a change here, in the slot's own terms?
4. **Planning:** which intervention or forecast is being tested?

The middle link is where geomorphology chains usually break: Fig 3 :27 - *"A link with no instrument is not a link that works. It is a link nobody has looked at."* A global climate projection applied to a catchment with no vocabulary for sediment storage is effort above the break.

Across scales, each settled first-order process can become an atom for the next level: a measured landslide regime becomes an operand in the catchment sediment budget. Fig 12 :104 licenses this without a new primitive. No import is needed.

### Q3 - Where does the loop close?

Fig 12 :121: *"cycle. The output re-enters as input. The sign of the loop decides whether it closes, and a chain cannot close by construction."*

Sediment delivered by a landslide changes channel morphology and vegetation, which change the next failure and the next erosion event. The output re-enters as input, so the union is a cycle, not a chain. The sign is a measured quantity: sediment cover can armour a bed (damping) or provide tools that increase incision (amplifying). Sheikh et al. (2026) report that *"antecedent channel conditions ... strongly influenced the magnitude and spatial distribution of channel adjustment"* and that the *"legacy of earlier events"* matters, which is the re-entry. No figure line signs it; the ground must.

### Q4 - Two currencies, not one

Fig 12 :126: *"Bits price the description. Actions price finding out whether it holds."*

A regional model can state an arrangement cheaply (bits) while the field campaign that would settle it costs a season of surveys (actions). A system holding one accounting cannot compare them and will propose arrangements it cannot afford to test. This is the practical reason the packet asks for a twenty-minute check first: it prices the action before committing the budget.

## CONCLUSION

The three processes are a **functional group** as written, sharing the `condition -> structure -> response` substructure, and only a conditional **isomer** claim if each is rewritten over the full shared atom set. Their order is by dependency, perception before vocabulary before objective before planning, and the link most likely broken is the middle one. The union closes as a **cycle**, with a sign that must be measured rather than assumed. The falsifier above stands: if a process can be written without the others' atoms, the union weakens toward a partition.
