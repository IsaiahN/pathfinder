## What in the wider literature bears on this question

1. **The average hides the slot.** IPCC reports non-uniform alteration and low confidence in the sign of global land runoff, while USGS documents specific thresholds and pore-pressure triggers. That is exactly the scale at which a global number stops being diagnostic. Fig 1 :22: *"Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average."*
2. **Conditions and antecedent state, not forcing alone.** The Spöl experiment found that response magnitude and form varied markedly among floods even though all mobilised sediment, and that antecedent conditions and sediment availability strongly influenced channel adjustment. Fig 11 :46 lists *"conditions: light, water, temperature, season, terrain"* as habitat items to enumerate.
3. **Nonlinearity and scale dependence are already in the assessment.** IPCC attributes non-linear water-cycle response to interacting drivers, feedbacks and time scales. Fig 13 :31-32 makes the compounding remainder structural: *"a remainder compounding through a chain that amplifies is chaos, and the detail a crossing discards reappears above it as noise"*.
4. **A source cannot be named from one reading.** A sediment-yield time series can arise from climate forcing, land-use change, fire, or sediment exhaustion. *The Hard Problem is About Source* gives the metamerism case: an instrument reading green *"cannot comment on the source."* This is why the shape claim does not sign the conditions and why it asks for source-discriminating instruments.

All three earth-science sources are independent of the framework author, so their agreement with the figure-derived shape is corroboration from different fields, not the author's own pool talking. The three essays share the author's pool and are labelled high-overlap in the ledger.
