**Subject:** Check one output about erosion and landslides before you decide whether the framework is worth reading

> Hi [name],
>
> I have one specific output I would like you to check against what you already know from your own ground. I am not asking you to read the framework first, and I am not asking you to agree with it. If the output does not survive, there is nothing else to look at.
>
> **The output (`specified` shape, not `grounded` result).** The question "how will climate change alter erosion, landslides and sediment flows?" is not one question. It is a union of three overlapping conditions: a landslide delivers sediment to a channel, sediment cover modulates erosion, and channel storage decouples flux from forcing. So it needs its own loop, not three answers added together. The figures give three constraints on any answer:
>
> 1. The residual is indexed by object slot, a slope, a reach, a catchment, not by a global mean. A regional average can be nearly flat while one slope crosses its failure threshold.
> 2. Climate acts as conditions in a contact cascade, not as a single external knob. Water, temperature, season and terrain are already in the habitat; the cascade runs outward through pore pressure, failure, sediment delivery, storage and channel response.
> 3. Sediment flow is a directed flow over a spendable gradient, and failure is a threshold comparison. The remainder left at each step compounds, so the response can be nonlinear and scale-dependent. Feedback loops are licensed, and their sign decides whether they amplify or damp.
>
> **What would refute the shape claim (written before this message):**
> 1. The three processes form a partition and the whole response is the arithmetic sum of three independent levers.
> 2. A monitored set shows homogeneous, threshold-free, linear response across all slots, so a global mean preserves every live residual.
> 3. A response is measured that is first-order only, with no cascade through vegetation, land use, sediment storage or antecedent conditions.
> 4. A monitoring record is shown whose degradation cannot alter the reading.
>
> If any one holds, I will retract the corresponding claim. A one-line "this fails because..." is more useful to me than agreement. If you would rather not engage, no reply is needed.
>
> **What I am not claiming:** that the framework predicts more erosion, more landslides or more sediment under warming in general. It does not sign the conditions; their valence is relative to the residual. I am not claiming a number, a date or a region. The figures return a kind of thing, never the answer itself, so the sign and magnitude for any particular slope remain an open, instrument-bound question. I am also not claiming that model agreement settles anything: the ground settles it.
>
> **If it survives, and only then:** the sources are below. The figures are the authority, not my summary of them.
> - The verbatim figure text extract with line numbers (local file): `FRAMEWORK/reference/03-FIGURES_TEXT.md`
> - Figures and formula, branch `general-agent`: https://github.com/IsaiahN/tether/tree/general-agent/figures
> - Full architecture (Medium, 19 min): https://medium.com/@IsaiahNwukor/arrival-of-the-fittest-the-tether-architecture-1de7edf641ec
>
> **If you want to use the machinery:** run it on a problem you know well and can check against a ground. Write down what would refute your result before you run it. The framework's own rule is that a loop cannot check the direction of its own question, and a reported failure is more useful to me than a reported success.
