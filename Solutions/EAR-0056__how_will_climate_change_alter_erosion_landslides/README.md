## Restatement as an effect

The question asks what the residual will be between the erosion, landslide and sediment regime we predict and the regime the ground will actually return under a changed climate. Option A treats the answer as one global trend per process (a number to report). Option B treats the residual as indexed by object slot (a slope, a channel reach, a catchment), with thresholds, cascades and feedbacks that a global average hides. A leads to a headline number that can be right in the mean and wrong everywhere that matters. B leads to a checkable shape: a union of overlapping conditions, per-slot residuals, directed flows with thresholds, and a ground that must settle each slot separately.

## What is deliberately not here

- No personal emails of named individuals, even though the USGS program contact page publishes them. Public institutional channels only.
- No ranking of people by how likely they are to agree that climate change will intensify geomorphic hazards.
- No bulk-send plan.

Three reasons, in the figures' own words:

1. **A room does not upgrade the label.** A pile of agreeing model groups is a weighted average of influence, not an anchor. Fig 2 :35: *"Alignment is triangulation, not negotiation. A mirror cannot correct you."*
2. **Selecting for receptiveness manufactures the disqualifying correlation.** Fig 2 :40: *"agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool."* A list chosen because its members already expect more landslides is one pool wearing many names.
3. **Asking for trust before a verdict spends the only thing the outside frame contributes.** Fig 10 :42: *"A verifier that can reconstruct the claim cannot verify it."*

Legally: unsolicited bulk email to harvested addresses breaches GDPR and CAN-SPAM style rules in many jurisdictions. Public opt-in submission venues avoid that failure as well as the structural one.
