# The Tether's answer: How will climate change alter erosion, landslides, and sediment flows?

**Mode: `specified` on the shape, `general` on the frame, not `grounded`.** Simulated derivation checked for internal consistency against the figures, not a ground reading. It cannot settle the question it reports on.

## 1. The residual it answers

We want the gap between the erosion, landslide and sediment regime a frame predicts and what the ground returns under a changed climate. The question is a **union compound**, not three separate questions (Fig 9 :55: *"A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones."*). A landslide delivers sediment; sediment cover modulates erosion; channel storage decouples flux from forcing. The union has its own loop and its own ground, so it cannot be answered by adding three global trends.

## 2. The shape

1. **Slot-indexed, not global.** Fig 1 :19: *"The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated."* The answer is a distribution over slopes, reaches and catchments. Fig 1 :22: *"Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average."* A near-flat regional mean with one failing slope is a legal state, not noise.

2. **Climate acts as conditions in a cascade.** Fig 11 :46: *"conditions: light, water, temperature, season, terrain"*; Fig 11 :44: *"List everything in contact with the residual, then what is in contact with those, and outward until the cascade stops mattering."* Water, temperature, season and terrain are already in the habitat, so no new primitive is imported. Fig 11 :49: *"An actor's valence is relative to the residual, not a property it carries."* The figures do not sign the conditions: warming does not enter as "more erosion" universally.

3. **Flows are directed, thresholded and nonlinear.** Fig 13 :76: *"Energy and a gradient compose a flow."* Operators table :49: *"a threshold relation between two quantities"*; slope failure is a comparison of driving stress against resisting strength. Fig 13 :31-32: *"a remainder compounding through a chain that amplifies is chaos, and the detail a crossing discards reappears above it as noise"*. Nonlinearity is structural, and scale matters.

4. **Feedback cycles are licensed, and their sign is measured.** Fig 12 :121: *"cycle. The output re-enters as input. The sign of the loop decides whether it closes, and a chain cannot close by construction."* Sediment cover can armour or provide tools; the response is a cycle, not a one-way chain.

5. **The ground settles each slot; agreement does not.** Fig 2 :29: *"An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks."* Fig 2 :35: *"Alignment is triangulation, not negotiation. A mirror cannot correct you."* Independent instruments (gauges, repeat topography, inventories, sediment budgets), not an ensemble vote, are the anchor. Fig 10 :24: *"the ground does not decay, the channel does"*: the monitoring network itself must be checked before a trend is believed.

6. **Sign and magnitude are an honest gap.** Fig 9 :32: *"you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself."* Fig 13 :134: *"Unreachable is a verdict here, not a silence"*. No figure line yields a percentage, a direction or a date for a specified slope. That quantity is out of reach with the library as it stands and is owed to instrument work, not to a stronger assertion.

## 3. What it explicitly does not say

- Not that climate change increases or decreases erosion, landslides or sediment flux in general. The figures list conditions; valence is relative to the residual.
- Not a global number, not a date, not a ranked list of regions.
- Not that a model ensemble agreeing is evidence. Shared evidence pools disqualify agreement as a verdict (Fig 2 :40).
- Not that the six-term substrate or the habitat list is nature's own list. They are a visible guess at compression axes; declaring them makes the choice visible, not neutral.
- Not grounded. No measurement was taken in producing this answer.

## 4. What would settle it, the ground

A source-discriminating instrument, not a further argument. *The Hard Problem is About Source* notes that an instrument reading green *"cannot comment on the source"* and that the spectrometer, not an argument, resolved the case. Here:

- **Slot instruments:** paired catchments, pore-pressure loggers, repeat topographic surveys, landslide inventories with known completeness, sediment budgets. Each resolves a different possible source of the same yield trend.
- **Threshold test:** does failure occur at a reproducible comparison of driving stress with resisting strength, and does that threshold move under the new regime?
- **Cycle-sign test:** does increased sediment supply armour the bed (damping) or provide tools that raise incision (amplifying)?
- **Channel check:** does the apparent trend survive replacement of the monitoring network or inventory method (Fig 10 :25-26)?
- **Standing falsifiers** from `01`: a clean partition of the three processes; homogeneous, threshold-free, linear slot response; a first-order-only response with no cascade; an immune monitoring channel; or a figure line that names the magnitude.

## 5. The honest stop

**Unreached, not unreachable.** The answer stops where the instruments stop resolving sources. Fig 6 :32: *"An instrument is not built from a description. It is improved from a worse instrument already returning something."* SEARCHED was run first (a census of Figures 1 to 13, Operators and Symbols found no magnitude line), so the live track is INWARD: sharpen the gauges, surveys and inventories already returning something. OUTWARD is conditional and must be described in effect terms first (Fig 6 :30). Importing a magnitude against a budget limit would add an atom that is not needed. The lineage is recorded so the stop is checkable; indiscernibility is not identity.

## 6. One-paragraph version for outreach

> The Tether does not give a number for how climate change will alter erosion, landslides and sediment flows, and it does not sign the response. It says the question is a union of overlapping conditions, not three separate trends; that the residual must be tracked per slope, reach and catchment, because a global average hides the live slot; that climate acts through conditions in a contact cascade, so second-order effects through vegetation, storage and antecedent state are part of the answer; that flows are directed, thresholded and nonlinear, with feedback loops whose sign has to be measured; and that the ground, not model agreement, settles each slot. The sign and magnitude for any particular slope remain an open, instrument-bound question. What is offered is the shape any answer must have, four refuters written in advance, and the instruments that would close it.
