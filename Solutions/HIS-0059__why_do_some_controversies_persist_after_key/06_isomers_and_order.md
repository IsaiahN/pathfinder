# Isomers, order and analogues in persistent controversy

## RESIDUAL

Two questions are owed after `01`: (1) if two controversies share the same settled facts, are they **isomers** under Fig 12, and (2) what is the dependency order among settling, refusing, importing and composing in a controversy? A third supporting question is which world analogues may be used without being mistaken for independent witnesses.

Atoms available for the comparison: the settled factual claims (the shared "ingredients"), plus the bond types that can join them: values, identity, institutional interest, jurisdiction, evidence-pool membership, channel state, and the refusal of a held term. No new primitive is available without import. The bond set is exactly the point: Fig 12 :5 *"A set of ingredients is not one. ## DERIVATION

### Q1 : Same settled facts, different controversy : are they isomers?

- Fig 12 :117: *"Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."*
- Fig 12 :94: *"The arrangement is the substance."*
- Fig 12 :92: under a symmetric bond, k operands have k! spellings and one term, so *"a code that charges for the sequence overcharges by log₂(k!) while the bond term undercharges"*. A controversy that merely lists its facts as if order were free is making exactly this mistake in reverse: it charges for ingredients and records no bond.
- Two controversies over the same settled evidence, joined one by identity and one by institutional interest, share atoms and differ in bond. By Fig 12 they are isomers, not variants of one dispute.
- **Falsifier, written before the run:** exhibit two controversies with identical settled atoms and identical bond arrangement but different persistence behaviour. The isomer claim fails and the difference must be sought in an unenumerated habitat actor (Fig 11 :48: *"You do not invent the list. You read it off the world, and what you cannot perceive or measure yet is the residual."*).
- Status: triangulated for the figure definition (Fig 12 :94 and :117); single-source for the social application, since no figure line selects which bond a given controversy uses.

### Q2 : Is there a functional group that fixes the kind?

- Fig 12 :124: *"functional group. A substructure whose presence fixes the outcome. The group says what kind; the qualifier says which member."*
- The recurring substructure across persistent controversies is not a particular fact; it is a **union of at least two conditions with anchors that differ**, one of which has already settled. That fixes the kind: a persisting compound. The qualifier, which condition is live in a given case, names the member.
- Fig 12 :119: *"catalyst. A part that survives its own use."* An institution or a prestigious name that survived the earlier dispute is a catalyst under this definition, matching Fig 10 :61 *"And standing is a stake that survives the outcome it was earned on."* The two are the same structural observation at different levels, not two independent witnesses.
- **Falsifier:** show a persisting controversy with no settled-plus-live union structure. Then the functional-group reading is wrong.

### Q3 : What is the order of operations?

- Fig 3 :20: *"The chain is ordered by dependency, not by importance."* Fig 3 :19: *"Optimising a link that already works cannot rescue one that does not."*
- `THE_FORMULA.md` step 2 routes REFUTED before mint: a term held false owes a refusal, *"not an explanation"*, and *"no arrangement of the other four reaches it"*. Operator ¬ is unary and is not one of the seven bonds (Operators table :70 to :74).
- Fig 12 :104: *"A settled molecule becomes an atom for whatever composes over it. So irreducible is a fact about a level and never about the world"*. A settled fact can be composed over at the next level, but only after settlement; settling is necessary and not sufficient (Fig 5 :69).
- Fig 9 :27 to :28: *"describe it before you go looking, or any frame you pick will seem to fit"*. The residual must be described before choosing which literature or arena to consult.
- Therefore the order is: describe the per-slot residual, name which slot is REFUTED and write the refusal, settle or import at the level where the slot lives, then compose. Doing these in another order is how a campaign ends up "solving" a stake dispute with another review of the facts.
- **Falsifier:** exhibit a case where composition over an unsettled term succeeds and the ground pays for it. That would break the settling-before-composition order.

## CONCLUSION

The two-c controversy intuition is partly right and partly a notation error. Controversies over the same settled facts can be isomers, but what makes them different substances is the bond, not the ingredient list. Their recurrence has a functional group: a union of a settled condition and a still-live condition with a different anchor. The operations have a dependency order, with the refusal before mint and the description before the frame. All four world analogues are low-overlap corroborators for mechanism shape; none supplies a verdict and none can name which slot is live in a given case.
