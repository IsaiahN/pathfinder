**Subject:** One checkable claim about early supermassive black holes, before any theory

> Hi [name],
>
> I have one specific output I would like you to check against what you already know. I am not asking you to read a framework first, and I am not asking you to agree with anything. If the output does not survive, there is nothing else to look at.
>
> **The output (`general` on the shape, `specified` on the domains, not `grounded`):** "how did the earliest supermassive black holes form" is not one question with two candidate answers. The figures split it into overlapping conditions: the seed mass at birth, the growth loop and its duty cycle, the observation that anchors the effect, and the agreement between models. A case in two conditions is not a harder case of either. The observation is real: massive black holes exist within the first billion years, and some are overmassive relative to their hosts. What the measurement settles is that something formed early, not which channel did it. Several channels project to the same mass and redshift reading, so the source is underdetermined until an instrument discriminates it.
>
> The structure the figures do give is this. Growth is a cycle whose sign decides whether it closes: accretion feeds on gas, the output of accretion changes the gas supply, and a channel works when that loop closes in time. Starting heavy buys time; accreting fast and often is the other way to close it. The channels in the literature form a continuum between those extremes, not a dichotomy. I am not claiming that the figures select DCBH, a Pop III remnant, a cluster runaway or a primordial black hole. They return a shape and an owed instrument, not a name.
>
> **What I am not claiming:** not that this is a theory of everything, not that simulations agreeing with each other corroborate the claim, and not that a high-redshift mass estimate is the ground. Several models draw on the same observed mass function and the same sub-grid physics, so their agreement is one evidence pool, not two witnesses.
>
> **What would refute the shape claim, written down before this run:**
> 1. an instrument that separates the seed channels at low overlap and selects one, for example a 21 cm depth that fixes the direct-collapse abundance or a gravitational-wave background that fixes the merger contribution;
> 2. one condition shown sufficient with the other three held at zero, which would dissolve the union;
> 3. a growth history in which accretion output never re-enters as fuel, so there is no loop to close;
> 4. a systematic revision of high-redshift mass estimates that removes the timing residual altogether.
>
> If any one holds, I withdraw the shape claim. A one-line "this fails because..." is more useful to me than agreement. If you would rather not engage, no reply is needed.
>
