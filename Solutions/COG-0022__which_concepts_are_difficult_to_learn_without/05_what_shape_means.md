## 1. A shape is a constraint, not a filler

The canonical README states the limit: the framework returns *"what a missing thing would have to look like (a shape, not a name)."* Fig 9 :32: *"you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself."* Fig 13 :135: *"It cannot say which primitive is missing, because naming it would mean already holding it."*

From *The Library of Babel*, opened this session: *"You can cut your search short if the set of books returned is small and descriptive enough that it provides a general shape of the answer that satisfies your question."*

So a **shape** is the narrowed form of an answer you can state before you can name its instances. For COG-0022 the shape is the boundary condition, *language required iff a needed bond or atom is absent from the nonlinguistic closure and no differing closure is in contact*, not a list of concepts. "Exact number on one side, approximate number on the other" is an illustration drawn from world sources; the figure-derived claim is the condition, not the list.

## 2. How the shape is obtained

- *Library of Babel*: use the vocabulary you already have that is *closest* to the thing, look for recurring new terms that overlap or correlate with it, recurse. *"With each new term gained, you are able to narrow the space by definition and deepen your vocabulary."*
- Fig 6 :30: *"The description travels. Fails by being sharpened into terms only we can read. Cast it in effect terms."* Fig 9 :30-31: *"your words are borrowed too, so describe the effect rather than the cause."* Hence the residual was stated in effect terms (R stays high until an import) before any field was named.
- *Decoding Tacit Knowledge*, opened this session: *"When contact isn't possible, the best you can send is a plain description of what you'd expect to see, because anyone can check that against the world."* This is why the packet asks for a refuter rather than a theory readout.
- *Library of Babel* on bundling: *"Perhaps your problem was a bundle or conflation of several problems masquerading as one?"* That is the source of the union reading in `01`, not a stylistic choice.

## 3. What licenses a name

Two things, both outside the shape itself:

1. **The ground settles it.** Formula step 5: *"the ground settles it. Verified only by what pays. A gate is not the ground."* For this question, a behavioral measurement per concept slot.
2. **A provenanced import from a frame whose closure differs** (Fig 6 :23, Fig 8 :50-52), with a source gate and a shadow test. A word from another language is exactly such an import, but its provenance must be recorded and it adds no atom to the world (Fig 6 :25).

*Decoding Tacit Knowledge* sharpens the limit on what language can transfer: *"Writing carries the context well and the subtext partly, but it almost never carries the pretext, the 'you had to be there' part."* And: *"we can know more than we can tell"* (Polanyi, quoted in the essay). A name handed over in words does not hand over the contact that made the name meaningful. Fig 4 :22: *"Only methods cross up. Only head starts come down."*

## 4. Why "which concepts" is the forbidden move, and what replaces it

The direct question asks for a list. The framework cannot produce a figure-derived list: naming which primitive is missing would mean already holding it (Fig 13 :135), and the canonical README says the output is a shape, not a name. Applying a list request to the figures would be the same error as naming Gradient in the exemplar.

What replaces it is a discriminator. *The Hard Problem is About Source*, opened this session: an instrument that reads green *"cannot comment on the source"*, and *"If several paths create the same behavior, then behavior is a projection with lossy information."* It was the spectrometer, not an argument, that resolved the case; the essay says *"The answer is unreached until a better instrument exists."* For COG-0022 the discriminator is a design that presents a concept under nonverbal conditions and records whether R falls, crossed with a design that varies which bonds and atoms the learner's library holds. Until that runs, the list is a `gap`.

## 5. The source and prestigem rules

*The Recursive Self-Improvement Trap*, opened this session: *"the output is only as good as the validator. The agent, or the tool, or the frame, or the theory, cannot validate itself."* And: *"Prestige must never be allowed to validate an action directly... Prestige can fund an action. It can give an action a budget to be tried. But it cannot settle an action. Only the ground can settle it."*

Applied here: a survey of well-known linguists about which concepts are language-bound is a prestige and agreement channel, not a ground. It can fund the test by getting it run. It cannot decide the answer. Fig 2 :42: *"Buy independence in proportion to the cost of being wrong."*

## 6. My own audit is a simulation, not a ground reading

Same essay: *"LLMs can quickly simulate an approximate answer/output with the formula found in the architecture post... However, the truly testable output would need to be derived from the real architecture or working the formula yourself."* LLM output is a simulated reconstruction of the derivation. So `01` is a simulated derivation, checked for internal consistency against the figures. It is **not** a test of the question and must be labelled as such. It cannot settle anything it reports on.
