# Expert verification packet - the Tether on concepts and language

**What this is:** a claim in a form a researcher in language and conceptual development can check. Mode, derivation and refuters are stated before the conclusion, so a reader knows what to look for and what would settle it. One to one only.

**Mode: `specified` on the structural and conditional claims, `general` on the union and anchor rules, not `grounded`.** No behavioral measurement is claimed. This is a shape and a route, not a result.

## 1. The claim, in one paragraph

Language's distinctive contribution is a grammar of bonds, not a stock of labels. Fig 12 :111 says a vocabulary without bonds can name a state and cannot name a route to one. From the closure rule it follows that a concept is hard to learn without a particular language exactly when acquiring it needs a bond or an atom the learner's nonlinguistic library does not hold and no differing closure is in contact to supply it. Core categories, approximate quantity and first-order regularities are acquirable through nonlinguistic experience because capability is agent-and-habitat and the habitat is enumerated, never composed. Exact integer, reflective truth and norm kinds lean on imported sequence, comparison and negation. The question is a union of two failures with different instruments, so verdicts stay per domain and are never averaged. Which named concepts fall on which side is a declared `gap`, not a hidden answer.

## 2. Three checkable sub-claims

### C1 - framework-internal; checkable by reading Figures 12 and 6

- **Statement:** the boundary is a bond-and-atom condition, not a concept list. Language supplies bonds and grammar; a concept needing an absent bond is hard without a language that carries it, unless a differing closure supplies it.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 12 :111, *"A vocabulary without bonds can name a state and cannot name a route to one."* Fig 12 :112, *"A plan is a sequence. A goal is a comparison. Progress is a subtraction. A refutation is a negation, and that one is not a bond."* Fig 12 :115, *"having terms for what a situation affords was taken for a dictionary and it is a grammar."* Fig 6 :23, *"No frame escapes its own closure. The bound moves only on contact with a frame whose closure differs."*
- **Refuter:** exhibit a concept requiring a bond absent from a learner's nonlinguistic library yet acquired nonlinguistically at the same rate and with the same transfer as one using held bonds; or show a concept composed only of held atoms and bonds that remains inaccessible without language after adequate contact. Either breaks the condition.

### C2 - world-facing; checkable against the developmental and cross-cultural literature

- **Statement:** approximate number and first-order categories are nonlinguistic; exact number and second-order truth lean on language. Over a shared substrate these are isomers, not degrees of one ability: Fig 12 :117, *"isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."*
- **What to check:** do the Mundurukú and core-knowledge results survive the distinction between nonverbal approximation and language-based exact number? Does the label-versus-no-label effect remain conditional on uncertainty and removable by verbal but not visual interference? Is the second-order/first-order split in the animal-cognition literature drawn where the claim puts it?
- **Sources to check (all opened 2026-11-22):** Pica, Lemer, Izard and Dehaene, Science 2004, *"they fail in exact arithmetic with numbers larger than 4 or 5"*; Spelke and Kinzler, Child Development Perspectives 2009, symbolic addition *"draws crucially on a nonsymbolic numerical ability that monkeys, pigeons, and newly hatched chicks share"*; Cibelli et al., PLoS ONE 2016, *"the apparent effect of language on color cognition disappears when participants are given a verbal (but not a visual) interference task"*; Parellada, Philosophia 2021, *"language would not be required for direct belief and concept possession, but it would for beliefs about beliefs"*. These corroborate; they do not decide. The core-knowledge reviews share a pool and count as at most two witnesses.
- **Refuter:** a culture without a count list doing exact arithmetic beyond five under controls; a nonlinguistic subject passing a true-versus-false-belief design with associative cues excluded; or a label that deterministically switches nonlinguistic categorization under high perceptual certainty with two independent evidence pools.

### C3 - framework-internal, with a live gap

- **Statement:** the question is a union of bond-fit and closure-contact, and teaching transfers a head start, not capability. Fig 9 :55: *"A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones."* Fig 4 :22: *"Only methods cross up. Only head starts come down."* Fig 11 :39: *"capability is a property of agent-and-habitat, never of the agent alone."*
- **Refuter:** a single intervention that restores both conditions with no categorically different residue (collapses the union); or a verbal instruction with zero corrective contact producing a grounded capability indistinguishable from one built through practice (breaks the head-start claim).
- **Live gap, named not hidden:** recursion permits a settled first-order term to become an atom for second order (Fig 12 :104), but whether a nonlinguistic frame can carry the negation operator that says a claim is false is not settled here (Operators table :58). This is S3 in `01`, reported as `gap` at this budget, not smoothed over.

## 3. What is explicitly not claimed

- Not a list of named language-dependent concepts, not a percentage, not a ranking. The framework returns *"a kind of thing, never the answer itself"* (Fig 9 :32).
- Not strong linguistic determinism. *"a convention that decides an outcome has stopped being one"* (Fig 10 :47).
- Not that nonlinguistic learners lack concepts. The habitat is enumerated, never composed (Fig 11 :43).
- Not that the six physical terms of Fig 13 are psychological concepts. That reading was a strain and was re-censused (S1 in `01`).
- Not `grounded`. Nothing here is asserted as settled by a behavioral measurement.
- Not a claim that the framework is the only true account of concept acquisition; a frame cannot certify its own limit (Fig 8 :34).

## 5. What would settle it

1. A bond test: nonverbal presentation crossed with variation in the learner's available bonds. Does R fall only when the bond is present?
2. A contact test: bonds held constant, contact with a differing closure varied. Does R fall only when the import is available?
3. A second-order test: true versus false belief, or refutation, with associative cues and experimenter cueing excluded. This closes the S3 gap.
4. A determinism test: label versus no label under high perceptual certainty with two independent evidence pools.
5. The five standing falsifiers in `01` section 4, any one of which retracts the shape.

## 6. What an expert can check in twenty minutes

1. Read Fig 12 :111-112, Fig 6 :23 and Fig 4 :22, four short passages.
2. Check C2 against the Mundurukú, core-knowledge and interference literature. Are those characterizations faithful?
3. Name one design that would distinguish a bond failure from a contact failure, or say why none is possible.
4. Send the refutation. A failure is more useful than an endorsement.
