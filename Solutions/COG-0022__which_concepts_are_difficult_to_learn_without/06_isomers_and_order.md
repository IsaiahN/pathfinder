# Are the two acquisition routes isomers? Bonds, order, and analogues

**Mode: `specified`.** This is a simulated derivation checked against the figures, not a ground reading (see `05_what_shape_means.md` section 6).

## RESIDUAL

`01` F1 says the boundary is a bond-and-atom condition. Two questions are owed: (1) do the nonlinguistic route and the language-mediated route stand as **isomers** over a shared substrate, or only as members of one **functional group**; and (2) what is the dependency order of the acquisition chain, and where does it break?

## DOMAIN CENSUS

Bonds held without language: the perceptual and cross-kind arrangements available to contact. Bonds imported with language: sequence `→`, comparison `⋛`, production `⇒`, negation `¬` (Fig 12 :72, :87, Operators table :58). ## DERIVATION

### Q1 - Isomer or functional group?

- Fig 12 :117: **"isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."**
- Fig 12 :124: **"functional group. A substructure whose presence fixes the outcome. The group says what kind; the qualifier says which member."**
- Fig 12 :5: **"A set of ingredients is not one. The bond is the rest of the information, and it is the half nothing was recording."**

Two learners over the same perceptual substrate, one with a bond vocabulary and one without, do **not** differ in their atom list. They differ in which bonds can be composed and therefore in which concepts can be settled. Over the full shared atom set they are **isomers**: same atoms, different bond, different reachable concepts. This is the stronger and more useful reading because it predicts that the two routes are not degrees of one ability but differently arranged substances whose difference is invisible if you record only the atoms (the stimuli, the vocabulary size).

Weaker reading, kept for honesty: if the language-mediated route requires an atom the nonlinguistic route genuinely lacks (for example a discrete symbol that has no perceptual counterpart), then the two are **not** isomers but members of one functional group over a partially shared set. Fig 12 :124: *"the group says what kind; the qualifier says which member."*

- **Falsifier, written before the run:** if a concept acquired through language can be shown to use no atom or bond available to nonlinguistic contact at all, the isomer claim fails and the functional-group reading stands. If the two routes can be written with identical atoms and identical bonds, the difference claim fails outright and F1 of `01` falls with it.

### Q2 - Why the difference is hard to see

- Fig 12 :94: **"The arrangement is the substance."**
- Fig 12 :95-96: two entries with an identical ingredient set are *"different things whose notation cannot tell them apart, and the difference is carried entirely by the bond."*

A test that records only which objects a learner saw, or how many words they heard, records the ingredients. It cannot tell the routes apart. That is why a vocabulary-size correlation is a filter for the budget, not a witness for the verdict (Fig 9 :60). The discriminating measurement must vary the bond, for example verbal versus visual interference in the Cibelli et al. design, which is exactly what makes that result load-bearing for F5.

### Q3 - Dependency order (Fig 3)

- Fig 3 :20: **"The chain is ordered by dependency, not by importance."**
- Fig 3 :19: **"Optimising a link that already works cannot rescue one that does not."**
- Fig 3 :7: **"it has terms for what things afford: what can be moved, opened, joined, blocked"**
- Fig 3 :27: **"A link with no instrument is not a link that works. It is a link nobody has looked at."**

Order: perception, vocabulary, objective, planning, learn and carry. Language acts at vocabulary and objective. The chain breaks at the middle link because that is where terms for what a situation affords and a statement of what would count as progress are both required and neither has an obvious instrument (Fig 3 :25-26). A purely perceptual training program funds the link that already works; a purely verbal one assumes the perception link and never measures it. Fig 12 :113: *"A system holding only ingredients can say what a situation contains and cannot say what would count as getting somewhere from it."*

- **Falsifier:** a documented learner whose perception, vocabulary and objective links all measure as working and whose concept failure is located at planning. That would falsify the "usually at the middle" claim, though not the dependency order.

### Q4 - The right analogue, and a refused one

The count list is the lawful analogue: a set of discrete atoms bound by sequence and comparison, imported from a differing closure (Fig 6 :23), which lets a learner settle exact cardinality where approximate magnitude alone could not. The generic statement is a second analogue: it fixes a norm and an essence across instances, which perceptual contact alone does not carry efficiently (Gelman and Roberts 2017, world source, secondary).

The refused analogue is the six-term physical substrate. An earlier draft called a perceptual category "a spectrum" and a spatial relation "space" to make Fig 13 fit. That is a strain, so Fig 13 does not govern psychological content; it governs the shared substrate and the honest stop only. Re-census recorded as S1 in `01`.

- **Falsifier for the analogue:** a derivation of a psychological concept directly from the six physical terms without renaming any of them. If that succeeds, S1 was a false strain and Fig 13 governs more than this folder allows.

### Q5 - What the recursion permits and what it does not

- Fig 12 :104: **"A settled molecule becomes an atom for whatever composes over it. So irreducible is a fact about a level and never about the world"**
- Operators table :58: **"a refutation is a negation, which is why a vocabulary without it can only accumulate"**

Once a first-order concept is settled, it can become an atom for a second-order composition. This is why a language-using learner can build reflective concepts: the first-order term is already admitted, and the unary operator can refuse a claim. A nonlinguistic frame can still compose at first order (Fig 11 :43) and may still detect its own errors (Parellada's Allen criterion, world source), but whether it can carry the refusal operator is the open question recorded as S3 in `01`. Recursion licenses the composition in principle; it does not supply the operator.

## CONCLUSION

The two acquisition routes are best read as **isomers over a shared perceptual atom set**: same atoms, different bond vocabulary, different reachable concepts, with the difference invisible to any measurement that records only ingredients. The weaker functional-group reading is kept if a genuinely new atom is required. Dependency order puts language at the vocabulary and objective links, where the fewest instruments exist, so a lever applied to perception or planning alone will not rescue a break there. The count list and the generic are the lawful analogues; the six physical terms are not. The recursion makes second-order concepts composable once first-order terms settle, but the availability of negation without language remains an honest `gap`.
