## What the wider literature bears on the question

1. **A universal substrate survives.** Cibelli et al. model language effects as a language-specific category distribution over a universal perceptual space. Pica et al. and Spelke and Kinzler separate nonverbal approximation from language-based exact number. This corroborates F2 and F3 and refuses a wholesale language-determinism reading.
2. **Language contributes a tool, not a new mind.** Gelman and Roberts call language a technology that expands the categorization capacities we share with other animals, and explicitly note that nonhuman transmission proceeds without language. This corroborates F1 and F8: a head start crosses, the capability does not travel.
3. **The effect is conditional.** The verbal-but-not-visual interference result and the uncertainty modulation in Cibelli et al. corroborate F5: words bias reconstruction when the ground signal is uncertain, and the bias can be switched off.
4. **The reflective layer is the plausible language-leaning case.** Parellada's Davidson discussion places language at beliefs about beliefs rather than at direct first-order concepts. This corroborates F4 but is a single conceptual source, not a measurement.
5. **Overlap warning.** Gelman and Roberts cite the same core-knowledge and number-word literature as Pica et al. and Spelke and Kinzler. The three agree, but they are at most two evidence pools. Fig 2 :31: *"Independence is how little they draw on the same evidence."*

None of these sources decides the figure verdict. Fig 9 :60: *"Use filters for the budget and witnesses for the verdict. Never let a filter hand you a verdict."*
