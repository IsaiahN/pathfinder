## The question restated as an effect

R = |Γ(b,a) - o'| is measured per concept slot, not averaged (Fig 1 :19, Fig 1 :22). Here **b** is the learner's belief, formed from private history and inherited prior mixed by the weighting w (Symbols table :24-25); **a** is the learning act (perceiving, attending, being corrected, hearing a label); **Γ(b,a)** is what the learner's library predicts about the concept; and **o'** is what the habitat and ground actually return. The slot **s** is the concept. The phenomenon to explain is why R falls to near zero through nonlinguistic contact for some slots and stays high until a term, a count list, or a grammar arrives from another frame for others.

- Option A, "concepts split into linguistic and nonlinguistic kinds": leads to the prediction that language is a property of certain concepts.
- Option B, "the split is a condition on composition and contact": leads to the prediction that a concept is hard without language exactly when acquiring it needs a bond or an atom the learner's nonlinguistic Γ does not hold and no differing closure is in contact.

**Bind the sought-for shape as an ordinary term** (Formula, "Direction"), before the run: the answer would be a condition of the form "language required iff a needed bond or atom is absent from the nonlinguistic library and no differing closure supplies it," plus an instrument that discriminates an absent-bond failure from an absent-contact failure. Until bound, that shape predicts nothing and no loop check can see it. It is now bound, so the refuter in `01` section 5 can fire on it.

The verdict recorded in `09_watson_answer.md` is Option B in `specified` mode, kept per domain and never averaged. The specific list of concept names is a `gap`, not a hidden answer.

## What is deliberately not here

- No personal emails of named individuals.
- No ranking of people by how likely they are to agree.
- No bulk-send plan.
- No claim that this derivation is `grounded`, no list of named concepts asserted as figure-derived, and no claim that the framework is the only true account of concept acquisition.

Three figure-grounded reasons, plus one practical one:

1. A room is not an anchor. *"Alignment is triangulation, not negotiation. A mirror cannot correct you."* (Fig 2 :35). Gathering agreeable readers manufactures the correlation that disqualifies a verdict (Fig 2 :40).
2. Agreement about words is a convention, not a verdict. *"efficient for a convention: agreement about a shared meaning is the product, not a corruption of it"* (Fig 2 :41). A poll of which concepts a language names cannot settle which concepts a speaker can acquire.
3. A verifier must be outside the interior. *"A verifier that can reconstruct the claim cannot verify it."* (Fig 10 :42). The first contact therefore carries the residual and its refuter, not the framework.
4. Legally, unsolicited bulk email to scraped addresses breaches GDPR and CAN-SPAM style rules in many jurisdictions and gets the sending domain blacklisted. Public opt-in routes avoid that failure as well as the structural one.
