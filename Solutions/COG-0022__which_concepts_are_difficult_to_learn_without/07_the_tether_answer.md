# The Tether's answer: concepts, language, and nonlinguistic experience

**Mode: `specified` on the structural and conditional claims, `general` on the union and anchor rules, not `grounded`.** The named concept examples are world-reported illustrations, not figure derivations. This is a shape and a route, not a result. Simulated derivation checked against the figures (`05_what_shape_means.md` section 6), not a ground reading.

## 1. The residual it answers

R = |Γ(b,a) - o'| stays small for some concept slots under nonlinguistic contact and stays large for others until a term, a count list, or a grammar arrives from another frame. R is indexed per concept and per learner, never averaged (Fig 1 :19, Fig 1 :22). The question is a **union compound** of two conditions, not a binary split of concepts into kinds (Fig 9 :54, Fig 9 :57): bond fit with the learner's nonlinguistic library, and contact with a closure that supplies what is missing.

## 2. The shape

1. **Language contributes bonds, not just labels.** Fig 12 :111: *"A vocabulary without bonds can name a state and cannot name a route to one."* Fig 12 :112: *"A plan is a sequence. A goal is a comparison. Progress is a subtraction. A refutation is a negation, and that one is not a bond."* A concept is hard without a particular language exactly when acquiring it needs a bond or an atom the learner's nonlinguistic Γ does not hold and no differing closure is in contact (Fig 6 :23).

2. **The universal foundation is nonlinguistic.** Fig 11 :39: *"capability is a property of agent-and-habitat, never of the agent alone."* Fig 11 :43: *"The habitat is enumerated, never composed."* Core categories, object continuity, approximate quantity, and habitat regularities are available through contact because the habitat supplies them and the loop runs without a maintainer (Fig 11 :36). Same kernel at every size, only actors, currency and anchor change (Fig 4 :20).

3. **The discriminator cases.** Approximate number is an ordering already held; exact integer is a count list bound by sequence and comparison, an import from a differing closure. World sources report that Mundurukú speakers handle large approximate quantities but fail exact arithmetic beyond five (Pica et al. 2004) and that symbolic addition draws on a system shared with monkeys, pigeons and chicks (Spelke and Kinzler 2009). Over the shared substrate the two number systems are **isomers**: Fig 12 :117, *"Same atoms, different bond, different substance."* Reflective and truth concepts lean on the same structure at second order: a settled first-order term becomes an atom (Fig 12 :104), and a vocabulary without negation *"can only accumulate"* (Operators table :58; Parellada 2021 on beliefs about beliefs). These examples are illustrations, not a figure-derived list.

4. **Language is a conditional cue, not a determinant.** Strong determinism is refused structurally: *"a convention that decides an outcome has stopped being one"* (Fig 10 :47), and agreement about shared meaning is *"efficient for a convention"*, not a verdict (Fig 2 :41). What survives is conditional: language-specific categories bias memory most under uncertainty and the bias disappears under verbal but not visual interference (Cibelli et al. 2016). Words fund a test; they do not settle it.

5. **Teaching passes a head start, not capability.** Fig 4 :22: *"Only methods cross up. Only head starts come down."* Language makes the next learner's search cheaper but does not transfer the contact that built the concept, so even language-using humans must rebuild it (Fig 11 :40; Decoding Tacit Knowledge: *"we can know more than we can tell"*).

6. **The two failures have different instruments.** A bond failure shows up in the learner's nonlinguistic composition; a contact failure shows up as no differing closure in reach. A single lever clears one region and leaves the intersection (Fig 9 :55). Verdicts are kept per domain and never averaged.

## 3. What it explicitly does not say

- Not a list of which concepts are linguistic. No figure line names a psychological concept; the framework returns *"a kind of thing, never the answer itself"* (Fig 9 :32).
- Not a percentage, a ranking, or a claim that any named concept is on one side by figure derivation.
- Not strong linguistic determinism, and not the claim that words create capacities.
- Not that nonlinguistic learners lack concepts. Fig 11 :43 permits the habitat to supply them; Fig 12 :111 is about what a vocabulary can express, not about what a perceptual Γ can hold.
- Not `grounded`. No behavioral measurement per concept slot was taken here.

## 4. What would settle it - the ground

A source-discriminating design, not a further argument. From *The Hard Problem is About Source*: an instrument that reads green *"cannot comment on the source"*, and *"If several paths create the same behavior, then behavior is a projection with lossy information."* Here the discriminators are:

- **Bond test:** present a concept under nonverbal conditions while varying which bonds the learner's library holds. Does R fall only when the bond is present?
- **Contact test:** hold bonds constant and vary contact with a differing closure. Does R fall only when the import is available?
- **Second-order test:** a true-versus-false-belief or refutation design with associative cues and experimenter cueing excluded. Does a nonlinguistic subject carry the negation operator? This is the live `gap` S3.
- **Determinism test:** label versus no-label under high perceptual certainty with two independent evidence pools. If the label still switches the nonlinguistic categorization, F5 breaks.
- Standing falsifiers from `01` section 4: absent-bond concepts acquired nonlinguistically at the same rate; core capacities that fail without labeling; a figure-derived concept list or global percentage.

## 5. The honest stop

**Unreached, not unreachable, and not silence.** The bond condition is a shape; the instrument that would name the slots is not held here. Fig 13 :134: *"Unreachable is a verdict here, not a silence"*; Fig 8 :39: *"So a search that finds nothing is never a proof of absence."* The answer stops where behavioral instruments can no longer distinguish bond from contact, and the lineage is recorded so the stop is checkable. The simulated second seat in `11` shares this evidence pool, so its agreement is internal consistency, not corroboration.

## 6. One-paragraph version for outreach

> The Tether does not produce a list of language-dependent concepts, and it does not claim that words create concepts. It says that language's distinctive contribution is a grammar of bonds, not a stock of labels, so a concept is hard to learn without a particular language exactly when acquiring it needs a bond or an atom the learner's nonlinguistic library does not hold and no other frame is in contact to supply it. Core categories, approximate quantity, and first-order regularities come from nonlinguistic contact because capability lives in agent-and-habitat; exact integer, reflective truth, and norm kinds lean on imported sequence, comparison, and negation. Language's measured effects are conditional cues under uncertainty, not determinants, and a convention that decides an outcome has stopped being a convention. What the framework offers is the boundary condition, two failure modes with different instruments, and falsifiers written in advance. Which named concepts fall on which side stays open until the ground settles it per slot.
