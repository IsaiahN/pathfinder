## QUESTION

Which concepts are difficult to learn without a particular language, and which can be acquired through nonlinguistic experience?

Restated as an effect: why does R = |Γ(b,a) - o'| stay small for some concept slots under nonlinguistic contact and stay large for others until a linguistic import arrives? R is indexed per concept and per learner, never averaged (Fig 1 :19). Anchor: a behavioral measurement per slot, named as the measurement class in Fig 2 :29; agreement about which words exist is a convention (Fig 2 :41), not the anchor.

## DOMAIN CENSUS

Arity: a relation between learner library Γ, learning act a, concept slot s and ground return o', ordered in time and carrying a sequence and a comparison bond, not symmetric. Symmetry: adding a label lowers R for some slots and raising it is not the reverse move, because the imported term persists as a head start (Fig 4 :22). Scale: the single learner loop, the population sharing a language, and the transmission channel across generations; the membrane is lossy (Fig 4 :30).

## VERDICT (from the figures)

**The figure-derived verdict:** a concept is difficult to learn without a particular language exactly when acquiring it needs a bond or an atom the learner's nonlinguistic library does not hold and no differing closure is in contact; concepts whose atoms and bonds are supplied by the habitat and the perceptual substrate are acquirable through nonlinguistic experience. Approximate number, object and causal categories, and first-order kinds fall on the nonlinguistic side; exact integer, second-order truth and belief, and norm or generic kinds fall on the language-leaning side as world-reported illustrations. The two conditions are kept separate per domain and never averaged. Strong determinism is refused structurally, language effects are conditional, and the specific list of named concepts is a declared `gap`. Mode `specified` on the shape, `general` on union and anchor rules, not `grounded`.

- **Cost if wrong:** misdirected diagnosis. A learner failing contact would be given more vocabulary, or a learner missing a bond would be given more perceptual practice, and a single lever would be applied to a union that needs two. In outreach terms, an expert's twenty minutes would be spent refuting a shape that was never the right shape. The cost is real but bounded and locatable, because the refuters are written down in advance (Fig 2 :42).
- **Undoable:** yes. No term is deleted if the claim fails; a refutation is added and the reach falls because *"something was added rather than because anything was deleted"* (Formula step 3; Fig 6 amendment). The shape and its five falsifiers are recorded with provenance so a later reader can locate the error (Fig 10 :62-64).
- **Reversed by:** any of the five pre-registered falsifiers in `01` section 4, in particular an absent-bond concept acquired nonlinguistically at the same rate, a core capacity that appears only after labeling, a nonlinguistic subject passing a true-versus-false-belief design with associative cues excluded, or a label that deterministically switches nonlinguistic categorization under high certainty with independent pools. A source with a genuinely different evidence pool showing that the bond condition does not predict which concepts are hard would also reverse it. Until one fires, the verdict stands as `candidate`.
