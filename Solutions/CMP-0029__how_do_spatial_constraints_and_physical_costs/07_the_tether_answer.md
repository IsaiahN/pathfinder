# The Tether's answer: spatial constraints and physical costs versus abstract network models

**Mode: `general` on the structural shape, `specified` on the behavioral consequences. Not `grounded`.** Simulated derivation checked for internal consistency against the figures (`05_what_shape_means.md` §6), not a ground reading.

## 1. The residual it answers

Abstract network models predict short mean paths, hub-driven robustness, low spreading thresholds and scale-free spread from a library of nodes, links, degree and hops. Real transportation, power and communication systems occupy space and pay for links. The residual is the gap between the topology-only prediction and the observed behavior of such a system, and it is indexed by link and region. Geometry and cost are two conditions that can bind at once, so this is a union compound with its own loop (Fig 9 :57: "a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either").

## 2. The shape

1. **Embedding is an import, not a calibration.** Fig 6 :21: "MINT composes inside the closure and can never add an atom." Fig 6 :23: "No frame escapes its own closure. The bound moves only on contact with a frame whose closure differs." Distance, capacity and budget are not derivable from degree and hops, so adding them moves the wall of Γ_A.
2. **A link must compose across kinds.** Fig 13 :74: "Everything else is a composition across the two kinds, never within one." Fig 13 :75: "Two states never bond directly. It is not space-time but space, a structure, time; and two structures join only through a state." A link is a structure; nearness and budget are states/structures of a different kind. Once they are in the closure, free long-range links of equal cost are not composable.
3. **The failure is slot-indexed.** Fig 1 :19: "The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated." Fig 1 :22: "Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average." A small shift in mean path length can hide a large live residual on one corridor.
4. **Topological and metric distance are two currencies.** Fig 12 :125: "A composition costs in two currencies, and they do not add." Fig 4 :30: "So take the round trip and measure the gap. Without it you are navigating by dead reckoning: confident about a position nobody checked." The detour index of the spatial-networks literature is the empirical form of that gap.
5. **Two loops plus a compound loop.** Fig 9 :54: "when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again." Geometry and cost are characterized separately, then recombined.
6. **The ground is measurement, not agreement.** Fig 2 :29: "An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks." Fig 2 :45: "Frames range against the ground. They do not negotiate with it." Abstract models must be ranged against measured infrastructure; two models sharing topological assumptions are one evidence pool.
7. **No numeric law is supplied.** Fig 9 :32: "you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself." The output is the shape any candidate answer must have, the refuters written in advance, and the instruments that would close it.

## 3. What it explicitly does not say

- Not a universal revised value for mean path length, mean degree, or an epidemic threshold. Those need a ground.
- Not a claim that abstract network models are wrong everywhere. They are right inside their closure; the claim is about what happens when the closure changes.
- Not a derivation of geometry from topology, and not a new primitive. Space and energy are already in the six-term set; network is already a structure.
- Not a binding of "cost" to a single held atom. Gradient is "a difference that can be spent" (Fig 13 :22); energy is "conserved, finite, directionless alone" (Fig 13 :13-14). Which one carries a particular cost is a provenanced mapping.
- Not `grounded`. No measurement of a real system was taken in this derivation.

## 4. What would settle it  -  the ground

A discriminating instrument, not a further argument. From *The Hard Problem is About Source*: an instrument that reads green "cannot comment on the source"; the spectrometer resolved it. Here the corresponding tests are:

- Does a measured link-length distribution show the predicted cutoff and local concentration, or is the degree distribution still heavy-tailed after cost is accounted for?
- Does the detour index (route distance over Euclidean distance) carry the measured gap, or can a single scalar absorb both path measures?
- Do cost-bound failures appear on particular links and regions (slot-indexed), or uniformly, so that F3 fails?
- Do interdependent systems show the reversed robustness reported by Buldyrev et al., confirming that the compound is categorically different from either single condition?
- Standing falsifiers from §4 of the audit: derive a spatial quantity inside Γ_A; match a real system with free unpriced links; show a uniform residual; close the two-currency gap with one scalar.

## 5. The honest stop

**Unreached, not unreachable.** *They're Scanning Books*: "A minimum viable set of six terms is a human's careful guess at reality's compression axes; another sensorium's minimum set need not be those six." Declaring the atoms makes the choice visible, not neutral. The answer stops at the limit of the available instruments and records the lineage so the stop is checkable. The figures README says the framework reports what a missing thing "would have to look like (a shape, not a name)" and "Indiscernibility is not identity." Averaged topology is indiscernible from several spatial sources; that is not evidence they are the same source.

## 6. One-paragraph version for outreach

> The Tether does not give a new number for how much mean path length changes when you add space. It says that a topology-only model holds no atom for distance or cost, so embedding a network is an import from a different frame, not a better fit of the same one. Once those atoms are present, a link has to compose across kinds: free long-range links of equal cost are no longer composable, and the error shows up per link and per region rather than in the global average. Topological hops and route distance are two currencies that do not add, so the empirical residue is a measurable gap, not a single revised scalar. Geometry and cost are two conditions that can bind at once, so they need separate loops and a third for the compound. What the framework returns is that shape, the four things that would refute it, and the instruments that would settle it. The numeric answer stays open until a measured infrastructure system closes it.
