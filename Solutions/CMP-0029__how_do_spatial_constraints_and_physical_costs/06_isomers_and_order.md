# Order, isomers and analogues for CMP-0029

## RESIDUAL

Two supporting questions are owed after `01_figure_grounded_audit.md`: (1) at which link of the dependency chain does an abstract network model actually stop when space and cost are introduced; and (2) are two spatial realizations of the same abstract topology isomers under Fig 12's definition, so that the difference is decisive in behavior and invisible in the topology alone?

Atoms held: nodes and edges (network structure), space and energy (states), spectrum, gradient, network (structures). Bonds held: the seven plus negation. No new primitive is available without import. The spatial/infrastructure frame supplies the imported atoms; the figures decide their admissibility.

## DERIVATION

### Q1  -  Where the chain breaks

- Fig 3 :19: "Optimising a link that already works cannot rescue one that does not."
- Fig 3 :27: "A link with no instrument is not a link that works. It is a link nobody has looked at."
- Fig 3 :6-7 names the missing link: "it has terms for what things afford: what can be moved, opened, joined, blocked". For spatial networks the affordances are distance, capacity and cost.
- A network model that improves its degree estimator, clustering coefficient or community detection is working above the break. If the vocabulary has no term for what a link costs, those improvements arrive at a step that cannot use them.
- **Order by dependency:** perceive which links exist in the real system -> acquire vocabulary for distance and cost -> state what counts as predicting the observed structure -> plan interventions -> learn and carry. The residual that matters is often three steps out, the way Fig 11 :52-53 describes a cascade that reaches beyond direct contact.
- **Falsifier, written before the run:** if a model that holds no term for distance or cost matches the measured link-length distribution, failure pattern and flow data of a real spatial system, the vocabulary-break claim fails.

### Q2  -  Are two embeddings of one topology isomers?

- Fig 12 :117: "isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."
- Fig 12 :5: "A set of ingredients is not one. The bond is the rest of the information, and it is the half nothing was recording."
- An abstract adjacency list records which pairs connect. It does not record the length, capacity or cost of each connection. Two systems with the same adjacency list but different link lengths are, in the extended closure, the same atoms under a different bond: one substance or two, depending on whether behavior diverges.
- The literature says it diverges: the review reports that spatial constraints produce a cutoff in the degree distribution, larger shortest paths in hops, and a detour index that separates route distance from Euclidean distance. If those measures differ between the two embeddings, the Fig 12 definition is met: invisible in the formula, decisive in the behaviour.
- The weaker claim is a **functional group** under Fig 12 :124: "functional group. A substructure whose presence fixes the outcome. The group says what kind; the qualifier says which member." Two spatial networks can share the `state -> structure -> state` motif while differing in which structure mediates which states.
- **Falsifier, written before the run:** if two realizations of the same abstract topology, differing in link length and cost, are indistinguishable on every measured behavioral quantity, the isomer claim fails and only the functional-group claim stands.

This is an analogue at `specified` mode. Fig 12 defines isomers for a bonded molecule; applying it to a spatial graph is a mapping, not a figure identity. It is kept because the structural condition (same atoms, different typed relation, different behavior) is exactly the one the figure names, and because the falsifier is checkable.

### Q3  -  Analogues and their limit

Fig 9 :30-31: "your words are borrowed too, so describe the effect rather than the cause." Borrowed analogues used here:

- **Planar and geometric graphs.** A null model in which links cannot cross captures the effect of space, not its cause. The cause is the composition rule in F2.
- **Route factor or detour index.** A measured ratio of route to Euclidean distance captures the gap in F4, not the gap itself.
- **Cascades in interdependent infrastructure.** Fig 12 :121: "cycle. The output re-enters as input. The sign of the loop decides whether it closes, and a chain cannot close by construction." The 2003 Italy blackout reported by Buldyrev et al. is a positive-sign cycle: power failure propagated to communication and back. An abstract one-layer model is a chain and cannot close it.
- **Hubs as catalysts.** Fig 12 :119: "catalyst. A part that survives its own use. Spent and regenerating parts behave differently across uses, and nothing records which a term is." A hub that is loaded but not consumed behaves as a catalyst; a hub that fails under load does not. Which one a model assumes changes the robustness prediction.

Each analogue is corroboration from a world frame. None decides the verdict. The source ledger records their overlap.

### Q4  -  What order is the compound?

- Geometry and cost each get a loop; their union gets a third loop (Fig 9 :54, :57).
- Fig 12 :104: "A settled molecule becomes an atom for whatever composes over it. So irreducible is a fact about a level and never about the world." A settled spatial network can become an atom for a larger system of systems, which is how interdependency enters.
- Refusal before mint: if the topological-only prediction is the term that keeps producing a persistent, flat residual, the formula's step 2 owes a refusal of that term's standing to be composed over, not another refinement of it. Negation is a unary operator and not one of the seven bonds (Operators table :70-78), so the refusal is an operator, not a new link type.

## CONCLUSION

The chain breaks at vocabulary: what a link affords under distance and cost. Two spatial realizations of the same abstract topology satisfy Fig 12's isomer definition at `specified` mode, with a stated falsifier. Cascades and hubs are real analogues (cycle and catalyst), but analogues are secondary. The compound is union-ordered: two loops and a third, not one blended parameter.
