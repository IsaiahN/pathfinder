# Expert verification packet  -  the Tether on spatial constraints and physical costs

**What this is:** a claim in a form a spatial-networks or infrastructure researcher can check. Mode, derivation and refuters are stated before the conclusion.

**Mode: `general` on the structural claim, `specified` on the behavioral consequences. Not `grounded`.** No measurement of a real system is claimed here.

## 1. The claim, in one paragraph

An abstract network model's library holds nodes, links, degree and hops. Distance, capacity and budget are not derivable from those atoms, so embedding a network in space and pricing its links is an import from a frame whose closure differs, not a calibration of the same model. Once the imported atoms are present, a link has to compose across kinds, which bars free long-range links of equal cost. The resulting residual is indexed by link and region rather than by the network average, and topological path length and route distance are two currencies with a measurable gap. Geometry and cost are two conditions that can bind at once, so they require separate loops and a third for the compound. The framework returns that shape, with refuters written in advance, and refuses to name a numeric law before a ground settles it.

## 2. Three checkable sub-claims

### C1  -  framework-internal; checkable by reading Figures 6 and 13

- **Statement:** an abstract topological closure cannot generate spatial or cost atoms; embedding is a wall-moving import, and links must then compose across kinds.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 6 :21  -  "MINT composes inside the closure and can never add an atom." Fig 6 :23  -  "No frame escapes its own closure. The bound moves only on contact with a frame whose closure differs." Fig 13 :74  -  "Everything else is a composition across the two kinds, never within one." Fig 13 :75  -  "Two states never bond directly. It is not space-time but space, a structure, time; and two structures join only through a state."
- **Refuter:** derive a spatial or cost quantity inside the topological closure with no import from a differing frame; or exhibit a model with free unpriced long-range links that still matches the measured structure of a real spatial system.

### C2  -  empirical-facing; checkable against published spatial-network data

- **Statement:** the cost-induced residual is slot-indexed, so global means hide where the prediction fails; and topological hops and route distance are two currencies with a nonzero gap.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 1 :19  -  "The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated." Fig 1 :22  -  "Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average." Fig 12 :125  -  "A composition costs in two currencies, and they do not add." Fig 4 :30  -  "So take the round trip and measure the gap. Without it you are navigating by dead reckoning: confident about a position nobody checked."
- **What to check:** in a road, rail or power network, is the deviation from the topology-only prediction concentrated on particular links and regions, with a measurable route factor or detour index, or is it uniform across the system? Is the relevant quantity the mean path length or the spatial distribution of usable links?
- **Refuter:** show the residual is uniform across links and regions, or provide one scalar that predicts both topological and route distance with no remaining gap.

### C3  -  framework-internal derivation, empirically testable

- **Statement:** space and cost form a union compound, not one hard question; each gets a loop and their intersection gets a third.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 9 :54  -  "when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again." Fig 9 :57  -  "a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either."
- **What to check:** does a fix that changes only geometry leave cost-bound failures standing, and vice versa? The published result on interdependent networks (Buldyrev et al., Nature 464, 2010) is the relevant case: adding interdependency reverses the single-network robustness rule.
- **Refuter:** exhibit one lever that clears geometry-only cases, cost-only cases, and the intersection with equal success.

## 3. What is explicitly not claimed

- Not a universal revised value for mean path length, mean degree, clustering, or an epidemic threshold.
- Not that abstract network models are wrong inside their own closure.
- Not a derivation of geometry from topology, and no new primitive.
- Not a provenance-free binding of cost to gradient or energy. Gradient is "a difference that can be spent" (Fig 13 :22); which held atom carries a given cost is an import and must be recorded as such.
- Not `grounded`. No measurement was taken in this derivation.

## 5. What would settle it

A ground reading, not agreement. From *The Hard Problem is About Source*: an instrument that reads green "cannot comment on the source"; the spectrometer resolved it.
