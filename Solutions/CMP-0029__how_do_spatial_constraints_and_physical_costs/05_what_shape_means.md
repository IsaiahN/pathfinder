## 1. A shape is a constraint, not a filler

The figures README states the limit: the framework returns "what a missing thing would have to look like (a shape, not a name)". Fig 9 :32: "you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself."

From *The Library of Babel*: "you can cut your search short if the set of books returned is small and descriptive enough that it provides a general shape of the answer that satisfies your question."

So a **shape** is the narrowed form of an answer you can state before you can name it. It constrains; it does not fill. For this question the shape is: *a topology-only closure must import spatial and cost atoms; links then compose across kinds and become locally constrained; the resulting residual is indexed by link and region; and topological and metric distance are two currencies with a gap.* That is a shape. A universal revised value of the mean path length would be a name, and is refused (F8).

## 2. How the shape is obtained

- *Library of Babel* on proximity: "use the existing vocabulary you do know that is closest to the words that you wish to describe and grasp the shape of." Here the closest held vocabulary is the network structure and the space and energy states of Fig 13, so the search starts there, not in a new physics.
- *Library of Babel* on recursion: "you are able to narrow the space by definition and deepen your vocabulary." Each figure line narrows what an answer may look like.
- Fig 9 :27-28: "describe it before you go looking, or any frame you pick will seem to fit." The residual is described in effect terms first; only then is the spatial-networks literature consulted.
- *Library of Babel* on stopping: "You will know that you are done because the finish line will also be the starting place." The stop here is when the residual stops registering, not when a preferred number is reached.
- Fig 9 :32 again: "you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself." The kind of thing here is an import plus a composition rule plus a slot-indexed residual.

## 3. What licenses a name

Only two things, both outside the shape itself:

1. **The ground settles it.** Formula step 5: "the ground settles it. Verified only by what pays." Here that means measured link lengths, capacities, failures and flows, not another model's efficiency score.
2. **A provenanced import from a frame whose closure differs** (Fig 6 :23, Fig 8), with a source gate and a shadow test. The spatial-networks literature is such a frame, and it must be recorded as imported, with field, author, year and quote, as done in `10_source_ledger.csv`.

*They're Scanning Books* sharpens the second point: "The real import is not a new term in the existing space. It is a new dimension of the space." Adding a cost parameter to a topological model is a new term inside the same space. Adding geometry and budget as atoms that change which links may compose is closer to a new axis of representation. And: "A minimum viable set of six terms is a human's careful guess at reality's compression axes; another sensorium's minimum set need not be those six." The six-term substrate is a visible choice, not nature's own list; "Declaring the primitives does not make them neutral. It makes the choice visible."

## 4. The source-discrimination problem in this question

*The Hard Problem is About Source* gives the mechanism. An instrument that reads green "cannot comment on the source": one green light, or yellow plus blue, or RGB, or CMYK could give the same reading. "If several paths create the same behavior, then behavior is a projection with lossy information." The error was corrected "not by argument, but by the arrival of a better instrument", and "An instrument that can't distinguish these cases demonstrates its own inadequacy."

In CMP-0029 the projection is the abstract topology: the same degree sequence and the same mean path length can arise from many different spatial and cost arrangements. An abstract model that reads the topology and then names the generating geometry repeats the metamerism error. What is owed is a discriminating instrument (route factor, link-length distribution, capacity and failure records), not a name appended to a projection. This is the concrete reason F3 requires slot-indexed residuals: the averaged reading cannot comment on the source.

## 5. Q: does "shape, not a name" forbid naming space and cost? ANSWERED

**No, and I initially overstated it.** The README's phrase governs "a **missing** thing". Space and energy are already among the six states of Fig 13; network is already a structure. Naming them is reading the census, not inventing a primitive. What is forbidden here is two different moves:

1. a figure-derived numeric law for path length or cost exponent (F8), since that is a name where the framework returns a shape;
2. a provenance-free binding of "cost" to a specific held atom (gradient versus energy), since that is an import without a source gate.

The corrected claim: the atoms are nameable; their binding to a particular physical quantity and the numeric consequence are not.

Only the ground can settle it. The Ground Maintainer must be blind to prestige." A list of famous network scientists selected for likely agreement is the named collapse, not corroboration. The legitimate ask gives the claim a budget to be tried: one checkable output, one criterion of failure, one ground.
