## The question restated as an effect

Abstract network models hold a closure Γ_A of topological atoms: nodes, links, degree, hops, centrality. From it they predict behaviors B: short mean paths, hub-driven robustness, low epidemic thresholds, scale-free spread. Real infrastructure and mobility systems are embedded in space and pay for their links. Two readings are possible.

- **Reading A:** space and cost are small perturbations; B survives and the model only needs calibration. This preserves the elegant predictions but never ranges them against the ground, and it averages away the slots where the prediction actually fails.
- **Reading B:** space and cost are atoms absent from Γ_A; embedding is an import that expands the closure. This yields a checkable shape, locates the slots where B breaks, and keeps the two conditions (geometry and cost) separate until their compound is characterized.

What an answer would look like is bound here as an ordinary term: *a set of atoms that must be added to Γ_A, the cross-kind rule by which they constrain links, the slot-indexed residual this produces, and the instrument that would settle which numeric prediction follows.* That shape is refusable by the test in `01_figure_grounded_audit.md` §4 and in `08_expert_verification_packet.md` §2.

## What is deliberately not here

- No personal emails of named individuals.
- No ranking of people by how likely they are to agree with the claim.
- No bulk-send plan.

Three figure-grounded reasons. First, Fig 2 :35: *"Alignment is triangulation, not negotiation. A mirror cannot correct you."* Selecting readers for receptiveness manufactures the agreement that would make their agreement worthless. Second, Fig 2 :40: *"agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool"*, so later assent is never pooled as corroboration. Third, Fig 10 :42: *"A verifier that can reconstruct the claim cannot verify it."* The first contact carries the residual and its refuter, not the framework interior.
