## The wider literature that bears on this question

The literature is secondary corroboration only. The figures decide.

1. **Degree heterogeneity is suppressed by cost.** Barthélemy's review reports that when physical constraints are strong or link creation is costly, "a cut-off appears in the degree distribution" and that for planar networks the degree distribution is "usually peaked ... due to the spatial constraints." This corroborates F2: cross-kind composition restricts which nodes may connect. Overlap with the other sources: moderate, since several of the papers cite the same spatial-networks tradition.
2. **Paths split into two measures.** The same review defines the route factor (detour index) as the ratio of route distance to Euclidean distance and reports that the increasing tendency to connect locally "leads to a large average shortest path" in hops. This corroborates F4: two currencies, with a measurable gap. Overlap: same review as 1, so these are two findings of one witness, not two witnesses.
3. **Interdependency reverses a topology-only robustness result.** Buldyrev et al. show that a broader degree distribution can increase vulnerability to random failure in interdependent networks, "which is opposite to how a single network behaves." This corroborates F5: adding a second condition creates a compound with different behavior, not a harder instance of the first. Overlap: low with Barthélemy's structural review, though both draw on the same network-science pool.
4. **Short paths and findable paths are different properties.** Kleinberg shows that the ease of navigating a small world depends on how links are distributed in space, not merely on the existence of short chains. This corroborates F4 and the slot-indexing claim F3: a global mean does not tell you where the usable links are. Overlap: low with the other three.
5. **Limits of abstract models are explicitly acknowledged in the field.** The epidemic review by Pastor-Satorras et al. frames its contribution as making model "limits and assumptions clear." This corroborates F6: a model must be ranged against the ground; its own score is not a verdict. Only the abstract was opened, so this is a weak witness.
