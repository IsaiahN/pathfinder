**Subject:** One checkable output on spatial networks, before any theory

> Hello,
>
> I have one specific output I would like you to check against what you already know about spatial and infrastructure networks. I am not asking you to read a framework first, and I am not asking you to agree with anything. If the output does not survive, there is nothing else to look at.
>
> **The output (`general` on the structure, `specified` on the consequences, not `grounded`):** an abstract network model's library holds nodes, links, degree and hops. Distance, capacity and budget are not derivable from those atoms. So embedding a network in space and pricing its links is not a calibration of the same model. It is the import of atoms from a frame whose closure differs. Once those atoms are present, a link has to compose a spatial state with a network structure and a finite energy state, so free long-range links of equal cost are no longer composable. The resulting error is indexed by link and region, not by the network average: a small change in mean path length can coexist with a live failure on one corridor. Topological distance and route distance are two currencies that do not add, so the right report is a shape with a measured round-trip gap, not a single revised number.
>
> **What I am not claiming:** a new numeric law for mean path length, a universal cost exponent, or that the framework names which physical quantity carries the cost. Those are bindings from the infrastructure frame onto held atoms; they need a ground and a provenance record. Until then they carry no evidentiary weight.
>
> **What would refute the shape claim:**
> 1. derive a spatial or cost quantity inside the topological closure with no import from a differing frame;
> 2. show a model with free, unpriced long-range links that still matches the measured structure and behavior of a real spatial system;
> 3. show the cost residual is uniform across links and regions, so slot indexing adds nothing;
> 4. provide one scalar that predicts both topological and route distance with no remaining gap.
>
> I wrote these down before sending this. If any one holds, I withdraw the claim. A one-line "this fails because..." is more useful to me than agreement. If you would rather not engage, no reply is needed.
>
> **If it survives, and only then**, the sources are below. The figures are the authority, not my summary of them.
> - Spatial Networks, Marc Barthelemy, Physics Reports 499 (2011): https://arxiv.org/abs/1010.0302
> - Catastrophic cascade of failures in interdependent networks, Buldyrev et al., Nature 464 (2010): https://www.nature.com/articles/nature08932
> - Navigation in a small world, Jon Kleinberg, Nature 406 (2000): https://www.nature.com/articles/35022643
>
> **If you want to use the machinery:** the formula is in the local framework files. Run it on a problem you know well and can check against a ground. Write down what would refute your result before you run it, and state the mode of what you get. A reported failure is more useful to me than a reported success.
