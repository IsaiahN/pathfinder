## Relevant wider literature (read, with overlap declared)

The substantive business and economics pool is **one witness**, not several: Page and Lopatka 1997 (the encyclopedia review quoting Katz and Shapiro, Arthur, Liebowitz and Margolis), Arthur 1989, and Rochet and Tirole 2003 all draw on the same network-effects and industrial-organization literature (Fig 2 :31, :40). The genuinely lower-overlap corroborators of the mechanism shape are Centola and Macy 2007 (sociology and complex systems), the *Allee effect* source ( ecology and population biology), and the *Preferential attachment* source (network science and physics). They corroborate the cycle-sign and threshold shape, not the platform-level fact; Fig 4 :20 requires the anchor to be renamed at the new scale. The three framework essays share the figure author's evidence pool completely and carry no independent witness weight.

No world source conflicts with the figure verdict. The apparent conflict between the lock-in literature (tipping, excess inertia) and the sceptical literature (scale benefits exhausted at small critical mass, multiple networks coexist) is not resolved by choosing a side; it is returned to the census as evidence for the union of conditions (Fig 9 :54-57).

## Distribution: offer, do not push

- **Fig 4 :22** : *"Only methods cross up. Only head starts come down."* Sending the full architecture before anyone has described a residual sends a recording, not a generator. Lead with the shape claim and the refuter; offer links after.
- **Fig 10 :42** : *"A verifier that can reconstruct the claim cannot verify it."* A recipient who has absorbed the whole framework before being asked for a verdict has joined the frame being checked.
- **Fig 9 :27-28** : *"describe it before you go looking, or any frame you pick will seem to fit."* Ask what they would expect to see if the shape claim were true before giving them the article.
- **Fig 2 :40** : *"agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool"*. A mass send selected for people who already agree produces a convention, not corroboration.

**Recommended order in a reply:** (1) the two condition sets and their refuter; (2) ask what they would count as a failure in each half; (3) record the verdict; (4) only then the links, with the note that the figures, not the summary, are the authority.

## The two-stage offer (with a third, opt-in stage)

- **Stage 1 before stage 2** is Fig 10 :52 : *"Bring a characterised residual, never the source"* : and Fig 2 :45 : *"Frames range against the ground. They do not negotiate with it."* The recipient checks the separation without holding the framework's interiors.
- **Stage 2 is opt-in by construction.** Once a recipient has absorbed the theory, their agreement stops being evidence. Their verdict on stage 1 must be recorded *before* any theory is shown, and later agreement is never pooled as corroboration (Fig 2 :40).
- **Stage 3 sends a method, which is permitted.** Fig 4 :22. The formula is a generator, so giving it away does not give away an answer.
- **Stage 3 still needs a ground per problem.** `THE_FORMULA.md` "Direction": a frame *"cannot check the direction of its own question, because the shape a frame is searching for is not a term it holds."* Each user writes a refuter before running and labels the mode of the result.

**Hard precondition:** stages 1 to 3 only work if the stage-1 output survives. If any of the eight refuters in `01` fires, the shape is withdrawn before any link is sent.
