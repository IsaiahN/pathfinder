# Cycle sign, catalysts, and platform types: the supporting derivation for BUS-0067

**Mode: `specified` on the platform application, `general` on the structural distinction. This is a simulated derivation checked against the figures, not a ground reading (see `05_what_shape_means.md` §6).**

## RESIDUAL

Two questions are owed after `01`: (1) what is the ordered structure of a network effect, that is, is it a chain or a cycle, and what decides the sign; and (2) are a useful-open platform and a hard-to-challenge-closed platform the same atoms arranged differently (isomers) or different atoms (functional groups)? A falsifier is written for each.

## DOMAIN CENSUS

Atoms held in the vocabulary: the network relation, the contribution of a participant, the user value read at the ground, the closure and wall of a platform, a head start, and the differing frame a challenger imports from. Bonds held: `→` sequence, `⋛` comparison, `+` conjunction, `−` subtraction, `¬` refusal; the structural forms cycle, catalyst, spent part, isomer, functional group (Fig 12). No new primitive is available without import.

## DERIVATION

### Q1 : Is a network effect a chain or a cycle, and what fixes the sign?

- Fig 12 :121: **"cycle. The output re-enters as input. The sign of the loop decides whether it closes, and a chain cannot close by construction."**
- A chain would be: users join → value rises → stop. No re-entry. A cycle is: users join → value rises → more users join → value rises further. The output (more users) re-enters as input.
- Fig 12 :119: **"catalyst. A part that survives its own use. Spent and regenerating parts behave differently across uses, and nothing records which a term is."** A participant whose contribution regenerates across uses is a catalyst; one whose contribution is exhausted after one transaction is a spent part. Nothing in the user count records which.
- ⇒ A network effect is a **cycle** when contributions regenerate, and the sign is decided elsewhere: by whether the added participant's valence for the relevant residual is positive (Fig 11 :49). A positive sign closes and tips; a negative sign unwinds through congestion or noise. The framework does not compute the sign; it says the sign is the variable and an instrument is owed.
- World corroboration, low overlap: Centola and Macy 2007, *"for complex contagions to spread, multiple sources of activation are required"*; the *Allee effect* source gives a critical density below which *"the population growth rate becomes negative"*; the *Preferential attachment* source gives cumulative advantage, *"those who are already wealthy receive more than those who are not."* Three fields, one mechanism shape.
- **Falsifier, written before the run:** if a platform shows a strictly linear, state-independent value response to added users at every observed `N`, with no threshold and no dependence on contribution valence, the cycle reading fails and the chain reading stands. A field experiment that exogenously varies `N` and finds no change in subsequent adoption would fire this.

### Q2 : What is the order of the wall-moving step?

- Fig 6 :14: **"the only operator that moves the wall"**; Fig 6 :21: **"MINT composes inside the closure and can never add an atom."**; Fig 6 :23: **"No frame escapes its own closure. The bound moves only on contact with a frame whose closure differs."**
- Fig 4 :22: **"Only methods cross up. Only head starts come down."** A challenger imports a method from a differing closure; an incumbent hands a head start down to new participants. These run in opposite directions and are not the same object.
- ⇒ Order by dependency: first describe the residual that the challenger's entry cannot close (Fig 9 :27-28, *"describe it before you go looking, or any frame you pick will seem to fit"*), then find the differing closure, then apply the source gate and shadow test (Fig 8 :50-52), then narrow and let the ground decide (Fig 9 :42, *"narrowing gets you close, the ground decides"*). Reversing this order is echo before shadow: the challenger picks an attractive frame and maps the residual onto it afterward.
- **Falsifier:** a documented case in which a challenger displaces an incumbent without any contact with a differing closure and without any import, and where the entrant's own library already contained the decisive atom from the start. That would break the necessity of the ordered import step.

### Q3 : Are an open-useful platform and a closed-hard platform isomers?

- Fig 12 :117: **"isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."**
- Fig 12 :124: **"functional group. A substructure whose presence fixes the outcome. The group says what kind; the qualifier says which member."**
- Reading A (as the business literature usually states it): an open interoperable protocol and a closed integrated platform share very few atoms. One has interoperable access, multiple providers, and cross-side subsidisation; the other has proprietary control, single-provider integration, and an installed base. Under Fig 12's definition they are **not isomers**; they are two members of a functional group, "platform that intermediates two sides", with different atoms in the slots.
- Reading B (the stronger, checkable form): hold the full atom set fixed, `{users, complementors, contributions, closure, head start, user-value ground, entry ground}`, and vary only the bonds: open interoperability uses a symmetric disjunction (`∥`, *"either suffices; not both required"*, Fig 12 :61) between providers, while closed integration uses an ordered production or sequence (`→`/`⇒`, Fig 12 :71, :76) in which one provider's contribution feeds the next. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. That **is** an isomer claim under Fig 12 :117.
- **Falsifier, written before the run:** if either platform type can be written without an atom the other requires (for example, the closed platform with no complementor side, or the open platform with no shared value ground), the isomer claim fails and the functional-group reading stands. This is the same fixed-atom condition the BUS-0014 and BUS-0015 solutions name as a live strain: it is a condition on the claim, not a hidden assumption.

### Q4 : What order is the union?

- Fig 9 :54: **"And when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again."** Fig 9 :57: **"What remains is smaller and it is also categorically different: a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either."**
- Usefulness U and challenge-hardness H are two conditions over overlapping atoms. A platform may be U-only (useful, easy to challenge: an open standard), H-only (hard to challenge, no longer useful: a locked-in declining incumbent), both, or neither. The four cases are not points on one scale.
- Fig 12 :125-126: **"A composition costs in two currencies, and they do not add."** **"Bits price the description. Actions price finding out whether it holds."** A claim about usefulness and a claim about defensibility are priced in different currencies; a single "platform strength" score that sums them is the error this names. Keep the verdicts separate.
- **Falsifier:** an instrument that measures both conditions in one currency and predicts, prospectively and per platform, which named platform will lose usefulness and which will lose defensibility next. If such a single-currency measure exists, the union reading is unnecessary.

### Q5 : Is the head start a settled molecule or a recording?

- Fig 4 :11: **"a head start, never a recording"**; Fig 4 :25: **"A recording carried upward looks like knowledge and is a description of one occasion."**; Fig 12 :104: **"A settled molecule becomes an atom for whatever composes over it."**
- An installed base is a head start only when it lowers the next participant's search cost (Fig 4 :12, *"it makes the next agent's search cheaper"*). It becomes a recording when the platform replays a past success (the old growth hack, the old subsidy) in a changed habitat. A methodology of growth, verified against the ground and compressed to essentials, can cross as a molecule and become an atom at the population level; a screenshot of one occasion cannot.
- **Falsifier:** a platform whose historical growth playbook keeps producing ground-verified value in a changed habitat with no re-derivation at all, where the playbook is demonstrably a recording of one occasion rather than a reapplicable method. That would break the method/recording distinction here.

## CONCLUSION

The network effect is a **cycle**, not a chain, whose sign is decided by participant valence relative to the residual and which an instrument must measure (Q1). Wall-moving is ordered: describe the residual, find a differing closure, gate and shadow-test the import, then let the ground narrow (Q2). An open-useful and a closed-hard platform are **isomers only over a fixed shared atom set**; as usually described they are a functional group with different atoms (Q3). Usefulness and challenge-hardness form a **union** with two currencies that do not add (Q4). A head start is a method-derived head start, not a recording, and the distinction is falsifiable (Q5). Nothing here returns a critical mass.
