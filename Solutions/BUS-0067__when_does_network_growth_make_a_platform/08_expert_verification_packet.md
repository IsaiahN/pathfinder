# Expert verification packet : the Tether on network growth and platform challenge

**What this is:** a claim in a form a platform-strategy researcher or industrial-organisation economist can check. Mode, derivation, and refuters are stated before the conclusion, so a reader knows what to look for and what would settle it.

**Mode: `specified` on the platform claims, `general` on the structural shape, `gap` on the numerical threshold. Not `grounded`.** No market measurement is claimed. This is a shape and a route, not a result. It is a simulated derivation checked for internal consistency against the figures, not a ground reading.

## 1. The claim, in one paragraph

Network growth and competitive entrenchment are two different conditions with different readings of the market anchor, so the question is a union, not one dial. Growth makes a platform more useful where added participants stay in contact with the user residual as regenerating contributors; the effect is a cycle whose sign decides whether it closes, and the same participant can help matching while hurting attention, so a pooled mean hides both signs. Growth makes a platform harder to challenge where the installed base forms a closure that a challenger can move only by importing from a frame whose closure differs, carried by a head start that lowers the next search cost without proving the method transferred. Entrenchment can grow while ground contact decays, because a chain of mirrors compounds agreement without adding evidence. The framework returns the shape, the conditions, and refuters written in advance; it does not return a critical mass, a tipping number, or a winning design.

## 2. Three checkable sub-claims

### C1 : framework-internal, but with a direct platform-facing test

- **Statement:** participant valence is relative to the residual, so the sign of a network effect is not a property of size; the feedback is a cycle, not a chain.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 11 :49 : *"An actor's valence is relative to the residual, not a property it carries."* Fig 11 :50 : *"The same actor is a benefactor to one residual and a malfactor to another, so mutualism and parasitism are relations rather than kinds."* Fig 12 :121 : *"cycle. The output re-enters as input. The sign of the loop decides whether it closes, and a chain cannot close by construction."* Fig 12 :119 : *"catalyst. A part that survives its own use. Spent and regenerating parts behave differently across uses, and nothing records which a term is."*
- **What to check:** is there any platform where a marginal user cohort increases value on one axis (matches, content) and decreases it on another (attention, moderation load, congestion) as size grows? If yes, valence is residual-relative in the platform domain and the pooled "network effect" is the wrong object.
- **Refuter:** a platform on which every added user strictly raises every existing user's value at all observed sizes with no offsetting cost in any residual; or a strictly linear, state-independent value response to `N` at all observed sizes. Either falsifies the cycle-plus-valence claim.

### C2 : economics-facing; checkable against the entry and tipping literature

- **Statement:** hardness to challenge is a wall moved only by contact with a differing closure; minting inside a challenger's own library cannot move it. Size is a head start, not a generator.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 6 :23 : *"No frame escapes its own closure. The bound moves only on contact with a frame whose closure differs."* Fig 6 :21 : *"MINT composes inside the closure and can never add an atom."* Fig 8 :32 : *"Closure is idempotent. Composing inside a closed set never leaves it, so no search, however long, adds an atom. This one is definitional: it is what closure means."* Fig 4 :22 : *"Only methods cross up. Only head starts come down."*
- **What to check:** in documented entry cases against a large incumbent, did the decisive change come from a differing closure (adjacent market, new technology, new user segment, interoperability rule) or from the entrant's own internal optimization? Does the incumbent's size advantage provide a head start (lower search cost for users) without proving that its growth method transfers?
- **Refuter:** a challenger that displaces a large incumbent purely by internal composition, with no new atom and no contact with a differing closure; or a platform whose advantage survives removal of its head start while its method is held constant; or a head start that alone produces new ground-verified value with no method crossing (breaks Fig 4 :22, :30).

### C3 : framework-internal derivation of the union

- **Statement:** usefulness and challenge-hardness are two conditions with different anchors; a single "platform strength" score that sums them is a pooled reading. Over a fixed shared atom set, an open-useful and a closed-hard platform are isomers; as usually described they share only a functional group.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 9 :55 : *"A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones."* Fig 9 :57 : *"What remains is smaller and it is also categorically different: a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either."* Fig 12 :117 : *"isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."* Fig 12 :124 : *"functional group. A substructure whose presence fixes the outcome. The group says what kind; the qualifier says which member."* Fig 12 :125 : *"A composition costs in two currencies, and they do not add."*
- **Refuter:** a single size lever that raises usefulness and entrenchment across all regimes with no categorically different residue; or a single-currency instrument that predicts prospectively and per platform which named platform loses usefulness and which loses defensibility next. Either would collapse the union into a partition. Also: either platform type written without an atom the other requires kills the isomer claim and leaves the functional-group claim.

## 3. What is explicitly not claimed

- Not that growth is always good; valence is residual-relative (Fig 11 :49).
- Not that lock-in or tipping is inevitable; the world literature records both tipping and the exhaustion of scale benefits above a small critical mass, and the conflict is kept as evidence for the union.
- Not that data or an installed base is automatically a moat; a wall claim needs a differing closure and a provenanced import (Fig 8 :50-52).
- Not that a size-value correlation establishes causation; correlation is symmetric, the claim is ordered (Fig 2 :39-40; Fig 12 :87).
- Not a theory of everything, no final room (Fig 7 :6, *"the limit: no last room"*).
- Not `grounded`. Nothing here is asserted as settled by a market measurement.
- Not a critical mass, tipping number, subsidy split, or winning design. That is a declared `gap` (Fig 9 :32, Fig 13 :134).

## 5. What would settle it

A source-discriminating instrument, not a further argument. From *The Hard Problem is About Source*: an instrument that reads green *"cannot comment on the source"*; the spectrometer resolved it. Here:

1. A catalyst / spent-part discriminator that reads, per cohort and per residual, whether a marginal participant regenerates or is spent. This names the cycle's sign.
2. A closure-crossing record with provenance showing whether a challenger's decisive atom came from a differing closure or was already in its own library (Fig 8 :50-52).
3. A measured head-start round trip `R_T` between the incumbent's growth method at the population level and its concretisation in a new habitat (Fig 4 :30).
4. Exogenous variation in `N` with a declared source gate, so the arrow between size and value can be chosen (Fig 2 :39-40).
5. Any of the eight refuters in section 2 and `01` section 4.
