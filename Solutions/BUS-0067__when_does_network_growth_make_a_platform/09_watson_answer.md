## QUESTION

When does network growth make a platform more useful, and when does it make the platform harder to challenge? Restated as an effect: for which user slots does adding a participant shrink `R = |Γ(b,a) − o′|`, and for which platform slots does a growing installed base raise the viability gap of a challenger? `R` is indexed per slot and never aggregated (Fig 1 :19). The market is the named anchor (Fig 2 :29), read two ways. Option A treats it as one dial with two directions and reports a single "network effect". Option B treats it as a union of two conditions with different anchors and returns two verdicts kept separate. A leads to a pooled score that hides which users gain, which lose, and which challengers are kept out; B leads to a checkable shape, written refuters, and an open threshold.

## DOMAIN CENSUS

R by arity, symmetry and scale, then one line per domain with actors / currency / anchor, partition or union, and order by dependency.

- **R (the residual).** Two families of slot. Arity: platform Γ, action `a`, outcome `o′`, indexed by user and by platform and ordered in time; carries an ordered bond and a comparison bond, not a symmetric one. Symmetry: adding a participant is not reversible, and the marginal effect depends on the current state, which is a threshold signature. Scale: single user, population of users, population of platforms; the membrane between scales is lossy. Fig 9 :57 names the split: a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either.
- **D1 User-value habitat.** Actors: users, content, complementors, platform Γ. Currency: residual reduction per user. Anchor: realized user value at the market, Fig 2 :29. Status: admitted.
- **D2 Competitive field.** Actors: incumbent, challengers, entrants, multi-homing users. Currency: reach and closure height. Anchor: entry, survival, and tipping at the market, Fig 2 :29. Status: admitted.
- **D3 Composition of the effect.** Actors: the network relation, catalysts, cycles, settled molecules. Currency: contact and closure, the sign of a loop. Anchor: settlement against the ground. Status: admitted as a compound with D1 and D2.
- **D4 The channel.** Actors: platform instruments, metrics, percepts. Currency: contact. Anchor: the ground, which does not decay. Status: admitted.
- **X1 "Network effects" as a free-floating property of size.** No anchor independent of the market verdict. Not a domain yet; excluded before figure lines are applied.
- **X2 "Platform" as a legal or brand category.** Already absorbed into D1 and D2.

Union, not partition: D1/D3 and D2/D4 overlap, each overlap is a compound with its own loop (Fig 9 :54-57). Dependency order: perception, vocabulary, objective, planning, learn and carry (Fig 3 :20). Strain test: applying Fig 13's six physical terms to a user network only fits with a strain, so Fig 13 is confined to the honest-stop role (S1 in `01` section 6). No unresolved strain after re-census apart from the fixed-atom condition on the isomer claim, which is named in `06` Q3.

## VERDICT (from the figures)

The shape survives. Network growth makes a platform more useful where added participants stay in contact with the user residual as regenerating contributors, because the effect is a cycle whose sign decides whether it closes and because valence is residual-relative rather than a property of size. It makes a platform harder to challenge where the installed base forms a closure that a challenger can move only by importing from a differing closure, carried by a head start that lowers the next search cost without proving the method transferred. Entrenchment can grow while ground contact decays, because a chain of mirrors compounds agreement without adding evidence. The two halves are a union with different anchors and two currencies that do not add; their verdicts are kept separate and never averaged. No figure line returns a critical mass, tipping number, or winning design, so that is a declared `gap`, unreachable at this budget rather than silence. Mode `specified` on the platform claims, `general` on the structural shape, not `grounded`. The business/economics sources are one witness; the ecology, contagion, and network-science sources corroborate the mechanism shape only.

- **cost if wrong:** misdirected diagnosis and wasted intervention. A platform failing the usefulness condition would be sold more user-acquisition spend, while a platform failing the challenge condition would be told to open its API, and a single lever would be applied to a union that needs two. In outreach terms, an expert's twenty minutes would be spent refuting a shape that was never the right shape. The cost is real but bounded and largely reversible when the two verdicts are recorded separately with their refuters.
- **undoable:** yes. No term is deleted if the claim fails; a refutation is added and the reach falls because something was added rather than deleted. The shape and its eight falsifiers are recorded in a provenance trail so a later reader can locate the error (Fig 6 :42-43, Fig 10 :62-64). A sent claim can be superseded by the corrected packet, and no primitive has to be withdrawn.
- **reversed by:** a ground reading, not agreement. A catalyst / spent-part discriminator that names the cycle's sign per cohort and per residual; a provenanced record showing whether a challenger's decisive atom came from a differing closure or was already in its own library; a measured head-start round trip `R_T`; exogenous variation in size with a declared source gate that chooses the arrow; or any of the eight pre-registered refuters in `01` section 4. A source with a genuinely different evidence pool showing that per-slot value is fully predicted by a single size variable with no condition dependence would also reverse it. Until one of these fires, the verdict stands as `candidate`.
