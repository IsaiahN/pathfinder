# Outreach draft: check the claim before you decide whether the framing is worth reading

**Use:** one-to-one, or as a cover note attached to a submission through the public venues in `03_public_contact_points.csv`. **Not** for bulk send.

**Hard precondition:** do not send until the shape claim and its refuter in `01_figure_grounded_audit.md` section 4 are intact and unchanged. The offer collapses on contact if the refuter is edited after the verdict is recorded.

---

**Subject:** One question about early-warning signals: what would you count as a failure here?

> Hi [name],
>
> I am working on a narrow question in tipping-points research and would like your verdict on one output before I show you any of the framework behind it. I am not asking you to agree with anything, and I am not asking you to read the theory first. If the output does not survive, there is nothing else to look at.
>
> **The output (`specified` on the shape, `gap` on the rate):**
>
> 1. A tipping is a threshold comparison, and the warning is a sequence that precedes it. These are different relations and should be scored separately.
> 2. The process that makes a crossing possible also puts variation into the observable. The amplifying remainder and the detail discarded at a crossing reappear as noise in the same record. A variance or autocorrelation rise therefore carries information *and* is ambiguous at source.
> 3. Early warning is a chain of four links: there is a real threshold; the monitoring channel preserves the local signal; the indicator reads it without borrowing its neighbours' evidence; a decision uses it under declared costs. These are separate verdicts. A success in one does not repair a failure in another.
> 4. Variance, lag-1 autocorrelation, skewness and flickering all descend from the same slowing-down mechanism. Their agreement is one evidence pool talking, not independent corroboration.
> 5. I am **not** claiming a universal false-alarm rate or miss rate, and I am **not** naming one indicator as the reliable one for a given system. I could not derive either from the framework. I record that as a `gap`, not an estimate.
>
> **What would refute this, written down before I sent it:**
>
> - A figure line, from a genuine re-census, that directly governs a universal detection rate or selects the indicator for a named system.
> - Two indicators shown to draw on independent evidence rather than a common slowing-down variable, whose agreement predicts crossings prospectively at declared error rates on at least two low-overlap real systems.
> - A real monitoring setup that provably preserves slot-level variation and all transients, checked against the ground, where detection still fails for a reason the four-link chain does not cover; or a genuinely closed system in which no warning proves no tipping.
> - A measured system in which the precursor's variation is demonstrably not the discarded detail of a crossing.
>
> I wrote these before writing the conclusion. A one-line "this fails because..." is more useful to me than agreement. If you would rather not engage, no reply is needed.
>
> **What I am not asking:** I am not asking you to trust a framework, to endorse a claim that early warnings are unreliable, or to join a list. I am asking what you would expect to see if the shape claim were true, and what would make you reject it. I will record your verdict on this stage before showing any theory, and I will not count later agreement as corroboration.
>
> **If it survives, and only then:** the sources are below. The figures, not my summary of them, are the authority.
> - Scheffer et al., "Early-warning signals for critical transitions", Nature 461, 53-59 (2009): https://www.nature.com/articles/nature08227
> - Yi and Seo, "Early warning of road icing ... without consistent critical slowing down" (2026): https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.33528
> - Ditlevsen and Ditlevsen, "Early Warning Signals Can Vanish or Amplify" (2026): https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.01164
>
> **If you want to use the machinery:** it is a method for deriving the shape of an answer and a refuter before you run a check. Run it on a problem you know well and can check against a ground. Write down what would refute your result before you run it, and state the mode of what you get. A reported failure is more useful to me than a reported success.
>
> Thanks for reading this far.
> [name]
