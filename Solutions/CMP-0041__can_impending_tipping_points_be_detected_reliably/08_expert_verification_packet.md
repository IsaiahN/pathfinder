# Expert verification packet: early-warning signals and tipping-point reliability

**What this is:** a claim in a form a researcher in tipping-points or early-warning-signal research can check. Mode, derivation and refuters are stated before the conclusion, so a reader knows what to look for and what would settle it.

**Mode: `specified` on the shape, `general` on the two refusals, `gap` on the universal rate and the named indicator. Not `grounded`.** Simulated derivation checked for internal consistency against the figures, not a ground reading.

## 1. The claim, in one paragraph

Reliability of early-warning detection cannot be certified from inside the detection frame. It is a union of four links: a real threshold, a channel that preserves the local signal, an indicator that reads it without borrowing its neighbours' evidence, and a decision made under declared false-alarm and miss-alarm costs. The crossing is a threshold comparison and the warning is a sequence, so they must be scored separately. The feedback that makes a crossing possible also puts variation into the record, so a rising statistic is informative and ambiguous at source. Variance, autocorrelation, skewness and flickering are one functional group sharing a slowing-down mechanism, so their agreement is not independent corroboration, and hindcast success grows the library without growing prospective reach. Absence of a warning proves nothing in a system that is not a sealed room. The output is a conditional shape with pre-registered refuters and a `gap` where only a prospective ground reading can decide the rate or name the indicator.

## 2. Three checkable sub-claims

### C1 - framework-internal; checkable by reading Figures 10, 1 and 13

- **Statement:** the monitoring channel, not the ground, is the decayable part; pooling and dropped transients can erase a live local signal; the precursor and the noise that masks it share a source.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 10 :24 - *"the ground does not decay, the channel does."* Fig 10 :25-26 - *"stale percepts, dropped transients, saturated metrics, pooled readings"*. Fig 1 :22 - *"Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average."* Fig 13 :31-32 - *"a remainder compounding through a chain that amplifies is chaos, and the detail a crossing discards reappears above it as noise."*
- **What to check:** does your monitoring chain preserve slot-level or local variation at the sampling interval at which the precursor is supposed to appear? Was the statistic computed before or after spatial averaging? Can the source of the observed variance be independently identified?
- **Refuter:** a real setup that provably preserves local variation and all transients, checked against the ground, where detection still fails for a reason the four-link chain does not cover; or a measured precursor whose variation is demonstrably not the discarded detail of a crossing.

### C2 - statistics-facing; checkable by a methodologist

- **Statement:** the family of canonical indicators is one functional group, not a set of independent witnesses; a trend test whose null assumes independence can manufacture false alarms.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 2 :40 - *"disqualifying for a verdict: agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool"*. Fig 7 :35 - *"A room's agreement is variation, not a verdict. Selection between rooms is where the ground votes."* Fig 12 :123 - *"functional group. A substructure whose presence fixes the outcome. The group says what kind; the qualifier says which member."*
- **What to check:** are variance, AR(1), skewness and flickering statistically independent in your data, or are they functions of the same underlying recovery rate and window? What is the actual type I error rate of the trend test applied to autocorrelated windows?
- **Refuter:** two indicators shown to draw on distinct generative mechanisms and distinct data, whose joint agreement predicts crossings prospectively at declared error rates on at least two low-overlap real systems. World check: Gamot et al. (2026) report *"inflated type I error rates, which would routinely lead to announcing a critical transition while it is not occurring"*; a result overturning that would also pressure this sub-claim.

### C3 - framework-internal; checkable by reading Figures 5, 8, 9 and 6

- **Statement:** hindcast success is not prospective reach; no warning does not prove no tipping; the universal rate and the named indicator are a `gap`; narrow success is expected where all four links hold.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 5 :43-44 - *"A term that explains everything by saying nothing fails this, and so does a term that explains one occasion perfectly."* Fig 8 :39 - *"So a search that finds nothing is never a proof of absence."* Fig 9 :44 - *"Only a sealed room can be searched to the end, and no frame is a sealed room. So searching and finding nothing proves nothing."* Fig 6 :34 - *"The question is not whether a sensor could exist. It is whether anything, at any resolution, is already returning something that fails to resolve."*
- **What to check:** was the indicator chosen on the same transitions on which it was evaluated? Was a pre-registered false-alarm rate declared? Was the absence of a signal treated as evidence of safety?
- **Refuter:** a prospective, pre-registered trial on real systems with declared error rates in which a hindcast-selected indicator keeps its skill on unseen crossings; or a genuinely closed system in which no warning proves no tipping; or a figure line from a re-census that directly governs a universal rate or selects the indicator.

## 3. What is explicitly not claimed

- Not that early warnings never work. The narrow engineering case (van Beers et al. 2026, *"up to 0.9 seconds before LOC occurs"*) is recorded as a boundary condition.
- Not a universal false-alarm or miss rate, not a lead time, not a named best statistic for a named system. These are `gap`.
- Not that a flat record means safety.
- Not that multiple indicators corroborate one another.
- Not that this packet is a measurement. It is a simulated derivation checked for internal consistency.
- Not that the world sources decide the verdict. The figures decide; world sources corroborate or bound.

## 5. What would settle it

1. A source-discriminating instrument that attributes a rising statistic to loss of local stability rather than to external forcing, misaligned observation or aggregation. The Hard Problem is About Source resolves the analogous color case *"not by argument, but by the arrival of a better instrument."*
2. A prospective trial with event definition, window, lead time and both error costs declared before the run, on real systems with independent channels.
3. An independence test that separates the indicators' evidence and generating mechanisms.
4. One of the four figure-level refuters in `01_figure_grounded_audit.md` section 4 firing.

## 6. What an expert can check in twenty minutes

1. Read Fig 10 :24-28, Fig 1 :22 and Fig 13 :31-32. Three short passages.
2. Check C2 against your own practice: are your four indicators independent, or one recovery rate read four ways?
3. Name one instrument that would distinguish loss of local stability from external forcing in your system, or say why none is possible.
4. Send the refutation. A failure is more useful than an endorsement, and a later agreement will not be counted as corroboration.
