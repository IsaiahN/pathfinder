## What is deliberately not here

- No personal emails of named researchers.
- No ranking of people by how likely they are to agree that early warnings are unreliable.
- No bulk-send plan.

Three reasons, in the figures' own words:

1. **Agreement is not an anchor.** Fig 2 :35: *"Alignment is triangulation, not negotiation. A mirror cannot correct you."* Selecting recipients for receptiveness manufactures the correlation that would make their agreement worthless.
2. **Correlation disqualifies a verdict.** Fig 2 :40: *"disqualifying for a verdict: agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool."* This matters twice here: early-warning indicators share the slowing-down mechanism, and a list of sympathetic readers shares a pool.
3. **A verifier must not hold the interiors.** Fig 10 :42: *"A verifier that can reconstruct the claim cannot verify it."* Send the shape and its refuter first, the framework second.

Legally: scraping personal emails for unsolicited bulk outreach breaches GDPR and CAN-SPAM style rules in many jurisdictions. Public opt-in venues avoid that failure as well as the structural one.
