# Outreach draft: check the residual before you decide about the architecture claim

**Use:** one-to-one, or as a cover note attached to a submission through a public venue in `03_public_contact_points.csv`. **Not** for bulk send.

**Hard precondition:** do not send until the per-domain findings in `01_figure_grounded_audit.md` are stated with their conditions attached. A claim that strips off "on a named habitat with a non-updating anchor" will fail on contact.

---

**Subject:** One conditional claim about small specialist vs large general systems, with a refuter written in advance

> Hi [name],
>
> I have one narrow output to check. I am not asking you to read the framework first and I am not asking you to agree. If the output fails, there is nothing else to look at.
>
> **The shape claim, `specified`, not `grounded`:** "small specialized systems outperform large general-purpose models" is not one question. It is a union of two axes (reliability and adaptability) over two overlapping conditions (stable habitat and habitat change). Reliability is measured against a fixed anchor; adaptability is measured across a change of habitat. Those are two currencies and do not add.
>
> What the derivation predicts, with its condition attached: on a fixed habitat with an anchor that does not update when a model is tuned on it, a specialist in contact with a different frame can reach a term that a wide general closure cannot derive from its own library. This is not a claim that small beats large. Parameter count is a quantity the frame produces and cannot use to score itself. An agent-only improvement that does not change contact changes nothing, whichever direction it goes.
>
> **What I am not claiming:** that every specialist beats every general model; that a static benchmark score is a capability measurement; that reliability and adaptability can be averaged; that scaling cannot help; or that this derivation settles a live empirical dispute. It is a simulated derivation checked for internal consistency, not a ground reading.
>
> **What would refute it, written down before this message:**
> 1. On a dynamic, provably non-leaked anchor, with size varied independently of contact, the general model matches or beats the specialist on reliability in the specialist's own habitat at equal contact.
> 2. A specialist moved to a new habitat keeps its adaptability with no import channel and no re-enumeration of contact.
> 3. The two axes move together under one intervention with no interaction, so the union is actually a partition.
>
> A one-line "this fails because..." is more useful to me than agreement. If you would rather not engage, no reply is needed.
>
> **If it survives, and only then:** the sources are below. The figure text is the authority, not my summary of it.

## The two-stage offer

1. **Stage 1.** Send the residual, the two axes kept separate, the habitat and anchor requirement, and the three refuters. Ask: what would you count as a failure here? Record the verdict before showing any theory.
2. **Stage 2, opt-in only.** If they are curious, offer the three links. Tell them the figures outrank the summary. Their later agreement is never pooled as corroboration (Fig 2 :40).
3. **Stage 3.** Offer the formula as a method to reuse, with the instruction that each user names their own ground and writes their own refuter first.
