## What an answer would look like (binding the sought-for shape as an ordinary term)

The formula's "Direction" rule says a frame cannot check the direction of its own question, so the sought-for shape must be written down, bound, and allowed to accrue a residual. The shape bound here is:

> For a named habitat H and a non-updating anchor A, and for axis g in {reliability, adaptability}, an architecture X outperforms Y iff the residual `R_X(H,A,g)` is smaller than `R_Y(H,A,g)` at equal contact with the ground.

This is a shape, not a verdict. It is refusable: name H and A, run the measurement, and watch whether the inequality holds. A result without H and A named is not an answer to this question.

## What is deliberately not here

- No personal emails of named individuals.
- No ranking of people by how likely they are to agree with the conclusion.
- No bulk-send plan.
- No claim that a simulated derivation settles a live empirical dispute.
- No single yes or no verdict on the bundled question, because the figures say the bundle must be split before it is searched (Fig 9 :5).

Three figure-grounded reasons for the outreach restrictions:

1. Fig 2 :35 "Alignment is triangulation, not negotiation. A mirror cannot correct you." Selecting recipients for agreement manufactures the correlation that disqualifies a verdict.
2. Fig 10 :42 "A verifier that can reconstruct the claim cannot verify it." The first contact carries the residual and its refuter, not the framework's interiors.
3. Fig 9 :60 "Use filters for the budget and witnesses for the verdict. Never let a filter hand you a verdict." A public venue is a way to get a claim tried, not a source of endorsement.
