**What this is:** a claim in a form a specialist can check. Mode, derivation and refuters are stated before the conclusion, so a reader knows what to look for and what would settle it.

**Mode: `specified` on the structural claims, `general` on the meta-shape, `gap` on the absolute ranking. Not `grounded`.** No controlled comparison is claimed. This is a simulated derivation checked for internal consistency against the figures.

## 1. The claim, in one paragraph

The question "can smaller specialized systems outperform large general-purpose models" is a union of two axes (reliability, adaptability) over overlapping conditions, and the axes are two currencies that do not add. Size is frame-internal and cannot decide the comparison. The figure-grounded shape is: on a fixed habitat with a non-updating anchor, a specialist in contact with a different closure can reach a term a wide closure cannot derive from its own library; across a habitat change, maintained contact wins and agent-only scaling changes nothing. Scaling searches a closure more efficiently but adds no atom, and it cannot repair a broken objective or vocabulary link. The absolute ranking is a `gap` until a habitat and an anchor are named and the two axes are measured separately. Three falsifiers are written below, before the conclusion.

## 2. Three checkable sub-claims

### C1: framework-internal, checkable by reading the figure text

- **Statement:** size is not a deciding variable. The kernel is unchanged with scale; only actors, currency and anchor change.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 4 :20 "Only the actors, the currency and the anchor change." Also Fig 1 :27 "They are frame-internal. A frame cannot score itself with a quantity it also produces."
- **Refuter:** a derivation from the figures showing that changing size changes the loop or the anchor rather than only the actors and currency.

### C2: framework-internal, checkable by reading Figure 8 and Figure 6

- **Statement:** on a fixed habitat with a non-updating anchor, a narrow closure with a live import can reach a term that a wide closure cannot derive; a wide closure searched longer does not add an atom.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 8 :32 "Closure is idempotent. Composing inside a closed set never leaves it, so no search, however long, adds an atom." Fig 6 :23 "No frame escapes its own closure. The bound moves only on contact with a frame whose closure differs." Anchor condition: Fig 2 :29 "An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks."
- **Refuter:** on a dynamic, provably non-leaked anchor with size varied independently of contact, the general model matches or beats the specialist in the specialist's own habitat at equal contact.

### C3: framework-internal, checkable against the ecology analogue and the habitat law

- **Statement:** adaptation across a habitat change depends on maintained contact, not on agent-only improvement; the ecology frame supplies a matching conditional shape, not a magnitude.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 11 :40 "A system cannot be made more capable by improving the agent in isolation. An improvement that does not change contact changes nothing, however much it improves." Fig 11 :70 "A test harness is a substituted habitat. Which is why a synthetic solve proves wiring and never capability." Import rule: Fig 8 :51 "Convergent derivation and adopted import look identical in the contents. Only the record of where each came from separates them."
- **Refuter:** a specialist moved to a new habitat retains adaptability without an import channel, re-enumeration of contact, or a new instrument. Fig 11 :72 itself states that such an observation would refute the capability claim.

## 3. What is explicitly not claimed

- Not that small beats large, or that large beats small. Size is excluded as a deciding variable.
- Not that every specialist beats every general model; the surplus is conditional on differing closures and an anchor that holds.
- Not that reliability and adaptability can be averaged. Fig 12 :130 "Summing them into one figure is the error this names, not the fix for it."
- Not that scaling never helps, and not that a static benchmark win proves a capability (Fig 11 :70).
- Not grounded. No controlled comparison was run by this derivation.
- Not that the ecology sources are independent witnesses. They share one evidence pool and are recorded as one witness in `10_source_ledger.csv`.
