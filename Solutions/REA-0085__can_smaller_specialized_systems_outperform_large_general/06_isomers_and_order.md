# Supporting derivation: union, order, and the specialist/generalist analogue

**Mode: `specified` on the structural result, `general` on the meta-shape.** This is a simulated derivation checked against the figures, not a ground reading (see `05_what_shape_means.md` §6).

## RESIDUAL

The question joins two axes and two conditions. Determine (1) whether the join is a partition or a union, (2) what dependency order the four cells must be evaluated in, and (3) what the ecology specialist/generalist analogue does and does not license as an import.

## DOMAIN CENSUS

- Atoms held for this derivation: one loop (perceive, route, mint, accept, settle, promote, repeat), one closure per architecture, an anchor, a habitat, and two currencies (description cost in bits, test cost in actions).
- Bonds held: → sequence, ⋛ comparison, − subtraction, + conjunction. No new primitive is imported without a source gate.
- Frames in view: the architecture frame (models, size, objective), the evaluation frame (benchmarks, held-out sets), the habitat frame (deployment conditions), and the ecology frame (resource axes, disturbance regime). The ecology frame's closure differs from the ML frame's, so a union surplus may exist, but it must be described in effect terms before it is used (Fig 9 :28).

## DERIVATION

### Q1: Partition or union?

Fig 9 :5: "are not one hard question but several well-formed ones. Split it rather than search."
Fig 9 :55: "A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones."

The conditions overlap: a large model can be fine-tuned narrowly, and a small model can be trained broadly. So "small specialist" and "large general" are not disjoint classes. The axes are also not disjoint in practice, because a system can be reliable by being narrow and adaptable only by paying a retargeting cost. Fig 12 :125: "A composition costs in two currencies, and they do not add."

**Result:** union, not partition. Unbundle before evaluating. Fig 9 :56: "A single lever clears the region where only one condition holds and leaves the intersections standing, because the second condition still binds." A single headline benchmark score is that single lever; it clears the simple cells and leaves the compound standing.

**Falsifier:** one intervention that moves both axes together with no interaction term, and two system descriptions that are disjoint by construction. Then the union is a partition and this result fails.

### Q2: What is the dependency order?

Fig 3 :20: "The chain is ordered by dependency, not by importance."
Fig 3 :19: "Optimising a link that already works cannot rescue one that does not."

Order by what each step consumes:

1. **Describe R in effect terms** (arity, symmetry, scale). Nothing can be chosen before this; Fig 9 :28.
2. **Name the anchor and demonstrate it does not update** (Fig 2 :29). Without this, both reliability and adaptability readings are mirror readings.
3. **Enumerate the habitat** (Fig 11 :43, "The habitat is enumerated, never composed"). List actors, conditions and relations in contact with the residual before ranking architectures.
4. **Settle reliability on the fixed habitat** (first-order loop closes within one scale).
5. **Then test adaptability across a habitat change** (second-order, crosses scales with `R_T` as its error signal, per `THE_FORMULA.md` step 6).
6. **Only then compare architectures.**

Effort spent at step 6 before steps 2 and 3 lands above the break (Fig 3 :23: "A step whose input never arrives cannot be diagnosed, only its predecessor can").

### Q3: Are the two architectures isomers?

Fig 12 :117: "isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."
Fig 12 :124: "functional group. A substructure whose presence fixes the outcome. The group says what kind; the qualifier says which member."

If a small specialist and a large general model are described only by their shared ingredients (data, objective, compute, loop), the ingredient list cannot distinguish them. What distinguishes them is the bond arrangement: which frame supplies the imported atom, which anchor is ranged against, and which habitat is maintained. Over the shared atom set they are therefore **isomers in the figure's sense**, not different substances by ingredient.

If, instead, one system requires an atom the other does not (a domain corpus and instrument the general model cannot derive, or a breadth of contact the specialist has substituted away), then they are not isomers; they are two members of the same functional group with different atoms in the slots.

**Which reading applies depends on the habitat, so it cannot be decided from the question as posed.** This is a second reason for the `gap` recorded in `01` F9.

**Falsifier:** a proof that the two architectures are built from disjoint atom sets with no shared loop. Then neither isomer nor functional-group language applies and this derivation is void.

### Q4: What does the ecology analogue import, and at what price?

Fig 8 :51: "Convergent derivation and adopted import look identical in the contents. Only the record of where each came from separates them."

The ecology frame already holds a shape matching the residual: specialists are favoured in stable or homogeneous conditions, generalists under variability and disturbance (Peers et al., Fried et al., Morris et al., all opened this session). Cast in effect terms, that shape says: *narrow contact wins where the contact is stable and the atom is importable from a maintained frame; broad contact wins where the habitat changes*.

Fig 6 :23: "No frame escapes its own closure. The bound moves only on contact with a frame whose closure differs." Fig 8 :45: "Two frames together reach what neither reaches alone."

**Source gate:** the three ecology papers were opened and are recorded with author, venue, year and quote in `10_source_ledger.csv`. **Shadow test:** they explain a gap the ML frame does not explain on its own, namely why specialist wins cluster in stable, narrow tasks and generalist wins cluster under distribution change. **Debit:** all three papers share one evidence pool (ecology specialist/generalist theory), so they count as one witness, not three. They corroborate a shape, never a magnitude, and they cannot settle an ML measurement.

Peers et al. add the correction that the classic peak-performance trade-off often failed to appear on their measured axes. That correction is imported too: the analogue licenses a conditional ordering, not a claim about how much better either architecture is.

## CONCLUSION

The join is a union. Dependency order runs: residual description, anchor, habitat, reliability, adaptability, architecture. Under a shared atom set the two architectures are isomers by bond arrangement; where the atom sets differ they are functional-group members. The ecology analogue imports a conditional shape at the price of one witness and no magnitude. Every one of these results is `specified` or `general`, none is `grounded`.
