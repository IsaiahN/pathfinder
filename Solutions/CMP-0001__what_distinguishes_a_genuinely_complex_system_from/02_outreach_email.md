**Use:** one-to-one, or as a cover letter attached to a submission through the public venues in `03_public_contact_points.csv`. **Not** for bulk send.

**Hard precondition:** do not send until you have checked the shape claim yourself against a system you know well. If the output does not survive, there is nothing else to look at.

---

**Subject:** One checkable output on complex versus complicated systems

> Hi [name],
>
> I have one specific output I would like you to check against a system you already know. I am not asking you to read a theory first, and I am not asking you to agree with me. If the output does not survive, there is nothing else to look at.
>
> **The output (`specified`, not `grounded`).** The folk separator, how many parts a system has, does not survive the figures. A system's part list records the ingredients and drops the arrangement. What separates a genuinely complex system from a merely complicated one is the arrangement: the number and kind of bonds, whether a loop closes, whether a residual compounds instead of washing out, and whether settled wholes become parts at a higher level. A merely complicated system is the degenerate case: many parts, one bond type, no branching, no closing loop, one level, its behaviour recoverable from its ingredient list. A jet engine is a large chain. An ant colony is not.
>
> This is a shape and a check list, not a number. I am explicitly not claiming a single complexity scalar, and I am explicitly not claiming that chaos is the separator. A published world source (Wiesner and Ladyman, 2020) notes that chaos and complexity are two distinct phenomena, which forced me to move chaos from the criterion to a companion of rich arrangement. I recorded that correction rather than hiding it.
>
> **What would refute the output:**
> 1. a one-bond, acyclic, one-level chain that shows the same non-decomposable, per-slot compounding behaviour as a multi-bond cyclic recursive system under the same external ground;
> 2. two systems with identical part inventories but different bonds and levels that behave identically under every available ground instrument;
> 3. a candidate "complex" system whose apparent higher levels turn out to be composed over unsettled terms, so the recursion is fake;
> 4. a single external scalar that predicts every distinguishing behaviour across low-overlap fields.
>
> I wrote these down before sending this. If any one holds, the shape is wrong and I will retract it. A one-line "this fails because..." is more useful to me than agreement. If you would rather not engage, no reply is needed.
>
> **What I am not asking for:** I am not asking whether you trust the framework, whether the conclusion sounds right, or whether you already work in complexity science. Those questions would select for people who share my evidence pool, which the figures treat as disqualifying for a verdict. I am asking only whether the check list separates the two kinds of system on a case you can actually test.
>
> **If it survives, and only then:** here are the sources. The figures are the authority, not my summary of them.
> - Figures text extract with line numbers (local framework file): `FRAMEWORK/reference/03-FIGURES_TEXT.md`. Look at Fig 12 :5, :48, :103, :121 and Fig 13 :28, :113, :124.
> - Figures and formula, public repository: https://github.com/IsaiahN/tether/tree/general-agent/figures
> - Canonical article (Medium, 2026-08-19): https://medium.com/@IsaiahNwukor/arrival-of-the-fittest-the-tether-architecture-1de7edf641ec
>
> **If you want to use the machinery:** the formula is in the figures link. Run it on a problem you know well and can check against a ground. Write down what would refute your result *before* you run it, and state the mode of what you get. The architecture itself warns that outputs can be "contrived to fit the formula", so a reported failure is more useful to me than a reported success.

> Thanks,
