## QUESTION

Decide, in effect terms, what separates two classes of system that complexity science names but does not crisply define. Option A: the folk separator, namely numerosity, parts, detail, description length. Option B: the arrangement, namely bonds, loop closure, recursive levels and a compounding per-slot residual. What each leads to: A keeps a quantity that aggregates away the live signal and lets a frame score itself with what it produces; B gives a per-slot, ground-ranged check list with a pre-registered refuter and no scalar. The anchor is the system's behaviour, which does not move when our description of it changes.

## DOMAIN CENSUS

R by arity, symmetry and scale, then one line per domain with actors / currency / anchor, partition or union, and order by dependency.

- **R (the residual).** We hold the folk pair "genuinely complex" / "merely complicated" and no figure-derived criterion that separates them. Arity: one-place predicate, but comparing two classes makes the separator a threshold relation (comparison, ⋛, Fig 12 :85-88: *"a threshold relation between two quantities"*). Symmetry: ordered, not a symmetric join. Scale: any stratum, because composition is recursive and the level decides what is irreducible (Fig 12 :103).
- **Domain 1, the composition of the system.** Actors: parts (atoms) and their relations (bonds). Currency: description bits and test actions, held apart (Fig 12 :125-126). Anchor: the behaviour of the whole. Question type: partition of compositions into degenerate chain versus richly bonded arrangement. Inside this domain, structure and dynamics are a union, because they alternate (Fig 13 :28) and a cycle is both a bond and a dynamic.
- **Domain 2, the diagnostic.** Actors: the frame, the object slots, the residual. Currency: residual indexed per slot. Anchor: the ground, measured behaviour. Question type: how we tell; whether the test aggregates slots or keeps them.
- **Partition or union.** The two domains are a partition by question type with a union at the shared anchor. Behaviour is D1's output and D2's reference. Fig 9 :54: *"And when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again."* Both loops are run separately.
- **Order by dependency.** Anchor reachable first, then vocabulary for bonds and loops, then the criterion, then classification (Fig 3 :19-20). Effort above a broken link cannot rescue one below it.

## VERDICT (from the figures)

A genuinely complex system is distinguished from a merely complicated one not by how many parts it has but by the arrangement: the merely complicated system is the degenerate chain, one bond type, no branching, no closing loop, one level, its behaviour recoverable from its ingredient list; the genuinely complex system carries information in the bonds, closes a loop, compounds a per-slot residual and has settled wholes that become atoms at a higher level. The criterion is a per-slot, ground-ranged check list, not a scalar, and agreement among frames sharing an evidence pool is disqualified as evidence. Chaos, emergence and adaptation are products a composition leaves behind, not the separator. This is a `specified`, ungrounded result. No numeric cut-off is claimed.

- **cost if wrong:** bounded and locatable. The packet is mode-labelled, states refuters before the conclusion and records the lineage, so an error is findable rather than invisible. The real cost is spending the independence of outside readers by sending a recording instead of a generator. Fig 2 :42 *"Buy independence in proportion to the cost of being wrong."* That cost is avoidable by leading with the shape claim and its refuter.
- **undoable:** yes. The defective draft readings (chaos as criterion, complex and complicated as different kinds, any single scalar) are removable without touching the arrangement derivation. `01` section 5 keeps them visible while `07` and `08` already remove them. No primitive has to be withdrawn, and nothing was hosted or sent.
- **reversed by:** a ground reading, not agreement. An isomer-style experiment showing that identical parts with different bonds behave identically under a discriminating instrument would kill the bond claim; an acyclic one-level chain that matches a cyclic recursive system under the same ground would kill the loop claim; a single external scalar predicting every distinguishing behaviour across low-overlap fields would kill the no-scalar claim; a derivation of one of the six from the other five would break the substrate these lines assume. Until one of these fires, the verdict stands as `candidate`.
