# The Tether's answer: complex versus complicated

**Mode: `specified` on the criterion, `general` on the structural shape, not `grounded`.** This is a shape and a check list, not a result. Simulated derivation checked against the figures (`05_what_shape_means.md` section 6), not a ground reading.

## 1. The residual it answers

Two folk terms, "genuinely complex system" and "merely complicated system", with no figure-derived criterion separating them. The folk separator is numerosity (parts, detail, description length). The residual is the missing criterion C*: a per-slot, ground-checkable test of arrangement that returns a different verdict for systems we call complex than for systems we call complicated. It is a **union** by question type with a **shared anchor** at behaviour, not one hard question (Fig 9 :54: *"And when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again."*).

## 2. The shape, per domain

**Domain 1, the composition of the system. Triangulated (Fig 12 + Fig 13, low overlap).**

1. **The parts are not the thing.** Fig 12 :5 *"A set of ingredients is not one. The bond is the rest of the information, and it is the half nothing was recording."* and Fig 12 :94 *"The arrangement is the substance."* Complexity is carried by the bonds, not the atoms.
2. **The merely complicated system is the degenerate case.** Fig 12 :48 *"A molecule is a bonded arrangement. A chain is the degenerate case: one bond type, no branching. Nothing about a chain is a different kind of object."* Many parts, one bond type, no branching, one level, behaviour recoverable from the ingredient list. A jet engine is a large chain. This is *not* a different kind of object from a complex system; it is the same machinery at lower richness.
3. **The genuinely complex system closes loops and stacks levels.** Fig 12 :121 *"cycle. The output re-enters as input. The sign of the loop decides whether it closes, and a chain cannot close by construction."* and Fig 12 :104 *"A settled molecule becomes an atom for whatever composes over it. So irreducible is a fact about a level and never about the world, and there is no bottom"*. Fig 12 :106: *"Which is why a system that cannot settle has one level however large its library."* The positive test is: at least one loop closes, and settled wholes become parts at a higher level.
4. **More parts do not add a kind.** Fig 13 :113 *"So the array is unbounded and the reach is not. More compositions never add a member, and no chain reaches outside what the six and their bonds already span."*
5. **Chaos is a companion, not the separator.** Fig 13 :124 *"Smoothness, symmetry, chaos and discreteness are each what a particular composition leaves behind, and none is a seventh member."* The remainder compounds through an amplifying chain (Fig 13 :31-32), but the criterion stays in bonds, loops and levels. This was retracted from an earlier draft after a world-source conflict; the retraction is visible in `01` section 5.

**Domain 2, the diagnostic. Triangulated (Fig 1 + Fig 2, low overlap).**

6. **Per slot, never an aggregate.** Fig 1 :19 *"The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated."* and Fig 1 :22 *"Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average."* A single "complexity score" is exactly the aggregate that hides the signal.
7. **The criterion must range against the ground.** Fig 1 :27 *"They are frame-internal. A frame cannot score itself with a quantity it also produces."* Fig 2 :29 *"An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks."* Behaviour, not our description of it, is the anchor.
8. **Agreement is not corroboration.** Fig 2 :35 *"Alignment is triangulation, not negotiation. A mirror cannot correct you."* Two experts who share an evidence pool are one witness (Fig 2 :40).

**The C* check list (the shape bound as an ordinary term).** For a candidate system, ask: (a) is the arrangement a single-bond chain or a multi-bond composition? (b) does any loop close? (c) do settled wholes become atoms at a higher level? (d) does the per-slot residual compound instead of washing out? (e) is each reading rangable against an external behaviour? A system passes to the extent that it answers yes to (b)-(e) and no to the degenerate reading of (a). That is a check list, not a number.

## 3. What it explicitly does not say

- Not a complexity scalar, and not a ranking of systems by part count, entropy or description length.
- Not a claim that chaos, emergence, adaptation or self-organization *is* complexity. They are companions and products that different compositions leave behind.
- Not a claim that complex and complicated are different kinds of object. Fig 12 :48 says the opposite about the chain.
- Not a claim that every complex system shares the same parts. Fig 13 :121: *"Different routes can arrive at the same substance."*
- Not a derivation of any particular field's complexity measure. Fig 9 :32: *"you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself."*
- Not `grounded`. No measurement was taken; a pre-registered refuter is in `01` section 4 and carried in `08`.

## 4. What would settle it, the ground

A source-discriminating ground, not a further argument. From *The Hard Problem is About Source*, an instrument that reads a projection *"cannot comment on the source"*; the spectrometer resolved the colour case. Here the analogous tests are:

- **Isomer test:** two systems with identical part inventories but different bonds and levels, measured under a discriminating instrument. Do they behave differently? If not, the bond does not carry the difference.
- **Loop test:** does an acyclic one-level chain with the same number of parts show the same non-decomposable per-slot behaviour as a cyclic recursive arrangement under the same ground? If yes, F3 fails.
- **Settlement test:** are the higher levels of a candidate complex system composed over terms the ground actually paid for? If not, the recursion is fake (Fig 12 :106).
- **Scalar test:** does a single external scalar predict every distinguishing behaviour across low-overlap fields? If yes, F5 and F8 fail.
- **Convergent-derivation test:** do independent, low-overlap frames reach the same shape by separate routes? Only then is agreement evidence, and only because the routes differ (Fig 8 :50-52).

## 5. The honest stop

**Unreached, not unreachable.** The six-term substrate and the bond vocabulary are *"a human's careful guess at reality's compression axes; another sensorium's minimum set need not be those six"* (Isaiah N, *They're Scanning Books*, 2026). Declaring them makes the choice visible, not neutral. Fig 8 :39: *"So a search that finds nothing is never a proof of absence."* The answer stops at the limit of what the available instruments can distinguish and records the lineage so the stop is checkable. *"Indiscernibility is not identity."*

## 6. One-paragraph version for outreach

> The Tether does not offer a number for complexity. It says that a system's parts are not the thing: a merely complicated system is the degenerate case, many parts joined by one kind of bond with no closing loop and no higher level, its behaviour readable off its ingredient list, while a genuinely complex system carries information in the arrangement, closes loops, compounds a residual instead of washing it out, and has settled wholes that become parts at the next level. The figures call this the difference between a formula and a molecule, and they say explicitly that a chain is not a different kind of object, only the degenerate case of the same machinery. The test must be read per slot against the system's behaviour, never aggregated into a part-count score, and never decided by experts who share the same evidence pool. Chaos and emergence are products a composition leaves behind, not the separator; an earlier draft that made chaos the criterion was retracted after a source conflict. What is offered is the shape any candidate answer must have, the refuters written in advance, and the instruments that would close it. The slot stays open until the ground settles it.
