## What the wider literature bears on the question

1. **The field does not treat "ritual" as one thing.** Boyer and Liénard 2020 state that "It is a fool's errand to search for causally relevant common features in such disparate behaviours" and propose four mechanisms instead. Legare and Nielsen 2020 describe a "fractionating strategy." This is the world-level counterpart of F1 in `01_figure_grounded_audit.md`: a union of conditions, not one lever. Cite these when explaining why the shape is split into three domains.

2. **The "functional" claim is contested inside the field.** Gelfand et al. 2020 explicitly argue against a "rose-coloured" functionalist picture and document conformity, obedience and reduced creativity. This is the only opened source that conflicts with the naive reading in §0 of the audit. By the conflict rule it was returned to the census and produced the valence-relative finding F5. Do not present it as a refutation of the figures; it corroborates Fig 11 :49-51.

3. **The emotion effect is well measured but not source-discriminating.** Pizarro et al. 2022 give a large meta-analysis of collective effervescence, with group identity and self-transcendent emotion among the outcomes. What it cannot show is whether the live residual sits in each participant or only in the pooled average. That distinction is exactly what the framework's per-slot rule makes, and it is why F7 marks the instrument as owed. A meta-analytic correlation is a filter for the budget, not a witness for the verdict (Fig 9 :60).

4. **A low-overlap frame corroborates the structure rule.** Dal Pesco and Fischer 2020 show that ritualized greetings in baboons track social tolerance and cooperation rather than being a fixed adaptation. This is an animal-behaviour witness, separate from the human ritual literature, for the claim that valence and function are relational. Overlap with the other Royal Society papers is moderate (same theme issue, different taxon and method).

5. **The doctrinal/imagistic distinction is a scale claim.** White et al. 2025 contrast rare, emotionally intense rites in small localized groups with frequent, mundane rituals in large imagined communities. This corroborates the membrane finding F6: what crosses scale is a method of coordinating, and the format changes with the size of the assembly.

**Overlap warning.** Boyer and Liénard, Legare and Nielsen, Gelfand et al. and Dal Pesco and Fischer all belong to the same 2020 Royal Society theme issue. They are four chapters of one evidence pool at the venue level, though their methods and taxa differ. Their agreement is weak corroboration, not four independent witnesses. Pizarro et al. (Frontiers, 2022) and White et al. (Religion, Brain & Behavior, 2025) are separate venues but overlap in authors and literature. All world sources here are secondary; the figures decide.
