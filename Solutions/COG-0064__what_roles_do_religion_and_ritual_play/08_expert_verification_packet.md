# Expert verification packet - the Tether on religion and ritual

**What this is:** a claim in a form an anthropologist or ritual-studies scholar can check. Mode, derivation and refuters are stated before the conclusion, so a reader knows what to look for and what would settle it.

**Mode: `specified` on the three roles, `general` on the structural constraints, `gap` on the slot bindings. Not `grounded`.** No measurement is claimed to settle which form occupies a slot.

## 1. The claim, in one paragraph

"Religion and ritual create shared beliefs, emotional experiences, and social obligations" is not one mechanism. Under the framework's loop it is a union of three conditions, each with a different anchor and a different bond. Shared belief is a convention: the agreement a rite produces is the product of a shared meaning and is disqualifying as a verdict about the world. Emotional experience is a structure over bodily states, a directed and spendable difference, and the person's own felt state is the ground for whether it is meaningful, measured per person and never averaged. Social obligation is a convention installed so it can be violated; when a rule decides an outcome it has stopped being a mere convention and that stake must be declared. The same rite is benefactor or malfactor depending on which residual is named, so "functional" is a relation, not a property. Which specific doctrine, structure or rite fills a slot is a declared gap. What is offered is a shape, six falsifiers written in advance, and the instruments that would close it.

## 2. Three checkable sub-claims

### C1 - framework-internal and convention-facing; checkable by reading Figure 2

- **Statement:** agreement produced by shared doctrine or synchronized performance is a convention, not a world-anchor. It is efficient for a shared meaning and disqualifying for a verdict about the world.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 2 :41 - *"efficient for a convention: agreement about a shared meaning is the product, not a corruption of it."* Also Fig 2 :40 - *"disqualifying for a verdict: agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool."* And Fig 2 :33 - *"The anchor must not update."*
- **What to check:** does the case you know fit the convention reading, or is there a doctrine whose content genuinely does not change when the community changes its mind? The latter would be a real non-updating anchor and would refute C1.
- **Refuter:** C1 fails if such a doctrine is found, or if ritual-aligned belief predicts the world at least as well as independent frames with a different evidence pool. Refuter 2 in `01_figure_grounded_audit.md` §2.

### C2 - affect-facing; checkable against per-person data

- **Statement:** the rite is a structure over bodily states, an ordering of actions and a directed, spendable difference; the felt state is a per-slot ground and must not be averaged into a group mood.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 13 :22 - *"a difference that can be spent."* Fig 13 :74 - *"Everything else is a composition across the two kinds, never within one."* Fig 1 :22 - *"Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average."* Fig 2 :36 - *"An anchor is legitimate when the question is constitutively about it."*
- **What to check:** in a study of collective effervescence or synchrony, does the reported effect survive when the analysis is rerun per person rather than as a group mean? Is there any measure of a live residual on some participants and not others? A pooled correlation alone is silent on this.
- **Refuter:** C2 fails if emotional alignment is purely a shared-pool average with no slot carrying positive live mass, or if the rite leaves the felt-state channel decayed. Refuter 3 in `01`.

### C3 - obligation-facing; checkable against ethnographic or historical cases

- **Statement:** an obligation is a convention that must be installable so it can be violated; a declared meaning is not grounded; a convention that decides an outcome has stopped being a convention. The rite binds it with a sequence and a threshold comparison.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 10 :18 - *"a declared meaning is not a grounded one."* Fig 10 :47 - *"a convention that decides an outcome has stopped being one."* Fig 12 :74 - *"A then B; B needs A to have happened."* Fig 12 :87 - *"a threshold relation between two quantities."*
- **What to check:** can the rule actually be broken, and do people recognize a violation as a violation? When a sanction decides who eats or who belongs, is the enforcement declared as a stake or hidden inside "tradition"? Does the in-group boundary behave as a threshold rather than a quantity?
- **Refuter:** C3 fails if an obligation is purely declared with no possible violation, or if it decides outcomes while still functioning only as a convention. Refuter 4 in `01`.

## 3. What is explicitly not claimed

- Not that religion or ritual is a world-anchor. Doctrine updates on the frames; the anchor does not (Fig 2 :33).
- Not that believer agreement is evidence of truth. It is the product of a convention (Fig 2 :41).
- Not that a rite produces an emotion that did not exist before. Baseline affect precedes it; the re-census in `01` F3 replaced strict production with a gradient/structure reading.
- Not that ritual is universally functional. Valence is relative to the residual (Fig 11 :49-51), and the synchrony literature documents conformity and reduced dissent alongside cohesion.
- Not a single group-benefit verdict. The residual is indexed per person (Fig 1 :19).
- Not which doctrine, structure or rite fills a slot. That is a `gap` (Fig 9 :32), and a binding onto a held atom is permitted but unproven.
- Not `grounded`. Nothing here is asserted as settled by experiment.

## 5. What would settle it

A source-discriminating instrument, not a further argument. From *The Hard Problem is About Source*: an instrument that reads green *"cannot comment on the source."*

1. **Belief:** an instrument that separates a triangulated anchor reading from a shared evidence pool reading, so that a belief's survival tracks the non-updating anchor rather than agreement with co-believers.
2. **Affect:** a per-person measurement showing live residual on some slots after the rite, not only a pooled mean.
3. **Obligation:** evidence that the convention can be violated in fact, and a declaration of what changes when enforcement decides an outcome.
4. **Slot bindings:** a figure re-census that derives which structure fills a slot, or a provenanced import with a source gate and a shadow test (Fig 6 :23).

## 6. What an expert can check in twenty minutes

1. Read Fig 2 :33, :40, :41 and Fig 10 :18, :47. Six short passages.
2. Take one case you know well. Decide whether it fits C1 (convention), C2 (structure over felt states) or C3 (installable obligation), or whether it breaks one.
3. Try to name one real violation of the obligation in your case. If no violation is possible, say so; that is a refutation of C3, not a gap in your knowledge.
4. Name one instrument that would distinguish a pooled average from a per-person residual in your case, or say why none is possible.
5. Send the refutation. A failure is more useful than an endorsement.
