# Union, bonds and order in the religion-and-ritual question

**Mode: `specified`, with `general` on the bond rules. This is a simulated derivation checked against the figures, not a ground reading (see `05_what_shape_means.md` §6).**

## RESIDUAL

The claim "religion and ritual create shared beliefs, emotional experiences, and social obligations" bundles three effects. Two questions are owed: (1) is this a partition or a union, and (2) what bond type does each role use, and in what dependency order?

## DOMAIN CENSUS

Atoms held: Space, Time, Energy (states) and Spectrum, Gradient, Network (structures). Bonds held: the seven plus negation. No new primitive is needed for any of the three effects. The three domains D1 belief, D2 affect, D3 obligation were admitted in `01_figure_grounded_audit.md` §3; X1 and X2 were refused or absorbed.

## DERIVATION

### Q1 - Partition or union?

- **Fig 9 :55** "A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones."
- **Fig 9 :57** "What remains is smaller and it is also categorically different: a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either."
- **Fig 9 :55** adds the consequence: "A single lever clears the region where only one condition holds and leaves the intersections standing, because the second condition still binds."

A single rite is typically doing all three at once: it orders actions, synchronizes arousal, and marks who is inside. Removing the belief condition does not leave a smaller version of the same problem; it leaves a different compound. Therefore **union**, and the union has its own loop with its own ground.

- **Falsifier, written before the run:** if one intervention clears all three effects and the residue is only a smaller amount of the same thing, the union reading fails and a partition should have been used. This is refuter 1 in `01_figure_grounded_audit.md` §2.

### Q2 - Which bond does each role use?

**D1 belief: symmetric bonds, plus a convention boundary.**

- Shared belief among frames is `+` (conjunction, both hold) and `≡` (identity, two names one referent, *"substitute one for the other. Is anything lost? No."* from the Operators table :27). The symmetry means k participants have k! spellings and one shared term, so overcharging for the sequence would be the error Fig 12 :92 names.
- But the agreement is a convention, not a verdict (Fig 2 :41). The boundary between convention and world-claim is a comparison, ⋛, because it is about which relation holds, not about both operands being present.

**D2 affect: a structure over a state, with a sequence.**

- **Fig 13 :74** "Everything else is a composition across the two kinds, never within one."
- The rite is a structure (spectrum ordering the actions, gradient directing arousal, network wiring the participants) composed over the bodily state of the assembly. Two states never bond directly; two structures join only through a state (Fig 13 :75).
- The performed rite is a **sequence**: **Fig 12 :74** "A then B; B needs A to have happened." Swap the order and it is a different rite, which is why the ordered bond, not the symmetric one, is charged.
- The arousal is a **gradient**: **Fig 13 :22** "a difference that can be spent." The Symbols table sharpens it: "A difference that can be spent. Not merely a distribution: what makes it a gradient is directedness."

**D3 obligation: sequence, comparison, and a cycle.**

- Obligation is a **sequence** (Fig 12 :74) because the rite must be performed in order to count.
- The in-group boundary is a **comparison**: **Fig 12 :87** "a threshold relation between two quantities." The Operators table adds: "a goal is a comparison, which is why a vocabulary without it cannot state one."
- Recurrent ritual is a **cycle**: **Fig 12 :121** "cycle. The output re-enters as input. The sign of the loop decides whether it closes, and a chain cannot close by construction." A rite that renews the obligation that motivated it is a closing loop; a rite that merely recalls a past occasion is a chain.
- The office that performs the rite is a **catalyst**: **Fig 12 :119** "catalyst. A part that survives its own use." Spent and regenerating parts behave differently across uses, and Fig 12 :119 notes that "nothing records which a term is." Declaring which offices are catalysts is a provenance obligation, not a free observation.

### Q3 - Are the three roles isomers?

- **Fig 12 :117** "isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."
- As the three effects are normally described, they share no full atom set: belief uses Γ and w, affect uses bodily states and a gradient, obligation uses a rule and a sanction. By Fig 12's definition they are **not isomers**. They are three members of the same broad family, each with a different anchor.
- The stronger reading: if all three are written over the full six-term set, differing only in which structure mediates which state pair and which bond is charged, then they **are** isomers, same atoms and different arrangement. That is the checkable structural claim worth testing, not a claim about any particular religion.
- **Falsifier, written before the run:** if one role can be written without an atom another requires, for example obligation with no state at all or affect with no structure, the isomer claim fails and the family reading stands. This is consistent with refuter 3 and 5 in `01_figure_grounded_audit.md` §2.

### Q4 - What is the order?

Dependency order (Fig 3): perception of the cues → vocabulary for what the rite affords → objective → planning the performance → learn and carry.

> **Fig 3 :27** "A link with no instrument is not a link that works. It is a link nobody has looked at."

The middle link, having terms for what the situation affords and saying what would count as progress, is the one the world literature leaves least measured. Boyer and Liénard 2020 give a vocabulary for the mechanisms, Legare and Nielsen 2020 give a vocabulary for the functions, and the framework asks for the missing instrument that would tell them apart at the ground. That instrument is the owed thing in F7.

Across generations the order is a membrane, not a queue:

> **Fig 4 :22** "Only methods cross up. Only head starts come down."

The round trip is lossy, so:

> **Fig 4 :30** "So take the round trip and measure the gap. Without it you are navigating by dead reckoning: confident about a position nobody checked."

## CONCLUSION

The question is a **union**, not a partition. The three roles use different bonds: symmetric bonds plus a convention boundary for shared belief, a cross-kind structure plus sequence and gradient for affect, and sequence plus comparison plus cycle for obligation. Over the full six-term set they are a real isomer claim; as normally described they are a family with different anchors. The dependency order runs through the usually unmeasured vocabulary link, and transmission across generations is a lossy membrane that carries methods, not recordings.

This framing rescues what is defensible in the ordinary claim without the part that failed. What is refused: one lever, an aggregate benefit verdict, a doctrine-as-anchor, a producible emotion, and a name for the slot occupant. What remains: a checkable structure with six falsifiers written in advance.
