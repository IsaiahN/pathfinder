**Use:** one to one, or as a cover letter attached to a submission through the public venues in `03_public_contact_points.csv`. **Not** for bulk send.

**Hard precondition:** do not send until the three effects in `01_figure_grounded_audit.md` are stated separately and the slot-binding gap is marked as a gap. The output must survive validation or the two-stage offer collapses on contact.

**Subject:** Check one shape before you decide whether the framework is worth reading

> Hi [name],
>
> I have one specific output I would like you to check against what you already know about ritual and religion. I am not asking you to read the theory first, and I am not asking you to agree with it. If the output does not survive, there is nothing else to look at.
>
> **The output (`specified`, not `grounded`):** "religion and ritual create shared beliefs, emotional experiences, and social obligations" is not one mechanism. Under the framework's loop it is a union of three conditions, and each has a different anchor.
>
> 1. **Shared belief is a convention, not a world-anchor.** Shared doctrine and synchronized performance give members a shared library and a shared weighting, so they converge. But when the agreeing frames draw on the same pool, the agreement is the product of a shared meaning, not evidence that the belief is about the world. The framework states this as a valence rule: agreement is disqualifying for a verdict and efficient for a convention.
> 2. **Emotional experience changes contact with a ground that is private to each participant.** The rite is a structure over bodily states: an ordering of the actions, a directed and spendable difference in arousal, a network among the participants. For "is this meaningful for a person," the person's own felt state is the legitimate anchor. That state must not be averaged into a group mood, because the framework measures the residual per person, never as a single number.
> 3. **Social obligation is a convention installed so it can be violated.** A taboo can be broken, an oath can be false. A declared meaning by itself is not grounded. When a convention decides an outcome, it has stopped being a mere convention, and that stake needs to be declared rather than hidden inside the word "tradition."
>
> This is a shape and a route, not a result. The framework does not say which specific doctrine, which specific structure, or which specific rite fills a slot. It returns a kind of thing, never the answer itself. Where the three conditions overlap, it forms a compound with its own loop, not a harder case of any one of them.
>
> **What I am not claiming:** that religion promotes group benefit in general, that ritual is adaptive in all contexts, that a rite produces an emotion that did not exist before, or that doctrine is an anchor. The same rite can be a benefactor to one residual and a malfactor to another, so "functional" is a relation to a named question, not a property the rite carries.
>
> **What would refute the shape:**
> 1. one intervention clears all three conditions and leaves no categorically different residue;
> 2. a doctrine is found whose content does not change when the community changes its mind, that is, a real non-updating anchor;
> 3. emotional alignment is shown to be only a shared-pool average, with no person carrying a live residual of their own;
> 4. an obligation is purely declared with no possible violation, or it decides outcomes while still being only a convention;
> 5. the same rite has invariant valence across a documented change of residual;
> 6. a figure re-census derives which specific structure fills a slot.
>
> I wrote these down before sending this. If any one holds, the shape is wrong and I will retract or narrow it. A one-line "this fails because..." is more useful to me than agreement. If you'd rather not engage, no reply is needed.
>
> **If it survives, and only then:** the sources are below. The figures are the authority, not my summary of them.
> - Figures text extract with line numbers (local framework file): `FRAMEWORK/reference/03-FIGURES_TEXT.md`
> - Figures README, canonical usage rule (local framework file): `FRAMEWORK/reference/FIGURES_README_canonical.md`

>

Write down what would refute your result *before* you run it, and state the mode of what you get. A reported failure is more useful to me than a reported success.
