# Spin extraction versus disc extraction: isomers, order and analogues

**Mode: `specified` on the structural claims, `general` on the bond-type observation. This is a simulated derivation checked against the figures, not a ground reading (see `05_what_shape_means.md` §7).**

## RESIDUAL

The world literature offers at least three bindings of the same shape: spin extraction (Blandford-Znajek), disc extraction (Blandford-Payne, and the Livio et al. position that the inner disc dominates), and frame-dragging extraction (Penrose). Two questions are owed: (1) are the spin and disc bindings different *things* under the figure's definition of a molecule, or just different spellings of one thing; (2) at what order do they compose, and does the jet add a primitive?

## DOMAIN CENSUS (supporting derivation only)

Atoms held: Space, Time, Energy (states) and Gradient, Network, Spectrum (structures). Bonds held: the seven plus negation. No new primitive is available without import. World bindings are imports from D2 with source gates, never new atoms of the framework.

## DERIVATION

### Q1 — Are the two mechanisms first-order or second-order?

- Fig 13 :74: *"Everything else is a composition across the two kinds, never within one."*
- A first-order molecule is `state → structure → state` (one crossing). Fig 12 :48 describes the chain as the degenerate case of a molecule, and Fig 13 :78-83 lists the route "a state, a structure, relations, fields, gradients, flows, networks".
- Spin binding: Time(state) + Gradient(structure, spin-down over time) → Energy-as-flow. Time is the state the gradient is bonded to. Fig 13 :76 explicitly licenses gradients distinguished by their bonded state: *"A gradient over space and one over time are different terms, because which state it is bonded to is part of what it is."*
- Disc binding: Space(state) + Gradient(structure, field geometry over radius) → Energy-as-flow.
- Both are first-order and legal under Fig 13 :74-76. The world check (not a figure line) is that GRMHD simulations do produce relativistic outflows from both reservoirs; McKinney 2005 argues only the spin one reaches ultrarelativistic speed. Mark `specified`, unmeasured, triangulated for legality, single-source for the physics claim.

### Q2 — Are they isomers?

- Fig 12 :117: **"Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."**
- Fig 12 :124: **"functional group. A substructure whose presence fixes the outcome. The group says what kind; the qualifier says which member."**
- Fig 12 :5: **"A set of ingredients is not one. The bond is the rest of the information, and it is the half nothing was recording."**

Two readings, kept separate:

1. **As usually written in the literature** the spin binding uses `{Time, Energy, Gradient, Network}` and the disc binding uses `{Space, Energy, Gradient, Network}`. They share Energy, Gradient and Network but not the bonded state. By Fig 12's definition they are **not** strict isomers, because the atom sets differ. They are two members of the same **functional group**: a `state → gradient → flow` arrangement in which the qualifier (which state) decides which member. This is the weaker, safer claim.
2. **Over the full shared atom set** `{Space, Time, Energy, Gradient, Network, Spectrum}`, both can be written with the same atoms and different bonds (gradient bonded to Time for spin, to Space for disc; network bonded differently in the collar region). Then they are genuine **isomers**: same atoms, different arrangement, different substance. This is the stronger claim worth testing, because the observable difference (speed, collimation, luminosity) is precisely the "decisive in the behaviour" clause of Fig 12 :117.

**Falsifier, written before the run:** if either mechanism requires an atom the other does not, even over the full shared set (for example if disc extraction needs a Spectrum term that spin extraction cannot be written with), the isomer claim fails and only the functional-group claim stands. Conversely, if both can be written over the full set with the *same* bond, they are one term under a symmetric bond (Fig 12 :92: under a symmetric bond k operands have k! spellings and one term), and the distinction collapses. Neither outcome rescues the claim by fiat.

### Q3 — What order is the jet?

- Fig 12 :104: **"A settled molecule becomes an atom for whatever composes over it. So irreducible is a fact about a level and never about the world"**.
- The energy-to-flow molecule is settled in the weak sense that outflows are observed across many systems, not that any mechanism is settled. A settled molecule may serve as an atom at the next level.
- The bipolar, collimated jet is therefore a **second-order composition**: `(settled flow) + network relation → bipolar structure`. Fig 12 :121: **"cycle. The output re-enters as input. The sign of the loop decides whether it closes, and a chain cannot close by construction."** The magnetosphere is a loop in this sense; a one-way chain cannot produce a collimated pair of streams without the return relation.
- No new primitive is introduced. Fig 13 :115: **"The set is closed. The arrangements are not. That gap is why import exists."** The extra structure is an arrangement of held atoms, not a seventh member.

**Type caveat, live:** Fig 12 :104 permits a settled molecule to become an atom, but whether the bipolar structure is a single second-order term or a chain of third-order terms is not decided by the figure. The number of levels is a `gap`, not a claim. Same honest treatment as the ⋛ operand-type question in the exemplar.

### Q4 — Analogues, and what they do not license

Three low-overlap analogues from sources opened this session. They corroborate the *shape*, never the astrophysical name.

1. **Engineering, the homopolar generator.** Wikipedia, *Homopolar generator* (opened): "Like all dynamos, the Faraday disc converts kinetic energy to electrical energy." And: "the rotation of a conductor in a magnetic field produces an electric field in the system at rest. This phenomenon is well known from laboratory experiments and is usually called 'homopolar' or 'unipolar' induction." The page also records that "Unipolar inductors have been associated with ... black holes." This is the same shape, rotation plus field plus circuit producing a directed current, in a laboratory device with a different closure. It corroborates that a spendable difference on a rotating store yields a directed flow. It does **not** corroborate any particular black-hole mechanism, and it shares no evidence pool with the jet literature.
2. **Fundamental GR, the Penrose process.** Wikipedia, *Penrose process* (opened): "the Penrose process ... is theorised by Sir Roger Penrose as a means whereby energy can be extracted from a rotating black hole." And: "rotational energy is extracted from the black hole, resulting in the black hole being spun down to a lower rotational speed." This is the pure-GR version of spending a finite store. It corroborates the finiteness and spendability clauses, and it is the same evidence pool as the other jet wiki pages, so it is not an independent witness.
3. **Metamerism, the colour-instrument case.** *The Hard Problem is About Source* (opened): "the instrument is speaking only about what it measured, it cannot comment on the source." This corroborates the need for a discriminator and the `gap` verdict, not any name. It is framework-side, maximal overlap with the figures, zero independent witness weight.

## CONCLUSION

Over the full shared atom set, spin extraction and disc extraction are **isomers** under Fig 12 :117, with a stated falsifier. As written in the literature they are two members of a **functional group** under Fig 12 :124. The energy-to-flow molecule is first order; the bipolar jet is second order over a settled flow and introduces no new primitive. The number of orders beyond two is a live `gap`. The engineering and pure-GR analogues corroborate the shape in low-overlap fields, and the framework essay corroborates the need for a discriminator. None of them names the process.
