## Two findings that bear directly on this question

1. **The literature contains a live disagreement, not a consensus.** McKinney 2005 argues the Blandford-Znajek mechanism is the only one able to produce an ultrarelativistic jet; Livio, Ogilvie and Pringle 1999 argue the spin of the hole is "probably irrelevant" when the field threading the hole is not stronger than the field threading the disc. Fig 9 :4-5 says a disagreement that does not shrink with effort, where each rule works on a different subset, is several well-formed questions rather than one. Cite **both** papers together. Presenting only one as "the answer" would manufacture a consensus that the opened sources do not support.
2. **The open question is a source-discrimination problem.** *The Hard Problem is About Source* (opened): "the instrument is speaking only about what it measured, it cannot comment on the source." The bright, narrow jet is a projection with several possible sources. What would settle it is a spectrometer analogue, not another argument or another citation.

## Distribution: offer, do not push

Figure-grounded reasons:

- **Fig 10 :52** - *"Bring a characterised residual, never the source."* Lead with the shape claim and the refuter; offer the links after a verdict on stage 1 is recorded.
- **Fig 10 :42** - *"A verifier that can reconstruct the claim cannot verify it."* A recipient who has absorbed the whole framework before being asked for a verdict has joined the frame being checked.
- **Fig 9 :27-28** - *"describe it before you go looking, or any frame you pick will seem to fit."* Ask what they would expect to see if the shape claim were true before showing them the article.
- **Fig 2 :35** - *"Alignment is triangulation, not negotiation. A mirror cannot correct you."* Do not select recipients for likely agreement.
- **Fig 9 :60** - *"Use filters for the budget and witnesses for the verdict. Never let a filter hand you a verdict."* A venue's prestige is a budget for trying a claim, not a verdict about it.

**Recommended order in a reply:** (1) the shape claim and its refuter; (2) ask what they would count as a failure and what instrument would discriminate the sources; (3) only then the links, with the note that the figures, not the summary, are the authority.

## The two-stage offer

1. **Stage 1.** Send the residual in effect terms, the shape claim and the pre-registered refuter. Ask what would refute it. Record the verdict **before** any theory is shown. A one-line failure is more useful than an endorsement.
2. **Stage 2, opt-in only.** If they are curious, offer the three source links (the Blandford-Znajek and Livio records, and the arXiv listing). The figures are the authority, not this summary.
3. **Stage 3.** Offer the formula as a method to reuse, with the instruction that each user writes their own refuter and states their own mode. Fig 4 :22: *"Only methods cross up. Only head starts come down."*

Stage 2 is opt-in by construction because a recipient's later agreement, after absorbing the framework, is not evidence (Fig 2 :40). Never pool it as corroboration.
