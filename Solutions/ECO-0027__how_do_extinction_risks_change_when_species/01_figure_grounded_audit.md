## 0. The residual, in effect terms

The question asks how the extinction risk of a species changes as a function of its dependence on
other species for food, pollination, habitat, or reproduction. Stated as an effect, not a topic:

> R = the change in a species' failure probability when the set of contact relations that can carry
> failure into or out of it changes. Option A: risk is a property of the species alone, so dependence
> adds nothing that traits cannot predict. Option B: risk is a property of the species-and-contact-web,
> so dependence changes risk by opening transmission paths, and the direction and size of the change
> depend on which relations hold and on their arrangement, not on a species count.

What an answer would look like, bound as an ordinary term before the run (Formula, "Direction"): a
rule that takes an enumerated contact web and returns, per species, the sign and the reach of the
risk change, with a stated refuter. That shape is binding here; it is not yet a measurement.

## 1. Domain census, before any figure line is chosen

### R by arity, symmetry, scale

- **Arity:** relational, at least two species (a focal species and its partners), with higher-order
  cases when a partner's partner matters. A single-species risk number has the wrong arity.
- **Symmetry:** mixed. Food and habitat relations can be directed. Pollination and reproduction are
  often mutual, but mutualism and parasitism are relations rather than fixed kinds (Fig 11 :50).
  Any verdict that assumes a symmetric dependency graph has already lost half the cases.
- **Scale:** first order at the direct partner, second and third order along the cascade. The figure's
  own example puts the residual three steps out (Fig 11 :52-53). Local loss and global loss are
  different slots, not one averaged quantity.

### One line per domain: actors / currency / anchor

1. **Habitat and contact domain.** Actors: species, conditions, relations (food, pollination, habitat,
   reproduction, predation, competition). Currency: contact and cascade. Anchor: the ground, namely
   what actually survives or fails. The question is constitutively about this domain.
2. **Bond and arrangement domain.** Actors: species as operands, dependency relations as typed bonds.
   Currency: arrangement and the information the bond carries beyond the operands. Anchor: observed
   survival, the ground. Question type: union, because one species sits in several relations at once.
3. **Assessment and verification domain.** Actors: risk assessors, models, monitoring instruments.
   Currency: per-slot residual and agreement. Anchor: measured survival and extinction, never
   agreement between models. "An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its
   answer because of what any frame thinks" (Fig 2 :29-30, attributed to the figure text).

No nameable anchor means not yet a domain. Domain 3 has one only as long as model agreement is kept
out of the verdict slot; if agreement is allowed in, domain 3 collapses into the mirror chain.

### Partition or union

A **union**, not a partition. The four dependency types in the question overlap: a pollinator is also
food for something, a habitat former is also a competitor. Fig 9 :54: "And when the split lands on a
union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again." Fig 9 :55-56: "A bundled problem is
usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones. A single lever clears the
region where only one condition holds and leaves the intersections standing, because the second
condition still binds." So the answer must be built per condition and per intersection, then
recombined. A single global "extinction risk under dependence" figure would be a blend.

### Order by dependency, Fig 3

1. Enumerate the habitat before assigning bond types (Fig 11 :43-44).
2. Describe the residual in effect terms before choosing a frame (Fig 9 :27-28).
3. Settle first-order contacts before composing second-order cascades (Fig 12 :104).
4. Refuse an invariance claim before minting a mechanism (Formula step 2).
5. Only then run the per-domain loops.

Fig 3 :20-21: "The chain is ordered by dependency, not by importance. Each link consumes what the one
above produces, so effort spent above the break arrives at a step that cannot use it."

## 3. Findings

### F1 - The governing figure names the question's own relations (decisive, triangulated)

Fig 11 enumerates what the habitat contains. Fig 11 :45-47:

> "actors: what acts on it, and what it acts on"
> "conditions: light, water, temperature, season, terrain"
> "relations: pollination, predation, competition, dependency"

The question's four items, food, pollination, habitat, reproduction, are instances or close relatives
of lines the figure already lists. This is not an analogy imported from elsewhere. The derived claim
is: a species' risk is a function of its contact web, not of the species in isolation. Fig 11 :39
supports the same shape with an explicit scope note: "So the answerable question is not what
intelligence is, but what makes a system capable. And capability is a property of agent-and-habitat,
never of the agent alone." Fig 11 :40: "A system cannot be made more capable by improving the agent in
isolation. An improvement that does not change contact changes nothing, however much it improves."

**Scope correction, kept visible.** Fig 11 :39 is written about capability in agents, not about
extinction probability in species. Applying it to this question is an extension, mode `specified`,
not a direct ecological reading. The canonical architecture essay also declines the mapping of this
figure's "habitat" onto an ecological niche, because the figure's habitat is defined by contact with
a residual rather than by an organism's relation to its environment. What transfers is the rule
(enumerate contacts, valence relative, introduction not exclusion), not the term "habitat."

- **Mode:** `specified`, triangulated (Fig 11 :45-47 plus :39-40, low overlap within the figure set because one is an enumeration and one is a capability claim).
- **Refuter:** show a species whose extinction probability is invariant under changes to its enumerated contact set, with the change measured at the ground.

### F2 - Enumerate, never compose; the residual sits beyond direct contact (triangulated)

Fig 11 :43-44:

> "The habitat is enumerated, never composed."
> "List everything in contact with the residual, then what is in contact with those, and outward until the cascade stops mattering."

Fig 11 :48: "You do not invent the list. You read it off the world, and what you cannot perceive or
measure yet is the residual." Fig 11 :52-53: "And contact cascades. Scoping the habitat to direct
contact is how DDT reached the eagles: first-order enumeration, second-order effect, and the residual
that mattered was three steps out." Fig 11 :54 gives the historical case: "Carson, Silent Spring,
1962. The mechanism, biomagnification through the food chain to eggshell thinning, was established
over the following decade."

**Derived:** there is no closed-form risk from a species list and its traits. The contact web has to be
read off the world, outward until the cascade stops mattering. A first-order-only assessment is
structurally the DDT error.

- **Mode:** `general` for the rule, `specified` for any particular cascade. Triangulated with world sources: Rezende et al. 2007 report simulated extinctions triggering cascades of related species; the coextinction literature records host-specific affiliates rising quickly as host loss rises.
- **Refuter:** produce an assay that predicts cascade size from traits alone, with no contact enumeration, and survives a held-out community.

### F3 - Risk is indexed per species, never averaged (triangulated)

Fig 1 :19: "The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated." Fig 1 :22: "Aggregating across
slots is how a live signal disappears into an average." Fig 1 :13: "a global R near zero with one live
slot residual is a legal state."

**Derived:** each species is a slot. A single ecosystem-level "extinction risk under dependence" number
can be near zero while one dependent species is live. The world literature agrees that the pruning is
non-random (Rezende et al. 2007, "non-random pruning of the evolutionary tree"), which is exactly what
an average conceals.

- **Mode:** `general`. Triangulated (Fig 1 plus the non-random-pruning result, low overlap between a framework rule and an empirical network study).
- **Refuter:** exhibit an aggregate index that both preserves which species is live and predicts the next loss out of sample.

### F4 - The sign of the change is not fixed; valence is relative to the residual (triangulated)

Fig 11 :49: "An actor's valence is relative to the residual, not a property it carries." Fig 11 :50:
"The same actor is a benefactor to one residual and a malfactor to another, so mutualism and parasitism
are relations rather than kinds." Fig 11 :51: "No standing list of good actors survives a change of residual."

**Derived:** the answer to "how do risks change" cannot be a single sign. A dependency can be a buffer
for one species and a transmission path for another. This is why "more dependence means more risk"
is the wrong shape even when dependence is genuinely causal.

World corroboration from a low-overlap field: the systemic-risk literature reports that "up to a
certain point, interconnectedness enhances financial stability" and that beyond a critical density
further connection propagates risk; the diversification entry describes connection first as
"shock-absorber" and then as "shock-amplifier." May 1972 in theoretical ecology found random
connectance stable only up to a critical level and then suddenly unstable. These are two independent
fields (economics, mathematics/ecology), neither drawing on the framework author.

- **Mode:** `general`. Triangulated (Fig 11 :49-51, May 1972, systemic-risk literature; three low-overlap pools).
- **Refuter:** exhibit one dependency type whose effect on extinction risk has the same sign for every residual, measured across at least two different communities.

### F5 - Arrangement carries what the species list does not (triangulated)

Fig 12 :5: "A set of ingredients is not one. The bond is the rest of the information, and it is the half
nothing was recording." Fig 12 :46-47: "A bond is the typed relation between two operands. There are
seven, they are not interchangeable, and which one holds is not recoverable from the operands. A recipe
that names only its ingredients has recorded half of itself." Fig 12 :94: "The arrangement is the
substance." Fig 12 :117: "isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is
invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."

**Derived:** two communities with the same species list but different dependency bonds can have
different extinction risk. The object that changed is the bond arrangement, not the cast. This is the
community-level isomer claim developed in `06_isomers_and_order.md`. Rezende et al. 2007 supply
corroboration from outside the framework: network architecture and phylogeny jointly shape which
species are lost.

- **Mode:** `specified`. Triangulated (Fig 12 definition plus the empirical network-architecture result).
- **Refuter:** show two communities with identical species sets and different bond arrangements whose per-species risk trajectories are identical under the same perturbation.

### F6 - Dependency chains are ordered; an upstream break is not rescued downstream (triangulated)

Fig 3 :19: "Optimising a link that already works cannot rescue one that does not." Fig 3 :20-21
(given in section 1) makes the order explicit. Fig 11 :65: "Introducing an actor for its cascade is
ecosystem engineering, after Jones, Lawton and Shachak, 1994. Cane toads and kudzu are the same move
with the cascade unenumerated."

**Derived:** losing a basal provider, pollinator, or habitat former propagates; spending the
conservation budget on a downstream species cannot repair a break upstream. The coextinction
literature's practical rule, conserve the host on which affiliates depend, is a world-side witness to
the same ordering.

- **Mode:** `specified`. Triangulated (Fig 3 plus the host-conservation strategy in the coextinction sources).
- **Refuter:** demonstrate a downstream intervention that fully prevents an upstream-caused loss without changing the upstream contact.

### F7 - The four dependency types are a union and must be unbundled (single-source for the figure rule)

Fig 9 :54 and :55-56 (quoted in section 1) govern. Fig 9 :57-58: "What remains is smaller and it is
also categorically different: a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either. It stops being
a mixture and becomes a specific compound."

**Derived:** a species that is both a pollinator and a food source is not in two easy cases; it is in a
compound with its own loop. Searching "food dependence" and "pollination dependence" separately and
adding the answers leaves the intersections standing.

- **Mode:** `general`, single-source (Fig 9 only; the ecology sources did not speak to this specific unbundling rule).
- **Refuter:** show the four dependency sets are disjoint in a real community across multiple seasons.

### F8 - The ground decides; agreement between models is not a verdict (triangulated)

Fig 2 :29-30 (quoted in section 1), Fig 2 :35: "Alignment is triangulation, not negotiation. A mirror
cannot correct you." Fig 2 :40: "disqualifying for a verdict: agreement about the world is worthless
when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool." Fig 11 :56: "Unmaintained. No maintainer and no live
frame in the seat. The habitat is pure contact, nothing selects, and what survives is what the ground kept."
Fig 11 :73: "The habitat is not the ground. The habitat is where actors come from and it updates; the
ground is what settles, and it does not."

**Derived:** a risk model is a frame. Two risk models that share the same survey data are one witness
wearing two names. Survival and extinction are the anchor. Note that "survival of the fittest" here is
not a slogan: Fig 11 :36 says "Where nothing maintains anything, intelligence is a label applied
afterwards to a lineage that met its ground. Nothing was intelligent; selection ran."

- **Mode:** `general`. Triangulated (Fig 2 plus Fig 11 :56, :73; two distinct figure arguments).
- **Refuter:** show two models' agreement, with no ground reading, settling a claim about which species is at risk.

### F9 - Management is asymmetric and permanent (triangulated)

Fig 11 :61-62: "The seat's power is asymmetric. Introduction is available; exclusion is not. You can
add an actor and you cannot subtract the habitat, because contact is a fact about the world and does
not consult you." Fig 11 :63-64: "Which is why a managed ecosystem is permanent work rather than a
solved state. And in neither mode is the habitat composed: unmaintained nothing composes it, maintained
the seat adds to a habitat it still did not author." Fig 11 :66: "Isolation is not removal of the
habitat. It is substitution of one habitat for another."

**Derived:** conservation can add a partner or a corridor; it cannot subtract the ambient contact web.
Translocation is a substituted habitat, with "what you failed to reproduce" and "what you
unintentionally introduced" invisible until the goal fails or the actor acts (Fig 11 :68-69).

- **Mode:** `specified`. Triangulated (Fig 11 :61-66 plus the coextinction host-conservation rule).
- **Refuter:** demonstrate an exclusion that removes a contact from a community rather than substituting one.

## 4. Pre-registered refuter

Written before the conclusion, per the standing rule that a falsifier written before the run is a
design instrument. The shape claim is refuted if any of the following holds:

1. A species' extinction probability is invariant under a measured change to its enumerated contact set.
2. Two communities with the same species set and different bond arrangements have identical risk trajectories under the same perturbation.
3. One dependency type has the same sign of effect on risk for every residual, measured in at least two communities.
4. An aggregate risk index preserves which species is live and predicts the next loss out of sample.
5. Agreement between two models that share a data source, with no ground reading, settles a risk claim.

If any fires, the corresponding finding is withdrawn and the shape is re-censused, not softened.

## 5. Recommendation

1. Lead with the shape and its refuter, not with the framework. The first contact is the residual and what would count as a failure.
2. Enumerate the habitat for the specific species before naming a mechanism (F2). Do not stop at direct partners.
3. Report per species, never as one ecosystem average (F3).
4. Do not answer "risk goes up." Answer "the sign is relative to the residual, and the arrangement decides the reach" (F4, F5).
5. Unbundle food, pollination, habitat and reproduction, then examine their intersections (F7).
6. Keep the ground separate from the models (F8), and record what management added versus what it merely substituted (F9).
7. Fig 4 :30: "So take the round trip and measure the gap. Without it you are navigating by dead reckoning: confident about a position nobody checked." Send the generator, that is the enumeration rule and the refuter, not a recording of this run.
