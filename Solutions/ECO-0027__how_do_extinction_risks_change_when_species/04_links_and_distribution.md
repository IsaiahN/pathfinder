## Wider literature that bears on the question

These are the world-side witnesses. They are corroboration only; the figures decide.

1. **May, R. M. (1972), "Will a Large Complex System be Stable?", Nature 238, 413-414.**
   https://www.nature.com/articles/238413a0 . Abstract: random connectance is stable only up to a
   critical level and then "suddenly become[s] unstable". Predicts a threshold, not a monotonic rule.
   Corroborates the residual-relative valence finding (F4) and the arrangement finding (F5).
2. **Rezende, E. L. et al. (2007), "Non-random coextinctions in phylogenetically structured mutualistic
   networks", Nature 448, 925-928.** https://www.nature.com/articles/nature05956 . Abstract:
   "simulated extinction events tend to trigger coextinction cascades of related species" and a
   "non-random pruning of the evolutionary tree". Corroborates cascade and per-slot findings (F2, F3).
3. **Coextinction, Wikipedia article (page last edited 2026-11-09).**
   https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coextinction . Quotes Koh et al. 2004 Science: "Species coextinction
   is a manifestation of the interconnectedness of organisms in complex ecosystems", and the
   curvilinear result that affiliate extinctions "may be modest at low levels of host extinction but
   can be expected to rise quickly as host extinctions increase". Corroborates F2 and F4. Note that
   the same article records coextinction "may not be the most important cause of species extinctions",
   which bounds magnitude, not mechanism.
4. **Cascading failure, Wikipedia article (page last edited 2026-07-03).**
   https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cascading_failure . "A cascading failure is a failure in a system of
   interconnected parts in which the failure of one or few parts leads to the failure of other parts,
   growing progressively as a result of positive feedback." Low-overlap engineering analogue. Also
   notes that a species whose loss causes many others is called a keystone species. Corroborates F2, F6.
5. **Systemic risk, Wikipedia article (page last edited 2026-11-01).**
   https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Systemic_risk . Risks "imposed by interlinkages and interdependencies
   in a system or market, where the failure of a single entity or cluster of entities can cause a
   cascading failure". Reports Acemoglu et al. 2015: connection helps up to a point, then propagates
   risk. Low-overlap economics analogue. Corroborates F4.

Overlap note: items 1 to 3 share a broad ecology pool with one another, so they are not three fully
independent witnesses. Items 4 and 5 come from engineering and economics and are genuinely
low-overlap analogues. No world source comes from the framework author's evidence pool except the
framework essays themselves, which are treated separately in `05_what_shape_means.md`.

## Distribution: offer, do not push

- **Fig 10 :52** "Bring a characterised residual, never the source." Lead with the shape claim and
  its refuter. Do not send the full architecture before a reader has described what they would expect
  to see.
- **Fig 2 :35** "Alignment is triangulation, not negotiation. A mirror cannot correct you." Do not
  ask for agreement.
- **Fig 2 :40** "disqualifying for a verdict: agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing
  frames share an evidence pool". Record the stage-1 verdict before showing any framework material,
  and never pool later agreement as corroboration.
- **Fig 4 :22** "Only methods cross up. Only head starts come down." Stage 3 gives away a method,
  which is permitted; it does not give away an answer.
- **Fig 9 :60** "Use filters for the budget and witnesses for the verdict. Never let a filter hand you
  a verdict." Choosing venues by prestige or by likely agreement is a budget filter, not a witness.

**Recommended order in a reply:** (1) the output and its five refuters; (2) ask what the recipient
would count as a failure; (3) only then the three links, with the note that the figures, not the
summary, are the authority; (4) offer the formula as a method for their own problem, with their own
refuter written first.

## The two-stage offer, with a third optional stage

1. **Stage 1:** validate the output against the ground. The recipient checks the shape without holding
   the framework's interior. This is Fig 10 :52 and Fig 2 :45 "Frames range against the ground. They
   do not negotiate with it."
2. **Stage 2, opt-in only:** if curious, read the theory that produced it. Their stage-1 verdict is
   already recorded, so later agreement cannot be counted as evidence.
3. **Stage 3:** reuse the formula on a problem they know and can check, writing a refuter in advance.
   The architecture essay warns that outputs "could never be verified if the solutions presented were
   not contrived to fit the formula", so each user must supply their own ground.

**Hard precondition:** stages 1 to 3 only work if the stage-1 output survives. This solution's output
is a shape with refuters, not a numerical claim; if a recipient fires any of the five refuters, the
corresponding finding is withdrawn before any further material is sent.
