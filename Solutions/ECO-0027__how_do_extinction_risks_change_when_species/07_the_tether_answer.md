# The Tether's answer on extinction risk and species dependence

**Mode: `specified` on the substantive claims, `general` on the structural shape, not `grounded`.**
This is a shape and a route, not a measurement. It is a simulated derivation checked for internal
consistency against the figures (`05_what_shape_means.md` section 6), not a ground reading.

## 1. The residual it answers

The question asks how a species' extinction risk changes when it depends on others for food,
pollination, habitat or reproduction. The residual is relational, mixed in symmetry (directed and
mutual relations both occur), and multiscale (first-order partners, second and third-order cascades).
It is a **union compound**, not four separate questions: Fig 9 :54 says "And when the split lands on a
union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again." A case in two conditions is not a
harder case of either; it is a specific compound with its own loop.

## 2. The shape

1. **Risk belongs to the species and its contact web, not to the species alone.** Fig 11 :47 names
   "relations: pollination, predation, competition, dependency". Fig 11 :39 adds "capability is a
   property of agent-and-habitat, never of the agent alone", applied here as a `specified` extension.
2. **The contacts must be enumerated outward.** Fig 11 :43-44: "The habitat is enumerated, never
   composed. List everything in contact with the residual, then what is in contact with those, and
   outward until the cascade stops mattering." Fig 11 :52-53 puts the residual three steps out in the
   DDT/eagles case. A direct-partner-only scope is structurally the wrong scope.
3. **Risk is per species, not an ecosystem average.** Fig 1 :19: "The residual is indexed by object
   slot, not aggregated." A global near-zero risk with one live dependent species is a legal state.
4. **There is no single sign.** Fig 11 :49-50 make valence relative to the residual: "The same actor
   is a benefactor to one residual and a malfactor to another, so mutualism and parasitism are
   relations rather than kinds." The same dependency can buffer one species and transmit failure to
   another. This is corroborated from low-overlap fields by May 1972 (connectance stable only to a
   critical point) and by the systemic-risk literature (connection as shock-absorber, then amplifier).
5. **Arrangement decides reach.** Fig 12 :117: "isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different
   substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour." Two
   communities with the same species list but different bonds are different objects with different
   risk. Where species sets differ but a provider-consumer substructure is shared, the weaker
   functional-group claim applies (Fig 12 :123).
6. **Dependency is ordered.** Fig 3 :20-21: "The chain is ordered by dependency, not by importance."
   An upstream break is not repaired by downstream effort.
7. **Management adds but cannot subtract.** Fig 11 :61-62: "The seat's power is asymmetric.
   Introduction is available; exclusion is not." Isolation substitutes a habitat rather than
   removing one (Fig 11 :66), so relocation carries unseen imports and unmet requirements.
8. **The ground decides.** Fig 11 :73: "The habitat is not the ground. The habitat is where actors
   come from and it updates; the ground is what settles, and it does not." Survival and extinction
   are the anchor; agreement among risk models sharing one dataset is one witness, not two.

## 3. What it explicitly does not say

- Not a numerical extinction probability for any species, and not a rank order of species at risk.
- Not that dependence always raises risk. The sign is residual-relative.
- Not that a standing list of "good partners" or "keystone species" can be read off a trait table.
- Not that the framework's "habitat" is an ecological niche. That mapping is declined in the
  canonical architecture essay; only the rules transfer.
- Not that Fig 12's formal bond types are ecological relations. Those are bindings needing a source
  gate, not derivations.
- Not `grounded`. No community was measured in this derivation.

## 4. What would settle it - the ground

A source-discriminating instrument, not a further argument. *The Hard Problem is About Source* shows a
colour reading that "cannot comment on the source"; the spectrometer resolved the case. Here the
corresponding tests are:

- **Contact-invariance test:** change a species' enumerated contact set in a real community and
  measure whether its extinction probability moves. No movement fires refuter R1.
- **Isomer test:** compare two communities with the same species set and different bond arrangements
  under the same perturbation. Identical trajectories fire R2.
- **Sign test:** measure one dependency type across two residuals. A constant sign fires R3.
- **Aggregate test:** find an ecosystem-level index that preserves the live species and predicts the
  next loss. Success fires R4.
- **Ground test:** show two models sharing one dataset settling a claim with no ground reading. If
  that counts as a verdict, the framework's anchor rule is wrong (R5).

## 5. The honest stop

**Unreached, not unreachable.** Fig 8 :39: "So a search that finds nothing is never a proof of absence."
The world sources establish cascades, thresholds and non-random pruning, but none of them is a ground
reading of this specific shape claim. The answer stops where the available instruments stop
discriminating buffer from transmission path, and records the lineage so the stop is checkable.
Indiscernibility is not identity. A `general` structural rule has been derived; a `grounded` ecological
verdict has not.

## 6. One-paragraph version for outreach

> The Tether does not give a number for how much extinction risk rises when species depend on one
> another. It gives the shape any such answer must have. Risk is a property of a species together with
> its contact web, so the contacts have to be enumerated outward past direct partners, and risk has to
> be reported per species rather than averaged. The sign of the change is not fixed: the same partner
> can buffer one species and carry failure to another, which is why connection helps up to a point and
> then propagates. What decides the reach is the arrangement of dependency bonds, so two communities
> with the same species list can behave differently. Dependencies are ordered from provider to
> dependent, so an upstream break is not repaired downstream, and management can add partners but
> cannot subtract the ambient web. That is a checkable shape with five refuters written in advance.
> The center stays open until a source-discriminating instrument settles it.
