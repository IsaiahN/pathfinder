## 1. A shape is a constraint, not a filler

The canonical README states the limit: the framework reports "what a missing thing would have to look
like (a shape, not a name)". Fig 9 :32: "you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself". Fig 13
says it can report that an answer is out of reach and characterise what is missing in effect terms,
but "It cannot say which primitive is missing, because naming it would mean already holding it."

From *The Library of Babel*: "You can cut your search short if the set of books returned is small and
descriptive enough that it provides a general shape of the answer that satisfies your question."

For this question a **shape** is the form of the risk change that can be stated before a particular
probability is measured. "Risk is a property of the species and its enumerated contact web" is a
shape. "Species X has a 34 percent extinction risk under a 20 percent pollinator loss" is a name and a
measurement, neither of which this derivation produces.

## 2. How a shape is obtained

- *Library of Babel*: use the vocabulary already held that is closest to the thing being described,
  then look for recurring new terms that overlap or correlate with it. "With each new term gained,
  you are able to narrow the space by definition and deepen your vocabulary."
- Fig 6 :30 "The description travels. Fails by being sharpened into terms only we can read. Cast it in
  effect terms." Fig 9 :30-31 "your words are borrowed too, so describe the effect rather than the cause."
- *Decoding Tacit Knowledge*: "When contact isn't possible, the best you can send is a plain
  description of what you'd expect to see, because anyone can check that against the world."
- *Library of Babel* on stopping: the answer is reached when "the finish line will also be the
  starting place"; by then "you have already composed the answer through the process of locating it."

Applied here: the effect terms are "change in failure probability when a contact relation changes",
not "biodiversity loss" or "ecosystem collapse". Naming the broad topic first would let any ecological
frame seem to fit (Fig 9 :27-28).

## 3. What licenses a name, and why this question does not reach one

Only two things, both outside the shape itself:

1. **The ground settles it.** Formula step 5: "the ground settles it. Verified only by what pays. A
   gate is not the ground." The README says the framework "stops at the limit of what the available
   instruments can distinguish, and records the lineage so the stop is honest."
2. **A provenanced import from a frame whose closure differs** (Fig 6 :23, Fig 8 :50-52), with a
   source gate and a shadow test.

*They're Scanning Books* sharpens the second point: "The real import is not a new term in the existing
space. It is a new dimension of the space." And: "A minimum viable set of six terms is a human's
careful guess at reality's compression axes; another sensorium's minimum set need not be those six."
Also: "Declaring the primitives does not make them neutral. It makes the choice visible."

For extinction risk, a name would require a discriminating instrument: an assay that shows, in a named
community, whether a given dependency acted as a buffer or a transmission path. The world sources
supply the mechanism-level evidence (cascades, thresholds, non-random pruning), but none of them is a
ground reading of the specific shape claim made here. So the answer stops at a shape.

## 4. The source error this question is prone to

*The Hard Problem is About Source* gives the mechanism. A colour instrument reads green and "cannot
comment on the source": one green light, or yellow plus blue, or RGB, or CMYK, or a scheme not yet
discovered. "If several paths create the same behavior, then behavior is a projection with lossy
information." An instrument that cannot distinguish these cases "demonstrates its own inadequacy".
It was the **spectrometer**, not an argument, that resolved it.

The ecological analogue: observing that two species declined together does not tell you whether the
dependency was a buffer, a transmission path, or a shared external cause. A coextinction reading is a
source named from a projection. What is owed is a source-discriminating instrument, not a story.

*The Recursive Self-Improvement Trap*: "the output is only as good as the validator. The agent, or the
tool, or the frame, or the theory, cannot validate itself." And "You may all agree and be wrong."

## 5. Q: Does "shape, not a name" forbid using ecological words here?

**Answered: no, with a scope note.** The README's phrase governs a **missing** primitive, one not held
in the library. The ecological relations in this question (food, pollination, habitat, reproduction)
are not missing primitives in the framework; Fig 11 :47 already lists "relations: pollination,
predation, competition, dependency". Using those words is reading the world's contact list, which is
exactly what Fig 11 :48 instructs: "You do not invent the list. You read it off the world."

What remains forbidden is naming a seventh mechanism or claiming that a binding moves the wall. The
canonical architecture essay also declines the habitat-as-niche mapping: this figure's "habitat" is
contact with a residual, not an organism's relation to its environment. So the answer uses the
figure's **rules** (enumerate contacts, valence relative to the residual, introduction without
exclusion) and explicitly does not claim that a food web is the framework's habitat.

## 6. Two consequences for this solution

**Prestige is not action.** From *The Recursive Self-Improvement Trap*: "Prestige must never be
allowed to validate an action directly... Prestige can fund an action. It can give an action a budget
to be tried. But it cannot settle an action. Only the ground can settle it. The Ground Maintainer must
be blind to prestige." A set of well-known ecologists agreeing with the shape is the named collapse,
not corroboration. The legitimate ask is to give the claim a budget to be tried and let a ground
reading settle it.

**My own audit is a simulation, not a ground reading.** Same essay: "LLMs can quickly simulate an
approximate answer/output with the formula" and "the truly testable output would need to be derived
from the real architecture or working the formula yourself." The architecture essay calls such
outputs "simulacra" and warns they "could never be verified if the solutions presented were not
contrived to fit the formula." So `01_figure_grounded_audit.md` is a simulated derivation checked for
internal consistency against the figures. It is **not** a test of the ecological claim and cannot
settle anything it reports on.

## 7. Correction to my own §3

I initially wrote that no name is reachable here because the framework only returns shapes. That is
too strong. The framework returns shapes **about what is missing from its own library**; a name
licensed by a ground reading or a provenanced import is allowed. For this question the gap is not the
framework's vocabulary, it is the missing source-discriminating instrument. The honest statement is
therefore: a name is not licensed *at this budget*, not that names are categorically forbidden. Both
readings are recorded so neither is quietly lost. This matches Fig 8 :39: "So a search that finds
nothing is never a proof of absence."
