## QUESTION

Decide, in effect terms, what the Tether says about the change in a species' extinction risk when it
depends on others for food, pollination, habitat or reproduction. Option A: risk is a property of the
species alone, so dependence adds nothing that traits cannot predict. Option B: risk is a property of
the species and its contact web, so dependence opens transmission paths and the sign and reach of the
change depend on which relations hold and how they are arranged. What each leads to: A produces a
trait-only risk index that is structurally blind to cascades and hides the live species in an average;
B produces a per-species shape, a union reading of the four dependency types, and five refuters
written in advance, but no numerical probability until a ground reading exists.

## DOMAIN CENSUS

R by arity, symmetry and scale, then one line per domain giving actors / currency / anchor, partition
or union, and order by dependency.

- **R (the residual).** The change in a species' failure probability when its contact relations change. Arity: relational, at least two species, with higher-order cases. Symmetry: mixed, directed food and habitat relations alongside mutual pollination and reproduction; mutualism and parasitism are relations rather than kinds. Scale: first order at direct partners, second and third order along the cascade. Fig 9 makes this a union compound, because a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either.
- **Domain 1, habitat and contact.** Actors: species, conditions, relations (food, pollination, habitat, reproduction, predation, competition). Currency: contact and cascade. Anchor: survival and extinction, the ground. Question type: enumeration of the field of contact, not composition.
- **Domain 2, bond and arrangement.** Actors: species as operands, dependency relations as typed bonds. Currency: arrangement and the information the bond carries beyond the operands. Anchor: observed survival. Question type: union, because one species sits in several relations at once, so unbundling is required before search.
- **Domain 3, assessment and verification.** Actors: risk assessors, models, monitoring instruments. Currency: per-slot residual and agreement. Anchor: measured survival and extinction, never agreement. In this simulated run all seats draw on one evidence pool, so this domain's independence is zero.
- **Order by dependency.** Enumerate contacts before assigning bonds; describe the residual before choosing a frame; settle first-order contacts before composing second-order cascades; refuse an invariance claim before minting a mechanism (Formula step 2).

## WORLD (secondary sources read)

World sources are corroboration only. They are independent of the framework author, though items 1 to 3 share a broad ecology pool with one another.

- **Theoretical ecology / mathematics.** Robert M. May, "Will a Large Complex System be Stable?", Nature 238, 1972. Quote: random connectance is stable up to a critical level and then "suddenly become[s] unstable". Predicts a threshold rather than a monotone increase. Corroborates the relative-valence and arrangement findings. Overlap: distinct from the framework; shares the ecology pool with the coextinction sources.
- **Ecology / mutualistic networks.** Enrico L. Rezende et al., "Non-random coextinctions in phylogenetically structured mutualistic networks", Nature 448, 2007. Quote: "simulated extinction events tend to trigger coextinction cascades of related species. This results in a non-random pruning of the evolutionary tree." Predicts cascades and non-uniform loss. Corroborates the per-slot and enumerate-outward findings. Overlap: distinct from the framework.
- **Ecology / evolutionary biology (secondary compilation).** Wikipedia contributors, "Coextinction", accessed 2026-11-23. Quotes Koh et al. 2004 Science: "Species coextinction is a manifestation of the interconnectedness of organisms in complex ecosystems"; and the curvilinear result that affiliate extinctions "may be modest at low levels of host extinction but can be expected to rise quickly as host extinctions increase to levels predicted in the near future." Predicts host-specific, accelerating loss. Corroborates cascade and threshold findings. The same article notes coextinction "may not be the most important cause of species extinctions", which bounds magnitude, not mechanism. Overlap: distinct from the framework, shares the ecology pool.
- **Reliability engineering / network science (analogue).** Wikipedia contributors, "Cascading failure", accessed 2026-11-23. Quote: "A cascading failure is a failure in a system of interconnected parts in which the failure of one or few parts leads to the failure of other parts, growing progressively as a result of positive feedback." Predicts propagation from a single loss. Corroborates the cascade and cycle findings. Overlap: low, different field.
- **Economics / finance (analogue).** Wikipedia contributors, "Systemic risk", accessed 2026-11-23. Quote: risks "imposed by interlinkages and interdependencies in a system or market, where the failure of a single entity or cluster of entities can cause a cascading failure". Reports Acemoglu et al. 2015: connection stabilises up to a point, then propagates risk. Predicts the same signed, threshold-like shape. Corroborates the relative-valence finding. Overlap: low, different field.

## VERDICT (from the figures)

The shape survives; any numerical or monotone answer does not. Extinction risk under dependence is a
property of the species and its enumerated contact web. Contacts must be read outward past direct
partners, risk reported per species, and the four dependency types unbundled as a union with their
intersections examined. The sign of the change is relative to the residual: connection buffers in one
range and propagates past a threshold, so dependence does not simply raise risk. Same species with
different bonds are a different object, with different risk. Dependencies are ordered from provider to
dependent, management adds but cannot subtract, and the ground is survival and extinction, not model
agreement. This is a `specified`, ungrounded candidate: no community was measured here, and the
canonical architecture essay's refusal of the habitat-as-niche mapping limits how far Fig 11 transfers.

- **cost if wrong:** bounded and locatable. The packet is mode-labeled, states refuters before the conclusion, and records the lineage, so an error is findable rather than invisible. The real cost is spending the independence of outside readers by sending a recording instead of a generator, which Fig 2 :42 prices ("Buy independence in proportion to the cost of being wrong."). That cost is avoidable by leading with the shape claim and its refuter.
- **undoable:** yes. The disputed readings (a monotone sign, the habitat-as-niche mapping, the treatment of formal bonds as ecological relations) are removable without touching the core enumeration rule. `07_the_tether_answer.md` and `08_expert_verification_packet.md` already remove them. No primitive has to be withdrawn.
- **reversed by:** a ground reading, not agreement. A contact-invariance experiment that leaves extinction probability unchanged would break the dependence claim; a study showing identical risk trajectories for same-species communities with different bonds would break the isomer claim; a dependency type with a constant sign across two communities would break the relative-valence claim; a source-discriminating instrument that shows what actually propagated a loss would name the mechanism. Until one of these fires, the verdict stands as `candidate`.
