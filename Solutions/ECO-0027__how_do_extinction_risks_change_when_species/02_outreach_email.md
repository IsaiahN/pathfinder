**Subject:** One checkable claim about extinction risk and species dependence

> Hi [name],
>
> I have one specific output I would like you to check against work you already know. I am not asking
> you to read the framework first, and I am not asking you to agree with it. If the output does not
> survive, there is nothing else to look at.
>
> **The output (`specified`, not `grounded`):** when a species depends on others for food, pollination,
> habitat or reproduction, its extinction risk is a property of the species and its contact web, not of
> the species alone. Three consequences follow from a figure-governed derivation:
>
> 1. The contacts have to be enumerated outward, first the direct partners and then the partners of
>    those partners, until the cascade stops mattering. A first-order-only assessment is structurally
>    the wrong scope.
> 2. Risk has to be reported per species. An ecosystem-level average can be near zero while one
>    dependent species is live.
> 3. There is no single sign. The same partner can lower one species' risk and raise another's. What
>    changes is the arrangement of the dependency bonds, so two communities with the same species list
>    can have different risk trajectories.
>
> **What I am not claiming:** that dependence always raises risk, that a named class of "good partners"
> exists, that this gives a numerical probability for any species, or that the framework's "habitat"
> is an ecological niche. The framework returns a shape, not a name, and this is a simulated
> derivation checked for internal consistency, not a measurement.
>
> **What would refute it:**
> 1. a measured change to a species' contact set that leaves its extinction probability unchanged;
> 2. two communities with the same species set but different dependency arrangements and identical
>    risk trajectories under the same perturbation;
> 3. one dependency type whose effect has the same sign for every residual, in two different communities;
> 4. an aggregate index that preserves which species is live and predicts the next loss out of sample;
> 5. two models sharing one data source settling a risk claim with no ground reading.
>
> I wrote these down before sending this. If any holds, I retract the corresponding claim. A one-line
> "this fails because..." is more useful to me than agreement. If you would rather not engage, no reply
> is needed.
>
> **If it survives, and only then:** the sources are below. The figures, not my summary of them, are
> the authority.
> - Canonical figure text with line numbers (local extract): `FRAMEWORK/reference/03-FIGURES_TEXT.md`
> - The Habitat and The Bond figures, Figures 11 and 12, in the same extract
> - Arrival of the Fittest: The Tether Architecture: https://adventuresinml.substack.com/p/arrival-of-the-fittest-the-tether
>
> **If you want to use the machinery:** write down what would refute your own result *before* you run
> it, and state the mode of what you get. The framework's own rule is that a loop cannot check the
> direction of its own question. A reported failure is more useful to me than a reported success.
>
> Thanks for your time.

## Distribution rules

One recipient at a time. No list ranked by likely agreement, because that manufactures exactly the
correlation that disqualifies a verdict (Fig 2 :40). Keep the recipient's stage-1 verdict on record
before any framework material is shown; later agreement is never pooled as corroboration.
