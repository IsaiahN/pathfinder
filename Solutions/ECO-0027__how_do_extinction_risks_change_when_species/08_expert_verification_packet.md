# Expert verification packet - ECO-0027 extinction risk under species dependence

**What this is:** a claim in a form an ecologist or conservation scientist can check. Mode, derivation
and refuters are stated before the conclusion, so a reader knows what to look for and what would settle it.

**Mode: `specified` on the substantive claims, `general` on the structural shape, not `grounded`.**
No numerical extinction probability is claimed. This is a shape and a route, not a result.

## 1. The claim, in one paragraph

When a species depends on others for food, pollination, habitat or reproduction, its extinction risk
is a property of the species and its enumerated contact web, not of the species alone. The contacts
must be read outward past direct partners; risk must be reported per species, never as an ecosystem
average; and the sign of the change is not fixed, because the same partner can buffer one species and
transmit failure to another. What decides the reach is the arrangement of dependency bonds, so two
communities with the same species list can have different risk trajectories. Dependencies are ordered
from provider to dependent, and management can add partners but cannot subtract the ambient web. The
framework returns this shape, not a number, and states five refuters in advance.

## 2. Three checkable sub-claims

### C1 - framework-internal; checkable by reading Figures 11 and 1

- **Statement:** the contact web must be enumerated outward, and risk must be indexed per species.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 11 :43 "The habitat is enumerated, never composed." Fig 11 :44 "List
  everything in contact with the residual, then what is in contact with those, and outward until the
  cascade stops mattering." Fig 11 :47 "relations: pollination, predation, competition, dependency".
  Fig 1 :19 "The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated."
- **What to check:** whether the quoted lines say what is claimed, and whether a direct-partner-only
  scope is structurally the same error as the DDT case at Fig 11 :52-53.
- **Refuter:** a measured change to a species' enumerated contact set that leaves its extinction
  probability unchanged (R1), or an aggregate index that preserves the live species and predicts the
  next loss out of sample (R4).

### C2 - ecology-facing; checkable against the coextinction and network literature

- **Statement:** the sign of the risk change is relative to the residual, and the arrangement of bonds
  carries information the species list does not. Same species, different bonds, different risk.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 11 :49 "An actor's valence is relative to the residual, not a property it
  carries." Fig 11 :50 "The same actor is a benefactor to one residual and a malfactor to another, so
  mutualism and parasitism are relations rather than kinds." Fig 12 :117 "isomer. Same atoms,
  different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the
  behaviour."
- **What to check:** does the ecology literature support a threshold or signed effect of connection
  rather than a monotone one? May 1972 (Nature 238, 413-414) reports connectance stable only to a
  critical level; the systemic-risk literature reports connection helping up to a point and then
  propagating risk; Rezende et al. 2007 (Nature 448, 925-928) report non-random coextinction cascades
  shaped by network architecture.
- **Refuter:** one dependency type with the same sign of effect across two different communities (R3),
  or two communities with identical species sets and different bond arrangements and identical risk
  trajectories under the same perturbation (R2).

### C3 - framework-internal derivation, with an ecology-facing consequence

- **Statement:** dependencies are ordered from provider to dependent, and management is asymmetric.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 3 :20-21 "The chain is ordered by dependency, not by importance. Each link
  consumes what the one above produces, so effort spent above the break arrives at a step that cannot
  use it." Fig 11 :61-62 "The seat's power is asymmetric. Introduction is available; exclusion is not.
  You can add an actor and you cannot subtract the habitat, because contact is a fact about the world
  and does not consult you."
- **What to check:** is the provider-to-dependent ordering faithful to the system being assessed, and
  can a conservation intervention actually remove a contact rather than substitute one?
- **Refuter:** a downstream intervention that fully prevents an upstream-caused loss without changing
  the upstream contact, or a demonstrated exclusion that removes a contact rather than substituting
  a habitat (R6).

## 3. What is explicitly not claimed

- Not a numerical extinction probability, a species ranking, or a forecast for any named ecosystem.
- Not that dependence always raises risk. Valence is residual-relative.
- Not a standing list of "good partners", "keystone species" or "most important interactions".
- Not that the framework's "habitat" is an ecological niche. The canonical architecture essay
  declines that mapping; only the rules transfer.
- Not that Fig 12's formal bond types are ecological relations. Ecological readings are bindings
  needing a source gate and a shadow test, not derivations.
- Not `grounded`. This packet is a simulated derivation checked for internal consistency against the
  figures, not a ground reading. It cannot settle the claim it reports on.

## 5. What would settle it

A ground reading, not more agreement. From *The Hard Problem is About Source*, a colour instrument
reads green but "cannot comment on the source"; the spectrometer, not an argument, resolved the case.
The corresponding tests here are:

1. Contact-invariance: change a species' contact set and measure its extinction probability.
2. Isomer: compare same-species, different-bond communities under one perturbation.
3. Sign: measure one dependency type across two residuals.
4. Aggregate: find an index that preserves the live species and predicts the next loss.
5. Ground: require an actual survival or extinction outcome, never model-to-model agreement.

## 6. What an expert can check in twenty minutes

1. Read Fig 11 :43-47 and :49-50, Fig 1 :19, Fig 12 :117, Fig 3 :20-21. Six short passages.
2. Check C2 against their own knowledge: is the effect of connection on extinction risk signed or
   threshold-like in the systems they know?
3. Name one instrument or dataset that would discriminate a buffer from a transmission path in a
   named community, or say why none is available.
4. Send the refutation. A failure is more useful than an endorsement.
