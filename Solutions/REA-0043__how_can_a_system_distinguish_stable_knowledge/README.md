## The question restated as an effect

What is being decided is not *how much* a system remembers but *what a term is entitled to be used for*. In effect terms: the library Γ holds terms; a term can be treated as stable knowledge (a generator that can be re-applied and composed over), as a temporary fact (a recording of one occasion that paid once), or as a context-specific assumption (true only inside one frame's closure). The discrimination is hard because all three look identical in the stored artifact. Fig 4 :25: *"A recording carried upward looks like knowledge and is a description of one occasion."*

- **Option A, "stability is a property written into the term":** leads to the prediction that a term carries its own certificate and a system need only read it. The figures refuse this: the bond and the origin are not recoverable from the operands (Fig 12 :5, Fig 8 :51).
- **Option B, "stability is a relation between a term, a ground, a slot, and a closure, maintained by a channel":** leads to the prediction that the certificate can move and must be re-earned, that verdicts are per slot, and that a term can be stable in one frame and an assumption in another. This is the verdict recorded in `09_watson_answer.md`.
- **What an answer would look like, bound as an ordinary term before the run** (Formula, "Direction"): a *shape* naming the four instruments that must agree before a term is treated as stable, plus an instrument that would discriminate a failure of one from a failure of another. Until that shape is written down and bound, it is a preference, predicts nothing, and no check in the loop can see it (Fig 5 :62).

The shape the figures license: a term is stable **only if** (1) the ground has settled it, (2) it remains usable as a composer-over atom, (3) it survives the round trip as a method rather than a recording, with a measured gap `R_T`, and (4) its provenance and closure are recorded, so a convergent derivation is not silently confused with an adopted assumption. These are four conditions in four domains with different anchors. They are never averaged into one score.

## What is deliberately not here

- No personal emails of named individuals.
- No ranking of people by how likely they are to agree.
- No bulk-send plan.
- No claim that this derivation is `grounded`, no claim that it settles the memory question, and no claim that the framework is the only true account of stable knowledge.
- No numerical stability threshold. The figures return a shape, not a cutoff (Fig 9 :32, Fig 13 :134).

Three figure-grounded reasons, plus one practical one:

1. A room is not an anchor. *"A room's agreement is variation, not a verdict. Selection between rooms is where the ground votes."* (Fig 7 :35) and *"Alignment is triangulation, not negotiation. A mirror cannot correct you."* (Fig 2 :35). Gathering agreeable readers manufactures the correlation that disqualifies a verdict.
2. Correlation has valence. *"disqualifying for a verdict: agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool"* (Fig 2 :40). A list selected for receptiveness is one witness wearing many names.
3. A verifier must be outside the interior. *"A verifier that can reconstruct the claim cannot verify it."* (Fig 10 :42). The first contact therefore carries the residual and its refuter, not the framework.
4. Legally, unsolicited bulk email to scraped addresses breaches GDPR and CAN-SPAM style rules in many jurisdictions and gets the sending domain blacklisted. Public opt-in routes avoid that failure as well as the structural one.
