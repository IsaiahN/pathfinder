## DERIVATION

### Q1 - What atoms are held?

Every class is a term in Γ, so the atom set is the same at the start:

```
proposition p   (what is taken to be the case, Symbols b)
slot s          (one object or region, Symbols s)
ground contact  (what pays, Fig 5 :70)
origin record   (where p came from, Fig 10 :62)
closure tag     (which frame's closure derived p, Fig 6 :23)
```

No new primitive is available without import (Fig 6 :21 *"MINT composes inside the closure and can never add an atom."*). The available bonds are the seven plus negation (Operators table). The question is therefore about **arrangement**, not inventory.

### Q2 - Are the three classes isomers? (`specified`, unmeasured)

Fig 12 :117: **"Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."**
Fig 12 :95: **"Two entries with an identical ingredient set are not variants of one thing. They are different things whose notation cannot tell them apart, and the difference is carried entirely by the bond."**

If all three classes are written over the atom set above, differing only in how ground contact and origin are bonded to the proposition, then they satisfy the figure's definition: same atoms, different arrangement, different behaviour. Concretely:

| class | arrangement (proposed, `specified`) | behaviour |
|---|---|---|
| stable knowledge | ground contact bonded re-applicably; proposition can become an atom for a later composition (Fig 12 :104) | survives the producing occasion, can be composed over |
| temporary fact | ground contact bonded to one slot only; standing to be composed over is refused by ¬ | pays once, cannot generate a new success |
| context-specific assumption | origin and closure tag bonded into the proposition's identity within one frame (Fig 8 :51) | pays here, fails on a frame swap |

The **difference is decisive in behaviour** and invisible in the stored proposition, exactly as Fig 12 requires. That is the real isomer claim worth testing.

**What is not figure-derived:** which specific bond symbol realises each row. The figures give the test for a bond, not the assignment. Assigning → to "re-applicable" or ¬ to "refused" is a binding from our vocabulary onto the bond set, and must be recorded as such (Fig 9 :30: *"your words are borrowed too, so describe the effect rather than the cause"*). Calling the assignment a derivation would be the apophenia error the exemplar names in its own retraction log.

**Falsifier, written before the run:** if any class requires an atom the others do not (for example, if a context-specific assumption needs a frame atom that stable knowledge lacks), the isomer claim fails. The classes are then different functional groups, not arrangements of one set. A second falsifier: if two classes with the same atoms and the same bonds behave identically under a ground test, the arrangement is not decisive and Fig 12 :117 does not apply.

### Q3 - The weaker functional-group claim (survives if Q2 fails)

Fig 12 :124: **"functional group. A substructure whose presence fixes the outcome. The group says what kind; the qualifier says which member."**

All three classes share the substructure `term bonded to a slot`. The qualifier (ground-settled, once-paid, closure-bound) says which member. This reading requires no claim about which bond attaches where, and it holds even if the atom sets differ slightly. It is the honest fallback and is recorded separately, never averaged with the isomer claim.

### Q4 - Catalyst and cycle analogues (`specified`)

Fig 12 :119: **"catalyst. A part that survives its own use. Spent and regenerating parts behave differently across uses, and nothing records which a term is."**
Fig 12 :121: **"cycle. The output re-enters as input. The sign of the loop decides whether it closes, and a chain cannot close by construction."**

- Stable knowledge behaves as a **catalyst**: it survives its own use and stays available for the next composition. A temporary fact is spent. Fig 12 :119 says nothing records which a term is, which is precisely why an external instrument is needed.
- A memory system that re-ingests its own outputs as evidence for those same outputs is a **positive-feedback cycle**. Fig 2 :33-34 describes the result: *"everything drifts together, and the drift is coherent, which is why nobody notices."* This is a different failure from a temporary fact, even though both show a residual that grows.
- **Falsifier:** a system that re-ingests its own outputs and yet keeps its certificate stable under a ground test with an independent anchor. Then the cycle analogy as a failure mode is wrong for that system and must be narrowed.

### Q5 - Dependency order of the four instruments (`general`)

Fig 3 :20: **"The chain is ordered by dependency, not by importance. Each link consumes what the one above produces, so effort spent above the break arrives at a step that cannot use it."**
Fig 3 :19: **"Optimising a link that already works cannot rescue one that does not."**
Fig 3 :27: **"A link with no instrument is not a link that works. It is a link nobody has looked at."**

```
D4 anchor reachable  ->  D1 ground settlement  ->  D2 round trip  ->  D3 closure provenance
     (perception)          (vocabulary/objective)      (planning)         (carry)
```

- D4 is perception: if the channel is stale, every later instrument reads a dead ground (Fig 10 :25 *"stale percepts, dropped transients, saturated metrics, pooled readings"*).
- D1 is the middle link that usually breaks: having a word for "settled" is not a test for what counts as settlement here. This is Fig 3 :25-27's least-instrumented link.
- D2 requires D1: there is no point measuring the round trip of a term the ground never paid for.
- D3 requires D2: a term that fails as a method is classified as temporary before its closure tag becomes relevant.
- **Reading below the break is reading nothing** (Fig 3 :23: *"It is a reading of nothing."*). Running D3 on a system with a dead channel produces a closure report about stale data.

**Falsifier:** an ordering in which D3 is run first and still correctly classifies a term when D4 is broken. If that is possible, the dependency chain is wrong and the four instruments are independent, not ordered.

### Q6 - Competing explanations when a term stops paying (union, unbundle)

Three candidates explain the same observation (a term that used to pay and no longer does):

1. **The world moved.** The ground changed and the term is now false. Anchor: the ground, re-measured.
2. **The channel decayed.** The ground did not move; the reading did. Fig 10 :24: *"the ground does not decay, the channel does."* Anchor: anchor reachability (D4).
3. **It was a recording all along.** The term paid for one occasion and has no re-applicable structure. Fig 4 :25: *"A recording carried upward looks like knowledge and is a description of one occasion."* Anchor: the round trip (D2).

Fig 9 :54: *"when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again."* These three overlap: a stale channel can make a stable term look temporary, and a recording can be mistaken for a changed world. Fig 9 :55: *"A single lever clears the region where only one condition holds and leaves the intersections standing, because the second condition still binds."* So the system must run all three tests and keep the intersection as a **compound** with its own loop, not average them into one "stability score".

**Falsifier:** a single instrument that discriminates all three without a ground reading and without a channel check. None of the figures licenses such an instrument; if one is exhibited, the union claim is refuted.

## CONCLUSION

The three classes are **isomers** if they share the atom set and differ only in arrangement, with a stated falsifier on atom-set equality. The weaker **functional-group** reading survives if that falsifier fires. Their dependency order is D4 to D1 to D2 to D3, because each instrument consumes what the previous one produces (Fig 3). Three competing explanations for a term that stops paying form a union, not a partition, and must be unbundled before search.

Every proposal in this file is `specified` and unmeasured. Which bond realises each class, whether the atom sets are truly equal, and whether the dependency order holds in an implemented memory system are all open. Nothing here is `grounded`.
