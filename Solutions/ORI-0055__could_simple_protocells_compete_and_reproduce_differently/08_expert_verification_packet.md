# Expert verification packet: ORI-0055, protocell competition and differential reproduction

**What this is:** a claim in a form a origins-of-life researcher, evolutionary biologist or physical chemist can check. Mode, derivation and refuters are stated before the conclusion, so a reader knows what to look for and what would settle it.

**Mode: `general` on the structural shape, `specified` on the per-domain verdicts, `gap` on the historical chemistry. Not `grounded`.** Simulated derivation checked for internal consistency against the figures, not a ground reading.

## 1. The claim, in one paragraph

"Could simple protocells compete and reproduce differently?" is a union of two conditions, not one yes/no. Competition: yes, because an unmaintained habitat needs no selector seat and the ground settles which variant persists. Differential reproduction: conditional, because progress across generations is a product of heritability and selection differential; competition supplies the second factor, and a heritable carrier plus a compartment supplies the first. Difference from the same ingredients is an arrangement difference, an isomer over a fixed atom set, or the weaker functional-group claim when atom sets differ. Which protocell actually did this on early Earth is a declared `gap`: the laboratory demonstrations are substituted habitats and prove wiring, not a historical capability. The framework returns this shape and its refuters, then stops.

## 2. Three checkable sub-claims

### C1 - framework-internal; checkable by reading Figures 11 and 7

- **Statement:** no maintainer is required for selection to run. Differential persistence is the ground's vote among varying compartments; "nothing selects" means no intentional selector, not no differential survival.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 11 :36 "Where nothing maintains anything, intelligence is a label applied afterwards to a lineage that met its ground. Nothing was intelligent; selection ran." Also Fig 11 :56 "Unmaintained. No maintainer and no live frame in the seat. The habitat is pure contact, nothing selects, and what survives is what the ground kept." And Fig 7 :35 "A room's agreement is variation, not a verdict. Selection between rooms is where the ground votes."
- **Refuter:** show two chemically identical populations under identical conditions drifting with no measurement that discriminates which persists, so the ground does not settle the outcome; or show that an intentional selector seat is necessary for any differential persistence in a chemical system.

### C2 - evolution-facing; checkable against the heritability requirement

- **Statement:** differential reproduction requires both a selection differential and heritable variation, and the compartment keeps genotype and phenotype linked. Competition alone is pre-Darwinian.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 7 :26 "progress per handoff: R = h² × S". Also Fig 7 :33 "→ variation inside, selection between", and the Fig 7 :37 line that both factors are required and zero either stops the chain. Fig 12 :121 "cycle. The output re-enters as input. The sign of the loop decides whether it closes, and a chain cannot close by construction."
- **What an expert should check:** in the cited experiments (Chen 2004, Adamala and Szostak 2013, Katla 2023, Abil 2024), is a heritable trait actually measured, or only a one-generation growth advantage? Adamala and Szostak explicitly say their catalyst is not heritable, so their system cannot yet evolve. Abil et al. show parasite takeover in bulk but persistence in liposomes. Is the genotype-phenotype link the operative variable in both cases?
- **Refuter:** show a population with a heritable advantage, fixed environment and limiting resource whose trait frequency does not change across generations; or show that population-average growth predicts the winning lineage and fixing trait without per-lineage data. This would break Fig 7 :26 and Fig 1 :19.

### C3 - structural; checkable by reading Figure 12

- **Statement:** "reproduce differently" is an arrangement or bond difference, not an ingredient difference. Two protocell populations sharing a chemistry but differing in membrane composition or encapsulated catalyst stand as isomers over a fixed atom set; where atom sets differ, the claim reduces to a functional group.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 12 :117 "isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour." Fig 12 :94 "The arrangement is the substance." Fig 12 :123 "functional group. A substructure whose presence fixes the outcome. The group says what kind; the qualifier says which member." Also Fig 12 :119 "catalyst. A part that survives its own use."
- **Refuter:** show that the differing populations have different ingredient lists (isomer claim fails); or that they share ingredients and arrangement yet differ in persistence under identical conditions (valence claim needs re-census).

## 4. What is explicitly not claimed

- Not that a laboratory flask demonstrates abiogenesis. Fig 11 :70: a test harness is a substituted habitat; a synthetic solve proves wiring and never capability.
- Not that competition is Darwinian evolution. The heritability factor must be measured and reported.
- Not that any named lipid mixture, catalyst or genotype is the historical winner. A shape, not a name, and the correction in `05` section 5 narrows that rule to missing primitives without licensing a historical name from a projection.
- Not that protocells possess fitness as an intrinsic property. Valence is relative to the residual (Fig 11 :49).
- Not that all protocells compete under all conditions. With abundant resource there is no exclusion.
- Not `grounded`. No measurement was taken by this agent.

## 6. What would settle it

1. A per-lineage heritability measurement for the trait that confers faster growth, across several generations.
2. A test of `R = h² × S`: does trait frequency track the advantage under a fixed environment and limiting resource?
3. A reconstructed prebiotic habitat in which the flask wiring transfers. That retires the `gap` rather than confirming the mechanism.

## 7. What an expert can check in twenty minutes

1. Read Fig 11 :36, :56, :70 and Fig 7 :26, :33, :35, :37, :26 (short passages, two figures).
2. Look at Adamala and Szostak 2013 discussion: is the "cannot yet evolve" sentence present? It is the cleanest real-world check of the `h² = 0` branch.
3. Look at Abil et al. 2024 comparison of liposome and bulk evolution: did compartments change the outcome, as the genotype-phenotype-link claim predicts?
4. Decide whether C1 to C3 survive. Name one failure. A failure is more useful than an endorsement.
