# The Tether's answer: could simple protocells compete and reproduce differently?

**Mode: `general` on the structural shape, `specified` on the three per-domain verdicts, `gap` on which chemistry won on early Earth. Not `grounded`.** Simulated derivation checked against the figures, not a ground reading (`05_what_shape_means.md` section 6).

## 1. The residual it answers

The question bundles two conditions. Competition is an ecological relation among compartments in one habitat. Differential reproduction is a hereditary relation across a chain of compartments. Fig 9 :54-58 treats a case in two conditions as a specific compound, not a harder case of either, so the answer is a separator with per-domain verdicts. The effect to explain: why one protocell lineage persists and its trait increases in frequency while another shrinks, with the residual indexed per lineage (Fig 1 :19).

## 2. The shape

1. **No maintainer is required.** In an unmaintained habitat the loop still runs. Fig 11 :36 "Where nothing maintains anything, intelligence is a label applied afterwards to a lineage that met its ground. Nothing was intelligent; selection ran." A prebiotic protocell population needs no breeder and no selecting seat. Fig 11 :56 "Unmaintained. No maintainer and no live frame in the seat. The habitat is pure contact, nothing selects, and what survives is what the ground kept." Re-census (F8 of `01`): "nothing selects" means no intentional selector; the ground still settles which variants persist. Fig 7 :35 "A room's agreement is variation, not a verdict. Selection between rooms is where the ground votes." The protocells are the rooms.

2. **Competition is a habitat relation with residual-relative valence.** Fig 11 :47 "relations: pollination, predation, competition, dependency". Fig 11 :49 "An actor's valence is relative to the residual, not a property it carries." A vesicle is a competitor or a resource depending on which lineage's residual is tracked. Where a resource is limiting, advantage is real and one population can exclude another.

3. **Reproduction "differently" needs two positive factors.** Fig 7 :26 "progress per handoff: R = h² × S". Fig 7 :33 "→ variation inside, selection between". Fig 7 :37 says both factors are needed and zero either stops the chain. Competition supplies `S`; a heritable carrier supplies `h²`. Without the second, the process is pre-Darwinian: competition happens, but descendants do not inherit the winning difference and no accumulation occurs. Adamala and Szostak 2013 state this limit for their own system: the catalyst is not heritable, so the system cannot yet evolve.

4. **The compartment is what keeps the two factors together.** Abil et al. 2024 found the full-length DNA replicator persisted in liposomes but was displaced by shorter parasites in bulk. The genotype-phenotype link is a structural requirement, not a detail. Fig 12 :121 "cycle. The output re-enters as input. The sign of the loop decides whether it closes, and a chain cannot close by construction." A lineage that loses contents faster than it grows is an open loop.

5. **"Different" is a bond difference.** Fig 12 :117 "isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour." Fig 12 :94 "The arrangement is the substance." Two vesicle populations with the same ingredients but different membrane composition or encapsulated catalyst are isomers over a fixed atom set; when the atom sets differ, the weaker and safer claim is a functional group (Fig 12 :123). The objection about how they could be made of the same stuff and behave differently is answered by the bond, not by a new ingredient.

6. **A heritable polymer enters by import, not by lipid composition.** Fig 6 :21 "MINT composes inside the closure and can never add an atom." Composing amphiphiles forever does not yield a template. The RNA-world or DNA frame supplies the atom, with a source gate and a shadow test (Fig 8). Calling the genotype a product of vesicle chemistry would be an unprovenanced import.

7. **The historical answer is a `gap`.** Fig 11 :70 "A test harness is a substituted habitat. Which is why a synthetic solve proves wiring and never capability." Every cited experiment is a prepared flask. Fig 13 :134 "So the system can report that it lacks something rather than producing an answer anyway. Unreachable is a verdict here, not a silence," and Fig 9 :32 "you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself." No figure line names the prebiotic chemistry that actually ran this, and no instrument available here can discriminate it.

## 4. What it explicitly does not say

- Not that the laboratory demonstrations prove abiogenesis. A flask is a substituted habitat.
- Not that competition alone is Darwinian evolution. The heritability factor must be measured.
- Not that a named lipid mixture or genotype is the historical answer. The framework returns a shape, not a name, and the correction in `05` section 5 narrows that rule to missing primitives without licensing a historical name from a projection.
- Not that a protocell has "fitness" as a property. Fitness here is a contact relation with a habitat, indexed per lineage.
- Not that all protocells compete. With abundant resource there is no exclusion; valence flips with the residual.
- Not `grounded`. Nothing in this folder is an experimental measurement by this agent.

## 5. What would settle it, and the standing refuters

A discriminator instrument, not more agreement. The questions a ground could answer:

1. Does a specific protocell lineage have measurable `h²` for the trait that confers faster growth, per lineage and across at least several generations?
2. In a fixed environment with a limiting resource, does trait frequency track the advantage, as `R = h² × S` requires?
3. Can a proposed prebiotic habitat be reconstructed well enough that the wiring demonstrated in a flask transfers to it? That would retire the `gap`, not merely confirm the mechanism.

Standing refuters (pre-registered in `01` section 6): no trait-frequency change despite heritable advantage and limiting resource; two identical populations drifting with no discriminating measurement; a new heritable atom derived by composition with no import; a population average predicting the winner without per-lineage data; one lever clearing both conditions at once. Any one fires against the shape claim.

## 6. The honest stop

**Unreached, not unreachable in principle.** The three-condition separator is available now and is checkable in any competent laboratory. The historical claim is unreachable at this budget because no instrument here discriminates among the sources of an ancient growth reading. Fig 9 :44: *"Only a sealed room can be searched to the end, and no frame is a sealed room. So searching and finding nothing proves nothing."* The lineage of the derivation is recorded so the stop is checkable, and indiscernibility is not identity.

## 7. One-paragraph version for outreach

> The Tether does not name the first protocell and does not claim a flask proves abiogenesis. It says that no maintainer is needed for selection to run, so simple compartments can compete by ordinary chemistry, and a compartmentalised difference with a non-updating ground is enough for one lineage to persist at another's expense. It also says that "reproduce differently" is a separate condition: progress across generations is a product, heritability times selection differential, so competition alone is pre-Darwinian until a heritable carrier and a compartment keeping genotype and phenotype together are present. Different behaviour from the same ingredients is a difference of arrangement, an isomer in the figure's sense, not a new ingredient. What the framework returns is that shape, a per-lineage measurement rule, five refuters written in advance, and a recorded gap for which chemistry actually won. The gap is a verdict, not silence.
