## QUESTION

Decide, in effect terms, whether simple protocells can compete and reproduce differently from one another. Option A: yes, differential competition and reproduction are already available to simple chemistry, so a cell-level Darwinian process follows. Option B: no, vesicle competition is mere physics and differential reproduction needs an imported heritable polymer and machinery, so the answer is no until that import arrives. What each leads to: A risks presenting a substituted laboratory habitat as an abiogenesis result and conflating competition with evolution; B risks ignoring that an unmaintained habitat runs selection without a seat and that competitive growth is experimentally real. The figure-derived third option is a union separator: competition yes, differential reproduction conditional on a measured heritable carrier and a compartment, and the historical which-one a `gap`.

- **Domain 3, heredity and evolution chain.** Actors: variants, genotype and phenotype carriers, catalysts, parasites, compartments as rooms. - **X2, "life" as a seventh primitive.** No nameable anchor; capability is a property of agent-and-habitat (Fig 11 :39). Absorbed into Domain 3.
- **Partition or union.** Union. D1 and D2 overlap because competition is a membrane-level physical process; D2 and D3 overlap because a selection differential is produced by competition. A single lever clears only one condition and leaves the intersection standing (Fig 9 :55-58).
- **Order by dependency.** Measure differential growth before scoring competition; settle competition before composing heredity over it (Fig 12 :104); describe the residual before choosing a frame (Fig 9 :27-28).

## VERDICT (from the figures)

The question is a union. Competition: yes, `specified`, triangulated. An unmaintained habitat runs selection without a seat, competition is a habitat relation with residual-relative valence, and the ground settles which protocell persists. Differential reproduction: conditional, `specified`. Progress across generations is `R = h² × S`; competition supplies `S`, and a heritable carrier plus a compartment that keeps genotype and phenotype linked supplies `h²`. Competition alone is pre-Darwinian, and Adamala and Szostak 2013 state exactly that limit for their system. Difference from shared ingredients is an isomer or functional-group difference, not a new ingredient. Historical which-one: `gap`, unreachable at this budget. Every flask is a substituted habitat and proves wiring, never capability. Verdicts are kept per domain and never averaged. This is a shape and a refuter set, not a ground reading.

- **cost if wrong:** bounded and locatable. The packet states mode, three checkable sub-claims and five refuters before any conclusion, so an error is findable. The real cost is presenting a substituted habitat as an abiogenesis result, which would spend the independence of outside readers by handing them a recording instead of a generator (Fig 4 :22, :25). That cost is avoidable by leading with the separator and its refuters.
- **undoable:** yes. The three per-domain verdicts can be narrowed without touching the derivation. A measured `h²` in a specific protocell would move Domain 3 from conditional to affirmative for that system; a reconstructed prebiotic habitat would retire the `gap`. Nothing has to be withdrawn wholesale, and no primitive is lost.
- **reversed by:** a ground reading, not agreement. A per-lineage heritability measurement plus a test of `R = h² × S` under a fixed environment and limiting resource would confirm or break Domain 3; two identical populations drifting with no discriminating measurement would break the ground claim; a new heritable atom derived by composition with no import would break Fig 6 :21; a population average predicting the winner without per-lineage data would break Fig 1 :19. Until one of these fires, the verdict stands as `candidate`.
