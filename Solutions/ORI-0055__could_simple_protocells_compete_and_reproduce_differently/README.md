## The residual in effect terms

The question splits into two conditions joined by "and": (1) can one simple compartment grow and persist at the expense of another when a resource is limited, and (2) can the difference be carried across generations so that descendants reproduce differently? Option A says yes to both, so a cell-level Darwinian process is already available to simple chemistry. Option B says the first is mere physics and the second needs an import (a heritable polymer, an enzyme), so the answer is no until that import arrives. The residual is indexed per protocell lineage, never averaged into a population growth rate (Fig 1 :19, Fig 1 :22). What an answer would look like, bound as an ordinary term before the run: a separator that tells whether a given protocell population has variation, a non-updating ground that pays for differential persistence, and a heritable carrier that keeps the difference across generations, plus an instrument that discriminates a failure of each condition. That shape is now refusable by the refuter in `01_figure_grounded_audit.md` section 6.

## Verdict in one paragraph

The question is a union of three conditions with different anchors, not one yes/no (Fig 9 :54-58). Competition: yes, `specified` and triangulated. Simple vesicles compete by physical chemistry, and one population can exclude another (Fig 11 :47, Fig 7 :35). Differential reproduction: conditional, `specified`. Fig 7 :26 gives the shape `progress per handoff: R = h² × S`. Both a selection differential and heritable variation must be positive; if either is zero the chain runs in place (Fig 7 :37). Competition alone supplies `S`, but without a heritable carrier `h²` is zero and the result is pre-Darwinian (this is exactly the limit the Adamala and Szostak 2013 system states about itself). Which protocell actually did this on early Earth: a declared `gap` of the unreachable-at-this-budget kind (Fig 13 :134, Fig 8 :39). A laboratory flask is a substituted habitat, and a synthetic solve proves wiring and never capability (Fig 11 :70). Verdicts are kept per domain and never averaged.
