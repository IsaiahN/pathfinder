## Relevant wider literature, and what each bears on

- **Mechanism shape, chemistry pool.** Chen 2004, Adamala and Szostak 2013, Shirt-Ediss 2014, Joyce and Szostak 2018, Armstrong 2018. All on physically driven vesicle growth and competition. They are one broad origin-of-life chemistry evidence pool, not five independent witnesses. They bear on D1 and D2, not on the historical claim.
- **Heredity and the compartment's role.** Abil et al. 2024 is the strongest single result for the genotype-phenotype link: full-length replicator persists in liposomes and is lost in bulk. It bears on D3 and on the `h² > 0` condition. It is a synthetic biology lab result, therefore a substituted habitat (Fig 11 :70).
- **Biochemistry-free competition.** Katla et al. 2023 and 2025 bear on whether the competition condition needs modern biochemistry. Answer: it does not. Same lab, one witness. It corroborates the Fig 11 :49 valence claim (advantage is relative to the habitat, not carried as an essence).
- **Union of reproducers and replicators.** PNAS 2023 bears on the structural claim that cells are a compound of compartment and genome. It corroborates the union reading in `01` section 1.3.
- **Framework essays.** The Recursive Self-Improvement Trap supplies the simulation disclaimer and the prestige rule; Library of Babel supplies the shape vocabulary. Both share the framework author's evidence pool with the figures, so their agreement with the figures is expected and carries little independent weight.

No world source was found that conflicts with the figure verdict. The conflict rule (return to the domain census, never flip the verdict) was therefore not triggered. The one near conflict, "the catalyst system competes but cannot evolve", was resolved as a confirmation of the `h² = 0` branch rather than a contradiction.

## Distribution: offer, do not push

Figure-grounded reasons:

- **Fig 10 :52**, *"Bring a characterised residual, never the source."* Sending the full framework before anyone describes a residual sends a recording, not a generator. Lead with the three-condition separator and its refuters.
- **Fig 4 :22**, *"Only methods cross up. Only head starts come down."* A method can be given away; a lab result replayed into a new habitat is a recording of one occasion (Fig 4 :25).
- **Fig 9 :27-28**, *"describe it before you go looking, or any frame you pick will seem to fit."* Ask what the recipient would count as a failure of the heritability condition before giving them the article.
- **Fig 2 :40**, agreement from a shared evidence pool is the pool talking. Do not select recipients for likely agreement, and never pool later agreement as corroboration.
- **Fig 11 :70**, a test harness is a substituted habitat. Do not present a flask result as a historical claim.

**Recommended order in a reply:** (1) the three-condition separator and the five refuters; (2) ask what they would count as a failure, especially of heritability; (3) record their verdict; (4) only then send the three links, with the note that the figures, not the summary, are the authority; (5) offer the formula as a method, with the instruction to state a mode and write a refuter before running it.

## The two-stage offer

1. **Stage 1.** Send the separator and the pre-registered refuter. Ask for a failure condition. Record the verdict before any theory is shown.
2. **Stage 2, opt-in only.** If they are curious, send the three source links. Their later agreement is a conversation, not corroboration.
3. **Stage 3.** Offer the formula as a generator to reuse on a problem with a real ground. Each user writes their own refuter.

Nothing is sent by this agent. You host and send. Prefer an opt-in download page over a pushed attachment so a recipient can decline contact.
