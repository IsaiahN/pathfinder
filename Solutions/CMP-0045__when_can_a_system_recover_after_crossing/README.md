## Restating the question as an effect

The ordinary term "threshold" binds to the comparison bond `⋛`, defined by the figures as *"a threshold relation between two quantities"* (Operators table :49; Fig 12 :87). The ordinary term "recover" is ambiguous, and that ambiguity is part of the answer: resilience research uses it for both a return time to one equilibrium and the amount of disturbance a system absorbs before reorganizing (Gunderson 2000; Allen et al. 2016). We therefore bind the sought-for shape as an ordinary term before deriving: **a system has recovered when the set of states composable from what it currently holds is again at least what it was before the crossing, measured per slot against a ground that does not update on the system's own report.** This is a shape, not a number. It makes "recovery" refusable by the same test as anything else (Formula, step 2 "Direction").

Two options, stated in effect terms:

- **Option A (symmetric reversal):** crossing only reclassifies the system; reversing the control restores the prior reach at equal cost, because no term lost its standing to be composed over.
- **Option B (hysteresis):** crossing adds a refusal or substitutes a habitat, so the reach falls or the return path is not the forward path reversed; restoring the prior reach requires new growth (a mint or a provenanced import) and a different, ordered comparison.

What each leads to: A sends an intervention that reverses the lever and expects a replay. B sends an intervention that imports a new atom, keeps cuts ranked and reversible, and expects permanent work rather than a solved state. The figures return **B as the governing shape where a refusal was added or a habitat was substituted, and A only where the reach was never lost.** Verdicts are kept per domain and never averaged.

## Why the two options cannot be averaged

The question is a union of overlapping conditions, not a partition (Fig 9 :54-58). A case where a refusal was added and a case where only the control value moved are not the same case with different magnitudes. One lever (reverse the input) clears the region where only the second condition holds and leaves the first standing, because the refusal still binds. A single "recovery probability" across both would aggregate away the only signal that says which intervention applies (Fig 1 :19, :22).

## What is deliberately not here

- No personal emails of named individuals.
- No ranking of people by how likely they are to agree with this derivation.
- No bulk-send plan.
- No claim that this derivation settles whether any real ecosystem, climate element, organization or person will recover. No ground reading was taken.

Three reasons, in the figures' own words: a room does not add an anchor (Seat Map :283); selecting for agreement manufactures the correlation that disqualifies a verdict (Seat Map :163, Fig 2 :40); and a verifier that can reconstruct the claim cannot verify it (Fig 10 :42).
