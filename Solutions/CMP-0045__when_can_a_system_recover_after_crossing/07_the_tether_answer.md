# The Tether's answer: recovery after crossing, and hysteresis

**Mode: `specified` on the per-domain claims, `general` on the structural shape, not `grounded`.** Simulated derivation checked for internal consistency against the figures, not a ground reading (`05_what_shape_means.md` §7). No real system was measured.

## 1. The residual it answers

A system's prediction that the current control setting preserves its regime fails after a crossing: reversing the control does not restore the old state, or requires a much larger reversal. The residual is indexed per slot (Fig 1 :19: **"The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated."**), and the question's own word "recover" must be bound as an ordinary term before the loop can check it: recovered means per-slot reach restored against a ground that does not update on the system's own report. The question is a union of four overlapping conditions (threshold, reach, habitat, channel), not one dial with two opposite settings.

## 2. The shape, per domain (never averaged)

**D1, the threshold.** A threshold is an ordered comparison: Operators table :49, *"a threshold relation between two quantities"*, symmetric = no. The forward comparison and the return comparison are different terms with different firing values. A zero-width loop in both directions is the special case, not the default.

**D2, the reach.** This is where hysteresis lives. Fig 6 :42: **"What is recorded only grows. What is reachable is derived, and it can fall."** A crossing that adds a refusal removes a term's standing to be composed over; it deletes nothing. Fig 6 :45-46: **"a term cannot cycle in and out, because the refusal never leaves the record: it becomes reachable again only if something new is minted, which is growth."** So reversal is difficult for a structural reason: the record does not un-add. The system that could simply un-cross would have to delete the refusal, and a grow-only record cannot. Recovery is nevertheless possible, by growth. The three routes are ordered: SEARCHED first (is the old reach merely unfound?), then INWARD (extend an instrument already returning something), then OUTWARD (import from a frame whose closure differs, with provenance and a shadow test). Fig 8 :32: **"Closure is idempotent. Composing inside a closed set never leaves it, so no search, however long, adds an atom."**

**D3, the habitat and membrane.** A crossing that substitutes a habitat is not undone by returning the control. Fig 4 :21: **"The membrane is the boundary between the sizes, and the rule about what may cross it. Not a wall: a filter with a direction."** Fig 11 :67: **"Move the residual and you must reproduce everything its goal required, and you inherit the new habitat's actors, which you did not choose."** Two failures are silent: what you failed to reproduce and what you unintentionally introduced. Fig 11 :61-63: **"Introduction is available; exclusion is not. You can add an actor and you cannot subtract the habitat, because contact is a fact about the world and does not consult you. Which is why a managed ecosystem is permanent work rather than a solved state."** The round-trip gap `R_T` must be measured, not assumed zero (Fig 4 :30).

**D4, the channel and ground.** The ground does not decay; the channel does (Fig 10 :24). After a crossing a checker can lose touch through stale percepts, dropped transients, saturated metrics and pooled readings, and then the output is not a verdict but *"you have lost touch"* (Fig 10 :28). Recovery requires both restored reach in the system and a reachable anchor for the checker. Fig 11 :40: **"capability is a property of agent-and-habitat, never of the agent alone."**

**What the before and after states are.** Over the same atoms they are Fig 12 isomers: *"Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."* A positive feedback loop (Fig 12 :121, *"The sign of the loop decides whether it closes"*) is what makes the new state self-maintaining. The recorded variables alone cannot discriminate the arrangements, which is why an instrument is owed (see §4).

## 3. What it explicitly does not say

- Not a recovery time, a critical value, a hysteresis loop width, or an intervention ranking for any named system. No measurement was taken.
- Not that all abrupt changes arise from gradual loss of resilience, nor that all hysteresis is irreversible. Fig 6 :45 permits recovery through growth; Fig 9 :44 says a search that finds nothing proves nothing.
- Not that "resilience" is one quantity. Return time and disturbance absorbed are two objectives with different anchors (Gunderson 2000; Allen et al. 2016). The choice between them is a declared binding, recorded as such; template `01` F8 reports this as a `gap` of the shape-not-a-name kind.
- Not a standing list of good recovery interventions. Actor valence is relative to the residual (Fig 11 :51: *"No standing list of good actors survives a change of residual."*).
- Not a room verdict. Agreement among frames sharing one evidence pool is one witness (Fig 2 :40).
- Not `grounded`. Every claim is `candidate` until a ground pays (Formula step 5).

## 4. What would settle it: the ground

A source-discriminating instrument, not a further argument. From *The Hard Problem is About Source*: an instrument that reads green *"cannot comment on the source"*; the spectrometer resolved the case. Here the corresponding questions are:

1. **Discriminator test.** Does an instrument exist that distinguishes the pre-crossing and post-crossing bond arrangements while the recorded state variables are equal? Without it, recovery and a different state with the same reading are indiscernible.
2. **Reach test.** Before and after a crossing, enumerate the terms derivable under everything held. Did a term lose standing (reach fell), or was the old reach intact and merely unfound? These demand opposite remedies (growth versus more search).
3. **Loop-width test.** Measure the forward and return comparison values against a non-updating reference. A zero loop at all driving rates would fire refuter 2 in `01` §4.
4. **Habitat and channel test.** Was a habitat substituted (two silent failures owed), and is the checker's channel to the ground still live? A dead channel makes any verdict about recovery unsafe (Fig 10 :25-29).
5. **Objective test.** State which definition of recovery is in use, return time or disturbance absorbed, and bind it as an ordinary term with its own refuter.

Standing falsifiers from `01` §4: a refused term cycling back without growth; a symmetric threshold; monotone reach; recovery by closure change without import; zero habitat gap; exclusion as clean as introduction; one instrument settling both objectives. Any of these sends the derivation back to the census.

## 5. The honest stop

**Unreached, not unreachable.** The shape stops where the available instruments stop discriminating. The six-term set is a visible guess, and the choice of recovery objective is a declared primitive: *"Declaring the primitives does not make them neutral. It makes the choice visible"* (*They're Scanning Books*). The framework records the lineage so the stop is checkable, and *"Indiscernibility is not identity"* (canonical README): two states that read the same are not thereby the same state. What the framework returns here is the condition structure of recovery, not the fate of any particular lake, climate element, organization or person.

## 6. One-paragraph version for outreach

> The Tether does not return a recovery time or a critical threshold value. It returns the shape any answer must have. A threshold is an ordered comparison, so crossing and recrossing are different terms. When a crossing adds a refusal in the system's record, the record grows while what is reachable falls, and a refused term cannot cycle back without something new being minted or imported. That is why reversal is difficult: you cannot un-add a refusal. Recovery is still possible, but by growth, in a fixed order: first check whether the old reach is merely unfound, then extend an instrument already returning something, then import from a frame whose closure differs, with provenance. If the crossing also substituted a habitat, introduction is available and exclusion is not, so managed recovery is permanent work rather than a solved state. All of this is per component, against a ground that does not update on what the system says about itself. The shape is `specified` and ungrounded; a source-discriminating instrument, not agreement, would close it.
