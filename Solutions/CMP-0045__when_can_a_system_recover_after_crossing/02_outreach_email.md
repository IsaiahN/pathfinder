**Subject:** One check on why reversing a tipping point is not the same as causing it

> Hi [name],
>
> I have one specific output I would like you to check against what you already know about resilience and recovery. I am not asking you to read the theory first, and I am not asking you to agree with it. If the output does not survive, there is nothing else to look at.
>
> **The output (`specified`, not `grounded`):** a threshold is an ordered comparison between two quantities, "a threshold relation between two quantities," not a two-way gate. So the comparison that crosses the system into a new regime and the comparison that would bring it back are different terms with different values. When the crossing also adds a refusal in the system's record, something was added rather than deleted: "What is recorded only grows. What is reachable is derived, and it can fall." A refused term "cannot cycle in and out, because the refusal never leaves the record: it becomes reachable again only if something new is minted, which is growth."
>
> That gives the following shape. A system recovers symmetrically only where the crossing did not lower what is reachable. Where a refusal was added or the habitat was substituted, reversal is difficult for a structural reason: the record does not delete, and "Introduction is available; exclusion is not," so managed recovery is permanent work rather than a solved state. Recovery is still possible, but only by growth: first check whether the old reach is intact and merely unfound, then extend an instrument already returning something, then seek a frame whose closure differs and import from it with provenance.
>
> **What I am not claiming:** that this is a number, a recovery time, or a threshold value for any named system. I have measured nothing. The framework returns a shape, not a name. I am not claiming that all abrupt changes have alternative stable states, that every hysteresis loop is irreversible, or that resilience is one quantity. "Return time" and "amount of disturbance absorbed" are two objectives with different anchors, and the question as asked bundles them. I am not claiming that a room of people who agree with this makes it true.
>
> **What would refute the shape claim.** I wrote these down before sending this.
> 1. A refused term cycles back into reach with no new mint or import and the prior record intact.
> 2. A crossing is symmetric in the sense that the reverse comparison is the same term at every value, with zero loop width at every driving rate.
> 3. The reachable set is monotone non-decreasing under all crossings, or a hysteresis loop appears with no change in reach.
> 4. Recovery changes the closure with no import and no instrument extension after the search was exhausted.
> 5. A scale or habitat crossing has a zero round trip gap and an unchanged habitat, so the return is an exact replay.
>
> If any one holds, the relevant part of the claim is withdrawn and the domain is re-censused. A one-line "this fails because..." is more useful to me than agreement. If you would rather not engage, no reply is needed.
