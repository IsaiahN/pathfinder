## QUESTION

Decide, in effect terms, what the Tether says about recovery after a threshold crossing. Option A: reversal is symmetric, so undoing the control replays the old state at equal cost. Option B: reversal is asymmetric wherever the crossing added a refusal or substituted a habitat, because the record grows while reach can fall and a refused term cannot return without growth. What each leads to: A sends a lever-reversal intervention and expects a replay; B sends a growth and import intervention, keeps cuts ranked and reversible, and expects permanent work where the habitat changed. The word "recover" is bound as an ordinary term before the run: per-slot reach restored against a ground that does not update on the system's own report.

## DOMAIN CENSUS

R by arity, symmetry and scale, then one line per domain with actors / currency / anchor; partition or union; order by dependency.

- **R (the residual).** The prediction that the current control setting preserves the regime fails after crossing; reversing the control does not restore the old state. Arity: a binary ordered comparison plus an action and an observation, so a compound. Symmetry: broken, because `⋛` is ordered. Scale: local to one frame, with cross-level propagation through settled molecules. Per-slot, never global (Fig 1 :19, :22). Anchor: a measurement of state against a non-updating ground. Currency: reach.
- **Domain 1, the threshold.** Actors: two quantities, the `⋛` comparison, the control action. Currency: which is larger, a classification. Anchor: the measurement. Partition (which side of the critical value). Single domain, anchor nameable.
- **Domain 2, closure and reach.** Actors: `Γ`, atoms, closure, refusals (`¬`), candidate terms. Currency: reach, non-monotone. Anchor: derivability under everything held, plus the ground for settlement. Partition of reachable versus unreachable, becoming a union when two refusals bind. Single domain, anchor nameable.
- **Domain 3, membrane and habitat.** Actors: methods up, head starts down, habitats, actors in contact. Currency: contact and round-trip gap `R_T`. Anchor: the measured gap and the non-updating ground, distinct from the updating habitat. Substitution, not removal. Single domain, anchor nameable.
- **Domain 4, ground and channel.** Actors: ground, channel, maintainer seat, outside frames. Currency: whether a reading reaches the checker. Anchor: the ground. Single domain, anchor nameable.
- **No fifth domain.** A "recovery percentage" has no anchor and aggregates away the per-slot signal, so it is refused as a domain rather than admitted as one (Fig 1 :22).
- **Union, not partition.** The four conditions overlap: a refusal in D2 constrains imports in D3; a dead channel in D4 makes D1's comparison a stale percept. One lever leaves the intersections standing (Fig 9 :57).
- **Order by dependency (Fig 3).** A working channel (D4) is needed to perceive the crossing (D1); the vocabulary of what remains reachable (D2) is consumed by the objective and the route (D3). The middle link is where chains usually break, because it has no single instrument (Fig 3 :27).

Strain test applied: Fig 4's between-scale irreversibility and Fig 6's within-level reach fall were first read as one claim. Re-census assigned them to D3 and D2 respectively; different objects, so no contradiction. See `01` §7.

## VERDICT (from the figures)

A system recovers symmetrically only where the crossing did not lower its reach. Where a crossing added a refusal, the record grows while reach falls, and a refused term cannot cycle back without new growth; reversal is therefore difficult for a structural reason, not a contingent one. Recovery is possible, by growth: SEARCHED first, then INWARD, then OUTWARD import with provenance and a shadow test. If the crossing substituted a habitat, introduction is available and exclusion is not, so recovery is permanent work. All verdicts are per slot, against a ground that does not update, and the two resilience objectives are kept separate because no figure selects between them. This is a `specified`, ungrounded, candidate result. The world sources corroborate the effect shape but do not supply the mechanism.

- **cost if wrong:** bounded and locatable. The packet states mode, refuters and per-domain verdicts before the conclusion, so an error is findable rather than invisible. The real cost is spending an outside checker's independence by sending the framework before the residual, which Fig 2 :42 prices: "Buy independence in proportion to the cost of being wrong." That cost is avoidable by leading with the shape claim and its refuter. A second cost is recommending permanent work where the reach was merely unfound, which spends intervention budget on an unnecessary import (Fig 8 :42-43).
- **undoable:** yes. The five refuters in `01` §4 each isolate a single domain, so a failed domain is withdrawn without touching the others. No primitive is withdrawn, and a sent claim can be superseded by a corrected packet. What is not undoable is an unsolicited push: Fig 11 :61 says exclusion is not available, which is why distribution is opt-in only.
- **reversed by:** a ground reading, not agreement. A source-discriminating instrument that separates the before and after bond arrangements while state variables read equal would close the discriminator gap. An enumeration of reachable terms before and after a crossing that shows no term lost standing would fire refuter 3 and move D2 toward Option A for that system. A zero loop width at all driving rates would fire refuter 2. Exclusion demonstrated as cleanly as introduction would fire refuter 6. A single instrument settling both resilience objectives would fire refuter 7. Until one of these fires, the verdict stands as `candidate`.
