# Expert verification packet: when can a system recover after crossing

**What this is:** a claim in a form a resilience researcher, complex-systems scientist or dynamical-systems theorist can check. Mode, derivation and refuters are stated before the conclusion, so a reader knows what to look for and what would settle it.

**Mode: `specified` on the per-domain claims, `general` on the structural shape, not `grounded`.** No measurement of any named system was taken. This packet is a simulated derivation checked for internal consistency against the figures, not a ground reading. It cannot settle the question it reports on.

## 1. The claim, in one paragraph

A threshold is an ordered comparison between two quantities, not a two-way gate, so the forward and return comparisons are different terms. A crossing that adds a refusal in the system's record lowers what is reachable while the record grows, and a refused term cannot cycle back without new growth; that is the structural reason hysteresis makes reversal difficult. Recovery is therefore possible but not as an un-crossing: check first whether the old reach is merely unfound, then extend an instrument already returning something, then import from a frame whose closure differs with provenance and a shadow test. If the crossing substituted a habitat, introduction is available and exclusion is not, so recovery is permanent work rather than a solved state. All of this is per slot, measured against a ground that does not update on the system's own report. The framework returns this shape, not a recovery time for any named system.

## 2. Three checkable sub-claims

### C1 : Framework-internal; checkable by reading Figures 6 and 12 and the Operators table

- **Statement:** hysteresis corresponds to a fall in reach caused by an addition to the record, and the reverse comparison is an ordered, different term. The before and after states are isomers over the same atoms.
- **Source, verbatim:**
  - Fig 6 :42: *"What is recorded only grows. What is reachable is derived, and it can fall."*
  - Fig 6 :45-46: *"a term cannot cycle in and out, because the refusal never leaves the record: it becomes reachable again only if something new is minted, which is growth."*
  - Operators table :49 and :51: *"a threshold relation between two quantities"*; *"is it about which is larger, rather than both being present? Yes."* (symmetric = no)
  - Fig 12 :117: *"Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."*
- **Refuter:** a refused term cycles back into reach with no new mint or import and the record intact; or a crossing has zero loop width at every driving rate; or a loop is observed with no change in the reachable set. Any one breaks C1.

### C2 : World-facing; checkable against the resilience and hysteresis literature

- **Statement:** the field independently describes the same effect shape: history dependence, a different reverse threshold, more work to return than to leave, and alternative states under identical external conditions. The figures add a mechanism the field does not supply, so the world sources corroborate shape only.
- **What to check:** Beisner (2012) states *"This contingency represents hysteresis: a condition wherein the reverse path is not the same as the forward path"* and *"going back is not as simple as was going forward."* The Scheffer et al. (2001) reproduction reports that responses *"strongly depend on historical events"* and models the fold with a threshold feedback function. Gunderson (2000) records two definitions of resilience in one literature (amount absorbed versus return time). The physics overview defines *"Hysteresis is the dependence of the state of a system on its history"* and gives the agar melting/solidifying case. Are those characterizations faithful, and do the two objective definitions really have different anchors?
- **Refuter:** a single instrument that measures both resilience objectives in the same units and settles both for the same system; or a well-documented hysteresis loop in which the reachable set is unchanged and the bond arrangement is identical in both directions. Either collapses the union reading or the isomer reading.

### C3 : Framework-internal derivation of the recovery route

- **Statement:** recovery is ordered SEARCHED, then INWARD, then OUTWARD, and guards on a new term are a product rather than a checklist. A crossing that substitutes a habitat is permanent work because exclusion is unavailable.
- **Source, verbatim:**
  - Formula step 7: *"SEARCHED  is it in here and unfound? The closure does not change: same atoms, same wall, more of the space actually visited."*
  - Fig 6 :23: *"No frame escapes its own closure. The bound moves only on contact with a frame whose closure differs."*
  - Fig 8 :32: *"Closure is idempotent. Composing inside a closed set never leaves it, so no search, however long, adds an atom."*
  - Fig 11 :61-63: *"Introduction is available; exclusion is not. You can add an actor and you cannot subtract the habitat, because contact is a fact about the world and does not consult you. Which is why a managed ecosystem is permanent work rather than a solved state."*
  - Fig 5 :33: *"The three guards are a product, not a checklist."*
- **Refuter:** recovery by closure change with no import and no instrument extension after SEARCHED was exhausted; or exclusion demonstrated as cleanly as introduction for the relevant actors; or a new reachable state produced while one guard was at zero throughout.

## 3. What is explicitly not claimed

- Not a recovery time, critical value, loop width, or intervention ranking for any named system.
- Not that all abrupt change requires a gradual loss of resilience, and not that all hysteresis is irreversible.
- Not that resilience is one quantity. Return time and disturbance absorbed are two objectives with different anchors; this packet does not choose between them.
- Not a standing list of interventions. Actor valence is relative to the residual (Fig 11 :51).
- Not a room verdict. Agreement among frames sharing one evidence pool is one witness (Fig 2 :40).
- Not `grounded`. The simulated status is stated above and is not removed downstream.

## 5. What would settle it

A source-discriminating instrument, not more agreement. The concrete checks are: (1) an instrument that distinguishes the before and after bond arrangements while the recorded variables are equal; (2) an enumeration of reachable terms before and after a crossing, showing whether a term lost standing; (3) forward and return comparison values measured against a non-updating reference; (4) a habitat and channel test, checking whether a habitat was substituted and whether the checker's channel to the ground is still live; (5) an explicit declaration of which resilience objective is in use. A ground reading that contradicts C1, C2 or C3 gets copied into `11_audit.md` with provenance and re-evaluated by the auditor seat as if it came from any other source.
