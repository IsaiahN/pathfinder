# Expert verification packet — the Tether on centralization, coordination, local knowledge and accountability

**What this is:** a claim in a form a public-administration researcher or practitioner can check. Mode, derivation and refuters are stated before the conclusion, so a reader knows what to look for and what would settle it.

**Mode: `specified` on the per-domain separators, `general` on the structural shape, not `grounded`.** No institution was measured. This is a simulated derivation checked for internal consistency against the figures, not a ground reading.

## 1. The claim, in one paragraph

The question "when does centralization improve coordination, and when does it undermine local knowledge and accountability?" is a union of three conditions with different currencies and different anchors, not one dial with an optimal midpoint. Coordination improves when a re-applicable method crosses the scale boundary and a shared meaning stays a convention; it fails when a recording of one success is replayed as policy. Local knowledge is per-slot contact with a habitat, so an aggregate without the slot index destroys the signal, and a center can only receive local knowledge as an import with provenance and a shadow test. Accountability needs a ground that does not update on the organization's own reports plus a seat able to refuse; one central review room hearing one evidence pool is one room however many people sit in it. Over a fixed atom set, centralized and decentralized forms are isomers: same actors and instruments, different bond, different behaviour. No figure returns a ratio, span, or org chart; that is a declared gap. What is offered is the separator, refuters written in advance, and the instruments that would close it.

## 2. Three checkable sub-claims

### C1 — The union framing and the per-slot residual (framework-internal, plus a public-administration check)

- **Statement:** coordination, local knowledge and accountability have different anchors, so a single scalar cannot order them; and the residual must be read per slot, not pooled.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 9 :57 — "a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either". Fig 1 :19 — "The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated." Fig 1 :22 — "Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average."
- **What to check:** in a decision class you know, do the coordination outcome, the local accuracy outcome and the accountability outcome have different grounds? Does a pooled central indicator ever reverse a per-unit result? (Loeper 2011 in the Journal of Public Economics formalizes the first point: decentralization can be optimal when coordination is very important.)
- **Refuter:** show one anchor ordering all three conditions, or an aggregate that preserves the per-slot where-to-look signal. The claim narrows to the cases where those fail.

### C2 — Method versus recording, and local contact (two domains)

- **Statement:** central coordination pays when a re-applicable method crosses up and a head start comes down, not when a one-occasion recording is replayed. Local advantage is contact with a habitat; moving the residual without reproducing the contact fails silently.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 4 :22 — "Only methods cross up. Only head starts come down." Fig 4 :25 — "A recording carried upward looks like knowledge and is a description of one occasion." Fig 11 :40 — "An improvement that does not change contact changes nothing, however much it improves." Fig 11 :68 — "what you failed to reproduce is invisible until the goal fails".
- **What to check:** is the central standard a method a local unit can re-apply, or a transcript of one success? After a centralization, did anyone enumerate the local contact that was lost, or only the reporting that was gained? Street-level bureaucracy research (Lipsky 1980, as reported by Hupe and Buffat 2007) is the closest in-field check: "public policy is not best understood as made in legislatures or top-floor suites of high-ranking administrators, because in important ways it is actually made in the crowded offices and daily encounters of street-level workers."
- **Refuter:** show a case where a centralization that removed local contact still settled the local ground at every slot, or where a replayed one-occasion recording kept producing new local successes. The claim narrows to contact-preserving reforms.

### C3 — The non-updating anchor and the checking seat (framework-internal, decisively)

- **Statement:** accountability survives only while a ground exists that does not move when the graded organization changes its reports, and a seat outside the room can refuse. A convention that decides an outcome stops being a convention.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 2 :29 — "An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks." Fig 2 :34 — "If it does, panel 3 turns into panel 1: the anchor becomes another frame, everything drifts together, and the drift is coherent, which is why nobody notices." Fig 10 :42 — "A verifier that can reconstruct the claim cannot verify it." Fig 10 :47 — "a convention that decides an outcome has stopped being one".
- **What to check:** does the performance indicator come from the units it grades? Can any seat refuse a verdict without having authored the standard? Is the review body drawing on a different evidence pool from the body it reviews? The JPART study by Altamimi, Liu and Jimenez (2023) found that "moderate centralization and decentralization have distinct influences on the implementation of different types of organizational change", which is the per-type rather than per-degree reading this claim predicts.
- **Refuter:** name the independent instrument that tracks the ground when the reference updates on reports, or show a checker who did not author the standard and still cannot refuse. If neither exists, the drift finding stands.

## 3. What is explicitly not claimed

- Not a universal centralization percentage, span of control, layer count, or org chart. No figure line returns one.
- Not that centralization is always harmful. It is the right carrier for methods, conventions and genuinely missing instruments.
- Not that decentralization is always good. A local room with one evidence pool is still one room (Fig 7 :36, Fig 2 :40).
- Not that a named court, audit office, ombudsman or chamber is the answer. Naming a held arrangement is provenance, not a derivation (`05` §6).
- Not that the isomer claim is unconditional. It holds over a fixed set of actors and instruments; otherwise the relation is composition plus import. This is a live strain, named in `06_isomers_and_order.md`.
- Not grounded. No institutional measurement was made. Every substantive claim is `specified` or `general`.

## 5. What would settle it

A ground reading, not agreement. The instruments are:

1. **Per-slot test.** Does the central reading preserve the slot index and the where-to-look signal, or does it pool them?
2. **Anchor test.** Does the reference move when the graded units change their reports? If yes, it is a frame.
3. **Import test.** Can the center name the source and the gap that a local reading explained before importing it?
4. **Room test.** Is the checker's evidence from a closure that differs from the checked one, with a ground neither can move?
5. **Isomer test.** Did the reform keep the atom set fixed? If it added an actor, the comparison is composition plus import.

## 6. What an expert can check in twenty minutes

1. Read Fig 9 :57, Fig 1 :19 and :22, Fig 4 :22 and :25, Fig 11 :40, Fig 2 :29 and :34, Fig 10 :42 and :47. Nine short passages.
2. Pick one decision class you know. Name the three grounds. Do they differ?
3. Say which of the three refuters in section 2 you would fire first, and why.
4. Name one instrument that would distinguish a method from a recording, or an anchor from a report, in your domain. Or say why none exists.
5. Send the refutation. A failure is more useful than an endorsement.
