# Outreach draft — check one separator before you decide about the framework

**Use:** one-to-one, or as a cover letter attached to a submission through the public venues in `03_public_contact_points.csv`. **Not** for bulk send.

**Hard precondition:** do not send until the seven refuters in `01_figure_grounded_audit.md` §3 are stated in the message and the second-seat check in `11_audit.md` is read. The output must survive validation or the two-stage offer collapses on contact.

**Subject:** Would this refute a claim about centralization and local knowledge?

> Hi [name],
>
> I have one specific output I would like you to check against what you already know. I am not
> asking you to read the framework first, and I am not asking you to agree with it. If the output
> does not survive, there is nothing else to look at.
>
> **The output (`specified` on the separators, `general` on the shape, not `grounded`):** the
> question "when does centralization improve coordination, and when does it undermine local
> knowledge and accountability?" is a union of three conditions, not one dial with an optimal
> midpoint. Coordination, local knowledge, and accountability register different currencies and
> range against different grounds, so a lever that clears one can leave the others standing.
>
> - Coordination improves when what crosses the boundary between scales is a re-applicable
>   method or a head start, and when a shared meaning stays a convention. It fails when what
>   crosses is a recording of one success that gets replayed as policy.
> - Local knowledge is per-slot contact with a local habitat. A central aggregate that drops the
>   slot index loses the only signal that says where to look. A center can only import local
>   knowledge from a frame whose closure differs, with provenance and a test of whether it
>   explains a gap; more central deliberation over the same reports adds no new atom.
> - Accountability is a checking arrangement, not a reporting line. It requires a ground that does
>   not update on the organization's own reports, plus at least one seat able to refuse. A single
>   review room hearing only centrally routed reports is one evidence pool, however many people
>   sit in it.
>
> **What I am not claiming:** that there is a universal percentage, span, or org chart; that
> centralization is always bad for local knowledge; that local discretion is always good; or that
> a named chamber, court, or office is the answer. The framework returns a shape, not a name. The
> isomorphism between a centralized and a decentralized form holds only over a fixed set of actors
> and instruments, and I flag that condition as a live strain rather than hiding it.
>
> **What would refute the output.** I wrote these down before running the derivation:
> 1. a central aggregate that preserves the per-slot where-to-look signal;
> 2. a reference that updates on the organization's reports and still tracks the ground
>    independently at every slot, with a named instrument;
> 3. a center that derives a correct local particular from general rules alone, with no import
>    and no local contact;
> 4. a local decision that stays correct after its habitat is substituted and contact removed;
> 5. a centralized and a decentralized form with the same atoms and the same bonds that behave
>    differently;
> 6. a figure line that returns a centralization number, span, or named organ;
> 7. a demonstration that the three conditions share one anchor so that one scalar orders them.
>
> If any one holds, I will narrow or withdraw the claim. A one-line "this fails because..." is more
> useful to me than agreement. If you would rather not engage, no reply is needed.
>
> **If it survives, and only then:** the sources are below. The figures, not my summary, are the
> authority.
> - Figures and formula for direct download (branch `general-agent`): https://github.com/IsaiahN/tether/tree/general-agent/figures
> - Worked architecture essay: https://medium.com/@IsaiahNwukor/arrival-of-the-fittest-the-tether-architecture-1de7edf641ec
> - Tacit knowledge and contact, with figure citations: https://adventuresinml.substack.com/p/decoding-tacit-knowledge-why-context
>
> **If you want to use the machinery:** the formula is in the figures link. Run it on a decision
> class you know well and can check against a ground. Write down what would refute your result
> *before* you run it; the framework's own limit is that a loop cannot check the direction of its
> own question. A reported failure is more useful to me than a reported success.
>
> — [name]
