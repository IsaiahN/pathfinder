## 0. The residual, in effect terms

The question asks "when". That is a request for a condition, not a number. Stated as an effect, the residual is this:

> Decision rights over many local slots are being allocated between a center and local units. We want to know which allocation makes the policy prediction match what actually happens at each slot, while keeping the reading of each slot available to someone who can refuse the verdict.

**R** is not "how centralized should a state be". That phrasing already bundles the answer into a single scalar. R is the per-slot gap between what the governing frame predicts and what the local ground returns, plus the gap between the reading a superior sees and the reading the local ground could have settled. It is indexed per slot.

Arity: at least three conditions are in play, coordination, local knowledge, accountability. Symmetry: "improves coordination" is an ordered claim (a lever produces a result), while "undermines local knowledge and accountability" names two different currencies. The word "and" does not make them one outcome. Scale: local unit and center are related by a scale membrane, not by a larger copy of the same frame.

**What an answer would look like, written down before the census (Formula "Direction").** An answer would (a) name the currency of each condition, (b) identify which condition a central room can settle and which need local contact, (c) state a refuter per verdict, and (d) decline a universal optimum. Binding this shape as an ordinary term lets it accrue a residual like anything else. It is tested in `11_audit.md`.

**First reading, kept visible.** My first pass read the question as one dial with opposite effects and went looking for a figure that returned the right setting. That reading is retracted in §7 (R1). No figure returns a setting.

## 1. Domain census, before choosing any figure line

**R by arity / symmetry / scale.** Arity three or more (coordination, local knowledge, accountability, and the measurement of each). Symmetry mixed: coordination is ordered, agreement is symmetric but carries no verdict weight, accountability is a checking relation with an asymmetric refusal. Scale: two or more strata joined by a membrane, with a lossy transform between.

**One line per domain. A domain needs nameable actors, a currency, and an anchor. No anchor means it is not yet a domain.**

| domain | actors | currency | anchor | question type |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| D1 coordination / composition | local units, center, shared standards, decision rights | a composition that pays (cost saved versus confusion removed) | a ground that decides whether the composed action worked, plus the Figures as metalaws | partition boundary: what composes across a seam, and what may cross |
| D2 local knowledge / contact | frontline staff, citizens, local conditions, habitat | contact with the local ground, per slot | the local outcome that does not move when the center re-describes it | union: contact is necessary, not sufficient, and valence is residual-relative |
| D3 accountability / verification | maker, interior auditor, ground maintainer, outside frame | verdicts, refusals, provenance | the ground, which must not update on any frame's report | arrangement: which seat can refuse, and who cannot see the interior |

**Not domains yet.** "Efficiency" alone has no anchor until a measurement is named. "Local knowledge" is not a domain until the local outcome that could settle it is named. "Coordination" is not a domain until the thing coordinated has a currency (a method crossing, not a meeting held).

**Partition or union.** Union. The three conditions have different anchors, so a lever that clears coordination can leave the local-knowledge and accountability intersections standing. Fig 9 :54: "And when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again." Fig 9 :55: "A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones." Fig 9 :57: "a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either." One lever does not clear the compound.

**Order by dependency (Fig 3).** Fig 3 :20: "The chain is ordered by dependency, not by importance." The order here is: (1) keep the residual indexed per slot; (2) have terms for what a local situation affords; (3) say what would count as progress at that slot; (4) route a method, not a recording, across the seam; (5) keep the ground reachable and let a seat refuse. Effort spent above a break cannot rescue one below it (Fig 3 :19: "Optimising a link that already works cannot rescue one that does not."). The link most likely to be missing is (2) to (3): having vocabulary for local conditions but no instrument for local progress. Fig 3 :27: "A link with no instrument is not a link that works. It is a link nobody has looked at."

## 2. Findings, each quoted before the conclusion and each with a refuter

### F1 — The either/or is a union compound, not one question with two answers

Fig 9 :4-5: "are not one hard question but several well-formed ones. Split it rather than search."
Fig 9 :54: "And when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again."
Fig 9 :57: "a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either."

"Centralization" is not one lever. It bundles at least four: moving decision rights up a scale, imposing a shared vocabulary, routing information upward, and authoring a common convention. Coordination, local knowledge, and accountability register these differently. A single verdict on the bundle would be an average across slots, which is the failure Fig 1 names.

**Verdict (D1, D2, D3 kept separate):** each domain gets its own answer; they are never averaged into an optimal degree. **Mode: `specified`; triangulated** (Fig 9 plus Fig 1 low overlap).
**Refuter:** show that the three conditions share one anchor such that a single scalar orders all three, and that a lever clearing one clears the others in every tested case.

### F2 — Centralization improves coordination when what crosses is a method, and the kernel is renamed at the new scale

Fig 4 :19-20: "Only the actors, the currency and the anchor change."
Fig 4 :22: "Only methods cross up. Only head starts come down."
Fig 4 :25: "A recording carried upward looks like knowledge and is a description of one occasion."

Coordination is not the number of meetings or the uniformity of forms. It is a composition that pays: a local method that can be re-applied is carried up, compressed to essentials, and a head start comes back down. The loop and the rule for naming what worked stay the same at both sizes; only actors, currency, and anchor change. Where a recording of one success is carried up and replayed down, the system repeats a past success and cannot produce a new one. That is the failure mode of centralization that looks most like success.

The center also helps where a genuinely absent instrument is owed. Fig 5 requires support, reachability, and novelty as a product. A shared measurement system built once and re-applied locally is a mint; a shared report format that merely renames what locals already knew is not.

**Verdict (D1):** centralization improves coordination when it carries a re-applicable method or a head start across the seam, keeps the non-changing kernel, and installs an instrument that was genuinely absent. **Mode: `specified`; triangulated** (Fig 4 plus Fig 5, low overlap on method versus term).
**Refuter:** shown that central coordination pays where what crossed was a one-occasion recording with no re-application, or where actors, currency and anchor were not renamed at the new scale.

### F3 — The mechanism by which centralization loses local knowledge is aggregation

Fig 1 :19: "The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated."
Fig 1 :22: "Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average."
Fig 1 :27: "The gap is measured per object, not per step. A system that averages across objects has thrown away the only signal that says where to look."

A central room that sees only totals has thrown away the index. This is not a complaint about sample size; it is a structural claim. Two world sources corroborate from different fields (secondary only). Hayek 1945 writes that central planning "based on statistical information by its nature cannot take direct account of these circumstances of time and place." Simpson's paradox is defined as a case where "a trend appears in several groups of data but disappears or reverses when the groups are combined." Both are consequences of the same operation the figure names. They do not decide the verdict; they corroborate the shape.

**Verdict (D2):** centralization undermines local knowledge where it replaces per-slot residuals with an aggregate, because the aggregate cannot say where to look. **Mode: `specified`; triangulated** (Fig 1 plus two low-overlap world witnesses, economics and statistics).
**Refuter:** an aggregate that preserves the per-slot index, for example a distribution with the slot identifier intact and a reading at each slot, would not fire this failure. So the refuter is narrower than "centralization always loses local knowledge": it is "aggregation without per-slot indexing loses the signal."

### F4 — Accountability needs a ground that does not update on the organization's own reports

Fig 2 :29: "An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks."
Fig 2 :33: "The anchor must not update."
Fig 2 :34: "If it does, panel 3 turns into panel 1: the anchor becomes another frame, everything drifts together, and the drift is coherent, which is why nobody notices."
Fig 10 :24: "the ground does not decay, the channel does"
Fig 10 :26: "stale percepts, dropped transients, saturated metrics, pooled readings"

Accountability fails not when reports are absent but when the reference becomes a report. If the center's performance indicator is itself produced by the units it grades, the indicator is a frame, not an anchor. The coherent drift that follows is invisible from inside, which is why a centralized reporting system can be perfectly self-consistent and still be wrong. The ground does not decay; the channel to it does. The failure signature is stale readings and saturated metrics, and the output of the maintainer is "you have lost touch", not a verdict on the world.

**Verdict (D3):** centralization supports accountability only while a non-updating ground remains reachable and at least one seat can refuse the reading. **Mode: `specified`; triangulated** (Fig 2 plus Fig 10, different offices).
**Refuter:** a system whose reference updates on the reports yet whose agreement tracks the ground independently at every slot would break this claim; name the independent instrument.

### F5 — A shared standard is a convention until it decides an outcome

Fig 2 :41: "efficient for a convention: agreement about a shared meaning is the product, not a corruption of it"
Fig 10 :47: "a convention that decides an outcome has stopped being one"
Fig 10 :49: "Declaring a meaning does not ground it."

This is the sharpest discriminator in the question. A common definition, form, or accounting rule is a convention, and agreement about it is the product. Centralization is genuinely good at conventions. But the moment the convention fixes who is blamed, who is funded, or what counts as success, it is a verdict; the seat that authored it has decided the outcome and is no longer outside it. The seat map's rule is that a convention nothing can check is a constant carrying the seat's authority, and a convention that decides an outcome has stopped being a convention.

Seat Map :163: "High correlation is disqualifying for a verdict and efficient for a convention." Buying uniformity among people who read the same central report is efficient for the convention and worthless as a check on the world.

**Verdict (D1 and D3 intersection):** centralization is good at shared meanings and bad at turning them into unchallengeable outcomes. **Mode: `specified`; triangulated** (Fig 10 plus Seat Map :163 plus Fig 2 :41).
**Refuter:** a standard that decides an outcome and remains checkable by a seat that did not author it, with evidence the seat can see, would narrow this finding to a scope condition rather than a failure.

### F6 — Centralizing the checking into one agreeing room produces motion, not verification

Fig 7 :35: "A room's agreement is variation, not a verdict. Selection between rooms is where the ground votes."
Fig 7 :36: "A chain of mirrors is one long room. The next room must differ from the last, and match the residual it surfaced."
Fig 2 :40: "disqualifying for a verdict: agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool"
Fig 8 :48: "How much two frames draw on the same evidence decides both whether their agreement means anything and whether their union has any surplus."

A single central review room that hears only centrally routed reports is one room, however many people sit in it. Seat Map :283: "A room does not upgrade the label." Adding reviewers raises the vocabulary and does not add an anchor. Accountability requires low overlap between the checker and the checked: a court, an audit office with its own instrument, a legislature with its own reading, citizens who can see the local outcome. These are frames whose closures differ. Agreement among them is evidence; agreement inside one room is the pool talking.

**Verdict (D3):** centralization undermines accountability when it collapses the checking arrangement into one room drawing on one evidence pool. **Mode: `specified`; triangulated** (Fig 7 plus Fig 2 plus Fig 8, three distinct diagrams).
**Refuter:** show low overlap between checker and checked while both remain inside the central room, with separate instruments and a ground neither can move; then the finding is about overlap, not about centralization.

### F7 — A center cannot derive local knowledge it has never received, and an import needs a gate

Fig 6 :21: "MINT composes inside the closure and can never add an atom."
Fig 6 :23: "No frame escapes its own closure. The bound moves only on contact with a frame whose closure differs."
Fig 6 :30: "The description travels. Fails by being sharpened into terms only we can read. Cast it in effect terms."
Fig 8 :32: "Closure is idempotent. Composing inside a closed set never leaves it, so no search, however long, adds an atom."

The center can only compose from what is in its closure. Local knowledge of the particular case is an atom it does not hold until someone sends it, and sending it as a center-only abstraction is the failure Fig 6 :30 names. If the center does acquire it from a local frame, that is an import: it needs a source gate (provenance) and a shadow test (does it explain a gap the center had?), and it is debited against the local frame's independence (Fig 8). Hayek's "knowledge of the particular circumstances of time and place" is the same shape from economics, and the tacit-knowledge essay's "we can know more than we can tell" is the same shape from cognition. Both are corroboration only.

**Verdict (D2):** local knowledge reaches a center only by contact as an import with provenance, or not at all; more central deliberation over the same reports adds no atom. **Mode: `specified`; triangulated** (Fig 6 plus Fig 8 plus two low-overlap world witnesses).
**Refuter:** a case where a center derived a correct local particular from general rules alone, with no import and no local contact, would refute the cannot-derive half of this finding.

### F8 — Local advantage is contact, and centralization can substitute a habitat without noticing

Fig 11 :39: "capability is a property of agent-and-habitat, never of the agent alone"
Fig 11 :40: "An improvement that does not change contact changes nothing, however much it improves."
Fig 11 :49: "An actor's valence is relative to the residual, not a property it carries."
Fig 11 :51: "No standing list of good actors survives a change of residual."
Fig 11 :66: "Isolation is not removal of the habitat. It is substitution of one habitat for another."
Fig 11 :68: "what you failed to reproduce is invisible until the goal fails"

Decentralization's genuine advantage is not virtue in local people. It is contact: frontline actors meet the local ground at a resolution the center does not have. Move the decision to the center and you have substituted a habitat. You inherit the center's actors, which you did not choose, and two failures are silent: what you failed to reproduce (the local contact) and what you unintentionally introduced (central incentives, central metrics). The same formal rule may help one residual and harm another, so no standing list of which decisions belong where survives a change of residual.

Herbert-Read 2016 in animal behaviour provides a genuinely low-overlap corroboration: groups achieve coordination "when individuals adjust their movement in response to their neighbours' movements and positions", without choreography. Coordination can be a contact property, not a command property. This corroborates the shape; it does not decide the public-administration question.

**Verdict (D2):** the local advantage is per-slot contact with a habitat; centralization that moves the residual without reproducing the contact fails silently until the goal fails. **Mode: `specified`; triangulated** (Fig 11 plus low-overlap animal-behaviour witness).
**Refuter:** show a local decision that remains correct after the local habitat has been substituted and contact removed, with the ground still settling it; then contact is not the variable.

### F9 — Over a fixed atom set, centralized and decentralized forms are isomers; type is the variable

Fig 12 :5: "A set of ingredients is not one. The bond is the rest of the information, and it is the half nothing was recording."
Fig 12 :104: "A settled molecule becomes an atom for whatever composes over it. So irreducible is a fact about a level and never about the world"
Fig 12 :117: "isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."

If a reform changes only who holds the refusal seat and which direction the method crosses, while keeping the same actors, instruments, and standards, the two forms are isomers: same atoms, different bond, different substance. A chart that lists only the boxes has recorded the ingredients and not the arrangement. This is why two governments with identical formal structures can behave differently; the bond is not in the organigram.

**Live strain, not hidden.** The isomer claim holds only over a fixed atom set. Real reforms often add an actor (an audit office, an ombudsman) or remove one, which is composition plus import, not isomerism. I keep this as a live type question in `06_isomers_and_order.md` and `09_watson_answer.md` rather than smoothing it over.

**Verdict (structural):** where the atom set is fixed, the difference is a bond, not a degree. **Mode: `general` shape, `specified` per case, with one live strain.**
**Refuter:** exhibit a centralized and a decentralized form with the same atoms and the same bonds but different behaviour; then the bond is not the carrier.

### F10 — The formula is a lossy condensation of the figures, so the round trip must be measured

Fig 4 :30: "So take the round trip and measure the gap. Without it you are navigating by dead reckoning: confident about a position nobody checked."
Fig 4 :31: "The gap is not an error to eliminate. It is the measurement of what the coarser description cannot hold, and it is the only honest report of how much was given up."

Any one-line slogan drawn from this audit, for example "centralize methods, decentralize judgments", drops constraints. The dropped constraints are enumerated in `11_audit.md` §Round trip gap R_T. Treating the slogan as identical to the figure set would set R_T to zero, which is the dead-reckoning error.

**Verdict:** the slogan is a generator with a measured gap, not a theorem. **Mode: `general`.**
**Refuter:** a round trip that returns all constraints intact would set R_T to zero; no such trip was found.

### F11 — No figure line returns a centralization optimum. That is a gap, reported as unreachable, not silence

Fig 9 :32: "you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself"
Fig 13 :134: "Unreachable is a verdict here, not a silence"
Fig 8 :39: "So a search that finds nothing is never a proof of absence."

I searched the figure set for a line that selects a span of control, a layer count, a centralization percentage, or which named agency should hold a given decision. None exists. This is a shape-not-a-name gap, not underivable in principle: an instrument could settle a specific case. The right report is "unreached at this budget; an instrument is owed", not "no answer exists" and not an invented number. Fig 13 :134 makes unreachability a verdict.

**Verdict:** `gap` for the universal optimum, `specified` for the per-domain separators. **Mode: `general` on the gap.**
**Refuter:** cite a figure line that returns a number or a named organ; then the gap closes. (The pre-registered version of this refuter is in §3.)

## 3. Pre-registered refuter, written before the conclusion

Seat Map :287: "And a falsifier written before the run is a design instrument, not only an honesty device." I commit to withdrawing or narrowing the claim if any of the following fires:

1. **Aggregation firing.** A central aggregate is shown to preserve the per-slot index and the where-to-look signal, so that no local residual disappears into the average (breaks F3).
2. **Anchor firing.** A reference is shown to update on the organization's own reports and still track the ground independently at every slot, with a named instrument that does the tracking (breaks F4).
3. **Derivation firing.** A center derives a correct local particular from general rules alone, with no import and no local contact (breaks F7).
4. **Contact firing.** A local decision remains correct after its habitat is substituted and its contact removed, with the ground still settling it (breaks F8).
5. **Bond firing.** A centralized and a decentralized form with the same atoms and the same bonds are shown to behave differently (breaks F9).
6. **Gap firing.** A figure line is produced that returns a span, layer count, ratio, or named organ for this question (closes F11).
7. **Union firing.** The three conditions are shown to share one anchor such that one scalar orders all three (breaks F1).

If any fires, the affected finding is narrowed or withdrawn in this file with the original reading kept visible. None was fired by this run.

## 4. Recommendation

1. Stop asking for a single degree of centralization. Ask per decision class: what is the currency, where is the ground, who can refuse (F1, F11).
2. Centralize methods, shared meanings, and genuinely missing instruments; send head starts down, never recordings of one success (F2).
3. Keep residuals indexed per slot; an aggregate without the slot index is a filter for the budget, never a witness for a verdict (F3, Fig 9 :60: "Use filters for the budget and witnesses for the verdict. Never let a filter hand you a verdict.").
4. Keep the ground non-updating. If the indicator is produced by the units it grades, restore an independent reading before deciding anything (F4, F6).
5. Treat shared standards as conventions until they are deliberately promoted into verdicts; if promoted, supply an outside checker who did not author them (F5).
6. Import local knowledge with provenance and a shadow test; never sharpen it into terms only the center can read (F7).
7. Record the arrangement, not just the boxes. Ask which bond holds and what would have to be true for a different bond to work (F9).
8. Do not send a conclusion to a checker. Send the residual and one of the seven refuters; record the verdict before showing the framework (Fig 10 :52: "Bring a characterised residual, never the source.").
