# Isomers and order in the inference to the earliest life

**Mode: `specified` on the isomer claim, `general` on the order rule, not `grounded`.** This is a simulated derivation checked against the figures, not a ground reading (see `05_what_shape_means.md` §6). No fossil, cell, genome or experiment was measured here.

## RESIDUAL

The shared modern inventory (genetic code, ribosome, ATP as energy currency, shared chirality, core metabolism) is one observed formula. The question asks how much of the earliest life that formula licenses. The residue is the gap between the formula (what is recorded in living things now) and the source (which ancestral state, under which history of loss, transfer and convergence, produced it). Fig 12 :5 "A set of ingredients is not one. The bond is the rest of the information, and it is the half nothing was recording." The bond here is the historical relation between an ancestral state and a modern trait, and Fig 12 :46 says it is "not recoverable from the operands".

## DOMAIN CENSUS: atoms and bonds held

- **Atoms held.** The observable formula: the shared molecular inventory of modern organisms. The candidate ancestral states are *not* held; they are the unobserved sources.
- **Bonds held.** The seven typed relations plus negation (Fig 12, Operators table). The ones that do work in this inference are sequence (→, descent with modification), production (⇒, a trait arising), subtraction (−, gene loss), comparison (⋛, a threshold such as universality), and negation (¬, refusal of a common-cause explanation).
- **No ancestral bond is held.** The historical relation that connects a particular modern trait to a particular ancestral state is exactly what 4 billion years of loss and transfer erase. So the transform from formula to source is underdetermined before any probability is assigned.
- **Question type.** Union, not partition (from `01` §1): the comparative-reconstruction domain and the independent-ground domain overlap at calibration points, and a single lever (more genomes) clears only the region where one condition binds.

## Q1 : Are competing ancestral histories isomers of one modern formula?

Fig 12 :117 "Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."

Fig 12 :97 "silver fulminate and silver cyanate have the same composition and different properties, which is what forced structure into the notation."

Fig 13 :123 "Two chains built from different links may compose functionally identical terms, and the converse holds too: the same ingredients under a different bond are a different substance."

The chemistry case is not a decorative analogy. In the figures it is the proof that an ingredient list cannot fix a substance. Applied here: gene loss (−) can make an old gene look young, horizontal transfer (⇒ across lineages) can make a young gene look old, and convergence can produce the same trait from independent starts. These are different bonds over the same modern ingredients, so they are different ancestral "substances" that present one formula. The modern distribution cannot select among them. Fig 9 :32 "you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself" is the resulting limit: the inference returns the kind of ancestor that could produce the formula, not the particular source.

**Scope correction, carried from `05` §5 and applied here, not inherited.** This does not forbid saying the last universal common ancestor had a genetic code. The code is an *ingredient*, observed in modern life and inferred to be ancient under vertical transmission; it is not a missing primitive. What Fig 12 refuses is reading the *source* off the *formula* when several histories give the same formula. The unheld thing is the historical bond, not the ingredient. The named portrait (acetogen, 4.2 Ga, ~2600 proteins, an early Cas system) is a source assigned to one of several histories, so it is held as a `specified` candidate from the phylogenetics frame, provenanced as such.

- **Mode:** `specified`, falsifiable.
- **Status:** triangulated (Fig 12 bond law, Fig 13 isomerism and Fig 9 shape limit are three figures with low overlap; the metamerism analogue from cognition corroborates from a different field).
- **Refuter written before the conclusion:** show that the modern inventory uniquely determines the ancestral history under all allowed mechanisms, that is, that the transform is invertible for these traits. A simulated ancestor recovered uniquely from its descendants would do it. If that holds, the isomer claim is false for those traits and the slot closes.

## Q2 : What is the order of the inference?

The chain is ordered by dependency, not by importance (Fig 3). Effort spent above a break arrives at a step that cannot use it.

Fig 3 :19 "Optimising a link that already works cannot rescue one that does not."

Fig 3 :22 "It is a reading of nothing."

Fig 9 :27 "describe it before you go looking, or any frame you pick will seem to fit"

Fig 10 :52 "Bring a characterised residual, never the source."

Fig 4 :22 "Only methods cross up. Only head starts come down."

The lawful order for this question is:

1. **Establish the transmission mechanism of the shared trait** (the common-cause domain). A reconstruction whose input is a trait whose transmission is unmodelled is, in Fig 3 :22's words, "a reading of nothing". Optimising the tree model cannot rescue a break here.
2. **Describe R in effect terms** before selecting a frame: what would the earliest life have to have been capable of for this formula to appear? Fig 9 :27 requires the description before the lookup. Fig 6 :30 adds "Cast it in effect terms", so the description travels to a frame that does not share the genomic closure.
3. **Reconstruct on the tree**, with posterior probabilities used as filters for the search budget, not as witnesses for the verdict (Fig 9 :60 "Use filters for the budget and witnesses for the verdict. Never let a filter hand you a verdict.").
4. **Calibrate and test against a different closure** (fossils, isotopes, prebiotic chemistry, experimental evolution with a known ancestor). This is the import that moves the wall (Fig 6 :23).
5. **Only then** state what a verification community may count as a failure, and record the verdict before showing the interior (Fig 10 :42).

Two sub-orders follow from the formula and the habitat figure:

- **Refuse before mint.** Formula step 2 sorts a BROKEN·mechanism residual before a mint. Concretely: the common-cause and transfer explanation of a shared trait must be modelled or refused before an ancestral state is named. Naming first and reconciling later reverses the order.
- **Introduce, never subtract.** Fig 11 :32 "introduce, never subtract". The early habitat cannot be recovered by deleting modern complexity from the cell. Its geochemistry must be introduced from an independent frame, because isolation substitutes a habitat rather than removing one (Fig 11 :66).

## Q3 : Analogues, as this question needs them

- **Metamerism.** *The Hard Problem is About Source* (2026): "If several paths create the same behavior, then behavior is a projection with lossy information." A spectrometer reading green "cannot comment on the source". The modern shared trait is the reading; the ancestral state is the source. The case was resolved "not by argument, but by the arrival of a better instrument", which is why this derivation asks for a different closure rather than a more confident portrait.
- **The tree of life.** Fig 10 :70 "The tree of life is what a non-invertible transform looks like from outside." The branches are the isomers: many descendants, one lost ancestral bond, no inverse.
- **Known-ancestor check.** The T7 phage experimental-evolution case recorded in `04_links_and_distribution.md` is the closest world analogue to a ground reading of the method: parsimony failed to recover a known ancestral state until fossilized samples were included. That is an independent closure imported at step 4, and it is the strongest practical reason to spend the budget on rocks and experiments rather than more genomes.

## FALSIFIER (written before the conclusion)

The isomer claim and the order claim are refused if any of the following holds:

1. The modern distribution of the traits uniquely determines the ancestral history, with no multiplicity left under loss, transfer and convergence. That breaks Q1.
2. A reconstruction is shown to be reliable when the transmission mechanism is left unmodelled, so that step 2 of the order is not a break. That breaks Q2.
3. An independent ground reading (fossil, isotope, experiment) settles a specific ancestral trait that the shape excluded. That breaks the honest stop carried from `01` §4.

A report that the genomes agree, or that a posterior probability is high, fires none of these. Until one is run, no part of this derivation is `grounded`.

## CONCLUSION

The modern formula admits many ancestral histories, so under Fig 12's definition those histories are isomers: same ingredients, different bonds, different substances, with the difference invisible in the formula. That is why a shared feature constrains the *shape* of the earliest life and does not name its *state*. The inference has a lawful order: mechanism first, residual description second, reconstruction third, independent ground fourth, verification verdict last. Refusal comes before naming, and introduction comes before subtraction. The falsifiers are recorded above and in `01` §4, so the claim can be checked rather than believed.
