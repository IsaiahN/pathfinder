## 0. The claim under audit

> Shared features of modern organisms (the genetic code, the ribosome, ATP as energy currency, shared chirality, core metabolism) license an inference to the earliest life. The stronger form names that life: a complex, prokaryote-grade, acetogenic, membrane-bounded cell with an early immune system at about 4.2 Ga. The weaker form says only that a common ancestral population existed and had some molecular machinery.

Restated as an effect: many extant observations bear on one unobservable target. The residual is the gap between what is measured now (traits in living organisms) and what is asserted about the target (the ancestral state). What fails to resolve is the step from "shared now" to "present then", because gene loss, horizontal transfer, convergence and the erasure of 4 billion years all act between the two.

What an answer would look like, bound as an ordinary term before the run (Formula "Direction"): a claim of the form "shared feature F constrains the earliest life to shape S; the specific state of the slot stays `???` until ground G settles it." This prediction is written down here so it can accrue a residual and be refused by the same test as anything else.

## 1. Residual and domain census (before any figure line is chosen)

### R by arity, symmetry and scale

- **Arity:** many to one. Thousands of extant lineages bear on one ancestral node. This is the shape of Fig 2's common-cause panel and of the "many readings, one source" problem, not a two-frame comparison.
- **Symmetry:** the extant lineages are largely interchangeable as witnesses for a shared trait. Two bacteria reading the same inherited genome are "two people reading the same report" (Fig 2 :31), not two instruments. Their symmetry is the symmetry of a single witness repeated.
- **Scale:** three scales are in play. Step (one character in one genome). Lineage (descent with modification over 4 billion years). Root (the deepest node reconstructable, and below it the origin of life, which is a different question). The transform between scales is lossy and non-invertible.

### One line per domain: actors / currency / anchor

| domain | actors | currency | anchor | nameable anchor? |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| D1 Comparative reconstruction | extant genomes, traits, trees, reconciliation models | probabilistic ancestral-state estimates | the true ancestral state | no direct anchor; the target is unobserved |
| D2 The shared-feature pool | lineages, genes, ribosomes, the genetic code, HGT | inheritance and transfer of traits | common descent as the common cause | yes, the pool itself (but it is one pool) |
| D3 Independent ground frames | fossils, isotopes, geochemistry, prebiotic experiments, laboratory evolution | physical traces and reproducible outcomes | rock, experiment, measurement | yes, a real ground, weakly connected to LUCA |
| D4 Verification / outreach | expert readers, journals, societies | agreement and residual | the ground, never agreement | yes, but independence is the scarce quantity |

### Partition or union

This is a **union**, not a partition. D1 and D2 overlap: the comparative method runs on the shared pool. D1 and D3 overlap at calibration points (fossils date the molecular clock) but draw on different evidence. The overlap D1 intersect D3 is a **compound with its own loop**: the molecular-clock calibration problem, where errors propagate from tips to root and information is missing for the branch incident on the root (Moody et al. 2024, read 2026-11-23). Per Seat Map "when a split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before searching again." A single lever (more genomes) clears the region where only D1 binds and leaves the compound standing, because D2 and D3 still bind.

### Order by dependency (Fig 3)

1. Establish which features are shared and by what mechanism (D2).
2. Reconstruct the ancestral state on a tree from those features (D1).
3. Calibrate and test against independent frames (D3).
4. Only then ask what the verification community may count as a failure (D4).

Optimising step 2 cannot rescue a break at step 1: Fig 3 :19 "Optimising a link that already works cannot rescue one that does not." A reconstruction whose input is a trait whose transmission is unmodelled is "a reading of nothing" (Fig 3 :22).

## 3. Findings, each with verbatim quotes and its own refuter

### F1: Shared features are a common pool talking, not independent corroboration (decisive for the strength of the inference)

Fig 2 :19-21:

> **"they never talk, and still agree so the agreement is the pool talking, not two frames confirming each other"**

Fig 2 :31:

> **"Two people reading the same report are not two frames; two instruments pointed at the same thing are."**

Fig 2 :40:

> **"agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool"**

Fig 8 :48:

> **"How much two frames draw on the same evidence decides both whether their agreement means anything and whether their union has any surplus."**

Every extant organism inherits from the same ancestor and many exchange genes horizontally. For a trait shared by all of them, the lines are not independent instruments; they are one report copied and edited. So the observation "universal in modern life" is strong evidence that the trait was present in the last universal common ancestor when transmission is vertical and the trait is not readily lost or transferred, but it is not corroboration from independent frames. It is one frame (the biosphere's shared inheritance) read many times. The inference is therefore bounded by the mechanism that produced the sharing, not by the number of organisms sharing it.

The world agrees, from a low-overlap source. Moody et al. (2024, Nature Ecology & Evolution) write: "Most gene families have experienced gene transfer since the time of LUCA and so explicitly modelling transfers enables us to include many more gene families in the analysis than has been possible using previous approaches." Their whole method exists because the pool is not clean descent. Quanta (Lambert, 2024) reports Goldman: "There's a lot of evolutionary processes that can get in the way of a clear signal."

- **Mode:** `general` (structural law about evidence pools).
- **Status:** triangulated (Fig 2 and Fig 8 are low-overlap: the anchor law and the surplus law).
- **Refuter:** a demonstration that two lineages acquired the trait independently, with no common cause and no transfer, so that their agreement is genuine triangulation. If shown for the traits in question, F1 is false for them.

### F2: The descendant-to-ancestor transform is non-invertible, so a feature constrains a shape, not a name

Fig 10 :70:

> **"The tree of life is what a non-invertible transform looks like from outside."**

Fig 4 :27:

> **"The transform between scales loses information, and that is the point."**

Fig 4 :30-32:

> **"So take the round trip and measure the gap. Without it you are navigating by dead reckoning: confident about a position nobody checked. The gap is not an error to eliminate. It is the measurement of what the coarser description cannot hold, and it is the only honest report of how much was given up."**

Fig 9 :32:

> **"you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself"**

Evolution runs ancestor to descendants. Reading descendants back to the ancestor is a round trip through a lossy transform, and a lossy transform has no inverse. Different ancestral states can produce the same modern sharing (gene loss can make an old gene look young; transfer can make a young gene look old, as the Quanta source states in detail). So the shared feature licenses a **shape**: the ancestor had to be capable of producing that feature under some transmission history. It does not license the **name**: the specific state, date, metabolism or genome size.

The canonical limit is stated in `FIGURES_README_canonical.md`: the framework returns "what a missing thing would have to look like (a shape, not a name)" and stops where instruments can no longer distinguish. Note the scope correction carried from QMGR-0037 and recorded in `05_what_shape_means.md`: the phrase governs a *missing* primitive, not the selection of atoms already held. LUCA's genetic code is not a missing primitive, so saying the ancestor "had a genetic code" is not forbidden. What is forbidden is reading the *source* (one specific ancestral state) off a *projection* (the modern reading) when several sources give the same projection. That is the metamerism error of *The Hard Problem is About Source* (2026): "If several paths create the same behavior, then behavior is a projection with lossy information."

- **Mode:** `specified`, falsifiable.
- **Status:** triangulated (Fig 10 non-invertibility and Fig 4 round-trip gap are different figures with low overlap; the cognition essay corroborates from a different field).
- **Refuter:** show that the transform is invertible for the traits in question, that is, that the modern distribution uniquely determines the ancestral state with no remaining multiplicity. A simulated-ancestral experiment with a known ancestor that is recovered uniquely would do it. Until then, the slot stays `???`.

### F3: The ground is absent, so nothing is grounded; the reachable target is a boundary, not the origin

Fig 2 :29:

> **"An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks."**

Fig 13 :115:

> **"The set is closed. The arrangements are not."**

Fig 13 :134:

> **"Unreachable is a verdict here, not a silence"**

Fig 6 :40:

> **"What is unavailable is a sensor for something that has never registered anywhere, at any resolution."**

There is no discovered fossil of LUCA (Wikipedia, LUCA, 2026) and the oldest traces of life date to about 3.5 Ga, long after the inferred 4.2 Ga node (Quanta, 2024). The anchor for "what was the earliest life" is therefore not presently in contact with the question. Moody et al. (2024) put the boundary explicitly: "As the common ancestor of sampled, extant prokaryotic life, LUCA is the oldest node on the species tree that we can reconstruct via phylogenomics" and "By definition, we cannot reconstruct LUCA's contemporaries using phylogenomics." The framework's own rule is that a search that finds nothing is never a proof of absence (Fig 8 :39), so this is **unreached**, not a claim that the earliest life is unknowable by any future instrument. It is a verdict about current reach.

- **Mode:** `specified` on the boundary, `general` on the rule.
- **Status:** triangulated (Fig 2 anchor test plus Fig 13 unreachable-as-verdict, with the Nature paper as a world witness).
- **Refuter:** a ground reading, that is, a fossil, isotopic or experimental trace that directly settles a specific ancestral trait excluded by the shape inference. That would move the claim from `candidate` toward `grounded` for that trait.

### F4: Adding genomes refines the same closure; independence must be bought from a different frame

Fig 8 :32:

> **"Closure is idempotent. Composing inside a closed set never leaves it, so no search, however long, adds an atom."**

Fig 6 :23:

> **"No frame escapes its own closure. The bound moves only on contact with a frame whose closure differs."**

Fig 2 :42:

> **"Buy independence in proportion to the cost of being wrong."**

Fig 7 :35:

> **"A room's agreement is variation, not a verdict. Selection between rooms is where the ground votes."**

Sequencing more genomes searches the same closure more thoroughly. It improves the estimate (inward: Fig 6 :29 "inward: is our representation adequate to hold it? The instrument extends access"), but it does not add a new kind of evidence. The outward frame is rocks and experiments: fossil calibrations, isotope records, prebiotic chemistry, laboratory evolution with known ancestors. Wikipedia's ancestral-reconstruction entry supplies a checkable instance: an experimental evolution study of bacteriophage T7 found that "maximum parsimony methods were unable to accurately reconstruct the known ancestral state of a continuous character (plaque size)", and that including fossilized samples fixed it. That is a different closure being imported, and it is the legitimate move.

- **Mode:** `general`.
- **Status:** triangulated (Fig 8 idempotence plus Fig 6 import; the T7 experiment corroborates from experimental biology).
- **Refuter:** show that additional genomes of the same domains supply an independent closure for the trait (low overlap with the original sample), so that more sequencing buys surplus rather than spelling.

### F5: Probabilities are filters for the budget, not witnesses for the verdict

Fig 9 :60:

> **"Use filters for the budget and witnesses for the verdict. Never let a filter hand you a verdict."**

Fig 5 :29-30 names "nothing new here" and "the machinery worked; the answer was already known" as the output when a guard is at zero. A gene family assigned posterior probability 0.75 of being in LUCA is a thresholded estimate inside a model. It is a filter that decides which candidates get carried into the metabolic reconstruction. It is not the ground settling that the gene was there. Moody et al. (2024) are explicit that "The result is an incomplete picture of a cellular organism that was prokaryote grade rather than progenotic" and that "We should therefore expect reconstructions of metabolic pathways to be incomplete due to this phylogenetic noise and other limitations of the analysis pipeline." A filter with a stated threshold is good practice and bad evidence if promoted to a verdict.

- **Mode:** `general`.
- **Status:** single-source for the figure rule (Fig 9); the limitation is corroborated by the Nature source at low overlap.
- **Refuter:** a demonstration that the thresholded probability is itself settled by a ground reading (for example, a dated fossil containing the gene product), at which point it stops being only a filter.

### F6: Arrangement, not inventory: a shared gene list is not the ancestral organism

Fig 12 :5:

> **"A set of ingredients is not one. The bond is the rest of the information, and it is the half nothing was recording."**

Fig 12 :117:

> **"Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."**

Fig 12 :104:

> **"A settled molecule becomes an atom for whatever composes over it. So irreducible is a fact about a level and never about the world."**

Moody et al. (2024) report a gene count and a probability for each family, then reconstruct metabolism. The inventory is not the organism. Which genes are bonded into which pathways, which are regulated, which came by transfer, and which were lost later determine the phenotype. Fig 12 says the arrangement carries information the operands do not. So even a perfect list of genes present in LUCA would not deliver the portrait without the bonds, and the bonds are exactly what 4 billion years of loss and transfer erase. A further consequence: because a settled molecule can become an atom at the next level, a well-supported reconstruction can be composed over, but only if it was settled. A probability-filtered inventory is not settled, so composing a full portrait over it is premature.

- **Mode:** `general`.
- **Status:** single-source for the figure definition (Fig 12), corroborated by the Nature authors' own "incomplete picture" wording.
- **Refuter:** show that the arrangement is recoverable from the inventory alone for the traits at issue, so that the bond adds nothing.

### F8: Strain test, with one live strain named

Fig 12 :104 ("a settled molecule becomes an atom") could be read as licensing the move: shared features are settled in modern organisms, therefore they become atoms that compose upward into a picture of LUCA. Read that way, it strains against F2's non-invertibility. **Re-census:** Fig 12 governs composition inside a frame after settlement. It does not govern the reverse traversal of a lossy transform across scales. Different objects, so there is no contradiction. The strain is a warning about premature composition, not a correction. It is recorded here and carried to `11_audit.md`, not hidden.

### F9: The honest stop

Fig 13 :140-141:

> **"what could have been concluded, and what would have had to be true for the answer to differ."**

The framework can report "unreachable" and can characterise what is missing in effect terms. It cannot name the missing primitive, because naming it would mean already holding it (Fig 13 :135). So the answer to "how much" is: a shape and a boundary, with the specific portrait held as a `specified` candidate and the first life's chemistry explicitly out of reach of this method. This is not a silence and it is not a denial that progress is possible.

## 4. Pre-registered refuter (written before the conclusion)

A falsifier written before the run is a design instrument (Seat Map :287: "a falsifier written before the run is a design instrument, not only an honesty device"). The shape claim is refuted if any of the following holds:

1. **Invertibility.** A demonstration that the modern distribution of a shared trait uniquely determines its ancestral state, with no remaining multiplicity under loss, transfer and convergence. That breaks F2.
2. **Independence.** A demonstration that the extant lineages carrying the trait acquired it independently, with no common cause and no transfer, so their agreement is genuine triangulation. That breaks F1.
3. **Ground.** A fossil, isotopic or experimental reading that directly settles a specific ancestral trait in a way the shape inference excluded. That breaks F3's "unreached" status for that trait.

A report of "the genomes agree" or "the probability is 0.9" fires none of these. If any fires, the relevant finding is withdrawn and the answer rewritten. Until one is run, no part of this folder may be labelled `grounded`.

## 8. Verdict of the audit

The shape survives; the portrait is held as a candidate and the name is refused. Shared modern features license an inference to the **shape** of the earliest life (a common ancestral population with heritable molecular machinery, bounded by the mechanisms that produced the sharing), and they establish a **boundary** (the deepest node reconstructable is not the origin of life). They do not license a unique named state, because the descendant-to-ancestor transform is non-invertible and the evidence is one pool, not independent frames. Mode: `specified`, ungrounded, candidate. Strains after re-census: one, F8, named and carried forward. Gaps: the first life's chemistry is out of reach of this method, and the MDPI critique could not be fetched.
