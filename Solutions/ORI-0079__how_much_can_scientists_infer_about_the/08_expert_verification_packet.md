# Expert verification packet: what shared modern features license about the earliest life

**What this is:** a claim in a form an origins-of-life researcher or evolutionary biologist can check. The mode, the derivation and the refuters are stated before the conclusion, so a reader knows what to look for and what would settle it. This is one-to-one, not a bulk send; use it with the public venues in `03_public_contact_points.csv`.

**Mode: `specified` on the verdict, `general` on the structural shape, not `grounded`.** No fossil, cell, genome or experiment was measured for this packet. It reports a shape and a route, not a result.

## 1. The claim, in one paragraph

Features shared by all modern organisms (genetic code, ribosome, ATP currency, shared chirality, core metabolism) constrain the shape of the earliest life but do not name it. The agreement of many genomes is one evidence pool read many times, not independent corroboration; its strength is set by the transmission mechanism (vertical descent versus loss, transfer and convergence), not by the number of organisms. The descendant-to-ancestor step is a lossy, non-invertible transform, so a modern formula admits many ancestral histories (isomers under Fig 12) and returns a kind of thing rather than a specific state. The deepest node reconstructable from genomes is a boundary, not the origin of life. Posterior probabilities filter the search budget; they are not the ground settling a verdict. What is offered is that shape, written refuters, and the independent frames that would move the boundary.

## 2. Three checkable sub-claims

### C1 : framework-internal; checkable by reading Figures 2 and 8

- **Statement:** universality among modern organisms is one evidence pool talking, not many frames confirming each other. More of the same genomes refine an estimate; they do not buy independence. Buy independence in proportion to the cost of being wrong.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 2 :19 "they never talk, and still agree so the agreement is the pool talking, not two frames confirming each other". Fig 2 :31 "Two people reading the same report are not two frames; two instruments pointed at the same thing are." Fig 2 :40 "agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool". Fig 8 :48 "How much two frames draw on the same evidence decides both whether their agreement means anything and whether their union has any surplus." Fig 2 :42 "Buy independence in proportion to the cost of being wrong."
- **What to check:** are the traits in question transmitted vertically and rarely lost or transferred? If so, universality is strong evidence for ancestry. If transfer and loss are common, the strength of the inference drops. The claim is mechanism-dependent, not a denial of common ancestry.
- **Refuter:** a demonstration that the extant lineages acquired the trait independently, with no common cause and no transfer, so their agreement is genuine triangulation for those traits. Or a demonstration that additional genomes of the same domains supply a low-overlap closure for the trait, so that more sequencing buys surplus rather than spelling. Either breaks C1.

### C2 : framework-internal; checkable by reading Figures 4, 9, 10 and 12

- **Statement:** the descendant-to-ancestor transform is non-invertible, so a shared feature constrains a shape, not a name. Many ancestral histories over the same modern ingredients are isomers: same atoms, different bond, different substance.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 10 :70 "The tree of life is what a non-invertible transform looks like from outside." Fig 4 :27 "The transform between scales loses information, and that is the point." Fig 4 :30 "So take the round trip and measure the gap. Without it you are navigating by dead reckoning: confident about a position nobody checked." Fig 9 :32 "you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself". Fig 12 :117 "Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."
- **What to check:** can two different ancestral states (with different histories of gene loss, horizontal transfer and convergence) produce the same modern distribution of the trait? The Quanta explainer by Jonathan Lambert (2024) documents that gene loss can make an old gene look young and transfer can make a young gene look old, which is exactly the multiplicity the claim requires.
- **Refuter:** show that the transform is invertible for the traits in question, that is, that the modern distribution uniquely determines the ancestral state with no remaining multiplicity. A simulated-ancestral experiment in which a known ancestor is recovered uniquely would do it. That breaks C2.

### C3 : biology-facing; checkable against the reconstruction literature

- **Statement:** the deepest node reconstructable from genomes is a boundary, not the origin of life, and a probability threshold inside a model is a filter, not a verdict. Ground reading, not agreement, decides a specific ancestral trait.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 13 :134 "Unreachable is a verdict here, not a silence". Fig 6 :40 "What is unavailable is a sensor for something that has never registered anywhere, at any resolution." Fig 9 :60 "Use filters for the budget and witnesses for the verdict. Never let a filter hand you a verdict." Fig 10 :42 "A verifier that can reconstruct the claim cannot verify it."
- **What to check:** Moody et al. (2024, Nature Ecology & Evolution) write "By definition, we cannot reconstruct LUCA's contemporaries using phylogenomics" and describe their result as "an incomplete picture of a cellular organism that was prokaryote grade rather than progenotic". The Quanta explainer calls LUCA "the furthest point in evolutionary history that we can glimpse by working backward from what's alive today." The ancestral-reconstruction reference records a known-ancestor T7 phage experiment in which "maximum parsimony methods were unable to accurately reconstruct the known ancestral state of a continuous character (plaque size)", repaired by including fossilized samples.
- **Refuter:** a fossil, isotopic or experimental reading that directly settles a specific ancestral trait in a way the shape inference excluded, or a dated fossil containing the gene product that settles a thresholded probability. That moves the claim toward `grounded` for that trait and breaks the "unreached" status in C3.

## 3. What is explicitly not claimed

- Not that the earliest life was a complex acetogen at 4.2 Ga with ~2600 proteins and an early CRISPR system. That is a candidate model from phylogenetics, held as `specified` and ungrounded, not a result of this derivation.
- Not that saying the ancestor had a genetic code, a ribosome or shared chirality is forbidden. Those ingredients are observed; the shape-not-a-name limit governs a *missing* primitive, not the selection of atoms already held (`05` §5).
- Not that the earliest life is unknowable. It is unreached by this method at this reach, which is a verdict about the method, not a denial that a better instrument could move it.
- Not that the first life's chemistry is reachable from shared modern features alone. That is a `gap` for this method, not for science.
- Not `grounded`, not a measurement, and not a theory of everything. No last room is claimed (Fig 7 :6 "the limit: no last room").
- Not a verdict assembled by counting agreeable recipients. Correlation among a selected audience disqualifies a verdict (Fig 2 :40).

## 5. What would settle it

A source-discriminating ground reading, not a further argument. The three refuters in §2 are written in advance. The productive next step is a different closure: fossils, isotopes, prebiotic chemistry, or an evolution experiment with a known ancestor (Fig 6 :23 "No frame escapes its own closure. The bound moves only on contact with a frame whose closure differs."). Send the claim and the refuters first; record the verdict before showing the theory; never pool later agreement as corroboration.

## 6. What an expert can check in twenty minutes

1. Read Fig 2 :19, :31, :40 and Fig 8 :48. Is the one-pool reading of shared traits right for the traits you work on? Name a mechanism that would make the lineages independent, if one exists.
2. Read Fig 10 :70, Fig 4 :27 and Fig 9 :32. Can you exhibit a pair of ancestral states that give the same modern distribution for a trait you know? If not, C2 may be too strong for that trait.
3. Read Fig 13 :134 and Fig 9 :60. Check the Moody et al. wording in §2 C3 against the paper. Is the boundary claim accurate, and is the probability threshold being used as a filter in the paper rather than as a verdict?
4. Name one fossil, isotopic or experimental reading that would settle a specific ancestral trait excluded by the shape. If you can name one, the honest stop is refuted for that trait.
5. Check the refuter before the claim, not after. If any of the three refuters holds for the traits in question, say so; a failure is more useful than an endorsement.
6. Send the refutation to a public institutional venue in `03_public_contact_points.csv`, or reply with a one-line failure. No reply is required if you would rather not engage.
