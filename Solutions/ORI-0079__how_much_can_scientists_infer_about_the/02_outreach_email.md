# Outreach draft: check the reach of the inference before you read the theory

**Use:** one-to-one, or as a cover letter attached to a submission through the public venues in `03_public_contact_points.csv`. **Not** for bulk send.

**Hard precondition:** the stage-1 output below must survive the three refuters in `01_figure_grounded_audit.md` §4. If one fires, do not send. The two-stage offer collapses on contact if the output does not survive.

---

**Subject:** One checkable claim about what shared modern traits can tell us about the earliest life

> Hi [name],
>
> I have one specific output I would like you to check against what you already know. I am not asking you to read a theory first, and I am not asking you to agree with anything. If the output does not survive, there is nothing else to look at.
>
> **The output (`specified`, not `grounded`).** Thousands of living organisms share a small set of molecular features: a genetic code, ribosomes, a common energy currency, a shared chirality. The tempting inference is that whatever is universal today was present in the earliest life, and that we can name that life from the shared set. The output says that inference has a reach and a limit.
>
> 1. The reach: a shared feature constrains the *shape* of the ancestor. The earliest life had to be capable of producing the feature under some transmission history. A common ancestral population with heritable molecular machinery is supported. This part is structural, not a measurement.
> 2. The limit: the descendant-to-ancestor step is a lossy, non-invertible transform. Gene loss can make an old gene look young; horizontal transfer can make a young gene look old. So the shared feature does not name the specific ancestral state, and it does not by itself reach back to the origin of life, only to the deepest node reconstructable from genomes.
> 3. The evidence status: the agreement of many modern genomes is one evidence pool read many times, not many independent confirmations. More of the same genomes refine the estimate; they do not add a new kind of evidence. What would add one is a different closure: fossils, isotopes, prebiotic chemistry, or an evolution experiment with a known ancestor.
>
> **What I am not claiming.** Not that the earliest life was a complex acetogen at 4.2 Ga, or that it had the specific genome reconstructed in recent papers. Those are candidate models from phylogenetics, held as `specified` and ungrounded, not results of this derivation. Not that the earliest life is unknowable. It is unreached by this method at this reach, which is a verdict about the method, not a denial that a better instrument could move it. Not a theory of everything, and not the only true account.
>
> **What would refute the output.** I wrote these down before sending this.
> 1. Show that the modern distribution of a shared trait uniquely determines its ancestral state, with no multiplicity left under loss, transfer and convergence.
> 2. Show that the lineages carrying the trait acquired it independently, with no common cause and no transfer, so their agreement is real triangulation.
> 3. Produce a fossil, isotopic or experimental reading that settles a specific ancestral trait in a way the shape inference excluded.
>
> If any one holds, I withdraw the corresponding claim. A one-line "this fails because..." is more useful to me than agreement. If you would rather not engage, no reply is needed, and I will not follow up.
>
> **If it survives, and only then:** here are the sources I actually read. The figures, not my summary of them, are the authority.
> - Moody et al., "The nature of the last universal common ancestor and its impact on the early Earth system", Nature Ecology & Evolution 8, 1654 to 1666 (2024): https://www.nature.com/articles/s41559-024-02461-1
> - Jonathan Lambert, "All Life on Earth Today Descended From a Single Cell. Meet LUCA.", Quanta Magazine (2024): https://www.quantamagazine.org/all-life-on-earth-today-descended-from-a-single-cell-meet-luca-20241120
> - "Ancestral reconstruction" (method limits, including the known-ancestor T7 experiment): https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ancestral_reconstruction
> - "Underdetermination of Scientific Theory", Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy (2009, revised 2023): https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/scientific-underdetermination/
> - Figure text extract with line numbers (local): `FRAMEWORK/reference/03-FIGURES_TEXT.md`
>
> **If you want to use the machinery:** the formula is in the framework's public figures folder. Run it on a problem you know well and can check against a ground. Write down what would refute your result *before* you run it, and label the mode of what you get. A reported failure is more useful to me than a reported success.
>
> Thanks for reading,
> [name]

## Two-stage rule, stated plainly

1. **Stage 1:** send the shape claim and the three refuters. Ask what they would count as a failure. Record their verdict **before** showing any theory.
2. **Stage 2, opt-in only:** if they are curious, send the source links. Their later agreement is never pooled as corroboration, because they have now seen the interior (Fig 2 :40).
3. **Stage 3, opt-in only:** offer the formula as a method to reuse, with the instruction that each user writes their own refuter and states their own mode.

No stage asks for trust, belief, endorsement, or a show of hands. A failure is the useful outcome.
