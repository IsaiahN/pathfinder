> **The output (`specified` on shape, `general` on structure, `gap` on existence):** the framework's minimum set has six terms in two kinds, states (space, time, energy) and structures (spectrum, gradient, network), with the rule that composition runs across kinds and never within one. So a smooth regime and a foam-like regime are derivatives of one six-term set at different levels, not two different substances. Second, there is no bottom: whatever scale you reach is itself composable further, so "foam at the smallest scale" is refused as written; foam, if it exists, is a level, not a terminal floor. The framework returns that shape, not a name.
>
> **What it does not say:** it does not say foam exists. That is a ground question and I record it as a gap. Existing experiments (Chandra, Fermi, VERITAS, the Holometer) have set bounds, and finding nothing is not a proof of absence. The framework's own rule is that the relevant question is whether anything is already returning a reading that fails to resolve, not whether a sensor could in principle exist. So I am not offering an existence claim.
>
> **What I am not claiming:** that any named program, spin foam, causal set, noncommutative geometry, is the answer the framework derives. Those are bindings from physics frames onto the small-scale slot. Convergent derivation and adopted import look identical in the contents and only the record separates them. Until provenance is shown they carry zero evidentiary weight.
