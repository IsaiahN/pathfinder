# Smoothness, foam and levels: the supporting derivation

**Mode: `specified` on the arrangement claim, `general` on the no-bottom rule.** Simulated derivation checked against the figures, not a ground reading (see `05_what_shape_means.md` section 6).

## RESIDUAL

The main audit leaves three structural questions open. (1) Is smoothness a thing a level contains or a remainder a level leaves? (2) Is there a first, smallest level at which foam would be the floor? (3) If a smooth regime and a foam regime are both arrangements of the six, what is their structural relation: isomers, a functional group, or something else?

## DOMAIN CENSUS

Atoms held: space, time, energy (states); spectrum, gradient, network (structures). Bonds held: the seven plus negation. No new primitive is available without import. Smoothness and discreteness are not atoms; they are what a turn leaves (Fig 13 :124). Currency: composition inside closure(Γ) and the remainder each turn leaves. Anchor: the figures as metalaws. This is D1 from `01`; D2 (instruments) and D3 (verification) are not touched here.

## DERIVATION

### Q1 - Smoothness is a remainder, not an occupant

- Fig 13 :43-44: *"A continuum was the seventh and was removed for failing exactly this"* and *"A generating set must not contain what its own members produce."*
- Fig 13 :124: *"Smoothness, symmetry, chaos and discreteness are each what a particular composition leaves behind, and none is a seventh member."*
- `THE_FORMULA.md` substrate: the derived quantities *"are what a turn leaves rather than members of the set."*

⇒ A smooth regime is not the presence of a smooth primitive. It is what some cross-kind composition leaves at a level. A foam-like regime is what a different composition leaves at another level. Both are remainders. Neither adds a member.

**Falsifier:** produce a term in the minimum set whose removal leaves smoothness undefined, or derive smoothness from the other five and show it is irreducible. Either breaks the fig's derivative reading.

### Q2 - There is no first, smallest level

- Fig 12 :104: *"A settled molecule becomes an atom for whatever composes over it. So irreducible is a fact about a level and never about the world, and there is no bottom"*
- Fig 4 :33: *"Which is why there is no top and no infinite regress."*
- Fig 13 :90: *"One chain is one route through a space that has no last route."*

⇒ Whatever scale is reached, its settled composition can become an atom for a further composition. "Foam at the smallest scale" has no figure-licensed referent; there is no smallest scale. The honest rephrasing is: foam at scales where the smoothness-leaving composition stops leaving smoothness, with no claim of finality.

**Falsifier:** exhibit a level at which composition stops because there is no further level to compose over, or a first level with no predecessor. Both are refused by the lines above; either would overturn the reading.

### Q3 - Smooth and foam regimes are a functional group, not necessarily isomers

- Fig 12 :117: **"Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."**
- Fig 12 :124: **"functional group. A substructure whose presence fixes the outcome. The group says what kind; the qualifier says which member."**

Both regimes are built from the same six. If they are written over the full shared atom set, differing only in which structure mediates which state pair, they satisfy the figure's **isomer** definition: same atoms, different bond, different substance. If they are written with different atoms in the slots (smoothness left by a gradient-over-space arrangement, foam left by a network arrangement), they share a **functional group** (a cross-kind composition leaving a remainder) with different members. The isomer claim is the stronger one and needs the full shared set; the functional-group claim is the weaker one and is already licensed.

**Falsifier (written before the run):** if a foam regime requires an atom the smooth regime does not, or vice versa, the isomer claim fails and only the functional-group claim stands. If some third regime leaves neither smoothness nor discreteness, both need re-census.

### Q4 - The order is a round trip with a measured gap, not a derivation from a description

- Fig 4 :30: *"So take the round trip and measure the gap. Without it you are navigating by dead reckoning: confident about a position nobody checked."*
- Fig 6 :30: *"The description travels. Fails by being sharpened into terms only we can read. Cast it in effect terms."*

Carry the small-scale shape up as an effect description (what would be seen if the smoothness-leaving composition stopped leaving it), bring back a concrete instrument prediction (photon-speed fuzziness, image blur, correlated interferometer wander), and compare. The comparison is the ground's job, not the figures'. Current null results land on the "no detectable degradation" side; per Fig 8 :39 that is not proof of absence.

**Falsifier:** a concrete instrument prediction derived from the shape that the ground decisively refutes, with the refutation recorded against the specific figure line that generated it. That would be a real strain and would send us back to the census.

## CONCLUSION

Smoothness and foam are remainders of composition, not primitives; there is no smallest level; the two regimes are at least a functional group and, over the full shared atom set, isomers with a stated falsifier. The order between them is a round trip whose gap must be measured against instruments, not derived from a description. This rescues the structural content of the foam question without naming a microstructure or claiming an existence verdict.
