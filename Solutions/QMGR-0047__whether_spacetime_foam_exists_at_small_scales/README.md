## Question restated in effect terms

Decide two things that are often bundled. (1) Existence: does the smooth continuum description of spacetime fail at sufficiently small scales, giving way to a fluctuating, discrete or topologically complex regime (foam)? (2) Shape: if it does, what must that regime look like structurally? Option A keeps smoothness as fundamental at every scale. Option B treats smoothness as a scale-dependent derivative and asks what is left when that derivative stops being produced. A leads to a terminal bottom, which the figures forbid. B leads to a level with its own structure, which the figures can describe as a shape but cannot name.

What an answer would look like: an instrument that already returns a reading failing to resolve at small scales (existence), plus a structural constraint any foam candidate must satisfy (shape). The shape is bound as an ordinary term in `01` and `09`.
