## Wider literature that bears on this question

1. **Pross and Pascal 2013** is the strongest low-overlap corroboration. It argues that abiogenesis and biological evolution are one continuous physico-chemical process, which is the world-pool analogue of F1's union claim. It also says the precise historic path is probably unknowable, which is the analogue of F7. It is cited as corroboration only, never as the source of the verdict.
2. **Definition of life (Wikipedia)** contains the question's own two options in one sentence, which is useful as a check that the residual was not invented. It also records Carol Cleland's position that science needs a general theory of living systems rather than a definition, which corroborates F6.
3. **Sorites paradox (Wikipedia)** supplies the boundary-placement shape from logic. Its warning about the continuum fallacy is kept visible: the Tether answer does not deny that real transitions occurred, only that one of them is *the* boundary by virtue of the word.
4. **Pereto, Bada and Lazcano 2009** supplies the historical point that the origin question was deliberately left untouchable by Darwin, which supports the honest stop rather than a date.
5. **NASA Astrobiology FAQ** supplies the working definition as a *filter*, which is exactly the role Fig 9 :60 permits for filters. It is not used as a witness.

Overlap warning: the two Wikipedia pages and the NASA pages are tertiary or institutional summaries drawing on overlapping literature. They are **one pool**, not independent witnesses. The two peer-reviewed papers are a different pool from each other by author and method (systems chemistry versus history of science), but both concern the same origin-of-life literature, so their agreement is expected. No world source here was generated by the Tether framework, which is an improvement over the exemplar's all-author evidence pool; it still does not make world agreement a verdict.
