## 0. The claim under audit

The question offers two candidate anchors for the first instance of "life":

- **A.** Life began only when a system could reproduce independently.
- **B.** Life began earlier, when chemical selection began.

Stated as an effect: a continuous physical process is being cut by a discrete predicate, and we are asked which event the cut attaches to. A attaches it to a closed individual capability, late. B attaches it to a population-level process, early. A yields a sharp boundary and a nameable first living thing. B yields a graded boundary and no clean first individual. The residual is not "which answer is right" but "is this one question, and which anchor is legitimate for it."

Binding the sought-for shape as an ordinary term, per `THE_FORMULA.md` "Direction": an answer would look like (i) a decision about whether the question is a partition or a union, (ii) a named legitimate anchor, (iii) a statement of what the other option is (a later capability, or a convention), and (iv) a refuter that would fire if the ordering were wrong. That is what is tested below.

## 1. Domain census (done before choosing figure lines)

**R by arity, symmetry, scale.**

- **Arity:** a binary predicate ("alive" / "not alive") applied to a temporal series of states, with two named candidate anchors. It is a **union of overlapping conditions** (selection, replication, energetic coupling, compartmentalisation), not a partition into two mutually exclusive events. Fig 9 :4-5: "First: is there a fact here? Disagreements that do not shrink with effort, where each rule keeps working well on a different subset, are not one hard question but several well-formed ones. Split it rather than search."
- **Symmetry:** the two options are not symmetric alternatives at one level. Selection is a relation over a population extended in time; reproduction is a capability of a bounded individual at a moment. They cannot be compared by a single threshold without a category error.
- **Scale:** chemical and molecular (replicators, autocatalytic networks, gradients) versus organismal (a bounded system that reproduces). The boundary claim therefore crosses a membrane, so the level rule applies (Fig 10 :68-69: "Different actors, a different currency, a different ground, and a lossy transform between. That is why there is no top rung and no regress: a generator crosses, an instance does not.").

**One line per domain: actors / currency / anchor.** A domain without a nameable anchor is not a domain yet.

| domain | actors | currency | anchor | nameable anchor? |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| D1 Chemical selection, unmaintained habitat | replicators, autocatalytic sets, monomers, gradients, compartments | differential persistence, which variant the ground kept | the ground, read as physical persistence | yes: "nothing selects; what survives is what the ground kept" (Fig 11 :14-15) |

**Partition or union.** Union. Selection and reproduction overlap: a reproducing system is also subject to selection, and selection can run among replicators before any one of them is an independently reproducing individual. Fig 9 :54-55: "And when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again. A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones." The source question set itself says the programmes "are not always mutually exclusive" (world source, Abiogenesis).

**Order by dependency (Fig 3).**

1. Split the union into its three questions before searching any of them (Fig 9 :4-5, :54-58). Otherwise any frame will seem to fit.
2. Settle the process question (D1, selection) before the capability question (D2, reproduction), because reproduction is a later link consuming what selection supplies. Fig 3 :19-20: "Optimising a link that already works cannot rescue one that does not. The chain is ordered by dependency, not by importance."
3. Choose the naming convention last, and label it a convention (Fig 2 :43: "a convention that decides an outcome has stopped being one").
4. Read the historic record last, against the split questions, not before them (Fig 9 :27-28: "describe it before you go looking, or any frame you pick will seem to fit").

**Strain test, applied before concluding.** Fig 11 :56 says of the unmaintained habitat "nothing selects", while Fig 11 :36 says "selection ran". Read as two claims about one quantity, they contradict. Re-census: :56 denies a *selecting agent or seat* (no maintainer in that habitat), while :36 affirms *ground-driven differential persistence*. Different objects, so no strain after re-census. This matches the canonical rule: a reading that makes two figures contradict one another has misidentified its domain.

## 3. Findings

### F1 — The two options are a union, not a partition (decisive for the question's form)

Fig 9 :54-55: "And when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again. A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones." Fig 9 :57-58: "a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either."

Selection and reproduction are not alternatives at one threshold. Selection can operate on a population of imperfect replicators before any one of them closes a reproductive loop; conversely a loop that closes is immediately subject to selection. The question's "or" is therefore a bundle. Unbundled, it yields three questions: when selection began, when closure appeared, and which word we will use. Only the first two have grounds.

Fig 9 :4-5: "First: is there a fact here? Disagreements that do not shrink with effort, where each rule keeps working well on a different subset, are not one hard question but several well-formed ones. Split it rather than search."

The world literature agrees from a different evidence pool: the Abiogenesis source says the transition "was not a single event, but a process of increasing complexity involving ... molecular self-replication, self-assembly, autocatalysis, and the emergence of cell membranes." Pross and Pascal 2013 add that "replication first" and "metabolism first" are "not necessarily mutually exclusive." This is corroboration only; it does not decide.

**Refuter:** show that selection and reproduction are mutually exclusive at the relevant scale, that is, that no differential persistence can occur before a bounded individual reproduces. If that holds, the union reading fails and the question is a genuine partition.

### F2 — The label is applied afterwards to a lineage that met its ground (governing finding for B)

Fig 11 :36: "Where nothing maintains anything, intelligence is a label applied afterwards to a lineage that met its ground. Nothing was intelligent; selection ran. The word does no work there."

Fig 11 :34-35: "What is intelligence? The wrong question, and this figure is the replacement for it."

The figure names a family of questions of which this is an instance. "When did life begin?" has the same form as "What is intelligence?": a label is being asked to mark a boundary in a process. The figure's replacement move is stated at Fig 11 :41: "Which is the same move as asking what generates lift rather than whether a thing is a bird." Asking whether something is a bird is the label question; asking what generates lift is the contact question. Applied here, the contact question is "when did differential persistence start running," not "at what instant does the word become true."

Two consequences:

1. The process precedes the label. So the underlying event (selection) precedes the name (life). This is B's structural core.
2. The figure does not license a name for the boundary instant. Fig 9 :32: "you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself." The shape is "a label applied afterwards to a lineage that met its ground," not a date.

Fig 11 :56, re-censused in section 1: "Unmaintained. No maintainer and no live frame in the seat. The habitat is pure contact, nothing selects, and what survives is what the ground kept." No selector is needed for selection to run. Option A quietly assumes a selector-like threshold (an individual that can reproduce on its own); the figure says the ground was doing the work throughout.

**Refuter:** a ground-visible demonstration that the "label applied afterwards" rule governs only seated, self-maintained habitats and not unmaintained chemical ones. That would remove the figure's support for B and return the question to A.

### F3 — The legitimate anchor is the process, not the threshold capability

Fig 2 :36: "An anchor is legitimate when the question is constitutively about it." Fig 2 :37-38: "It is only an instrument for is this code correct, and instruments can be wrong on axes they do not measure."

For a question constitutively about the origin of the capacity to evolve, the anchor is the onset of differential persistence. Independent reproduction is an instrument: a threshold that can be measured, but one that is wrong on the axis this question asks about, because it is blind to selection running before closure. Virus cases make the same point from the world pool: the Definition of life source notes viruses "are incapable of reproduction on their own" yet are "considered a borderline case," so the reproduction instrument fails on a case the frame itself admits.

Fig 2 :35: "Alignment is triangulation, not negotiation. A mirror cannot correct you." A vote on which definition to use is a convention, not an anchor.

**Refuter:** show that the question is constitutively about the word "life" rather than about the process, in which case there is no ground and the correct output is a declared convention, not a verdict. This is a scope condition that would redirect, not a flat contradiction.

### F4 — Reproduction is a later link in a dependency chain, not the origin

Fig 12 :121: "cycle. The output re-enters as input. The sign of the loop decides whether it closes, and a chain cannot close by construction." Independent reproduction is exactly such a closed cycle. Fig 12 :119: "catalyst. A part that survives its own use." Chemical autocatalysis is already a part that survives its own use, so the structural material of reproduction is present before any individual reproduces independently.

Fig 3 :19-20: "Optimising a link that already works cannot rescue one that does not. The chain is ordered by dependency, not by importance." Closure is downstream of the links that supply it. Fig 3 :22-23: "A step whose input never arrives cannot be diagnosed, only its predecessor can. Which is why a reading taken below the break is not a weak reading. It is a reading of nothing." Placing the origin at closure takes a reading below the break.

Fig 11 :39: "So the answerable question is not what intelligence is, but what makes a system capable. And capability is a property of agent-and-habitat, never of the agent alone." Independent reproduction is a capability of system and habitat, so it cannot be the starting point.

**Refuter:** a measurement showing that a closed reproductive cycle appears with no prior differential persistence, that is, that the input to the link arrives before the link that supplies it.

### F5 — Level-relativity forbids a privileged boundary stratum

Fig 12 :104: "A settled molecule becomes an atom for whatever composes over it. So irreducible is a fact about a level and never about the world." Fig 13 :121: "Different routes can arrive at the same substance."

Once a replicating system is settled, it becomes an atom for whatever composes over it. Each level can draw its own boundary, and none is the world's boundary. So the phrase "life began at" is always indexed to a level: chemical, protocellular, cellular, organismal. A single unindexed boundary is a category mistake, not a discovery.

The world source Sorites paradox makes the same point from logic: "The boundary, wherever it may be set, remains arbitrary, and so its precision is misleading." It adds the honest caveat that vagueness alone does not make the claim invalid (the continuum fallacy), which is why the shape claim does not deny that there were real transitions. It denies only that one of them is *the* boundary.

**Refuter:** derive one of the six substrate terms from the other five, or show a level at which irreducibility is absolute rather than level-relative. Either breaks the general result.

### F6 — The naming frame has no ground of its own

D3 has no nameable anchor. Fig 2 :43: "a convention that decides an outcome has stopped being one." Seat Map :167: "Declaring a meaning does not ground it." Fig 9 :60: "Use filters for the budget and witnesses for the verdict. Never let a filter hand you a verdict."

A working definition such as "a self-sustaining chemical system capable of Darwinian evolution" (world source, NASA Astrobiology FAQ) is useful as a filter for what to look for. It is not a witness for when the world changed. The Definition of life source records the same structural fact: Carol Cleland "considers that the problem is caused by the vagueness of spoken language, and that science does not need a definition of life, but rather a general theory of living systems." Pross and Pascal 2013 put it in their own vocabulary: "Issues such as identifying historic transitions, the definition of life, would become to some extent arbitrary and ruled by scientific conventions, rather than by matters of principle."

**Refuter:** a demonstrable ground that settles word usage. If such a ground exists, D3 becomes a real domain and the verdict must be recomputed.

### F7 — The precise historic boundary is unreachable, and that is a verdict

Fig 8 :39: "So a search that finds nothing is never a proof of absence." Fig 13 :134: "Unreachable is a verdict here, not a silence." Fig 10 :24: "the ground does not decay, the channel does."

D4's channel is weak for prebiotic stages. Pross and Pascal 2013 say much of the historic facet "will probably never be known" and that speculation about the precise path may be "effectively unfalsifiable, and therefore of limited scientific value." Pereto, Bada and Lazcano 2009 show Darwin himself deliberately left the origin untouched: "My theory leaves quite untouched the question of spontaneous generation."

`THE_FORMULA.md` step 7 separates three questions. SEARCHED asks whether the transition is in the closure and unfound. INWARD asks whether any instrument already returns something that fails to resolve. OUTWARD asks whether another frame already holds it. For the historic path, INWARD fails: the source ledger's Pross and Pascal paper says there is no known geological record of prebiotic systems and phylogenetics loses resolution further back. OUTWARD succeeds partially: systems chemistry holds an ahistoric account of the same process. So the honest output is "unreached for the historic path, reached for the ahistoric shape," with the lineage recorded. Importing an invented first-life date against this budget limit would add an atom that was not needed.

**Refuter:** a rock record or experimental replay that resolves a specific transition with a dated, checkable signature. That would move D4 from unreachable to grounded.

### F8 — Prestige and agreement cannot settle this

Fig 7 :13: "agreement compounds, evidence does not." Seat Map :163: "High correlation is disqualifying for a verdict and efficient for a convention." Fig 2 :42: "Buy independence in proportion to the cost of being wrong."

A campaign that gathers famous names to agree that selection came first produces a convention about a definition. It does not touch D1 or D4. The correct ask is a refutation of the shape claim, from a frame that has not read the framework's interiors.

**Refuter:** none within the figures. This is a standing constraint on method, not a falsifiable claim about the world. Recorded as such.

## 4. Pre-registered refuter (written before the conclusion)

Required before outreach: Seat Map :287 "And a falsifier written before the run is a design instrument, not only an honesty device."

The shape claim is refuted if any of the following fires:

1. **Ordering falsifier.** A ground-visible measurement shows a sharp onset of a closed reproductive cycle with no prior differential persistence among replicators. That would put the label before the process and restore A.
2. **Scale falsifier.** A demonstration that differential persistence cannot occur at chemical scale without a bounded, independently reproducing individual. That would make selection a downstream consequence of reproduction, not its predecessor.
3. **Figure-scope falsifier.** A re-census showing that Fig 11 :36 "intelligence is a label applied afterwards" governs only seated, self-maintained habitats and not unmaintained chemical habitats. That removes F2's support.
4. **Structure falsifier.** A derivation of one of the six substrate terms from the other five, or a proof that irreducibility is absolute at some level. That breaks F5's level-relativity.
5. **Scope condition, not a refuter.** If the question is constitutively about word usage rather than the process, D3 is a convention and the correct output is a declared convention, not a verdict on A or B. Stated in advance so it cannot be smuggled in after the fact.

If any of 1 to 4 holds, the claim is withdrawn. Until one is run, nothing here is `grounded`.

### Seven-question recursive derivation, summary

1. **One question or several?** Several, a union, per Fig 9 :4-5 and :54-58.
2. **Which anchor is legitimate?** Selection, for a process question (Fig 2 :36). Reproduction is an instrument blind to the earlier axis (Fig 2 :37-38).
