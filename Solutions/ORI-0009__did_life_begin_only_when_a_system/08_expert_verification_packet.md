# Expert verification packet — the Tether on the boundary of life

**What this is:** a claim in a form an origins researcher, philosopher of biology or astrobiologist can check. Mode, derivation and refuters are stated before the conclusion, so a reader knows what to look for and what would settle it.

**Mode: `general` on the shape, `specified` on the historical ordering, not `grounded`.** No measurement of the first transition is claimed. This is a shape and a route, not a result. It is a simulated derivation checked for internal consistency against the figures, not a ground reading.

## 1. The claim, in one paragraph

The question "did life begin at independent reproduction or at the start of chemical selection?" is a bundle, not a partition. Selection and reproduction overlap, so it must be split into three questions: when differential persistence began, when a reproductive loop closed, and which word we will use. The figure-grounded result is an ordering: the process runs before the label, so selection is the earlier anchor, and independent reproduction is a later milestone, a closed cycle downstream of the process that supplies it. The naming frame has no ground of its own, and the precise historic boundary is unreached rather than absent. The boundary stays open until a ground settles it.

## 2. Three checkable sub-claims

### C1 — framework-internal and structural; checkable by reading Figures 9 and 11

- **Statement:** the A-or-B form is a union of overlapping conditions, and a label is applied after the process it names. The ordering (process before label) is `general`; applying it to chemical selection is `specified`.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 9 :54-55 — "And when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again. A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones." Fig 11 :36 — "Where nothing maintains anything, intelligence is a label applied afterwards to a lineage that met its ground. Nothing was intelligent; selection ran. The word does no work there." Fig 11 :41 — "Which is the same move as asking what generates lift rather than whether a thing is a bird."
- **Refuter:** show that selection and reproduction are mutually exclusive at the relevant scale, that is, that no differential persistence can occur before a bounded individual reproduces; or show that the "label applied afterwards" rule governs only seated habitats and not unmaintained chemical ones.

### C2 — process-facing; checkable by an origins researcher against the chemistry

- **Statement:** differential persistence among replicators is upstream of reproductive closure; catalytic, self-maintaining material is already present before any individual reproduces independently. This is the `specified` ordering claim.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 12 :121 — "cycle. The output re-enters as input. The sign of the loop decides whether it closes, and a chain cannot close by construction." Fig 12 :119 — "catalyst. A part that survives its own use." Fig 3 :19 — "Optimising a link that already works cannot rescue one that does not."
- **What to check:** does the chemistry support closure as a later, dependent link, or can a closed reproductive cycle appear with no prior differential persistence? Is autocatalytic persistence observable at chemical scale without a bounded individual? Consider whether virus-like or network-like cases violate the individual-based threshold.
- **Refuter:** a ground-visible measurement showing a sharp onset of a closed reproductive cycle with no prior differential persistence, or a demonstration that differential persistence at chemical scale requires a bounded, independently reproducing individual.

### C3 — framework-internal derivation; checkable by reading Figures 2, 8 and 12

- **Statement:** the naming frame has no ground of its own, so a working definition is a filter and not a witness; the precise historic path is unreached rather than absent; the boundary is level-relative.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 2 :36 — "An anchor is legitimate when the question is constitutively about it." Fig 2 :43 — "a convention that decides an outcome has stopped being one." Fig 8 :39 — "So a search that finds nothing is never a proof of absence." Fig 12 :104 — "A settled molecule becomes an atom for whatever composes over it. So irreducible is a fact about a level and never about the world."
- **Refuter:** a demonstrable ground that settles word usage; or a dated, checkable signature in the record that resolves a specific historic transition; or a derivation of one of the six substrate terms from the other five.

## 3. What is explicitly not claimed

- Not a date, a named first organism, or a single sharp transition.
- Not that reproduction is irrelevant. It is a real milestone; it is downstream.
- Not that the working definition "a self-sustaining chemical system capable of Darwinian evolution" is wrong. It is a useful filter, not a witness.
- Not that there were no real transitions. Vagueness is not invalidity.
- Not `grounded`. Nothing here is asserted as settled by experiment.
- Not a verdict from agreement. The agreement of a room, a field or a list of famous names cannot move this.

## 5. What would settle it

A ground-visible result, not a further argument. In descending order of decision value:

1. A measurement showing a sharp onset of a closed reproductive cycle with no prior differential persistence.
2. A demonstration that chemical-scale differential persistence requires a bounded reproducing individual.
3. An instrument that discriminates the source behind a "living" reading, as the spectrometer discriminated sources behind "green" in *The Hard Problem is About Source*.
4. A dated, checkable signature in the rock record or an experimental replay resolving a specific transition.

## 6. What an expert can check in twenty minutes

1. Read Fig 9 :54-55, Fig 11 :36 and :41, Fig 12 :121, Fig 2 :36 and :43 — five short passages.
2. Check C2 against your own knowledge: in an origin-of-life system, can differential persistence run without a bounded reproducing individual? If yes, the ordering stands; if no, it fails.
3. Name one instrument that would discriminate an earlier selection phase from a first reproductive closure, or say why none is possible.
4. Send the refutation. A failure is more useful than an endorsement.
