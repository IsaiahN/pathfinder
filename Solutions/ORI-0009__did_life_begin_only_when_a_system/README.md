## The residual in effect terms

Two candidate anchors compete for the word "life": (A) a system that can reproduce independently, and (B) the onset of chemical selection. Option A gives a late, sharp, capability-based threshold and implies a discernible first living individual. Option B gives an earlier, process-based boundary and implies a continuum from chemistry to biology. The dispute does not shrink with effort, because each option is working well on a different subset of the phenomena. Fig 9 :4-5 says split such a disagreement rather than search it. These are not two answers to one question; they are two well-formed questions plus a naming convention, bundled together.

## What is deliberately not here

- No personal emails of named individuals. The ISSOL contact page is included as an institutional route, not as a source of private addresses.
- No ranking of people by how likely they are to agree that selection came first.
- No bulk-send plan.

Three reasons in the figures' own words:

1. **A room does not upgrade the label.** Seat Map :283: "A room reaching a conclusion without the ground is agreement, and agreement among frames is a weighted average of influence. Adding people raises the vocabulary and does not add an anchor." Fig 7 :13: "agreement compounds, evidence does not."
2. **Selecting for receptiveness manufactures the disqualifying correlation.** Seat Map :163: "High correlation is disqualifying for a verdict and efficient for a convention."
3. **Sending the theory before the residual spends the reader's independence.** Fig 10 :42: "A verifier that can reconstruct the claim cannot verify it." Fig 10 :52: "Bring a characterised residual, never the source."

Legally: scraping personal emails for unsolicited bulk outreach breaches GDPR and CAN-SPAM style rules in many jurisdictions. Public opt-in channels avoid that failure and the structural one together.
