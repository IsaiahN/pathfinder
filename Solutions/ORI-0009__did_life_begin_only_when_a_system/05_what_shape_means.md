## 1. A shape is a constraint, not a filler

The canonical rule: the framework returns "what a missing thing would have to look like (a shape, not a name)." Fig 9 :32: "you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself."

From *The Library of Babel*: "you can cut your search short if the set of books returned is small and descriptive enough that it provides a general shape of the answer that satisfies your question."

For ORI-0009 the shape is therefore **an ordering plus a refusal**, not a date:

> The process (differential persistence among chemical replicators) runs before the label ("life") is applied; the boundary of the label is a convention; the exact historic transition is unreached.

That is a shape. "Life began 3.8 billion years ago at the first protocell" is a name, and the framework does not supply names for things it has not settled at the ground.

## 2. How the shape is obtained here

- *The Library of Babel* on proximity: the searcher uses "the existing vocabulary you do know that is closest to the words that you wish to describe and grasp the shape of," then recurses. "With each new term gained, you narrow the space and deepen your vocabulary."
- The same essay on bundles, which is the decisive move in F1: "Perhaps your problem was a bundle or conflation of several problems masquerading as one?" That is exactly what the A-or-B form is. Unbundling is not a rhetorical preference; it is the essay's own instruction for a search that is not converging.
- Fig 6 :30: "The description travels. Fails by being sharpened into terms only we can read. Cast it in effect terms." Fig 9 :30-31: "your words are borrowed too, so describe the effect rather than the cause." So the residual was stated as a boundary-placement effect, not as a dispute over the word "life."
- *Decoding Tacit Knowledge*: "When contact isn't possible, the best you can send is a plain description of what you'd expect to see, because anyone can check that against the world." This is why `02_outreach_email.md` leads with three things a reader would expect to see if selection-first were wrong.
- *The Library of Babel* on stopping: "the finish line will also be the starting place, as there is nowhere else to search and nothing else that can describe the answer better." Here the stop is reached when the three split questions are separated and each has its own verdict; further searching the bundled question would only re-mix them.

## 3. What licenses a name

Only two things:

1. **The ground settles it.** `THE_FORMULA.md` step 5: "the ground settles it. Verified only by what pays. A gate is not the ground." The README says the framework stops "at the limit of what the available instruments can distinguish, and records the lineage so the stop is honest."
2. **A provenanced import from a frame whose closure differs** (Fig 6 :23, Fig 8 :50-52), with a source gate and a shadow test.

For the first origin, neither is available. *The Hard Problem is About Source* states the exact failure: "The answer is unreached until a better instrument exists." And: "An instrument that can't distinguish these cases demonstrates its own inadequacy, it cannot claim anything substantial about what is being measured or what causes it." The definition-of-life instruments (does it reproduce? does it evolve?) are readings that cannot distinguish the source. Pross and Pascal's paper makes the same point from the chemistry side: the historic path "will probably never be known."

So the place where an answer would be named is exactly the place where the instrument is missing. That is why the shape stops where it does.

## 4. Why the label question is the wrong question, from the essays

*How Intelligence Actually Works* opens with the family resemblance: "the intelligence we experience is the emergent result of extensive mapping of a complex system against unknown and unforgiving environments." It then says: "The test that environment applies is what we call fitness." Intelligence is not a thing marked by a label; it is the mapping itself, and the seed "is the seedline," not the recording of a tree.

Fig 11 :36 is the figure-level version: "Where nothing maintains anything, intelligence is a label applied afterwards to a lineage that met its ground. Nothing was intelligent; selection ran. The word does no work there."

Applied to this question: "life" is a label applied afterwards to a lineage that met its ground. The word does no work at the boundary; selection is already running. The lift move at Fig 11 :41, "Which is the same move as asking what generates lift rather than whether a thing is a bird," says the same thing in the essay's own idiom: replace the category question with the contact question.

*How Intelligence Actually Works* adds the honesty condition: "indiscernibility is not identity." Even if a definition were indistinguishable from the process for every current instrument, that would not make them the same thing. The lineage must be kept.

## 5. Q: does "a shape, not a name" forbid using the phrase "chemical selection began earlier"?

No, and the distinction matters.

The canonical phrase governs a **missing** primitive, a thing not held in the library. Chemical selection is not a missing primitive here: it is the process the question itself names, and it is corroborated by peer-reviewed world sources (Pross and Pascal 2013; the abiogenesis literature). Naming it as the earlier event is not inventing a seventh term or naming a slot the framework cannot fill.

What is forbidden is naming the *boundary instant* as if the framework had settled it. So:

- permitted: "the process runs before the label; selection is the earlier anchor";
- forbidden: "the first life occurred at t = X, and the framework derived X."

This is the same narrowing the exemplar made when it corrected its own overstatement about naming Gradient. Kept visible so the answer is not read as either a date or a refusal to speak.

## 6. Two consequences for outreach

**Prestige is not action.** From *The Recursive Self-Improvement Trap*: "Prestige can fund an action. It can give an action a budget to be tried. But it cannot settle an action. Only the ground can settle it. The Ground Maintainer must be blind to prestige." A list of famous people who agree that selection came first is the named collapse. The legitimate ask is a refutation of the ordering claim.

**Shared pretext makes agreement worthless.** From *Decoding Tacit Knowledge*: "Shared pretext is good for one thing and ruins another. Agreement is the whole point when you're settling what words mean. It's worthless for settling what's true when both sides learned from the same sources." This is the crispest statement of why D3 (the definition frame) and D1/D4 (the process domains) must not be pooled. Consensus on a definition succeeds at D3 and says nothing at D1.

## 7. My own audit is a simulation, not a ground reading

Same essay: "LLMs can quickly simulate an approximate answer/output with the formula found in the architecture post. It will often be better than the generic outputs that you will usually get otherwise too. However, the truly testable output would need to be derived from the real architecture or working the formula yourself." And: "the output is only as good as the validator. The agent, or the tool, or the frame, or the theory, cannot validate itself."

So `01_figure_grounded_audit.md` and this file are simulated derivations checked for internal consistency against the figures. They are not tests of the claim, and they cannot settle an origin-of-life question that the instruments have not settled. Mode is stated on every substantive claim in the folder because of this.
