**Use:** one to one, or as a cover letter attached to a submission through the public venues in `03_public_contact_points.csv`. **Not** for bulk send.

**Hard precondition:** do not send until the shape claim and its pre-registered refuter in `01_figure_grounded_audit.md` sections 0 and 4 are understood as `specified`, not `grounded`. The output must survive validation or the two-stage offer collapses on contact.

**Subject:** Check one shape claim before you decide whether the framework is worth reading

> Hi [name],
>
> I am working on a question in autonomy research: what capacities does an AI system need to act autonomously over extended periods? I have one specific output I would like you to check against what you already know. I am not asking you to read the theory first, and I am not asking you to agree with it. If the output does not survive, there is nothing else to look at.
>
> **The output (`specified`, not `grounded`):** the framework's dependency chain runs perception -> vocabulary -> objective -> planning -> learn and carry, and it is ordered by dependency, not importance. Its ground rule is that the ground does not decay, the channel does. Its habitat rule is that capability is a property of agent-and-habitat, never of the agent alone. From these it follows that sustained autonomous operation is not a stock the model owns. It is a union of conditions that must all hold: an unbroken and measured dependency chain, a live channel to a ground that does not update on the system's opinion, contact with a habitat that is enumerated rather than composed, and a reach renewed by contact with a frame whose closure differs. Over time this is a chain of differing rooms, and progress per handoff has two factors: bracket fidelity and ground pressure. Either at zero and the chain runs in place.
>
> The shape does **not** name an architecture. It says what any candidate answer must look like, not what implements it. Which mechanisms fill those conditions is left open.
>
> **What I am not claiming:** that the framework returns a list of capacities a system has, that it names memory or planning or a world model as the required thing, or that it settles the question. It returns a shape. The specific mechanisms that the AI literature offers are bindings from that literature onto the conditions, and until their provenance is shown they carry zero evidentiary weight.
>
> **What would refute the shape claim:**
> 1. sustained small prediction error over a long horizon achieved by an agent-only improvement that leaves contact unchanged, with the habitat held fixed;
> 2. one intervention that restores all the conditions at once and leaves no categorically different residue;
> 3. a dependency-chain link demonstrably broken while operation continues, with the reading taken from above the break rather than below it;
> 4. the channel demonstrably stale or saturated while the system still reports success and nothing reads the loss of contact;
> 5. reach renewing over a long horizon with no import from a differing closure;
> 6. a chain of same-closure, high-correlation rooms showing positive progress with ground pressure held constant;
> 7. a verbatim figure line naming a specific implemented capacity as required for autonomy.
>
> I wrote these down before sending this. If any one holds, the shape is wrong and I will retract it. A one-line "this fails because..." is more useful to me than agreement. If you would rather not engage, no reply is needed.
>
> **If it survives, and only then:** the sources are below. The figures are the authority, not my summary of them.
> - Figures and formula for direct download (SVG): https://github.com/IsaiahN/tether/tree/general-agent/figures
> - Canonical figures README (usage rule): https://github.com/IsaiahN/tether/blob/general-agent/figures/README.md
> - Worked narrative and the six-term derivation: https://adventuresinml.substack.com/p/how-intelligence-actually-works
>
> **If you want to use the machinery:** the formula is in the figures link. Run it on an autonomy problem you know well and can check against a ground. Write down what would refute your result *before* you run it: a frame cannot check the direction of its own question, and a reported failure is more useful to me than a reported success.
>
> Thanks,
> [name]
