# The Tether's answer: when informal payments help and when they harm

**Mode: `specified` — not `grounded`.** This is a shape and a route, not a result. It is a simulated derivation checked against the figures (`05_what_shape_means.md` section 7), not a ground reading.

## 1. The residual it answers

A person is entitled to something from an institution and does not get it: the drug is "out of stock," the queue does not move, the record is lost. R is the gap between the entitlement and what was delivered, measured per person and per transaction (Fig 1 :19: *"The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated."*). An informal payment is an action in a side channel with a different currency and its own settlement. The question is whether that action reduces R by restoring contact with the entitlement, or increases R for others and for the system by becoming a standing gate.

The question is a **union**, not one hard problem. Fig 9 :55: *"A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones."* It must be unbundled before it is searched.

## 2. The shape

1. **Valence is relative to the residual, not a property of the payment.** Fig 11 :49: *"An actor's valence is relative to the residual, not a property it carries."* Fig 11 :50: *"The same actor is a benefactor to one residual and a malfactor to another, so mutualism and parasitism are relations rather than kinds."* There is no standing list of helpful payments (Fig 11 :51: *"No standing list of good actors survives a change of residual."*). The same envelope payment can be a benefactor to the payer's blocked entitlement and a malfactor to a poorer person's equal entitlement at the same time.
2. **The first discriminator is contact with a measured outcome.** Fig 11 :40: *"An improvement that does not change contact changes nothing, however much it improves."* A payment helps only if the drug arrives, the queue shortens, or the record is produced. The anchor is the patient outcome or the counted delay, because Fig 2 :29: *"An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks. A measurement, an experiment, a counter, a market, a patient outcome."* Self-reported satisfaction is not that anchor when it moves with who is asked.
3. **The second discriminator is whether the payment becomes a standing gate.** A one-off payment that is spent and does not change future access is a different substance from a solicited, repeated payment that regenerates the gatekeeper's extraction. Fig 12 :119: *"catalyst. A part that survives its own use. Spent and regenerating parts behave differently across uses, and nothing records which a term is."* Fig 12 :121: *"cycle. The output re-enters as input. The sign of the loop decides whether it closes, and a chain cannot close by construction."* An amount-ledger records the ingredients and not the bond (Fig 12 :5: *"A set of ingredients is not one. The bond is the rest of the information, and it is the half nothing was recording."*).
4. **The help/harm split is a union of two conditions.** C1: contact with the entitlement is restored. C2: the payment does not become a standing gate sorted by ability to pay. C1 without C2 is navigation help that later curdles; C2 failing is exclusion; C1 failing is inefficiency, a private substitute replacing the public good. Fig 9 :57: *"What remains is smaller and it is also categorically different: a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either."* No pooled effect should be reported (Fig 9 :60: *"Use filters for the budget and witnesses for the verdict. Never let a filter hand you a verdict."*).
5. **The framework gives the conditions, not a named verdict.** Fig 9 :32: *"you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself."* "Grease" and "sand" are imports from the economics frame with their own evidence pools; only the record of where each came from separates convergent derivation from adopted import (Fig 8 :50-52). They carry no figure authority until a source gate and shadow test are run.

Per-domain verdicts, kept separate and never averaged:

| domain | verdict | status |
|---|---|---|
| Formal transaction (seeker, entitlement, outcome) | payment helps iff it changes the measured outcome the person was owed | `specified`, triangulated (Fig 11 :40, Fig 2 :29-30) |
| Side channel across uses (payer, gatekeeper, repetition) | spent payment and regenerating gate are isomers; solicitation closes the cycle | `specified`, triangulated (Fig 12 :117, :119, :121) |
| Habitat (other seekers, the poor, the institution) | second and third-order effects dominate; direct-contact scoping misses them | `specified`, single-source (Fig 11 :52-54) |

## 3. What it explicitly does not say

- Not that all informal payments are beneficial, and not that all are harmful. Both are refused by Fig 11 :49-51.
- Not that "corruption is efficient grease" is a figure result. That term is an import; the figures speak of valence, contact, catalyst, and cycle.
- Not that any specific hospital, permit office, or country's payments are helpful or harmful. No ground reading was taken.
- Not a prevalence claim. A percentage is a filter for where to look (Fig 9 :60), not a verdict.
- Not a derivation of one theory from the other, and no new primitive.
- Not `grounded`. Nothing here is asserted as settled by experiment.

## 4. What would settle it — the ground

A discriminating instrument, not a further argument. *The Hard Problem is About Source* (opened this session) says a reading *"cannot comment on the source"* and that *"The answer is unreached until a better instrument exists."* The corresponding checks here are:

- **Outcome test:** does the measured entitlement (drug received, delay length, record produced) change when the payment is made, compared with a case where it is not? This is the anchor, not satisfaction.
- **Bond test:** is the payment solicited, expected, and repeated? Record the relation, not just the amount. If solicitation does not predict later access conditions, the catalyst/cycle distinction fails.
- **Union test:** do C1 and C2 separate in the data? A design that only reports a pooled average cannot answer the question (Fig 1 :19, :22).
- **Standing falsifiers:** same-sign effect for every residual; unchanged outcome still claimed as help; no regeneration from repeated payments. All three are pre-registered in `01_figure_grounded_audit.md` section 4 and in `08_expert_verification_packet.md`.

## 5. The honest stop

**Unreached, not unreachable.** No source read this session supplies the per-payment, per-residual, outcome-anchored comparison the shape requires. Kabia et al. (2021) and Jalali et al. (2026) report prevalence, determinants, and associations; Campos and Saleh (2010) and Nur-tegin and Jakee (2020) report cross-country and disaggregated correlations. None is the instrument. The derivation stops at the limit of the available instruments and records the lineage so the stop is checkable. The canonical README closes with *"Indiscernibility is not identity."*

## 6. One-paragraph version for outreach

> The Tether does not say informal payments are good or bad. It says their valence is relative to the gap they are measured against, so the same envelope can be a benefactor to one person's blocked entitlement and a malfactor to another's. Two conditions must be split before any study: does the payment change a measured outcome the person was entitled to, and does it become a standing gate that sorts access by ability to pay and regenerates through repeated solicitation? A one-off payment that restores contact helps a person navigate; a payment that closes the second cycle deepens exclusion and inefficiency. Spent workaround and regenerating gate are the same atoms under a different bond, which is why an amount-ledger cannot tell them apart. What the framework offers is that shape, the falsifiers written in advance, and the instrument class that would close it. The answer for any real institution stays open until the ground settles it.
