# Expert verification packet — the Tether on informal payments

**What this is:** a claim in a form a health-systems researcher, institutional economist, or anti-corruption analyst can check. Mode, derivation, and refuters are stated before the conclusion, so a reader knows what to look for and what would settle it.

**Mode: `specified` — not `grounded`.** No measurement of a real institution is claimed. This is a shape and a route, not a result.

## 1. The claim, in one paragraph

An informal payment is not "grease" or "corruption" by kind; its valence is relative to the residual it is measured against. Two conditions must be separated: C1, does the payment change contact with a measured outcome the person was entitled to; C2, does it become a standing gate that sorts access by ability to pay and regenerates through repeated solicitation? A payment that meets C1 and not C2 helps a person navigate a dysfunctional institution. A payment that fails C1, or closes the C2 cycle, deepens exclusion and inefficiency. A spent workaround and a regenerating gate are isomers: the same atoms under a different bond, which an amount-ledger cannot record. The framework returns these discriminating conditions, not a verdict on any named payment.

## 2. Three checkable sub-claims

### C1 — framework-internal; checkable by reading Figures 11 and 2
- **Statement:** valence is relative to the residual; there is no standing list of helpful payments. The first discriminator is contact with a measured outcome, and the anchor must not move when different parties are asked.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 11 :49 *"An actor's valence is relative to the residual, not a property it carries."* Fig 11 :50 *"The same actor is a benefactor to one residual and a malfactor to another, so mutualism and parasitism are relations rather than kinds."* Fig 11 :40 *"An improvement that does not change contact changes nothing, however much it improves."* Fig 2 :29 *"An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks. A measurement, an experiment, a counter, a market, a patient outcome."*
- **Refuter:** a controlled comparison showing the same payment type has the same sign of effect on measured access regardless of the residual; or a payment that leaves the measured outcome unchanged yet is still claimed to restore access, with an anchor that shifts when payer, provider, and regulator are asked.

### C2 — structural; checkable by reading Figure 12
- **Statement:** a one-off spent payment and a solicited, repeated payment are different substances under the same currency. The sign of the loop decides whether the gate closes. An amount-ledger cannot distinguish them.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 12 :117 *"isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."* Fig 12 :119 *"catalyst. A part that survives its own use. Spent and regenerating parts behave differently across uses, and nothing records which a term is."* Fig 12 :121 *"cycle. The output re-enters as input. The sign of the loop decides whether it closes, and a chain cannot close by construction."*
- **What to check:** does the literature record solicitation, expectation, and repetition as distinct from amount? Kabia et al. (2021) report that payments were *"initiated mostly by health workers"* and that *"the poor and people who were unaware of service charges, were more likely to pay informally"* (ledger). If the bond is not recorded, the two substances cannot be told apart.
- **Refuter:** repeated solicitation shown to be uncorrelated with later access conditions, or one-off payments shown to reliably become standing gates. Either collapses the distinction.

### C3 — framework-internal derivation; checkable by reading Figures 9, 1, and 3
- **Statement:** the help/harm question is a union, not one question, so C1 and C2 must be unbundled and effects read per slot. Dependency order runs from perceiving the residual, through vocabulary and objective, to planning and carrying; the under-instrumented middle is where to look.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 9 :55 *"A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones. A single lever clears the region where only one condition holds and leaves the intersections standing, because the second condition still binds."* Fig 1 :19 *"The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated."* Fig 3 :19 *"Optimising a link that already works cannot rescue one that does not."* Fig 9 :60 *"Use filters for the budget and witnesses for the verdict. Never let a filter hand you a verdict."*
- **Refuter:** a design that unbundles C1 and C2 yields only the same pooled average; or a single intervention reliably removes both conditions with no residue; or the side channel demonstrably fails while links 1 to 3 are intact with a measured outcome. Each would send the claim back to re-census.

## 3. What is explicitly not claimed

- Not that all informal payments help, and not that all harm.
- Not that "corruption is efficient grease" is a figure result. "Grease" and "sand" are imports from the economics frame; convergent derivation and adopted import look identical in the contents and only the record separates them (Fig 8 :50-52).

## 5. What would settle it

A discriminating instrument, not a further argument. From *The Hard Problem is About Source*: a reading *"cannot comment on the source"*, and *"The answer is unreached until a better instrument exists."*

1. **Outcome test:** measured entitlement with payment versus without payment. Does the drug arrive, the delay shorten, the record appear?
2. **Bond test:** is the payment solicited, expected, and repeated? Does solicitation predict later access conditions?
3. **Union test:** do C1 and C2 separate in the data, or does only a pooled average survive?

## 6. What an expert can check in twenty minutes

1. Read Fig 11 :40, :49-51 and Fig 12 :117, :119, :121, six short passages.
2. Check C2 against a study you know: does its instrument record solicitation and repetition, or only amounts? If only amounts, say so, because that is the missing discriminator.
3. Name one design that would give an outcome-anchored answer for a specific service, or say why none is feasible.
4. Send the refutation. A failure is more useful than an endorsement.
