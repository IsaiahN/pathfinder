# Isomers, catalysts, and order: the supporting derivation

**Mode: `specified`.** This is a simulated derivation checked against the figures, not a ground reading (see `05_what_shape_means.md` section 7).

## RESIDUAL

The primary audit splits the question into two conditions: C1, the payment restores contact with a measured entitlement; C2, the payment does not become a standing second gate. Two structural questions are owed: (1) can two payments with identical ingredients be different substances under Fig 12, and (2) what is the dependency order among transaction, side channel, and habitat, so that a reading at one level is not mistaken for a reading at another?

## DOMAIN CENSUS

Atoms held: an entitlement, a gatekeeper's discretion, a transfer of value, and a measured outcome. Bonds held: the seven plus negation, in particular sequence (→), production (⇒), comparison (⋛), and the structural readings catalyst and cycle. No new primitive is available without import. "Grease" and "sand" are imported names from the economics frame, not atoms of this derivation.

## DERIVATION

### Q1 — Are a "spent grease" and a "standing gate" the same thing in different words?

No. Figure 12 :117: **"isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."** Both arrangements contain the same atoms: a payer, a gatekeeper, and a cash transfer. The bond differs.

- Arrangement G (grease): `blocked entitlement → transfer → delivered entitlement`, with the transfer spent once and not repeated. The output does not re-enter as input.
- Arrangement R (racket): `transfer → gatekeeper discretion → solicitation of next transfer → ...`, where the output re-enters as input.

Figure 12 :121: **"cycle. The output re-enters as input. The sign of the loop decides whether it closes, and a chain cannot close by construction."** A flat chain of payments (amount, amount, amount) cannot represent the closure. The sign of the loop, whether the payment reduces future need for payment or creates it, decides whether arrangement G stays a chain or becomes arrangement R.

Figure 12 :119: **"catalyst. A part that survives its own use. Spent and regenerating parts behave differently across uses, and nothing records which a term is."** This is the precise structural difference. A payment whose part is spent behaves differently across uses from one whose part regenerates, and a ledger recording only the amount does not tell them apart.

- **Falsifier, written before the run:** if repeated solicitation is shown to be uncorrelated with later access conditions (that is, the loop does not close in the data), the catalyst/cycle distinction collapses into a single chain and this claim fails. `01_figure_grounded_audit.md` F5 records the same refuter; it is repeated here deliberately as a design instrument.

### Q2 — What is the order, and what breaks first?

Figure 3 gives dependency order, not importance. Figure 3 :19: **"Optimising a link that already works cannot rescue one that does not."**

1. **Link 1, perception of the residual.** Can the seeker and the instrument tell what is owed from what was delivered? Fig 3 :5: *"the system can tell what is in front of it, and tell it apart from what was there before"*.
2. **Link 2, vocabulary.** Are there terms for what the situation affords: what can be paid for, what is entitled, what is discretionary? Fig 3 :7: *"it has terms for what things afford: what can be moved, opened, joined, blocked"*. Kabia et al. (2021) report that people unaware of service charges were more likely to pay informally (ledger), which is a failure at this link, not a moral failure of the payer.
3. **Link 3, objective.** Can the seeker say what would count as progress in their own terms? Fig 3 :10: *"it can say what would count as progress here, in its own terms"*. Here: the drug arrives, the queue shortens, the record is produced.
4. **Link 4, planning.** The side payment is a route chosen under incomplete information.
5. **Link 5, learn and carry.** Fig 3 :15: *"name what worked, verify it, and carry it to the next problem"*. This is where the catalyst can become a cycle: what worked once is carried forward without verification that it still restores contact.

Figure 3 :26: *"The link between them, having terms for what a situation offers and being able to say what would count as progress, has neither, so it attracts the least attention and the fewest instruments."* For informal payments, link 2 and link 3 are exactly the under-instrumented middle. Surveys record amounts (link 4) and perceptions (link 1) but rarely record entitlements known and outcomes awaited.

- **Falsifier:** a design that shows the side channel failing while links 1 to 3 are demonstrably intact, with a measured outcome, would relocate the break to link 4 or 5 and would refute the claim that the middle link is the usual failure point here. That is a re-census, not a correction of Fig 3.

### Q3 — What does the union split buy that a pooled effect does not?

Figure 9 :55: **"A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones. A single lever clears the region where only one condition holds and leaves the intersections standing, because the second condition still binds."**

The two levers are: fix the formal channel (so C1 is no longer needed) and break the gate (so C2 holds). Applying only the first clears cases where the payment was a one-off workaround but leaves the intersections, cases where a standing gate has formed, because the second condition still binds. Jalali et al. (2026) reach the same structural conclusion from the health pool: *"Isolated interventions are unlikely to be effective"* and effective responses require *"integrated, multi-level strategies"* (ledger). Fig 9 :57: **"What remains is smaller and it is also categorically different: a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either. It stops being a mixture and becomes a specific compound."**

- **Falsifier:** a single intervention that reliably removes both conditions with no residue would show this was a partition after all, not a union, and Fig 9 :54 was misapplied.

### Q4 — What is imported, and what does the import cost?

Figure 6 :23: **"No frame escapes its own closure. The bound moves only on contact with a frame whose closure differs."** The economics frame supplies "grease" and "sand"; the health frame supplies prevalence, solicitation, and equity. Importing either into the figure vocabulary is allowed only with provenance and a shadow test (Fig 8 :50-52: *"Convergent derivation and adopted import look identical in the contents. Only the record of where each came from separates them"*). The cost is recorded in `04_links_and_distribution.md`: the two pools overlap little in instruments, so they are worth triangulating, but each pool's internal agreement is one witness.

- **Falsifier:** showing that "grease" or "sand" is already a term in the figure set would remove the import and the provenance requirement. It is not; the figures speak of valence, contact, catalyst, and cycle.

## CONCLUSION

A spent workaround payment and a regenerating standing gate are **isomers** under Fig 12: identical atoms, different bond, decisive difference in behaviour. The closure is decided by the sign of the loop, which a flat amount-ledger cannot record. Dependency order runs from perceiving the residual through vocabulary and objective to planning and carrying, with the under-instrumented middle (links 2 and 3) the usual place to look. The help/harm question is a **union** of two conditions, so fixing only the formal channel leaves the gate cases standing. These are structural claims at `specified` mode; none of them is a measurement of a real institution.
