## 0. The residual under audit, stated in effect terms

Let a person (a patient, a permit seeker, a litigant) face an institution whose formal channel is supposed to return an entitlement. Let `R = |Γ(b,a) − o′|` be measured **per slot** (per person and per transaction), not as a social average (Fig 1 :19, Fig 1 :22). A dysfunctional formal channel is one where R stays positive: the queue does not move, the drug is "out of stock," the record is lost.

An informal payment is an action `a` in a side channel. It carries a different currency from the formal channel (cash, gift, favour) and its own settlement (the transfer completes). The question is not its moral label but its effect on R at three scales: the transaction, the side channel across repeated uses, and the habitat of other seekers and the institution itself.

Two readings are to be tested, not assumed:
- A: the payment **reduces** transaction R by restoring contact with the entitlement (the "grease" reading).
- B: the payment **increases** R for others and for the system by becoming a standing second gate (the "exclusion and inefficiency" reading).

An answer, bound as an ordinary term before the run, is a **shape**: the set of conditions under which A holds without B, plus an instrument that discriminates them. Until bound, that shape predicts nothing.

## 1. Domain census, before choosing any figure line

### R by arity, symmetry, scale
- **Arity:** two. There is the payer's access gap and the system's exclusion/inefficiency. Fig 9 :54-57 says a split on a union rather than a partition must be unbundled before it is searched. This is a union, not one hard question.
- **Symmetry:** not symmetric. The payment is directed from payer to gatekeeper; the same transfer repeated changes the gatekeeper's incentives, so the loop has a sign (Fig 12 :121).
- **Scale:** first order at the transaction, second order at the side channel across uses, third order at the habitat (Fig 11 :52: the DDT eagles case, where *"the residual that mattered was three steps out"*).

### One line per domain: actors / currency / anchor
- **Domain 1, the formal transaction.** Actors: seeker, gatekeeper (clerk, provider), entitlement. Currency: the formal right (published tariff, queue position, free-service entitlement). Anchor: the delivered service or patient outcome, because Fig 2 :29-30 says an anchor is *"a measurement, an experiment, a counter, a market, a patient outcome"* and *"If it moves when you push on it, it is not an anchor."* No nameable anchor means this is not yet a domain, so the anchor here is the measured service, not either party's opinion of it.
- **Domain 2, the side channel.** Actors: payer and gatekeeper in a repeated relation. Currency: informal money or gift. Anchor: whether contact changes, because Fig 11 :40 says *"An improvement that does not change contact changes nothing, however much it improves."* A payment that buys nothing the payer was entitled to is inert with respect to R.
- **Domain 3, the habitat and second-order effects.** Actors: other seekers, the poor, regulators, rival providers. Currency: access, trust, aggregate cost. Anchor: counted system outcomes (access rates, delay counts, exclusion counts). Fig 11 :52-54 supplies the cascade rule: *"And contact cascades."*

### Partition or union
Union (Fig 9 :54-58). A payment can be in the "helps" condition, the "harms" condition, or both. A single lever clears the region where only one condition holds and leaves the intersections standing, because the second condition still binds. The intersections are specific compounds, not blends.

### Order by dependency (Fig 3)
1. Establish whether the formal channel has a live residual, per slot (Domain 1).
2. Establish whether the side payment changes contact with the ground (Domain 2).
3. Only then trace the cascade to other seekers and the institution (Domain 3).

Fig 3 :19: *"Optimising a link that already works cannot rescue one that does not."* Effort spent at Domain 3 cannot rescue a break at Domain 1, and a reading taken below a break is a reading of nothing (Fig 3 :22-23).

## 3. Findings

### F1 — Valence is relative to the residual (decisive)

Figure 11 :49-51:

> **"An actor's valence is relative to the residual, not a property it carries."**
> **"The same actor is a benefactor to one residual and a malfactor to another, so mutualism and parasitism are relations rather than kinds."**
> **"No standing list of good actors survives a change of residual."**

An informal payment is an actor in the habitat. The figures forbid classifying it as "beneficial grease" or "harmful corruption" by kind. Its valence is a relation to a specific residual. Against a payer's blocked entitlement it may be a benefactor; against another seeker's equal entitlement it is a malfactor. Both are true at once, and no averaging is allowed (Fig 1 :22).

- **Triangulated:** Fig 11 (habitat valence) and Fig 1 :19-22 (per-slot residual) are low-overlap routes to the same conclusion. The economic literature's own turn to disaggregated types of corruption (Nur-tegin and Jakee 2020, `10_source_ledger.csv`) corroborates this from a different pool, as one witness.
- **Refuter:** a controlled comparison showing the same payment type has the same sign of effect on R regardless of which residual it is measured against would break Fig 11 :49-51.

### F2 — The contact test and the anchor

Figure 11 :40: **"An improvement that does not change contact changes nothing, however much it improves."**
Figure 2 :29-30: **"An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks. A measurement, an experiment, a counter, a market, a patient outcome. If it moves when you push on it, it is not an anchor."**

A payment helps only if it changes the payer's contact with the thing the formal channel owes: the drug actually arrives, the queue actually shortens, the record is produced. If the payer merely feels better about a transaction that did not change, R did not move. The anchor for deciding this is the patient outcome or the counted delay, not the payer's satisfaction, the clerk's account, or a perception index. Kabia et al. (2021) report informal payments made *"to access services, skip queues, receive higher quality of care, and express gratitude"*, and also that they *"limited access especially among the poor"* (ledger). The same act is both, measured against different slots.

- **Mode:** `specified`. **Triangulated** (Fig 11 :40 plus Fig 2 :29-30, low overlap).
- **Refuter:** a payment that leaves a measured patient outcome unchanged but is still claimed to "help"; or an anchor that moves when different parties are asked (perception-only evidence).

### F3 — Per-slot residual, not a social average

Figure 1 :19: **"The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated."**
Figure 1 :22: **"Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average."**

The grease/sand literature is itself evidence for this rule: Nur-tegin and Jakee (2020) report that *"Using aggregate indices to study corruption leads to misleading results"* and that *"While certain types of corruption facilitate growth, most types impede it"* (ledger). Campos and Saleh's meta-analysis (2010) reports that across 460 estimates, *"about 32 percent ... support a significant and negative impact of corruption on growth, 62 percent suggest a statistically insignificant relationship, while approximately only 6 percent support a positive and significant relation"* (ledger). An average over all payments and all people destroys the only signal that says where to look.

- **Mode:** `general` (structural) plus `specified` (the literature prediction). **Single-source** for the figure rule itself; the economics literature is corroboration from a different pool, one witness.
- **Refuter:** a design that reports only a national or sector-wide average effect of informal payments and refuses to split by payer, service, and second-order effect.

### F4 — "Help or harm" is a union, not a partition

Figure 9 :54: **"And when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again."**
Figure 9 :55: **"A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones. A single lever clears the region where only one condition holds and leaves the intersections standing, because the second condition still binds."**
Figure 9 :57: **"What remains is smaller and it is also categorically different: a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either. It stops being a mixture and becomes a specific compound."**

Unbundle into two conditions:
- **C1, contact restored:** the payment delivers the entitlement the formal channel owes (restores contact with the ground).
- **C2, no standing second gate:** the payment does not become a permanent condition of access that sorts by ability to pay or regenerates itself.

Where C1 holds and C2 holds: navigation help. Where C2 fails: exclusion (access sorts by wealth). Where C1 fails: inefficiency (a private substitute replaces a public good, the formal channel atrophies). Where both fail: a specific compound, not a worse instance of either. Fig 9 :60: **"Use filters for the budget and witnesses for the verdict. Never let a filter hand you a verdict."** A prevalence percentage is a filter for where to look, not a verdict.

- **Mode:** `specified`. **Single-source** (Fig 9), corroborated by the disaggregated-corruption result in F3.
- **Refuter:** a design that treats "payment helps" and "payment harms" as mutually exclusive categories, or that reports only a single pooled effect.

### F5 — Catalyst versus cycle: same currency, different substance

Figure 12 :119: **"catalyst. A part that survives its own use. Spent and regenerating parts behave differently across uses, and nothing records which a term is."**
Figure 12 :121: **"cycle. The output re-enters as input. The sign of the loop decides whether it closes, and a chain cannot close by construction."**
Figure 12 :117: **"isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."**

Two payments with identical atoms (a cash transfer to a gatekeeper) can be different substances. A one-off payment that is spent and does not change future access is a spent catalyst. A payment that becomes expected, is solicited on the next encounter, and funds the gatekeeper's continued extraction is a regenerating catalyst and a positive cycle. The figure warns that *"nothing records which a term is"*, so a ledger that records amounts but not whether the payment is solicited, expected, and repeated has recorded the ingredients and not the arrangement.

The health evidence supplies the discriminator: Kabia et al. (2021) find payments were *"initiated mostly by health workers"* and that *"the poor and people who were unaware of service charges, were more likely to pay informally"* (ledger). Jalali et al. (2026) find *"low salaries and wages being the most frequently cited determinant"* and *"corruption identified as the most frequently reported issue"* (ledger). The Malawi survey (2026) warns that *"better-off households would be able to secure services through additional informal payments, potentially creating unequal access"* (ledger). Solicitation, repetition, and sorting by ability to pay are the bond that makes the payment a cycle.

- **Mode:** `specified`. **Triangulated** across Fig 12 :117, :119, :121 (same figure, distinct claims with low conceptual overlap) and corroborated from the health pool as one witness.
- **Refuter:** evidence that solicited, repeated payments do not regenerate access conditions, or that a one-off payment reliably becomes a standing gate. Either would collapse the catalyst/cycle distinction.

### F6 — The framework returns a shape, not a verdict on a named payment

Figure 9 :32: **"you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself"**

This derivation gives the discriminating conditions (C1, C2) and the instrument class (outcome / count), not a yes or no for a particular payment in a particular hospital. Naming a specific payment as "the helpful one" would be a binding from the field frame onto the shape, requiring its own provenance and ground (see `05_what_shape_means.md`).

- **Mode:** `general`. **Triangulated** (Fig 9 :32 plus the canonical README's *"a shape, not a name"*, high overlap but two documents).
- **Refuter:** a claim that the figures, without a ground reading, settle a specific real institution's payment as helpful or harmful.

### F7 — Agreement is not the anchor

Figure 7 :35: **"A room's agreement is variation, not a verdict. Selection between rooms is where the ground votes."**
Figure 2 :35: **"Alignment is triangulation, not negotiation. A mirror cannot correct you."**
Figure 2 :42: **"Buy independence in proportion to the cost of being wrong."**

A survey of experts agreeing that corruption is bad is a convention, not an anchor. The grease/sand economics literature and the health informal-payments literature overlap little in data and instruments, so they are two frames worth triangulating, but each pool's internal agreement is one witness. The IZA meta-analysis (Campos and Saleh 2010) is itself a warning: *"authors' affiliation (academics systematically report less negative coefficients)"* (ledger), meaning even the observers' pool leaks into the reading.

- **Mode:** `general`. **Triangulated** across Fig 7 :35 and Fig 2 :35, :40, :42.
- **Refuter:** treating a consensus statement, a prestige-ranked author list, or a perception index as the thing that settles the question.

### F8 — Stakes in the reading must be declared

Figure 10 :59: **"an interest in the outcome is unavoidable, an interest in the reading is disqualifying."**
Figure 10 :60: **"Where a stake cannot be removed, declare it. An undeclared interest is modelled anyway and an unnamed model runs unchallenged."**

The payer wants the service, the provider wants the supplement, the reformer wants the payment abolished, and the researcher wants a publishable result. Each has a stake in how "help" and "harm" are read. Fig 2 :29 says the anchor must not move when pushed; a definition of "help" that changes with who is asked is not an anchor and the verdict is unverifiable, not true.

- **Mode:** `general`. **Single-source** (Fig 10) for the rule, corroborated by the meta-analysis finding on author affiliation.
- **Refuter:** a study of informal payments that reports no declaration of the researcher's or funder's interest in the outcome.

### F9 — Strain test and re-census

The "efficient grease" reading strains Fig 11 :49 when it treats the payment as helpful by kind. Per the canonical rule (*"a reading that makes one contradict another has misidentified its domain"*, `FIGURES_README_canonical.md`), this is not a flaw in the figure: it is a sign the reading has assigned the payment to the wrong domain. Re-census sends it back to the residual: the payment is helpful relative to a specific blocked entitlement (Domain 1), not in itself. After re-census, no figure line requires forcing. One live strain remains and is named, not hidden: the catalyst/cycle test depends on an instrument (repeated solicitation counts) that most prevalence studies do not record, so F5 is `specified` and not settled.

## 4. Pre-registered refuter

Written before the conclusion, per the seat rule that *"a falsifier written before the run is a design instrument, not only an honesty device"* (`THE_SEAT_MAP_Field_Guide_amended.md`, line 287). The shape claim is refuted if any of the following holds:

1. A controlled comparison shows the same payment type has the **same sign** of effect on measured access for every residual it is measured against (breaks Fig 11 :49-51).
2. A payment that leaves the measured patient outcome unchanged is still shown to reduce the person's real access, with an anchor that does not move when different parties are asked (breaks Fig 11 :40 and Fig 2 :29-30).
3. Solicited, repeated payments are shown not to regenerate access conditions, or one-off payments are shown to reliably become standing gates (breaks the catalyst/cycle test, Fig 12 :119, :121).
4. A design that unbundles C1 and C2 produces only the same pooled average (breaks Fig 9 :54-57 and Fig 1 :19).

If any fires, the shape is withdrawn or revised and the change is logged here. Until one is run, no part of this may be labelled `grounded`.

## 5. Recommendation

1. Ask the residual-relative question first: blocked entitlement for whom, measured how (F1, F2).
2. Unbundle C1 from C2 before any search (F4). Never report a single pooled "effect of informal payments."
3. Record solicitation, expectation, and repetition as part of the bond, not just the amount (F5). Fig 12 :5: *"A set of ingredients is not one. The bond is the rest of the information, and it is the half nothing was recording."*
4. Use patient outcomes and counted access as the anchor; treat perception indices as one frame among several (F2, F7).
5. Declare every stake in the reading (F8).
6. Figure 4 :22: **"Only methods cross up. Only head starts come down."** Send the generator (the shape and the refuter), not a conclusion.

### Derived loop, compact

1. **Residual?** Positive per slot: entitlement not delivered (Fig 1 :19).
2. **Which domain?** Formal transaction first; side channel second; habitat cascade third (Fig 3).
3. **Does the payment change contact?** Only if the measured outcome moves (Fig 11 :40).
4. **Is it a standing gate?** Solicited and repeated payments close a cycle (Fig 12 :119, :121).
5. **Is it one question?** No, a union to unbundle (Fig 9 :54-57).
6. **Import check?** "Grease" and "sand" are frame imports; only the record of where each came from separates them (Fig 8 :50-52).
7. **Settlement?** None. Frames range against the ground (Fig 2 :45). Status: `candidate`.

### Updated verdict

The shape survives: valence is residual-relative, contact with a measured outcome is the discriminator, and the help/harm split is a union of unbundled conditions. Everything specific that would name a particular payment as good or bad is refused without a ground reading. Strains after re-census: one named (the instrument for the catalyst/cycle test), none hidden.
