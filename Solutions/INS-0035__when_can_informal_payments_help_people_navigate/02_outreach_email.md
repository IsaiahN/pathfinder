**Use:** one to one, or as a cover letter attached to a submission through the public venues in `03_public_contact_points.csv`. **Not** for bulk send.

**Subject:** Check one discriminator before you decide whether the theory is worth reading

> Hi [name],
>
> I have one specific output I would like you to check against what you already know. I am not asking you to read a theory first, and I am not asking you to agree with me. If the output does not survive, there is nothing else to look at.
>
> **The output (`specified`, not `grounded`):** the framework's habitat figure says an actor's valence is relative to the residual, not a property it carries. Applied to informal payments, that means a side payment is not "grease" or "corruption" by kind. Whether it helps depends on the gap it is measured against. From that I derive two conditions that must be separated before any study: (1) does the payment change contact with a measured outcome the person was entitled to, and (2) does it become a standing gate that sorts access by ability to pay and regenerates itself through repeated solicitation? A payment that meets the first and not the second helps a person navigate; a payment that fails the first or closes the second cycle deepens exclusion and inefficiency. This is a **shape** with a discriminator, not a measurement of any one hospital or bureaucracy.
>
> **What I am not claiming:** that all informal payments are beneficial, that all are harmful, that a payment is "efficient grease" in the economist's sense, or that this derivation settles the corruption debate. "Grease" and "sand" are terms imported from other fields; convergent derivation and adopted import look identical in the contents and only the record separates them. Until a ground settles a case, it carries no verdict weight. I am also not claiming the framework is the only true account.
>
> **What would refute the shape claim:**
> 1. a controlled comparison showing the same payment type has the same sign of effect on measured access for every residual it is measured against;
> 2. a payment that leaves the measured patient outcome unchanged but is still shown to restore real access, with an anchor that does not move when different parties are asked;
> 3. evidence that solicited, repeated payments do not regenerate access conditions, or that one-off payments reliably become standing gates.
>
> I wrote these down before sending this. If any one holds, I will retract or revise the shape. A one-line "this fails because..." is more useful to me than agreement. If you would rather not engage, no reply is needed.
>
> **If it survives, and only then:** the sources are below. The figure text, not my summary of it, is the authority.

Write down what would refute your result *before* you run it, and state the mode of what you get. A reported failure is more useful to me than a reported success.
