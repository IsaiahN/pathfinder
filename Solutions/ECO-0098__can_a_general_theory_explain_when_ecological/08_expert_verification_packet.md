# Expert verification packet - the Tether on ecological network resilience

**What this is:** a claim in a form an ecologist can check. Mode, derivation and refuters are stated before the conclusion, so a reader knows what to look for and what would settle it.

**Mode: `specified` on the ecological claim, `general` on the structural shape, `gap` on the trait inventory. Not `grounded`.** No monitoring campaign is claimed. This is a shape and a route, not a result.

**Status:** a simulated derivation checked for internal consistency against the figures, not a ground reading. It cannot settle the question it reports on.

## 1. The claim, in one paragraph

Asked whether a general theory can explain when ecological networks are resilient, the derivation refuses the either/or. The question is a union of two overlapping conditions: a maintained channel to a ground that does not update on the network's own report, so recovery is read per species or function slot; and contact with a closure that differs from the network's own, so the network has terms for a residual that changed. Both must hold. Capacity is a property of network-and-habitat, never of the network alone, and an actor's valence is relative to the residual, so there is no standing list of resilience-conferring traits. Much of what is called context dependence is a channel failure with an instrument remedy; the rest is a cascade scoped too narrowly. A general shape is available, the specific trait inventory is a declared gap, and the structure in the slot is not named by the framework.

## 2. Three checkable sub-claims

### C1 - framework-internal and ecological; checkable against the residual description

- **Statement:** the question is a union of two conditions, not a partition into "general" and "context", and the residual must stay indexed per slot.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 9 :55 *"A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones."* Fig 9 :57 *"What remains is smaller and it is also categorically different: a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either."* Fig 1 :19 *"The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated."* Fig 1 :22 *"Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average."*
- **What to check:** in a network you know, can recovery be read for each function or species slot, or has it been pooled? Do the two conditions overlap in that system (for example, does the monitoring programme itself change reach)?
- **Refuter:** show C1 and C2 independent with zero overlap, or show an aggregated index predicting a slot failure earlier than the slot readings with those readings withheld from the fit.

### C2 - ecological; checkable against the stability literature

- **Statement:** no trait has a fixed valence; the same structural feature changes sign with the perturbation and the measured function. Over a fixed species set, two differently wired networks are isomers, not variants of one thing.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 11 :49 *"An actor's valence is relative to the residual, not a property it carries."* Fig 11 :51 *"No standing list of good actors survives a change of residual."* Fig 12 :117 *"isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."*
- **What to check:** is there a named trait whose sign is invariant across two independent residuals? The world source to test against is Allesina and Tang 2012, which finds predator-prey stabilizing and mutualistic and competitive destabilizing under one assumption set, and reports that realistic food-web structure can decrease stability. Does that support a fixed list, or a binding under a residual? For the isomer claim, was the species set actually held fixed?
- **Refuter:** exhibit one trait with invariant sign across two independently measured residuals, or show identical slot-level recovery for two networks with identical species and different bond arrangements under two perturbations.

### C3 - framework-internal; checkable by reading the figures

- **Statement:** the specific trait inventory is unreached with the current library and must be reported as a gap, not filled with a name; the framework returns a shape.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 13 :115 *"The set is closed. The arrangements are not. That gap is why import exists."* Fig 13 :118 *"infinite effort inside a finite reach."* Fig 8 :39 *"So a search that finds nothing is never a proof of absence."* Fig 9 :32 *"you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself."*
- **What to check:** does any figure line select a structural feature for the resilience slot? If not, naming one is a binding from the ecology frame with provenance owed, not a derivation. Is the gap being reported as unreachable (a verdict) rather than as silence?
- **Refuter:** derive an exhaustive trait inventory whose validity survives a change of residual and habitat, or derive one of the two conditions from the other so the union collapses to a single lever.

## 3. What is explicitly not claimed

- Not that either side of the either/or wins. The union is the verdict.
- Not that diversity, connectance, modularity, redundancy or response diversity is *the* general predictor. No figure line names one, and Fig 11 :51 refuses a standing list.
- Not that context dependence is only bad measurement. The channel test in `01` F6 separates a correctable instrument failure from a genuine cascade.
- Not that a pooled resilience index is the ground (Fig 9 :60: effect sizes are filters for the budget, witnesses for nothing).
- Not that the habitat is the ground. Fig 11 :73: *"The habitat is not the ground. The habitat is where actors come from and it updates; the ground is what settles, and it does not."*
- Not `grounded`. No measurement was taken in this derivation.
- Not that the framework is the only true account. Fig 8 :37: *"A frame cannot certify its own limit."*

## 5. What would settle it

A source-discriminating instrument, not a further argument. From *The Hard Problem is About Source*: an instrument that reads green *"cannot comment on the source"*; the spectrometer resolved it. Here:

1. **Recovery of what, to what?** State the slot and the perturbation before measuring (Carpenter et al. 2001).
2. **Source discrimination.** Can the measurement distinguish a redundant group taking over, a slow variable that never crossed a threshold, a refuge maintaining contact, and a transient that was missed?
3. **Channel test.** Replace the indicator with one that resolves the previously saturated range, keep all readings unpooled, and re-run.
4. **Union test.** Check whether C1 and C2 are independent with no overlap, or whether one lever clears both.
5. **Valence test.** Check one trait across two independently measured residuals.
