# Expert verification packet - the Tether on trust in AI systems

**What this is:** a claim in a form a researcher in trust, human-AI interaction or human factors can check. Mode, derivation and refuters are stated before the conclusion, so a reader knows what to look for and what would settle it.

**Mode: `specified` - not `grounded`.** No human-subject measurement is claimed here. This is a shape and a route, not a result.

## 1. The claim, in one paragraph

A well-calibrated trust judgment has a shape. It is indexed per decision slot, not averaged. It is ranged against a legitimate anchor decided by what the question is about: human judgment for a person-facing question, a measurement for a code-correctness question, with human judgment then acting only as an instrument that can be wrong on axes it does not measure. It uses at least one check that does not draw on the system's own evidence. It is refreshed when task or habitat changes, because the ground does not decay while the channel to it does. It rests on a vocabulary and an objective established before any confidence display or explanation. The framework does not return a universal trust threshold; that is a declared gap owed to a source-discriminating instrument.

## 2. Three checkable sub-claims

### C1 - framework-internal and conceptual; checkable by reading Figures 2 and 1

- **Statement:** the anchor type is determined by what the question is constitutively about; calibration is per slot, not a global average.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 2 :36 - *"An anchor is legitimate when the question is constitutively about it."* Fig 2 :37 - *"Human judgment is the right anchor for is this a good answer for a person, because the question is about people."* Fig 2 :38 - *"It is only an instrument for is this code correct, and instruments can be wrong on axes they do not measure."* Fig 1 :19 - *"The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated."*
- **What to check:** is the anchor-type distinction preserved in your own account of trust calibration, or is human judgment silently used as the anchor for correctness questions? Does your measurement report per-case residuals or only an aggregate?
- **Refuter:** show a case where human judgment legitimately settles a code-correctness question without an instrument, or where a system's own confidence settles a person-facing question without a person; or show that a global score preserves every live slot signal.

### C2 - human-factors and HCI facing; checkable against the empirical literature

- **Statement:** shared-evidence checks cannot corroborate, the channel decays while the ground does not, and transparency is inert until an instrument makes it checkable.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 2 :35 - *"Alignment is triangulation, not negotiation. A mirror cannot correct you."* Fig 2 :40 - *"disqualifying for a verdict: agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool"*. Fig 10 :24 - *"the ground does not decay, the channel does"*. Fig 3 :27 - *"A link with no instrument is not a link that works. It is a link nobody has looked at."*
- **What to check:** do the studies you know distinguish a check that reuses the system's own output from an independent one? Does reported overtrust track accuracy, or does high accuracy sometimes increase it? Are citations and confidence displays consulted, or merely shown?
- **Refuter:** two checks sharing one evidence pool shown to give independent corroboration; or calibration improving with no refresh across a documented habitat change; or a transparency feature shown to calibrate users with no engagement mechanism and no external feedback.

### C3 - framework-internal derivation with one live strain

- **Statement:** calibration is a ⋛ comparison between stated confidence and observed correctness in one slot. The person-facing and code-correctness cases are isomers under Fig 12's definition: same atoms, different anchor, different behaviour.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 12 :117 - *"isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."* Fig 9 :32 - *"you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself"*. Fig 8 :39 - *"So a search that finds nothing is never a proof of absence."*
- **What to check:** is the comparison asymmetric in your framework? If confidence and correctness are swapped, does the claim change? Is the threshold derived, or borrowed from a measurement tradition without provenance?
- **Refuter:** a symmetric bond shown sufficient for calibration; or a universal numeric trust threshold derived from the figures alone; or a source-discriminating instrument already held in the framework that separates every fluent-but-wrong case from every fluent-and-right one.

## 3. What is explicitly not claimed

- Not that any confidence score, explanation, citation, or transparency panel calibrates anyone by itself.
- Not that more transparency is the answer. The world evidence reports inertness, not absence; the figures report filters versus witnesses.
- Not that higher model accuracy produces better-calibrated human trust. A systematic review in health informatics found the opposite can occur.
- Not that agreement among users or endorsement by experts is evidence.
- Not a universal trust threshold, a single calibration number, or a specific interface design.
- Not `grounded`. No human-subject measurement was taken in this folder.

## 5. What would settle it

A ground reading, not agreement. Concretely:

1. An anchor-type task switch: person-facing versus code-correctness, with the correct deference rule changing.
2. A per-slot failure injection: one live failure in an otherwise accurate system.
3. An independence comparison: reused explanation versus held-out source.
4. A habitat change with and without refreshed readings.
5. A source-discriminating instrument that reads the cause behind an identical fluent output.

Until one of these fires, the verdict stands as `candidate`.

## 6. What an expert can check in twenty minutes

1. Read Fig 2 :36-38, Fig 2 :35 and Fig 1 :19. Three short passages.
2. Check C2 against one study you know well: did it separate shared-evidence checks from independent ones?
3. Name one instrument that would discriminate a fluent-but-wrong output from a fluent-and-right one, or say why none is possible.
4. Send the refutation. A failure is more useful than an endorsement.
