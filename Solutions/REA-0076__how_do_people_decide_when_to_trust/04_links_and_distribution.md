## What the wider literature bears on the question

1. **Accuracy and calibration are different quantities.** Guo and colleagues show that a model can be accurate and poorly calibrated in the confidence sense. This corroborates the figure demand for per-slot residuals (Fig 1 :19, :22) at the model level. It does not decide the human question, and it is one evidence pool (machine learning) distinct from the human-factors pool.
2. **High reliability can degrade human monitoring.** Lyell and Coiera's review corroborates the channel-decay finding (Fig 10 :24, :26) from health informatics. Users with greater trust are less likely to detect failures, and the bias appears in single-task diagnosis as well as multitask settings. This is a genuine low-overlap witness.
3. **Disclosure is inert without engagement.** Jaidka and Cai report that cited sources in AI summaries are clicked in about 1 percent of visits and that users most at risk are least likely to act on transparency features. This corroborates Fig 3 :27 (a link with no instrument is not a link that works) and Fig 9 :60 (filters are not witnesses).
4. **Explanations can narrow the comparison set.** Pham and colleagues place the failure upstream of belief evaluation: the AI-supported hypothesis becomes focal before alternatives are retrieved. This corroborates the vocabulary/objective middle link (Fig 3) and the describe-before-looking rule (Fig 9 :27-28).
5. **Self-reported calibration is not ground calibration.** Rehman's own limitation statement makes exactly the distinction the figures require. A person saying their trust is calibrated is a reading inside D1, not an anchor reading from D3. This is why the packet asks for an independent check rather than a confidence rating.
6. **Affect drives trust judgments.** Vastfjall's comment in Nature Human Behaviour reports that people trust or distrust AI outputs because of how they feel rather than how reliable the outputs are. This corroborates the fluency/affect pathway described by Jaidka and Cai and the anchor-type error in F1, but it is a comment, so it carries less weight than a study.

No world source conflicts with the figure verdict. The conflict rule was not triggered. The human-trust sources overlap in the HCI/psychology pool, so they count as one witness for the mechanism shape, not four. Guo et al. and Lyell and Coiera are the two low-overlap witnesses.
