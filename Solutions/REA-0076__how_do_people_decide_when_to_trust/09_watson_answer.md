## QUESTION

Decide, in effect terms, what the Tether says about a person's decision to trust an AI system and about what makes that judgment well calibrated. Option A: trust is a property of the system that the person reads off, so a benchmark score, a confidence display or a fluent explanation is sufficient. Option B: trust is a relation among person, system and task ground, maintained through contact, so calibration fails when the anchor type is wrong, when checks share evidence, when the channel decays, or when the habitat changes. What each leads to: A sends a frame-internal score as a verdict and spends the independence of the people checking it; B sends a per-slot shape, written refuters, and an open threshold until a ground settles it.

## DOMAIN CENSUS

R by arity, symmetry and scale, then one line per domain with actors, currency and anchor; partition or union; order by dependency.

- **R (the residual).** Whether a person's confidence in an AI output matches what the system does in that decision slot. Arity: three-place (person, output, task ground), indexed per slot. Symmetry: ordered, since trust precedes or follows verification, and comparative, since calibration compares confidence with observed correctness. Scale: individual decision loop, repeated interaction, and social or population frame. Fig 1 :19 says *"The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated."* This is a union compound, not one hard question: at least two conditions must hold, and a single lever clears only one of them.
- **Domain 1, the person's judgment loop.** Actors: the person, belief b, weighting w, action rely or check or override. Currency: confidence and per-slot residual reduction. Anchor: the task ground when the question is legitimately about it. Status: admitted.
- **Domain 2, the AI system frame.** Actors: model, outputs, confidence scores, explanations, citations. Currency: predictions. Anchor: the task ground, external to the system. Status: admitted. The system's own confidence is a frame output, never an anchor.
- **Domain 3, the task ground and habitat.** Actors: task, data, environment, affected stakeholders, instruments, outcome. Currency: correctness, harm, payment, patient outcome. Anchor: a measurement, experiment, counter, market or patient outcome. Status: admitted.
- **Domain 4, the social and reputational frame.** Actors: other users, reviews, leaderboards, publicity, prestige. Currency: agreement and reputation. Anchor: none of its own that does not update on opinion. Status: **not a domain yet**, and excluded from verdict weight. It becomes a channel inside D3 only when it carries a discriminating reading.
- **Order by dependency.** Perception, vocabulary, objective, planning, learn and carry (Fig 3). Vocabulary and objective are the middle link and the usual break; effort spent above the break arrives at a step that cannot use it.
- **Union.** Calibration is C1 (channel to ground maintained) and C2 (system reliability in this habitat independently measured). A case in two conditions is not a harder case of either.

Strain test applied: Fig 2 makes human judgment an anchor for person questions and an instrument for code questions. The apparent contradiction is resolved by the legitimacy condition; two object types, one figure. No re-census changed a domain boundary.

Reading: decide the anchor type before deciding trust, then track confidence against the ground per slot. Mode `specified`. Refuter: human judgment shown to settle a code-correctness question with no instrument, or a global score shown to preserve every live slot signal.

**D2, the AI system frame, status triangulated (Fig 1 and Fig 8, frame-internal quantities versus provenance).**

- Fig 1 :27 *"They are frame-internal. A frame cannot score itself with a quantity it also produces."*
- Fig 8 :50 *"Two frames can hold the same atom by convergence, reaching it separately, which is corroboration at maximum independence."*
- Fig 8 :51 *"Convergent derivation and adopted import look identical in the contents."*

Reading: a confidence score or an explanation is an output of the system frame. Agreement between it and the person's first reading means nothing unless the two draw on different evidence. Mode `general` for the frame rule, `specified` for the AI binding. Refuter: a self-report shown to be an independent measure of the same quantity.

**D3, the task ground and habitat, status triangulated (Fig 10 and Fig 11, channel decay versus habitat relativity).**

- Fig 10 :24 *"the ground does not decay, the channel does"*
- Fig 10 :26 *"stale percepts, dropped transients, saturated metrics, pooled readings"*
- Fig 11 :40 *"A system cannot be made more capable by improving the agent in isolation. An improvement that does not change contact changes nothing, however much it improves."*
- Fig 4 :20 *"Only the actors, the currency and the anchor change."*

Reading: overtrust and undertrust are channel states, and capability is a property of the system in its habitat. Benchmark trust does not transfer without a measured gap. Mode `specified`. Refuter: calibration improved by an isolated model change with deployment contact unchanged.

**D4, the social and reputational frame, status single-source and disqualified as an anchor.**

- Fig 7 :35 *"A room's agreement is variation, not a verdict. Selection between rooms is where the ground votes."*
- Fig 2 :35 *"Alignment is triangulation, not negotiation. A mirror cannot correct you."*
- Fig 2 :40 *"disqualifying for a verdict: agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool"*

Reading: a room of agreeing users adds vocabulary, not an anchor. This domain has no nameable independent anchor, so it is not yet a domain and contributes no verdict weight. Refuter: a high-overlap room producing a ground-discriminating reading that no low-overlap frame can produce.

**Union and method, status triangulated (Fig 3, Fig 8, Fig 9).**

- Fig 3 :27 *"A link with no instrument is not a link that works. It is a link nobody has looked at."*
- Fig 8 :45 *"Two frames together reach what neither reaches alone."*
- Fig 9 :60 *"Use filters for the budget and witnesses for the verdict. Never let a filter hand you a verdict."*
- Fig 2 :42 *"Buy independence in proportion to the cost of being wrong."*

Reading: build vocabulary and objective before adding displays; buy low-overlap checks in proportion to the cost of being wrong; never let transparency hand you a verdict.

**The universal trust threshold, status `gap`.** No figure line governs a numeric threshold or a source-discriminating instrument after a genuine re-census. Fig 9 :32 *"you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself"*. Fig 8 :39 *"So a search that finds nothing is never a proof of absence."* Fig 13 :134 *"Unreachable is a verdict here, not a silence"*. What is missing is an instrument characterized in effect terms but not held.

## WORLD (secondary sources read)

World sources corroborate only. Most share the HCI and psychology evidence pool, so overlap is declared rather than hidden.

- **Machine learning, calibration measurement.** Chuan Guo, Geoff Pleiss, Yu Sun and Kilian Weinberger, *On Calibration of Modern Neural Networks*, PMLR 70:1321-1330, 2017. Quote: *"modern neural networks, unlike those from a decade ago, are poorly calibrated"*. Prediction: accuracy and calibration are distinct quantities. **Corroborates** the per-slot demand at the model level. Overlap: low, different field and different instrument.
- **Health informatics and human factors.** David Lyell and Enrico Coiera, *Automation bias and verification complexity: a systematic review*, JAMIA 24(2):423-431, 2016. Quote: *"accuracy engenders trust, and it has been shown that users who have greater trust in automation are less likely to detect automation failures."* Prediction: high reliability degrades monitoring. **Corroborates** the channel-decay claim. Overlap: low, health-care and aviation studies rather than LLM chatbot studies.
- **Communication and HCI.** Kokil Jaidka and Mengxuan Cai, *Why we believe chatbots: trust calibration as a design problem*, Frontiers in Psychology 17:1935527, 2026. Quote: *"the problem this article addresses is therefore the inertness, not the absence, of transparency"*. Prediction: disclosure alone does not recalibrate trust. **Corroborates** the vocabulary and objective middle link. Overlap: high with the next two sources, one witness.
- **Cognitive psychology.** Mai Phuong Pham, David A Illingworth-Vera and Rickey P Thomas, *AI interventions as hypothesis cues*, Frontiers in Psychology 17:1967356, 2026. Quote: *"an inadequately populated comparison set, in which plausible alternatives were never retrieved, maintained, or activated strongly enough to compete with the AI-supported interpretation"*. Prediction: the defect is upstream of belief evaluation. **Corroborates** the dependency-order claim. Overlap: high with Jaidka and Cai and Rehman.
- **Psychology and measurement.** Usman Rehman, *Cognitive transparency and trust calibration in explainable AI systems*, Frontiers in Psychology 17:1924913, 2026. Quote: *"the results describe associations among users' perceptions rather than causal effects, independently verified calibration, or objectively appropriate reliance"*. Prediction: self-reported calibration is not ground calibration. **Corroborates** the distinction between D1 readings and D3 anchors. Overlap: high with the HCI pool; given zero independent weight for the verdict.
- **Psychology and governance commentary.** Daniel Vastfjall, *AI governance needs to consider users' emotional responses*, Nature Human Behaviour, Comment, 26 Oct 2026, read via journal home. Quote: *"People will often trust or distrust an artificial intelligence (AI) output because of how it makes them feel, rather than how reliable it is."* Prediction: affect drives deference independently of reliability. **Corroborates** the anchor-type error. Overlap: high with the affect pathway in Jaidka and Cai; a comment, so lower weight.

## VERDICT (from the figures)

The shape survives; a universal threshold does not. A well-calibrated trust judgment is per slot, ranged against an anchor whose legitimacy follows from what the question is about, checked by at least one reading that does not share the system's evidence, refreshed when the task or habitat changes, and built on a vocabulary and objective that precede any display. Overtrust and undertrust are channel failures, not changes in the ground. Agreement is a convention until independence is bought. Capability is agent-and-habitat, so benchmark trust does not transfer without a measured gap. The specific numeric threshold and the source-discriminating instrument are a declared `gap`. This is a `specified`, ungrounded result, kept per domain and never averaged.

- **Cost if wrong:** bounded and locatable. The corrected packet states mode, refuters and provenance before the conclusion, so an error is findable rather than invisible. The real cost is spending the independence of outside readers by sending a recording instead of a generator, or by letting a global accuracy score hide the one live failure in a high-stakes deployment. Fig 2 :42 prices that cost: *"Buy independence in proportion to the cost of being wrong."* That cost is avoidable by leading with the shape and its refuter.
- **Undoable:** yes. No term is deleted if the claim fails; a refutation is added and the reach falls because something was added, not because anything was struck out. The shape and its six falsifiers are recorded in a provenance trail so a later reader can locate the error (local Fig 10 :62-64 reading: a ruling carries provenance like an atom). No primitive has to be withdrawn.
- **Reversed by:** a ground reading, not agreement. An anchor-type task switch showing the same deference rule correct in both cases; a per-slot failure injection that a global score detects; two shared-evidence checks shown to corroborate independently; calibration improved by an isolated model change with no deployment contact change; or a source-discriminating instrument that separates fluent-but-wrong from fluent-and-right. A figure re-census that derives a universal numeric threshold would also reverse the gap verdict. Until one of these fires, the verdict stands as `candidate`.
