**Question:** How can researchers distinguish self-organization from organization imposed by external constraints or hidden coordination?

**Source:** complexity science.txt line 17

**Section:** 2. Emergence, self-organization, and collective behavior

**Field for public contact points:** self-organization and design research

**Mode:** `specified` on the diagnostic claims, `general` on the structural shape. No ground reading settles the causal attribution, so nothing here is `grounded`. **Status:** done

**Registry status:** pending

## What the answer is, in effect terms

A researcher sees one observable, large-scale order O, and must choose among three causes: local interactions with no template (self-organization), an external constraint or designer (imposed order), and a hidden shared source that makes components agree without their coordinating (hidden coordination). O can be the same under all three, so O alone cannot decide. What decides is an intervention that changes contact: remove the suspected maintainer, break the shared source, or range the pattern against an anchor that does not update. If no such instrument exists at the current budget, the honest verdict is indiscernible now, not identical, and that is a stop, not a silence.

What an answer would look like, bound as an ordinary term: a ranked list of discriminating interventions, each tied to a figure line, each with a refuter written before the run. That bound shape is the preference this solution tests, following `THE_FORMULA.md` "Direction".

## What is deliberately not here

- No personal emails of named individuals.
- No ranking of people by how likely they are to agree with the verdict.
- No bulk-send plan.

Three reasons, in the figures' own words:

1. **Agreement is not a verdict.** Fig 7 :35: "A room's agreement is variation, not a verdict. Selection between rooms is where the ground votes." Choosing recipients for receptiveness manufactures the correlation that would make their agreement worthless.
2. **Shared evidence disqualifies corroboration.** Fig 2 :40: "disqualifying for a verdict: agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool." This is the very failure the question asks about, so the outreach cannot commit it.
3. **Do not spend the reader's independence.** Fig 10 :42: "A verifier that can reconstruct the claim cannot verify it." The first contact carries the residual and its refuter, never the framework's interiors.

Legally: gathered personal emails for unsolicited bulk outreach breach GDPR and CAN-SPAM style rules in many jurisdictions. Public opt-in venues avoid that failure and the structural one.
