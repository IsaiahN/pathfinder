# Outreach draft: check the output first, then decide about the framework

**Use:** one-to-one, or as a cover letter attached to a submission through the public venues in `03_public_contact_points.csv`. **Not** for bulk send.

**Hard precondition:** do not send until the shape claim and its refuter below survive the recipient's own check. The two-stage offer collapses on contact if stage 1 fails.

---

**Subject:** Check one attribution test before you decide whether the framework is worth reading

> Hi [name],
>
> I have one specific output I would like you to check against what you already know. I am not asking you to read the framework first, and I am not asking you to agree with it. If the output does not survive, there is nothing else to look at.
>
> **The output (`specified`, not `grounded`):** when a researcher sees large-scale order in a system, the order alone cannot say whether it self-organized, was imposed by an external constraint, or resulted from a hidden shared source that made the parts agree without their coordinating. Those three causes can produce the same observable pattern. The framework's claim is that the distinction is not in the pattern but in interventions: remove the suspected maintainer and see whether the order persists; cut the suspected shared source and see whether the agreement dissolves; range the parts separately against an anchor that does not update. Read the residual per part, not as one global order parameter.
>
> **What I am not claiming:** that a pattern's appearance identifies its cause; that hidden coordination means direct communication (indirect coordination through a shared environmental trace is often self-organization); that every self-organizing system is free of external input (they all need energy and contact); or that an instrument will always settle the case. Some orders leave no trace in the reading, and then only provenance can decide. I am also not claiming the framework is a final theory or the only true one.
>
> **What would refute the output:**
> 1. an invariant of the order alone, needing no intervention, that reliably separates the three causes across systems;
> 2. order persisting after the maintainer is removed while the template is shown to have been authored by that maintainer;
> 3. agreement surviving the cut of the shared source and separate ranging against a pinned anchor, with no residual shared evidence.
>
> I wrote these down before sending this. If any holds, the test is wrong and I will retract the claim. A one-line "this fails because..." is more useful to me than agreement. If you would rather not engage, no reply is needed.
>
> **If it survives, and only then:** the sources are below. The figures are the authority, not my summary of them.
> - Arrival of the Fittest: The Tether Architecture (all 13 figures, formula, citation list): https://adventuresinml.substack.com/p/arrival-of-the-fittest-the-tether
> - How Intelligence Actually Works (provenance, projection, trace cases): https://adventuresinml.substack.com/p/how-intelligence-actually-works
> - Figures and formula for direct download (SVG, branch `general-agent`): https://github.com/IsaiahN/tether/tree/general-agent/figures
>
> **If you want to use the machinery:** write down what would refute your result *before* you run it. The framework's own rule is that a loop cannot check the direction of its own question, so the preference has to be made an ordinary term first. A reported failure is more useful to me than a reported success.
>
> - Isaiah
