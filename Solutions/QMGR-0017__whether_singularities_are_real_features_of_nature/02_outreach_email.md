**Subject:** A checkable split for "are singularities real?", written before the answer

> Hi [name],
>
> I have one specific output I would like you to check against what you already know. I am not asking you to read a framework first, and I am not asking you to agree with it. If the output does not survive, there is nothing else to look at.
>
> **The output (`specified` on the per-domain claims, `general` on the split, `gap` on the literal reality verdict):**
>
> The question "real feature or artifact of classical theory" bundles three different questions with different anchors:
> 1. What classical general relativity licenses: the Penrose and Hawking theorems return a boundary, a maximal spacetime with an inextendible path of finite generalized affine length. That is a proof inside the classical closure.
> 2. Whether the ground registers such a thing: no instrument, at any resolution, has returned a reading of a singularity. A black hole singularity is hidden behind a horizon, and even the horizon is globally defined and locally unverifiable.
> 3. Whether a different frame would remove the term: candidate quantum gravity programmes predict a finite replacement, but no such prediction has been settled by an observation, and known results apply only to special, unphysical cases.
>
> Those are not opposite values of one variable. They have different anchors, so they cannot be averaged. One lever does not clear all three. The honest report is a union with one declared gap, not a yes or no.
>
> **What the figures add, in their own words:**
> "An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks." (Fig 2 :29). The anchor here is an instrument reading, not a census of opinion. "Where nothing is, there is no edge to extend from, and the search for a proto-instrument is a search for one that is already there." (Fig 6 :34-35). And crucially, "theory routinely precedes its instrument, and is welcome to" (Fig 6 :37-38), so predicting a singularity from classical GR is legitimate. The limit is on the instrument path, not on prediction.
>
> I am also refusing one common adjective. "Irreducible is a fact about a level and never about the world, and there is no bottom" (Fig 12 :104). So even if a singularity is irreducible inside classical GR, that is a fact about that level, not a licence to call it an irreducible feature of nature. I am not using that line to decide that singularities are unreal; that would be a different claim and this figure does not govern it.
>
> **What I am not claiming:** that I have settled whether nature contains singularities. I have not, and no figure does. That verdict is a `gap` of the unreached kind, reported rather than hidden. I am not claiming that a chart change removes the curvature singularity: the invariant divergence survives every coordinate system. I am not claiming that any particular quantum gravity programme is right. Naming loop quantum gravity or regular black holes is a binding from the physics literature, not a derivation.
>
> **What would refute the shape claim:**
> 1. An instrument reading, at any resolution, that registers a divergent curvature invariant or an unextendible local path.
> 2. A derivation showing that the classical singularity term is obtainable in another frame's closure without an import.
> 3. A coordinate change that removes a divergent curvature scalar.
> 4. A level at which composition stops and irreducibility becomes absolute.
> 5. A single anchor and instrument shown to govern all three sub-questions, so the union split was wrong.
>
> I wrote these down before running the derivation. If any holds, I retract the shape claim. A one-line "this fails because..." is more useful to me than agreement. If you would rather not engage, no reply is needed.
