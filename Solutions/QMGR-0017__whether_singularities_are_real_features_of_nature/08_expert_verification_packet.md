## 1. The claim, in one paragraph

The question "real feature or artifact of classical theory" is a union of three conditions with different anchors, not one binary variable. Classical general relativity returns a boundary term inside its own closure through the singularity theorems, which settles what the theory licenses and not what the world contains. No instrument at any resolution registers that term, and even the event horizon that hides a black-hole singularity is globally defined and locally unverifiable, so the reality verdict is a `gap` of the unreached kind, reported rather than hidden. The word artifact covers two different structures: a chart artifact removed by re-description and a theory-limit artifact that requires a new closure. The adjective irreducible is level-relative, so it cannot be promoted to a claim about nature. If a replacement arrives it will be an import with a described effect and a provenance stamp, not a re-description and not more search inside general relativity. What the framework offers is that shape, five falsifiers written in advance, and the kinds of instrument that would close it.

## 2. Three checkable sub-claims

### C1: Framework-internal; checkable by reading Figures 2, 6 and 9

- **Statement:** the classical theorem is a closure result and a legitimate anchor only for the sub-question about what GR says. The either/or bundles conditions with different anchors, so verdicts must be kept separate.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 2 :36 "An anchor is legitimate when the question is constitutively about it." Fig 6 :21 "MINT composes inside the closure and can never add an atom." Fig 9 :54 "And when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again."
- **What to check:** whether the singularity theorems are being used as a proof inside classical GR or as a claim about the world. The two uses have different anchors and cannot be averaged.
- **Refuter:** show that all three conditions share one anchor and one instrument, so one lever clears every intersection; or produce a ground reading that settles the classical sub-question without the theorem; or show the theorem does not follow from its premises.

### C2: Ground-facing; checkable against the observation record

- **Statement:** nothing at any resolution is returning a reading whose failure to resolve is due to a singularity. INWARD is closed; prediction is not.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 6 :40 "What is unavailable is a sensor for something that has never registered anywhere, at any resolution. That is the whole of it." Fig 3 :27 "A link with no instrument is not a link that works. It is a link nobody has looked at." Held apart from Fig 6 :37-38 "theory routinely precedes its instrument, and is welcome to. Neptune was calculated before it was seen, and the prediction is what told anyone where to point"
- **What to check:** Wikipedia states that it is unknown whether black hole singularities truly exist in real black holes. The SEP entry states that in the absence of any conceivable observational data about the interior of black holes, the only means of approaching these issues are mathematical methods, and that locating the horizon locally is impossible in principle and its presence a strictly unverifiable hypothesis. Is there an existing instrument reading, at any resolution, that fails to resolve because of a singularity? If not, the `gap` stands.
- **Refuter:** exhibit such a reading, or exhibit a local measurement that registers the horizon. Either reopens INWARD. Note the limit: this is `unreached` at this budget, not `unreachable in principle`; Fig 8 :40-41 "Underivable-in-principle and unreachable-at-this-budget are different facts, and they issue different instructions."

### C3: Structural; checkable by reading Figures 8 and 12

- **Statement:** a chart artifact and a theory-limit artifact are different structures, and irreducibility is a fact about a level. The realist's adjective is refused; the realist's existence claim is not decided by these lines.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 12 :5 "A set of ingredients is not one. The bond is the rest of the information, and it is the half nothing was recording." Fig 8 :32 "Closure is idempotent. Composing inside a closed set never leaves it, so no search, however long, adds an atom. This one is definitional: it is what closure means." Fig 12 :104 "A settled molecule becomes an atom for whatever composes over it. So irreducible is a fact about a level and never about the world, and there is no bottom"
- **What to check:** the Kretschmann scalar is diffeomorphism invariant and diverges at the centre of a non-rotating black hole, while the metric divergence at the horizon is removable in Kruskal coordinates. Does any chart remove the invariant divergence? Does any level stop composition so that irreducibility becomes absolute?
- **Refuter:** a coordinate change that removes a divergent curvature scalar, collapsing the two artifact domains; or a level at which composition stops. Either defeats C3. Using Fig 12 :104 to decide existence was tried and retracted as a strain in `01` F4; do not re-introduce it.

## 3. What is explicitly not claimed

- Not that singularities are real, and not that they are unreal. The literal verdict is `gap`.
- Not that the curvature singularity is a coordinate artifact. The invariant divergence survives every chart.
- Not that a replacement frame is right. Loop quantization, fuzzballs and regular black holes are bindings from the physics literature, provenanced as such, and none has passed a source gate and shadow test for this residual.
- Not that the SEP entry and the Wikipedia pages are independent witnesses. They share a review pool; by Fig 2 :40 "disqualifying for a verdict: agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool".
- Not grounded. No measurement was used, and this packet is a simulation.

## 5. What would settle it

1. Any instrument reading, at any resolution, registering a divergent curvature invariant or unextendible local path. Closes D2.
2. A local measurement that registers the horizon, or a revised definition under which the horizon is locally measurable.
3. A candidate replacement whose closure predicts the described residual shape, with provenance and shadow test recorded, settled by an instrument. Gives D3 an anchor.
4. A derivation showing the classical term obtainable in another frame's closure without an import. Defeats the closure bound in C1.
5. A single anchor and instrument shown to govern all three conditions. Defeats the union split in C1.
