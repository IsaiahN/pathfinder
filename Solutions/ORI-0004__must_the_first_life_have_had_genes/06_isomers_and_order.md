## DERIVATION

### Q1 : Are the three programs isomers?

- Fig 12 :117: **"isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."**
- Fig 12 :94: **"The arrangement is the substance."**
- Write each program over the shared atom set {slot, spendable gradient, carried record}:
  - RNA-first takes the record as the first settled molecule and bonds the other two to it.
  - Metabolism-first takes the gradient as the first settled molecule and bonds the other two to it.
  - Membrane-first takes the slot as the first settled molecule and bonds the other two to it.
- Same atoms, different bond, different substance. Under Fig 12 :117 they are **isomers**, not three answers of which one must be right. This matches the source file's own preamble, which says the programs *"are not always mutually exclusive"*.
- Valence caveat, Fig 11 :49: **"An actor’s valence is relative to the residual, not a property it carries."** Which atom is foregrounded depends on which residual the researcher is trying to close, so the programs are not even stable kinds across questions.
- **Falsifier, written before the run:** if any program requires an atom that the others cannot express even at the origin (for example metabolism-first requiring a heritable polymer before any record exists), the isomer claim fails and the weaker **functional-group** reading stands: Fig 12 :123, **"functional group. A substructure whose presence fixes the outcome. The group says what kind; the qualifier says which member."**

### Q2 : What is the dependency order?

- Fig 3 :20: **"The chain is ordered by dependency, not by importance."**
- Fig 1 :19: **"The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated."** So a slot must exist before a residual can be indexed. D2 precedes D3.
- Fig 2 :23: **"The ground enters as the only metric."** A gradient must be ranged against a ground that does not update before selection can register. D3 precedes D4.
- Fig 5 :70: **"A term is settled when the ground pays for it."** Fig 12 :104: **"A settled molecule becomes an atom for whatever composes over it. So irreducible is a fact about a level and never about the world, and there is no bottom"** A carried record becomes composable only after settlement. D4 follows D3.
- Fig 3 :19: **"Optimising a link that already works cannot rescue one that does not."** Spending effort on the order-of-origin question before the three conditions are characterised is effort above the break.
- **Conclusion for Q2:** the licensed order is a **dependency order**, slot → gradient contact → carried record, not a chronological order. Confusing the two is a category error: Fig 3 :18 says *"Where a given chain stops is a reading, never a property of the chain."*

### Q3 : Does the framework license a temporal order?

- Fig 9 :54: **"And when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again."** The order question bundles at least three overlaps: which bond was settled first, what habitat held it, and what counts as the threshold.
- Fig 9 :57: **"What remains is smaller and it is also categorically different: a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either."**
- No anchor exists. Wikipedia's abiogenesis article: *"The transition from non-life to life has not been observed experimentally."* The Nature Communications Hadean paper says *"Estimates for when life emerged on Earth range from ca. 3.8 Ga (Eoarchean) to the Hadean eon"*, and its 4.33 Ga result is a thermal model, not a trace.
- Fig 8 :39: **"So a search that finds nothing is never a proof of absence."** Fig 13 :134: **"Unreachable is a verdict here, not a silence."**
- **Conclusion for Q3:** temporal order is a `gap`, unreachable at this budget. This is not the same as underivable in principle (Fig 6 :40, *"What is unavailable is a sensor for something that has never registered anywhere, at any resolution"*); a future instrument could register it. At present no instrument is shown that discriminates the three sources.

### Q4 : Is the cycle structure present?

- Fig 12 :121: **"cycle. The output re-enters as input. The sign of the loop decides whether it closes, and a chain cannot close by construction."**
- A carried record that makes the next round cheaper is a positive feedback loop: the output (a settled term) re-enters as the input for the next search. That is why cumulative selection is a cycle and a one-off reaction is a chain.
- Fig 12 :119: **"catalyst. A part that survives its own use. Spent and regenerating parts behave differently across uses, and nothing records which a term is."** A slot that persists across rounds is catalyst-like; a slot that is consumed is not. Distinguishing them needs an instrument, not an argument.
- The world literature supplies a candidate instance: Wikipedia's autopoiesis article describes a network of processes that *"continuously regenerate and realize the network of processes (relations) that produced them"* and constitute a concrete unity in space. That is a cycle plus a seam, arrived at independently in systems theory. It is corroboration of the mechanism shape only, and the same article records the criticism that autopoiesis is not commonly used as the criterion for life.
- **Falsifier:** show that the carried record in the first evolving system was spent on one round and not regenerated, with no other catalyst-like part. That breaks the cycle reading and leaves a linear chain, which cannot accumulate.

## CONCLUSION

The three programs are **isomers over a shared atom set** (Fig 12 :117) in the `specified` mode, with a stated falsifier. The framework licenses a **dependency order** (slot → gradient contact → carried record) from Fig 1 :19, Fig 2 :23, Fig 5 :70 and Fig 3 :20, but it does **not** license a chronological order; that is a `gap`, unreachable at this budget, because no anchor and no discriminating instrument exist. The cycle structure (Fig 12 :121) is what separates cumulative selection from a reaction that merely repeats. This rescues what is defensible in the question's "some combination" option without naming a modern form or taking sides among programs.
