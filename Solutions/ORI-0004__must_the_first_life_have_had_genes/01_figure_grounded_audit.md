## 0. The claim under audit

> Must the first life have had genes, metabolism, a boundary, or some combination?

Restated in effect terms. The question is about a *threshold*, the point at which chemistry becomes a system that can be selected. The field's own operational marker is cumulative Darwinian evolution, captured in the NASA working definition fetched this session: *"a self-sustaining chemical system capable of Darwinian evolution"*. So define the target property P: a system whose residual stays indexed to a slot across rounds and whose changes are carried, so selection can compound.

The three candidates are not at the same level of description:

- **genes**: a carried, heritable record that makes the next round cheaper, a method or head start;
- **metabolism**: a spendable, directed gradient in contact with something that does not update on the system's own report;
- **boundary**: an individuation that makes "one system" a slot with an inside and an outside.

Options and what each leads to:

| option | leads to |
|---|---|
| A, all three in modern form | genes, metabolic pathways and a lipid membrane were simultaneously present at the threshold; removing any one removes life |
| B, none of them | the transition involved no carried record, no spendable gradient and no individuated slot, so nothing could be selected per system |
| C, some combination | the three are conditions of different kinds, in dependency order, none sufficient alone, and their first forms were not their modern forms |

**The sought-for shape, bound as an ordinary term before the run** (Formula, "Direction"): *a bonded arrangement of three shape-constraints, slot individuation, spendable gradient contact, and a carried method, with modern forms excluded at the origin and the temporal order left open.* Binding it now lets it accrue a residual like anything else, so the preference can be refused by the same test as everything else.

## 1. The residual, described by arity, symmetry and scale

Before naming any figure, describe R (Fig 9 :22, *"arity · symmetry · scale"*).

- **Arity: three conditions.** The question names three candidates and asks about combinations. A three-condition union has seven non-empty subsets, so "which subset" is already a combinatorial question. Fig 9 :54: *"And when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again."* A single lever clears the region where only one condition holds and leaves the intersections standing.
- **Symmetry: the three are not interchangeable.** A boundary is a seam, a gradient is a spendable difference, a record is a carried term. Their bonds differ. Swapping their roles changes the thing (Operators table :33 on sequence: *"swap them. Does it become a different thing? Yes."*). So this is not a symmetric conjunction where order is free.
- **Scale: the question asks about a level transition.** Chemistry to biology is a crossing, not a step inside one frame. Fig 4 :19-20: *"Only the actors, the currency and the anchor change."* Fig 10 :68: *"Different actors, a different currency, a different ground, and a lossy transform between."* The candidate parts must be composed at the lower level and only then become atoms at the upper one (Fig 12 :104).

R itself is the gap between what a proposed mechanism predicts about which conditions are necessary at the threshold and what the ground shows. No ground reading of the transition exists: Wikipedia's abiogenesis article states *"The transition from non-life to life has not been observed experimentally, but many proposals have been made for different stages of the process"* (fetched 2026-11-23). R arrives, but it is not settled.

## 4. Findings

### F1 : No figure line enumerates genes, metabolism or a boundary (decisive for the `gap`)

I grepped the verbatim figure text for each candidate term and for "minimum" in the biological sense. Fig 13 :3 says:

> **"No system functions without any of them, none is built from the others, and everything else is a composition across the two kinds."**

The "them" are the six terms listed at Fig 13 :9-24: space, time, energy (states) and spectrum, gradient, network (structures). Genes, metabolism and boundary are not among them. The figures' own limit applies:

Fig 13 :134-135:

> **"Unreachable is a verdict here, not a silence"**
> **"It cannot say which primitive is missing, because naming it would mean already holding it."**

And Fig 9 :32: *"you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself."*

So the literal version of the question, "which of these named biological parts must a first life have had," is a `gap`. The figures return a kind of thing, not the list. This is a verdict, not a silence, and it is not an invitation to invent a parts list. **This finding does not mean the figures are silent about the question in every form.** It means they govern the effect form, developed in F2 to F6.

**Refuter:** a figure line is produced that directly names genes, heredity, metabolism or a membrane as a primitive or as a necessary first-order molecule. Until that happens this finding stands.

### F2 : The minimum-set shape rules out any single candidate being sufficient

Fig 13 :33:

> **"Neither kind generates anything alone. Neither denotes anything alone. That is why they are two kinds rather than one list."**

Fig 13 :29-30:

> **"A state with no structure has no magnitude: three of what, ordered how, varying across what?"**
> **"A structure with no state is empty: an ordering over nothing, a network with nothing at the nodes."**

Apply to the three candidates as *shapes*: a carried record with no gradient to spend it against is an ordering over nothing; a gradient with no slot to accrue to is directedness with nothing to direct; a seam with nothing held inside and nothing carried across is a boundary around nothing. Each alone fails the figure's condition. So Option B is refused by the figure's own lockstep rule, and any one-candidate answer (genes only, metabolism only, boundary only) is refused for the same reason.

Fig 13 :41 adds indivisibility: *"Each is indivisible: no arrangement of the other five yields it."* The biological three are not indivisible in that sense; each is a composition. That is precisely why they are a derived stratum, not a new minimum set.

**Refuter:** demonstrate a system that undergoes cumulative Darwinian evolution with only one of the three shape-constraints present and no substitute for the other two. That breaks the lockstep prediction at the ground.

### F3 : The boundary requirement is a slot, not a named membrane

Fig 1 :19: *"The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated."* The Symbols table defines a slot as *"One object, region or entity being tracked."* Fig 10 :21 gives the membrane its own, narrower reading:

> **"The membrane is the boundary between the sizes, and the rule about what may cross it. Not a wall: a filter with a direction."**

So what the figures require is *individuation*: something that makes one object, region or entity trackable so R can be indexed to it. A lipid membrane is one way to do that in familiar biology; it is not the figure's requirement. The world literature agrees on the distinction in its own terms: NASA's Barge asks *"We don't know what pieces of the first life came when. What came first? What came later? Was it before or after the cell emerged?"*, and the abiogenesis literature explicitly allows earlier cells to have had *"a leaky membrane"* powered by a natural proton gradient. So the figure-grounded claim is boundary-shaped individuation, not a membrane at the threshold.

**Refuter:** show that R can be indexed per slot with no individuation of any kind, or show that the figures force a lipid membrane specifically. The first breaks the D2 verdict; the second would upgrade a name that the figures currently withhold.

### F4 : The metabolism requirement is spendable gradient plus non-updating ground, not a modern pathway

Fig 13 :22: *"a difference that can be spent."* Fig 13 :46: *"What makes a gradient a gradient is that it can be spent, and neither an ordering nor a span of space has directedness."* Fig 11 :40:

> **"A system cannot be made more capable by improving the agent in isolation. An improvement that does not change contact changes nothing, however much it improves."**

Fig 2 :29: *"An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks."*

The metabolism-shaped condition is therefore: a directed difference that can be spent, ranged against a ground that does not move when the system's own report changes. It does not require the modern metabolic network. The world literature supplies the reason: Knowable Magazine reports that Preiner and Martin reproduced core metabolic reactions with iron and nickel instead of 127 enzymes, and quotes Moran's caveat, *"we have not identified any conditions that allow an entire metabolism to emerge"*. NASA's Barge puts the precondition as a question: *"What gradients existed before life?"* So the gradient can be environmental and pre-biological; it is the contact that matters, not the pathway.

Joyce's Q&A is often read as conflicting here: he lists metabolism as implicit in Darwinian evolution but calls compartmentalization an adaptation. Re-census dissolves it. Joyce's "compartmentalization" is the biological adaptation built on a seam; Fig 10 :21's membrane is the seam as a filter with direction. Different objects, no contradiction. The conflict rule was not triggered.

**Refuter:** show cumulative selection with no spendable directed difference anywhere in the system or its habitat, or show that a spendable gradient whose ground updates on the system's own report still settles a verdict. Either breaks D3.

### F5 : The genes requirement is a carried method, and modern genes were not available at the origin

Fig 3 :14-15: *"name what worked, verify it, and carry it to the next problem."* Fig 4 :22: *"Only methods cross up. Only head starts come down."* Fig 12 :107-110:

> **"Whether that cost must be paid again depends on the substrate: where the arrangement is recorded, re-deriving it is a lookup and the actions were spent once."**

The heredity-shaped condition is a carried method that makes the next search cheaper. It does not require a DNA genome. And it cannot have required one at the origin, because a term must be settled before it can be composed over:

Fig 5 :70: *"A term is settled when the ground pays for it."*
Fig 12 :104: *"A settled molecule becomes an atom for whatever composes over it. So irreducible is a fact about a level and never about the world, and there is no bottom"*

Modern genes are settled molecules at a later stratum. At the threshold they were not yet settled, so they could not serve as atoms for the first life. Joyce's own lab result makes the same point in world terms: the Lincoln and Joyce RNA system met the NASA definition technically, yet Joyce said *"It doesn't make it"* because there was *"not enough information carrying capacity"*. So a carried record was present; the modern form was not. Option A is refused at the ground by the very researcher whose definition Option A leans on.

**Refuter:** demonstrate that a modern DNA gene was present and settled at the first transition, or show a cumulative selection process with no carried term of any kind. The first breaks "not modern forms"; the second breaks D4.

### F6 : The programs are isomers over a shared atom set, not rival answers to one question

Fig 12 :117:

> **"isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."**

The three programs can be written over the same atom set: a slot, a spendable gradient, a carried record. They differ in which atom is taken as the first settled molecule and which bond mediates the other two. RNA-first bonds the record first; metabolism-first bonds the gradient first; membrane-first bonds the slot first. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. That is Fig 12's isomer, not three answers of which one must be right.

Fig 11 :51 is a warning against the standing-list version: *"No standing list of good actors survives a change of residual."* Here the "actors" are the three candidates; their valence is relative to the residual (Fig 11 :49: *"An actor’s valence is relative to the residual, not a property it carries."*). Fig 12 :123 gives the weaker shared reading: *"functional group. A substructure whose presence fixes the outcome. The group says what kind; the qualifier says which member."*

**Refuter:** show that the programs do not share an atom set, for example that metabolism-first requires an atom that RNA-first cannot express even at the origin. That kills the isomer claim and leaves the functional-group or disjoint reading.

### F7 : The order question is a union compound and its anchor is missing

Fig 9 :54: *"And when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again."* Fig 9 :57-58:

> **"What remains is smaller and it is also categorically different: a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either."**

The "which came first" debate bundles at least three overlaps: which bond was settled first, what habitat held it, and what counts as the threshold for selection. One lever clears the region where only one condition holds and leaves the intersections standing. There is no anchor for the order claim: no experiment observed the transition (Wikipedia abiogenesis, fetched 2026-11-23), and the Nature Communications Hadean-timing paper states *"Estimates for when life emerged on Earth range from ca. 3.8 Ga (Eoarchean) to the Hadean eon"*, with its own estimate depending on a thermal model rather than a direct trace. Fig 2 :33: *"The anchor must not update."* An order inferred from a model that updates on the programs it is testing is a frame, not an anchor.

Fig 8 :39 settles the honesty: *"So a search that finds nothing is never a proof of absence."* D5 is `unreachable` at this budget, which is a verdict and not silence.

**Refuter:** produce a ground reading, not a model, that shows the temporal order of the three bonds at the threshold. Until one exists, D5 stays `gap`.

### F8 : Import cannot supply the missing parts, because there was no living peer

Fig 6 :14: *"the only operator that moves the wall"*. Fig 6 :23: *"No frame escapes its own closure. The bound moves only on contact with a frame whose closure differs."* Fig 8 :32: *"Closure is idempotent. Composing inside a closed set never leaves it, so no search, however long, adds an atom."*

At the origin there is no living peer frame to import from. So the three conditions must be composed from the prebiotic closure, and whatever crosses the threshold is a generator (a method that can be reapplied), not a recording of one success. Fig 4 :25: *"A recording carried upward looks like knowledge and is a description of one occasion."* This is the structural reason why "the first life borrowed genes from somewhere" is not an answer available to the figures: it would require a living source that by hypothesis does not yet exist. Panspermia merely moves the transition elsewhere and owes the same three conditions at the new location.

**Refuter:** show a figure-licensed import at the threshold whose source is not itself a first life. Until then F8 stands.

### F9 : Prestige and agreement cannot settle the order

The programs have famous proponents, and the temptation is to rank them by authority. Fig 7 :35: *"A room’s agreement is variation, not a verdict. Selection between rooms is where the ground votes."* Fig 2 :40: *"disqualifying for a verdict: agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool"*. The three programs also share evidence (the same geochemical and molecular record), so their agreement or disagreement is one pool talking.

Fig 10 :59: *"an interest in the outcome is unavoidable, an interest in the reading is disqualifying."* My own stake here is structural: I am producing a derivation and would prefer it to survive. That interest is declared, and the pre-registered refuter in section 5 is the instrument that keeps it from deciding the result.

**Refuter:** a ranking of programs by proponent prestige is offered as evidence for the order. That is the named collapse, not a refutation of this finding, and it is recorded as a failure.

### F10 : The isomer and order claims need an instrument, not another argument

Fig 6 :34-35:

> **"The question is not whether a sensor could exist. It is whether anything, at any resolution, is already returning something that fails to resolve."**

What is needed is a discriminator that distinguishes a carried record from a spendable gradient and from an individuated seam at the threshold, and a trace that shows which was settled first. The framework essay *The Hard Problem is About Source* states the general rule in world terms: *"If several paths create the same behavior, then behavior is a projection with lossy information"* and *"The answer is unreached until a better instrument exists."* At present several programs can produce the same reading, "a system that evolves," so the reading cannot comment on the source. That is the metamerism error, and it is why D5 is a `gap` rather than a choice among programs.

**Refuter:** exhibit an instrument already returning a discriminating signal at some resolution. If none is shown, F10 holds and the stop is honest.

## 5. Pre-registered refuter

Written before the conclusion, per the standing instruction: a falsifier written before the run is a design instrument. The shape claim is refuted if any of the following holds:

1. A system undergoing cumulative Darwinian evolution is demonstrated with only one shape-constraint and no substitute for the other two (breaks F2).
2. A figure line directly names genes, metabolism or a membrane as a primitive or a necessary first-order molecule (breaks F1).
3. A ground reading shows the first evolving system had all three in their modern form simultaneously (breaks F5 and Option A).
4. The programs are shown not to share an atom set (breaks the isomer claim in F6, leaves the functional-group reading).
5. A single mechanism with one anchor accounts for all three conditions, so they are one condition rather than a union (breaks F7).
6. A figure-licensed import at the threshold is shown whose source is not itself a first life (breaks F8).

If any fires, the derivation is withdrawn in whole or in the named part. Until one is run, no part may be labelled `grounded`.

## 10. Final verdict

The question as literally posed, "which named parts must the first life have had," is a `gap`: no figure line returns a biological parts list, and no ground reading of the transition exists. The question in effect form has a figure-derived shape: a system enters selection only when an individuated slot, a spendable gradient in contact with a non-updating ground, and a carried method are bonded together, in that dependency order. No one of them suffices; their first forms were not their modern forms; and the research programs that foreground one each are isomers over the shared atom set, not mutually exclusive answers. The temporal order of the three bonds is unreachable at this budget. The overall reading is Option C, `specified` on the per-domain claims, `general` on the shape, `gap` on the order, and not `grounded`. A discriminator at the threshold, not another argument, is what would settle it.
