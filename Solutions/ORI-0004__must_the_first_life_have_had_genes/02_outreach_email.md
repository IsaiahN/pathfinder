# Outreach draft : check the shape before you decide which origin-of-life program is right

**Use:** one to one, or as a cover letter attached to a submission through the public venues in `03_public_contact_points.csv`. **Not** for bulk send.

**Hard precondition:** do not send until the strain named in `01_figure_grounded_audit.md` section 9 is understood by the sender. The offer rests on a shape claim and a refuter, not on a verdict about RNA-first, metabolism-first or membrane-first. If the sender cannot state the refuter without looking, the two-stage offer collapses on contact.

---

**Subject:** one checkable claim about the first life, before the theory

> Hi [name],
>
> I have one specific claim I would like you to check against what you already know. I am not asking you to read a theory first, and I am not asking you to pick a side in the RNA-first, metabolism-first or membrane-first debate. If the claim does not survive, there is nothing else to look at.
>
> **The claim (`specified` on the parts, `general` on the shape, not `grounded`):** the question "must the first life have had genes, metabolism, a boundary, or some combination?" is a union of conditions, not a parts list. In effect terms, a system enters cumulative selection only when three conditions are bonded together: an individuated slot so a residual can be indexed to one system rather than averaged away; a spendable directed gradient ranged against a ground that does not update on the system's own report; and a carried term that makes the next round cheaper. In dependency order, the slot comes before the residual, the gradient comes before the selection, and the carried record comes after settlement. No one of the three is sufficient alone.
>
> **What I am not claiming:** that the first life had DNA genes, a modern metabolic network or a lipid membrane. Those are settled molecules and structures of a later stratum. A carried record, a spendable gradient and a seam are required; their first forms are not named by the derivation. I am also not claiming that any one of the three research programs came first. The figures return a shape, not a name and not a date.
>
> **What is a genuine gap:** the literal order question. No figure line returns the temporal order of the three conditions, and the transition from non-life to life has not been observed. NASA's own working definition is a marker, not a mechanism, and the field still asks what came first. I report that as unreachable at this budget, which is a verdict and not silence.
>
> **What would refute the claim:** write these down before we discuss anything else.
> 1. A system undergoing cumulative Darwinian evolution is shown with only one of the three conditions and no substitute for the other two.
> 2. A modern gene is shown to have been present and settled at the first transition, with no earlier carried record.
> 3. The three programs are shown not to share an atom set (each requires something the others cannot express even at the origin).
> 4. A single mechanism with one anchor accounts for all three conditions, so they are one condition rather than a union.
> 5. A ground reading, not a model, establishes the temporal order of the three bonds at the threshold.
>
> I wrote these down before sending this. If any holds, I will retract the claim or the named part of it. A one-line "this fails because..." is more useful to me than agreement. If you would rather not engage, no reply is needed.
>
> **If it survives, and only then:** here are the sources. The figures are the authority, not my summary of them.
> - Canonical figures README: https://github.com/IsaiahN/tether/blob/general-agent/figures/README.md
> - Figures and formula for direct download (SVG, branch `general-agent`): https://github.com/IsaiahN/tether/tree/general-agent/figures
> - The architecture essay, which states the simulated-output caveat in its own words: https://adventuresinml.substack.com/p/arrival-of-the-fittest-the-tether
>
> **If you want to use the machinery:** the figures link has the formula. Run it on a problem you know well and can check against a ground. Write down what would refute your result *before* you run it, and state its mode. A reported failure is more useful to me than a reported success.
>
> [your name]

## Practical notes for sending

- Send to public, opt-in institutional channels only (see `03_public_contact_points.csv`). Do not use personal addresses harvested from author pages.
- One message, one recipient, one question. Do not cc a room; a room is not an anchor (Fig 7 :35).
- Record the stage-1 verdict in `Logs/derivation_log.md` format before sending any link. A later agreement is a conversation, never corroboration.
- Do not frame the ask as "which program do you support." That manufactures the correlation that disqualifies a verdict (Fig 2 :40).
- The three links come only after a verdict on stage 1. Until then the recipient must not hold the framework's interior (Fig 10 :42).
