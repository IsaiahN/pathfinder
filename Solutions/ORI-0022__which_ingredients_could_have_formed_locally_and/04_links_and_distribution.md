## Wider literature that bears on the answer

These were opened via retrieved page text and are recorded in the ledger with the same accessed date. They are corroboration, never verdicts.

- **Glavin et al. 2021**, *Meteoritics & Planetary Science*: alanine in Murchison is *"both racemic (D approximately L) and heavily enriched in 13C,"* with non-proteinogenic amino acids whose carbon isotopes *"fall well outside the typical terrestrial range."* This is the isotope-and-chirality discriminator in action. The publisher page returned 403 on a second direct fetch; the abstract text was retrieved and the result is cross-checked against Burton and Berger 2018, which was fetched in full.
- **Altwegg et al. 2016**, *Science Advances*: glycine and phosphorus in comet 67P, with methylamine and ethylamine as precursor molecules. Cross-checked against the ESA page fetched above.
- **Lazcano and Bada 2012**, *Evolution: Education and Outreach*: Miller-Urey apparatus, Strecker synthesis, and the statement that *"A variety of prebiotic processes can form amino acids."*
- **Lambert et al. 2010**, *Science*: silicate ions catalyse the formose reaction and *"sequesters them from rapid decomposition,"* extending the range of environments in which sugars could form.
- **Ritson et al. 2024**, *Nature Astronomy*: cosmic dust sedimentation in glacial systems as a concentrated feedstock for P, S, N and C.
- **Nucleobase and amino acid formation through impacts on the early ocean**, *Earth and Planetary Science Letters* 2015: cytosine and uracil plus amino acids formed from meteoritic minerals, water, nitrogen and ammonium bicarbonate in impact-induced reactions. This is the compound case, not a clean delivery.
- **Johnson et al. 2008**, *Science* supplemental: reanalysis of Miller's volcanic spark experiment found 22 amino acids and five amines, supporting *"localized prebiotic synthesis"* even if the global atmosphere was not reducing.
