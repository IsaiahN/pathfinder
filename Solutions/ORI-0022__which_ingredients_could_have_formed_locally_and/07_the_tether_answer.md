# The Tether's answer: ORI-0022

**Mode: `specified` on the per-slot shape and the chemistry bindings, `general` on the reachability rule. Not `grounded`.** Simulated derivation checked for internal consistency against the figures (`05_what_shape_means.md` §6), not a ground reading.

## 1. The residual it answers

For each prebiotic ingredient slot, is its presence at the origin site reachable by composition from the local early-Earth chemical closure, or does accounting for it require an import from a frame whose closure differs? The residual is per slot. Fig 1 :19: *"The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated."* A single global answer would be the aggregated average that hides the live slots.

## 2. The shape

1. **The two options are not a partition.** Local formation is composition inside a closure; delivery is import from a differing closure. A molecule can be both. Fig 9 :54-56 requires unbundling a union before searching again. The answer is therefore two independent columns per slot plus a provenance column, not one forced bucket.

2. **Local formation is composition, not creation of atoms.** Fig 6 :21: *"MINT composes inside the closure and can never add an atom."* Fig 13 :115: *"The set is closed. The arrangements are not. That gap is why import exists."* So a slot is locally reachable when a reaction chain from plausible local feedstocks reaches its bonded arrangement. On the world evidence: amino acids via spark discharge and Strecker chemistry, sugars via formose with silicate or borate assistance, nucleobases via HCN oligomerisation and cyanoacetylene plus urea, fatty alcohols and fatty acids via Fischer-Tropsch type synthesis over metal catalysts. These are `specified`, ungrounded bindings, not figure derivations.

3. **Delivery is an import that adds no atom to the world.** Fig 6 :14 calls import *"the only operator that moves the wall"*; Fig 6 :25: *"An import adds no atom to the world."* Meteorites, comets and dust move molecular forms and access into the local frame. On the world evidence: carbonaceous chondrites contain a broad amino-acid suite and all five canonical nucleobases, ribose and other sugars, and reduced phosphorus as schreibersite; comet 67P has glycine and phosphorus in its coma. Delivery is `import-available` for those slots, not proven to be the dominant source.

4. **The molecule does not carry its origin.** Fig 8 :50: *"Convergent derivation and adopted import look identical in the contents. Only the record of where each came from separates them"* This is the decisive point. An alanine made in a flask and one extracted from Murchison are the same arrangement of the same atoms. Isotope enrichment, enantiomeric excess, isomer distribution and a known vector separate them. Without a discriminator the honest entry is `undetermined`.

5. **The figures give the shape, not the ingredient list.** Fig 9 :32: *"you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself."* Fig 13 :135: *"It cannot say which primitive is missing, because naming it would mean already holding it."* Every ingredient name in this answer comes from a world source, recorded as such.

6. **Two slots do not fit either bucket.** Reactive phosphorus is a depth-versus-absence case: P is present locally in insoluble apatite, reactive reduced P can be made by lightning and serpentinization locally, and meteorites supply schreibersite. From inside the frame the two look the same (Fig 6 :27). Impact synthesis uses an extraterrestrial catalyst and shock to make new bonds in the early ocean, so it is a local composition triggered by an import. Both are recorded as compound or undetermined, not hidden.

## 4. What it explicitly does not say

- Not that meteorites delivered the majority of the building blocks. That is a flux question (source question 27), and Fig 9 :60 forbids letting a budget filter hand you a verdict.
- Not that any specific molecule must have come from space, or must have been made locally. Both columns are reachability claims, not actual-origin claims without a discriminator.
- Not that laboratory conditions reproduce the Hadean site. The 2026 review states there is *"no direct way of verifying if one or the other experiment exactly repeats an event from the actual life formation period."*
- Not that an ingredient list is a pathway. Fig 12 :47: *"A recipe that names only its ingredients has recorded half of itself."*
- Not `grounded`. No source-discriminating measurement was taken by this agent.

## 5. What would settle it - the ground

A source-discriminating instrument, not another review. The relevant questions are:

1. Does the molecule carry a heavy-isotope (13C, D, 15N) signature outside the terrestrial range at the origin-relevant site?
2. Is the chiral distribution racemic or non-biologically skewed, with a landing-site soil blank that rules out contamination?
3. Is the isomer and methylated-analog distribution biological or abiological?
4. For reactive P, what is the local reduced-P flux at the specific site compared with the meteoritic flux?
5. For impact synthesis, do the products carry a meteoritic or a local isotopic signature?

Standing falsifiers from `01_figure_grounded_audit.md` §4: provenance inversion, convergent collapse, discriminator failure, figure re-census failure, union refutation.

## 6. The honest stop

**Unreached, not unreachable.** The local closure and the extraterrestrial closure are both enumerable, but which one actually supplied a given molecule at a given site is a measurement question. Fig 13 :134: *"Unreachable is a verdict here, not a silence"* - so the two undetermined slots are reported, not omitted. Fig 8 :39: *"So a search that finds nothing is never a proof of absence."* The lineage is recorded so the stop is checkable; indiscernibility is not identity.

## 7. One-paragraph version for outreach

> The question "formed locally or arrived on meteorites?" is not a two-column sort. Local formation is composition from atoms already in a site's chemical closure; delivery is an import from a frame whose closure differs, and it adds no new element to the world. A molecule can be both. So the answer is a per-ingredient shape: two independent reachability columns plus a provenance column. Amino acids, sugars, nucleobases and fatty acids are locally reachable in laboratory analogues and are also found in meteorites or comets; reactive phosphorus and impact-generated molecules sit in the overlap and stay undetermined. The decisive point is that the molecule cannot tell you its origin: a flask-made alanine and a Murchison alanine are the same arrangement of the same atoms, and only isotopic enrichment, chirality, isomer distributions and a known delivery vector separate them. The figures give that shape and the refuters written in advance; the ingredient names come from measurements, not from the theory.
