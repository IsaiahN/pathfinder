# Expert verification packet: ORI-0022

**What this is:** a claim in a form a prebiotic chemist, meteoriticist or planetary scientist can check. Mode, derivation and refuters are stated before the conclusion, so the reader knows what to look for and what would settle it.

**Mode: `specified` on the shape and the chemistry bindings, `general` on the reachability rule. Not `grounded`.** No provenance measurement is claimed by this agent.

**Status of this packet:** a simulated derivation checked for internal consistency against the figures, not a ground reading. It cannot settle the question it reports on.

## 1. The claim, in one paragraph

"Could this ingredient have formed locally?" and "did it arrive on a meteorite or comet?" are not two boxes that partition the ingredients. They are two independent reachability questions per ingredient slot. Local formation is composition inside a chemical closure; delivery is an import from a frame whose closure differs. A molecule can be both. The output is therefore a per-slot table with three columns: locally reachable, import available, and the observation that would discriminate them. The decisive claim is that the molecule's contents cannot determine its origin; only the provenance record can. Where no discriminator exists, the honest entry is `undetermined`, not a guess.

## 2. Three checkable sub-claims

### C1 - framework-internal; checkable by reading the figures

- **Statement:** local formation and delivery are different operators, and delivery adds no atom to the world. The two are a union, not a partition.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 6 :21 - *"MINT composes inside the closure and can never add an atom."* Fig 6 :14 - *"the only operator that moves the wall"*. Fig 6 :25 - *"An import adds no atom to the world."* Fig 9 :54 - *"And when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again."*
- **Refuter:** show that an extraterrestrial delivery adds a new element to the local frame, or that the two options form a clean partition with no overlap, or that import is not the operator that moves the wall.
- **Mode:** `general`.

### C2 - provenance; checkable by an isotope or chirality specialist

- **Statement:** a locally synthesised molecule and a delivered molecule of the same composition are indiscernible by their contents; only the record separates them. Therefore provenance, not presence or abundance, decides the bucket.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 8 :50 - *"Convergent derivation and adopted import look identical in the contents. Only the record of where each came from separates them"*. Fig 2 :29 - *"An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks."*
- **What to check:** Burton and Berger 2018 (*Life* 8(2):14) write that *"Compound-specific stable isotope measurements, therefore, are a powerful tool for establishing an extraterrestrial origin for meteoritic amino acids,"* and report heavy-isotope enrichment in meteoritic amino acids against biology's light-isotope preference. Oba et al. 2022 (*Nature Communications* 13:2008) use the landing-site soil as a contamination blank and non-biological isomers to argue indigenous origin. Is this a valid discriminator for the slot you know?
- **Refuter:** a local route that produces the same isotope enrichment and chiral signature at the same magnitude, so the record no longer separates the two frames.
- **Mode:** `general` for the structural claim, `specified` for the specific discriminators.

### C3 - chemistry-facing; checkable against the synthesis literature

- **Statement:** amino acids, sugars, nucleobases and fatty acids are locally reachable in laboratory analogues; reactive phosphorus and impact synthesis do not fit either bucket cleanly and must stay compound or undetermined.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 6 :27 - *"Which is why some unreachability is depth and some is genuine absence, and from inside the frame the two look the same."* Fig 12 :47 - *"A recipe that names only its ingredients has recorded half of itself."*
- **What to check:** Pianowski 2026 (*Communications Chemistry* 9:337) reports Miller-Urey/Strecker amino acids, formose and borate-stabilised sugars, HCN and cyanoacetylene routes to nucleobases, and Fischer-Tropsch type lipids, while warning that *"strongly depend on precise regulation and sequential additions of reactants, which might be difficult to achieve under non-laboratory prebiotic conditions."* Galloway et al. 2025 (*Phil. Trans. R. Soc. B* 380:20240288) define the *"P-problem"* and list both meteoritic schreibersite and local lightning/serpentinization as reduced-P sources. Are those two slots genuinely mixed, or does one side dominate at the site you study?
- **Refuter:** a measured local reduced-P flux at the origin site sufficient without meteoritic input (moves P to local), or a demonstration that impact products carry an intact meteoritic signature with no ocean bond formation (moves impact synthesis to delivered).
- **Mode:** `specified`, ungrounded as to the Hadean site.

## 3. What is explicitly not claimed

- Not that meteorites delivered the majority of life's building blocks. That is a flux question.
- Not that any specific molecule must have come from space or must have been made locally.
- Not that laboratory conditions reproduce the Hadean site.
- Not that an ingredient list is a prebiotic pathway. Fig 12 :47 - *"A recipe that names only its ingredients has recorded half of itself."*
- Not `grounded`. No measurement was taken by this agent.
- Not a claim that the figures name the ingredients. Fig 9 :32 - *"you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself."*

## 5. What would settle it

1. Heavy-isotope (13C, D, 15N) measurements at the origin-relevant site outside the terrestrial range.
2. Racemic or non-biologically skewed chirality with a clean landing-site soil blank.
3. Non-biological isomer and methylated-analog distributions.
4. A measured local reduced-P flux at the specific site versus the meteoritic flux.
5. For impact synthesis, whether products carry a meteoritic or a local isotopic signature.

A failure is more useful than an endorsement. If any of the three refuters above fires, the claim is withdrawn or narrowed for that slot.

## 6. What an expert can check in twenty minutes

1. Read Fig 6 :14, :21, :25 and Fig 8 :50, four short passages. Do the two operators differ as claimed?
2. Check C3 against your own field: are the four locally reachable slots actually reachable, and are P and impact synthesis genuinely mixed?
3. Name one measurement that would discriminate local from delivered for the slot you know best, or say why none is possible.
4. Send the refutation. A failure is more useful than an endorsement.
