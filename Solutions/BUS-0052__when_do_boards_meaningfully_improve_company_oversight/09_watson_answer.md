# WATSON answer, BUS-0052: when do boards improve oversight?

**Mode: `specified` on the board application, `general` on the structural shape. Not `grounded`.** Figures are primary and decide. World sources are secondary, corroborate but never overrule, and a conflict returns the derivation to the domain census. This is a simulated derivation checked for internal consistency against the figures, not a ground reading. No board was measured.

## QUESTION

When do boards meaningfully improve company oversight, and when do they mainly ratify executives' decisions? Restated as an effect: for decision slot s, R_s = |Gamma_board(b,a) - o'|, the gap between what the board expects and what the ground returns. "Improve" means the board keeps R_s live and reduces it; "ratify" means the board's reading of o' is management's report, so no residual registers. Option A: treat "good governance" as a property of a board and rank boards by a score. Option B: treat the board as a checking seat and derive the conditions under which it occupies that seat. A produces an average that hides the signal and scores the board with a quantity management produces; B produces a product of four conditions with a per-slot ground and pre-registered refuters. Anchor: the ground, named as an anchor in Fig 2 :29.

## DOMAIN CENSUS

R by arity, symmetry and scale: three-place (board, management, ground), indexed by slot; ordered, not symmetric (the board ranges against the ground, management acts); scale from one decision item to one board cycle to the market ground, with a lossy membrane between board and firm (Fig 4 :20).

Union, not partition: four overlapping conditions form a product. Dependency order: perception (D3) -> vocabulary (D2) -> objective (D1a) -> planning (D1a) -> learn and carry (D4), Fig 3 :20. Strain: the board both appoints the CEO and monitors it; re-census splits D1 into D1a and D1b as different loops with different anchors, so no figure contradiction remains.

- **Human cognition and risk assessment.** Gary Klein, *Premortem method*, gary-klein.com, 2007. Quote: "The team leader will ask his or her team to imagine that the proposed plan has failed." ## VERDICT (from the figures)

The shape survives and the names do not. Boards meaningfully improve oversight when four conditions hold as a product: an unmovable anchor, a closure differing from management, a live channel to the ground, and an office that can refuse. They mainly ratify when any one fails, through a moved anchor, an authored room, a dead channel, or an approval-only vocabulary. The question is a dependency-ordered union, not one lever. Which named board composition wins in a given firm is a declared `gap`. Mode `specified`, ungrounded, candidate. Verdicts are kept per domain and never averaged: D1a, D2, D3 and D4 triangulated; the union single-source; the specific board `gap`.

- **cost if wrong:** a single lever is applied to a union. A board with a dead channel is given more independence training, or a board with no refusal office is given a better dashboard, and the intersections stand. In outreach terms, an expert's twenty minutes are spent refuting a shape that was never the right shape. The cost is real, bounded and mostly reversible, because the product is stated and each failure class is named (Fig 2 :42 "Buy independence in proportion to the cost of being wrong.").
- **undoable:** yes. No term is deleted if one condition fails; a refutation is added and the reach falls because "something was added rather than because anything was deleted" (Fig 6 :42). The four conditions and their refuters are recorded with provenance, so a later reader can locate the error rather than only feel it (Fig 10 :62).
- **reversed by:** a ground reading, not agreement. A per-decision measurement showing that a board with one evidence pool repeatedly catches concealed facts (kills C3), that a CEO-nominated board with the CEO's closure outperforms outside boards on ground-verified events (kills C2), that a board whose approval moves the metric still produces ground-verified oversight (kills C1), or that a board with no refusal office matches one with written dissent (kills C4). A single lever clearing all four conditions with no different residue would kill the union claim. Until one of these fires, the verdict stands as `candidate`.
