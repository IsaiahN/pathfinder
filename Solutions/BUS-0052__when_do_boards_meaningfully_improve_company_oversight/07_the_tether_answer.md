# The Tether's answer: when boards improve oversight

**Mode: `specified` on the board application, `general` on the structural shape. Not `grounded`.** Simulated derivation checked for internal consistency against the figures (`05_what_shape_means.md` §7), not a ground reading.

## 1. The residual it answers

For each decision slot s, R_s = |Gamma_board(b,a) - o'|, the gap between what the board expects a decision to return and what returns. "Improve" means the board keeps R_s live and reduces it. "Ratify" means the board's reading of the outcome is management's report, so no residual registers. This is a union of conditions, not one hard question, and the improve/ratify split is a comparison across that union.

> Fig 1 :19 "The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated."
> Fig 9 :57 "a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either"

## 2. The answer: one checking seat, four conditions, a product

A board meaningfully improves oversight when all four conditions hold:

1. **An anchor it cannot move.** The board ranges management against something that does not change because anyone thinks differently: audited outcomes, regulator findings, realized results. If the board's approval changes the metric it is graded on, the anchor has become a frame. Fig 2 :33 "The anchor must not update." Fig 2 :47 "A ground listed as one of the parties has already become a frame."

2. **A closure that differs from management.** Directors nominated by the person they check are an authored room. Independence is a difference in evidence and vocabulary, not a label. Fig 2 :31 "Two people reading the same report are not two frames; two instruments pointed at the same thing are." Fig 10 :73 "A candidate set chosen by a participant is an authored room however well the selection is reasoned"

3. **A live channel to the ground.** Reports alone are one evidence pool. The board needs direct access to audit, regulators, employees and customers, so a reading can fail to resolve and register as a residual. Fig 10 :24 "the ground does not decay, the channel does" Fig 2 :40 "agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool"

4. **An operator that can refuse.** A board whose vocabulary holds only approvals can accumulate decisions and cannot write that one is wrong. Refusal has to be an office with a written case against material recommendations, and it has to be rotated so it does not become collegial. Operators table :58 "a refutation is a negation, which is why a vocabulary without it can only accumulate"

These four are a product. Any one at zero means the board mainly ratifies, and the failure modes are different:

- Anchor moved: management sets the metric and the board grades against it.
- Closure same: the board and management converge and nobody notices, because the drift is coherent (Fig 2 :34).
- Channel dead: saturated metrics and pooled readings; the internal instruments stay green (Fig 10 :26).
- No refusal: every recommendation is approved; the board is a mirror (Fig 7 :36).

> Fig 5 :41 "Any one of them at zero stops the mint entirely."

The conditions are ordered by dependency: see the ground, have terms for the report, state what counts as failure, act, refresh the room (Fig 3 :20). Effort spent above a broken link cannot rescue it.

## 3. What it explicitly does not say

- Not a percentage. No figure gives an optimum number of independent directors, a committee structure, or a tenure limit. Those are names, and the framework returns a shape.
- Not "independence improves performance." Aggregate firm performance is the wrong ground; it averages away the per-decision signal, and a metric the board helped set is frame-internal (Fig 1 :22, :27). Black and Bhagat's null result and Weisbach's per-event result are not in conflict once the quantity is re-censused.
- Not that every collegial board is captured. The claim is structural: a condition failed, not a person blamed.
- Not that the board as mandate-giver and adviser is illegitimate. That is a different loop (D1b); only the monitoring loop (D1a) is in scope.
- Not `grounded`. No board-level measurement in this folder settles any of this.

## 4. What would settle it: the ground

A source-discriminating instrument, per decision slot, not a better argument. From *The Hard Problem is About Source*: an instrument that reads green "cannot comment on the source"; the spectrometer resolved it. Here the measurements would be:

- **Channel fidelity:** compare what the board was told with what an independent source (forensic audit, regulator finding, customer data, whistleblower report) later returned, per item.
- **Refusal events:** count material recommendations with a written case against them, and whether any was changed or rejected.
- **Anchor test:** did the metric used to grade management change after the board approved it? If yes, the anchor moved.
- **Turnover sensitivity:** the per-event form already measured in the governance literature, the probability of CEO departure given performance, compared across board conditions.

Ground verdicts would also fire the pre-registered refuters in `01` §3. Any one of those, if it holds, withdraws the shape.

## 5. The honest stop

The specific board that would improve a specific firm is a `gap`, not a hidden answer.

> Fig 9 :32 "you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself"
> Fig 13 :134 "Unreachable is a verdict here, not a silence,"

What the derivation can deliver is the shape any candidate board must have and the instruments that would close it. It stops there and records the lineage. Agreement from governance colleagues who share the same evidence pool is one witness, not corroboration (Fig 2 :40). Prestige cannot settle it (see `05` §6).

## 6. One-paragraph version for outreach

> The Tether does not say that a particular board ratio or governance score improves oversight. It says a board is a checking seat, and it checks only when four conditions hold together: it ranges management against an anchor it cannot move, it brings a closure different from management's, it has a channel to the ground that does not run only through management's reports, and it has an office that can refuse a material recommendation. When any one is missing the board mainly ratifies, and the failure is specific: a moved anchor, an authored room, a dead channel, or an approval-only vocabulary. These are not ends of one scale and a single lever clears only one region. Which named board fixes a given firm is left open; the framework returns a shape, not a name. What closes it is a per-decision measurement of channel fidelity and refusal, with the refuters written down first.
