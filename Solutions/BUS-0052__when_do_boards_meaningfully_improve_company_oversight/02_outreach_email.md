**Subject:** One checkable claim about board oversight, before any theory

> Hi [name],
>
> I have one specific output to check against what you already know about boards. I am not asking you to read the framework first, and I am not asking you to agree with it. If the output does not survive, there is nothing else to look at. No reply is needed if you would rather not engage.
>
> **The output (`specified`, not `grounded`).** A board is a checking seat. For each decision, the thing to measure is the gap between what the board expects a decision to return and what actually returns, measured per decision and never averaged into a single governance score. From that, four conditions come out as necessary, and they act as a product:
>
> 1. **An anchor the board cannot move.** The board must range management against something that does not change because management or the board thinks differently: audited outcomes, regulator findings, realized results. A metric the board or management can change by approving it is not an anchor.
> 2. **A closure that differs from management.** Directors nominated by the person they check form an authored room. The outside frame must bring a different vocabulary and a different evidence pool.
> 3. **A live channel to the ground.** Reports alone are one evidence pool; two people reading the same report are not two frames. If every reading comes through management, the board cannot see what the report failed to register.
> 4. **An operator that can refuse.** A board with only approval language can accumulate decisions and cannot write that one is wrong. Refusal is a separate operation, and it has to be installed as an office, not left to personality.
>
> When all four hold, the board reduces the per-decision gap. When any one is zero, the board mainly ratifies. These are not ends of one scale, and a single lever (for example, adding independent directors) clears only the region where independence was the sole missing condition.
>
> **What I am not claiming.** I am not claiming a particular ratio of independent directors, a particular committee structure, or that "board independence" by itself improves performance. Aggregate performance is the wrong measure here: it averages away the per-decision signal, and a board evaluated by a metric it helped produce is scoring itself. I am not claiming that a named director or a named governance score wins. This returns a shape, not a name.
>
> **What would refute it, written before sending this:**
> 1. A board that gets every item through management-filtered reports, with no other channel, repeatedly catches concealed material facts management did not disclose.
> 2. A CEO-nominated board sharing the CEO's evidence pool reduces the per-decision gap at a higher rate than genuinely outside boards, across ground-verified events.
> 3. A board whose approval changes the metric it is judged by still produces ground-verified oversight at the same rate.
> 4. One lever, applied alone, clears all four conditions and leaves no categorically different residue.
>
> A one-line "this fails because..." is more useful to me than agreement. If any of those holds, I withdraw the shape.
>
> **If it survives, and only then:** the sources are below. The figures are the authority, not my summary of them.
> - Canonical figures and formula (the figures are primary, not this summary): https://github.com/IsaiahN/tether/blob/general-agent/figures/README.md
> - The full derivation with the refutation log: `01_figure_grounded_audit.md` in this packet.
> - The verification packet with three checkable sub-claims: `08_expert_verification_packet.md`.
>
> **If you want to reuse the machinery:** the eight-step formula is in the figures link. Run it on a problem you know well and can check against a ground. Write down what would refute your result *before* you run it, and state the mode of what you get. A reported failure is more useful to me than a reported success.

> Thanks for your time,
> [name]
