## Independent domain census, from the residual line alone

I redid the census before relying on the primary's conclusions, using the WATSON order: R by arity, symmetry and scale, then one line per domain with actors, currency and anchor, partition or union, and order by dependency.

- **R (arity / symmetry / scale).** The residual is the unregistered correction per board decision. Arity: three places (board, management, ground) indexed by slot. Symmetry: ordered; the board ranges against the ground and management acts. "Improve" is a threshold comparison, not a symmetric join. Scale: one decision item, one board cycle, the market ground. The per-slot indexing is not optional: a global governance score would average the signal away.
- **Domain 1, the checking seat.** Actors: monitoring directors and committees. Currency: rulings and refusal. Anchor: the unmovable ground. Question type: partition boundary (which readings count as ground rather than as management's report).
- **Domain 2, the frame under check.** Actors: CEO and executives. Currency: decisions and reported results. Anchor: the same ground. Question type: a frame, not a ground.
- **Domain 3, the channel.** Actors: reports, audits, metrics, whistleblowers, regulators, customers. Currency: contact fidelity. Anchor: the ground, which does not decay while the channel does. Question type: the main failure site.
- **Domain 4, the habitat and room pool.** Actors: outside auditors, regulators, analysts, staff, directors' other experience. Currency: independence. Anchor: the ground, never agreement. Question type: enumerated, not composed.
- **Refused: board quality as a standing property.** No nameable anchor; a declared meaning is not a grounded one. Absorbed: rubber stamp as a cause, which is the name of a channel death and a mutual-update collapse.

**Union, not partition:** four overlapping conditions; a product with any factor at zero forcing inertness. Dependency order: see the ground, have terms for the report, state what counts as failure, act, refresh the room. My starting figures were Fig 9 (union/partition) and Fig 2 (the two collapse panels), not the primary's Figure 13 route. Different starting figures, same domain boundaries, low overlap between routes. Still one evidence pool.

## Round trip gap R_T

R_T is gap(x, (T_E o T_A)(x)), the gap between what was sent up and what came back down. Fig 4 :30 requires it to be measured rather than assumed zero. I ran the trip on the condensation the folder actually uses.

1. **Sent up (x):** the figure set, chiefly Fig 2 :33, :31, :40, Fig 10 :24, :42, Fig 9 :55-57, and the Operators table's negation entry.
2. **Abstraction (T_A):** the shorthand "independent, informed boards oversee better", which is what the governance literature and most board reform language actually carry.
3. **Concretisation (T_E):** the four-condition product with per-slot indexing and a named refusal office.

**R_T is nonzero.** Four constraints are dropped on the way back down:

- the anchor non-update condition (a metric the board can move is not an anchor);
- the channel condition (reports alone are one evidence pool);
- the refusal operator (approval language cannot write that a claim is wrong);
- the per-slot indexing (an averaged score hides the live signal).

What survives cleanly is the differing-closure condition and the general idea that a checker must be outside the frame it checks. That is the residue worth sending. R_T was not measured numerically, because no board measurement exists in this folder; it is recorded qualitatively as the set of dropped constraints. Treating "governance score as a proxy for oversight" as identity would set R_T to zero and would be the dead-reckoning error Fig 4 :30 names.
