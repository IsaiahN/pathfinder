## Four findings in the wider literature that bear directly on this question

1. **The independence result is conditional, not universal.** Black and Bhagat find no long-horizon correlation between board independence and firm performance. Weisbach finds that CEO turnover is predicted by performance and board composition together. Hermalin and Weisbach show composition responds to CEO succession and performance. Read together, they say the room is partly authored by the process it checks and that the choice of ground decides what appears to work. This corroborates the re-census in `01` F8 rather than flipping the verdict.

2. **The single most direct governance corroboration of a refusal operator is the Contrarian Director proposal.** The Cambridge account says collegial consensus is propelled by social and psychological incentives and proposes a director with a duty to write the case against each material recommendation. That is the negation operator of Fig 12 / the Operators table, installed as an office rather than a virtue.

3. **The channel failure is named in practitioner literature.** The Effective Governing Boards brief says oversight becomes theoretical when directors lack executives' information. That is Fig 10 :24 in governance language: the ground does not decay, the channel does.

4. **Low-overlap corroboration of the product shape.** Klein's premortem asks a team to imagine the plan has failed and generate reasons, which is a pre-registered refuter in a different field. Janis's groupthink literature names mindguards who screen out disagreement before it reaches the group, which is a channel blocked by the frame being checked. The Simply Psychology page also records that the cohesiveness claim has weak empirical support, so this source is used for the symptom shape only, not for a causal claim about cohesion.
