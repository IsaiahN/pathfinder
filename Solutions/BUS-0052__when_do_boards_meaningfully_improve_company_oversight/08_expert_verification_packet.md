# Expert verification packet: the Tether on board oversight

**What this is:** a claim in a form a governance researcher or board practitioner can check. Mode, derivation and refuters are stated before the conclusion.

**Mode: `specified` on the board application, `general` on the structural shape. Not `grounded`.** No board measurement is claimed. This is a shape and a route, not a result. It is a simulated derivation checked for internal consistency against the figures, not a ground reading.

## 1. The claim, in one paragraph

A board meaningfully improves oversight when four conditions hold as a product: an anchor the board cannot move, a closure that differs from management, a live channel to the ground, and an office that can refuse a material recommendation. When any one fails, the board mainly ratifies, and the failure class is specific (moved anchor, authored room, dead channel, approval-only vocabulary). The conditions are ordered by dependency, the improve/ratify split is a comparison across a union, and the framework does not name a winning board composition. It returns the shape any candidate must have, refuters written in advance, and the per-decision instruments that would close it.

## 2. Three checkable sub-claims

### C1: Framework-internal; checkable by reading Figures 2 and 10

- **Statement:** a board that grades management against a metric management can move has no anchor. A board that holds management's interior cannot verify it. A board that receives every reading through management shares one evidence pool, so its agreement with management carries no information.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 2 :33 "The anchor must not update." Fig 10 :42 "A verifier that can reconstruct the claim cannot verify it." Fig 2 :31 "Two people reading the same report are not two frames; two instruments pointed at the same thing are."
- **Refuter:** a board whose approval changes the metric it is judged by, and which holds the full management interior, still produces ground-verified corrections to management at the same rate as boards with an independent audit channel.

### C2: Governance-facing; checkable against board data

- **Statement:** board independence is one condition, not the answer. Two boards with the same formal ingredients but different bonds (channel to the ground versus channel to management; refusal on material items versus non-material items) are isomers under Fig 12 and behave differently. An ingredient-list score cannot tell them apart.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 12 :117 "isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour." Fig 9 :56 "A single lever clears the region where only one condition holds and leaves the intersections standing, because the second condition still binds."
- **What to check:** is the four-condition product distinguishable in data from an independence-only account? Look for boards with high independence and a dead channel, and boards with low formal independence and a live external channel.
- **Refuter:** a single lever, applied alone, clears all four conditions with no categorically different residue (Fig 9 :57); or two boards with identical formal bonds differ systematically in ground-verified oversight with no further atom.

### C3: Practice-facing; checkable against board process

- **Statement:** refusal must be installed as an office with a written case against material recommendations, and it must be rotated, because a checking frame calibrated against the frame it checks loses independence through use. Without negation, a board can only accumulate approvals.
- **Source, verbatim:** Operators table :58 "a refutation is a negation, which is why a vocabulary without it can only accumulate"; Fig 10 :80 "Frames whose independence is renewed by separate experience can be maintained; frames whose correlation is fixed at origin can only be rotated,"; Fig 12 :119 "catalyst. A part that survives its own use."
- **What to check:** does a named contrarian or written-dissent function change decisions, or only add a document? Based on the Cambridge account of Sweeney's proposal, collegial consensus is propelled by social and psychological incentives and the contrarian role is "currently a dangerous career path".
- **Refuter:** a board with no refusal office at all produces ground-verified corrections to management at the same rate as one with a written-dissent function.

## 3. What is explicitly not claimed

- Not an optimum percentage of independent directors, a tenure limit, or a committee design.
- Not that independence alone improves firm performance. Aggregate performance is the wrong ground; the per-decision residual is the right one (Fig 1 :22).
- Not that all collegial boards are captured. The claim is structural, not a judgement of individuals.
- Not that the board's mandate and advisory loop is illegitimate. Only the monitoring loop is in scope.
- Not `grounded`. Nothing here is asserted as settled by measurement.
- Not a claim that any named mechanism (for example, a Contrarian Director) is the required atom. That is a binding from the governance frame, provenanced as such, and still unproven.

## 5. What would settle it

A per-decision, source-discriminating measurement, not a further argument:

1. **Channel fidelity:** compare the item the board received with what an independent source later returned, slot by slot.
2. **Refusal events:** count material recommendations with a written case against them and record whether any changed.
3. **Anchor test:** did the grading metric change after board approval?
4. **Turnover sensitivity:** the probability of CEO departure given performance, compared across board conditions (the per-event form measured by Weisbach).

## 6. What an expert can check in twenty minutes

1. Read Fig 2 :33, :31 and Fig 10 :42, :24 (four short passages).
2. Decide whether C1 describes a failure mode they have actually seen, and name one where it did not.
3. Name one instrument that would distinguish a live channel from one evidence pool per board item, or say why none is possible.
4. Send the refutation. A failure is more useful than an endorsement.
