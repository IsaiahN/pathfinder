# Structure and order: are retain, modify, and reject one loop or three?

**Mode: `specified`.** This is a simulated derivation checked against the figures, not a ground reading (see `05_what_shape_means.md` §6).

## RESIDUAL

The question names three effects with one verb. Are they three spellings of one operation, three different operations, or three compounds in different domains? And if they are different, what is their dependency order? A related analogue test: does the same loop run in D1 and D2 with only the cast changed?

## DOMAIN CENSUS

Atoms held: one practice P, a slot s, a ground G, a library Γ, a belief b, a weighting w, a seat S (present or absent). Operators held: the seven bonds, plus negation ¬ (which is not a bond, Operators table :70-72). No new primitive is available without import.

## DERIVATION

### Q1 — Are the three effects isomers under Fig 12?

- Fig 12 :117: **"isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."**
- As written, all three effects share the same atoms: P, s, G, Γ, b, w. What differs is the operator applied:
  - **retain**: no operator fires; the term stays composable. Fig 5 :11 *"held: the model was right, nothing owed"*
  - **modify**: either a reweighting of w (Symbols table :25) or a refit, Fig 5 :13 *"mis-attached: refit what you have, do not invent"* or a mint, Fig 5 :14 *"mechanism broken: a new term is owed."*
  - **reject**: a refusal, Fig 5 :53 *"refuted: a term we hold is wrong; a term is owed that says so."*
- So retain and modify are differences of bond and weighting inside the operator set, but reject is a difference of **operator class**: ¬ is unary and is not a bond. Operators table :73: *"Negation is the refusal of a claim."* Operators table :58: *"a refutation is a negation, which is why a vocabulary without it can only accumulate."*
- **Conclusion:** retain and modify are variants within the composition rules; reject is not their mirror image, because it requires an operator the other two do not. Calling the three "isomers" is a useful first approximation but is strictly wrong: Fig 12 :117 is about bonds, and ¬ is outside the bond set.
- **Falsifier, written before the run:** if a vocabulary with no negation can nonetheless drop a practice's standing to be composed over (without merely averaging it away), the operator-class distinction fails and the isomer reading stands.

### Q2 — Does the same loop run in D1 and D2 with only the cast changed?

- Fig 4 :19-20: *"The kernel is the part that does not change with size: the same loop and the same rule for naming what worked, whether one agent is running it or a thousand. Only the actors, the currency and the anchor change."*
- Fig 11 :36: *"Where nothing maintains anything, intelligence is a label applied afterwards to a lineage that met its ground. Nothing was intelligent; selection ran."*
- Fig 11 :58: *"Maintained. A seat exists and can act: choose the instruments, distil the impactful actors, introduce what was not there."*
- *The Recursive Self-Improvement Trap* states the bridge directly: *"Natural selection is what the loop looks like when there is no one maintaining the ground."* And: *"The same code runs when there is a Ground Maintainer. It runs in cultivation, when humans select which plants to breed."*
- **Conclusion:** yes, the loop is the same; what changes is whether a seat is present. This is the kernel claim, triangulated across Fig 4 and Fig 11 (low overlap: membrane vs habitat) plus the essay. Mode `general`.
- **Falsifier:** a population in which a seat demonstrably changes the ground rather than the channel would break the kernel claim (Fig 10 :24 *"the ground does not decay, the channel does"*). Such a case would need a measurement, not an argument.

### Q3 — What is the dependency order?

- Fig 3 :19: *"Optimising a link that already works cannot rescue one that does not."* Fig 3 :25: *"The chain is ordered by dependency, not by importance."*
- The order for this question, from formula step 2 and Fig 5:
  1. **Perceive**: is the practice's residual indexed per slot? Fig 1 :19 *"The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated."*
  2. **Describe the residual in effect terms** before naming a domain or a frame. Fig 9 :27-28 *"describe it before you go looking, or any frame you pick will seem to fit."*
  3. **Sort the failure kind**: held, new, mis-attached, mechanism broken, or refuted (Fig 5 :11-14, :53). This determines which of the three effects is owed.
  4. **Refuse before mint** if the held term is false. Formula step 2 separates REFUTED from the four "what is missing" bins, and "no arrangement of the other four reaches it." The exemplar's QMGR-0037 audit found the same ordering error in a different domain; it is not specific to physics.
  5. **Settle** against the ground. Formula step 5: *"the ground settles it. Verified only by what pays."*
  6. **Carry** the method, not the recording (Fig 4 :22).
- **Why the order matters here:** reformers commonly introduce a replacement practice before withdrawing the old one. Under the figures, if the old term is false, adding the new one does not remove its standing to be composed over; the reach stays built on a refuted term. Fig 6 :42 *"What is reachable is derived, and it can fall."* The refusal is owed first.
- **Falsifier:** a case where modifying a practice first demonstrably improved ground contact more than refusing it first would reverse steps 3 to 4. That is a decisionable empirical claim in D2, not a matter of taste.

### Q4 — What analogue actually carries?

Two analogues are offered and one is declined, following the exemplar's discipline of naming near-misses.

- **Accepted, biological.** The unmaintained domain is natural selection with no seat. *The Recursive Self-Improvement Trap*: *"Natural selection is what the loop looks like when there is no one maintaining the ground."* Fig 11 :5 *"survival of the fittest"* This is an instance, not a borrowed mechanism.
- **Accepted, horticultural.** The maintained domain is cultivation. *How Intelligence Actually Works* describes *"Cultivation or Breeding is unnatural selection by another name."* Fig 11 :58 calls it *"the garden."* An instance again.
- **Declined, simple memetics.** "A practice is a replicator that copies with variation and selection" is the tempting mapping. It fails on provenance and on the seat: it cannot distinguish a head start from a recording (Fig 4 :23-24), and it has no place for the convention domain where agreement is the product. The article *Arrival of the Fittest* records a section titled *"Three mappings offered and declined"* as precedent for exactly this refusal.

## CONCLUSION

The three effects are not one operation. Retain and modify are internal to the composition rules, while reject requires the unary operator ¬ and is therefore a different class of move, not a symmetric opposite. The same loop runs in D1 and D2 with only the seat changed, and the dependency order requires describing the residual, sorting its kind, and refusing before minting when a held term is false. The biological and horticultural analogues carry; the simple memetic analogue does not.
