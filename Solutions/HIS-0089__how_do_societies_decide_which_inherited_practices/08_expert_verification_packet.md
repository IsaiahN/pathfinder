# Expert verification packet: the Tether on inherited practices

**What this is:** a claim in a form a researcher in cultural transmission can check. Mode, derivation and refuters are stated before the conclusion, so a reader knows what to look for and what would settle it.

**Mode: `general` on the shape, `specified` on the per-domain verdicts, not `grounded`.** No measurement of a society is claimed. This is a shape and a route, not a result.

## 1. The claim, in one paragraph

The question "how do societies decide which inherited practices to retain, modify, or reject?" is a union of three different conditions, not one decision procedure. Where nobody maintains anything, nobody decides: selection ran, and what survives is what the ground kept. Where a seat exists, reform is asymmetric permanent work: a practice can be introduced, the habitat cannot be subtracted, and rejection is a refusal added to the record rather than an erasure. Where a practice is a pure convention, agreement about a shared meaning is the product, not a corruption, until the convention starts deciding an outcome and owes an answer to a ground. Across all three, a transmitted practice is a head start or a recording, handed-down agreement adds no evidence, and selection needs variants that do not share one evidence pool. The framework returns this shape, not the name of a practice.

## 2. Three checkable sub-claims

### C1 — framework-internal; checkable by reading Figures 11 and 7

- **Statement:** in the unmaintained domain there is no decider; retention is differential persistence against a ground, and handed-down agreement is not evidence.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 11 :36 *"Where nothing maintains anything, intelligence is a label applied afterwards to a lineage that met its ground. Nothing was intelligent; selection ran."* Fig 11 :56 *"Unmaintained. No maintainer and no live frame in the seat. The habitat is pure contact, nothing selects, and what survives is what the ground kept."* Fig 7 :13 *"agreement compounds, evidence does not"*
- **What to check:** does the figure really make "selection ran" a claim about the unmaintained mode, and does it distinguish that mode from the maintained one? Is the shared-evidence-pool disqualification (Fig 2 :40) about verdicts rather than conventions?
- **Refuter:** a practice retained or rejected with no differential contact with a ground and no role for drift; or handed-down agreement shown to be independent re-derivations against separate grounds, so Fig 2 :40 does not apply.

### C2 — world-facing; checkable by a cultural transmission researcher against the literature

- **Statement:** high transmission fidelity does not discriminate a method from a recording, and "modify" is not one mechanism but three (reweighting w, refitting, minting), with different costs.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 4 :11 *"a head start, never a recording"* and Fig 4 :22 *"Only methods cross up. Only head starts come down."* Symbols table :25 *"How far the belief leans on private history versus inherited prior."* Fig 5 :13 *"mis-attached: refit what you have, do not invent"* and Fig 5 :14 *"mechanism broken: a new term is owed."*
- **What to check:** do the over-imitation results (Whiten et al. 2009) and the cultural intelligence account (van Schaik & Burkart 2011) actually show that fidelity can preserve a causally irrelevant action, and that social learning is not always adaptive? Do Richerson, Boyd and Henrich (2010) name "decision-making forces" and drift as distinct mechanisms?
- **Refuter:** an experiment in which high-fidelity copying of a causally opaque action improves the copyer's later novel problem solving, showing the copy carried a method; or a case of modification with no change in w, no refit and no new term (which would add a fourth mechanism).

### C3 — framework-internal derivation

- **Statement:** rejection is a refusal added to the record, and it requires the unary operator ¬, so it is not the mirror image of retention; the dependency order requires refusing before minting when a held term is false.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 5 :55 *"What it removes is not a term but a term's standing to be composed over. Nothing leaves the library. What falls is the reach."* Fig 6 :42 *"A refutation adds a term that refuses; it removes nothing, and the reach falls because something was added rather than because anything was deleted."* Operators table :58 *"a refutation is a negation, which is why a vocabulary without it can only accumulate."*
- **What to check:** is ¬ genuinely outside the seven bonds (Operators table :70-72), and does the formula's ROUTE step put REFUTED in a separate bin from the four "missing" kinds?
- **Refuter:** a vocabulary with no negation that can nonetheless remove a practice's standing to be composed over; or a case where modifying a practice first demonstrably improved ground contact more than refusing it first. This second one is empirical and decisionable in D2.

## 3. What is explicitly not claimed

- Not one decision rule covering all three domains.
- Not a derivation of which practice a given society retained or rejected. The framework returns a shape, not a name (Fig 9 :32).
- Not a claim that a ban erases a practice. The record never deletes; what falls is the reach.
- Not a claim that agreement is disqualified for conventions. Fig 2 :41 makes it the product.
- Not a claim that handed-down practices are useless. Transmitted as a method, a practice is a head start.
- Not `grounded`. Nothing here was settled by measuring a society.

## 5. What would settle it

A ground reading, not more agreement.

1. **D1:** a transmission-chain design that separates selection, drift, and reweighting of w in the retention of a practice.
2. **D2:** a reform case comparing "refuse first" with "mint first," measuring whether the reach built on the old term falls when the refusal is added. First-hand outcomes are the votes; committee agreement is not.
3. **D3:** observe a convention at the moment it starts deciding an outcome, and check whether it can remain legitimate without ground contact.
4. **Cross-cutting:** an instrument that discriminates a method from a recording at the point of transmission.

## 6. What an expert can check in twenty minutes

1. Read Fig 11 :36 and :56, Fig 2 :40-41, and Fig 4 :11 and :22, six short passages.
2. Check C2 against your own knowledge of the field: do the cited studies support the fidelity and mechanism claims, or do they show something different?
3. Name one instrument that would distinguish a head start from a recording in a transmission chain, or say why none is possible.
4. Send the refutation. A failure is more useful than an endorsement.
