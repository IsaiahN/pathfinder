# Outreach draft: check the output before you decide about the theory

**Use:** one-to-one, or as a cover letter attached to a submission through the public venues in `03_public_contact_points.csv`. **Not** for bulk send.

**Hard precondition:** do not send until the shape claim and its refuter in `01_figure_grounded_audit.md` §3 are stated on the page in your own words. The output must survive checking or the two-stage offer collapses on contact.

---

**Subject:** One checkable claim about inherited practices, before any theory

> Hi [name],
>
> I have one specific output I would like you to check against what you already know. I am not asking you to read a theory first, and I am not asking you to agree with it. If the output does not survive, there is nothing else to look at.
>
> **The output (`general` on the shape, `specified` on the branches, not `grounded`):** when a society transmits a practice, there are three different effects that all get called "deciding," and they do not share a decision rule.
>
> 1. Where nobody maintains anything, nobody decides. The outcomes are what the ground kept. The word "decide" is a label applied afterwards to a lineage that met its ground.
> 2. Where there is a seat that can act, reform is asymmetric permanent work: you can introduce a practice, you cannot subtract the habitat that produced it, and what falls when a practice is rejected falls because a refusal was added.
> 3. Where a practice is a pure convention, agreement about a shared meaning is the product, not a corruption. But a convention that decides an outcome has stopped being a convention and owes an answer to a ground.
>
> The rule I would like checked is the boundary between 2 and 3, because it determines whether agreement is evidence or is the thing being produced.
>
> **What I am not claiming:** that the framework tells us which practice a particular society will keep. It returns a shape and a set of refuters, not a name. I am not claiming that handed-down agreement is evidence; it is agreement. I am not claiming that a ban erases a practice; in the framework's record nothing is ever deleted.
>
> **What would refute the shape claim:**
> 1. a practice retained or rejected with no differential contact with a ground and no role for drift;
> 2. handed-down agreement shown to be independent re-derivations against separate grounds;
> 3. a society that genuinely deletes a practice from its record with no refusal added;
> 4. a convention that decides an outcome yet stays legitimate with no ground contact;
> 5. a closed cultural frame that generates a genuinely new atom from its own closure.
>
> I wrote these down before sending this. If any one holds, I will retract the corresponding branch. A one-line "this fails because..." is more useful to me than agreement. If you would rather not engage, no reply is needed.
>
> **If it survives, and only then:** the sources are below. The figures are the authority, not my summary of them.
> - Verbatim figure text with line numbers (local extract): `FRAMEWORK/reference/03-FIGURES_TEXT.md`
> - Cultural Evolution Society, "What is cultural evolution?": https://culturalevolutionsociety.org/about-cultural-evolution/what-is-cultural-evolution/
> - Richerson, Boyd and Henrich, "Gene-culture coevolution in the age of genomics" (PNAS, 2010): https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC3024025/
> - Whiten et al., "Emulation, imitation, over-imitation and the scope of culture for child and chimpanzee" (Phil Trans R Soc B, 2009): https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC2865074/
>
> **If you want to use the machinery:** the eight-step formula is in the framework. Run it on a problem you know well and can check against a ground. Write down what would refute your result *before* you run it, because a loop cannot check the direction of its own question. A reported failure is more useful to me than a reported success.
