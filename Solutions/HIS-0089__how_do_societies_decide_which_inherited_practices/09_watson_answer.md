## QUESTION

Decide, in effect terms, what the Tether says about the claim that societies decide which inherited practices to retain, modify, or reject. Option A: treat this as one decision procedure and ask which rule a society uses. Option B: unbundle it into three domains with different anchors and keep their verdicts separate. What each leads to: A averages across practices and applies the agreement rule to conventions where agreement is the product; B sends a per-slot shape, separate verdicts, and refuters that can be checked domain by domain. All claims below are `general` or `specified`, none `grounded`.

## DOMAIN CENSUS

R by arity, symmetry and scale, then one line per domain with actors / currency / anchor; partition or union; order by dependency.

- **R (the residual).** For each inherited practice, the gap between what the inherited repertoire predicts and what the ground returns. Arity: one slot per practice, so a vector; Fig 1 :19 "The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated." Symmetry: none; a practice's valence is relative to the residual (Fig 11 :49). Scale: one carrier to a population across generations, with the Fig 4 membrane where scale enters.
- **Domain 1, unmaintained transmission.** Actors: practices as lineages, individual carriers, environment. Currency: persistence. Anchor: the ground (material and social consequences). Question type: survival, not verdict.
- **Domain 2, maintained reform.** Actors: a seat (institution, council, editor, reformer), instruments, practices. Currency: residuals, verified terms, head starts. Anchor: the ground still decides. Question type: deliberate selection, asymmetric work.
- **Domain 3, convention and coordination.** Actors: members, shared meaning. Currency: agreement about shared meaning. Anchor: constitutive and internal to the society, not an external world ground. Question type: agreement is the product, not a verdict.
- **Partition or union.** Union compound. A practice is usually in two or three domains at once, so unbundle (Fig 9 :54). The verdicts are never averaged.
- **Order by dependency.** Perceive the per-slot residual, describe it in effect terms, sort its kind, refuse before mint when a held term is false, settle against the ground, carry the method not the recording. The middle link (vocabulary and objective) is where such chains break (Fig 3 :27).

Strain test: the apparent conflict between Fig 2 :40 (agreement disqualifies) and Fig 2 :41 (agreement is the product) was resolved by re-census. The first governs verdicts about the world, the second governs conventions. Different objects, so no strain after re-census. The remaining strains (cultural drift in D1, observable deletion versus the non-deleting record, precision of w as a measured quantity) are named in `01_figure_grounded_audit.md` §5, not hidden.

## VERDICT (from the figures)

The question does not have one answer; it has three, kept separate. In D1 nobody decides and selection ran against a ground. In D2 a seat does asymmetric permanent work: introduction is available, exclusion is not, and rejection is a refusal added to the record rather than a deletion. In D3 agreement is the product until a convention decides an outcome and becomes a verdict. Across all three, transmission carries a head start or a recording, agreement is not evidence against a shared pool, and selection requires low-overlap variation. The framework returns this shape and its refuters, not the identity of any practice. This is a `general`/`specified`, ungrounded, candidate result. The single most important correction is that "decide" is only literally true in one of the three domains.

- **cost if wrong:** bounded and locatable. The verdicts are mode-labelled, state refuters before the conclusion, and keep the domains separate, so an error is findable rather than invisible. The real cost is applying the verdict rule to conventions, where agreement is the product (Fig 2 :41), or averaging three domains into one and losing the case that sits in two conditions at once. That cost is avoidable by unbundling before searching (Fig 9 :54). A secondary cost is spending an outside reader's independence by sending a recording rather than a generator; Fig 2 :42 prices it: "Buy independence in proportion to the cost of being wrong."
- **undoable:** yes. The wrongly-scoped rule is removable without touching the derivation: reassign the case to its proper domain, apply that domain's anchor, and re-run the refuter. `07_the_tether_answer.md` and `08_expert_verification_packet.md` already do this. No primitive has to be withdrawn, and a sent claim can be superseded by a corrected packet.
- **reversed by:** a ground reading, not agreement. Six pre-registered conditions in `01_figure_grounded_audit.md` §3 would each retract a branch: retention with no ground contact and no drift; independent re-derivations behind handed-down agreement; genuine deletion with no refusal; a convention that decides an outcome without a ground; a closed frame generating a new atom; or the three domains proved to be a partition. Until one fires, the verdict stands as `candidate`. Fig 2 :45: "Frames range against the ground. They do not negotiate with it."
