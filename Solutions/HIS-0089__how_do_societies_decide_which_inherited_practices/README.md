## Boundary and standing

Nothing outside this folder was modified. Figure quotes are taken verbatim from `FRAMEWORK/reference/03-FIGURES_TEXT.md` and cited as `Fig N :line`. World sources are real URLs opened during this derivation and recorded in `10_source_ledger.csv`.

**This is a simulated derivation checked for internal consistency against the figures, not a ground reading.** The framework's own rule applies here: LLM output is *"simulacra"* and *"the truly testable output would need to be derived from the real architecture or working the formula yourself"* (*The Recursive Self-Improvement Trap*). This package cannot settle the question it reports on. It can only state the shape any answer must have, keep the verdicts per domain separate, and name what would settle each one.

## What is deliberately not here

- No personal emails of named individuals.
- No ranking of people by how likely they are to agree with the answer.
- No bulk-send plan.

Three reasons, in the figures' own words:

1. **Agreement is not a verdict across an evidence pool.** Fig 2 :40: *"disqualifying for a verdict: agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool."* The cultural transmission literature is one shared pool on this question, so a list selected for agreement would manufacture the very correlation that makes agreement worthless.
2. **A mirror cannot correct you.** Fig 2 :35: *"Alignment is triangulation, not negotiation. A mirror cannot correct you."*
3. **A verifier must not hold the interiors.** Fig 10 :52: *"Bring a characterised residual, never the source."* The first contact carries the residual and its refuter, never the framework.

## Suggested next step

Take `01` and `08` to the public venues in `03`. The audience that can help is the one that has opted in to receiving claims and is positioned to refute them, not a purchased list of believers. A failure is more useful than an endorsement.
