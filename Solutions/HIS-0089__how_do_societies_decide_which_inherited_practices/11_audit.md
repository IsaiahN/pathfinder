## Independent domain census

I redid the census before comparing with the primary's conclusions, using the WATSON order: R by arity, symmetry and scale; one line per domain with actors / currency / anchor; partition or union; order by dependency.

- **R (arity / symmetry / scale).** The residual is, for each inherited practice, the gap between the inherited repertoire's prediction and what the ground returns. Arity: N slots, so a vector; aggregating into "what the society decided" is the Fig 1 error. Symmetry: none, because valence is relative to the residual. Scale: one carrier to a population across generations; Fig 4 is where scale enters.
- **Domain 1, unmaintained transmission.** Actors: practice lineages, carriers, environment. Currency: persistence. Anchor: material and social consequences. Question type: survival, not verdict. Nameable anchor: yes.
- **Domain 2, maintained reform.** Actors: a seat, instruments, practices. Currency: residuals, head starts. Anchor: the ground, still. Question type: deliberate selection under asymmetric power. Nameable anchor: yes.
- **Domain 3, convention.** Actors: members, shared meaning. Currency: agreement about shared meaning. Anchor: constitutive and internal. Question type: agreement is the product. Nameable anchor: yes, though not external.
- **Union, not partition.** A practice can be in two or three domains at once. Fig 9 :57: *"a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either."* Unbundle before searching.
- **Order by dependency.** Describe the residual in effect terms (Fig 9 :27-28), sort its kind (Fig 5), refuse before mint when a held term is false (Formula step 2), settle against the ground (Formula step 5), carry the method (Fig 4 :22).

My census matches the primary's three domains and its union reading. Where the primary derived from Fig 11's two modes and Fig 2's convention clause, I derived from Fig 1's per-slot residual and Fig 9's union/partition split. Different starting figures, same domain boundaries: low overlap between routes, so the agreement is weak but not circular within the framework. It is still one evidence pool.

## Round trip gap R_T

The Symbols table defines R_T as `gap(x, (T_E ∘ T_A)(x))`, measuring what the coarser description cannot hold. Fig 4 :30 requires it to be measured rather than assumed. I ran the round trip on the condensation the folder actually uses.

1. **Sent up (x):** the verbatim figure set, chiefly Fig 11 :36 and :56 (no decider in the unmaintained mode), Fig 11 :61 (asymmetric seat), Fig 2 :40-41 (verdict vs convention), Fig 5 :55 and Fig 6 :42-43 (refusal, not deletion), Fig 4 :22 (head start).
2. **Abstraction (T_A):** the shorthand carried in `07_the_tether_answer.md`: three domains, one loop, three effects kept separate, agreement is the product in D3 only.
3. **Concretisation (T_E):** a concrete case, for example a society deciding whether to keep a ritual food taboo.

**R_T is nonzero.** Three things the shorthand holds do not survive the trip back down:

- which domain the taboo belongs to is not decided by the shorthand; it can be D1 (material consequence), D2 (a maintained rule) and D3 (identity marker) at once, so the case must be unbundled before a lever is applied;
- the operator class of "reject" (¬ is not a bond) disappears if the shorthand is read as three symmetric options;
- the refusal-before-mint order is lost if the reader imagines a replacement being introduced before the old term is refused.

Conversely, one thing survives cleanly: in the unmaintained case nobody decides, and in the maintained case introduction is available while exclusion is not. That is the residue worth sending. R_T was **not** measured numerically, because no ground reading exists; it is recorded qualitatively as the enumerated set of dropped constraints. Treating "the formula is the condensed version of the figures" as identity would set R_T to zero and would be the dead-reckoning error Fig 4 :30 names.
