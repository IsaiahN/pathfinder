## 0. The residual in effect terms

The question asks for a decision procedure. The figures require that a residual be stated as an effect before any figure line is chosen.

> A society holds a library Γ of inherited practices (techniques, norms, rituals, taboos, styles, rules). At any moment, for each practice, there is a gap R between what the inherited repertoire predicts ("this practice works, is proper, is right") and what the ground actually returns (survival, coordination, material and social consequences). The question asks what produces three different effects: **retain** (the practice stays composable), **modify** (the weighting of inherited prior against private experience shifts, or the practice is refitted), **reject** (the practice loses its standing to be composed over).

Bound the sought-for shape as an ordinary term before searching, per Formula step "Direction": *"make the sought-for shape an ORDINARY TERM - write down what an answer would look like, bind it, and let it accrue a residual like anything else."* The shape bound here is:

> **An answer is a rule that maps a per-practice residual to one of three effects, states which domain the rule lives in, and names the ground that settles it. A candidate answer that does not name the ground or that averages across practices is not an answer.**

R is indexed per practice, not per society and not per generation. Fig 1 :19: *"The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated."* Fig 1 :28: *"The gap is measured per object, not per step. A system that averages across objects has thrown away the only signal that says where to look."* Asking "what does this society decide?" already averages; the figure-correct form is "what happens to this practice, in this slot, against this ground?"

**Arity.** N slots, one per inherited practice; the decision is a vector, not a scalar. **Symmetry.** None: a practice has no fixed valence. Fig 11 :49: *"An actor's valence is relative to the residual, not a property it carries."* The same practice (a dietary rule, a kinship norm) is a benefactor to one residual and a malfactor to another. **Scale.** One carrier to a population across generations; the membrane at Fig 4 is where scale enters. Fig 4 :19-20: *"The kernel is the part that does not change with size: the same loop and the same rule for naming what worked, whether one agent is running it or a thousand. Only the actors, the currency and the anchor change."*

## 2. Findings

### F1 — In D1, nobody decides. Selection ran (decisive against the question's framing)

Fig 11 :36:

> **"Where nothing maintains anything, intelligence is a label applied afterwards to a lineage that met its ground. Nothing was intelligent; selection ran. The word does no work there."**

Fig 11 :56:

> **"Unmaintained. No maintainer and no live frame in the seat. The habitat is pure contact, nothing selects, and what survives is what the ground kept."**

The word "decide" in the question is a label applied after the fact to a lineage that met its ground. In the unmaintained domain there is no decider, only differential persistence. Retention here is not a verdict; it is what the ground kept. Triangulated across Fig 11 and Fig 7 :33 *"→ variation inside, selection between"* and Fig 7 :35 *"A room's agreement is variation, not a verdict. Selection between rooms is where the ground votes."* The two figures are different in construction (habitat vs room chain), so the overlap is low, but both are framework figures; see the internal-consistency caveat in §6. Mode: `general`.

Refuter: a case where a practice persists with no differential contact with a ground (pure drift in a sealed population) would show that "what the ground kept" is not the whole story in D1. Cultural drift is a real candidate; world sources below name it. If drift alone can retain a practice indefinitely, F1 needs narrowing to "where the ground bites."

### F2 — Handed-down agreement is not evidence for a practice

Fig 7 :13:

> **"agreement compounds, evidence does not"**

Fig 2 :40:

> **"disqualifying for a verdict: agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool"**

A practice that has been transmitted for fifty generations has more agreement behind it, not more evidence. Cultural transmission is the paradigm shared evidence pool: each generation learns from the previous one, so the "agreement" is the pool talking. Fig 2 :42: *"Buy independence in proportion to the cost of being wrong."* This is why "we have always done it this way" is not a reason to retain, and "everyone does it" is not a reason to keep or to drop. Triangulated (Fig 7 + Fig 2). Mode: `general`.

Refuter: if a tradition's agreement were shown to be composed of independent re-derivations against separate grounds, Fig 2 :40 would not apply and the agreement would carry information. Fig 8 :50: *"Two frames can hold the same atom by convergence, reaching it separately, which is corroboration at maximum independence."* That is an empirical question about the transmission chain, not a figure question.

### F3 — Rejection is a refusal added to the record, not a deletion

Fig 6 :42:

> **"What is recorded only grows. What is reachable is derived, and it can fall."**

Fig 6 :42-43:

> **"A refutation adds a term that refuses; it removes nothing, and the reach falls because something was added rather than because anything was deleted."**

Fig 5 :55:

> **"What it removes is not a term but a term's standing to be composed over. Nothing leaves the library. What falls is the reach."**

Fig 5 :53 names the branch:

> **"refuted: a term we hold is wrong; a term is owed that says so"**

So "reject" is not the mirror image of "retain." The practice stays in the record; what changes is its standing to be composed over, and the reach falls because something was added. This matters sociologically: a society that bans a practice has not erased it, it has added a refusal and changed what can be built on top of it. The Operators table :58: *"a refutation is a negation, which is why a vocabulary without it can only accumulate."* A culture with no operator of refusal can only add and refit; it cannot genuinely reject. Triangulated (Fig 6 + Fig 5 + Operators table). Mode: `general`.

Refuter: a case where a practice disappears from the record without any refusal term (pure extinction of the last carrier) would show that cultural rejection sometimes is deletion in fact, even if the framework's record never deletes. Record the distinction rather than smoothing it; this is a strain, named in §6.

### F4 — Modification is two different mechanisms, and only one is cheap

Symbols table :20-21:

> **"Formed from its own history and from what it inherited, mixed by w. Not the observation."**

Symbols table :25-26:

> **"How far the belief leans on private history versus inherited prior."**

and *"learnable from the outcomes of past weightings."*

So "modify" splits into:

1. **Reweighting w.** Shift the mix between inherited prior and private experience. Continuous, cheap, learnable from outcomes. This is what happens when a practitioner adjusts a inherited technique after direct contact with a changed environment.
2. **Minting a new term.** When the gap is a broken mechanism rather than a weighting error, Fig 5 :14 *"mechanism broken: a new term is owed"* applies. This is gated. Fig 5 :42: *"The acceptance test is a bargain, not a threshold."* A new term must pay: *"A new term is worth having when stating it, plus what remains unexplained after it, costs less than what was unexplained before"* (Fig 5 :43-44, across the line wrap).

Most reform talk conflates these. Changing how much weight a society gives to its ancestors (w) is not the same as inventing a new practice. Triangulated (Symbols table + Fig 5). Mode: `specified`, because the mechanism is described but its value in any given society is unmeasured.

Refuter: a case of modification with no change in w and no new term (pure mis-attachment, Fig 5 :13 *"mis-attached: refit what you have, do not invent"*) would show a third mechanism. The figure admits it, so this is not a refutation of F4 but a required third branch; recorded as a correction in §5.

### F5 — What crosses generations is a head start, not a recording

Fig 4 :11:

> **"a head start, never a recording"**

Fig 4 :22:

> **"Only methods cross up. Only head starts come down."**

Fig 4 :23-24:

> **"upward: a method that can be reapplied. Never the recording of a particular success."**
> **"downward: something that makes the next search cheaper. Never a replay of what worked once."**

Fig 4 :25:

> **"A recording carried upward looks like knowledge and is a description of one occasion."**

A practice transmitted as a blind replay is a recording; a practice transmitted as a method that makes the next search cheaper is a head start. The same observable behavior can be either. This is the figure-grounded reason that transmission fidelity alone cannot tell you whether a society retained a practice well or badly: high fidelity can preserve a recording that "produces a system that repeats a past success and cannot produce a new one" (Fig 4 :26). Triangulated (Fig 4 + Fig 7 :12-13 *"same closure, handed along; agreement compounds, evidence does not"*). Mode: `specified`.

Refuter: an experiment showing that high-fidelity copying of a causally opaque action nonetheless improves the copyer's later novel problem solving would show the copy carried a method, not a recording. This is decisionable at the ground with a transmission-chain design.

### F6 — In D2, reform is asymmetric permanent work, not a solved state

Fig 11 :61:

> **"The seat's power is asymmetric. Introduction is available; exclusion is not."**

Fig 11 :63:

> **"Which is why a managed ecosystem is permanent work rather than a solved state."**

The seat can add a practice (innovation, import, reform) but cannot subtract the habitat, *"because contact is a fact about the world and does not consult you"* (Fig 11 :61-62). A ban, a reform, or a curriculum change does not remove the conditions that produced the practice; it adds a refusal that must be maintained. This is why "we abolished it" is never a terminal state. Single-source on the asymmetry rule (Fig 11); supported by Fig 6 :42 (reach falls by addition) and Fig 10 :24 *"the ground does not decay, the channel does"* for the maintenance requirement. Mode: `general`.

Refuter: a case where removing an actor from a habitat genuinely removed a causal relation (rather than substituting a habitat) would qualify the asymmetry. Fig 11 :66 says *"Isolation is not removal of the habitat. It is substitution of one habitat for another."* The framework predicts the qualification will fail at the ground; the experiment is cheap and worth running.

### F7 — D3 is the one domain where agreement is the product, not the corruption

Fig 2 :41:

> **"efficient for a convention: agreement about a shared meaning is the product, not a corruption of it"**

Fig 10 :46:

> **"A convention makes no claim about the world; a verdict and an atom both do."**

Fig 10 :47:

> **"a convention that decides an outcome has stopped being one"**

This is the boundary the question most needs. For greetings, dress, etiquette, and other pure conventions, there is no external ground and the decision *is* agreement. The framework's anti-agreement rules (Fig 2 :40) do not apply, because no claim about the world is being made. But the moment a convention starts deciding an outcome (allocating resources, determining truth, excluding persons), it has become a verdict and must answer to a ground. Fig 2 :43: *"Guard, from Figure 10: a convention that decides an outcome has stopped being one."* Triangulated (Fig 2 + Fig 10). Mode: `general`.

Refuter: a convention that demonstrably settles an outcome yet remains legitimate without ground contact would break the guard. The framework would call it a verdict wearing a convention's clothes; an ethnography that disagrees is a genuine conflict and sends us back to the census, not to a new verdict.

### F8 — Selection needs independent variation; variation inside one frame is just agreement

Fig 8 :32:

> **"Closure is idempotent. Composing inside a closed set never leaves it, so no search, however long, adds an atom."**

Fig 8 :29:

> **"low overlap: B does not draw on A's evidence"**

Fig 7 :35 (above): *"A room's agreement is variation, not a verdict. Selection between rooms is where the ground votes."*

A society with a single cultural frame cannot genuinely select among practices; it can only rehearse them. What selects is a population of low-overlap variants: dissenters, outsiders, independent experiments, adjacent communities. Fig 2 :42: *"Buy independence in proportion to the cost of being wrong."* This is the figure-grounded reason cultural contact and migration matter in the history of knowledge, and why a closed tradition can refine a practice for centuries without discovering that its ground moved. Triangulated (Fig 8 + Fig 7 + Fig 2). Mode: `general`.

Refuter: a closed frame that generates a genuinely new atom from its own closure would refute idempotence. Fig 8 :39 admits the limit case: *"a search that finds nothing is never a proof of absence."* The refuter is therefore a positive demonstration, not a failed search.

## 3. Pre-registered refuter

Required before any outreach. The framework's own reason: a falsifier written before the run is a design instrument.

The shape claim is refuted if any of the following holds:

1. A practice is retained or rejected with no differential contact with a ground and no role for drift, that is, the outcome is uncoupled from consequences in a way neither selection nor Fig 11's unmaintained mode predicts.
2. Handed-down agreement is shown to be composed of independent re-derivations against separate grounds, so Fig 2 :40's shared-pool disqualification does not apply to it.
3. A society genuinely deletes a practice from its record with no refusal term, so Fig 6 :42's monotone record fails for cultural transmission.
4. A convention that decides an outcome is shown to remain legitimate with no ground contact, so Fig 10 :47's guard fails.
5. A closed frame generates a new atom from its own closure, refuting Fig 8 :32's idempotence.
6. The three domains are shown to be a partition with no overlapping cases, so the union unbundling at Fig 9 :54 is unnecessary.

If any fires, the relevant finding is withdrawn and the census is redone. Until one is run, no part of this may be labelled `grounded`.

## 4. Recommendation

1. Lead with F1. The question's own verb ("decide") is the thing most in need of correction, and the correction is figure-derived, not rhetorical.
2. Keep the three verdicts separate and never average them. F7 in particular forbids applying the anti-agreement rule to conventions.
3. For D2 and D1, ask what the ground is for the practice in question before asking what the society decided. If no ground can be named, the case is D3 and belongs to the convention rule.
4. Write down the sought-for shape as an ordinary term before looking for a society to test it on (Formula step "Direction"), so the choice of case cannot manufacture the fit. Fig 9 :27-28: *"describe it before you go looking, or any frame you pick will seem to fit."*
5. Send the generator (the residual, the three domains, and the refuter), not the conclusion. Fig 4 :25: *"A recording carried upward looks like knowledge and is a description of one occasion."*

### Strains sent to the log, not hidden

1. **Rejection as deletion.** The framework's record never deletes; observable cultural loss looks like deletion. Whether this is two scopes (record vs world) or a real limit of the record for cultural transmission is not decidable here. Recorded, not smoothed.
2. **Drift in D1.** Fig 11 says what the ground kept. Cultural drift, a real mechanism in the world sources, can retain a variant without ground contact. The framework has no separate drift operator at this level; the refuter F1.1 covers it.
3. **Precision of "w is learnable."** The Symbols table says w is learnable from outcomes but gives no instrument for measuring it in a society. `specified`, not `grounded`.

### Updated verdict

The shape survives: the question un-bundles into three domains with different anchors and different decision rules, and "decide" is only literally true in one of them. What does not survive is any single mechanism, any averaged verdict, and any claim to name which practice a particular society will keep. Strains after re-census: three, named above, none hidden.
