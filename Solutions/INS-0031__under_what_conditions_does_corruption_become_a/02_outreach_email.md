# Outreach draft: check one claim before deciding whether the framework is worth reading

**Use:** one-to-one, or as a cover letter attached to a submission through the public venues in `03_public_contact_points.csv`. **Not** for bulk send.

**Hard precondition:** do not send until `01_figure_grounded_audit.md` §7 has been read and the two apparent strains recorded there are understood. The stage-1 claim must survive on its own, without the theory behind it.

---

**Subject:** One checkable claim about corruption as governance, and what would refute it

> Hi [name ],
>
> I have one specific claim I would like you to check against what you already know. I am not asking you to read the framework first, and I am not asking you to agree. If the claim does not survive, there is nothing else to look at.
>
> **The claim (`specified`, not `grounded`):** corruption stops being isolated misconduct and becomes a system of governance at a specific transition. An informal practice (a fixed fee, a fixed recipient list, a routine favour) starts as a convention alongside the written rule. When that practice begins to decide who actually receives the public good, it is no longer a convention. It is doing the work of a verdict, and the written rule survives only as a declared meaning. The anchor has moved: the thing that settles the outcome now updates on what the office believes.
>
> This is a shape, not a measurement of any country or ministry. It says what must be true for the transition to have happened. It does not say which practice crossed, or when.
>
> **What I am not claiming:** that every unofficial practice is corrupt; that all officials in any office are corrupt; that a high corruption score proves a captured institution; that a single reform (more law, more audits, more training) clears the problem; or that informal rules are always harmful. Corruption indices are filters for deciding where to look, not witnesses for the verdict.
>
> **What would refute the claim:**
> 1. Show an informal regularity that decides binding allocations while remaining a convention with no verdict-bearing force, with the written rule still settling disputes.
> 2. Show an oversight body whose reference moves under pressure from the body it oversees, yet whose corrections stay sound and discriminating against an independent ground.
> 3. Show one intervention, applied entirely inside the original frame with no outside witness, that removes both the convention decision and the captured anchor at once.
>
> I wrote these down before sending this. If any one holds, I will retract the corresponding part. A one-line "this fails because..." is more useful to me than agreement. If you would rather not engage, no reply is needed.
>
> **If it survives, and only then:** the sources are below. The figures are the authority, not my summary of them.
> - Figures and formula for direct download (branch `general-agent`): https://github.com/IsaiahN/tether/tree/general-agent/figures
> - Canonical figures README (usage rule): https://github.com/IsaiahN/tether/blob/general-agent/figures/README.md
> - Worked architecture and citation list: https://adventuresinml.substack.com/p/arrival-of-the-fittest-the-tether
>
> **If you want to use the machinery:** the formula is in the figures link. Run it on an institution you know well and can check against a ground. Write down what would refute your result *before* you run it. The framework's own rule is that a loop cannot check the direction of its own question, and the write-up warns that outputs *"could never be verified if the solutions presented were not contrived to fit the formula."* A reported failure is more useful to me than a reported success.
>
> Thanks,
> [name ]

## Stage order

1. **Stage 1.** Send the claim, the mode, and the three refuters. Ask what the recipient would count as a failure. Record their verdict before any theory is shown.
2. **Stage 2, opt-in only.** If they are curious, offer the three links. The figures, not the summary, are the authority.
3. **Stage 3.** Offer the formula as a method to reuse, with the instruction to state a mode and a refuter before the run.

A Stage 2 or Stage 3 agreement is never pooled as corroboration. By Fig 2 :40, once the recipient holds the same evidence pool as the author, their agreement is the pool talking.
