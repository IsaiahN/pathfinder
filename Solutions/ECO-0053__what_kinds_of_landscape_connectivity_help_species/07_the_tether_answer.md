# The Tether's answer on landscape connectivity and harm

**Mode: `general` on the structural rules, `specified` on the union-compound and no-standing-list
predictions. Not `grounded`.** Simulated derivation checked for internal consistency against the
figures (`05_what_shape_means.md` section 7), not a ground reading.

## 1. The residual it answers

A connectivity change can reduce the residual of a focal species (isolation, lost gene flow, failed
recolonisation) and, through the same contact, reduce the residual of a harm actor (pathogen,
vector, invader, competitor) relative to the conservation goal. The question asks for kinds of
connectivity that separate these effects. The answer is a shape, not a name: the figures say that
valence is a relation to a residual, that the help and harm conditions overlap as a union compound,
and that the named safe kind is a `gap` until a ground settles it.

This is not a single hard problem with a net benefit. Fig 9 :57-58: *"a case in two conditions is not
a harder case of either. It stops being a mixture and becomes a specific compound."* The overlap
has its own loop and its own ground.

## 2. The shape

1. **Connectivity has no sign apart from a residual.** Fig 11 :49: *"An actor's valence is relative
   to the residual, not a property it carries."* Fig 11 :50: *"The same actor is a benefactor to one
   residual and a malfactor to another, so mutualism and parasitism are relations rather than
   kinds."* So the unit of answer is four-part: arrangement, actor, residual, cascade distance.
2. **There is no standing list of safe kinds.** Fig 11 :51: *"No standing list of good actors
   survives a change of residual."* A corridor that is safe for one species and one pathogen is not
   safe for another pair. Fig 9 :32: *"you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself"* governs the
   limit of what the framework returns.
3. **The benefit and the harm form a union compound.** Fig 9 :55: *"A bundled problem is usually a
   union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones."* Fig 9 :56: *"A single lever clears
   the region where only one condition holds and leaves the intersections standing, because the
   second condition still binds."* Adding connectivity is that single lever. It helps the
   benefit-only cases and leaves the overlap standing.
4. **Report per slot, never averaged.** Fig 1 :19: *"The residual is indexed by object slot, not
   aggregated."* Fig 1 :22: *"Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an
   average."* A network that looks net beneficial can hide a live reservoir host or a rare
   pathogen.
5. **Enumerate the habitat outward until the cascade stops mattering.** Fig 11 :44: *"List everything
   in contact with the residual, then what is in contact with those, and outward until the cascade
   stops mattering."* Fig 11 :52-53: *"Scoping the habitat to direct contact is how DDT reached the
   eagles: first-order enumeration, second-order effect, and the residual that mattered was three
   steps out."* The harm is often not at the corridor entrance.
6. **Reducing connectivity is substitution, not subtraction.** Fig 11 :66: *"Isolation is not removal
   of the habitat. It is substitution of one habitat for another."* Fig 11 :67: *"Move the residual
   and you must reproduce everything its goal required, and you inherit the new habitat's actors,
   which you did not choose."* Two costs stay invisible (Fig 11 :68-69): what you failed to
   reproduce, and what you unintentionally introduced.
7. **The "kind" is the bond arrangement.** Fig 12 :94: *"The arrangement is the substance."* Fig 12
   :111: *"A vocabulary without bonds can name a state and cannot name a route to one."* Continuous
   versus stepping-stone corridors are members of one functional group (Fig 12 :124), not isomers,
   because stepping stones add nodes.
8. **The ground decides, not agreement or structure counts.** Fig 2 :29: *"An anchor is whatever
   settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks. A
   measurement, an experiment, a counter, a market, a patient outcome."* Fig 7 :35: *"A room's
   agreement is variation, not a verdict. Selection between rooms is where the ground votes."*
   Kilometres of corridor built are frame-internal; they do not close a residual.

## 3. What it explicitly does not say

- Not a list of safe corridor types, crossing designs or matrix treatments. That is the `gap`.
- Not that connectivity is bad. The frame says valence is relative to a residual; it does not pick a
  sign in advance.
- Not that disconnecting removes harm. Isolation substitutes a habitat and inherits actors.
- Not that a multi-species average is a decision metric. It throws away the live slot.
- Not a claim about any named landscape, species or pathogen. No measurement here settles one.
- Not `grounded`. Nothing in this folder was settled by an instrument.

## 4. What would settle it - the ground

A source-discriminating instrument, not a further argument. *The Hard Problem is About Source*
(opened 2026-11-23): an instrument that reads green *"cannot comment on the source"*; the
spectrometer resolved it. *"The answer is unreached until a better instrument exists."* Here the
corresponding questions are:

- **Help test:** for a named focal residual, does the connectivity change reduce the measured
  residual (occupancy, gene flow, successful colonisation), against a counted baseline?
- **Harm test:** for every actor enumerated to the cascade's end, does the same change fail to
  reduce its residual? Sullivan et al. 2011 is an instance of the instrument needed: it separated
  biotically from abiotically dispersed parasites.
- **Substitution ledger:** for any disconnection proposed, what was failed to reproduce and what
  was unintentionally introduced?
- **Standing falsifiers R1 to R5** from `01_figure_grounded_audit.md` section 6: same-sign effects
  for all actors; a partition instead of a union; no harm beyond first order; contact removal
  without substitution; an aggregate metric sufficient to decide every residual. Any one fires
  against the shape claim.

The instruments are not hypothetical. Telemetry, genetic assignment, mark-recapture, camera traps
and pathogen surveillance already return something and can be sharpened (Fig 6 :32: *"An instrument
is not built from a description. It is improved from a worse instrument already returning
something."*). What is missing is the coupling of those instruments to the same connectivity map
in the same landscape, not the invention of a new sensor.

## 5. The honest stop

**`gap` for a named safe kind, `specified` for the shape.** The distinction matters. Fig 8 :39:
*"So a search that finds nothing is never a proof of absence."* A failure to find a universally safe
corridor type here is not a proof that none exists; it is the framework declining to name one from
inside its closure. Fig 13: *"Unreachable is a verdict here, not a silence"*, and the usage rule says
the framework returns a shape and *"Indiscernibility is not identity."*

So the answer stops where the instruments stop: with the four-part shape, the union reading, the
enumeration rule, the substitution ledger, five refuters, and a list of measurements that would
close it. The lineage is recorded so the stop is checkable.

## 6. One-paragraph version for outreach

> The Tether does not name a kind of connectivity that is safe everywhere, and it does not claim
> that all connectivity is good. It says that connectivity has no sign apart from a residual, so an
> answer must name four things: the arrangement, the actor, the residual, and how far the contact
> cascade was enumerated. Helping a species move and helping a pathogen spread are two conditions
> borne by the same link, so this is a union compound; a single lever such as "add a corridor"
> clears the benefit-only cases and leaves the overlap standing. Residuals must be reported per
> species and per harm actor, never averaged, and the harm is often several steps out, as with DDT
> and the eagles. Cutting connectivity is not subtraction but substitution of one habitat for
> another, with two invisible costs: what you failed to reproduce and what you unintentionally
> introduced. What the framework offers is that shape, the refuters written in advance, and the
> instruments (movement and disease measurements coupled to the same connectivity map) that would
> close it. The named safe kind stays open until the ground settles it.
