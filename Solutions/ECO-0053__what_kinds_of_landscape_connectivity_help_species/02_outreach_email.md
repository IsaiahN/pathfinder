# Outreach draft - check the shape first, then decide about the framework

**Use:** one-to-one, or as a cover letter attached to a submission through the public venues in
`03_public_contact_points.csv`. **Not** for bulk send.

**Hard precondition:** do not send until `01_figure_grounded_audit.md` sections 2 to 6 have been
read and the five refuters R1 to R5 are stated exactly as written. The offer collapses on contact
if the stage-1 output cannot survive a refutation.

---

**Subject:** One checkable shape claim about connectivity, before any theory

> Hi [name],
>
> I have one specific output about landscape connectivity that I would like you to check against
> what you already know. I am not asking you to read the framework first, and I am not asking you
> to agree with anything. If the output does not survive, there is nothing else to look at.
>
> **The question I am working on:** what kinds of landscape connectivity help species move without
> also facilitating disease, invasive species, or other harms?
>
> **The output (`general` on the shape, `specified` on two predictions, not `grounded`):** a
> connectivity change reduces the residual of a focal species (isolation, lost gene flow, failure
> to recolonise) and, in the cases that matter, reduces the residual of a harm actor (a pathogen, a
> vector, an invader) at the same time, because both use the same contact. The figure-governed
> result is that there is no standing list of intrinsically safe corridor types. The unit of an
> answer has four parts: the arrangement, the actor, the residual, and how far you enumerate the
> contact cascade. The benefit and the harm conditions overlap, so this is a union compound, not a
> single problem with a net benefit. Adding connectivity is a single lever that clears the
> benefit-only cases and leaves the overlap standing.
>
> **What I am not claiming:** I am not claiming that any named corridor type, stepping-stone
> design, or wildlife crossing is safe everywhere. I am not claiming that disconnecting is a way to
> remove harm: isolation substitutes one habitat for another, and you inherit the new actors
> unchosen. I am not claiming a result about any particular landscape, because no measurement in
> this work settles one.
>
> **What would refute the shape claim. I wrote these down before sending this:**
> 1. A connectivity arrangement whose effect has the same sign for every residual and every
>    actor, independent of which residual is named.
> 2. A demonstration that the help and harm conditions form a partition, with no case where the
>    same link serves both.
> 3. A class of interventions where no harm residual ever appears beyond first-order contact.
> 4. A management operation that removes contact with no substituted habitat and no inherited
>    actors.
> 5. An aggregate connectivity metric sufficient to decide every residual with no external
>    measurement.
>
> If any one holds, I will retract the claim. A one-line "this fails because..." is more useful to
> me than agreement. If you would rather not engage, no reply is needed.
>
> **If it survives, and only then:** the source material is below. The figures are the authority,
> not my summary of them.
> - Local figure text with line numbers: `FRAMEWORK/reference/03-FIGURES_TEXT.md` in the project
>   folder (Fig 11 :43-69 is the relevant figure).
> - Canonical usage rule: `FRAMEWORK/reference/FIGURES_README_canonical.md`.
> - The world studies that bear on this: Sullivan et al. 2011, Ecology (corridor effects on plant
>   parasites depend on dispersal mode); McRae et al. 2012, PLOS ONE (barrier detection is
>   species-specific); Faustini et al. 2025, PLOS ONE and Goicolea et al. 2024, Scientific Reports
>   (the same connectivity models predict pathogen spread).
>
> **If you want to use the machinery:** the formula is in the figure folder. Run it on a problem
> you know well and can check against a ground. Write down what would refute your result *before*
> you run it. A reported failure is more useful to me than a reported success.
>
> Thanks,
> [name]

## Two-stage record to keep

1. **Stage 1:** send the residual, the four-part shape claim, and refuters R1 to R5. Record the
   recipient's verdict *before* any framework material is shown.
2. **Stage 2, opt-in only:** if they are curious, send the three source pointers above, with the
   note that the figures, not the summary, are the authority. Their later agreement is never
   pooled as corroboration (Fig 2 :40).
3. **Stage 3:** offer the formula as a method to reuse, with the instruction that each user writes
   their own refuter and states their own mode.
