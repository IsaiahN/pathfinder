## 1. The claim under audit, stated as an effect

The question as written assumes that "connectivity" is a kind of thing that can carry a sign: some
kinds help species move, some kinds also facilitate harms, and the task is to sort the first from
the second. Restated in effect terms:

> Let `R_s` be the residual of a focal species or ecological process (isolation, lost gene flow,
> failure to recolonise, inability to track climate). Let `R_h` be the residual of a harm actor
> (a pathogen, a vector, an invasive plant, a predator, a competitor) relative to the conservation
> goal. A connectivity change `C` (a corridor, a stepping-stone chain, a removed barrier, a more
> permeable matrix) changes contact. In the case that matters, `C` reduces both `R_s` and `R_h`
> because the same link is used by both. The question asks for the shape of a `C` that reduces
> `R_s` without reducing `R_h`.

What an answer would look like, bound as an ordinary term (formula step "Direction"): a rule of
the form *"build connectivity of kind K for actor A against residual R, enumerated to cascade
distance D"*, where K, A, R and D are stated before any site is chosen, and where the rule says
what observation would refute it. A list of corridor types without A, R and D is not an answer;
it is an ingredient list, which Fig 12 :111 says cannot name a route.

## 2. Domain census (before choosing any figure line)

**R by arity, symmetry and scale.**

- **Arity:** at least two conditions (benefit to the focal residual, harm to the conservation
  residual) that are borne by the same structure. This is a **union compound**, not one hard
  problem and not a partition. Fig 9 :57-58: *"a case in two conditions is not a harder case of
  either. It stops being a mixture and becomes a specific compound."*
- **Symmetry:** the physical link is symmetric (a corridor between patches A and B transmits in
  both directions), but its valence is not symmetric under a change of residual. Fig 11 :50:
  *"The same actor is a benefactor to one residual and a malfactor to another, so mutualism and
  parasitism are relations rather than kinds."*
- **Scale:** first order is direct movement of the focal species; second and third order are
  transmission, establishment and trophic or hydrological effects. Fig 11 :52-53: *"Scoping the
  habitat to direct contact is how DDT reached the eagles: first-order enumeration, second-order
  effect, and the residual that mattered was three steps out."*

**One line per domain: actors / currency / anchor.**

| domain | actors | currency | anchor (nameable? if not, not yet a domain) | question type |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| D1 Movement and gene flow | focal species, patches, dispersers, alleles, demographic rescue | movement, colonisation, gene flow | occupancy and genetic assignment data; yes | one condition |
| D2 Harm and contagion | pathogens, vectors, invasive plants, predators, competitors | transmission, establishment, spread | disease incidence and invasion records; yes | one condition, negative valence relative to D1 |
| D3 The connector / landscape pattern | corridors, stepping stones, matrix resistance, edges, barriers | contact created or restored | the landscape as enumerated from the world; yes (Fig 11 :48) | structure, a composition of state and network |
| D4 Management and the seat | planners, landowners, agencies, stakeholders | introductions, restorations, substitutions | monitored ground outcomes; yes | asymmetric intervention |
| D5 Verification and outreach | expert readers, manuscript reviewers, venue editors | agreement and residual | the ground, never agreement; yes, but simulated here | one evidence pool in this run |

**Partition or union.** D1 and D2 overlap in D3: the same corridor is in contact with both. So the
whole is a **union**, and the overlap is a compound with its own loop. D4 and D5 are not separate
ecologies; they are the seat and the checking frame acting on the union.

**Order by dependency (Fig 3).** Enumerate the habitat first (perception), then have terms for what
a link affords (vocabulary), then name which residual counts as progress (objective), then choose
an intervention (planning), then monitor and carry (learn and carry). Fig 3 :19: *"Optimising a
link that already works cannot rescue one that does not."* The middle link is the one that
typically breaks here: a project can count kilometres of corridor built while never measuring
whether any residual closed. Fig 3 :27: *"A link with no instrument is not a link that works. It is
a link nobody has looked at."*

## 4. Findings F1 to F10

Every quote below is verbatim from `03-FIGURES_TEXT.md`. Quotes attributed to an essay or to
`THE_FORMULA.md` are labelled as such and are not figure lines.

### F1 - Valence belongs to the residual, not to the corridor (decisive)

Fig 11 :49: *"An actor's valence is relative to the residual, not a property it carries."*
Fig 11 :50: *"The same actor is a benefactor to one residual and a malfactor to another, so
mutualism and parasitism are relations rather than kinds."*
Fig 11 :51: *"No standing list of good actors survives a change of residual."*

The question asks for "kinds of connectivity" as if a kind carried a sign. The figure denies this
for actors, and a corridor is an actor in the habitat once it is built (it acts on contact). So a
list of intrinsically safe corridor types is forbidden at the same level as a list of intrinsically
good species. The unit of answer must be a four-tuple: connectivity arrangement, actor, residual,
cascade distance.

- **Mode:** `general` (structural rule about valence).
- **Status:** `single-source` at figure level (Fig 11). Corroborated at world level by Sullivan et
  al. 2011 (Ecology), who found corridor effects on parasites changed with parasite dispersal mode:
  connectivity increased biotically dispersed galls but not abiotically dispersed foliar fungi.
  That is a different instrument (a replicated field experiment), so it is weak corroboration of
  a relation, not a second derivation of the figure.
- **Refuter:** exhibit a connectivity arrangement whose effect on all enumerated residuals has the
  same sign independent of which actor and which residual is named. If that holds, valence is a
  property of the structure and Fig 11 :49-51 has been misapplied here.

### F2 - The benefit and the harm are a union compound, not a trade-off to be averaged

Fig 9 :54: *"And when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you
search again."*
Fig 9 :55: *"A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of
separate ones."*
Fig 9 :56: *"A single lever clears the region where only one condition holds and leaves the
intersections standing, because the second condition still binds."*

"Add connectivity" is a single lever. It helps where only D1 binds (an isolated focal population).
It leaves standing exactly the overlap (the corridor that the pathogen or invader also uses),
because the second condition still binds there. A net-benefit average across a landscape is the
wrong instrument for the overlap.

- **Mode:** `specified` (falsifiable prediction about single-lever interventions).
- **Status:** `triangulated` at figure level between Fig 9 :54-58 (union logic) and Fig 11 :49-51
  (valence relative to residual), which are low-overlap figure lines governing different things:
  one the structure of the question, the other the sign of an actor.
- **Refuter:** show that the help and harm conditions form a partition, so that for every
  connectivity change the set of helped residuals and the set of harmed residuals are disjoint.
  Then the union reading and the unbundling instruction are inapplicable to this case.

### F3 - Report per slot; an average can hide a live harm

Fig 1 :19: *"The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated."*
Fig 1 :22: *"Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average."*
Fig 1 :13: *"a global R near zero with one live slot residual is a legal state"*; Fig 1 :20-21
says such a state is *"a legal state and not an inert one"*.

A corridor network can look net beneficial when summed over species while one slot, a reservoir
host or a rare pathogen, is live. The D1 result and the D2 result must be reported separately, by
actor, at the same scale. This is the diagnostic that makes F2 checkable.

- **Mode:** `general` (measurement rule).
- **Status:** `triangulated` between Fig 1 and the per-species warning in McRae et al. 2012 (PLOS
  ONE), which states that what constitutes a barrier and what impact it has *"will differ among
  species"*. Different instrument, low overlap.
- **Refuter:** demonstrate a case where a single aggregate connectivity metric is sufficient to
  decide both the focal benefit and every harm residual, with no residual trending differently in
  any slot. Then the per-slot requirement adds nothing here.

### F4 - Enumerate the cascade, not just the direct contact

Fig 11 :43: *"The habitat is enumerated, never composed."*
Fig 11 :44: *"List everything in contact with the residual, then what is in contact with those,
and outward until the cascade stops mattering."*
Fig 11 :48: *"You do not invent the list. You read it off the world, and what you cannot perceive
or measure yet is the residual."*
Fig 11 :52-53: *"Scoping the habitat to direct contact is how DDT reached the eagles: first-order
enumeration, second-order effect, and the residual that mattered was three steps out."*

Harm from a corridor is frequently not at the corridor itself. A crossing that helps a focal
ungulate also moves a tick, the tick carries a pathogen, and the pathogen reaches a host three
links out. The enumeration rule (not a recipe) is what the figures give. This is also the point at
which the world literature agrees: Faustini et al. 2025 and Goicolea et al. 2024 both use
landscape-connectivity models to predict African swine fever spread along wild boar dispersal
routes, i.e. they model the harm by the same structure used for conservation.

- **Mode:** `general` (enumeration rule); the DDT case is a historical instance, not a derivation.
- **Status:** `triangulated` between Fig 11 :43-48 and Fig 11 :52-53 (enumeration and cascade are
  distinct lines in the same figure but govern different moves: scope and depth). Corroborated by
  disease-connectivity studies that share a modeling pool with each other (see F9 on overlap).
- **Refuter:** show a class of connectivity interventions in which no harm residual ever appears
  beyond first-order contact, so enumeration to the point where the cascade stops mattering
  terminates at the corridor edge in every case. Then the cascade-depth requirement is empty here.

### F5 - The answer cannot be composed from an ingredient list

Fig 12 :111: *"A vocabulary without bonds can name a state and cannot name a route to one."*
Fig 12 :94: *"The arrangement is the substance."*
Fig 9 :32: *"you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself"*

A corridor, a stepping stone and a permeable matrix are ingredients. Which patch connects to
which, in which direction, at which season, is the bond. Fig 12 says the arrangement, not the
ingredient set, is the substance; Fig 9 says the lookup returns a kind of thing. So the figures
return the shape of the answer (a route with bonds and a per-residual valence), not a named recipe
that is safe everywhere.

- **Mode:** `general`.
- **Status:** `triangulated` between Fig 12 :94/:111 and Fig 9 :32. Corroborated by the canonical
  figures README, which says the framework returns *"what a missing thing would have to look like
  (a shape, not a name)"* (local file, same evidence pool as the figures, so weak corroboration).
- **Refuter:** produce a connectivity vocabulary that, from ingredients alone and with no bond or
  actor attached, correctly predicts the sign of the effect for every residual in every landscape.
  Then the route/arrangement requirement fails.

### F6 - Management is asymmetric; isolation is substitution, not subtraction

Fig 11 :61: *"The seat's power is asymmetric. Introduction is available; exclusion is not."*
Fig 11 :66: *"Isolation is not removal of the habitat. It is substitution of one habitat for
another."*
Fig 11 :67: *"Move the residual and you must reproduce everything its goal required, and you
inherit the new habitat's actors, which you did not choose."*
Fig 11 :68: *"what you failed to reproduce,"* and Fig 11 :69: *"what you unintentionally introduced
is invisible until it acts"*
Fig 11 :65: *"Introducing an actor for its cascade is ecosystem engineering, after Jones, Lawton
and Shachak, 1994. Cane toads and kudzu are the same move with the cascade unenumerated."*

The tempting response to F1-F4 is "then disconnect": close the corridor, remove the crossing,
fence the matrix. The figures say that is not subtraction. You substitute one habitat for another,
you inherit the new habitat's actors unchosen, and two costs stay invisible until the goal fails or
the introduced actor acts. The frame's power is to add, not to subtract contact, because contact is
a fact about the world and does not consult the manager. This is the same move the figure names as
ecosystem engineering when the cascade was not enumerated (cane toads, kudzu).

- **Mode:** `general` (asymmetry is a rule this framework imposes on itself, per Fig 11 :71).
- **Status:** `single-source` at figure level (Fig 11). Corroborated at world level by Dong et al.
  2025 (Ecology and Evolution), who found blacklegged-tick gene-flow corridors and concluded that
  *"targeted interventions in seemingly less tick-favorable landscapes may help mitigate the
  spread"*, i.e. managers act by targeted addition/substitution, not by erasing contact.
- **Refuter:** exhibit a management operation that genuinely removes contact from the world
  (not substitutes a habitat, not adds a barrier with its own actors, but removes the relation
  with no inherited actors and no reproduction failure). Then the asymmetry law fails here.

### F7 - Connectivity is a structure, and a structure with no state is empty

Fig 13 :23-24: *"network that entities relate, and influence travels"*
Fig 13 :29: *"A structure with no state is empty: an ordering over nothing, a network with nothing
at the nodes."*
Fig 13 :35-36: *"adjacency is not duration, an ordering is not a pathway, a pathway is not a
direction."*

"Connectivity" on its own is the network structure, one of the six substrate terms. A network
requires nodes and a state it is bonded to; otherwise it is an ordering over nothing. It is also
not the same thing as a gradient or a spectrum: adjacency is not a directedness and not an
ordering. So "more connectivity" does not name a quantity with one sign. It names a structure whose
meaning is fixed by which state (which species, which pathogen) and which other structure (which
gradient of resistance, which ordering of patches) it is composed with. This explains, from the
substrate, why F1 holds.

- **Mode:** `general` (substrate-level claim).
- **Status:** `single-source` at figure level (Fig 13). The Fig 11 valence rule corroborates the
  consequence but not the substrate claim, so this is not upgraded to triangulated.
- **Refuter:** derive a case in which the network structure alone, with no state bonded to it,
  determines a unique sign for an ecological effect. Then the empty-structure claim is wrong here.

### F8 - The ground is an outcome, not agreement or a count of structures built

Fig 2 :29: *"An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of
what any frame thinks. A measurement, an experiment, a counter, a market, a patient outcome."*
Fig 2 :35: *"Alignment is triangulation, not negotiation. A mirror cannot correct you."*
Fig 1 :27: *"They are frame-internal. A frame cannot score itself with a quantity it also
produces."*
Fig 7 :35: *"A room's agreement is variation, not a verdict. Selection between rooms is where the
ground votes."*
Fig 2 :42: *"Buy independence in proportion to the cost of being wrong."*

Kilometres of corridor, number of crossings, hectares reconnected and reviewer consensus are all
frame-internal for the claim that connectivity helped. The anchor is a measurement of movement and
of harm incidence that does not move when a planner wants it to. The world literature supplies
exactly these instruments: mark-recapture, genetic assignment and camera traps (Wildlife corridor,
Wikipedia), least-cost and circuit models with field checking (McRae et al. 2012).

- **Mode:** `general`.
- **Status:** `triangulated` between Fig 2, Fig 1 and Fig 7, three low-overlap figures governing
  anchor, self-scoring and room agreement respectively.
- **Refuter:** show that a corridor programme's own output metric (structures built) is sufficient
  to settle whether a residual closed, with no external measurement. Then the anchor requirement
  is needless here.

### F9 - Which kind works is an instrument question, and `gap` is a verdict

Fig 6 :32: *"An instrument is not built from a description. It is improved from a worse instrument
already returning something."*
Fig 8 :39: *"So a search that finds nothing is never a proof of absence."*
Fig 9 :44: *"Only a sealed room can be searched to the end, and no frame is a sealed room. So
searching and finding nothing proves nothing."*
Fig 9 :30-31: *"your words are borrowed too, so describe the effect rather than the cause"*

The three formula questions stay distinct. `SEARCHED`: has anyone already measured this corridor
type against both residuals and simply not found the result? `INWARD`: sharpen the instruments we
already have (telemetry, genetic assignment, pathogen surveillance) from a worse instrument that
already returns something. `OUTWARD`: find a frame whose closure already predicts the shape (the
disease-connectivity studies are such a frame). None of these licenses naming a universally safe
kind from the armchair.

The source overlap matters here. Faustini et al. 2025 and Goicolea et al. 2024 both model African
swine fever spread with landscape connectivity and share an author (Bosch); they are one witness
with two sites, not two independent corroborations. Sullivan et al. 2011 is a field experiment in a
different system (plant parasites) and is the lower-overlap witness for F1.

- **Mode:** `specified` on the stop; `general` on the instrument rule.
- **Status:** `triangulated` between Fig 6, Fig 8 and Fig 9.
- **Refuter:** produce a general argument that derives a named safe connectivity kind from the six
  substrate terms plus the valence rule without any site measurement. Then `gap` was premature.

## 5. Strain test and re-census

Two apparent strains were found and resolved by re-census, not by softening a reading.

1. **Fig 11 :43 "the habitat is enumerated, never composed" versus Fig 13's rule that everything
   is a composition across the two kinds.** Re-census: Fig 11 governs the *list of actors and
   conditions in contact with R* (you read it off the world, you do not invent it). Fig 13 governs
   how a *state and a structure compose into a term*. A landscape network is a composed term; the
   census of what is in contact with it is enumerated. Different objects, no strain. This matches
   the canonical rule that a reading which makes figures contradict each other has misidentified
   its domain.
2. **Fig 9 :54 "unbundle before you search" versus Fig 11 :61 "Introduction is available; exclusion is not."**
   Re-census: Fig 9 governs a *description move* (split the question into its overlapping
   conditions before choosing a frame). Fig 11 :61 governs an *intervention constraint* (you can
   add an actor but cannot subtract the habitat). Unbundling the description does not require
   subtracting contact. Different quantities, no strain.

**Strains remaining after re-census: none hidden.** One live limitation is recorded as a limit,
not a strain: the second-seat audit in `11_audit.md` shares this evidence pool, so its agreement is
internal consistency and not corroboration.

## 6. Pre-registered refuter (written before the conclusion)

Required by Seat Map :287 (local file, opened this session): *"a falsifier written before the run
is a design instrument, not only an honesty device."* The shape claim is refuted if any of the
following holds:

1. **R1 (valence):** a connectivity arrangement is exhibited whose effect has the same sign for
   every residual and every actor, with no dependence on which residual is named (breaks F1).
2. **R2 (union):** the help and harm conditions are shown to form a partition, with no case in
   which the same link serves both (breaks F2).
3. **R3 (cascade):** a class of interventions is shown in which no harm residual ever appears
   beyond first-order contact, so enumeration depth adds nothing (breaks F4).
4. **R4 (asymmetry):** a management operation is exhibited that removes contact with no
   substituted habitat, no inherited actors and no reproduction failure (breaks F6).
5. **R5 (ground):** an aggregate connectivity metric is shown sufficient to decide every residual
   with no external measurement of movement or harm (breaks F3 and F8).

If any fires, the shape is wrong and the folder is withdrawn or rewritten. Until one is run, no
claim here may be labelled `grounded`. The refuter is sent in stage 1 before any theory is shown.

### Seven-question recursive derivation (summary)

1. **Is there one residual?** No. At least D1 and D2, overlapping in D3. Union compound (Fig 9 :54-58). Bind both before searching.
2. **Does connectivity carry a sign?** No. Valence is relative to the residual (Fig 11 :49-51). The requested "kind" is a preference, not a term (Fig 5 :62-63), so it cannot be found by the mint.
3. **What is the unit of answer?** A four-tuple: arrangement, actor, residual, cascade distance (Fig 12 :94, :111).
4. **How far does the habitat go?** Outward until the cascade stops mattering (Fig 11 :44, :52-53). Not the corridor edge.
5. **Can we subtract the harm?** No. Introduction available, exclusion not; isolation is substitution with two invisible costs (Fig 11 :61, :66-69).
6. **What settles a site-specific kind?** The ground: measured movement and harm outcomes, from instruments already returning something (Fig 2 :29, Fig 6 :32-34).
7. **What is the stop?** `gap` for a standing safe list, `specified` for the shape. Not silence: the refuters R1-R5 and the instrument list are recorded. Fig 8 :39, Fig 13 :134 (*"Unreachable is a verdict here, not a silence"*).
