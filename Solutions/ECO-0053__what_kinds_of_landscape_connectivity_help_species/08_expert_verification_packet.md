# Expert verification packet - the Tether on connectivity and harm

**What this is:** a claim in a form a landscape ecologist or disease ecologist can check. Mode,
derivation and refuters are stated before the conclusion, so a reader knows what to look for and
what would settle it.

**Mode: `general` on the structural rules, `specified` on the union-compound and no-standing-list
predictions. Not `grounded`.** No site measurement is claimed.

**Status:** a simulated derivation checked for internal consistency against the figures, not a
ground reading. It cannot settle the ecology question it reports on.

## 1. The claim, in one paragraph

Connectivity has no sign apart from a residual. An answer to "which kinds help without harming"
must therefore name four things: the arrangement, the actor, the residual, and how far the contact
cascade is enumerated. The focal benefit and the harm to a non-target actor are two conditions borne
by the same link, so the question is a union compound and a single lever (add connectivity) clears
the benefit-only cases while leaving the overlap standing. Residuals must be reported per slot,
never averaged. The habitat is enumerated outward until the cascade stops mattering. Reducing
connectivity is substitution of one habitat for another, not subtraction, with two invisible costs.
The framework returns this shape and refuses to name a standing safe kind until a ground settles it.

## 2. Three checkable sub-claims

### C1 - framework-internal; checkable by reading Figure 11

- **Statement:** valence is relative to the residual; no standing list of good actors or safe
  connectivity kinds survives a change of residual.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 11 :49 - *"An actor's valence is relative to the residual, not a property
  it carries."* Fig 11 :50 - *"The same actor is a benefactor to one residual and a malfactor to
  another, so mutualism and parasitism are relations rather than kinds."* Fig 11 :51 - *"No standing
  list of good actors survives a change of residual."*
- **What to check:** is the claim about the figure's rule correctly stated? Does "corridor" fall
  under the figure's "actor" once built, given that it acts on contact?
- **Refuter (R1):** exhibit a connectivity arrangement whose effect has the same sign for every
  residual and every actor, independent of which residual is named. Then valence is a property of
  the structure and this sub-claim fails.

### C2 - ecology-facing; checkable against the connectivity literature

- **Statement:** the help and harm conditions form a union compound, so a single connectivity lever
  leaves the overlap standing; effects are species- and process-specific and must be reported per
  slot.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 9 :55 - *"A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping
  conditions, not a list of separate ones."* Fig 9 :56 - *"A single lever clears the region where
  only one condition holds and leaves the intersections standing, because the second condition
  still binds."* Fig 1 :19 - *"The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated."*
- **What to check:** is the union reading a fair description of the case? World witnesses: Sullivan
  et al. 2011 (Ecology) found corridors increased biotically dispersed parasites but not
  abiotically dispersed ones; McRae et al. 2012 (PLOS ONE) states barrier identity and impact
  *"will differ among species"*; Wikipedia's landscape-connectivity article says quantification is
  *"organism-, process- and landscape-specific"*. Note that the ASF connectivity studies share an
  author and a modeling pool, so they count as one witness for the harm-side use of connectivity.
- **Refuter (R2 + R3):** show that help and harm form a partition with no shared link, or show a
  class of interventions in which no harm residual ever appears beyond first-order contact. Either
  breaks the compound reading. An aggregate metric sufficient to decide every residual (R5) also
  breaks the per-slot requirement.

### C3 - management-facing; checkable by a practitioner

- **Statement:** reducing connectivity is substitution, not subtraction; you inherit unchosen
  actors and two invisible costs.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 11 :66 - *"Isolation is not removal of the habitat. It is substitution
  of one habitat for another."* Fig 11 :67 - *"Move the residual and you must reproduce everything
  its goal required, and you inherit the new habitat's actors, which you did not choose."* Fig 11
  :61 - *"The seat's power is asymmetric. Introduction is available; exclusion is not."*
- **What to check:** does this match the practitioner's experience of fencing, barrier installation
  or matrix management? World witnesses: the invasive-species article notes that many established
  invaders become essential and *"their removal could be harmful"*; Dong et al. 2025 describes
  targeted tick interventions rather than erasure of contact.
- **Refuter (R4):** exhibit a management operation that removes contact with no substituted
  habitat, no inherited actors and no reproduction failure. Then the asymmetry law fails here.

## 3. What is explicitly not claimed

- Not a named safe corridor type, crossing design or matrix treatment. That is a `gap`.
- Not that connectivity is harmful in general. The frame does not pick a sign in advance.
- Not that disconnecting removes harm. Isolation substitutes a habitat and inherits its actors.
- Not that a multi-species average is a decision metric.
- Not a result for any particular landscape, species or pathogen.
- Not `grounded`. Nothing here is asserted as settled by an instrument.
- Not that more expert agreement would settle the question. Fig 7 :35: *"A room's agreement is
  variation, not a verdict. Selection between rooms is where the ground votes."*

## 5. What would settle it

A ground reading, not an argument. From *The Hard Problem is About Source*: an instrument that reads
green *"cannot comment on the source"*, and *"The answer is unreached until a better instrument
exists."*

1. **Help test:** does a named connectivity change reduce a measured focal residual against a
   counted baseline?
2. **Harm test:** for every actor enumerated to the cascade's end, does the same change fail to
   reduce its residual?
3. **Substitution ledger:** for any proposed disconnection, what was failed to reproduce and what
   was unintentionally introduced?
4. **Falsifiers R1 to R5** in `01_figure_grounded_audit.md` section 6, written before this packet
   was compiled.

## 6. What an expert can check in twenty minutes

1. Read Fig 11 :49-51, :61, :66-69 and Fig 9 :55-56 - six short passages.
2. Check C2 against your own system: does the help condition and the harm condition ever ride the
   same link in your landscape? If never, R2 fires.
3. Name one instrument that would measure both residuals on the same connectivity map, or say why
   none is possible.
4. Send the refutation. A failure is more useful than an endorsement.
