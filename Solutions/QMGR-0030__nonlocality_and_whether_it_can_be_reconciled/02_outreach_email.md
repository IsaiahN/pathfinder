# Outreach draft: check the output first, then decide about the theory

**Use:** one-to-one, or as a cover letter attached to a submission through the public venues in `03_public_contact_points.csv`. **Not** for bulk send.

**Hard precondition:** do not send until the shape claim, the split and the refuter in `01_figure_grounded_audit.md` §§0, 3 and 5 are checked for internal consistency. The offer collapses on contact if the stage-1 output does not survive.

---

**Subject:** Two conditions, one refuter: check a claim about nonlocality and relativistic causality

> Hi [name],
>
> I have one specific output I would like you to check against what you already know. I am not asking you to read a theory first, and I am not asking you to agree. If the output does not survive, there is nothing else to look at.
>
> **The output (`specified`, not `grounded`):** Bell-violating correlation and relativistic causality are not one question. They are two conditions that happen to be observed together, and the phrase "relativistic causality" hides a second split:
>
> 1. no controllable superluminal signalling, which is settled by experiment and by the no-signalling theorem;
> 2. no preferred foliation in the fundamental spacetime structure, which is *not* settled by the first condition. A theory can respect no-signalling and still need a distinguished foliation.
>
> So the residual owed is not "explain spooky action". It is: find a theory that reproduces the observed correlations, keeps no-signalling, and has no observable preferred foliation. That is a shape, not a name. The framework I am testing does not select among Bohmian mechanics, superdeterminism, retrocausal or Everettian readings; it says only what any reconciling theory must do.
>
> Its figure-grounded claim is that the local-realist closure cannot reach the observed correlations. This is a refusal, not a new explanatory story: the local-realist term keeps its place in the record and loses its standing to be composed over. A deeper reconciliation therefore needs an import from a frame whose closure differs, not more searching inside the same assumptions.
>
> **What I am not claiming:** that a specific interpretation is the answer, that nonlocality conflicts with relativity at the operational level, that Bell's theorem rules out hidden variables without qualification, or that anything here is settled by experiment. The theorem rules out local hidden variables under measurement independence. Agreement among physicists is not the anchor; the experiment is.
>
> **What would refute the shape claim, written down before this message:**
> 1. a loophole-free experiment showing controllable superluminal signalling;
> 2. a local hidden-variable model reproducing all quantum predictions while keeping statistical independence;
> 3. a proof that no-signalling already entails a Lorentz-invariant fundamental dynamics, so my two conditions are one;
> 4. a derivation of the correlations inside the local-realist closure with no new atom.
>
> If any one holds, I retract the claim. A one-line "this fails because..." is more useful to me than agreement. If you would rather not engage, no reply is needed.
>
> **If it survives, and only then:** the sources are in `04_links_and_distribution.md`. The figures are the authority, not my summary of them. I am happy to be told that the split is wrong; that would be the most useful result.
>
> **If you want to use the machinery:** the formula is public. Run it on a problem you know well and can check against a ground. Write down what would refute your result *before* you run it, and state the mode of what you get. A reported failure is more useful to me than a reported success.
