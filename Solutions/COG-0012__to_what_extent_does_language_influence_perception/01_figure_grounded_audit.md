## 0. The claim under audit

The question is not a yes/no. It is a request for the extent of a mapping and the conditions that maximize it. Restated as an effect:

> Given a slot s that a perceiver is tracking, what does changing the perceiver's language change about the observation o' at s, and under which conditions is |change| largest?

Three options, each with a consequence:

- **A, determinism.** Language fixes the perceptual content. Different languages yield incommensurable perceptual worlds. Consequence: no shared ground remains between language communities, so no cross-linguistic test can be anchored.
- **B, zero effect.** Perception is fixed by biology and instrument; language only relabels a ready-made world. Consequence: all cross-linguistic differences are naming differences, and the non-linguistic task must show nothing.
- **C, conditional modulation.** Language changes what a frame can represent, discriminate and recall, without changing the world; the effect is concentrated where no instrument pins the slot. Consequence: a per-slot prediction, with a specific set of conditions under which the effect is strong and a specific set under which it washes out.

The sought-for shape is bound here in advance: **a rule that names which slots shift, by what kind of change, and which instrument would settle each case.** This is the "Direction" discipline of `THE_FORMULA.md`: make the sought-for shape an ordinary term so it can accrue a residual and be refused.

## 2. Domain census (before choosing any figure line)

**R by arity, symmetry and scale.**
Arity: at least two operands, a language frame and a perceptual slot, with culture and instrument as further operands. Symmetry: asymmetric at a given level, language acts on representation while the world does not change; the inverse mapping from a reading to its source is lossy and non-invertible (metamerism). Scale: slot level (perception), frame level (vocabulary and closure), habitat level (language community and transmission). Cross-scale movement is lossy (Fig 4).

**One line per domain: actors / currency / anchor.**

- **Domain 1, the perceptual slot.** Actors: slots s, predictions Γ(b,a), observations o', instruments. Currency: residual R per slot. Anchor: the measurement or experiment, which must not update because of what any language frame thinks. Partition: slots are kept separate.
- **Domain 2, the language frame.** Actors: speaker, Γ, atoms, bonds, grammatical categories, w. Currency: what composes inside closure(Γ) and how cheaply. Anchor: none internal; the frame cannot certify its own limit (Fig 8). The anchor for this domain is borrowed from domain 1: settlement against the non-linguistic task.
- **Domain 3, the habitat and transmission.** Actors: community, apprentices, other speakers, bilingual switching, shared context and pretext. Currency: methods and head starts crossing the membrane, contact. Anchor: separate experience plus the ground (Fig 2, Fig 10). Question type: **union**, because language and culture share an evidence pool.
- **Domain 4, verification and outreach.** Actors: primary seat, second seat, expert reader. Currency: agreement and residual. Anchor: the ground, never agreement. In this simulated run all four domains draw on one pool of essays, figures and web pages, so independence is zero.

**Partition or union.** Domains 1 and 2 partition at first order (a slot and a representation are different objects). Domain 3 is a union compound: "A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones" (Fig 9 :55). Splitting it is required before any verdict about "language" can be isolated from "culture".

**Order by dependency (Fig 3).** perception (1) -> vocabulary (2) -> objective (3) -> planning (4) -> learn and carry (5). Effort spent above the break arrives at a step that cannot use it. The language effect is predicted at link 2 and its downstream consequences at links 3 to 5, not at link 1.

## 3. Findings

### F1 - The effect sits at the second dependency link, not at transduction (governs the conditions)

Fig 3 :6-7:

> "it has terms for what things afford: what can be moved, opened, joined, blocked"

and Fig 3 :8: "what the search can reach".

Fig 3 :25-26:

> "The link between them, having terms for what a situation offers and being able to say what would count as progress, has neither, so it attracts the least attention and the fewest instruments."

Fig 3 :27: "A link with no instrument is not a link that works. It is a link nobody has looked at." And Fig 3 :19: "Optimising a link that already works cannot rescue one that does not."

The chain is ordered by dependency. Link 1 (perception) has "obvious failures and obvious tests" (Fig 3 :25); link 4 (planning) has obvious failures and obvious tests; the middle, link 2 to link 3, has neither. This is exactly where a vocabulary acts: it supplies terms for what a situation affords and it frames what counts as progress. So the conditions for strong effects are conditions in which the slot is **not** pinned by a link-1 instrument. Where an instrument exists, the anchor is reachable and frames range against it separately.

**Refuter.** An instrument at link 2 that fixes the categories independently of the language and shows no cross-linguistic difference on a non-linguistic task, while a linguistic difference remains. That would move the effect back to labeling only.

### F2 - Vocabulary is a grammar, not a dictionary (governs the kind of effect)

Fig 12 :111:

> "A vocabulary without bonds can name a state and cannot name a route to one."

Fig 12 :112-113:

> "A plan is a sequence. A goal is a comparison. Progress is a subtraction. A refutation is a negation, and that one is not a bond."

Fig 12 :114-115:

> "having terms for what a situation affords was taken for a dictionary and it is a grammar."

So the relevant property of a language is not its list of labels but the bonds it obliges the speaker to encode: sequence (->), comparison (⋛), subtraction (-), identity (≡) and the capacity to refuse (¬). Cross-linguistic work reports effects in spatial reference frames (absolute vs relative), time metaphors (horizontal vs vertical, distance vs volume), grammatical number and classifiers, pronoun dropping and intentionality, and future-tense marking. Each is a bond, not a bare name. By contrast, the color-lexicon findings wash out toward biological universals where the anchor is strong (see F3). This is a prediction of the figures, not a survey result: **strong effects where grammar carries a relation, weak effects where a label merely names.**

**Refuter.** Two languages with identical label sets but different obligatory grammatical bonds show no difference on any non-linguistic task, or two languages with identical bonds but different labels show a large difference on a task with a live instrument. Either would break the grammar-not-dictionary reading.

### F3 - Language extends access; it does not move the world (bounds the extent)

Fig 2 :29:

> "An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks."

Fig 6 :25: "An import adds no atom to the world." Fig 6 :26:

> "The world does not gain primitives; a frame gains access. Composition explores what the operators reach; instruments extend what can be represented at all."

Fig 11 :40: "An improvement that does not change contact changes nothing, however much it improves."

This is the barrier against determinism and against triviality at once. A language cannot change the measured wavelength of a focal color, and it cannot be irrelevant to what the frame can represent. Adding words to a speaker without changing contact is an agent-only improvement; it "changes nothing, however much it improves." The world source on color naming reports that "color perception, unlike other semantic domains, is hardwired into the neural system and as such is subject to more universal restrictions than other semantic domains", and the infant habituation work reports that infants respond to focal hues much as adults do. Those are exactly the link-1 cases where the anchor is live.

**Refuter.** A controlled language manipulation that changes a measurement an independent instrument already pins, with the instrument shown not to update on the language frame. That would break the bound and support determinism.

### F4 - Closure, import and bilingual contact (governs one of the strongest conditions)

Fig 8 :32:

> "Closure is idempotent. Composing inside a closed set never leaves it, so no search, however long, adds an atom."

Fig 6 :23: "No frame escapes its own closure. The bound moves only on contact with a frame whose closure differs." Fig 8 :45: "Two frames together reach what neither reaches alone." Fig 8 :53: "Residual first, frame second."

A monolingual frame searching its own vocabulary cannot manufacture a new perceptual distinction. A second language is contact with a frame whose closure differs, so import becomes possible. The world literature reports that "Experimental research suggests that this linguistic influence on thought diminishes over time, as when speakers of one language are exposed to another" and that bilinguals "can flexibly switch between different conceptual systems, depending on the language they are using." Those are the predicted strong cases under import. The provenance caveat is live: Fig 8 :51 says "Convergent derivation and adopted import look identical in the contents. Only the record of where each came from separates them", so a bilingual difference must be sourced before it is called an import.

**Refuter.** A monolingual speaker produces a new perceptual distinction with no instrument, no second frame and no import, and the distinction is settled at the ground. That would break the closure bound.

### F5 - Language and culture are a union compound (governs the strongest design requirement)

Fig 9 :54: "And when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again." Fig 9 :55:

> "A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones."

Fig 2 :40: "agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool." Fig 9 :60: "Use filters for the budget and witnesses for the verdict. Never let a filter hand you a verdict."

Between-group designs confound language with ecology, subsistence and social norms. The future-tense savings literature is the named case: a study found effects, but a within-language test using "will" found none, and the report notes the effect "may be due to culture or other non-linguistic factors." The Symbols table's w ("How far the belief leans on private history versus inherited prior") is the effect term: it mixes two sources and must be decomposed. The strongest design is therefore within-subject language switching, experimental language training, or closely matched communities. A between-culture difference alone is one evidence pool wearing two names.

**Refuter.** A within-subject language switch that reproduces the between-culture effect exactly, with culture held fixed and the ground pinned by a task instrument. That would dissolve the union reading and support a direct language effect.

### F6 - Measure per slot; a global effect score hides the signal

Fig 1 :19: "The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated." Fig 1 :22:

> "Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average."

A question phrased as "how much does language influence perception" invites a single coefficient. The figures forbid it. A strong effect on, say, temporal sequence orientation and a null effect on focal-color discrimination average into a middling global number that describes no slot. Every verdict below is per domain and per slot; they are never averaged.

**Refuter.** A reported global effect with no slot carrying positive live mass R+. Under Fig 1 that is not a weak effect, it is a measurement error.

### F7 - Describe the residual before choosing the frame

Fig 9 :27-28:

> "describe it before you go looking, or any frame you pick will seem to fit"

Fig 9 :30-31: "your words are borrowed too, so describe the effect rather than the cause." Fig 9 :32: "you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself."

The historical Whorfian method often started from a striking grammatical fact and then looked for a cognitive consequence. Fig 9 calls that echo: the frame was chosen before the residual was described. The defensible order is to describe the perceptual failure first (which slot fails to resolve, under which task, with which instrument) and then look for the language frame whose closure predicts its shape. The psycholinguistics literature's own gold standard is consistent: "finding non-linguistic cognitive differences" in speakers of different languages. The linguistic fact cannot be the test and the explanation at once.

**Refuter.** A study that registers the perceptual residual and the predicted language difference before sampling, and the prediction fails. That would falsify the residual-first derivation, not rescue the frame-first one.

### F8 - Gap on magnitude: no figure line governs a coefficient

Fig 13 :134: "Unreachable is a verdict here, not a silence". Fig 6 :30: "outward: does another frame already hold it? The description travels. Fails by being sharpened into terms only we can read. Cast it in effect terms."

After a genuine re-census, figure lines govern the *placement* of the effect (Fig 3), its *kind* (Fig 12), its *bound* (Fig 2, Fig 6, Fig 11), and its *conditions* (Fig 3, Fig 6, Fig 8, Fig 9). No figure line names "language" or returns a magnitude for "extent". The mapping of language onto Γ is an import/binding, not a derivation, and it carries provenance debt (Fig 8 :51). So the honest answer to "how much" is: **gap on magnitude, shape available**. This is a verdict, not a silence.

**Refuter.** A figure line that directly constrains the size of a cross-frame perceptual shift without a binding from the psycholinguistics frame. Until one is shown, no coefficient may be stated as figure-derived.

### F9 - Strain: the anchor is an idealization when the instrument is language-laden

Fig 2 :31-32:

> "Independence, between two frames, is how little they draw on the same evidence."

Fig 2 :35: "Alignment is triangulation, not negotiation. A mirror cannot correct you." Fig 6 :34-35:

> "Where nothing is, there is no edge to extend from, and the search for a proto-instrument is a search for one that is already there."

The "color is anchored, so language has no effect" reading assumes the instrument is outside the language frame. The essay *They're Scanning Books When They Should Be Studying Nature* says "Every instrument is a crystallized hypothesis about what is worth measuring, and the hypothesis comes from human awareness." An instrument built inside the same linguistic categories is panel 1 of Fig 2, not panel 3: it drifts with the frame and "nobody notices" (Fig 2 :34). Re-census does not dissolve this strain; it relocates it. The claim is not "instruments are neutral" but "the anchor is reachable to the degree that the instrument can be corrected by something other than the language under test". This is named, not hidden, and it is the reason the strong-effect and weak-effect verdicts are `specified`, not `grounded`.

**Refuter.** An instrument used to test a language effect is shown to update on that language's categories (its calibration depends on the same frame). That would collapse the anchor and move the case to Fig 2 panel 1.

## 4. Pre-registered refuter

Required before any outreach, per Seat Map :287: "a falsifier written before the run is a design instrument, not only an honesty device." The shape claim is refuted if any of the following holds:

1. A non-linguistic task with a live, independently calibrated instrument shows a language-dependent shift at a slot where the derivation predicts no shift (breaks F3 and the dependency-order claim).
2. Two languages with the same grammatical bonds but different label inventories show a large non-linguistic difference, or two languages with different bonds and the same inventory show none (breaks F2).
3. A within-subject language switch replicates a between-culture effect with culture and instrument held fixed (breaks F5 and moves the effect from union compound to direct mapping).
4. A monolingual frame mints a new perceptual distinction with no import and no instrument, settled at the ground (breaks F4).
5. No slot carries positive live mass while a global language effect is reported (breaks F6).

If any fires, the conditional shape is withdrawn or re-censused. Until one is run, no part of this may be labelled `grounded`.

## 5. Recommendation

1. Lead with F1 and F2: the placement and the kind of the effect. They are the strongest figure-grounded points and they answer "under what conditions" directly.
2. State the mode on every sentence (F1 to F9 are `specified`; the structural skeleton is `general`; nothing is `grounded`).
3. State the gap on magnitude (F8) openly. Do not supply a coefficient.
4. Name the union compound (F5). Ask for within-subject or matched-community designs, not more between-culture correlations.
5. Name the strain (F9). Ask for the calibration provenance of every instrument used to claim a null language effect.
6. Fig 10 :52: "Bring a characterised residual, never the source." Send the shape claim and its refuter, then the links only after a verdict is recorded.

### Seven-question recursive derivation (summary)

1. **Which link?** Vocabulary is link 2 (Fig 3 :6-7). Language acts before the objective is fixed, not at transduction.
2. **Which kind?** Bonds, not labels (Fig 12 :111-115). The Operators table supplies the carriers: sequence, comparison, subtraction, negation.
3. **Which bound?** The world gains no atom (Fig 6 :25). Language changes access, never the measured ground.
4. **Which conditions?** No instrument at the middle link (Fig 3 :27), and no proto-instrument to extend (Fig 6 :34-35).
5. **Which contact?** A second frame moves the wall (Fig 6 :23), but provenance must separate import from convergence (Fig 8 :51).
6. **Which compound?** Language and culture share a pool (Fig 9 :54-55), so within-subject or matched designs are required.
7. **Which stop?** Shape available, magnitude unreachable (Fig 13 :134, Fig 9 :32). Record the lineage and stop.

### Updated verdict

The shape survives: **language influences perception as a frame-internal grammar that changes what is reachable and how per-slot residuals are categorized, strongest where the middle dependency link has no instrument and where contact with a different closure supplies an import, weakest where an independently calibrated anchor pins the slot.** Determinism is refused (Fig 6 :25); zero effect is refused (Fig 12 :111); a magnitude is not available (F8). After re-census: one live strain (F9, instrument independence), named and not hidden.
