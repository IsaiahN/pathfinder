# Expert verification packet: language and perception

**What this is:** a claim in a form a psycholinguist or linguistic-relativity researcher can check. Mode, derivation and refuters are stated before the conclusion, so a reader knows what to look for and what would settle it.

**Mode: `specified` on the conditional rule, `general` on the structural placement, not `grounded`.** No measurement is claimed. This is a shape and a route, not a result. It is a simulated derivation checked for internal consistency against the figures, not a ground reading.

## 1. The claim, in one paragraph

Language behaves as a frame's vocabulary plus its grammatical bonds. In the dependency chain of Fig 3, vocabulary is link 2, between perception (link 1) and the objective (link 3), and the figures say that this middle link has no instrument. The predicted result is therefore conditional: language effects on perception are strongest where no independent measurement pins the slot and where grammar carries an obligatory relation (sequence, comparison, subtraction, a spatial reference frame, a classifier); they are weakest where an independently calibrated anchor is live and where only labels differ. A second language is contact with a frame whose closure differs, so within-subject switching is a strong test; language and culture form a union compound, so between-culture correlations must be unbundled first. The world gains no primitive, so determinism does not follow, and no magnitude is claimed.

## 2. Three checkable sub-claims

### C1 - framework-internal; checkable by reading Figures 3 and 12

- **Statement:** vocabulary is the uninstrumented middle link, and the effect is carried by bonds rather than by a label inventory.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 3 :25-26 - "The link between them, having terms for what a situation offers and being able to say what would count as progress, has neither, so it attracts the least attention and the fewest instruments." Fig 3 :27 - "A link with no instrument is not a link that works. It is a link nobody has looked at." Fig 12 :111 - "A vocabulary without bonds can name a state and cannot name a route to one." Fig 12 :114-115 - "having terms for what a situation affords was taken for a dictionary and it is a grammar."
- **Refuter:** two languages with the same obligatory bonds but different labels show a large non-linguistic difference, or two languages with different bonds and the same labels show none. Either breaks the bond claim.

### C2 - psycholinguistics-facing; checkable against the experimental literature

- **Statement:** strong cases are the grammatical and relational ones (absolute vs relative spatial reference, temporal metaphors, numeral classifiers, pronoun dropping, thinking for speaking); weak cases are biologically anchored ones (focal color discrimination). A second language is import from a differing closure, and language plus culture is a union compound.
- **What to check:** is the observed difference non-linguistic, per slot, and obtained with a task whose instrument was calibrated independently of the language under test? Is there a within-subject language switch, or only a between-culture correlation? Is the "purity" of the absolute-frame case disputed, as the Guugu Yimithirr entry itself notes?
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 6 :23 - "No frame escapes its own closure. The bound moves only on contact with a frame whose closure differs." Fig 9 :54 - "And when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again." Fig 1 :19 - "The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated."
- **Refuter:** a within-subject language switch that reproduces a between-culture effect with culture and instrument held fixed, or a global effect reported with no slot carrying positive residual.

### C3 - framework-internal; the bound and the live strain

- **Statement:** language extends access, not the world; determinism is refused. The one live strain is that an instrument used to declare a null effect may be calibrated inside the language being tested.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 6 :25 - "An import adds no atom to the world." Fig 6 :26 - "The world does not gain primitives; a frame gains access. Composition explores what the operators reach; instruments extend what can be represented at all." Fig 11 :40 - "An improvement that does not change contact changes nothing, however much it improves." Fig 2 :31-32 - "Independence, between two frames, is how little they draw on the same evidence."
- **Refuter:** a controlled language manipulation that shifts a measurement an independent instrument already pins, with the instrument shown not to update on the language frame. Or the opposite failure: the instrument is shown to be calibrated inside that frame, which collapses the anchor into Fig 2 panel 1.

## 3. What is explicitly not claimed

- Not that language determines perception, and not that different languages yield incommensurable worlds.
- Not that language has no effect, and not that all cross-linguistic differences are naming differences.
- Not a number. No magnitude, percentage or universal effect size is offered. Magnitude is a declared gap.
- Not that language and culture have been separated in the existing literature. They are a union compound until a matched design unbundles them.
- Not that the instruments in the cited studies are neutral. Their calibration provenance is part of the check.
- Not `grounded`. No measurement was taken, and this packet is a simulated derivation.
- Not that the framework derived which grammatical category is responsible. That is a binding from the psycholinguistics frame, provenanced as such.

## 5. What would settle it

A source-discriminating design, not another correlation. Specifically:

1. **Bond-only contrast:** hold the atom set and the task fixed, change one obligatory grammatical bond, and measure a non-linguistic per-slot response.
2. **Within-subject language switch:** randomise task language, match proficiency and culture, and record the residual per slot.
3. **Calibration provenance:** document that the instrument used for the null result can be corrected by something outside the language frame under test.
4. **Standing falsifiers:** a shift at a pinned slot; same bonds but different labels producing a large effect; a within-subject switch reproducing a between-culture effect; a global effect with no live slot.

2. Check C2 against one study you know well: was the dependent measure non-linguistic, per slot, and independently calibrated?

4. Send the refutation. A failure is more useful than an endorsement. The verdict on stage 1 is recorded before any framework material is shown (Fig 10 :52 - "Bring a characterised residual, never the source.").
