## What in the wider literature bears directly on this question

1. **Weak, conditional effects are the current consensus, not determinism.** The relativity article reports that "Some effects of linguistic relativity have been shown in several semantic domains, although they are generally weak", while the psycholinguistics article says the field is "no longer seen as a rigid determinism of thought by language, but rather as a gradual, experience-based modulation of cognition by linguistic structures." That is the same shape as F1 to F3, arrived at in a different field. It is corroboration, not a verdict.
2. **The strongest cases are grammatical and relational.** Obligatory spatial reference frames, temporal metaphors, numeral classifiers and pronoun dropping are bonds in the figures' sense. The Guugu Yimithirr case reports absolute directions, with a note that the "purity" is disputed, which is an honest limit worth keeping.
3. **The weakest cases are biologically anchored.** The color debate reports that infants respond to focal hues much as adults do and that "Berlin/Kay found universal typological color principles that are determined by biological rather than linguistic factors." By the figures this is a live anchor, not a refutation of all language effects.
4. **Contact moves the wall.** The relativity article: "Experimental research suggests that this linguistic influence on thought diminishes over time, as when speakers of one language are exposed to another." The psycholinguistics article reports bilingual flexibility in switching conceptual systems. Fig 6 :23 governs this, and Fig 8 :51 demands provenance.
5. **The instrument problem is real.** The essay *The Hard Problem is About Source* shows that one reading (green) can have several sources, and that only a better instrument resolved it. Applied here, a null result is only as good as the independence of the instrument from the language being tested.
6. **Culture is confounded with language.** The future-tense savings case in the relativity article is the named union compound: a cross-country effect and a within-language null. Fig 9 :54 requires unbundling before searching again.

## The two-stage offer

- **Stage 1 before stage 2** is Fig 10 :52 and Fig 2 :45: "Frames range against the ground. They do not negotiate with it." The reader checks the output without holding the framework's interior.
- **Stage 2 is opt-in by construction.** Seat 4 must not hold the interiors. The stage-1 verdict must be recorded before any theory is shown, and later agreement is never pooled as corroboration (Fig 2 :40).
- **Stage 3 sends a method, which is permitted.** Fig 4 :22: "Only methods cross up. Only head starts come down." The formula is a generator, so giving it away does not give away an answer.
