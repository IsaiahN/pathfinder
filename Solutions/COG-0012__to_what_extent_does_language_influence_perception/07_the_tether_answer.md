# The Tether's answer: to what extent does language influence perception, and under what conditions are its effects strongest?

**Mode: `specified` on the conditional rule and each domain verdict, `general` on the structural placement of vocabulary in the dependency chain, not `grounded`.** Simulated derivation checked against the figures (`05_what_shape_means.md` section 6), not a ground reading. No measurement was taken.

## 1. The residual it answers

We are deciding the size and the conditions of a mapping from a speaker's language to the contents of perceptual slots. Determinism says language fixes perception. Zero effect says language only relabels a ready-made world. The figures refuse both and give a third: language is a frame-internal grammar that changes what a frame can reach and how a per-slot residual is categorized, while the ground stays unchanged. This is a **union compound** with culture, not one hard question: "A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones" (Fig 9 :55). It has its own loop and its own ground.

## 2. The shape

1. **Vocabulary sits at the second dependency link.** Fig 3 :6-7: "it has terms for what things afford: what can be moved, opened, joined, blocked". Fig 3 :27: "A link with no instrument is not a link that works. It is a link nobody has looked at." Perception is link 1, vocabulary is link 2, objective is link 3. The language effect belongs at link 2 and in its consequences downstream, not at transduction.
2. **The carrier is the bond, not the label.** Fig 12 :111: "A vocabulary without bonds can name a state and cannot name a route to one." Fig 12 :114-115: "having terms for what a situation affords was taken for a dictionary and it is a grammar." Obligatory relations (sequence, comparison, subtraction, a frame of reference, a classifier) carry the effect. A label inventory alone mostly does not.
3. **The ground does not move.** Fig 6 :26: "The world does not gain primitives; a frame gains access. Composition explores what the operators reach; instruments extend what can be represented at all." Fig 11 :40: "An improvement that does not change contact changes nothing, however much it improves." So determinism is refused; a speaker cannot change the measured wavelength of a focal color by renaming it.
4. **Strongest conditions.** Effects are strongest where the middle link has no instrument (Fig 3 :25-27), where an ordered bond supplies a route (Fig 12 :71-73), and where contact with a different closure allows an import: Fig 6 :23 "No frame escapes its own closure. The bound moves only on contact with a frame whose closure differs." Bilingual switching and experimental language training are the predicted strong cases, with provenance recorded (Fig 8 :51).
5. **Weakest conditions.** Effects wash out where an independently calibrated anchor pins the slot (Fig 2 :29), where only labels differ, and where the reported difference is a between-culture correlation whose conditions were never unbundled (Fig 9 :54-55). Fig 1 :19: "The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated", so a single global "extent" number is not a legal report.
6. **Magnitude is a gap.** Fig 9 :32: "you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself." Fig 13 :134: "Unreachable is a verdict here, not a silence". No figure line returns a coefficient for "how much". That is a verdict, not an omission.

## 3. What it explicitly does not say

- Not determinism. No incommensurable worlds; the anchor does not update on any frame.
- Not zero effect. Link 2 is in the dependency chain and a vocabulary without bonds cannot compose a route.
- Not a number. No magnitude, no percentage, no universal effect size.
- Not that culture and language have been separated. They are a union compound until a within-subject or matched-community design unbundles them.
- Not that instruments are neutral. See section 4, the live strain.
- Not `grounded`. No experiment was run. The derivation is a simulation.
- Not a claim that the framework derives which grammatical category is responsible. That is a binding from the psycholinguistics frame, provenanced as such.

## 4. What would settle it - the ground

- **Source-discriminating design, not a further argument.** From *The Hard Problem is About Source*: an instrument that reads green "cannot comment on the source"; the spectrometer resolved it. Here the corresponding question is which candidate (grammar, vocabulary, ecology, task framing, instrument calibration) produced a task difference.
- **Within-subject language switch** with culture, proficiency and task language matched. A persistent difference supports the import reading; a vanishing difference moves the case to the union residue.
- **Instrument calibration provenance.** Show that the instrument used to declare a null language effect was not calibrated inside the language categories under test (Fig 2 :31-32). This is the live strain F9: the anchor is reachable only to the degree that the instrument can be corrected by something outside the frame.
- **Bond-only contrast.** Hold the atom set and the task fixed, change only one obligatory grammatical bond, and test a non-linguistic task (Fig 12 :117).
- **Standing falsifiers from the audit:** a language-dependent shift at a pinned slot; same bonds with different labels producing a large effect; a within-subject switch replicating a between-culture effect; a global effect with no live slot; a monolingual mint with no import and no instrument.

## 5. The honest stop

**Unreached, not unreachable.** The eight essays and the figure set are one evidence pool; the second seat in `11_audit.md` shares it, so its agreement is internal consistency, not corroboration. World sources from psycholinguistics are secondary and corroborate the shape, but they cannot settle it. The derivation stops at the limit of what the available instruments can distinguish and records the lineage so the stop is checkable. "Indiscernibility is not identity." The one live strain (instrument independence) is named, not hidden. The magnitude gap is named, not filled.

## 6. One-paragraph version for outreach

> The figures do not say language determines perception, and they do not say it has no effect. They put vocabulary at the second link of a five-link dependency chain, between perception and the objective, and they say that link has no instrument. From that it follows that language's influence should be strongest where no measurement pins the slot and where grammar obliges a speaker to encode a relation (a route, a comparison, a frame of reference), and weakest where an independently calibrated anchor is live and only labels differ. A second language is contact with a different closure, so bilingual switching is a predicted strong case; language and culture together are a union compound, so a between-culture correlation is not yet a language effect. The world does not gain a primitive when a frame gains a term, so no incommensurable worlds follow. What the framework returns is this conditional shape and four written refuters, not a number for "how much". The magnitude stays open until a source-discriminating design settles it, and this derivation is a simulation, not a ground reading.
