## QUESTION

Decide, in effect terms, the extent of the mapping from a speaker's language (a vocabulary plus grammatical bonds) to the contents of perceptual slots, and the conditions under which that mapping is strongest. Option A: determinism, language fixes perception and yields incommensurable worlds. Option B: zero effect, language only labels a ready-made world. Option C: conditional modulation, language changes what a frame can reach and how a per-slot residual is categorized while the ground stays unchanged, with the effect concentrated where no instrument pins the slot. What each leads to: A dissolves the shared anchor that cross-linguistic tests need; B ignores Fig 3's vocabulary link and Fig 12's grammar claim; C gives a per-slot rule, written refuters and a named instrument for each case. The magnitude of the influence is a declared gap.

## DOMAIN CENSUS

R by arity, symmetry and scale, then one line per domain with actors / currency / anchor, partition or union, and order by dependency.

- **R (the residual).** A per-slot mismatch between what a language-framed prediction returns and what an observation returns. Arity: at least two operands (language frame and slot), with culture and instrument as further operands. Symmetry: asymmetric at a level, the frame acts on representation while the world does not change; the inverse mapping from reading to source is lossy. Scale: slot, frame and habitat. Fig 9 names language plus culture a union compound: a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either.
- **Domain 1, the perceptual slot.** Actors: slots s, prediction Γ(b,a), observation o', instruments. Currency: residual R per slot. Anchor: measurement or experiment that does not update on any language frame. Question type: partition, slots kept separate.
- **Domain 2, the language frame.** Actors: speaker, Γ, atoms, bonds, grammatical categories, w. Currency: what composes inside closure(Γ) and its cost. Anchor: none internal; borrowed from domain 1 through the non-linguistic task. Question type: closure and reachability.
- **Domain 3, habitat and transmission.** Actors: community, apprentices, bilingual switching, shared pretext and context. Currency: methods and head starts crossing the membrane, contact. Anchor: separate experience plus the ground. Question type: union, because language and culture share an evidence pool.
- **Domain 4, verification and outreach.** Actors: primary seat, second seat, expert reader. Currency: agreement and residual. Anchor: the ground, never agreement. In this simulated run all four domains draw on one evidence pool, so independence is zero.
- **Order by dependency.** perception (1) -> vocabulary (2) -> objective (3) -> planning (4) -> learn and carry (5). Language acts at link 2, before the objective is fixed. Describe the residual before choosing a frame (Fig 9 :27-28).

Strain test applied: the claim that the anchor is live assumes instrument independence. The essay *They're Scanning Books* says every instrument is "a crystallized hypothesis about what is worth measuring". Re-census does not dissolve the strain; it relocates it to the calibration provenance of the instrument. This is F9 and it stays live.

## VERDICT (from the figures)

The figures give a conditional shape, not an extent. Vocabulary sits at link 2 of the dependency chain, between perception and the objective, and that link has no instrument. Language effects are therefore strongest where no independent measurement pins the slot and where grammar carries an obligatory relation; weakest where a calibrated anchor is live and where only labels differ. A second language moves the wall by import, but language and culture form a union compound, so a between-culture correlation is not yet a language effect. The world gains no primitive, so determinism is refused; link 2 is in the chain, so zero effect is refused. No figure line returns a magnitude: **gap** on "how much", with the shape and the stop recorded. This is `specified`, ungrounded, candidate. The strongest single figure-grounded point is the placement at the uninstrumented middle link (Fig 3 :25-27), triangulated with Fig 12 :111-115 and the instrument-reach rule at Fig 6 :34-35.

- **cost if wrong:** bounded and locatable. The packet is mode-labelled, states refuters before the conclusion and records the lineage, so an error is findable rather than invisible. The real cost is spending an outside reader's independence by sending the framework before the verdict, which Fig 2 :42 prices: "Buy independence in proportion to the cost of being wrong." That cost is avoidable by leading with the shape and its four refuters.
- **undoable:** yes. The conditional rule can be narrowed without touching the figure set: drop the bond claim if C1's refuter fires, drop the import claim if the bilingual switch fails, drop the union reading if a matched design replicates it. No primitive has to be withdrawn, and a sent claim can be superseded by a corrected packet.
- **reversed by:** a ground reading, not agreement. A bond-only contrast showing no non-linguistic per-slot difference would break C1; a within-subject switch replicating a between-culture effect with culture and instrument fixed would break C2; a language-dependent shift at a pinned, independently calibrated slot would break C3 and support determinism; a global effect with no live slot would break Fig 1 :19. A proof that the instrument used for a null result updates on the language frame would move the case into Fig 2 panel 1 and collapse the anchor. Until one of these fires, the verdict stands as `candidate`.
