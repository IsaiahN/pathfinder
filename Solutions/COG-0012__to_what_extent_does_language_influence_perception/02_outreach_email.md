**Subject:** Check one prediction about language and perception before you decide whether the framework is worth reading

> Hi [name],
>
> I have one specific output I would like you to check against what you already know. I am not asking you to read a theory first, and I am not asking you to agree with anything. If the output does not survive, there is nothing else to look at.
>
> **The output (`specified`, not `grounded`):** a language is treated as a frame's vocabulary plus the grammatical bonds it obliges a speaker to encode. A perceptual task is treated as a set of object slots, each with its own residual. The dependency chain puts perception at link 1, vocabulary at link 2, and the objective at link 3. Link 2 is the one the figures say has no instrument: it is "having terms for what a situation offers and being able to say what would count as progress" that "attracts the least attention and the fewest instruments."
>
> The prediction is therefore conditional, not a single number. Language effects on perception should be strongest where no instrument pins the slot and where grammar carries a relation (sequence, comparison, subtraction, a frame of reference, an obligatory category). They should be weakest where an independently calibrated anchor is live, such as focal-color discrimination, and where the reported difference is only the effect of a label. The world is not changed by any of this: a language "gains access", it does not gain a primitive.
>
> **What I am not claiming:** that language determines perception, that speakers of different languages inhabit incommensurable worlds, or that there is a coefficient I can give you. The framework returns a shape, not a name, and it has no line that returns a magnitude for "how much". No measurement was taken here; this is a simulated derivation checked for internal consistency against the figures.
>
> **Two things I have flagged myself:** (1) language and culture are a union compound, so a between-culture correlation is not a language effect; I would want a within-subject language switch or a matched-community design. (2) an instrument used to declare a null language effect may itself be built inside the language categories under test. I would want its calibration provenance before accepting the null.
>
> **What would refute the prediction, written before this message:**
> 1. A non-linguistic task with a live, independently calibrated instrument shows a language-dependent shift at a slot where the prediction says no shift.
> 2. Languages with the same grammatical bonds but different labels show a large non-linguistic difference, or languages with different bonds and the same labels show none.
> 3. A within-subject language switch reproduces a between-culture effect with culture and instrument held fixed.
> 4. A global language effect is reported while no individual slot carries positive residual.
>
> If any one holds, I withdraw the conditional claim. A one-line "this fails because..." is more useful to me than agreement. If you would rather not engage, no reply is needed.
>
> **If it survives, and only then:** here are the sources. The figures are the authority, not my summary of them.
> - Verbatim figure text with line numbers (local project file): `FRAMEWORK/reference/03-FIGURES_TEXT.md`
> - Canonical usage rule (local project file): `FRAMEWORK/reference/FIGURES_README_canonical.md`

>

Write down what would refute your result *before* you run it, and state the mode of what you get. A reported failure is more useful to me than a reported success.
