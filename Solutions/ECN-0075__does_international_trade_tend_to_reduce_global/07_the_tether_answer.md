# The Tether's answer: does trade reduce global inequality while raising inequality within nations?

**Mode: `specified` on the structural verdict, `general` on the meta-claim, not `grounded`.** This is a shape and a route, not an empirical result. Simulated derivation checked against the figures (`05_what_shape_means.md` section 6), not a ground reading.

## 1. The residual it answers

A single cause (trade opening) is reported to move inequality in opposite directions at two measurement slots: the world distribution (slot G) and the distribution inside a trading nation (slot N). The apparent contradiction exists only if "inequality" is read as one scalar. Fig 9 :57 "What remains is smaller and it is also categorically different: a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either." The question is a union compound and must be unbundled before it is searched.

## 2. The shape

1. **The residual is per slot.** Fig 1 :19 "The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated." Fig 1 :22 "Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average." A fall in G and a rise in N are two slot-indexed readings with opposite signs, not a contradiction. There is no single "inequality" residual to reconcile.
2. **Unbundle before answering.** Fig 9 :54 "And when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again." Run the global question and the within-nation question as two searches, then recombine. A single lever clears only the region where one condition holds.
3. **Each slot gets its own anchor.** Fig 2 :36 "An anchor is legitimate when the question is constitutively about it." This question is about measured dispersion, so the anchor is data (surveys, tax records, national accounts), not agreement among researchers. Expert opinion is an instrument here, not the anchor.
4. **Name the effect, not the cause.** Fig 9 :30 "your words are borrowed too, so describe the effect". The answerable forms are "dispersion across slot G under a trade shock" and "dispersion within nation k under a trade shock", each with a population, an index and a period.
5. **The mechanism is conditional.** The trade-theory mechanism (Stolper-Samuelson, read this session) predicts that the scarce factor loses, so the sign inside a nation depends on factor endowment and policy. The within-country data likewise show different paths among countries exposed to the same global forces. "Tend to" is therefore a per-slot specification, not a universal direction.
6. **The two effects are one functional group, isomer-like as an analogue.** Fig 12 :123 "functional group. A substructure whose presence fixes the outcome. The group says what kind; the qualifier says which member." The stronger isomer analogy (same cause atom, different slot, different substance) carries a stated falsifier in `06_isomers_and_order.md`.

## 3. What it explicitly does not say

- Not that trade has in fact lowered global inequality or raised within-country inequality in any period. No figure line returns an empirical sign.
- Not that the economics literature is wrong. A critical review (Ravallion 2018) disputes the robustness of both premises; that dispute is recorded as a conflict and sent back to the census, not used to flip the structural verdict.
- Not that agreement among studies corroborates the answer. Most share the same small family of datasets (Fig 2 :40).
- Not that the two inequality components are independent. They decompose within one distribution; the non-aggregation rule governs the residual, not the quantity (Fig 1 :19, and the re-census in `01` F8 and `06` Q4).
- Not `grounded`. This derivation took no measurement.

## 4. What would settle it, slot by slot

The ground, not more argument. Fig 2 :45 "Frames range against the ground. They do not negotiate with it."

- **Slot G:** a robust series for world income dispersion over the trade-expansion period, with the dataset and the between/within decomposition stated, that shows whether dispersion fell or rose. Ravallion's 2018 critique specifically challenges the robustness of the "declining global inequality" result, so the measurement must address that critique rather than assume it away.
- **Slot N:** country-level dispersion series (LIS or PIP) with the same measure before and after the trade shock, split by factor endowment and policy regime, that shows whether dispersion rose, fell, or stayed flat in each group. The Our World in Data within-country article shows U-shaped and L-shaped paths among countries facing the same global forces, so a single pooled sign is unlikely to be the right object.
- **Falsifiers of the shape:** a demonstration that inequality must be treated as one scalar here (breaks F1); that the conjuncts form a partition (breaks F2); or that the two components are the same slot measured twice (breaks the scale distinction).

## 5. The honest stop

**Unreached, not unreachable.** Fig 8 :39 "So a search that finds nothing is never a proof of absence." Fig 13 :134 "Unreachable is a verdict here, not a silence". The figure lines do govern the structure, so this is not a `gap`. What is missing is a measurement in each slot, which is a different kind of thing. The answer stops at the limit of the instruments actually read this session and records the lineage so the stop is checkable. "Indiscernibility is not identity" (canonical README).

## 6. One-paragraph version for outreach

> The Tether does not answer whether trade "really" reduced global inequality or raised inequality at home. It says the question is bundled: world dispersion and within-nation dispersion are two different slots of one measurement, and the framework indexes its residual by slot, never by an aggregate. Opposite signs in two slots are therefore legal, not paradoxical, and the compound has to be unbundled and each part ranged against its own data before the two are recombined. The mechanism's sign is conditional on a country's factor endowment and policy, so "tend to" has to be spoken per slot and per period. What the framework offers is the shape any answer must have, three refuters written in advance, and an honest stop at the point where a figure gives way to a dataset. The empirical direction stays open until the ground settles it.
