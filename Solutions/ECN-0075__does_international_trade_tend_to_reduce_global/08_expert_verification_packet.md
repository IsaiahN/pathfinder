# Expert verification packet: the Tether on trade and inequality

**What this is:** a claim in a form a trade or inequality researcher can check. Mode, derivation and refuters are stated before the conclusion, so a reader knows what to look for and what would settle it.

**Mode: `specified` on the structural verdict, `general` on the meta-claim, not `grounded`.** No empirical sign is asserted. This is a shape and a route, not a result. Simulated derivation checked for internal consistency against the figures, not a ground reading.

## 1. The claim, in one paragraph

The question "does trade reduce global inequality while increasing inequality within trading nations?" bundles two effects at two measurement slots: dispersion across the world population and dispersion within a trading nation. The framework's residual is indexed by slot and never aggregated, so opposite signs in the two slots are not a contradiction. The compound is a union of overlapping conditions and must be unbundled; each part has its own ground in data and its own conditional mechanism. The framework returns the shape of the answer, not the empirical direction, and names three refuters in advance.

## 2. Three checkable sub-claims

### C1: framework-internal; checkable by reading Figure 1

- **Statement:** inequality change must be read per measurement slot, not as one global scalar. A fall in the between-country slot and a rise in the within-country slot are two slot-indexed readings, not a contradiction to be averaged away.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 1 :19 "The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated." Also Fig 1 :22 "Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average." And Fig 1 :28 "The gap is measured per object, not per step. A system that averages across objects has thrown away the only signal that says where to look."
- **Refuter:** show that this domain requires one scalar inequality residual, so that opposite signs across scales genuinely conflict and cannot be read per slot.
- **Low-overlap corroboration (world, secondary):** Simpson's paradox (Wikipedia, read 2026-11-23): "a trend appears in several groups of data but disappears or reverses when the groups are combined." MAUP (Wikipedia, read 2026-11-23): "The resulting summary values (e.g., totals, rates, proportions, densities) are influenced by both the shape and scale of the aggregation unit." These come from statistics and geography, not from the inequality datasets, so they are genuine low-overlap witnesses for the *form* of the claim, not for its empirical content.

### C2: framework-internal derivation; checkable by reading Figure 9

- **Statement:** the two conjuncts form a union with an overlap, not a partition, so they must be unbundled before being searched and recombined only afterwards.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 9 :54 "And when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again." Also Fig 9 :55 "A single lever clears the region where only one condition holds and leaves the intersections standing, because the second condition still binds." And Fig 9 :57 "What remains is smaller and it is also categorically different: a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either."
- **Refuter:** show that the two conjuncts concern disjoint populations with no shared cause, so a partition reading is correct and unbundling adds nothing. Note the actual overlap: people inside a trading nation are also members of the world population, and the same trade flow enters both components.
- **What to check from your own knowledge:** is there a shared cause and a shared population? If the answer is yes, the union reading holds.

### C3: boundary claim; checkable by reading Figure 2 and the figure README

- **Statement:** the empirical direction of either slot is not returned by the figures. The proper anchor is data, and the figures stop at a shape.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 2 :36 "An anchor is legitimate when the question is constitutively about it." Fig 9 :32 "you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself". Fig 13 :134 "Unreachable is a verdict here, not a silence". And the canonical figures README (local): *"what a missing thing would have to look like (a shape, not a name)."*
- **Refuter:** produce a figure line that directly predicts the sign of a trade-induced inequality change for a named slot, or show that the question is constitutively about expert agreement rather than measured dispersion. Neither exists in the extract read this session.
- **What to check:** the world sources already show why this boundary matters. Ravallion 2018 (*Journal of Economic Literature* 56(2), read 2026-11-23) states in his abstract (dash-free excerpts): "questions the thesis of both books" and "This paper also questions the robustness of the evidence for declining global inequality" Our World in Data (read 2026-11-23) reports that within-country paths differ among countries exposed to the same global forces. A figure line that returned one sign would have to override both, which it does not do.

## 3. What is explicitly not claimed

- Not that trade has in fact lowered global inequality or raised within-country inequality in any period.
- Not that the economics literature is wrong, and not that Ravallion 2018 is right. The conflict is recorded and sent back to the census; it does not flip the structural verdict.
- Not that agreement among studies is corroboration. Most draw on the same datasets (Fig 2 :40, read as: "disqualifying for a verdict: agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool").
- Not that the two inequality components are independent. They decompose within one distribution; the non-aggregation rule governs the residual, not the quantity.
- Not that the isomer analogy is a derivation. The measurement slot is not one of Fig 12's seven bonds; `06_isomers_and_order.md` records the analogue and its falsifier.
- Not `grounded`. No measurement was taken by this derivation.
