## 0. The claim under audit, stated as a residual

> Does international trade tend to reduce global inequality while increasing inequality within trading nations?

In effect terms: one action (a change in trade openness) is reported to produce two inequality residuals with opposite signs at two different measurement slots. Slot G is the distribution of income across the world population (the "global" or between-nation comparison). Slot N is the distribution of income within a trading nation. The apparent paradox is created by reading "inequality" as one scalar: if trade reduces inequality, a simultaneous rise looks like an exception or a contradiction.

What an answer would look like, bound in advance (formula step "Direction"): either (A) one scalar, so the two conjuncts genuinely conflict and one must be explained away; or (B) two slots, each with its own residual and its own ground, so opposite signs are legal and the compound must be unbundled before it is searched. The verbatim figure lines below decide between A and B.

---

## 1. Domain census, before choosing any figure line

**R by arity, symmetry, scale.** Arity: two conjuncts (G-down, N-up), each itself a claim about a distribution. Symmetry: inequality is an ordering, a "more and less" comparison, not a symmetric join; the two conjuncts are not interchangeable, since one compares nations and the other compares persons inside a nation. Scale: two scales of the same underlying world income distribution, related by a decomposition (global inequality is conventionally split into a between-country component and a within-country component). This decomposition is a fact from the world frame, not a figure line; it is named here so it cannot smuggle itself in later.

One line per domain, with actors, currency and anchor. A domain with no nameable anchor is not yet a domain.

| domain | actors | currency | anchor | partition or union |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| D1 Measurement / distribution frame | world population, national populations, income or consumption vectors, Gini / Theil / P90-P10 indices, the slots "between" and "within" | magnitude of an ordering (more or less inequality) | household surveys, tax records, distributional national accounts (PIP, LIS, WID) | partition question at the level of *which slot is being measured* |
| D2 Mechanism / trade-theory frame | labour, capital, land, goods, prices, firms, workers, endowments | prices, wages, factor returns | the model plus its empirical tests | not a competitor to D1; supplies the "why" inside each slot |
| D3 Verification / evidence frame | studies, reviews, datasets, expert readers | evidence and residuals | data, never agreement | union of conditions across D1 and D2; independence is low because studies share datasets |

**Order by dependency (Fig 3).** Fig 3 :20 "The chain is ordered by dependency, not by importance. Each link consumes what the one above produces, so effort spent above the break arrives at a step that cannot use it." The order here is: (1) perceive and name the distribution at each slot (what exactly is being measured, and at what aggregation), (2) state what would count as progress in each slot (a fall in G, a rise in N), (3) supply the mechanism that moves each slot, (4) range the whole against the ground. The common failure is to argue about the mechanism at step 3 before steps 1 and 2 have an instrument. Fig 3 :27 "A link with no instrument is not a link that works. It is a link nobody has looked at."

**Partition or union.** The two conjuncts are a **union compound**, not a partition. They overlap because the same trade flow changes both the between-country and the within-country component of the same world distribution; a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either. This is a figure decision, quoted in F2 below, not a preference.

## 2. Findings, with verbatim figure lines and refuters

### F1: The residual is per slot, never aggregated (decisive structural finding)

Fig 1 :19 "The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated."
Fig 1 :22 "Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average."
Fig 1 :28 "The gap is measured per object, not per step. A system that averages across objects has thrown away the only signal that says where to look."

"Global" and "within trading nation" are two object slots. The framework forbids collapsing them into one scalar inequality residual. Therefore a fall in slot G and a rise in slot N are **not** a contradiction: they are two slot-indexed readings that happen to carry opposite signs. Option A in section 0 is refused by the figure set. No re-census can rescue A without discarding Fig 1.

**Refuter.** If this domain requires inequality to be treated as one global scalar, so that the two signs must be reconciled inside one number, Fig 1 does not govern and the structural verdict falls.

### F2: The compound is a union and must be unbundled

Fig 9 :54 "And when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again."
Fig 9 :55 "A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones."
Fig 9 :55 "A single lever clears the region where only one condition holds and leaves the intersections standing, because the second condition still binds."
Fig 9 :57 "What remains is smaller and it is also categorically different: a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either."

The Library of Babel essay (read 2026-11-23) says the same thing in search terms: *"Perhaps your problem was a bundle or conflation of several problems masquerading as one? You will need to search all of those problems individually to find their answers and then combine them in a satisfactory way."* So the question as posed is not one hard empirical question. It is two questions that must be run separately, then recombined. A single lever such as "trade reduces inequality" clears only the region where one condition holds and leaves the overlap standing.

**Refuter.** If G-down and N-up form a genuine partition (disjoint populations, no shared mechanism), a single lever could clear both and unbundling would be unnecessary. The decomposition relation in the world frame would have to be false for that refuter to fire.

### F3: Inequality is a spectrum; the claim is a comparison, and words must be cashed as effects

Fig 13 :20 "ordering, so more and less can be said"
Fig 12 :87 "a threshold relation between two quantities" (the ⋛ comparison bond; the Operators table defines the same glyph)
Fig 9 :30 "your words are borrowed too, so describe the effect"

"Reduce" and "increase" are comparisons over an ordering. The figures license saying more and less; they do not hand us the empirical direction. And because our words are borrowed, we must describe the effect, not the cause: the answerable version is "income dispersion across slot G moves down / up under a trade shock", not "globalization is inequality".

**Refuter.** If "inequality" in this question names something other than an ordering over a population (for example a normative verdict rather than a dispersion), the spectrum reading fails and a different census is owed.

### F4: The anchor for this question is data, not agreement

Fig 2 :29 "An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks."
Fig 2 :36 "An anchor is legitimate when the question is constitutively about it."
Fig 2 :45 "Frames range against the ground. They do not negotiate with it."

This question is constitutively about measured dispersion, so data (surveys, tax records, national accounts) is the right anchor. Expert opinion is only an instrument here, not the anchor, because the question is not about people's judgments. Fig 2 :37-38 (essay-level reading of the figure) makes the distinction: human judgment is the anchor for a question about people, and only an instrument for a question about a world state.

**Refuter.** If the question were really about whether economists find the claim persuasive, expert agreement would be the anchor and this finding would invert. It is not; the question says "tend to", which is a claim about the world.

### F5: Agreement among studies is one evidence pool, not corroboration

Fig 2 :31 "Independence, between two frames, is how little they draw on the same evidence."
Fig 2 :40 "disqualifying for a verdict: agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool"
Fig 2 :42 "Buy independence in proportion to the cost of being wrong."
Fig 7 :35 "A room’s agreement is variation, not a verdict. Selection between rooms is where the ground votes."

Much of the trade and inequality literature draws on the same small family of datasets (World Bank PIP, LIS, WID, and the Milanovic-Lakner global distribution). Studies that use the same series are not independent witnesses even when they never cite each other. Fig 7 :13 "agreement compounds, evidence does not". Our low-overlap witnesses for the structural claim therefore come from statistics and geography, not from more economics papers (see `09_watson_answer.md`).

**Refuter.** If two bodies of work could be shown to rest on genuinely disjoint measurements, their agreement would be corroboration and this finding would be weakened for them. It is a claim about the actual evidence pool, so it is checkable.

### F6: The figures settle the shape, not the empirical tendency

Fig 9 :32 "you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself"
Fig 13 :134 "Unreachable is a verdict here, not a silence"
Fig 8 :39 "So a search that finds nothing is never a proof of absence."
Fig 9 :60 "Use filters for the budget and witnesses for the verdict. Never let a filter hand you a verdict."

The figures decide that the residual is per slot and that the compound must be unbundled. They contain no line that returns the sign of the change in either slot. That is an empirical matter, owed to the ground. This is `unreached`, not `gap`: figure lines do govern the structure, and the missing piece is a measurement, not a primitive. Treating a literature summary as if it were a figure line would be exactly the "filter handing you a verdict" error.

**Refuter.** If a figure line were found that directly predicts the sign of a trade-induced inequality change for a named slot, this section would be narrowed to that line and the empirical part would move from `unreached` toward `specified`. No such line exists in the extract.

### F7: The mechanism's sign is conditional; "tend to" is a specification, not a general law

Fig 9 :30 "your words are borrowed too, so describe the effect"
Fig 3 :19 "Optimising a link that already works cannot rescue one that does not."

The Stolper-Samuelson mechanism (world source, read 2026-11-23) predicts that the scarce factor loses from trade opening, so the sign of the within-country effect depends on a country's factor endowment, not on trade alone. The Our World in Data within-country article (read 2026-11-23) reports that countries exposed to the same global forces followed different inequality paths. The figures frame this as a slot-and-context question (F1), but the conditional sign itself is a world finding, single-source for the figures and marked as such. "Tend to" must therefore be spoken per slot and per sample period.

**Refuter.** If the mechanism delivered the same sign in every country regardless of endowment and policy, the conditional reading would fail and a universal direction could be stated.

### F8: Strain named and resolved: decomposition is not a second frame

An apparent strain: the world frame says global inequality decomposes additively into between and within components, while Fig 2 :31 insists on independence between frames. Are the two slots two frames drawing on the same evidence, which would make their agreement worthless?

Re-census resolves it. Independence is a relation between **frames** (bounded systems with a library), not between **slots of one measurement**. G and N are components of the same distribution, not two theories claiming the same fact. Their relatedness is a measurement fact from the world frame, not a figure line, and Fig 1 :19 governs precisely because residuals must be read per slot even when the underlying quantity decomposes. No figure contradiction; the strain dissolves. It is recorded here so the route stays visible.

## 3. Pre-registered refuter (written before the conclusion)

Required before any outreach: a falsifier written before the run is a design instrument.

The structural shape claim is refuted if any of the following holds.

1. Fig 1 :19 does not govern this domain, that is, inequality must be treated as one global scalar, so opposite signs across scales are a genuine contradiction.
2. Fig 9 :54 does not govern, that is, the two conjuncts form a partition rather than a union, so unbundling is not required.
3. A measurement shows the between and within components are the same slot measured twice, so the scale distinction collapses.

The empirical claim is not protected by a figure refuter at all. It is settled or overturned by ground data: robust series showing global inequality rising and within-country inequality falling over the main trade-expansion period would reverse the common tendency claim. The conflicting world source (Ravallion 2018) already challenges the robustness premise, and the challenge is recorded, not smoothed away.

---

## 4. Recommendation

1. Unbundle before answering. Run slot G and slot N as two searches and recombine only at the end (F2).
2. Describe the effect, not the cause: state which dispersion, which population, which period, which dataset (F3).
3. Use data as the anchor; do not let study agreement stand in for measurement (F4, F5).
4. State the mode on every claim. The structural verdict is `specified`; the empirical signs are `unreached` here (F6).
5. Lead with the shape and its refuter when offering this to a researcher, not with a literature summary (Fig 10 :52 "Bring a characterised residual, never the source.").
