# Outreach draft: check one structural claim before you decide whether the framework is worth reading

**Use:** one-to-one, or as a cover note attached to a submission through a public venue in `03_public_contact_points.csv`. **Not** for bulk send.

**Hard precondition:** do not send until you have stated the two slots and the refuter in your own words. If the stage-1 claim does not survive, there is nothing else to look at.

**Subject:** One structural check on the "trade reduces global inequality, raises inequality within countries" claim

> Hello,
>
> I have one specific output I would like you to check against what you already know. I am not asking you to read the framework first, and I am not asking you to agree with it. If the output does not survive, there is nothing else to look at.
>
> **The output (`specified`, not `grounded`).** The question as usually posed bundles two claims: that trade lowers inequality between nations, and that it raises inequality within nations. The framework's first figure says a residual is indexed by an object slot, not aggregated: *"The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated."* (Fig 1 :19) and *"Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average."* (Fig 1 :22). "Global" and "within a trading nation" are two slots. Their inequality residuals can carry opposite signs without contradiction, in the same way that a treatment can help two subgroups and lose on the pooled data. So the compound must be unbundled and each slot answered against its own data before the two are recombined. Fig 9 :54 puts the unbundling rule as *"And when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again."*
>
> **What this does not claim.** It does not say that trade has in fact lowered global inequality or raised within-country inequality in any particular period. Those are empirical claims about data, and this output is not a measurement. It does not rank the literature, does not claim the economics profession is wrong, and does not offer a new dataset. It returns a shape for the answer, not the answer.
>
> **What would refute the shape claim** (written down before this message was sent):
> 1. showing that inequality must be treated as one global scalar here, so that the two signs genuinely contradict;
> 2. showing that the two conjuncts form a partition rather than a union, so that one lever clears both and unbundling is unnecessary;
> 3. a measurement showing the between and within components are the same slot measured twice, collapsing the scale distinction.
>
> If any of those holds, I will retract the claim. A one-line "this fails because..." is more useful to me than agreement. If you would rather not engage, no reply is needed.
>
> **If it survives, and only then:** here are the sources. The figures are the authority, not my summary of them.
> - Verbatim figure text with line numbers (local extract in this project): `FRAMEWORK/reference/03-FIGURES_TEXT.md`
> - Canonical usage rule (local copy in this project): `FRAMEWORK/reference/FIGURES_README_canonical.md`
> - The eight-step formula (local): `FRAMEWORK/figures/THE_FORMULA.md`
>
> **If you want to use the machinery:** the formula is in the figures link. Run it on a problem you know well and can check against a ground. Write down what would refute your result *before* you run it, and state the mode of what you get. A reported failure is more useful to me than a reported success.

## Why this version is ordered this way

| the move | figure-grounded reason |
|---|---|
| Output first, framework second | Fig 10 :52 "Bring a characterised residual, never the source." A recipient who has absorbed the framework before giving a verdict has joined the frame being checked. |
| Do not ask for agreement | Fig 2 :35 "Alignment is triangulation, not negotiation. A mirror cannot correct you." |
| Pre-registered refuter before the conclusion | A falsifier written before the run is a design instrument. |
| Mode on every substantive claim | `THE_FORMULA.md` mode rule: `general`, `specified`, `grounded`. State which. |
| Empirical direction withheld | Fig 9 :32 "you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself". No figure line returns an empirical sign; claiming one would be a verdict in `general` mode. |
| Data named as the anchor | Fig 2 :36 "An anchor is legitimate when the question is constitutively about it." This question is constitutively about measured dispersion. |
| Links come last, after the stage-1 verdict | Fig 4 :22 and :25: only methods cross up; a recording carried upward looks like knowledge and is a description of one occasion. |
| No request for trust or prestige | *The Recursive Self-Improvement Trap* (essay, read 2026-11-23): *"Prestige can fund an action. It can give an action a budget to be tried. But it cannot settle an action. Only the ground can settle it."* |
