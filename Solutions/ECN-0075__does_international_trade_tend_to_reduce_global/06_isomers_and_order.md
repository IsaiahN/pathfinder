## DERIVATION

### Q1: Union or partition?

Fig 9 :54 "And when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again."
Fig 9 :55 "A single lever clears the region where only one condition holds and leaves the intersections standing, because the second condition still binds."
Fig 9 :57 "What remains is smaller and it is also categorically different: a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either."

The two conjuncts are not disjoint populations: the people in a trading nation are also members of the world population, and the same trade flow enters both components. They therefore form a union with an overlap, not a partition. A single lever such as "trade reduces inequality" acts on one condition and leaves the intersection standing. Both conditions must be searched separately and recombined afterwards. This is `general` on the figure reading, `specified` on the claim that this particular question has the overlapping structure.

**Falsifier:** if the two conjuncts concerned disjoint populations with no shared cause, the partition reading would be correct and unbundling would add nothing.

### Q2: Isomers, or one functional group?

Fig 12 :117 "isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."
Fig 12 :123 "functional group. A substructure whose presence fixes the outcome. The group says what kind; the qualifier says which member."

Take the trade shock as the shared atom and the slot as the arrangement. The two effects then have the same atom, a different arrangement, and a materially different behaviour (one residual falls, the other rises). In the figure's language that is **isomer-like**: same ingredients, different substance, and the difference is carried by something the ingredient list does not record.

But the honesty test bites. Fig 12's bond is a typed relation between two operands, and the seven bonds are conjunction, disjunction, identity, sequence, production, subtraction and comparison. A "measurement slot" is none of these. So the isomer reading is an **analogue at the level of the claim**, not a literal figure derivation and not a mint. The defensible literal claim is the weaker one: the two effects are members of the same functional group (*"trade changes dispersion"*), with different qualifiers naming which slot. The stronger isomer claim is a checkable analogy, not a figure output.

**Falsifier, written before the run:** if changing the slot does not change the substance, that is, if both residuals carry the same sign under the same shock and sample, the isomer analogy fails and only the functional-group claim stands. If a figure bond is found that literally connects a cause to a measurement slot, the analogy would be upgraded to a derivation; none is present in the extract.

### Q3: Dependency order

Fig 3 :20 "The chain is ordered by dependency, not by importance. Each link consumes what the one above produces, so effort spent above the break arrives at a step that cannot use it."
Fig 3 :19 "Optimising a link that already works cannot rescue one that does not."
Fig 3 :27 "A link with no instrument is not a link that works. It is a link nobody has looked at."

The chain for this question is:

1. **Perception**: define the income vector and the population for each slot. What is being measured, from which survey or tax source?
2. **Vocabulary**: name the ordering, that is, the inequality index and the reference period. Without this, "reduce" and "increase" have no referent.
3. **Objective**: state what counts as progress in each slot (a fall in G, a rise in N). This is the middle link that usually has no instrument (Fig 3 :25-27).
4. **Mechanism**: bring in Stolper-Samuelson or any other trade model, with provenance, to explain the direction in each slot.
5. **Verification**: range the result against the data for that slot.

Attempting step 4 before steps 1 to 3 is the named failure: effort spent above the break arrives at a step that cannot use it. This matches the world literature's own lesson that within-country paths differ among countries exposed to the same global forces, because steps 1 to 3 are country-specific.

**Falsifier:** if a mechanism could deliver a determinate sign without a defined slot, index and period, the dependency order would be wrong. No such mechanism appears in the sources read.

### Q4: Decomposition versus per-slot residuals (strain)

The world frame gives an additive decomposition: global inequality is conventionally split into a between-country component and a within-country component (Theil-style decomposition; see the Our World in Data history article read this session). The figures give a non-aggregation rule:

Fig 1 :19 "The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated."
Fig 1 :22 "Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average."

Is this a contradiction? No, and the re-census is worth recording. The world decomposition is a statement about a **quantity** (how a dispersion index splits across groups). The figure rule is a statement about a **residual** (where a prediction fails). A quantity may decompose while the error signal still has to be read per slot. Conflating them would be the flat-formula error of recording ingredients and not arrangement. The strain is dissolved by re-census, not by softening either rule.

**Falsifier:** if a single scalar residual could locate the failure as well as two slot-indexed residuals, Fig 1's rule would be unnecessary here and the re-census would fail.

## CONCLUSION

The bundle is a union, not a partition. The two effects are literally members of one functional group and isomer-like as an analogue, with the analogy carrying a stated falsifier. They must be answered in dependency order, with measurement vocabulary before mechanism. The additivity of the underlying inequality index does not license aggregating the residual. Nothing in this derivation supplies an empirical sign; that is owed to the ground.
