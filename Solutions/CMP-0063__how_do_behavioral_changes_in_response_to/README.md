# Solution CMP-0063

**Question:** How do behavioral changes in response to an epidemic alter the epidemic's own dynamics?

**Source:** complexity science.txt line 78

**Section:** 7. Epidemics, information, and social dynamics

**Mode:** `specified` on the substantive claims, `general` on the structural shape. No measurement in this folder settles the binding, so nothing here is `grounded`. Simulated derivation checked for internal consistency against the figures, not a ground reading.

**Status:** done

**Registry status:** pending

## What this folder is

A figure-grounded package that treats behavioral response and epidemic transmission as one coupled system. It derives a shape from the figures before consulting the literature, then uses five low-overlap world sources only as corroboration. The verdict comes from the figures. The literature is offered as a witness, not as the judge.

## What is deliberately not here

- No personal emails of named individuals.
- No ranking of people by how likely they are to believe the claim.
- No bulk-send plan.

Reason, in the figures' own words: *"High correlation is disqualifying for a verdict and efficient for a convention"* (Seat Map :163). Selecting recipients for receptiveness manufactures the correlation that would make their agreement worthless. The public venues in `03_public_contact_points.csv` are opt-in and ground-visible, not a list of believers.

## Suggested next step

Take `08_expert_verification_packet.md` and `01` to a public venue in `03`. Ask what would refute the shape claim. Record the verdict before showing any theory. A failure is more useful than an endorsement.
