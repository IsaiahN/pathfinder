# The Tether's answer: how behavioral change alters an epidemic's own dynamics

**Mode: `specified` on the substantive claims, `general` on the structural shape. Not `grounded`.** This is a shape and a route, not a measurement. Simulated derivation checked for internal consistency against the figures (`05_what_shape_means.md` §6), not a ground reading.

## 1. The residual it answers

An epidemic produces cases; cases produce information and perceived risk; perceived risk drives protective action; protective action changes contact and transmission; transmission changes cases. Held-fixed-behavior models mispredict peak timing, peak size, final size, R_t, and wave count. The question is whether behavior is an external push on an otherwise autonomous epidemic or a term inside the epidemic's own loop. The figures decide: it is a term inside the loop.

## 2. The shape, per domain (verdicts kept separate, never averaged)

1. **It is a union compound, not one hard problem.** Fig 9 :57: "a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either. It stops being a mixture and becomes a specific compound." The disease loop and the behavior loop overlap, and the overlap has its own loop and its own ground. A pathogen-only lever clears the disease-only region and leaves the intersection standing (Fig 9 :55).

2. **Behavior acts only through contact.** Fig 11 :40: "An improvement that does not change contact changes nothing, however much it improves." Fig 4 :20: "Only the actors, the currency and the anchor change." A response that leaves the field of contact untouched cannot alter the epidemic, however sincerely it is believed to help. This is the load-bearing predicate of the answer, and it is triangulated across the habitat and membrane figures.

3. **The coupling is a cycle, not a chain.** Fig 12 :121: "cycle. The output re-enters as input. The sign of the loop decides whether it closes, and a chain cannot close by construction." A chain cannot represent a process whose output becomes its own input. The sign of the return decides damping versus amplification; the delay around the loop decides whether damping becomes oscillation. Fig 13 :31-32: "a remainder compounding through a chain that amplifies is chaos, and the detail a crossing discards reappears above it as noise."

4. **Behavior keys on perceived risk, held per slot.** Symbols table :20-21: "What the system takes to be the case before it acts. Formed from its own history and from what it inherited, mixed by w. Not the observation." Fig 1 :19: "The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated." The input to the behavioral loop is a belief, not the ground, and it lives in groups and regions, not in a national average.

5. **Waves reopen because a part survives its own use.** Fig 12 :119: "catalyst. A part that survives its own use. Spent and regenerating parts behave differently across uses, and nothing records which a term is." Information that persists after cases fall, or a susceptible cluster left by a previous wave, changes the next wave's conditions. This is the cycle operating normally, not a model defect.

6. **The ground settles it; agreement does not.** Fig 2 :29: "An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks." Fig 2 :35: "Alignment is triangulation, not negotiation. A mirror cannot correct you." Three models fitted to the same case series are one witness when they share a data pool.

## 3. What it explicitly does not say

- Not that any particular sign, delay, or magnitude is the true one. The framework returns a shape, not a name; those require an instrument.
- Not that "awareness" or "risk perception" is a primitive the framework derives. They are bindings from the epidemiology frame onto held terms, provenanced as such.
- Not that distancing, masking, vaccination, and information sharing are interchangeable. They are isomers at the level of contact (Fig 12 :117) and a functional group at the level of the cycle (Fig 12 :124).
- Not that a vaccine or any single lever clears the compound (Fig 9 :55).
- Not that a case curve determines its own source. That is the metamerism error of *The Hard Problem is About Source*.
- Not `grounded`. No measurement in this folder settles left(R,phi) for any real outbreak.

## 4. What would settle it, the ground

A source-discriminating instrument, not a further argument. From *The Hard Problem is About Source*: an instrument that reads green "cannot comment on the source"; the spectrometer resolved it. Here the corresponding tests are the four pre-registered refuters in `01` §6, restated operationally:

- **Contact test:** in a controlled comparison, hold measured contact constant and vary declared protective intent. If the epidemic trajectory still changes, Fig 11 :40 is broken.
- **Sign test:** reverse or remove the behavior-to-contact feedback. If the qualitative dynamics are unchanged, Fig 12 :121 is broken.
- **Perception and slot test:** compare ground-truth-driven with perceived-risk-driven behavior, and aggregate with per-group counts. If both pairs predict equally well, the Symbols-table and Fig 1 claims are broken.
- **Union test:** solve the disease and behavior loops independently and recombine. If no residual is left at the intersection, Fig 9 :57 is broken.

Fig 6 :32: "An instrument is not built from a description. It is improved from a worse instrument already returning something." Each of these tests is an improvement on an instrument already returning a reading (contact surveys, case series, behavioral time series), not a sensor specified from nothing.

## 5. The honest stop

**Unreached, not unreachable.** The shape is in `closure(Γ)`: Fig 11 and Fig 12 already hold the contact rule and the cycle. What is out of reach at this budget is the numerical binding for any named population and pathogen, and no amount of fitting the same case curve adds an atom. The answer stops at the limit of the available instruments and records the lineage so the stop is checkable. The figures README: *"Indiscernibility is not identity."*

## 6. One-paragraph version for outreach

> The Tether does not say that behavior is a sidecar attached to an otherwise fixed epidemic. It says the two form one compound: cases produce information, information changes perceived risk, perceived risk changes contact, and contact changes cases again. That is a cycle, not a chain, and the sign and delay of the return decide whether it damps, oscillates, or amplifies. Behavior can only act by changing contact, and it acts on belief held per group and per region, not on a national average of truth. Waves reopen because part of the response survives its own use. What the framework gives you is that shape, four refuters written in advance, and the kind of instrument that would settle the sign. It refuses to name the sign or the size for your outbreak, because a case curve does not comment on its own source. The center stays open until the ground settles it.
