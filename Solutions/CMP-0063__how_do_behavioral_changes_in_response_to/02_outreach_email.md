# Outreach draft: check the output first, then decide about the theory

**Use:** one to one, or as a cover note attached to a submission through a public venue in `03_public_contact_points.csv`. **Not** for bulk send.

**Hard precondition:** do not send until the four refuters in section 6 of `01_figure_grounded_audit.md` are stated in the recipient's own terms. The offer collapses on contact if the output cannot survive.

---

**Subject:** One checkable claim about behavior in an epidemic, before any theory

> Hi [name],
>
> I have one output I would like you to check against what you already know. I am not asking you to read a theory first, and I am not asking you to agree with anything. If the output does not survive, there is nothing else to look at.
>
> **The output (`specified`, not `grounded`):** when cases rise, people change contact. That change is not an external push applied to an epidemic that otherwise runs on its own; it is a term inside the epidemic's own loop. Cases produce information, information produces protective behavior, protective behavior changes contact and transmission, and transmission changes cases again. This is a cycle, not a chain. Its sign and its delay decide whether the loop damps, oscillates, or amplifies. Behavior responds to perceived risk, held per group and per region, not to a national average of ground truth. This is a **shape**, not a measurement of any particular outbreak.
>
> **What would refute it, written before this message:**
> 1. A behavioral response that leaves measured contact unchanged still changes peak timing, peak size, final size, or R_t in a controlled comparison.
> 2. Reversing the sign of the behavior-to-contact feedback, or removing the feedback, leaves the qualitative dynamics unchanged: same wave count, no bistability.
> 3. Ground-truth prevalence predicts behavior as well as perceived prevalence, and aggregate case counts predict the epidemic as well as per-group counts.
> 4. The disease and behavior loops solve independently and recombine with no residual left at the intersection.
>
> If any one holds, I will retract the claim. A one-line "this fails because..." is more useful to me than agreement. If you would rather not engage, no reply is needed.
>
> **What I am not claiming:** that I can name the sign, the delay, or the magnitude for your disease or your population. The framework returns a shape, not a name. "Awareness" and "risk perception" are bindings from the epidemiology literature onto terms the framework already holds, and I record them as such. I am not claiming three models that agree are three witnesses; if they share a data pool they are one witness with three names.
>
> **If it survives, and only then:** here are the public sources. The figures, not my summary, are the authority.
> - The spread of awareness and its impact on epidemic outbreaks (Funk, Gilad, Watkins and Jansen, PNAS 2009): https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2672559/
> - Oscillations in epidemic models with spread of awareness (Just, Saldana and Xin, 2017): https://arxiv.org/html/1606.08788v2
> - Adaptive coevolutionary networks: a review (Gross and Blasius, J R Soc Interface 2008): https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2405905/
>
> **If you want to use the method:** the formula is public at `FRAMEWORK/figures/THE_FORMULA.md` in the project folder or in the figures repository. Run it on a problem you know well and can check against a ground. Write down what would refute your result *before* you run it, and state the mode of what you get. A reported failure is more useful to me than a reported success.
>
> Thanks for reading,
> [your name]
