## DERIVATION

### Q1 What is the order of the loop, and which bonds express it?

- Operators table :29-33: `→` is sequence, "A then B; B needs A to have happened", with the test "swap them. Does it become a different thing? Yes."
- Operators table :35-39: `⇒` is production, "A produces B; B did not exist before", with the test "is there a B before A fires? No."
- Fig 3 :20: "The chain is ordered by dependency, not by importance."
- The loop reads: cases `⇒` information (information did not exist before the cases); information `→` belief (belief need not follow instantly); belief `→` protective action; action `−` contact (subtraction is the right bond for a reduction); reduced contact `→` reduced transmission; transmission `⇒` new cases.
- Reversing any ordered bond gives a different system: a response that anticipates cases is not the same as one that follows them. So `+` is the wrong bond for this compound. Using `+` would yield Fig 12's collapse 6, the flat formula: "the ingredients are recorded and the arrangement is not".
- **Falsifier, written before the run:** show that reversing the order of two adjacent links leaves the model's qualitative dynamics unchanged. If so, the bond is symmetric and this ordering claim fails.

### Q2 What determines the sign, and why is the sign not the wave count?

- Fig 12 :121: "cycle. The output re-enters as input. The sign of the loop decides whether it closes, and a chain cannot close by construction."
- A risk-averse response (more cases produce more protection) reduces contact as cases rise: a damping, balancing loop. A risk-seeking or fatigue response (information fades, protection falls while cases are still high) increases contact: an amplifying loop. Both are cycles; they differ only in sign.
- The world corroborates the split. The negative-feedback source says "Negative feedback (or balancing feedback) occurs when some function of the output of a system, process, or mechanism is fed back in a manner that tends to reduce the fluctuations in the output" and that "negative feedback systems can still be subject to oscillations. This is caused by a phase shift around any loop." Just et al. prove that information degradation can sustain flare-ups with constant rates.
- **The sign is not the wave count.** A negative loop with a long delay can oscillate; a positive loop can saturate. Conflating sign with wave count would be a strain, so they are held as two quantities: sign governs direction of correction, delay governs phase.
- **Falsifier:** reverse the response sign while holding the delay fixed; if peak timing and wave count are unchanged in a controlled comparison, the sign claim fails.

### Q3 Are the behavioral mechanisms isomers or a functional group?

- Fig 12 :117: "isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."
- Fig 12 :124: "functional group. A substructure whose presence fixes the outcome. The group says what kind; the qualifier says which member."
- Distancing, self-quarantine, mask use, and information sharing are often listed as if they were the same thing. Under Fig 12 they are **not** the same substance if they act on different parts of the contact process: distancing changes who meets whom (network), mask use changes per-contact transmission conditions, information sharing changes belief upstream. Same high-level ingredient list, different bond and different level, so they are **isomers** in the framework's sense: invisible in a formula that records only "behavioral response", decisive in the epidemic curve.
- Written at the level of the cycle rather than the individual act, they are a **functional group**: every member performs the same structural role, feeding perceived risk back into contact. The group says what kind (a feedback response); the qualifier says which member (distancing versus masking versus information sharing).
- **Falsifier:** show two response mechanisms that share the same atom set and the same bond placement yet produce identical epidemic dynamics under every anchor we can measure. Then the distinction is cosmetic and the isomer claim fails.
- Triangulated for the definition (Fig 12 plus Fig 13 :121, "Different routes can arrive at the same substance"), single-source for the application to behavioral mechanisms.

### Q4 What makes a wave close and reopen? The catalyst and the cycle

- Fig 12 :119: "catalyst. A part that survives its own use. Spent and regenerating parts behave differently across uses, and nothing records which a term is."
- Fig 12 :121 (above): the cycle closes when output re-enters as input.
- Gross and Blasius describe two antagonistic effects of rewiring: it isolates infected nodes, lowering prevalence, but also accumulates a tightly connected susceptible cluster, which later permits a new outbreak. That is a cycle with a catalyst: the susceptible cluster survives the wave that produced it. Just et al. show awareness decay and information degradation can produce the same reopening without a time-lag assumption.
- So reopening is not a failure of the model's first wave; it is the cycle's normal operation when a part survives its own use. A response that disappears immediately after cases fall behaves differently from one that persists, and nothing in a curve records which. **Anchoring requirement:** measure whether protective behavior decays before, with, or after the next rise.
- **Falsifier:** a system in which the persisting part is removed still shows the same wave spacing. That breaks the catalyst reading.

### Q5 Where does the union split, and why does one lever not clear it?

- Fig 9 :55: "A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones. A single lever clears the region where only one condition holds and leaves the intersections standing, because the second condition still binds."
- A vaccine clears a large part of the disease-only region. It leaves the intersection standing: people change behavior in response to the vaccine's availability, to the outbreak, and to each other's behavior. A lever aimed only at the pathogen is therefore not a lever aimed at the compound.
- Fig 9 :57: "a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either. It stops being a mixture and becomes a specific compound." This is why `01` refuses to average the two domains and why the four refuters are separate.

## CONCLUSION

The loop's grammar is ordered (`→` and `⇒`, with `−` for contact reduction), not a flat sum. Its sign and its delay are two distinct quantities: sign sets the direction of correction, delay sets the phase. Behavioral response mechanisms are isomers at the level of the contact process and a functional group at the level of the cycle. Waves reopen because a part survives its own use (catalyst) and because information degrades, not because the first wave was mis-specified. Every one of these claims is `specified`, ungrounded, and carries a falsifier written before the run.
