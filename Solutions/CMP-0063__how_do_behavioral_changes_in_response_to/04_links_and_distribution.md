## Three things in the literature that bear directly on the claim

1. **The feedback is already named in the field, but it is not figure-derived.** Funk et al. write "a mutual feedback between information and disease emerges." Gross and Blasius write "a feedback loop between the state and topology of the network is formed." Fig 12 :121 governs the same structure from the framework side, at lower overlap with the epidemiology vocabulary. Two frames converging on the same shape is corroboration, not one frame quoting another.
2. **The sign and the delay are load-bearing, not decoration.** The negative-feedback entry says oscillation comes from a phase shift around the loop; Just et al. prove waves can arise from information degradation with constant rates. This is exactly Fig 12 :121 read with Fig 13 :31-32. It is also the reason the specific sign and delay are refused as names in our answer: the framework gives the cycle and the sign rule, not the numerical value.
3. **Perceived risk, not ground risk, drives behavior.** Bleicher defines the landscape of fear as a spatially explicit distribution of *perceived* predation risk; Funk et al. distinguish awareness of a disease from its mere presence. Both match the Symbols table's `b` ("Not the observation") and Fig 1 :19 (residual per slot). These are independent fields, so the agreement is not one evidence pool speaking twice.

## Distribution: offer, do not push

Figure-grounded reasons:

- **Fig 10 :52**, "Bring a characterised residual, never the source." Send the shape claim and its four refuters first. The literature is stage 2, opt-in only.
- **Fig 2 :35**, "Alignment is triangulation, not negotiation. A mirror cannot correct you." Never ask the recipient whether they agree with the framework.
- **Fig 2 :40**, "agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool." Two people who read the same review are one witness. Record their verdict on stage 1 before any theory is shown, and never pool later agreement.
- **Fig 9 :27-28**, "describe it before you go looking, or any frame you pick will seem to fit." Ask what they would expect to see if the behavioral loop were real before linking the papers.
- **Fig 4 :22**, "Only methods cross up. Only head starts come down." The formula is a generator and can be given away; the conclusion is a recording and should not be pushed.

**Recommended order in a reply:** (1) the shape claim and its four refuters; (2) ask what they would count as a failure; (3) record their answer; (4) only then the three links, with the note that the figures, not this summary, are the authority; (5) if they ask, offer the formula as a method with the instruction to write their own refuter and state their own mode.

## The two-stage offer

- **Stage 1:** the residual in effect terms, the shape claim, and the pre-registered refuter. The recipient checks the output without holding our framework's interior.
- **Stage 2, opt-in:** if curious, the three literature links and the figure text. Their agreement after this point is never pooled as corroboration (Fig 2 :40).
- **Stage 3, opt-in:** the formula as a method to reuse, with the instruction that each user names their own ground and writes their own refuter before running it.

**Hard precondition:** stages 1 to 3 only work if stage 1 survives. The four refuters are in section 6 of `01_figure_grounded_audit.md`. Until one of them is actually run against a ground, this remains `candidate`.
