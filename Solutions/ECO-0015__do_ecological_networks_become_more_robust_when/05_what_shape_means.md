## 1. A shape is a constraint, not a filler

The figures README states the limit: the framework returns "what a missing thing would have to look like (a shape, not a name)". Fig 9 :32 "you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself." Fig 13 :132-133: "A system with a closure can answer that before it searches, and what it returns is a shape rather than a name."

From *The Library of Babel*: "you can cut your search short if the set of books returned is small and descriptive enough that it provides a general shape of the answer that satisfies your question."

So for this question a **shape** is the narrowed form of an answer that can be stated before it can be named. It constrains; it does not fill. The shape here is a set of conditions on arrangements of interaction strengths and loop signs, not a number and not a universal rule. "Weaken everything" and "strengthen everything" are names. "A union of damping and load-bearing coupling, judged per residual" is a shape.

## 2. How the shape is obtained

- *Library of Babel*: use the vocabulary already held that is closest to the thing, look for recurring new terms that overlap or correlate with it, and recurse. "With each new term gained, you narrow the space by definition and deepen your vocabulary."
- Fig 9 :30-31 "your words are borrowed too, so describe the effect rather than the cause." Applied here: describe what weakening a link does to persistence and return time, not what "strength" means in someone else's matrix notation.
- *Decoding Tacit Knowledge*: "When contact isn't possible, the best you can send is a plain description of what you'd expect to see, because anyone can check that against the world."
- *Library of Babel* on bundles: "Perhaps your problem was a bundle or conflation of several problems masquerading as one?" This is the essay's version of Fig 9 :55 and is the reason the weak/strong binary was split in `01` section 1.3.
- *Library of Babel* on stopping: the finish line "will also be the starting place", because by then the answer has been composed through the process of locating it. Here that means the question dissolves into two well-formed condition questions, each with its own instrument.

## 3. What licenses a name

Only two things, both outside the shape itself:

1. **The ground settles it.** Formula step 5: "the ground settles it. Verified only by what pays. A gate is not the ground." For ecology that means a per-slot perturbation measurement, not an eigenvalue from an assumed matrix (Fig 2 :29).
2. **A provenanced import from a frame whose closure differs** (Fig 6 :23, Fig 8 :50-52), with a source gate and a shadow test. Allesina and Tang's tightly-coupled-pairs result is an import from the matrix-stability frame; it is recorded with its provenance and carries the status `specified`, not `grounded`.

*They're Scanning Books* sharpens this: "The real import is not a new term in the existing space. It is a new dimension of the space." Naming a universal strength rule would add a dimension the framework does not hold. It would be a preferred outcome presented as a result.

## 4. Why "weak is good" and "strong is necessary" are both the specific forbidden error

*The Hard Problem is About Source* gives the mechanism. Metamerism: an instrument reads green and "cannot comment on the source" - one green light, or yellow plus blue, or RGB, or CMYK, or a scheme not yet discovered. "If several paths create the same behavior, then behavior is a projection with lossy information." The reading does not determine the source. "An instrument that can't distinguish these cases demonstrates its own inadequacy." It was the spectrometer, not an argument, that resolved it.

Applied here: a measured persistence outcome is a projection. Many different strength arrangements can produce the same "the web persisted" reading. Naming the source as "weak interactions" from that reading is the metamerism error. What is owed is a discriminator, an instrument that measures per-capita strength in situ at the relevant loop scale, not a name.

This is also why the Allesina and Tang (2012) conflict with McCann et al. (1998) is not settled by choosing a side. The two instruments read different projections: asymptotic matrix stability versus oscillation damping away from equilibrium. The source is underdetermined by either reading alone.

## 5. Q: Does the framework license a universal weak/strong rule? ANSWERED, no further question needed

**Nothing in the framework does.** Fig 11 :49 "An actor's valence is relative to the residual, not a property it carries." Fig 11 :51 "No standing list of good actors survives a change of residual." The framework returns a shape: a union of conditions, a per-slot residual, a sign-dependent loop rule, a ground that is a perturbation experiment. A particular strength rule is licensed only by the ground settling a minted term, or by a provenanced import with a shadow test. Until one of those, the rule is the searcher's own contribution and must be recorded as such.

**Does the derivation commit the error the README forbids?** Not if it stops at the shape. The moment it asserts "weak interactions make networks robust", it names a source from a projection and refuses the union. The correct form is the two-condition shape with the slot for the specific web left open.

## 5b. Correction to my own §3 (see `06_isomers_and_order.md`)

I initially wrote that "shape, not a name" forbids naming any particular structural feature in a web. That overstates the rule. The README's phrase governs a **missing** primitive, one not held in Γ. `network`, `gradient` and `spectrum` are all already among the six substrate terms (Fig 13), so referring to a network, a loop, or a gradient is not inventing a seventh term. What remains forbidden is promoting such a term into a universal strength rule without ground settlement. My §3 metamerism point is **not** retracted: the error is naming the source of a persistence reading without a discriminator. Both claims are recorded so neither is quietly lost.

## 6. Two consequences for outreach

**Prestige is not action.** From *The Recursive Self-Improvement Trap*: "Prestige must never be allowed to validate an action directly... Prestige can fund an action. It can give an action a budget to be tried. But it cannot settle an action. Only the ground can settle it. The Ground Maintainer must be blind to prestige." A campaign that gathers well-known ecologists to agree with the shape is the named collapse, not corroboration. The legitimate ask gives the claim a budget to be tried and lets the ground, a perturbation experiment, settle it.

**My own audit is a simulation, not a ground reading.** Same essay: "LLMs can quickly simulate an approximate answer/output with the formula found in the architecture post... However, the truly testable output would need to be derived from the real architecture or working the formula yourself." *The Hard Problem is About Source* adds: "the output is only as good as the validator. The agent, or the tool, or the frame, or the theory, cannot validate itself." So `01` is a simulated derivation, checked for internal consistency against the figures. It is **not** a test of the ecological claim and must be labelled as such. It cannot settle anything it reports on.
