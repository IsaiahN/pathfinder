# WATSON answer: ECO-0015, weak versus strong interactions in ecological networks

**Mode: `specified` on the ecological claims, `general` on the structural metalaws, not `grounded`.** Figures are primary and decide. World sources are secondary, corroborate but never overrule, and a conflict returns the derivation to the domain census. This is a simulated derivation checked for internal consistency against the figures, not a ground reading. No perturbation measurement was taken.

## QUESTION

Do ecological networks become more robust when interactions are weak, or can strong interactions be necessary for stability?

Restated as an effect: for which species, links and loops does R = |Γ(b,a) - o'| stay small when a perturbation arrives, and which arrangements of strengths and signs keep it there? R is indexed per slot, never averaged (Fig 1 :19 "The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated."). Anchor: a perturbation experiment, named as an anchor in Fig 2 :29 "A measurement, an experiment, a counter, a market, a patient outcome." Option A, take the binary at face value and answer "weak" or "strong". Option B, split it into two overlapping conditions and answer with a shape. A delivers a universal rule the figures refuse and discards the slot-level signal; B delivers a checkable union with falsifiers and an open slot until the ground settles it.

## DOMAIN CENSUS

Arity: a relation among a web structure, a perturbation and an outcome, indexed by slot and ordered in time; it carries an ordered bond and a comparison bond, not symmetric ones. Symmetry: "robust" is a threshold relative to a perturbation regime, so the comparison is relative. Scale: the single trophic loop, the local neighbourhood of a species, and the web across a perturbation sequence; the membrane between scales is lossy (Fig 4 :20 "Only the actors, the currency and the anchor change.").

Union, not partition: D1/D2 and D3/D4 form two compounds; the whole is a union of C1 damping and C2 load-bearing coupling (Fig 9 :54 "And when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again."). Dependency order: measure links, name what a link affords, state what counts as progress for a slot, choose the perturbation, carry the result (Fig 3 :20 "The chain is ordered by dependency, not by importance."). Strain test: two candidate readings were re-censused, Fig 13's six physical terms applied to food webs and Fig 12's bit-price applied to interaction magnitude; neither governs after re-census. No unresolved figure strain.

## WORLD (secondary, each source read)

World sources corroborate or conflict but never overrule. Field, author, venue, year, verbatim quote, prediction and status are given for each.

1. **Ecological theory.** Robert M. May, *Will a Large Complex System be Stable?*, Nature 238, 1972. Read via the Nature abstract, 2026-11-23. Quote: "large complex systems which are assembled (connected) at random may be expected to be stable up to a certain critical level of connectance, and then, as this increases, to suddenly become unstable." Prediction: random strong coupling destabilises with complexity. **Corroborates** the need to consider arrangement, but the stability score is model-internal, so it is a filter, not a witness for the verdict.
2. **Ecological theory.** Kevin McCann, Alan Hastings and Gary R. Huxel, *Weak trophic interactions and the balance of nature*, Nature 395, 1998. Read via the Nature abstract, 2026-11-23. Quote: "weak to intermediate strength links are important in promoting community persistence and stability"; "Weak links act to dampen oscillations between consumers and resources"; "many weak interactions and a few strong interactions." Prediction: C1 damping. **Corroborates** C1 and the non-flat distribution. Same theory pool as May, so one witness, not two.
3. **Ecology review.** Kevin Shear McCann, *The diversity–stability debate*, Nature 405, 2000. Read via the Nature abstract, 2026-11-23. Quote: "Both theory and empirical evidence agree that we should expect declines in diversity to accelerate the simplification of ecological communities." Prediction: diversity loss simplifies communities. **Corroborates** the habitat framing at a different residual (diversity, not strength); not used to settle C1 or C2.
4. **Ecological network analysis.** A. M. Neutel, J. A. P. Heesterbeek and P. C. De Ruiter, *Stability in real food webs: weak links in long loops*, Science 296, 2002. Read via PubMed abstract, 2026-11-23. Quote: "long loops contain relatively many weak links" and this "reduces maximum 'loop weight' and thus reduces the amount of intraspecific interaction needed for matrix stability." Prediction: weakness matters through position in a loop. **Corroborates** Fig 12 :121 and Fig 12 :117 at the loop scale. Shares strength-data sources with McCann, so partially overlapping.
5. **Mathematical ecology.** Stefano Allesina and Si Tang, *Stability criteria for complex ecosystems*, Nature 483, 2012. Read via the Nature abstract, 2026-11-23. Quote: "probability of stability for predator–prey networks decreases... if there is a large preponderance of weak interactions"; "Stable predator–prey networks can be arbitrarily large and complex, provided that predator–prey pairs are tightly coupled." Prediction: C2 load-bearing coupling. **Conflicts** with a naive all-weak reading of source 2. The conflict was returned to the census and split by residual: matrix stability versus damping away from equilibrium. Verdict unchanged.
6. **Ecological network analysis.** Si Tang, Shan Pawar and Stefano Allesina, *Correlation between interaction strengths drives stability in large ecological networks*, Ecology Letters 17, 2014. Read via PubMed abstract, 2026-11-23. Quote: "the correlation between the effects of consumers on resources and those of resources on consumers, substantially accounts for their stability." Prediction: arrangement over marginal distribution. **Corroborates** Fig 12 :117. Same Chicago pool as source 5, so not an independent witness for the union.
7. **Field ecology.** R. T. Paine, *Food-web analysis through field measurement of per capita interaction strength*, Nature 355, 1992. Read via the Nature abstract, 2026-11-23. Quote: "documenting on a per capita basis mainly weak or positive interactions, and a few strong interactions." Prediction: the empirical distribution is a few strong plus many weak. **Corroborates** Fig 12 :101 and Fig 13 :48 with field data, low overlap with the pure theory pool.
8. **Physics / nonlinear dynamics.** T. Banerjee, P. S. Dutta and A. Gupta, Physical Review E 91:052919, 2015. Read via PubMed abstract, 2026-11-23. Quote: "These states emerge through spatial synchrony of the oscillating patches in the strong-coupling strength." Prediction: strong coupling can suppress oscillations in a specific regime. **Corroborates** the C2 pole from a low-overlap field; does not transfer a numeric threshold.
9. **Engineering / control theory.** *Small-gain theorem*, Wikipedia summary citing G. Zames 1966 and Georgiou, Khammash and Megretski 1997, fetched 2026-11-23. Quote: "the closed loop system is input-output stable if ‖S1‖⋅‖S2‖<1 and both S1 and S2 are stable by themselves"; the large-gain theorem gives "the minimum loop-gain needed to stabilize an unstable, possibly nonlinear and time-varying, plant; the minimum loop-gain being 1." Prediction: weak gain stabilises stable components, large gain can be required for unstable ones. **Corroborates** the union shape from a very low-overlap field. Tertiary source; primary papers not opened, so this is one weak witness.

## VERDICT (from the figures)

The figures decide the shape, not the empirical binary. The "or" is a union of C1 damping and C2 load-bearing coupling, kept separate and never averaged. Strength carries no valence apart from the residual; an all-weak web is the flat case; loop sign and placement decide closure; a few strong links can be load-bearing; the anchor is a per-slot perturbation experiment, not a model eigenvalue; and which strengths stabilise a named web is a declared `gap`. Mode `specified` with `general` structural parts. The world literature corroborates the two conditions from different pools and conflicts only under a naive all-weak reading, which the sources themselves do not support. This is a candidate, not a ground reading.

- **Cost if wrong:** misdirected diagnosis and intervention. A web failing C2 would be sold uniform weakening and lose load-bearing coupling; a web failing C1 would be told to strengthen links and destabilise loops. In outreach terms, an expert's twenty minutes would be spent refuting a binary that was never the right shape. The cost is bounded and locatable because the refuters are written down before the conclusion (Fig 4 :30 "So take the round trip and measure the gap. Without it you are navigating by dead reckoning: confident about a position nobody checked.").
- **Undoable:** yes. Fig 6 :42 "A refutation adds a term that refuses; it removes nothing, and the reach falls because something was added rather than because anything was deleted." A failed claim is added to the record and no primitive is withdrawn. `07_the_tether_answer.md` and `08_expert_verification_packet.md` already carry the refuters and provenance so a later reader can locate the error.
- **Reversed by:** any of the nine pre-registered falsifiers in `01` section 4, in particular uniform rescaling that removes the union, invariant link-effect sign across a change of residual, a regulated all-zero web, sign reversal at fixed loop gain leaving closure unchanged, or a model metric predicting extinction at a named slot without calibration. A source-discriminating instrument for per-capita interaction strength in situ would move the `gap` toward a name. Agreement or prestige is not such an instrument (Fig 2 :35 "Alignment is triangulation, not negotiation. A mirror cannot correct you.").
