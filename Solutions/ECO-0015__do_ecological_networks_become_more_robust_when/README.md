**Question:** Do ecological networks become more robust when interactions are weak, or can strong interactions be necessary for stability?

**Source:** `Questions/ecology.txt` line 20 (question 15).

**Section:** 2. Species interactions and ecological networks.

**Mode:** `specified` on the substantive ecological claims, `general` on the structural metalaws. Nothing here is `grounded`: no perturbation experiment was read or run for this derivation, and the framework cannot settle the empirical question it reports on.

**Status:** done.

**Honest standing.** This is a simulated derivation checked for internal consistency against the figures, not a ground reading. The essay *The Recursive Self-Improvement Trap* (read 2026-11-23) states that "LLMs can quickly simulate an approximate answer/output with the formula found in the architecture post" and that "the truly testable output would need to be derived from the real architecture or working the formula yourself." Every substantive claim below carries a mode and a refuter.

**Residual in effect terms.** The residual is not a network-wide "stability score". It is R = |Γ(b,a) - o'| measured per slot s, where a is a perturbation, Γ(b,a) is what the web's current interaction structure predicts, o' is what actually happens (persistence, extinction, return time), and s is a species, a link, or a loop. The question's "or" bundles two overlapping conditions, so they are kept separate rather than averaged.

**What the figures decide.** The shapes: a union, not a binary; strength does not carry valence, the residual does; an all-weak web is the flat case and carries nothing; a few load-bearing strong links and many weak links coexist; loop sign and placement decide closure; the anchor is a measured perturbation outcome, not a model-internal eigenvalue. Which strengths stabilise which real web is a declared `gap` that needs an instrument and a ground reading.
