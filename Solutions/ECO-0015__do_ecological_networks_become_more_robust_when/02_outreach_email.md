**Subject:** One checkable claim about weak and strong interactions in food webs

> Hi [name],
>
> I have one specific output I would like you to check against what you already know about ecological networks. I am not asking you to read the framework first, and I am not asking you to agree with it. If the output does not survive, there is nothing else to look at.
>
> **The residual (`specified`):** not "how robust is this web?" but which species, link, or trophic loop keeps a small gap between what the web's interaction structure predicts and what a perturbation actually returns. That gap is measured per slot, never averaged into one web-level score. Averaging across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average.
>
> **The shape claim (`specified`, not `grounded`):** "weak interactions are good, or strong interactions are necessary" is not one binary question. It is a union of two conditions. Many weak links damp consumer-resource oscillations and lower the weight of long loops; a few strong links are load-bearing, and some tightly coupled pairs are what let a complex web persist at all. Strength itself does not carry valence: the same link can stabilise one residual and destabilise another, so the sign and placement of the loop and the perturbation being defended decide the outcome. An all-weak web is the flat case and carries no coupling at all. Which strengths stabilise a named real web is something the framework does not say; that needs a per-slot perturbation measurement.
>
> **What I am not claiming:** that weak interactions are always stabilising, that strong interactions are always destabilising, that a leading eigenvalue from an assumed interaction matrix is the ground, or that this settles the diversity-stability debate. The model score is a filter for which experiment to run, not the verdict. The anchor is a measured persistence or extinction outcome.
>
> **What would refute the shape claim:**
> 1. one intervention that uniformly rescales every interaction strength and makes every tested web robust across perturbation regimes, leaving no categorically different residue;
> 2. a link whose effect on persistence keeps the same sign across a documented change of residual, with magnitude and position held fixed;
> 3. a web with all interaction strengths at zero that still shows network-level regulation;
> 4. a loop whose closure is unchanged when one link's sign is reversed at fixed absolute loop gain;
> 5. one web-level scalar that predicts prospectively which named species will fail under a new perturbation, with no slot-level data.
>
> I wrote these down before sending this. If any one holds, I will retract the claim. A one-line "this fails because..." is more useful to me than agreement. If you would rather not engage, no reply is needed.
>
> **If it survives, and only then:** the sources are below. The figures are the authority, not my summary of them.
> - Figures and formula, branch `general-agent` (SVG, direct download): https://github.com/IsaiahN/tether/tree/general-agent/figures
> - Canonical figure text with line numbers (local extract in this solution): `FRAMEWORK/reference/03-FIGURES_TEXT.md`
> - Worked architecture narrative: https://medium.com/@IsaiahNwukor/arrival-of-the-fittest-the-tether-architecture-1de7edf641ec
>
> **If you want to use the machinery:** the formula is in the figures link. Run it on a web you know well and can check against a perturbation experiment. Write down what would refute your result *before* you run it, and state the mode of what you get. A reported failure is more useful to me than a reported success.
>
> Thanks for reading,
> [name]
