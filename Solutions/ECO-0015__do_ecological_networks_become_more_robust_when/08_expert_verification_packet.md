# Expert verification packet: the Tether on weak versus strong interactions in ecological networks

**What this is:** a claim in a form an ecologist can check. Mode, derivation and refuters are stated before the conclusion, so a reader knows what to look for and what would settle it.

**Mode: `specified` on the ecological claims, `general` on the structural metalaws. Not `grounded`.** No perturbation measurement is claimed. This is a shape and a route, not a result.

**Status:** a simulated derivation checked for internal consistency against the figures, not a ground reading. It cannot settle the claim it reports on.

## 1. The claim, in one paragraph

"Do ecological networks become more robust when interactions are weak, or can strong interactions be necessary for stability?" is not one binary question. It is a union of two conditions: C1, many weak links that damp consumer-resource oscillations and lower the weight of long loops, and C2, a few strong links that are load-bearing and, in tightly coupled predator-prey pairs, can be what allows a large complex web to persist. Strength does not carry valence by itself; the same link can stabilise one perturbation and destabilise another, so the sign and placement of the loop and the residual being defended decide the outcome. An all-weak web is the flat case and carries no coupling. The anchor is a per-slot perturbation experiment, not a model-internal eigenvalue. Which strengths stabilise a named real web is a `gap` that needs an instrument, not a further argument.

## 2. Three checkable sub-claims

### C1: The question is a union and strength carries no fixed valence

- **Statement:** the weak and strong conditions overlap and must be kept separate; the effect of a link's magnitude on persistence is relative to the perturbation, not a property of the magnitude.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 9 :55 "A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones." Fig 11 :49 "An actor's valence is relative to the residual, not a property it carries." Fig 11 :51 "No standing list of good actors survives a change of residual."
- **What to check:** do the two literatures actually measure different quantities? McCann, Hastings and Huxel (1998) study damping away from equilibrium; Allesina and Tang (2012) study the asymptotic probability of matrix stability by interaction type. If they are the same quantity, the union is unnecessary.
- **Refuter:** one intervention uniformly rescaling every strength makes every tested web robust across perturbation regimes with no categorically different residue; or a link's effect on persistence keeps the same sign across a documented change of residual while magnitude and position are fixed.

### C2: Loop sign and placement decide closure, and a few strong links are load-bearing

- **Statement:** a trophic loop is a cycle whose sign governs closure; weakness matters through its position in a long loop, not merely through being small; some strong links are load-bearing, so uniform weakening is unsafe.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 12 :121 "cycle. The output re-enters as input. The sign of the loop decides whether it closes, and a chain cannot close by construction." Fig 13 :48 "And the set is minimal: remove one and something stops." Fig 12 :101 "A bond that costs nothing is a bond that carries nothing, which is the flat case."
- **What to check:** Neutel, Heesterbeek and De Ruiter (2002) report that long loops in real webs contain relatively many weak links, reducing maximum loop weight. Allesina and Tang (2012) report that stable predator-prey networks can be arbitrarily large and complex "provided that predator–prey pairs are tightly coupled". Do those two findings, taken together, support the union rather than a single pole?
- **Refuter:** reversing one link's sign at fixed absolute loop gain leaves loop closure unchanged; or every strong link in a web can be weakened to near zero with measured persistence and function unchanged.

### C3: The anchor is a per-slot ground reading, not a model-internal score

- **Statement:** a leading eigenvalue or a web-level robustness scalar is frame-internal. The anchor is a perturbation experiment with outcomes measured per species, link or loop.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 1 :27 "They are frame-internal. A frame cannot score itself with a quantity it also produces." Fig 2 :29 "A measurement, an experiment, a counter, a market, a patient outcome." Fig 9 :60 "Use filters for the budget and witnesses for the verdict. Never let a filter hand you a verdict."
- **What to check:** can any existing model-derived stability metric predict, prospectively and at a named slot, which species will fail under a new perturbation without ground calibration? If yes, it is an instrument and this sub-claim is false.
- **Refuter:** such a metric, demonstrated on a real web; or one web-level scalar that predicts per-species failure without slot-level data.

## 3. What is explicitly not claimed

- Not that weak interactions are always stabilising, nor that strong ones are always destabilising.
- Not a universal strength threshold, connectance value, or ranking of "good" links.
- Not that an all-weak web is robust. It is the flat case and carries nothing.
- Not that a model eigenvalue is the ground. It is a filter for choosing an experiment.
- Not a resolution of the diversity-stability debate. Diversity and interaction-strength arrangement are different residuals.
- Not a management prescription. Uniform weakening or uniform strengthening can each fail.
- Not `grounded`. No perturbation measurement is asserted here.

## 5. What would settle it

A per-slot perturbation experiment with a source-discriminating instrument, not another argument. From *The Hard Problem is About Source*: an instrument that reads green "cannot comment on the source"; the spectrometer resolved it. Here:

1. Does an in-situ measurement of per-capita interaction strength at the loop scale discriminate damping from load-bearing in a named web?
2. Does weakening the weak links in long loops change measured amplitude and return time as C1 predicts?
3. Does weakening the few strongest links remove a measured function as C2 predicts?
4. Does reversing one link's sign at fixed loop gain change closure as Fig 12 :121 requires?
