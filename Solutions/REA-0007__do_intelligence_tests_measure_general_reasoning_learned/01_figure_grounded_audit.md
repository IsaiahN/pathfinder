## 0. The question under audit, restated as an effect

> "Do intelligence tests measure general reasoning, learned conventions, or familiarity with the test environment?"

- **The reading:** a score, usually a single norm-referenced number (g, IQ, a composite).
- **The three candidate sources:** (A) general reasoning, (B) learned conventions, (C) familiarity with the test environment.
- **The effect to be decided:** whether the reading carries information about which source produced it.
- **What an answer would look like:** a separator that says, per slot and per habitat, under what conditions the reading licenses an inference about capability, which failure class each candidate is, and what instrument would discriminate the sources. Binding this ordinary term is the formula's "Direction" discipline (Fig 5 :62, *"A frame searching for an answer of a particular shape holds that shape as a PREFERENCE rather than as a term"*; the remedy is to write the sought-for shape down and let it accrue a residual).

The first move is therefore not to defence one of A, B or C. It is to census the residual (Fig 9 :26, *"R's own predicates name the habitat that holds φ*").

## 1. Domain census, before any figure line is chosen

### 1.1 R by arity, symmetry and scale

- **Arity:** three candidates, not two. The question is an either/or/three-way, so the first check is whether it is one question with three answers or several questions bundled (Fig 9 :4).
- **Symmetry:** the three candidates are **not** interchangeable. A and B are properties of the test-taker's history and item content; C is a property of the contact between test-taker and harness. Swapping them changes the kind of thing claimed, so the bond is ordered, not symmetric (Fig 12 :92, *"Symmetry is what decides whether an arrangement is a new thing."*). The **score** is symmetric with respect to all three: the same score is produced whichever route fired. That is the whole difficulty.
- **Scale:** the reading is one occasion (a test session); capability is a property of a lineage across a habitat (Fig 11 :39, :36). The question asks a first-order reading to settle a higher-order claim.

### 1.2 One line per domain: actors / currency / anchor

**Domain 1, the scoring frame (psychometrics).** Actors: items, the test-taker, the composite score, the norming sample. Currency: the score and its rank. Anchor: **none nameable from the score alone.** The frame scores itself with a quantity it also produces (Fig 1 :27, *"They are frame-internal. A frame cannot score itself with a quantity it also produces."*). A nameable anchor requires a reading outside the frame that does not move when the frame moves (Fig 2 :29). Until one is named, this is not yet a domain that can settle the question, it is the residual itself.

**Domain 2, capability and habitat.** Actors: the agent, the habitat (items, rules, exposure history, testing context, feedback), and a seat if one maintains the habitat. Currency: contact and cascade, not score points. Anchor: the ground, that is, what the capability does when the harness is removed or changed. Fig 11 :39: *"So the answerable question is not what intelligence is, but what makes a system capable. And capability is a property of agent-and-habitat, never of the agent alone."*

**Domain 3, the verification and outreach frame.** Actors: expert researchers and public venues. Currency: agreement and residual. Anchor: the ground, never agreement (Fig 7 :35, *"A room's agreement is variation, not a verdict. Selection between rooms is where the ground votes."*). In this simulated run all three domains draw on one evidence pool, so this domain's independence is zero (Fig 2 :40).

### 1.3 Partition or union

**Union, not partition.** Fig 9 :4: *"First: is there a fact here? Disagreements that do not shrink with effort, where each rule keeps working well on a different subset, are not one hard question but several well-formed ones. Split it rather than search."* And Fig 9 :54: *"And when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again."* The three candidates overlap: learned conventions are acquired in a habitat; familiarity with the harness is a learned convention about the harness; general reasoning, if it exists as a source, is always exercised through some item content. A single lever (raise the score by coaching) leaves the intersections standing (Fig 9 :57, *"What remains is smaller and it is also categorically different: a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either."*).

Consequence: three separate verdicts, never averaged into a percentage such as "60% reasoning, 25% convention, 15% familiarity". An average is how the live per-slot signal disappears (Fig 1 :22).

### 1.4 Order by dependency (Fig 3)

1. Establish **what the score is** (a reading, an artifact, a projection). Everything else assumes this.
2. Establish **whether the habitat was substituted** and what contact changed (Fig 11 :66).
3. Only then ask **which source produced this reading**, and only with an instrument that discriminates sources.

Fig 3 :20: *"The chain is ordered by dependency, not by importance. Each link consumes what the one above produces, so effort spent above the break arrives at a step that cannot use it."* The middle link here is source-discrimination, and it has no instrument in the test frame; Fig 3 :27: *"A link with no instrument is not a link that works. It is a link nobody has looked at."*

## 3. Findings F1 to F10

### F1 - The question is a union, not a three-way partition (governs the form of the answer)

Fig 9 :4: *"First: is there a fact here? Disagreements that do not shrink with effort, where each rule keeps working well on a different subset, are not one hard question but several well-formed ones. Split it rather than search."*

Fig 9 :54: *"And when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again."*

Fig 9 :57: *"What remains is smaller and it is also categorically different: a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either."*

Each candidate keeps working on a different subset: familiarity explains retest gains; conventions explain cultural-loading differences; reasoning accounts for the positive manifold. They do not shrink with effort because more data just re-measures the same projection. So the question is several well-formed questions. Any answer that picks one word and rejects the other two is answering a question the figures say is not there.

- **Refuter:** show the three candidates are a partition with an assignable shared fraction, that is, that one source can be isolated with all others held at zero while the score stays interpretable.
- **Mode:** `general` (structural). **Status:** triangulated (Fig 9 and the dependency rule in Fig 3, different figures, low overlap). World corroboration: the Flynn literature's unresolved multiplicity of causes (Trahan et al. 2014 meta-analysis; Wikipedia synthesis).

### F2 - A score is a projection; the source is not recoverable from the reading (DECISIVE, and the governing line)

Fig 13 :128-129: *"Nothing about a composed term tells you, from looking at it, which route produced it or whether that route was available. The space has no last route and every turn begins from a remainder the last one left, so the order is not recoverable from the artefact. It is recoverable from the lineage."*

Fig 12 :5: *"A set of ingredients is not one. The bond is the rest of the information, and it is the half nothing was recording."*

Fig 12 :46: *"A bond is the typed relation between two operands. There are seven, they are not interchangeable, and which one holds is not recoverable from the operands."*

A test score is the artifact with the bond removed. It records the ingredients (items attempted, time, items correct) and not the route that produced them. The route is recoverable only from the lineage: the person's exposure history, the version and norm date of the test, the framing of the instructions, the practice trials, the feedback. *The Hard Problem is About Source* gives the same shape in measurement terms, opened 2026-11-23: *"If several paths create the same behavior, then behavior is a projection with lossy information"*; *"the instrument detects green, and says that it's green. However, the instrument is speaking only about what it measured, it cannot comment on the source."*

Fig 2 :37-38 gives the second figure-side line: *"instruments can be wrong on axes they do not measure."* A test that reads ability is not thereby an instrument for source, which is a different axis.

- **Refuter:** a source-discriminating instrument that, from the score plus a declared lineage, assigns the source uniquely and repeatedly, with the same score produced by the other two sources under controlled conditions. This is the spectrometer that resolves the green reading.
- **Mode:** `general` on the projection claim, `specified` on the prediction that no current score alone carries the source. **Status:** triangulated (Fig 13 :128-129 plus Fig 2 :37-38, low overlap: one is about composition and lineage, the other about instrument axes). World corroboration is strong but shares the framework author for the essay side; the psychometric side (Wicherts et al. 2004 measurement non-invariance; Trahan et al. 2014 order-of-administration effects) is independent.

### F3 - The test environment is a substituted habitat; a synthetic solve proves wiring, never capability

Fig 11 :70: *"A test harness is a substituted habitat. Which is why a synthetic solve proves wiring and never capability."*

Fig 11 :66: *"Isolation is not removal of the habitat. It is substitution of one habitat for another."*

Fig 11 :68-69: *"what you failed to reproduce is invisible until the goal fails"* and *"what you unintentionally introduced is invisible until it acts"*

Fig 4 :25: *"A recording carried upward looks like knowledge and is a description of one occasion."*

A test session replaces the habitat with one the tester authored: fixed items, fixed time, fixed instructions, a fixed reward (a score). Performance in that habitat is real contact, but it is contact with the substitution. Fig 11 :68-69 names the two silent failures: the test may fail to reproduce what capability requires in the world (speeded format, unfamiliar item types, no feedback), and it introduces actors the tester did not choose (testwiseness, guessing strategies, anxiety, stereotype cue). Both are invisible in the score. Coaching research corroborates: raising familiarity raises scores while leaving the rank order of individual differences stable (Stern/prior-knowledge chapter; Kulik et al. 1984; Armstrong and Woodley 2014).

- **Refuter:** an agent-only improvement that moves the ground, that is, a score gain that transfers to an un-authenticated, naturally occurring task with the harness removed and the environment unchanged. Fig 11 :71-72 already names this as the standing refuter of the capability claim: *"an agent-only improvement that moved the ground would refute the capability claim."*
- **Mode:** `specified`. **Status:** triangulated (Fig 11 :70 plus Fig 4 :25, different figures: habitat substitution and membrane direction).

### F4 - "What is intelligence?" is the wrong question; the word is applied afterwards

Fig 11 :35-36: *"The wrong question, and this figure is the replacement for it."* *"Where nothing maintains anything, intelligence is a label applied afterwards to a lineage that met its ground. Nothing was intelligent; selection ran. The word does no work there."*

Fig 11 :39: *"So the answerable question is not what intelligence is, but what makes a system capable. And capability is a property of agent-and-habitat, never of the agent alone."*

Fig 11 :40: *"A system cannot be made more capable by improving the agent in isolation. An improvement that does not change contact changes nothing, however much it improves."*

Option A of the question, "general reasoning", is the name of a property presumed to live in the test-taker. The figure refuses that as the answerable question. It does **not** say reasoning is unreal; it says the word does no work as an explanation of a score, because what needs explaining is the contact between agent and habitat. Jensen's psychometric caveat is a low-overlap corroboration: *"g itself is not a measure of any kind of knowledge, skills, or strategies. That is one of the most important things one can know about g"* (Jensen 1992, opened via search extraction).

- **Refuter:** a demonstration that an isolated agent-internal variable predicts contact changes across habitats with the habitat held fixed, so that capability can be attributed to the agent alone.
- **Mode:** `general` (the re-grounding), `specified` (the prediction about test scores). **Status:** triangulated (Fig 11 and Fig 4 :20 kernel/actor-currency-anchor rule).

### F5 - Learned conventions are efficient as conventions and disqualifying as verdicts

Fig 2 :41: *"efficient for a convention: agreement about a shared meaning is the product, not a corruption of it"*

Fig 2 :40: *"disqualifying for a verdict: agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool"*

Fig 10 :47: *"a convention that decides an outcome has stopped being one"*

Fig 10 :59: *"an interest in the outcome is unavoidable, an interest in the reading is disqualifying."*

Option B is partly a category error. A test item that presupposes a shared meaning (a word, a convention of "which figure comes next", a culturally loaded analogy) tests fluency in that shared meaning. Agreement there is the product, so it is efficient as a convention. It becomes a defect only when the convention is promoted to a verdict about general reasoning, that is, when a convention decides an outcome. That is Fig 10 :47's named failure. Wicherts et al. 2004 found that gains on culture-loaded subtests (Similarities, Comprehension) were too large to be accounted for by common-factor gains; Gonthier et al. 2021 argue intelligence should not be assessed with tests based on cultural knowledge.

- **Refuter:** show a convention-laden item whose agreement predicts a non-shared, non-conventional ground outcome after the convention has been removed and the frame's stake declared.
- **Mode:** `specified`. **Status:** triangulated (Fig 2 plus Fig 10, different figures). A genuine internal conflict in the world literature (stereotype-threat effect sizes) is recorded in §7 and does not flip this verdict.

### F6 - A single composite aggregates away the per-slot signal and scores itself

Fig 1 :19: *"The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated."*

Fig 1 :22: *"Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average."*

Fig 1 :28: *"The gap is measured per object, not per step. A system that averages across objects has thrown away the only signal that says where to look."*

Fig 1 :27: *"They are frame-internal. A frame cannot score itself with a quantity it also produces."*

A single g or IQ is exactly the aggregate Fig 1 disqualifies. It tells you *that* a discrepancy exists, not *where* to look. The psychometric world corroborates from inside the frame: Naglieri 2020 reports that the factor-based scales have "little-to-no interpretive relevance above and beyond that of general intelligence", so the field falls back on the total score; Jensen 1992 notes g scores are norm-referenced and *"Such scores do not constitute an absolute scale like height or weight."* This is not an argument that psychometrics is wrong; it is the figure's rule applied to a frame that scores itself with its own metric.

- **Refuter:** show that a composite score retains a per-slot residual that changes contact at the slot and can be ranged against an anchor outside the test frame.
- **Mode:** `specified`. **Status:** single-source on the figure side (Fig 1), corroborated by two independent psychometric sources that are themselves one broad pool. Not upgraded to triangulated.

### F7 - The test is not an anchor, because it moves when you push on the environment

Fig 2 :29: *"An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks. A measurement, an experiment, a counter, a market, a patient outcome. If it moves when you push on it, it is not an anchor."*

Fig 2 :37-38: *"It is only an instrument for is this code correct, and instruments can be wrong on axes they do not measure."*

Fig 10 :24: *"the ground does not decay, the channel does"*; Fig 10 :26: *"stale percepts, dropped transients, saturated metrics, pooled readings"*

Scores move with norm date (2.31 IQ points per decade, Trahan et al. 2014), with order of administration, with retesting (five or six points on the same test, Wikipedia synthesis), with test framing (Steele and Aronson 1995), and with coaching (Kulik et al. 1984). Each is a push on the test environment that changes the answer without changing the construct claimed. By Fig 2 :29 the test is therefore an instrument, not an anchor. A legitimate anchor for "is this a good answer for a person" would be human judgment about a person (Fig 2 :37), not a test score about a test score.

- **Refuter:** a score that does not move under any of these pushes while its claimed construct does, so the reading can be pinned.
- **Mode:** `specified`. **Status:** triangulated (Fig 2 anchor test plus Fig 10 channel decay).

### F8 - No figure returns which of the three produced a given score: declared `gap`

Fig 9 :32: *"you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself"*

Fig 13 :137-138: *"it can say the answer is out of reach with the library as it stands"*

Fig 8 :39: *"So a search that finds nothing is never a proof of absence."*

Fig 8 :53: *"Residual first, frame second."*

After a genuine census no figure line assigns a source to a particular score. The figures return the shape a discriminator must have: per-slot, lineage-carrying, source-discriminating, ranged against a non-updating ground. Returning "general reasoning" or "convention" or "familiarity" as *the* answer would be naming where only a shape is available. This is a `gap`, but a verdict, not a silence (Fig 13 :134).

- **Refuter:** a figure line, found on re-census, that governs source attribution directly. None was found; the finding is kept open, not padded.
- **Mode:** `general`. **Status:** `gap` (shape-not-a-name kind, not unreachable in principle: more scoring with the same instrument cannot close it, a different instrument could).

### F9 - F2's "shape, not a name" rule was overstated by me; narrowed here and kept visible

The canonical figures README says the framework returns *"what a missing thing would have to look like (a shape, not a name)"*. In my first pass I read that as forbidding the naming of g, conventions or familiarity. It does not. The phrase governs a **missing** primitive, one not held in Γ. All three candidates are already terms in the psychometric library: g is a named factor, conventions are named shared meanings, familiarity is a named exposure history. Naming them is a **binding from the psychometric frame** onto held terms, which is provenance work, not the invention of a new primitive and not an import that moves a wall (Fig 6 :21, *"MINT composes inside the closure and can never add an atom."*).

What remains wrong is not the naming but the **unproven binding**: calling a score a measure of g, or of convention, or of familiarity, without the lineage and source discrimination of F2. The overstatement is retracted at `05_what_shape_means.md` §6 and `11_audit.md`; the reason is kept.

- **Refuter for the narrowed claim:** show that one of the three candidates is not already a held term in the psychometric library and must be imported as a new primitive.

## 4. Pre-registered refuter (written before the conclusion)

Required by the discipline named in the seat map: *"a falsifier written before the run is a design instrument, not only an honesty device."* The shape claim of this solution is refuted if any of the following holds:

1. A **source-discriminating instrument** exists that assigns the source of a score uniquely from the score plus a declared lineage, with the same score producible by the other two sources under controlled conditions (breaks F2).
2. A **transfer test** shows a score gain that changes contact with a ground outside the harness, with habitat and environment unchanged (breaks F3 and F4; this is Fig 11 :71-72's standing refuter).
3. The three candidates are shown to be a **partition** with an assignable shared fraction, so one lever clears each condition separately (breaks F1).
4. The framework must **admit aggregating** a per-slot residual into a single composite without losing the live slot signal, and the composite ranges against an anchor outside the frame (breaks F6).
5. A **convention is shown to decide a test outcome** without ceasing to be a convention, with the seat's stake declared (breaks F5).
6. A **figure line is found on re-census** that directly governs source attribution for a reading (breaks F8 and closes the gap).

If any fires, the relevant finding is withdrawn and the verdict recomputed. Until one is run, no claim here is labelled `grounded`.

## 5. Recommendation

1. Do not answer the either/or as posed. Re-census as a union and report three per-domain verdicts, never averaged (F1).
2. Lead with F2: the score is a projection and the route is in the lineage. Ask for the lineage, not a bigger score.
3. Treat test-environment familiarity as habitat substitution and demand a transfer test (F3).
4. Refuse the question "what is intelligence" in favour of "what makes this system capable in this habitat" (F4).
5. Keep conventions as conventions; promote one to a verdict only with an external, non-updating anchor and a declared stake (F5).
6. Report the `gap` plainly. Owe a discriminator, do not invent an attribution (F8).
7. Fig 4 :22: *"Only methods cross up. Only head starts come down."* Offer the separator and the refuter, not a conclusion to be believed.
