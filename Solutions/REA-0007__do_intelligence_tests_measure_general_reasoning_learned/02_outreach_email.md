**Use:** one to one, or as a cover letter attached to a submission through the public venues in `03_public_contact_points.csv`. **Not** for bulk send.

**Subject:** One checkable claim about what an IQ score can and cannot tell you

> Hi [name],
>
> I have one specific output I would like you to check against what you already know. I am not asking you to read a theory first, and I am not asking you to agree with anything. If the output does not survive, there is nothing else to look at.
>
> **The output (`general` on the structure, `specified` on the per-domain parts, not `grounded`):** a test score is a projection. It records ingredients (items correct, time, norm date) and not the route that produced them. The route is in the lineage: exposure history, item-content familiarity, retest or coaching history, the norm version, and how the task was framed. Several different routes can produce the same score. So from the score alone you cannot tell whether it came from general reasoning, learned conventions, or familiarity with the test environment. Those are not three competing answers to one question; they are overlapping conditions in one habitat, so they need separate verdicts rather than an average.
>
> Three consequences follow, and each has a failure condition:
> 1. The test environment is a substituted habitat. A good performance in it proves that the wiring works in that harness, and does not by itself prove capability with the harness removed. What would refute this: a score gain that transfers to a naturally occurring task with the environment unchanged.
> 2. Items that run on shared meanings measure a convention. Agreement about a shared meaning is efficient as a convention and worthless as a verdict about the world. What would refute this: a convention-laden item that predicts a non-conventional ground outcome after the convention is removed and the tester's stake declared.
> 3. A single composite aggregates away the per-slot information that would say where to look. What would refute this: a composite that retains a live slot residual and ranges against an anchor outside the test frame.
>
> I wrote these down before sending this. A one-line "this fails because..." is more useful to me than agreement. If you would rather not engage, no reply is needed.
>
> **What I am not claiming:** that intelligence tests measure nothing, that g does not exist, that the Flynn effect is an artifact, or that coaching explains every score. I am not claiming to know which source produced any particular person's score. The framework I am using returns the shape of the discriminator that would settle that, not the attribution itself. I also make no claim to have measured anything here: this is a simulated derivation checked for internal consistency, not a ground reading, so it cannot settle the question it reports on.
>
> **If it survives, and only then:** the figures are the authority, not my summary of them. The relevant verbatim lines are Fig 13 :128-129 (the route is not recoverable from the artifact, it is recoverable from the lineage), Fig 11 :70 (a test harness is a substituted habitat), Fig 2 :40-41 (convention versus verdict), and Fig 9 :4 and :54 (union, unbundle before search). I can send the local figure extract and the source ledger on request.
>
> **If you want to reuse the method:** write down what would refute your own result before you run it, and state whether your claim is general, specified, or grounded. The framework's own rule is that a loop cannot check the direction of its own question, so an unwritten expectation will smuggle itself in as a finding.
