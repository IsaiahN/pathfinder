## Wider literature that bears on the question

- **Test familiarity and retesting** (Wikipedia synthesis; Armstrong and Woodley 2014; Kulik et al. 1984). A score can rise without the construct rising. This is the habitat-substitution finding of `01` F3 at the population scale.
- **Norm obsolescence and measurement non-invariance** (Trahan et al. 2014; Wicherts et al. 2004 via the Wikipedia reference list; Gonthier et al. 2021 via search index). The reading moves when the instrument's dating or cultural loading is pushed, so it fails the anchor test of Fig 2 :29.
- **Framing and situation** (Steele and Aronson 1995 via Schmader et al. 2008; the operational meta-analysis above). The same items yield different scores under different descriptions, which is direct evidence that the harness is part of what is measured.
- **Cross-species contextual variables** (Colombo and Scarf 2020; the 2020 Royal Society meta-analysis). Where a test's contextual variables are not accounted for, an apparent difference in general ability collapses. Low-overlap corroboration of agent-and-habitat.
- **Construct critique from inside the field** (Naglieri 2020; van Hoogdalem and Bosman 2024; Jensen 1992). g is a useful summary of a positive manifold, not an absolute scale and not a source attribution.
