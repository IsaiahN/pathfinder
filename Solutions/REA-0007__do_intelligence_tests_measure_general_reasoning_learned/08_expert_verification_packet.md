# Expert verification packet: the Tether on what intelligence tests measure

**What this is:** a claim in a form a researcher in intelligence testing, psychometrics, or comparative cognition can check. Mode, derivation and refuters are stated before the conclusion, so a reader knows what to look for and what would settle it.

**Mode: `general` on the structural shape, `specified` on the per-domain verdicts, not `grounded`.** No measurement is claimed. This is a shape and a separator, not a result.

**Status:** simulated derivation checked for internal consistency against the figures, not a ground reading. It cannot settle the question it reports on.

## 1. The claim, in one paragraph

The question is a union of overlapping conditions, so it needs separate verdicts, not an average. A test score is a projection: it records items correct, time and version, and not the bond that produced them; the route is recoverable only from the lineage. The test environment is a substituted habitat, so a good score proves wiring in the harness and does not by itself prove capability with the harness removed. Items that run on shared meanings measure a convention, efficient as a convention and disqualifying as a verdict about the world. Over a shared item set the three candidates are isomers (conditional on that set), which is why the score cannot distinguish them. What is owed is a source-discriminating instrument; which source produced a particular score is a declared `gap`.

## 2. Three checkable sub-claims

### C1 - framework-internal plus measurement-facing; checkable by reading Figure 13 and by considering any retest design

- **Statement:** a score does not carry its own source. The route is in the lineage.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 13 :128-129 - *"Nothing about a composed term tells you, from looking at it, which route produced it or whether that route was available."* The same line adds *"It is recoverable from the lineage."* Fig 12 :5 - *"A set of ingredients is not one. The bond is the rest of the information, and it is the half nothing was recording."* Fig 2 :37-38 - *"instruments can be wrong on axes they do not measure."*
- **What to check:** does any current score, plus its standard error and subtest profile, record the bond (exposure history, retest history, framing, norm version) rather than only the ingredients? World anchors: retest gains of five or six points (Wikipedia synthesis), order-of-administration effects in the Trahan et al. 2014 meta-analysis, measurement non-invariance in Wicherts et al. 2004, and the green-reading metamerism in *The Hard Problem is About Source*.
- **Refuter:** a source-discriminating instrument that assigns the source uniquely from the score plus a declared lineage, with the same score reproducibly produced by the other two sources under controlled conditions.

### C2 - capability-facing; checkable by a researcher who knows transfer designs

- **Statement:** a test harness is a substituted habitat; a good score in it proves wiring, not capability with the harness removed. Capability is agent-and-habitat, never agent alone.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 11 :70 - *"A test harness is a substituted habitat. Which is why a synthetic solve proves wiring and never capability."* Fig 11 :39 - *"capability is a property of agent-and-habitat, never of the agent alone."* Fig 11 :66 - *"Isolation is not removal of the habitat. It is substitution of one habitat for another."* Fig 11 :71-72 - *"an agent-only improvement that moved the ground would refute the capability claim."*
- **What to check:** is there a transfer measurement in the study being discussed, or only a within-harness score? Colombo and Scarf 2020 (Frontiers in Psychology, opened) show that adjusting contextual variables makes apparent species differences in a matching task disappear; this is the mechanism shape at low overlap with psychometrics.
- **Refuter:** a score gain that changes contact with a ground outside the harness while habitat and environment are held unchanged.

### C3 - framework-internal; checkable by reading Figures 2, 9 and 12

- **Statement:** the three candidates are a union of overlapping conditions, and, over the full shared item set, isomers; a convention is efficient as a convention and fails only when promoted to a verdict.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 9 :4 - *"Split it rather than search"*; Fig 9 :57 - *"a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either"*; Fig 12 :117 - *"Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour"*; Fig 2 :41 - *"efficient for a convention: agreement about a shared meaning is the product, not a corruption of it"*; Fig 10 :47 - *"a convention that decides an outcome has stopped being one."*
- **What to check:** is the isomer claim stated conditionally? It holds only when all three candidates are written over the same atom set (strain S3 in `01` §7 and `06` Q1, not hidden). In narrower framings the functional-group reading of Fig 12 :123 applies.
- **Refuter:** show either that the three form a partition with an assignable shared fraction, or that one candidate requires a load-bearing atom the others do not (which kills the isomer claim and leaves the functional-group claim).

## 3. What is explicitly not claimed

- Not that intelligence tests measure nothing, and not that g is unreal. g is a replicated positive manifold; the refusal is of source attribution.
- Not that all Flynn gains are familiarity, and not that all stereotype-threat effects are large in operational settings (the 2016 meta-analysis reports d = -.14 with publication bias; kept as union evidence).
- Not that one of the three words is the true answer. Which source produced any particular score is a declared `gap`.
- Not `grounded`, and not a claim to know any named person's score source.
- Not a claim that the framework forbids naming held terms. "Shape, not a name" governs missing primitives; g, conventions and familiarity are held terms and are named as provenanced bindings (`05` §6, `01` F9).

## 6. What an expert can check in twenty minutes

2. For a study you know well, ask: does it report the lineage and a transfer measurement, or only a within-harness score?

5. Send the refutation. A failure is more useful than an endorsement.
