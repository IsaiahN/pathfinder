# Solution REA-0007

**Question:** Do intelligence tests measure general reasoning, learned conventions, or familiarity with the test environment?

**Source:** `Questions/reasoning.txt` line 10 (question 7).

**Section:** 1. Defining intelligence.

**Mode:** `general` on the structural shape, `specified` on the per-domain verdicts, not `grounded`. Every substantive file is labelled a simulated derivation checked for internal consistency against the figures, NOT a ground reading. This solution cannot settle the question it reports on.

**Status:** done.

**Registry status:** pending.

## The residual in effect terms

We hold a reading (a test score) and three candidate sources that can each produce a high reading: general reasoning, learned conventions, and familiarity with the test environment. The question asks which one the reading measures. The effect is that a single number is being asked to carry a source-identification it cannot carry. From the score alone the three sources are metameric: several routes to one reading, with the route lost in the projection. What is owed is a source-discriminating instrument and the lineage, not a larger score or a fourth opinion.

## What an answer would look like (Direction, bound as an ordinary term)

An answer would be a separator: a set of conditions under which a score licenses an inference about capability in a habitat, plus a named failure class for each of the three candidates, plus the instrument that would discriminate among them and a failure condition written before the run. Anything that merely picks one of the three words and defends it is not an answer under this rule (Fig 9 :30-31, *"your words are borrowed too, so describe the effect rather than the cause"*).

## What is deliberately not here

- No personal emails of named individuals.
- No ranking of people by how likely they are to agree with the derivation.
- No bulk-send plan and no receptiveness column.

Reasons in the figures' own words: Fig 2 :35 *"Alignment is triangulation, not negotiation. A mirror cannot correct you."*; Fig 2 :41 *"efficient for a convention: agreement about a shared meaning is the product, not a corruption of it"* (selecting for agreement manufactures exactly the correlation that disqualifies a verdict); Fig 10 :42 *"A verifier that can reconstruct the claim cannot verify it."* Legally, public opt-in channels avoid the GDPR/CAN-SPAM failure that a scraped list would create.

## Verdict in one line

The either/or is refused as a union (Fig 9 :4, :54-58); a score is a projection whose source is not recoverable from the reading but is recoverable only from the lineage (Fig 13 :128-129); the test environment is a substituted habitat so a synthetic solve proves wiring and never capability (Fig 11 :70); capability is agent-and-habitat, not agent alone (Fig 11 :39-40); conventions are efficient as conventions and disqualifying as verdicts (Fig 2 :40-41). No figure returns which of the three produced a particular score. That is a declared `gap` of the shape-not-a-name kind, and a verdict, not a silence.
