# Isomers, order and the rent-seeking cycle

## DERIVATION

### Q1: Partition or union?

- Fig 9 :4-5: **"Disagreements that do not shrink with effort, where each rule keeps working well on a different subset, are not one hard question but several well-formed ones."**
- Fig 9 :55: **"A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones."**
- Fig 9 :57: **"What remains is smaller and it is also categorically different: a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either."**

The three conditions from the main audit (knowledge of the future residual, cleanliness of the reading, enumeration of the habitat cascade) overlap. A case with a clean reading but an unenumerated cascade is not a harder case of clean reading; it is a different compound. A single lever that only clears the clean-reading region leaves the intersection of clean reading plus substituted habitat standing.

- **Implication for the question's either/or form.** "Can governments pick winners, or do they misallocate" is not a partition of cases. It is two labels applied to different regions of a union. The correct response is to unbundle (Fig 9 :54) and give a verdict per domain, which `01` section 5 does.
- **Falsifier:** demonstrate that the three conditions are mutually exclusive and exhaustive across a defined policy population, with every case in exactly one condition. Then they form a partition and a single lever may be sufficient.

**Status:** `triangulated` (Fig 9 plus Fig 3 dependency order).

### Q2: Are the two allocators isomers?

- Fig 12 :117: **"Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."**
- Fig 12 :94: **"The arrangement is the substance."**
- Write market-led allocation as a price bond between demand and firm over time: `demand → price → firm`. Write state-led allocation as an administrative bond between a published objective and firm over time: `objective → criterion → firm`. Both use demand, capital, firms, time, information and a ground. They differ in the typed relation placed between the atoms.
- Under Fig 12 they are **isomers**, not variants of one allocator with a different setting. That is why "which allocator is better" is not a scalar question without further work: Fig 12 :126-127 says the two currencies, bits of description and actions of testing, **"do not add."**
- Fig 12 :119: **"A part that survives its own use."** A selection office can be a catalyst (spent and regenerating across uses) or a spent part that only looks like one. A policy's ingredient list never records which.
- Fig 12 :123: **"functional group. A substructure whose presence fixes the outcome. The group says what kind; the qualifier says which member."** Both allocators share the functional group `state → relation → firm over time`. The qualifier (price versus criterion) says which member.
- **Falsifier, written before the run:** if the two allocators can be written with identical typed bonds over identical atoms and still differ only by a scalar parameter, the isomer claim fails and the scalar comparison is licensed. Conversely, if either allocator requires an atom the other does not (a market ground on one side, an electoral ground on the other), the fixed-atom condition fails and the relation is composition plus import, not isomerism.

**Status:** `single-source` on the figure definition (Fig 12), `specified`, with the world check unmeasured. This is the one live boundary condition in the folder and it is named rather than hidden.

### Q3: What is the order of composition?

Dependency order follows Fig 3, not importance:

1. **Objective and instrument** (Fig 3 :9-11, link 3). Can the frame say what would count as progress here, in its own terms, and does an instrument exist? This is the usual break: Fig 3 :27: **"A link with no instrument is not a link that works. It is a link nobody has looked at."** In the policy debate, the "did it work" literature often has a welfare metric but no source-discriminating instrument.
2. **Knowledge** (D1). Can the selector read a future residual at all? Fig 6 :32: **"An instrument is not built from a description. It is improved from a worse instrument already returning something."**
3. **Incentive and reading** (D2). Does the reference update on the reader? Fig 2 :13-15.
4. **Outcome** (D3). Only now can the allocation result be assessed. Fig 3 :19: **"Optimising a link that already works cannot rescue one that does not."**

Fig 3 :22-23 warns that a reading taken below the break is not a weak reading: **"A step whose input never arrives cannot be diagnosed, only its predecessor can... It is a reading of nothing."** A study that measures the welfare effect of a program before establishing whether the selection ground was clean is a reading of nothing in this order.

### Q4: The rent cycle

- Fig 12 :121: **"The output re-enters as input. The sign of the loop decides whether it closes, and a chain cannot close by construction."**
- The public-choice description of rent-seeking maps directly onto a positive loop: connection produces selection, selection produces rent, part of the rent purchases the next connection. The loop closes. The framework's own version of the closed positive loop is the coherent-drift panel of Fig 2, where parties update on one another and converge on whoever had the most influence.
- The alternative sign: a selection that pays and then loses the standing to select again. A fixed term, a blind renewal rule, an office that is genuinely spent by its own use. That loop does not close on itself, and it is the structural form of a category-one anchor.
- Fig 12 :121 also says a chain cannot close by construction. A policy described as a linear sequence (announce, select, disburse, evaluate) cannot represent the feedback at all. The loop is invisible in the chain notation, which is why the connection mechanism is invisible in process charts.
- **Falsifier:** exhibit a rent-seeking process whose output does not re-enter as input, or a positive loop that demonstrably never closes across repeated selections. Either refutes the application of Fig 12 :121 here.

**Status:** `triangulated` in shape (Fig 12 cycle plus Fig 2 collapse-1 plus the low-overlap world source on regulatory capture in `09` WORLD), not `grounded`.

### Q5: Habitat, substitution and the asymmetry of introduction

- Fig 11 :61: **"The seat's power is asymmetric. Introduction is available; exclusion is not."** A government can introduce a firm, a subsidy, a tariff. It cannot subtract the habitat, because market contact is a fact about the world and does not consult it.
- Fig 11 :66: **"Isolation is not removal of the habitat. It is substitution of one habitat for another."** Protection is a substituted habitat, not a sheltered version of the same one.
- Fig 11 :70: **"A test harness is a substituted habitat. Which is why a synthetic solve proves wiring and never capability."** A pilot program run inside a protected procurement channel proves that the administrative wiring works. It does not prove that the selected firm can meet a market.
- Fig 11 :49: **"An actor's valence is relative to the residual, not a property it carries."** A large incumbent is a benefactor to a residual defined as export volume and a malfactor to a residual defined as entry and competition. There is no standing list of good firms, matching Fig 11 :51: **"No standing list of good actors survives a change of residual."**

### Q6: Round trip gap R_T

Fig 4 :30: **"So take the round trip and measure the gap. Without it you are navigating by dead reckoning: confident about a position nobody checked."** The Symbols table defines R_T as `gap(x, (T_E ∘ T_A)(x))`.

1. **Sent up (x):** the verbatim figure set used here, chiefly Fig 11 :36/:51 (winner as post-hoc label, no standing list), Fig 2 :13-15 (no-anchor convergence on influence), Fig 9 :55/:57 (union), Fig 7 :35-36 (ground-voted selection), Fig 12 :117/:121 (isomer, cycle).
2. **Abstraction (T_A):** the four-part separator in `01` and `02`.
3. **Concretisation (T_E):** a concrete claim such as "country X's industrial agency picked winners" or "subsidies caused Y jobs."

**R_T is nonzero.** Three things held in the abstraction do not survive the trip back down:

- the **per-slot indexing** (a national gain cannot name a slot);
- the **provenance and shadow test** on any cited success (a recording is not a method);
- the **source-discriminating instrument** (growth does not reveal whether the cause was advantage or subsidy).

One thing survives cleanly: an unanchored reading converges on influence, and a winner is assigned after the ground pays. That is the residue worth sending. R_T was not measured numerically because no ground reading was taken; it is recorded qualitatively as the enumerated set of dropped constraints. Treating the shorthand as identical to the figures would set R_T to zero and commit exactly the dead-reckoning error Fig 4 :30 names.

## CONCLUSION

The question is a union that must be unbundled before it is searched. Market-led and state-led allocation are isomers over a fixed atom set, with a stated falsifier on the fixed-atom condition. The rent-seeking mechanism is a positive cycle invisible in chain notation, and the order of composition runs from instrument and objective to knowledge, reading and outcome. A transferred success is a substituted habitat and proves wiring, not capability. The round trip from the figures to a policy claim loses at least three constraints, and the loss is the honest measurement, not an error to erase.
