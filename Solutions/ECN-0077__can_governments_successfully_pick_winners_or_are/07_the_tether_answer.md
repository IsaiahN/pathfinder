# The Tether's answer: can governments pick winners?

**Mode: `specified` on the per-domain verdicts, `general` on the structural shape, `gap` on the numerical rate. Not `grounded`.** Simulated derivation checked for internal consistency against the figures (`05_what_shape_means.md` section 6), not a ground reading. No policy outcome was measured here.

## 1. The residual it answers

Not "are governments good or bad at industrial policy," but: per firm and per sector slot, what is the gap between a public selection and what the paying ground will settle, once the resources have moved and the cascade has run? Fig 1 :19: **"The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated."** Fig 1 :22: **"Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average."**

The question's either/or form is a union, not a partition. `06` Q1 derives this. Fig 9 :55: **"A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones."** So the answer is four verdicts, not one.

## 2. The four verdicts

### D1: Can a selector know the winner in advance? No, not as a nameable object

Fig 11 :36: **"Where nothing maintains anything, intelligence is a label applied afterwards to a lineage that met its ground. Nothing was intelligent; selection ran. The word does no work there."** A winner is assigned after the ground pays. Fig 6 :23: **"No frame escapes its own closure. The bound moves only on contact with a frame whose closure differs."** A public frame cannot derive the future market verdict from inside itself; it would have to import from the market frame, with a source gate and shadow test.

What is returned is a shape: the conditions a lineage must meet. Not a name. Fig 11 :51: **"No standing list of good actors survives a change of residual."**

**Mode:** `specified`. **Status:** triangulated (Fig 11 plus Fig 6, low overlap).

### D2: Does an unanchored selector reward connections? Structurally, yes; with a real anchor, not necessarily

Fig 2 :13-15: **"no anchor; each updates on the others / they converge, and what they converge on / is whoever had the most influence."** This is not an empirical claim about a named agency. It is the behaviour of any checking arrangement whose reference is another party to the disagreement. Fig 2 :35: **"Alignment is triangulation, not negotiation. A mirror cannot correct you."**

The disqualifying condition is explicit: Fig 10 :59: **"an interest in the outcome is unavoidable, an interest in the reading is disqualifying."** Where the anchor is real (blind published criteria, competitive tender, an independent verifier who cannot reconstruct the pick, a ground that does not move when pushed), the same arrangement can triangulate and connection need not decide.

**Mode:** `specified` (mechanism), `general` (the anchor rule). **Status:** triangulated (Fig 2 plus Fig 10).

### D3: Does misallocation follow? Under at least five conditions, as a union

The conditions are derived in `01` F3 and `06` Q5: unenumerated cascade (Fig 11 :52-53), substituted habitat (Fig 11 :66, :70), asymmetric introduction (Fig 11 :61), aggregated signal (Fig 1 :22), and a convention promoted into a verdict (Fig 10 :47). Each is sufficient in the union; none is a universal majority claim. Where a genuine source-discriminating ground exists, the same instruments can settle a slot instead of distorting it.

**Mode:** `specified` on conditions. The **numerical rate is a `gap`**: no figure line returns a success percentage, and Fig 13 :135 says the framework **"cannot say which primitive is missing, because naming it would mean already holding it."**

### D4: What is the usable output? A separator, not a verdict

1. residual registered per slot, not pooled;
2. a ground that does not update on the selecting frame;
3. two or more low-overlap frames ranging against it separately;
4. a failure condition written before the run (Fig 9 :60: **"Use filters for the budget and witnesses for the verdict. Never let a filter hand you a verdict."**).

And selection is permitted, but only as selection between genuinely differing rooms: Fig 7 :35: **"A room's agreement is variation, not a verdict. Selection between rooms is where the ground votes."** Fig 7 :36: **"The next room must differ from the last, and match the residual it surfaced."**

**Mode:** `general` structural. **Status:** triangulated (Fig 9 plus Fig 7 plus Fig 2).

## 3. What it explicitly does not say

- Not "governments can never pick winners." The maintained-seat case is permitted (D4, `06` Q3).
- Not "all industrial policy is rent-seeking." That is a causal claim the figures do not license; they license the no-anchor mechanism.
- Not a nationality ranking. No country is named as proof, and success stories are recordings of one occasion until provenance is shown (Fig 4 :25).
- Not a numerical success rate, an optimal subsidy, or an industrial target. Those are world-source estimates or `gap`s.
- Not that market allocation is automatically grounded. A market frame can also be an unmaintained habitat where survivorship bias hides the failed firms; the low-overlap witness in `09` WORLD is the Wald aircraft problem.
- Not `grounded`. Nothing here is settled by measurement.

## 4. What would settle it

A **source-discriminating instrument**, not a further argument. From *The Hard Problem is About Source* (opened this session): an instrument that reads green *"cannot comment on the source"*; the spectrometer resolved it. Here the corresponding tests are:

1. **Slot test:** for each supported firm, does an unsubsidized market test separate underlying advantage from the effect of the support? If no discriminator exists, the slot stays a `gap`.
2. **Anchor test:** does the evaluation ground move when the selecting office pushes on it? An anchor that moves is not an anchor (Fig 2 :29-30).
3. **Loop test:** does the selection output re-enter as political input and close a positive cycle (Fig 12 :121)?
4. **Habitat test:** was the policy evaluated in the original habitat or a substituted one, and were the second- and third-order cascades enumerated (Fig 11 :66-70, :52-53)?
5. **Standing falsifiers from `01` section 7:** a selector with an interest in the reading that still separates winners; a pooled gain sufficient to decide a slot; correlated frames carrying full verdict weight; a figure line returning a number.

## 5. The honest stop

**Unreached, not unreachable.** The instrument that would name the source is owed, not forbidden. Fig 8 :39: **"So a search that finds nothing is never a proof of absence."** Fig 13 :134: **"So the system can report that it lacks something rather than producing an answer anyway."** The framework returns the shape any honest answer must have, the four conditions, and the refuters written in advance. It stops where the available instruments stop distinguishing, and records the lineage so the stop is checkable. Prestige, agreement, and country case lists are not such instruments.

## 6. One-paragraph version for outreach

> The Tether does not answer "yes" or "no" to government picking winners, because that question is a union of conditions rather than a binary. It says four things. A winner is a label assigned after a lineage meets its ground, so no standing pre-payout list is licensed. A selector whose reference updates on itself converges on whoever had the most influence, while a selector with a real, unmoved anchor can triangulate. Misallocation follows from a union of conditions, unenumerated cascades, substituted habitats, aggregated signals, conventions that have become verdicts, none of which is a universal majority. And selection itself is allowed, but only as selection between genuinely different rooms where the ground, not the room's agreement, votes. What the framework offers is the four-part separator that any honest answer must satisfy, the failure conditions written in advance, and the source-discriminating instrument still owed. It does not return a success percentage. The slot stays open until the ground settles it.
