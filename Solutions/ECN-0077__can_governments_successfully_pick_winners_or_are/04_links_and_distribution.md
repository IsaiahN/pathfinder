## Figure-grounded distribution rules

- **Fig 4 :22** - *"Only methods cross up. Only head starts come down."* Sending the full framework before anyone has described a residual sends a recording, not a generator. Lead with the separator and its refuter; offer links after.
- **Fig 10 :42** - *"A verifier that can reconstruct the claim cannot verify it."* A recipient who has absorbed the architecture before being asked for a verdict has joined the frame being checked.
- **Fig 9 :27-28** - *"describe it before you go looking, or any frame you pick will seem to fit."* Ask what they would expect to see if the separator were true before giving them any source.
- **Fig 2 :40** - *"disqualifying for a verdict: agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool."* Most industrial-policy economists read the same case literature. Do not count their agreement twice.
- **Fig 2 :42** - *"Buy independence in proportion to the cost of being wrong."* The cost here is a misdirected public budget, so independence is worth paying for: blind criteria, a ground that does not update, an outside reader who cannot reconstruct the pick.

## The two-stage offer

**Stage 1.** Send the four-part separator and the pre-registered refuter. Ask: what would you count as a failure of this test? Record their verdict **before** showing any framework material.

**Stage 2, opt-in only.** If they are curious, send the figure text and the canonical README. The figures are the authority, not this folder's summary.

**Stage 3.** Offer the formula as a reusable method, with the instruction that each user writes their own refuter and states their own mode. Stage 3 material is a generator, so giving it away does not give away an answer.

**Hard precondition.** Stages 1 to 3 only work if the stage-1 output survives. The output here is a separator, not a verdict on industrial policy, and its one `gap` is declared. Do not upgrade it to "industrial policy works" or "governments always fail" in the email.
