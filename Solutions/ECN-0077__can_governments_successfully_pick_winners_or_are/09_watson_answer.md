## QUESTION

Decide, in effect terms, what the Tether says about the either/or: can a government selector name future winners and allocate accordingly, or does the selection necessarily misallocate and reward political connections? Option A asserts that winners can be picked. Option B asserts that misallocation and connection-reward dominate. What each leads to: A requires reading a residual that has not arrived from inside the selector's own closure; B requires a mechanism by which the selector's reference comes to be the selector's own influence. The figures return neither A nor B as a verdict. They return a union of conditions and a four-part separator, with the empirical rate left as a `gap`.

## DOMAIN CENSUS

R by arity, symmetry and scale, then one line per domain with actors, currency and anchor; partition or union; order by dependency.

- **R (the residual).** Per firm and per sector slot, the gap between a public selection and what the paying ground settles once resources have moved. Arity: three or more overlapping conditions (knowledge, cleanliness of reading, habitat cascade). Symmetry: the selection-to-outcome relation is ordered (`→`), while the connection-to-selection correlation is symmetric (`+`), so a pooled correlation cannot choose the arrow. Scale: first order per firm, second order at sector and national welfare, with a lossy transform between. Fig 9 :55 says this is a union, not a partition.
- **Domain 1, selection knowledge.** Actors: public analysts, firm managers, prices, customers. Currency: predictions and residuals per firm slot. Anchor: the paying market, which does not update on the analyst's belief. Question type: partition boundary, what is reachable inside the public closure.
- **Domain 2, political incentive.** Actors: officials, lobbyists, rent-seekers, firms. Currency: influence, rents, careers. Anchor: an unmoved counterfactual such as blind criteria or independent review; where absent, collapse 1 governs. Question type: union, connection and competence overlap.
- **Domain 3, allocation outcome.** Actors: instruments (subsidy, tariff, procurement), firms, habitat. Currency: resources moved, entry, exit, output, welfare. Anchor: the counterfactual welfare or market test. Question type: union of sufficient conditions, with the numerical magnitude a `gap`.
- **Domain 4, instrument and verification.** Actors: auditors, datasets, experimental designs, outside frames. Currency: residuals, refuters, provenance. Anchor: the ground, which does not decay while the channel does. Question type: dependency, it precedes the other three.
- **Order by dependency.** Instrument and objective first (Fig 3 :27), then knowledge (Fig 6 :32), then cleanliness of the reading (Fig 10 :59), then outcome (Fig 3 :19).
- **Strain test.** Fig 11 :36 (post-hoc winner) versus Fig 7 :35 (a seat can select) dissolves on re-census: the first governs the unmaintained habitat with no seat, the second the maintained case with a seat enumerating rooms. No live strain after re-census.

## VERDICT (from the figures)

The either/or is refused as a form. The figures return a union of conditions and a separator, not a side. A winner is a post-hoc label, so no standing pre-payout list is licensed; an unanchored reading converges on influence, while an anchored reading can triangulate; misallocation follows from a union of sufficient conditions rather than one malfunction; and selection is permitted only as ground-voted selection between differing rooms. The numerical success rate is a declared `gap`, of the unreachable-at-this-budget kind rather than underivable in principle. Status: `specified` on the derivation, `general` on the structural shape, `gap` on the number, not `grounded`. The strongest single figure-grounded point is the no-anchor collapse in Fig 2 :13-15, corroborated from public choice and from the low-overlap survivorship literature.

- **cost if wrong:** bounded and locatable. The packet states its mode, writes its refuters before the conclusion and records its lineage, so an error is findable rather than invisible. The real cost is spending the independence of outside readers by sending a conclusion instead of a separator, which Fig 2 :42 prices: "Buy independence in proportion to the cost of being wrong." That cost is avoidable by leading with the refuter.
- **undoable:** yes. The four-part separator can be revised without withdrawing the derivation; `07_the_tether_answer.md` and `08_expert_verification_packet.md` already strip the causal and numerical overstatements. A reply can be superseded, and no primitive has to be retracted.
- **reversed by:** a ground reading, not agreement. A firm-slot discriminator that separates underlying advantage from subsidy effect would name the source; a demonstration that a selector with an interest in the reading still separates winners out of sample would break D2; a demonstration that the three conditions partition a policy population would break the union verdict; a figure line returning a numerical rate would close the `gap`. Until one of these fires, the verdict stands as `candidate`.
