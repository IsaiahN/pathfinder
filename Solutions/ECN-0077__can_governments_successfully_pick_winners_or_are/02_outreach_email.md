# Outreach draft: check the separator before you decide about the theory

**Use:** one-to-one, or as a cover note attached to a submission through the public venues in `03_public_contact_points.csv`. **Not** for bulk send. No personal emails, no list of people selected for likely agreement.

**Hard precondition:** do not send until the retraction in `01` F8 is understood and the pre-registered refuter in `01` section 7 is attached. The output must survive validation or the two-stage offer collapses on contact.

---

**Subject:** Can you break a four-part test for distinguishing picked winners from connected winners?

> Hi [name],
>
> I have one checkable output about the "governments picking winners" debate. I am not asking you to read a theory first, and I am not asking you to agree with a position on industrial policy. If the output does not survive, there is nothing else to look at.
>
> **The output (`specified`, not `grounded`).** The question is not a binary with two opposite answers. It is a union of at least three overlapping conditions: whether the selector can read a future market residual at all; whether the selector's reading is corrupted by its own interest; and whether the policy's cascade through the surrounding habitat was enumerated. A single lever leaves the intersections standing. What the derivation returns is a four-part separator:
>
> 1. the residual is registered **per firm and per sector slot**, not pooled into one national welfare number;
> 2. there is a **ground that does not update** on what the selecting frame thinks, a market test or equivalent that does not move when pushed;
> 3. **two or more frames with low evidence overlap** range against that ground separately, and their agreement is not counted twice;
> 4. a **failure condition is written before the run**, so the result can be refused rather than only confirmed.
>
> Under that separator, two things follow. First, a "winner" is a label assigned after a lineage meets its ground, so no standing pre-payout list is licensed. Second, when the reference updates on the selector instead of on a ground, the selection converges on whoever had the most influence. That is a structural statement about checking arrangements, not an accusation about any named official or agency.
>
> **What I am not claiming:** that governments never succeed at industrial policy; that every connected firm is undeserving; that a particular country or agency proves the case; that the framework returns a numerical success rate. It does not. The numerical rate is a declared gap, and I am not going to invent one.
>
> **What would refute the separator (written down before sending this):**
> 1. a selection rule whose ground moves when the selector pushes on it, yet which still separates winners from connected winners out of sample;
> 2. a pooled welfare gain shown sufficient to decide which individual slot was correctly selected, with no residual left anywhere;
> 3. two frames drawing on the same evidence pool shown to carry full verdict weight, or an outside checker who can reconstruct the claim shown to verify it soundly;
> 4. a figure line that returns a numerical success rate for picking winners, derived from the framework's six-term set.
>
> If any one holds, I withdraw the relevant claim. A one-line "this fails because..." is more useful to me than agreement. If you would rather not engage, no reply is needed.
>
> **If it survives, and only then:** the sources are below. The figures are the authority, not my summary of them.
> - Verbatim figure text with line numbers (local framework file): `FRAMEWORK/reference/03-FIGURES_TEXT.md`
> - Canonical usage rule (local framework file): `FRAMEWORK/reference/FIGURES_README_canonical.md`
> - Public source material on the empirical debate: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Industrial_policy
> - Barwick, Kalouptsidi and Zahur, China's Industrial Policy: an Empirical Evaluation, NBER WP 26075: https://www.nber.org/papers/w26075
>
> **If you want to use the machinery:** the formula is in the figures link. Run it on a policy question you know well and can check against a ground. Write down what would refute your result **before** you run it, and state the mode of what you get. A reported failure is more useful to me than a reported success.
>
> - [name]

## Distribution rule

Stage 1: send the separator and the refuter. Record the verdict before showing theory.
Stage 2, opt-in only: send the three links, with the note that the figures, not the summary, are the authority.
Stage 3: offer the formula as a reusable method, with the instruction that each user writes their own refuter and states their own mode.

Later agreement is never pooled as corroboration (Fig 2 :40). A reply is a conversation, not a witness.
