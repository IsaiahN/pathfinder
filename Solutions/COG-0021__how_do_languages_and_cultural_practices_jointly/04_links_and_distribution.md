## Two findings in the literature that bear directly on the shape claim

1. **Wang et al. separate meanings from relations.** Their result is not that languages have different words for the same things; it is that "languages may express corresponding basic meanings while differing in the relations connecting them." That is Fig 12 :117's isomer (same atoms, different bond, different substance) measured in a corpus-computational instrument with no connection to the Tether. It is the strongest low-overlap corroboration in this folder. Because it is a corpus result, it predicts a psychological test rather than settling one; the paper itself says establishing correspondence to speakers' judgments "remains necessary."

2. **Cibelli et al. supply the discriminating instrument already used in the field.** The apparent language effect on colour memory "disappears when participants are given a verbal (but not a visual) interference task." That is a source-discriminating move of exactly the kind *The Hard Problem is About Source* calls for: an instrument that separates the reading from its source. It also makes the effect conditional on uncertainty, which is why an averaged "language effect" is the wrong object.

## Distribution: offer, do not push

- **Fig 10 :52** "Bring a characterised residual, never the source." Leading with the framework sends a recording before anyone has described a residual. Lead with the shape claim and its refuter; offer the links after.
- **Fig 10 :42** "A verifier that can reconstruct the claim cannot verify it." A recipient who has absorbed the architecture before giving a verdict has joined the frame being checked.
- **Fig 9 :27-28** "describe it before you go looking, or any frame you pick will seem to fit." Ask what they would expect to see if the shape claim were true before showing the article.
- **Fig 2 :40** "disqualifying for a verdict: agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool." Never select recipients by likely agreement, and never pool a later "yes" as corroboration.
- **Seat Map :163** "High correlation is disqualifying for a verdict and efficient for a convention." A shared category system is a convention; asking people to endorse it as a verdict about the world is the named collapse.

**Recommended order in a reply:** (1) shape claim and refuter; (2) ask what they would count as a failure and record it; (3) only then the links, with the note that the figures, not the summary, are the authority; (4) only after that, the formula as a method to reuse with a self-written refuter.
