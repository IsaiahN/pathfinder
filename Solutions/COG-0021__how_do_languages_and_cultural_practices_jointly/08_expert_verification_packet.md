# Expert verification packet: the Tether on language, culture and categories

**What this is:** a claim in a form a language-and-cognition researcher can check. Mode, derivation and refuters are stated before the conclusion, so a reader knows what to look for and what would settle it.

**Mode: `specified` on structure, `general` on the union and dependency rules. Not `grounded`.** No nonverbal measurement is claimed. This is a shape and a route, not a result.

**Status:** simulated derivation checked for internal consistency against the verbatim figures, not a ground reading. It cannot settle the question it reports on.

## 1. The claim, in one paragraph

Category differences across language and culture are differences of *bonding over a shared inventory*, not differences of word lists. A perceptual and bodily kernel is shared; what varies is the arrangement installed by language and practice; the carrier is a convention with recorded provenance; and cross-language comparison must use effect descriptions, because names carry the frame they came from. Strong determinism is refused: a convention that decided the outcome would have stopped being a convention. "Jointly" is a union of overlapping conditions, so verdicts must be kept per domain and never averaged. The framework returns a shape, five refuters written in advance, and the kind of instrument that would close it. It does not name any particular category system, and it leaves the human-kinds case open.

## 2. Three checkable sub-claims

### C1. Structure: isomer, not inventory

- **Statement:** two languages over the same basic conceptual inventory are properly compared as isomers (same atoms, different bond, different substance) when their relational structure differs, and as two names for one frame only when relations are identical.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 12 :117 "isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour." Also Fig 12 :5 "A set of ingredients is not one. The bond is the rest of the information, and it is the half nothing was recording." and Fig 8 :13 "same atoms, different names".
- **What to check:** does the distinction between a relation change and a naming change correspond to a real difference in the literature? Wang, Qin and Liu (arXiv:2610.30778, 2026) report "Shared basic meanings, however, need not imply shared relations among them" over 196 concepts and 81 languages. Is that an isomer result, or does the corpus method confound it?
- **Refuter:** matched nonverbal tasks show zero relational difference after translation equivalence is controlled and uncertainty manipulated. Then the identity reading stands and this sub-claim fails.

### C2. Verdict: strong determinism is refused, weak versions stay open

- **Statement:** a category convention cannot be the thing that settles what the categories pick out in the world; if it does, it has stopped being a convention. This refuses the strong reading without denying conditional, task-specific effects.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 10 :47 "a convention that decides an outcome has stopped being one". Also Seat Map :163 "High correlation is disqualifying for a verdict and efficient for a convention." and Fig 2 :41 "efficient for a convention: agreement about a shared meaning is the product, not a corruption of it".
- **What to check:** is this a fair reading of the strong/weak distinction? Cibelli et al. (PLoS ONE, 2016) write that "taking that hypothesis seriously need not entail a wholesale rejection of important universal components of human cognition", and that effects appear when "perceptual information is uncertain." Does the figure rule apply only to the strong version, as claimed?
- **Refuter:** a naming convention demonstrably changes the physical facts its category picks out, with no instrument or practice mediating, and an independent ground registers the change. Then this sub-claim fails. Note the live strain: for human kinds, Gelman and Roberts report a "looping effect" in which classification changes the classified. Fig 12 :121 "cycle. The output re-enters as input. The sign of the loop decides whether it closes, and a chain cannot close by construction." The sign is read by no available instrument; this is recorded as open, not resolved.

### C3. Comparison: effect terms travel, names do not

- **Statement:** the unit of cross-language comparison is the discrimination a category licenses under specified conditions, not the category name.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 9 :30-31 "your words are borrowed too, so describe the effect rather than the cause". Also Fig 6 :30 "The description travels. Fails by being sharpened into terms only we can read. Cast it in effect terms."
- **What to check:** the *Linguistic relativity* entry reports that "philosophers including Donald Davidson and Jason Josephson Storm have argued that Whorf's Hopi examples are self-refuting, as Whorf had to translate Hopi terms into English in order to explain how they are untranslatable." Is the figure's rule a fair generalisation of that failure? Does the verbal versus visual interference result in Cibelli et al. support the effect-term reading?
- **Refuter:** a translation that preserves every discrimination the source term licenses, verified in matched nonverbal tasks. Then names do travel and this sub-claim fails.

## 3. What is explicitly not claimed

- Not a theory of language, not a version of the Sapir-Whorf hypothesis, and not a verdict on how much language or culture contributes.
- Not a name for any category system, any language, or any culture. The framework returns a shape, not a name.
- Not a percentage, ranking, or "degree of shaping" number.
- Not that the six-term substrate is nature's own inventory. It is "a human's careful guess at reality's compression axes" (*They're Scanning Books*), used here only for the kernel/variable distinction.
- Not that the specific carrier (labels, generics, looping) is figure-derived. It is a `single-source` world finding from Gelman and Roberts (2017), recorded with provenance in `10_source_ledger.csv`.
- Not grounded. No measurement was taken, and the second seat shares the primary's evidence pool.
- Not that the human-kinds strain is closed. It is `gap` at this budget.

## 5. What would settle it

A source-discriminating instrument, not a further argument. *The Hard Problem is About Source*: an instrument that reads green "cannot comment on the source"; the spectrometer, not an argument, resolved that case. Here:

1. **Bond test:** nonverbal relational comparisons across maximally different languages, with translation equivalence controlled and uncertainty manipulated.
2. **Union test:** one lever held constant against the others across three semantic domains.
3. **Effect-term test:** translation fidelity measured by discriminations, not by glosses.
4. **Convention test:** a convention shown to change its own ground, with independent registration.
5. **Human-kind sign test:** an instrument that distinguishes tracking from manufacturing in a social kind.
