# Isomers, identity and order in COG-0021

**Mode: `specified` on the structural claims, `general` on the ordering rule. Not `grounded`.** This is a simulated derivation checked against the figures, not a ground reading (`05_what_shape_means.md` section 7).

## RESIDUAL

The question's "jointly" hides two structural questions the main audit does not settle:

1. When two languages differ in categories, are they **isomers** (same atoms, different bond) or merely two names for one frame (same atoms, different name)? These are different operators in Fig 12 and Fig 8, and choosing between them changes what evidence would count.
2. In what **order** do language and practice act, and at what scale? Order matters because Fig 3 says a chain is ordered by dependency, and Fig 4 says the transform between scales is lossy.

## DOMAIN CENSUS (kept from the residual line)

- R: a union of three conditions (language, practice, substrate), measured at three scales (individual, intergenerational, cross-community). Fig 9 :54 "And when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again."
- D1 substrate: actors are perceptual capacities; currency is discrimination; anchor is the shared sensorium.
- D2 language: actors are labels and generics; currency is conventional meaning; anchor is the speech community convention.
- D3 practice: actors are practices and norms; currency is transmission fidelity; anchor is outcomes in the world.
- D4 comparison: actors are experimenters; currency is nonverbal residuals; anchor is the ground.

Order by dependency: substrate before vocabulary before objective before comparison. Refuse before mint (Formula step 2). Describe the residual before picking a frame (Fig 9 :27-28).

## Q1. Isomer or identity?

Fig 8 :13 "same atoms, different names", with :14 "their union adds nothing", is the **identity** case (the `≡` bond: "two names, one referent", Operators table :25). If two languages merely named the same relational structure differently, their union would add nothing and cross-language comparison would be a lookup, not a discovery.

Fig 12 :117 "isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour." is the **bond** case. Same inventory, different arrangement, different substance.

The distinction is not terminological. Fig 12 :94 "The arrangement is the substance." and Fig 12 :5 "A set of ingredients is not one. The bond is the rest of the information, and it is the half nothing was recording."

Wang, Qin and Liu (2026) report: "Shared basic meanings, however, need not imply shared relations among them." And in the Discussion: "languages may express corresponding basic meanings while differing in the relations connecting them." On the same 196-concept inventory, relational dissimilarity differed by domain, with socio-cultural relations corresponding more closely than nature-and-motion relations. That is the isomer reading, measured with an instrument that has no contact with the Tether.

**Verdict:** the isomer reading is `triangulated` across Fig 12 and Wang et al. The identity reading is `single-source` for the figure definition (Fig 8 :13) and is contradicted by the world data. It is not erased: it remains the correct description for the pure translation case, which is a real but narrower phenomenon.

**Falsifier, written before the run:** if matched nonverbal tasks show zero relational difference after translation equivalence is controlled and uncertainty is manipulated, the isomer claim fails and the identity claim stands for that domain. Recorded in `01_figure_grounded_audit.md` F1 and section 5.

## Q2. Functional group, not a single kind

Fig 12 :124 "functional group. A substructure whose presence fixes the outcome. The group says what kind; the qualifier says which member."

Kinship and colour systems across languages are members of a functional group: they balance informative distinctions against representational cost. Wang et al. cite Kemp and Regier (2012) on kinship and Gibson et al. (2017) on colour use to make exactly this point: "kinship and color systems balance informative distinctions against representational cost, while color naming also reflects communicative usefulness."

So the answer is not "language X has category system Y." It is: a functional group is universal (the kind), and its members are locally specified (the qualifier). Fig 12 :124 is the figure licence; the specific universal is a world finding and is marked `single-source` here.

**Falsifier:** if a category system appears in one community with no counterpart functional structure anywhere else, the functional-group claim fails for that case.

## Q3. Dependency order: where the chain breaks

Fig 3 :20 "The chain is ordered by dependency, not by importance. Each link consumes what the one above produces, so effort spent above the break arrives at a step that cannot use it."

The chain in this field:

1. **Perception.** The system can tell what is in front of it. Gelman and Roberts note that categorisation is shared with other animals: baboons track matrilineal kin groups, macaques privilege inner kind over appearance. "language was not required" for these cases.
2. **Vocabulary.** The system has terms for what things afford (Fig 3 :7). Labels and generics arrive here.
3. **Objective.** It can say what would count as a category difference in its own terms. This is the contested link.
4. **Planning.** Choose the comparison that would discriminate a label change from a bound change.
5. **Carry.** Name what worked and verify it.

Fig 3 :26 describes why link 3 is the break: "having terms for what a situation offers and being able to say what would count as progress, has neither, so it attracts the least attention and the fewest instruments." Fig 3 :27: "A link with no instrument is not a link that works. It is a link nobody has looked at."

Cibelli et al. supply an instrument for link 3: the verbal versus visual interference manipulation and the uncertainty manipulation. When the verbal code is disabled, the category effect disappears; when perceptual information is uncertain, the category effect grows. That is a reading of link 3.

**Falsifier:** if link 3 can be shown to work without any instrument, by introspection alone, Fig 3 :27 is wrong for this domain. No such demonstration is in the sources read.

## Q4. Scale order and the round trip

Fig 4 :20 "Only the actors, the currency and the anchor change." The kernel is the loop; the cast changes with scale.

## Q5. Analogues and their limits

- **Chemistry / isomerism.** Fig 12 :97-98: "Chemistry hit it first: silver fulminate and silver cyanate have the same composition and different properties, which is what forced structure into the notation. Liebig and Wöhler, 1824 to 1826; named isomerism by Berzelius, 1830." Use: same inventory, different bond, different behaviour. Limit: a chemical bond is not a social convention, and the ground for chemical properties is an instrument, not a speech community.
- **Metamerism / source discrimination.** Use: a reading does not determine its source. Limit: *The Hard Problem is About Source* says the spectrometer resolved the case; for social kinds no equivalent spectrometer exists (F7).
- **Tacit knowledge / pretext.** Use: the same words mean different things with different history. Limit: this is a mechanism for individuals, not a measure of category structure across communities.
- **Babel search.** Use: narrow by proximity until the shape is statable. Limit: the essay's "finish line will also be the starting place" is a convergence claim about a searcher, not a ground for the categories found.

## CONCLUSION

Two languages over a shared basic inventory stand to each other as **isomers** when their relations differ, and as **two names for one frame** when only their labels differ. Wang et al.'s data support the first reading; the identity reading is retained for the narrower translation case. The functional group is the universal kind, the local system is the qualifier. The chain at which language and practice act is ordered perception to vocabulary to objective to planning, and it breaks at the objective, where an instrument is only now appearing. Across scales the kernel is the loop and only the cast changes; the round trip is lossy, with three named constraints dropped. The falsifiers are written above and in `01_figure_grounded_audit.md` section 5.
