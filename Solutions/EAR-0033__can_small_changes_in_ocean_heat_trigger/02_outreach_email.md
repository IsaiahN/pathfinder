**Subject:** Check one shape claim about ocean heat and ice flow before you decide about the theory

> Hi [name],
>
> I have one specific output I would like you to check against what you already know. I am not asking
> you to read the theory first, and I am not asking you to agree with it. If the output does not
> survive, there is nothing else to look at.
>
> **The output (`specified`, not `grounded`):** a small change in ocean heat is not, by itself, a
> trigger for a large or persistent change in ice-sheet flow. The response has to be assessed per
> glacier or grounding-line slot, not as a basin average. Two conditions have to hold jointly:
>
> 1. the heat must actually reach the ice-ocean contact and the grounding zone, and
> 2. the ice that is contacted must be configured so that retreat feeds back on itself (for example
>    a retrograde bed and reduced ice-shelf buttressing).
>
> Where both hold, a modest forcing can sit past a threshold and produce a large, self-sustaining
> response that continues after the forcing is removed. Where only one holds, the expected response
> is smaller and recoverable. "Large" and "persistent" are two different tests: a large response can
> be linear and reversible, so it is not automatically a tipping event.
>
> **What would refute the shape claim:**
> 1. a per-slot response curve that is large but fully recovers when the heat anomaly is removed,
>    with no self-sustaining retreat;
> 2. warm water delivered to the cavity with no change in grounding-line flux across several slots
>    configured near a retrograde bed;
> 3. one intervention that restores small recoverable responses in both a forcing-only case and a
>    configuration-only case, with no categorically different residue;
> 4. a reversal experiment at a named slot that re-stabilises the grounding line where a threshold
>    was claimed;
> 5. a documented case where one slot sustains rapid retreat while the basin mean is reported stable
>    and used as the verdict.
>
> I wrote these down before sending this. If any one holds, I will narrow or retract the claim. A
> one-line "this fails because..." is more useful to me than agreement. If you'd rather not engage,
> no reply is needed.
>
> **What I am not claiming:** that any particular glacier is about to tip, that a specific number of
> degrees of ocean warming is the threshold, or that ocean heat is the sole cause of ice loss. Those
> are ground readings and imports from other frames. The framework returns a shape, not a name. The
> 1.2 °C figure in the ice-sheet literature is a result from that literature, not a derivation from
> this one, and I am recording it as an import.
>
> **If it survives, and only then:** the sources are below. The figures, not my summary of them, are
> the authority.
> - Verbatim figure text with line numbers (local extract): `FRAMEWORK/reference/03-FIGURES_TEXT.md`
> - Canonical usage rule (local): `FRAMEWORK/reference/FIGURES_README_canonical.md`
> - Hill, Gudmundsson and Chandler 2024, *Ocean warming as a trigger for irreversible retreat of the
>   Antarctic ice sheet*: https://www.nature.com/articles/s41558-024-02134-8
> - Rosier et al. 2021, *The tipping points and early warning indicators for Pine Island Glacier*:
>   https://tc.copernicus.org/articles/15/1501/2021
>
> **If you want to use the machinery:** the formula is in the figure files. Run it on a problem you
> know well and can check against a ground. Write down what would refute your result *before* you
