## What the wider literature bears on this question

1. **The phenomenon the question names is real in models, and it is conditional.** Hill et al. 2024
   show that a cold-to-warm cavity shift can cause irreversible grounding-line retreat in particular
   catchments, while explicitly refusing a single runaway West Antarctic collapse. Rosier et al. 2021
   find multiple tipping points for one glacier. Winkelmann et al. 2026 find that some basins decline
   gradually and others tip. This is the per-slot, union-shaped result the figures predict, not a
   single global switch.

2. **A large response is not the same as a tipping response.** Rosier et al. 2021 separate a large
   recoverable response from hysteresis. This is the figure's distinction between ⋛ ("large", Fig 12
   :87) and the cycle ("persistent", Fig 12 :121). Cite it when explaining why the packet does not
   call every strong response a tipping event.

3. **Small forcings can be amplified by geometry, not only by magnitude.** Zhou et al. 2026 show an
   order-of-magnitude melt amplification from modest warm-water intrusions in a channelised cavity.
   This is the contact-and-gradient claim (Fig 11 :40, Fig 13 :46), and it is the strongest single
   world corroboration for the "small changes can matter" half of the question.

4. **The detector is real but lossy.** Rosier et al. 2021 report robust early-warning detection in
   model simulations; Clarke et al. 2026 show the skill depends on forcing rate and spatial coupling,
   and that false positives occur. This is why the figures call for a source-discriminating instrument
   and a `gap` verdict on the threshold number (Fig 6 :34, Fig 9 :32).

5. **Evidence pool warning.** Hill 2024, Rosier 2021 and Winkelmann 2026 all use ice-sheet models
   initialised from BedMachine/Bedmap-type topography and ocean products. They are a pool, not three
   independent witnesses. Zhou 2026 (physical oceanography) and Clarke 2026 (complex-systems methods)
   are lower-overlap frames; they corroborate the mechanism and the detector, not the specific
   glacier-level fact.

## AI-policy caveat

A simulated derivation is not a submittable empirical result. Several earth-science publishers and
unions require disclosure of AI use and require authors to be accountable for the submitted work.
The BUS-0001 pilot documents one such policy explicitly (AOM); equivalent AGU, Copernicus, Nature and
IOP policies must be checked on the venue's own author page before any submission. The honest use of
this folder is as a private refuter and a methods note offered to a willing checker, not as a paper
presenting new measurements.
