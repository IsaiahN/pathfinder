# The Tether's answer: small changes in ocean heat and ice-sheet flow

**Mode: `specified` - not `grounded`.** This is a shape and a route, not a result. Simulated
derivation checked against the figures (`05_what_shape_means.md` section 7), not a ground reading.

## 1. The residual it answers

The question is not "how much heat?" It is "what is the shape of the map from a heat increment to a
flow response, per ice-stream slot?" R = |Γ(b,a) - o'| is indexed per glacier or grounding-line
sector, never averaged (Fig 1 :19). The map has two candidate forms: proportional and recoverable, or
threshold-like and self-sustaining. "Large" and "persistent" are different predicates and need
different tests: a large response can be linear and reversible, so it is not automatically a tipping
event (Fig 12 :87, Rosier et al. 2021).

## 2. The shape

1. **Index by slot.** A basin mean can sit near zero while one ice stream is live; that is a legal
   state, not an inert one (Fig 1 :20, Fig 1 :22). A claim that the whole ice sheet tips is not a
   well-formed residual.
2. **"Large" is a comparison; "persistent" is a cycle.** Fig 12 :87 defines the comparison as *"a
   threshold relation between two quantities."* Fig 12 :121: *"cycle. The output re-enters as input.
   The sign of the loop decides whether it closes, and a chain cannot close by construction."* A
   chain from heat to melt to flow cannot by itself produce a response that continues after the
   forcing is removed.
3. **Heat must reach the contact.** Fig 11 :40: *"A system cannot be made more capable by improving
   the agent in isolation. An improvement that does not change contact changes nothing, however much
   it improves."* Ocean heat is an energy state; it becomes a spendable flow only when bonded to a
   gradient at the ice-ocean interface (Fig 13 :46, Fig 13 :76). The relevant cascade is
   water-mass access, cavity intrusion, basal melt, thinning, buttressing loss, grounding-line
   retreat and increased flux (Fig 11 :52).
4. **The response is a union of two conditions.** Small heat plus threshold-ready ice gives the
   named phenomenon. Either alone does not (Fig 9 :55, Fig 9 :57). World sources support this:
   Hill et al. 2024 find irreversible retreat in particular catchments after a cavity regime shift,
   and Winkelmann et al. 2026 find that some basins decline gradually while others tip.
5. **The threshold number is a `gap`.** Fig 9 :32: *"you get a kind of thing, never the answer
   itself."* Fig 13 :134: *"Unreachable is a verdict here, not a silence,"* and Fig 8 :39: *"So a
   search that finds nothing is never a proof of absence."* The 1.2 °C value in Rosier et al. 2021 is
   an import from that frame, provenanced as such, not a derivation from these figures.
6. **The detector is an edge, not an oracle.** Early-warning indicators already return something
   (variance, lag-1 autocorrelation), but their skill depends on forcing rate and spatial coupling
   and they have false positives (Rosier et al. 2021, Clarke et al. 2026). Fig 6 :34 requires an edge
   that is already returning something; it does not license inventing a sensor from a description.

## 3. What it explicitly does not say

- Not that a particular glacier is about to tip, and not that any specific ocean-heat increment is *the* threshold.
- Not that ocean heat is the sole cause. Basal-channel geometry, subglacial discharge, bed slope and buttressing are co-actors in the contact cascade (Zhou et al. 2026, Hill et al. 2024).
- Not that every large response is a tipping event, and not that every steep response is persistent.
- Not a single global ice-sheet switch. The result is per basin and per slot.
- Not `grounded`. No reversal experiment or ground measurement is held in this folder.

## 4. What would settle it - the ground

A source-discriminating instrument, not a further argument. *The Hard Problem is About Source*: an
instrument that reads green *"cannot comment on the source"*; the spectrometer resolved it. Here:

- **Threshold Test:** at a named slot, step the ocean forcing and identify the forcing value at which
  recovery time stops shortening and the response becomes recoverable only well below the trigger
  (hysteresis), as in Rosier et al. 2021.
- **Reversal Test:** remove or halve the heat anomaly at the identified point and observe whether the
  grounding line re-stabilises or keeps retreating. Hill et al. 2024 report continued retreat under
  reduced melt in some catchments and re-advance in others; that is the discrimination the shape needs.
- **Per-slot Test:** show a slot in sustained retreat while the basin mean stays near zero, or show
  that this cannot happen. The first confirms slot indexing; the second refutes it.
- **Standing falsifiers from `01`:** a large but fully recoverable response with no self-sustaining
  retreat; no flux change when warm water reaches a near-threshold cavity; one lever clearing both
  union conditions; a ground reversal at a named slot.

## 5. The honest stop

**Unreached, not unreachable.** The six-term substrate is *"a human's careful guess at reality's
compression axes"*; declaring it makes the choice visible, not neutral. The ice-ocean bindings are
imports from other frames and are recorded as such. The answer stops where the instruments stop
(early-warning detectors are conditional, thresholds are model-derived), and it records the lineage
so the stop is checkable. *"Indiscernibility is not identity."*

## 6. One-paragraph version for outreach

> The Tether does not say that a small change in ocean heat flips the ice sheet, and it does not name
> a threshold. It says that the response must be read per glacier, not as a basin average; that a
> *large* response and a *persistent* response are two different things, the first a threshold and
> the second a self-sustaining feedback that a simple chain cannot produce; and that heat only acts
> where it reaches the ice-ocean contact and the contacted ice is already configured so that retreat
> feeds on itself. Those two conditions are a union, not one cause. The shape also says what would
> refute it: a large response that fully recovers when the heat is removed, warm water that changes
> no grounding-line flux, one lever that clears both conditions, or a reversal experiment that
> re-stabilises a grounding line where a threshold was claimed. The threshold number stays open until
> the ground settles it. What is offered is the shape any candidate answer must have, the falsifiers
> written in advance, and the instruments that could close it.
