# Outreach draft: check the output before you decide about the framework

**Use:** one-to-one, or as a cover letter attached to a submission through the public venues in `03_public_contact_points.csv`. **Not** for bulk send.

**Hard precondition:** do not send until a checker can see the residual and its refuter without first absorbing the framework. The two-stage order is the whole design.

---

**Subject:** One checkable claim about cascades and connection, before any theory

> Hi [name],
>
> I have one specific output about systemic risk that I would like you to check against what you already know. I am not asking you to read the framework first, and I am not asking you to agree with it. If the output fails your check, there is nothing else to look at.
>
> **The output (`specified`, not `grounded`):** "how tightly should we connect a system" is the wrong object to optimize. The signal that matters is per slot, not a system-wide average: a global risk score near zero can coexist with one live slot residual. The lever is the typed bond and the sign of the feedback loop, not the number of links. Under the same set of components, a different arrangement is a different substance, so two networks with the same institutions can have opposite cascade behaviour. Connection therefore behaves as a threshold compound: it absorbs shocks over one range and amplifies them past a tipping point. A safety verdict declared by frames that share one evidence pool is not corroboration, and a stress test that finds nothing is not proof of absence.
>
> **What I am not claiming:** I am not claiming a universal optimal coupling percentage, buffer size or diversification ratio. No figure line returns one, so I record that as a gap rather than invent a number. I am not claiming that tight connection is always bad, that diversification always helps, or that managers can subtract risk. Buffers and circuit breakers substitute a habitat, they do not remove contact.
>
> **What would refute this, written before the run:**
> 1. A single system-wide scalar that preserves every per-slot cascade residual under perturbation.
> 2. Two arrangements over the same components with identical cascade behaviour under the same shock.
> 3. A measured monotone relation between coupling and cascading loss across the whole operating range.
> 4. A safety claim settled by agreement inside one evidence pool and then paid for by the ground anyway.
> 5. A test battery proven to contain every mechanism that can produce a cascade.
>
> I wrote these down before sending this. If any one holds, I will retract the claim. A one-line "this fails because..." is more useful to me than agreement. If you would rather not engage, no reply is needed.
>
> **If it survives, and only then:** the sources are below. The figures are the authority, not my summary of them.
