## QUESTION

Decide, in effect terms, what the Tether says about the question of how a regulator or manager should balance the efficiency of tightly connected systems against exposure to cascading failure. Option A: treat it as one dial with an optimal coupling fraction. Option B: treat it as a union of conditions with a per-slot residual, where the lever is the bond arrangement and the ground contact of the monitoring frame. What each leads to: A delivers a single score that hides the live slot and invites a verdict from agreeing frames that share one evidence pool; B delivers a shape, written refuters, and the kind of instrument that would close the fraction.

## DOMAIN CENSUS

R by arity, symmetry and scale, then one line per domain with actors, currency and anchor; partition or union; order by dependency.

- **R, the residual.** Prediction of continued cheap flow fails against observation at particular slots while the system-wide average looks healthy. Arity: at least three conditions (flow efficiency, failure propagation, monitoring and governance), so a union compound, not one dial. Symmetry: the conditions are not interchangeable and are priced in different currencies. Scale: step to cycle to level to seat to lineage.
- **Domain 1, exposure and topology.** Actors: nodes, typed bonds, shocks, load. Currency: flow and overload propagation; bits describe the arrangement, actions test it. Anchor: a real shock and its loss record. Question type: bond and arrangement, not node count.
- **Domain 2, measurement and ground contact.** Actors: stress tests, exposure data, regulator models. Currency: residual per slot, prediction against observation. Anchor: the real crisis and loss data, which do not update on the model's report. Question type: channel reachability and frame independence.
- **Domain 3, governance and the seat.** Actors: regulators, managers, boards, regulated firms. Currency: verdicts, conventions, influence versus ground contact. Anchor: the ground, never agreement. Question type: a seat that can refuse, with a declared stake.
- **Domain 4, the model as a frame.** Actors: the internal model and its vocabulary. Currency: composition inside the closure. Anchor: the figures as metalaws, the ground for any measurement. Question type: the seam between domains 1 and 2.
- **Union, not partition.** An overlap is a compound with its own loop. A case that is both efficient and exposed is categorically different from a harder case of either.
- **Order by dependency.** Reach a non-updating ground first, then enumerate contact, then choose the bond, then install the refusal seat, then require an outside frame for a safety claim.

## VERDICT (from the figures)

The balance question is a union, not one dial. The residual is per slot, and a system-wide score hides the live signal. Govern the typed bond and the sign of the redistribution cycle rather than the link count: same components under a different arrangement are isomers, and connection absorbs shocks up to a tipping point and then amplifies them. Enumerate contact outward because cascades run several steps out. Keep a non-updating ground reachable, treat stress tests as instruments with a round-trip gap, buy independent readings, and keep a seat able to refuse with its stake declared. A safety claim from inside the system's own model is a candidate, not settled. The universal coupling fraction is a declared gap. This is `specified` on the per-domain claims, `general` on the shape, ungrounded, candidate.

- **cost if wrong:** high and asymmetric. Under-connecting forgoes the real efficiency the source question values; over-connecting converts an absorbent shock into a systemic cascade. A wrong verdict also spends the independence of outside readers if an internal recording is sent instead of a generator. Fig 2 :42 "Buy independence in proportion to the cost of being wrong." That cost is avoidable by leading with the shape and its refuter.
- **undoable:** partly. Bonds, buffers, compartments and independent audits can be changed and re-tested, so the governance lever is reversible. A cascade that has already happened is not. A wrong rule installed as a convention that decides outcomes is hard to unwind because it stops being checkable (Fig 10 :47). Keep cuts ranked and reversible.
- **reversed by:** a ground reading, not agreement. A coupling-to-loss relation measured across the operating range would locate or dissolve the tipping point; a scalar preserving every per-slot residual would break the per-slot verdict; two arrangements with identical cascade behaviour would break the isomer verdict; a test battery proven to contain every cascade mechanism would break the searched-not-proven verdict; a frame proving its own completeness would break the closure verdict. Until one of these fires, the verdict stands as candidate.
