# Isomers, cycles and order in the efficiency-exposure question

**Mode: `specified` on the structural claims, `general` on the dependency order. Simulated derivation checked against the figures, not a ground reading (see `05` section 6).**

## RESIDUAL

The question asks for a balance point. Two supporting questions are owed: (1) what is the structural relation between a tightly coupled network and a modular one over the same components, and (2) in what order must the governance levers be applied for effort to arrive anywhere. A third, the sign of the feedback loop, decides whether there is a threshold at all.

Atoms held for this derivation: the network and gradient structures (Fig 13 :24, :22), the seven bonds and negation, the six-term substrate as a physical reference only. No new primitive is available without import. Currency: bits price the description, actions price settling it (Fig 12 :125-126). Anchor: the real shock and loss record.

## DERIVATION

### Q1 Are a tightly coupled and a modular network isomers?

- Fig 12 :117 *"isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."*
- Fig 12 :94 *"The arrangement is the substance."*
- Fig 13 :115 *"The set is closed. The arrangements are not. That gap is why import exists."*
- Over a fixed set of institutions, a dense arrangement and a compartmentalized arrangement are same atoms, different bond, different substance. The ingredient list (the firms, the utilities) cannot tell them apart; the bond carries the difference.
- **Falsifier, written before the run:** if two arrangements over the same atom set are shown to have identical cascade behaviour under the same shock, the isomer claim fails and the bond carries no information. Kept live and repeated in `08` and `09`.

### Q2 What is the order of the levers?

- Fig 3 :20 *"The chain is ordered by dependency, not by importance."*
- Fig 3 :19 *"Optimising a link that already works cannot rescue one that does not."*
- Fig 3 :27 *"A link with no instrument is not a link that works. It is a link nobody has looked at."*
- The order derived in `01` section 1: reach a non-updating ground (D2), enumerate the habitat of contact (D1), choose the bond topology (D1), install a seat able to refuse (D3), require an outside frame for a safety claim (D4).
- The middle link is the one with no instrument in most practice: having terms for what a connection affords and saying what would count as progress. Fig 3 :25-26 notes this link has neither obvious failures nor obvious tests, so it attracts the least attention. Effort spent tuning connection metrics above a break there cannot arrive.
- **Falsifier:** if a regulator can show that changing the bond topology reliably improves cascade outcomes while the ground channel remains decayed and unmeasured, the dependency order is wrong. That result would send the census back to Fig 3.

### Q3 The cycle sign is the threshold variable

- Fig 12 :121 *"cycle. The output re-enters as input. The sign of the loop decides whether it closes, and a chain cannot close by construction."*
- Fig 13 :31-32 *"a remainder compounding through a chain that amplifies is chaos, and the detail a crossing discards reappears above it as noise."*
- A redistribution of load after a failure re-enters as input to neighbours, so the system is a cycle, not a chain. Whether it closes and amplifies depends on the sign. This is the structural reason there is a tipping point rather than a monotone trade-off. The world corroborates (Acemoglu et al. 2015 threshold; Lever et al. 2014 simultaneous collapse), but the verdict comes from these two lines.
- **Falsifier:** a measured monotone relation between coupling and loss across the whole operating range, or a proof that the redistribution loop cannot close, dissolves the threshold compound.

### Q4 Catalysts and spent parts

- Fig 12 :119 *"catalyst. A part that survives its own use."* Spent and regenerating parts behave differently across uses, and nothing records which a term is (same line).
- A circuit breaker, a buffer or an independent audit that is regenerated and remains able to refuse is a catalyst: it survives its own use. A stress test calibrated once and then replayed is a spent part: it paid once and cannot settle again. The difference is not recorded by the ingredient list, so a governance report that lists "we have stress tests" records the atoms and not the kind.
- Fig 12 :107-110 (carried from the exemplar's reading, checked against the extract): where the arrangement is not recorded, the result carries forward and the deriving does not, so every lineage pays again across a boundary that drops it. A protocol that records only the passing result, not how it was obtained, loses the method.
- **Falsifier:** a one-shot instrument shown to settle repeatedly without regeneration would break the catalyst/spent distinction.

### Q5 Living-system analogues

Fig 4 :19-20 *"Only the actors, the currency and the anchor change."* The living-system test maps relations, not looks.

| analogue | actors | currency | anchor | what it corroborates |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Food webs and pollination networks (opened ecology synthesis) | species, interaction links | disturbance spread and persistence | extinction and collapse records | compartmentalization limits cascading extinction; a tipping point can collapse many species at once (F1, F2, F3) |
| Interdependent infrastructures (opened cascading-failure synthesis) | grids, transport, communication | load and dependency | blackout and outage records | a failure in one network triggers iterative cascades in others; a control signal no faster than the overload (F2, F7) |
| Financial counterparty networks (opened systemic-risk synthesis) | banks, exposures | debt claims and liquidity | defaults and crisis losses | connection absorbs then amplifies; correlated models are one evidence pool (F2, F6) |

These are three genuinely different closures. Their agreement on the threshold shape is corroboration, not identity, and none outranks the figures.

### Q6 The two currencies do not add

- Fig 12 :125 *"A composition costs in two currencies, and they do not add."*
- Fig 12 :126 *"Bits price the description. Actions price finding out whether it holds."*
- Fig 12 :130 *"Summing them into one figure is the error this names, not the fix for it. A price in bits and a price in actions are two readings, held apart."*
- Efficiency is largely a description-cost and throughput quantity; exposure is an action-and-settlement quantity (what a real shock pays). A "balance score" adds them. Fig 12 :128-129 notes the readings come apart in both directions, so neither figure predicts the other and both must be read or the choice is blind.

## CONCLUSION

The tightly coupled and the modular network are isomers over a fixed atom set: same components, different bond, different substance, difference decisive in behaviour and invisible in the ingredient list. The cycle sign, not the link count, governs whether a shock is absorbed or amplified, so the threshold is structural. The levers are ordered by dependency: ground reachability before habitat enumeration before bond choice before refusal seat before outside frame. Catalysts and spent parts must be told apart by whether they survive their own use. Efficiency and exposure are two currencies held apart, not two ends of one scale. No figure returns a balance fraction.
