## Question restated as an effect

Two effects of a rule on a person are being separated:

- Effect A, value change: the rule's content becomes a reason for action that persists when the external contingency (audience, sanction, reward) is removed, and is integrated with what the person already holds.
- Effect B, compliance without adoption: behavior tracks the rule, but the underlying assumption is not taken as the person's own reason. Remove the contingency and the behavior can change or disappear.

An answer would look like: a condition C such that when C holds, Effect A occurs, and when C fails, Effect B occurs. This package binds C as an ordinary term (import of the rule's content as a grounded atom into the person's library, with a shift in the weighting w and settlement against the ground) so that C can accrue a residual and be refused. Formula step "Direction".

## What is deliberately not here

- No personal emails of named individuals.
- No ranking of people by how likely they are to agree that rules shape values.
- No bulk-send plan.

Three reasons, in the figures' own words:

1. A room does not upgrade the label: agreement reached without the ground is a weighted average of influence (Seat Map :283). Adding people raises the vocabulary and does not add an anchor.
2. Correlation has valence: "High correlation is disqualifying for a verdict and efficient for a convention" (Seat Map :163, Fig 2 :39-41). Selecting recipients for receptiveness manufactures exactly the correlation that would make their agreement worthless as evidence about values.
3. Asking for trust before a check spends the verifier's independence: Fig 10 :42, "A verifier that can reconstruct the claim cannot verify it."

Legally: scraping personal emails for unsolicited bulk outreach breaches GDPR and CAN-SPAM style rules in many jurisdictions. Public opt-in submission routes avoid that failure as well as the structural one.
