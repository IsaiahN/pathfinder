# The Tether's answer: when rules change values, and when they only change behavior

**Mode: `specified` on the conditionals, `general` on the structural shape. Not `grounded`.** This is a shape and a route, not a result. Simulated derivation checked against the figures (`05_what_shape_means.md` section 7), not a ground reading.

## 1. The residual it answers

A rule can produce two observable effects that look the same from outside: behavior that tracks the rule with the reason adopted, and behavior that tracks the rule without the reason adopted. The residual is the gap between the rule as declared meaning and the rule as grounded term in the person's library. It is indexed per person-rule slot, never aggregated: Fig 1 :19 "The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated." The question is a union compound, since the two effects overlap in the same person across rules and across time (Fig 9 :55).

## 2. The shape

1. **A declared rule is not a value.** Fig 10 :18 "a declared meaning is not a grounded one" and Fig 10 :46 "A convention makes no claim about the world; a verdict and an atom both do." The institution legitimately authors conventions to coordinate across a seam (Fig 10 :45). Obeying a convention does not ground a reason in anyone.
2. **Value change requires a crossing from a differing closure.** Fig 6 :23 "No frame escapes its own closure. The bound moves only on contact with a frame whose closure differs." Composing the rule with terms the person already holds adds nothing new: Fig 6 :21 "MINT composes inside the closure and can never add an atom." What must cross is the grounded reason, as an import with provenance and a shadow test.
3. **The internal transition is a shift in the weighting w.** The Symbols table defines w as: "How far the belief leans on private history versus inherited prior. w_A + w_B = 1, and w is learnable from the outcomes of past weightings." When w shifts, action is generated from the held belief rather than from the external contingency. Fig 11 :40 "An improvement that does not change contact changes nothing, however much it improves."
4. **The signature is persistence without the contingency.** Structurally, an internalized value is a cycle (Fig 12 :121 "cycle. The output re-enters as input. The sign of the loop decides whether it closes, and a chain cannot close by construction") with a part that survives its use (Fig 12 :119 "catalyst. A part that survives its own use."). Mere compliance is an open chain: remove the audience, sanction or reward and the behavior stops.
5. **Agreement is the product of a convention, not a verdict about values.** Fig 2 :41 "efficient for a convention: agreement about a shared meaning is the product, not a corruption of it" while Fig 2 :40 says agreement is "disqualifying for a verdict: agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool." Therefore compliance rates and endorsement surveys are filters for where to look, never witnesses for whether anyone holds the reason.
6. **Enforcement has a characteristic failure.** Fig 10 :47 "a convention that decides an outcome has stopped being one." A rule that operates only through sanctions becomes an authored constant in the person's frame (Fig 10 :48), not a grounded atom.

**Answer in one line:** a rule changes a person's values when its content crosses into their library as a grounded reason from a frame whose closure differs, the weighting w shifts so the behavior is generated from that reason, and the content settles against the ground; people follow rules without adopting their assumptions when the rule remains a declared convention or enforced constant acting only on the action channel, so behavior tracks the rule while Γ, b and w are unchanged.

## 3. What it explicitly does not say

- Not that any particular institution has or has not changed anyone's values. That needs a ground reading.
- Not a numeric threshold of w, nor a count of trials required to distinguish a cycle from a chain. The framework returns a shape, not a name or a number (Fig 9 :32).
- Not that the psychological vocabulary of any one discipline (introjection, integration, legitimacy, moralization, conditional preference) is derived by the Tether. Those are imports from world frames, recorded as such.
- Not that compliance is worthless. It is the product of a convention and is efficient for coordination. It is worthless only as evidence for the different question about values.
- Not grounded. No measurement settles the crossing.

## 4. What would settle it: the ground

A discriminator, not another argument. From *The Hard Problem is About Source*: an instrument that reads green "cannot comment on the source"; the spectrometer, not a further debate, resolved the case. Here:

1. **Removal test.** Observe behavior when the audience, sanction and reward are removed. A chain stops; a cycle continues. This is the anchor for the person-rule slot, and it must not move when the person is asked what they believe.
2. **Conditional/unconditional test.** Ask whether the reason for acting depends on expectations about what peers do or approve. Bicchieri's conditional preference and the SEP convention case give an independent, effect-level instrument for the same distinction.
3. **Provenance test.** Record where the reason came from and when it entered. Convergent derivation and adopted import "look identical in the contents. Only the record of where each came from separates them" (Fig 8 :51-52).
4. **Standing refuters** from `01_figure_grounded_audit.md` section 4, including value change with no import or w shift, and a grounded reason whose action does not persist without the contingency.

## 5. The honest stop

**Shape reached, mechanism and number unreached.** The universe of possible institutional rules is not a sealed room, so a search that finds no crossing is never a proof that none occurred (Fig 8 :39). The answer stops where the instruments cannot distinguish a chain from a cycle in a single observation, and records the lineage so the stop is checkable. "Indiscernibility is not identity." Declaring the primitives makes the choice visible, not neutral (*They're Scanning Books*, 2026-09-06). No part of this may be labelled `grounded` until a discriminator is run.

## 6. One-paragraph version for outreach

> The Tether does not say that institutions cannot change values, nor that compliance is fake. It separates two effects that look the same from outside. A declared rule is a convention: it coordinates behavior and makes no claim about the world, so obeying it is not evidence that anyone adopted the reason. Values change only when the reason crosses into what a person holds from a frame whose closure differs, the person's weighting of inherited reasons shifts, and the reason settles against experience. The signature is that the behavior persists when the audience, sanction and reward are removed, an internal cycle rather than an external chain. Enforcement alone does the opposite: a convention that decides an outcome has stopped being a convention and has become an authored constant. Compliance rates and endorsement surveys are useful for deciding where to look, not for deciding what anyone holds. What the framework offers is the shape of the condition, the refuters written in advance, and the instruments that would close it. The slot stays open until the ground settles it.
