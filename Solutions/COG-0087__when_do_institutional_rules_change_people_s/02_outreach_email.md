# Outreach draft: check the two-effect split before you decide about the theory

**Use:** one-to-one, or as a cover letter attached to a submission through the public venues in `03_public_contact_points.csv`. **Not** for bulk send.

**Hard precondition:** do not send until the reader can answer stage 1 without seeing the framework. If the stage-1 shape does not survive their check, the two-stage offer collapses on contact.

---

**Subject:** One checkable distinction between following a rule and adopting it

> Hi [name],
>
> I have one specific output I would like you to check against what you already know. I am not asking you to read the framework first, and I am not asking you to agree with it. If the output does not survive, there is nothing else to look at.
>
> **The output (`specified`, not `grounded`):** a rule changes a person's values only if its content crosses into what the person actually holds as a grounded reason, from a contact with someone whose frame already holds it, and the person's weighting of inherited reasons shifts so that the behavior is generated from that reason. The observable signature is that the behavior persists when the audience, the sanction and the reward are removed, and the content is treated as a reason to give. If that crossing does not happen, the rule has changed only the person's actions, not their values. In that case "everyone complies" is exactly what a working convention looks like, and it is not evidence that anyone adopted the reason.
>
> This follows a hard distinction in the source material I am using: a declared meaning is not a grounded one, and a convention that decides an outcome has stopped being a convention. An institution legitimately authors conventions to coordinate across a seam. What it cannot do by declaring is ground a value in a person.
>
> **What I am not claiming:** that compliance rates or endorsement surveys measure value change. They are budget filters, not witnesses for that verdict. I am not claiming a numeric threshold at which a convention becomes a value, and I am not claiming any particular institution has or has not changed anyone's values. That would need a ground reading I have not taken.
>
> **What would refute the shape claim:**
> 1. A rule changing a held value with no contact with a differing frame and no shift in the person's weighting of reasons.
> 2. A grounded reason whose behavior does not persist when the external contingency is removed, or behavior that persists with no such shift.
> 3. A population agreement measure that discriminates a person who holds the reason from one who does not, when the contingency is removed.
>
> I wrote these down before sending this. If any one holds, the shape is wrong and I will retract it. A one-line "this fails because..." is more useful to me than agreement. If you would rather not engage, no reply is needed.
>
> **If it survives, and only then:** the source material is below. The figures are the authority, not my summary of them.
> - Canonical figures and line-numbered text (local framework copy): `FRAMEWORK/reference/03-FIGURES_TEXT.md` and `FRAMEWORK/reference/FIGURES_README_canonical.md`
> - Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy, Social Norms (open access, 2011, revised 2023): https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/social-norms/
> - Self-determination theory paper set on internalization and integration: https://selfdeterminationtheory.org/SDT/documents/1994_DeciEghrariPatLeone.pdf
>
> **If you want to use the machinery:** the loop is in the figures. Run it on a problem you know well and can check against a ground. Write down what would refute your result *before* you run it, and state the mode (general, specified or grounded) of what you get. A reported failure is more useful to me than a reported success.
>
> Thanks for reading, whether or not you reply.
