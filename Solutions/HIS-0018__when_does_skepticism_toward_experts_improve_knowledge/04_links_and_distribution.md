## Wider literature that bears on the question

Grouped by field so that overlap is visible. The framework essays are all by the figure author and are high-overlap; the others are independent.

- **Philosophy of skepticism (high authority, low overlap with the framework author).** SEP entry on skepticism (Comesaña and Klein), Hume's *Enquiry* §10, SEP entry on Popper (Thornton). These separate suspension of judgment from denial, require belief to track evidence, and require refutation criteria to be fixed before a test.
- **Cognitive and social psychology (low overlap).** Lord, Ross and Lepper 1979 shows that the same evidence polarizes opposed groups rather than converging them. This is the empirical form of Fig 2 :40 on shared evidence pools.
- **History of science (low overlap).** Oreskes and Conway 2010 shows organized doubt using the provisionality of science against science itself. This is the empirical form of Fig 2 :47 on promoting the ground to a party.
- **Science studies (low overlap).** Collins and Evans 2002 argues explicitly against both excessive deference and unrestrained suspicion. This is the closest independent statement of the two-regime structure.
- **Science communication (low overlap).** Shermer 2015 distinguishes proper skepticism from pseudoskepticism and notes that the possibility of doubt is what makes manufactured doubt cheap.
- **Framework essays (high overlap).** The three essays above. Use them to explain the figures, never as independent corroboration.

No source conflicts with the figure verdict. The conflict rule was therefore not triggered. Agreement among the framework essays is expected, since they share an author and an evidence pool with the figures (Fig 2 :40).

## Distribution: offer, do not push

- **Fig 4 :22 "Only methods cross up. Only head starts come down."** Sending the full architecture before anyone has described a residual sends a recording. Lead with the condition set and its refuter. Offer the links after.
- **Fig 10 :42 "A verifier that can reconstruct the claim cannot verify it."** A recipient who has absorbed the framework before giving a verdict has joined the frame being checked. Record the stage-1 verdict first.
- **Fig 9 :27-28 "describe it before you go looking, or any frame you pick will seem to fit."** Ask what they would expect to see if the condition set were true before giving them the sources.
- **Fig 2 :40 "agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool".** Do not select recipients for likely agreement, and do not pool later agreement as corroboration.
- **Fig 1 :22 "Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average."** Ask for case-level verdicts, not a general endorsement.

**Recommended order in a reply:** (1) the condition set and its refuter; (2) ask which condition decided a case they know, and what they would count as a failure; (3) record that verdict; (4) only then the links, with the note that the figures, not the summary, are the authority.

## The two-stage offer (three steps)

1. **Stage 1:** validate the condition set against a real case, with no theory shown. Fig 10 :52 "Bring a characterised residual, never the source."
2. **Stage 2, opt-in:** if curious, offer the three source links: the figures repository, the canonical README, and one independent source matched to the reader's field. Their later agreement is never pooled as corroboration (Fig 2 :40).
3. **Stage 3:** offer the formula as a method to reuse, with the instruction that each reader writes their own refuter and states their own mode. Fig 4 :22 permits sending a method; it does not permit sending an answer.

**Hard precondition:** stages 1 to 3 only work if stage 1 survives. The condition set has no ground reading yet, so it is explicitly `specified`, not `grounded`, and is presented as a checkable shape rather than a result.
