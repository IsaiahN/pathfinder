## Independent domain census (from the residual line alone)

I redid the census before reading the primary's conclusions, using the residual line as stated in the question folder: "When does skepticism toward experts improve knowledge, and when does it make reliable knowledge harder to establish?"

- **R (arity / symmetry / scale).** The residual is the variation in the valence of a refusal directed at expert standing or an expert claim. Arity: at least three terms (skeptic, expert claim, what settles it), and a fourth (the channel) is forced by the degrading cases, because a doubt can cut the route to the reference without touching the claim. Symmetry: the refusal is unary and asymmetric. Scale: claim, standing of the person, certifying institution.
- **Domain 1, claim and reference.** Actors: held claim, instrument or outcome. Currency: settlement. Anchor: the reference that does not move when a frame pushes on it. Type: settleable or not.
- **Domain 2, frames.** Actors: two frames and what each draws on. Currency: overlap and residual. Anchor: the reference as selection pressure between frames. Type: selection, not negotiation.
- **Domain 3, the channel.** Actors: the maintainer, instruments, journals, credentials, public. Currency: contact and convention. Anchor: reachability of the reference. Type: maintenance.
- **Union, not partition.** A single event can be improving in D1 and degrading in D2 and D3. That is a compound, not a mean.
- **Order.** Settleability first (otherwise there is no input), independence second (otherwise no new frame), channel third (otherwise no carry).

My census matches the primary's three domains, union reading, and dependency order. ## Round trip gap R_T

The Symbols table defines R_T as the gap between what was sent up and what came back down, `gap(x, (T_E ∘ T_A)(x))`, and Fig 4 :30 requires it to be measured rather than assumed.

1. **Sent up (x):** the verbatim figure set, chiefly Fig 2 (anchor, independence, collapses), Fig 7 (selection between rooms), Fig 10 (channel and stake), Fig 11 (valence relative to residual).
2. **Abstraction (T_A):** the shorthand carried in the folder, "skepticism has no intrinsic valence; three conditions decide it", plus the isomer reading of the two regimes.
3. **Concretisation (T_E):** the concrete claim about cases, "an outsider who doubts on different evidence improves knowledge; a campaign that doubts the instrument degrades it".

**R_T is nonzero.** Three things the shorthand holds do not survive the trip back down cleanly:

- the **measurement of overlap ρ** between belief systems. The shorthand treats independence as a check one can perform. Fig 2 :31-32 defines it, but no figure supplies an instrument (Fig 3 :27).
- the **pre-set refusal criterion**. The shorthand says "settleable aim"; concretising it requires a criterion written before the run, which the figures give only as a discipline (formula Direction step) and not as a step in the loop.
- the **stake symmetry**. The shorthand says "declare stakes"; the figures require it on both sides, including the expert's standing (Fig 10 :61), which is easy to drop when writing about skeptics.

One thing survives cleanly: a refusal whose frame differs from the expert's and whose reference cannot be moved is a selection, not a negotiation. That is the residue worth sending. R_T was **not** measured numerically, because there is no ground reading; it is recorded qualitatively as the enumerated set of dropped constraints. Treating "the formula is the condensed version of the metalaws" as identity would set R_T to zero, which is the dead-reckoning error Fig 4 :30 names.
