## The question restated as an effect

A skepticism event S is an act of withholding assent from, or refusing, an expert claim E or the standing of the expert who carries it. The same kind of event is observed to be followed by two different outcomes. Sometimes errors are corrected, a new frame contacts the ground, and knowledge improves. Sometimes settled findings are reopened without a ground reading, the instruments that could settle the matter are discredited, and reliable knowledge becomes harder to establish. What is being decided is the variable on which this valence indexes. Option A: treat skepticism as a virtue whose valence is intrinsic. This leads to the instruction "doubt more" and cannot explain the second outcome. Option B: treat skepticism as a relation between a doubting frame, a held term, and a ground. This leads to a conditional answer with two regimes, each with a stated falsifier. The derivation below takes Option B.

**What an answer would look like, bound as an ordinary term:** a *shape*, that is, a set of conditions stated in effect terms such that a reader can test a case against them before knowing our conclusion. The shape is: skepticism improves knowledge when the refusal aims at a settleable held term, comes from a frame with low overlap with the expert frame, and preserves the channel to the ground; it degrades knowledge when the refusal aims at the ground or the channel, comes from a correlated pool, or is driven by a stake in the reading. This shape is written down and given a refuter in `01_figure_grounded_audit.md` §4 before the verdict is stated.

## What is deliberately not here

- No personal emails of named individuals.
- No ranking of people by how likely they are to agree with this answer.
- No bulk-send plan, and no receptiveness column.

Three reasons, in the figures' own words:

1. **A room does not give you a ground.** Fig 7 :35 "A room's agreement is variation, not a verdict. Selection between rooms is where the ground votes." Selecting recipients for likely agreement produces a convention, not a check.
2. **Correlation disqualifies a verdict.** Fig 2 :40 "agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool". A list chosen for receptiveness manufactures the disqualifying correlation.
3. **Asking for trust before a check spends the reader's independence.** Fig 10 :42 "A verifier that can reconstruct the claim cannot verify it."

Legally: unsolicited bulk email to scraped addresses breaches GDPR and CAN-SPAM style rules in many jurisdictions. The practical failure is added to the structural one. Opt-in institutional channels avoid both.
