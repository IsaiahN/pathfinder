## 1. A shape is a constraint, not a filler

The canonical rule: the framework reports *"what a missing thing would have to look like (a shape, not a name)"* (figures README, local copy at `FRAMEWORK/reference/FIGURES_README_canonical.md`). Fig 9 :32 "you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself."

*The Library of Babel* states the operational form: *"You can cut your search short if the set of books returned is small and descriptive enough that it provides a general shape of the answer that satisfies your question."* And: *"This anchor is provided by your own question: If the question is answered by your present collection of books from your search you know whether to stop or continue and which direction to turn next."*

For HIS-0018, a shape is therefore **not** a verdict about whether skepticism is good or bad, and not a list of historical cases. It is the smallest condition set that lets a reader decide a new case before knowing our conclusion. The shape derived in `01` §0 is:

> skepticism improves knowledge iff its refusal aims at a settleable held term, comes from a frame with low overlap with the expert frame, and preserves the channel to the ground; it degrades knowledge when the refusal aims at the ground or the channel, comes from a correlated pool, or is driven by a stake in the reading.

That is a shape, because it says what an answer must look like without naming a winner.

## 2. How the shape is obtained: describe the effect first

Fig 6 :30 "The description travels. Fails by being sharpened into terms only we can read. Cast it in effect terms." Fig 9 :27-28 "describe it before you go looking, or any frame you pick will seem to fit".

*The Library of Babel* gives the bootstrapping method: use the vocabulary you already have that is closest to the thing, look for recurring new terms that overlap or correlate with it, recurse. *"With each new term gained, you are able to narrow the space by definition and deepen your vocabulary."* The essay also states the reason outsider doubt can be productive: *"This is why people new to a research field can often make connections that the seasoned experts may miss."* That is the shape's D2 condition in plain language, and it is why the answer to this question is not simply "defer to expertise".

## 3. What decides the valence: three echoes across the essays

### 3.1 Drift is the failure mode, not disagreement

*Decoding Tacit Knowledge*: *"Two people who only check against each other drift, and they drift together, so neither notices."* And: *"Shared pretext is good for one thing and ruins another. Agreement is the whole point when you're settling what words mean. It's worthless for settling what's true when both sides learned from the same sources."* This is Fig 2 :40 in prose. It decides the independence condition: a skeptic who shares the expert's pool adds spelling, not search.

*How Intelligence Actually Works* gives the room version: *"the room session only produces an outcome that you agree on, having come from diverse frames with different vantage points of the problem. It does not mean that the output was objective truth."* And: *"Diversity is a natural hedge."*

*How a Society of Agents Would Work*: *"A group is only worth having while its members stay different. Figure 2 defines independence as 'how little they draw on the same evidence.' Ten copies watching the same thing are one opinion said ten times."* Also: *"Use the success rate per try, not the total. Whatever gets tried most piles up votes."* This is the per-slot rule (Fig 1 :19) applied to expertise.

### 3.2 Three institutions already embody the drift-stoppers

*Decoding Tacit Knowledge* names them explicitly: *"Science's independent replication is separate experience plus something real to check against; Apprenticeship is two-way contact with correction; Courts of law rule on evidence, which neither side can move."*

This is the closest thing in the essays to a direct answer to HIS-0018. Skepticism improves knowledge when it is routed through one of these three structures. It degrades knowledge when it bypasses all three. The figures present the same three as D1 (settlement), D2 (separate experience), and D3 (a ground neither party can move).

### 3.3 Standing is a reading of a projection, not the source

*The Hard Problem is About Source* gives the metamerism case: a color instrument reads green and *"is speaking only about what it measured, it cannot comment on the source."* Several sources can produce the same reading. *"If several paths create the same behavior, then behavior is a projection with lossy information"*, and *"An instrument that can't distinguish these cases demonstrates its own inadequacy."*

Applied to expertise: a credential or a prestigious publication is a reading of a projection. It cannot comment on whether the person currently has contact with the ground. This is the figure rule that *"Prestige can fund an action. It can give an action a budget to be tried. But it cannot settle an action."* (*The Recursive Self-Improvement Trap*.) It runs both ways: the skeptic's standing is no more a verdict than the expert's.

*How Intelligence Actually Works* adds the lineage requirement: *"The projection that no instrument distinguishes from the thing is still a projection, which is why the lineage has to be kept even when the copy is perfect."* So the check is not "who is the expert" but "what is the provenance of this claim against the ground".

## 4. What licenses a name here

Ordinarily the figures forbid naming the missing primitive. For this question nothing is missing in that sense: the terms (claim, frame, ground, channel, stake) are already held. So the question is a **binding** problem, not a minting problem. What must be recorded is the provenance of each binding: which frame supplied it and what it predicts.

*They're Scanning Books When They Should Be Studying Nature* warns against treating the corpus as the generator: *"A corpus is the compressed output of human minds; the capacity that produced it is a generator, and outputs do not contain their generator."* A list of famous skeptics, or a list of famous experts, is an output. It is not the generator of a check. And: *"Declaring the primitives does not make them neutral. It makes the choice visible, which is worth a great deal, because a visible choice can be revised and an emergent one cannot."* Declaring the three conditions makes our choice visible and revisable; it does not make it neutral.

So the honest form names **conditions**, not people, and records where each condition came from.

## 5. Order inside the shape

*How a Society of Agents Would Work* gives the dependency rule for correction: a shared store only helps if what rises in it is what works, and *"the ground sits under all three"* stores. The essay also gives the reason vote counts mislead: success rate per try beats total votes. This is Fig 1 :19 "The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated" stated for institutions.

Dependency order for the shape: settle the claim against a ground first (D1); then check whether the doubting frame is independent and selected against the surfaced residual (D2); then check whether the correcting institution keeps the anchor reachable (D3). Fig 3 :19 "Optimising a link that already works cannot rescue one that does not."

## 6. Simulation caveat

*The Recursive Self-Improvement Trap*: *"LLMs can quickly simulate an approximate answer/output with the formula found in the architecture post."* and *"the truly testable output would need to be derived from the real architecture or working the formula yourself."* The canonical article adds that the outputs must be seen as *"simulacra"* and that the approach *"could never be verified if the solutions presented were not contrived to fit the formula."*

Therefore `01_figure_grounded_audit.md` and this file are simulated derivations, checked for internal consistency against the figures. They are not a ground reading and cannot settle the question they report on. The pre-registered refuter in `01` §4 is the instrument offered in place of self-validation.

## 7. Correction: the Popper mapping is declined by the framework

In `01` F3 the SEP entry on Popper was used to corroborate **the discipline of fixing a refusal criterion in advance**. That use is narrow and stands. But the canonical article's section "Three mappings offered and declined" explicitly declines *"The seat as a Popperian critic. Popper's critic is inside the community and draws on its evidence. The seat's defining property is that it cannot reconstruct the claim... and the opposite of the critical-rationalist picture rather than a version of it."*

So this solution does **not** claim that critical rationalism is the framework's verification arrangement. Popper corroborates one move (a refutation criterion fixed before a test); he does not supply the seat. That distinction is recorded as a strain and a retraction in `01` §7 and in `11_audit.md`. The framework's own version of the pre-run commitment is the formula's Direction step: *"Committing a prediction before a run is this, already working."*
