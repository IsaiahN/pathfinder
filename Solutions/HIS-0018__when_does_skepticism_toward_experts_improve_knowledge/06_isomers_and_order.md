# Two regimes as a union, and the order of the three conditions

**Mode: `general` on the structure, `specified` on the order.** This is a simulated derivation checked against the figures (`05_what_shape_means.md` §6), not a ground reading.

## RESIDUAL

`01` derived three conditions for the improving regime (settleable aim, independence, channel preservation). Two questions are still owed. Are the improving and degrading regimes two kinds of skepticism, or one set of conditions arranged differently? And in what order do the three conditions bind, since a case can satisfy two and fail the third?

## DOMAIN CENSUS

Atoms held: a held claim E, a ground G, a skeptic frame B, an expert frame A, a channel C, and a stake σ. Bonds held: the seven, with ¬ as the unary refusal. No new primitive is needed. The census is D1 (claim and ground), D2 (frames), D3 (channel and habitat), ordered by dependency as in `01` §1.

## DERIVATION

### Q1  /  Are the two regimes isomers or a partition?

Fig 12 :117 "isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour." Fig 9 :54 "And when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again." Fig 9 :55 "A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones."

A single skepticism event can be well-aimed at a claim (D1 improving) while coming from a correlated pool (D2 degrading) and cutting the channel (D3 degrading). That is not one harder case of either regime. It is a compound with its own loop. Fig 9 :57 "What remains is smaller and it is also categorically different: a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either. It stops being a mixture and becomes a specific compound."

**Conclusion:** the regimes are **isomers over the same atom set**, not two kinds of skepticism and not ends of a single scale. Same atoms (claim, ground, frames, channel, stake), different bond arrangement, different outcome. The "formula" that hides the difference is the sentence "skepticism is useful" or "skepticism is harmful", which records the ingredients and not the arrangement (Fig 12 :14-17).

### Q2  /  Are the conditions additive or a product?

Fig 5 :33 "The three guards are a product, not a checklist." Fig 5 :41 "Any one of them at zero stops the mint entirely. Which is why a system that is minting nothing may have nothing wrong with its minting."

The three conditions are a **product**. Any one at zero stops the improvement, regardless of the other two. This matters because it reverses the usual rhetorical move: a skeptic who is brilliantly independent but aims at the channel produces no improvement; an institution with perfect replication but a correlated pool produces no improvement. There is no compensation between conditions.

**Cost distinction, held apart:** Fig 12 :125-127 "A composition costs in two currencies, and they do not add. Bits price the description. Actions price finding out whether it holds." Describing the three conditions is cheap; running the checks (finding the ground, measuring overlap, auditing the channel) is expensive. A claim that the shape is "simple" counts the description and ignores the actions.

### Q3  /  In what order do the conditions bind?

Fig 3 :20-21 "The chain is ordered by dependency, not by importance. Each link consumes what the one above produces". Fig 12 :71 "A then B; B needs A to have happened".

1. **Aim (D1) first.** Without a claim that a ground could settle, there is nothing for a second frame to be independent *about*. Fig 3 :22-23 "A step whose input never arrives cannot be diagnosed, only its predecessor can. Which is why a reading taken below the break is not a weak reading. It is a reading of nothing."
2. **Independence (D2) second.** Given a settleable claim, look for a frame whose closure differs and whose doubt is selected against the residual the expert frame surfaced. Fig 7 :40 "The room is summoned by the residual rather than composed."
3. **Channel (D3) third.** Given a corrected claim from an independent frame, check that the institution can carry the correction forward. Fig 10 :29 "keep the anchor reachable".

Fig 3 :27 "A link with no instrument is not a link that works. It is a link nobody has looked at." D1 and D3 have instruments (experiments, replication, courts). D2 does not have a stated figure instrument for measuring overlap between belief systems. This is recorded as a gap in `01` §7, not filled by assertion.

### Q4  /  Institutional analogues: same atoms, different bond

The *Decoding Tacit Knowledge* essay names three drift-stoppers: *"Science's independent replication is separate experience plus something real to check against; Apprenticeship is two-way contact with correction; Courts of law rule on evidence, which neither side can move."*

Under Fig 12 these are **isomers**, not instances of one institution:

| arrangement | atoms | bond | what decides |
|---|---|---|---|
| replication | claim, ground, second instrument | → sequence then ⋛ comparison | does the independent instrument pay? |
| apprenticeship | learner, practitioner, correction | → sequence, two-way | does the learner's next attempt match? |
| court | two parties, evidence, judge | ⋛ comparison with an anchor neither party can move | does the evidence settle? |

All three refuse a held term (¬) and all three keep a ground unreachable by negotiation. *"The arrangement is the substance"* (Fig 12 :94). This is why importing one institution's rule into another without the bond fails: a court that lets parties move the evidence has the atoms and not the arrangement.

### Q5  /  The mirror failure is a fourth arrangement, not a missing third condition

Fig 2 :35 "Alignment is triangulation, not negotiation. A mirror cannot correct you." Fig 7 :36 "A chain of mirrors is one long room. The next room must differ from the last, and match the residual it surfaced."

A community of skeptics who all draw on the same pool is not a stronger version of skepticism; it is the same arrangement with the ground removed. *How a Society of Agents Would Work*: *"Ten copies watching the same thing are one opinion said ten times."* The fix is not more skeptics (Fig 6 :49-50: more search at the same reach) but a different closure (Fig 8 :45 "Two frames together reach what neither reaches alone").

## CONCLUSION

The improving and degrading outcomes are **isomers over one atom set**, governed by Fig 9 :54-58 and Fig 12 :117. The three conditions form a **product**, not a checklist (Fig 5 :33, :41), and bind in dependency order **aim, independence, channel** (Fig 3 :20-21). The three institutional arrangements that already implement them (replication, apprenticeship, courts) are themselves isomers: same atoms, different bond, different behaviour. No new primitive was needed.

## FALSIFIER (pre-registered)

The union/order claim fails if a case is found in which the three conditions are satisfied in a different order (channel first, then independence, then aim) and the outcome is still an improvement in contact with the ground, with no hidden compensating condition. It also fails if any one condition at zero can be compensated by the other two at full strength. Both are written here before the verdict in `01` §8.
