**Subject:** One condition set to check about skepticism and expertise

> Hi [name],
>
> I have one specific output I would like you to check against what you already know. I am not asking you to read the theory first, and I am not asking you to agree with it. If the output does not survive, there is nothing else to look at.
>
> **The question in effect terms:** skepticism toward experts sometimes improves knowledge and sometimes makes reliable knowledge harder to establish. The same act of withholding assent produces both outcomes. I am asking what variable the valence indexes on.
>
> **The output (`general` structure, `specified` conditions, not `grounded`):** skepticism carries no intrinsic valence. It is a refusal applied to a held term, and its effect depends on three relations:
>
> 1. **Aim.** Is the refusal directed at a claim that a ground could settle, with a criterion fixed in advance for what would end the doubt? Or is it directed at the instrument and the settled record themselves?
> 2. **Independence.** Does the skeptical frame draw on materially different evidence from the expert frame, and is it selected against the residual the expert frame surfaced? Or does it draw on the same pool?
> 3. **Channel.** Does the act keep the anchor reachable, with stakes declared on both sides? Or does it cut the channel to the ground without putting a better instrument in its place?
>
> All three improving predicts that knowledge improves. Inverting any one predicts that reliable knowledge becomes harder to establish. These are filters for what to measure, not a verdict about who is right.
>
> **What this does not say:** that experts are usually right, that doubt is usually useful, that credentials prove anything, or that any particular historical case has been settled by this. The framework returns a shape and a route, not a name and not a ground reading. Everything here is a simulation checked for internal consistency against the figures.
>
> **What would refute it, written before I ran this:**
> 1. A case where skepticism improved knowledge while the skeptic shared the expert's evidence pool, aimed at the channel rather than a settleable claim, and declared a stake in the outcome.
> 2. A case where skepticism degraded knowledge while independence, settleable aim and channel preservation all held.
> 3. A case where a doubt improved contact although the skeptic could not say in advance what observation would end it.
>
> If any one holds, I withdraw the claim. A one-line "this fails because..." is more useful to me than agreement. If you would rather not engage, no reply is needed.
>
> **Two questions I would value your verdict on, before you see anything else:**
> - For a case you know well, which of the three conditions actually decided the outcome?
> - What would you count as a failure of this condition set?
>
> **If it survives, and only then:** here are the sources. The figures are the authority, not my summary of them.
> - Figures and formula, public repository: https://github.com/IsaiahN/tether/tree/general-agent/figures
> - Canonical usage rule: https://github.com/IsaiahN/tether/blob/general-agent/figures/README.md
> - The Third Wave of Science Studies (Collins and Evans, 2002): https://orca.cardiff.ac.uk/id/eprint/78065
> - Skepticism (Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy): https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/skepticism/
>
> **If you want to use the machinery:** the formula is in the figures link. Run it on a problem you know well and can check against a ground. Write down what would refute your result *before* you run it, and state the mode of what you get. A reported failure is more useful to me than a reported success.
