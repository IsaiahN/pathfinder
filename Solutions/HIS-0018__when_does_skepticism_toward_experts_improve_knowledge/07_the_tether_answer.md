# The Tether's answer: when skepticism improves knowledge and when it degrades it

**Mode: `general` on the structure, `specified` on the conditions. Not `grounded`.** This is a shape and a route, not a measurement. Simulated derivation checked against the figures (`05_what_shape_means.md` §6), not a ground reading.

## 1. The residual it answers

The same act of withholding assent from an expert claim is followed by improvement in some cases and degradation in others. The residual is the variation in valence. It is not one hard question with a middle answer; it is a union of conditions that must be unbundled (Fig 9 :54-55). Per domain:

- **D1, claim and ground:** is the refusal aimed at a term a ground could settle, with a criterion fixed in advance?
- **D2, frames:** does the skeptical frame draw on materially different evidence, and is it selected against the residual the expert frame surfaced?
- **D3, channel:** does the act keep the anchor reachable, with stakes declared on both sides?

## 2. The answer

1. **Skepticism carries no intrinsic valence.** It is a negation, a refusal of a held term (Fig 12 :112). Fig 11 :49 "An actor's valence is relative to the residual, not a property it carries." So "doubt more" and "trust more" are both non-answers. The right question is what makes a skepticism event capable, and capability is a property of skeptic-and-habitat (Fig 11 :39).

2. **It improves knowledge when three conditions hold as a product.** Any one at zero stops the improvement (Fig 5 :33, :41).
   - **Aim:** the refusal points at a claim with a persistent, flat residual, and the skeptic can say in advance what would end the doubt. Formula step 2: what is owed is a refusal, not an explanation. Fig 5 :58-59 "a term whose residual PERSISTS AND DOES NOT TREND DOWN is the one to point the refusal at".
   - **Independence:** Fig 2 :31-32 "Independence, between two frames, is how little they draw on the same evidence." Fig 7 :24-25 "low ρ between rooms; the next room is picked against the residual the last one surfaced". Fig 7 :35 "A room's agreement is variation, not a verdict. Selection between rooms is where the ground votes."
   - **Channel:** Fig 10 :24 "the ground does not decay, the channel does". Fig 10 :29 "keep the anchor reachable". Stakes must be declared on both sides, since credentials are "a stake that survives the outcome it was earned on" (Fig 10 :61).

3. **It degrades knowledge when the relations are inverted.** The refusal aims at the ground or the settled record (Fig 2 :47 "A ground listed as one of the parties has already become a frame"), the pool is shared (Fig 2 :40 "agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool"), or the channel is cut without a replacement instrument (Fig 11 :66 "Isolation is not removal of the habitat. It is substitution of one habitat for another."). The drift is coherent, which is why nobody notices (Fig 2 :34).

4. **The two outcomes are isomers, not two kinds of skepticism.** Same atoms, different bond, different behaviour (Fig 12 :117). A case can be improving in D2 and degrading in D3 at once; that is a compound with its own loop, not an average (Fig 9 :57).

5. **The conditions bind in dependency order:** aim, then independence, then channel (Fig 3 :20-21). Optimising the third cannot rescue a missing first.

6. **The shape is cheap to describe and expensive to check.** Fig 12 :125-127 holds the two currencies apart: bits price the description, actions price finding out whether it holds. A condition set that looks obvious still costs the work of contacting the ground.

## 3. What it explicitly does not say

- Not that experts are usually right, or that skepticism is usually wrong. Valence is relational.
- Not that any particular historical case has been settled here. No measurement was taken.
- Not that credentials are meaningless. They are a reading of a projection and cannot comment on the source (*The Hard Problem is About Source*); they can still fund an action being tried.
- Not that critical rationalism supplies the verification seat. That mapping is declined by the canonical article; see `05_what_shape_means.md` §7.
- Not a verdict for a whole field or a whole public. Fig 1 :22 "Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average."
- Not `grounded`. The claim is a candidate with a pre-registered refuter.

## 4. What would settle it  /  the ground

The world sources supply the instruments that the figures do not:

- **Independence:** measure the overlap between the skeptical and expert frames by asking what evidence each draws on separately. Lord, Ross and Lepper (1979) show that identical evidence polarizes opposed groups; Fig 2 :40 predicts exactly that. World source: https://fbaum.unc.edu/teaching/articles/jpsp-1979-Lord-Ross-Lepper.pdf
- **Aim:** require a refutation criterion fixed before the doubt is counted. The SEP entry on Popper reports the rule that *"criteria of refutation have to be laid down beforehand"*. World source: https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/popper/
- **Channel:** ask whether the correction survives an institution that neither party can move. *Decoding Tacit Knowledge* names replication, apprenticeship and courts as the three existing arrangements. World source: https://adventuresinml.substack.com/p/decoding-tacit-knowledge-why-context
- **Standing:** declare the stake on both sides. Collins and Evans (2002) argue against *"both an excessive reliance on science and an unrestrained suspicion of expertise."*
- **Pre-registered falsifiers:** `01` §4 lists five. The first is decisive: a documented case where all three conditions are inverted and knowledge still improves.

## 5. The honest stop

**Unreached, not unreachable.** There is no figure instrument for measuring overlap ρ between belief systems, and no ground reading of the condition set. Fig 3 :27 "A link with no instrument is not a link that works. It is a link nobody has looked at." Fig 8 :39 "So a search that finds nothing is never a proof of absence." The derivation stops at the limit of what can be distinguished and records the lineage so the stop is checkable. Fig 13 :134 "Unreachable is a verdict here, not a silence".

## 6. One-paragraph version for outreach

> Skepticism toward experts has no fixed valence. It is a refusal, and what the refusal is aimed at decides the outcome. It improves knowledge when three conditions hold together: the doubt targets a claim that some ground could settle and the doubter can say in advance what would end it; the doubting frame draws on evidence genuinely different from the expert's and is selected against the problem the expert's own frame surfaced; and the act leaves the channel to the ground open, with stakes declared on both sides. It degrades knowledge when the doubt targets the instrument or the settled record, comes from a pool that shares the expert's evidence, or cuts the channel without putting a better one in its place. These are filters for what to check, not a verdict about who is right. The shape comes with falsifiers written in advance, and the case where all three conditions are inverted yet knowledge improves would break it.
