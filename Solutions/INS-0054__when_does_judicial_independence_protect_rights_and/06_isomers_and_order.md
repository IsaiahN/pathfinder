# Supporting derivation: union, isomer, threshold and order

**Mode: `general` on the structure, `specified` on the court application, not `grounded`.** This is a simulated derivation checked against the figures, not a ground reading (see `05_what_shape_means.md` section 6).

## RESIDUAL

The audit left two questions open: are the rights effect and the accountability effect one thing arranged differently, and in what order does the derivation run? A third follows: can the two be priced on one scale? All three are answered before any verdict is stated in `07_the_tether_answer.md`.

## DOMAIN CENSUS

Atoms held for this derivation: a judge, a tenure or removal rule, law and facts, a person affected, an electorate, a reason-giving duty. Bonds held: the seven plus negation. No new primitive is available without import (Fig 6 :23: "No frame escapes its own closure. The bound moves only on contact with a frame whose closure differs."). Because both effects are built from the same atoms, the question is one of arrangement, not of inventory.

## DERIVATION

### Q1 - Partition or union, and are the two effects isomers?

- Fig 9 :54: "And when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again."
- Fig 9 :57: "a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either".
- Fig 12 :117: "isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."
- Fig 12 :123: "functional group. A substructure whose presence fixes the outcome. The group says what kind; the qualifier says which member."

The two effects share the full atom set. What differs is which bond carries the insulation:

- `E1` rights protection: judge + law and facts + person, with the insulation placed between the judge and the political frames. The judge-to-ground bond is kept, so the anchor ranges against law and facts.
- `E2` accountability insulation: judge + law and facts + person + electorate, with the insulation moved onto the judge-to-ground channel. The anchor still looks like an anchor, but the channel that used to carry correction is gone.

So under Fig 12 :117 the two are **isomers**: same atoms, different bond, different substance. Their common substructure (a judge ranging against a ground under a tenure rule) is the **functional group** of Fig 12 :123: it says what kind, and the placement of the insulation says which member. This is a structural claim, not an empirical one.

**Falsifier, written before the run:** if one effect can be produced without an atom the other requires (rights protection with no electorate at all, or accountability insulation with no law-and-facts ground), the isomer claim fails and the two must be treated as different inventories rather than different arrangements.

### Q2 - The discriminating quantity is an ordered threshold, not a symmetric balance

- Operators table :49: comparison is "a threshold relation between two quantities"; the test is "is it about which is larger, rather than both being present? Yes."
- Fig 12 :72: "A then B; B needs A to have happened" for sequence, and the Operators table notes that sequence is ordered.
- Fig 2 :42: "Buy independence in proportion to the cost of being wrong."

Independence is tuned against a cost, and the tuning is ordered: as insulation increases past the point where it shields the anchor from political frames and onto the point where it severs the anchor's channel, the substance changes. This is not a symmetric join of two equal terms. Writing it as a balanced scale (a 'plus' bond) would be a notation error: Fig 12 :59 says of conjunction, "swap the operands. Does the meaning change? No." Here swapping the relation changes everything, so the bond is ordered.

A second notation error is to write the threshold between "rights" and "democracy" as if they were two quantities on one axis. Fig 12 :125 says two currencies do not add, and Fig 1 :22 forbids averaging across slots. The threshold is between two *placements of insulation*, not between two values of one score.

### Q3 - Two currencies, no common denominator

- Fig 12 :125: "A composition costs in two currencies, and they do not add."
- Fig 12 :126: "Bits price the description. Actions price finding out whether it holds."
- Fig 1 :19: "The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated."

Rights protection prices the description: what does the law require for this person? Accountability prices the actions: what did the ruling do, and did review catch the error? A system holding only one accounting will propose arrangements it cannot afford to test. This is why a reform that increases tenure may look excellent in the description currency and fail in the action currency, with no signal that anything was lost.

### Q4 - Order by dependency, and the second-order compound

- Fig 3 :20: "The chain is ordered by dependency, not by importance."
- Fig 3 :19: "Optimising a link that already works cannot rescue one that does not."
- Fig 3 :27: "A link with no instrument is not a link that works. It is a link nobody has looked at."
- Fig 12 :104: "A settled molecule becomes an atom for whatever composes over it."

The order is: can the judge perceive the ground (evidence, reasons), does the vocabulary contain terms for right and rule, can the court say what counts as settling a case, then adjudication, then precedent with provenance. Only after a first-order ruling is settled can it become an atom for the second-order review compound C12. Judicial review is therefore not a third branch of government in this derivation; it is a second-order composition over settled first-order rulings. If the perception link is missing, adding a removal rule optimises above the break and rescues nothing.

### Q5 - Low-overlap analogues

- **Accounting and information systems (separation of duties).** "Increased protection from fraud and errors must be balanced with the increased cost/effort required"; two signatures on a cheque is the traditional example; "In a perfect system, no one person should handle more than one type of function." Predicts that role separation is a priced control whose benefit depends on the cost of error and that compensating controls are needed when separation is impossible. **Corroborates** F4 and F6 at the mechanism level. Single witness from this field; not a measurement of courts.
- **Economics and institutional theory (principal-agent).** The problem "worsens when there is a greater discrepancy of interests and information... as well as when the principal lacks the means to punish the agent"; "elected officials (agent) and citizens (principal)"; multiple principals intensify it. Predicts that accountability is a channel problem, not a quantity of independence. **Corroborates** F3 and F5 at the mechanism level. Single witness from this field.
- **Constitutional theory (Federalist No. 78, 1788).** Tenure is "one of the most valuable of the modern improvements in the practice of government" because the judiciary has "neither FORCE nor WILL, but merely judgment" and must be "an intermediate body between the people and the legislature". Predicts that the insulation is between the judge and the branches, not between the judge and the law. **Corroborates** F2. This is a primary text in the same broad field as the counter-majoritarian literature, so it is one pool with that literature, not two.

No conflict with the figure lines was found. If one had appeared, the instruction is to return to the census, not to flip the verdict.

## CONCLUSION

The two effects are **isomers** over a shared atom set, differing in which bond carries the insulation; their common substructure is a functional group. The discriminating quantity is an **ordered threshold**, not a symmetric balance. Rights and accountability are **two currencies** that do not add. The derivation is **ordered by dependency** from perception to precedent, with the review compound at second order over settled rulings. The falsifier above is the one to test first.
