## 0. The residual in effect terms

Let `I` be the insulation of the adjudicative frame from the political and electoral frames. The question reports two effects of the same setting:

- `E1` rights protection: a judge ranges against law, facts and the person affected without being moved by majority pressure, so a minority right survives a majority's momentary will;
- `E2` accountability insulation: the judge stops ranging against that ground, the channel decays, and electoral or reason-giving correction cannot reach the seat.

`R = |Gamma(b,a) - o'|` per slot. The slots are `E1` and `E2`, and they are never aggregated. Fig 1 :19: "The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated." Fig 1 :22: "Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average." A single "balance between independence and accountability" score is the exact error Fig 1 names.

The residual itself is: **the same insulation setting is claimed to produce both effects, and nothing in the question says which relation the insulation is between.** That is the gap. The answer must name the relation, not the scalar.

Binding the sought-for shape as an ordinary term (THE_FORMULA, "Direction"): a candidate answer would look like a discriminating quantity `D`, in effect terms, with the property that `D` holds when insulation runs between the judge and political pressure (E1) and fails when insulation has moved onto the judge's channel to the ground (E2). Once bound, `D` can accrue a residual and be refused by the same test as everything else.

## 1. Domain census (before any figure line is chosen)

**R by arity, symmetry, scale.** Arity: two effects sharing one setting, a union with an overlap compound, not one hard question (Fig 9 :54-57). Symmetry: independence is a relation between two frames, and the question is directional, the judge is insulated *from* the political frames while those frames still act on the judge through appointment and budget; the bond is ordered and threshold-like, not a symmetric join. Operators table :49 defines comparison as "a threshold relation between two quantities". Scale: first order is a case (judge, litigant, law and facts); second order is the institutional rule (tenure, appointment, removal, reason-giving). The membrane between them is lossy (Fig 4), and a settled first-order ruling becomes an atom at the next level (Fig 12 :104).

## 3. Findings

### F1 - The question is a union compound; unbundle before searching

Fig 9 :54: "And when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again." Fig 9 :55: "A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones." Fig 9 :57: "a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either". Fig 1 :19: "The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated."

The two effects overlap at judicial review but do not reduce to one another. A lever that clears rights protection (more insulation) can leave the accountability condition standing, and vice versa. Any single index that averages them has thrown away the only signal that says which slot is live.

- Mode: `general` (structural).
- Status: **triangulated** across Fig 9 and Fig 1 (low overlap: union/partition logic versus per-slot indexing).
- Refuter: a single score that predicts both effects without residual on either slot, across a documented change of one condition only.

### F2 - Rights protection is the anchor rule: the judge must not update on political frames

Fig 2 :33: "The anchor must not update." Fig 2 :34: "If it does, panel 3 turns into panel 1: the anchor becomes another frame, everything drifts together, and the drift is coherent, which is why nobody notices." Fig 2 :45: "Frames range against the ground. They do not negotiate with it." Fig 2 :36: "An anchor is legitimate when the question is constitutively about it." Fig 2 :37: "Human judgment is the right anchor for is this a good answer for a person, because the question is about people."

The adjudicative seat is the anchor for a legal dispute. Its insulation from majority pressure is what keeps it from updating on the political frames. Because rights questions are constitutively about persons, human judgment is the legitimate anchor here, not merely an instrument. Hamilton's "good behaviour" tenure and "neither FORCE nor WILL, but merely judgment" (Federalist No. 78) are a world-facing restatement of the same structure, recorded as corroboration only.

- Mode: `specified` for courts, `general` for the anchor rule.
- Status: **triangulated** across Fig 2 and Fig 7 (the anchor rule versus selection between rooms).
- Refuter: a system in which rights improved while the adjudicative frame demonstrably updated on majority preference and no separate correction against law and facts occurred. That would mean insulation was not the cause.

### F3 - The insulation becomes harmful when it moves onto the ground channel

Fig 10 :24: "the ground does not decay, the channel does". Fig 10 :25: "stale percepts, dropped transients, saturated metrics, pooled readings". Fig 10 :27: "the output is not a verdict, it is you have lost touch". Fig 2 :47: "A ground listed as one of the parties has already become a frame."

The discrimination is not the amount of insulation but which relation it insulates. If insulation runs between the judge and political pressure, the anchor stays pinned to law and facts. If insulation runs between the judge and the ground, the channel decays and the seat becomes another frame. At that point it is not independent; it is self-referential, and the drift is coherent, which is why nobody notices. Note that a verdict of "you have lost touch" is not a verdict about the case (Fig 10 :27-28).

- Mode: `specified`.
- Status: **triangulated** across Fig 10 and Fig 2 (channel maintenance versus anchor update), low overlap between the two lines' roles.
- Refuter: a system with a fully intact channel to law, facts and persons where political correction still cannot reach the seat and no rights loss or error correction follows. That would break the channel account of E2.

### F4 - Independence is bought in proportion to the cost of being wrong, and a guard that decides an outcome has stopped being one

Fig 2 :42: "Buy independence in proportion to the cost of being wrong." Fig 2 :43: "Guard, from Figure 10: a convention that decides an outcome has stopped being one." Fig 10 :47: "a convention that decides an outcome has stopped being one". Operators table :49: "a threshold relation between two quantities".

Independence is a guard, not a verdict. It is priced by the cost of a wrong outcome, which is why a rights-heavy domain buys more of it. But a guard that fixes the outcome rather than protecting the channel has changed kind: it is now a frame deciding the case, which is the named collapse. The discriminating quantity is ordered and threshold-like (more insulation helps until it reaches the channel), not a symmetric balance of two equal terms.

- Mode: `general` for the guard rule, `specified` for the threshold.
- Status: **single-source** for the guard rule: Fig 2 :43 and Fig 10 :47 are the same statement in two places, one figure lineage, so this is not two witnesses. The threshold form is corroborated by the Operators table, a separate notation file.
- Refuter: a scheme in which a fixed insulation rule is admitted as a decision of outcomes without being refusable, and the framework still calls it a guard.

### F5 - Democratic accountability is legitimate as selection between rooms, not as negotiation with the judge

Fig 7 :35: "A room's agreement is variation, not a verdict. Selection between rooms is where the ground votes." Fig 7 :36: "A chain of mirrors is one long room. The next room must differ from the last, and match the residual it surfaced." Fig 2 :46: "The anchor is not a third party to the disagreement and it is not the tie-breaker in a vote."

Elections, appointments and retention mechanisms are legitimate where they select the next room against the residual the last one surfaced, and where they transmit a ground-bearing reason rather than a preference. They become capture when the political frame updates the anchor (F2) or when the chain becomes mirrors, same closure handed along, "agreement compounds, evidence does not" (Fig 7 :13). Judicial elections that increase polarisation are a world-facing instance of the mirror risk, not a figure line.

- Mode: `specified`.
- Status: **triangulated** across Fig 7 and Fig 2 (selection versus anchor non-update), low overlap.
- Refuter: a correction channel that transmits only influence, with no ground-bearing content, yet reliably reduces judicial error.

### F6 - Rights and accountability are two currencies, not one balance

Fig 12 :125: "A composition costs in two currencies, and they do not add." Fig 12 :126: "Bits price the description. Actions price finding out whether it holds." Fig 1 :22: "Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average."

Rights protection prices the description of what the law requires. Accountability prices the actions that find out whether the ruling holds. They are in different units, so a system holding one accounting cannot compare them and will not notice the shortfall. Summing them is the named error, not the fix. This is why the question's "when does X, and when can Y" form must be kept as two verdicts.

- Mode: `general`.
- Status: **triangulated** across Fig 12 and Fig 1 (two-currency pricing versus per-slot residual).
- Refuter: a demonstrated commensurable measure of both effects that predicts outcomes without losing either live slot.

### F7 - Stakes are structural; life tenure is standing that survives the outcome

Fig 10 :58: "A seat with no stake at all does not exist." Fig 10 :59: "an interest in the outcome is unavoidable, an interest in the reading is disqualifying." Fig 10 :60: "Where a stake cannot be removed, declare it." Fig 10 :61: "And standing is a stake that survives the outcome it was earned on."

A judge's interest in the outcome is unavoidable and not disqualifying; an interest in how the reading is produced is disqualifying. Permanent tenure is precisely "standing that survives the outcome it was earned on", so it must be declared and checked, not denied. The seat's power is asymmetric in the same way Fig 11 :61 names for a habitat maintainer: introduction is available, exclusion is not. A designer can add a review mechanism and cannot subtract the fact of contact with political actors.

- Mode: `specified`.
- Status: **single-source** at figure level (Fig 10; the Seat Map :171 restates it and shares the same framework evidence pool, so it is not an independent witness).
- Refuter: a seat whose tenure creates no standing that survives a case and whose interest in the reading is demonstrably absent, with outcomes unchanged.

### F8 - Which specific rule is best is a declared gap, not a hidden answer

Fig 9 :32: "you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself". Fig 13 :134: "Unreachable is a verdict here, not a silence". Fig 8 :39: "So a search that finds nothing is never a proof of absence." Fig 9 :27: "describe it before you go looking, or any frame you pick will seem to fit".

After a genuine re-census no figure line gives a tenure length, a removal threshold, or an electoral design. The figures return the shape of the discriminating condition, not its value. Naming a specific rule first and mapping the figures onto it would be the apophenia Fig 9 :27 names. Searched first (is the value in the closure and unfound? No atom in the six-term set or the operator set carries a duration). Inward (extend the instrument): V-Dem's high court independence index and comparable measures are real instruments already returning something, so INWARD is available. Outward (IMPORT): a different frame with a provenance and a shadow test could supply a design, but no such import is owed by this question and importing against a budget limit adds an unneeded atom (THE_FORMULA step 7).

- Mode: `general` for the gap verdict, no `specified` value.
- Status: **gap** after census. Unreachable is a verdict, not a silence.

### F9 - The ground settles it; agreement and provenance do not

Fig 2 :35: "Alignment is triangulation, not negotiation. A mirror cannot correct you." Fig 2 :40: "agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool". Fig 10 :62: "A ruling carries provenance like an atom." Fig 10 :65: "Provenance shows a ruling was reached legitimately." Fig 10 :80: "Frames whose independence is renewed by separate experience can be maintained".

What settles E1 and E2 is a ground reading: does the ruling track law, facts and the affected person, and does the review channel actually correct error? Agreement between judges and public does not settle either, because it may be one evidence pool talking. A clean record proves legitimacy only, and a perfectly provenanced wrong ruling stays wrong. Independence between the checking frames must be renewed by separate experience, otherwise it can only be rotated, not refreshed.

- Mode: `general`.
- Status: **triangulated** across Fig 2 and Fig 10 (anchor independence versus provenance and channel maintenance).
- Refuter: a case where agreement alone, with no ground reading and no separate experience, reliably corrected a judicial error.

## 4. Pre-registered refuter (written before the conclusion)

Required before the run: Seat Map :287: "And a falsifier written before the run is a design instrument, not only an honesty device."

The shape claim in `07_the_tether_answer.md` is the discriminating condition `D`: insulation protects rights while it runs between the judge and political frames, and insulates from accountability once it moves onto the judge's channel to the ground. It is refused if any of the following holds:

1. A system shows rights improving while the adjudicative frame updated on majority preference and no separate correction against law and facts occurred (breaks Fig 2 :33-34, :45).
2. A system shows the channel to law, facts and persons fully intact while political correction cannot reach the seat, and no rights loss or error correction follows (breaks Fig 10 :24-28).
3. Rights protection and accountability are shown to be commensurable and additive on a single score that predicts both slots without residual (breaks Fig 12 :125-126 and Fig 1 :22).
4. The discriminating quantity is shown to be insulation duration alone, with no dependence on which frame the insulation is from (breaks the anchor and channel distinction; the figure lines govern relations, not times).
5. A frame selected for agreement, drawing on the same evidence pool, produces the same result as an independent frame, so agreement substitutes for the ground (breaks Fig 2 :40).

If any fires, the shape is withdrawn. Until one is run, no part of this may be labelled `grounded`.

## 5. Recommendation

1. Keep the two verdicts separate and never publish a combined score (F1, F6).
2. State the relation the insulation is between, not its magnitude (F2, F3). That is the whole discriminating condition.
3. Treat independence as a priced guard, refusable when it starts deciding outcomes (F4).
4. Treat elections and retention as selection between rooms, not as negotiation with a sitting judge (F5).
5. Declare the structural stake of tenure instead of pretending it is absent (F7).
6. Publish the specific-rule value as `gap`, with the instruments that could close it, rather than naming a favourite design (F8).
7. Send the shape and its refuter, not the framework, to public opt-in venues (F9, Fig 10 :52 "Bring a characterised residual, never the source.").
