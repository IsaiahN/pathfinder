**Subject:** One discriminating condition about judicial independence, checked before any theory

> Hi [name],
>
> I have one structural claim about judicial independence that you can check against what you already know. I am not asking you to read a framework first, and I am not asking you to agree. If the claim does not survive, there is nothing else to look at.
>
> **The claim (`general` in shape, `specified` in application, not `grounded`):** judicial independence does not protect rights by being large or small. It protects rights while the insulation runs *between the judge and political pressure*. The judge is then an anchor that ranges against law, facts and the person affected, and does not update on a majority's momentary will. The same insulation starts to insulate from democratic accountability when it moves onto the other relation, the judge's channel to that ground. Then the channel decays, the reasons stop tracking evidence, and no election or reason-giving correction reaches the seat. The discriminating quantity is **which relation the insulation is between**, not how much insulation there is.
>
> What follows from this: rights protection and accountability are two effects in one union, not two ends of one scale. They are priced in different currencies, a description of what the law requires and the actions that find out whether a ruling holds. You cannot average them into one score without losing the live signal in each slot. Elections are legitimate as selection between rooms, not as negotiation with a sitting judge. Permanent tenure is a structural stake, standing that survives the case it was earned on, so it should be declared and checked rather than denied.
>
> **What I am not claiming:** I am not naming a tenure length, a removal rule or an electoral design. The figures return a shape, not the value. I am not claiming that any country's courts are independent or not, or that a particular decision was correct. I am not claiming this is settled by measurement. It is a `specified` structural claim, and no ground reading was taken in producing it.
>
> **What would refute it, written before this run:**
> 1. Rights improved while the adjudicative frame demonstrably updated on majority preference and no separate correction against law and facts occurred.
> 2. The channel to law, facts and persons was fully intact while political correction could not reach the seat, and no rights loss or error correction followed.
> 3. Rights protection and accountability were shown to be commensurable and additive on one score that predicts both effects without residual.
> 4. The discriminating quantity turned out to be insulation duration alone, with no dependence on which relation the insulation is between.
> 5. A group selected for agreement, drawing on the same evidence pool, produced the same result as an independent group, so agreement substituted for the ground.
>
> I wrote these down before sending this. If any holds, I retract the claim. A one-line "this fails because..." is more useful to me than agreement. If you would rather not engage, no reply is needed.
>
> **If it survives, and only then:** the sources are below. The figures are the authority, not my summary of them.
> - Verbatim figure text with line numbers: `FRAMEWORK/reference/03-FIGURES_TEXT.md` in the public Pathfinder workspace (local path, see the ledger in the packet).
> - Canonical usage rule: https://github.com/IsaiahN/tether/blob/general-agent/figures/README.md
> - Figures and formula for direct download: https://github.com/IsaiahN/tether/tree/general-agent/figures
>
> **If you want to use the machinery:** the formula is in the figures link. Run it on a problem you know well and can check against a ground. Write down what would refute your result before you run it, and state the mode of what you get. The framework's own rule is that a loop cannot check the direction of its own question, and a reported failure is more useful to me than a reported success.
>
> Thanks for your time.
