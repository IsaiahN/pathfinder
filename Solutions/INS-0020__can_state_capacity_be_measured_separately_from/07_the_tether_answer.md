# The Tether's answer on state capacity and fair use

**Mode: `specified` on the application, `general` on the structural two-condition shape, not `grounded`.** This is a shape and a route, not a result. Simulated derivation checked against the figures (`05_what_shape_means.md` section 7), not a ground reading.

## 1. The residual it answers

The question asks whether a state's capacity can be measured apart from a government's willingness to use it fairly. In effect terms, R = |Γ(b,a) - o′| is measured per citizen and per case, not as a national average (Fig 1 :19, :22). The proposed split assumes one administrative reality with two independent dials. The figures refuse that assumption: the residual is a **union** of two overlapping conditions, and a case in two conditions is *"categorically different"* (Fig 9 :57). It has its own loop and its own ground.

## 2. The shape

1. **A national score is not the object.** Fig 1 :19: *"The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated."* A composite capacity index is a budget filter, not a witness (Fig 9 :60). The WGI programme's own usage advisory says scores *"should be treated as estimates rather than definitive ratings."*
2. **Capacity is state-and-habitat, not an agency stock.** Fig 11 :39: *"capability is a property of agent-and-habitat, never of the agent alone."* A list of registries, staff numbers and data links is Γ, a candidate library whose reach must be tested against the ground.
3. **The same tools change sign with the residual.** Fig 11 :49: *"An actor’s valence is relative to the residual, not a property it carries."* A data link that closes "the benefit did not arrive" can open "a person was flagged or a favour was dispensed." The fair and unfair arrangements are **isomers** over the same atom set (Fig 12 :117), or at the safest reading members of one functional group (Fig 12 :124). Instrument inventories cannot separate them.
4. **Fairness becomes measurable only as a refusal.** Fig 10 :42: *"A verifier that can reconstruct the claim cannot verify it."* The checking seat must not author what it checks (Fig 10 :43), and a vocabulary without negation *"can only accumulate"* (Fig 12 :140). Willingness is not a reading; it is a record of decisions questioned, errors corrected and uses disallowed.
5. **Two conditions, kept separate.** C1: a maintained channel to a ground that settles each case from a source the agency cannot move (Fig 2 :29, Fig 10 :24-29). C2: a checking seat that cannot author what it checks and can refuse a use (Fig 10 :42-43, Fig 5 :53). They have different instruments and different failure signatures. They are never averaged.
6. **The break is usually upstream of planning.** The chain stops at vocabulary or objective: having terms for what a citizen's situation affords, and saying what would count as a correct decision (Fig 3 :24, :27). Capacity investment at perception does not rescue it (Fig 3 :19).
7. **The institution that fills the seat is not named.** Fig 9 :32: *"you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself."* The specific oversight body, statute or audit design is a `gap`, reported as unreached rather than silence (Fig 13 :134, Fig 8 :39).

## 3. What it explicitly does not say

- Not that capacity and fairness are the same thing. They are two conditions with different instruments; the claim is against one instrument and one averaged score, not against the distinction.
- Not that existing governance indices are worthless. They are budget filters (Fig 9 :60), and their own documentation warns against verdict use.
- Not that intentions never matter. "Willingness" has no anchor until bound to a decision and correction record (Fig 2 :29-30).
- Not that any named checker is unbiased. Every checker has a stake, and *"an interest in the outcome is unavoidable, an interest in the reading is disqualifying"* (Fig 10 :59). The stake is declared, not denied.
- Not a `grounded` answer. No per-case measurement pays for the shape here.

## 4. What would settle it - the ground

A source-discriminating instrument, not a further argument. From *The Hard Problem is About Source*: a reading that *"cannot comment on the source"* is resolved by an instrument, in that case the spectrometer. Here the corresponding questions are:

- **Per-slot check:** does the instrument that reports "the administrative act happened" also change when the person was unfairly treated, holding the act constant? If it does not, the two readings are not independent and the question's premise fails.
- **Valence test:** does the same data link change sign across a documented change of residual, with the same atoms and arrangement? ICTD's Robodebt case is the world's instance.
- **Checker test:** can a seat that did not author a decision refuse it and have the correction reach every institution that relied on the record? If not, C2 has not been built.
- **Threshold test:** does a ⋛ comparison hold between the capacity reading and the fairness reading per case, or are they in different units and held apart (Fig 12 :125)?
- **Standing falsifiers from `01` section 4:** a national index that prospectively predicts unfair cases per slot; an invariant atom valence; an agency's own metric that predicts its own failures; one lever restoring both conditions with no residue.

## 5. The honest stop

**Unreached, not unreachable.** The six-term physical substrate, where invoked, is *"a human's careful guess at reality's compression axes"* (carried essay reading); declaring it makes a choice visible, not neutral. The institutional atom set used here is likewise a visible guess. The answer stops at the limit of the available instruments and records the lineage so the stop is checkable. *"Indiscernibility is not identity."* The missing thing is characterized in effect terms: a source-discriminating instrument that ranges capacity and fair use per case against a non-updating anchor. It is not named, and inventing a name without provenance would be an import with no source gate (Fig 6 :23, Fig 8 :50).

## 6. One-paragraph version for outreach

> The Tether does not answer "yes, put capacity and fairness in two columns." It says the two are not two readings of one instrument: capacity is a relation between a state and its citizens, and the same administrative tools change sign with the problem being solved, so the fair and unfair uses of one registry are isomers, same tools arranged differently. What can be measured separately are two conditions: a working channel to a ground that settles each case from a source the agency cannot move, and a checking seat that cannot author what it checks and can refuse a use. Readings must be per citizen and per case, because a national average hides the signal that says where fairness failed. The framework does not name the oversight body that fills the seat; it returns a shape, not a name. What it offers is that shape, four refuters written in advance, and the kind of instrument that would close the question. The ground has not settled it here.
