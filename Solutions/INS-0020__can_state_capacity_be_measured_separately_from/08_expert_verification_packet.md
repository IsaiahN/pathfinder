# Expert verification packet - the Tether on state capacity and fair use

**What this is:** a claim in a form a researcher on state capacity or public administration can check. Mode, derivation and refuters are stated before the conclusion, so a reader knows what to look for and what would settle it.

**Mode: `specified` on the application, `general` on the structural shape, not `grounded`.** No per-case measurement is claimed. This is a shape and a route, not a result.

## 1. The claim, in one paragraph

State capacity cannot be measured separately from fair use as two independent readings of one instrument. Capacity is a property of state-and-habitat, not an agency stock, and the administrative atoms it uses have no fixed sign: a data link that closes "the benefit did not arrive" can open "a person was flagged or a favour was dispensed." The fair and unfair arrangements are isomers over a shared atom set, at the strongest reading, and members of one functional group at the safest. What *can* be delivered separately are two conditions with different instruments, reported per citizen-slot and never averaged: a maintained channel to a ground that settles each case from a source the measuring frame cannot move, and a checking seat that cannot author what it checks and can refuse a use. The framework does not name the institution that fills the seat. It returns a shape, four refuters written in advance, and the kind of instrument that would close the question.

## 2. Three checkable sub-claims

### C1 - framework-internal; checkable by reading Figures 1, 11 and 12

- **Statement:** the residual is per citizen-slot, never a national average; the same administrative atom changes valence with the residual; fair and unfair uses are isomers over a shared atom set.
- **Sources, verbatim:** Fig 1 :19 - *"The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated."* Fig 1 :22 - *"Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average."* Fig 11 :49 - *"An actor’s valence is relative to the residual, not a property it carries."* Fig 12 :117 - *"isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."* Fig 12 :124 - *"functional group. A substructure whose presence fixes the outcome. The group says what kind; the qualifier says which member."*
- **Refuter:** an administrative atom whose measured valence is invariant across a documented change of residual with the same atoms and arrangement; or either use can be written without an atom the other requires, which kills the isomer claim and leaves the functional-group claim standing; or a national composite score that prospectively predicts a named citizen-slot's unfair case with no per-slot input.

### C2 - institutional; checkable against the literature on oversight and administrative justice

- **Statement:** fairness becomes a measurable term only as a refusal by a seat that cannot author what it checks. A capacity metric produced by the agency scores itself with a quantity the agency also produces.
- **Sources, verbatim:** Fig 10 :42 - *"A verifier that can reconstruct the claim cannot verify it."* Fig 10 :43 - *"Soundness requires the checker to be unable to produce what it is checking."* Fig 1 :27 - *"They are frame-internal. A frame cannot score itself with a quantity it also produces."* Fig 12 :140 - *"A vocabulary that cannot refuse can only accumulate."* Fig 5 :53 - *"refuted: a term we hold is wrong; a term is owed that says so"*
- **What to check:** is this a fair reading of Robodebt and of the safeguards ICTD recommends? ICTD writes that *"Independent oversight bodies need the powers, expertise, and resources to examine whether those limits are respected"* and that human review requires *"staff who can question the output and reconsider the case."* The Royal Commission found the scheme *"neither fair nor legal."* Does that support the refusal reading, or is fair use better measured as an attitude or a distribution of outcomes?
- **Refuter:** an agency's own capacity metric that prospectively predicts its own fairness failures at the per-case ground with no external checker; or a demonstration that a self-reported "willingness to act fairly" measure predicts per-case decisions as well as a refusal record. Note that Fig 2 :29-30 says a construct that moves when pushed is not an anchor, so this would need a non-updating reference.

### C3 - framework-internal derivation about union and instrument choice

- **Statement:** the question is a union of two conditions with different instruments, not a two-column partition. Fig 9 :54 - *"And when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again."* Fig 9 :57 - *"What remains is smaller and it is also categorically different: a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either."* Fig 3 :27 - *"A link with no instrument is not a link that works. It is a link nobody has looked at."*
- **What to check:** do the two failure signatures actually differ in practice? C1 failure shows up as pooled readings and a bracket that no longer matches the case (Fig 10 :26, Fig 7 :38). C2 failure shows up as a checker that authors what it checks and a correction that does not reach every institution that relied on the record (Fig 10 :42-43).
- **Refuter:** one intervention that restores both conditions in both failure types and leaves no categorically different residue, which would collapse the union into a partition (Fig 9 :57); or a documented case where perception, vocabulary and objective all measure as working and the failure is at planning, which would break the middle-link claim (Fig 3 :19, :24).

## 3. What is explicitly not claimed

- Not that capacity and fairness are the same thing. They are two conditions with different instruments. The claim is against one instrument and one averaged score.
- Not that governance indices are worthless. They are budget filters, and the WGI usage advisory says treat scores *"as estimates rather than definitive ratings."*
- Not that intentions never matter. "Willingness" has no anchor until bound to a decision and correction record (Fig 2 :29-30).
- Not that any named checker is unbiased. *"an interest in the outcome is unavoidable, an interest in the reading is disqualifying"* (Fig 10 :59). The stake is declared, not denied.
- Not a `grounded` answer. No per-case measurement settles the shape here.
- Not a name for the oversight body. That is a binding of a held atom, recorded as provenance, and the source-discriminating instrument is a declared `gap` (Fig 9 :32, Fig 13 :134, Fig 8 :39).

## 5. What would settle it

A source-discriminating instrument, not a further argument. From *The Hard Problem is About Source*: an instrument that reads green *"cannot comment on the source"* - the spectrometer resolved it. Here:

1. Does an instrument that reports "the administrative act happened" also change when the person was unfairly treated, holding the act constant? If not, the two readings are not independent.
2. Does the same atom change sign across a documented change of residual (ICTD's Robodebt case is the world instance)?
3. Can a checker that did not author a decision refuse it, and does the correction reach every institution that relied on the record?
4. Are capacity description cost and per-case checking cost in different units and held apart (Fig 12 :125)? A single measure predicting both from one number would dissolve that claim.

## 6. What an expert can check in twenty minutes

1. Read Fig 1 :19, :22; Fig 10 :42-43; Fig 11 :49; Fig 12 :117, :125, :140; Fig 9 :54, :57 - eight short passages.
2. Check C2 against what you know of Robodebt or a comparable case: is the failure better described as a refusal failure than as a capacity shortfall?
3. Name one instrument that would distinguish a lawful from an unlawful use of the same data link per case, or say why none is possible.
4. Send the refutation. A failure is more useful than an endorsement.
