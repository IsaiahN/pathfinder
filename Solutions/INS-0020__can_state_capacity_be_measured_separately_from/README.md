## The question restated as an effect

What is being decided is not the *level* of a state's capacity, but whether one instrument can return two independent readings: one for the state's ability to bring about an outcome, and one for the fairness of the use of that ability. In effect terms, R = |Γ(b,a) - o′| measured per citizen-slot s, never as a national average (Fig 1 :19, Fig 1 :22).

- **b** is the government's belief about the citizen and the situation, formed from its own records and inherited categories.
- **a** is the administrative act: register, match, assess, levy, deliver, flag, deny, correct.
- **Γ(b,a)** is what the administrative library predicts: the benefit will arrive, the tax will be due, the record is accurate, the rule will be complied with.
- **o′** is what actually happens to the person: the payment arrived or not, the debt was owed or not, the record was corrected or not, a favour was dispensed or not.
- **s** is the citizen or case. R is indexed per citizen and per case.

- Option A, "capacity and fair use are two independent dials": leads to the prediction that a capacity instrument can be built that is silent on fairness, and that a fairness instrument can be added later without changing the first.
- Option B, "they are two conditions of one state-and-habitat relation": leads to the prediction that the same administrative atom changes sign with the residual, so the fairness reading is a property of the arrangement and of the checking seat, not a second variable measured by the same instrument (Fig 11 :49, Fig 12 :117).
- What an answer would look like, written down and bound as an ordinary term before the run (Formula, "Direction"): a *shape* naming two separately instrumented conditions, a channel to a ground that settles each case from a source the measuring frame cannot move, and a checking seat that cannot author what it checks and can refuse a use. Until bound, that shape predicts nothing and no loop check can see it.

The verdict recorded in `09_watson_answer.md` is Option B in `specified` mode, kept per domain and never averaged. The literal "measure them separately" framing is refused as a partition; the two readings are deliverable only as two conditions with distinct instruments.

## What is deliberately not here

- No personal emails of named individuals.
- No ranking of people by how likely they are to agree.
- No bulk-send plan.
- No claim that this derivation is `grounded`, no claim that it settles the institutional question, and no claim that the framework is the only true account of state capacity.

Three figure-grounded reasons, plus one practical one:

1. A room is not an anchor. *"A room’s agreement is variation, not a verdict. Selection between rooms is where the ground votes."* (Fig 7 :35) and *"Alignment is triangulation, not negotiation. A mirror cannot correct you."* (Fig 2 :35). Gathering agreeable readers manufactures the correlation that disqualifies a verdict.
2. Correlation has valence. *"disqualifying for a verdict: agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool"* (Fig 2 :40). A list selected for receptiveness is one witness wearing many names.
3. A verifier must be outside the interior. *"A verifier that can reconstruct the claim cannot verify it."* (Fig 10 :42). The first contact therefore carries the residual and its refuter, not the framework.
4. Legally, unsolicited bulk email to scraped addresses breaches GDPR and CAN-SPAM style rules in many jurisdictions and gets the sending domain blacklisted. Public opt-in routes avoid that failure as well as the structural one.
