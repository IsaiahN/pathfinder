## DERIVATION

### Q1 - Are the two uses isomers?

- Fig 12 :117: **"isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."**
- Fig 12 :124: **"functional group. A substructure whose presence fixes the outcome. The group says what kind; the qualifier says which member."**
- Fair-delivery arrangement F: identity to registry to data link, assessment rule bounded by a reviewer, correction channel back to the citizen.
- Targeted-extraction arrangement U: identity to registry to data link, assessment rule with no reviewer bound, correction channel unavailable to the flagged person.
- Both use the same six atoms. They differ in which bond attaches the reviewer and in whether the correction channel re-enters. By Fig 12 :117 they are **isomers**: same atoms, different bond, different substance. The inventory of instruments cannot tell them apart.
- **Falsifier 1, written before the run:** if either arrangement can be written without an atom the other requires, for example fair delivery with no identity system, or targeted extraction with no data link, the isomer claim fails and the weaker **functional-group** claim stands instead (Fig 12 :124). Both arrangements would then share the kind but differ in the member.

### Q2 - Is the difference a catalyst or a cycle?

- Fig 12 :119: **"catalyst. A part that survives its own use. Spent and regenerating parts behave differently across uses, and nothing records which a term is."**
- Fig 12 :121: **"cycle. The output re-enters as input. The sign of the loop decides whether it closes, and a chain cannot close by construction."**
- The reviewer is a catalyst if it survives its own use and is regenerating. Where review is perfunctory, the part is spent and nothing in the organigram records which.
- The correction channel is the cycle. Its sign decides whether the loop closes: a correction that reaches every institution that relied on the faulty record is a closing loop; a correction that stops at the issuing agency is a chain that cannot close. ICTD reports the failure exactly: *"Resolving that error can require discovering which organisation supplied the information, obtaining the relevant records, and persuading several bodies to correct them, a major undertaking."*
- **Falsifier 2:** if a reviewer's spent or regenerating state is recorded in the instrument itself, or if a correction that stops at one institution is observed to correct all downstream records, the catalyst and cycle readings are false.

### Q3 - Two currencies, not one score

- Fig 12 :125: **"A composition costs in two currencies, and they do not add."**
- Fig 12 :126: *"Bits price the description. Actions price finding out whether it holds. They are in different units,"* with the two readings *"held apart."*
- At the institutional stratum this becomes: the cost of describing a capacity arrangement (how many rules, how many data links) and the cost of finding out whether its use is fair (how many cases must be examined by a checker who cannot author the decision). They do not add. A composite index that sums them is the flat-formula collapse Fig 12 names.
- **Falsifier 3:** a single measure that predicts both the description cost and the per-case checking cost from the same number, validated at the ground, would dissolve the two-currency claim.

### Q4 - What is the dependency order of the measurement chain?

- Fig 3 :20: **"The chain is ordered by dependency, not by importance."**
- Fig 3 :19: **"Optimising a link that already works cannot rescue one that does not."**
- Fig 3 :27: **"A link with no instrument is not a link that works. It is a link nobody has looked at."**

Order for a fairness measurement:

1. **Perception.** Can the agency read the citizen's actual situation at all? Registries and data links usually work here. This is where capacity investment lands.
2. **Vocabulary.** Does Γ have terms for what the citizen affords? Does it have "fluctuating earnings," "household transfer," "business revenue," or only "income as reported"? This link has no instrument and is where Robodebt broke.
3. **Objective.** Can the agency say what would count as progress here? "Debts raised" is not the same objective as "correct assessments and promptly corrected errors." A vocabulary without ⋛ cannot state a threshold.
4. **Planning.** Given 2 and 3, is there a route to the objective under incomplete information?
5. **Learn and carry.** Does the correction reach every institution that relied on the record?

The diagnostic consequence: a system with high capacity and unfair use is not one problem with a fairness add-on. It is typically a chain that stops at link 2 or 3 while investment continues at link 1. Fig 3 :29: *"Before asking whether a system is good, ask which link it currently stops at, and whether the answer was measured or assumed."*

**Falsifier 4:** a documented case where links 1, 2 and 3 all measure as working and the failure is located at planning. That falsifies the "usually the middle link" claim, though not the dependency ordering itself.

### Q5 - Does "shape, not a name" forbid naming an oversight body?

- The README's phrase is *"what a **missing** thing would have to look like (a shape, not a name)."* The subject is a missing primitive.
- Oversight bodies, courts, ombudspersons and correction procedures are already in the institutional vocabulary at this stratum. They are held atoms, not missing ones.
- ⇒ Naming one is a **binding** from the institutional frame onto a held kind, not the invention of a seventh primitive. What is forbidden is claiming that the binding moves the wall: Fig 6 says *"IMPORT adds an atom,"* and this binding adds none. Fig 8 :50: *"Convergent derivation and adopted import look identical in the contents."* Only the record separates them.
- **I narrowed my own earlier reading.** See `05_what_shape_means.md` section 6. The `gap` on the specific institution survives for a different reason: no source-discriminating instrument has been supplied, so the name has no ground payment and no provenance.

## CONCLUSION

The fair and unfair uses of one administrative capacity are **isomers over a shared atom set** at the strongest reading, and members of one **functional group** at the safest. The reviewer is a catalyst whose spent or regenerating state is unrecorded; the correction channel is a cycle whose sign decides whether the loop closes. Capacity and fairness are priced in two currencies, not one score. The measurement chain is ordered by dependency and usually stops at the vocabulary or objective link, not at planning, which is why capacity investment alone does not close a fairness residual. Four falsifiers are written down: Q1, Q2, Q3 and Q4. Naming a specific oversight body is a binding of a held atom, recorded as provenance, not a wall-moving import, and the `gap` on the discriminator remains.
