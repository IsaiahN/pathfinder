**Subject:** One checkable claim about separating state capacity from fair use

> Hello,
>
> I have one specific output I would like you to check against what you already know about measuring the state. I am not asking you to read a theory first, and I am not asking you to agree with anything. If the output does not survive, there is nothing else to look at.
>
> **The output (`specified`, not `grounded`):** the question asks whether state capacity can be measured separately from a government's willingness to use that capacity fairly. The framework's answer is no, not as two readings of one instrument. It reaches that answer before naming any institution, from three rules:
>
> 1. The quantity being explained is indexed per citizen and per case, never averaged into a national score. The source figure says *"The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated"* (Fig 1 :19) and that aggregating across slots is *"how a live signal disappears into an average"* (Fig 1 :22).
> 2. The tools of administration have no fixed sign. *"An actor’s valence is relative to the residual, not a property it carries"* (Fig 11 :49). A data link that closes "the benefit did not arrive" can open "a person was flagged or a favour was dispensed." The two uses are same atoms arranged differently, an isomer in the framework's vocabulary (Fig 12 :117).
> 3. Fairness becomes measurable only as a refusal by someone who cannot author what they check. *"A verifier that can reconstruct the claim cannot verify it"* (Fig 10 :42), and a vocabulary without negation *"can only accumulate"* (Fig 12 :140).
>
> What is therefore offered is a shape: two conditions with different instruments, kept separate and never averaged. One is a channel to a ground that settles each case from a source the agency cannot move. The other is a checking seat that cannot author what it checks and can refuse a use. The framework does not name which oversight body or audit design fills that seat. It returns a shape, not a name.
>
> **What I am not claiming:** that any existing governance index is useless, that intentions never matter, that an ombudsperson or a court is unbiased, or that the framework settles which institution a country should build. Composite indices are useful budget filters. Willingness is not a measurable thing until it is bound to a record of decisions and corrections. Any checker has a stake, and the framework requires that stake to be declared rather than removed.
>
> **What would refute the shape claim:**
> 1. A national composite score that prospectively predicts, for a named citizen-slot and case type, which case will be unfairly decided next, with no per-slot information fed in.
> 2. An administrative tool whose measured valence stays the same across a documented change of the residual it is ranged against, with the same atoms and the same arrangement.
> 3. An agency's own capacity metric that prospectively predicts its own fairness failures at the per-case ground, with no external checker.
> 4. One intervention that restores both conditions in both failure types and leaves no categorically different residue.
>
> I wrote these down before sending this. If any one holds, the shape is wrong and I will retract it. A one-line "this fails because..." is more useful to me than agreement. If you would rather not engage, no reply is needed.
>
> **If it survives, and only then:** the sources are below. The figures are the authority, not my summary of them.
> - Figures and formula for direct download (SVG, branch `general-agent`): https://github.com/IsaiahN/tether/tree/general-agent/figures
> - Worked statement of the figures as metalaws: https://adventuresinml.substack.com/p/arrival-of-the-fittest-the-tether
> - The measurement argument that named the source problem: https://adventuresinml.substack.com/p/the-hard-problem-is-about-source
>
> **If you want to use the machinery:** the formula is in the figures link. Run it on a measurement problem you know well and can check against a ground. Write down what would refute your result *before* you run it, and state the mode of what you get. A reported failure is more useful to me than a reported success.
>
