# The Tether's answer: what kinds of transparency help, and when does disclosure overwhelm or mislead?

**Mode: `specified` on the substantive claims, `general` on the shape limit, not `grounded`.** Simulated derivation checked against the figures (`05_what_shape_means.md` section 6), not a ground reading. No controlled comparison of disclosure formats was run.

## 1. The residual it answers

A discloser must decide what to reveal so that another person can judge reliability. The gap is between what a disclosure lets the evaluator range against the ground and what they need to judge reliably. It is a **union compound** of overlapping conditions, not one hard question (Fig 9 :54, *"unbundle before you search again"*). Its two harms are separate domains: overwhelm is a channel-and-budget failure; misleading is a declared-meaning failure. They are never averaged into "transparency was net positive."

## 2. The shape

1. **Ground-contacting, not self-scoring.** Fig 1 :23: *"The ground enters as the only metric."* Fig 1 :27: *"They are frame-internal. A frame cannot score itself with a quantity it also produces."* Coverage counts, flag counts, and credibility claims are quantities the discloser produces. A helpful disclosure exposes something that does not move when the discloser changes its mind.
2. **Per-slot, not one aggregate.** Fig 1 :19: *"The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated."* Fig 1 :22: *"Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average."* A single trust score can be near zero while one slot is failing. The useful form is per-claim, per-decision, per-slot readings.
3. **Structured, not an ingredient list.** Fig 12 :5: *"A set of ingredients is not one. The bond is the rest of the information, and it is the half nothing was recording."* Fig 12 :111: *"A vocabulary without bonds can name a state and cannot name a route to one."* Provenance and relation are the atom; a flat list records half of itself.
4. **Two budgets, held apart.** Fig 12 :125: *"A composition costs in two currencies, and they do not add."* Fig 12 :126: *"Bits price the description. Actions price finding out whether it holds."* More bits can raise the cost of checking. Disclosure overwhelms when bits rise while ground-contacting actions do not; the predicted signature is an inverted U, not a monotonic curve.
5. **Violable, not declared.** Fig 10 :18: *"a declared meaning is not a grounded one"* and Fig 10 :19: *"→ install what can be violated."* A label such as "explainable" or "audited" declares a meaning until something about it can fail. An explanation one can access but does not read can produce a feeling of understanding without a discrimination.
6. **Independently checkable.** Fig 2 :40: *"disqualifying for a verdict: agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool."* More readers of the same disclosure are one pool. Checkers must be able to range the claim against a different ground.

The shape is a constraint on any helping kind, not a catalogue. Fig 9 :32: *"you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself."* The set of disclosure kinds is closed while arrangements over it are not (Fig 13), so an exhaustive, settled list is not obtainable from the figures.

## 3. What it explicitly does not say

- Not "more transparency is better." Volume is one currency, not the verdict.
- Not "less disclosure is safer." Hiding a live slot residual is the other failure.
- Not a ranking of disclosure techniques. Naming a best format is outside what the framework returns.
- Not that perceived trust is ground contact. A disclosure can raise trustworthiness ratings while leaving reliability untouched.
- Not that the world sources prove the framework. They are secondary, independent in most cases, and corroborate rather than decide.
- Not `grounded`. Nothing here was settled by an instrument in this run.

## 4. What would settle it, the ground

A source-discriminating instrument, not a further argument. From *The Hard Problem is About Source*: a color instrument reads green *"cannot comment on the source"*; the spectrometer resolved it. Here the corresponding tests are:

- **Discrimination test:** do two disclosures with identical ingredients but different arrangements produce distinguishable ground-contacted judgments? If not, the bond claim (2 and 3 above) fails.
- **Aggregation test:** can every failing slot be recovered from the single aggregate score alone? If yes, the per-slot requirement fails.
- **Inverted-U test:** as bits rise and checking cost stays flat or falls, does ground-contacted decision quality peak and then decline? If it keeps rising without bound, the two-currency and overload claims fail.
- **Availability test:** does mere access to an unread explanation improve ground-contacted evaluation? If yes, the declared-meaning claim fails for that case.
- **Standing falsifiers from `01`:** a self-scoring disclosure that improves ground contact; a closed, ground-settled enumeration of helpful kinds with no remainder.

## 5. The honest stop

**Unreached, not unreachable.** The figures return a shape and stop where instruments stop. The world literature already supplies part of the instrument (inverted-U curves, illusion-of-understanding experiments), but it measures human judgment, not the framework's ground-contact quantity. Mapping one onto the other is an import that needs its own source gate and shadow test (Fig 6 :23, Fig 8 :50-52). The lineage is recorded in `01`, `05`, `06`, and `10_source_ledger.csv` so the stop is checkable. *"Indiscernibility is not identity"* (canonical README): a disclosure that *looks* helpful is not thereby helpful.

## 6. One-paragraph version for outreach

> The Tether does not rank transparency techniques and does not say "more disclosure is better." It says that transparency helps evaluation only when it changes a person's contact with the ground: the error signal is per slot, so one aggregate trust score hides the failing case; the relation between disclosed facts carries the information a bare ingredient list loses; and bits and actions are two currencies that do not add, so adding pages can raise the cost of checking and produce an inverted U rather than a better judgment. It misleads when a declared label such as "explainable" substitutes for something that can be violated, and when an explanation one can access but does not read creates a feeling of understanding. More readers of the same disclosure are one evidence pool, not a second witness. What the framework offers is the shape any helpful disclosure must have, four refuters written in advance, and the tests that would close it. The concrete formats stay open until the ground settles them.
