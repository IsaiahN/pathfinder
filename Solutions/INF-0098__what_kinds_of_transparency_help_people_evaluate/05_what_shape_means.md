## 1. A shape is a constraint, not a filler

The figures README states the limit: the framework returns *"what a missing thing would have to look like (a shape, not a name)."* Fig 9 :32: *"you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself."* Fig 8 :39: *"So a search that finds nothing is never a proof of absence."*

From *The Library of Babel*: *"you can cut your search short if the set of books returned is small and descriptive enough that it provides a general shape of the answer that satisfies your question."*

So a **shape** is the narrowed form of an answer you can state before you can name it. It constrains; it does not fill. For this question the shape is:

> A disclosure helps evaluation when it changes the evaluator's contact with the ground, is structured rather than a mere ingredient list, stays per-slot rather than aggregating into one score, fits within the verifier's channel capacity, and offers something violable. It overwhelms when bits rise while ground-contacting actions do not, and it misleads when a declared meaning substitutes for a checkable test.

That is a set of constraints on any helping kind. It is not a list of named techniques and not a ranking. "Publish a model card" or "show a confidence score" are names, and the framework does not license naming (Fig 9 :32).

## 2. How the shape is obtained

- *The Library of Babel* on proximity: use the vocabulary already held that is closest to the thing, look for recurring new terms that overlap or correlate with it, and recurse. *"With each new term gained, you narrow the space by definition and deepen your vocabulary."*
- Fig 9 :30-31: *"your words are borrowed too, so describe the effect rather than the cause."* So the residual was stated in effect terms in `01` section 0, not as "people dislike long documents."
- *The Library of Babel* on steering: returned candidates must be *"tested or ranged against the problem you want answered in order to steer the direction of the next search."* The criterion here is whether a candidate disclosure changes ground contact, not whether it feels open.
- *The Library of Babel* on stopping: the answer is reached when *"the finish line will also be the starting place"* and the paradox is that by then you have *"composed the answer through the process of locating it."*

Applied here: the four conditions in the shape were not chosen first and then matched to literature. The residual was described in effect terms, the figure lines were quoted (Fig 1, Fig 10, Fig 12, Fig 9), and the world sources were then read as corroboration. The note in `01` F6 that a complete catalogue is unavailable is the honest stop.

## 3. What licenses a name

Only two things, both outside the shape itself:

1. **The ground settles it.** `THE_FORMULA.md` step 5: *"the ground settles it. Verified only by what pays. A gate is not the ground."* For this question that means a controlled comparison in which a specific disclosure format measurably improves ground-contacted evaluation, with the mode moved from `specified` to `grounded`.
2. **A provenanced import from a frame whose closure differs** (Fig 6 :23, Fig 8 :50-52), with a source gate and shadow test. An empirical finding from cognitive psychology can be imported as an instrument for measuring overload, provided its provenance is recorded and it explains a gap the figures leave open.

The world sources in this folder are genuine imports of that kind, but they corroborate the shape rather than name a best disclosure format. None of them settles the framework's own claim.

## 4. The ground and the source problem

*The Hard Problem is About Source* gives the mechanism that matters most for "misleading transparency":

> *"If several paths create the same behavior, then behavior is a projection with lossy information"*

An instrument reads green and *"cannot comment on the source"*: one green light, or yellow plus blue, or RGB, or CMYK. The reading does not determine the source. *"An instrument that can't distinguish these cases demonstrates its own inadequacy."* It was the **spectrometer**, not an argument, that resolved it.

Applied here: a "trust score," a high explainability rating, and a long disclosure document can all produce the same reading of *"this looks transparent"* while their sources (ground contact, or merely more bits, or a declared label) differ. Naming the source from the reading is the metamerism error. What is owed is a source-discriminating instrument, that is, a test that separates changed contact from changed confidence. This is why the outreach claim asks what would count as a failure before offering the framework: it is an attempt to build that discriminator.

## 5. Answer to the shape question

**What is the shape of the answer to "what kinds of transparency help, and when does disclosure overwhelm or mislead"?**

It is a six-part constraint, with the two harms held as separate domains rather than averaged:

1. **Ground-contacting, not self-scoring** (Fig 1 :23, :27). Helpful disclosure exposes something that does not move when the discloser changes its mind.
2. **Per-slot, not aggregated** (Fig 1 :19, :22). The live signal must survive.
3. **Structured, not an ingredient list** (Fig 12 :5, :111). Bonds and lineage carry the information the operands do not.
4. **Within channel capacity, with two budgets held apart** (Fig 10 :24-28; Fig 12 :125-127). Bits and actions are different currencies; overload is the confusion of the two.
5. **Violable, not declared** (Fig 10 :18-19). A convention that cannot be failed is a constant carrying authority, not a check.
6. **Independently checkable** (Fig 2 :40; Fig 13 :38). Agreement from one evidence pool is not a second witness.

The harms are the negation of these, not a seventh kind: a disclosure overwhelms when condition 4 fails while bits rise; it misleads when condition 5 fails while confidence rises. Fig 9 :32 governs the form: *"you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself."* So the answer is the shape any helping kind must have, and the concrete kinds are left to the ground.

## 6. Consequences for contact

**These essays are the author's, so their agreement with the figures is high-overlap and weak corroboration.** They are used here to explain what "shape" means, not to independently verify the shape claim.

**Prestige is not action.** From *The Recursive Self-Improvement Trap*: *"Prestige must never be allowed to validate an action directly."* And: *"Prestige can fund an action. It can give an action a budget to be tried. But it cannot settle an action. Only the ground can settle it. The Ground Maintainer must be blind to prestige."* A transparency program that gathers prestigious endorsers is the named collapse, not corroboration. The legitimate ask gives the claim a budget to be tried.

**My own audit is a simulation, not a ground reading.** Same essay: *"LLMs can quickly simulate an approximate answer/output with the formula"* but *"the truly testable output would need to be derived from the real architecture or working the formula yourself."* So `01` is a simulated derivation checked for internal consistency against the figures. It cannot settle anything it reports on, and no part of this solution is `grounded`.
