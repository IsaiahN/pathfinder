## 0. The residual, stated in effect terms

A discloser (a system, an institution, a platform) must decide what and how much to reveal about a claim or a process so that another person can judge whether it is reliable. The options and what each leads to:

- **More and more detailed disclosure.** Leads to more cues, more apparent openness, and a larger sense of understanding. Also leads to saturated attention, pooled readings, and a channel that stops carrying a verdict. Can mislead when the *availability* of an explanation substitutes for reading one.
- **Less disclosure.** Leads to a quieter channel and less risk of overload. Also leads to no way to detect a failure, so unreliability is hidden rather than corrected.

The residual `R` is the gap between what a disclosure lets the evaluator range against the ground (the actual reliability of the thing) and what the evaluator needs in order to judge reliably. The aim is not "more transparency" and not "less transparency." The aim is disclosure that **changes contact** with the ground rather than only changing the evaluator's vocabulary, confidence, or agreement with other people.

Fig 11 :40: *"An improvement that does not change contact changes nothing, however much it improves."*

This residual is a **union compound**, not one hard question. Helpful evaluation requires ground contact, structure, sufficient but not saturating volume, and an independent verifier. These conditions overlap. A single lever ("add more detail") clears the region where only quantity is binding and leaves the intersections standing (Fig 9 :54, *"unbundle before you search again"*). So the figure-derived answer is a per-condition shape, not an average and not a single ranked list.

## 1. Domain census (before choosing any figure line)

**R by arity, symmetry, scale.** Arity: at least four conditions (ground contact, bonds/structure, channel volume, verifier independence), so a union, not a scalar. Symmetry: not symmetric. Direction and timing matter, like `→` sequence and `⇒` production in Fig 12. A disclosure can produce a new discrimination in the evaluator (`⇒`), or merely precede a judgment without changing it (`→`). The same facts in a different order are a different disclosure. Scale: the same loop runs at the individual evaluator scale (per object slot) and at the population scale (rooms, institutions, markets), with a lossy transform between (Fig 4).

**One line per domain: actors / currency / anchor.** No nameable anchor means it is not a domain yet.

| domain | actors | currency | anchor | nameable? |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| D1 Verification frame (the evaluator) | evaluator, claim/system, disclosure artifact | residual `R` per slot; actions spent to check | the ground: an instrument, record, outcome, or board that does not move when a frame changes its mind (Fig 2 :29) | yes |
| D2 Disclosure-making frame (the discloser) | discloser, metrics, explanations, labels, lineage | bits (cost of stating) and actions (cost of checking), two currencies that do not add (Fig 12 :125) | the same external ground for the claim; the disclosure itself is a composition inside the discloser's closure | yes, for the claim; the disclosure alone has no anchor |
| D3 Institutional / population frame | multiple evaluators, rooms, rankings, reputation systems | agreement, votes, credibility | the ground, never agreement (Fig 2 :35, Fig 7) | yes |
| D4 Verifier capacity (channel) | attention, working memory, the disclosure stream | cognitive load; bits arriving per unit time | measured decision quality against the ground (a controlled comparison) | yes, but only through an instrument; figures speak of it via channel imagery, so its figure verdict is single-source |

**Partition or union.** D1 to D4 are a union with overlap. They are not four independent questions, and their verdicts are never averaged. D4 is not a fifth kind of information; it is a constraint on D1's channel.

**Order by dependency (Fig 3).**
1. Perception: can the evaluator perceive the disclosure at all (channel not saturated, Fig 10 :24-26)?
2. Vocabulary: does it carry bonds and relations, not just an ingredient list (Fig 12 :5)?
3. Objective: can the evaluator say what would count as progress or failure (a pre-registered refuter; `THE_FORMULA.md`, Direction)?
4. Planning: can they range the disclosure against the ground (Fig 2 :35)?
5. Learn and carry: methods cross up, recordings do not (Fig 4 :22-25).

Fig 3 :29: *"Before asking whether a system is good, ask which link it currently stops at, and whether the answer was measured or assumed."* The usual break is between 2 and 3: transparency work floods perception and produces ingredient lists, but never installs an objective, so effort lands above the break where the measurements already are. Fig 3 :19: *"Optimising a link that already works cannot rescue one that does not."*

## 3. Findings F1 to F8

### F1 - Helpful disclosure is ground-contacting; self-scoring disclosure is not (D1)

Fig 1 :23: *"The ground enters as the only metric."*

Fig 1 :27: *"They are frame-internal. A frame cannot score itself with a quantity it also produces."*

Fig 1 :24-26 names the disqualified quantities: coverage, compression achieved, predicates minted, credibility accrued. Transparency that reports how much data was collected, how many flags were generated, or how credible the system judges itself to be is the system scoring itself with a quantity it also produces. It changes the evaluator's vocabulary without changing contact.

Fig 11 :40: *"An improvement that does not change contact changes nothing, however much it improves."*

**Verdict D1: `triangulated`** across Fig 1 (metric disqualification) and Fig 11 (contact change), two figures with different mechanisms. **Mode `specified`.**

**Refuter:** show a disclosure whose only contents are quantities the discloser itself produces, yet which reliably improves a ground-contacted judgment in a controlled comparison. If it does, F1 is false.

**World corroboration (secondary):** Kizilcec 2016 (CHI) found that added transparency changed trust only for participants whose expectations were violated, that is, only where a live residual existed; those whose expectations were met did not vary with transparency. This is what "contact change" predicts. Corroborates, does not decide.

### F2 - An aggregate score hides the live slot (D1)

Fig 1 :19: *"The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated."*

Fig 1 :22: *"Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average."*

Fig 1 :13: *"a global R near zero with one live slot residual is a legal state"*

Symbols table: *"R is measured per slot, never as a single global number."*

A single "trust score," "transparency rating," or "accuracy percentage" is a global `R`. It can be near zero while one slot is failing. Per-slot, per-claim, per-decision readings are the form that survives.

**Verdict D1 (aggregate form): `triangulated`** across Fig 1 (signal routing) and Fig 10 :26 (pooled readings as a failure of the channel). Different mechanisms, low overlap. **Mode `specified`.**

**Refuter:** a single aggregate score that preserves every live per-slot residual, demonstrated by recovering each failing slot from the aggregate alone. If one exists, F2 is false.

**World corroboration:** Impink, Paananen and Renders 2022 (*Abacus*) found that above a level of mandated disclosure, analyst *dispersion* rose and accuracy fell. Dispersion is the multi-slot signal reappearing after aggregation was assumed away. Corroborates.

### F3 - The kind is carried by bonds, not by an ingredient list (D2)

Fig 12 :5: *"A set of ingredients is not one. The bond is the rest of the information, and it is the half nothing was recording."*

Fig 12 :17: *"the parts are not the thing"*

Fig 12 :33: *"the arrangement is the substance"*

Fig 12 :111: *"A vocabulary without bonds can name a state and cannot name a route to one."*

Disclosing a list of facts (training data, model card fields, metrics, dates) without the relations among them records half of the disclosure. The relation is the part that decides behavior. Fig 13 makes the same point about lineage: the route is not recoverable from the artefact, only from the record (Fig 13 :126-130). For transparency, this means **provenance and structure are the atom**, not decoration around the numbers.

**Verdict D2: `single-source`** for the figure definition (Fig 12), with the lineage point from Fig 13 as a corroborating figure in the same framework, so not independent. **Mode `specified`.**

**Refuter:** two disclosures with identical ingredient sets but different arrangements that a trained evaluator cannot tell apart in their judgments. If they cannot, the bond does not carry the claimed information and F3 fails.

**World corroboration:** Bracamonte and Isohara 2022 (SCITEPRESS) compared four kinds of disclosure and found data provenance and human involvement were perceived as important as personal-data processing and algorithm explanation, and that relative importance depended on the app. Corroborates "kind is about relation, not just amount."

### F4 - Disclosure overwhelms when bits rise while ground-contacting actions do not (D2, D4)

Fig 10 :24: *"the ground does not decay, the channel does"*

Fig 10 :25-26: *"stale percepts, dropped transients, saturated metrics, pooled readings"*

Fig 10 :27-28: *"the output is not a verdict, it is you have lost touch"*

Fig 12 :125: *"A composition costs in two currencies, and they do not add."*

Fig 12 :126: *"Bits price the description. Actions price finding out whether it holds."*

Fig 5 :42: *"The acceptance test is a bargain, not a threshold."*

Two currencies are being confused in ordinary transparency talk. **Bits** are how much is disclosed. **Actions** are what it costs the evaluator to check whether any of it holds. They are in different units and cannot be added (Fig 12 :127). Adding pages raises bits and may leave actions unchanged or even lower them, because a larger field is harder to search. The MDL bargain (Fig 5 :43-44) says a disclosure is worth having only when stating it plus what remains unexplained after it costs less than the confusion it removed before. A disclosure that explains everything by saying nothing fails. The empirical signature of failure is an inverted U: quality rises with information, then falls.

**Verdict D2/D4: `triangulated`** across Fig 10 (channel saturation), Fig 12 (two currencies), and Fig 5 (the bargain), three figures with different mechanisms and low overlap. **Mode `specified`.**

**Refuter:** a disclosure family where bits rise without bound, the actions needed to check each claim fall to zero, and ground-contacted decision quality keeps rising with no peak. That breaks the inverted U and F4 with it.

**World corroboration:** Kizilcec 2016 found "too much information eroded this trust" and concluded that design "requires balanced interface transparency, not too little and not too much." Impink et al. 2022 found a measured inverted-U curve. Che, Sun, Xiao and Li 2019 (*Advances in Psychological Science*) explain the inverted U by limited attention and working memory. Margondai and Mouloua 2026 (arXiv) derive an interior optimum from cognitive-load dynamics. Four independent author groups and four fields. Strong corroboration, still secondary.

### F5 - Disclosure misleads when a declared meaning substitutes for a violable test (D1, D3)

Fig 10 :18: *"a declared meaning is not a grounded one"*

Fig 10 :19: *"→ install what can be violated"*

Fig 2 :35: *"Alignment is triangulation, not negotiation. A mirror cannot correct you."*

Fig 9 :27: *"describe it before you go looking, or any frame you pick will seem to fit"*

A label such as "we are transparent," "explainable," or "audited" declares a meaning. Until something about it can be violated, it carries no ground contact. The same applies to an explanation the evaluator can access but does not read: its availability produces a feeling of understanding rather than a discrimination. The right form is a convention that can be violated, supplied before the verdict, not a claim about the world dressed as one (Fig 10 :45-46).

**Verdict D1/D3: `triangulated`** across Fig 10 (declared meaning) and Fig 2 (mirror cannot correct). **Mode `specified`.**

**Refuter:** show that the mere *availability* of an explanation, with no reading and no content inspected, improves ground-contacted evaluation in a controlled comparison. If it does, the declared-meaning finding is wrong for that case.

**World corroboration:** Ostinelli, Bonezzi and Lisjak 2025 (*Journal of Consumer Psychology*) found that "merely knowing that one can access an explanation for how an algorithm works may lull consumers into a false sense of understanding that yields unfounded reliance on algorithmic recommendations." Direct corroboration of the precise failure mode.

### F6 - The framework returns a shape, not a catalogue of kinds (answer form)

Fig 9 :32: *"you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself"*

Fig 8 :39: *"So a search that finds nothing is never a proof of absence."*

The question asks "what kinds." The figures license a constraint that any helping kind must satisfy, not an exhaustive, settled list. Fig 13 :115-118: the set of kinds is closed while the arrangements over it are not, so a search for every possible disclosure format does not terminate. The honest answer is the shape: ground-contacting, structured, per-slot, within capacity, violable, and independently checkable. Which concrete formats instantiate it is a matter for the ground.

**Verdict: `single-source`** for the limit (Fig 9), corroborated within the framework by Fig 8 and Fig 13 but not independently. **Mode `general`** on the limit, `specified` on the listed constraints.

**Refuter:** a closed enumeration of disclosure kinds, each settled against a ground as helping or harming, with no remainder. If produced, the "shape not catalogue" limit is too strong.

### F7 - Verification by agreement is a mirror, not a check (D3)

Fig 7 :13: *"agreement compounds, evidence does not"*

Fig 2 :40: *"disqualifying for a verdict: agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool"*

Fig 13 :38: *"independence is how little two frames draw on the same evidence, and it is what makes their agreement mean anything"*

A transparency program that gathers many readers who all received the same disclosure has one evidence pool. Their agreement is the pool talking. Adding more readers raises the vocabulary and does not add an anchor. The audience to look for is one that can range the disclosure against a different ground, not one likely to agree.

**Verdict D3: `triangulated`** across Fig 7 (room chain), Fig 2 (correlation valence), and Fig 13 (independence applied to the set). **Mode `specified`.**

**Refuter:** two evaluators drawing on the same evidence whose agreement, purely by agreeing, improves prediction of an independent ground measurement. If it does, agreement here is evidence and F7 is false.

**World corroboration:** Fang et al. 2025 (*Public Administration*) replicated the finding that explainability raises perceived trustworthiness while showing the effect varies with decision context and population. Perceived trust is not ground contact. Corroborates the distinction, does not settle it.

## 4. Pre-registered refuter (written before the conclusion)

"A falsifier written before the run is a design instrument." The shape claim is refuted if any of the following holds:

1. A disclosure whose only contents are frame-internal quantities the discloser produces itself reliably improves ground-contacted judgment in a controlled comparison (breaks F1).
2. A single aggregate score preserves every live per-slot residual, so no failing slot is lost by aggregation (breaks F2).
3. Two disclosures with identical ingredients but different arrangements produce indistinguishable judgments in trained evaluators (breaks F3).
4. Bits rise without bound while checking cost falls to zero and ground-contacted decision quality shows no peak (breaks F4).
5. Mere availability of an explanation, unread, improves ground-contacted evaluation (breaks F5).
6. A closed, ground-settled enumeration of helpful disclosure kinds is produced with no remainder (breaks F6).

If any fires, the corresponding part of the shape is withdrawn. Until one is run, no part of this may be labelled `grounded`.

## 5. Recommendation

1. Lead with the residual and the refuter, not with a menu of transparency techniques (Fig 10 :52, *"Bring a characterised residual, never the source."*).
2. Publish per-slot, ground-contacting readings and the lineage of each number, not a single score (F2, F3).
3. Track two budgets separately: bits disclosed and actions a reader must spend to check. Do not trade one for the other (F4).
4. Install conventions that can be violated, and state what would count as a failure before disclosure begins (F5, `THE_FORMULA.md` Direction).
5. Select checkers for independence from the disclosure's evidence pool, never for likely agreement (F7).
6. Offer the method, not the recording (Fig 4 :22); measure the round-trip gap `R_T` rather than assuming the condensed formula is identical to the figures (Fig 4 :30).
