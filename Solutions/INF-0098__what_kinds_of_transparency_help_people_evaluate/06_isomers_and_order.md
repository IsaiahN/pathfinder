# Supporting derivation: structure, order, and analogues for disclosure

**Mode: `specified`.** Simulated derivation checked against the figures, not a ground reading (`05_what_shape_means.md` section 6).

## RESIDUAL

The main audit (`01` F4, F8) says disclosure volume and verifier work are two currencies that do not add, and that the residual is a union of conditions. Three supporting questions are owed:

1. Are the four conditions (ground contact, structure, per-slot granularity, capacity) a **partition** or a **union**? This decides whether one lever can clear them.
2. What **order** do the disclosure stages have, and which bond type does each stage use? A list of stages written with the wrong bond is a different claim.
3. Are two disclosures with the same ingredients but different arrangements **isomers** under Fig 12, and what would falsify that?

## DOMAIN CENSUS

Atoms held: the six substrate terms (Fig 13) are not the atoms here; the atoms are *disclosure ingredients*: metrics, lineage, labels, raw records, explanations, uncertainty statements. Bonds held: the seven, especially `→` sequence, `⇒` production, `⋛` comparison, `+` conjunction, and negation `¬` (which is not a bond). Currency: bits and actions, held apart. Anchor: measured ground-contacted decision quality. No new atom is available without import.

## DERIVATION

### Q1 - Union or partition?

Fig 9 :54: *"when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again"*

Fig 9 :55-58: a bundled problem is a union of overlapping conditions. A single lever clears the region where only one condition holds and leaves the intersections standing, because the second condition still binds. The residue is *"categorically different: a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either."*

The four conditions overlap:

- A per-slot disclosure can still be unstructured (condition 2 fails).
- A structured, ground-contacting disclosure can still saturate the channel (condition 4 fails).
- A disclosure within capacity can still be a declared meaning with no violable test (condition 5 in `01` fails).

⇒ **Union, not partition.** Therefore "add more detail" is not a lever on the compound; it is a lever on one condition and can worsen another (volume). This is the derivational reason the answer must be a per-condition shape, not one number.

**Falsifier, written before the run:** if a single manipulation (for example, total disclosure volume) monotonically improves ground-contacted evaluation across all conditions with no interaction, the compound is a partition in disguise and the union reading is false.

### Q2 - What order, and which bonds?

Fig 12 defines the bonds by tests, not by names. Operators table: `→` is *"A then B; B needs A to have happened"* and is ordered; `⇒` is *"A produces B; B did not exist before"* and is ordered; `⋛` is *"a threshold relation between two quantities"* and is ordered.

Applying the tests:

1. **Residual before frame** is `→` sequence, not `⋛` comparison. Fig 9 :27: *"describe it before you go looking, or any frame you pick will seem to fit."* The frame needs the residual to have been described. Swap them and it becomes a different thing (the Operators test: *"swap them. Does it become a different thing? Yes."*).
2. **Explanation after expectation violation** is `→` sequence in the empirical record. Kizilcec 2016 found transparency changed trust only for participants whose expectations were violated. The violation preceded the processing of the explanation. Writing this as `⋛` (a threshold between two quantities) would mistype it.
3. **A disclosure that installs a discrimination** is `⇒` production, not `→` sequence. The Operators test: *"is there a B before A fires? No."* A disclosed per-slot failure that the evaluator did not have before is a produced competence. A disclosed label that restates what the evaluator already believed is only sequence, and often no new term at all.
4. **The decision to disclose at all** is `⋛` comparison: a threshold between the disclosure's benefit and the verifier's remaining capacity. This is where "when does it overwhelm" lives; it is a threshold relation between two quantities (bits and actions considered as capacities), exactly the Operators definition.
5. **Refusal is `¬`, not a bond.** If a disclosure is found to be misleading, the right term refuses its standing to be composed over; nothing is deleted from the record (Fig 6 amendment, Fig 12 amendment). A vocabulary with only `+` and `−` can add disclosures and remove them, and cannot say one is wrong. This is why the outreach claim carries a pre-registered refuter rather than a corrections list.

Fig 12 :111: *"A vocabulary without bonds can name a state and cannot name a route to one."* A flat list of disclosure types can say what a disclosure contains; it cannot say the order in which stages must fire. The sequence above is the route the flat list cannot write.

**Falsifier:** if a disclosure program that shows the explanation *before* the expectation violation performs identically on ground-contacted judgment to one that shows it after, stage 2 is not a sequence and the claimed order fails.

### Q3 - Are two disclosures isomers?

Fig 12 :117: *"Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."* Fig 12 :124: *"functional group. A substructure whose presence fixes the outcome. The group says what kind; the qualifier says which member."*

Take two disclosures over the same ingredient set `{metric, raw record, lineage, label, uncertainty statement}`:

- **D1:** `raw record → metric → (lineage + uncertainty) ⇒ discrimination`, with a `⋛` capacity check before release.
- **D2:** `label + metric + raw record + lineage + uncertainty` as a flat conjunction, released at once.

As the figures define it, these are **isomers**: same atoms, different bonds, different substance. The difference is invisible if you count only ingredients (both are "five items disclosed") and decisive in behavior (one produces a discrimination, the other produces a feeling of completeness). This is the structural version of F3 and F4 in `01`, and it is why "how much was disclosed" is the wrong accounting.

The weaker, safer claim is that both share a **functional group**: each contains metric and raw record in a `state → structure → state` style relation. *"The group says what kind; the qualifier says which member."*

**Falsifier, written before the run:** if evaluators given D1 and D2 make indistinguishable ground-contacted judgments, and no capacity check changes the result, then the bond does not carry the behavioral difference and the isomer claim collapses to the functional-group claim. This is the same falsifier as `01` F3, stated for the compound.

### Q4 - What does the analogue from source discrimination add?

*The Hard Problem is About Source* (essay read 2026-11-23): *"If several paths create the same behavior, then behavior is a projection with lossy information."* A color instrument reads green whether the source was one green light or a mixture. The reading does not determine the source, and *"an instrument that can't distinguish these cases demonstrates its own inadequacy."*

This is a **source analogue**, not a figure line. It predicts that two disclosures with the same observable reading ("looks transparent") will be treated as the same kind until an instrument discriminates their sources. It corroborates Q3 and gives the falsifier its operational form: build the discriminator, then see whether D1 and D2 separate. Overlap with the figures is high (framework author), so it is not independent corroboration.

## CONCLUSION

The four conditions are a union, so no single disclosure lever clears them (Q1). The stages are ordered and use different bonds: `→` for residual-before-frame and for explanation-after-violation, `⇒` for a disclosure that produces a new discrimination, `⋛` for the capacity threshold, and `¬` for refusing a misleading disclosure without deleting the record (Q2). Two disclosures with the same ingredients and different arrangements are isomers under Fig 12, with a functional-group fallback (Q3). The source analogue explains why the difference is normally invisible (Q4). Each claim has a falsifier stated before the result.
