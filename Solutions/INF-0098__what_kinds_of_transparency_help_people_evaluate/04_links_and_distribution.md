## What in the wider literature bears on the question

Four findings, from four independent author groups and fields, converge on the same shape. They are corroboration only; the figures decide.

1. **An interior optimum exists.** Kizilcec (HCI), Impink et al. (accounting), Che et al. (cognitive psychology) and Margondai and Mouloua (human factors) each report or derive a point past which more disclosure reduces decision quality. This is what Fig 12 :125 predicts when bits and actions are held as two currencies: *"A composition costs in two currencies, and they do not add."*
2. **Availability is not inspection.** Ostinelli et al. (consumer psychology) show that the mere prospect of an explanation creates unfounded reliance. This is the empirical face of Fig 10 :18, *"a declared meaning is not a grounded one."*
3. **Kind matters, not only amount.** Bracamonte and Isohara (HCI) found provenance and human involvement as important as more obvious disclosure types, and that importance varied by context. Progressive Disclosure (TIIS) found staged, simplified feedback helped users build a working model. This is Fig 12 :5: *"A set of ingredients is not one. The bond is the rest of the information, and it is the half nothing was recording."*
4. **Perceived trust is not ground contact.** Fang et al. (public administration) show explainability raises perceived trustworthiness while effects depend on context. The figures separate those quantities: Fig 1 :23, *"The ground enters as the only metric."*

Agreement among these fields is not one pool. Each has different authors, methods, and data; the only declared overlap is the accuracy-disclosure paper citing Kizilcec. Even so, none of them outranks the figures, and none settles the framework's shape claim.

## Distribution: offer, do not push

Figure-grounded reasons:

- **Fig 4 :22-25** - *"Only methods cross up. Only head starts come down."* A disclosure of the full framework before anyone has described a residual is a recording, not a generator. Lead with the shape claim and its refuter.
- **Fig 10 :42** - *"A verifier that can reconstruct the claim cannot verify it."* A recipient who has absorbed the framework before giving a verdict has joined the frame being checked.
- **Fig 9 :27** - *"describe it before you go looking, or any frame you pick will seem to fit."* Ask what the recipient would expect to see if the shape claim were true before offering the papers.
- **Fig 2 :40** - *"agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool."* A send selected for likely agreement produces a convention, not corroboration.

**Recommended order in a reply:** (1) the shape claim and its four refuters; (2) ask what they would count as a failure; (3) record that verdict; (4) only then the links, with the note that the figures, not the summary, are the authority.

## The two-stage offer

1. **Stage 1:** residual and shape claim plus the pre-registered refuter. Ask what the recipient would count as a failure. No theory shown.
2. **Stage 2, opt-in only:** the figure extract, the canonical README, and the empirical papers. Their later agreement is never pooled as corroboration.
3. **Stage 3:** the formula as a reusable method, with the instruction that each user names a ground and writes their own refuter before running it.

Stage 1 before stage 2 is Fig 10 :52, *"Bring a characterised residual, never the source."* Stage 3 is permitted because a generator, unlike a recording, can be given away without giving away an answer (Fig 4 :22). Each user still needs a ground per problem: `THE_FORMULA.md`, Direction, says a frame cannot check the direction of its own question, so the sought shape must be made an ordinary term and allowed to accrue a residual.

**Hard precondition:** stages 1 to 3 only work if the stage-1 claim survives. It is currently `specified` and ungrounded; nothing in this folder has been settled by a controlled comparison. Say so in the first message.
