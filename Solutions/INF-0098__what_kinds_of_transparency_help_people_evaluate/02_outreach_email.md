**Subject:** Check one claim about transparency and disclosure before you decide about the theory

> Hi [name],
>
> I have one specific claim I would like you to check against what you already know. I am not asking you to read the framework first, and I am not asking you to agree with it. If the claim does not survive, there is nothing else to look at.
>
> **The claim (`specified`, not `grounded`):** transparency helps a person evaluate information only when it changes their contact with the ground, that is, with whatever actually settles whether the information is reliable. Three consequences follow, and each is checkable on its own:
>
> 1. A single aggregate score (a trust rating, an accuracy percentage) is the wrong form. The error signal is indexed per object, not aggregated. A global score can look healthy while one slot is failing, and aggregation is how that live signal disappears.
> 2. A list of disclosed facts is not the same as a disclosure. The relation between the facts carries the information that decides behavior. A recipe that names only its ingredients has recorded half of itself.
> 3. There are two budgets, and they do not add. Bits measure how much is disclosed. Actions measure what a reader must spend to check any of it. Adding pages raises bits and can leave the cost of checking unchanged or higher. The predicted signature of overload is an inverted U: quality rises with disclosure, then falls.
>
> **What the claim is not.** It is not "more transparency is always better," and it is not "less disclosure is safer." It is not a ranking of transparency techniques. The framework returns a shape any helpful disclosure must have, not a name for the best one. Perceived trust is not the same thing as ground-contacted correctness, so a disclosure that makes people feel informed has not thereby helped them evaluate.
>
> **The failure mode worth naming.** A declared meaning is not a grounded one. A label such as "explainable" or "audited" declares a meaning until something about it can be violated. An explanation a person can access but does not read can produce a feeling of understanding and unfounded reliance. What is owed is a test that can be failed, stated before the verdict, not a statement of openness.
>
> **What would refute the claim:**
> 1. A disclosure whose contents are only quantities the discloser produces itself, yet which reliably improves ground-contacted judgment in a controlled comparison.
> 2. A single aggregate score that preserves every live per-slot residual.
> 3. A disclosure family where volume rises without bound and checked decision quality keeps rising with no peak.
> 4. Mere availability of an explanation, unread, improves ground-contacted evaluation.
>
> I wrote these down before sending this. If any holds, I will withdraw the corresponding part of the claim. A one-line "this fails because..." is more useful to me than agreement. If you would rather not engage, no reply is needed.
>
> **If it survives, and only then:** here are the sources. The figures, not my summary of them, are the authority.
> - Verbatim figure text with line numbers (local extract): `FRAMEWORK/reference/03-FIGURES_TEXT.md`. See Fig 1 :19-27, Fig 10 :18-28, Fig 12 :5 and :125-127, Fig 9 :32.
> - Canonical figures README: https://github.com/IsaiahN/tether/blob/general-agent/figures/README.md
> - Empirical background: Kizilcec, CHI 2016, "How Much Information? Effects of Transparency on Trust in an Algorithmic Interface": https://rene.kizilcec.com/wp-content/uploads/2016/01/kizilcec2016information.pdf
> - Impink, Paananen and Renders, *Abacus* 2022, "Regulation-induced Disclosures: Evidence of Information Overload?": https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1111/abac.12246
> - Ostinelli, Bonezzi and Lisjak, *Journal of Consumer Psychology* 2025, on the illusion of understanding from the mere prospect of an explanation: https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1002/jcpy.1416
>

Write down what would refute your result *before* you run it, and state the mode of what you get. A reported failure is more useful to me than a reported success.

## Why this version is ordered this way

| the move | figure-grounded reason |
|---|---|
| Shape claim and refuter first, theory second | Fig 10 :52, *"Bring a characterised residual, never the source."* A recipient who has absorbed the framework before being asked for a verdict has joined the frame being checked. |
| Per-slot rather than a single score | Fig 1 :19, *"The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated."* |
| Structure before volume | Fig 12 :5, *"A set of ingredients is not one. The bond is the rest of the information, and it is the half nothing was recording."* |
| Two budgets stated separately | Fig 12 :125, *"A composition costs in two currencies, and they do not add."* |
| Do not ask for agreement or trust | Fig 2 :35, *"Alignment is triangulation, not negotiation. A mirror cannot correct you."* |
| Pre-registered refuter | Seat reasoning: a falsifier written before the run is a design instrument. Mode stated on every substantive claim per `THE_FORMULA.md`. |
| No catalogue of techniques | Fig 9 :32, *"you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself."* |
| Prestige is not action | *The Recursive Self-Improvement Trap*: *"Prestige can fund an action... but it cannot settle an action. Only the ground can settle it."* The ask gives the claim a budget to be tried; it does not ask for endorsement. |
| Links after the verdict | Fig 4 :22, *"Only methods cross up. Only head starts come down."* The empirical papers are corroboration only; they are not the authority. |
| Ask users to state their own ground and refuter | `THE_FORMULA.md`, Direction: a frame cannot check the direction of its own question, so make the sought shape an ordinary term that can accrue a residual. |

## Stage order (opt-in throughout)

1. **Stage 1:** send the shape claim and its four refuters. Ask what the recipient would count as a failure. Record their verdict before any framework material is shown.
2. **Stage 2:** only if they are curious, offer the figure extract, the canonical README, and the three empirical papers. Their later agreement is never pooled as corroboration (Fig 2 :40).
3. **Stage 3:** offer the formula as a method to reuse, with the instruction that each user names a ground and writes a refuter before running it.

Nothing here asks anyone to trust the framework, and nothing here is sent by this agent. You host and send.
