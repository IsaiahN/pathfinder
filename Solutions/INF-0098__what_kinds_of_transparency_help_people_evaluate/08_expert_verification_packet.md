# Expert verification packet: transparency, overload, and misleading disclosure

**What this is:** a claim in a form a transparency researcher, HCI researcher, or information scientist can check. Mode, derivation, and refuters are stated before the conclusion, so a reader knows what to look for and what would settle it.

**Mode: `specified` on the substantive claims, `general` on the shape limit, not `grounded`.** No controlled comparison was run in this derivation.

## 1. The claim, in one paragraph

Transparency helps a person evaluate information only when it changes their contact with the ground, that is, with whatever actually settles whether the information is reliable. The error signal is per slot, so a single aggregate score can be healthy while one slot is failing; the relation between disclosed facts carries the information a bare ingredient list loses; and bits (how much is disclosed) and actions (what a reader must spend to check) are two currencies that do not add, predicting an inverted U rather than a monotonic benefit. Disclosure overwhelms when volume rises without a matching increase in ground-contacting checks. It misleads when a declared label substitutes for a violable test, or when an explanation a person can access but does not read produces unfounded confidence. The framework returns this shape any helpful disclosure must have, not a ranked catalogue of formats.

## 2. Three checkable sub-claims

### C1 - framework-internal; checkable by reading Figures 1, 10, and 12

- **Statement:** three constraints follow from the figures: per-slot rather than aggregate, structured rather than an ingredient list, and two non-additive budgets.
- **Sources, verbatim:**
  - Fig 1 :19 *"The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated."* and Fig 1 :22 *"Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average."*
  - Fig 12 :5 *"A set of ingredients is not one. The bond is the rest of the information, and it is the half nothing was recording."*
  - Fig 12 :125 *"A composition costs in two currencies, and they do not add."* and Fig 12 :126 *"Bits price the description. Actions price finding out whether it holds."*
- **Refuter:** produce a single aggregate score from which every failing slot can be recovered; or show two disclosures with identical ingredients but different arrangements that yield indistinguishable judgments; or show a disclosure family in which bits rise without bound, checking cost falls to zero, and ground-contacted quality never peaks.

### C2 - empirically checkable; the overload curve

- **Statement:** disclosure quality follows an inverted U, not a monotonic rise. The mechanism is limited attention and working memory; the practical failure is that more detail raises bits while leaving the reader's ability to check unchanged.
- **What to check:** Kizilcec (CHI 2016) found that too much information eroded trust and that medium transparency helped only where expectations were violated; Impink, Paananen and Renders (*Abacus* 2022) measured an inverted U in analyst decision quality as mandated disclosure rose; Che, Sun, Xiao and Li (*Advances in Psychological Science* 2019) explain the curve by limited attentional and working-memory resources; Margondai and Mouloua (arXiv 2026) derive an interior optimum. Are those characterizations faithful, and do they measure *ground-contacted* quality or only perceived trust?
- **Refuter:** a preregistered experiment in which bits rise without bound, checking effort is held constant or falls, and ground-contacted decision quality shows no peak. That breaks the overload half of the claim.

### C3 - empirically checkable; the misleading half

- **Statement:** a declared meaning is not a check; the mere availability of an explanation can create an illusion of understanding and unfounded reliance.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 10 :18 *"a declared meaning is not a grounded one"* and Fig 10 :19 *"→ install what can be violated"*; Fig 2 :35 *"Alignment is triangulation, not negotiation. A mirror cannot correct you."*
- **What to check:** Ostinelli, Bonezzi and Lisjak (*Journal of Consumer Psychology* 2025) found that "merely knowing that one can access an explanation for how an algorithm works may lull consumers into a false sense of understanding that yields unfounded reliance on algorithmic recommendations." Does their effect survive when the explanation is actually read, and is it about ground-contacted accuracy or self-reported confidence?
- **Refuter:** show that mere *availability* of an unread explanation improves ground-contacted evaluation in a controlled comparison. That breaks the declared-meaning half of the claim.

## 3. What is explicitly not claimed

- Not a ranked list of transparency techniques, and not a claim that any specific format is best. Fig 9 :32: *"you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself."*
- Not "more disclosure is better" or "less disclosure is safer." Volume and contact are different quantities.
- Not that perceived trustworthiness equals reliable evaluation. Fang, Zhou and Chen (2025) show explainability raises perceived trust; that is a frame-internal reading, not a ground verdict.
- Not that the empirical literature proves the framework. Most of it is independent of the framework and corroborates a shape already derived from the figures.
- Not `grounded`. No controlled comparison of disclosure formats was run in this derivation.

## 5. What would settle it

1. **Discrimination test:** do disclosures with identical ingredients but different bonds produce distinguishable ground-contacted judgments? Build the discriminator, as the spectrometer did for the color-reading problem in *The Hard Problem is About Source*.
2. **Aggregation test:** can every live failing slot be recovered from the aggregate score alone?
3. **Inverted-U test:** with checking effort held constant, does quality peak and then decline as bits rise?
4. **Availability test:** does an unread explanation's mere presence improve ground-contacted evaluation?
5. **Enumeration test:** can a closed, ground-settled list of helpful disclosure kinds be produced with no remainder? If yes, the shape-not-a-name limit is too strong.

## 6. What an expert can check in twenty minutes

1. Read Fig 1 :19-27, Fig 10 :18-28, Fig 12 :5 and :125-127, Fig 9 :32. Five short passages.
2. Check C2 and C3 against your own field's results: are the inverted-U and the availability effect described faithfully, and are they about ground-contacted accuracy or perceived confidence?
3. Name one instrument that would discriminate a structured disclosure from an ingredient list in terms of *decision quality*, or say why none is possible.
4. Send the refutation. A failure is more useful than an endorsement. Later agreement is never pooled as corroboration (Fig 2 :40).
