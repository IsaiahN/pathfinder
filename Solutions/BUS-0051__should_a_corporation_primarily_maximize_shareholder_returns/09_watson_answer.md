# WATSON answer, BUS-0051

**Mode: `specified`, not `grounded`.** Figures are primary and decide. World sources are secondary, corroborate but never overrule, and a conflict returns the derivation to the domain census. This is a simulated derivation checked for internal consistency against the figures, not a ground reading.

## QUESTION

Should a corporation primarily maximize shareholder returns, or balance shareholder interests with those of workers, customers, suppliers, and communities?

Restated as an effect: which slots are counted in R = |Γ(b,a) - o'|, and which ground settles each one? R is indexed per party and per time, never averaged (Fig 1 :19). The market is a named anchor for the capital slot (Fig 2 :29), but the question is constitutively about other parties too (Fig 2 :36). Each option leads somewhere: Option A keeps one signal visible and loses the rest; Option B admits the rest but usually blends them and loses the signal again.

## DOMAIN CENSUS

Arity: a relation among firm library Γ, action a and realized outcomes o', indexed by slot and ordered by time; it carries an ordered bond and a comparison bond, not a symmetric one. Symmetry: "primary" and "balance" are threshold comparisons (Fig 12 :112). Scale: the slot is a board decision, while "superior" and "stakeholder" exist at the population scale; the membrane between them is lossy (Fig 4).

Dependency order: perception -> vocabulary -> objective -> planning -> learn and carry (Fig 3). The middle link has no instrument (Fig 3 :27).

## VERDICT (from the figures)

**The figure-derived verdict:** the dichotomy is a union masquerading as one objective. A corporation's decision procedure should keep each slot's residual against its own ground, enumerate the stakeholder habitat from contact rather than ranking it, keep each channel reachable, and keep reach renewed through contact with closures that differ from its own. Verdicts are kept per domain and never averaged. Option A promotes one slot's anchor to the whole union; Option B's usual blended form destroys the per-slot signal. Which weights settle a genuine conflict, and the final normative "should", are declared `gap`s, not hidden answers. Mode `specified`. The governance evidence pool corroborates only after re-mapping Friedman to D2 and Jensen to decision coherence; the low-overlap fields corroborate the mechanism and the gap, not the corporate verdict.

- **Cost if wrong:** misdirected governance. A board failing C1 (a stale or pooled reading of one party) would be told to import more stakeholders, and a board failing C2 (a closure handing itself the same room) would be sold better metrics, and a single lever would be applied to a union that needs two. In outreach terms, an expert's twenty minutes would be spent refuting a shape that was never the right shape. The cost is real but bounded and largely reversible.
- **Undoable:** yes. No term is deleted if the claim fails; a refutation is added and the reach falls because *"something was added rather than because anything was deleted"* (Fig 6 :42-43). The shape and its eight falsifiers are recorded in a provenance trail so a later reader can locate the error rather than only feel it (Fig 10 :62-64).
- **Reversed by:** any of the eight pre-registered falsifiers, in particular (1) a single return measure that predicts, prospectively and per firm, which party fails next; (2) an aggregate index that preserves the per-slot diagnostic; (4) a governance metric that stays discriminating after a decade as the sole target; or (6) a firm that renews its reach purely by internal composition across a habitat change. A derivation inside the figures that yields the weights would close the gap and change the verdict from `gap` to `specified`. A source with a genuinely different evidence pool showing that per-slot persistence is fully predicted by a single return measure would also reverse it. Until one of these fires, the verdict stands as `candidate`.
