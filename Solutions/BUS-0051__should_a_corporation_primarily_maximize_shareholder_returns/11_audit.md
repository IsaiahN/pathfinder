## 2. Independent re-census from the residual line

### 2.1 Arity, symmetry, scale

- **Arity:** a relation among firm-library Γ, action a and realized outcomes o', indexed by slot s and ordered by time t. At least three operands plus an ordering. A two-place reading (firm, return) loses the action and every other party, so it is rejected.
- **Symmetry:** not symmetric. The market is an anchor and does not update on the board's opinion (Fig 2 :25). "Primarily" and "balance" are threshold comparisons (Fig 12 :112), so one operand is compared against another rather than merely conjoined with it.
- **Scale:** the slot is a board decision, but "superior" and "stakeholder" exist only at the population scale (Fig 4 :5). The time index crosses the membrane between the decision loop and the market and habitat selection loops. The residual is intrinsically cross-scale, and the transform is lossy.

**Divergence from the primary census, recorded.** The primary called its first two domains D1 (decision procedure) and D2 (capital market). On re-census they share one anchor, a degradable channel to a non-decaying ground, so they are one domain D; the pledge-versus-document result is an instrument within it, not a separate anchor. This seat also tested merging C and D because both involve the market's response; it declined, because the anchors differ in kind: C is settled by each party's realized outcome, D by the persistence of the channel across time. The primary kept them separate for the same reason without stating the test. Both censuses arrive at four admitted domains.

**Partition or union:** union. The two named conditions are channel maintenance (A and D) and reach renewal (C, with B supplying one differing closure). Their overlap is a compound with its own loop (Fig 9 :54-57). A third item, the normative weights, sits outside the closure and is not a condition at all.

**Dependency order:** perception -> vocabulary -> objective -> planning -> learn and carry (Fig 3). ## 4. Round trip gap R_T

Fig 4 :30: *"So take the round trip and measure the gap. Without it you are navigating by dead reckoning: confident about a position nobody checked."*

- **T_A, going up:** carry the primary's shape to a one-sentence abstraction. Result: *good governance keeps the right measures live and brings in outside views.*
- **T_E, coming back:** put that abstraction into a specific boardroom. Result: *keep the shareholder-return dashboard current and invite more investors to speak.*

**R_T = gap(x, (T_E ∘ T_A)(x)).** The gap is what the abstraction cannot hold:

1. The one-sentence form collapses the union into one lever. It loses Fig 9 :57, *"a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either."* Coming back down, it tells a board failing C2 merely to measure harder, which is effort above the break (Fig 3 :29-30).
2. It loses the anchor limit. "Right measures" does not say that the market settles the capital slot only and that the question is constitutively about other parties (Fig 2 :36). Coming back down, it permits one price to anchor the whole.
3. It loses the valence flip. "Bring in outside views" has no place for the possibility that the currently winning arrangement changed sign (Fig 11 :49-51, Fig 12 :119).
4. It loses the source-discriminator requirement and the preference-provenance requirement. The abstraction cannot say what instrument would separate a protected asset from a paying habitat, or where the weights came from, which are the actual gaps in the answer.

This gap is not an error to eliminate; it is the measurement of what the coarser description cannot hold (Fig 4 :31). It is also the reason the outreach packet sends the generator, not the one-sentence version (Fig 4 :25). The one-paragraph version in `07` is a deliberately lossy artefact and is labelled as such.
