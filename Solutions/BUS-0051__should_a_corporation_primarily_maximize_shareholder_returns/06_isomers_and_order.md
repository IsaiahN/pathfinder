## Q1 - Are the two options isomers?

> **"isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."** (Fig 12 :117)
> **"functional group. A substructure whose presence fixes the outcome. The group says what kind; the qualifier says which member."** (Fig 12 :124)
> **"Symmetry is what decides whether an arrangement is a new thing."** (Fig 12 :92)
> **"A set of ingredients is not one. The bond is the rest of the information, and it is the half nothing was recording."** (Fig 12 :5)

Take the full atom set of the firm: `{capital, labour, customer relation, supply relation, community relation, Γ of routines}`.

- Under **shareholder primacy as usually stated**, the atoms present in the objective are mostly the capital atom and Γ, with the other relations entering only as constraints or as instruments of value. The bond is a single comparison: return ⋛ threshold.
- Under **stakeholder balancing as usually stated**, the objective admits all five party atoms, bound by a weighted conjunction into one score. The bond is still a single comparison at the output, but the ingredient list is longer.

Because the two ingredient sets differ, they are **not isomers** as usually stated. They share a pattern, `objective -> decision -> outcome`, so they are members of one **functional group** (Fig 12 :124), the qualifier saying which member. This is weaker than the isomer claim and is the honest reading.

The stronger version: if both positions are rewritten over the **full shared atom set**, with all five party relations present as operands and differing only in which bond connects them (a single aggregate comparison versus a set of per-slot comparisons kept separate), then they **are** isomers under Fig 12 :117: same atoms, different arrangement, different behaviour, invisible in a list of stated principles. That is the checkable structural claim, and it is the one worth putting to an expert.

**Falsifier, written before the run:** if both positions can be written over the identical atom set with identical bonds and still differ in what a board actually does, the isomer framing is insufficient and the difference must be habitat (D3), which moves the explanation out of composition.

**Status:** `specified`, triangulated for the legality of the reading (Fig 12 and Fig 11 :50, different figures), single-source for the governance application.

## Q2 - What order do the conditions bind in?

> **"The chain is ordered by dependency, not by importance. Each link consumes what the one above produces, so effort spent above the break arrives at a step that cannot use it."** (Fig 3 :20-21)
> **"Optimising a link that already works cannot rescue one that does not."** (Fig 3 :19)
> **"The link between them, having terms for what a situation offers and being able to say what would count as progress, has neither, so it attracts the least attention and the fewest instruments."** (Fig 3 :26)

Order for a board facing a changed stakeholder relation:

1. **Perception.** Can it read the party's outcome at all? Failure here is visible and has obvious tests (wage data, safety incidents, complaint rates, defect rates, supplier failure, local complaints).
2. **Vocabulary.** Does Γ have terms for what the changed situation affords, and can the board say what would count as progress there? This is the middle link. It has no standard instrument in governance reporting.
3. **Objective.** Can it state a comparison that would count as getting somewhere? Fig 12 :112: *"A plan is a sequence. A goal is a comparison. Progress is a subtraction."* A vocabulary with only financial ingredients cannot state a stakeholder goal.
4. **Planning.** A route to that objective under incomplete information.
5. **Learn and carry.** Name what worked, verify it, carry it to the next decision.

The two conditions from the primary audit map onto this chain: C1 (channels reachable) is mostly a failure of links 1 and 2; C2 (reach renewed) is mostly a failure of link 5, because the board names what worked but imports no new atom from a differing closure. That is why a single lever cannot clear both (Fig 9 :55).

The "primarily maximize" move sets the objective (link 3) before link 2 has terms for the other parties. The "balance by aggregating" move builds link 3 as a weighted sum over terms that link 2 never had to validate. Both are effort arriving above the break.

**Falsifier:** a board whose perception, vocabulary and objective links all measure as working and whose failure is located at planning. That would falsify the "usually middle" claim of F9 in the primary audit.

## Q3 - What does the comparison bond license?

> **"A plan is a sequence. A goal is a comparison. Progress is a subtraction."** (Fig 12 :112)
> Operators table: *"a threshold relation between two quantities"* for ⋛ comparison.
> **"A vocabulary without bonds can name a state and cannot name a route to one."** (Fig 12 :111)

"Maximize" is one ⋛ comparison: a quantity against a threshold, with a direction. "Balance" is a family of ⋛ comparisons, one per slot, and the figures contain no operator that composes a family of comparisons into a single one. The weighted sum is an ordinary import: a preference supplied from outside the optimization, which must be declared (Fig 10 :60, *"Where a stake cannot be removed, declare it"*). Declaring it makes it a **convention**, and a convention that decides an outcome has stopped being one (Fig 10 :47).

The multiobjective-optimization literature corroborates the structure from a low-overlap field. Martins and Ning (2022) write that *"In some sense, there is no such thing as a multiobjective optimization problem. Although many metrics are important to the engineer, only one thing can be made best at a time,"* and present the weighted-sum method as requiring a decision maker to choose the weights. Jensen 2001 reaches a similar structural point from finance: *"purposeful behavior requires a single‐valued objective function."* The two agree with each other, but from different fields, so their agreement is about the shape of optimization, not about corporate purpose. Neither supplies the weights; both confirm that the weights must come from somewhere else. That is the `gap` of F10, not a hidden answer.

**Falsifier:** a derivation within the figures that yields the weights from the six-term substrate or the eight operators without an import. If it exists, F10's gap row is wrong and the verdict changes from `gap` to `specified`.

## Q4 - Competing explanations, and what each can and cannot reach

| candidate explanation | what it names | figure-line bearing | what it cannot reach |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Shareholder primacy** (Friedman 1970: agent of owners, within rules) | the capital slot's ground and the executive's agency | D2; Fig 2 :29, :36 | The other parties the question names; it is an instrument for one slot, not the anchor of the union |
| **Enlightened value maximization** (Jensen 2001) | a single scorecard with stakeholder content | close to C2 as content, but one comparison as score | It does not refute per-slot indexing, because accountability and diagnosis are different quantities; it also does not supply a source gate for its own import |
| **Stakeholder theory** (Business Roundtable 2019 and the academic literature) | the enumerated habitat | Fig 11 :43-44, :49-51 | No ranking rule and no instrument that separates a pledge from a change in treatment (Bebchuk and Tallarita 2023) |
| **Balanced Scorecard** (business practice) | a family of metrics | Fig 10 :26 warns of pooled readings | Jensen's objection: no single-valued score, so no principled tradeoff; the figures add that pooling destroys the per-slot signal |
| **Pareto / multiobjective optimization** (engineering mathematics) | a family of non-dominated options | Fig 9 :32 returns a kind of thing, not the answer | It requires preferences from a decision maker; preferences are not derived from the optimization |
| **Resilience / rigidity trap** (ecology) | the valence flip and loss of novelty | Fig 12 :119, Fig 7 :36 | No firm-level ground measurement; it is a low-overlap analogue |
| **Luck / information asymmetry** | an unearned reading that persists while unfuted | Fig 8 :40: *"Underivable-in-principle and unreachable-at-this-budget are different facts"* | Cannot be excluded from inside a frame; Fig 8 :39: *"So a search that finds nothing is never a proof of absence."* Honest `gap`, not a rival verdict |

No candidate is discarded by prestige or familiarity. Each is ranged against the same residual, in the order Fig 8 :53 requires. The point is not to pick one; it is to notice that the first four are in one broad governance evidence pool and that the two low-overlap fields corroborate structure, not the corporate verdict.

## Q5 - Does the shape generate the literature's answers?

Partly. The shape can generate:

- the shareholder emphasis on a measurable return, as the capital slot's ground under D2;
- the stakeholder emphasis on enumeration, as C2 and Fig 11 :43-44;
- the core-rigidity and rigidity-trap emphasis on the flip, as Fig 11 :49-51 and Fig 12 :119;
- the Balanced Scorecard's plurality of readings, as per-slot residuals, while refusing its weighted aggregate;
- the optimization literature's family of options, as a kind of thing, never the chosen weight.

It cannot generate the *name* of the winning weighting or the party that should prevail in a specific conflict. That is the `gap` recorded in F10 of `01`. It is a gap at the instrument boundary, not a silence: Fig 13 :134 (*"Unreachable is a verdict here, not a silence,"*) and Fig 8 :39 apply. The source essay states why: an instrument that reads a result *"cannot comment on the source,"* and *"the answer is unreached until a better instrument exists."*

## CONCLUSION

The two options as usually stated are members of one functional group, not isomers, because their ingredient sets differ; rewritten over the full shared atom set with different bonds they are a real isomer claim with a written falsifier. The conditions bind in dependency order, with the middle link (vocabulary and objective) and the carry link the usual breaks, which is why C1 and C2 need different instruments. "Maximize" licenses one comparison, "balance" licenses a family, and nothing in the figures composes the family without an imported preference; the optimization literature confirms from an independent field that preferences must be supplied from outside. The standard governance explanations are mostly bindings of one evidence pool and are generated by the shape only up to the point where a name or a weight would be required. The naming gap and the weight gap are declared rather than filled.
