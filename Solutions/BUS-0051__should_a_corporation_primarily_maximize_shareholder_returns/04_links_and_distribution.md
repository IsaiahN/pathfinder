## 2. What the wider literature bears on the question

**One evidence pool, one witness.** Friedman, Jensen and the Business Roundtable are all in the corporate-governance and finance conversation. They disagree in conclusion, but they draw on the same canonical cases, the same shareholder-value lineage and the same business-school vocabulary. Per Fig 2 :31, *"Independence, between two frames, is how little they draw on the same evidence."* Agreement or conflict inside that pool is still one witness. Bebchuk and Tallarita are legally positioned differently (empirical corporate law rather than finance theory), which gives them a different instrument, but they still study the same BRT statement.

- **Friedman 1970** states the shareholder side: the executive is *"an employee of the owners of the business"* whose responsibility is to conduct business *"in accordance with their desires, which generally will be to make as much money as possible while conforming to the basic rules of the society, both those embodied in law and those embodied in ethical custom."* Note the constraint is in the sentence. It is not a claim that returns trump all rules; it is a claim about whose agent the executive is. **Conflicts** with Fig 2 :36 as a total anchor; re-mapped to D2 (re-census R1 in `01`).
- **Jensen 2001** makes the scorecard case: *"With no clear way to keep score, stakeholder theory effectively makes managers unaccountable for their actions."* He proposes "enlightened value maximization," which keeps stakeholder interests as content while using long-run firm value as the single tradeoff criterion. **Conflicts** with per-slot indexing if read as a claim about diagnostic signal; re-census R2 separates decision coherence from residual indexing. His "balanced scorecard" objection is the one strong business-side statement of F2.
- **Business Roundtable 2019** enumerates five stakeholder commitments, including customers, employees, suppliers, communities and shareholders, and says *"Each of our stakeholders is essential."* **Corroborates** F3 at the level of habitat enumeration. It does not establish what any signatory did.
- **Bebchuk and Tallarita 2023** test the pledge against governance documents and find it *"mostly for show."* This is the source-discriminator result: the pledge and the documents are two readings of the same "stakeholder commitment," and only a document-level instrument separates them. **Corroborates** F5 and the metamerism rule, without telling us which corporate purpose is right.

**Low-overlap witnesses**, each from a different field and each read directly:

1. **Measurement and audit (economics and social science).** Goodhart 1975, as quoted on the Cambridge DAMTP page, says an observed regularity *"will tend to collapse once pressure is placed upon it for control purposes"*; Strathern 1997 generalizes it to *"When a measure becomes a target, it ceases to be a good measure."* This corroborates F4: promoting shareholder return from a reading to a target degrades it as a reading of what pays. No shared evidence pool with corporate governance.
2. **Optimization mathematics and engineering.** Martins and Ning, *Engineering Design Optimization*, chapter 9 (Cambridge University Press, 2022): *"In some sense, there is no such thing as a multiobjective optimization problem. Although many metrics are important to the engineer, only one thing can be made best at a time."* The chapter shows that a weighted sum requires the decision maker to choose the weights; the Pareto front is a family, not a single answer. This corroborates the `gap` in F10 from an independent field. It also agrees with Jensen from a completely different direction, but the agreement is about the structure of optimization, not about corporate purpose.
3. **Ecology and social-ecological resilience.** MathisonSlee et al. 2022 report a *"rigidity trap"* in which a system becomes *"highly connected, rigid, and inflexible"* and loses *"capacity for novelty."* They also note that efforts to enhance adaptability can unintentionally reduce resilience. This corroborates F7 and F6 without sharing a pool with management research. The anchor at this stratum is different, so this is mechanism corroboration, not a firm-level measurement.

**Framework-internal essays** are used for the canonical reading of "shape" in `05_what_shape_means.md` only. Their author wrote the figures, so they are not independent world witnesses. That overlap is recorded in the ledger.

## 3. Distribution rules

Figure-grounded reasons for how material is offered:

- **Fig 4 :22:** *"Only methods cross up. Only head starts come down."* Send the generator, the residual and the refuter, not the conclusion. A recording carried upward *"looks like knowledge and is a description of one occasion"* (Fig 4 :25).
- **Fig 10 :42:** *"A verifier that can reconstruct the claim cannot verify it."* A recipient who has absorbed the framework before being asked for a verdict has joined the frame being checked.
- **Fig 9 :28:** *"any frame you pick will seem to fit"* if the residual is not described first. Ask what they would expect to see if the shape claim were true before showing the source.
- **Fig 2 :40:** agreement from a shared pool is worthless as a verdict. Later agreement about the framework is never pooled as corroboration.
- **Fig 10 :59-60:** declare the stake; do not ask for belief. An interest in the outcome is unavoidable, an interest in the reading is disqualifying.

**A hard practical constraint.** The Journal of Business Ethics submission page states: *"Large Language Models (LLMs), such as ChatGPT, do not currently satisfy our authorship criteria. Notably an attribution of authorship carries with it accountability for the work, which cannot be effectively applied to LLMs. Use of an LLM should be properly documented in the Methods section."* This whole solution is a simulated derivation produced with an LLM and is labelled as such everywhere. It must **not** be submitted as authored work. The legitimate use is as a private checking instrument: post the residual and the refuter, invite a human to run the check, and let that human author whatever they conclude, disclosing the AI contribution as the venue requires. The SBE extended-abstract category is for substantive work in progress from a qualified author; it is not a route for an unowned LLM artifact.

**Order in any reply:** (1) the shape claim and the pre-registered refuter; (2) the question "what would you count as a failure here?"; (3) only after a verdict is recorded, the three source links with the note that the figures, not the summary, are the authority; (4) the formula as a reusable method, with the instruction that each user writes their own refuter and states their own mode.

## 4. The two-stage offer, compressed

**Stage 1.** Send `08_expert_verification_packet.md` sections 1 to 3 only: the one-paragraph claim, the three checkable sub-claims with their verbatim figure lines and refuters, and the non-claims. No provenance bundle, no essay links.

**Stage 2, opt-in only.** If the recipient is curious and has already recorded a verdict on stage 1, offer the three public links: the figure text, the formula and usage rule, and the essays. Their later agreement is a conversation, not corroboration (Fig 2 :40).

**Stage 3.** Offer the formula as a method, never as an answer (Fig 4 :22). Each user writes a refuter before running it and states the mode of what they get.

**Stop rule.** If stage 1 fails, the theory is not offered. A failure is more useful than an endorsement.

**Hosting.** Nothing here is hosted or sent by this agent. The human operator hosts the packet on an opt-in page and sends one to one. Prefer a download page that lets a recipient decline contact over an attachment pushed to a list.
