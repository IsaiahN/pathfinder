## The question restated as an effect

What is being decided is not the *level* of a corporation's return but *which slots are counted in the residual and which ground settles them*. In effect terms: a board or executive holds a belief b about what the firm's actions will return, takes action a, the firm's library Γ predicts an outcome Γ(b,a), and what actually happens to the firm and to the parties in contact with it is o'. Then R = |Γ(b,a) - o'| is measured per slot s, never averaged (Fig 1 :19, Fig 1 :22).

- Option A, "primarily maximize shareholder returns": index R by one slot, the capital-provider slot, and treat a single market reading as the objective for the whole firm.
- Option B, "balance shareholder interests with those of workers, customers, suppliers, and communities": admit the multiple slots named in the question itself. As usually stated it proposes a weighted blend, which is a single aggregate with more terms and therefore the same aggregation failure (Fig 1 :28).
- What an answer would look like, written down and bound as an ordinary term before the run (Formula, "Direction"): a shape naming (i) which slots are in the habitat of the residual, enumerated from contact, (ii) which ground settles each slot, and (iii) an instrument that discriminates a failure of one slot's channel from a failure of another's. Until bound, that shape predicts nothing and no check in the loop can see it. It is bound in `01_figure_grounded_audit.md` section 0.

**The verdict recorded in `09_watson_answer.md`:** neither option survives as stated. The figure-derived shape is to keep each slot's residual against its own ground, enumerate the stakeholder habitat, and keep verdicts per domain and never averaged. The normative weights that would decide a genuine conflict between slots are a declared `gap`, not a hidden answer. Mode `specified`.

## What is deliberately not here

- No personal emails of named individuals.
- No ranking of people by how likely they are to agree.
- No bulk-send plan.
- No claim that this derivation is `grounded`, no claim that it settles the governance question, and no claim that the framework is the only true account of corporate purpose.

Three figure-grounded reasons, plus one practical one:

1. A room is not an anchor. *"Alignment is triangulation, not negotiation. A mirror cannot correct you."* (Fig 2 :35) and *"A room’s agreement is variation, not a verdict. Selection between rooms is where the ground votes."* (Fig 7 :35). Gathering agreeable governance scholars manufactures the correlation that disqualifies a verdict.
2. Correlation has valence. *"disqualifying for a verdict: agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool"* (Fig 2 :40). A list selected for receptiveness is one witness wearing many names.
3. A verifier must be outside the interior. *"A verifier that can reconstruct the claim cannot verify it."* (Fig 10 :42). The first contact therefore carries the residual and its refuter, not the framework.
4. Legally, unsolicited bulk email to scraped addresses breaches GDPR and CAN-SPAM style rules in many jurisdictions and gets the sending domain blacklisted. Public opt-in routes avoid that failure as well as the structural one.
