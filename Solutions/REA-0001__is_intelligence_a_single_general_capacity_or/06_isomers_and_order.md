## DERIVATION

### Q1 — Partition or union?

- Fig 9 :4-5: *"Disagreements that do not shrink with effort, where each rule keeps working well on a different subset, are not one hard question but several well-formed ones. Split it rather than search."*
- Fig 9 :54: *"And when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again."*
- A bundled problem is a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones. A single lever clears the region where only one condition holds and leaves the intersections standing.
- Here the conditions are D1 definitional, D2 operational, D3 evolutionary. A hierarchical factor model (g at the apex plus broad factors) is exactly the intersection of D1 and D2: you can confirm a general factor **and** multiple broad abilities at once. That is why the either/or has not converged in a century.
- ⇒ **Union**, not partition. **Mode `general`. Triangulated** (Fig 9 :4-5 and :54 plus the three-domain census from `01`).
- **Falsifier:** show that one construct closes all three domains with no residue, so the union is really a partition.

### Q2 — What order do the domains run in?

- Fig 3 :20-21: *"The chain is ordered by dependency, not by importance. Each link consumes what the one above produces, so effort spent above the break arrives at a step that cannot use it."*
- Fig 3 :22-23: *"A step whose input never arrives cannot be diagnosed, only its predecessor can. Which is why a reading taken below the break is not a weak reading. It is a reading of nothing."*
- Formula step 2: the first four bins *"sort R by what is MISSING; the fifth by what is WRONG, and no arrangement of the other four reaches it."* A refusal precedes a mint. The question's shared premise owes a refusal before any ability list is offered.
- Fig 9 :27-28: describe the residual before choosing a frame.
- ⇒ Order: **refuse the premise (D1) → measure per slot (D2) → explain the evolutionary origin (D3)**. Running D2 before D1 produces a number whose referent was never fixed. **Mode `general`. Single-source for the ordering** (Fig 3 + Formula), corroborated by Fig 9.
- **Falsifier:** show that measurement can proceed with the definitional question left open and still yield a referent that survives a ground check.

### Q3 — Are the one-factor and multiple-factor accounts isomers?

- Fig 12 :117: **"isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."**
- The atoms of both accounts are the same observed test scores. What differs is the bond: one common cause (`⇒` from a latent g to every slot) versus reciprocal coupling among processes (`+` and `→` over development, as in mutualism and sampling theory).
- Fig 12 :123: **"functional group. A substructure whose presence fixes the outcome. The group says what kind; the qualifier says which member."** Both accounts share the functional group (they explain the positive manifold) and differ in the qualifier.
- World check, not a figure line: Savi et al. (2019) state that *"g theory and sampling theory and factor models and network models cannot be statistically distinguished on the basis of correlation indexes alone."* McFarland (2012) and Paterson and Deary (2026) reach the same identification result independently. That is the isomer claim in the literature's own vocabulary: invisible in the formula, decisive in which intervention works.
- ⇒ **Yes, they are isomers** over the shared atom set of observed test scores. This is the strongest analogue the question needs, because it explains why a better factor analysis cannot settle the dispute: the difference is in the bond, not the ingredients. **Mode `specified`. Triangulated** (Fig 12 :117 + three independent world fields).
- **Falsifier, written before the run:** if two accounts with the same atoms and different bonds produce identical behaviour under every intervention and time-series probe, the isomer claim fails and the accounts are one substance with two spellings.

### Q4 — Does identity (`≡`) hold across slots?

- Fig 12 :67: *"two names, one referent."* The identifying test, Fig 12 :69: *"substitute one for the other. Is anything lost? No."*
- Substitute one task slot for another in a cognitive battery: information is lost. The slots are not two names for one referent, so `≡` does not hold across slots.
- A single general capacity is often read as if it made slots interchangeable under `≡`. It does not. A hierarchical model keeps the broad abilities precisely because substitution loses information. This triangulates `01` F2 and F3.
- ⇒ **No identity across slots.** **Mode `general`. Triangulated** (Fig 12 :67/:69 + Fig 13 :34-35).
- **Falsifier:** exhibit two slots where substitution loses nothing, under every ground check. Then those two slots are one referent and the non-identity claim narrows.

### Q5 — Why the debate accumulated instead of closing

- Operators table :58: *"a refutation is a negation, which is why a vocabulary without it can only accumulate."* Negation is unary and joins nothing; the seven bonds cannot produce it.
- Formula step 2 names the fifth bin: REFUTED, *"a term held is false; what is owed is a refusal, not an explanation."*
- The debate kept minting new inventories (g, primary mental abilities, 180 factors, eight intelligences, three strata) because it never refused the premise that capability is an agent-internal inventory. A vocabulary that can only accumulate can be corrected and cannot carry the correction.
- ⇒ The century of accumulation is the predicted failure mode, not evidence that nobody was clever enough. **Mode `general`. Single-source** (Operators table + Formula), corroborated by the historical pattern in the world sources.
- **Falsifier:** show that the debate closed by minting alone, with no refusal of the shared premise.

## CONCLUSION

"One or many" is a union cut the wrong way. The dependency order is refuse, then measure per slot, then explain the evolutionary origin. The two rival accounts are Fig 12 isomers: same observed atoms, different bond, invisible in the correlation formula and decisive in what an intervention would do. Identity does not hold across slots. The debate's failure to converge is the predicted result of a vocabulary that accumulates without a refusal operator. None of this asserts that g is false, and none of it settles the empirical factor structure, which remains a `gap` from the figures and reachable only by the ground.
