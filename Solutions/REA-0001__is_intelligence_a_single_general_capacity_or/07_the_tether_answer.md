# The Tether's answer: one general capacity or many independent abilities?

**Mode: `general` on the structural refusal, `specified` on the operational predictions. Not `grounded`.** Simulated derivation checked against the figures (`05_what_shape_means.md` §6), not a ground reading. No measurement settles the empirical factor structure here.

## 1. The residual it answers

The question offers two inventories of the agent: one general capacity, or many abilities that vary independently. Both presuppose that capability is a property of the agent. The residual is the mismatch between that presupposition and the contact question the figures ask instead. It is a union of three conditions, not one hard question: definitional (is the term legitimate?), operational (what is the unit of measurement?), evolutionary (where does capacity come from?). Verdicts are kept separate and never averaged.

## 2. The shape, per domain

And capability is a property of agent-and-habitat, never of the agent alone."* Fig 11 :35: *"The wrong question, and this figure is the replacement for it."* Both options inherit the refused premise. Spearman-style g and Gardner-style multiple intelligences are refused for the same reason: both are lists of properties of the agent. Triangulated** with Formula step 2; world-corroborated by Burkart et al. (2017) and Savi et al. (2019).

**D2, operational. Per slot, never aggregate.** Fig 1 :19: *"The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated."* Fig 1 :22: *"Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average."* Where measurement is used, the figure-derived unit is the slot. ## 3. What it explicitly does not say

- Not that g is false, not that factor analysis is invalid, and not that psychometric g is controversial. It is well established as a fitted quantity.
- Not that the framework endorses multiple intelligences. That list is refused for the same reason g is.
- Not an empirical factor structure, not a number of abilities, and not a new primitive.
- Not grounded. No measurement in this folder settles `left(R, φ)` for any factor model.
- Not a theory of everything. The union stays open; there is no last room.

## 4. What would settle it — the ground

A discriminating instrument and interventional data, not a further argument. From *The Hard Problem is About Source*: a reading of green *"cannot comment on the source,"* and it was the spectrometer, not a debate, that resolved the case. Here:

1. **Slot test.** A broad multi-habitat battery in which partialling out one general factor leaves no slot with positive residual mass, and the single scalar out-predicts the slot vector out of sample.
2. **Contact test.** A demonstration that capability is invariant across habitats and unchanged by contact, so a fixed agent-internal inventory closes every residual.
3. **Identification test.** Time-series or experimental evidence that distinguishes one latent cause from a coupled network on the same correlation data. Savi et al. (2019) explicitly allow that *"Time-series data and experimental interventions may very well distinguish between the models."*
4. **Substrate test.** Derive one of the six substrate members from the other five, or show two members drawing on the same underlying fact so one is redundant.

Standing falsifiers are written before the run in `01_figure_grounded_audit.md` §4. If any fires, the affected claim is withdrawn in the retraction log there.

## 5. The honest stop

**Unreached, not unreachable.** The framework can say what is reachable from what it holds, what is owed, and what a missing thing would look like. It cannot name the empirical factor structure, because naming it would mean already holding the discriminating instrument. Indiscernibility is not identity. The record stops where the instruments stop and keeps the lineage so the stop is checkable.

## 6. One-paragraph version for outreach

> The Tether does not pick a side between one general capacity and many independent abilities. It refuses the question's shared premise: capability is not a property of the agent alone but of the agent-and-habitat contact, so both a single g and a list of intelligences are inventories of the wrong thing. Where measurement is unavoidable, it reads residuals per slot and refuses to aggregate them into one global score, because aggregation is how a live signal disappears into an average; it permits a near-zero global score, but forbids using that score to decide where to intervene. A positive correlation matrix does not decide the issue either: the one-cause and the many-coupled-process accounts are isomers, same observed atoms arranged with a different bond, invisible in the formula and decisive in which intervention works. On the empirical factor structure of human ability the figures report a gap, not an answer. What they offer is the shape any answer must have, four falsifiers written in advance, and the ground tests that would close it.
