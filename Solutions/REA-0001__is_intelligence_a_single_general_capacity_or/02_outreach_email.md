**Hard precondition:** do not send until the stage-1 claim in `08_expert_verification_packet.md` survives a check against the ground. The claim below is a shape and a refusal, not a result. If it does not survive, there is nothing else to look at.

**Subject:** Check one claim about "intelligence" before you decide whether the framework is worth reading

> Hi [name],
>
> I have one specific output I would like you to check against what you already know. I am not asking you to read the theory first, and I am not asking you to agree with it. If the output does not survive, there is nothing else to look at.
>
> **The output (`general` on the structure, `specified` on the prediction, not `grounded`):** the question *"is intelligence one general capacity or many independent abilities?"* presupposes that capability is a property of the agent. The framework's habitat figure answers that a different way: *"the answerable question is not what intelligence is, but what makes a system capable. And capability is a property of agent-and-habitat, never of the agent alone."* So both options as stated are refused, Spearman-style g and Gardner-style multiple intelligences alike. They are inventories of the agent, and an improvement that does not change contact changes nothing.
>
> Where the question is made operational, the shape runs the other way from an aggregate. The agent figure indexes the residual *"by object slot, not aggregated,"* and says *"Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average."* A global score can be near zero while one slot is still live. The framework therefore reads performance per slot and does not collapse the vector to one number to decide where to intervene. This is a structural preference, not an empirical claim that g does not exist.
>
> **What I am not claiming:** that human abilities have been shown to be many rather than one; that factor analysis is invalid; that a positive manifold proves nothing. psychometric g is well established and noncontroversial as a fitted quantity. The claim is narrower: a correlation matrix alone does not identify one cause rather than many, and the framework cannot settle that empirical point from the armchair. On the factor structure itself I report a gap, not an answer.
>
> **What would refute the shape claim:**
> 1. a broad battery, repeated across habitats, in which partialling out one general factor leaves no slot with positive residual mass and the single scalar out-predicts the slot vector out of sample;
> 2. a demonstration that capability is invariant across habitats and unchanged by contact, so a fixed agent-internal inventory closes every residual;
> 3. an intervention or time-series design that distinguishes one latent cause from a coupled network on the same correlation data (this would close the gap, not confirm the shape).
>
> I wrote these down before sending this. If any one holds, the claim is withdrawn. A one-line "this fails because..." is more useful to me than agreement. If you would rather not engage, no reply is needed.
>
> **What I would like from you first:** what would you count as a failure of this shape claim? I will record your answer before showing you any theory, so that the verdict is not shaped by the framework.
>
> **If it survives, and only then:** the sources are below. The figures are the authority, not my summary of them.
> - Full architecture (Medium): https://medium.com/@IsaiahNwukor/arrival-of-the-fittest-the-tether-architecture-1de7edf641ec
> - Worked narrative and the habitat argument: https://adventuresinml.substack.com/p/how-intelligence-actually-works
> - Figures and formula for direct download (SVG, branch `general-agent`): https://github.com/IsaiahN/tether/tree/general-agent/figures
>
> **If you want to use the machinery:** the formula is in the figures link. Run it on a problem you know well and can check against a ground. Write down what would refute your result *before* you run it, and state the mode of what you get. A reported failure is more useful to me than a reported success.
>
> — Isaiah
