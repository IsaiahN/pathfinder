**What this is:** a claim in a form an intelligence researcher can check. Mode, derivation and refuters are stated before the conclusion, so a reader knows what to look for and what would settle it.

**Mode: `general` on the structural refusal, `specified` on the operational predictions. Not `grounded`.** No empirical factor-structure claim is made.

**Status:** a simulated derivation checked for internal consistency against the figures, not a ground reading. It cannot settle the question it reports on (`05_what_shape_means.md` §6).

## 1. The claim, in one paragraph

The question "is intelligence one general capacity or many independent abilities?" presupposes that capability is a property of the agent. The figures refuse that premise: capability is a property of agent-and-habitat, so both a single g and a list of intelligences are inventories of the wrong object. Where measurement is unavoidable, residuals are indexed per slot and never aggregated, because aggregate scores hide live slot signals. A positive correlation matrix does not discriminate between one common cause and multiple coupled causes: those accounts are isomers, same observed atoms with a different bond, invisible in the formula and decisive in the intervention that works. Whether human abilities in fact load on one factor or several is a gap from the figures, reachable only by the ground.

## 2. Three checkable sub-claims

### C1 — framework-internal; checkable by reading Figure 11

- **Statement:** the question's shared premise is false; capability is agent-and-habitat, not an agent-internal inventory.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 11 :39 — *"So the answerable question is not what intelligence is, but what makes a system capable. And capability is a property of agent-and-habitat, never of the agent alone."* Also Fig 11 :35 — *"The wrong question, and this figure is the replacement for it."* Fig 11 :36 — *"Where nothing maintains anything, intelligence is a label applied afterwards to a lineage that met its ground. Nothing was intelligent; selection ran. The word does no work there."*
- **Mode:** `general`.
- **Refuter:** a demonstration that capability is invariant across habitats and unchanged by contact, so that a fixed agent-internal inventory closes every residual. Also: two of the six substrate members shown to draw on the same underlying fact, making one redundant (Fig 13 :34-35).
- **What an outside reader can add:** evolutionary and comparative work that puts skill construction in social inputs during development (Burkart et al. 2017) already corroborates the replacement. This is corroboration only, not a witness.

### C2 — measurement-facing; checkable by a psychometrician against a correlation matrix

- **Statement:** the operational unit is the slot, not the aggregate; a global score is permitted but cannot be used to locate an intervention.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 1 :19 — *"The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated."* Fig 1 :22 — *"Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average."* Fig 1 :13 — *"a global R near zero with one live slot residual is a legal state."* Triangulated with Fig 13 :34 — *"And within a kind the members are distinct too, which is what makes them separate frames."*
- **Mode:** `specified`, ungrounded.
- **What to check:** is the slot reading faithful to how a battery is scored? Does partialling out a general factor ever leave no live slot, and if so, in which domains? Are the broad abilities in a hierarchical model the slots this figure describes, or a different object?
- **Refuter:** a broad, multi-habitat battery in which partialling out one general factor leaves no slot with positive residual mass, and the single scalar out-predicts the slot vector out of sample.
- **Honest limit:** this is a structural preference, not the empirical claim that g does not exist. Psychometric g is well established as a fitted quantity (Carroll 2003; Savi et al. 2019).

### C3 — identification-facing; checkable against the one-factor versus network literature

- **Statement:** a positive manifold does not identify one cause rather than many; the two accounts are isomers under Fig 12's definition.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 12 :117 — *"isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."* Fig 12 :123 — *"functional group. A substructure whose presence fixes the outcome. The group says what kind; the qualifier says which member."* Fig 2 :40 — *"disqualifying for a verdict: agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool"*
- **Mode:** `specified`.
- **What to check:** McFarland (2012) simulates eight independent factors producing a positive manifold; Paterson and Deary (2026) show hierarchically ordered correlations do not uniquely identify a single factor; Savi et al. (2019) say factor, sampling and network models cannot be statistically distinguished on correlation indexes alone. Carroll (2003) confirms that a general factor can be fitted. Do these claims conflict, or is one about model confirmation and the other about causal identification? The solution records the latter reading (re-census in `01` F4) and invites correction.
- **Refuter:** time-series or experimental evidence that distinguishes one latent cause from a coupled network on the same data. Savi et al. explicitly allow this: *"Time-series data and experimental interventions may very well distinguish between the models."* If it fires, the empirical gap closes and C3 is retired.
- **Isomer falsifier:** if two accounts with the same atoms and different bonds produce identical behaviour under every intervention and time-series probe, the isomer claim fails and the accounts are one substance with two spellings.

## 3. What is explicitly not claimed

- Not that g is false, not that factor analysis is invalid, not that psychometric g is controversial.
- Not that the framework endorses multiple intelligences. That list is refused for the same reason g is.
- Not an empirical factor structure, not a number of abilities, not a new primitive.
- Not grounded. Nothing here is asserted as settled by experiment.
- Not a theory of everything, and no final room. The union stays open.
- Not that a global score is illegal. Fig 1 :13 permits it; the prohibition is on using it to hide a live slot signal.

## 5. What would settle it

1. **Slot test:** partialling out one general factor leaves no live slot across a broad multi-habitat battery, and the scalar out-predicts the vector out of sample.
2. **Contact test:** capability invariant across habitats and unchanged by contact.
3. **Identification test:** time-series or experimental discrimination between a single latent cause and a coupled network.
4. **Substrate test:** derive one of the six from the other five, or show two drawing on the same underlying fact.

## 6. What an expert can check in twenty minutes

1. Read Fig 11 :35-40, Fig 1 :13/:19/:22 and Fig 12 :117 — six short passages.
2. Check C2 against a battery you know: does partialling out g leave slot-level residuals that predict outcomes?
3. Say whether you read the Carroll and McFarland/Paterson-Deary results as conflicting, or as different quantities. Name the instrument that would distinguish them.
4. Send the refutation. A failure is more useful than an endorsement.
