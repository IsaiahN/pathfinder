## What in this literature bears on the question

1. **Kuhn, 1962.** The single most relevant finding for F2 and F3. Textbooks rewrite the past as an "inevitable process leading up to the current, established framework of thought", and earlier successes generate confidence that a solution to the next puzzle exists. That is a narrative supplying both the bond and the expectation. The correction from `01` section 7 matters: Kuhn is not a denial-of-progress witness, so this source cannot be used to claim that revolutionary stories lower expected returns. It changes the *shape* of the expected return.
2. **Whig history, Butterfield 1931.** The clearest low-overlap corroborator for the comparison bond. The story selects events by their contribution to the present and subtracts dead ends. It predicts an expectation that the present is the goal and that the successful chain was the only possible one. Hindsight bias is the cognitive-scale analogue of the same selection.
3. **Linear model of innovation.** The bridge from a historiographical narrative to innovation policy expectation: basic research flows into development and then the market. Its critics note that it ignores feedbacks and loops, and one source argues it was an academic construction "never believed in practice". That disagreement is kept as evidence for the union reading, not used to flip a verdict.
4. **Matthew effect, Merton and Zuckerman 1968.** A sociology-of-science mechanism by which visibility compounds and changes what risky work gets attempted. It corroborates the room-chain shape of F3 at maximum distance from the framework's own author. It is not the same claim: the figures decide whether agreement compounds evidence; the Matthew effect supplies a mechanism by which it might.
5. **Hindsight bias.** The genuinely outside-field witness, cognitive psychology. It predicts that reading a completed success story inflates the perceived predictability of the path and the expected return on the next one. It corroborates the shape of F4 without deciding it.
6. **Progress / scientific progress.** The ordinary-language anchor: "today's chemists reasonably expect that chemists in future centuries will know more than they do." This is the expectation the question asks about, stated in its own terms. It also records the historic shift from accumulation narrative to paradigm narrative, which is F2 in world vocabulary.

**Overlap.** Items 1, 2 and 6 are history and philosophy of science and share one broad evidence pool about how progress is narrated. Items 3 and 4 are innovation studies and sociology of science and share a science-policy pool. Item 5 is cognitive psychology and is the lowest-overlap witness. These are corroborators only. None overrules a figure line, and a conflict would send the derivation back to the domain census.
