## QUESTION

Decide, in effect terms, how a story about how science advanced changes an expectation held now about research and innovation. Option A: treat the story as a transparent report, so the expectation is a direct reading of the evidence and nothing is owed. Option B: treat the story as a frame with a closure, a currency, a bond-typed account of progress, and a channel to a habitat. What each leads to: A makes the story invisible, disqualifies no anchor, and owes no instrument, so a consensus can stand in for a verdict; B returns a four-condition separator, a non-updating anchor requirement, six refuters written in advance, and a declared gap where no figure line speaks. The figures decide in favour of B as a shape, not as a measured fact.

## DOMAIN CENSUS

R by arity, symmetry and scale, then one line per domain with actors, currency and anchor, union or partition, and order by dependency.

- **R (the residual).** The per-slot gap between an expectation held about research and what the present ground would return if the inherited story were not mediating the reading. Arity: three conditions in contact (narrative frame, expectation decision, ground). Symmetry: ordered, story then expectation, so a sequence or production bond, not a symmetric join. Scale: population or institutional scale for the story, decision scale for the expectation, with a lossy membrane between. Fig 1 :19 keeps R indexed by slot; a single average throws away the live signal.
- **Domain 1, the narrative as frame.** Actors: past events and persons as atoms, the bonds that order them, the library and its closure. Currency: compression and explanatory purchase. Anchor: the frozen historical record used as the frame's own reference. Question type: partition question about what is inside the closure and which bond carries progress.
- **Domain 2, transmission.** Actors: textbooks, curricula, panels, funding bodies, societies, media. Currency: credibility and agreement. Anchor: ought to be measured outcomes, in practice often the concordance of the rooms. Question type: handoff chain; competing stories form a union, not a partition.
- **Domain 3, expectation formation.** Actors: researchers, funders, policymakers. Currency: predictions and allocations. Anchor: an outcome that does not update on what the frame thinks. Question type: the vocabulary-to-objective link, which has no instrument, plus the convention that becomes a verdict.
- **Domain 4, research habitat.** Actors: instruments, labs, datasets, markets, the research ecosystem. Currency: settled terms and ground contact. Anchor: the ground, which does not decay. Question type: agent-and-habitat contact; import moves the wall.
- **Union, not partition.** Four overlapping conditions (Fig 9 :55, :57). A lever on one leaves the intersections standing. Verdicts are kept per domain and never averaged.
- **Order by dependency (Fig 3 :20).** Register the residual per slot; supply vocabulary and bonds; state the objective; carry the story; select a differing room; range against the ground. The chain breaks at vocabulary to objective because no instrument measures it.

Strain test applied twice: Fig 13's six-term substrate governs primitives, Fig 12's bond set governs the arrangements a historiographical story uses, so assigning "progress" to the gradient was a category error and is withdrawn. Fig 7's room chain and Fig 6's closure wall govern different quantities, so they are not in conflict. Both re-censuses are visible in `01` section 7.

- **Philosophy of science / historiography.** Thomas S. Kuhn (1962), summarized at Wikipedia, 2026: "Once a paradigm shift has taken place, the textbooks are rewritten. - **Historiography.** Herbert Butterfield (1931), summarized in the Whig history article at Wikipedia, 2026: "The term is also used extensively in the history of science to refer to historiography that focuses on the successful chains of hypotheses and experiments that led to present-day theories, while ignoring rejected hypotheses and dead ends." ## VERDICT (from the figures)

The question is a union of four conditions, not one lever. A historical narrative shapes a present expectation by bounding the closure from which the expectation can be composed (Fig 6), by supplying the bond that encodes what progress means (Fig 12), by travelling as a recording through rooms that compound agreement unless the next room differs and matches the surfaced residual (Fig 4, Fig 7), and by changing the habitat to which the community has contact (Fig 11). The decisive failure is a reference that updates on the story, producing coherent drift that resembles triangulation (Fig 2 :33-35, Fig 10 :24). The chain breaks at vocabulary to objective, where no instrument measures the bond (Fig 3 :27). No figure line returns which narrative dominates in an institution or how large the effect is; that is a `gap` of the unreachable-at-this-budget kind, with a story-variation instrument described but not run. Verdicts per domain are kept separate and are never averaged. Mode is `specified` on the mechanism and each domain verdict, `general` on the structural shape, `gap` on the prevalence and magnitude, not `grounded`.

- **cost if wrong:** bounded and locatable. The packet states mode, refuters and lineage before the conclusion, so an error is findable rather than invisible. The real cost is spending the independence of outside readers by sending a recording instead of a generator, which Fig 2 :42 prices: "Buy independence in proportion to the cost of being wrong." That cost is avoidable by leading with the separator and its refuter. A second cost is recommending a story-variation design that never runs, leaving the gap as an excuse rather than an owed instrument.
- **undoable:** yes. Any of the four conditions can be withdrawn independently without touching the others; the earlier readings that failed re-census are visible in `01` section 7 rather than erased. Nothing asserts a named culprit, so no narrative has to be retracted. A sent packet can be superseded by a corrected one.
- **reversed by:** a ground reading, not agreement. A story-variation experiment that ranges allocations against non-updating outcomes would name the bond that moves the forecast and close the gap; an expectation formed with no supporting atom, two equal-bond stories with opposite directions, a chain whose agreement tracks the ground at every handoff, a ground that updates yet still corrects, or an instrument for the story-to-expectation bond would each break one of the six pre-registered refuters and change the verdict. A figure re-census that returns a dominant narrative or a numerical effect size would also close the gap. Until one of these fires, the verdict stands as `candidate`.
