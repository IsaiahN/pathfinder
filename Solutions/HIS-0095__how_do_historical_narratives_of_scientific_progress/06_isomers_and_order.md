# Are progress narratives isomers? Bonds, order, and the handoff chain

**Mode: `specified` on the isomer and order claims, `general` on the structural relation, not `grounded`.** This is a simulated derivation checked against the figures, not a ground reading (see `05_what_shape_means.md` section 6).

## DERIVATION

### Q1 - Are the three stories isomers?

- Fig 12 :117, *"isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."*
- Fig 12 :95-96, *"Two entries with an identical ingredient set are not variants of one thing. They are different things whose notation cannot tell them apart, and the difference is carried entirely by the bond."*

Take a shared atom set over a real stretch of history: the Copernican and Galilean work, the Newton synthesis, the rejection of phlogiston, the germ-theory controversy, the Einstein and quantum revolutions. The three stories use essentially this set.

| story | bond chain over the atoms | what the bond does | expectation licensed |
|---|---|---|---|
| Linear / cumulative | `→` sequence, then `−` subtraction of the superseded | each stage follows the last and leaves more than before | steady return on basic research, short and predictable horizons |
| Whig | `⋛` comparison of past events against the present as threshold, with the rejected paths `−` removed | the present selects which events count | the current framework is the goal; the successful chain was inevitable; fund the continuation |
| Revolutionary / Kuhnian | `⋛` comparison across incommensurable closures plus `¬` refusal of the old term, with a preservation constraint | a new framework replaces the old but must preserve solved problems | discontinuous return; fund the alternative, keep the solved problems |

- Fig 12 :112, *"A plan is a sequence. A goal is a comparison. Progress is a subtraction. A refutation is a negation, and that one is not a bond."*
- Fig 12 :94, *"The arrangement is the substance."*

If the atom sets really are the same, the three stories are **isomers**: same atoms, different bond, different behavioural consequence, and the difference is invisible if you record only the list of events. That is exactly what a historian does when they write "the story is just the facts."

**Caveat, kept visible.** The atom sets are rarely literally identical. The Whig story subtracts dead ends, so it holds fewer atoms than the revolutionary story, which keeps the anomalies. If the atom sets differ, the relation is not pure isomerism but composition plus subtraction under subtraction's bond (Operators :42-45, *"subtraction. A with B removed or absent"*). The isomer claim therefore holds **only for a fixed atom set**. This is the same live condition the exemplar records for its own isomer claim. It is named here, not hidden.

**Falsifier, written before the run:** exhibit two progress stories over a fixed atom set with the same bond chain yet different expected directions. That breaks the isomer-to-expectation step, and the difference must then be sought in the slot binding rather than in the bond. A second falsifier: show that one story requires an atom the other does not while still producing the same expectation; that breaks the shared-atom premise.

### Q2 - Functional group: what all three stories share

- Fig 12 :123, *"functional group. A substructure whose presence fixes the outcome. The group says what kind; the qualifier says which member."*

All three stories share a functional group: *past events arranged into a route to a present*. That substructure is what makes any of them capable of generating a future expectation at all. The particular bond is the qualifier that says which forecast comes out. So the union of stories is not three unrelated explanations; it is one functional group with three bond variants. This is why a debate about "which story is right" misses the mechanism: changing the member of the group changes the output more than adding more events.

World corroboration, not figure decision: the *Progress* source records the historic shift from an accumulation presentation to Kuhn's competing-paradigms presentation and notes that these interpretations "have met with opposition" for portraying history as incommensurable paradigms. The disagreement is between bond variants inside one functional group. That is what the source shows; the figures decide the structure.

### Q3 - What is the order of the handoff?

The question of order is a chain question, and chains are ordered by dependency (Fig 3 :20):

1. **Register the residual at the slot** (Fig 1 :19: *"The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated."*). Which expectation failed, and where?
2. **Supply the vocabulary and the bond** (Fig 12 :111, *"A vocabulary without bonds can name a state and cannot name a route to one."*). Does the community have a term for the route, or only for the discoveries?
3. **State the objective in the frame's own terms** (Fig 3 :9-11). What would count as progress here?
4. **Compress and carry** (Fig 4 :22-26). The story is carried up as a method only if it is reapplicable; it comes down as a head start, never as a replay of one occasion.
5. **Select the next room** (Fig 7 :36, *"A chain of mirrors is one long room. The next room must differ from the last, and match the residual it surfaced."*).
6. **Range against the ground** (Fig 2 :45, *"Frames range against the ground. They do not negotiate with it."*).

The chain usually breaks between 2 and 3 because that link has no instrument (Fig 3 :27, *"A link with no instrument is not a link that works. It is a link nobody has looked at."*). The stories are fluent; the test of whether their bond holds at the present slot is not.

**Falsifier for the order:** exhibit an institution where the objective is set before the story is supplied, and where that order produces a better match to a non-updating outcome than the reverse order. That would break the dependency order claimed here.

### Q4 - Is any of this a seventh term?

No. Fig 13 :115, *"The set is closed. The arrangements are not."* Fig 13 :116, *"Six members, and no amount of arranging produces a seventh"*. Progress narratives are arrangements of bonds over held atoms; they are not new members of the substrate. My first pass tried to map "progress" onto the gradient, which was a category error (retracted in `01` section 7). The correct figure for this question is Fig 12's bond set and Fig 6's closure, not Fig 13's six primitives.

## CONCLUSION

The three progress stories are isomers **over a fixed atom set**: same events, different bonds, different expectations. Where the atom sets differ, the relation is composition plus subtraction, and the stricter claim does not apply. They share a functional group, *past events arranged into a route to a present*, which is what gives any of them the power to generate an expectation. The handoff from story to expectation is an ordered chain of six links that typically breaks at the vocabulary-to-objective link, where no instrument exists. No seventh progress concept is introduced at any point.

What survives is the structural distinction: type of bond, not amount of evidence, is the variable that changes the forecast. What does not survive is any claim about which story dominates or how large the effect is; that remains the `gap` in `01` F7.
