**Hard precondition:** do not send until the refuter in `01_figure_grounded_audit.md` section 4 has been read and can be restated in your own words. The offer collapses on contact if the output does not survive validation.

**Subject:** One separator to check, before you decide whether any of this is worth reading

> Hello [name],
>
> I have one specific output I would like you to check against what you already know about how accounts of scientific progress affect expectations in research and innovation. I am not asking you to read the framework first, and I am not asking you to agree with it. If the output does not survive, there is nothing else to look at.
>
> **The question as an effect (`specified`):** present-day expectations about research, what is tractable, how fast returns should arrive, what counts as progress, which risks are acceptable, are not read straight off the evidence. They are generated by frames that inherited a story about how science advanced. The residual has to be indexed per research domain. An average "effect of narratives on expectations" would hide the live signal.
>
> **The output, written down before I ran anything:** a four-condition separator. A progress story shapes an expectation when (1) it supplies the atoms and bonds from which the expectation can be composed at all, (2) the bond it uses to encode progress, sequence, subtraction, comparison, or refusal, selects the direction of the forecast, (3) the story travels through a chain of rooms that compounds agreement without adding evidence unless the next room genuinely differs and tests what the last one surfaced, and (4) the community's habitat changes contact, not just belief. The decisive failure is when the reference used to test the expectation is itself a product of the story: citations, prestige, panel consensus. Then everyone agrees, the drift is coherent, and nobody notices.
>
> **What I am explicitly not claiming.** I am not claiming that the linear model, the Whig story, or the revolutionary story is false, and I am not naming one as the dominant narrative in any institution. The framework returns a shape, not a name. I have no effect size, no survey, and no measurement. The claim is about the conditions under which a story can do this work, not about which story wins. Agreement with me is not evidence; a shared evidence pool talking is not two witnesses.
>
> **What would refute the separator.** I wrote these down before sending this:
> 1. an expectation formed at a slot with no atom and no bond present in the inherited story at that slot;
> 2. two stories with the same bond over the same events producing opposite expected directions;
> 3. a transmission chain whose agreement tracks an independent, non-updating outcome at every handoff;
> 4. a "ground" that updates on the story, yet still corrects expectations;
> 5. an instrument that measures the story-to-expectation bond at the slot;
> 6. a result showing which narrative dominates somewhere, with a measured magnitude.
>
> If any one holds, the separator is wrong in that part and I will retract it. A one-line "this fails because..." is more useful to me than agreement. If you would rather not engage, no reply is needed.
>
> **What I would actually like from you:** tell me what you would count as a failure of condition 3, the room-chain claim. That is the one I am least certain of, and the one an historian of science or an STS scholar can check fastest against a real case. Record your verdict before you see any of the source material below.
>
> **If it survives, and only then, here are the sources.** The figures are the authority, not my summary of them.
> - Canonical figure text with line numbers (local extract): `FRAMEWORK/reference/03-FIGURES_TEXT.md`
> - Figures repository, branch `general-agent`: https://github.com/IsaiahN/tether/tree/general-agent/figures
> - The architecture essay, including the mappings that were offered and declined: https://medium.com/@IsaiahNwukor/arrival-of-the-fittest-the-tether-architecture-1de7edf641ec
>
> **If you want to use the machinery:** the formula is in the figures link. Run it on a problem you know well and can check against a ground. Write down what would refute your result *before* you run it, and state the mode of what you get. A reported failure is more useful to me than a reported success.

- **Stage 2, opt-in only:** if curious, the three links. The figures, not this summary, are the authority. Their later agreement is not counted as corroboration (Fig 2 :40).
