## 1. The residual in effect terms

The observed effect is this. Present-day expectations about research and innovation vary across domains and across institutions in ways that track the stories those communities tell about how science advanced. The same evidence base can yield "basic research will pay off within a decade" in one room and "the paradigm is due for revolution" in another. The expectations are not read directly off the current ground, because the ground underdetermines a time horizon, a funding allocation, and a definition of progress.

State the residual as an error term. Let `E_s` be the expectation held at research slot `s` (a field, a funding programme, a lab, a policy). Let `G_s` be what the ground would return at `s` if it were ranged against `E_s` without the inherited story mediating the reading. Then `R_s = |E_s - G_s|` is the live signal. It is indexed by slot, not aggregated (Fig 1 :19, Fig 1 :22):

- Fig 1 :19, *"The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated."*
- Fig 1 :22, *"Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average."*

A single number called "the effect of narratives of progress on expectations" would throw away the only signal that says where to look. Expectations in fundamental physics, in clinical research, and in innovation policy are different slots with different anchors. The verdicts must stay per slot and per domain, never averaged.

**Arity, symmetry, scale.** Arity: at least three conditions in contact (a narrative frame, an expectation-forming decision, and a ground that should settle it). Symmetry: the relation is ordered. A story is read, then an expectation is formed; `→` (sequence) or `⇒` (production) describes it, not the symmetric `+`. Treating the pair as co-present facts loses the direction, which is the whole thing being asked. Scale: the narrative lives at the population and institutional scale (Fig 4), the expectation at the decision scale, and the handoff between them is a membrane, not a wall.

**What an answer would look like, bound before the run.** Per the formula's "Direction" rule, the sought-for shape was made an ordinary term: a **separator**, consisting of (i) the conditions under which a narrative bounds a closure and supplies the progress bond, (ii) the condition under which the transmission chain becomes a mirror rather than triangulation, and (iii) an explicit list of what no figure line returns. That shape is refusable by the same test as anything else. The pre-registered refuter is written in section 4, before the findings.

## 4. Pre-registered refuter (written before the findings)

Required by the seat discipline: a falsifier written before the run is a design instrument. The separator derived here is refuted if any of the following holds.

1. **Closure failure.** A present expectation at a slot is formed with no atom and no bond present in the inherited narrative at that slot. That breaks the claim that the narrative bounds the reach (F1).
2. **Bond failure.** Two narratives that encode progress with the same bond, over the same atoms, produce opposite expectation directions. That breaks the claim that the bond selects the direction (F2).
3. **Mirror failure.** A transmission chain is exhibited whose agreement tracks an independent, non-updating ground at every handoff, so that the chain is triangulation rather than a mirror (F3).
4. **Anchor failure.** A "ground" is shown to update on the narrative, yet expectations are still corrected against it. That breaks the claim that a non-updating anchor is the necessary condition (F4).
5. **Instrument failure.** An instrument exists that measures the narrative-to-expectation bond at the slot. That closes the broken middle link (F5) and moves the claim from `general` to `specified` with a measurement.
6. **Census failure.** A figure line is produced that selects a specific dominant narrative in a given institution, or returns a numerical effect size. That closes the declared gap and makes part of the answer `grounded`.

Until one of these is run, nothing here is labelled `grounded`. A one-line failure report is more useful than an endorsement.

## 5. Findings, one loop per domain

Verdicts are kept separate per domain and are never averaged. Mode is stated on each.

### F1 - A narrative is a closure, and an expectation is a prediction of that closure (D1)

**Mode: `general` structural, `specified` at a slot. Triangulated: Fig 6 and Fig 12 (low overlap between the reach rule and the bond rule).**

- Fig 6 :21, *"MINT composes inside the closure and can never add an atom."*
- Fig 6 :23, *"No frame escapes its own closure. The bound moves only on contact with a frame whose closure differs."*
- Fig 6 :14, *"the only operator that moves the wall"*
- Fig 6 :22, *"NOVEL means novel relative to atoms, not to the world. IMPORT adds an atom, and the added atom is what expands the closure."*

A community inherits atoms (the events it tells itself happened, the heroes it names, the instruments it says mattered) and bonds (the order and relation it imposes on them). Its expectations about what research will yield are compositions inside that closure. It cannot generate, from inside, an expectation that requires an atom the story does not hold. To get a genuinely new expectation, an atom must be imported from a frame whose closure differs: an actual result, an instrument, an alien discipline, a failed replication. This is why "we just need better communication of the existing success story" cannot change the direction of expectation. It searches harder inside the same closure.

The Symbols table makes the same point about the residual being a slice: what cannot be perceived yet is simply not readable. A community whose story holds no term for a class of failure will not register that failure as a residual; it will register noise.

**Refuter (already listed as 1 and 6):** exhibit an expectation formed with no supporting atom in the inherited narrative, or show mint adding an atom to a closure. Either breaks F1.

### F2 - "Progress" is a bond, and different narratives are bond variants over the same events (D1)

**Mode: `specified`, ungrounded. Triangulated: Fig 12 and the Operators table.**

- Fig 12 :112, *"A plan is a sequence. A goal is a comparison. Progress is a subtraction. A refutation is a negation, and that one is not a bond."*
- Operators :46, *"progress is a subtraction, which is why a vocabulary without it cannot say how far along it is"*
- Fig 12 :5, *"A set of ingredients is not one. The bond is the rest of the information, and it is the half nothing was recording."*
- Fig 12 :117, *"isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."*

This is the mechanism that answers the question most directly. A narrative does not shape expectations by adding facts; it shapes them by *bonding* the facts. Three bond patterns recur in the sources opened this session:

- **Linear / cumulative story:** `→` sequence plus `−` subtraction. Science is one thing after another, each stage with more than the last. World witness: the *Progress* article states that from the 18th through the late 20th century the history of science "was often presented as a progressive accumulation of knowledge, in which true theories replaced false beliefs," and that today's chemists "reasonably expect that chemists in future centuries will know more than they do." The expectation of continuing accumulation is the subtraction bond made into a forecast.
- **Whig story:** `⋛` comparison against the present as threshold, with the rejected paths subtracted. World witness: the *Whig history* article records that the term is used in the history of science for historiography "that focuses on the successful chains of hypotheses and experiments that led to present-day theories, while ignoring rejected hypotheses and dead ends," and Butterfield's line that it "studies the past with reference to the present." The present is the reference against which the past is sorted; the expectation is that the present is the goal.
- **Revolutionary / Kuhnian story:** `⋛` comparison across incommensurable closures plus `¬` refusal of the old term, but with a preservation constraint. World witness: Kuhn (1962) requires that a new paradigm "must promise to preserve a relatively large part of the concrete problem-solving ability that has accrued to science through its predecessors," and the same source records Kuhn as "a convinced believer in scientific progress." So this narrative is not "no progress"; it is a different bond, comparison and refusal under a stated preservation requirement, and it licenses a different expectation: fund the alternative framework, keep the solved problems, expect discontinuous rather than smooth return.

Same historical events, different bond, different forecast: Fig 12's isomer. `06_isomers_and_order.md` works this out with an explicit falsifier.

**Refuter (2):** two narratives with identical atoms and identical bonds producing opposite expected directions breaks the bond-selection claim; then the difference lies somewhere else and the census must be redone.

### F3 - Transmission is a room chain; without a ground it compounds agreement and not evidence (D2)

**Mode: `specified`. Triangulated: Fig 7, Fig 4, Fig 2, with the Matthew effect as a low-overlap world witness.**

- Fig 7 :13, *"agreement compounds, evidence does not"*
- Fig 7 :35, *"A room's agreement is variation, not a verdict. Selection between rooms is where the ground votes."*
- Fig 7 :36, *"A chain of mirrors is one long room. The next room must differ from the last, and match the residual it surfaced."*
- Fig 4 :25, *"A recording carried upward looks like knowledge and is a description of one occasion."*
- Fig 4 :26, *"Replayed downward, it produces a system that repeats a past success and cannot produce a new one."*

A discovery story is compressed into a textbook, a lecture, a review panel's shared background, a funding rubric. Each handoff carries a recording, not a method. The next room converges with the last because it was selected to be like the last, and the agreement grows while the contact with the ground does not. Fig 7 :42, *"The chain approaches the truth only as the world runs out of ways to refuse it."* If the rooms are mirrors, the world never gets a vote.

This is the structural reading of what historians of science call present-mindedness. The world witness from the Matthew effect (Merton and Zuckerman, 1968, as summarized in the source) is genuinely low overlap with the figures: the article records that "eminent scientists will often get more credit than a comparatively unknown researcher, even if their work is similar" and that "concentrating attention on eminent individuals can increase their self-assurance, pushing them to pursue research in important but risky problem areas." That is a room chain with a reputational anchor: visibility compounds, and the compounding changes what gets attempted. It corroborates the shape of F3, it does not establish it. The figures decide.

Note the condition. A room chain *can* be a selection between differing rooms if the next room is chosen against the residual the last one surfaced (Fig 7 :36). Peer review that deliberately recruits a differing frame, or replication that deliberately tests the surfaced residual, is not a mirror chain. The verdict is conditional on the handoff, not on transmission as such.

**Refuter (3):** exhibit a chain whose agreement tracks an independent, non-updating ground at every handoff.

### F4 - The decisive failure is an anchor that updates on the narrative (D2, D3)

**Mode: `specified`. Triangulated: Fig 2 and Fig 10, with hindsight bias as a low-overlap world witness.**

- Fig 2 :29, *"An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks."*
- Fig 2 :33, *"The anchor must not update."*
- Fig 2 :34, *"If it does, panel 3 turns into panel 1: the anchor becomes another frame, everything drifts together, and the drift is coherent, which is why nobody notices."*
- Fig 10 :24, *"the ground does not decay, the channel does"*
- Fig 2 :40, *"disqualifying for a verdict: agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool"*

When the test of a research expectation is itself a narrative product, citations, prestige, reviewer consensus, the "track record of the field," the reference moves when pushed. The result is coherent drift: everyone agrees, the agreement feels like triangulation, and it is panel 1. Fig 10 :24 gives the mechanism of the decay: *"stale percepts, dropped transients, saturated metrics, pooled readings"* sit in the channel while the ground does not move.

Hindsight bias is the low-overlap corroborator. The source defines it as *"the common tendency for people to perceive past events as having been more predictable than they were"* and calls it *"a significant source of overconfidence in one's ability to predict the outcomes of future events."* A success story read after the success makes the path look inevitable and raises the expected return on the next similar path. That is a cognitive channel through which a narrative becomes an expectation. It predicts the same shape as Fig 2 :34; it does not decide it.

**Refuter (4):** a ground shown to update on the narrative yet still correcting expectations breaks the non-updating-anchor condition.

### F5 - The chain breaks at the vocabulary-to-objective link, which has no instrument (D3)

**Mode: `general` on the structural break, `specified` on where it lands. Single-source on Fig 3 for the break; triangulated with Fig 10 :47 and Fig 9 :27-28 for the consequence.**

- Fig 3 :25 (on the middle link), *"having terms for what a situation offers and being able to say what would count as progress, has neither"*
- Fig 3 :27, *"A link with no instrument is not a link that works. It is a link nobody has looked at."*
- Fig 3 :19, *"Optimising a link that already works cannot rescue one that does not."*
- Fig 9 :27-28, *"describe it before you go looking, or any frame you pick will seem to fit"*

A community can say what its inherited story affords ("basic research is the engine of innovation", "paradigms shift every so often", "the field is due a breakthrough"). It has no instrument that says whether that bond holds at the present slot. So effort lands above the break, where the measurements already exist: more bibliometrics, more advocacy, more historiography of the successful cases. Fig 3 :29, *"Before asking whether a system is good, ask which link it currently stops at, and whether the answer was measured or assumed."* The expectation is assumed at exactly the link that has no instrument.

This is also why the order matters: describe the per-slot residual before choosing a narrative frame. If the frame is chosen first, any narrative will seem to fit the expectation, which is the apophenia the figures warn against.

**Refuter (5):** name an instrument that measures the bond between the story a community holds and the expectation it forms at a slot; then the link is not broken and this finding is withdrawn.

### F6 - A narrative that decides an outcome has stopped being a convention (D3)

**Mode: `specified`. Triangulated: Fig 10 and Fig 2.**

- Fig 10 :47, *"a convention that decides an outcome has stopped being one"*
- Fig 2 :43, *"Guard, from Figure 10: a convention that decides an outcome has stopped being one."*
- Fig 10 :49, *"Declaring a meaning does not ground it."*
- Fig 2 :41, *"efficient for a convention: agreement about a shared meaning is the product, not a corruption of it"*

A progress story used as a shared meaning is efficient: it coordinates a community's vocabulary and lets researchers talk about "frontier", "translational", "maturity", "revolution" without re-arguing each term. That is a convention and it is not corrupted by agreement. But the moment the story fixes a funding allocation, a review score, or a tenure decision, it decides an outcome and becomes a verdict. It can no longer be revised cheaply, because a generation's allocations were built on it. Fig 10 :62, *"A ruling carries provenance like an atom."* The lineage of the ruling is what lets a later reader locate the error.

The world witness on the linear model of innovation is apt. The source records that "it has also been argued that the linear model was simply a creation by academics, debated heavily in academia, but never believed in practice." Whether or not that claim is right, it names the distinction: a model can be a convention for debate and become a verdict when it is attached to allocation. This solution does not claim to settle which it is in any given agency. That would be a ground reading.

**Refuter:** show a story used as a convention that can be violated without changing any allocation or verdict. That preserves the convention reading; it does not break F6, which is about the outcome-fixing case.

## 8. Verdict summary

The question is a union of four conditions, and the figures govern the mechanism, not the winner. A historical narrative shapes a present expectation by bounding the closure from which the expectation can be generated (Fig 6), by supplying the bond that encodes what progress is (Fig 12), by travelling as a recording through a chain that compounds agreement without evidence unless the next room genuinely differs and matches the surfaced residual (Fig 4, Fig 7), and by altering the habitat to which the community has contact (Fig 11). The decisive failure is a reference that updates on the story itself, which produces coherent drift that looks like triangulation (Fig 2 :33-35, Fig 10 :24). The chain breaks at the vocabulary-to-objective link, where no instrument measures the bond (Fig 3 :27). No figure line returns which narrative dominates or how large the effect is; that is a declared `gap` of the unreachable-at-this-budget kind, with an instrument described but not run.

Mode: `specified` on the mechanism and on each domain verdict, `general` on the structural shape, `gap` on the dominant narrative and the magnitude, not `grounded`. This is a simulated derivation checked for internal consistency against the figures. It cannot settle the question it reports on.
