# The Tether's answer: mutualistic network response to partner decline

**Mode: `specified` on the per-domain verdicts, `general` on the structural shape, not `grounded`.** Simulated derivation checked for internal consistency against the figures (`05_what_shape_means.md` section 7), not a ground reading.

## 1. The residual it answers

A partner declines or changes behavior; the monitoring frame predicted it would not. R is indexed per partner and per function. The question asks what the network does next. The answer is not one response with a magnitude: the decline is a union compound of service loss, competitive release, behavioral signaling and altered contacts, with its own loop and its own ground (Fig 9 :54-57).

## 2. The shape, per domain

### D1, the pairwise mutualism slot

- The same actor's sign is relative to the residual, not carried by it. Fig 11 :50: "The same actor is a benefactor to one residual and a malfactor to another, so mutualism and parasitism are relations rather than kinds." So a partner that facilitated one function can compete for another after the decline. Verdict: `specified`, triangulated at the compound level by Fig 9, single-source for the valence line itself (Fig 11 only), with world corroboration from Bain 2022.
- The answer at this slot is not "robust" or "collapsed". It is: which service stopped, which partner took it over or failed to, and what measurement shows the function was or was not maintained.

### D2, the habitat and the network

- The response propagates by contact. Fig 11 :52: "And contact cascades. Scoping the habitat to direct contact is how DDT reached the eagles". The habitat must be enumerated outward until the cascade stops mattering (Fig 11 :43-44). A first-order partner list is not the answer.
- Rebonding is composition over partners already in the habitat and season; it adds no atom. Fig 11 :61: "The seat's power is asymmetric. Introduction is available; exclusion is not." Fig 6 :22: "IMPORT adds an atom, and the added atom is what expands the closure." Import remains a separate question with a source gate and shadow test. Verdict: `specified`, triangulated (Fig 11 plus Fig 6 and Fig 13).
- Over a fixed species set, the before and after arrangements are Fig 12 :117 isomers, same atoms, different bond, different substance. Species turnover is not an isomer; it changes the atom set. See `06_isomers_and_order.md`.

### D3, the observation and management frame

- Structure metrics are filters, not witnesses. Fig 9 :60: "Use filters for the budget and witnesses for the verdict. Never let a filter hand you a verdict." Nestedness, connectance and modularity tell you where to sample. They do not tell you whether the function survived.
- Only a non-updating anchor settles it. Fig 2 :29: "An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks." Fig 10 :24: "the ground does not decay, the channel does". Fig 7 :12-13: "same closure, handed along; agreement compounds, evidence does not". Agreement among models that read the same interaction matrix is one evidence pool talking, not corroboration. Verdict: `general` on the filter rule, `specified` on the application, triangulated (Fig 9 plus Fig 2 and Fig 7).

Verdicts are kept separate per domain and never averaged into a single network-level score.

## 3. What it explicitly does not say

- Not a universal collapse threshold, critical decline fraction, or optimal number of alternative partners. No figure line returns one; that is a declared `gap`.
- Not that nestedness or connectance causes robustness. The world literature conflicts on that point (Memmott et al. 2004 versus Maia et al. 2021 and Staniczenko et al. 2013), and the conflict was returned to the census, not used to flip a verdict.
- Not that mutualistic services are unreal or that all compensation fails. "Mutualism and parasitism are relations rather than kinds" means valence is per residual, not that visitation and pollen transfer are imaginary.
- Not that rebonding imports a new species.
- Not a general lesson about all ecological networks. The derivation is specific to networks where the currency is a service and the anchor is persistence of a function.
- Not `grounded`. No field measurement was taken in this session.

## 4. What would settle it: the ground

A per-slot removal instrument, not a further argument. *The Hard Problem is About Source* (opened this session): an instrument that reads green "cannot comment on the source"; the spectrometer resolved it. Here the discriminating questions are:

1. **Source test:** after the decline, is the stable visitation count produced by the same species switching partners, by different species replacing them, or by abundance effects that leave the count flat while pollen deposition falls? Noreika et al. 2019 show that most turnover is species and link turnover, not true rewiring; Leimberger et al. 2023 show mobility and energy-saving adaptations are live alternatives. A visitation-only instrument cannot separate these.
2. **Cascade test:** are there second- and third-order effects on partners not directly connected to the declined species, measured per slot? Fig 11 :52 makes this the decisive test of the contact claim.
3. **Objective test:** what counts as the function being maintained, stated before the model (Fig 3 :26-27)? Without this, a stable network metric can coexist with a lost function.
4. **Hysteresis test:** does removing the stressor restore the network, or does it stay in the low-function state? Baruah and Wittmann 2024 found 94 percent of 115 networks showed strong hysteresis and that perturbing one high-degree species could resurrect them.
5. **Standing falsifiers from `01` section 4:** clean partition of conditions; strictly first-order cascades; invariant partner valence; rewiring without contact; a structure metric that is a witness; a one-off permanent fix.

## 5. The honest stop

**Unreached, not unreachable.** The network metrics in common use are a visible choice of compression axes, not nature's own list (the "shape, not a name" correction in `05`). Another instrument could return the source that a presence-absence matrix cannot. Fig 8 :39: "So a search that finds nothing is never a proof of absence." The derivation stops at the limit of what the available instruments distinguish and records the lineage so the stop is checkable. "Indiscernibility is not identity."

## 6. One-paragraph version for outreach

> The Tether does not return a robustness score for mutualistic networks. It returns the shape a checkable answer must have. A partner's decline is a union of overlapping conditions, not one response, so the residual stays indexed per partner and per function. The response propagates by contact, so the habitat is enumerated outward until the cascade stops mattering, not stopped at direct neighbors. "Mutualist" is a relation relative to the residual, so a species that helped one function can compete for another after the change. Partners switching to new partners is composition over species already in the habitat, not the arrival of a new species, and a managed network stays permanent work because a seat can introduce actors but cannot subtract the habitat. Nestedness and connectance are filters for where to look, not witnesses for whether the function survived. The framework refuses to name a collapse threshold or a universal responder; those need a field instrument that can distinguish switching from replacement. What it offers is the shape, five refuters written in advance, and the tests that would close it.
