# Rewiring versus turnover: isomers, bond order and loop signs

**Mode: `specified`.** This is a simulated derivation checked against the figures, not a ground reading (see `05_what_shape_means.md` section 7).

## RESIDUAL

After a partner declines, the same network can look stable on a presence-absence matrix for two categorically different reasons: the same species re-bonded to different partners (interaction rewiring), or different species arrived and left (species turnover). Section F2 of `01` says the response propagates by contact, and section F4 says rebonding is composition over held contact. This file derives what kind of thing each response is, and what would make the distinction fail.

## DOMAIN CENSUS

Atoms held: the species present in the habitat and time window, plus conditions (season, resource availability). Bonds held: the seven, including `+` conjunction, `→` sequence, `⇒` production, `−` subtraction. No new primitive is available without import. The currency of the description is the bond, not the atom list, because Fig 12 :5 says "A set of ingredients is not one. The bond is the rest of the information, and it is the half nothing was recording."

## DERIVATION

### Q1: Are rewiring and species turnover isomers?

- Fig 12 :117: "isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."
- Fig 12 :94: "The arrangement is the substance."
- **Rewiring** holds the species set fixed and changes which pairs interact. Same atoms, different bonds. That is an isomer under Fig 12 :117.
- **Species turnover** changes the species set. Different atoms. It is not an isomer; it is a different molecule, or a composition plus an import or subtraction (Fig 6 :22: "IMPORT adds an atom, and the added atom is what expands the closure").
- This is not a terminological nicety. Noreika et al. 2019 found that most interaction turnover among networks came from species and link differences, with only about 17 percent of interactions truly rewired; Bain 2022 found experimental removal changed structure mostly through species turnover rather than rewiring. If a study pools the two into one "interaction turnover" number, it adds two different substances into one ingredient list, which is the flat-formula collapse Fig 12 names.
- **Falsifier, written before the run:** if turnover can be rewritten as a rebonding over the same atom set (the incoming species was already a held atom in the habitat and time window), the distinction collapses and the isomer claim fails. The test is empirical: enumerate the metaweb before and after, and ask whether the new partner was already present.

### Q2: What order is the response?

- Fig 12 :111-112: "A vocabulary without bonds can name a state and cannot name a route to one." "A plan is a sequence. A goal is a comparison. Progress is a subtraction."
- The response has at least three ordered bonds:
  1. `partner declines → alternative partner is visited` (sequence, `→`).
  2. `visitation ⇒ pollen deposited and fruit set` (production, `⇒`): the second operand did not exist before the first fired.
  3. `function after − function before` (subtraction, `−`): the comparison that says whether the function was maintained.
- Fig 12 :23 states what symmetric bonding would do: "this bond is symmetric, so the four are one term". Treating the mutualistic interaction as a plain `+` makes the order unrecoverable, and a frame that records only `A + B` cannot tell the route from the state. The Operators table (opened this session) gives the test for `→`: "swap them. Does it become a different thing? Yes."
- **Falsifier:** if the interaction can be written as a symmetric `+` with no order and still predict the observed sequence of visitation, pollen deposition and fruit set, the order claim fails.

### Q3: Catalyst, cycle and functional group

- Fig 12 :119: "catalyst. A part that survives its own use. Spent and regenerating parts behave differently across uses, and nothing records which a term is." A generalist pollinator that visits the same set of plants across many seasons is a catalyst in this sense. A pollinator whose populations are depleted by the decline is a spent part. A network dataset that records only presence and absence cannot tell them apart, so it cannot tell whether compensation is sustainable.
- Fig 12 :121: "cycle. The output re-enters as input. The sign of the loop decides whether it closes, and a chain cannot close by construction." Plant reproduction supplies the resource that maintains the pollinator, which supplies pollination. The sign of the loop decides whether the mutualism holds or turns into exploitation. The audit's F3 (valence relative to the residual) is the ecological reading of this: when the residual changes, the same loop can change sign.
- Fig 12 :124: "functional group. A substructure whose presence fixes the outcome. The group says what kind; the qualifier says which member." Generalist partners form a functional group. This dissolves an apparent strain with Fig 11 :51: "No standing list of good actors survives a change of residual." Fig 12 :124 says which *kind* of part fixes the outcome; Fig 11 :51 says no *member* keeps its standing across residuals. Different objects, no contradiction.
- **Falsifier:** a case where a generalist partner keeps its identity and its positive effect after the residual changes in a way that makes the service irrelevant. That would show the functional group, not the residual, fixes the outcome, and F3 would need re-census.

### Q4: Two currencies, and why one number is not enough

- Fig 12 :125-127: "A composition costs in two currencies, and they do not add." "Bits price the description. Actions price finding out whether it holds."
- A network matrix is cheap to describe and expensive to settle: a matrix can be downloaded from Web of Life in minutes, while establishing whether a partner switch actually maintained pollen deposition takes a field season. Conversely, a single decisive removal experiment can refute a complex robustness narrative in one action. Summing matrix complexity and field effort into one "robustness score" is the error Fig 12 names, not the fix.
- **Falsifier:** a study that prices both currencies in one unit and shows the sum predicts the response without loss. Then the two-currency claim is false.

## CONCLUSION

Over a fixed species set, rewiring is an isomer of the pre-decline arrangement: same atoms, different bonds, different substance. Species turnover is not an isomer; it changes the atom set and asks an import question, which needs a source gate and shadow test. The response is an ordered route (`→`, `⇒`, `−`), not a symmetric co-occurrence, and its parts divide into catalysts, spent parts and cycles whose loop sign is relative to the residual. Generalist partners form a functional group, which fixes the kind of part and not the standing of any member. All of this is `specified` and ungrounded: no field measurement was taken, and the falsifiers above are written before the run.
