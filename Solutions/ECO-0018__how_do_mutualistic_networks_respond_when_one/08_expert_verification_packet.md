# Expert verification packet: the Tether on mutualistic network response

**What this is:** a claim in a form a mutualistic-network ecologist can check. Mode, derivation and refuters are stated before the conclusion, so a reader knows what to look for and what would settle it.

**Mode: `specified` on the sub-claims, `general` on the structural shape, not `grounded`.** No field measurement is claimed. This is a shape and a route, not a result. The whole packet is a simulated derivation checked for internal consistency against the figures, not a ground reading.

## 1. The claim, in one paragraph

How a mutualistic network responds to one partner's decline is a union of overlapping conditions, not one response with a magnitude. The residual stays indexed per partner and per function. The response propagates by contact, so the habitat is enumerated outward from the declined partner until the cascade stops mattering. A species' sign is relative to the residual: a facilitator of one function can become a competitor for another. Surviving partners switching partners is composition over species already present in the habitat and season; it adds no new species. Nestedness, connectance and modularity are filters for where to sample, never witnesses for whether the function survived. The framework returns this shape, five refuters written in advance, and the instruments that would close it. It does not return a universal threshold or name a universal responder.

## 2. Three checkable sub-claims

### C1: union compound, indexed per slot (checkable by reading Fig 9 and Fig 11)

- **Statement:** the response conditions overlap; they are not a partition, and pooling them into one "network robustness" score destroys the signal about where to look.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 9 :54 "And when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again." Fig 9 :57 "a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either". Fig 1 :19 "The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated." Fig 11 :50 "The same actor is a benefactor to one residual and a malfactor to another, so mutualism and parasitism are relations rather than kinds."
- **What to check:** in a removal series, do the conditions (service loss, competitive release, turnover, behavioral change) act on disjoint species sets with one mechanism, or do they overlap at the same slots with different signs? If the former, a partition suffices and C1 is wrong.
- **Refuter:** replicated removals in which the conditions partition cleanly, with one mechanism and no partner-specific residual. Also: a single structure metric that predicts every affected slot with no residual about which partner or which function.

### C2: contact cascade and residual-relative valence (checkable by reading Fig 11)

- **Statement:** effects propagate beyond direct contact, and the sign of an actor's effect is not carried by the actor.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 11 :52 "And contact cascades. Scoping the habitat to direct contact is how DDT reached the eagles". Fig 11 :43 "The habitat is enumerated, never composed." Fig 11 :44 "List everything in contact with the residual, then what is in contact with those, and outward until the cascade stops mattering." Fig 11 :49 "An actor's valence is relative to the residual, not a property it carries." Fig 11 :51 "No standing list of good actors survives a change of residual."
- **What to check:** in a replicated removal, are there second- or third-order effects on partners not directly connected to the declined species? Does a partner that helped one measured function become neutral or harmful for another after the decline? Bain 2022 observed exactly this kind of sign difference between two co-flowering plants after removal.
- **Refuter:** all effects strictly first order across more than one habitat; or a partner retaining the same sign of effect on all counterparts across a change of residual, with the function measured at the ground.

### C3: rebonding is composition over held contact; metrics are filters (checkable by reading Fig 11, Fig 6 and Fig 9)

- **Statement:** partner switching composes over species and conditions already in the habitat and time window; it is not an import of a new atom. A seat can introduce an actor but cannot subtract the habitat. Structure metrics budget the search; they do not decide the verdict.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 11 :61 "The seat's power is asymmetric. Introduction is available; exclusion is not." Fig 11 :66 "Isolation is not removal of the habitat. It is substitution of one habitat for another." Fig 6 :22 "NOVEL means novel relative to atoms, not to the world. IMPORT adds an atom, and the added atom is what expands the closure." Fig 9 :60 "Use filters for the budget and witnesses for the verdict. Never let a filter hand you a verdict." Fig 2 :29 "An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks."
- **What to check:** are the replacement partners already in the local metaweb and the seasonal window? Noreika et al. 2019 found only about 17 percent of interactions truly rewired, with most turnover due to species and link differences. Does a change in nestedness coincide with an independently measured change in pollen deposition or recruitment, or only with a change in the matrix?
- **Refuter:** a viable new interaction with a species absent from the habitat and outside its overlap window, with no source gate owed; or a one-off intervention that permanently restores the function with no further contact work, with the ground rather than the manager's report settling it.

## 3. What is explicitly not claimed

- Not a universal collapse threshold, critical decline fraction, optimal partner number, or named universal responder. That is a declared `gap` (Fig 9 :32 "you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself"; Fig 13 :134 "Unreachable is a verdict here, not a silence").
- Not that nestedness or connectance causes robustness. The world literature conflicts and the conflict was returned to the census.
- Not that mutualistic services are unreal. The claim is about the valence of an actor relative to a residual, not about the reality of pollen transfer.
- Not that rebonding adds a species to the world.
- Not `grounded`. Nothing here is asserted as settled by experiment.
- Not a general theory of all ecological networks. The currency here is a service and the anchor is persistence of a function.

## 5. What would settle it

A per-slot source-discriminating field instrument, not further argument.

1. **Source test:** same species switching partners, different species replacing them, or abundance effects that keep visit counts flat while pollen deposition falls? A visitation-only reading cannot comment on the source.
2. **Cascade test:** second- and third-order effects on partners not directly connected to the declined species.
3. **Objective test:** state what counts as the function being maintained before fitting a model (Fig 3 :26-27).
4. **Hysteresis test:** does removing the stressor restore function? Baruah and Wittmann 2024 found strong hysteresis in 94 percent of 115 networks.
5. **The five standing refuters** in `01` section 4 and repeated in C1 to C3 above.

5. Send the refutation. A failure is more useful than an endorsement.
