## QUESTION

Decide, in effect terms, what the Tether says follows when one partner of a mutualistic network declines or changes behavior. Option A: return a single network-level response magnitude (robust or fragile, with a critical decline fraction). Option B: split the event into its overlapping conditions, index the residual per partner and per function, and return the shape a checkable answer must have. What each leads to: A hides the slot that failed inside an average and lets a structure metric hand you a verdict; B returns per-slot verdicts, an enumerated contact cascade, five refuters written in advance, and an open source slot until a field instrument discriminates. The question is a union compound, so A is a category error, not a weaker version of B.

## DOMAIN CENSUS

R by arity, symmetry and scale, then one line per domain with actors / currency / anchor, partition or union, and order by dependency.

- **R (the residual).** A monitoring frame predicted the partner's abundance, behavior and interaction set would remain in range; the field returned a decline or a behavior change. Arity: at least three overlapping conditions (service loss, competitive release, behavioral signaling, altered contacts), so a **union compound**, not one hard question (Fig 9 :54-57). Symmetry: asymmetric, since the effect of A's decline on B differs from B's on A, so a pooled symmetric strength cannot choose the arrow. Scale: first order at direct contact, second and third order along the cascade (Fig 11 :52).
- **Domain 1, the pairwise mutualism slot.** Actors: the declining partner and its direct partners. Currency: the exchanged service, visitation, pollen transfer, dispersal, nectar. Anchor: measured pollen deposition, fruit or seed set at that slot; nameable, so this is a domain. Question type: union at the slot (F3).
- **Domain 2, the habitat and network.** Actors: every species in contact directly and indirectly, plus conditions (light, water, season, terrain). Currency: persistence, abundance, functional contribution. Anchor: persistence of species and of the function in the ground; nameable. Two modes: unmaintained (no seat, selection ran) and maintained (a seat can introduce actors but not subtract habitat). Question type: union with a cascade (F1, F2, F4).
- **Domain 3, the observation and management frame.** Actors: researchers, monitoring instruments, interventions. Currency: predictions, residuals, agreement. Anchor: field data, which must not update on the frame's own report. Question type: filter versus witness and anchor test (F5, F6).
- **Order by dependency.** Perceive which partner changed; acquire terms for what the remaining interactions afford; state what counts as the function maintained; then plan an intervention; then carry what worked (Fig 3 :26-27). The chain usually stops between vocabulary and objective, where there is no instrument.
- **Partition or union.** Union. A single lever clears the region where only one condition holds and leaves the intersections standing (Fig 9 :55-56).

Strain test applied: Fig 13's network structure governs what a network is (influence travels), not how it responds; re-census assigns the dynamics to Fig 11. Fig 12's functional group says which kind of part fixes the outcome, while Fig 11 :51 says no member keeps standing across residuals. Different objects, no figure contradiction. Strains are listed in `01` section 7.

## VERDICT (from the figures)

The question is a union compound, so there is no single network-level response magnitude. The figure-derived shape is: index the residual per partner and per function; enumerate the habitat outward from the declined partner until the cascade stops mattering; treat an actor's sign as relative to the residual rather than carried by the actor; treat partner switching as composition over held contact rather than an import of a new species; and keep structure metrics as filters for the budget, never witnesses for the verdict. In maintained systems the response depends on whether a seat keeps a non-updating ground reachable, because the ground does not decay while the channel does. No figure line returns a collapse threshold, a critical decline fraction, or the identity of a universal responder; that is a declared `gap`, which is a verdict and not silence. Per-domain verdicts: D1 `specified` and single-source on the valence lines, D2 `specified` and triangulated, D3 `specified` on the application and `general` on the filter rule and triangulated. The whole result is `specified`, ungrounded, candidate.

- **cost if wrong:** bounded and locatable. The packet states mode, three checkable sub-claims and refuters before the conclusion, so an error is findable rather than invisible. The real cost is spending the independence of outside readers by sending a framework summary instead of a checkable claim, which Fig 2 :42 prices: "Buy independence in proportion to the cost of being wrong." That cost is avoidable by leading with the shape claim and the refuter.
- **undoable:** yes. The shape is separable from any particular binding. A refutation of "rewiring compensated the loss" removes that binding without touching the per-slot union framing or the contact-cascade rule. A sent claim can be superseded by a corrected packet, and no primitive has to be withdrawn.
- **reversed by:** a ground reading, not agreement. A source-discriminating field instrument showing whether persistence came from switching, replacement, or abundance effects would name the source; a replicated removal showing cleanly partitioned conditions, strictly first-order cascades or invariant partner valence would break F1 to F3; a structure metric that predicts every slot with no residual would break F5; a one-off permanent restoration settled by the ground would break F6. Until one of these fires, the verdict stands as `candidate`.
