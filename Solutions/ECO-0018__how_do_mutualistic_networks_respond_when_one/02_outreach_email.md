# Outreach draft: validate the checkable claim first, then decide about the framework

**Use:** one to one, or as a cover note attached to a submission through the public venues in `03_public_contact_points.csv`. **Not** for bulk send.

**Hard precondition:** do not send until the three sub-claims in `08_expert_verification_packet.md` are checked against the figures on your own machine. The claim must survive validation or the two-stage offer collapses on contact.

---

**Subject:** One checkable claim about mutualistic network response, and the observation that would refute it

> Hi [name],
>
> I have one specific output I would like you to check against what you already know about mutualistic networks. I am not asking you to read any theory first, and I am not asking you to agree with it. If the output does not survive, there is nothing else to look at.
>
> **The output (`specified`, not `grounded`).** Declining or changing one partner does not produce one response with a magnitude. It produces a union of overlapping conditions, so the residual has to be indexed per partner and per function, never averaged into a single "network robustness" score. Three things follow that are checkable:
>
> 1. The response propagates by contact. A first-order list of who visited the declining partner is not yet the answer; the habitat has to be enumerated outward until the cascade stops mattering.
> 2. "Mutualist" is a relation relative to the question, not a standing kind. After a decline, a species that helped one function can compete for another. No fixed list of good responders survives the change of residual.
> 3. Surviving partners switching to other partners is composition over species already in the habitat and the season. It is not the arrival of a new species from outside. The seat can introduce an actor; it cannot subtract the habitat, so a managed network is permanent work.
>
> **What I am not claiming:** that nestedness or connectance determines the outcome. Those are filters for where to look and how much effort to spend, not witnesses for whether the function survived. I am not claiming a universal collapse threshold, a critical decline fraction, or a named species that always compensates. The framework returns a shape, not a name; those numbers need a field measurement in a named habitat.
>
> **What would refute it:**
> 1. A replicated set of removals in which the conditions partition cleanly, with one mechanism and no partner-specific residual.
> 2. All effects strictly first order, with no cascade three steps out, across more than one habitat.
> 3. A partner keeping the same sign of effect on all counterparts across a change of residual.
> 4. A viable new interaction with a species absent from the habitat and outside its temporal overlap window.
> 5. A one-off intervention that permanently restores the function with no further contact work, measured at the ground rather than in a manager's report.
>
> I wrote these down before sending this. If any one holds, the shape is wrong and I will retract it. A one-line "this fails because..." is more useful to me than agreement. If you would rather not engage, no reply is needed.
>
> **If it survives, and only then:** here are the sources. The figures are the authority, not my summary of them.
> - Verbatim figure text with line numbers (local extract in the project): `FRAMEWORK/reference/03-FIGURES_TEXT.md`. The governing passages are Fig 11 :43-44, :49-51, :52, :61, :66, :73; Fig 9 :54-57, :60; Fig 6 :22-23.
> - Web of Life, the public ecological networks database the field actually uses: https://www.web-of-life.es/
> - Baruah and Wittmann 2024, "Reviving collapsed plant-pollinator networks from a single species", PLOS Biology: https://journals.plos.org/plosbiology/article?id=10.1371/journal.pbio.3002826
> - Noreika et al. 2019, "Pollinator foraging flexibility mediates rapid plant-pollinator network restoration", Scientific Reports: https://www.nature.com/articles/s41598-019-51912-4
>
> **If you want to use the machinery:** the eight-step loop is in `FRAMEWORK/figures/THE_FORMULA.md`. Run it on a network you know well and can check against field data. Write down what would refute your result *before* you run it, and label the mode of what you get. A reported failure is more useful to me than a reported success.
>
> Thanks,
> [name]

## Why this version is ordered this way

| the move | figure-grounded reason |
|---|---|
| Output and refuter first, framework never in stage 1 | Fig 10 :52: "Bring a characterised residual, never the source." A recipient who has absorbed the framework before being asked for a verdict has joined the frame being checked. |
| Do not ask for agreement | Fig 2 :35: "Alignment is triangulation, not negotiation. A mirror cannot correct you." |
| Mode stated on the claim | `THE_FORMULA.md`: "Mode. general · specified · grounded. State which." |
| Pre-registered refuter | "a falsifier written before the run is a design instrument, not only an honesty device." |
| Union, not one robustness score | Fig 9 :54 and Fig 1 :19. A single number throws away the signal that says where to look. |
| No claim about nestedness as a witness | Fig 9 :60: "Use filters for the budget and witnesses for the verdict." |
| The framework returns a shape, not a name | Fig 9 :32: "you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself." |
| No request for prestige or trust | *The Recursive Self-Improvement Trap*: "Prestige can fund an action. It can give an action a budget to be tried. But it cannot settle an action. Only the ground can settle it." |
| Offer the formula as a method only at stage 3 | Fig 4 :22: "Only methods cross up. Only head starts come down." |
| Ask users to write their own refuter | `THE_FORMULA.md` "Direction": a frame cannot check the direction of its own question. |

**Stage 2 is opt-in.** Once a recipient has read the theory, their agreement stops being evidence. Their verdict on stage 1 must be recorded before any framework material is shown, and later agreement is never pooled as corroboration (Fig 2 :40).
