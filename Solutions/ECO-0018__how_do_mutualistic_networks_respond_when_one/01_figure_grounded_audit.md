## 0. The residual, stated in effect terms

A monitoring frame predicts that a partner's abundance, behavior and set of interaction partners will remain within the range already observed. Field observation returns something else: the partner declines, or changes when and how it interacts. R is the gap between that prediction and that observation.

Stated as an effect, not a theory: **something a network relied on stopped arriving, and we need to know what the network does next.** The candidate responses are not one response with different strengths. They are categorically different events: surviving partners switch to other partners (interaction rewiring); the identities of the partners present change (species turnover); a partner that was a benefactor becomes a competitor or a malfactor (valence flip); a feedback loop crosses a threshold and the network moves to a low-function state, sometimes with hysteresis; or the function is simply lost while the species list still looks intact.

What an answer would look like, bound as an ordinary term before the run: an answer is checkable if for a named partner, in a named habitat, at a named time, it says **which slots change, in which order, through which contacts, and what measurement would show the response failed to occur**. A sentence that says only "the network is robust" or "the network collapses" is not an answer, because it names no slot and no instrument. This is the "Direction" discipline from `THE_FORMULA.md`: write down what an answer would look like, bind it, and let it accrue a residual like anything else.

R is indexed per slot, never pooled. Fig 1 :19: "The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated." Fig 1 :22: "Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average." A single "network robustness" number is exactly the aggregate that hides where the failure is.

## 1. Domain census (before choosing any figure line)

### R by arity, symmetry and scale

- **Arity.** One partner declines, but the response has at least three conditions in the same event: the service that stopped, the dependencies of other partners on it, and the behavior change itself as a signal to other species. That is a union compound, not one hard question (Fig 9 :54).
- **Symmetry.** Asymmetric. The effect of partner A's decline on B is not the effect of B's decline on A, and "mutualistic" does not make the effects symmetric. A pooled symmetric interaction strength cannot choose the arrow.
- **Scale.** First order at direct contact, second and third order along the cascade. Fig 11 :52: "And contact cascades. Scoping the habitat to direct contact is how DDT reached the eagles".

### One line per domain: actors / currency / anchor

| domain | actors | currency | anchor (nameable?) |
|---|---|---|---|
| D1, the pairwise mutualism slot | the declining partner and its direct partners | the exchanged service: visitation, pollen transfer, dispersal, nectar, recruitment | yes: measured visitation, pollen deposition, fruit or seed set at that slot |
| D2, the habitat and network | every species in contact directly and indirectly, plus conditions (light, water, season, terrain) | persistence, abundance, functional contribution | yes: persistence of species and of the function in the ground; two modes, unmaintained and maintained |
| D3, the observation and management frame | researchers, monitoring instruments, interventions | predictions, residuals, agreement | yes: field data, which must not update on the frame's own report |

No domain here is admitted without a nameable anchor. D1 and D2 have different anchors (a slot measurement versus persistence of the function), so they are separate domains with separate verdicts, not two readings of one quantity. D3's anchor is the same ground but a different office: it ranges against the ground, it does not negotiate with it (Fig 2 :45).

### Partition or union

**Union.** A partner's decline simultaneously removes a service, releases competitors from competition, changes the behavioral cues read by other species, and alters contacts several steps out. These conditions overlap; they are not a list of separate cases. Fig 9 :55: "A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones." One lever clears the region where only one condition holds and leaves the intersections standing (Fig 9 :56). If the census had returned a partition, a single mechanism could be fitted; it did not.

### Order by dependency (Fig 3)

1. **Perception:** which partner changed, and when (link 1).
2. **Vocabulary:** which interactions exist and what they afford; which alternative partners are even representable (link 2).
3. **Objective:** what counts as "the function was maintained", stated in the frame's own terms (link 3).
4. **Planning:** an intervention, chosen under incomplete information (link 4).
5. **Learn and carry:** name what worked, verify it, carry it to the next habitat (link 5).

The chain usually breaks between 2 and 3. Fig 3 :26: "having terms for what a situation offers and being able to say what would count as progress, has neither, so it attracts the least attention and the fewest instruments." A network dataset can list who visited whom and still have no term for what would count as the function surviving. A reading taken below that break is a reading of nothing (Fig 3 :22-23), so effort spent on elaborate cascade models before the objective is stated lands at a step that cannot use it (Fig 3 :20).

## 3. Findings F1 to F7

### F1: The response is a union compound, not one response with a magnitude

Fig 9 :54: "And when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again." Fig 9 :57: "a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either". Fig 11 :50, a low-overlap figure: "The same actor is a benefactor to one residual and a malfactor to another, so mutualism and parasitism are relations rather than kinds."

The decline event bundles service loss, competitive release, behavioral signaling and altered contacts. Asking "how does the network respond?" as if there were one response is the bundled question the figure tells us to split. Verdict: **triangulated** (Fig 9 and Fig 11, different figures, low overlap). Mode `general` on the union shape, `specified` on the particular conditions in a given habitat.

**Refuter:** a replicated set of removals in which the conditions partition cleanly, that is, disjoint species sets, one mechanism, and one measured outcome that accounts for every affected slot with no residual about which partner or which function. If that holds, a partition suffices and this finding is false.

### F2: The response propagates by contact, and must be enumerated outward, not measured only at direct neighbors

Fig 11 :52: "And contact cascades. Scoping the habitat to direct contact is how DDT reached the eagles". Fig 11 :43: "The habitat is enumerated, never composed." Fig 11 :44: "List everything in contact with the residual, then what is in contact with those, and outward until the cascade stops mattering." Fig 13 :24, low overlap: "that entities relate, and influence travels". Fig 1 :19, low overlap: "The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated."

A first-order enumeration that stops at the declining partner's direct partners is the exact error the DDT example names: the residual that mattered was three steps out. This is why "who visited the plant that declined" is not yet an answer; the answer needs the cascade until it stops mattering, per slot. Verdict: **triangulated** (Fig 11 plus Fig 13 and Fig 1, low overlap). Mode `specified`.

**Refuter:** a controlled removal in which every measured effect on every unmeasured partner is strictly first order, with no second- or third-order effect on any species not directly connected to the declining partner, across more than one habitat. That would dissolve the contact-cascade claim.

**World corroboration (secondary only):** Noreika et al. 2019 found interaction turnover extremely high among networks while only about 17 percent of interactions were truly rewired, so most change came from species and link turnover rather than pairwise rebonding; Bain 2022 found that experimental removal of a generalist plant made networks more specialized, less nested and less robust, and that the change was driven more by species turnover than by rewiring. Both predict propagation by changes in who is present, not only by rebonding of the same pair. Neither was used to choose the figure line.

### F3: "Mutualist" is a relation with residual-relative valence, not a standing kind

Fig 11 :49: "An actor's valence is relative to the residual, not a property it carries." Fig 11 :50: "The same actor is a benefactor to one residual and a malfactor to another, so mutualism and parasitism are relations rather than kinds." Fig 11 :51: "No standing list of good actors survives a change of residual."

When the partner declines, a species that was a facilitator for one function can become a competitor for another. This is not a paradox in the network; it is the frame's rule about valence. The figure census refuses the question's implicit request for a fixed class of "good responders". Verdict: **single-source** on the figure side (both lines are in Fig 11, so this is one figure, not two), with world corroboration from Bain 2022, who observed increased visitation to one co-flowering plant after removal and no change in another, that is, opposite signs of effect at the same removal event. Mode `specified`.

**Refuter:** a replicated experiment in which a partner retains the same sign of effect on all counterparts across a change of residual, with the function measured at the ground rather than inferred from the species list.

### F4: Rewiring is composition over held contact, not an import that adds an atom

Fig 11 :61: "The seat's power is asymmetric. Introduction is available; exclusion is not." Fig 11 :66: "Isolation is not removal of the habitat. It is substitution of one habitat for another." Fig 11 :68: "what you failed to reproduce is invisible until the goal fails"; Fig 11 :69: "what you unintentionally introduced is invisible until it acts". Fig 6 :23, low overlap: "No frame escapes its own closure. The bound moves only on contact with a frame whose closure differs." Fig 6 :22: "NOVEL means novel relative to atoms, not to the world. IMPORT adds an atom, and the added atom is what expands the closure."

A surviving pollinator that switches to a different co-flowering plant is composing over partners already in its habitat. It does not add a new species to the world. So "rewiring increases robustness" is a statement about which bonds are realized, not about an import moving a wall. The seat can introduce an actor it did not author, but it cannot subtract the habitat; managed networks are permanent work (Fig 11 :63: "Which is why a managed ecosystem is permanent work rather than a solved state"). Verdict: **triangulated** (Fig 11 plus Fig 6, low overlap). Mode `specified`.

**Refuter:** a documented case in which a partner forms a viable interaction with a species absent from its habitat and outside its temporal overlap window, with the new partner imported from a closure that differs and the source gate and shadow test supplied. If contact is not required, the contact claim is false.

**World corroboration:** Kaiser-Bunbury et al. 2010 found rewired networks more stable than static ones but also a sigmoidal collapse when the strongest interactor was removed; Vizentin-Bugoni et al. 2020 found rewiring generally increased estimated robustness, most when generalists were lost first; Leimberger et al. 2023 found only modest evidence of behavioral change after experimental removal of an abundant plant, and proposed mobility or energy-saving adaptations as alternative mechanisms. The literature agrees that partner switching helps, but disagrees on how much; that disagreement is kept as evidence, not smoothed into a single effect size.

### F5: Structure metrics are filters for the budget, never witnesses for the verdict

Fig 9 :60: "Use filters for the budget and witnesses for the verdict. Never let a filter hand you a verdict." Fig 2 :29: "An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks."

Nestedness, connectance, modularity and specialization are measured properties of the interaction matrix. They can tell you where to look and how much effort to spend. They cannot tell you which partner failed or whether the function persisted; that needs a ground measurement at the slot. The world literature conflicts sharply here: Memmott et al. 2004 attributed tolerance to pollinator redundancy and nested topology; Maia et al. 2021 and Staniczenko et al. 2013 report that nestedness need not confer robustness and may minimize local stability. The conflict rule sends this back to the census, not to a flipped verdict. The two camps measure different quantities: binary presence-absence topology versus quantitative preferences, and persistence under a removal scenario versus local stability at equilibrium. Verdict: **triangulated** (Fig 9 :60 plus Fig 2 :29, low overlap). Mode `general` on the filter rule.

**Refuter:** a single structure metric that, across networks and habitats, determines the response outcome with no per-slot residual about which partner or which function. Then the metric is a witness and this finding is false.

### F6: In maintained systems, the response depends on whether a seat keeps the ground reachable

Fig 11 :56: "Unmaintained. No maintainer and no live frame in the seat. The habitat is pure contact, nothing selects, and what survives is what the ground kept." Fig 11 :73: "The habitat is not the ground. The habitat is where actors come from and it updates; the ground is what settles, and it does not." Fig 10 :24: "the ground does not decay, the channel does". Fig 2 :29: "An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks."

An unmaintained network has no seat: selection ran, and the survivor set is the answer. A maintained network has a seat that can introduce actors and instruments but never subtract the habitat. Conservation monitoring that decides success from its own report has replaced a non-updating anchor with a frame; that is the coherent drift collapse Fig 2 names. Verdict: **triangulated** (Fig 11 plus Fig 10 and Fig 2, low overlap). Mode `specified`.

**Refuter:** a one-off intervention that permanently restores the function with no further contact work, where the ground (visitation, recruitment, persistence) rather than the manager's report settles it, replicated across habitats.

**World corroboration:** Baruah and Wittmann 2024 found that restoring original conditions rarely revived collapsed plant-pollinator networks because of hysteresis, while perturbing one well-connected species could resurrect them; this is contact work by a seat, not a one-off return to a prior state.

### F7: No figure line returns a universal response magnitude or names a universal responder

This is a declared `gap` of the shape-not-a-name kind, not silence. Fig 9 :32: "you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself". Fig 13 :134: "Unreachable is a verdict here, not a silence". The framework can return the form a checkable answer must have. It cannot return, for all mutualistic networks, the critical decline fraction, the optimal number of alternative partners, or the identity of the species that will compensate. Those require a ground reading at a named habitat. Verdict: **gap**. Mode `general` on the limit.

**Refuter:** a figure line that supplies a numerical robustness threshold or names a species as the universal responder. Until one is produced, the honest report is unreached, not unreachable in principle: an instrumented removal experiment could settle a particular habitat (Fig 8 :39: "So a search that finds nothing is never a proof of absence").

## 4. Pre-registered refuter (written before the conclusion)

Required before outreach: "a falsifier written before the run is a design instrument, not only an honesty device." The shape claim is refuted if any of the following holds:

1. A replicated set of removals shows the response conditions partition cleanly (disjoint species sets, one mechanism, no per-slot residual), breaking F1.
2. All measured effects are strictly first order across more than one habitat, with no cascade three steps out, breaking F2.
3. A partner keeps the same sign of effect on all counterparts across a change of residual, breaking F3.
4. A viable interaction forms with a species absent from the habitat and outside temporal overlap, with no source gate owed, breaking F4.
5. A single structure metric predicts the response outcome with no per-slot residual, breaking F5.
6. A one-off intervention permanently restores the function with no further contact work, with the ground settling it, breaking F6.
7. A figure line appears that returns a universal response magnitude or names the universal responder, breaking F7.

In addition, the **instrument condition**: if a source-discriminating field instrument shows that the apparent compensation came from a different mechanism than the one the shape predicts (for example mobility rather than partner switching, as Leimberger et al. 2023 propose), the particular binding is withdrawn while the shape is re-censused. No part of this folder may be labelled `grounded` until one of these tests is run and survives.

## 5. Recommendation

1. Split the question before searching it. Ask four separate questions: what service stopped, who else was in contact, what the seat can introduce, and what measurement would show the function failed (F1, F2, F6).
2. Enumerate the habitat outward from the declining partner until the cascade stops mattering, and keep the residual indexed per slot (F2). Do not average across partners.
3. Record valence per residual at each slot; do not keep a standing list of "good mutualists" (F3).
4. Treat rewiring as composition over held contact. Ask what is already in the habitat and time window before asking what was imported (F4).
5. Use nestedness, connectance and modularity as filters for where to sample, not as evidence that the function survived (F5).
6. State the objective before the model: what counts as the function being maintained, in the terms of the system being studied (section 1, Fig 3 :26-27).
7. Lead with the shape claim and the refuter. Fig 4 :30: "So take the round trip and measure the gap. Without it you are navigating by dead reckoning: confident about a position nobody checked."
