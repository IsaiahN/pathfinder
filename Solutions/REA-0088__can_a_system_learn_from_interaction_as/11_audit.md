## Independent domain census

I redid the census from the residual line before reading the primary's conclusions, using the WATSON order: R by arity, symmetry and scale; one line per domain with actors, currency and anchor; partition or union; order by dependency.

- **R (arity / symmetry / scale).** The residual is the unranked comparison between acting-and-observing and training on a stored corpus. Arity: two regimes plus a comparison operator. The operator is a threshold relation between two quantities, so the question asks for one number from two operands. Symmetry: the operands are not the same kind of quantity because one prices actions and one prices stored bits. Scale: the same loop at one scale, but contact differs between a live environment and a substituted habitat. R is per slot, never aggregate.
- **Domain 1, the loop.** Actors: Γ, belief, weighting, action, observation, per-slot residual. Currency: residual reduction. Anchor: the ground as the only metric. Type: partition of what composes inside the closure.
- **Domain 2, the habitat.** Actors: environment, corpus, instrument, seat. Currency: contact and cascade. Anchor: the non-updating ground. Type: union of live contact and substituted habitat.
- **Domain 3, the accounting.** Actors: bits and actions. Currency: two non-additive prices. Anchor: ground settlement. Type: union of two conditions.
- **Domain 4, the channel.** Actors: live and stored percepts. Currency: freshness. Anchor: the ground, which does not decay. Type: partition of observations that still resolve.

Order by dependency: bind objective and unit before planning; describe the residual before choosing a frame; refuse before mint; settle each within-habitat claim before comparing across habitats.

My census matches the primary's four domains and the union reading. Where the primary derived from Fig 12's two currencies and Fig 11's capability claim, I derived from Fig 3's dependency break and Fig 8's closure idempotence. Different starting figures, same domain boundaries: low overlap between routes, so the agreement is weak but not circular inside the framework. It is still one evidence pool.

## Round trip gap R_T

The Symbols table defines R_T as the gap between what was sent up and what came back down, `gap(x, (T_E ∘ T_A)(x))`, measuring what the coarser description cannot hold. Fig 4 :30 requires it to be measured rather than assumed. I ran the round trip on the condensation the folder actually uses.

1. **Sent up (x):** the verbatim figure set, chiefly Fig 12 :125-127 (two currencies), Fig 11 :39-40 (agent and habitat), Fig 10 :24 (channel decay) and Fig 9 :54-55 (union compound).
2. **Abstraction (T_A):** the shorthand carried in `01` §7 and `07`: the comparison is a union and a type error, the loop is invariant, capability is agent-and-habitat, the single ratio is a `gap`.
3. **Concretisation (T_E):** a concrete expert answer, "interaction is more sample efficient because it uses fewer samples", which is the form the original question invites.

**R_T is nonzero.** Three things the shorthand holds do not survive the trip back down:

- the **unit of the efficiency claim**, because "fewer samples" collapses bits and actions, which Fig 12 :125-127 keeps apart;
- the **habitat qualifier**, because a corpus result does not transfer to a live habitat without a contact test, which Fig 11 :66 and :70 require;
- the **per-slot reporting rule**, because "fewer samples" is an aggregate and Fig 1 :19 and :22 forbid aggregation.

Conversely, one thing survives cleanly: a structure of comparison in which currency, slot, habitat and ground must all be stated before a verdict. That is the residue worth sending. R_T was **not** measured numerically, because no ground reading exists; it is recorded qualitatively as the enumerated set of dropped constraints. Treating the formula as identical to the figures would set R_T to zero and would be the dead-reckoning error Fig 4 :30 names. The honest form is a lossy condensation with a measured gap, which is what `09_watson_answer.md` states.
