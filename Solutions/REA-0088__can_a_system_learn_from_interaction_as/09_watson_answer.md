## QUESTION

Decide, in effect terms, what the Tether says about the comparison of two learning regimes: acting in a live environment and observing outcomes, versus training on a corpus collected earlier. Option A: report one efficiency ratio and rank the regimes on it. Option B: treat the comparison as a union compound, split it by currency, slot and habitat, and return per-domain verdicts plus a named `gap`. What each leads to: A ships a `general`-mode verdict that compares quantities in different units and spends the independence of the people checking it; B ships a checkable shape, a set of per-domain verdicts, five written refuters and an honest stop where no figure line licenses a number.

## DOMAIN CENSUS

R by arity, symmetry and scale, then one line per domain giving actors, currency and anchor; partition or union; order by dependency.

- **R (the residual).** The unranked comparison between live and stored learning. Arity: two regimes plus a comparison operator. Symmetry: the comparison `⋛` is a threshold relation between two quantities, not a symmetric join, and the two operands are in different units (Fig 12 :125-127). Scale: both regimes run the same loop at one scale (Fig 4 :19-20), but contact differs: live environment versus substituted habitat (Fig 11 :66). R is indexed per slot (Fig 1 :19), never averaged.
- **Domain 1, the learning loop.** Actors: Γ, belief, weighting, action, observation, per-slot residual, candidate φ. Currency: residual reduction per slot. Anchor: the ground, the only metric (Fig 1 :23). Question type: partition boundary of what composes inside closure(Γ).
- **Domain 2, habitat and contact.** Actors: environment, corpus, instrument, seat. Currency: contact and cascade. Anchor: the non-updating ground (Fig 2 :29). Question type: union of live contact and substituted habitat.
- **Domain 3, accounting and budget.** Actors: bits and actions. Currency: two prices in different units. Anchor: ground settlement, not agreement. Question type: union of two conditions that cannot be added (Fig 12 :125-127).
- **Domain 4, channel freshness.** Actors: live percepts, stored percepts, channel. Currency: freshness of contact. Anchor: the ground, which does not decay (Fig 10 :24). Question type: partition, which observations still resolve.
- **Partition or union.** D1 and D4 are partitions; D2 and D3 are unions that must be unbundled before search (Fig 9 :54-55).
- **Order by dependency.** Bind the objective and its unit before planning (Fig 3 :9-10); describe the residual before choosing a frame (Fig 9 :27-28); refuse before mint (Formula step 2); settle each within-habitat claim before comparing across habitats (Fig 12 :104).

Strain test applied: Fig 1 (per-slot residual inside one run) and Fig 4 (kernel invariance across sizes) initially appear to pull in different directions. Re-census resolves it: they govern different quantities, so no strain. One restriction remains live: `⋛` requires quantities in one unit, and after unbundling the two residuals still sit in two habitats. The comparison is well typed only inside one declared currency and one declared habitat. Named, not hidden.

## VERDICT (from the figures)

The headline comparison is a union compound and a type error, not one hard question with one answer. The loop is the same kernel in both regimes; what changes is the habitat and the channel. Bits and actions are two currencies that do not add, so no single efficiency ratio is well typed until a currency, a slot and a habitat are declared. Capability is agent-and-habitat: a stored corpus is a substituted habitat that proves wiring and cannot by itself prove live capability, while the ground does not decay even though the channel does. The frameworks returns a shape: a per-slot residual reduction in a declared currency against a ground that does not update on the system's own report, with a recorded refuter. The single number is a `gap` of the unreachable kind, reported as a verdict rather than a silence or an invented estimate. The regimes share a functional group and are not isomers. Mode `specified` on domain verdicts, `general` on the shape, not `grounded`, candidate.

- **cost if wrong:** moderate and locatable. If the non-additivity verdict is wrong, the cost is a needless refusal of a useful aggregate and a delayed comparison. The larger cost runs the other way: shipping an untyped ratio and calling it a verdict, which Fig 2 :42 prices with "Buy independence in proportion to the cost of being wrong" and Fig 4 :25 calls a recording carried upward. Both costs are bounded because the refuters are written before the run and the error is findable rather than invisible.
- **undoable:** yes. No primitive is withdrawn. The per-domain verdicts are separable, so if one currency is declared the comparison inside it can be added without touching the habitat verdict. A sent framing can be superseded by a corrected packet. What is not undoable is an independence already spent by sending the full source before asking for a verdict.
- **reversed by:** a ground reading, not agreement. A declared-currency experiment showing one regime dominating at every slot at the same habitat would close the `gap`; an agent-only change with contact held fixed that moves the ground would refute the capability verdict (Fig 11 :71-72); a figure re-census showing the two prices are additive, or that `⋛` accepts operands in different units, would dissolve the type error; a corpus channel that cannot go stale for the question asked would narrow the channel verdict. Until one of these fires, the verdict stands as `candidate`.
