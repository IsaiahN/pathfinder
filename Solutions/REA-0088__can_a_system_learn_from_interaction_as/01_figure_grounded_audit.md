## 0. The residual in effect terms

A system has a finite learning budget. It can spend it by acting in an environment and observing the result, or by training on a corpus collected earlier. The question asks whether the first route can be as efficient as the second.

Stated as an effect, not a theory: what is being decided is whether one efficiency number can rank two regimes that differ in what they spend and in what they stay in contact with. Option A says yes: report a ratio and compare. Option B says the comparison is a **union compound** that must be split by currency, by slot and by habitat before any verdict is possible.

What an answer would look like, written down and bound as an ordinary term before the run (Formula "Direction"): a per-slot residual reduction `R = |Γ(b,a) − o'|`, priced in a declared currency, against a ground that does not update on the system's own report, with a refuter already committed. Until those terms are bound, "efficiency" is a preference for a shape of answer, not a term the loop can test. Fig 5 :61-64: "A frame searching for an answer of a particular shape holds that shape as a PREFERENCE rather than as a term. It is not bound, it predicts nothing, and it accrues no residual."

The two candidate readings are kept separate throughout. They are not averaged.

## 1. Domain census, before choosing any figure line

**R by arity, symmetry and scale.** Arity: two regimes plus a comparison operator. The comparison is `⋛`, defined by the Operators table as "a threshold relation between two quantities", which is an ordered relation, not a symmetric join. Symmetry: the two operands are not the same kind of quantity, because one prices actions and the other prices stored bits (Fig 12 :125-127). Scale: both regimes run the same loop at one scale (Fig 4 :19-20), but they meet the ground at different strata of contact: live environment versus a substituted habitat (Fig 11 :66, :70). R is indexed per object slot (Fig 1 :19), never per aggregate run.

One line per domain, with actors, currency and anchor. A domain without a nameable anchor is not a domain yet.

| domain | actors | currency | anchor | question type |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| D1, the learning loop | Γ, belief b, weighting w, action a, observation o', residual R per slot | residual reduction per slot | the ground, "The ground enters as the only metric" (Fig 1 :23) | partition boundary: what composes inside closure(Γ) |
| D2, habitat and contact | environment, stored corpus, instrument, seat | contact and cascade | the ground, which does not update (Fig 2 :29) | union: live contact plus substituted habitat |
| D3, accounting and budget | bits, actions, the selector | two prices that "do not add" (Fig 12 :125) | ground settlement, not agreement | union of two conditions, unbundled before search |
| D4, channel freshness | live percepts, stored percepts, the channel | freshness of contact | "the ground does not decay, the channel does" (Fig 10 :24) | partition: which observations still resolve |

**Partition or union.** D1 and D4 are partition boundaries inside one loop: a term either composes inside the closure or it does not; a channel either still resolves or it does not. D2 and D3 are unions, not partitions. Fig 9 :54-55: "And when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again." A single lever applied to a union clears the region where only one condition holds and leaves the intersections standing.

**Dependency order (Fig 3).** The chain is perception, vocabulary, objective, planning, learn and carry. Fig 3 :20: "The chain is ordered by dependency, not by importance." Efficiency presupposes link 3, the objective: "it can say what would count as progress here, in its own terms" (Fig 3 :10). Without a declared currency and a stated ground, the efficiency question is a reading taken below the break. Fig 3 :22-23: "A reading taken below the break is not a weak reading. It is a reading of nothing." Order for this question: bind the objective (currency and ground) first, then the habitat, then the channel, then the budget comparison.

Strain test applied before any conclusion: the loop figure (Fig 1) and the membrane figure (Fig 4) both speak about the same kernel. If one is read as saying interaction changes the loop while the other says the loop does not change with size, that is a strain. Re-census resolves it: Fig 4 governs the kernel across sizes; Fig 1 governs the residual inside one slot. Different quantities, so no strain.

## 3. Findings, each with its verbatim line and its own refuter

### F1 - The headline comparison is mistyped (decisive against Option A)

Fig 12 :125-126:

> **"A composition costs in two currencies, and they do not add. Bits price the description. Actions price finding out whether it holds."**

Fig 12 :126-127:

> **"They are in different units, so a system holding one accounting cannot compare them and will not notice the shortfall."**

Fig 12 :130:

> **"Summing them into one figure is the error this names, not the fix for it."**

A stored corpus prices bits: description length, compression, samples already present. Interaction prices actions: environment steps, robot trials, clinical or driving risk. The Operators table defines `⋛` as "a threshold relation between two quantities", and Fig 12 says the two prices are in different units. Asking whether interaction is *as efficient as* pre-collected data compares two quantities in different units with a comparison operator that requires one unit. That is a type error, not a close result.

This is not a refusal to compare anything. It is a refusal to compare before unbundling. Once a currency is declared, the comparison becomes well typed within that currency, and the other currency is recorded as a separate verdict.

**Refuter:** exhibit one declared currency in which both regimes price the same units at the same slot and the same habitat, and show one dominates at every slot. If that holds, F1 is withdrawn and a single ratio is licensed.

### F2 - The loop is the same kernel in both regimes

Fig 4 :19-20:

> **"The kernel is the part that does not change with size: the same loop and the same rule for naming what worked, whether one agent is running it or a thousand. Only the actors, the currency and the anchor change."**

Fig 1 :19: **"The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated."** Fig 1 :23: **"The ground enters as the only metric."**

So "learns more efficiently" cannot be a property of the loop alone. The kernel is invariant; what changes is the currency and the anchor, which Fig 4 names as the variables. A claim about efficiency that never states which currency and which anchor changed is a claim about nothing in particular.

**Refuter:** show an architecture whose loop changes between interaction and stored-data training in a way that alters residual indexing or the anchor, and that this alone accounts for the efficiency difference. Then the kernel-invariance claim fails for that case and must be narrowed.

### F3 - Capability is a property of agent and habitat, not of the agent

Fig 11 :39-40:

> **"So the answerable question is not what intelligence is, but what makes a system capable. And capability is a property of agent-and-habitat, never of the agent alone."**
>
> **"A system cannot be made more capable by improving the agent in isolation. An improvement that does not change contact changes nothing, however much it improves."**

Fig 11 :70: **"A test harness is a substituted habitat. Which is why a synthetic solve proves wiring and never capability."**

This is the strongest figure-grounded point in the whole question. A pre-collected dataset is a substituted habitat: it was collected under some other policy, in some other contact. A system that learns from it can be shown to work inside that habitat. That is wiring. Whether it works in the live habitat is a different question with a different anchor. Interaction is not merely a second source of bits; it is a change of contact.

The honest qualification, kept visible: a substituted habitat is still a habitat, and the ground can legitimately be the distribution the corpus was drawn from. Fig 11 :61-62: "The seat's power is asymmetric. Introduction is available; exclusion is not." So offline learning is not useless. It is limited to the ground its habitat actually carries.

**Refuter:** show an agent-only change, with contact held fixed, that moves the ground and changes capability. Fig 11 :71-72 already names this as the standing falsifier of the capability claim: "an agent-only improvement that moved the ground would refute the capability claim."

### F4 - The question is asked below the objective link

Fig 3 :10: **"it can say what would count as progress here, in its own terms"** (link 3, the objective).

Fig 3 :22-23:

> **"A step whose input never arrives cannot be diagnosed, only its predecessor can. Which is why a reading taken below the break is not a weak reading. It is a reading of nothing."**

Fig 3 :27: **"A link with no instrument is not a link that works. It is a link nobody has looked at."**

"Efficiency" is link 4 and above. It consumes an objective. The question as posed supplies no currency, no slot and no ground, so link 3 has no input. Any answer given at that point is a reading of nothing. This is why the derivation starts with a census and a bound term instead of a number.

**Refuter:** supply a stated objective with a declared unit and an instrument that reads progress in that unit, for both regimes, at the same slot. Then link 3 has input and the reading is no longer below the break.

### F5 - It is a union compound, not one hard question

Fig 9 :54-57:

> **"And when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again. A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones."**

Fig 9 :4-5:

> **"Disagreements that do not shrink with effort, where each rule keeps working well on a different subset, are not one hard question but several well-formed ones. Split it rather than search."**

The literature itself shows the split. Offline RL works where the corpus covers the ground and fails where the query leaves the corpus; active learning works where labels are costly and a query can be chosen; model-based RL trades generation bias against sample cost. Each rule works on a different subset. That is the signature Fig 9 names. Splitting is the instruction, not a failure to answer.

**Refuter:** show a single condition whose presence alone explains every reported efficiency difference across both regimes, with no residual subset left over. Then the union reading collapses to a partition.

### F6 - The ground does not decay; the channel does

Fig 10 :24-26:

> **"the ground does not decay, the channel does. stale percepts, dropped transients, saturated metrics, pooled readings"**

Fig 10 :27-28: **"the output is not a verdict, it is you have lost touch."**

A pre-collected corpus is a channel, not a ground. It can be stale, pooled and saturated. Interaction keeps the channel fresh by re-reading. This gives a real, figure-derived asymmetry between the regimes, but it is an asymmetry of contact, not of the loop. It also gives the correct failure report for offline learning: not "wrong answer" but "lost touch".

**Refuter:** show a stored corpus whose channel cannot go stale for the question being asked, meaning the ground sampled by the corpus is the ground at test and no unobserved state is admissible. Then this asymmetry does not bite for that case.

### F7 - A recording is not a method

Fig 4 :22: **"Only methods cross up. Only head starts come down."** Fig 4 :25: **"A recording carried upward looks like knowledge and is a description of one occasion."** Fig 4 :25-26: **"Replayed downward, it produces a system that repeats a past success and cannot produce a new one."**

Pre-collected data is a recording of occasions. Training on it can produce a system that repeats past successes. Interaction supplies the residual that lets a method be re-applied. This is a structural difference between the regimes and it is sharper than a difference of sample count. It also constrains what may be offered to a checker: the generator and its refuter, not a table of results (Fig 4 :23: "upward: a method that can be reapplied. Never the recording of a particular success.").

**Refuter:** show a stored corpus that carries a generator rather than occasions, in the sense that a new success can be composed from it without a new observation. Then the recording claim fails for that corpus.

### F8 - Residual per slot, not global average

Fig 1 :22: **"Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average."**

An efficiency ratio is an aggregate by construction. If it averages across object slots, it destroys the very signal that says where the system still fails. So even within one currency, the comparison must be reported per slot. This is a reporting rule, not an extra empirical claim.

**Refuter:** show that the residual for this question is genuinely global, meaning no slot-wise decomposition is available and no live slot exists under either regime. Then aggregation does no damage here.

### F9 - A filter for the budget is not a witness for the verdict

Fig 9 :60:

> **"Use filters for the budget and witnesses for the verdict. Never let a filter hand you a verdict."**

Fig 2 :40: **"disqualifying for a verdict: agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool"**

Sample count, wall-clock time and benchmark rank are budget filters. They decide what gets tried first. They do not decide which regime is right. A result aggregating many papers that all use the same benchmark is one evidence pool wearing many names (Fig 8 :48-49: "Two frames that overlap almost completely are one frame wearing two names.").

**Refuter:** exhibit a case where the budget quantity is itself the anchor, meaning the question is constitutively about cost and the ground returns a cost. Then the filter becomes a witness legitimately (Fig 2 :36: "An anchor is legitimate when the question is constitutively about it.").

### F10 - The single-number verdict is a `gap`, and `gap` is a verdict

Fig 8 :39: **"So a search that finds nothing is never a proof of absence."**

Fig 13 :134: **"Unreachable is a verdict here, not a silence."**

Fig 9 :32: **"you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself"**

After a genuine census, no figure line licenses a single efficiency ratio across both regimes. That is a `gap` of the unreachable kind: the answer cannot be composed from what the figures hold, and importing a number would add an atom that is not needed. The shape is still governed: a per-slot, currency-declared, habitat-indexed comparison with a non-updating anchor and a committed refuter. Reporting the gap is the honest stop, not a failure to derive.

**Refuter:** find the figure line that prices both actions and bits in one unit and licenses the comparison. Until that line is produced, the gap stands.

### F11 - Interaction can manufacture a probe; a fixed corpus cannot score itself

Fig 5 :46-48:

> **"When nothing is scoring, the drive manufactures a gradient. So a system that starts with nothing can still be shaped, by seeking the gap that is large and compressible, which is the same quantity the acceptance test scores. Its own output is never a measure of success."**

Interaction can aim a new probe at the live gap (Fig 5 :12: "new: extend perception, aim the probe here"). A stored corpus has a fixed gradient fixed by what was collected. This is a second structural asymmetry in favour of interaction, but it is conditional on the live channel actually returning something. Fig 6 :34-35: "Where nothing is, there is no edge to extend from, and the search for a proto-instrument is a search for one that is already there."

**Refuter:** show a corpus-driven system whose own output is a valid measure of success against a non-updating ground, meaning the corpus itself contains the discriminator. Then this asymmetry does not apply to that system.

### F12 - Closure differs, so the union has a surplus

Fig 6 :23: **"No frame escapes its own closure. The bound moves only on contact with a frame whose closure differs."**

Fig 8 :32: **"Closure is idempotent. Composing inside a closed set never leaves it, so no search, however long, adds an atom."**

A fixed corpus is a fixed closure. A live environment is a frame whose closure differs, so contact with it can move the wall. This explains why interaction can supply something no amount of extra training on the same corpus can, and it explains why the surplus is not guaranteed: independence decides whether the contact is new evidence or one pool wearing two names (Fig 8 :47-49).

**Refuter:** show that the live environment's closure is the same as the corpus's closure for the question at hand, meaning every observation the environment could return is already in the corpus. Then the union surplus is zero and interaction adds only cost.

## 4. Pre-registered refuter, written before the conclusion

Required before any outreach, per Seat Map :287: "A falsifier written before the run is a design instrument, not only an honesty device."

The compound verdict is refuted if any of the following holds:

1. One currency is declared, both regimes are priced in it at the same slot and the same habitat, and one dominates at every slot. This closes the single-number `gap`.
2. A measurement shows the same agent, with contact held fixed, achieves the same per-slot residual reduction per unit cost in both regimes, across a ground that does not update on the agent's report.
3. A figure re-census shows the two prices in Fig 12 :125-127 are additive, or that the Operators table's `⋛` accepts operands in different units.
4. A stored corpus is shown to change contact with a non-updating ground as much as live interaction does, which breaks the agent-and-habitat verdict in F3.
5. A corpus-driven system's own output is shown to be a valid discriminator against a non-updating ground, breaking F11.

If any fires, the verdict is withdrawn or narrowed. Until one is run, no part of this may be labelled `grounded`.

## 5. Recommendation

1. Do not answer the headline as one question. Split it by currency, slot and habitat (F5, F8).
2. Bind the objective before comparing anything: state the unit, the slot and the anchor (F4).
3. Report per-domain verdicts separately, never as a weighted average (F1, Fig 9 :60).
4. Treat stored data as a substituted habitat and report its ground explicitly (F3, F6).
5. Send the generator and the refuter, not a table of results (F7, Fig 4 :23).
6. Record the `gap` plainly where no figure line governs a single ratio (F10).

## 7. Updated verdict

The shape survives and the single number does not. The loop is the same kernel; the habitat and the channel are not. Capability belongs to agent and habitat, so an efficiency claim that names only the agent is unreadable. The comparison is a union compound that must be split by currency, slot and habitat; within one currency and one habitat a comparison is licensed, while across currencies it is a type error. The headline question therefore ends in a `gap` of the unreachable kind: a shape, a per-domain verdict set and a refuter, but no single efficiency ratio.
