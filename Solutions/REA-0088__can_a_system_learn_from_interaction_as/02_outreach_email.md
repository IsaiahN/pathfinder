**Subject:** One checkable claim about interactive versus offline learning, and the failure condition I wrote first

> Hi [name],
>
> I have one specific output I would like you to check against what you already know. I am not asking you to read the framework first, and I am not asking you to agree with it. If the output does not survive, there is nothing else to look at.
>
> **The output (`specified`, not `grounded`):** the question "can a system learn from interaction as efficiently as from pre-collected data" is not one question. It is a union of at least two currencies. A stored corpus prices description bits; interaction prices actions and the risk of taking them. The framework states that a composition has two currencies and that they do not add, and that the comparison operator requires quantities in the same units. So a single efficiency ratio between the two regimes is a type error until a currency, a slot and a habitat are declared. This is a claim about the shape of a valid answer, not a measurement and not a claim that one regime is better.
>
> The structural part is also checkable. The learning loop is the same kernel in both regimes: bet, act, observe, residual per object slot, ground as the only metric. What changes is not the loop but the habitat and the channel. Capability is a property of agent and habitat, never of the agent alone, and an improvement that does not change contact changes nothing however much it improves. A stored corpus is a substituted habitat: it can prove wiring and cannot by itself prove capability in the live habitat. The ground does not decay; the channel does.
>
> **What I am not claiming:** that interaction always wins, that offline learning is useless, that sample count is one currency, that the two regimes are isomers, or that any benchmark rank settles the question. Agreement among people who read the same benchmark is one evidence pool, not two confirmations.
>
> **What would refute the output:**
> 1. Declare one currency, price both regimes in it at the same slot and the same habitat, and show one dominates at every slot.
> 2. Show the same agent, with contact held fixed, achieving the same per-slot residual reduction per unit cost in both regimes against a ground that does not update on the agent's report.
> 3. Show that the two prices are additive, or that the comparison operator accepts operands in different units.
>
> I wrote these down before sending this. If any holds, I withdraw the framing. A one-line "this fails because..." is more useful to me than agreement. If you would rather not engage, no reply is needed.
>
