# Solution REA-0088

**Question:** Can a system learn from interaction as efficiently as it learns from pre-collected data?

**Source:** reasoning.txt line 107

**Section:** 9. Limits, scaling, and architecture

**Mode:** `specified` on the substantive verdicts, `general` on the structural shape, not `grounded`. Every substantive claim below is a simulated derivation checked for internal consistency against the figures, not a ground reading. It cannot settle the question it reports on.

**Status:** done

**Registry status:** pending

## The question restated as an effect

A learning system must spend a budget two ways: acting and observing (interaction), or training on a fixed stored corpus (pre-collected data). "Efficiency" is a comparison of what is learned per unit spent. Option A: the two regimes can be ranked on one efficiency scale, so the question has one answer. Option B: the comparison is a union of conditions with different currencies, slots and habitats, so it must be split before it is searched; each part gets its own verdict and the single ratio is a `gap`. What an answer would look like is bound in advance as an ordinary term (Formula "Direction"): a per-slot residual reduction, stated in a declared currency, against a ground that does not update on the system's own report, with a refuter written before the run.

## Headline verdict

Option B. No figure line licenses a single efficiency number across the two regimes (Fig 12 :125-127, Fig 9 :60). The loop is the same kernel in both (Fig 4 :19-20), but the habitat and the channel are not (Fig 11 :39-40, Fig 10 :24). Interaction is live contact with a ground that can refuse; a stored corpus is a substituted habitat that can still prove wiring and never capability (Fig 11 :70). Verdicts are kept per domain and never averaged.

## What is deliberately not here

- No personal emails of named researchers.
- No ranking of people by how likely they are to agree with the verdict.
- No bulk-send plan.

Reasons, in the figures' own terms: agreement among frames that share an evidence pool is the pool talking (Fig 2 :40), correlation that is disqualifying for a verdict cannot be bought by selecting receptive readers (Seat Map :163), and a verifier who has already absorbed the source cannot verify the claim (Fig 10 :42). A failure is more useful than an endorsement.
