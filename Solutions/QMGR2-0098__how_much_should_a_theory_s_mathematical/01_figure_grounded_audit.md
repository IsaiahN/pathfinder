## 0. The claim under audit

> How much should a theory's mathematical elegance matter compared with its testable predictions?
>
> Two readings are possible. Reading A takes the question literally: elegance and predictive success are two quantities on one scale, and an answer is a weight (for example, "elegance should count for 20 percent"). Reading B takes the question as a sign that two different quantities have been put on one scale: elegance has an office in building and choosing what to try, predictions have the office of settling what is true, and the comparison is refused as a category error.

The derivation below derives before concluding. The pre-registered refuter is in section 5, written before the verdict in section 8.

---

## 1. Residual in effect terms

A frame Gamma holds a library, predicts Gamma(b,a), receives o', and reads a residual R = |Gamma(b,a) - o'| (Symbols table :38-40: *"It is the aim, not only the error signal"*). A candidate theory phi has a description cost |phi| (Symbols table :52-55). The question asks how |phi| (its elegance, its compression) should be weighed against the size of R (its failure to match the world).

- **Arity:** two quantities, not one. The comparison assumes they are commensurable; that assumption is what the census tests.
- **Symmetry:** strongly asymmetric. Elegance is produced inside the frame that cites it; prediction is a reading from outside the frame. Fig 1 :27: *"They are frame-internal. A frame cannot score itself with a quantity it also produces."*
- **Scale:** elegance is within-library, within-level (a property of phi at a level). Testability crosses the frame-ground boundary. Fig 4 :21: *"The membrane is the boundary between the sizes, and the rule about what may cross it. Not a wall: a filter with a direction."*

**The residual is not "what is the right weight?" It is "why are two quantities of different kinds being asked to share one scale?"** That is the effect to be explained.

**Direction, bound as an ordinary term before the run (Formula, "Direction"):** the sought-for answer is a *shape* with three marks: (1) each quantity has a named office, (2) the single-scale trade-off is refused, (3) an instrument is named that would change the verdict if it returned differently. Writing this down makes the preference refusable by the same test as everything else, instead of an invisible preference for a numerical answer.

## 4. Findings F1 to F10

### F1 - The comparison is a category error: two currencies, held apart (decisive)

Fig 12 :125: *"A composition costs in two currencies, and they do not add."*
Fig 12 :126: *"Bits price the description. Actions price finding out whether it holds."*
Fig 12 :130: *"Summing them into one figure is the error this names, not the fix for it. A price in bits and a price in actions are two readings, held apart."*

Mathematical elegance is a price in **bits**: description length, compression, the |phi| term. Testable predictions are a price in **actions**: finding out whether the theory holds. The figure says explicitly that these are in different units, that a system holding one accounting "will not notice the shortfall," and that summing them is the error, not the fix. The question as posed ("how much X compared with Y") asks for exactly that sum.

So the honest answer is not a coefficient. It is the refusal of the scale. The framework does not say "elegance should matter 20 percent"; it says elegance and prediction are two readings held apart. **Mode: `general` (structural), triangulated across Fig 12 and Fig 9 :60.**

**Refuter:** a figure line that treats |phi| and R as additive terms in one acceptance score would falsify this finding. None was found after re-census of Fig 5 and Fig 12.

### F2 - Elegance is a frame-internal self-scoring quantity, disqualified as the metric

Fig 1 :23: *"The ground enters as the only metric."*
Fig 1 :27: *"They are frame-internal. A frame cannot score itself with a quantity it also produces."*
Fig 1 :25-26 names the excluded quantities: coverage and compression achieved, predicates minted and credibility accrued.

Elegance is produced by the same frame that would cite it as a reason to believe. That is the named disqualification. A theory's author can score their own description length; the ground cannot be moved by that score. This does not make elegance useless (see F3); it makes it ineligible for the verdict office.

**Mode: `specified`, triangulated (Fig 1 and Fig 2 anchor rule). Refuter:** a case where elegance alone moves a candidate from `candidate` to `settled` without any change in R would break this. No such case is supplied by the figures; a historical case must be checked at the ground.

### F3 - Elegance is legitimate in the budget gate, not in the verdict

Fig 5 :42: *"The acceptance test is a bargain, not a threshold."*
Fig 5 :26: *"does the new term cost less to state than the confusion it removes?"*
Fig 5 :45: *"This is minimum description length, Rissanen 1978, which is itself a composition of Kolmogorov complexity and Shannon coding put to work on model selection."*
Fig 9 :60: *"Use filters for the budget and witnesses for the verdict. Never let a filter hand you a verdict."*

Formula step 3 accepts phi iff `cost(phi) + left(R,phi) < cost(R)`. The compression term is exactly where elegance belongs: it is the price of the term and whether it pays. But Formula step 5 says *"the ground settles it. Verified only by what pays. A gate is not the ground."* A gate is not the ground. Elegance passes or fails the gate; it cannot settle the claim. Fig 9 :60 makes the separation explicit: filters for the budget, witnesses for the verdict.

This is the resolution of the apparent conflict between Fig 1 :27 (compression does not count) and Fig 5 (compression is charged). Different offices. See section 6 (strain test).

**Mode: `specified`, triangulated (Fig 5 + Fig 9 + Formula steps 3 and 5). Refuter:** showing that the MDL bargain itself is the verdict, not a gate, breaks this.

### F4 - Testable predictions are the anchor and the only verdict

Fig 2 :29: *"An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks."*
Fig 2 :45: *"Frames range against the ground. They do not negotiate with it."*
Fig 1 :23: *"The ground enters as the only metric."*

Testability has the two properties an anchor needs: it settles, and it does not move when a frame admires the theory. Elegance fails the second property. The anchor is not a third party in a vote; each frame is corrected by it separately (Fig 2 :46). So a theory's score for elegance is inadmissible as evidence in the verdict, and its predictions are admissible. **Mode: `general` (structural), triangulated (Fig 1 + Fig 2). Refuter:** a definition of "anchor" that includes frame-generated scores would break it; Fig 2 :30 rules that out.

### F5 - An elegant change with no change in contact changes nothing

Fig 11 :40: *"An improvement that does not change contact changes nothing, however much it improves."*
Fig 5 :48: *"Its own output is never a measure of success. A system can be enthusiastically curious and produce nothing."*

If a theory is made more elegant while its predictions are unchanged, the residual R against o' is unchanged. By Fig 11 :40 the improvement changes nothing at the ground. By Fig 5 :48 the frame's enthusiasm for its own output is not a measure of success. This is why "more beautiful but equally predictive" cannot be a verdict win in the framework's terms: it has not touched the only metric.

**Mode: `specified`, triangulated (Fig 11 + Fig 5). Refuter:** an experiment in which a purely presentational change in a theory (no new prediction) changes the measured outcome would break it. Such an experiment would mean the ground is updating on frame aesthetics, which is Fig 2 panel 1.

### F6 - Elegance aims the probe; the prediction directs the instrument

Fig 5 :18: *"seeks the gap that is large and compressible"*
Fig 6 :38: *"Neptune was calculated before it was seen, and the prediction is what told anyone where to point"*
Fig 9 :28: *"any frame you pick will seem to fit"*

The curiosity drive seeks the gap that is large and compressible, so compression legitimately helps choose where to look. Theory routinely precedes its instrument (Fig 6 :38), so an elegant calculation is welcome. But the figure's own example says it was **the prediction** that told anyone where to point the instruments, not the beauty of the calculation. And if the residual is not described first, any frame will seem to fit (Fig 9 :28), which is how an aesthetic preference smuggles in a conclusion. Elegance is a search heuristic, not a settlement.

**Mode: `specified`, single-source for the Neptune reading (Fig 6), triangulated for the budget role (Fig 5). Refuter:** a case where beauty, with no discriminating prediction, told an instrument where to point and the instrument then paid would break the narrowing of D1b to heuristic. The historical record on this is mixed and must be checked at the ground, not by argument.

### F7 - Aesthetic consensus is a convention, not corroboration

Fig 2 :35: *"Alignment is triangulation, not negotiation. A mirror cannot correct you."*
Fig 2 :40: *"agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool"*
Fig 7 :35: *"A room's agreement is variation, not a verdict. Selection between rooms is where the ground votes."*
Fig 10 :47: *"a convention that decides an outcome has stopped being one"*

A community that admires the same elegant theory shares an evidence pool (the same training, the same journals, the same aesthetic). Their agreement is the pool talking (Fig 2 :20-21). If that agreement decides which theory is accepted, the convention has become a verdict (Fig 10 :47), which the framework forbids. World corroboration here is Hossenfelder's own observation (opened this session): *"Troubles begin if data disagree with the hypotheses but scientists continue to rely on their beautiful hypotheses rather than taking clues from evidence."*

**Mode: `specified`, triangulated (Fig 2 + Fig 7 + Fig 10). Refuter:** an agreement pool that is shown to be independent in the Fig 2 :31 sense (two instruments, not two readers of the same review) would be a genuine witness and would require re-census of D3.

### F8 - Buy independence in proportion to the cost of being wrong

Fig 2 :42: *"Buy independence in proportion to the cost of being wrong."*
Fig 2 :39: *"Correlation has valence."*
Fig 10 :59: *"an interest in the outcome is unavoidable, an interest in the reading is disqualifying."*

The question "how much should elegance matter" becomes answerable in one restricted sense when the cost of being wrong is named. If the cost is high, buy independence: choose checkers whose closure differs and who can range against the ground separately. Elegance cannot be used to select those checkers, because selecting for aesthetic agreement manufactures the disqualifying correlation. The outside checker sees the residual and its refuter, not the framework's interiors (Fig 10 :52: *"Bring a characterised residual, never the source."*).

**Mode: `specified`, triangulated (Fig 2 + Fig 10). Refuter:** a selection rule that improves a verdict by increasing prior agreement would break this; Fig 2 :40 already names that as the collapse.

### F9 - The answer is a shape, not a number

Fig 9 :32: *"you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself"*
Fig 9 :30: *"your words are borrowed too, so describe the effect"*
Fig 13 :134: *"Unreachable is a verdict here, not a silence,"*
Fig 13 :118: *"still not arrive: infinite effort inside a finite reach."*

The framework returns a shape, not a numerical weight. The shape here is: two quantities, two offices, one refusal, one instrument. Fig 13 :134 says an out-of-reach answer is reported as a verdict, not left as silence. So the honest report is: no figure line supplies a coefficient, and the absence of a coefficient is itself the answer at this level, not a gap to be filled by a guessed number.

**Mode: `general` (structural), triangulated (Fig 9 + Fig 13 + figures README). Refuter:** a figure line that yields a weight would replace the shape with a name; none was found.

## 5. Pre-registered refuter (written before the conclusion)

Required before any outreach: a falsifier written before the run is a design instrument. The shape claim is refuted if any of the following holds:

1. A figure line licenses trading |phi| and R on one additive scale, or treats bits and actions as one currency (would break F1 and Fig 12 :130).
2. A case is shown in which elegance alone, with no change in contact or prediction, moves a theory from `candidate` to `settled` (would break F2, F5, Fig 1 :23, Fig 11 :40).
3. The MDL bargain is shown to be the verdict rather than the gate (would break F3 and Fig 9 :60).
4. A community agreement pool with genuine Fig 2 :31 independence is shown to improve a verdict about the world (would break F7).

If any fires, the shape is withdrawn or narrowed. Until one is run, no part of this may be labelled `grounded`.

## 6. Strain test and re-census

**Apparent strain 1: Fig 1 :27 says compression does not count; Fig 5 :45 says compression prices the acceptance test.** Re-census: Fig 1 governs the *metric* (what settles a claim), Fig 5 governs the *gate* (what is worth minting). Different objects. Matches the README's rule that a reading which makes figures contradict one another has misidentified its domain. Not strained after re-census.

**Apparent strain 2: Fig 6 :38 says theory precedes its instrument, which can be read as elegance substituting for evidence.** Re-census: the figure's own example attributes the direction to the prediction, not to the beauty of the calculation. The sentence governs discovery order, not settlement. Not strained.

**Apparent strain 3: Dirac (world source, opened this session) says beauty matters more than immediate fit, which conflicts with the verdict.** Re-census: his quote is a discovery-context claim about working from "a sound insight" and expecting discrepancies to be "cleared up with further development," that is, it assumes eventual ground settlement. It occupies the D1b heuristic office, not D2 verdict. No flip of the verdict; the conflict sends us back to the census and is resolved there. Recorded, not hidden.

After re-census: **one live strain, named, none hidden.** The live one is historical, not structural: whether elegance has in fact been a reliable guide to truth is a ground question for case studies, not something the figures decide.

## 7. Recommendation

1. Answer the question by refusing its scale (F1), then assign the two offices (F3, F4). Do not supply a coefficient; the figures do not license one (F9).
2. Use elegance as a budget and heuristic term: what is worth trying, how cheaply it can be carried, where to aim the probe (F3, F6).
3. Reserve the verdict for contact with the ground. A more elegant theory with unchanged predictions is not a better-settled theory (F2, F5).
4. When the cost of being wrong is high, buy independence rather than agreement (F8). Ask for a refutation, not endorsement.
5. Fig 4 :30: *"So take the round trip and measure the gap. Without it you are navigating by dead reckoning: confident about a position nobody checked."* Send the generator (the shape and its refuter), not a recorded number.
