## RESIDUAL

The main audit refuses a single scale for elegance and prediction. Two supporting questions follow. (1) In what order do the gates run? (2) What is the structure of two theories that share a predictive surface but differ in elegance? The second is the "isomer" analogue this question needs.

## DERIVATION

### Q1 - Are bits and actions the same kind of quantity?

- Fig 12 :125 *"A composition costs in two currencies, and they do not add."*
- Fig 12 :126 *"Bits price the description. Actions price finding out whether it holds."*
- Fig 12 :130 *"Summing them into one figure is the error this names, not the fix for it. A price in bits and a price in actions are two readings, held apart."*
- ⇒ They are two readings, not two terms of one sum. Elegance is the bits reading; testability is the actions reading. This is a structural claim (`general`), not a measurement.

### Q2 - What order do the gates run, and where does refusal sit?

- Formula step 2 routes R first: REFUTED means a term held is false and what is owed is a refusal, not an explanation. The first four bins sort by what is MISSING; the fifth by what is WRONG.
- Formula step 3 gates admission: SUPPORT, REACHABILITY, NOVELTY as a product, with the MDL bargain cost(phi) + left(R,phi) < cost(R).
- Formula step 5 settles: *"the ground settles it. Verified only by what pays. A gate is not the ground."*
- Fig 3 :20 *"The chain is ordered by dependency, not by importance."*
- ⇒ Order: refuse what is wrong; describe R; price and admit phi (elegance enters here); settle at the ground (prediction enters here); then let outside frames check. Effort spent polishing elegance at the checking stage cannot rescue a theory that never reached settlement. **Falsifier:** if a framework admitted a term on compression alone with no ground payment and then composed over it as settled, the order claim fails. Fig 12 :106 (*"Nothing composes over what was never admitted as a part"*) and step 5 forbid it in this framework, so the falsifier is internal and checkable.

### Q3 - Are two empirically equivalent theories isomers?

- Fig 12 :117 *"Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."*
- Two theories with identical predictions have the same reading at the ground. If they are built from the same conceptual atoms arranged differently, they meet the figure's definition of **isomers**: same constituents, different arrangement, different substance. Their elegance is a property of the arrangement, not of the reading.
- If they do not share the same atoms, they are not isomers; they are simply two different molecules that happen to give the same reading. The isomer claim is therefore conditional on a shared atom set and must be stated as such.
- World corroboration (secondary, opened this session): *The Hard Problem is About Source* gives the metamerism case: *"If several paths create the same behavior, then behavior is a projection with lossy information."* One green reading can come from one green light, or yellow plus blue, or RGB, or CMYK. The instrument *"cannot comment on the source."* It was resolved *"not by argument, but by the arrival of a better instrument."*
- ⇒ Elegance is a preference over sources read off a projection. Without a source-discriminating instrument it cannot pick the right one. The figure's isomer definition plus the metamerism case predict exactly this. **Falsifier:** a case where elegance alone discriminates two empirically equivalent theories and the discriminating instrument later confirms the more elegant one. That would not refute the figure definition, but it would move elegance from a budget preference to a successful heuristic and require re-census of D1b.

### Q4 - What is the union of theory choice?

- Fig 9 :54 *"And when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again."*
- Fig 9 :57 *"What remains is smaller and it is also categorically different: a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either. It stops being a mixture and becomes a specific"* (line :58 continues *"compound."*).
- A theory choice that mixes "is it elegant?" and "is it confirmed?" is a union of two conditions, not one hard question. Unbundle: price the description in bits, settle the claim in actions, then face the compound (which theory to fund, which to publish) with its own loop and its own ground. Fig 9 :60 *"Use filters for the budget and witnesses for the verdict. Never let a filter hand you a verdict."*

## CONCLUSION

The two currencies are two readings and cannot be summed. The gates run in a fixed order: refuse, describe, price and admit, settle, check. Two empirically equivalent theories are isomers only under a shared atom set, and elegance cannot discriminate them without a source-discriminating instrument. The union of elegance and confirmation is unbundled before search, not blended. The main audit's refusal is therefore supported by a second, independent route through gate order and the isomer definition.
