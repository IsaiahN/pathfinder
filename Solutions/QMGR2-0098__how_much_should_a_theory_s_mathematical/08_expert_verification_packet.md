# Expert verification packet - elegance versus testable predictions

**What this is:** a claim in a form a philosopher of science or physicist can check. Mode, derivation and refuters are stated before the conclusion.

**Mode: `general` on the refusal of a common scale, `specified` on each office assignment. Not `grounded`.** No measurement of theory-choice outcomes is claimed.

## 1. The claim, in one paragraph

The question "how much should elegance matter compared with predictions?" assumes one scale. The framework prices two different currencies: elegance is a price in bits (description length and compression), and testable predictions are a price in actions (finding out whether a theory holds). These do not add. Elegance therefore has a legitimate office in the budget and in aiming the probe, and predictions have the sole office of settling the verdict. A more elegant theory with unchanged predictions has not moved toward the ground, and a community that admires the same beautiful theory has a shared evidence pool rather than corroboration. The framework returns this shape and refuses to supply a coefficient; that refusal is a verdict, not a silence.

## 2. Three checkable sub-claims

### C1 - framework-internal; checkable by reading Figure 12

- **Statement:** bits and actions are two readings held apart; summing them is the error the figure names.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 12 :125 *"A composition costs in two currencies, and they do not add."* Fig 12 :126 *"Bits price the description. Actions price finding out whether it holds."* Fig 12 :130 *"Summing them into one figure is the error this names, not the fix for it. A price in bits and a price in actions are two readings, held apart."*
- **Refuter:** show a figure line that admits an additive score combining |phi| and R, or a derivation that treats the MDL bargain as the verdict rather than the gate (Fig 9 :60).

### C2 - framework-internal plus physics-facing; checkable by a physicist or philosopher of science

- **Statement:** elegance is frame-internal and cannot score the verdict; predictions are the anchor and the only metric. A more elegant theory with unchanged contact changes nothing.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 1 :27 *"They are frame-internal. A frame cannot score itself with a quantity it also produces."* Fig 1 :23 *"The ground enters as the only metric."* Fig 2 :29 *"An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks."* Fig 11 :40 *"An improvement that does not change contact changes nothing, however much it improves."*
- **What to check:** is there a real case where elegance alone, with no new prediction, changed the empirical status of a theory? If so, name the ground that paid. Dirac's 1963 line about beauty in equations is a discovery-context claim, not such a case: his own passage expects discrepancies to be cleared up with further development.
- **Refuter:** a measured case where a purely aesthetic change altered what the ground returned, or a definition of anchor that includes frame-generated scores.

### C3 - framework-internal derivation with a named strain

- **Statement:** two empirically equivalent theories are isomers only under a shared atom set; elegance cannot discriminate them without a source-discriminating instrument. Aesthetic consensus is a convention, not corroboration.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 12 :117 *"Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."* Fig 2 :40 *"agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool"* Fig 7 :35 *"A room's agreement is variation, not a verdict. Selection between rooms is where the ground votes."*
- **What to check:** do two theories with identical predictions but different elegance satisfy the figure's isomer definition? The metamerism case in *The Hard Problem is About Source* (read 2026-11-23) says a reading cannot comment on its source; a spectrometer, not an argument, resolved it.
- **Refuter:** a source-discriminating instrument that confirms the more elegant of two empirically equivalent theories. That would move elegance from budget preference to successful heuristic and require re-census of the heuristic office.

## 3. What is explicitly not claimed

- Not that elegance is worthless, or that beauty has never guided a discovery.
- Not that prediction is the only virtue a theory can have, only the sole verdict-settling one here.
- Not that a weighting exists but is hidden. No figure line supplies one.
- Not a theory of everything, no final room.
- Not `grounded`. Nothing here is asserted as settled by experiment.
- Not a claim that this derivation settles the physics or the history of science.

## 5. What would settle it

1. **Coefficient test:** a study where different elegance weightings yield different, checkable predictions about theory choice and the ground pays for one.
2. **Discriminating instrument:** a measurement separating two empirically equivalent theories of differing elegance, naming the source of the shared reading.
3. **Historical refutation:** a case where elegance alone moved a theory from candidate to settled with no change in prediction.
4. **Standing refuters:** an additive bits-plus-actions score in the figures, or the compression gate shown to be the verdict.

## 6. What an expert can check in twenty minutes

1. Read Fig 12 :125-126, :130, Fig 1 :23 and :27, Fig 11 :40 (five short passages).
2. Say whether C1's refusal of a common scale is a fair reading of those lines.
3. Name one real case that tests C2: did elegance ever change a theory's empirical status without new contact?
4. Say whether the isomer claim in C3 is conditional on a shared atom set in a way the figure supports.
5. Send the refutation. A failure is more useful than an endorsement.
