# The Tether's answer on elegance versus testable predictions

**Mode: `general` on the refusal of the common scale, `specified` on each office assignment. Not `grounded`.** This is a shape and a route, not a result. Simulated derivation checked against the figures (`05_what_shape_means.md` section 6), not a ground reading.

## 1. The residual it answers

The question asks for a weight: how much should elegance matter compared with predictions? In effect terms, it asks whether the cost of a description, |phi|, and the residual against observation, R = |Gamma(b,a) - o'|, can be traded on one scale. The figures answer before any coefficient is offered: they are two currencies in different units and they do not add (Fig 12 :125, :126, :130). The residual is therefore a category error in the framework's terms, and the answer is a shape that refuses the scale rather than a number on it.

## 2. The shape, per domain, never averaged

1. **The budget office (elegance).** Elegance is a price in bits: description length, compression, the cost of stating phi. It is legitimate in the mint gate and as a search heuristic. Fig 5 :42: *"The acceptance test is a bargain, not a threshold."* Fig 5 :26: *"does the new term cost less to state than the confusion it removes?"* But it is frame-internal and cannot score the verdict: Fig 1 :27 *"They are frame-internal. A frame cannot score itself with a quantity it also produces."* It is a filter for the budget, never a witness for the verdict (Fig 9 :60).
2. **The verdict office (predictions).** Testable predictions are the anchor and the only metric. Fig 2 :29 *"An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks."* Fig 1 :23 *"The ground enters as the only metric."* Elegance cannot enter here; a more elegant theory whose predictions are unchanged has not moved toward the ground (Fig 11 :40).
3. **The community office (theory choice by peers).** Aesthetic consensus is a convention, not corroboration. Fig 2 :35 *"Alignment is triangulation, not negotiation. A mirror cannot correct you."* Fig 7 :35 *"A room's agreement is variation, not a verdict. Selection between rooms is where the ground votes."* If the cost of being wrong is high, buy independence, not agreement (Fig 2 :42).
4. **The heuristic sub-office.** Elegance can aim the probe: the curiosity drive *"seeks the gap that is large and compressible"* (Fig 5 :18), and theory may precede its instrument. But the prediction, not the beauty, is what told anyone where to point the instruments (Fig 6 :38). Describe the residual first or any frame will seem to fit (Fig 9 :28).
5. **The refusal.** Fig 12 :130 *"Summing them into one figure is the error this names, not the fix for it. A price in bits and a price in actions are two readings, held apart."* A coefficient for elegance is not available from the figures; that is a verdict, not a silence (Fig 13 :134).

## 3. What it explicitly does not say

- Not that elegance is worthless. It is a budget term and a useful heuristic.
- Not that beauty has never led to a discovery. That is a ground question for case studies, and the pro-elegance quote (Dirac, Scientific American 1963) is a discovery-context claim.
- Not that prediction is the only virtue a theory can have. It is the only one that settles the verdict in this framework.
- Not that a number is hidden somewhere. No figure line supplies one, and inventing one would be the error Fig 12 :130 names.
- Not `grounded`. No measurement of theory-choice outcomes was taken.

## 4. What would settle it - the ground

A discriminating instrument or a provenanced case study, not a further argument. From *The Hard Problem is About Source*, a reading cannot comment on its source; the colour case was settled *"not by argument, but by the arrival of a better instrument."* Here:

- **Coefficient test:** a study in which different weightings of elegance produce different, checkable predictions about which theory is accepted, and the ground pays for one. Without this, a weight is a preference.
- **Discriminating instrument:** a measurement that separates two empirically equivalent theories whose elegance differs, so the source of the shared reading is named. That would move the isomer claim from structural to grounded.
- **Historical refutation:** a case where elegance alone, with no change in prediction, moved a theory from candidate to settled. That would break Fig 1 :23 and Fig 11 :40.
- **Standing refuters:** a figure line that prices bits and actions on one additive scale; a demonstration that the compression gate *is* the verdict.

## 5. The honest stop

**Unreached, not unreachable.** The framework prices two currencies and refuses their sum; it does not deliver a weighting, and Fig 9 :32 says *"you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself."* Fig 13 :134 makes the stop a verdict: *"Unreachable is a verdict here, not a silence,"*. This derivation stops at the limit of what the figures distinguish and records the lineage. The historical question of whether elegance has in fact been a reliable guide remains live and is owned by the ground, not by this simulation.

## 6. One-paragraph version for outreach

> The Tether does not give a number for how much elegance should matter. It says the question puts two different currencies on one scale. Elegance is a price in bits, the cost and compression of a description; testable predictions are a price in actions, finding out whether the description holds. The figures say these do not add and that summing them is the error, not the fix. So elegance has a real office in the budget and in aiming the probe, and predictions have the office of settling the claim: the ground is the only metric and the anchor. A more elegant theory with unchanged predictions has not moved toward the ground, and a community that admires the same beautiful theory has a shared evidence pool, not corroboration. What the framework offers is the shape any answer must have, refuters written in advance, and the instrument that would close it. The coefficient stays open until the ground pays for one.
