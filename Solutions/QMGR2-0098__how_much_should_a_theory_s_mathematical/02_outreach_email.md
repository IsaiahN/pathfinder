**Subject:** Check one output on elegance versus prediction before you decide about the claim

> Hi [name],
>
> I have one specific output I would like you to check against what you already know. I am not asking you to read a theory first, and I am not asking you to agree with it. If the output does not survive, there is nothing else to look at.
>
> **The question I started with:** how much should a theory's mathematical elegance matter compared with its testable predictions?
>
> **The output (`general` on the structure, `specified` on the offices):** the question asks for a weight on one scale, but the framework I am testing prices two different currencies. Elegance is a price in *bits*: description length, compression, the cost of stating the theory. Predictions are a price in *actions*: finding out whether the theory holds. The source line I am checking says the two "do not add" and that "a price in bits and a price in actions are two readings, held apart" (Fig 12 :125-130). So the output is a refusal of the scale, not a number: elegance earns a place in the *budget* (what is worth trying, where to aim the probe), and predictions alone earn the *verdict* (what the ground settles). A source line used for the budget is never allowed to hand over the verdict (Fig 9 :60).
>
> **What I am not claiming:** that elegance is worthless, that beauty has never guided a discovery, or that I can give you a coefficient. Elegance is a legitimate search heuristic and a legitimate compression term. What I am claiming is narrower: it cannot be the thing that settles whether a theory is true, and a more elegant theory whose predictions have not changed has not moved toward the ground (Fig 11 :40).
>
> **What would refute the output:**
> 1. a source line that prices description length and predictive contact on one additive scale;
> 2. a case where elegance alone, with no change in prediction, moved a theory from candidate to settled;
> 3. a demonstration that the compression gate *is* the verdict rather than a gate.
>
> I wrote these down before sending this. If any one holds, the output is wrong and I will retract it. A one-line "this fails because..." is more useful to me than agreement. If you would rather not engage, no reply is needed.
>
> **If it survives, and only then:** the sources are below. The figures are the authority, not my summary of them.
> - Local verbatim figure text with line numbers: `FRAMEWORK/reference/03-FIGURES_TEXT.md` (the section titled "Fig 12 - The Bond" and "Fig 1 - The Agent")
> - Canonical usage rule: `FRAMEWORK/reference/FIGURES_README_canonical.md`
> - The eight-step loop and its mode rule: `FRAMEWORK/figures/THE_FORMULA.md`
>
> **If you want to use the machinery:** write down what would refute your own result before you run it. A reported failure is more useful to me than a reported success.
>
> Thanks,
> [name]
