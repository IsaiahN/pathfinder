## What the wider literature bears on the question

1. **The pro-elegance case is a discovery claim, not a verdict claim.** Dirac's famous line is embedded in a passage about working from a sound insight and expecting an incomplete fit to be *"cleared up with further development of the theory."* That is the heuristic office (D1b in the audit), not the settlement office (D2). Reading it as a verdict claim sends us back to the census, not to a flipped verdict (audit section 6).
2. **The anti-overreliance case is about the cost of being wrong.** Hossenfelder's target is not elegance itself but continuing to rely on a beautiful hypothesis after data disagree. Butterfield's counter-position, quoted in her post, is that beauty is advocated as a *"fallible heuristic."* Both sides agree on the distinction the figures draw; they disagree about how often it is respected in practice. That disagreement is a ground question for case studies, not a figure question.
3. **Empirical equivalence cannot be settled by elegance.** The metamerism point from *The Hard Problem is About Source* is that several sources can create the same reading. Two theories can have the same predictive surface and different internals. A preference for the more elegant one does not discriminate them; only a better instrument does. This is the supporting derivation in `06_isomers_and_order.md`.

## The two-stage offer

- **Stage 1 before stage 2** is Fig 10 :52 - *"Bring a characterised residual, never the source"* - and Fig 2 :45 - *"Frames range against the ground. They do not negotiate with it."* The recipient checks the output without holding the framework's interior.
- **Stage 2 is opt-in by construction.** Once a recipient has absorbed the theory, their agreement stops being evidence (Fig 2 :40). The stage-1 verdict must be recorded before any theory is shown, and later agreement is never pooled as corroboration.
- **Stage 3 sends a method, which is permitted.** Fig 4 :22. The formula is a generator, so giving it away does not give away an answer.
- **Stage 3 still needs a ground per problem.** Formula "Direction": a frame cannot check the direction of its own question, so each user writes down what would refute their result before running it and states the mode of what they get.

**Hard precondition:** stages 1 to 3 only work if the stage-1 output survives. As drafted, the question itself presupposes a single scale, which is the defect the output names. Keep the refusal and the refuter in front of the links.
