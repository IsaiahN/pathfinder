## The question restated as an effect

What is being decided is not a coefficient but an **office assignment**. A frame Gamma predicts an observation, Gamma(b,a); the observation o' returns; the residual R = |Gamma(b,a) - o'| is the gap (Symbols table :38-40). Two things are on the table:

- **Mathematical elegance**, which is a property of the description phi itself, its description length |phi|, its compression, the price of stating it (Fig 5 :26, Fig 12 :125-126). It is generated by the frame that proposes it.
- **Testable predictions**, which are the frame's contact with o', the anchor, the only metric (Fig 1 :23, Fig 2 :29-30).

- Option A, "elegance is a strong or decisive criterion for theory choice": leads to the prediction that a better description can be preferred even when its contact with the ground has not changed.
- Option B, "testable predictions are decisive and elegance has a different office": leads to the prediction that elegance prices the *budget* (what is worth trying, how cheaply a term can be carried) and never enters the *verdict* (what the ground settles).

What an answer would look like, written down and bound as an ordinary term before the run (Formula, "Direction"): a **shape** that (1) assigns each quantity an office, (2) refuses the single-scale trade-off the question presupposes, and (3) names an instrument that could discriminate the two cases. Until that shape is bound it predicts nothing and no check in the loop can see it.

The verdict recorded in `09_watson_answer.md` is Option B in `specified` mode, kept per domain and never averaged. The strongest single line is Fig 12 :130: *"Summing them into one figure is the error this names, not the fix for it. A price in bits and a price in actions are two readings, held apart."*

## What is deliberately not here

- No personal emails of named individuals.
- No ranking of people by how likely they are to agree.
- No bulk-send plan.
- No claim that this derivation is `grounded`, no claim that it settles how much elegance should matter, and no claim that the framework is the only true account of theory choice.

Three figure-grounded reasons, plus one practical one:

1. A room is not an anchor. Fig 7 :35 *"A room's agreement is variation, not a verdict. Selection between rooms is where the ground votes."* and Fig 2 :35 *"Alignment is triangulation, not negotiation. A mirror cannot correct you."* Gathering agreeable readers manufactures the correlation that disqualifies a verdict.
2. Correlation has valence. Fig 2 :40 *"disqualifying for a verdict: agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool"* A list selected for receptiveness is one witness wearing many names.
3. A verifier must be outside the interior. Fig 10 :42 *"A verifier that can reconstruct the claim cannot verify it."* The first contact therefore carries the residual and its refuter, not the framework.
4. Legally, unsolicited bulk email to scraped addresses breaches GDPR and CAN-SPAM style rules in many jurisdictions and can get the sending domain blacklisted. Public opt-in routes avoid that failure as well as the structural one.
