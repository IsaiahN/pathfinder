# Outreach draft — validate the shape first, then decide about the framework

**Use:** one-to-one, or as a cover letter attached to a submission through the public venues in `03_public_contact_points.csv`. **Not** for bulk send.

**Hard precondition:** do not send until the claim is checked against the stage-1 shape below. The output must survive validation or the two-stage offer collapses on contact.

---

**Subject:** Check one shape about method portability before you decide whether the framework is worth reading

> Hi [name],
>
> I have one specific output I would like you to check against what you already know about methods moving between places. I am not asking you to read the framework first, and I am not asking you to agree with it. If the output does not survive, there is nothing else to look at.
>
> **The output (`specified`, not `grounded`):** portability is not a property a method owns. It is a conjunction of two conditions, and the two have different failure modes.
>
> 1. **What crosses must be a generator, not a recording.** A fixed recipe that worked once is a description of one occasion. Replayed somewhere else it repeats a past success and cannot produce a new one. What can cross is the compressed, verified, reapplicable method and the head start it gives the next searcher. The receiving place remakes whatever lands there.
> 2. **The receiving habitat must be reproduced, not assumed.** Moving a method to a new place is a habitat substitution, not an object moving into an empty room. Two failures are silent: you do not notice what you failed to reproduce until the goal fails, and you do not notice the new actors you unintentionally introduced until they act. A pilot site proves the wiring, not the capability in the real place.
>
> The description has to travel in effect terms, with its lineage rather than just its ingredient list. The loss on the round trip from source to target and back is a quantity to measure, not to assume away. The ground of the receiving place settles whether the method paid there. Adoption counts and practitioner agreement do not.
>
> **What I am not claiming:** that any particular method is universally portable, that local knowledge is inherently superior, that a method's prestige helps it port, or that this framework settles the history of a particular transfer. It returns a shape, not a name: it will not tell you which protocol will work in which place until a ground reading there pays for it.
>
> **What would refute the shape:**
> 1. a method keeps paying in a new place while only a fixed recipe crossed, with no reapplicable generator and no head start;
> 2. a method's performance survives a habitat substitution that removes a condition its goal required, and the new habitat's unchosen actors measurably do not affect the outcome;
> 3. a method transported in source-only terms, with no lineage recorded, still keeps paying against a measured target ground;
> 4. a measured round trip matches exactly, so the loss is zero across a real place change;
> 5. a method keeps paying while its practitioners never contact a differing closure and only optimise inside their own library.
>
> I wrote these down before sending this. If any one holds, the shape is wrong and I will retract it. A one-line "this fails because..." is more useful to me than agreement. If you would rather not engage, no reply is needed.
>
> **If it survives, and only then:** the sources are below. The figures are the authority, not my summary of them.
> - Figures and formula for direct download (SVG): https://github.com/IsaiahN/tether/tree/general-agent/figures
> - Canonical usage rule: https://github.com/IsaiahN/tether/blob/general-agent/figures/README.md
> - Full architecture (Medium): https://medium.com/@IsaiahNwukor/arrival-of-the-fittest-the-tether-architecture-1de7edf641ec
>
> **If you want to use the machinery:** the formula is in the figures link. Run it on a method transfer you know well and can check against a ground in the receiving place. Write down what would refute your result *before* you run it, and state the mode of what you get. A reported failure is more useful to me than a reported success.
>
> — [name]
