## Q1 — Are two methods with the same components isomers?

> **"isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."** (Fig 12 :117)
> **"functional group. A substructure whose presence fixes the outcome. The group says what kind; the qualifier says which member."** (Fig 12 :124)
> **"A set of ingredients is not one. The bond is the rest of the information, and it is the half nothing was recording."** (Fig 12 :5)

Two methods with an identical list of steps, tools, skills and documents have the same atoms. If their bonds differ, that is, if one composes those atoms as a fixed sequence replayed in the same order and the other as a reconfigurable loop that re-reads the ground, they are **isomers** under Fig 12 :117: same atoms, different bond, different behaviour, with the difference invisible in the ingredient list. That is the figure-grounded version of the intuition that "it is not what a method contains, it is how the parts are arranged", but stronger: the arrangement is itself a substance and carries information the ingredients do not.

If the methods share only a pattern (both do "diagnose, intervene, check") while differing in which atoms occupy the slots, the weaker claim holds: they are members of the same **functional group** (Fig 12 :124), the qualifier saying which member. The presence of the pattern fixes the kind, not the portability.

The distinction is testable. An ingredient-list comparison that finds no difference between two methods cannot distinguish isomer from functional-group; the bond has to be recorded. FRAME's requirement to report *"when and how in the implementation process the modification was made"* and the *"reasons for the modification"* is the world-source version of recording the bond.

**Falsifier, written before the run:** if two methods with identical atoms and identical bonds nevertheless diverge in portability with no difference in ground contact or imported atoms, the isomer framing is insufficient and the difference must be habitat (D2), which moves the explanation out of D1/D3.

**Status:** `specified`, triangulated for the legality of the reading (Fig 12 and Fig 11 :50, different figures), single-source for the world application. The world corroboration is FRAME's function-over-form distinction, which is one witness from implementation science.

## Q2 — What order do the conditions bind in?

> **"The chain is ordered by dependency, not by importance. Each link consumes what the one above produces, so effort spent above the break arrives at a step that cannot use it."** (Fig 3 :20-21)
> **"Optimising a link that already works cannot rescue one that does not."** (Fig 3 :19)
> **"A link with no instrument is not a link that works. It is a link nobody has looked at."** (Fig 3 :27)

Order for a method arriving in a new place:

1. **Perception.** Can the receiver read the target ground at all? Failure here is visible and has obvious tests.
2. **Vocabulary.** Does the receiving Γ have terms for what the new situation affords, and can it say what would count as progress there? This is the middle link. Fig 3 :26: *"having terms for what a situation offers and being able to say what would count as progress, has neither, so it attracts the least attention and the fewest instruments."*
3. **Objective.** Can it state a comparison that would count as getting somewhere? Fig 12 :112: *"A plan is a sequence. A goal is a comparison. Progress is a subtraction."* A vocabulary with only ingredients cannot state a goal.
4. **Planning.** A route to that objective under incomplete information.
5. **Learn and carry.** Name what worked, verify it, carry it to the next place.

The two conditions from the primary audit map onto this chain: C2 (habitat reproduced) is mostly a failure of links 1 and 2; C1 (generator crosses) is mostly a failure of link 5 and the membrane. That mapping is why a single lever cannot clear both (Fig 9 :55).

The order also decides which instrument to buy first. Fig 3 :27 names the problem: the ground-facing link (1) usually has measurement infrastructure, while link 5 almost never does. Budget naturally piles up at links 1 and 4 while the break sits at 2 and 5.

**Falsifier:** a documented transport whose perception, vocabulary and objective links all measure as working and that fails only at planning or execution. That would falsify the "usually middle" claim of F7 in the primary audit.

## Q3 — Competing explanations, and what each can and cannot reach

| candidate explanation | what it names | figure-line bearing | what it cannot reach |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Diffusionism** (a true method spreads because it is true) | the method's correctness as a free-standing property | X1 fails the anchor test; Fig 2 :29 names a ground, not a reputation | Why the same method pays in one place and not another; no contact variable |
| **Universal best practice / standardisation** | the method as a recipe that works anywhere | Fig 4 :25: *"A recording carried upward looks like knowledge and is a description of one occasion"* | Cannot say what was compressed or what the round trip dropped; replay gives repetition, not new success |
| **Pure local knowledge** (only local context matters) | the receiving habitat alone | D2 is real, but Fig 6 :23 requires contact with a differing closure for reach to move | Cannot explain renewal: a habitat with no import keeps its closure and stops (Fig 8 :32) |
| **Fidelity to the original** | adherence to the protocol as written | FRAME's fidelity-consistent versus fidelity-inconsistent distinction is a world binding | FRAME itself says function can be prioritised over form, so fidelity alone cannot decide which elements are core in a new population |
| **Prestige of the adopting institution** | reputation and number of adoptions | Fig 2 :35: *"Alignment is triangulation, not negotiation. A mirror cannot correct you"* | Prestige can fund a trial, not settle it (essay, RSI Trap); excluded from verdict weight |
| **Trial result transports directly** (external validity as automatic) | the recorded estimate | Deaton and Cartwright: *"credibility in estimation can lead to incredibility in use"* | Needs selection diagrams or mechanism knowledge; Pearl and Bareinboim make the differences explicit |
| **Luck / unmeasured local condition** | an unearned early reading that persists while unfuted | Fig 8 :40: *"Underivable-in-principle and unreachable-at-this-budget are different facts"* | Cannot be excluded from inside a frame; Fig 8 :39: *"So a search that finds nothing is never a proof of absence."* Honest `gap`, not a rival verdict |

No candidate is discarded by prestige or familiarity. Each is ranged against the same residual, in the order Fig 8 :53 requires. The first five are bindings from overlapping historical and methodological frames; the three low-overlap fields (statistics, implementation science, history of science) corroborate the mechanism, not a specific transfer.

## Q4 — Does the shape generate the literature's answers?

Partly. The shape can generate:

- the diffusionist emphasis on a method's truth, as a Γ operand whose settlement must still be re-tested in the target place;
- the standardisation emphasis on compression, as C1 and Fig 4 :22-23, with the round-trip gap as the cost;
- the local-knowledge emphasis on habitat, as C2 and Fig 11 :66-70;
- the fidelity emphasis on core elements, as Fig 12 :124 functional group and FRAME's function-over-form rule;
- the external-validity emphasis on population differences, as Fig 6 :30 and Pearl and Bareinboim's selection diagrams.

It cannot generate the *name* of the specific method that will work in a named place. That is the `gap` recorded in F9 of `01`. It is a gap at the instrument boundary, not a silence: Fig 13 :134 (*"Unreachable is a verdict here, not a silence,"*) and Fig 8 :39 apply. The essay's source rule states why: an instrument that reads a result *"cannot comment on the source"*, and *"the answer is unreached until a better instrument exists"*.

## CONCLUSION

Two methods with the same atoms but different bonds are isomers under Fig 12 :117; two methods sharing only a `state → routine → ground` pattern are members of one functional group under Fig 12 :124. The conditions bind in dependency order, with the middle link (vocabulary and objective) and the carry link the usual breaks, which is why C1 and C2 need different instruments. The standard transfer explanations are mostly bindings of overlapping pools and are generated by the shape only up to the point where a name would be required. The isomer claim has a written falsifier; the order claim has one; the naming gap is declared rather than filled.
