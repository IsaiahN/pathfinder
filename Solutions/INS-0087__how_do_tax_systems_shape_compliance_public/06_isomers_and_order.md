## DERIVATION

### Q1  -  Dependency order inside a slot

Fig 3 :20: *"The chain is ordered by dependency, not by importance."*
Fig 3 :27: *"A link with no instrument is not a link that works. It is a link nobody has looked at."*

For a compliance slot the chain reads: perceive the assessment and one's own stake → have terms for what the system affords (pay, contest, delay, conceal) → say what counts as progress (a correct return, a settled liability) → plan a response → learn and carry to the next year. For the capacity slot the same five links run through the authority. For the fairness slot they run through the citizen's experience of procedure.

The break is usually between the second and third links: there are many instruments for perception (notices, statements) and for outcomes (payments, revenue, surveys), but few for "what does this base afford" and "what would count as fair here." Fig 3 :26 says this middle link attracts the least attention and the fewest instruments. A reading taken below the break is a reading of nothing (Fig 3 :23), so an improvement effort aimed at planning or outcome metrics cannot rescue a broken objective link.

**Mode `specified`. Refuter:** a documented instrument returning a reading on "what this base affords" and "what counts as fair" where none existed, with no change to the links above it.

### Q2  -  Union structure and the shape of the split

Fig 9 :55: *"A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones."*
Fig 9 :57: *"What remains is smaller and it is also categorically different: a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either."*

Write the four slots as conditions C1 (compliance), C2 (capacity), C3 (distribution), C4 (fairness). They overlap: the same household appears in C1, C3 and C4; the same record is an atom in C1 and C2. A lever that clears the region where only C1 holds leaves the intersections standing, because C4 still binds. The residue after such a lever is smaller and categorically different, a specific compound rather than a harder case of C1. That is why the four verdicts are never averaged and why a single "tax system quality" score is a filter for the budget, not a witness for the verdict (Fig 9 :60).

**Refuter:** one intervention restoring all four slots with no categorically different residue.

### Q3  -  Are two tax designs isomers?

Fig 12 :117: *"isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."*
Fig 12 :99: *"A bond costs what it could have been, and nothing more."*

Let design A be a graduated income tax with an employer withholding record and a refundable credit. Let design B be a flat consumption tax with a uniform rebate and the same information instruments (records, reporters, audits). If the full atom set is the same (rate, base, threshold, exemption, record, reporter, audit, penalty), the two designs are **isomers**: same atoms, different bonds, different substance. The statutory formula records the ingredients but not the arrangement, so nothing downstream can tell them apart from the schedule alone (Fig 12 :14-16), while their compliance, revenue, distribution and fairness behavior differs.

If the designs use *different* atom sets (A needs an employer record that B cannot reach), they are not isomers; they are different compositions with different reach, and the comparison is governed by Fig 6 instead. The claim worth testing is the full-set isomer claim, not the loose "both are taxes" claim.

World corroboration (secondary, public-finance pool): the *Tax incidence* reference states that *"the tax burden does not depend on where the revenue is collected, but on the price elasticity of demand and price elasticity of supply."* Nominal and economic incidence differ despite identical statutory atoms, which is the incidence-level version of the same structural point.

**Falsifier, written before the run:** if two designs over the full shared atom set, differing only in incidence arrangement, produce identical per-slot residuals in compliance, capacity, distribution and fairness, the isomer claim fails and the weaker "same functional group" reading stands instead.

### Q4  -  What is the operand type of the comparison bond?

Fig 12 :87: *"a threshold relation between two quantities"*

At the citizen level, "fair" compares two quantities: my burden and a reference burden (neighbour, previous year, benefit received). The operands are quantities, so `⋛` is typed correctly at first order and there is no strain. At the system level, comparing "compliance" and "fairness" would be comparing two settled molecules now serving as atoms. Fig 12 :104 lets a settled molecule become an atom, but whether that atom counts as a quantity at the next level needs an instrument, not an argument. **This is a live strain and is named, not hidden.** It is the same strain the exemplar records for ⋛ at second order, carried here to a different domain.

**Refuter:** an instrument that returns a quantity reading for two settled institutional molecules at the same level, or a decision that ⋛'s operands must be bare quantities at every level.

### Q5  -  Do the low-overlap analogues change the shape?

Two analogues were opened, both outside the public-finance evidence pool:

- **Common-pool resource institutions.** The *Elinor Ostrom* reference lists "collective-choice arrangements: most individuals affected by the operational rules can participate in modifying the operational rules," "monitoring," and "graduated sanctions" among the design principles of long-enduring institutions. This corroborates the union shape (no panacea) and the valence shape (sanctions are habitat-relative) at the level of ecological and institutional design.
- **Altruistic punishment.** Fehr and Gächter (*Nature*, 2002) report that *"cooperation flourishes if altruistic punishment is possible, and breaks down if it is ruled out."* This corroborates Fig 11 :39-40: capability is a property of agent-and-habitat, and an instrument's effect depends on whether the habitat supports it.

Neither analogue decides anything. They are low-overlap witnesses for the mechanism shape, and their agreement is not a second ground reading. A conflict from either would send us back to the census, not flip a figure verdict.

## CONCLUSION

The four slots form a union with a dependency order that usually breaks at the objective link. Two designs over the same atom set but different incidence arrangements are isomers in Fig 12's exact sense, with a falsifier stated before the run. The comparison bond is well typed at the citizen level and strained at the system level; the strain is recorded. The two low-overlap analogues corroborate the shape without deciding it. The full falsifier set for this file: (1) identical per-slot residuals for full-set incidence isomers, (2) a capacity gain with no new atom in reach, (3) an intervention restoring all four slots with no compound residue, (4) a quantity instrument for settled institutional molecules clearing the ⋛ strain.
