## 1. A shape is a constraint, not a filler

The canonical figures README states the limit: the framework reports *"what a missing thing would have to look like (a shape, not a name)."* Fig 9 :32: *"you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself"* Fig 13 :134: *"Unreachable is a verdict here, not a silence,"*

From *The Library of Babel*: *"you can cut your search short if the set of books returned is small and descriptive enough that it provides a general shape of the answer that satisfies your question."*

So for this question a **shape** is the narrowed form of an answer we can state before we can name the design. It constrains; it does not fill. "Four slots with four anchors, kept separate, with capability through contact and reach through import" is a shape. "A flat tax with a broad base and a single rate" is a name. The shape can be true or false; the name is a choice made from outside the framework.

## 2. How a shape is obtained

- *Library of Babel* on vocabulary: use the terms you already have that are nearest to the thing, look for recurring new terms that overlap or correlate with it, and recurse. *"With each new term gained, you are able to narrow the space by definition and deepen your vocabulary."*
- That essay on bundles, which is the key move for INS-0087: *"Perhaps your problem was a bundle or conflation of several problems masquerading as one? You will need to search all of those problems individually to find their answers and then combine them in a satisfactory way."* This is the essay-level counterpart of Fig 9 :55.
- Fig 9 :27-28: *"describe it before you go looking, or any frame you pick will seem to fit"* and :30: *"your words are borrowed too, so describe the effect rather than the cause."* For tax questions the effect terms are "payment observed," "revenue collected," "per-group income share," and "judgment expressed," not "tax morale" or "fiscal citizenship," which are already names from a frame.
- *Library of Babel* on stopping: the finish line is also the starting place, because by then you have *"composed the answer through the process of locating it."* Here that means we stop when the four-slot shape stops registering against the four anchors, not when we have found a named design.

## 3. What licenses a name

Only two things, both outside the shape itself:

1. **The ground settles it.** Formula step 5: the ground settles a term and it is verified only by what pays. For this question that means actual payment records, collected revenue, per-group distributional data, and the expressed judgments of citizens. A gate is not the ground.
2. **A provenanced import from a frame whose closure differs** (Fig 6 :23, Fig 8 :50-52), with a source gate and a shadow test. Naming a rate or a base as "the answer" is an import from the economics frame. It must be recorded as such and it adds no atom to the framework's six-term set.

*The Hard Problem is About Source* sharpens the limit: an instrument that reads green *"cannot comment on the source."* A compliance rate is a reading. It does not tell us whether the source was deterrence, a norm, perceived legitimacy, or a convenient opportunity. *"If several paths create the same behavior, then behavior is a projection with lossy information"*. What is owed is a source-discriminating instrument, not a name assigned to the reading.

## 4. The ground and the source problem

For D1, D2 and D3 the ground is a counter, a dataset or a market outcome, named as an anchor in Fig 2 :29. For D4 the question is constitutively about people, so Fig 2 :37 applies: *"Human judgment is the right anchor for is this a good answer for a person, because the question is about people."* This is the one slot where survey and interview evidence is the ground rather than a merely instrument reading, so its independence from the other three slots matters all the more.

Trust is a channel: *"the ground does not decay, the channel does"* (Fig 10 :24). A fairness perception can be lost through pooled readings and stale percepts even while the underlying treatment is unchanged. And *"Declaring a meaning does not ground it"* (Fig 10 :49): a tax authority's statement that the system is fair is a convention that has decided an outcome, which the framework forbids.

## 5. Answer to the shape question

**What shape does "shape" have here?** The four-slot union shape, stated in `01` and `07`:

1. R is per slot, never a single composite score (Fig 1 :19, :22).
2. The question is a union of overlapping conditions, so split before searching and keep the intersections as compounds (Fig 9 :55, :57; *Library of Babel* on bundles).
3. Compliance and capacity are properties of design-and-habitat, not of either alone (Fig 11 :39, :40).
4. Instrument valence is relative to the residual; there is no standing list of good tax instruments (Fig 11 :49, :51).
5. Capacity is reach: moved only by an import of a new instrument, record or counterparty (Fig 6 :23; Fig 8 :32).
6. Distribution is a per-slot composition in which same atoms under different bonds are isomers (Fig 12 :117).
7. Fairness is anchored in human judgment and maintained as a channel, not declared (Fig 2 :37; Fig 10 :24, :49).
8. The name of the best design is a `gap`, not a hidden answer (Fig 9 :32; Fig 13 :134).

## 5b. Correction to my own §3

I first wrote that the "shape, not a name" rule forbids naming any tax instrument at all. That overstates it. The README's phrase governs a **missing** thing, a primitive not held in Γ. Rates, bases, thresholds, records and audits are bindings from the economics and administration frames onto atoms the framework can already express; naming one is not inventing a seventh term. What remains forbidden is claiming the binding moves the wall (Fig 6 :23) or treating a binding as a derivation. This is Method M6 from the project derivation log, applied here. The source-discrimination point in §3 is **not** retracted: a compliance reading still does not identify its cause without an instrument that distinguishes the causes.

## 6. Consequences for contact

**Prestige is not action.** From *The Recursive Self-Improvement Trap*: *"Prestige can fund an action. It can give an action a budget to be tried. But it cannot settle an action. Only the ground can settle it."* A campaign that gathers well-known names to agree that tax systems shape these outcomes is the named collapse, not corroboration. The legitimate ask is the opposite: give the claim a budget to be tried, and let the ground (a counter, a dataset, a citizen judgment) settle it.

**This whole folder is a simulation, not a ground reading.** The same essay says *"the truly testable output would need to be derived from the real architecture or working the formula yourself"* and calls LLM output a simulacrum. So `01` and `09` are simulated derivations checked for internal consistency against the figures. They are **not** a test of any tax claim and must be labelled as such. They cannot settle the question they report on.
