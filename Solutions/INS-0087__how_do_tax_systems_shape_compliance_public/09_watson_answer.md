# WATSON answer: how do tax systems shape four outcomes?

**Mode: `specified` on the four slot verdicts, `general` on the structural shape, not `grounded`.** Figures are primary and decide. World sources are secondary, corroborate but never overrule, and a conflict returns the derivation to the domain census.

**Status: SIMULATED DERIVATION, not a ground reading.** This run synthesizes material already in `01_figure_grounded_audit.md`, `05_what_shape_means.md`, `06_isomers_and_order.md`, `07_the_tether_answer.md` and `08_expert_verification_packet.md`. It is checked for internal consistency against the figures. By its own rule (*The Recursive Self-Improvement Trap*: *"the truly testable output would need to be derived from the real architecture or working the formula yourself"*) it cannot settle the question it reports on. No ground measurement was taken.

## QUESTION

Decide, in effect terms, what the Tether says about the claim that tax systems shape compliance, public capacity, inequality and perceptions of fairness. Option A: average the four into one claim about "how tax systems shape outcomes." Option B: treat the question as a union of four slots with four anchors, keep the verdicts separate, and record the name of the best design as a `gap`. What each leads to: A produces a composite score in which a compliance gain can cancel a fairness loss and spends the independence of the people asked to check it; B produces a checkable four-slot shape, refuters written in advance, and an open slot until a ground settles it.

## DOMAIN CENSUS

R by arity, symmetry and scale, then one line per domain with actors / currency / anchor, partition or union, and order by dependency.

- **R (the residual).** Four indexed gaps between a designed tax system Γ and observed outcomes: payment, collected revenue, per-group distribution, expressed fairness judgment. Arity: four slots, not one scalar. Symmetry: mixed, with `⋛` comparison between own burden and a reference burden at first order; ordered contribution and benefit relations. Scale: individual return, household, firm, administration, national distribution, intergenerational, with lossy membranes between (Fig 4). Fig 1 :19: *"The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated."*
- **Domain 1, compliance loop.** Actors: taxpayers, audits, penalties, revenue authority. Currency: perceived payoff, deterrence, norm. Anchor: actual payment, a counter (Fig 2 :29). Union member, not a standalone answer.
- **Domain 2, fiscal capacity.** Actors: authority, base, records, third-party reporters. Currency: revenue and reach. Anchor: revenue actually collected and the information instruments available. Union member.
- **Domain 3, distribution.** Actors: households, income groups, incidence. Currency: effective rates, income shares. Anchor: per-group distributional data. Union member.
- **Domain 4, fairness perceptions.** Actors: citizens, norms, procedures, trust. Currency: perceived fairness, legitimacy. Anchor: human judgment, legitimately, because the question is about people (Fig 2 :37). Union member.
- **Domain 5, the designed system as Γ.** Actors: rules, bases, rates, exemptions, instruments. Currency: description length and reach. Anchor: closure(Γ) and the four anchors above.
- **X1, "the optimal tax system" as a single name.** No nameable anchor. Not a domain yet.
- **Union, not partition.** The four conditions overlap; a lever clearing one region leaves the intersections. Order by dependency: perception → vocabulary → objective → planning → learn and carry (Fig 3). The objective link is the one usually without an instrument (Fig 3 :27).

Strain test applied: Fig 11 :39-40 governs capability across D1 and D2 jointly, while their anchors differ. Re-census resolved the apparent strain by keeping the anchors distinct and never averaging them, not by smoothing the figure text.

## VERDICT (from the figures)

Keep the four verdicts separate and never average them. Compliance is settled by payment and is a property of design-and-habitat; capacity is reach moved only by an import of a new information atom; distribution is a per-slot composition in which same atoms under different incidence bonds are isomers; fairness is anchored in human judgment and maintained as a channel, not declared. This is a union of overlapping conditions, so one lever cannot be assumed to clear the intersections. The framework does not name the best design: that remains a declared `gap`, not a hidden answer. The verdict is `specified`, ungrounded, candidate. Low-overlap fields corroborate the mechanism shape, not a tax-policy fact. Which named design actually closes all four slots is outside what the figures decide.

- **cost if wrong:** bounded and locatable. Reporting a single composite "tax systems shape outcomes" score hides a live slot, which is the exact failure Fig 1 :22 names. In outreach terms, an expert's twenty minutes would be spent refuting a shape that was never the right shape. The cost is avoidable by leading with the four-slot claim and its refuter; buying independence in proportion to the cost of being wrong (Fig 2 :42).
- **undoable:** yes. No instrument is deleted if the claim fails; a refutation is added and the reach falls because something was added rather than struck out (Fig 6 amendment). The shape and its five falsifiers are recorded with provenance so a later reader can locate the error rather than only feel it.
- **reversed by:** a ground reading, not agreement. A source-discriminating instrument separating deterrence, norm and legitimacy would name the compliance mechanism; a documented capacity gain with no new atom in reach would break D2; identical per-slot outcomes for incidence isomers would break D3; a fairness judgment moved by declaration alone would break D4; a composite predictor with no live residual would break D1; and a figure re-census yielding a single named design would replace the `gap`. Until one of these fires, the verdict stands as `candidate`.
