# Outreach draft — check one output before you decide about the theory

**Use:** one to one, or as a cover letter attached to a submission through the public venues in `03_public_contact_points.csv`. **Not** for bulk send.

**Hard precondition:** do not send the theory links until the recipient has returned a verdict on stage 1. If they answer the shape claim before seeing the framework, their answer is usable. If they read the framework first, it is not.

---

**Subject:** One checkable claim about chemical gradients and early metabolism

> Hi [name],
>
> I have one specific output I would like you to check against what you already know. I am not asking you to read a theory first, and I am not asking you to agree with anything. If the output does not survive, there is nothing else to look at.
>
> **The output (`specified`, not `grounded`):** the framework I am using holds six terms in two kinds, states (space, time, energy) and structures (spectrum, gradient, network). The gradient is defined as *"a difference that can be spent"*, and the definition adds that a distribution which stays put is not a gradient because it has no directedness. Energy is defined as *"conserved, finite, directionless alone"*. From those two definitions it follows that a chemical difference, by itself, is not what powers a reaction network; the spendable difference composes with energy into a directed flow, and that flow is what can do work. A primitive network then needs two more structural things: a part that survives its own use (a catalyst) and a loop whose output re-enters as input (a cycle). This is a claim about the **shape** any answer must have. It is not a measurement and it does not name a geological site.
>
> **What I am not claiming:** that this happened at Lost City or any other vent; that a laboratory reactor that makes formate has settled the historical question (a test harness proves wiring, not capability); that the figures name which gradient (proton, redox or thermal) did the work. They do not. Which gradient and which network is a ground question, and the published evidence is genuinely contested.
>
> **What would refute the shape claim:**
> 1. a self-maintaining reaction network demonstrated with no spendable difference anywhere across it;
> 2. a spendable difference shown not to compose with energy into a directed flow at the relevant scale;
> 3. no catalyst that survives its own use, or no cycle that can close, under the claimed conditions;
> 4. a measurement showing that no plausible habitat maintains such a difference at the relevant scale.
>
> I wrote these down before running the derivation. If any one holds, I retract the corresponding part. A one-line "this fails because..." is more useful to me than agreement. If you would rather not engage, no reply is needed.
>
> **What I would like from you:** what would you count as a failure of that shape claim? What measurement, not what argument, would distinguish a maintained natural gradient from one supplied by an external pump?
>
> **If it survives, and only then:** here are the sources. The figures, not my summary, are the authority.
> - Verbatim figure text with line numbers (local framework file): `FRAMEWORK/reference/03-FIGURES_TEXT.md`
> - Canonical usage rule (local framework file): `FRAMEWORK/reference/FIGURES_README_canonical.md`
> - The eight-step loop and the mode rule (local framework file): `FRAMEWORK/figures/THE_FORMULA.md`
> - A peer-reviewed reactor study of natural proton gradients in simulated alkaline vents: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4247476/
> - The 1978 Nobel press release on Mitchell's chemiosmotic theory: https://www.nobelprize.org/prizes/chemistry/1978/press-release/
>
> **If you want to use the machinery:** the loop is in the formula link. Run it on a problem you know well and can check against a ground. Write down what would refute your result before you run it. A reported failure is more useful to me than a reported success.
>
> — [name]
