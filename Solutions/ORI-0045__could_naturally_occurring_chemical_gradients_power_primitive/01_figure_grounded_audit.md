## 0. The question under audit, restated as an effect

Question ORI-0045: *"Could naturally occurring chemical gradients power primitive metabolic networks?"*

An answer is needed to a specific effect, not to a vocabulary dispute. A primitive metabolic network must do three things that are hard to get for free: (1) harvest usable energy from what is around it; (2) keep itself going rather than relaxing to equilibrium; (3) do both before it has encoded enzymes, membranes built by itself, or a genetic system to inherit the trick. The residual is the gap between what such a network needs and what bare chemistry supplies:

> **R:** A mixture of H2, CO2, FeS minerals and water, left to itself, does not by itself constitute a directed, self-maintaining flow. Energy is *"conserved, finite, directionless alone"* (Fig 13 :14). What supplies the directedness, and what keeps the supply in contact with the network long enough for the network to persist?

The ordinary term to bind is **"power"**. In effect terms, "power" here means: a spendable difference across a barrier that composes with available energy into a flow, where the flow sustains reaction cycles and mineral catalysts that survive their own use. That is the shape the answer would have. Bind it now, before searching, because a frame cannot check the direction of its own question otherwise (Formula, "Direction").

Two options and what each leads to:

- **Option A, yes as a fact:** asserts that naturally occurring gradients did power the first networks. This asks the figures to name a historical event, which they cannot do, and it outruns the world evidence, which is contested.
- **Option B, yes as a shape with conditions, historical case open:** the figures license the mechanism (a held structure composing with energy into a flow) and the minimum conditions (a maintained supply, a barrier, a catalyst that survives, a cycle that closes). They do not name which gradient or which network. This is the defensible reading.

## 4. Findings

### F1 — The gradient is a held primitive structure, not an import (D1, `specified`, triangulated)

Fig 13 :21-22 lists the six and names the gradient:

> **"gradient"** / **"a difference that can be spent"**

Symbols table, same definition independently phrased:

> **"A difference that can be spent. Not merely a distribution: what makes it a gradient is directedness."**

Fig 13 :45-47 gives the argument rather than an assumption:

> **"The gradient is the thin case, so it is argued rather than assumed."**
> **"An ordering varying across space gives a distribution, and a distribution is a fact that stays put. What makes a gradient a gradient is that it can be spent, and neither an ordering nor a span of space has directedness."**
> **"If the whole carries a property neither part has, the whole is not the parts arranged."**

So the answer to the existence part of the question is not an extrapolation from biology. The gradient is already in the set the loop assumes throughout. Asking whether a chemical gradient can do work is asking whether a held structure can do what it is defined to do. Fig 13 :41: *"Each is indivisible: no arrangement of the other five yields it."*

**Mode:** `specified` on the composition claim, `general` on the placement in the six. **Triangulation:** Fig 13 and the Symbols table are two statements of the definition in different wording, and the Fig 13 derivation gives a reason (directedness is absent from both an ordering and a span of space). Overlap is high in subject but the argument is not a copy, so this is weak triangulation, not two independent witnesses. **Refuter:** derive gradient from the other five terms (would break Fig 13 :41), or show a spendable difference with no directedness (would break the Symbols definition).

### F2 — Energy alone cannot do it; energy plus gradient composes a flow (D1, `specified`, triangulated)

Fig 13 :14: **"conserved, finite, directionless alone"** (energy). Fig 13 :69-71, the removal test for the gradient:

> **"no direction to drive the flow, and no remainder left after each step"**

Fig 13 :76 gives the composition:

> **"Energy and a gradient compose a flow. A gradient over space and one over time are different terms, because which state it is bonded to is part of what it is."**

This is the precise figure-grounded answer to "power". The chemical free energy of H2 plus CO2 is the energy; the pH or redox or thermal difference across the barrier is the gradient; their composition is a directed flow (protons, electrons, heat). Fig 13 :74: *"Everything else is a composition across the two kinds, never within one."* A gradient is a structure, energy is a state, so the composition is legal; joining two gradients or two energies directly would not be.

**Mode:** `specified`. **Triangulation:** the positive composition (Fig 13 :76) and the removal test (Fig 13 :69-71) are different bearings inside the same figure, corroborated outside the framework by non-equilibrium thermodynamics (Kondepudi et al. 2020), which treats thermodynamic forces as gradients of intensive variables and flows as the corresponding time derivatives. That world source is low overlap with the origins-of-life pool, so it is the strongest secondary corroborator here. **Refuter:** a demonstrated self-maintaining reaction network at equilibrium, with no spendable difference anywhere across it, would break :46 and :71.

### F3 — The network needs a catalyst that survives and a cycle that closes (D2, `specified`, single-source)

Fig 12 :119: **"A part that survives its own use. Spent and regenerating parts behave differently across uses, and nothing records which a term is."** Fig 12 :121: **"The output re-enters as input. The sign of the loop decides whether it closes, and a chain cannot close by construction."**

A primitive metabolic network is not just a set of reactions. In the framework's vocabulary it needs at least a catalyst (Fe(Ni)S mineral surfaces are the candidate; Herschy et al. 2014 report *"catalytic Fe(Ni)S microcrystals"* precipitating in the reactor) and a cycle whose output re-enters as input. Fig 12 :111: *"A vocabulary without bonds can name a state and cannot name a route to one."* A list of metabolites is not a route; the arrangement is the substance.

Fig 11 :36 is also relevant and is often missed: **"Where nothing maintains anything, intelligence is a label applied afterwards to a lineage that met its ground. Nothing was intelligent; selection ran."** Applied here, a pre-enzyme network does not need a maintainer or a designer in order to be selected. "Metabolism" is the name applied afterwards to a reaction network that met its ground. This is the figure's own metabolism-first shape, and it is why the question is not incoherent.

**Mode:** `specified`. **Triangulation:** single-source on the figure side (Fig 12 only). The world corroboration (Mitchell's chemiosmosis; the Fe(Ni)S reactor) comes from the bioenergetics pool and shares assumptions with the claim, so it is one witness, not two. **Refuter:** show that no mineral surface can survive its own use under the claimed habitat conditions, or that no primitive cycle can close (a chain that cannot close by construction), and D2 collapses.

### F4 — Capability is network plus habitat, never network alone (D3, `specified`, triangulated)

Fig 11 :39: **"capability is a property of agent-and-habitat, never of the agent alone."** Fig 11 :40: **"An improvement that does not change contact changes nothing, however much it improves."** Fig 11 :43-44: **"The habitat is enumerated, never composed."** / **"List everything in contact with the residual, then what is in contact with those, and outward until the cascade stops mattering."**

The habitat here is not decorative. It is what maintains the difference. Fig 11 :46 lists *"conditions: light, water, temperature, season, terrain"*; for this question the enumerated conditions are vent fluid composition, ocean pH, temperature, mineralogy and flow. Fig 11 :48: *"You do not invent the list. You read it off the world, and what you cannot perceive or measure yet is the residual."*

The channel, not the ground, is the fragile thing. Fig 10 :24: **"the ground does not decay, the channel does"**, with the failure modes at Fig 10 :25-26: *"stale percepts, dropped transients, saturated metrics, pooled readings"*. If the flow stops renewing the gradient, the network has no drive. A laboratory reactor that holds a gradient by pumping is a substituted habitat: Fig 11 :70, *"A test harness is a substituted habitat. Which is why a synthetic solve proves wiring and never capability."* This is why Herschy et al.'s reactor result is evidence for wiring, not evidence that the historical event happened.

**Mode:** `specified`. **Triangulation:** Fig 11 (habitat) and Fig 10 (channel decay) are two different figures with different objects, so the "supply must be maintained" verdict is triangulated within the framework. **Refuter:** a network demonstrated in a substituted habitat whose gradient is maintained only by the pump must not be reported as `grounded`; doing so would break Fig 11 :70. A demonstration of a self-maintaining gradient with no external supply would break Fig 10 :24.

### F5 — This is a union compound; one lever does not clear it (all domains, `general`)

Fig 9 :54: **"And when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again."** Fig 9 :55: **"A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones."** Fig 9 :57: **"What remains is smaller and it is also categorically different: a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either."**

The four domains in §2 overlap. A pH gradient without a catalytic barrier (D1 without D2) does not drive carbon fixation; a barrier without a maintained supply (D2 without D3) runs down; a reactor that works (D4's instrument) does not show that the Hadean did. So the verdict is per domain and is never averaged into a single "yes, gradients work" or "no, they do not".

**Mode:** `general` on the union structure. **Refuter:** a partition proof, that is, a demonstration that exactly one of the four conditions is sufficient in all cases, would break the union reading.

### F6 — "Naturally occurring" is a binding from the geology frame, not a new primitive (D1/D3, `specified`)

Fig 6 :21: **"MINT composes inside the closure and can never add an atom."** Fig 6 :25: **"An import adds no atom to the world."** Fig 6 :23: **"No frame escapes its own closure. The bound moves only on contact with a frame whose closure differs."**

Naming proton motive force, a pH gradient across mackinawite, or a redox disequilibrium is binding an already-held atom (gradient) onto a term from the geology and biochemistry frame. It is provenance, not the minting of a seventh term. The distinction matters because an import needs a source gate and a shadow test (Fig 8): the source is the measured vent chemistry; the shadow test is whether it explains the gap (a directed flow where bare energy is directionless). Herschy et al. 2014 supply both for the reactor case; the field case is contested (F10).

**Mode:** `specified`. **Triangulation:** Fig 6 plus Fig 8, two figures on different aspects of import. **Refuter:** a seventh primitive would be needed if gradient were not already in the six; that is F1's refuter again and is the same test.

### F7 — The figures return a shape, not a name; which gradient is a ground question (D4, `gap`)

Fig 9 :32: **"you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself."** Fig 13 :134-135: **"Unreachable is a verdict here, not a silence"** and **"It cannot say which primitive is missing, because naming it would mean already holding it."**

The framework can say: a spendable difference is required, it must be in contact with a catalytic barrier, it must be maintained, and the network must close a cycle. It cannot say: it was a proton gradient of 3 units across mackinawite at Lost City, rather than a redox or thermal gradient somewhere else. That selection is a binding, and the honest label for the historical case is `gap`. Fig 8 :39: **"A search that finds nothing is never a proof of absence."**

**Mode:** `general` on the limit, `gap` on the case. **Refuter:** a measurement that names the gradient at a specific Hadean-analogue site and shows it drove a self-maintaining network would close the gap; that is a ground result, not a further argument.

### F8 — The chain probably breaks between vocabulary and objective (D4, `specified`)

Fig 3 :18: **"Where a given chain stops is a reading, never a property of the chain."** Fig 3 :20: **"The chain is ordered by dependency, not by importance."** Fig 3 :29: **"Before asking whether a system is good, ask which link it currently stops at, and whether the answer was measured or assumed."**

The literature has good perception (pH gradients measured; Herschy et al. 2014 report *"sharp gradients of several pH units to exist across distances of a few micrometres"*, quoting the geochemistry) and good vocabulary (proton motive force, acetyl phosphate, Fe(Ni)S catalysis, thermophoresis). What it lacks is an agreed objective measure of "primitive metabolic network has started": what flux, what yield, what persistence, over how many turnovers. Fig 3 :25-27 says the middle link attracts the least attention and the fewest instruments. So effort aimed at yet another rate measurement (above the break) cannot rescue a missing definition of what counts as the network (below it).

**Mode:** `specified`. **Refuter:** a published, operational criterion for a self-sustaining protometabolic network that is actually used by two independent groups would move this link from "no instrument" to "instrumented".

### F10 — World conflict: the gradient premise itself is contested (D3, conflict recorded, verdict not flipped)

The figure verdict is about the shape of what would be required. The world disagrees about whether the candidate habitat supplies it.

- Supporting: Martin and Russell 2007 describe a model that harnesses a *"naturally pre-existing proton gradient at the vent-ocean interface"*. Herschy et al. 2014 report that transected Fe(Ni)S precipitates *"appear to be capable of reducing CO2 to form low yields of simple 1C organics"* and that thermophoresis concentrated fluorescein *"up to ~5,000-fold"*.
- Weakening within the same programme: Herschy et al. 2014 say *"the formation of formaldehyde, especially, is variable and inconsistent between runs"* and that *"the precise mechanisms ... are uncertain, and theoretical ideas lack empirical support"*. Cartwright et al. 2019 record that the reduction *"did not reliably progress"* in the mackinawite-nanocrystal experiment.
- Directly conflicting: Tutolo et al. 2026 (PNAS, via the Astrobiology Web abstract) argue that *"neither modern nor ancient serpentinizing systems are characterized by strong pH gradients at hydrothermal temperatures"* because the hyperalkalinity of Lost City fluids develops only after cooling.

**Conflict rule applied.** A conflict sends the derivation back to the census; it never flips the figure verdict (method rule, item 22). Re-census of D3: the claim is not "alkaline vents have a gradient" but "a maintained, spendable difference must be in contact with a catalytic barrier at the relevant scale". The conflict bears on whether a particular site satisfies the condition, not on whether the condition is required. So D3 is narrowed from `specified site` to `specified condition, site contested`, and the figure verdict in §9 is unchanged. Fig 2 :42: *"Buy independence in proportion to the cost of being wrong."* The conflicting source is kept with full provenance precisely because it is low overlap with the pro-vent pool.

### F11 — Agreement among origin-of-life reviews is not a second witness (D4, `general`)

Fig 7 :13: **"agreement compounds, evidence does not."** Fig 2 :40: **"agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool."** Martin and Russell 2007, Cartwright et al. 2019, Herschy et al. 2014 and Sojo et al. 2016 cite one another and share the same vent programme. Four papers are one witness. The Nobel press release on Mitchell (different decade, different question, foundational bioenergetics) and Kondepudi et al. 2020 (non-equilibrium thermodynamics, different field) are the genuinely lower-overlap corroborators, and even they corroborate the mechanism shape, not the historical event.

## 5. Pre-registered refuter (written before the conclusion)

Per the seat map rule: a falsifier written before the run is a design instrument. These were written before §9.

The shape claim (a maintained, spendable difference composes with energy into a flow that can sustain a catalytic cycle) is refuted if any of the following holds:

1. **Equilibrium counterexample:** a self-maintaining reaction network is demonstrated with no spendable difference across it, breaking Fig 13 :46 and :71.
2. **Composition failure:** a spendable difference is shown not to compose with energy into a directed flow at the relevant scale, breaking Fig 13 :76.
3. **Primitive failure:** gradient is derived from the other five terms, breaking Fig 13 :41 and removing it from the substrate.
4. **Network failure:** no catalyst survives its own use, or no primitive cycle can close under the claimed conditions, breaking Fig 12 :119 and :121.
5. **Supply failure:** a ground measurement shows no maintained difference at the relevant scale in any plausible habitat, breaking Fig 10 :24's channel condition and F10's narrowed D3.
6. **Channel illusion:** a reported success is shown to depend entirely on an externally maintained gradient in a substituted habitat and is nevertheless reported as `grounded`, breaking Fig 11 :70.

If any fires, the relevant domain verdict is withdrawn. Item 5 is the live empirical question; item 1 is the cheap structural test.

**Not a refuter:** another paper agreeing or disagreeing without a measurement. Agreement is a budget filter here, never a witness (Fig 9 :60).

### Seven-question recursive derivation (summary)

1. **Is a spendable difference required?** Yes. Fig 13 :46, :71. Energy alone is directionless (:14).
2. **Is it available in the held set?** Yes, gradient is one of six (Fig 13 :21-22), indivisible (:41).
3. **What does it compose with?** Energy, into a flow (Fig 13 :76), across kinds (:74).
4. **What sustains a network?** A catalyst that survives (Fig 12 :119) and a cycle that closes (:121).
5. **What maintains the supply?** The habitat (Fig 11 :39, :48) through a channel that can decay (Fig 10 :24).
6. **Is the historical case settled?** No. Fig 9 :32; Fig 13 :134; the world evidence is contested (F10). `gap`.
7. **Where is the chain broken?** Between vocabulary and objective (Fig 3 :25-27): no agreed operational criterion for "a primitive metabolic network has started".

## 8. Strains and re-census

- **S1, spectrum versus gradient.** First pass read the pH difference as a spectrum (ordering). Strain: spectrum has no directedness (Fig 13 :46). Re-census: a pH difference varying across space is the gradient over space, bonded to the state space (Fig 13 :76). Spectrum remains the ordering that lets more and less be said (:20). Strain dissolved, correction kept in F9 and `06` Q1.
- **S2, reactor success versus capability.** Fig 11 :70 (harness proves wiring) versus the reactor's organic yields. Re-census: the reactor is D4 evidence about composition legality, not D3 evidence about the Hadean. No contradiction, different objects.
