# Which gradient, what order, and are the candidates isomers?

**Mode: `specified` on the composition and order claims, `general` on the structural split, not `grounded`.** Simulated derivation checked against the figures, not a ground reading (see `05_what_shape_means.md` §7).

## RESIDUAL

`01` established that a spendable difference composes with energy into a flow (F2) and that a catalyst plus a cycle are the minimum network structures (F3). Three questions are still owed before a shape can be handed to an expert: (1) which of the six members does a pH difference actually occupy, (2) in what order do the members compose into a network, (3) are the candidate gradients (proton, redox, thermal) the same substance under different arrangements, or different members of one functional group?

## DERIVATION

### Q1 — Does the pH difference occupy Gradient or Spectrum? CORRECTION TO MY OWN §1-§3 of `05`

- Fig 13 :20 describes spectrum: **"ordering, so more and less can be said"**. Ordering alone has no direction.
- Fig 13 :22 and the Symbols table describe gradient: **"a difference that can be spent"** and **"A difference that can be spent. Not merely a distribution: what makes it a gradient is directedness."**
- Fig 13 :45-47 is decisive: an ordering varying across space gives a distribution, and a distribution stays put; what makes a gradient a gradient is that it can be spent, and neither an ordering nor a span of space has directedness. *"If the whole carries a property neither part has, the whole is not the parts arranged."*
- A pH difference across a barrier is a spatial variation that can be spent by letting protons cross, so it is **Gradient bonded to the state Space**, with the ordering of pH values supplied by Spectrum as a qualifier at the level of the measurement. It is not Spectrum doing the driving.
- **I overstated this earlier.** My first pass assigned the driving role to spectrum because pH values are ordered. The strain test caught it: ordered values alone cannot drive a flow (Fig 13 :46). Retracted as applied to the driving role; kept as the qualifier role. Triangulated (Fig 13 plus Symbols table).

### Q2 — What is the order of composition?

- Fig 13 :74: **"Everything else is a composition across the two kinds, never within one."**
- Fig 13 :75: **"Two states never bond directly. It is not space-time but space, a structure, time; and two structures join only through a state."**
- Fig 13 :76: **"Energy and a gradient compose a flow. A gradient over space and one over time are different terms, because which state it is bonded to is part of what it is."**
- Legal chain for this question:

```
Energy(state) + Gradient(structure over Space)  ->  Flow
Flow + Space(state, the pore or barrier)          ->  local reaction region
region + Network(structure)                       ->  reaction network
Network + Catalyst(structure, via a state)        ->  self-maintaining network
```

- The alternation is the recursion Fig 13 :27-31 names: each step acts on one kind and yields the other, and the residual of the turn is what the next turn composes against. There is no privileged starting member; "Energy first" is a reading of the chain, not a property of it (compare Fig 3 :18 on the dependency chain).
- Fig 13 :86-89 gives the one chain: *"a state / a structure / relations / fields / gradients / flows / networks / and on"*. Our chain is a truncated instance of it.
- **Falsifier, written before the run:** if a flow is demonstrated that composes two structures directly with no intervening state, or two states with no intervening structure, the order claim fails and Fig 13 :74-75 is the wrong figure for this domain.

### Q3 — Are proton, redox and thermal gradients isomers or functional-group members?

- Fig 12 :117: **"Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."**
- Fig 12 :123: **"A substructure whose presence fixes the outcome. The group says what kind; the qualifier says which member."**
- As the candidate gradients are usually described in the literature, they do **not** share one atom set. A proton gradient over space uses Gradient + Space + Energy (chemical potential). A thermal gradient over space uses Gradient + Space + Spectrum (temperature ordering). A redox gradient over space uses Gradient + Space + Spectrum (redox ordering) plus Energy (electron free energy). By Fig 12's definition they are therefore **not** isomers as ordinarily written. They are members of one **functional group**, `gradient over space`, with different qualifiers (Fig 12 :123, *"the group says what kind; the qualifier says which member"*).
- The stronger version: if all three are written over the full shared atom set `{Space, Time, Energy, Spectrum, Gradient, Network}`, differing only in which bond connects Gradient to which state, they **are** isomers (Fig 12 :117). That is the claim worth testing, because it would predict that the three candidates are interchangeable at the level of shape and differ only in what the environment supplies.
- **Falsifier:** if one candidate requires an atom that another does not (for example a thermal gradient that needs no Energy term, or a redox gradient that needs a seventh primitive), the isomer claim fails and the functional-group reading stands. This is live: I have not measured it.

### Q4 — Catalyst and cycle: what actually survives a turnover?

- Fig 12 :119: **"A part that survives its own use. Spent and regenerating parts behave differently across uses, and nothing records which a term is."**
- Fig 12 :121: **"The output re-enters as input. The sign of the loop decides whether it closes, and a chain cannot close by construction."**
- Fig 12 :118-124 names four structures; the two that bear here are catalyst and cycle. Fe(Ni)S mineral surfaces are the catalyst candidate (Herschy et al. 2014 report *"catalytic Fe(Ni)S microcrystals"* and describe the reactor structures as *"thin-walled, inorganic structures"*). The acetyl phosphate / thioester step is the cycle candidate (Herschy et al. 2014: *"the whole process is driven by natural proton gradients"*).
- Fig 12 :107-110: *"Whether that cost must be paid again depends on the substrate: where the arrangement is recorded, re-deriving it is a lookup and the actions were spent once. Where it is not, the result carries forward and the deriving does not, so every lineage pays again."* A mineral catalyst that survives its own use is the recorded case; a purely solution-phase product that diffuses away is the unrecorded case. This is a checkable difference, not a metaphor.
- **Falsifier:** a claimed protometabolic cycle in which every catalytic part is consumed faster than it is regenerated, with no external replenishment, is not a cycle under Fig 12 :121.

### Q5 — What order is the union, and which link is missing?

- Fig 3 :19: **"Optimising a link that already works cannot rescue one that does not."**
- Fig 3 :20: **"The chain is ordered by dependency, not by importance."**
- Fig 3 :27: **"A link with no instrument is not a link that works. It is a link nobody has looked at."**
- The chain for this question runs: perception (a gradient is measured) → vocabulary (flow, catalyst, cycle are named) → objective (what counts as a self-maintaining network) → planning (design a reactor or a field campaign) → learn and carry. The first two links are well supplied in the literature. The third is not: Herschy et al. 2014 themselves say *"the formation of formaldehyde, especially, is variable and inconsistent between runs"* and the mechanisms *"lack empirical support"*. Effort spent on a fourth reactor design cannot rescue a missing criterion for what would count as success.
- **Falsifier:** an operational, published criterion for "a primitive metabolic network has started", used independently by two groups, would move the objective link from missing to instrumented and invalidate this finding.

## CONCLUSION

The pH difference is a gradient (not a spectrum doing the driving), and it composes with energy into a flow in a legal cross-kind order. The candidate gradients are, as ordinarily written, members of a **functional group** rather than isomers; the isomer version is a stronger, checkable claim over a fixed full atom set. The catalyst and cycle structures are what turn a flow into a network that persists, and the missing link in the current literature is the objective criterion, not the chemistry. This rescues the defensible part of the metabolism-first claim without importing a seventh primitive or treating a reactor as a settlement.
