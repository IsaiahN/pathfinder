## The question as an effect

Decide a fork. Given many agents that each learn from experience, which structural conditions make the collective's prediction error against a ground fall, and which make internal agreement rise while error against the ground stays put or rises? Option A treats "more learning, more agents, more agreement" as the answer. Option B treats agreement as a frame-internal quantity and asks what the frames are ranged against. What each leads to: A selects a metric the system produces itself and therefore cannot see its own error; B selects a ground, the independence between frames, and the selection rule between variants. The sought-for shape is bound as an ordinary term before the run: "collective intelligence is anchored, independent variation with selection between; amplified shared error is correlated convergence with no independent ground" (Formula, "Direction"). That binding is written down so it can be refused.

1. **A room is not an anchor.** Seat Map :283: "A room reaching a conclusion without the ground is agreement, and agreement among frames is a weighted average of influence. Adding people raises the vocabulary and does not add an anchor."

3. **Asking for trust before checking spends independence.** Fig 10 :42: "A verifier that can reconstruct the claim cannot verify it." The first contact carries the residual and its refuter, never the framework's interior.
