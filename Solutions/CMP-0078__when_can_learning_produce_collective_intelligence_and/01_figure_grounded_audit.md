## 0. The residual, stated in effect terms

The question is a fork about a configuration, not a quantity. Restated as an effect: many agents each update their terms from experience; an observer asks whether the collective's error against a ground falls, or whether internal agreement rises while error against the ground fails to fall. The two outcomes are observationally distinct only if the observer has a metric that is not produced by the frames themselves.

What an answer would look like, bound before the run (Formula, "Direction"):

> **Bound shape (ordinary term, stated before derivation).** Collective intelligence is *anchored, independent variation with selection between variants against a ground that does not move*. Amplified shared error is *correlated convergence, with no independent ground, in which agreement rises because the frames share a source or update on one another*. The control variable is not the amount of learning; it is whether learning changes contact with the ground.

That binding is deliberately writable-down and therefore refusable in the same test as everything else. Without this step, the loop can check the mechanism (how fast agents learn) and never the direction of the question (what "intelligence" is being measured). Fig 5 :61-64: "A frame searching for an answer of a particular shape holds that shape as a PREFERENCE rather than as a term. It is not bound, it predicts nothing, and it accrues no residual, so nothing here can see it."

## 1. Domain census (before choosing any figure line)

### R by arity, symmetry, scale

- **Arity:** plural. R is indexed by object slot, not by agent count. Fig 1 :19: "The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated." A collective with a near-zero global residual and one live slot is a legal state (Fig 1 :13), so the claim that the system is doing well overall is not a reading of anything.
- **Symmetry:** R is *not* invariant under averaging. Fig 1 :22: "Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average." The operation "take the mean" destroys the information about where to look, so it cannot be the metric that steers learning.
- **Scale:** the learning loop closes within one scale (one agent, or one room). Crossing from one agent to the population is a membrane crossing with a lossy transform and a round-trip gap R_T (Fig 4 :27-32). A collective is not a larger copy of an agent.

### One line per domain (actors / currency / anchor)

| domain | actors | currency | anchor (nameable?) | question type |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| D1, the single learning loop | one agent, Γ, belief b, action a, observation o' | residual R per slot, cost(φ), settlement | yes: a ground that pays, e.g. a scored environment (Fig 1) | partition: which terms may be admitted |
| D2, the collective / population | many agents, a shared library, "something that ranks what they produce" | methods up, head starts down, agreement between frames | yes only if pinned: "the anchor pinned: it does not update on A or B both range against it; it is not a third party to the disagreement" (Fig 2 :24-27) | union: two regimes can coexist in one system |
| D3, the design / verification seat | an outside checker, the habitat | evidence versus agreement; per-slot residual | yes: the ground, never agreement; "the ground does not decay, the channel does" (Fig 10 :24) | partition: which conditions to install |

No nameable anchor means not a domain yet. All three have one. D2's anchor is conditional: if it is allowed to update on the agents, D2 collapses into a single drifting frame and ceases to be a separate domain. That is Fig 2 panel 1, not a measurement problem.

### Partition or union

The two named outcomes are a partition of regimes by structural condition (independent plus anchored, versus correlated plus unanchored). A real system can instantiate both at once, in which case the split lands on a union and must be unbundled before search (Fig 9 :54-56: "And when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again. A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones."). "More learning" is a single lever: it enlarges both regimes and leaves their intersection standing, so it cannot clear the compound. This is why the question cannot be answered by "learning helps" or "learning hurts".

### Order by dependency (Fig 3)

1. perception: can the system tell a reading of the ground from internal agreement?
2. vocabulary: does it have terms for independence, anchor and per-slot residual?
3. objective: what counts as progress, ground error or agreement? (this is the middle link with no instrument)
4. planning: which intervention, install independence, pin the anchor, select between variants?
5. learn and carry: which methods cross up, and with what round-trip gap?

Fig 3 :19: "Optimising a link that already works cannot rescue one that does not." Fig 3 :29-30: "Before asking whether a system is good, ask which link it currently stops at, and whether the answer was measured or assumed. Most improvement effort in practice lands above the break, because that is where the measurements already exist." The standard answer to this question ("train more agents") is effort spent above the break.

## 3. Findings (verbatim figure lines first, then the conclusion)

### F1: Agreement is a frame-internal quantity and cannot be the objective (decisive for the metric)

Fig 1 :23: "The ground enters as the only metric."
Fig 1 :27: "They are frame-internal. A frame cannot score itself with a quantity it also produces."
Fig 2 :35: "Alignment is triangulation, not negotiation. A mirror cannot correct you."
Seat Map :283: "A room reaching a conclusion without the ground is agreement, and agreement among frames is a weighted average of influence. Adding people raises the vocabulary and does not add an anchor."

**Conclusion (`general`, structural).** A system that calls itself collectively intelligent because its members agree is scoring itself with a quantity it produces. Raising the number of agreeing agents raises vocabulary, not anchor. "Collective intelligence" must therefore be defined against a ground before it can be used as an objective at all. Triangulated across Fig 1, Fig 2 and Seat Map :283 (low overlap: per-slot metric, anchor geometry, checking arrangement).

**Refuter.** Exhibit a system whose internal agreement is demonstrably produced by something it does not itself generate and that is independent of its frames, and show that this exogenous quantity is what is being optimised. Then agreement is an instrument, not a self-score, and F1 is withdrawn for that system.

### F2: The two named amplification mechanisms are the figure's two collapses

Collapse 1, mutual update. Fig 2 :13: "no anchor; each updates on the others". Fig 2 :14-15: "they converge, and what they converge on is whoever had the most influence". Fig 2 :34: "If it does, panel 3 turns into panel 1: the anchor becomes another frame, everything drifts together, and the drift is coherent, which is why nobody notices."

Collapse 2, common cause. Fig 2 :18-21: "one evidence pool they never talk, and still agree so the agreement is the pool talking, not two frames confirming each other".

Fig 2 :33: "The anchor must not update."
Fig 2 :40: "disqualifying for a verdict: agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool"

**Conclusion (`general`, with a `specified` prediction for each mechanism).** Learning amplifies shared errors in exactly two structural situations: (a) agents update on one another without an unmovable anchor, so the group converges on whoever had most influence and the drift is coherent and invisible from inside; (b) agents share one evidence pool, so they agree without communicating and the agreement is the pool talking. Both are named collapses in Fig 2, not metaphors. The `specified` predictions: in (a) removing the highest-influence agent changes the convergence point; in (b) holding the frames fixed but de-correlating the evidence changes the agreement. Triangulated across Fig 2, Fig 7 :12-13 and Fig 8 :48-49.

**Refuter.** A multi-agent system with no external anchor whose members update only on each other, and whose error against an independent held-out ground falls monotonically while correlation stays high, refutes mechanism (a). A crowd drawn from one corpus whose accuracy improves with further rounds while correlation remains fixed at origin refutes mechanism (b).

### F3: Collective intelligence is independence plus a pinned anchor plus selection between variants

Fig 2 :31-32: "Independence, between two frames, is how little they draw on the same evidence. Two people reading the same report are not two frames; two instruments pointed at the same thing are. Independence is what makes agreement mean something."
Fig 2 :24-27: "the anchor pinned: it does not update on A or B both range against it; it is not a third party to the disagreement"
Fig 7 :35: "A room's agreement is variation, not a verdict. Selection between rooms is where the ground votes."
Fig 7 :33: "→ variation inside, selection between"
Fig 7 :26: "Progress per handoff: R = h² × S"
Fig 8 :45: "Two frames together reach what neither reaches alone."
Fig 8 :48-49: "How much two frames draw on the same evidence decides both whether their agreement means anything and whether their union has any surplus. Two frames that overlap almost completely are one frame wearing two names."

**Conclusion (`general` on the shape, `specified` on R = h² × S).** Collective intelligence is not consensus. It is three things together: low overlap between frames, a ground that no frame can move, and selection between variants rather than averaging them. Fig 7 makes the selection step explicit: progress per handoff is fidelity of the bracket times pressure from the ground; zero either and the chain runs in place. The same quantity (overlap) decides both whether agreement means anything and whether the union has surplus, which is why "many independent minds" is a structural claim, not a headcount claim. Triangulated across Fig 2, Fig 7 and Fig 8, three figures with different objects (anchor geometry, handoff dynamics, closure surplus).

**Refuter.** Show two rooms with low overlap, a non-updating ground and high bracket fidelity, where selecting between them produces no better held-out error than picking one at random, across repeated trials. That would break the progress claim. Or show that high overlap produces equal or greater union surplus, which would break the independence condition.

### F4: Aggregation hides the live slot, but it can still be a valid predictor against a ground (re-census)

Fig 7 :14: "blending: novelty swamped to the mean"
Fig 1 :22: "Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average."
Fig 1 :13: "a global R near zero with one live slot residual is a legal state"

**At first reading this seemed to conflict with the world literature**, where averaging many independent estimates improves accuracy against a known truth. Re-census resolves it. The two literatures govern different quantities. Averaging estimates is a *prediction aggregator* ranged against an external ground (a legitimate Fig 1 use of R). Fig 1 :22 governs the *search signal* that says where to look next: average it away and the live slot vanishes. Blending models before selection also removes the novelty that a selection rule needs (Fig 7 :14). Same word "averaging", different objects, so no strain.

**Conclusion (`specified`).** Average predictions if a ground scores the average. Do not average the residuals, and do not blend variants before the ground has selected between them. Triangulated for the distinction (Fig 1 plus Fig 7); the empirical averaging result is single-source in the world literature but comes from an independent field.

**Refuter.** Show that a blended model matches or beats selection between rooms on a held-out ground at equal budget while preserving a measurable per-slot residual. Then the "novelty swamped" claim fails for that task.

### F5: Learning must change contact, not merely the agent

Fig 11 :39: "So the answerable question is not what intelligence is, but what makes a system capable. And capability is a property of agent-and-habitat, never of the agent alone."
Fig 11 :40: "A system cannot be made more capable by improving the agent in isolation. An improvement that does not change contact changes nothing, however much it improves."
Fig 11 :43: "The habitat is enumerated, never composed."
Fig 11 :49: "An actor's valence is relative to the residual, not a property it carries."

**Conclusion (`general`, with a falsifier attached by the figure itself).** This is the direct answer to "when can learning produce collective intelligence": when the learning changes the contact structure (new independent instruments, a pinned ground, selection between rooms, a redirected habitat), not merely the agents' internal libraries. More training on the same evidence leaves contact unchanged, so it changes nothing however much it improves. Fig 11 :71-72 records the limit honestly: the capability claim "fit cases already known and have not been tested forward, so they can still come back wrong: an agent-only improvement that moved the ground would refute the capability claim." Triangulated with Fig 4 :22 (methods cross, recordings do not) and Fig 2 (contact is what is ranged); single-source for the precise capability sentence.

**Refuter.** Exhibit an agent-only improvement, with the habitat and all contact relations held fixed, that moves a ground-measured collective error. That refutes Fig 11 :40.

### F6: Correlation fixed at origin cannot be refreshed by more use

Fig 10 :80-81: "And divergence regenerates unevenly. Frames whose independence is renewed by separate experience can be maintained; frames whose correlation is fixed at origin can only be rotated, because no amount of use makes them less alike and replacement is the only refresh."
Fig 13 :38-39: "Which is Figure 2's condition applied to the set itself: independence is how little two frames draw on the same evidence, and it is what makes their agreement mean anything. Six members that overlapped would be fewer members wearing six names."

**Conclusion (`general`).** A crowd trained on one corpus is correlated at origin. Additional rounds of the same learning rotate it: they do not make its members less alike. Independence must be renewed by separate experience or imported from a frame with a different closure (Fig 8 :45, with a source gate and a shadow test). This is the sharpest statement of "when does learning amplify shared errors": when everyone learned the same thing first. Triangulated across Fig 10 and Fig 13, with independent empirical corroboration from Lorenz et al. 2011 (PNAS), which found "even mild social influence can undermine the wisdom of crowd effect in simple estimation tasks". The world result is secondary and a different field, so it is one witness, not the verdict.

**Refuter.** Show frames with provably identical training histories gaining measurable independence from additional use of the same shared process, with no new experience and no replacement. That refutes the rotation claim.

### F7: The broken link is the objective, and effort lands above it

Fig 3 :27: "A link with no instrument is not a link that works. It is a link nobody has looked at."
Fig 3 :19: "Optimising a link that already works cannot rescue one that does not."
Fig 3 :20-21: "The chain is ordered by dependency, not by importance. Each link consumes what the one above produces, so effort spent above the break arrives at a step that cannot use it."
Seat Map :281: "An unmeasured quantity is a specification of what to measure and is worth having, it is not a verdict."

**Conclusion (`general`).** Most collective-intelligence engineering spends effort on perception and planning (more agents, faster learning) because those are measurable. The objective link, "is this error against the ground or just agreement?", has no instrument in most settings, so the system runs as though it worked. Fig 3 :22-23: "A step whose input never arrives cannot be diagnosed, only its predecessor can. Which is why a reading taken below the break is not a weak reading. It is a reading of nothing." Triangulated across Fig 3, Fig 1 :27 and Seat Map :281.

**Refuter.** Exhibit a system whose objective link is explicitly instrumented and whose agreement still increases while ground error stays flat, with no other link changed. That would remove this link from the explanation.

### F8: Headcount is a budget filter, never a witness

Fig 6 :53-54: "And it inherits Figure 8's condition directly: searchers drawing on the same evidence are one searcher wearing many names. Independence decides whether more of them buy more search or only more spelling, which makes it the parameter rather than a caveat."
Fig 6 :49-50: "more search at the same reach: is it in here and unfound? The closure does not change. Same atoms, same wall, more of the space actually visited. Fails by spending budget on a region already swept. Distribute the search, and keep the searchers independent."
Fig 9 :60: "Use filters for the budget and witnesses for the verdict. Never let a filter hand you a verdict."
Fig 2 :42: "Buy independence in proportion to the cost of being wrong."

**Conclusion (`general` on the rule, `specified` on the spend).** Adding agents buys more search only if their evidence is independent. Otherwise it buys more spelling of the same state. Audience size, participant count and number of reviewers are filters for the budget (what gets tried first); they are never witnesses for the verdict. Triangulated across Fig 6, Fig 9, Fig 2 and Seat Map :283.

**Refuter.** Show a set of agents with measurable evidence overlap whose added members expand the searched space at the same rate as an independent set, under a fixed budget. That would demote independence from parameter to caveat.

## 4. Pre-registered refuter (written before the conclusion)

Required before outreach. Seat Map :287: "And a falsifier written before the run is a design instrument, not only an honesty device. Stating what failure looks like surfaces constraints the design does not, and the constraint arrives as a change to the experiment rather than as a note about it."

The bound shape in section 0 is refused if any of these fires:

1. **Mutual-update test.** A collective whose members update only on one another, with no external ground, shows a sustained fall in error against a separate held-out ground over more than three rounds while pairwise evidence overlap stays above 0.8. (breaks F2a)
2. **Correlated-origin test.** A set of frames with provably identical training data gains accuracy from additional rounds without replacement of frames or new separate experience. (breaks F6)
3. **Blend test.** A blend of independent models matches or beats selection between rooms on a held-out ground at equal action budget, while preserving a readable per-slot residual. (breaks F4)
4. **Agent-only test.** An improvement confined to agents' internal libraries, with habitat and all contact relations held fixed, moves a ground-measured collective error. (breaks F5)
5. **Metric test.** A system optimising its own internal agreement is shown to have an external, frame-independent quantity that generated that agreement, and that quantity is what is actually being optimised. (breaks F1)

If any fires, the shape is withdrawn or narrowed to the surviving conditions. Until one is run, no part of the answer is `grounded`.

## 5. Recommendation

1. Define the objective as ground error, measured by something the frames cannot move (F1, F3).
2. Keep residuals per slot. Never aggregate the search signal into a single global score (F4).
3. Buy independence deliberately: separate experience, different instruments, imports with a source gate and a shadow test. Do not assume a crowd is plural because it is large (F6, F8).
4. Select between variants against the ground; do not blend them before selection. Keep failed terms in the record but remove their standing to be composed over (F3, Fig 7 :33).
5. Pin the anchor. If it updates on the agents, D2 collapses into panel 1 and nobody notices (F2, Fig 2 :33-34).
6. Instrument the objective link before adding agents. Otherwise effort lands above the break (F7).
7. Treat audience size, participant count and prestige as budget filters only. Buy independence in proportion to the cost of being wrong (F8).

Fig 4 :25: "A recording carried upward looks like knowledge and is a description of one occasion. Replayed downward, it produces a system that repeats a past success and cannot produce a new one." Send the conditions and the refuter, not a success story.

## 7. The three questions of Formula step 7

No single line in `closure(Γ)` closes the residual, because the fork depends on a ground reading that this derivation does not have. So the three questions are asked separately, in order.

- **SEARCHED (first, cheapest).** Is the answer in here and unfound? Yes for the structural part: Fig 2, Fig 6, Fig 7, Fig 8 and Fig 11 already contain the shape. What is unfound is the empirical value of the overlap parameter that decides which regime a given system is in. Remedy: more search with independent instruments, not a new atom.
- **INWARD.** Extend the instrument from one already returning something. Per-slot residual logging, held-out ground sets, and overlap estimates are all improvements of instruments that already return readings. Fig 6 :32: "An instrument is not built from a description. It is improved from a worse instrument already returning something."
- **OUTWARD.** Import from a frame whose closure differs. Experimental social science (Lorenz et al. 2011), social psychology (groupthink, group polarization), and collective intelligence research (Page's diversity prediction theorem; Surowiecki's diversity, independence and decentralisation) all predict the shape of the fork. Each carries a source gate (author, venue, year, quote in `10_source_ledger.csv`) and a shadow test (each explains a gap the figures leave: what value of overlap is enough?). INWARD and OUTWARD were not read against each other.

## 9. Updated verdict

The fork survives with its conditions attached. Learning produces collective intelligence when it changes contact: independent frames, an anchor that does not update, per-slot residuals kept intact, and selection between variants against the ground. Learning amplifies shared errors when it increases correlation without changing contact: mutual update with no anchor, or a common evidence pool that makes agreement the pool talking. Headcount and agreement are budget filters, never witnesses. No part of this is settled by measurement. The pre-registered refuters in section 4 are the way it becomes testable, and the independent ground tests in `07` and `08` are what would move it from `candidate` to `grounded`.
