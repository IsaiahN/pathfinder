# Outreach draft: check the shape before deciding about the framework

**Use:** one-to-one, or as a cover letter attached to a submission through the public venues in `03_public_contact_points.csv`. **Not** for bulk send.

**Hard precondition:** do not send until the four refuters in `01_figure_grounded_audit.md` section 4 are stated exactly as written. The offer collapses on contact if the criterion is changed after the recipient answers.

---

**Subject:** One checkable claim about collective intelligence, and what would refute it

> Hi [name],
>
> I have one specific output I would like you to check against what you already know. I am not asking you to read a theory first, and I am not asking you to agree with anything. If the output does not survive, there is nothing else to look at.
>
> **The output (`general` on the shape, `specified` on the conditions, not `grounded`):** learning produces collective intelligence only when it changes contact, not merely the agents. Four conditions have to hold together:
>
> 1. the objective is error against a ground that does not change because of what any agent thinks, not internal agreement;
> 2. residuals are kept per object slot, not averaged into one global score;
> 3. the frames draw on genuinely different evidence, and that independence is renewed by separate experience;
> 4. variants are selected against the ground, not blended into a mean before selection.
>
> Learning amplifies shared errors in exactly two structures: agents updating on one another with no fixed anchor, so the group converges on whoever had the most influence; and agents sharing one evidence pool, so they agree without ever talking because the pool is what is talking. Growing the number of agents without independence buys more spelling of the same state, not more search.
>
> **What I am not claiming:** that a crowd is wise because it is large; that averaging estimates is always wrong (averaging a prediction against an external ground is fine, averaging the search signal is not); that independence means silence; or that any of this has been measured for a specific system here. This is a shape and a set of falsifiers, not a result.
>
> **What would refute it, written before this message:**
> 1. a group updating only on itself, with no external ground, whose error against a separate held-out ground falls for more than three rounds while pairwise evidence overlap stays above 0.8;
> 2. correlated frames with identical training data gaining accuracy from more rounds with no replacement or new experience;
> 3. a blend matching or beating selection on a held-out ground at equal budget while keeping a readable per-slot residual;
> 4. an agent-only improvement, with contact held fixed, moving a ground-measured collective error.
>
> If any of these holds, I withdraw the claim or narrow it to the conditions that survive. A one-line "this fails because..." is more useful to me than agreement. If you would rather not engage, no reply is needed.
>
> **If it survives, and only then:** the sources are below. The figures are the authority, not my summary of them.
>
> - Figures and formula, with line numbers (local): `FRAMEWORK/reference/03-FIGURES_TEXT.md`, `FRAMEWORK/figures/THE_FORMULA.md`
> - How a Society of Agents Would Work: https://adventuresinml.substack.com/p/how-a-society-of-agents-would-work
> - How social influence can undermine the wisdom of crowd effect, Lorenz et al., PNAS 2011: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3107299/
> - Complexity Science Hub, Collective Minds research topic: https://csh.ac.at/research/research-topic/collective-minds/
>
> **If you want to use the method:** run it on a problem you know well and can check against a ground. Write down what would refute your result *before* you run it, and state the mode of what you get. A reported failure is more useful to me than a reported success.
