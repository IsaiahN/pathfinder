# Expert verification packet: the Tether on learning and collective intelligence

**What this is:** a claim in a form a researcher on collective intelligence, complex systems or group decision making can check. Mode, derivation and refuters are stated before the conclusion.

**Mode: `general` on the shape, `specified` on the four conditions. Not `grounded`.** No measurement is claimed here. This is a simulated derivation checked for internal consistency against the figures.

## 1. The claim, in one paragraph

Collective intelligence and amplified shared error are two different bonds between the same atoms: agents, a shared library and a ranking. In one, frames are independent and range against a ground that does not move, residuals are kept per object slot, and variants are selected against the ground. In the other, frames update on one another with no anchor or draw on one common evidence pool, so agreement rises without accuracy. Learning produces the first only when it changes contact, not merely the agents' internal libraries. Headcount and internal agreement are budget filters, never witnesses to a verdict.

## 2. Three checkable sub-claims

### C1: framework-internal; checkable by reading Figures 1, 2 and 7

- **Statement:** agreement is not a ground reading. A collective cannot score itself with a quantity it produces, and selection between variants, not blending, is where the ground votes.
- **Source, verbatim:**
  - Fig 1 :27 "They are frame-internal. A frame cannot score itself with a quantity it also produces."
  - Fig 2 :35 "Alignment is triangulation, not negotiation. A mirror cannot correct you."
  - Fig 7 :35 "A room's agreement is variation, not a verdict. Selection between rooms is where the ground votes."
  - Fig 7 :14 "blending: novelty swamped to the mean"
- **Refuter:** exhibit a system whose internal agreement is generated by something frame-independent and exogenous, and show that this exogenous quantity, not agreement itself, is what is optimised. Or show a blend matching selection on a held-out ground at equal budget while a per-slot residual stays readable.

### C2: the empirical fork; checkable by a researcher against the crowd literature

- **Statement:** the two amplification mechanisms are (a) mutual update with no fixed anchor, converging on whoever had most influence, and (b) a shared evidence pool, so frames agree without communicating because the pool is talking. Independence, not headcount, is the parameter.
- **Source, verbatim:**
  - Fig 2 :14-15 "they converge, and what they converge on is whoever had the most influence"
  - Fig 2 :18-21 "one evidence pool they never talk, and still agree so the agreement is the pool talking, not two frames confirming each other"
  - Fig 2 :31-32 "Independence, between two frames, is how little they draw on the same evidence. Two people reading the same report are not two frames; two instruments pointed at the same thing are. Independence is what makes agreement mean something."
  - Fig 6 :53-54 "Independence decides whether more of them buy more search or only more spelling, which makes it the parameter rather than a caveat."
- **What to check against the world:** Lorenz, Rauhut, Schweitzer and Helbing, "How social influence can undermine the wisdom of crowd effect", PNAS 108(22), 2011 (opened 2026-11-22): "even mild social influence can undermine the wisdom of crowd effect in simple estimation tasks" and "social influence triggers the convergence of individual estimates and substantially reduces the diversity of the group without improving its accuracy." Is that the same structure as Fig 2 panel 1, or a different one? A difference is a useful result.
- **Refuter:** a collective updating only on itself, with no external ground, whose held-out error falls for more than three rounds while pairwise evidence overlap stays above 0.8; or correlated frames with identical training data gaining accuracy from more rounds with no replacement or new experience.

### C3: the contact claim; checkable by anyone running multi-agent systems

- **Statement:** an improvement that does not change contact changes nothing, however much it improves the agents in isolation. Independence fixed at origin can only be rotated by further use; replacement or separate experience is the only refresh.
- **Source, verbatim:**
  - Fig 11 :40 "A system cannot be made more capable by improving the agent in isolation. An improvement that does not change contact changes nothing, however much it improves."
  - Fig 10 :80-81 "Frames whose independence is renewed by separate experience can be maintained; frames whose correlation is fixed at origin can only be rotated, because no amount of use makes them less alike and replacement is the only refresh."
- **What to check:** run an agent-only improvement with habitat and contact relations held fixed and score it against a ground. Then run the same improvement with one contact relation changed. Does only the second move the ground reading? Note that the figure itself flags this claim as untested forward: Fig 11 :71-72 says "an agent-only improvement that moved the ground would refute the capability claim."
- **Refuter:** a measured move in ground error from the agent-only arm with contact held fixed.

## 3. What is explicitly not claimed

- Not that a bigger crowd is wiser, or that a larger audience corroborates anything.
- Not that averaging estimates is wrong. Averaging predictions against an external ground is legitimate; averaging the per-slot search signal is not.
- Not that independence means silence. Fig 2 panel 2 is frames that never talk and still agree.
- Not a threshold value, a named platform, or a collective IQ number. The framework returns a shape, not a name.
- Not `grounded`. Nothing here was settled by experiment in this derivation.
- Not a claim that this simulated derivation can validate itself. It is checked for internal consistency against the figures only.

## 5. What an expert can check in twenty minutes

1. Read Fig 1 :27, Fig 2 :14-15, :18-21, :31-32 and Fig 7 :35. Five short passages, all in the local figure text.
2. Say whether C2 describes the failure mode they actually observe in their crowd data, or name the failure mode it misses.
3. Name one instrument that separates held-out ground error from internal agreement in their setting, or say why none is available.
4. Send the refutation. A failure is more useful than an endorsement. A reply is a conversation, not corroboration; its verdict on stage 1 is recorded before any theory is shown.
