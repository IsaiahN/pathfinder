## Two findings in the wider literature that bear directly on this question

1. **Independence is the load-bearing condition, not headcount.** Lorenz et al. 2011 ran a controlled experiment with monetary incentives and no group-leader effects, and found that "even mild social influence can undermine the wisdom of crowd effect". Convergence reduced diversity without improving accuracy, moved the truth toward the edge of the estimate range, and boosted confidence. That is the empirical face of Fig 2's two collapses and Fig 6 :53-54. It corroborates; it does not decide.
2. **Averaging helps prediction but hides the search signal.** Page's diversity prediction theorem and the crowd literature support averaging independent estimates against a known truth. Fig 1 :22 says "Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average." These are different quantities. Re-census, not a conflict: use the average as a predictor, keep the per-slot residual as the steering signal. This distinction is recorded in `01` F4 and in `11`.

## Distribution: offer, do not push

Figure-grounded reasons:

- **Fig 4 :22-25**: "Only methods cross up. Only head starts come down." and "A recording carried upward looks like knowledge and is a description of one occasion." Sending the full architecture before a recipient has described a residual sends a recording. Lead with the four conditions and the refuter.
- **Fig 10 :42**: "A verifier that can reconstruct the claim cannot verify it." A recipient who has absorbed the framework before being asked for a verdict has joined the frame being checked.
- **Fig 9 :27-28**: "describe it before you go looking, or any frame you pick will seem to fit." Ask what they would expect to see if the shape claim were true before giving them the essay.
- **Seat Map :163**: "High correlation is disqualifying for a verdict and efficient for a convention." A send list ranked for likely agreement produces a convention, not corroboration.
- **Fig 2 :42**: "Buy independence in proportion to the cost of being wrong." Spend the budget on independent frames, not on audience size.

**Recommended order in a reply:** (1) the shape claim and the four refuters; (2) ask what they would count as a failure and what ground they would use; (3) record that verdict; (4) only then, if they are curious, the links, with the note that the figures, not this summary, are the authority.

## The two-stage offer (and the optional third stage)

1. **Stage 1, validation.** Send the residual, the four conditions and the pre-registered refuters. No theory. Fig 10 :52: "Bring a characterised residual, never the source." Fig 2 :45: "Frames range against the ground. They do not negotiate with it."
2. **Stage 2, opt-in.** If they are curious, offer the three most relevant links. Their stage-1 verdict is already recorded and is never pooled as corroboration after they read the theory (Fig 2 :40: "agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool").
3. **Stage 3, method reuse.** Offer the formula as a generator, with the instruction that each user writes their own refuter and states their own mode. Fig 4 :22 permits this: a method is a generator, not an answer.

**Hard precondition:** stages 1 to 3 only work if the stage-1 output survives. It is a simulated derivation, not a ground reading. It cannot settle the question it reports on. The refuters are how it becomes testable.
