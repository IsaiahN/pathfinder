## Independent domain census (redone before reading the primary's conclusions)

I started from the residual line alone: "When can learning produce collective intelligence, and when does it amplify shared errors?" Stated in effect terms, this asks which configurations make collective error against a ground fall, and which make internal agreement rise while that error fails to fall.

- **R (arity / symmetry / scale).** Arity: plural, indexed by object slot, not by agent count. Fig 1 :19: "The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated." Symmetry: not invariant under averaging; Fig 1 :22: "Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average." Scale: the individual learning loop closes at one scale; the population is a different scale with a lossy transform and a round-trip gap R_T (Fig 4 :30). So "more of the same learning" is not a scale-invariant remedy.
- **Domain 1, the single learning loop.** Actors: one agent, Γ, b, a, o'. Currency: per-slot residual, cost(phi), settlement. Anchor: yes, a paying ground. Question type: partition over which terms may be admitted.
- **Domain 2, the population.** Actors: many agents, a shared library, a ranking. Currency: methods up, head starts down, agreement between frames. Anchor: conditional: "the anchor pinned: it does not update on A or B both range against it; it is not a third party to the disagreement". If the anchor updates, this domain collapses into one drifting frame and the residual loses its referent. Question type: union, because the two regimes can co-occur.
- **Domain 3, the design and verification seat.** Actors: an outside checker and a habitat. Currency: evidence versus agreement. Anchor: yes, the ground, never agreement. Question type: partition over which conditions to install. In this simulated run its independence is zero.

Order by dependency: distinguish ground reading from agreement, acquire terms for anchor and independence, instrument the objective, then choose an intervention, then carry methods. Fig 3 :20-21: "The chain is ordered by dependency, not by importance. Each link consumes what the one above produces, so effort spent above the break arrives at a step that cannot use it."

## Round trip gap R_T

The Symbols table defines R_T as `gap(x, (T_E ∘ T_A)(x))`, and Fig 4 :30 requires it to be measured rather than assumed. I ran the round trip on the condensation the folder actually uses.

1. **Sent up (x):** the verbatim figure set, chiefly Fig 1 :19 and :27, Fig 2 panels 1 to 3 and :31-32, Fig 7 :35, Fig 10 :80-81, Fig 11 :39-40.
2. **Abstraction (T_A):** the shorthand in `07`: four conditions, two failure mechanisms, contact not agent, headcount as filter.
3. **Concretisation (T_E):** a concrete claim about a real crowd, for example "a 500-person prediction market with a shared Slack channel is collectively intelligent because it has many independent predictors."

**R_T is nonzero.** Four things the shorthand holds do not survive the trip back down:

- **What counts as a ground** in a setting where the "truth" is only settled later, or not at all. The shorthand says "measure ground error"; the concrete case may have no anchor that does not move.
- **Whether the frames are genuinely independent.** A shared channel can make correlation rise through mutual update (Fig 2 panel 1) even when membership looks diverse.
- **Which residuals are live.** The concrete case usually reports one aggregate score; the per-slot residual that says where to look is gone.
- **Who is selecting between rooms.** The shorthand says "select"; the concrete case often blends, which Fig 7 :14 says swamps novelty to the mean.

One thing survives cleanly: the two failure mechanisms and the requirement that the anchor not update. That is the residue worth sending. R_T was **not** measured numerically, because no ground reading exists; it is recorded qualitatively as the enumerated set of dropped constraints. Treating the formula as identical to the figure set would set R_T to zero, which is the dead-reckoning error Fig 4 :30 names.
