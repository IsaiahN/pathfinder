# Why the fork is a union of regimes, and the order of the intervention

**Mode: `specified` on the ordering claims, `general` on the union shape.** Simulated derivation checked against the figures, not a ground reading (see `05_what_shape_means.md` section 6).

## RESIDUAL

The question asks two "when" questions as though they were one. The first task of the supporting derivation is to decide whether the two outcomes (collective intelligence, amplified shared error) are two points on one axis, two separate problems, or two conditions of one compound. The second task is to order the interventions, because Fig 3 says a chain is ordered by dependency and effort above a break is wasted.

## DOMAIN CENSUS (carried from `01`, not repeated in full)

- D1, single learning loop: one agent, Γ, b, a, o'; currency is per-slot residual and settlement; anchor is a paying ground.
- D2, population: many agents, a shared library, a ranking; currency is methods up and head starts down; anchor exists only if pinned.
- D3, design seat: an outside checker and a habitat; currency is evidence versus agreement; anchor is the ground, never agreement.

Dependency: D1 settles terms before D2 can rank them; D3 ranges both against the ground and is last. Fig 12 :104: "A settled molecule becomes an atom for whatever composes over it. So irreducible is a fact about a level and never about the world."

## Q1: Partition, union, or one axis?

- Fig 9 :54: "And when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again."
- Fig 9 :55-56: "A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones. A single lever clears the region where only one condition holds and leaves the intersections standing, because the second condition still binds."
- Fig 9 :57: "What remains is smaller and it is also categorically different: a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either. It stops being a mixture and becomes a specific compound."

**Derivation.** "More learning" is one lever. It increases the number of updates in both regimes. In the anchored-independent regime it buys more search (Fig 6 :53-54). In the correlated-unanchored regime it buys more coherence and more confidence without accuracy (Lorenz et al. 2011: "This confidence gain happens despite a lack of improvements"). So the lever clears neither condition; it enlarges both. That is the signature of a union.

**Conclusion.** The fork is a **union of two conditions** that can co-occur: a real system may have some independent, grounded sub-processes and some correlated, unanchored ones at the same time. The intersection is a specific compound, not a harder version of either. It must be unbundled before search. Triangulated across Fig 9, Fig 2 and Fig 7.

**Falsifier, written before the run.** If a single lever (for example increasing agent count) moves both outcomes in the same direction at the same rate across all tested systems, the union claim fails and the question is one axis after all.

## Q2: What is the order of the conditions?

Fig 3 :20-21: "The chain is ordered by dependency, not by importance. Each link consumes what the one above produces, so effort spent above the break arrives at a step that cannot use it."

The conditions are not a checklist with equal weight. Reading Fig 3 into this question:

1. **Perception.** Can the system distinguish a ground reading from internal agreement? If not, every later step consumes a confused input.
2. **Vocabulary.** Does it have terms for per-slot residual, independence and anchor? Fig 12 :112: "A vocabulary without bonds can name a state and cannot name a route to one." A vocabulary without an anchor term can name agreement and cannot name error.
3. **Objective.** Is progress ground error or agreement? Fig 3 :24-26: "Why the middle link is where chains usually break. Perception has obvious failures and obvious tests. Planning has obvious failures and obvious tests. The link between them, having terms for what a situation offers and being able to say what would count as progress, has neither."
4. **Planning.** Which intervention: pin the anchor, de-correlate, select between rooms, keep per-slot residuals.
5. **Learn and carry.** Which methods cross up, with what R_T?

**Conclusion.** The objective link gates the rest. Installing independence (step 4) before the objective is measured (step 3) means the system cannot tell whether independence helped. This is why the outreach material leads with the metric and the refuter rather than with the design suggestions. Triangulated across Fig 3, Fig 1 :27 and Seat Map :281 ("An unmeasured quantity is a specification of what to measure and is worth having, it is not a verdict").

**Falsifier.** Show a system where de-correlating the frames improves held-out error even though its objective is still internal agreement and no ground metric was introduced. Then the objective link is not the gate.

## Q3: Are the two outcomes isomers under Fig 12?

- Fig 12 :117: "isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."
- Fig 12 :119: "catalyst. A part that survives its own use. Spent and regenerating parts behave differently across uses, and nothing records which a term is."
- Fig 12 :121: "cycle. The output re-enters as input. The sign of the loop decides whether it closes, and a chain cannot close by construction."
- Fig 12 :123: "functional group. A substructure whose presence fixes the outcome. The group says what kind; the qualifier says which member."

**Derivation.** The two regimes share the same atoms: agents, a library, a ranking, and a ground. They differ in the bond between the frames and the ground. In one, each frame ranges against a pinned anchor (Fig 2 panel 3). In the other, the frames either update on one another (panel 1) or share one evidence pool (panel 2). Same atoms, different bond, different behaviour. By Fig 12 :117 they are **isomers**, not different amounts of the same thing.

The shared substructure (many frames, one library, one ranking) is a **functional group**: it fixes the kind ("a collective") but not which member ("anchored" versus "drifting"). Fig 12 :124: "the group says what kind; the qualifier says which member."

Two further Fig 12 structures name the dynamics:

- **Cycle.** In the drifting regime the output re-enters as input: agreement feeds back as evidence of correctness. Fig 12 :121: "The sign of the loop decides whether it closes, and a chain cannot close by construction." The loop closes with a positive sign, which is why confidence rises without accuracy.
- **Catalyst.** A shared method survives its own use and is handed down as a head start (Fig 4 :22). In the anchored regime it is re-settled in each new context; in the drifting regime the same method is replayed as a recording, which Fig 4 :25 says "produces a system that repeats a past success and cannot produce a new one."

**Conclusion (`specified`, figure-internal).** The two outcomes are isomers over a shared atom set, distinguishable only by the bond to the ground. This is why headcount cannot tell them apart: the count is in the formula, the bond is not. Triangulated across Fig 12 (isomer, functional group, cycle, catalyst) and Fig 2 (the three bond configurations). The chemistry precedent is recorded in Fig 12 :96-98 (Liebig and Wöhler, 1824 to 1826; Berzelius, 1830), a low-overlap source already inside the figure.

**Falsifier.** If the two outcomes can be written with the same bond to the ground and differ only in the number of agents or the amount of training, the isomer claim fails and they are one thing at two doses.

## Q4: What would an instrument for the isomer difference look like?

Fig 6 :32-34: "An instrument is not built from a description. It is improved from a worse instrument already returning something. Nobody specified a telescope from an account of Mars. A wandering point in the sky was already a reading, and every sharper instrument was built on the residual the last one produced."

Instruments that already return something here:

- pairwise evidence overlap between frames (returns a number, not a verdict);
- held-out ground error, separated from internal agreement (returns two numbers that can diverge);
- per-slot residual logging (already returns the live slot);
- confidence without accuracy, exactly the divergence Lorenz et al. measured (PNAS 2011: "boosts individuals' confidence... despite lack of improved accuracy").

Improving these, not inventing a new "collective intelligence quotient", is the INWARD track. Fig 9 :32: "you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself." What the instrument returns is the kind of bond, not a name for the crowd.

## CONCLUSION

The fork is a union of two overlapping conditions, not one axis. Anchored-independent and correlated-unanchored configurations are isomers over a shared atom set: same agents, library and ranking, different bond to the ground. The intervention order is fixed by dependency, with the objective (ground error versus agreement) as the usually broken middle link. The first measurement to install is the separation of held-out ground error from internal agreement, because without it every later intervention is unreadable.

The falsifiers were written before the run in each section. None has fired. Strains after re-census: none hidden; the one live empirical question is whether the capability claim in Fig 11 :40 survives an agent-only improvement, which the figure itself flags as untested forward.
