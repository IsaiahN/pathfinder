## QUESTION

Decide, in effect terms, what the Tether says about a cell that must produce one fate from several pathway outputs when those outputs conflict or arrive at different times. Option A: answer with a single universal rule, such as summation or a winner pathway. Option B: split the question into its overlapping conditions, state the shape any answer must have, and refuse the molecular name. What each leads to: A gives a `general`-mode verdict that prices away the live per-pathway signal, mistakes the bond for the ingredient list, erases arrival order, and invites a list of famous interactors as corroboration. B gives a checkable four-constraint shape, pre-registered refuters, an honest `gap` for the mechanism, and the instruments that would close it.

## DOMAIN CENSUS

R by arity, symmetry and scale, then one line per domain giving actors, currency and anchor; partition or union; order by dependency.

- **R (the residual).** The composition between k pathway outputs, k at least 2, and one cell-fate decision, with a time index on arrival. Arity: k slots plus time. Symmetry: asymmetric, because pathway valence is relative to the residual and arrival order is ordered, so the governing bonds are ordered (→, ⇒, −, ⋛) at least in part. Scale: intracellular network, cell-fate output, organism or experimental phenotype as anchor. Fig 9 names this a union compound, not one hard question: a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either.
- **Domain 1, per-pathway decoding.** Actors: receptors, adaptors, kinases, transcription factors. Currency: concentration, occupancy, dwell time. Anchor: a measured phenotype that does not change because the decoder predicted it. Question type: how the residual is indexed and which typed bond joins pathway outputs.
- **Domain 2, temporal ordering.** Actors: pulse timing, duration, background level, stimulus schedule. Currency: time-ordered differences and fold changes. Anchor: elapsed time and an imposed schedule. Question type: which bond is ordered and what the swap test returns.
- **Domain 3, conflict and triangulation.** Actors: pathway frames, a shared upstream source, independent external conditions. Currency: agreement, disagreement, independence between evidence pools. Anchor: an externally imposed condition that does not update on the decision. Question type: whether agreement is a verdict or a convention.
- **Domain 4, channel maintenance.** Actors: receptors, phosphatases, desensitisation machinery, instruments. Currency: contact with the anchor over time. Anchor: the same non-updating external condition. Question type: what decays when integration fails.
- **Domain 5, molecular mechanism.** Actors: named proteins and circuits. Currency: kinetic constants. Anchor: none available to this derivation. Not a derivable domain here; recorded as `gap`.
- **Union, not partition.** Simultaneous combination, conflict and temporal order overlap in every real decision (Fig 9 :54, :58). One lever leaves the intersections standing.
- **Order by dependency.** Perception, then vocabulary for what each ligand affords, then the objective (what counts as progress against the ground), then the integration rule, then learn and carry (Fig 3 :20). The likely break is at the vocabulary-to-objective link.

Strain test applied: + is symmetric and → is ordered, but they govern different union conditions, so no contradiction. The internal fold-change reference is an instrument property, not the anchor. Re-census details in `01` section 7.

## VERDICT (from the figures)

Keep the shape, refuse the universal rule, and declare the mechanism a `gap`. The question is a union of three overlapping conditions. Per-pathway indexing is required; the relation between pathway outputs is a typed bond whose identity is not recoverable from the operand list; arrival order is an ordered bond wherever order carries the signal, making step-wise and simultaneous integration isomers over a fixed atom set; conflict is settled by triangulation against a condition that does not update on the decision, with agreement from one evidence pool counted as a convention; and what decays is the channel, not the ground. No figure line names a molecular implementation, so any such name is a provenanced import with a shadow test, not a result. This is a `specified`, ungrounded, candidate shape.

- **cost if wrong:** bounded and locatable. The shape claim states four failure conditions before the conclusion, so an error is findable rather than invisible. The real cost is spending the independence of an outside checker by sending a mechanism list or a famous-name endorsement instead of a refutable shape; Fig 2 :42 prices that ("Buy independence in proportion to the cost of being wrong."). That cost is avoidable by leading with the shape and the refuter, per Fig 10 :52 ("Bring a characterised residual, never the source.").
- **undoable:** yes. The molecular bindings are removable without touching the derivation; `07_the_tether_answer.md` and `08_expert_verification_packet.md` already remove them. A sent claim can be superseded by a corrected packet, and no primitive has to be withdrawn. A wrong bond assignment can be replaced by the correct one once the swap and ratio tests return.
- **reversed by:** a ground reading, not agreement. A pathway-resolved, time-resolved reporter that discriminates the four bond perturbations while aggregate activity is flat would settle C2; an independent external condition manipulable separately from the shared upstream source would settle C3; a channel-state measurement at fixed external condition would settle Domain 4; an instrument that separates the candidate molecular implementations would close the `gap`. A derivation of a specific circuit from the six-term set and the bond set alone, with no biological import, would also close it. A scalar that preserves all decision information, observational equivalence of the bonds, order invariance across tested pairs, or calibration from one evidence pool with a moving anchor would break the corresponding shape claim. Until one of these fires, the verdict stands as `candidate`.
