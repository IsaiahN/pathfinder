# The Tether's answer on integration of multiple cell signals

**Mode: `general` on the structural shape, `specified` on the per-domain claims, `gap` on the molecular mechanism. Not `grounded`.** This is a simulated derivation checked against the figures (`05_what_shape_means.md` section 7), not a ground reading. No cell was measured here.

## 1. The residual it answers

A cell holds several pathways, each a frame that predicts what a ligand affords. The question asks what happens when those predictions conflict or arrive at different times. The residual is the composition between k pathway outputs and one fate decision. Fig 9 :4-5: *"Disagreements that do not shrink with effort, where each rule keeps working well on a different subset, are not one hard question but several well-formed ones. Split it rather than search."* The question is a union of three overlapping conditions, simultaneous combination, conflict and temporal order (Fig 9 :54, :58), each with its own loop and its own anchor. One lever cannot clear all three.

## 2. The shape

1. **The signal stays indexed per pathway.** Fig 1 :19: **"The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated."** Fig 1 :22: **"Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average."** A single scalar of total pathway activity can sit near baseline while one pathway carries a large unsolved demand (Fig 1 :13). So integration must preserve which pathway is live. A flat average is not a neutral shorthand; it is a candidate bond with a stated cost.

2. **Integration is a typed bond, not an ingredient list.** Fig 12 :46: **"A bond is the typed relation between two operands. There are seven, they are not interchangeable, and which one holds is not recoverable from the operands."** Fig 12 :94: **"The arrangement is the substance."** The same two pathways under conjunction, disjunction, sequence, production, subtraction or comparison give different substances (Fig 12 :117: **"Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."**). The answer to "how" is at least: name the bond and state its identifying test.

3. **Arrival order is information when the bond is ordered.** Fig 12 :72: **"A then B; B needs A to have happened"**, with the test Fig 12 :74: **"swap them. Does it become a different thing? Yes."** Fig 13 :88: **"The residual of the turn just closed is what the next turn composes against, so no two passes begin from the same place."** A cell that receives antigen then cytokine is not composing the same thing as one that receives cytokine then antigen. Over a fixed pathway atom set the two are isomers (Fig 12 :117), not one case harder than the other. Where both inputs share one receptor and arrive together, the symmetric + or the ⋛ comparison may be the right bond; where they are sequential through different pathways, → or ⇒ is owed. The union is split before the bond is chosen.

4. **Conflict is triangulation, not negotiation.** Fig 2 :35: **"Alignment is triangulation, not negotiation. A mirror cannot correct you."** Fig 2 :29: **"An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks."** If pathways update on one another with no external ground, they converge on whoever had the most influence (Fig 2 :13-15), a coherent drift nobody notices from inside. Independence is the parameter: Fig 8 :47: **"Independence is one quantity read twice."** Two pathways driven by one ligand agree because the pool is talking, not because they corroborate each other (Fig 2 :40). Where the cost of a wrong fate is high, buy independence from a different external condition (Fig 2 :42).

5. **The ground does not decay; the channel does.** Fig 10 :24: **"the ground does not decay, the channel does"**. Receptor desensitisation, saturation and pooled readings (Fig 10 :26) break contact while the outside condition stays put. What decays is the pathway's channel to the ground, so the cell's office is to keep the anchor reachable. Fig 11 :40: **"An improvement that does not change contact changes nothing, however much it improves."** More downstream components do not repair a saturated receptor.

6. **The molecular mechanism is a declared gap.** Fig 9 :32: **"you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself"**. Fig 13 :134: **"Unreachable is a verdict here, not a silence,"**. No figure line returns a kinase, a scaffold, a promoter or a rate. Names drawn from cell biology are provenanced imports with a shadow test, not derivations, and carry no verdict weight until the ground settles them.

Across these six points the figure side is `specified`, with per-domain triangulations recorded in `01` section 3. The structural skeleton is `general`. Nothing is `grounded`.

## 3. What it explicitly does not say

- Not that cells average or sum pathway inputs as a general rule. That is one bond, and Fig 1 :22 prices it.
- Not that one pathway always wins, or that the fact was decided by cross-talk. That is the no-anchor collapse, not the answer.
- Not that a specific molecule implements the decoder. No molecule is named by the figures.
- Not that the framework returns a mechanism. It returns a shape and a refuter (Fig 9 :32).
- Not that agreement among pathway outputs is evidence about the world. From one evidence pool it is a convention (Fig 2 :41), useful for coordination and worthless as a verdict.
- Not that adding components increases capability. Capability is agent-and-habitat contact (Fig 11 :40).
- Not that this is a settled biological result. It is a simulated derivation and cannot settle the question it reports on.

## 4. What would settle it: the ground

A source-discriminating instrument, not a further argument. From *The Hard Problem is About Source*, a reading of green *"cannot comment on the source"*; the spectrometer resolved it. Here the corresponding tests are:

- **Per-slot test:** a reporter that resolves which pathway is live while the aggregate activity stays flat. Does the fate follow the live slot or the average? If the average, R1 in `01` fires and the per-slot claim fails.
- **Bond test:** perturb the relation, not just the ingredients. Remove one pathway (tests + and ∥), swap arrival order (tests →), change the ratio at fixed total activity (tests ⋛). If all four give the same fate, R2 and R3 fire.
- **Independence test:** manipulate the shared upstream source independently of the external condition. If a verdict persists with no independent ground, R4 fires.
- **Channel test:** vary receptor desensitisation and resensitisation while holding the external condition fixed. Does the decision track the condition or the receptor state? This is Fig 10 :24 at the bench.
- **Instrument test:** find a measurement that discriminates among the candidate molecular implementations that currently produce the same fate reading. Until one exists, the mechanism stays a `gap`.

Standing refuters from `01` section 4: a scalar that preserves all decision information; observational equivalence of the bonds; order invariance; calibration from one evidence pool with a moving anchor; a mechanism derived from the six-term set alone; and a perception-link diagnosis where the objective link was assumed.

## 5. The honest stop

**Unreached, not unreachable, for the mechanism; reached for the shape.** The six-term set is *"a human's careful guess at reality's compression axes"* (*They're Scanning Books*). Declaring it makes the choice visible, not neutral. Another sensorium's minimum set need not be these six. The answer stops at the limit of the available instruments and records the lineage so the stop is checkable. *"Indiscernibility is not identity"* (*How Intelligence Actually Works*). A shape that fits every known time-resolved dataset is still a projection until an instrument separates the sources that produce it.

The live type question stays live: ⋛ is *"a threshold relation between two quantities"* (Fig 12 :87), and whether a fold-change ratio supplies two quantities under the declared code needs an instrument. It is recorded in `06` Q2 and `11`, not hidden.

## 6. One-paragraph version for outreach

> The Tether does not name a molecular mechanism for how cells integrate signals, and it does not claim the cell uses one general rule. It says that the residual must stay indexed per pathway, because a scalar average can be near baseline while one pathway carries a large unsolved demand; that the relation between pathway outputs is itself information, so conjunction, disjunction, sequence and threshold are different substances over the same ingredients; that arrival order is an ordered bond wherever order carries the signal, making step-wise and simultaneous integration isomers rather than one harder case of the other; that conflict is settled by triangulation against a condition that does not move when the cell decides, not by pathways negotiating with one another; and that what decays is the channel to that condition, not the condition itself. The specific protein or circuit is a `gap`: the framework returns a shape, not a name. What it offers is the shape any candidate answer must have, the refuters written in advance, and the instruments that would close it. The mechanism stays open until a source-discriminating measurement settles it.
