# Outreach draft: validate the output first, then decide about the framework

**Use:** one-to-one, or as a cover letter attached to a submission through the public venues in `03_public_contact_points.csv`. **Not** for bulk send.

**Hard precondition:** do not send until F1 to F5 and F11 in `01_figure_grounded_audit.md` are settled in the text below. The stage-one output must survive a check or the two-stage offer collapses on contact.

---

**Subject:** Check one output before you decide whether the framework is worth reading

> Hi [name],
>
> I have one specific output I would like you to check against what you already know about organizational change and knowledge retention. I am not asking you to read the framework first, and I am not asking you to agree with it. If the output does not survive, there is nothing else to look at.
>
> **The output (`specified`, not `grounded`):** "losing knowledge, relationships or trust" is not one stock that a redesign either preserves or depletes. It is three conditions with three different anchors, and they must be reported separately:
>
> 1. **Knowledge** registers per slot, at the moment a needed term is unavailable. What crosses a structural boundary has to be a method someone can reapply, not a recording of one occasion. Where the arrangement that made the knowledge usable is not recorded, the result carries forward but the deriving does not, so each new team pays the bill again.
> 2. **Relationships** register at contact edges. A restructuring does not remove a habitat, it substitutes one, and it inherits actors you did not choose. The two failure modes are silent: what you failed to reproduce, and what you unintentionally introduced. Neither is visible until the goal fails or the new actor acts.
> 3. **Trust** is not a feeling to be protected. Re-grounded, it is the channel to a ground that does not move when the organization reports success, plus a seat that can refuse a verdict. If the people grading the transition are the people who designed it, the refusal is gone even when everyone is friendly.
>
> The framework returns a shape, not a name. It does not give a retention percentage, a span of control, a layer count, or a named ritual such as pairing or a documentation standard. Which concrete arrangement does the job in your setting is a ground question I am not claiming to have settled.
>
> **What would refute the shape claim:**
> 1. one ground-anchored measurement in which knowledge availability, required contact and refusal capacity always move together across every slot and every habitat, with no residue left over;
> 2. a retention gain that survives per-slot indexing while the needed contact edge is not reproduced and no new method crosses the boundary;
> 3. a recorded artifact that fully predicts post-change performance at every slot, with no residual attributable to lost arrangement;
> 4. an organization with durable refusal capacity whose verifiers can reconstruct the claims they check and whose reporting convention fixes the outcome;
> 5. a figure line that derives a unique optimal retention arrangement.
>
> I wrote these down before sending this. If any one holds, the shape is wrong and I will retract it. A one-line "this fails because..." is more useful to me than agreement. If you would rather not engage, no reply is needed.
>
> **What I am not claiming:** that any company was measured, that this is settled by experiment, that tacit knowledge is a primitive the framework discovered, or that a shared post-merger mindset proves anything was retained. Agreement among people who read the same internal report is disqualifying as a verdict. This is a simulated derivation checked for internal consistency, not a ground reading.
>
> **If it survives, and only then:** the sources are below. The figures, not my summary of them, are the authority.
> - Figure text with line numbers (local extract of the canonical SVGs): `FRAMEWORK/reference/03-FIGURES_TEXT.md` in the project folder.
> - Canonical usage rule: `FRAMEWORK/reference/FIGURES_README_canonical.md`.
> - The eight essays by Isaiah N, especially *Decoding Tacit Knowledge: Why Context, Subtext and Pretext Matter* (2026-11-10): https://adventuresinml.substack.com/p/decoding-tacit-knowledge-why-context
>
> **If you want to use the machinery:** the formula is in the framework files. Run it on a change you know well and can check against a ground. Write down what would refute your result *before* you run it, and label the mode of what you get. A reported failure is more useful to me than a reported success.
