# The Tether's answer: changing structure without losing knowledge, relationships or trust

**Mode: `specified` on the substantive claims, `general` on the structural shape, not `grounded`.** This is a shape and a route, not a result. Simulated derivation checked against the figures (`05_what_shape_means.md` §7), not a ground reading. No organization was measured.

## 1. The residual it answers

An organization is about to change which actors carry its loop and which bonds join them. Its model predicts that value will be preserved. The ground returns three distinct failures: a needed term is unavailable at a slot, a required contact edge no longer reaches, or nobody can refuse a verdict. The question asks how to avoid all three. The figures say these are a **union** of conditions, not one stock, so they are reported separately and never averaged (Fig 9 :55, Fig 9 :57).

## 2. The shape

1. **The losses are per slot.** Fig 1 :19: *"The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated."* A single "knowledge retention" score averages away the only signal that says where the loss occurred. The slot is the specific task, relationship or decision where the ground failed to pay.

2. **Knowledge crosses a structural boundary as a method, not a recording.** Fig 4 :22: *"Only methods cross up. Only head starts come down."* Fig 4 :25: *"A recording carried upward looks like knowledge and is a description of one occasion."* A handover package that captures what an expert did last quarter is a recording; a method for regenerating the answer under new conditions is a head start. This is decisive where the arrangement was never written down. Fig 12 :107-110: *"the result carries forward and the deriving does not, so every lineage pays again."*

3. **Knowledge lives in the bond, not the ingredient list.** Fig 12 :5: *"A set of ingredients is not one. The bond is the rest of the information, and it is the half nothing was recording."* Exporting the names of everyone who knew something records half of itself. The typed relation between them, who consults whom about what, is not recoverable from the names. Argote et al. (2025) call the equivalent object a *"supra-individual knowledge repository"* and measure it against patient outcomes.

4. **Relationships survive only if required contact is reproduced.** Fig 11 :66: *"Isolation is not removal of the habitat. It is substitution of one habitat for another."* Fig 11 :67: *"Move the residual and you must reproduce everything its goal required, and you inherit the new habitat's actors, which you did not choose."* The two silent failures are *"what you failed to reproduce, and what you brought with you"* (Fig 11 :68-69). Neither is visible until the goal fails or the new actor acts. So the instrument is a contact map of the residual, enumerated rather than composed (Fig 11 :43), not a list of teams one believes are important.

5. **Trust is a channel to a ground that does not update, plus a seat that can refuse.** Fig 2 :29: *"An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks."* Fig 10 :24: *"the ground does not decay, the channel does"*; Fig 10 :26 lists *"stale percepts, dropped transients, saturated metrics, pooled readings"*. A transition that replaces an outside checker with the design team, or pools every reading into one dashboard, has not "reduced trust" as a stock; it has broken the channel and made coherent drift invisible (Fig 2 :33-34). Fig 10 :42: *"A verifier that can reconstruct the claim cannot verify it."* A seat able to refuse a decision it did not author is the thing that goes missing.

6. **Old and new structure are isomers over a fixed atom set.** Fig 12 :117: *"Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."* This explains why an unchanged headcount and an unchanged document set can coexist with a decisive behavioural change. If the redesign removes an atom rather than re-bonding it, the isomer form fails and the weaker functional-group form stands (Fig 12 :124).

7. **The change is ordered by dependency, not by enthusiasm.** Fig 3 :19: *"Optimising a link that already works cannot rescue one that does not."* Diagnose perception, vocabulary, objective, planning and carry in that order. Tacit loss is a link with no instrument in most organizations (Fig 3 :27); the tacit-knowledge essay proposes one: watch the trend in correction size, not the count of sessions.

## 3. What it explicitly does not say

- Not a retention percentage, a layer count, a span of control, or a named ritual such as pairing. No figure line governs those. This is a declared `gap` (Fig 9 :32, Fig 13 :134), not a silence and not an invitation to invent a number.
- Not that shared culture or a post-merger mindset proves retention. Agreement from a common evidence pool is disqualifying as a verdict (Fig 2 :40) and efficient only as a convention (Fig 2 :41).
- Not that all knowledge should be kept. Fig 5 :26 (*"does the new term cost less to state than the confusion it removes?"*) is a bargain; terms that no longer pay their way are not assets. The question's word "valuable" is a threshold comparison that needs a ground (Operators table :51: *"a goal is a comparison, which is why a vocabulary without it cannot state one"*), not a figure answer.
- Not that people are interchangeable containers. The claim is about where knowledge is stored: in bonds and records, not in names.
- Not grounded. Nothing here is settled by an experiment on an organization.

## 4. What would settle it: the ground

A measurement, not another argument. The relevant discriminators are:

- **Availability test:** at each slot after the change, is the needed term a lookup or must it be re-derived? A rising re-derivation cost with no new contact is the failure of F3 and F5.
- **Correction-trend test:** does the size of corrections to newcomers shrink with contact, as the tacit-knowledge essay predicts? If it does not, contact is not rebuilding the arrangement.
- **Contact-reproduction test:** does every contact edge the residual required before the change still reach it after, and which new actors were unintentionally introduced? (F6.)
- **Refusal test:** is there a seat that can refuse a verdict it did not author, with a ground it cannot move? (F8, Fig 10 :42, Fig 2 :29.)
- **Standing falsifiers:** one condition moving all three losses together across every slot in every habitat; a retention gain with no reproduced contact and no method crossing; a recording that fully predicts post-change performance; durable refusal capacity with reconstructing verifiers; a figure line deriving a unique optimal retention arrangement.

## 5. The honest stop

**Unreached, not unreachable.** The six-term set and the figure rules fix the shape any working answer must have: a method crossing a boundary, reproduced contact, a non-updating ground, a refusal seat. They do not name the arrangement that achieves it in a particular company. Fig 9 :32: *"you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself."* Fig 13 :134: *"Unreachable is a verdict here, not a silence,"* and Fig 8 :39: *"So a search that finds nothing is never a proof of absence."* What closes it is an instrument at the ground, not a longer literature search. The lineage is recorded so the stop is checkable; *"Indiscernibility is not identity."*

## 6. One-paragraph version for outreach

> The Tether does not give a retention percentage or a new org chart. It says that knowledge, relationships and trust are three different quantities with different anchors, so they must be measured per slot and never averaged into one score. A structural change keeps knowledge when a method, not a recording, crosses the boundary and when the arrangement is recorded or rebuilt through contact; where it is not recorded, each new team pays the derivation cost again. It keeps relationships by reproducing the contact the work actually required, because a restructuring substitutes a habitat and always brings actors you did not choose. It keeps trust by preserving a channel to a ground that does not move when the organization reports success, and a seat that can refuse. Old and new structures over the same atoms are isomers: same ingredients, different bonds, different substance. What it offers is that shape, the refuters written in advance, and the instruments that would close it. The specific arrangement stays open until the ground settles it.
