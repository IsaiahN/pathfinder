## 0. The claim under audit

Restate the question as an effect. A monitoring program holds a belief `b`: the observed variation is stationary natural fluctuation around a stable state. An observation `o'` arrives: variance has risen, lag-1 autocorrelation has risen, the mean has drifted, skewness has changed. The prediction `Γ(b,a)` under the stationary belief did not include that observation, so a residual `R = |Γ(b,a) − o'|` exists on one or more monitored slots. The decision to be made is **which bin that residual belongs to**, because each bin implies a different next act:

- nothing owed (the fluctuation was inside the stationary model),
- extend perception and aim the probe (a genuinely new signal whose meaning is not yet known),
- refit a binding without minting a new mechanism (a parameter shift, not a new regime),
- a new mechanism term is owed (a loss of resilience toward a different state),
- a held term is false and must be refused (the "stationary" belief itself is wrong).

**What an answer would look like, bound as an ordinary term (Formula, "Direction").** The sought-for shape is not left as a preference. It is written down here so it can accrue a residual: *a procedure that (i) reads residuals per indicator/population/site, (ii) commits in advance to what a mechanism-specific early-change signal would look like, (iii) obtains corroboration only from frames with low evidence overlap, (iv) maintains the channel to the ecosystem rather than treating the metric as the ground, and (v) defers the verdict to the ecosystem's future trajectory or to an experiment.* This is the shape under audit. A procedure lacking any one of the five does not answer the question as posed.

## 0b. Residual R by arity, symmetry, scale

- **Arity:** at least two. A single rising-variance observation is jointly compatible with a stationary heavy-tailed process, a step change in a driver, a noise-induced transition, and an approaching bifurcation. The world literature is explicit: "not all systems that show regime shifts exhibit critical slowing down, or vice versa" (Boettiger, Ross and Hastings 2013). This is a **union compound**, not a binary partition (Fig 9 :54).
- **Symmetry:** the operative relation is a threshold comparison, ⋛-like, not a symmetric join. The question is whether a trend is large and sustained enough, relative to a named mechanism, to reclassify the slot. Two series with identical indicator values can have different referents.
- **Scale:** first order inside one indicator series; second order at the ecosystem level where several series and a driver compose. The two scales are not interchangeable: "A gradient over space and one over time are different terms, because which state it is bonded to is part of what it is" (Fig 13 :76).

## 2. Findings

### F1: The question is a union, not a binary (governs the whole program)

Fig 9 :4-5: "First: is there a fact here? Disagreements that do not shrink with effort, where each rule keeps working well on a different subset, are not one hard question but several well-formed ones. Split it rather than search." Fig 9 :54: "And when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again."

A rising variance reading is produced by at least four mechanisms. A single lever (say, variance alone) clears the region where only one condition holds and leaves the intersections standing, because the second condition still binds (Fig 9 :54-58). The Library of Babel essay states the same limit in search terms: "Perhaps your problem was a bundle or conflation of several problems masquerading as one? You will need to search all of those problems individually to find their answers and then combine them in a satisfactory way."

- **World corroboration:** O'Brien et al. 2023 found "many accepted regime shifts are not critical transitions, with different trophic levels often expressing different forms of abrupt change." Gsell et al. 2016 list three distinct mechanisms (competition, trophic cascade, intraguild predation) for the same class of transition.
- **Refuter:** show that a single indicator, calibrated once, discriminates fluctuation from change at controlled error rates across all the mechanisms above. If it does, the union reading is unnecessary.
- **Verdict:** `triangulated` across Fig 9 and *The Library of Babel* (different domains, low overlap), with world corroboration.

### F2: Residuals must be per slot, never pooled into a global index (decisive for monitoring design)

Fig 1 :19: "The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated." Fig 1 :22: "Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average." Fig 1 :13: "a global R near zero with one live slot residual is a legal state."

A single ecosystem-level average can be flat while one population, one trophic level, or one site carries the live signal. The anchor is not a pooled index: "The ground enters as the only metric" (Fig 1 :23).

- **World corroboration:** O'Brien et al. 2023: "Monitoring one representative variable is, therefore, unlikely to be sufficient and many facets of the system should be tracked, including plausible environmental drivers." Gsell et al. 2016 found low agreement among four indicators on the same series (AR-1, SD, SK, DR) and "agreement among the four indicators was low."
- **Refuter:** demonstrate that a pooled index preserves detection sensitivity and false-positive control relative to per-slot reading across independent ecosystems.
- **Verdict:** `triangulated` across Fig 1 and Fig 12 (isomer/functional-group, see F7), with world corroboration.

### F3: The sought-for shape must be an ordinary term written before the run

Fig 5 :65-66: "make the sought-for shape an ORDINARY TERM. Write down what an answer would look like, bind it, and let it accrue a residual like anything else." Fig 5 :63: "Every check above runs on the MECHANISM and none runs on the DIRECTION OF THE QUESTION". Fig 9 :27-28: "describe it before you go looking, or any frame you pick will seem to fit".

A monitoring frame that searches for "early warning" without writing down what it expects is running every check on the mechanism (is the trend significant, is the window stable) and none on the direction (would it have accepted the opposite result). The remedy in the figures is a discipline, not a branch: commit the prediction before the run. Seat Map :287: "And a falsifier written before the run is a design instrument, not only an honesty device."

- **World corroboration:** Gsell et al. 2016: "a priori knowledge of the underlying mechanisms driving ecosystem transitions is necessary to identify relevant state variables for successfully monitoring EWIs." The MDPI Entropy 2026 review (read via public search extract, direct fetch returned 403, recorded in `10_source_ledger.csv`) states: "Many early warning studies analyze historical time series selected specifically because they ended in a known transition, introducing conditional sampling bias."
- **Refuter:** show that a post hoc pattern matcher, with no pre-registered mechanism-specific expectation, has the same controlled error rates as a pre-registered detection procedure on prospective data.
- **Verdict:** `triangulated` across Fig 5, Fig 9, and the Seat Map (three distinct offices), with world corroboration.

### F4: Corroboration needs low evidence overlap, not more of the same series

Fig 2 :31-32: "Independence, between two frames, is how little they draw on the same evidence." Fig 2 :40: "disqualifying for a verdict: agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool." Fig 7 :12-13: "same closure, handed along; agreement compounds, evidence does not." Fig 8 :29: "low overlap: B does not draw on A's evidence."

Four indicators computed from one time series are one frame wearing four names. Two monitoring programs reading the same water body with the same sensor and the same preprocessing are one witness. Independence must be bought in proportion to the cost of being wrong (Fig 2 :42: "Buy independence in proportion to the cost of being wrong."): in situ measurement, remote sensing, Indigenous observation, and a mechanistic experiment are separate closures only to the extent that they do not draw on the same readings.

- **World corroboration:** Gsell et al. 2016: "these EWIs varied in reliability, and agreement between indicators was low." O'Brien et al. 2023: "the agreement among them was low." Thygesen 2025 frames the operational cost: "Thresholds for raising alarms involve a trade-off between missed and false alarms."
- **Refuter:** show that multiple statistics from one series carry independent information about the mechanism at the ground, not merely about the sampling path.
- **Verdict:** `triangulated` across Fig 2, Fig 7, Fig 8 (three separate figures), with world corroboration.

### F5: The channel decays, not the ground

Fig 10 :24: "the ground does not decay, the channel does." Fig 10 :25-26: "stale percepts, dropped transients, saturated metrics, pooled readings." Fig 10 :27-28: "the output is not a verdict, it is you have lost touch."

In ecological monitoring the failure is frequently not that the ecosystem stopped changing but that the reading stopped resolving it: a sensor saturated at a detection limit, a sampling interval too coarse for the return time, a population pooled across a change, a transient dropped as noise. The correct output of that state is "contact has been lost", not "the system is stable". Seat Map :171: "Where a stake cannot be removed, declare it." A monitoring program whose funding depends on a "stable" finding has a declared interest in the reading.

- **World corroboration:** Gsell et al. 2016 note "observation error, sparse sampling, and low signal-to-noise ratio" and that monthly averages "aggregate multiple generations of phytoplankton." O'Brien et al. 2023: "Each EWS method was strongly influenced by the data pre-processing performed."
- **Refuter:** show that detection reliability is invariant to saturation, pooling across the transition, and dropped transients at realistic rates.
- **Verdict:** `triangulated` across Fig 10 and the Seat Map, with world corroboration.

### F6: No finite series settles the question; unreachable is a verdict, not a silence

Fig 8 :39: "So a search that finds nothing is never a proof of absence." Fig 9 :44: "Only a sealed room can be searched to the end, and no frame is a sealed room. So searching and finding nothing proves nothing." Fig 13 :134: "Unreachable is a verdict here, not a silence". Symbols table: `R` "is the aim, not only the error signal, and it is always a slice: what cannot be perceived yet is residual and simply cannot be read."

A series that shows no trend is not evidence that no change is coming. Equally, a series that shows a trend is not proof that a critical transition is coming: false positives and false negatives occur. Gsell et al. 2016: "The expected trends in AR-1, SD, DR, and SK are not exclusive indicators of critical transitions as false positives and false negatives can occur." The distinction between underivable in principle and unreachable at this budget matters (Fig 8 :40-41: "Underivable-in-principle and unreachable-at-this-budget are different facts, and they issue different instructions."): a short noisy series is a budget problem, answerable by longer or higher-resolution monitoring, not by importing a new theory.

- **Refuter:** produce a finite-series decision rule with a proof of correctness for both outcomes, with no reference to future observations.
- **Verdict:** `triangulated` across Fig 8, Fig 9, Fig 13, with world corroboration.

### F7: Identical statistics can be different compounds

Fig 12 :94: "The arrangement is the substance." Fig 12 :117: "isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour." Fig 12 :124: "functional group. A substructure whose presence fixes the outcome. The group says what kind; the qualifier says which member."

Two series with the same variance and AR(1) values, one driven by a slowly moving nutrient load toward a bifurcation and one driven by a seasonal artifact, are the same ingredients under different bonds. The indicator formula cannot tell them apart; the bond to the driver can. Rising variance and rising autocorrelation form a **functional group** that says "loss of resilience" as a kind, not which member. Naming a particular mechanism from the pattern alone repeats the metamerism error described in *The Hard Problem is About Source*: "If several paths create the same behavior, then behavior is a projection with lossy information (it cannot define consciousness in full)." The essay's resolution is instrumental: "It was proven wrong not by argument, but by the arrival of a better instrument." See `06_isomers_and_order.md`.

- **Refuter:** show that two series matched on all indicator statistics and on their bond to the same driver can still diverge in mechanism at the ground; if they cannot, the isomer caution is empty for this question.
- **Verdict:** `triangulated` across Fig 12 and the source essay (different domains), with world corroboration from O'Brien et al. 2023 on processing dependence.

### F8: Mechanism knowledge enters as an import with provenance and a shadow test

Fig 6 :23: "No frame escapes its own closure. The bound moves only on contact with a frame whose closure differs." Fig 8 :50-52: "Convergent derivation and adopted import look identical in the contents. Only the record of where each came from separates them". Fig 9 :42: "narrowing gets you close, the ground decides."

 Knowing whether an indicator means "early change" requires a frame whose closure differs from the time-series frame: a mechanism model, a whole-ecosystem manipulation, an experiment. That import must carry provenance (which system, which driver, which date) and a shadow test (does it explain the gap?). Import from a live peer is "debited against that frame's independence" (Fig 6 :24), and a wrong atom costs more than starting from primitives.

- **World corroboration:** Gsell et al. 2016: "in-depth ecosystem knowledge" is required; O'Brien et al. 2023: "predictive ecology should start to move away from the concept of critical transitions, developing methods suitable for predicting resilience loss not limited to the strict bounds of bifurcation theory."
- **Refuter:** show that mechanism-free detection works generically, so no import is owed.
- **Verdict:** `triangulated` across Fig 6 and Fig 8 (two figures with different jobs), with world corroboration.

## 3. Pre-registered refuter

Written before the conclusion, per Seat Map :287. The shape claim is refuted if any of the following holds:

1. **Pooling refuter:** a single pooled indicator, applied without a pre-registered mechanism-specific shape, distinguishes fluctuation from long-term change at better-than-chance rates with controlled false-positive rates across independent ecosystems (breaks F2 and F3).
2. **Overlap refuter:** two analyses drawing on the same time series are shown to give independent corroboration of each other at the ground, not merely consistent descriptions of the same data (breaks F4).
3. **Finite-series refuter:** a finite time series alone settles the fluctuation-versus-change question with no reference to future observations, an experiment, or a mechanism, with a correctness proof for both outcomes (breaks F6).
4. **Channel refuter:** detection reliability is invariant to saturation, pooled readings across the change, and dropped transients at realistic monitoring rates (breaks F5).
5. **Union refuter:** one indicator calibrated once discriminates across critical transitions, step changes, noise-induced transitions, and linear driver tracking with controlled error (breaks F1).

If any fires, the shape claim is withdrawn. Until one is run, no part of the answer may be labelled `grounded`.

## 4. Recommendation

1. Split the bundle before searching: treat critical transition, step change, noise-induced transition, and linear tracking as separate compounds with their own indicators and their own ground (F1).
2. Publish per-slot residuals, not just an ecosystem average (F2).
3. Write the expected mechanism-specific signal down before the data are analysed, with an explicit refusal condition (F3).
4. Buy independence deliberately: in situ, remote, Indigenous, and experimental readings are separate frames only if their evidence pools differ (F4).
5. Treat monitoring maintenance as an office whose output is "contact lost", not "system stable" (F5).
6. Report the unreachable result honestly when a series is too short: the remedy is longer or higher-resolution monitoring, not a new theory (F6).
7. Carry mechanism knowledge with provenance and a shadow test, and never let a filter hand you the verdict (F8, Fig 9 :60).

Fig 4 :25: "A recording carried upward looks like knowledge and is a description of one occasion." Send the generator (the per-slot discipline, the pre-registered shape, and the refuter), not a single successful case study.

### Seven-question recursive derivation (summary)

1. **One question or several?** Several, a union (Fig 9 :4-5, :54). Split.
2. **What is the residual?** A gap on a per-slot observation, compatible with several mechanisms (Fig 1 :19).
3. **Which bin?** REFUTED is possible (the stationary belief may be false), but the first four bins must be searched before minting (Formula step 2; Fig 5 amendment).
4. **What closes it locally?** Mechanism knowledge imported from a differing closure, with provenance and a shadow test (Fig 6 :23; Fig 8 :50-52).
5. **Do more indicators help?** Only if their evidence pools differ (Fig 2 :31-32; Fig 7 :12-13).
6. **What if nothing registers?** Distinguish short series (more search) from genuinely absent signal (Fig 8 :39-43). Unreachable is a verdict.
7. **What settles it?** The ground: future trajectory, experiment, manipulation. Not agreement, not a threshold, not this simulated derivation (Fig 2 :35; Fig 9 :42).

### Updated verdict

The shape survives: split the union, read residuals per slot, pre-register the mechanism-specific shape, demand low-overlap corroboration, maintain the channel, and defer the verdict to the ground. Every specific statistic offered as "the" early warning signal is refused as a name. The result is `specified` and ungrounded. Strains after re-census: **one live, named** (the ⋛-like threshold comparison is a structural reading of the question's arrow, not a literal bond claimed by the figures); **none hidden**.
