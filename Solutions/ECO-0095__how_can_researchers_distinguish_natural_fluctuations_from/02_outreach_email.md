**Use:** one to one, or as a cover note attached to a submission through the public venues in `03_public_contact_points.csv`. **Not** for bulk send.

**Hard precondition:** send only after the three checkable sub-claims in `08_expert_verification_packet.md` have been re-checked against the verbatim figure lines by the sender. If the shape claim does not survive, the two-stage offer collapses on contact.

**Subject:** One claim about early warning signals, checked before you see the theory

> Hi [name],
>
> I have one specific claim about ecological monitoring that I would like you to check against what you already know. I am not asking you to read any framework first, and I am not asking you to agree with it. If the claim does not survive, there is nothing else to look at.
>
> **The residual (`specified`, not `grounded`).** A monitoring program watches an indicator through time and sees variance rise, or lag-1 autocorrelation rise, or a mean drift. The same observation is compatible with two different explanations: stationary natural variation around a stable state, and an early sign that the system is losing resilience before a long-term change. The question is not which statistic is largest. It is which bin the observed gap belongs to: nothing owed, extend the probe, refit a binding, a new mechanism term owed, or a held belief shown false.
>
> **The shape claim (`specified`).** A defensible distinction must have five properties, and it fails without any one of them:
>
> 1. Residuals are read per indicator, per population, per site. Aggregating across them is how a live early signal disappears into an average.
> 2. What "early change" would look like is written down before the run, for a named mechanism and a named state variable, and then allowed to be wrong. A frame searching for a signal it has not written down cannot see its own preference.
> 3. Corroboration comes from frames that do not share the same data pool. Three indicators computed from one time series are one witness, not three.
> 4. The monitoring channel is treated as the thing that decays. Stale readings, dropped transients, saturated metrics, and populations pooled across a change are losses of contact, not verdicts about the ecosystem.
> 5. The verdict is left to the ground, which here means the ecosystem's own future trajectory, observed or manipulated. A finite series that shows no trend is not proof that no change is coming.
>
> **What I am not claiming.** Not that any particular statistic is "the" early warning signal. The framework returns a shape, not a name. Not that a significant trend settles anything: thresholds are filters for the budget, never witnesses for the verdict. Not that this is a universal method: regime shifts, step changes, noise-induced transitions, and linear tracking of a driver can produce overlapping signatures, and a bundle has to be split before it is searched.
>
> **What would refute the shape claim.** I wrote these down before sending this:
> 1. A single pooled indicator, applied without a pre-registered mechanism-specific shape, is shown to distinguish fluctuation from change at better-than-chance rates with controlled false-positive rates across independent ecosystems.
> 2. Two analyses drawing on the same time series are shown to provide independent corroboration of each other.
> 3. A finite time series alone is shown to settle the fluctuation-versus-change question with no reference to future observations, an experiment, or a mechanism.
> 4. Degradation of the monitoring channel (saturation, pooling across a change, dropped transients) is shown not to affect detection reliability.
>
> If any one holds, the shape claim is wrong and I will retract it. A one-line "this fails because..." is more useful to me than agreement. If you would rather not engage, no reply is needed.
>
> **If it survives, and only then:** the sources are below. The figures, not my summary of them, are the authority.
> - Verbatim figure text with line numbers (local extract in this repository): `FRAMEWORK/reference/03-FIGURES_TEXT.md`
> - Gsell et al. 2016, PNAS, open access: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5167183
> - O'Brien et al. 2023, Nature Communications: https://www.nature.com/articles/s41467-023-43744-8
> - Boettiger, Ross and Hastings 2013, Theoretical Ecology: https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s12080-013-0192-6
>
> **If you want to use the machinery:** the figures and the formula are in the repository. Run them on a monitoring problem you know well and can check against a ground. Write down what would refute your result *before* you run it. A reported failure is more useful to me than a reported success.
>
> Thanks,
> [name]
