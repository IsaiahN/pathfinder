# Expert verification packet: the Tether on natural fluctuation versus long-term ecosystem change

**What this is:** a claim in a form an ecological time-series researcher can check. Mode, derivation and refuters are stated before the conclusion, so the reader knows what to look for and what would settle it.

**Mode: `specified` on the shape, `general` on the limit. Not `grounded`.** No ecological measurement is claimed to have been taken here. Simulated derivation checked for internal consistency against the figures (`05_what_shape_means.md` §6).

## 1. The claim, in one paragraph

"Natural fluctuation versus early sign of long-term change" is a union of several mechanisms, not one binary, and must be split before it is searched. A defensible distinction reads residuals per indicator, population and site rather than pooling them into an ecosystem average; commits in advance to what a mechanism-specific early-change signal would look like; requires corroboration from frames with low evidence overlap rather than more statistics from the same series; treats saturation, pooling and dropped transients as loss of contact rather than as evidence of stability; and leaves the verdict to the ecosystem's future trajectory or to an experiment. Thresholds and significance tests are filters for what to try next, not witnesses for what is true. The framework names no universal statistic.

- **Refuter:** show that a single pooled indicator, calibrated once with no pre-registered mechanism-specific shape, discriminates fluctuation from change at controlled error rates across independent ecosystems and across the union members. If it does, this sub-claim fails.

### C2: ecology-facing; checkable against the empirical EWS literature

- **Statement:** rising variance and lag-1 autocorrelation are a functional group that says "loss of resilience" as a kind, not a specific source; several mechanisms and several trophic levels produce overlapping readings, and indicators computed from the same series do not corroborate one another.
- **What to check:** is that characterization faithful? Gsell et al. 2016 report "these EWIs varied in reliability, and agreement between indicators was low" and require a priori mechanism knowledge. O'Brien et al. 2023 find "different trophic levels often expressing different forms of abrupt change" and "the agreement among them was low." Boettiger et al. 2013: "not all systems that show regime shifts exhibit critical slowing down, or vice versa." Fig 12 :124 "functional group. A substructure whose presence fixes the outcome. The group says what kind; the qualifier says which member."
- **Refuter:** show that two series matched on all indicator statistics and on their bond to the same driver at the same scale can still diverge in mechanism at the ground; or show that statistics from one series carry independent ground information. The first kills the isomer caution, the second kills the low-overlap requirement.

### C3: framework-internal derivation with an operational consequence

- **Statement:** the channel to the ground decays, and the verdict belongs to the ground, not to agreement or to a threshold.
- **Source, verbatim:**
  - Fig 10 :24 "the ground does not decay, the channel does."
  - Fig 10 :25-26 "stale percepts, dropped transients, saturated metrics, pooled readings."
  - Fig 10 :27-28 "the output is not a verdict, it is you have lost touch."
  - Fig 9 :60 "Use filters for the budget and witnesses for the verdict. Never let a filter hand you a verdict."
  - Fig 8 :39 "So a search that finds nothing is never a proof of absence."
  - Fig 9 :44 "Only a sealed room can be searched to the end, and no frame is a sealed room. So searching and finding nothing proves nothing."
- **Refuter:** show that detection reliability is invariant to channel degradation at realistic rates, or produce a finite-series decision rule with a correctness proof for both outcomes that needs no future observation or experiment.

## 3. What is explicitly not claimed

- Not a named statistic. No variance, AR(1), skewness, density ratio, spectral indicator or machine-learning model is asserted as the answer. Fig 9 :32 "you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself."
- Not a universal method. "no single technique fits all situations" (Thygesen 2025).
- Not that a non-significant trend proves stability, nor that a significant trend proves a critical transition. Gsell et al. 2016: false positives and false negatives occur.
- Not that this is `grounded`. No ecological measurement was taken in this derivation.
- Not a claim that the figures name the mechanism for a particular lake, forest or fishery. A mechanism enters as a provenanced import with a shadow test (Fig 6 :23, Fig 8 :50-52), not as a figure derivation.

## 5. What would settle it

The ground, not agreement. *The Hard Problem is About Source*: an instrument that reads green "cannot comment on the source"; "It was proven wrong not by argument, but by the arrival of a better instrument."

1. Does the ecosystem's own trajectory cross and persist beyond the predicted boundary, or return?
2. Does relaxing the driver produce hysteresis (a different return path), or not? Gsell et al. 2016: single step changes "cannot provide direct evidence of a critical transition."
3. Can a mechanism model or whole-ecosystem manipulation discriminate a bifurcation from a step change, a noise-induced transition, or linear driver tracking for the specific state variable?
4. Was the channel intact (no saturation, no pooling across the change, no dropped transients) over the interval that produced the warning?
