## What is deliberately not here

- No personal emails of named individuals.
- No ranking of people by how likely they are to agree with the shape claim.
- No bulk-send plan.
- No claim that the figures name a particular statistical test (rising variance, lag-1 autocorrelation, density ratio, skewness, or a machine-learning classifier) as the answer. The framework returns a shape, not a name (Fig 9 :32).

Three figure-grounded reasons for the omissions:

1. **Agreement is not settlement.** Fig 2 :35: "Alignment is triangulation, not negotiation. A mirror cannot correct you." A list selected for receptiveness manufactures the correlation that disqualifies a verdict.
2. **A verifier who holds the interiors cannot verify.** Fig 10 :42: "A verifier that can reconstruct the claim cannot verify it." Stage 1 therefore carries only the residual, the shape claim, and its refuter.
3. **Filters are not witnesses.** Fig 9 :60: "Use filters for the budget and witnesses for the verdict. Never let a filter hand you a verdict." Significance thresholds, AIC values, and false-alarm rates decide what to try, not what is true.

Legally: public opt-in institutional channels avoid the GDPR and CAN-SPAM style failures that attach to scraped personal addresses for unsolicited bulk outreach. That is a practical failure on top of the structural one.
