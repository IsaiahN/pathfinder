## Distribution: offer, do not push

Figure-grounded rules:

- Fig 10 :52 "Bring a characterised residual, never the source." Lead with the refuter and the shape claim. The framework is offered only after a verdict on stage 1 is recorded.
- Fig 2 :35 "Alignment is triangulation, not negotiation. A mirror cannot correct you." Do not ask for agreement. A failure is more useful than an endorsement.
- Fig 9 :27-28 "describe it before you go looking, or any frame you pick will seem to fit." Ask what the reader would expect to see if the shape claim were true before giving them the theory.
- Fig 4 :23 "upward: a method that can be reapplied. Never the recording of a particular success." Send the generator, not the conclusion.
- Fig 2 :31 independence is how little frames draw on the same evidence, so no bulk send and no list selected for likely agreement.

## The two-stage offer

1. **Stage 1.** Send the residual, the shape claim and the four pre-registered refuter conditions. Ask what the reader would count as a failure. Record their verdict before showing any theory.
2. **Stage 2, opt-in only.** If they are curious, offer the three framework links. The figures, not the summary, are the authority. Their later agreement is never pooled as corroboration (Fig 2 :40).
3. **Stage 3, also opt-in.** Offer the formula as a method to reuse. Ask each user to state their own ground, write their own refuter before running, and label the mode of what they get. The architecture's own demo note warns that outputs can be "contrived to fit the formula", so an ungrounded simulation is not a test.

**Hard precondition:** stages 1 to 3 work only if the stage-1 output survives. If a checker fires any of the four refuter conditions, the packet is revised and the retraction is recorded in `01_figure_grounded_audit.md`.
