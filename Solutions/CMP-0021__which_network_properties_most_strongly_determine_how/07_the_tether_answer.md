# The Tether's answer on network properties and spreading

**Mode: `specified` for the shape, `general` for the substrate rule, `gap` for the specific ranking. Not `grounded`.** This is a shape and a route, not a result. Simulated derivation checked against the figures (`05_what_shape_means.md` §7), not a ground reading.

## 1. The residual it answers

For one network/process/seed case `s`, `R_s = |Γ(b,a)_s − o'_s|` is the gap between predicted and observed spread. The question asks which structural property, substituted into Γ, shrinks `R_s` most across cases. Fig 1 :19 "The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated." Fig 2 :29 "An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks." The anchor is the measured spread trace, not the number of papers agreeing about hubs.

## 2. The shape

1. **Spreading is influence travelling over a network structure.** Fig 13 :24 "that entities relate, and influence travels". The network is not one candidate among three; it is the substrate in which the travel happens. Degree distribution, clustering and community structure are descriptions of its arrangement, not rival structures. `general` for the substrate reading.

2. **A spread is a flow and needs a spendable difference.** Fig 13 :22 "a difference that can be spent"; Fig 13 :76 "Energy and a gradient compose a flow." A process cannot spread where every node is already in the same state, and it cannot spread forever without a finite resource to spend. This puts process dynamics and seed policy on the same footing as topology. The "something else" in the question is at minimum this. `specified` for the field application.

3. **The three named properties form a union, not a partition.** Fig 9 :55 "A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones." Fig 9 :56 "A single lever clears the region where only one condition holds and leaves the intersections standing, because the second condition still binds." A network has a degree distribution and clustering and community structure simultaneously; the compound is not three easier versions of one question. So "which ONE most strongly" is a bundled question that must be unbundled before it is searched.

4. **The comparison must be per slot and the valence is relative.** Fig 1 :22 "Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average." Fig 11 :49 "An actor's valence is relative to the residual, not a property it carries." A property that wins for early speed in one process may lose for final reach in another, and a property that promotes spread may undermine robustness (the adjacent question CMP-0022). No standing list of "spread-promoting properties" survives a change of residual.

5. **The specific ranking is a `gap`.** No figure line ranks degree distribution against clustering against community structure. Fig 9 :32 "you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself." Fig 13 :134 "Unreachable is a verdict here, not a silence". Fig 8 :39 "So a search that finds nothing is never a proof of absence." The figures can say what any acceptable answer must look like; they cannot pick a winner. The world literature reinforces the refusal: one model reports no epidemic threshold from degree heterogeneity (Pastor-Satorras and Vespignani 2001), another reports geography beating hubs (Xu et al. 2006). That is a conflict inside one evidence pool, which is informative, not a verdict (Fig 2 :40).

6. **The three properties are isomer-like arrangements, not rival atoms.** Fig 12 :117 "isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour." Fig 12 :94 "The arrangement is the substance." Same nodes and edges, different arrangement, different spread. This is why a single-property lever loses the very information the question is about (see `06_isomers_and_order.md`).

## 3. What it explicitly does not say

- Not that degree distribution always wins, or that hubs are always the answer.
- Not that clustering or community structure is irrelevant. Each is a real habitat item (Fig 11 :44).
- Not that the framework has settled an empirical debate. The ranking is a `gap` and a falsifier is attached.
- Not that "structure alone" determines spread. Structure without a spendable difference has no flow.
- Not that the world sources corroborate independently. They share an evidence pool (Fig 2 :40).
- Not `grounded`. No controlled cross-process study was run by this derivation.

## 4. What would settle it - the ground

The missing instrument is a **source-discriminating comparator**: a study that fixes the process (SIS, SIR, threshold cascade, percolation), fixes the quantity (time to arrival versus final reach), fixes the seed policy, then varies one structural property at a time and reports per-slot residuals. From *The Hard Problem is About Source*: a spread curve is a projection that *"cannot comment on the source"*; the spectrometer, not an argument, resolved the green case. Here the analogous instrument is the controlled intervention.

Concrete settling questions:

- Holding degree sequence fixed, does changing transitivity move the per-slot residual for SIR but not for a threshold cascade? If yes, the ranking is process-conditional, and F3 stands.
- Holding process fixed, does a property's ranking reverse when the measured quantity changes from speed to reach? If yes, aggregation is forbidden and F4 stands.
- Holding everything else fixed, does geography beat hubs in a spatially embedded network, as Xu et al. report, or does the degree result survive? A clean conflict resolved at the ground would close one side of F6.
- Does a single property leave a residual that does not trend down across all process types? That would fire refuter 1 and reverse the verdict.

## 5. The honest stop

**Unreached, not unreachable.** The six-term set is *"a human's careful guess at reality's compression axes"* (*They're Scanning Books*); declaring it makes the choice visible, not neutral. The ranking is unreached at this budget and with these instruments, not proven absent. The record stops where the instruments stop and keeps the lineage so the stop is checkable. *How Intelligence Actually Works*: "indiscernibility is not identity." A shape is not an answer.

## 6. Standing falsifiers (pre-registered in `01`)

1. One property dominates speed and reach across all process types, seed policies, sizes and spatial embeddings, leaving no residual for the others.
2. A process spreads with no spendable difference and no finite resource.
3. The three properties form a disjoint partition, so one lever clears the intersections.
4. A figure line that ranks the three properties directly is found on re-census.
5. "Network" is derived from the other five substrate terms, or influence travel is not its defining content.

## 7. One-paragraph version for outreach

> The Tether does not pick a winner between degree distribution, clustering and community structure, and it does not claim to have settled a famous empirical debate. It says that spreading is influence travelling over a network structure, that any travel is a flow needing a spendable difference and a finite resource, so the process type and the seed policy belong in the answer alongside topology; that the three named properties are overlapping conditions of the same network, not rival options, so a single lever leaves the intersections standing; and that the comparison must be made per network/process/seed case, because a property's sign changes with the residual. Under the figure on bonds, the three properties are isomer-like arrangements of the same atom set: the same node and edge ingredients under a different arrangement give a different spread, and an ingredient-only formula records half of the information. The framework returns a shape, not a ranking. What it offers is that shape, four falsifiers written in advance, and the kind of source-discriminating experiment that would close the ranking. The slot stays open until the ground settles it.
