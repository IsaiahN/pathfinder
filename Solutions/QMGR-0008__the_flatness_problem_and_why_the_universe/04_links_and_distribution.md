## Wider literature that bears on this question

- **Robert Dicke (1969/1970)** and **Dicke and Peebles (1979)**, the original formulation of the flatness coincidence. Reached through the Wikipedia and Helbig source lists; the primary texts were not opened, so they are not counted as read sources in `10_source_ledger.csv`.
- **Alan Guth (1981)**, "Inflationary universe: A possible solution to the horizon and flatness problems", Physical Review D 23, 347. Reached through the Wikipedia reference list; the paper itself was not opened.
- **C. B. Collins and S. W. Hawking (1973)**, "Why is the Universe Isotropic?", ApJ 180, 317, the anthropic ensemble version. Not opened directly.
- **Phillip Helbig (2021)** and **Sabine Hossenfelder (2017)** are the two dissenting voices actually read. They disagree with each other on whether inflation helps (Helbig is no longer an inflation sceptic; Hossenfelder holds that it does not solve flatness), so their agreement on the measure problem is not a shared evidence pool.
- Framework essays by the author of the figures are secondary and high-overlap with the framework. They corroborate the *method* (shape, ground, instrument, prestige) but cannot independently corroborate the cosmology.

## Distribution: offer, do not push

Figure-grounded reasons, all quoted from the local verbatim extract:

- **Fig 4 :22** - *"Only methods cross up. Only head starts come down."* Sending a full theory before anyone has described a residual sends a recording. Lead with the refuter and the shape.
- **Fig 10 :42** - *"A verifier that can reconstruct the claim cannot verify it."* A recipient who has absorbed the framework before giving a verdict has joined the frame being checked.
- **Fig 9 :27-28** - *"describe it before you go looking, or any frame you pick will seem to fit."* Ask what they would expect to see if the shape claim were true before offering the article.
- **Fig 2 :40** - *"agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool."* A send list selected for people likely to agree manufactures the correlation that voids their agreement.

## The two-stage offer

1. **Stage 1.** Send the residual (near-flatness as a reachability failure), the shape claim, and the six pre-registered refuters from `01_figure_grounded_audit.md` §4. Ask what the recipient would count as a failure. Record their verdict **before** showing any framework material.
2. **Stage 2, opt-in only.** If they are curious, offer the figure text, the canonical README and the formula. The figures, not this summary, are the authority. Their later agreement is never pooled as corroboration (Fig 2 :40).
3. **Stage 3.** Offer the formula as a method to reuse, with the instruction that each user writes their own refuter and states their own mode. This is a generator, so giving it away does not give away an answer (Fig 4 :22).

**Hard precondition:** stages 1 to 3 work only if the stage-1 output survives. The refuters in `01` are written so that a failure is cheap to report. Do not soften them to make the claim easier to accept.

## What is deliberately not done here

No personal emails, no purchased list, no bulk send, no receptiveness rank. Unsolicited bulk outreach to scraped addresses breaches GDPR and CAN-SPAM style rules in many jurisdictions, and it is the structural failure described in Fig 2 :35 and Fig 10 :42 as well as a practical one. One-to-one contact through public institutional or opt-in channels only.
