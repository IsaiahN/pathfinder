## Restatement as an effect

What is being decided is not a number but a contact failure. The ground returns a near-flat spatial geometry (the density parameter Omega is within about one percent of 1). The standard Friedmann / general-relativity frame, run backward with generic initial conditions, returns a geometry that was even closer to flat in the early universe, and declares Omega = 1 an unstable fixed point. The two do not disagree about the reading; they disagree about whether the reading is explainable from what the frame holds. Option A: treat near-flatness as an initial condition with no further explanation (the minority "no problem" position). Option B: import a mechanism (inflation) or a selection rule (anthropic conditionalisation on a measure) that makes the reading typical. Each option leads somewhere different: A stops at a datum, B moves the wall and owes an account of the import's own ground.

**What an answer would look like, bound as an ordinary term so it can accrue a residual:** "A candidate answer to why Omega is near 1 is any operator that (i) accepts the ground reading, (ii) makes |Omega minus 1| small at the observed epoch without a second fine-tuning of equal size, and (iii) states what would refute it."
