# Outreach draft: check the output before deciding about the theory

**Use:** one-to-one, or as a cover letter attached to a submission through the public venues in `03_public_contact_points.csv`. **Not** for bulk send.

**Hard precondition:** do not send until the shape claim and its refuter in `01_figure_grounded_audit.md` §4 are checked. The offer collapses on contact if the stage-1 output does not survive.

---

**Subject:** One checkable claim about the flatness problem, before any theory

> Hi [name],
>
> I have one specific output I would like you to check against what you already know. I am not asking you to read a theory first, and I am not asking you to agree with anything. If the output does not survive, there is nothing else to look at.
>
> **The output (`specified`, not `grounded`):** the near-flatness of the universe's spatial geometry is a reachability failure of the standard Friedmann / GR frame, not a search failure. Omega = 1 is an unstable fixed point, so a generic initial condition does not yield the observed value. Inside that closure no amount of computation adds a mechanism. What is owed is either an import from a frame with a different closure (a dynamical mechanism such as inflation) or a selection principle with a measure. Both close part of the gap; neither is a final answer.
>
> The shape any admissible answer must have is: accept the measured value, make |Omega minus 1| small at the observed epoch without paying the same fine-tuning a second time, and state what would refute it. The framework returns that shape, not a name for the mechanism.
>
> **What I am not claiming:** that inflation is settled as the cause, that the flatness problem is unreal, that anthropic reasoning is verified, or that this derivation has settled any of them. Inflation is the dominant ground-supported import and it relocates the fine-tuning to its own initial conditions and potential. Selection is a legitimate operator but needs an enumerated ensemble and a measure that could have returned otherwise. I record that measure as an open instrument, not as a result.
>
> **What would refute the shape claim (written before I ran this):**
> 1. a derivation of near-flatness from FRW plus GR with a generic initial measure and no new atom;
> 2. a demonstration that inflation flattens the universe for all initial values with no new field or potential;
> 3. a grounded measure over the ensemble under which near-flatness is typical and a different value would be distinguishable;
> 4. a seventh primitive, or a derivation of one of the six from the other five;
> 5. a consistent CMB+BAO+SNe analysis showing Omega far from 1.
>
> If any of these holds, the shape is wrong and I will retract it. A one-line "this fails because..." is more useful to me than agreement. If you would rather not engage, no reply is needed.
>
> **If it survives, and only then:** the sources are below. The figures are the authority, not my summary of them.
> - Figure text with line numbers (local framework extract): `FRAMEWORK/reference/03-FIGURES_TEXT.md`
> - Canonical usage rule: https://github.com/IsaiahN/tether/blob/general-agent/figures/README.md
> - The loop and mode rule: `FRAMEWORK/figures/THE_FORMULA.md`
>
> **If you want to use the machinery:** the formula is in the figures link. Run it on a problem you know well and can check against a ground. Write down what would refute your result before you run it, and state the mode of what you get. The framework's own rule is that a loop cannot check the direction of its own question, so an unwritten refuter leaves the preference invisible.
>
> Thanks,
> [name]
