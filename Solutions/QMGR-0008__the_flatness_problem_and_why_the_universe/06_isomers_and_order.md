## DERIVATION

### Q1:What is the order of the three questions (Formula step 7)?

- Fig 6 :49: *"more search at the same reach: is it in here and unfound? The closure does not change. Same atoms, same wall, more of the space actually visited."* This is SEARCHED, and it is asked first because it is cheapest.
- Formula step 7: SEARCHED, then INWARD (extend the instrument from one already returning something), then OUTWARD (import from a frame whose closure differs).
- For flatness: SEARCHED is exhausted, since the instability of Omega = 1 is a theorem of the frame, not an unfound fact. INWARD is already returning a reading (CMB angular scale) and cannot explain why; sharpening it measures the number more precisely but does not move the wall (Fig 6 :28-29: *"inward: is our representation adequate to hold it? The instrument extends access. Fails by refining forever on an axis that cannot carry it."*). OUTWARD is therefore owed: the import is from particle physics (inflation) or from a selection/measure frame.
- Fig 3 :27: *"A link with no instrument is not a link that works. It is a link nobody has looked at."* The measure link is precisely such a link. Fig 3 :18: *"Where a given chain stops is a reading, never a property of the chain."*

**Falsifier (Q1):** if a sharper measurement of Omega (INWARD) could in principle derive why it is near 1 without an import, the order is wrong and SEARCHED/INWARD would govern. No such derivation is in the ledger.

### Q2:Why is Omega a comparison, not a magnitude?

- Operators table :49: *"a threshold relation between two quantities."* Omega = rho / rho_critical; the threshold is 1.
- Fig 12 :74: *"swap them. Does it become a different thing? Yes."* The comparison is ordered: Omega above the threshold and Omega below it lead to different universes.
- Operators table :51: *"a goal is a comparison, which is why a vocabulary without it cannot state one."* The "why" question is a threshold question, which is why a magnitude answer ("rho has this value") misses it.
- Fig 12 :101: *"A bond that costs nothing is a bond that carries nothing, which is the flat case."* This is about a free bond, not spatial flatness. Retraction recorded in `01` §6. It is kept here so the terminological trap stays visible.

### Q3:Is the import a cycle or a sequence?

- Fig 12 :121: *"cycle. The output re-enters as input."* Inflation's flattening is a loop with a sign: accelerated expansion dilutes curvature, curvature affects expansion, and the feedback is registered in the Friedmann equation. A chain cannot close by construction, so the flatness mechanism cannot be written as a bare `cause → effect → flatness` sequence.
- Fig 12 :124: *"functional group. A substructure whose presence fixes the outcome. The group says what kind; the qualifier says which member."* "An attractor that flattens" is the group; "slow-roll single-field inflation" is the qualifier.
- Fig 13 :88-89: *"The residual of the turn just closed is what the next turn composes against, so no two passes begin from the same place."* The residual handed forward after inflation is not the same residual; it is the measure question at a new level.

### Q4:Are inflation and anthropic selection isomers?

- Fig 12 :117: *"isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."*
- As usually written they do **not** share an atom set: inflation uses a field with a potential (imported from particle physics), while anthropic selection uses an ensemble of patches plus observers (imported from statistical reasoning). They are not isomers; they are different functional groups that both reach near-flatness.
- The stronger version worth testing: if both are reduced to the same atoms (a dynamics over an ensemble of initial conditions, differing only in which bond carries the selection: sequence → for the dynamical route, comparison ⋛ for the conditioning route), then they are isomers over that shared set and the difference is decisive in behaviour. Fig 12 :96: *"Two entries can share every ingredient and be inverses of one another."*
- **Falsifier, written before the run:** if either account requires an atom the other provably cannot use (for example, a real ensemble that inflation categorically excludes, or a field that selection cannot supply), the isomer claim fails and the functional-group reading stands. No source in the ledger supplies that proof, so this remains `specified`.

### Q5:What order is the union?

- Fig 12 :104: *"A settled molecule becomes an atom for whatever composes over it. So irreducible is a fact about a level and never about the world."* The instability fact is settled inside FRW and becomes an atom for the second-order question about initial conditions and measure.
- Fig 9 :55-56: *"A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones."* The union is ordered: settle the instability first, then ask the measure question, then ask what "likely" means. Effort spent on the measure before the instability is settled lands on a step that cannot use it (Fig 3 :20-21).

## CONCLUSION

The three questions run in a fixed dependency order: SEARCHED, INWARD, OUTWARD; instability, then measure, then semantics. Omega is an ordered threshold comparison, not a magnitude. The flattening mechanism is a cycle with a sign, not a sequence. Inflation and anthropic selection are different functional groups as usually written; the isomer version is a checkable structural claim over a shared atom set with a stated falsifier. What rescues the defensible residue is exactly what the standard slogans drop: the order, the threshold semantics, and the open measure. No new primitive, no free bond, and no final room are required.
