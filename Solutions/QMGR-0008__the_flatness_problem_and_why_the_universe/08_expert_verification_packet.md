**What this is:** a claim in a form a cosmologist or philosopher of science can check. Mode, derivation and refuters are stated before the conclusion, so a reader knows what to look for and what would settle it.

**Mode: `specified` on the structural claims, `general` on the shape, `grounded` only on the measured value of Omega.** No cosmological cause is asserted as settled. This is a simulated derivation checked for internal consistency against the figures, not a ground reading.

## 1. The claim, in one paragraph

Near-flatness is a reachability failure of the standard Friedmann / GR frame, not a search failure. Omega = 1 is an unstable fixed point, so a generic initial condition does not yield the observed reading, and no amount of computation inside that closure adds a mechanism. Import is the only operator that moves the wall: inflation is the dominant ground-supported import with provenance and a shadow test, and anthropic selection is a legitimate operator that needs an enumerated ensemble and a grounded measure. Neither is a final room. Spatial flatness is a derived leftover, not a substrate primitive. What is offered is the shape any answer must have, refuters written in advance, and the instruments that would close it. The framework does not name the cause.

## 2. Three checkable sub-claims

### C1:framework-internal; checkable by reading Figures 6, 8 and 13

- **Statement:** inside a closed composition frame, no search adds a new atom; import is the only operator that moves the wall. Therefore near-flatness cannot be derived from FRW plus GR plus known matter without a new degree of freedom or a measure.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 6 :21 - *"MINT composes inside the closure and can never add an atom."* Fig 8 :32 - *"no search, however long, adds an atom."* Fig 13 :115 - *"The set is closed. The arrangements are not. That gap is why import exists."* Fig 6 :14 - *"the only operator that moves the wall."*
- **What to check:** is the standard hot big bang frame a closure in this sense? (Einstein equations + FLRW + standard matter/radiation.) Is Omega = 1 unstable under it? Wikipedia ("Flatness problem", 2026) and Helbig (2021) say yes; Helbig's dissenting claim is about whether this constitutes a *problem*, not about the instability.
- **Refuter:** derive near-flatness from that closure with a generic initial measure and no new atom. If that succeeds, C1 is false and no import is owed.

### C2:physics-facing; checkable by a cosmologist against the record

- **Statement:** inflation moves the wall but relocates the residual; it is not a final explanation of flatness. Anthropic selection needs a measure or it is a convention deciding the outcome.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 7 :30 - *"every room closes some of the gap; no finite number of them ever arrives."* Fig 2 :43 - *"a convention that decides an outcome has stopped being one."* Fig 11 :43 - *"The habitat is enumerated, never composed."*
- **What to check:** three sources actually read agree on the relocation but not on everything: Wikipedia ("Flatness problem") records that some physicists argue inflation *"merely moves the fine-tuning from the probability distribution to the potential of a field"*; Hossenfelder (Backreaction, 2017) argues the initial-value assumption survives the exponential factor; Helbig (EPJ H, 2021, note 20) records that the instability returns after inflation if Lambda = 0. Helbig does not accept the claim that there is no flatness problem, so his agreement on relocation is not packaged with agreement on the diagnosis. Is there a source-discriminating observation (tensor modes, non-Gaussianity) that would distinguish the mechanisms?
- **Refuter:** show that inflation flattens for all initial values with no new field or potential, or supply a grounded measure under which near-flatness is typical and a different value would be distinguishable. Either would collapse the open slot.

### C3:framework-internal derivation of the question's form

- **Statement:** Omega is an ordered threshold comparison, and spatial flatness is a derived leftover rather than a seventh primitive. The union must be unbundled before search.
- **Source, verbatim:** Operators table :49 - *"a threshold relation between two quantities."* Fig 13 :43 - *"A continuum was the seventh and was removed for failing exactly this."* Fig 13 :124-125 - *"Smoothness, symmetry, chaos and discreteness are each what a particular composition leaves behind, and none is a seventh member."* Fig 9 :54 - *"And when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again."*
- **Refuter:** derive spatial flatness as a seventh primitive, or show the three conditions share one measure and dynamics such that resolving one resolves all (which would collapse the union to a partition). Note the terminology trap: Fig 12 :101's *"flat case"* means a costless bond, not a flat universe. That misreading was retracted in `01` §6.

## 3. What is explicitly not claimed

- Not that the flatness problem is unreal, and not that it is settled.
- Not that inflation is the unique or final cause; it is the dominant ground-supported import that relocates the residual.
- Not that anthropic reasoning is verified; the measure is an open instrument.
- Not that the six-term substrate explains the early universe; its indivisibility is a refutable claim (Fig 13 :41).
- Not `grounded` on any cause. Only the measured near-flatness is grounded, and it is a datum, not an explanation.
- Not a derivation of one theory from another, and no new primitive.

## 5. What would settle it

A source-discriminating instrument, not a further argument. From *The Hard Problem is About Source*: an instrument that reads green *"cannot comment on the source"*; the spectrometer resolved it. Here:

1. A primordial tensor-mode or non-Gaussianity measurement that distinguishes single-field slow-roll inflation from a bounce, varying-c, Einstein-Cartan torsion, or no inflation.

3. A consistent CMB+BAO+SNe analysis showing Omega far from 1.
4. A derivation of near-flatness inside the closure, or a seventh primitive, as in the refuters above.
