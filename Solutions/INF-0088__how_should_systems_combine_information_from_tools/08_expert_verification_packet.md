# Expert verification packet: the Tether on combining conflicting tool, user and parameter information

**What this is:** a claim in a form a fusion researcher, cognitive scientist or ML engineer can check. Mode, derivation and refuters are stated before the conclusion, so a reader knows what to look for and what would settle it.

**Mode: `specified` on the rule, `general` on the structural shape, not `grounded`.** No numeric weight is claimed and no measurement is asserted. This is a shape and a route, not a result.

**Status: simulated derivation checked for internal consistency against the figures, not a ground reading.** It cannot settle the question it reports on.

## 1. The claim, in one paragraph

When tools, users and learned parameters disagree on an object slot, a single weighted average discards the only signal that says where the conflict is. The figure-grounded rule is: keep the residual per slot; infer whether the sources bear on a common state, on different states, or on the same evidence pool under two names before fusing anything; range each source separately against a ground that does not move when the sources disagree; treat user judgment as the anchor only when the question is constitutively about people, otherwise as an instrument; treat parameters as a prior inside the frame, never as ground; fuse only redundant, genuinely independent readings, weighting by measured reliability and discounting shared evidence; keep a negation operator so a false source can be refused; and stamp every admitted reading with its origin and sequence. The figures constrain this shape but do not supply numeric weights, which are recorded as an open gap.

## 2. Three checkable sub-claims

### C1 - framework-internal; checkable by reading Figures 1, 2 and 9

- **Statement:** aggregation across sources or slots destroys the error signal, and the common-cause question must be settled before fusion.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 1 :19 "The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated." Fig 1 :22 "Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average." Fig 9 :54 "And when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again." Fig 2 :6-7 "collapse 2: common cause (hidden shared source)".
- **Refuter:** a controlled task with a settled ground in which a single symmetric blend across all sources, with no causal-structure check, matches or beats the per-slot rule on both accuracy and identification of the failing source.

### C2 - framework-internal, question-dependent; checkable by reading Figure 2 and the Symbols table

- **Statement:** the anchor role is delegated to the question. Human judgment is the anchor for people-about questions and an instrument otherwise; learned parameters are never an anchor.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 2 :37 "Human judgment is the right anchor for is this a good answer for a person, because the question is about people." Fig 2 :37-38 "It is only an instrument for is this code correct, and instruments can be wrong on axes they do not measure." Fig 2 :29 "An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks." Symbols table :23 "How far the belief leans on private history versus inherited prior. w_A + w_B = 1, and w is learnable from the outcomes of past weightings."
- **Refuter:** user feedback on a non-people question with a checkable ground improves the ground score as much as a direct ground test, with users having no access to that test; or a parameter-only system with no new contact improves its ground score by reweighting alone.

### C3 - framework-internal derivation; checkable by reading Figures 5, 8, 10 and 12

- **Statement:** a combining rule needs negation, provenance and channel maintenance. A rule of weights alone can reweight a false source but cannot say it is false; convergent derivation and adopted import are indistinguishable in the fused contents; the ground does not decay but the channel does.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 12 :112 "A refutation is a negation, and that one is not a bond." Fig 8 :51 "Convergent derivation and adopted import look identical in the contents. Only the record of where each came from separates them". Fig 10 :24 "the ground does not decay, the channel does".
- **Refuter:** a rule with no negation operator recovers as fast as one with refusal when a source becomes systematically false; or discarding all provenance causes no loss on an audit asking which source was wrong.

## 3. What is explicitly not claimed

- Not a numerical weighting formula. The numeric level is an open gap; any coefficient is an import owing a source gate and a shadow test (Fig 6 import rule).
- Not that averaging never works. Averaging is efficient for a convention whose weights an outcome prices (Fig 2 :41), which is not the same as treating agreement as a verdict.
- Not a fixed weight for users across questions, and not parameter confidence as evidence about the world.
- Not a theory of everything, and not a claim that this framework is the only true one. A frame cannot certify its own limit; no census of all frameworks has been run.
- Not grounded. Nothing here is asserted as settled by experiment.
- Not a claim that the three sources are isomers. They are members of one functional group with different atoms (Fig 12 :123), and the stronger form is left open with a stated falsifier in `06_isomers_and_order.md`.

## 5. What would settle it

A source-discriminating experiment, not a further argument. *The Hard Problem is About Source*: an instrument that reads green "cannot comment on the source"; the spectrometer resolved it. Here:

1. **Common-cause test:** does inferring shared source before fusion change the ground score versus unconditional fusion, on a task with independent sources?
2. **Correlation test:** do two sources with identical evidence add accuracy when agreement is counted twice? Effective-sample-size theory predicts no.
3. **Anchor test:** on a non-people question with a checkable ground, does user feedback match a direct ground test?
4. **Refusal test:** when a source flips to systematically false, does a rule with negation recover faster than a rule with weights only?

A derivation of the numeric weights from the figures alone would also settle the gap, in the direction of a stronger claim.
