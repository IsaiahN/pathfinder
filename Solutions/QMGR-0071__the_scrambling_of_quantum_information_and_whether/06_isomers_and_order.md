# What kind of thing is a speed limit? Bonds, order and analogues

**Mode: `specified`.** Simulated derivation checked against the figures, not a ground reading
(see `05_what_shape_means.md` §6).

## RESIDUAL

The question calls a bound a "speed limit" and calls it "universal". Two supporting questions are
owed: (1) what bond does a bound express under the operators table, and are the exponent bound and
the time bound the same substance or different compounds? (2) At what order does the universality
claim sit, and what would a first-order versus second-order derivation look like?

## DERIVATION

### Q1: What bond is a bound?

- Operators table: ⋛ is *"a threshold relation between two quantities"*. A speed limit is exactly a
  threshold: growth no faster than X, time no shorter than Y.
- Fig 12 :117: *"isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is
  invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."*
- The exponent bound and the time bound use the same atoms (time, energy, entropy as ordering) but
  different bonds and different directions: one is "growth no faster than", the other "time no
  shorter than". Under Fig 12 they are **not one substance written two ways**. They are different
  compounds whose shared ingredient set hides the difference in the flat phrase "speed limit".
- **Falsifier, written before the run:** if the exponent bound and the time bound can be derived
  from each other by a reversible transform with no extra assumption, the isomer reading fails and
  they are one limit with two spellings.

### Q2: Are they at the same order?

- A **first-order** molecule is one crossing: state -> structure -> state, one turn, residual
  re-entering. Scrambling inside one system is first order: an operator spreads across a chain,
  the remainder compounds, the OTOC grows then saturates.
- Fig 12 :104: *"A settled molecule becomes an atom for whatever composes over it. So irreducible
  is a fact about a level and never about the world."*
- Each first-order bound is settled in its own frame (the MSS argument; the 2026 PRL proof plus
  experiment). Each can therefore become an atom at the next level. Comparing the two bounds, and
  claiming the comparison holds everywhere, is **second order**.
- The universality quantifier is therefore not a stronger version of the first-order claim. It is a
  different-order claim (Fig 9 :54: when the split lands on a union rather than a partition,
  unbundle before you search again). A case in two conditions is not a harder case of one.

### Q3: The functional-group reading

- Fig 12 :124: *"functional group. A substructure whose presence fixes the outcome. The group says
  what kind; the qualifier says which member."*
- All the bounds found here share a functional group: "a threshold on a rate expressed in the
  currency of the frame". The qualifiers differ: 2πT/ħ (thermal field theory), entropy and
  temperature (2026 PRL), Lieb-Robinson velocity (lattice locality). The group says what kind of
  thing they are; the qualifier says which member.
- The stronger isomer reading (same full atom set, different bond) remains available but needs the
  operand-type strain in §4 resolved first. Mark `specified`, unmeasured.

### Q4: Order by dependency (Fig 3)

1. Perception: can the OTOCs and bounds be distinguished at all? Yes, in the literature (external
   frame), not by this simulation.
2. Vocabulary: do we have terms for what the bound affords? Yes, threshold, filter, currency,
   habitat, remainder.
3. Objective: what would count as progress? A bound stated in effect terms with its conditions
   named, and a refuter that can fire.
4. Planning: split the union (Fig 9 :4-5), settle first order, then compare at second order.
5. Learn and carry: the method (derive the shape, bind the refuter early) is carried up; the
   particular physics numbers are recordings and stay in their own frame (Fig 4 :23-25).

A reading taken below a broken link would be a reading of nothing (Fig 3 :22-23). Link 3 is the one
most at risk here: it is tempting to treat "there is a bound" as the objective when the objective
is "a bound with a named domain of validity".

### Q5: Cross-field analogues (secondary, low overlap)

| analogue | field | what it predicts here | overlap |
|---|---|---|---|
| Shannon-Hartley: *"the maximum rate at which information can be transmitted over a communications channel"* | engineering / information theory | a universal-form rate bound whose value depends on the channel (bandwidth, noise); form universality, not one number | low with physics |
| Hick's law: *"increasing the number of choices will increase the decision time logarithmically"* | human cognition | logarithmic time scaling in system size, with documented exceptions and a class/window condition | low |
| Bass diffusion: *"decision variables can shift the Bass curve in time, but that the shape of the curve is always similar"* | business / innovation | parameters shift, shape invariant; the right level of "universal" is the shape | low |

These are analogues, not witnesses for the verdict (Fig 9 :60: filters for the budget and witnesses
for the verdict). They corroborate the *form* of the answer. They do not decide the physics. The
saccade exception to Hick's law is a useful reminder that a "law" with exceptions is still a law
within a class and window, which is what the Scholarpedia review says about exponential OTOC growth.

## CONCLUSION

A speed limit is a threshold comparison (⋛), not a join. The exponent bound and the time bound are
different compounds sharing a functional group, not one limit with two spellings. Scrambling in one
system is first order; the comparison of bounds and the universality claim are second order and must
be settled after the first-order claims. Universality is defensible at the level of form (Fig 4 :20)
and not at the level of one constant (Fig 10 :68). Three low-overlap analogues corroborate the form
without deciding the physics.
