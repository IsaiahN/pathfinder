# Order, cascade closure and metamerism in the local-to-global bridge

**Mode: `specified` on the structural claims, `general` on the analogue. Not a ground reading.** This is a simulated derivation checked against the figures (see `05_what_shape_means.md` section 6).

## RESIDUAL

The bridge is not one step. A local interaction change is followed by a first-order response, a second-order effect on a different species, and often a third-order residual that is the thing that actually matters at the wider scale. Three questions are owed:

1. What is the order of the first step, sequence or production?
2. Can two different local arrangements produce the same global reading?
3. What is carried across the scale boundary, and what must be re-earned?

## DERIVATION

### Q1 - The first step is production, not bare sequence

Operators table :29-31 (sequence): "A then B; B needs A to have happened".
Operators table :35-37 (production): "A produces B; B did not exist before". Fig 12 :121: "cycle. The output re-enters as input. The sign of the loop decides whether it closes, and a chain cannot close by construction."

A predator removal followed by an herbivore increase is sequence: B needs A to have happened. But the subsequent state, an urchin irruption or an elk-grassland regime, is a condition that did not exist before at that site; it is production. The distinction matters for the global claim. If the cascade were only sequence, a global pattern would be a rearrangement of pre-existing local states. Because it is production, the pattern includes a new condition that has to be named at its own level. Fig 12 :103: "Composition is recursive, and the level decides what is irreducible."

The cascade can also close. The trophic-cascade source opened 2026-11-23 records that after wolf removal the northern Yellowstone range shifted to an elk-grassland state and did not return after reintroduction, a hysteresis attributed to creek incision and a lowered water table. That is a cycle with a sign: the output re-enters as input, and a chain alone cannot represent it. Fig 12 :121 governs it.

### Q2 - Metamerism as isomerism at the pattern level

Fig 12 :117: "isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."

Take the same species set and conditions, and change which interaction is disturbed. Two arrangements over the same atoms can yield different local dynamics yet converge on the same coarse pattern, for example a similar decline in site-level diversity. In formula terms the coarse reading records the atoms and not the bond, which is the flat-form collapse Fig 12 names: the ingredients are recorded and the arrangement is not.

The stronger analogue: over the full shared atom set of a biome, the same global intactness reading can be produced by different local bond arrangements. Under Fig 12 :117 those are isomers with respect to the underlying system, even though they are indistinguishable at the reading. This is the isomer claim worth testing. It does not say the global pattern is wrong; it says the pattern underdetermines the local source.

- **Falsifier, written before the run:** if a global pattern is found that decomposes uniquely into one local interaction arrangement across several biomes, so that the reading determines the bond, the isomer/metamerism claim fails and the pattern is invertible after all.

### Q3 - Recursion, and why each lineage pays again across the boundary

Fig 12 :104: "A settled molecule becomes an atom for whatever composes over it. So irreducible is a fact about a level and never about the world, and there is no bottom"

Once a local result is settled against the ground, it can serve as an atom at the regional level, and a settled regional result can serve as an atom at the global level. The order is local, then metacommunity or biome, then global. What crosses up is the method that produced the settled result; what comes down is a head start, never a replay (Fig 4 :22).

Fig 12 :107-110 states the cost asymmetry: "Where it is not, the result carries forward and the deriving does not, so every lineage pays again." In ecology a global pattern map carries forward, but the local derivation does not. Each new region, taxon or period pays the derivation again. Nyström et al. 2026 are the empirical instance: structured models interpolate inside sampled contexts and lose performance when extrapolating to other contexts. That is the boundary dropping the record, not a failure of the pattern itself.

## CONCLUSION

The first step from a local interaction change is production rather than bare sequence, so the downstream condition is genuinely new at its level. The cascade can close into a cycle with a sign, which is why a single-pass chain misrepresents hysteresis. Under the Fig 12 analogue, different local bond arrangements over the same atoms can be isomers that the coarse global reading cannot separate, which is the structural reason a pattern cannot name its source. Recursion lets a settled local result become an atom at the next level, but the deriving does not travel with the result; each new context pays again, as the measured extrapolation failure shows.

This framing adds order and structure to the bridge without importing a seventh primitive or naming a universal law. "metric equals method" would be the wrong carrying; what crosses is the generator.
