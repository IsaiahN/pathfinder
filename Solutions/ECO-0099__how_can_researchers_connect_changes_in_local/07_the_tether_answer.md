# The Tether's answer: connecting local interactions to global biodiversity patterns

**Mode: `general` on the structural shape, `specified` on the three checkable sub-claims. Not `grounded`.** Simulated derivation checked against the figures (`05_what_shape_means.md` section 6), not a ground reading. No measurement was taken by this derivation; one empirical instance of a measured gap is cited from the literature.

## 1. The residual it answers

Researchers can measure a change in a local species interaction and can measure a global pattern of biodiversity loss or ecosystem function. What is missing is the transform between the two scales. The figures diagnose this as a union compound, not one hard problem: local mechanism, global pattern and ecosystem function are overlapping conditions with different grounds. Fig 9 :54: "And when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again." Each condition gets its own loop; a single lever clears only the region where one condition holds.

## 2. The shape

1. **The global frame is not a larger local frame.** Fig 10 :68: "Different actors, a different currency, a different ground, and a lossy transform between. That is why there is no top rung and no regress: a generator crosses, an instance does not, and the level it lands on remakes it." The crossing is a membrane with a direction, not a magnification.

2. **Carry a method up, never a site transcript.** Fig 4 :22: "Only methods cross up. Only head starts come down." The thing that travels is a reapplicable rule for how a local interaction change propagates under changed context. A global index built from averaged study effects is a recording carried upward; it looks like knowledge and describes one occasion. The measured extrapolation failure in Nyström et al. 2026 is what that looks like at the ground.

3. **Enumerate the contact habitat and follow the cascade outward.** Fig 11 :44: "List everything in contact with the residual, then what is in contact with those, and outward until the cascade stops mattering." Fig 11 :52: "And contact cascades. Scoping the habitat to direct contact is how DDT reached the eagles: first-order enumeration, second-order effect, and the residual that mattered was three steps out." A focal pair plus its immediate neighbours is usually too small a habitat.

4. **Report the residual per slot.** Fig 1 :19: "The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated." The live failure is at a taxon, an interaction and a site. A global mean may describe the pattern, but it cannot locate the thing to fix.

5. **Measure the round-trip gap.** Fig 4 :30: "So take the round trip and measure the gap. Without it you are navigating by dead reckoning: confident about a position nobody checked." Carry the method up, bring it down at a held-out site, and report what the coarse description dropped. R_T is the measurement, not an error to be tuned away.

6. **The direction is asymmetric.** A local change produces a pattern; the pattern does not name the change. Fig 9 :32: "you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself." At the coarse reading, different local bond arrangements can be indistinguishable, so global maps underdetermine local mechanism.

7. **Triangulate; do not consolidate.** Fig 2 :35: "Alignment is triangulation, not negotiation. A mirror cannot correct you." Range a field-manipulation frame and an independent global-compilation frame separately against a ground neither can move. Select frames for low evidence overlap, never for likely agreement.

8. **A specific law is not supplied.** Fig 13 :134: "Unreachable is a verdict here, not a silence." The figures give the shape of the bridge and refuse to name the bridge. Any exponent, index or model is a binding from the world frame, provenanced as such and checkable at the ground.

## 3. What it explicitly does not say

- Not that any biodiversity indicator, species-area exponent or metabolic scaling law *is* the bridge. Those are world candidates, several of them contested.
- Not that global patterns are wrong or merely averaged local interactions. They are a different frame with a different currency and a different ground.
- Not that a local mechanism can be read off a global map. That is the metamerism error the shape refuses.
- Not that model fit is enough. Fit is a filter for the budget; held-out prediction is a witness for the verdict.
- Not `grounded`. Nothing here is asserted as settled by this derivation.

## 4. What would settle it - the ground

The three pre-registered refuters in `01_figure_grounded_audit.md` section 4 are the operative tests:

1. **Invertibility test:** find a global pattern that determines a unique local mechanism at a site. That would break the asymmetric projection claim.
2. **Round-trip test:** carry a local interaction method to global scale and back, and show R_T indistinguishable from zero across several biomes with the same predictions at both scales. That would break the lossy-transform claim.
3. **Cascade-depth test:** show in a comprehensive set of cases that effects stop at first-order contacts with no measurable second or third-order cascade. That would break the habitat-enumeration claim.

Additional positive instruments: held-out-site prediction across contexts (Nyström et al. 2026 already show this discriminating interpolation from extrapolation), source-discriminating measurement that separates candidate local mechanisms behind one coarse reading, and independent replication with data not drawn from the same repositories.

## 5. The honest stop

**Unreached, not unreachable.** The shape says what any acceptable bridge must look like and what would break it. It cannot name the bridge, because naming would require a ground reading the figures do not supply. The canonical README's rule applies: "Indiscernibility is not identity." The record stops at the limit of the available instruments and records the lineage so the stop is checkable. Agreement among reviewers is not such an instrument.

## 6. One-paragraph version for outreach

> The Tether does not supply a universal law connecting local species interactions to global biodiversity loss. It says that the two are separated by a directional membrane: global patterns have different actors, currency and ground from a local interaction, so what crosses is a reapplicable method, not a record of one site. Researchers should enumerate the contact habitat and follow cascades two or more steps out, keep the residual per slot instead of hiding it in a mean, carry the method up and bring it down at held-out sites while measuring the round-trip gap, and range field and global frames separately against a ground neither can move. The global reading cannot name its local source, so a map does not determine a mechanism. What is offered is the shape any candidate bridge must have, three falsifiers written in advance, and the instruments that would close it. The bridge stays open until the ground settles it.
