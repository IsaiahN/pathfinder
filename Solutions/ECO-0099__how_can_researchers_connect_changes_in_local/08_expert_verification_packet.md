# Expert verification packet: the Tether on local interactions and global biodiversity patterns

**What this is:** a claim in a form an ecologist or scaling researcher can check. Mode, derivation and refuters are stated before the conclusion, so a reader knows what to look for and what would settle it.

**Mode: `general` on the shape, `specified` on the three sub-claims. Not `grounded`.** No measurement is claimed by this derivation. This is a shape and a route, not a result.

## 1. The claim, in one paragraph

A local species-interaction change and a global biodiversity or ecosystem-function pattern are separated by a directional membrane, not a difference of magnification. The global frame has different actors, currency and ground from the local frame, so what crosses is a reapplicable method, never a site transcript. Researchers should enumerate the habitat around the changed interaction and follow contact cascades at least two steps out, keep the residual per slot rather than in a global mean, carry the method to the wider scale and back while measuring the round-trip gap at held-out sites, and range field and global frames separately against an anchor neither can move. The coarse pattern cannot name its local source, so the figures return a shape, not a law.

## 2. Three checkable sub-claims

### C1 - framework-internal; checkable by reading Figures 10 and 4

- **Statement:** the global frame is not a larger local frame; only a method crosses up.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 10 :68 - "Different actors, a different currency, a different ground, and a lossy transform between. That is why there is no top rung and no regress: a generator crosses, an instance does not, and the level it lands on remakes it." Also Fig 4 :22 - "Only methods cross up. Only head starts come down."
- **What to check:** is the distinction between actors, currency and ground actually drawn in the cited lines, and does the membrane have a direction rather than being a wall?
- **Refuter:** exhibit a local interaction measurement that is itself the global pattern at another size, with the same actors, currency and ground and no transform between them.

### C2 - ecology-facing; checkable by an ecologist against their own systems

- **Statement:** the local side of the bridge is an enumerated contact habitat with multi-step cascades, and the residual is per slot.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 11 :44 - "List everything in contact with the residual, then what is in contact with those, and outward until the cascade stops mattering." Fig 11 :52 - "And contact cascades. Scoping the habitat to direct contact is how DDT reached the eagles: first-order enumeration, second-order effect, and the residual that mattered was three steps out." Fig 1 :19 - "The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated."
- **What to check:** in systems you know, does the effect of an interaction change typically reach a second or third-order species, and does a global or biome-level mean hide which taxon or interaction is actually failing? The independent literature on trophic cascades and on global-model extrapolation is in `04_links_and_distribution.md`.
- **Refuter:** a comprehensive set of cases in which effects stop at first-order contacts and per-slot reporting carries no information the mean lacks.

### C3 - framework-internal derivation, with an empirical instance

- **Statement:** the transform is lossy and asymmetric; a global reading underdetermines the local source, and the gap must be measured by a round trip.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 4 :30 - "So take the round trip and measure the gap. Without it you are navigating by dead reckoning: confident about a position nobody checked." Fig 9 :32 - "you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself."
- **What to check:** Fig 4 :27 says "The transform between scales loses information, and that is the point." The independent paper by Nyström et al. 2026 reports "a challenging gap between effect size inference and local prediction" and a drop from Spearman 0.42 in sampled contexts to 0.13 under cross-study validation. Is that a fair instance of a measured R_T, or is it a different phenomenon?
- **Refuter:** a local method carried to global scale and back with R_T indistinguishable from zero across several biomes, with the same predictions recovered at both scales.

## 3. What is explicitly not claimed

- Not a universal scaling law, exponent, or index. No figure line names one; that part is a `gap`, recorded in `01_figure_grounded_audit.md` F9.
- Not that global indicators are wrong. They are a different frame with a different ground.
- Not that a global map determines a local mechanism. That is the metamerism error the claim refuses.
- Not that model fit is sufficient. Fit is a budget filter, held-out prediction is a witness.
- Not `grounded`. This derivation supplies no new measurement and cannot settle the question it reports on.
- Not a claim about Fig 13's six physical terms mapping onto ecological actors. That mapping was refused as a strain and recorded.

## 5. What would settle it

A ground reading, not agreement. The three refuters in section 2 are the operative tests. A source-discriminating instrument that separates candidate local mechanisms behind one coarse reading would name what the map cannot. Held-out-site prediction across contexts would measure R_T. Independent replication on data drawn from different repositories would test whether the two frames really are independent.

## 6. What an expert can check in twenty minutes

1. Read Fig 10 :68, Fig 4 :22, Fig 11 :44 and :52, Fig 1 :19, Fig 4 :30 and Fig 9 :32. Eight short passages.
2. Check C2 against a system you know: is the cascade depth and the per-slot point faithful, or is first-order scoping usually enough?
3. Decide whether Nyström et al. 2026 is a fair instance of a measured round-trip gap or a different phenomenon.
4. Name one instrument that would separate two local mechanisms producing the same global reading, or say why none is possible.
5. Send the refutation. A failure is more useful than an endorsement.
