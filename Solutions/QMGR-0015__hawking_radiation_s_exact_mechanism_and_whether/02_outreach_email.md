**Subject:** One check on a level-indexed claim about Hawking radiation and collapse

> Hi [name],
>
> I am working on a figure-grounded analysis of a specific question and would like one check on a single output. I am not asking you to read a theory first, and I am not asking you to agree with anything. If the output does not survive, there is nothing else to look at.
>
> **The output (`specified` on the shape, `gap` on the exact mechanism):**
>
> 1. The question "does Hawking radiation's spectrum depend on the details of collapse?" is two conditions bundled together, not one. Ask it at the coarse-grained thermal level and the answer is no by construction: a blackbody spectrum is a single parameter, temperature, and aggregation across modes is exactly the operation that discards per-mode detail. Ask it at the fine-grained level and the answer is unreached, because no instrument returns a gravitational fine-grained spectrum.
> 2. The emission mechanism has a legal composition shape: a structure at the horizon must mediate between energy and a thermal ordering. The framework returns that shape, not the name of the structure. Identifying surface gravity with a particular structure is a binding from the physics frame, not a derivation.
> 3. The analogue Bose-Einstein-condensate result is a real ground for analogue kinematics, but it is a substituted habitat. The transform back to gravity loses information, and that loss has to be enumerated, not assumed away.
>
> **What I am not claiming:** that the exact gravitational mechanism has been derived, that collapse information is provably absent, that no-hair settles the information question, or that an analogue experiment observes Hawking radiation from a gravitational black hole. Each of those is either `gap` or a provenanced binding, and I will say which when asked.
>
> **What would refute the shape claim, written down before this message:**
> 1. A gravitational measurement in which the coarse-grained spectrum at fixed mass, charge and spin varies with the collapse history.
> 2. A derivation in which energy composes directly to a thermal spectrum with no intervening structure.
> 3. A fine-grained measurement returning exactly thermal correlations with no collapse information while unitarity is independently confirmed.
> 4. An analogue experiment in which changing the collapse-like initial conditions changes the thermal form itself.
>
> A one-line "this fails because ..." is more useful to me than agreement. If you would rather not engage, no reply is needed.
