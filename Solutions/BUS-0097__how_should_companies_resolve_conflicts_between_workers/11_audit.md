## Round trip gap R_T

The Symbols table defines R_T as `gap(x, (T_E ∘ T_A)(x))`, the gap between what was sent up and what came back down. Fig 4 :30 requires it to be measured rather than assumed zero. I ran the round trip on the condensation this folder actually uses.

1. **Sent up (x):** the verbatim figure set, chiefly Fig 1 :19, :22 (per-slot residual), Fig 2 :29, :35 (anchor and triangulation), Fig 9 :54-57 (union), Fig 10 :42, :85-86 (seat), Fig 6 :34-35 and Fig 13 :134 (unreachable).
2. **Abstraction (T_A):** the shorthand in `07` and `08`: keep residuals per party, range against a non-updating anchor, install an outside refusal seat, unbundle the union, report the unborn party as `unreached`.
3. **Concretisation (T_E):** a possible world-level binding, "adopt a stakeholder scorecard with an independent ombudsman and a future-generations seat".

**R_T is nonzero.** Three things the shorthand holds do not survive the trip back down:

- the **non-updating anchor** can be replaced by an ESG score or a stakeholder survey that moves when the parties push, turning triangulation back into panel 1 of Fig 2;
- the **refusal operation** can be lost when the ombudsman is given an advisory role only, which is a vocabulary that accumulates claims and cannot negate one (Operators :58);
- the **union structure** can be flattened into one scorecard, which aggregates the slots the shorthand was designed to keep separate (Fig 1 :22, Fig 9 :57).

Conversely, one thing survives cleanly: a party's residual must be indexed separately and ranged against something that does not update on the party's own report. That is the residue worth sending. R_T was **not** measured numerically, because no company ground reading exists; it is recorded qualitatively as the enumerated set of dropped constraints. Treating the formula as identical to the figure set would set R_T to zero and would be the dead-reckoning error Fig 4 :30 names. The honest form is a lossy condensation with a checked gap.
