## 1. A shape is a constraint, not a filler

The figures README states the limit: the framework returns *"what a missing thing would have to look like (**a shape, not a name**)."* Fig 9 :32: *"you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself."* Fig 13 :134: *"Unreachable is a verdict here, not a silence"*; the same passage says it *"cannot say which primitive is missing, because naming it would mean already holding it."*

From *The Library of Babel*: *"You can cut your search short if the set of books returned is small and descriptive enough that it provides a general shape of the answer that satisfies your question."*

So a **shape** is the narrowed form of an answer you can state before you can name it. It constrains; it does not fill. For BUS-0097, the shape is: five residuals kept separate, each ranged against a non-updating anchor, a refusal seat outside the slice graded, the union unbundled, and the unregistering party marked `unreached` with an instrument owed. A priority list of the five parties is a name, not a shape.

## 2. How a shape is obtained

- *Library of Babel*: use the vocabulary you already have that is *closest* to the thing, look for recurring new terms that overlap or correlate with it, recurse. *"With each new term gained, you narrow the space and deepen your vocabulary."*
- Fig 6 :30: *"The description travels. Fails by being sharpened into terms only we can read. Cast it in effect terms."* Fig 9 :30-31: *"your words are borrowed too, so describe the effect rather than the cause."*
- *Decoding Tacit Knowledge*: *"When contact isn't possible, the best you can send is a plain description of what you'd expect to see, because anyone can check that against the world."*
- *Library of Babel* on stopping: the answer is reached when *"the finish line will also be the starting place"* - and the paradox is that by then you have *"composed the answer through the process of locating it."*

For this question the effect description comes first: what failed to resolve? A wage dispute, a price dispute, a siting dispute, and a long-horizon harm all look like "conflicts between stakeholders", but they are different compounds (Fig 9 :57). Describing the effect before naming a frame is what keeps the governance answer from being any frame you happen to prefer.

## 3. What licenses a name

Only two things, both outside the shape itself:

1. **The ground settles it.** Formula step 5: *"the ground settles it. Verified only by what pays."* The README: the framework *"stops at the limit of what the available instruments can distinguish, and records the lineage so the stop is honest."*
2. **A provenanced import from a frame whose closure differs** (Fig 6 :23, Fig 8 :50-52), with a source gate and a shadow test.

*They're Scanning Books* sharpens this: *"The real import is not a new term in the existing space. It is a new dimension of the space."* And: *"A minimum viable set of six terms is a human's careful guess at reality's compression axes; another sensorium's minimum set need not be those six."* Also: *"Declaring the primitives does not make them neutral. It makes the choice visible."*

Five named parties are a visible guess about what counts. Treating the list as nature's own partition is what makes the choice invisible. The figures return a shape; the list of parties is an input from the business frame and must be recorded as such.

## 4. The source rule: a reading does not determine its source

*The Hard Problem is About Source* gives the mechanism. Metamerism: an instrument reads green and *"cannot comment on the source"* - one green light, or yellow plus blue, or RGB, or CMYK, or a scheme not yet discovered. *"If several paths create the same behavior, then behavior is a projection with lossy information."* And: *"An instrument that can't distinguish these cases demonstrates its own inadequacy."* It was the spectrometer, not an argument, that resolved it.

Applied here: an ESG score reads "good governance" for a firm whose worker residual is growing because the measurement pooled it with customer satisfaction (Fig 10 :26, *"saturated metrics, pooled readings"*). The reading does not identify the source of the problem. What is owed is a discriminator, not a new label for the score.

## 5. Q: does "shape, not a name" forbid naming a governance binding such as an ombudsman?

**No.** Answered with the correction kept visible.

- The figures README's phrase is *"what a **missing** thing would have to look like (a shape, not a name)."* The subject is a primitive not held in Γ.
- For the four present parties, seats, contracts, measurements and courts are already available in the governance library. Naming one is a **binding** from the business frame onto something held. It is not the invention of a new primitive, and it does not move the wall (Fig 6 :21 - *"MINT composes inside the closure and can never add an atom."*).
- For future generations, the missing thing is a **channel that returns a reading**, not the title of an office. Naming an ombudsman before describing what reading it would carry is the inversion Fig 9 :27-28 forbids: *"describe it before you go looking, or any frame you pick will seem to fit."*
- **Correction to my own first pass:** I initially wrote that "the framework returns a shape, not a name" forbids naming any governance mechanism. That overstates the rule, exactly as the exemplar's §5b and the completed BUS solutions record. The rule governs missing primitives. Naming a held binding is provenance, not a forbidden invention. The earlier reading is preserved here rather than erased.

## 6. Prestige is not action

From *The Recursive Self-Improvement Trap*, opened 2026-11-22: *"Prestige must never be allowed to validate an action directly... Prestige can fund an action. It can give an action a budget to be tried. But it cannot settle an action. Only the ground can settle it. The Ground Maintainer must be blind to prestige."*

A certification, a respected board, a high-profile CEO signatory list, and a well-cited framework all give a governance claim a budget to be tried. None settles whether a party's residual closed. A stakeholder forum assembled from famous names is the named collapse, not corroboration. The legitimate ask is the opposite: give the claim a budget to be tried, let a non-updating ground settle it, and record the divergence.

## 7. A room does not upgrade the label

Fig 7 :35: *"A room's agreement is variation, not a verdict. Selection between rooms is where the ground votes."* Fig 2 :35: *"Alignment is triangulation, not negotiation. A mirror cannot correct you."* A room filled with the five parties who agree that the process was fair has produced a convention about a shared meaning (Fig 2 :41), which is a legitimate product and not a verdict about what happened to any party.

## 8. My own audit is a simulation, not a ground reading

Same essay: *"LLMs can quickly simulate an approximate answer/output with the formula found in the architecture post... However, the truly testable output would need to be derived from the real architecture or working the formula yourself."* *"The catch is that the output is only as good as the validator. The agent, or the tool, or the frame, or the theory, cannot validate itself."*

So `01_figure_grounded_audit.md` is a simulated derivation, checked for internal consistency against the figures. It is **not** a test of the governance claim and must be labelled as such. It cannot settle anything it reports on. Its five refuters are the instrument by which a real check could begin.
