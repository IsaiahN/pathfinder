## QUESTION

How should companies resolve conflicts between workers, customers, owners, communities, and future generations?

Restated as an effect: a company action changes outcomes for five claimant slots; R = |Γ(b,a) - o'| is measured per slot and never averaged (Fig 1 :19, :22). Option A, one party wins by right, produces a standing rank that does not track a change of harm. Option B, balance them into one score, produces an aggregate that hides the live signal. Option C, the figure-derived reading, keeps each residual separate, ranges it against an anchor that does not update, and installs a refusal seat outside the parties. What each leads to: A and B produce a convention or an average and cannot locate which party is being failed; C produces a checkable shape with five falsifiers and an honest `unreached` verdict for the party whose residual does not register.

## DOMAIN CENSUS

R by arity, symmetry and scale, then one line per domain with actors / currency / anchor, partition or union, and order by dependency.

- **R (the residual).** Arity: a four-place relation among company action, claimant slot, party outcome and time; five slots, not five values of one variable. Symmetry: asymmetric in kind and in time (a harm to a community is not undone by a dividend to an owner); "fair" and "adequate" are comparison bonds, not quantities (Fig 12 :85-87). Scale: single decision, firm governance room, claimant habitat, intergenerational time channel; the membrane is lossy (Fig 4 :21, :30).
- **D1 The decision loop.** Actors: managers, the firm's Γ, instruments. Currency: actions and residual reduction per party. Anchor: the measured outcome for each named party. Admitted.
- **D2 The claimant habitat.** Actors: workers, customers, owners, communities, suppliers, regulators. Currency: survival, consent, payment, trust, license to operate. Anchor: each party's own outcome plus market, contract and court (Fig 2 :29). Admitted.
- **D3 The governance library.** Actors: claims, rights, contracts, metrics, seats, records, instruments and their typed bonds. Currency: reach and |φ| cost. Anchor: settlement against the ground (Fig 5 :32). Admitted.
- **D4 The seat and the time channel.** Actors: boards, audits, ombuds, regulators, standards. Currency: contact with the ground. Anchor: *"the ground does not decay, the channel does"* (Fig 10 :24). Admitted.
- **X1 Future generations as a currently registering claimant.** No actor present, no currency returned, no nameable anchor. Not a domain yet (Fig 6 :34-35, Fig 10 :23).
- **X2 A fixed priority order of parties.** Absorbed into D2 as a candidate binding; refused by Fig 11 :51. **X3 Society as a sixth party.** Absorbed into the D2 plus D4 compound.

Partition or union: union, not partition. D1/D3 and D2/D4 overlap, and the claimants overlap each other, so a worker is also a community member and an owner also inherits the climate (Fig 9 :54-57). Dependency order: perception, vocabulary, objective, planning, learn and carry (Fig 3 :20). Strain test applied: mapping the five parties onto Fig 13's six physical terms only fit by renaming them as kinds of space and energy, so Fig 13 does not govern party identity; it governs the honest stop only (strain S1).

## VERDICT (from the figures)

The shape survives and no priority name is returned: a company resolves such conflicts by keeping each claimant party's residual indexed and separate, ranging each against a measurement that does not move when that party pushes, and installing a refusal seat outside the slice being graded; it does not resolve them by aggregating the parties into one objective, by negotiating them to agreement against a moving reference, or by applying a standing priority list. The four conditions form a union because the parties overlap, so one lever leaves the intersections standing. A convention that decides an outcome is no longer a convention. For future generations no residual currently registers, so the verdict is `unreached`: an instrument and a seat are owed, not a rank. Per-domain verdicts are kept separate and never averaged. Mode `specified` on the governance claims, `general` on structure, `gap` on named outcomes, not `grounded`.

- **cost if wrong:** a company is told to aggregate or to negotiate when the failure is in the anchor or the seat, so effort lands above the break (Fig 3 :19) and an inside seat grades its own resolution. The harm is a hidden live slot and a convention mistaken for a verdict. It is bounded and locatable because the packet states the five refuters and the lineage before the conclusion. The avoidable part is spending the independence of outside readers by sending a summary instead of the shape and its refuter (Fig 2 :42).
- **undoable:** yes. Nothing is deleted if the claim fails. A refutation adds a term that refuses and the reach falls because something was added rather than struck out (Fig 6 :42-43). The four conditions and the `gap` are recorded with provenance, so a later reader can locate the error rather than only feel it (Fig 10 :62-64).
- **reversed by:** a ground reading, not agreement. Falsifiers written before the run: (1) a single aggregate score predicting per-party residual growth prospectively; (2) negotiation against a moving reference with an allocation stable across three non-updating measurements; (3) a standing priority order predicting outcomes across three categorically different conflicts; (4) one lever restoring all four conditions with no categorically different residue; (5) a repeatable non-updating instrument returning a per-decision residual for people not yet born, accepted by two low-overlap frames. A derivation of one of Fig 13's six terms from the other five, or a failure of Fig 6 idempotence inside a closed set, would break the framework itself. Until one fires, the verdict stands as `candidate`.
