# Outreach draft - validate the output first, then decide about the theory

**Use:** one-to-one, or as a cover letter attached to a submission through the public venues in `03_public_contact_points.csv`. **Not** for bulk send.

**Hard precondition:** the stage-1 output below must survive the refuter before any theory is shown. If it does not, there is nothing else to look at.

---

**Subject:** Check one governance claim before you decide whether the framework is worth reading

> Hi [name],
>
> I am writing about conflicts between workers, customers, owners, communities and future generations. I have one specific claim I would like you to check against what you already know. I am not asking you to read the framework first, and I am not asking you to agree with it. If the claim does not survive, there is nothing else to look at.
>
> **The claim (`specified`, not `grounded`):** a company cannot settle such a conflict by aggregating the five parties into one score, and it cannot settle it by negotiating them to agreement against a reference that moves when they push on it. What the figures require is structural. Each party's residual, the gap between what the company predicted for it and what actually happened, must stay indexed per party, never averaged (Fig 1 :19, :22). Each party must range against a reference that does not change its answer because of what any party thinks: a payment record, a safety measurement, a contract performed or breached, a harm count, a court ruling (Fig 2 :29, :30, :33). *"Alignment is triangulation, not negotiation. A mirror cannot correct you."* (Fig 2 :35). And the seat that grades the resolution must be outside the slice being graded, and able to refuse (Fig 10 :42, :85-86).
>
> Two things follow that I expect you to test hardest. First, no standing priority order of the parties survives a change of the underlying harm: *"No standing list of good actors survives a change of residual."* (Fig 11 :51). Second, the five parties overlap. A worker is also a community member; an owner also inherits the climate. This is a union of conditions, not a list (Fig 9 :55), so one lever clears only the region where one condition holds.
>
> **What I am not claiming:** that any particular party should win; that "stakeholder capitalism" is the answer; that a purpose statement settles anything; that the framework can name a weight for each party. A declared meaning is not a grounded one (Fig 10 :49). For future generations in particular, no residual currently registers: there is no party in the room and no instrument returning their outcome. I report that as `unreached`, not as a priority rank. The framework returns a shape, not a name.
>
> **What would refute the claim, written down before this message:**
> 1. a single aggregate score that predicts, prospectively and per named party, which party's residual will grow next;
> 2. a conflict settled by negotiation against a moving reference, with the allocation stable across three independent non-updating measurements;
> 3. a standing priority order over the five parties that predicts which claim prevails across three categorically different conflicts;
> 4. a single lever that restores all four conditions in all four failure types and leaves no categorically different residue;
> 5. a repeatable, non-updating instrument that returns a per-decision residual for people not yet born, accepted by two frames with different evidence pools.
>
> If any one holds, the shape is wrong and I will retract it. A one-line "this fails because..." is more useful to me than agreement. If you would rather not engage, no reply is needed.
>
> **If it survives, and only then:** the sources are below. The figures are the authority, not my summary of them.
> - The figures and formula, with line numbers (local extract): `FRAMEWORK/reference/03-FIGURES_TEXT.md`
> - Canonical usage rule (GitHub, branch `general-agent`): https://github.com/IsaiahN/tether/tree/general-agent/figures
> - One opened world source that bears directly on the negotiation point: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stakeholder_theory (section "Criticism", Blattberg 2004 and Jensen 2002)
>
> **If you want to use the machinery:** the formula is in the figures link. Run it on a governance problem you know well and can check against a ground. Write down what would refute your result *before* you run it, and state whether your result is `general`, `specified` or `grounded`. A reported failure is more useful to me than a reported success.
