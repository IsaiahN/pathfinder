# Expert verification packet - the Tether on stakeholder conflict

**What this is:** a claim in a form a business-ethics or governance researcher can check. Mode, derivation and refuters are stated before the conclusion, so a reader knows what to look for and what would settle it.

**Mode: `specified` on the governance claims, `general` on the structural shape, `gap` on which party prevails. Not `grounded`.** No company measurement is claimed. This is a shape and a route, not a result.

**Status: simulated derivation checked for internal consistency against the figures, not a ground reading.** It cannot settle the question it reports on.

## 1. The claim, in one paragraph

A company cannot resolve a conflict between workers, customers, owners, communities and future generations by aggregating them into one score or one priority list, because the residual must stay indexed per party (Fig 1 :19, :22) and no standing list of good actors survives a change of residual (Fig 11 :51). Nor can it resolve the conflict by negotiating the parties to agreement against a reference that moves when they push, because *"Alignment is triangulation, not negotiation. A mirror cannot correct you."* (Fig 2 :35). What the figures require is a union of four conditions kept separate: a per-party residual register, a non-updating anchor each party ranges against (Fig 2 :29, :33), a refusal seat outside the slice being graded (Fig 10 :42, :85-86), and an instrument for the party whose residual does not yet register (Fig 6 :34-35, Fig 13 :134). A convention that decides an outcome has stopped being a convention (Fig 10 :47). The framework returns a shape; which party prevails in a named dispute is a `gap`.

## 2. Three checkable sub-claims

### C1 - governance frame; checkable by a researcher in business ethics or stakeholder theory

- **Statement:** a single objective (shareholder primacy) and a single weighted stakeholder score are the same structural object: an aggregate across slots that hides the live signal. The figure-derived requirement is per-party residuals ranged separately against a non-updating anchor.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 1 :19 - *"The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated."* Fig 1 :22 - *"Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average."* Fig 2 :29 - *"An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks."* Fig 2 :33 - *"The anchor must not update."*
- **What to check:** is the aggregate/per-slot distinction real in the literature? Jensen 2002 complains that stakeholder theory leaves managers with multiple objectives and no principled trade-off rule; the mirror-image complaint applies to shareholder primacy. Mitchell, Agle and Wood's power/legitimacy/urgency salience is a filter for attention, not a ground for a verdict.
- **Refuter:** a single aggregate score that predicts, prospectively and per named party, which party's residual will grow next, across three independent measurements. If it exists, C1 is false.

### C2 - dispute resolution frame; checkable by a researcher in conflict management, procedural justice or institutional governance

- **Statement:** resolving a conflict by negotiation among the parties, against a reference that moves when they push, produces agreement rather than evidence. Each party must range against the anchor separately, and the grading seat must stand outside the slice it grades and be able to refuse.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 2 :35 - *"Alignment is triangulation, not negotiation. A mirror cannot correct you."* Fig 2 :47 - *"A ground listed as one of the parties has already become a frame."* Fig 10 :42 - *"A verifier that can reconstruct the claim cannot verify it."* Fig 10 :85-86 - *"Outside the slice being graded. Whoever is measured by a reading cannot own it."* Operators table :58 - *"a refutation is a negation, which is why a vocabulary without it can only accumulate."*
- **What to check:** Blattberg 2004 criticises stakeholder theory for its emphasis on negotiation as the chief mode of dialogue; Rawls requires an independent criterion for a fair outcome; Leventhal's rules include bias suppression and correctability; Ostrom's commons guidelines put monitoring in the hands of users or someone accountable to them. Do these converge on the same structural requirement, or are they different quantities?
- **Refuter:** a negotiation against a moving reference that yields an allocation stable across three non-updating outcome measurements, or a seat inside the slice with an undeclared interest in the reading and no refusal that does the same. Either breaks C2.

### C3 - the unregistering party; checkable by a researcher in sustainability, intergenerational justice or organizational future studies

- **Statement:** future generations are not a sixth party to be weighted. No instrument currently returns their residual, so the honest verdict is `unreached`; what is owed is a source-discriminating instrument with provenance and a shadow test, not a priority name.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 10 :23 - *"R never arrives"*; Fig 6 :34-35 - *"Where nothing is, there is no edge to extend from, and the search for a proto-instrument is a search for one that is already there."* Fig 13 :134 - *"Unreachable is a verdict here, not a silence"*; Fig 6 :12 - *"source gate: provenance"* and Fig 6 :13 - *"shadow test: explains a gap"*.
- **What to check:** Waddock 2026 classifies *"unvoiced or silent humans who have no voice in the present"* and reports that such stakeholders may lack sufficient salience without specific attention. The intergenerational-equity literature proposes an ombudsman for future generations. Construal level theory reports that distant outcomes are construed abstractly and that this distances decision-makers from undesirable consequences. Do these describe an instrument gap rather than a weighting gap?
- **Refuter:** a repeatable, non-updating instrument that returns a per-decision residual for people not yet born, accepted by two frames with genuinely different evidence pools. If it exists, the `gap` narrows and C3 must be revised.

## 3. What is explicitly not claimed

- Not that any particular party should win, and not that stakeholder capitalism defeats shareholder primacy. Both are standing lists.
- Not a grounded result. No company measurement was taken and no figure line selects a weighting.
- Not that a purpose statement, certification or ESG score settles a dispute. A declared meaning is not a grounded one (Fig 10 :49).
- Not that stakeholder agreement is corroboration. Agreement inside one evidence pool is one witness (Fig 2 :40).
- Not that hearing a party changes its outcome. A hearing is a sequence; a changed outcome is a production (Fig 12 :70-79).
- Not that future interests are weightless. Their status here is `unreached` with an instrument owed, not dismissed.

## 5. What would settle it

A non-updating measurement per party, read independently by two frames with different evidence pools. Concretely: prospectively per-party outcome measurements for C1; three independent non-updating outcome measurements of a disputed allocation for C2; a long-horizon residual instrument accepted by two low-overlap frames for C3. The five refuters in `01` section 4 are written before the run.

## 6. What an expert can check in twenty minutes

1. Read Fig 1 :19, :22; Fig 2 :29, :33, :35; Fig 11 :51; Fig 10 :42, :85-86; Fig 6 :34-35; Fig 13 :134. Ten short passages.
2. Check C1 against Jensen 2002 and the stakeholder-salience literature: is the aggregation complaint symmetric?
3. Check C2 against Blattberg 2004, Rawls, Leventhal, or Ostrom: is the independent-reference requirement the same claim in their vocabulary?
4. Check C3 against Waddock 2026 or the intergenerational-equity proposals: is what is missing a reading or a rank?
5. Name one non-updating measurement that would falsify the claim, or say why none is possible. Send the refutation. A failure is more useful than an endorsement.
