## The question restated as an effect

The loop defines `R = |Γ(b,a) - o'|` per slot s (Formula step 1; Symbols table, `R`). Here the slots are the claimant parties. A company action `a` changes what workers receive, what customers receive, what owners receive, what communities bear, and what people not yet born will inherit. Each party is a slot with its own residual. The observed `o'` is the actual, measured outcome for that party. The question is not which party ought to be named the winner. It is how a governance process keeps each slot's residual visible and lets a non-updating ground settle the conflicts between them.

- Option A, "one party wins by right" (shareholder primacy, or any fixed priority list): leads to a standing ranking of claimants that does not change when the residual changes, and is refused by Fig 11 :51.
- Option B, "balance all parties into one score": leads to aggregation across slots, which is exactly how the live signal disappears (Fig 1 :22), and to a convention that decides an outcome (Fig 10 :47).
- Option C, the figure-derived reading: the five claimants form a **union** of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate parties (Fig 9 :55); each residual must stay per slot (Fig 1 :19); the conflict must be ranged against an anchor that does not update when the parties push on it (Fig 2 :29, :30, :33); the seat that resolves it must be outside the slice being graded (Fig 10 :85-86); and the future-generation slot has no nameable anchor yet, so it is a declared `gap` and what is owed is an instrument, not a name (Fig 6 :34-35, Fig 13 :134).

Written down and bound as an ordinary term before the run (Formula, "Direction"): the sought-for answer would be a **governance shape**, that is, a set of conditions that keeps every claimant's residual indexed and rangeable against a non-updating ground, plus an instrument that discriminates a failure of one condition from a failure of another. Until bound, that shape predicts nothing and no check in the loop can see it. It is now bound, so the refuter in `01` section 4 can fire on it.

The verdict recorded in `09_watson_answer.md` is Option C in `specified` mode, kept per domain and never averaged.

## What is deliberately not here

- No personal emails of named individuals.
- No ranking of people by how likely they are to agree.
- No bulk-send plan.
- No claim that this derivation is `grounded`, no claim that it settles the business-ethics question, no universal priority ordering of the five parties, and no claim that the framework is the only true account of corporate purpose.

Four reasons, three from the figures and one practical:

1. A room is not an anchor. *"A room's agreement is variation, not a verdict. Selection between rooms is where the ground votes."* (Fig 7 :35) and *"Alignment is triangulation, not negotiation. A mirror cannot correct you."* (Fig 2 :35). A stakeholder forum selected for agreeableness manufactures the correlation that disqualifies a verdict.
2. Correlation has valence. *"agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool"* (Fig 2 :40). A list selected for receptiveness is one witness wearing many names.
3. A verifier must be outside the interior. *"A verifier that can reconstruct the claim cannot verify it."* (Fig 10 :42). The first contact therefore carries the residual and its refuter, not the framework.
4. Legally, unsolicited bulk email to scraped addresses breaches GDPR and CAN-SPAM style rules in many jurisdictions and gets the sending domain blacklisted. Public opt-in routes avoid that failure as well as the structural one.
