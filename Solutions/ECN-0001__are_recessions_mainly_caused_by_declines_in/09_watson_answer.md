## QUESTION

Decide, in effect terms, whether one of three candidate mechanisms is the main cause of recessions. Option A: a decline in aggregate demand is the main cause, so a demand-management instrument closes the residual. Option B: a supply disruption is the main cause, so a supply-side or relative-price instrument closes it. Option C: interacting shocks are the main cause, so neither single lever clears the overlap and a compound with its own loop is owed. What each leads to: A and B each pick an instrument from a standing rule; C splits the question per episode and per slot and owes a discriminator. The ordinary term bound for the loop is "main cause": a single residual-owning mechanism with a nameable, non-updating anchor such that, if it were removed, the recession would not have occurred. The figures do not license that term; they license the split and the separator.

## DOMAIN CENSUS

R by arity, symmetry and scale, then one line per domain with actors / currency / anchor, partition or union, and order by dependency.

- **R (the residual).** A fall in output and employment read from one aggregate series while several mechanisms remain capable of producing that reading. Arity: one pooled reading, many possible sources; the map from causes to reading is many-to-one. Symmetry: demand and supply push output the same way and prices opposite ways, so a symmetric reading is being asked to decide an ordered, source-level question. Scale: first order inside a sector, second order through input-output cascades. Fig 9 names a disagreement that does not shrink with effort and works on different subsets; the question is a bundle to split, not one hard problem.
- **Domain 1, measurement and diagnostics.** Actors: GDP, unemployment, inflation, NBER dating, sectoral series. Currency: a pooled aggregate reading. Anchor: the data as a *reading*, not a source-discriminating instrument, so this domain ends in `gap`. Question type: inverse problem, underdetermined.
- **Domain 2, mechanism and contact.** Actors: households, firms, banks, government, central bank, external shocks. Currency: spending, production, credit, expectations. Anchor: an exogenous variation that does not update on the economy's own reports, imported from a differing frame. Question type: union of overlapping conditions; demand and supply cores are isomers over a fixed atom set.
- **Domain 3, identification and attribution.** Actors: VAR/SVAR restrictions, natural experiments, variance decompositions. Currency: identification and variance shares. Anchor: a source-discriminating instrument; the share is a budget filter. Question type: partition attempt that fails and returns a shape.
- **Domain 4, policy and response.** Actors: monetary and fiscal authorities, rule makers. Currency: policy instruments. Anchor: outcomes, which do not update on a policy's own announcement. Question type: convention-versus-verdict test.
- **Order by dependency.** Perceive per slot (Fig 1 :19); hold typed terms for the atoms and bonds (Fig 12 :46); state the objective, discriminating the source (Fig 3 :10); import a non-updating anchor with a source gate and shadow test (Fig 6 :23); name what paid and carry the method (Fig 4 :22). The chain breaks at the objective link, which has no instrument.

Strain test applied: the Fig 13 physical substrate mapped onto economics atoms is a level-relative binding, not a derivation; Fig 12 :104 permits the recursion and the README's re-census rule assigns "shape, not a name" to missing primitives only. ## VERDICT (from the figures)

The trichotomy is not a partition and the question has no universal single-cause answer at this budget. Per episode and per slot, demand and supply conditions overlap; a demand-recession and a supply-recession are isomers over a fixed atom set; "interacting shocks" is the intersection plus a contact cycle, not a third cause. A variance share is a filter for the budget, never a witness for the verdict. What is owed is a source-discriminating instrument with a non-updating anchor and low-overlap frames; the figures return its shape, not the name of what it would find. This is `specified` on the per-domain claims, `general` on the structural shape, and `gap` on the universal main cause. It is ungrounded and candidate.

- **cost if wrong:** bounded but real. The packet labels modes, states refuters before the conclusion and records the lineage, so an error is locatable rather than invisible. The larger cost is spending the independence of outside readers by sending a verdict-shaped claim before they have described what would refute it. Fig 2 :42 "Buy independence in proportion to the cost of being wrong." The cost here is a wrong attribution of recessions and therefore a wrong policy instrument, so independence is worth buying with genuinely differing frames.
- **undoable:** yes. The separator and the refuters can be revised or withdrawn without touching the figure text; no primitive is claimed and no atom has to be retracted. A sent claim can be superseded by a corrected packet. The live fixed-atom condition on the isomer claim can fail without collapsing the union verdict.
- **reversed by:** a ground reading, not agreement. A discriminator that holds one bond fixed and varies the other, returning the same cause across episodes with a non-updating anchor, would close the gap; a classification showing the conditions form a partition would reverse the union verdict; an atom required by one core and not the other would break the isomer claim; a source-discriminating instrument returning one name across sectors would move the answer toward `grounded`. Until one of these fires, the verdict stands as `candidate`.
