# Expert verification packet: the Tether on the causes of recessions

**What this is:** a claim in a form a macroeconomist can check. Mode, derivation and refuters are stated before the conclusion, so a reader knows what to look for and what would settle it.

**Mode: `specified` on the per-domain verdicts, `general` on the structural shape, `gap` on a universal single main cause. Not `grounded`.** No measurement is claimed here. This is a shape and a separator, not a result.

**Status:** a simulated derivation checked for internal consistency against the figures, not a ground reading. It cannot settle the question it reports on.

## 1. The claim, in one paragraph

"Are recessions mainly caused by demand declines, supply disruptions, or interacting shocks?" asks for the wrong object. The three options form a union of overlapping conditions, not a partition. Per episode and per slot a demand rule fits some cases and a supply rule fits others; interaction is the intersection plus a contact cascade, not a third cause. Over a fixed atom set the demand-recession and the supply-recession are isomers (same atoms, different typed arrangement, different substance, invisible in output and decisive in policy). A variance share is a filter for the budget, not a witness for a cause. What is owed is a source-discriminating instrument with a non-updating anchor and low-overlap frames; the figures can characterise its shape but cannot name its output, so the universal main cause is recorded as a `gap`.

## 2. Three checkable sub-claims

### C1: framework-internal, checkable by reading Figures 9 and 11

- **Statement:** the trichotomy is a union, not a partition; a case in two conditions is categorically different from a case in one.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 9 :4-5: "First: is there a fact here? Disagreements that do not shrink with effort, where each rule keeps working well on a different subset, are not one hard question but several well-formed ones. Split it rather than search." Also Fig 9 :57: "What remains is smaller and it is also categorically different: a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either." Also Fig 11 :51: "No standing list of good actors survives a change of residual."
- **What to check:** is the disagreement about the main cause actually a case where each rule works on a different subset, and do episodes exist in which both conditions hold? The Bekaert/Engstrom/Ermolov and Baqaee/Farhi world sources in `04` report such episodes.
- **Refuter:** show a classification in which every recession satisfies exactly one condition with a non-updating anchor that discriminates it; then the conditions form a partition and C1 falls.

### C2: cross-frame, checkable by a macroeconomist against the identification literature

- **Statement:** per-slot residuals carry the source information that aggregate readings discard; variance shares are filters, not witnesses.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 1 :19: "The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated." Also Fig 1 :22: "Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average." Also Fig 9 :60: "Use filters for the budget and witnesses for the verdict. Never let a filter hand you a verdict." Also Fig 2 :40: "disqualifying for a verdict: agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool."
- **What to check:** does a single aggregate series uniquely identify demand versus supply, or is the mapping many-to-one? Blanchard and Quah (NBER WP 2737) report a demand contribution ranging from 40 to over 95 per cent at four quarters under alternative assumptions, and call the dynamic characterization sharp but the shares imprecisely estimated.
- **Refuter:** exhibit a share that is invariant across identification frames, comes with a non-updating anchor, and is used as a verdict rather than a budget; then C2 is narrowed to that share.

### C3: framework-internal derivation with a live condition

- **Statement:** demand-recession and supply-recession are isomers over a fixed atom set; interaction is a union intersection plus a cycle; the universal main cause is refused, not replaced.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 12 :117: "isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour." Also Fig 12 :94: "The arrangement is the substance." Also Fig 9 :55: "A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones." Also Fig 12 :121: "cycle. The output re-enters as input."
- **What to check:** can each story be written over the same atoms (spending, capacity, credit, expectations, price ordering, time), or does one require an atom the other never uses? If one does, the isomer claim fails and the weaker functional-group reading stands (Fig 12 :123: "functional group. A substructure whose presence fixes the outcome. The group says what kind; the qualifier says which member.").
- **Refuter:** the fixed-atom condition above. It is live and is not hidden in `01`, `06`, `09` or `11`.

## 3. What is explicitly not claimed

- Not that demand or supply never matter.
- Not a universal percentage, a ranking of recessions, or a best single explanation.
- Not that "interacting shocks" is a third kind of cause or the ⋛ bond. A first-pass mapping to ⋛ was withdrawn and is preserved in `01` §4 S3.
- Not grounded. No measurement was taken and no natural experiment was run.
- Not that a discriminator is impossible; it is unreachable at this budget with the held instruments (Fig 8 :39).

## 5. What would settle it

A source-discriminating instrument, not another argument. Concretely: show in one episode that the output fall persists when the demand bond is held fixed and the capacity bond varies, or vice versa; or exhibit one mechanism with a non-updating anchor that works across episodes; or exhibit a discriminator that returns one name across sectors. Standing refuters (written before the conclusion): (1) one mechanism with a discriminating anchor across all episodes; (2) the conditions form a partition; (3) one core requires an atom the other does not; (4) a discriminator returns one cause across episodes and slots; (5) one aggregate series already carries the source information.

## 6. What an expert can check in twenty minutes

1. Read Fig 9 :4-5, :57 and Fig 1 :19, :22, four short passages.
2. Check C2 against a decomposition you know: is the demand/supply share a stable verdict or a budget-dependent filter?
3. Name one identification strategy that would hold one bond fixed and vary the other, or say why none is possible.
4. Send the refutation. A failure is more useful than an endorsement.
