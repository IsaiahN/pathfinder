## Independent domain census (written from the residual line before reading the primary's conclusions)

I redid the census from the residual in effect terms, using the WATSON order: R by arity, symmetry and scale; one line per domain with actors / currency / anchor; partition or union; order by dependency.

- **R (arity / symmetry / scale).** A fall in output read from one aggregate series while several mechanisms remain capable of producing that reading. Arity: one reading, many sources, so many-to-one. Symmetry: the two named mechanisms move output the same way and prices the opposite way, so a symmetric reading cannot orient an ordered source question. Scale: first order in a sector, second order through linkages. Question type: a disagreement that does not shrink, which Fig 9 :4-5 says to split rather than search.
- **Domain 1, measurement.** Actors: aggregate and sectoral statistics. Currency: a pooled reading. Anchor: the data as reading, not as discriminator. Ends in `gap`.
- **Domain 2, mechanism.** Actors: spending, capacity, credit, expectations, external shocks. Currency: real activity and prices. Anchor: an exogenous variation that does not update on reports. Union of overlapping conditions; the two cores are isomers over a fixed atom set.
- **Domain 3, identification.** Actors: restrictions, natural experiments, variance decompositions. Currency: identification and shares. Anchor: a source-discriminating instrument; the share is a filter.
- **Domain 4, policy.** Actors: authorities and rule makers. Currency: instruments. Anchor: outcomes that do not update on announcements. Convention-versus-verdict test.

Order by dependency: perceive per slot; hold typed terms; state the objective (discriminate the source); import a non-updating anchor; carry the method. I independently place the break at the objective link, which has no instrument.

My census matches the primary's four domains and its union reading. Where the primary derived the split from Fig 9 :4-5 and the isomer claim from Fig 12 :117, I derived the same boundaries from Fig 1 :19-22 (per-slot residual) and Fig 3 :27 (missing instrument at the objective link). ## Round trip gap R_T

The Symbols table defines R_T as the gap between what was sent up and what came back down, `gap(x, (T_E ∘ T_A)(x))`. Fig 4 :30 requires it to be measured rather than assumed. I ran the round trip on the condensation the folder actually uses.

2. **Abstraction (T_A):** the shorthand in `07` and `09`: the trichotomy is a union, cores are isomers, share is a filter, a discriminator is owed, universal cause is a gap.
3. **Concretisation (T_E):** the concrete answer an economist would act on: which instrument to use for which recession, and what number to attribute to demand versus supply.

**R_T is nonzero.** Three things the shorthand holds do not survive the trip back down:

- the **per-slot indexing** is flattened when the shorthand is applied to a national recession rate;
- the **union overlap** is flattened when "interaction" is treated as a third box rather than the intersection plus a cycle;
- the **source-discriminating instrument** is replaced by a variance share, which is a filter and cannot settle the verdict.

A fourth constraint is partly dropped: the non-updating anchor becomes an in-sample decomposition in most applications. Conversely, two things survive cleanly: the question must be split, and the two cores are isomers over a fixed atom set. R_T was **not** measured numerically, because no ground reading exists; it is recorded qualitatively as the enumerated set of dropped constraints. Treating "the formula is the condensed version of the metalaws" as identity would set R_T to zero and would be the dead-reckoning error Fig 4 :30 names. The honest form is a lossy condensation with a measured gap, which is what `09_watson_answer.md` states.
