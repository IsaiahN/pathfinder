## DERIVATION

### Q1: Does the isomer definition apply?

- Fig 12 :117: "isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."
- Fig 12 :94: "The arrangement is the substance."
- Fig 12 :46: "A bond is the typed relation between two operands."

Demand-recession over the atom set: a fall in S (spending) → a contraction of C (credit availability) → a fall in P (production actually used), with R moving down. Supply-recession over the *same* atom set: a loss of P (productive capacity) ⇒ a rise in R (relative prices) → a fall in S (real spending) and C. Both invoke S, P, C, R, E and T. They differ in which bond holds where and in the direction of R. By the definition they are isomers: same atoms, different typed arrangement, different substance. The difference is invisible in the headline output formula (both show falling GDP) and decisive in behaviour (demand stimulus helps one and may only inflate the other).

**Falsifier, written before the run:** if either story requires an atom the other never uses, for example a physical-capacity term that a pure demand recession cannot invoke, the isomer claim fails and the weaker functional-group reading stands (Fig 12 :123: "functional group. A substructure whose presence fixes the outcome. The group says what kind; the qualifier says which member."). This falsifier is live; it is carried into `01`, `08`, `09` and `11`.

### Q2: Why the "formula" cannot tell them apart

- Operators table :9-16: the flat formula has only `+`, conjunction, so it records ingredients and not arrangement. Fig 12 :5: "The bond is the rest of the information, and it is the half nothing was recording."
- A GDP series names the ingredients (output, employment, prices at a coarse level) but not the typed relation between spending, capacity and credit.
- Fig 12 :111: "A vocabulary without bonds can name a state and cannot name a route to one." The aggregate reading names the state (recession) and cannot name the route (demand route versus supply route).

This is the structural reason the inverse-problem source finds the mapping non-unique: the observation operator discards the bond information, so several causes map to one reading.

### Q3: Is "interacting shocks" a third isomer, a bond, or something else?

Not a third isomer over the same set with a different bond, and not the ⋛ bond. Withdrawn first-pass mapping (preserved): I initially assigned interaction to ⋛ because the word suggests a comparison of two shocks. The Operators table defines ⋛ as "a threshold relation between two quantities", which is not a general interaction operator, so that reading strains. Re-census:

- Interaction is **conjunction** where two conditions hold at once (Fig 9 :55, "A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones."). It is a compound, not a new kind of atom.
- Its propagation is the **contact cascade** (Fig 11 :52-53): a second-order effect whose residual is several steps out. The chain re-enters as input, which is Fig 12 :121's cycle: "cycle. The output re-enters as input. The sign of the loop decides whether it closes, and a chain cannot close by construction." A financial accelerator (housing prices → bank assets → fire sales → credit → spending → house prices) is a cycle whose sign decides whether it closes.
- The epistemic problem of assuming the wrong single cause is a **refusal**, using ¬ (Operators :58: "a refutation is a negation, which is why a vocabulary without it can only accumulate"). The universal main cause is not replaced by a new cause; it loses standing to be composed over.

So "interacting shocks" is a **union intersection plus a cycle**, and the universal-cause claim is the term refused. Both are compositions of held atoms and bonds. No new primitive is minted.

### Q4: What is the dependency order?

- Fig 3 :20: "The chain is ordered by dependency, not by importance."
- Ordered chain: (1) perceive falling output per slot (Fig 1 :19), (2) hold typed terms for spending, capacity, credit and their bonds (Fig 12 :46), (3) state the objective: discriminate the source (Fig 3 :10), (4) choose an identification strategy that imports a non-updating anchor with a source gate and shadow test (Fig 6 :23), (5) name what paid and carry the method (Fig 4 :22).
- The break is at step 3: the field can name ingredients and cannot state, in its own terms, what would count as progress on the source question. Fig 3 :27: "A link with no instrument is not a link that works. It is a link nobody has looked at." Refining step 1 (more aggregate data) cannot rescue a missing step 3 (Fig 3 :19).

### Q5: What would settle the isomer question?

An instrument, not a further argument. From *The Hard Problem is About Source* (carried in `05` §4): a reading *"cannot comment on the source"* until a discriminating instrument exists. Here the discriminator would have to show, in one episode, that the same output fall persists when the demand bond is held fixed and the capacity bond varies, or vice versa. Natural experiments that shift one while holding the other are imports from a differing closure and must be debited against independence (Fig 8 :47, "Independence is one quantity read twice.").

## CONCLUSION

The two cores are isomers over a fixed atom set, with a live falsifier on the fixed-atom condition. "Interacting shocks" is the union intersection plus a cycle, not a third cause and not a ⋛ bond; the universal main cause is the term refused via ¬. The chain breaks at the objective link, where a source-discriminating instrument is owed. No new primitive, no wall-moving import and no universal percentage is required. The fixed-atom condition on the isomer claim remains a strain, named rather than hidden.
