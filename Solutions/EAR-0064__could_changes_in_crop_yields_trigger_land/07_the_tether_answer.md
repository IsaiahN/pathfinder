# The Tether's answer on yield change, land conversion, biodiversity and climate

**Mode: `specified` on the per-domain verdicts, `general` on the structural shape, `gap` on magnitude. Not `grounded`.** This is a shape and a route, not a result. Simulated derivation checked against the figures (`05_what_shape_means.md` section 7), not a ground reading.

## 1. The residual it answers

The question asks whether a change in crop yield can trigger land conversion that worsens biodiversity loss and climate change. The residual is not a single causal coefficient. It is a chain with a union tail: a per-place quantity reading, a decision-mediated contact change, and two downstream limbs with different anchors. Fig 9 :4: *"are not one hard question but several well-formed ones. Split it rather than search."*

## 2. The shape, per domain, verdicts kept separate

**D1, the yield signal (per slot).** A yield change registers per region and per crop. Fig 1 :19: *"The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated."* Fig 1 :22: *"Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average."* A rising global total can hide a live local failure. Verdict: `specified`, triangulated (Fig 1 plus the regional structure in the IPCC land assessment).

**D2, the conversion decision (the trigger link).** Yield change does not convert land by itself. It enters a decision frame whose terms select the regime. Fig 3 :27: *"A link with no instrument is not a link that works. It is a link nobody has looked at."* Under one regime land is spared (the Ausubel claim via Our World in Data); under another, efficiency makes production more profitable and land is converted (the Jevons agriculture example). Both are real. Which applies is a measurement question. Once conversion happens it is an introduction and a substitution, not a subtraction: Fig 11 :61, *"Introduction is available; exclusion is not. You can add an actor and you cannot subtract the habitat, because contact is a fact about"*, and Fig 11 :66, *"Isolation is not removal of the habitat. It is substitution of one habitat for another."* Verdict: `specified`, `general` on the conditionality, triangulated across Fig 11 and the two regimes.

**D3, biodiversity.** The material loss is a cascade, not the first-order effect at the field edge. Fig 11 :52: *"And contact cascades. Scoping the habitat to direct contact is how DDT reached the eagles: first-order enumeration, second-order effect, and the residual that mattered was three"*. The habitat is enumerated, never composed (Fig 11 :43). Valence flips with the residual: the same high-yield cultivar is a benefactor to food and a malfactor to biodiversity (Fig 11 :49). Verdict: `specified`, triangulated across Fig 11, IPCC and the ecology reference.

**D4, climate.** A finite carbon stock becomes a spendable gradient when converted: Fig 13 :14, *"conserved, finite, directionless alone"*; Fig 13 :22, *"a difference that can be spent"*. The atmospheric anchor does not move because a frame reports it; the channel decays (Fig 10 :24, *"the ground does not decay, the channel does"*). Verdict: `specified` on the flux, `general` on the gradient mapping, single-source on the figure side.

**D5, the union.** Biodiversity and climate have different anchors and cannot be averaged. Fig 9 :55: *"A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones."* Conversion is the overlapping condition. A lever that clears one region can leave the intersection standing. Verdict: `general` on the union, `specified` on each limb.

The verdicts are not added. There is no overall "somewhat yes" score.

## 3. What it explicitly does not say

- Not a universal law. Yield change does not always cause conversion; the link is conditional on price, tenure, policy and demand (F8).
- Not a number. No hectares, species count or ppm is returned (Fig 9 :32, *"you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself"*).
- Not a standing list of good or bad actors. Valence is relative to the residual (Fig 11 :51).
- Not one averaged outcome. Biodiversity and climate are two verdicts (F6).
- Not reversible by exclusion. Conversion substitutes a habitat; restoration is another substitution (Fig 11 :66).
- Not `grounded`. No measurement was taken, and no figure was treated as a substitute for one.

## 4. What would settle it  -  the ground

A source-discriminating instrument, not a further argument. *The Hard Problem is About Source*: an instrument that reads green *"cannot comment on the source"*; the spectrometer resolved it. Here:

1. **Trigger test:** a per-slot record pairing yield change with land-cover change and the decision variables (price, tenure, policy, demand) that select the regime. Does the yield residual precede contact change in the relevant slots, and which regime obtains?
2. **Cascade test:** an enumeration to second and third order around a conversion, measuring whether the biodiversity residual that matters is more than first order. Fig 11 :44: *"List everything in contact with the residual, then what is in contact with those, and outward until the cascade stops mattering."*
3. **Carbon test:** a flux measurement showing net stock change and sink removal at the converted site, with the atmospheric anchor read independently.
4. **Standing refuters from section 4 of `01`:** reversible subtraction; no cascade; one evidence pool; per-slot null result; shared anchor; one lever with no displaced burden.

A filter is not a witness: Fig 9 :60, *"Use filters for the budget and witnesses for the verdict. Never let a filter hand you a verdict."*

## 6. One-paragraph version for outreach

> The Tether does not return a universal law or a number of hectares for the yield-land question. It returns a shape. A yield change registers per place, not as a global average; it changes land cover only through a decision frame whose terms select whether land is spared or converted; conversion is an introduction and a substitution of habitat rather than a reversible subtraction; the biodiversity damage that matters is a cascade three steps out; and biodiversity and climate are settled by two different anchors, so they need two verdicts and cannot be averaged. The figure laws also explain why the risk is undercounted: aggregate statistics hide the live per-slot signal, first-order impact assessment misses the cascade, and a yield reading cannot tell which decision regime produced it. What is owed is a source-discriminating instrument and a set of refuters written in advance, not another argument about which narrative is right. The center stays open until the ground settles it.
