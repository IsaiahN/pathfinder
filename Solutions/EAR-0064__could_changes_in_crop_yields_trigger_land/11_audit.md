I redid the census before reading the primary's findings, using the WATSON order: R by arity, symmetry and scale; one line per domain with actors / currency / anchor; partition or union; order by dependency.

- **R (arity / symmetry / scale).** A change in a per-place quantity is proposed to change a physical contact, which is proposed to move two outcomes. Arity: four links, with the tail splitting in two. Symmetry: the signal is a spectrum, the contact change is ordered and asymmetric, and the comparison that selects the regime is a threshold. Scale: slot, landscape, biome, planet, with trade coupling distant slots. This is a chain whose tail is a union compound, not one question and not a partition.

- **Domain 2, conversion decision frame.** Actors: farmer, price, tenure, policy, demand. Currency: profit or land rent. Anchor: observed land-cover change. This is where the trigger actually lives, and it is the only domain whose anchor is legitimately a market reading (Fig 2 :36). Question type: the mediation link and the expected break.
- **Domain 3, biodiversity frame.** Actors: species, relations, conditions, predators, pollinators. Currency: abundance and habitat area. Anchor: independent species and habitat counts. Question type: cascade-bearing downstream limb.
- **Domain 4, climate frame.** Actors: stocks, fluxes, sinks, atmosphere. Currency: carbon flux, a directed difference. Anchor: atmospheric concentration and forcing. Question type: gradient-bearing downstream limb.
- **Domain 5, coupling frame.** Actors: all four plus trade. Currency: cross-scale flows. Anchor: none of its own; each limb keeps its own. Question type: union with an intersection.
- **Order by dependency:** perceive the signal, name what the land affords, set an objective, plan, change contact, then the two downstream limbs, then the union verdict (Fig 3 :20). The break is expected between affordance and objective (Fig 3 :27).

## Round trip gap R_T

The Symbols table defines R_T as the gap between what was sent up and what came back down, `gap(x, (T_E ∘ T_A)(x))`, measuring what the coarser description cannot hold. Fig 4 :30 requires it to be measured rather than assumed. I ran the round trip on the condensation this folder actually uses.

1. **Sent up (x):** the verbatim figure set, chiefly Fig 1 :19/:22 (per slot), Fig 3 :27 (mediation link), Fig 11 :52/:61/:66 (cascade, asymmetry, substitution), Fig 9 :4/:55 (union), Fig 13 :14/:22 (stock and gradient).
2. **Abstraction (T_A):** the shorthand in `07` and `08`: yield change registers per place, acts through a decision frame, changes a contact that cannot be subtracted, produces a cascade, and faces two different anchors.
3. **Concretisation (T_E):** the ordinary-language claim "changes in crop yields trigger land conversion that worsens biodiversity loss and climate change."

**R_T is nonzero.** Four things the shorthand holds do not survive the trip back down into the ordinary claim:

- **per-slot indexing** collapses into a global "yields" subject;
- **the conditional decision link** collapses into the verb "trigger", which reads as a universal law;
- **the substitution asymmetry** collapses into "land conversion", which in policy language often implies a reversible change of land use;
- **the union tail** collapses into the single word "and", which reads as one averaged outcome.

Conversely, one thing survives cleanly: a contact change can move two different grounds, and the damage is downstream of the contact. That is the residue worth sending. R_T was **not** measured numerically, because no ground reading exists; it is recorded qualitatively as the enumerated set of dropped constraints. Treating the ordinary claim as identical to the derivation would set R_T to zero and would be the dead-reckoning error Fig 4 :30 names.
