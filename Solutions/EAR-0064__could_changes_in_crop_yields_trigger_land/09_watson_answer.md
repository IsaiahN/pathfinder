## QUESTION

Decide, in effect terms, what the Tether says about the claim that changes in crop yields could trigger land conversion that worsens biodiversity loss and climate change. Option A: accept it as a universal causal law with a knowable global magnitude. Option B: accept it as a structural possibility whose realisation is conditional on a decision frame, with two separate outcome verdicts and the magnitude left to measurement. What each leads to: A asserts a law the figures do not license, averages two different anchors, and hides a live per-slot signal; B returns a checkable shape, six written refuters, and an open magnitude until a ground settles it.

## DOMAIN CENSUS

R by arity, symmetry and scale, then one line per domain with actors / currency / anchor, partition or union, and order by dependency.

- **R (the residual).** The proposed chain: a yield reading changes a land contact, which moves biodiversity and climate. Arity: four links in a dependency chain, with the tail splitting into two conditions. Symmetry: yield is a spectrum (orderable, more or less); conversion is an ordered and asymmetric action; the yield-to-profit comparison is a threshold. Scale: per field or region slot at the trigger, landscape and biome at the cascade, planetary at the carbon flux. Fig 9 :4 says the tail is several well-formed questions: *"are not one hard question but several well-formed ones. Split it rather than search."*
- **Domain 1, yield-signal frame.** Actors: crop, measured harvest, region slot, instrument. Currency: mass per area, a quantity. Anchor: the measured harvest, a counter that does not move when a frame re-describes it (Fig 2 :29). Question type: upstream link.
- **Domain 2, land-use decision frame.** Actors: farmer, land manager, price, tenure, policy, demand. Currency: profit or land rent, decided by a threshold comparison. Anchor: observed land-cover change, a counter; the market is a legitimate anchor only because the question is constitutively about a decision (Fig 2 :36). Question type: the trigger link and the expected break point.
- **Domain 3, biodiversity frame.** Actors: species, pollinators, predators, conditions, relations. Currency: abundance, habitat area, extinction risk. Anchor: species and habitat counts measured independently of the yield frame. Question type: downstream limb, cascade-bearing.
- **Domain 4, climate frame.** Actors: carbon stocks, fluxes, sinks, atmosphere, albedo. Currency: carbon flux, a directed difference. Anchor: atmospheric concentration and forcing, which do not move when a frame reports them. Question type: downstream limb, gradient-bearing.
- **Domain 5, coupling frame.** Actors: all four plus trade and telecoupling. Currency: cross-scale flows. Anchor: each limb keeps its own. Question type: union, not partition.
- **Order by dependency.** Yield reading, then affordance terms, then objective, then plan, then contact change, then the two downstream limbs, then the union verdict (Fig 3 :20). The expected break is between vocabulary and objective, where no instrument usually exists (Fig 3 :27).

Strain test applied: Fig 11 relative valence and Fig 2 non-updating anchor are different objects, so no strain after re-census. The global-versus-per-slot reading of yield is resolved by treating the global index as a budget filter, not a witness. The world disagreement between land-sparing and land-expansion claims was returned to Domain 2 and converted a universal law into a conditional one. See `01_figure_grounded_audit.md` sections 6 and 7.

## VERDICT (from the figures)

Yes, the path is structurally possible, and the figures explain why it is systematically undercounted. Yield change registers per slot; it changes land cover only through a decision frame whose terms select the sparing or expanding regime; conversion is an introduction and a habitat substitution with asymmetric power; the biodiversity loss that matters is a cascade three steps out; and biodiversity and climate have different anchors and must be reported as two verdicts, never averaged. What is refused is the universal form of the claim, the idea that conversion is reversible subtraction, the averaged outcome, and any magnitude asserted without a ground reading. The magnitude is a declared `gap`, reported as unreached rather than as silence. Mode `specified` on the per-domain claims, `general` on the structural shape, ungrounded, candidate.

- **cost if wrong:** high and partly irreversible. If the trigger is denied and the path fires, conversion substitutes a habitat with asymmetric power (Fig 11 :61), and the cascade is discovered after the species are gone (Fig 11 :52). The cost is not only ecological: a verdict stated without a mode or a refuter spends the independence of the people who check it. Fig 2 :42 says *"Buy independence in proportion to the cost of being wrong."* The remedy is to lead with the shape and its six refuters and to buy independent frames (remote sensing, field ecology, local knowledge) before asserting a conclusion.
- **undoable:** only partially, and not by exclusion. Conversion cannot be subtracted; restoration is a further substitution into a habitat with actors that were not chosen, with two silent failures (what was not reproduced, what was unintentionally introduced). The carbon flux can be slowed and the sink rebuilt, but the released stock is not recalled on a human timescale. The claim itself is undoable: the shape can be withdrawn if any refuter fires, leaving a visible record of the failure.
- **reversed by:** a ground reading, not agreement. A per-slot instrument pairing yield change with land-cover change and decision variables would settle which regime applies; a cascade enumeration to second and third order would test F4; an independent flux measurement would test F7. A figure re-census that showed conversion is a reversible subtraction, that the biodiversity effect is strictly first order, that the two outcomes share one anchor, or that a single lever clears the intersection with no displaced burden would break the shape. Until one of these fires, the verdict stands as `candidate`. Agreement among land-use models drawing on the same assessment literature is one witness, not two (Fig 2 :40).
