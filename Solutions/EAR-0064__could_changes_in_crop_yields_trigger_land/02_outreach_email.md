**Subject:** One checkable claim about yield change, land conversion, and two different grounds

> Hi [name],
>
> I have one specific structural claim I would like you to check against what you already know. I am not asking you to read the framework first, and I am not asking you to agree with it. If the claim does not survive, there is nothing else to look at.
>
> **The output (`specified`, not `grounded`):** the chain from a change in crop yield to worsened biodiversity loss and climate change is not one causal law. It is a four-link dependency chain with a union tail.
>
> 1. The yield signal registers per place and per crop, not as a global average. A rising world yield total can hide a live local failure.
> 2. Yield change does not convert land by itself. It enters a decision frame (price, tenure, policy, demand) that selects the regime. Under one regime land is spared; under another, production shifts and land is converted. Both are real; which one applies is a measurement question.
> 3. Conversion is an introduction and a substitution of habitat, not a reversible subtraction. You can add agriculture to a place; you cannot subtract the habitat it replaced.
> 4. The biodiversity loss that matters is typically a second- or third-order cascade, not the first-order effect at the field edge. The climate limb is a carbon stock spent as a flux. Biodiversity and climate are settled by different anchors, so they need two verdicts, not an average.
>
> This is a prediction of a **shape**, not a measurement. It returns no number of hectares, species, or parts per million.
>
> **What I am not claiming:** that higher yields always cause conversion, that intensification is always bad, that any particular crop or actor is good or bad in itself, or that one lever can fix both outcomes. Valence is relative to the residual. A high-yield cultivar can be a benefactor to a food residual and a malfactor to a biodiversity residual at the same time.
>
> **What would refute the shape claim:**
> 1. land conversion shown to be a reversible subtraction, with exclusion available;
> 2. a biodiversity effect shown to be strictly first order, with no material cascade;
> 3. yield and conversion shown to be one frame reading one evidence pool, so the trigger is correlation only;
> 4. a per-region measurement showing yield change did not change land-cover contact where it matters;
> 5. biodiversity and climate shown to share one anchor, making the outcomes a partition;
> 6. one lever improving yield, biodiversity and climate at the same place with no burden shifted elsewhere.
>
> I wrote these down before sending this. If any one holds, the shape is wrong and I will retract it. A one-line "this fails because..." is more useful to me than agreement. If you would rather not engage, no reply is needed.
>
> **If it survives, and only then:** the sources are below. The figures are the authority, not my summary of them.
> - Local verbatim figure text with line numbers: `FRAMEWORK/reference/03-FIGURES_TEXT.md`
> - Canonical usage rule: `FRAMEWORK/reference/FIGURES_README_canonical.md`
> - The eight-step loop and mode rule: `FRAMEWORK/figures/THE_FORMULA.md`
