# Isomers, order, and analogues in the land-conversion chain

**Mode: `specified` on the structural claims, `general` on the isomer reading. Not `grounded`.** This is a simulated derivation checked against the figures, not a ground reading (see `05_what_shape_means.md` section 7).

## RESIDUAL

The chain in EAR-0064 has an order (which link consumes which), a boundary question (union or partition), and two candidate regimes (land sparing and land expansion after an efficiency gain) that look alike in aggregate data. Three questions are owed:

1. What is the dependency order, and where is the break likely to sit?
2. Are the two regimes a partition, a union, or something else over a fixed atom set?
3. Which of Fig 12's four structures (isomer, catalyst, cycle, functional group) actually applies, and with what falsifier?

## DOMAIN CENSUS

Atoms held: a yield reading, a land-contact change, a carbon stock, a species population, a price, a decision rule, trade. Bonds held: sequence (→), production (⇒), threshold comparison (⋛), subtraction (−), conjunction (+), negation (¬, unary). No new primitive is available without import, and none is needed. Scale runs from slot to biome to planet, so the same bond reads differently at each level.

## DERIVATION

### Q1  -  Dependency order and the break

Fig 3 :20: *"The chain is ordered by dependency, not by importance. Each link consumes what the one above produces, so effort spent above the break arrives at a step"*. Fig 3 :24-26 says the middle link, the one between having terms for what a situation affords and being able to say what counts as progress, has neither obvious failures nor obvious tests, so it attracts the least attention and the fewest instruments.

The chain parses as:

```
yield reading → affordance terms (what this land can do) → objective (profit, food security, target) → plan → contact change → cascade / carbon flux → two verdicts
```

The measured links are the first (harvest statistics) and the fifth and sixth (land-cover and flux measurement). The second and third links are the unmeasured middle. Fig 3 :27: *"A link with no instrument is not a link that works. It is a link nobody has looked at."*

**Falsifier:** show that the middle link has a working instrument with independent ground contact in the relevant datasets, and that it predicts conversion across slots. Then the break moves elsewhere and this reading is withdrawn.

### Q2  -  Union or partition

Fig 9 :4: *"are not one hard question but several well-formed ones. Split it rather than search."* Fig 9 :55: *"A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones."*

Biodiversity and climate are not opposite answers to one question and not two readings of one quantity. They have different anchors. Conversion is the shared condition. So the tail is a union with an intersection, and a lever that clears one region leaves the intersection standing (Fig 9 :57). The practical consequence: a land-sparing policy that reduces conversion pressure in one landscape does not thereby clear the climate limb if production is displaced to another.

**Falsifier:** demonstrate a single measurement that settles both limbs, or show that a lever removes the intersection without displacement.

### Q3  -  Are the two regimes isomers?

Fig 12 :117: *"isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."*

Over a fixed atom set {yield reading, land contact, price, decision rule, trade}, the land-sparing regime and the land-expanding regime hold the same atoms. They differ in the bond between the yield reading and the decision objective:

- **Land sparing:** yield improvement composes with an objective that holds land area fixed. The bond is a threshold comparison ⋛ against an area constraint. The substance is "same output, less contact."
- **Land expansion:** yield improvement composes with an objective that holds profit margin fixed. The bond is ⋛ against a profitability constraint. The substance is "more output, more contact."

Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in a national production statistic, which is exactly why F8's two opened sources can disagree while both being right in their own regime. Fig 12 :94: *"the arrangement is the substance"*; the arrangement is the bond, not the ingredient list.

**Falsifier, written before the run:** if one regime requires an atom the other does not (for example, a functioning land market in one and its absence in the other), the isomer claim fails. The pair is then a union of two different atom sets, and the honest comparison becomes composition plus import rather than isomerism.

### Q4  -  Catalyst, cycle, functional group

Fig 12 :119 gives the catalyst: *"A part that survives its own use. Spent and regenerating parts behave differently across uses, and nothing records which a term is."* In this chain, a pollinator population and a soil stock are catalysts for yield: they survive their use until they do not, and a yield statistic does not record which state they are in. That is why a yield reading cannot comment on the source (§4 of `05`).

Fig 12 :121 gives the cycle: *"The output re-enters as input. The sign of the loop decides whether it closes, and a chain cannot close by construction."* Carbon and water fluxes are cycles; yield intensification that extracts a stock faster than the cycle closes is a positive loop that does not close on a human timescale. A chain model of "yield → land → output" cannot represent this, so a chain-only answer will undercount the climate limb.

Fig 12 :123 gives the functional group: *"A substructure whose presence fixes the outcome. The group says what kind; the qualifier says which member."* "An asymmetric contact change with a cascade" is a functional group; the particular crop, frontier or policy is the qualifier. This is why no standing list of good actors survives a change of residual (Fig 11 :51): the group says what kind of thing to look for, and the residual decides which member is a benefactor.

**Falsifier:** show that the loop sign does not matter for the climate verdict (a stock extracted at any rate closes on the relevant timescale), or that the pollinator and soil parts do not survive use. Then Q4's analogues are withdrawn without touching Q1 to Q3.

### Q5  -  What order is the union?

Fig 12 :104: *"A settled molecule becomes an atom for whatever composes over it. So irreducible is a fact about a level and never about the world."* A per-slot yield reading and a per-slot land-cover reading are each settled by instruments. They can become atoms at the landscape level, where the decision rule composes over them. The carbon and species readings become atoms at the planetary level. The union verdict is therefore third order: first the slot readings, then the landscape decision, then the two planetary limbs. This is recursion, not import, and it needs no new primitive.

**Falsifier:** show that a settled slot reading cannot serve as an atom at the landscape level, or that the landscape decision cannot serve as an atom at the planetary level. That would break the recursion and require a new primitive, which the figures do not supply.

## CONCLUSION

The chain is dependency-ordered with the break expected at the affordance-to-objective link (Q1). The tail is a union of two conditions with different anchors (Q2). Land sparing and land expansion after an efficiency gain are isomers over a fixed atom set, differing in which threshold the yield improvement is compared against (Q3), which explains how two literatures can disagree without either being wrong. Pollinators and soils are catalysts, carbon and water are cycles whose sign decides closure, and "asymmetric contact change with a cascade" is a functional group whose members are selected by the residual (Q4). The union verdict is third order and recursive, needing no new primitive (Q5). Every claim carries a falsifier. The magnitudes still belong to the ground.
