# Union versus partition, dependency order, and the analogy check

**Mode: `specified`.** This is a simulated derivation checked against the figures, not a ground reading (see `05_what_shape_means.md` §6).

## RESIDUAL

The audit's F3 says skill and bargaining form a union. Two questions are owed: (1) is that union a compound with its own loop, or a list of two separate problems, and (2) what is the dependency order, so that an education lever is not spent on a link that cannot use it? A third question is the analogy check: is calling this a "union" a figure-derived structure or a borrowed word?

## DOMAIN CENSUS (restated briefly)

Atoms held: the worker (skill, credential), the habitat (jobs, bargaining power), the measurement (per-slot wage residual). Bonds available: ordered sequence `→`, symmetric conjunction `+`, comparison `⋛`. No new primitive is available without an import. The question is a union of two conditions, so D4 (the lever frame) is an overlap with its own loop, not a fourth independent domain.

## DERIVATION

### Q1: Union or partition?

- Fig 9 :57–58: **"a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either. It stops being a mixture and becomes a specific compound."**
- Fig 9 :54: **"And when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again."**
- A worker who is low-skilled and in a weak-bargaining job satisfies both conditions at once. The intersection is non-empty by construction in any labour market with occupational closure, which the Credentialism source documents as *"occupational closure"* and Weeden (2002) studies as social closure and earnings inequality.
- ⇒ The split is a **union**. The compound (D4) is its own domain with its own loop: training changes the skill condition, the habitat leaves the second condition binding, and the residue is a specific compound rather than a smaller mixture (Fig 9 :59).
- **Falsifier, written before the run:** if skill and bargaining status are independent with empty intersection in a population, the split is a partition and this union reading fails. A dataset with no overlap would falsify it.

### Q2: What order does the lever run in?

- Fig 3 :20: **"The chain is ordered by dependency, not by importance."**
- Fig 3 :19: **"Optimising a link that already works cannot rescue one that does not."**
- The chain for this question: perceive available jobs (1) → have terms for what jobs afford, that is skills and credentials (2) → be able to say what counts as progress, a wage goal (3) → plan a route to that job (4) → learn and carry the result (5).
- Education/training loads link 2 and link 5. The question's constraint (uneven jobs and bargaining power) loads link 3 and the habitat itself. If the break is at link 3 or in the habitat, more education at link 2 is effort spent above the break, which Fig 3 :20 says cannot arrive at a step whose input never comes.
- Fig 3 :27: **"A link with no instrument is not a link that works. It is a link nobody has looked at."** The bargaining link is often the uninstrumented one in education-only studies. That is not evidence it works.
- ⇒ The education lever is **upstream-valid but not sufficient**. It is correctly applied to link 2; it cannot repair link 3 or the habitat.
- **Falsifier:** an experiment in which training alone restores wage progression for workers in weak-bargaining jobs, with the job structure unchanged. That would mean the break was at link 2 after all.

### Q3: Is this an isomer or a functional group?

- Fig 12 :117: **"isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."**
- Fig 12 :124: **"functional group. A substructure whose presence fixes the outcome. The group says what kind; the qualifier says which member."**
- Two contrasting policies over the same atom set `{worker skill, job structure, bargaining power, wage residual}`:
  - Policy A (train the worker): changes the worker-skill atom while leaving the habitat atoms fixed.
  - Policy B (change bargaining coverage): changes the habitat atoms while leaving the worker-skill atom fixed.
- As written, A and B do **not** share an identical atom set with different bonds only, because each changes which atoms are active. By Fig 12's definition they are **not isomers**; they are two members of the same **functional group** (a lever acting on one condition of a union), with different qualifiers.
- The stronger isomer form: if both are written over the full shared condition set `{skill condition, bargaining condition, intersection}`, A and B are the same atoms arranged differently, so they are isomers in Fig 12's sense. Their outcomes differ decisively even though a flat formula listing the conditions cannot tell them apart. That is the checkable structural claim worth keeping.
- **Falsifier:** if A and B can be reduced to the same intervention under a different name (the lever changes both conditions identically), the isomer claim fails and only the functional-group reading stands.
- **Analogy status, named:** this is an analogy carried by Fig 12's own definition of isomer and functional group, not a claim about chemistry. Fig 12 :46–47: **"A bond is the typed relation between two operands."** In this derivation the typed relation is the policy's point of application (worker side vs habitat side). The analogy is a structure, not a name for a new economic entity.

### Q4: What is enumerated, and what is composed?

- Fig 11 :43: **"The habitat is enumerated, never composed."**
- The habitat (which jobs exist, which occupations have closure, which bargaining regimes cover which workers) must be read off the world, not invented from the education frame. The world sources in `10_source_ledger.csv` do that enumeration: BLS for per-group earnings, Our World in Data for cross-country institutional variation, the union literature for bargaining coverage.
- The **lever** is composed: training is built from what the worker frame already has (skills, credentials, links 2 and 5). It cannot add a habitat atom. Fig 6 :21: **"MINT composes inside the closure and can never add an atom."**
- ⇒ The union's habitat side is an import with provenance; the skill side is composition. That asymmetry is the boundary the ground reading must locate.

## CONCLUSION

The two conditions form a union with a non-empty intersection, so a single lever clears the skill-only region and leaves the compound standing. The dependency order puts education at links 2 and 5, upstream-valid but unable to repair link 3 or the habitat. Over the full shared condition set the two policy forms are a real isomer claim with a stated falsifier; over the conditions each actually changes they are members of one functional group. The habitat is enumerated and imported with provenance, while the education lever is composed inside the worker frame. No new primitive is claimed, and the analogy is declared as an analogy.
