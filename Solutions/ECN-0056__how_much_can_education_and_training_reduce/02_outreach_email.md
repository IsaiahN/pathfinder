# Outreach draft: check the output before you decide about the framework

**Use:** one-to-one, or as a cover letter attached to a submission through the public venues in `03_public_contact_points.csv`. **Not** for bulk send.

**Hard precondition:** do not send the claim as a number. The stage-1 output below is a decomposition shape with a refuter. If you attach a headline percentage, the two-stage offer collapses on contact (Fig 1 :22, Fig 9 :55–56).

---

**Subject:** Check one output on education and wage inequality before you decide whether the theory is worth reading

> Hi [name],
>
> I have one specific output I would like you to check against what you already know. I am not asking you to read the framework first, and I am not asking you to agree with it. If the output does not survive, there is nothing else to look at.
>
> **The output (`specified`, not `grounded`):** wage inequality under uneven jobs and bargaining power is a union of two conditions, not one problem. One condition is skill and credentials, which education and training can act on. The other is the job and bargaining structure, which education cannot act on directly. The figures say that capability is a property of agent and habitat together, and that an improvement which does not change contact changes nothing, however much it improves. So the reducible part is the portion of the per-slot wage gap attributable to skill differences within a fixed job structure. Training closes that part only when it changes which job slots and bargaining regimes a worker can actually reach. If credentials inflate while the number of suitable jobs stays fixed, the skill part improves and the distribution does not flatten.
>
> **What I am not claiming:** that education cannot raise anyone's pay. Within a fixed habitat, more schooling is associated with higher earnings, and that is a per-slot reading. I am also not claiming a universal percentage for the effect of training on inequality. A single percentage averages away the live signal and treats a union compound as one problem. The boundary between the skill part and the habitat part stays open until a measurement settles it.
>
> **What would refute the shape claim:**
> 1. An education-only intervention that holds job and bargaining structure fixed drives the per-slot wage residual to zero in the low-bargaining slots.
> 2. A properly identified decomposition shows education explains none of the per-slot residual within a fixed habitat.
> 3. Education demonstrably changes which job slots workers reach, and the distribution still does not move.
> 4. Skill and bargaining position have empty intersection in the population of interest, so the two conditions form a partition rather than a union.
>
> I wrote these down before sending this. If any one holds, I will retract or re-census. A one-line "this fails because..." is more useful to me than agreement. If you would rather not engage, no reply is needed.
>
> **If it survives, and only then:** the sources are below. The figures are the authority, not my summary of them.
> - Verbatim figure text with line numbers (local extract): `FRAMEWORK/reference/03-FIGURES_TEXT.md`
> - Figures README, canonical usage rule (GitHub, branch `general-agent`): https://github.com/IsaiahN/tether/blob/general-agent/figures/README.md
> - Education pays, US Bureau of Labor Statistics: https://www.bls.gov/emp/chart-unemployment-earnings-education.htm
>
> **If you want to use the machinery:** the formula is in the figures link. Run it on a problem you know well and can check against a ground. Write down what would refute your result *before* you run it, and state the mode of what you get. A reported failure is more useful to me than a reported success.
>
> Isaiah
