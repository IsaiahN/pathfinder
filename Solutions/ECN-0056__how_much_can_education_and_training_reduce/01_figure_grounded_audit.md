## 0. The residual, in effect terms

The question asks for the size of a change: how much does an intervention (education and training) reduce an outcome (wage inequality), under a stated constraint (jobs and bargaining power are unevenly distributed)?

**R** = the gap, per slot, between the wage distribution that would obtain if skill differences were the only active condition and the wage distribution actually observed. The slots are workers, groups and occupation cells, not the population taken as one number.

- **Arity:** two conditions are in play. A skill condition (who has credentials and training) and a habitat condition (which jobs exist, who can set wages, who has occupational closure). Two conditions make a union compound, not one hard problem (Fig 9 :54).
- **Symmetry:** the two conditions are not interchangeable. Training acts on the worker; bargaining institutions act on the job. Their order matters, so the bond between them is ordered, not symmetric (Fig 12 sequence vs conjunction).
- **Scale:** first order inside a working life (one worker moving between slots), second order at the distribution (the shape of the whole wage array).
- **What an answer would look like:** a bounded decomposition into an education-sensitive part and a habitat-sensitive part, with a stated anchor for each and a stated refuter. A single percentage with no decomposition is not an answer to this residual, because it aggregates away the signal (Fig 1 :22).

**Direction bound before the run:** the sought-for shape is *a union decomposition with an open boundary*, written down here so it can accrue a residual like any other term (Formula "Direction"; Fig 5 :62–68).

Education mostly loads link 2 and link 5. The question's constraint (uneven jobs and bargaining) loads link 3 and the habitat. A link that already works cannot be optimised to rescue one that does not (Fig 3 :19).

## 3. Findings

### F1: Wage inequality is a property of agent and habitat, not of the agent alone (decisive)

Fig 11 :39:

> **"capability is a property of agent-and-habitat, never of the agent alone"**

Fig 11 :40:

> **"A system cannot be made more capable by improving the agent in isolation. An improvement that does not change contact changes nothing, however much it improves."**

Education and training improve the agent (D1). The wage distribution is a property of the agent in contact with a habitat of jobs and bargaining institutions (D2). Therefore an education lever reduces wage inequality **only insofar as it changes the worker's contact with that structure**: which job slots become reachable, which bargaining regimes the worker can enter. If training raises skill while the number of suitable jobs and the distribution of bargaining power stay fixed, the worker has improved in isolation and the distribution does not move. This is the credential-inflation mechanism recorded in the world literature (Wikipedia, "Credentialism and degree inflation": *"devaluation of educational or academic credentials over time ... due to an excess of higher educated people who compete for too few jobs that require these degrees"*).

- **Mode:** `general` for the structural rule, `specified` for the prediction that a fixed-habitat education boost leaves the distributional residual unmoved.
- **Verdict: `triangulated`.** Fig 11 (habitat/contact) and Fig 3 :19 (*"Optimising a link that already works cannot rescue one that does not."*) are two figures with low overlap: one is about contact, the other about dependency order. They converge without sharing a mechanism. World corroboration is secondary.
- **Refuter:** an education-only intervention that holds job and bargaining structure fixed and drives the per-slot wage residual to zero in the low-bargaining slots. If that fires, the habitat-side component is not binding and this finding is withdrawn.

### F2: The residual is indexed by slot, not aggregated

Fig 1 :19:

> **"The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated."**

Fig 1 :22:

> **"Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average."**

A single Gini coefficient or a single headline training effect is the aggregation the figure forbids. The live signal is which slots still carry positive residual after training: the trained worker in a weak-bargaining occupation, the untrained worker in a strong one. The BLS earnings tables are published by sex and education group for exactly this reason, and its "Education pays" page records an honest measurement limit: *"These education categories reflect only the highest level of educational attainment. They do not take into account completion of training programs in the form of apprenticeships and other on-the-job training, which may also influence earnings and unemployment rates."*

- **Mode:** `general` (measurement form).
- **Verdict: `single-source`** for the figure (Fig 1), with world corroboration from official measurement practice. I did not find a second figure line governing per-slot indexing, so I do not claim triangulation.
- **Refuter:** a demonstration that the change in a single aggregate inequality measure fully predicts the per-slot changes, so no slot-level signal is lost. Until then, aggregation is a lossy transform (Fig 4 :27–32).

### F3: The question is a union compound, so one lever clears only part of it

Fig 9 :54:

> **"And when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again."**

Fig 9 :55–56:

> **"A single lever clears the region where only one condition holds and leaves the intersections standing, because the second condition still binds."**

Fig 9 :59:

> **"not that the pile is smaller, but that the residue is finally describable."**

Skill and bargaining overlap. A worker who is both low-skilled and in a weak-bargaining job is not a harder case of either condition; it is a specific compound (Fig 9 :57–58). Education is the single lever. It clears the skill-only region and leaves the intersection standing because the second condition still binds. The Library of Babel essay, read 2026-11-23, names the same failure from the search side: *"Perhaps your problem was a bundle or conflation of several problems masquerading as one? You will need to search all of those problems individually to find their answers and then combine them in a satisfactory way."*

- **Mode:** `general` for the union reading, `specified` for the prediction that a training-only intervention leaves the intersection standing.
- **Verdict: `single-source`** for the figure (Fig 9), with low-overlap world corroboration from a search essay (different field, same structure).
- **Refuter:** show that skill and bargaining position are independent conditions with empty intersection in the population of interest, in which case the split is a partition and a single-lever answer becomes legitimate.

### F4: A magnitude needs a ground reading, not more agreement

Fig 2 :29:

> **"An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks. A measurement, an experiment, a counter, a market, a patient outcome."**

Fig 1 :23:

> **"The ground enters as the only metric."**

Fig 2 :40:

> **"disqualifying for a verdict: agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool"**

The anchor for this question is a measurement of the wage distribution with a credible counterfactual: an experiment or natural experiment that changes education while holding the job and bargaining structure fixed, or a decomposition whose identification does not rest on the same survey pool as the claim. Agreement among studies that reuse the same earnings surveys is one witness wearing many names (Fig 2 :31–32). Fig 2 :42: **"Buy independence in proportion to the cost of being wrong."**

- **Mode:** `general`.
- **Verdict: `triangulated`.** Fig 2 (anchor/independence) and Fig 1 (ground as only metric) are distinct figures with low overlap. No new figure line needed.
- **Refuter:** produce a `grounded` number that does not depend on a counterfactual or measurement, and show the figures permit it. Until then the output stays `specified`.

### F5: The honest output is a shape, not a number the frame can certify

Fig 11 :61:

> **"Introduction is available; exclusion is not."**

Fig 8 :39:

> **"So a search that finds nothing is never a proof of absence."**

The lever frame can add training (introduction) but cannot subtract the habitat (exclusion). From inside the education frame, the boundary between the two components is not certifiable. What the framework can return is a kind of thing: a union decomposition with an open boundary. It cannot certify the boundary itself. Fig 9 :32: *"you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself."* This is an honest stop, not a silence.

- **Mode:** `general`.
- **Verdict: `single-source`** for the figure pair (Fig 11 + Fig 8), corroborated by the canonical README's "a shape, not a name" limit.
- **Refuter:** derive the boundary from inside the education frame alone, with no habitat measurement and no import from labour economics.

## 4. Strain test and re-census

**Apparent strain.** The BLS "Education pays" material and the World Bank education page report that earnings rise with schooling (the World Bank page states *"Globally, there is a 9% increase in hourly earnings for every extra year of schooling"*). Fig 11 :40 says an agent-only improvement changes nothing. Read literally against individual earnings, these conflict.

**Re-census.** They govern different quantities. The BLS and World Bank numbers are per-slot readings: within a given habitat, a more educated worker lands in a higher slot. Fig 11 :40 governs the **shape of the distribution at the system level**, where everyone's credentials rise together and the job structure is fixed. If all agents improve in isolation and the slots do not change, the distribution does not flatten. Different quantities, so no strain after re-census. This matches the canonical rule that a reading which makes figures contradict one another has misidentified its domain.

**Second strain, named.** The habitat result (which jobs and bargaining regimes exist) is not derived from the six-term set; it is an import from labour economics. Fig 6 :23: *"No frame escapes its own closure. The bound moves only on contact with a frame whose closure differs."* The import is legitimate only with a source gate and shadow test (Fig 8 import gates), which is why every habitat claim in this folder carries a world source and an overlap rating in `10_source_ledger.csv`. It is an import, not a derivation from the figures.

**After re-census: two strains, both named, none hidden.**

## 5. Pre-registered refuter (written before the conclusion)

Required before any outreach, per the seat discipline: a falsifier written before the run is a design instrument. The shape claim is refuted if any of the following holds:

1. An education-only intervention, holding job and bargaining structure fixed, moves the per-slot wage residual to zero in the low-bargaining slots. Then the habitat-side component is not binding (kills F1 and F3).
2. A properly identified decomposition shows the skill-sensitive component is empty, that is education explains none of the per-slot residual within a fixed habitat (kills the education branch of F6).
3. A ground reading shows education changes contact with the job structure (not merely credentials) and yet the distribution does not move. That strains Fig 11 :40 and sends the census back to D2 (kills F1's mechanism).
4. Skill and bargaining conditions have empty intersection in the population of interest, making the split a partition rather than a union (kills F3).

If any fires, the shape is withdrawn or re-censused. Until one is run, no part of this may be labelled `grounded`.

## 6. Recommendation

1. Do not publish a single headline percentage. Publish the union decomposition with the open boundary (F2, F3).
2. Measure per slot, not per average (F2). State the education categories actually used; BLS's own caveat about omitted apprenticeships and on-the-job training is a live measurement limit.
3. Treat job and bargaining structure as an import with provenance, not as a figure-derived primitive (strain 2).
4. Ask checkers for a refutation of the four refuters, not for agreement (F4, Fig 2 :35).
5. Send the generator (the residual, the census and the refuter), not the conclusion (Fig 4 :22–25).
