# The Tether's answer on education, training and wage inequality

**Mode: `specified` for the decomposition shape, `general` for the structural rule. Not `grounded`.** This is a shape and a route, not a result. Simulated derivation checked against the figures (`05_what_shape_means.md` §6), not a ground reading. No measurement was taken.

## 1. The residual it answers

The published question asks for a magnitude. Restated in effect terms: what part of the per-slot wage gap disappears when skill and training change, given that jobs and bargaining power are unevenly distributed? The two conditions (skill, habitat) form a **union compound**, not one hard problem (Fig 9 :54, :57–58). A single percentage answers a different question, because it aggregates away the slot-level signal (Fig 1 :22).

## 2. The shape

1. **Wage inequality is agent-and-habitat, not agent alone.** Fig 11 :39: *"capability is a property of agent-and-habitat, never of the agent alone"*; Fig 11 :40: *"A system cannot be made more capable by improving the agent in isolation. An improvement that does not change contact changes nothing, however much it improves."* Education improves the worker. The wage distribution is a property of the worker in contact with jobs and bargaining power. Training reduces inequality only when it changes contact, meaning which job slots and bargaining regimes become reachable.
2. **The residual is per slot.** Fig 1 :19: *"The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated."* The live question is which slots still carry a gap after training, not whether one average moves.
3. **The two conditions form a union; a single lever leaves the intersection standing.** Fig 9 :55–56: *"A single lever clears the region where only one condition holds and leaves the intersections standing, because the second condition still binds."* Education clears the skill-only region. Workers who are both low-skilled and in weak-bargaining jobs remain a specific compound.
4. **The education lever is upstream-valid but not sufficient.** Fig 3 :19: *"Optimising a link that already works cannot rescue one that does not."* Education loads the vocabulary and carry links of the dependency chain; uneven jobs and bargaining load the objective link and the habitat itself.
5. **The boundary needs a ground reading.** Fig 2 :29: *"An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks. A measurement, an experiment, a counter, a market, a patient outcome."* Fig 1 :23: *"The ground enters as the only metric."* The framework returns a decomposition shape with an open boundary, not a certified number. Unreachable here is a verdict, not a silence.

## 3. What it explicitly does not say

- Not that education cannot raise individual earnings. Within a fixed habitat, more schooling is associated with higher per-slot earnings. That is a per-slot reading, not a distributional result.
- Not a universal percentage. No ground reading was taken, and the union structure makes one number the wrong output form.
- Not that bargaining institutions are figure-derived primitives. They are a provenanced import from labour economics (Fig 6 :23), with sources recorded in `10_source_ledger.csv`.
- Not that training is useless. It is the correct lever for the skill condition and for changing contact, provided suitable slots and bargaining regimes exist to be entered.
- Not `grounded`. Nothing here was settled by an experiment or a decomposition with a credible counterfactual.
- Not that agreement among researchers settles it. Fig 2 :35: *"Alignment is triangulation, not negotiation. A mirror cannot correct you."*

## 4. What would settle it: the ground

A source-discriminating instrument, not a further argument. *The Hard Problem is About Source*, read this session: an instrument that reads green *"cannot comment on the source"*; *"It was proven wrong not by argument, but by the arrival of a better instrument."* Here the corresponding readings are:

- **Per-slot effect test:** an education-only intervention that holds the job and bargaining structure fixed. Does the per-slot wage residual in the low-bargaining slots fall, stay flat, or rise (credential inflation)?
- **Contact test:** does training change which job slots and bargaining regimes workers reach, or only their credentials within the same queue? This is the discriminator between F1's mechanism and its falsifier.
- **Intersection test:** does a compound intervention (training plus bargaining coverage) move the slot residual that training alone left standing? If it does, the union reading is confirmed at the ground.
- **Aggregation test:** does a single inequality measure hide slot-level movement in opposite directions (Fig 1 :22)? If it does, the headline percentage is demonstrably lossy.

**Measurement caution:** BLS's own "Education pays" page warns that its categories *"do not take into account completion of training programs in the form of apprenticeships and other on-the-job training, which may also influence earnings and unemployment rates."* Any ground reading must state which training it actually measures.

## 5. The standing falsifiers (pre-registered in `01_figure_grounded_audit.md` §5)

1. Education-only with fixed institutions drives the per-slot residual to zero in low-bargaining slots.
2. A credible decomposition shows the skill-sensitive component is empty.
3. Education changes contact and the distribution still does not move.
4. Skill and bargaining position have empty intersection, making the split a partition.

If any fires, the shape is withdrawn or re-censused. Their being written here after the census but before any conclusion is recorded in the audit's retraction log and mirrored in the expert packet.

## 6. The honest stop

**Unreached, not unreachable.** The labour-institutions frame is an import, not a derivation from the education frame. Fig 8 :39: *"So a search that finds nothing is never a proof of absence."* The answer stops where the available instruments cannot discriminate skill dispersion from bargaining dispersion in one aggregate reading. It records the lineage so the stop is checkable. *"Indiscernibility is not identity."*

## 7. One-paragraph version for outreach

> The Tether does not give a universal percentage for how much education reduces wage inequality, because the question is a union of two conditions rather than one problem. Wage inequality is a property of workers in contact with a habitat of jobs and bargaining power, so training reduces the distributional gap only when it changes which job slots and bargaining regimes workers can reach. An agent-only improvement that leaves that contact unchanged can raise individual earnings while leaving the distribution's shape intact. The framework returns a decomposition shape: an education-sensitive per-slot component, a habitat-sensitive component, and a compound intersection that a single lever clears only partly. It refuses to certify the boundary between those parts from inside the education frame. What would close it is a measurement with a counterfactual, not more agreement. Four falsifiers are written in advance, and the center stays open until the ground settles it.
