# Expert verification packet: the Tether on education, training and wage inequality

**What this is:** a claim in a form a researcher in education and inequality can check. Mode, derivation and refuters are stated before the conclusion, so a reader knows what to look for and what would settle it.

**Mode: `specified` for the decomposition shape, `general` for the structural rule. Not `grounded`.** No per-slot measurement with a counterfactual is claimed. This is a shape and a route, not a result.

## 1. The claim, in one paragraph

Wage inequality under uneven jobs and bargaining power is a union of two conditions, not one problem. One condition is skill and credentials, which education and training can act on. The other is the job and bargaining structure, which training cannot act on directly. Because capability is a property of agent and habitat together, an agent-only improvement that does not change contact leaves the distributional residual unmoved. The reducible part is therefore the portion of the per-slot wage gap attributable to skill differences within a fixed job structure, and training closes that part only when it changes which job slots and bargaining regimes workers actually reach. The boundary between the skill part and the habitat part is left open until a measurement with a counterfactual closes it.

## 2. Three checkable sub-claims

### C1: framework-internal plus economics-facing; checkable by reading Fig 11 and by your own knowledge of labour markets

- **Statement:** capability is agent-and-habitat; an improvement that does not change contact changes nothing at the distributional level. An education-only boost with a fixed job structure can raise individual earnings while leaving the shape of the wage distribution intact.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 11 :39: *"capability is a property of agent-and-habitat, never of the agent alone"*. Fig 11 :40: *"A system cannot be made more capable by improving the agent in isolation. An improvement that does not change contact changes nothing, however much it improves."* Triangulated with Fig 3 :19: *"Optimising a link that already works cannot rescue one that does not."* (low overlap: contact versus dependency order).
- **What to check:** does universal credential upgrading with a fixed occupational structure flatten the wage distribution, or just move everyone up within it? Does the world evidence on credential inflation support the contact mechanism? The Credentialism source states *"devaluation of educational or academic credentials over time ... due to an excess of higher educated people who compete for too few jobs that require these degrees"* (Wikipedia, read 2026-11-23).
- **Refuter:** an education-only intervention holding job and bargaining structure fixed drives the per-slot residual to zero in the low-bargaining slots. Or education demonstrably changes contact and the distribution still does not move.

### C2: framework-internal; checkable by reading Figs 1 and 9

- **Statement:** the residual is per slot, and the two conditions form a union, so a single lever clears the skill-only region and leaves the compound intersection standing.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 1 :19: *"The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated."* Fig 1 :22: *"Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average."* Fig 9 :55–56: *"A single lever clears the region where only one condition holds and leaves the intersections standing, because the second condition still binds."* Fig 9 :57–58: *"a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either. It stops being a mixture and becomes a specific compound."*
- **What to check:** in your data, is the intersection of low skill and weak bargaining non-empty and categorically different from either condition alone? Does one headline inequality measure hide slot-level movement in opposite directions? BLS's published earnings tables are per group for exactly this reason.
- **Refuter:** skill and bargaining status are independent with empty intersection in the population of interest (then the split is a partition, not a union); or a single aggregate measure fully predicts all per-slot changes.

## 3. What is explicitly not claimed

- Not a universal percentage for the effect of education on wage inequality. The shape is a decomposition with an open boundary.
- Not that education fails to raise individual earnings. Per-slot gains within a fixed habitat are expected and are documented by BLS and the World Bank.
- Not that bargaining institutions are primitives of the framework. They are an import with provenance (C3).
- Not that training is useless, and not that it is sufficient. It is the correct lever for the skill condition.
- Not `grounded`. No measurement with a counterfactual was taken in this derivation.
- Not a claim that agreement among researchers settles anything. Fig 2 :35: *"Alignment is triangulation, not negotiation. A mirror cannot correct you."*

## 5. What would settle it

A source-discriminating instrument, not a further argument. *The Hard Problem is About Source*: an instrument that reads green *"cannot comment on the source"*; *"It was proven wrong not by argument, but by the arrival of a better instrument."*

1. **Per-slot effect test:** education-only with fixed institutions. Does the low-bargaining slot residual fall, stay flat, or rise?
2. **Contact test:** does training change which job slots and bargaining regimes are reachable, or only credentials within the same queue?
3. **Intersection test:** does a compound intervention (training plus bargaining coverage) move the slot residual that training alone left standing?
4. **Aggregation test:** does one headline measure hide slot-level movement in opposite directions?

Measurement caution: BLS states its education categories *"do not take into account completion of training programs in the form of apprenticeships and other on-the-job training, which may also influence earnings and unemployment rates."* State which training is being measured.
