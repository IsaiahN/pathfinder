**Subject:** Check one shape against what you already know about tipping cascades

> Hi [name],
>
> I have one specific output I would like you to check against what you already know. I am not asking you to read the framework first, and I am not asking you to agree with it. If the output does not survive, there is nothing else to look at.
>
> **The output (`specified` on the shape, `general` on the skeleton, not `grounded`):** when several tipping points interact, they do not add hazard rates. Figure 9's union rule says a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either: it is categorically different, and a lever that clears the region where only one condition holds leaves the intersections standing, because the second condition still binds. A tipping point is itself a threshold comparison, an ordered relation, not a conjunction. Coupling is a bond between those comparisons. When the influence of one crossing feeds back on another, the arrangement becomes a cycle, and Figure 12 says the sign of the loop decides whether it closes. A chain cannot close by construction. So the likelihood of systemic change depends on the sign and arrangement of the couplings, not on the count of thresholds.
>
> **The consequence half is separate.** Figure 1 says the residual is indexed by object slot and that aggregating across slots throws away the only signal that says where to look. A global average near zero with one live slot is a legal state. Figure 11's habitat rule says the residual that matters can be three steps out, the way DDT reached the eagles. And crossing removes standing to compose over the prior regime, so recovery is a new lineage rather than the old one reversed. Consequences are a per-element vector with third-order effects, not a single magnitude.
>
> **What the framework does not give you:** a universal likelihood multiplier, a critical coupling strength, or the name of the missing quantity in a named system. Those are recorded as a `gap`, not filled in. The same roster of elements can appear as a reinforcing cascade, a buffer, a domino chain or a cluster depending on the bond; Figure 12 calls that isomerism, same atoms and different substance. Which one dominates in your system is a ground question, and I am not claiming the framework names it.
>
> **What would refute the shape:**
> 1. a universal multiplier or cascade magnitude derived from the figure set alone, with no import and no measurement;
> 2. a named domain where the threshold conditions form a disjoint partition, so clearing one clears the whole;
> 3. couplings that are provably acyclic, or a sign fixed by the count rather than by the bond;
> 4. consequences that are additive across slots with no live-slot asymmetry, where the average is enough to direct intervention;
> 5. a figure line that names which coupling topology wins in a named system;
> 6. a measurement where removing the driver restores exactly the prior composition with no new term.
>
> I wrote these down before sending this. If any one holds, I will retract or narrow the claim. A one-line "this fails because..." is more useful to me than agreement. If you would rather not engage, no reply is needed.
