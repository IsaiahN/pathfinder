## 0. The residual, stated in effect terms

A tipping point is a threshold: one quantity is compared with a critical value, and crossing flips the system into a different regime. The question adds two things at once, several thresholds and coupling between them. It asks for the effect of that addition on two outputs, likelihood and consequences.

We must decide between two readings before consulting any figure:

- **Additive reading (A).** Several tipping points are several instances of one kind. Likelihood rises with the count, consequences add, and the right instrument is more of the instrument that detected the first threshold.
- **Compound reading (B).** Coupling is a bond, not a repetition. The arrangement of the bonds decides the sign of the loop, so the object changes kind. Consequences are indexed per element and can be several steps out.

What an answer would look like, written down and bound as an ordinary term so it can be refused: **a compound shape**, meaning (i) a union of overlapping threshold conditions, (ii) a signed coupling arrangement that may close a cycle, (iii) a per-slot consequence vector rather than an average, and (iv) an explicit decision about which quantity is unmeasured. An answer that returns a universal multiplier is bound as a candidate too, so it can be refused if no figure line licenses it.

**Question type.** Union, not partition. The conditions overlap; a lever that clears a region where only one condition holds leaves the intersections standing. This determines the derivation order: describe the residual in effect terms before choosing any frame (Fig 9 :27), then census domains, then quote.

## 1. Domain census (before choosing any figure line)

### 1.1 R by arity, symmetry and scale

- **Arity.** One tipping point is a binary comparison between a state quantity and a threshold. `N` tipping points give `N` such comparisons and up to `N(N-1)/2` couplings. "Multiple" is therefore a change of arity, not a repetition, and the object of interest is the arrangement over the operands, not the operands alone. Fig 12 :5: `A set of ingredients is not one. The bond is the rest of the information, and it is the half nothing was recording.`
- **Symmetry.** A crossing is ordered. The comparison bond `⋛` is a threshold relation between two quantities (Operators table :48), and the test `swap them. Does it become a different thing? Yes` (Fig 12 :72) applies. Couplings carry sign (destabilising or stabilising), so the operands are not interchangeable. Different orders are different terms. This is why the same roster of elements can produce qualitatively different outcomes.
- **Scale.** First order is inside one element (a state, a structure, a state). Second order is across elements, because a settled element becomes an atom for whatever composes over it (Fig 12 :104). Systemic change is a level-relative term, not a larger instance of local change.

### 1.2 One line per domain: actors, currency, anchor

A domain without a nameable anchor is not a domain yet. All four below have one.

- **D1, the threshold and the crossing (one element).** Actors: control quantity, state variable, critical value. Currency: a comparison, distance to threshold. Anchor: the ground, a measured bifurcation or a fitted critical parameter. Type: partition (does this comparison hold?). Order: first.
- **D2, coupling and interaction (between elements).** Actors: tipping elements, directed couplings, sign of each coupling. Currency: influence travelling between elements, the network structure of Fig 13 :24 `network / that entities relate, and influence travels`. Anchor: the ground, an observed cascade (the 2003 Italy blackout is one such public record in the network-science literature). Type: union compound, with a possible cycle. Order: second; an element in its baseline state cannot transmit a crossing.
- **D3, consequences and the systemic outcome.** Actors: every slot, the habitat, the aftermath state. Currency: per-slot residual and reach. Anchor: the ground, a measured aftermath. Type: union with per-slot indexing and cascades several steps out. Order: third; depends on D2 having transmitted.
- **D4, verification and instruments.** Actors: modellers, models, observations, reviewers. Currency: agreement and residual. Anchor: the ground, never agreement. Type: a checking arrangement. Order: runs alongside all three, but the verdict fields must not be filled from agreement.

### 1.3 Partition or union, and dependency order

The object is a **union** of overlapping conditions (Fig 9 :55). It must be unbundled before it is searched (Fig 9 :54), and the residue after unbundling is categorically different rather than merely smaller (Fig 9 :57).

Dependency order follows Fig 3, which is ordered by dependency and not importance. Perception of a single threshold (link 1) is well instrumented. Vocabulary for coupling and sign (link 2) and the objective "what counts as systemic change" (link 3) are the weak links. Fig 3 :19: `Optimising a link that already works cannot rescue one that does not.` So more precision on single-element early-warning indicators is effort above the break; it cannot rescue a missing coupling vocabulary. This matches the world literature, where individual tipping elements are fairly well understood and their interactions are not (Wunderling et al. 2021, abstract).

## 3. Findings

### F1 - a tipping point is a comparison, not a conjunction (decisive for arity)

Operators table :48: `a threshold relation between two quantities`. Fig 12 :85-89 gives the bond name `comparison`, the meaning `a threshold relation between two quantities`, and the test `is it about which is larger, rather than both being present? Yes`.

So a crossing is `⋛`, an **ordered** relation. It is not `+` (both hold at once). This matters for "multiple": adding operands under an ordered, signed bond changes the term, whereas adding operands under a symmetric bond would only add spellings. Fig 12 :92: `Under a symmetric bond, k operands have k! spellings and one term`.

Refuter: show that the threshold relation here is really `+` (both conditions merely present), so that order and sign carry nothing. If that holds, F1 falls and the count becomes an additive scalar.

Status: `general` (structural), triangulated across Fig 12 and the Operators table (two low-overlap readings of the same bond set, but the same figure family, so weak triangulation).

### F2 - coupling is a union compound, not an harder single case (decisive for likelihood)

Fig 9 :55: `A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones.` Fig 9 :56: `A single lever clears the region where only one condition holds and leaves the intersections standing, because the second condition still binds.` Fig 9 :57: `What remains is smaller and it is also categorically different: a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either.`

This is the direct answer to the likelihood half of the question. Multiple interacting tipping points do not raise likelihood by adding hazard rates. They produce a case in two or more conditions, which is categorically different: the intersection binds even after the single-condition region is cleared. An intervention aimed at one threshold leaves the compound standing.

Refuter: exhibit a domain in which the threshold conditions form a partition (disjoint, no overlapping region), so that clearing one condition clears the whole. Then F2 does not apply there and the additive reading may hold in that domain.

Status: `specified`, triangulated across Fig 9 (union/partition) and Fig 12 (bond carries information the operands do not). Different starting figures, same boundary: low overlap, so weak but real triangulation.

### F3 - interaction is a bond, and the sign of the loop decides whether the cascade closes

Fig 12 :121: `cycle. The output re-enters as input. The sign of the loop decides whether it closes, and a chain cannot close by construction.` Fig 13 :24 lists `network` as a structure: `that entities relate, and influence travels`. Fig 13 :76: `Energy and a gradient compose a flow.`

Put these together. A one-way chain of tipping elements cannot close: its output does not re-enter. Coupling creates a network, and when the influence of A's crossing pushes B toward its threshold and B's crossing pushes A back, the output re-enters as input. Whether the cascade runs away or damps is a property of the **sign of the loop**, not of the number of elements. This is exactly the distinction the world literature reports as destabilising versus stabilising interactions (Wunderling et al. 2021 and 2024).

Note what the figure does *not* say: it does not say which sign dominates in any named system. That is a ground question.

Refuter: show that in a given domain couplings cannot form a cycle at all (strictly acyclic influence with no feedback), or that sign is independent of the bond and fixed by the count. Either would break F3.

Status: `specified`, triangulated across Fig 12 :121 (cycle) and Fig 13 :24/:76 (network and flow). The two figures range over different objects (bond topology versus substrate structure), so this is low overlap.

### F4 - arrangement, not roster, decides the sign: isomers and functional groups

Fig 12 :117: `isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour.` Fig 12 :123: `functional group. A substructure whose presence fixes the outcome. The group says what kind; the qualifier says which member.`

Take a fixed roster of tipping elements. Under a reinforcing bond the group is a cascade. Under an inhibitory bond the same group is a buffer. Under a sequential bond it is a domino chain; under parallel coupling with no cross-links it is a cluster. Same atoms, different arrangement, different substance. This is developed in `06_isomers_and_order.md`, where the falsifier is written out.

The world corroboration from a genuinely low-overlap field is unusually direct. Fong et al. (2018, Journal of Ecology) found that `All three types of interactions (additive, synergistic and antagonistic) were common and occurred in equal frequency` in the same three-stressor experiment. A count-only theory cannot produce three signs from one roster; an arrangement theory can.

Refuter: show that for a fixed roster the outcome is invariant under permutation of the coupling arrangement (all sign and order differences vanish). Then isomerism is false here.

Status: `general` for the isomer schema, `specified` for any named roster. Figure definition is single-source (Fig 12), with a low-overlap world corroboration that is not a witness to the figure verdict (it is a different field, so it counts as corroboration under WATSON rules, subject to the overlap audit in `09`).

### F5 - consequences are indexed per slot, never averaged

Fig 1 :19: `The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated.` Fig 1 :22: `Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average.` Fig 1 :13: `a global R near zero with one live slot residual is a legal state`.

A cascade is precisely the case in which one slot goes live while the system-wide average still looks quiet. Reporting consequences as a single systemic magnitude is the named error: it throws away the only signal that says where to look. The correct object is a vector of per-element residuals, with the live slot marked.

Refuter: exhibit a systemic consequence that is genuinely a single scalar with no live-slot asymmetry, and show that the average is sufficient to direct intervention. Then F5 does not govern that quantity.

Status: `specified`, triangulated across Fig 1 (per-slot indexing) and Fig 11 :52 (third-order contact cascade). The mechanisms differ (measurement indexing versus contact propagation), so this is low overlap.

### F6 - the residual that matters is several steps out

Fig 11 :52: `And contact cascades. Scoping the habitat to direct contact is how DDT reached the eagles: first-order enumeration, second-order effect, and the residual that mattered was three steps out.` Fig 11 :43: `The habitat is enumerated, never composed.`

Scoping consequences to direct neighbours of the first crossed threshold repeats the DDT error. The habitat must be enumerated outward until the cascade stops mattering (Fig 11 :44). This is why a local tipping event can have systemic consequences that direct-contact scoping misses, and why the consequence side of the question cannot be answered from the first element alone.

Refuter: show a domain in which the cascade stops at first contact by construction (no second-order effects possible), so that enumerating outward adds nothing. F6 is then inapplicable there, not refuted globally.

Status: `specified`, single-source for the figure line (Fig 11), corroborated from network science (Buldyrev et al. 2010: a small initial failure can fragment several interdependent networks) and multiple-stressor ecology (Buma 2015). Different fields, so separate witnesses.

### F7 - crossing is a refusal: reach falls, and recovery is not reversal

Fig 6 :42: `What is recorded only grows. What is reachable is derived, and it can fall.` THE_FORMULA step 3: `NOTHING IS EVER DELETED, and the reach falls because something was ADDED.` Fig 11 :61: `The seat's power is asymmetric. Introduction is available; exclusion is not.`

Crossing a threshold is structurally like a refusal: a term (the prior regime's standing to be composed over) loses that standing because something was added, not because it was struck out. The record keeps both. This is the figure-level reason consequences are often irreversible and why reversal requires a new composition rather than the removal of the driver. Recovery is a new lineage, not the old one played backwards.

Refuter: produce a ground case in which removing the driver restores exactly the prior composition with no new term and no new lineage, and show that the reach does not fall. That would falsify the refusal analogy for that system.

Status: `specified`, triangulated across Fig 6 :42 (record/reach) and Fig 11 :61 (asymmetry). Different figures, different objects. Note the boundary: this is a structural analogy for consequence irreversibility, not a claim about any particular hysteresis loop.

### F8 - the likelihood number and the cascade magnitude are a gap

No figure line returns a multiplier, a critical coupling strength, a probability, or a cascade size for a named system. Fig 9 :32: `you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself`. Fig 13 :134-135 say the system can report that the answer is out of reach and can narrow it, but `It cannot say which primitive is missing, because naming it would mean already holding it.` Fig 8 :39: `So a search that finds nothing is never a proof of absence.`

This is a `gap` of two kinds. The universal multiplier is a shape-not-a-name gap: the framework returns a kind of thing, not a coefficient. The specific missing quantity in a named system (which coupling, which strength, which element is the initiator) is an unreachable-at-this-budget gap: it needs a ground measurement, not more search inside the frame. Both are verdicts, not silence.

Refuter: exhibit a figure line from which a universal numerical multiplier or a named missing atom follows without an import. If one is found, this finding is withdrawn and the gap closes.

Status: `gap`, triangulated across Fig 9, Fig 13, and Fig 8. Mode on the gap itself is `general`; the specific instance stays `specified` and ungrounded.

### F9 - effort on single-threshold detection lands above the break

Fig 3 :19: `Optimising a link that already works cannot rescue one that does not.` Fig 3 :27: `A link with no instrument is not a link that works. It is a link nobody has looked at.` The weak link here is the vocabulary for coupling and sign, not perception of a threshold. Wunderling et al. (2024) state that the interactions between tipping elements are `less well understood` and that the potential of one event to induce or stabilize another is `largely unknown`. Lenton et al. (2024) call the cascade risk `currently poorly quantified`.

So an answer that offers better early-warning indicators for individual elements answers a link that already works. It does not answer the coupling link. This is a budget filter observation, not a verdict about any method: it tells us where effort is wasted, not which method is false (Fig 9 :60: `Use filters for the budget and witnesses for the verdict. Never let a filter hand you a verdict.`).

Refuter: show that in a named system the coupling link already has a working instrument and the broken link is perception. Then F9 reverses for that system.

Status: `general` (structural), single-source for the figure line, with world corroboration about where the measurement gap sits.

### F10 - agreement among tipping studies is one evidence pool, not several witnesses

Fig 2 :40: `agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool`. Fig 2 :42: `Buy independence in proportion to the cost of being wrong.` Fig 7 :13: `agreement compounds, evidence does not`.

The climate-tipping sources read here (Wunderling 2021, Wunderling 2024, Lenton 2024) share institutions, authors, and a conceptual-network modelling tradition. Their agreement that interactions tend to destabilise is one witness speaking in several papers. It cannot corroborate the figure verdict. The genuinely low-overlap witnesses are the network-science papers (Buldyrev 2010, Gao et al. 2019), the multiple-stressor ecology papers (Buma 2015, Pirotta 2022, Fong et al. 2018), and the engineered-systems review (Guo et al. 2020). Even those corroborate a shape, not a number.

Refuter: demonstrate that two cited sources draw on disjoint evidence and instruments. Then their agreement counts twice. Until shown, it counts once.

Status: `general`, triangulated across Fig 2 and Fig 7, with the overlap recorded per source in `10_source_ledger.csv`.

### F11 - the shape-not-a-name rule is narrower than it first appears

A first pass would say the framework cannot name a coupling topology or a specific tipping element. That overstates the rule. The canonical figures README says the framework returns `what a missing thing would have to look like (a shape, not a name)`, where the subject is a **missing** primitive, one not held in `Γ`. Coupling topology (network, cycle) and the roster of elements are already in the habitat and in the six structures. Naming them is a binding from a world frame onto held atoms, which is provenance, not the invention of a seventh term.

What remains forbidden is claiming that the framework fixes which binding is correct in a named system, or that a coupling strength is a new primitive it can name. The correction is preserved rather than erased because it changed an earlier reading of §8.

Refuter: show that naming a coupling topology adds a primitive outside the six and the bond set. Then the narrowing is wrong and the original ban stands.

Status: `general`, canonically grounded in the figures README plus Fig 13 :115 (`The set is closed. The arrangements are not.`).

### F12 - the crossing is a threshold at first order, and a comparison again at second order

Fig 12 :104: `A settled molecule becomes an atom for whatever composes over it.` Once element A has crossed and settled into its new regime, it becomes an atom for the next composition. The coupling `A ⋛ B` at second order is again a threshold relation, now between settled molecules serving as quantities at that level.

This is a strain, not a clean derivation, and it is named: the Operators table restricts `⋛` to `a threshold relation between two quantities`, and whether a settled molecule counts as a quantity at that level needs an instrument, not an argument. Refuter: show that settled molecules cannot serve as operands of `⋛` at any level. Then the second-order reading of a cascade is not composable and F12 fails.

Status: `specified`, triangulated for the level rule (Fig 12 :104 plus Fig 13 :110), strained on the operand type.

## 4. Pre-registered refuter (written before the conclusion)

Required before synthesis: a falsifier written before the run is a design instrument, not only an honesty device (Seat Map :287). The compound-shape claim is refused if any of the following holds:

1. A universal likelihood multiplier or cascade magnitude is derived from the figure set alone with no import and no ground reading. (Falsifies F8.)
2. In a named domain the threshold conditions form a disjoint partition, so clearing one clears the whole compound. (Falsifies F2 there.)
3. Couplings in an admissible domain are provably acyclic, so no loop can close, or sign is fixed by count rather than by bond. (Falsifies F3.)
4. Consequences are shown to be additive across slots with no live-slot asymmetry and the average is sufficient to direct intervention. (Falsifies F5.)
5. A figure line is found that names which coupling topology will dominate in a named system. (Falsifies F11 and the shape-not-a-name narrowing.)
6. A ground measurement shows that removing the driver restores exactly the prior composition with no new term and no fall in reach. (Falsifies F7.)

If any fires, the claim is withdrawn or narrowed as indicated. Until one is run, no part of this may be labelled `grounded`.

### Seven-question recursive derivation (summary)

1. **One threshold or several?** Several, and the arity change makes the arrangement the object (Fig 12 :5).
2. **What kind of bond is a crossing?** An ordered threshold comparison `⋛`, not a conjunction (Operators table :48).
3. **Partition or union?** Union. Unbundle; the intersection binds (Fig 9 :55-57).
4. **How can a cascade close?** A network with feedback is a cycle; the sign decides closure (Fig 12 :121; Fig 13 :24).
5. **Is the outcome fixed by the roster?** No. Same atoms, different bond, different substance (Fig 12 :117), with three signs observed in one experiment (Fong et al. 2018).
6. **Where are the consequences?** Per slot, third order out, in an enumerated habitat (Fig 1 :19; Fig 11 :52).
7. **Can the number be named?** No figure returns it. Narrow to a shape, record what an instrument would have to discriminate, and stop (Fig 9 :32; Fig 13 :134-135).

## 8. Updated verdict

The compound shape survives: coupled tipping points are a union of overlapping conditions whose couplings are signed bonds, so the sign of the loop, not the count, decides whether a cascade closes; consequences are a per-slot vector with third-order effects; crossing removes standing to compose over the prior regime; and no universal multiplier or named missing atom is returned. The only quantities that would move the verdict are a ground measurement of the coupling sign and reach in a named system, or a figure re-census that admits a numerical multiplier. Prestige and agreement are neither. Status: `specified` on the shape and per-domain verdicts, `general` on the skeleton, `gap` on the number, candidate and ungrounded throughout.
