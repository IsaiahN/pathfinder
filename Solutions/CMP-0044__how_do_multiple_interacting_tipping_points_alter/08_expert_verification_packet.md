# Expert verification packet: the Tether on interacting tipping points

**What this is:** a claim in a form a tipping-point researcher or complex-systems scientist can check. Mode, derivation and refuters are stated before the conclusion, so a reader knows what to look for and what would settle it.

**Mode: `specified` on the shape and per-domain verdicts, `general` on the skeleton, `gap` on the universal number. Not `grounded`.** No measurement at a named system is claimed. This is a shape and a route, not a result.

**Status of this packet:** a simulated derivation checked for internal consistency against the figures, not a ground reading. It cannot settle the question it reports on.

## 1. The claim, in one paragraph

Coupled tipping points are not N instances of one hazard. Figure 9's union rule makes them a compound whose intersections bind even after single-condition regions are cleared. A crossing is an ordered threshold comparison, and coupling is a signed bond between such comparisons. When the influence graph closes a loop, the sign of the loop decides whether the cascade runs away or damps; a chain cannot close by construction. Over a fixed roster, reinforcing cycles, inhibitory cycles, domino sequences and parallel clusters are isomers, same atoms and different substance. Consequences must be reported per element, with effects several steps out, because a global average can hide a live slot. Crossing removes standing to compose over the prior regime, so recovery is a new lineage. The framework returns this shape and its falsifiers, not a universal likelihood multiplier or a named coupling strength. That is a declared gap.

## 2. Three checkable sub-claims

### C1 - framework-internal; checkable by reading Figures 9 and 12

- **Statement:** coupled threshold conditions form a union compound, and the sign of the coupling loop decides closure. Counting thresholds cannot decide it.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 9 :55 "A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones." Fig 9 :57 "What remains is smaller and it is also categorically different: a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either." Fig 12 :121 "cycle. The output re-enters as input. The sign of the loop decides whether it closes, and a chain cannot close by construction."
- **Refuter:** exhibit a named domain where the threshold conditions form a disjoint partition so clearing one clears the whole; or prove the couplings are acyclic so no loop can close; or show that sign is fixed by the count rather than by the bond. Any one of these removes the compound claim in that domain.

### C2 - structure; checkable by comparing coupling arrangements over a fixed roster

- **Statement:** with the same roster of tipping elements, different coupling arrangements are isomers. A domino sequence and a reinforcing cycle are different terms, not different spellings of one term.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 12 :117 "isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour." Fig 12 :123 "functional group. A substructure whose presence fixes the outcome. The group says what kind; the qualifier says which member." Fig 12 :72-74 defines sequence: "A then B; B needs A to have happened".
- **What to check:** hold the roster fixed (the four elements in Wunderling et al. 2021 are a convenient test case), permute only the coupling signs and the presence or absence of return links, and ask whether closure and consequence change. If they do not, the isomer claim fails. World-facing evidence already goes the other way: Fong et al. (2018) found additive, synergistic and antagonistic interactions in equal frequency under one three-stressor roster.
- **Refuter:** show outcome invariance under permutation of the coupling arrangement for a fixed roster. Then only the roster-level claim survives. Also note the condition: if an arrangement requires an extra mediator atom, the two are not isomers.

### C3 - consequences; checkable against a measured cascade

- **Statement:** consequences are indexed per element and can be third order out; a global average may sit near zero while one slot is live. Crossing removes standing to compose over the prior regime.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 1 :19 "The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated." Fig 1 :22 "Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average." Fig 6 :42 "What is recorded only grows. What is reachable is derived, and it can fall." Fig 11 :52 "And contact cascades. Scoping the habitat to direct contact is how DDT reached the eagles: first-order enumeration, second-order effect, and the residual that mattered is three steps out."
- **What to check:** in a documented cascade, is there an element whose state changes while the system-wide index barely moves? Were the consequential effects found at one, two or three links from the first crossing? Does removing the driver restore the prior composition exactly?
- **Refuter:** show that consequences are additive across slots with no live-slot asymmetry and that the average is sufficient to direct intervention; or show a reversal that restores the prior composition with no new term. Either breaks C3.

## 3. What is explicitly not claimed

- Not a likelihood multiplier, critical coupling strength, probability, or cascade magnitude for any named system. That is a `gap` (Fig 9 :32, Fig 13 :134-135, Fig 8 :39).
- Not that all interactions are destabilising. The sign is a property of the bond, and the figure does not pick a winner. The climate-tipping literature's tendency that way is a world finding, not a figure verdict.
- Not that the framework names the initiator element or the dominant topology. Network and cycle are held structures; which one is instantiated is a binding from the world frame, provenanced as such.
- Not that a cascade is one big threshold. It is second order over settled first-order molecules.
- Not a derivation of one tipping element from the six-term substrate. The physical roster is a habitat, enumerated not composed (Fig 11 :43).
- Not a claim that better single-element early-warning indicators answer the coupling question. That link already works (Fig 3 :19).
- Not `grounded`. Nothing here is asserted as settled by experiment.
- **Two live strains, stated rather than hidden:** (i) at second order the comparison bond's operands are settled molecules, while the Operators table defines it over two quantities; whether an atom counts as a quantity at that level needs an instrument; (ii) a continuous coupling strength may be a bond weight or a new kind, and the framework cannot name which.

## 5. What would settle it

A sign-and-arrangement discriminator at a named system, not a further argument. Concretely:

1. **Sign test:** does crossing A raise or lower the probability that B crosses, holding the shared driver fixed?
2. **Loop test:** is there a return path from the crossed element to itself through the others? If not, only the sequence reading applies.
3. **Per-slot test:** at the moment of systemic change, is there a live slot whose residual is positive while the global average is near zero?
4. **Reach test:** after removing the driver, is the prior composition restored with no new term?
5. **Strain test:** do settled molecules count as quantities for the comparison at second order, and is coupling strength a weight or a new kind?

Six pre-registered failure conditions are listed in C1 to C3 and in `01` §4. A failure is more useful than an endorsement.

## 6. What an expert can check in twenty minutes

1. Read Fig 9 :55 and :57, Fig 12 :121 and :117, Fig 1 :19 and :22, and Fig 11 :52. Eight short passages.
2. Check C2 against a network you know: hold the roster fixed, permute the signs and return links, and see whether the outcome changes.
3. Name one instrument that would measure the sign of a coupling between two tipping elements, or say why none is possible. That instrument is what the `gap` is owed.
4. Send the refutation. If it resolves either live strain against the claim, the shape is narrowed in public.
