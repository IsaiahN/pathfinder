## What is deliberately not here

- No personal emails of named individuals.
- No ranking of people by how likely they are to agree with the shape claim.
- No bulk-send plan.

Three reasons, in the figures' own words. First, a room does not upgrade the label, so a larger audience adds vocabulary and not an anchor (Seat Map, `High correlation is disqualifying for a verdict and efficient for a convention`). Second, the anchor must not update: `Alignment is triangulation, not negotiation. A mirror cannot correct you.` (Fig 2 :35). Third, asking a reader to hold the whole framework before they give a verdict spends the only independence they contribute: `A verifier that can reconstruct the claim cannot verify it.` (Fig 10 :42).

Legally, scraping personal emails for unsolicited bulk outreach breaches GDPR and CAN-SPAM style rules in many jurisdictions. That is a practical failure on top of the structural one.

## Restating the question as an effect

The world contains systems with more than one threshold. Each threshold is a comparison between a changing quantity and a critical value. The question asks what happens to two outputs, the likelihood of systemic change and the consequences of systemic change, when several such thresholds are coupled rather than one being crossed alone. Option A treats "several" as "more of the same" and expects an additive scalar. Option B treats coupling as a change of kind: a compound with its own loop, whose sign depends on the arrangement, and whose consequences are indexed per element rather than averaged. What an answer would look like is not a coefficient but a shape: which conditions must hold, which arrangements give which sign, what is indexed where, and what instrument would settle a named instance. That shape is bound here as an ordinary term so it can accrue a residual and be refused (Formula, "Direction").
