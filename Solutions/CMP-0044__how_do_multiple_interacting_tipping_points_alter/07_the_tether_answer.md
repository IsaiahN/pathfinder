# The Tether's answer: multiple interacting tipping points

**Mode: `specified` on the conditional shape and on each per-domain verdict, `general` on the structural skeleton, `gap` on the universal number. Not `grounded`.** Simulated derivation checked for internal consistency against the figures (`05_what_shape_means.md` §6), not a ground reading.

## 1. The residual it answers

A system has several thresholds, and crossing one changes the likelihood of crossing another. We are asked for the effect on two outputs: the likelihood of systemic change and its consequences. The contact object is a **union compound**, not one hard problem. Fig 9 :55 "A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones." It has its own loop, its own ground, and its own per-slot indexing. Verdicts below are kept separate and never averaged.

## 2. The shape

1. **A crossing is an ordered threshold comparison, not a conjunction.** The Operators table defines the bond as a threshold relation between two quantities. "Multiple" therefore changes the arity of the object and makes the arrangement the thing to explain, not the count. Fig 12 :5 "A set of ingredients is not one. The bond is the rest of the information, and it is the half nothing was recording."

2. **Coupling changes the kind of object.** The conditions overlap, so a lever that clears the region where only one condition holds leaves the intersections standing because the second condition still binds (Fig 9 :56). A case in two conditions is not a harder case of either (Fig 9 :57). The additive reading is refused as written: it treats a union as a list and a bond as a repetition.

3. **The sign of the loop decides the cascade.** Coupling gives the group a network structure, so influence travels between elements. When the output of one crossing re-enters as input to another, the arrangement is a cycle. Fig 12 :121 "cycle. The output re-enters as input. The sign of the loop decides whether it closes, and a chain cannot close by construction." A domino sequence and a reinforcing cycle are different bonds, hence different terms. The literature's stabilising versus destabilising interactions are the two signs of one loop, not two mechanisms (Wunderling et al. 2021, 2024).

4. **Arrangement, not roster, decides the sign.** Over a fixed set of tipping elements, a reinforcing cycle, an inhibitory cycle, a domino chain and a parallel cluster are isomers: same atoms, different bond, different substance (Fig 12 :117). They share a `threshold - coupling` functional group, which is why one vocabulary covers all four while one number does not. Which one dominates in a named system is not given by the figure.

5. **Consequences are a per-slot vector, not a magnitude.** Fig 1 :19 "The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated." A global average near zero with one live slot is a legal state (Fig 1 :13). Aggregating across slots throws away the only signal that says where to look (Fig 1 :22). The consequence that matters can be third order out, the way DDT reached the eagles (Fig 11 :52). Scoping to direct contact repeats that error.

6. **Crossing is structurally a refusal.** Fig 6 :42 "What is recorded only grows. What is reachable is derived, and it can fall." A term loses its standing to be composed over because something was added, not because it was deleted. Recovery is a new lineage, not the prior one reversed. This is why consequences are often not undone by removing the driver.

7. **The number is a gap.** No figure line returns a multiplier, a critical coupling strength, a probability or a cascade size for a named system. Fig 9 :32 "you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself." The framework can narrow the answer to a shape and say what an instrument would have to discriminate. It cannot name the missing primitive, because naming it would mean already holding it (Fig 13 :134-135). Both the universal multiplier (shape-not-a-name) and the specific missing quantity in a named system (unreachable-at-this-budget) are verdicts, not silence (Fig 8 :39).

## 3. What it explicitly does not say

- Not that "risk goes up by X" for X tipping points. No coefficient is derivable.
- Not that all interactions are destabilising. Fig 12 :121 makes sign a property of the bond; the figure does not fix which sign wins in a named system. The climate-tipping literature's tendency toward destabilising interactions is a world finding, not a figure verdict.
- Not that a cascade is one big threshold. It is a second-order composition over settled first-order molecules, with a live strain on the operand type of the comparison (see §4).
- Not that the framework names the initiator element or the coupling strength. Those are bindings from a world frame, provenanced as such.
- Not that better early-warning indicators for single elements answer the question. That link already works; the weak link is the vocabulary for coupling and sign (Fig 3 :19, :27).
- Not grounded. No ground measurement settles `left(R, φ)` for any named network.

## 4. What would settle it: the ground

A sign-and-arrangement discriminator, not a further argument. The relevant questions:

- **Sign test:** for a named pair of elements, does crossing A increase or decrease the probability that B crosses, holding the shared driver fixed? This is a measurement of the bond, not of the roster. Wunderling et al. (2021) report both signs in the studied network; the figure predicts both are possible.
- **Loop test:** does the influence graph contain a return path from the crossed element to itself through the others? If not, the cycle term is unavailable and only the sequence reading applies.
- **Per-slot test:** at the moment of systemic change, is there a live slot whose residual is positive while the global average is near zero? If consequence is genuinely additive with no live-slot asymmetry, the per-slot claim fails.
- **Reach test:** after removing the driver, is the prior composition restored with no new term? If yes, the refusal analogy fails for that system.
- **Standing falsifiers:** derive a universal multiplier from the figure set alone; show a disjoint partition of threshold conditions; prove couplings are acyclic; show a settled molecule cannot serve as an operand of the comparison at second order; find a figure line that names the dominant topology in a named system. All six are written in advance in `01` §4 and `08` §2.

## 5. The honest stop

**Unreached, not unreachable.** The shape is available: union compound, signed bonds, isomer arrangements, per-slot consequences, second-order cascade. The number is not, and the missing primitive in any named system cannot be named from inside the frame. The six-term set is a visible guess at the compression axes, not nature's own roster of tipping elements. The answer stops at the limit of what the available instruments distinguish and records the lineage so the stop is checkable. `Indiscernibility is not identity.` Two strains stay live and named: the operand type of the comparison at second order, and whether coupling strength is a bond weight or a new kind.

## 6. One-paragraph version for outreach

> The Tether does not give a risk coefficient for N interacting tipping points. It says that coupling changes the kind of object: the thresholds form a union of overlapping conditions, so clearing one condition leaves the intersections standing, and the link between them is a signed bond. When that bond closes a loop, the sign of the loop decides whether the cascade runs away or damps, and the same roster under different bonds is a different substance, an isomer. Consequences are a per-element vector with effects several steps out, not a global average that hides the live slot. Crossing removes standing to compose over the prior regime, so recovery is a new lineage. What the framework returns is this shape, the falsifiers written in advance, and the instruments that would close it. The number stays open until the ground settles it.
