# The Tether's answer: what makes representatives responsive?

**Mode: `general` on the shape, `specified` on its application to electoral institutions, not `grounded`.** A simulated derivation checked for internal consistency against the figures (`05_what_shape_means.md` section 6), not a ground reading. Verdicts are kept per domain and are never averaged.

## 1. The residual it answers

A representative's action tracks the concentrated signal (donors, party leadership, organised minorities) more faithfully than the diffuse constituency signal. Fig 1 :19: "The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated." The gap is per district and per issue, not one national number. Fig 9 :55: "A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones." The question is a union of incentive, information and collective-action conditions, with their intersections.

## 2. The shape, per domain

**D1, constituency responsiveness.** There is a legitimate ground. Fig 2 :29: "An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks." Because the question is constitutively about what constituents get, measured policy congruence with an independently obtained constituency preference is an instrument for that anchor. The representative cannot score itself: Fig 1 :27 "A frame cannot score itself with a quantity it also produces"; Fig 10 :93 "A ground with a reputation to protect is a ground someone benefits from, and its keeper is thereby disqualified." **Verdict: `triangulated`, `general`.**

**D2, elite and interest-group influence.** This is unioned with D1, not a second independent witness. Gilens and Page (2014) measure influence on the same policy outcomes, so any agreement with the congruence literature is one evidence pool (Fig 2 :40). Olson (1965) supplies the mechanism: concentrated minor interests are overrepresented because the free-rider problem grows with group size. **Verdict: `triangulated` for the asymmetry, `single-source` for the warning against pooling.**

**Habitat, not a domain.** Fig 11 :40: "A system cannot be made more capable by improving the agent in isolation. An improvement that does not change contact changes nothing, however much it improves." Levers that change contact (electoral rules, boundaries, primaries, finance and disclosure rules, staff and oversight) are habitat changes. "Elect better people" is the agent-only failure. Fig 11 :49: "An actor's valence is relative to the residual, not a property it carries." No actor is good or bad by kind; the question is what it does to this residual. **Verdict: `triangulated` across Fig 11 and Olson, `specified` when a lever is named.**

**D3, verification.** Channels decay while the ground does not: Fig 10 :24 "the ground does not decay, the channel does". Polls, staff and oversight must be maintained or the output is "you have lost touch" (Fig 10 :28), not a verdict. Selection must be between differing rooms against that ground: Fig 7 :36 "The next room must differ from the last, and match the residual it surfaced." Agreement among frames sharing a pool is disqualified: Fig 2 :35 "Alignment is triangulation, not negotiation. A mirror cannot correct you." **Verdict: `triangulated`, `general`.**

**Naming the lever.** Fig 9 :32: "you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself." The figures return conditions, not a reform. Fig 6 :30: "The description travels. Fails by being sharpened into terms only we can read. Cast it in effect terms." **Verdict: `gap` for the naming question.** That is a verdict, not a silence.

## 3. What it explicitly does not say

- Not that any named reform (public financing, redistricting, primaries, term limits) is the answer. Those are bindings from the empirical frame, recorded as such.
- Not that representatives are corrupt or that donors are the only cause. Valence is relative to the residual (Fig 11 :49); the three conditions and their intersections all bind.
- Not that more representatives, more polls or more oversight automatically help. Fig 7 :35: "A room's agreement is variation, not a verdict. Selection between rooms is where the ground votes."
- Not that citizen preferences are coherent or available. Where a signal has never registered at any resolution, there is no edge to extend from (Fig 6 :34-35). That would be a different residual.
- Not `grounded`. No measurement in this folder settles which lever works.

## 4. What would settle it, the ground

A source-discriminating instrument, not a further argument. The metamerism case in *The Hard Problem is About Source*: a green reading cannot comment on its source; the spectrometer, not a debate, resolved it. Here the corresponding questions are:

1. Does an independent preference series separate constituency influence from donor and organised-interest influence on the same policy outcomes, per district and per issue?
2. Does a habitat-only change (rules, boundaries, staff, oversight) shift measured congruence without an agent-only change, and does an agent-only change shift it without a habitat change?
3. Is there a single lever that clears the incentive, information and collective-action conditions including their intersections, or do the intersections require separate treatment?
4. Does a maintained channel to the anchor continue to register after use, or does it saturate and go stale (Fig 10 :24)?

## 5. The honest stop

**Unreached, not unreachable.** Fig 8 :39: "So a search that finds nothing is never a proof of absence." The absence of a figure line that names a reform does not prove that no reform exists. The answer stops at the limit of the instruments and records the lineage so the stop is checkable. The canonical rule: "Indiscernibility is not identity." Fig 4 :30: "So take the round trip and measure the gap. Without it you are navigating by dead reckoning: confident about a position nobody checked." The round-trip gap between this shape and any concrete policy is enumerated in `11_audit.md`.

## 6. One-paragraph version for outreach

> The Tether does not name the single reform that makes representatives responsive, and it does not claim that electing better people is enough. It says the question is a bundle of at least three conditions, incentive, information and collective action, so one lever clears only the easy region and leaves the intersections standing. It says the constituency is a legitimate ground that no political frame may move; that the residual must be read per district and per issue, not averaged into one national opinion number; that the representative cannot score its own responsiveness; that the channel to the constituency decays while the ground does not; that capability lives in the agent and its institutional habitat, so rules and contact must change, not only personnel; and that selection must be between genuinely different rooms against that ground. It offers no name for the best reform, because the framework returns a shape. The four refuters were written before this run. The center stays open until a source-discriminating measurement settles which lever, alone or in combination, actually moves the ground.
