## Restating the question as an effect

The observation to be explained is a gap: a representative's action tracks the concentrated, organised, loud signal (donors, party leadership, narrow organised interests) more closely than the diffuse, numerous, weak signal of ordinary constituents. Option A says this is a defect of the people elected, so elect better people. Option B says it is a defect of the contact arrangement, so change what the representative is in contact with. What an answer would look like, bound as an ordinary term before the run (Fig 5 :65, "make the sought-for shape an ORDINARY TERM"): a set of conditions under which the representative's action ranges against a constituency anchor that none of the competing frames can move.

## What is deliberately not here

- No personal emails of named individuals.
- No ranking of people by how likely they are to agree with the claim.
- No bulk-send plan.

Three reasons, in the framework's own words:

1. **A room does not upgrade the label.** "A room does not upgrade the label. A room reaching a conclusion without the ground is agreement, and agreement among frames is a weighted average of influence. Adding people raises the vocabulary and does not add an anchor." (Seat Map :283)
2. **Correlation disqualifies a verdict.** "High correlation is disqualifying for a verdict and efficient for a convention." (Seat Map :163) Selecting recipients for receptiveness manufactures exactly the correlation that makes their agreement worthless.
3. **Asking for trust before checking spends independence.** "A verifier that can reconstruct the claim cannot verify it." (Fig 10 :42) An outside frame must not hold the interiors.

Legally: scraping personal emails for unsolicited bulk outreach breaches GDPR or CAN-SPAM style rules in many jurisdictions. That is a practical failure on top of the structural one.
