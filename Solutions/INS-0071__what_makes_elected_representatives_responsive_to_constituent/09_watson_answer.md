## QUESTION

Decide, in effect terms, what the Tether says about the observed gap between what a constituency wants and what its representative does when donors, party leadership and organised minorities also bear on the action. Option A: treat it as a defect of the people elected and replace them. Option B: treat it as a defect of the contact arrangement and change the habitat. What each leads to: A is an agent-only improvement and, under Fig 11 :40, changes nothing if contact is unchanged; B changes what the representative ranges against, but returns only a shape, not a named reform. The sought-for shape was bound as an ordinary term before the run (Fig 5 :65): conditions under which the action ranges against a constituency anchor that no competing frame can move.

## DOMAIN CENSUS

R by arity, symmetry and scale, then one line per domain with actors, currency and anchor; partition or union; order by dependency.

- **R (arity / symmetry / scale).** Arity: at least four principals bearing on one action. Symmetry: asymmetric; concentrated interests produce a high-amplitude signal per slot while the diffuse constituency produces a weak one, so the comparison is a threshold relation rather than a symmetric conjunction. Scale: first order at the representative, second order at the institution that selects and maintains representatives. Fig 1 :19 "The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated."
- **Domain 1, constituency responsiveness.** Actors: representative, constituents, independent measurement instruments. Currency: votes, opinion-policy congruence. Anchor: measured constituency preference and resulting policy, independent of the representative's own account. Legitimate because the question is constitutively about it.
- **Domain 2, elite and organised-interest influence.** Actors: donors, party, organised interests, lobbyists. Currency: contributions, endorsements, pressure, information. Anchor: measured independent impact on the same policy outcomes. Union with Domain 1, not a second independent witness, because both read the same outcome data.
- **Domain 3, verification and outreach.** Actors: expert readers, two simulated seats. Currency: agreement and residual. Anchor: the ground, never agreement. In this run all seats share one evidence pool, so their independence is zero.
- **Habitat, not a domain.** Constituents, primary electorate, donors, party, organised interests, district boundaries, finance and primary rules, staff, polls, media, oversight bodies, then their contacts. No anchor of its own, so it is enumerated rather than admitted as a fourth domain.
- **Partition or union.** Union. Fig 9 :55 "A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones."
- **Order by dependency.** Establish the anchor before judging responsiveness; describe the residual before choosing a frame (Fig 9 :27); refuse the agent-only reading before minting any reform; settle first-order congruence before second-order selection.

Strain test applied: Fig 11's capability claim governs agent-and-habitat; delegate and trustee models in political science govern descriptions of conduct. Different objects, so no strain after re-census.

## VERDICT (from the figures)

The shape survives; a single named reform does not. The question is a union compound, so incentive, information and collective-action conditions, including their intersections, must be separated before a lever is chosen. The constituency is a legitimate ground that no political frame may move; the residual must be read per district and per issue; the representative cannot score its own responsiveness; the channel decays while the ground does not; capability lives in agent and habitat, not the agent alone; and elections are progress only when they select between differing rooms against that ground. Agreement among frames sharing an evidence pool is disqualified as a verdict. Naming the best reform is a `gap` at the figure level and belongs to empirical institutional research. This is `general` on the shape, `specified` in application, ungrounded, candidate.

- **cost if wrong:** bounded and locatable. The packet states its mode, four refuters and its provenance before the conclusion, so an error is findable rather than invisible. The real cost is spending the independence of outside readers by sending a conclusion instead of a generator, which Fig 2 :42 prices: "Buy independence in proportion to the cost of being wrong." That cost is avoidable by leading with the shape claim and its refuter. A second cost is forcing a union into one lever and recommending a reform that clears only the singleton region.
- **undoable:** yes. No reform is asserted, so no policy claim has to be withdrawn. The union reading can be refined by splitting the conditions further, and the habitat levers can be added as provenanced bindings from empirical research. A sent packet can be superseded by a corrected one. What cannot be undone is an independence already spent by asking for endorsement before a verdict, so the two-stage order is the safeguard.
- **reversed by:** a ground reading, not agreement. An independent preference series that fails to separate constituency from donor influence on the same policy outcomes would break the anchor finding. A habitat-only change that does not shift congruence, or an agent-only change that does, would break Fig 11 :40. A single lever that clears all three conditions including their intersections would break Fig 9 :56. A channel shown not to decay would break Fig 10 :24. An instrument that settles ⋛'s operand type at second order would clear the named strain. Until one of these fires, the verdict stands as `candidate`.
