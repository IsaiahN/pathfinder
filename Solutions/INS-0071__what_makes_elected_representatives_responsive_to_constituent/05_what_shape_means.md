## 1. A shape is a constraint, not a filler

The canonical rule: the framework reports "what a missing thing would have to look like (a shape, not a name)". Fig 9 :32: "you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself." Fig 13 :134: "Unreachable is a verdict here, not a silence".

From *The Library of Babel*: "You can cut your search short if the set of books returned is small and descriptive enough that it provides a general shape of the answer that satisfies your question."

So a **shape** here is the set of conditions any answer must meet, statable before the specific reform is known. "A constituency signal that reaches action through a channel none of the competing frames can move" is a shape. "Adopt public financing with independent redistricting" is a name. The first constrains; the second fills a slot that the figures leave open.

## 2. How the shape is obtained

- *Library of Babel*: use the vocabulary already held that is closest to the thing, then recurse. "With each new term gained, you are able to narrow the space by definition and deepen your vocabulary." In this question the starting vocabulary is residual, anchor, channel, habitat, selection.
- Fig 6 :30: "The description travels. Fails by being sharpened into terms only we can read. Cast it in effect terms." So describe the failure in effects: the action tracks the concentrated signal more than the diffuse one.
- *Decoding Tacit Knowledge*: "When contact isn't possible, the best you can send is a plain description of what you'd expect to see, because anyone can check that against the world." An effect description is checkable by a reader who holds no framework vocabulary.
- *Library of Babel* on bundles: "Perhaps your problem was a bundle or conflation of several problems masquerading as one? You will need to search all of those problems individually." This is why the shape is a set of conditions rather than a single lever.

## 3. What licenses a name

Only two things, both outside the shape:

1. **The ground settles it.** `THE_FORMULA.md` step 5: "the ground settles it. Verified only by what pays. A gate is not the ground." The README: the framework "stops at the limit of what the available instruments can distinguish, and records the lineage so the stop is honest. Indiscernibility is not identity."
2. **A provenanced import from a frame whose closure differs** (Fig 6 :23, Fig 8 provenance rule), with a source gate and a shadow test. Empirical institutional research is such a frame. Its findings can name a lever, but the name arrives with provenance as the researcher's contribution, not as a figure-derived primitive.

*How Intelligence Actually Works* sharpens the stop: "the recursion converges toward indiscernibility, and indiscernibility is not identity. The projection that no instrument distinguishes from the thing is still a projection, which is why the lineage has to be kept even when the copy is perfect." A shape that fits every known case of responsiveness is still not the mechanism until a ground discriminates among rival mechanisms.

## 4. The source problem in this question

*The Hard Problem is About Source* supplies the discriminator. Metamerism: a spectrometer reads green and "cannot comment on the source"; one green light, or yellow plus blue, or RGB, or CMYK, or a scheme not yet discovered can produce the same reading. "If several paths create the same behavior, then behavior is a projection with lossy information." The resolution came "not by argument, but by the arrival of a better instrument."

Here the analogue is direct. A representative's record of votes and constituent services is a projection. It reads "responsive" whether the source was constituency preference, donor pressure, party discipline, or an organised minority. Naming the source from the reading is the metamerism error. What is owed is a source-discriminating instrument (congruence measured against an independent preference series, contribution records, roll-call and committee data), not a label.

## 5. Q: does "shape, not a name" forbid naming a reform?

No, with a condition. The README's phrase governs "a **missing** thing", that is a primitive not held in the library. Naming a reform is a **binding** from an empirical frame onto a shape already held. That is permitted but it is not a derivation. What is forbidden is presenting the binding as figure-derived, or claiming that it moves the wall (Fig 6: import adds an atom; a binding onto an existing shape adds none).

I overstated this on the first pass by refusing to name any lever at all. Retracted and narrowed: the figures return the shape; the empirical literature may name a lever, with provenance. Both claims are recorded so neither is quietly lost.

## 6. Consequence for this solution

**Prestige is not action.** From *The Recursive Self-Improvement Trap*: "Prestige must never be allowed to validate an action directly... Prestige can fund an action. It can give an action a budget to be tried. But it cannot settle an action. Only the ground can settle it. The Ground Maintainer must be blind to prestige." A list of eminent political scientists who endorse the shape is a convention, not a check.

**This audit is a simulation, not a ground reading.** Same essay: "LLMs can quickly simulate an approximate answer/output with the formula" and "the truly testable output would need to be derived from the real architecture or working the formula yourself." So `01` is a simulated derivation, checked for internal consistency against the figures. It is **not** a test of the claim and must be labelled as such. It cannot settle anything it reports on.
