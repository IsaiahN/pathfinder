# Expert verification packet - the Tether on representative responsiveness

**What this is:** a claim in a form a political scientist or institutional scholar can check. Mode, derivation and refuters are stated before the conclusion, so the reader knows what to look for and what would settle it.

**Mode: `general` on the shape, `specified` on its application, not `grounded`.** No measurement is claimed to settle which reform works. Simulated derivation checked for internal consistency against the figures, not a ground reading (`05_what_shape_means.md` section 6).

## 1. The claim, in one paragraph

"Why are representatives responsive to donors and parties rather than constituents?" is a union compound, not one lever. At least three conditions bind: incentive asymmetry, information asymmetry, and the collective-action asymmetry between concentrated minorities and a diffuse majority. The figure-derived shape is: a constituency anchor that no competing frame can move; a residual read per district and per issue rather than averaged; a channel maintained against decay; capability located in the agent and its habitat rather than the agent alone; and selection between differing rooms against that ground. The framework returns a shape, not a name. It does not say which reform is best. Four refuters were registered before this run.

## 2. Three checkable sub-claims

### C1 - framework-internal; checkable by reading Figures 1, 2 and 10

- **Statement:** the constituency is a legitimate ground; the representative cannot score its own responsiveness; the channel to the ground decays while the ground does not.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 2 :29 "An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks." Fig 1 :27 "A frame cannot score itself with a quantity it also produces." Fig 10 :24 "the ground does not decay, the channel does". Fig 10 :93 "A ground with a reputation to protect is a ground someone benefits from, and its keeper is thereby disqualified."
- **Refuter:** exhibit an instrument whose reading of constituency preference cannot be moved by the actor it grades and is not authored by any interested party, and show that the channel to it cannot become stale. If both hold, C1 narrows.

### C2 - institutional-facing; checkable against research on representation and collective action

- **Statement:** the question is a union of incentive, information and collective-action conditions; a single lever clears only the singleton region and leaves the intersections standing; the habitat, not the agent's virtue, is where the leverage is. Olson (1965) supplies the concentrated-minority mechanism, Gilens and Page (2014) supply an influence measure, and Fig 9 and Fig 11 supply the structural rule.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 9 :55 "A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones." Fig 9 :56 "A single lever clears the region where only one condition holds and leaves the intersections standing, because the second condition still binds." Fig 11 :40 "A system cannot be made more capable by improving the agent in isolation. An improvement that does not change contact changes nothing, however much it improves."
- **What to check:** are these three conditions the right decomposition, and is there evidence that a single lever clears all of them, intersections included? Is there evidence that an agent-only improvement shifts measured congruence with no habitat change?
- **Refuter:** a ground reading showing one lever clears all three conditions including their intersections, or that agent-only improvement alone produces a sustained congruence rise. C2 fails if either is demonstrated.

### C3 - framework-internal derivation about what a vocabulary must contain

- **Statement:** "responsiveness" cannot be stated by a vocabulary of nouns. It needs a comparison for the goal, a subtraction for progress, a sequence for the route, and negation for refusal. A settled congruence reading becomes an atom at the institutional level, so the second-order selection rule needs no new primitive.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 12 :111 "A vocabulary without bonds can name a state and cannot name a route to one." Fig 12 :112 "A plan is a sequence. A goal is a comparison. Progress is a subtraction. A refutation is a negation, and that one is not a bond." Fig 12 :104 "A settled molecule becomes an atom for whatever composes over it. So irreducible is a fact about a level and never about the world". Fig 7 :36 "The next room must differ from the last, and match the residual it surfaced."
- **Refuter:** show that a settled congruence reading cannot serve as an operand for an institutional selection rule, or that a noun-only vocabulary can state a route to responsiveness. C3 fails if either holds.
- **Named strain, not hidden:** the Operators table defines ⋛ as a threshold relation between two quantities. At second order the operands are settled readings now serving as atoms. Whether they count as quantities at that level needs an instrument, not an argument.

## 3. What is explicitly not claimed

- Not a named reform. Public financing, redistricting, primary rules, term limits and oversight are bindings from the empirical frame, recorded as the author's contribution, not figure-derived names.
- Not that donors are the sole cause, or that representatives are corrupt. Valence is relative to the residual (Fig 11 :49).
- Not that more of the same representation helps. Fig 7 :35 "A room's agreement is variation, not a verdict. Selection between rooms is where the ground votes."
- Not that citizen preferences are always coherent or measurable. Where nothing registers at any resolution, that is a different residual.
- Not `grounded`. Nothing here is asserted as settled by experiment.

## 5. What would settle it

A source-discriminating instrument. From *The Hard Problem is About Source*: a green reading cannot comment on its source; the spectrometer, not an argument, resolved it. Here:

1. Does an independent preference series separate constituency influence from donor and organised-interest influence on the same policy outcomes, per district and per issue?
2. Does a habitat-only change shift measured congruence without an agent-only change, and does an agent-only change shift it with the habitat unchanged?
3. Do the intersections of the three conditions require separate treatment, or does one lever clear them?
4. Does the channel to the anchor keep registering after use, or does it saturate?

## 6. What an expert can check in twenty minutes

1. Read Fig 2 :29, Fig 9 :55-56, Fig 11 :40 and Fig 12 :111-112. Four short passages.
2. Check C2 against your own knowledge: is the three-condition decomposition right, and is there evidence that one lever clears the intersections?
3. Name one instrument that would separate constituency from donor influence on the same policy outcome, or say why none is possible.
4. Send the refutation. A failure is more useful than an endorsement. If you would rather not engage, no reply is needed.
