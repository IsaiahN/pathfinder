**Subject:** Check one shape claim about representation before you decide whether the method is worth reading

> Hi [name],
>
> I have one specific output I would like you to check against your own work. I am not asking you to read the theory first, and I am not asking you to agree with it. If the output does not survive, there is nothing else to look at.
>
> **The output (`general` structural, `specified` in application, not `grounded`):** the question "why are representatives responsive to donors and parties rather than constituents?" does not resolve under one lever. It is a union of at least three conditions: incentive asymmetry, information asymmetry, and the collective-action asymmetry between a concentrated minority and a diffuse majority. A single reform clears the region where only one condition holds and leaves the intersections standing. So the defensible shape is not a named reform. It is a set of conditions: a constituency anchor that none of the competing frames can move; a signal kept per district rather than averaged into one national number; a channel to that anchor that is actively maintained because channels decay while the ground does not; a change to the institutional habitat rather than an attempt to improve the agent in isolation; and selection between genuinely different rooms against that ground. The framework reports a shape, not a name. I am explicitly not claiming to know which single reform is best.
>
> **What would refute it, written before this run:**
> 1. a ground reading showing that policy already tracks the constituency as faithfully as the concentrated signals, with no channel decay and no anchor loss;
> 2. a controlled improvement of the agent alone, with the habitat unchanged, that produces a sustained rise in measured constituency congruence;
> 3. a single lever that clears the incentive, information and collective-action conditions, including their intersections;
> 4. an aggregate opinion measure that preserves every district-level residual that matters for welfare.
>
> If any of these holds, the relevant part of the shape is wrong and I will retract it. A one-line "this fails because..." is more useful to me than agreement. If you would rather not engage, no reply is needed.
>
> **If it survives, and only then:** here are the sources. The figures are the authority, not my summary of them.
> - Verbatim figure text with line numbers (local extract): `FRAMEWORK/reference/03-FIGURES_TEXT.md`
> - Empirical ground for the influence asymmetry, Gilens and Page 2014: https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/perspectives-on-politics/article/testing-theories-of-american-politics-elites-interest-groups-and-average-citizens/62327F513959D0A304D4893B382B992B
> - The collective-action mechanism, Olson 1965: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Logic_of_Collective_Action
> - The measurement vocabulary of representation: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Political_representation
>
> **If you want to use the method:** the formula is in the figures link. Run it on a problem you know well and can check against a ground. Write down what would refute your result *before* you run it. A reported failure is more useful to me than a reported success.
