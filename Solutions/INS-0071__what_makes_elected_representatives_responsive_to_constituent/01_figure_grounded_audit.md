## 0. The residual, in effect terms

Representatives act. The action tracks some signals and not others. The observed gap is that the signal from donors, party leadership and narrow organised interests reaches the action with higher fidelity than the signal from the diffuse constituency. Stated as a residual R: the difference between what the constituency would have the representative do and what the representative does, indexed per constituency slot, not averaged across the polity.

## 1. Domain census, before choosing any figure line

**R by arity, symmetry, scale.** Arity: at least four principals (constituents, donors, party, organised interests) bearing on one action. Symmetry: the principals are not interchangeable. Donors and organised groups are concentrated and their signal is high-amplitude per slot; constituents are numerous and their per-slot signal is weak (Olson 1965, opened 2026-11-23; Gilens and Page 2014, opened 2026-11-23). This is a threshold/comparison shape (⋛), not a symmetric conjunction. Scale: first order at the individual representative, second order at the institution that selects and maintains representatives.

**One line per domain (actors / currency / anchor).** A domain is admitted only if an anchor can be named.

| domain | actors | currency | anchor | type |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| D1 constituency responsiveness | representative, constituents | votes, opinion-policy congruence | measured congruence of policy with constituency preference, independent of the representative's own account | partition boundary: what counts as the ground |
| D2 elite and organised-interest influence | donors, party, organised interests, lobbyists | contributions, endorsements, coordinated pressure | measured independent impact of those inputs on policy (Gilens and Page 2014) | union with D1, not a separate ground; same policy outcome data |
| D3 verification and outreach | expert readers, this author's two seats | agreement and residual | the ground, never agreement | shares one evidence pool in this simulation |

**Habitat, enumerated not composed (not counted as a fourth domain).** Everything in contact with R: constituents, primary electorate, donors, party organisation, organised interests, district boundaries, campaign finance rules, legislative staff, polls, media, oversight bodies, then what contacts those. Fig 11 :43: "The habitat is enumerated, never composed." It has no anchor of its own, so by the census rule it is not a domain. It is the surroundings of D1 and D2. Calling it a separate witness would be a category error and is retracted in section 7.

**Partition or union.** Union. Fig 9 :5: "Disagreements that do not shrink with effort, where each rule keeps working well on a different subset, are not one hard question but several well-formed ones. Split it rather than search." A case can be in more than one condition at once. Fig 9 :56: "A single lever clears the region where only one condition holds and leaves the intersections standing, because the second condition still binds."

**Order by dependency (Fig 3).** Establish the anchor before judging responsiveness; describe the residual before choosing a frame (Fig 9 :27); settle the first-order representative-to-constituent link before the second-order institutional link; refuse the agent-only reading before minting any reform (Formula step 2).

## 3. Findings F1 to F9 (one loop per domain, verdicts separate)

### F1 - There is a legitimate ground; this is not a frame-only question (D1, D3)

Fig 2 :29: "An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks." Fig 2 :36: "An anchor is legitimate when the question is constitutively about it." Fig 1 :23: "The ground enters as the only metric."

The question is constitutively about what constituents get, so the constituency's preference and the policy that results are the anchor. Measured opinion-policy congruence is an instrument for that anchor, available in the political-science record (Political representation, Wikipedia, opened 2026-11-23; Stimson, MacKuen and Erikson 1995 as described there). **Verdict: `triangulated`** across Fig 2 and Fig 1 (low overlap between the ground definition and the metric rule), with an independent world witness in the measurement literature. Mode `general`. **Refuter:** show that no instrument, at any resolution, returns anything that fails to resolve between constituent and donor influence on policy. Then there is no anchor and this finding fails.

### F2 - Keep the residual per slot; do not average the constituency into one number (D1)

Fig 1 :19: "The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated." Fig 1 :22: "Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average." Fig 1 :28: "A system that averages across objects has thrown away the only signal that says where to look."

A single national "public opinion" number hides the live residuals of specific districts and groups, while donor and interest-group signals are concentrated per slot. Gilens and Page (2014) report that "economic elites and organized groups representing business interests have substantial independent impacts on U.S. government policy, while average citizens and mass-based interest groups have little or no independent influence." That is exactly an aggregation asymmetry, not a claim that constituents have no preferences. **Verdict: `triangulated`** (Fig 1 plus the world measurement, different evidence pools). Mode `general`, `specified` when applied to district-level accountability. **Refuter:** a measurement showing that an aggregate opinion series preserves every per-slot residual that matters for district welfare, with no loss of the signal that says where to look.

### F3 - The representative cannot score its own responsiveness (D1, D3)

Fig 1 :27: "A frame cannot score itself with a quantity it also produces." Fig 10 :93: "A ground with a reputation to protect is a ground someone benefits from, and its keeper is thereby disqualified."

A representative's own "constituent service" metric is frame-internal. A poll commissioned by the campaign, or a "public opinion" figure authored by the party, is an interested keeper of the reading. Fig 10 :59: "an interest in the outcome is unavoidable, an interest in the reading is disqualifying." Fig 10 :60: "Where a stake cannot be removed, declare it. An undeclared interest is modelled anyway and an unnamed model runs unchallenged." **Verdict: `triangulated`** across Fig 1 (agent metric) and Fig 10 (seat condition), two different figures with low overlap. Mode `general`. **Refuter:** exhibit an instrument whose reading cannot be moved by the actor it grades and that is not authored by any interested party. Then the self-scoring bar is met and this finding narrows.

### F4 - The channel decays; the ground does not (D1, habitat)

Fig 10 :24: "the ground does not decay, the channel does." Fig 10 :28: "you have lost touch". Fig 10 :52: "Bring a characterised residual, never the source."

Elections, staff, polls, town halls and disclosure rules are the channel to the constituency. They go stale, saturate and pool readings. The constituency's welfare does not decay because a poll was not run. The output of a dead channel is "you have lost touch", not a verdict on what constituents wanted. *Decoding Tacit Knowledge* (opened 2026-11-23): "When contact isn't possible, the best you can send is a plain description of what you'd expect to see, because anyone can check that against the world." **Verdict: `triangulated`** (Fig 10 plus the cognition essay; the essay is by the framework author, so overlap is high and the corroboration is weak, recorded as such). Mode `general`. **Refuter:** a demonstration that a representation channel, once installed, cannot become stale or saturated, and that no maintained seat is needed to keep it readable.

### F5 - Change the habitat, not the agent (habitat, D1)

Fig 11 :40: "A system cannot be made more capable by improving the agent in isolation. An improvement that does not change contact changes nothing, however much it improves." Fig 11 :39: "capability is a property of agent-and-habitat, never of the agent alone." Fig 11 :49: "An actor's valence is relative to the residual, not a property it carries."

Electing "better people" without changing contact is the named agent-only failure. The levers that change contact are the institutional habitat: electoral system, district boundaries, primary rules, finance rules, disclosure, oversight, staff resources. Olson (1965) supplies the mechanism for why the habitat favours concentrated minorities: "concentrated minor interests will be overrepresented and diffuse majority interests trumped, due to a free-rider problem that is stronger when a group becomes larger." The accountability view in the political-science record adds that representatives are "held accountable if citizens can judge whether the representative is acting in their best interest and sanction the representative accordingly." **Verdict: `triangulated`** across Fig 11 and Olson (genuinely low overlap: a metatheory line and a 1965 political-economy argument). Mode `specified` when a particular lever is proposed. **Refuter:** a controlled improvement of the agent alone (training, selection for virtue) that produces a sustained rise in independently measured congruence with no habitat change. That would break Fig 11 :40 at the ground.

### F6 - The question is a union compound; unbundle before searching (D1, D2)

Fig 9 :54: "And when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again." Fig 9 :55: "A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones." Fig 9 :56: "A single lever clears the region where only one condition holds and leaves the intersections standing, because the second condition still binds."

"Donors, parties, narrow groups" names at least three conditions: incentive asymmetry (contributions versus votes), information asymmetry, and collective-action asymmetry (concentrated versus diffuse). A lever that only changes finance clears the incentive-only region; the intersection where party discipline and organised information still bind remains standing. The Library of Babel essay (opened 2026-11-23) states the same search rule independently: "Perhaps your problem was a bundle or conflation of several problems masquerading as one? You will need to search all of those problems individually." The Wikipedia account of Olson's critique records Lohmann's information-asymmetry alternative, which is a second condition rather than a refutation of the first. **Verdict: `triangulated`** (Fig 9 plus the essay plus the political-economy account; the essay shares the framework author, so weight is reduced but the structure is the same). Mode `general`. **Refuter:** a single institutional lever shown, by a ground reading, to clear the incentive, information and collective-action conditions including their intersections. Then the union claim is wrong.

### F7 - Selection must be against the ground and between differing rooms (D1, habitat)

Fig 7 :35: "A room's agreement is variation, not a verdict. Selection between rooms is where the ground votes." Fig 7 :36: "The next room must differ from the last, and match the residual it surfaced." Fig 7 :42: "the next room you book is the direction you travel".

Adding more representatives of the same closure is one long room: motion, no progress. Elections are selection between rooms only when the alternatives differ and are picked against the residual the last room surfaced. Fig 11 :61: "The seat's power is asymmetric. Introduction is available; exclusion is not." A designer can introduce a new actor or instrument into the habitat; it cannot subtract the fact of contact. **Verdict: `single-source` for the figure line (Fig 7), weakly corroborated by the political-science accountability view.** Mode `specified`. **Refuter:** a chain of identical rooms whose agreement is shown to increase correspondence with the independent constituency anchor. That would make agreement a verdict and break Fig 7 :35.

### F8 - Do not manufacture witnesses (D3)

Fig 2 :31: "Independence, between two frames, is how little they draw on the same evidence." Fig 2 :35: "Alignment is triangulation, not negotiation. A mirror cannot correct you." Fig 2 :40: "agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool". Fig 2 :42: "Buy independence in proportion to the cost of being wrong." Fig 8 :53: "Residual first, frame second."

A list of readers chosen for likely agreement is a room selected to concur. Fig 2 :40 also governs this very audit: the second seat in `11_audit.md` shares this author's evidence pool, so its agreement is internal consistency, not a second witness. **Verdict: `triangulated`** across Fig 2 and Fig 8 (alignment geometry versus residual-first lookup, low overlap). Mode `general`. **Refuter:** show that agreement among frames drawing on one pool can settle a fact about the world. Then the valence rule fails.

### F9 - Naming the single reform is `gap` at the figure level (all domains)

Fig 9 :32: "you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself." Fig 6 :23: "No frame escapes its own closure. The bound moves only on contact with a frame whose closure differs." Fig 8 :39: "So a search that finds nothing is never a proof of absence."

The figures yield the shape and the conditions, not the name of the reform. Fig 6 :30: "The description travels. Fails by being sharpened into terms only we can read. Cast it in effect terms." So the honest output is an effect description, for example "an arrangement in which the constituency signal reaches the action through a channel none of the competing frames can move", and then a lookup in empirical institutional research. This is `unreached`, not `unreachable`: Fig 8 :39 forbids reading the absence of a figure line as proof that no answer exists. **Verdict: `gap` for the naming question; `triangulated` for the limit** (Fig 9 plus the canonical figures README, which says the framework reports "a shape, not a name"). Mode `general`. **Refuter:** derive a specific optimal reform from the figures alone, with its ground settlement, without an import from a differing frame. Until that is done, any named reform is the author's contribution and must be recorded as such.

## 4. Pre-registered refuter (written before the conclusion)

Per Seat Map :287: "And a falsifier written before the run is a design instrument, not only an honesty device." Fig 5 :65: "make the sought-for shape an ORDINARY TERM". The shape claim is refuted if any of the following holds:

1. A ground reading shows that representatives' policy already tracks the constituency with the same independence and fidelity as the concentrated signals, with no channel decay and no anchor loss. F4 and F5 fail.
2. A controlled improvement of the agent alone, with no habitat change, produces a sustained increase in independently measured constituency congruence. Fig 11 :40 fails.
3. A single lever clears the incentive, information and collective-action conditions, including their intersections, against the ground. F6 fails.
4. An aggregate opinion measure preserves every district-level residual that matters for welfare. F2 fails.
5. The figures alone yield a specific optimal reform, settled by measurement, with no import from a differing frame. F9 is withdrawn.

If any fires, the affected finding is retracted in this log and the outreach packet is withdrawn until repaired. Until one is run, nothing here is `grounded`.

## 7. Strains and re-census

- **Fig 11 capability claim versus delegate/trustee models.** Political science models responsiveness as conduct of the agent (delegate, trustee, politico). Fig 11 says capability lives in agent-and-habitat. Re-census: those models are frames for describing conduct, not the ground mechanism. Different objects, so no contradiction. Not strained.
- **Interested anchor.** "Public opinion" is partly produced by the polling and media habitat. Re-census: the anchor is the underlying preference and policy outcome, not any single reading of it; instruments can be wrong on axes they do not measure (Fig 2 instrument rule). Strain named, handled by requiring an unmovable reading.
- **⋛ as threshold relation between two quantities.** Here the comparison is between a constituency preference and an action, which are quantities at the level of a district-policy slot. Named as `specified`, not smoothed into a general claim.
- **After re-census: no hidden strains.** The one live limitation is F9, which is a `gap` by design, not a strain.

## 8. Verdict summary

The figure-derived shape is: an unmovable constituency anchor read per slot; a channel maintained against decay; capability located in the agent-and-habitat rather than the agent; a union of at least three conditions that must be unbundled before any lever is chosen; and selection between differing rooms against the ground. Agreement among interested frames is disqualified as a verdict. Specific reform names are `gap` at the figure level and belong to empirical institutional research. Everything is `general` or `specified`, nothing `grounded`. The round-trip gap R_T between this shape and any concrete policy proposal is measured qualitatively in `11_audit.md`.
