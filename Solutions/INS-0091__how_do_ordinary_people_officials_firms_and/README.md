## What an answer would look like (bound as an ordinary term)

A candidate answer, written down before the run so it can accrue a residual like any other term:

1. It stays indexed per institution-slot, never aggregated into "social change" (Fig 1 :19).
2. It names, per domain, the actors, the currency and an anchor that does not update because a frame thinks something (Fig 2 :29).
3. It explains how a repeated local practice becomes a settled, composable atom at the institutional stratum (Fig 12 :104), and what crosses the scale membrane (Fig 4 :22).
4. It keeps the four named actor classes as a union of overlapping conditions, not a partition (Fig 9 :55), and refuses a standing list of who the good changemakers are (Fig 11 :51).
5. It states what would reverse it, written before the conclusion.

A mechanism that fails any of these is not the answer shape this folder is looking for.

## What is deliberately not here

- No personal emails of named individuals.
- No ranking of people by how likely they are to agree.
- No bulk-send plan.

Three reasons, from the figures. First: *"A room's agreement is variation, not a verdict. Selection between rooms is where the ground votes."* (Fig 7 :35). A list of agreeable recipients is a room, not an anchor. Second: *"disqualifying for a verdict: agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool"* (Fig 2 :40). Selecting for receptiveness manufactures the disqualifying correlation. Third: *"A verifier that can reconstruct the claim cannot verify it."* (Fig 10 :42). Sending the whole framework before asking for a verdict spends the reader's independence.

Legally: scraping personal emails for unsolicited outreach breaches GDPR and CAN-SPAM style rules in many jurisdictions. That is a practical failure on top of the structural one.
