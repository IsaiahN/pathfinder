# Outreach draft — check the output before you decide about the theory

**Use:** one-to-one, or as a cover note attached to a submission through the public venues in `03_public_contact_points.csv`. **Not** for bulk send.

**Hard precondition:** do not send until the shape claim and its refuter in `01` and `08` survive the reader's check. The output must survive validation or the two-stage offer collapses on contact.

---

**Subject:** Check one output about everyday practice and institutional change before you read the theory

> Hi [name],
>
> I have one specific output I would like you to check against what you already know about institutional change. I am not asking you to read a theory first, and I am not asking you to agree. If the output does not survive, there is nothing else to look at.
>
> **The question in effect terms.** Institutions at time t+1 differ from t in ways not explained by formal decisions. The residual is the difference per institution-site, not a single global "social change" number (Fig 1 :19: *"The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated."*). A global near-zero with one live site is a legal state. So the first claim is a bookkeeping claim: any answer that averages across sites has thrown away the signal.
>
> **The output (`specified`, not `grounded`).** Four things follow from the figures before any theory is named:
>
> 1. The four actor classes in the question, ordinary people, officials, firms and movements, are a union of overlapping conditions, not a partition. A single lever that works on one class leaves the intersections standing (Fig 9 :55: *"A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones."*).
> 2. There is no standing list of good changemakers. Whether an actor helps or harms an institution depends on the residual at that site (Fig 11 :51: *"No standing list of good actors survives a change of residual."*).
> 3. A practice changes an institution only if it changes contact at the site. An improvement that leaves contact alone changes nothing, however much it improves (Fig 11 :40: *"A system cannot be made more capable by improving the agent in isolation. An improvement that does not change contact changes nothing, however much it improves."*).
> 4. What settles at the local level becomes a building block at the institutional level; what crosses upward is a reapplicable method, not a recording of one occasion (Fig 4 :22: *"Only methods cross up. Only head starts come down."*).
>
> This is a **shape**, not a mechanism story. It does not say which protest, which rule, which procurement standard or which routine tips a given institution. It says what any answer must look like and what would refute it. The figures return a kind of thing, never the local answer (Fig 9 :32: *"you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself."*).
>
> **What I am not claiming.** Not that the framework names a specific institutional theory. Not that agreement among like-minded people changes an institution (Fig 2 :40: *"disqualifying for a verdict: agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool"*). Not that a movement can remove a habitat condition; addition and re-arrangement are available, subtraction is not (Fig 11 :61: *"Introduction is available; exclusion is not."*). Not that this is settled by measurement. It is a `specified` candidate.
>
> **What would refute it, written before this email:**
> 1. an institution changes reliably at a site while no practice changes contact there;
> 2. one lever clears all four actor-class conditions and leaves no residue in the intersections across three sites;
> 3. a recording of one occasion crosses upward and works as a method without re-derivation;
> 4. a standing list of actor classes predicts who is a benefactor across a change of residual;
> 5. a convention that fixes an outcome still behaves as a convention when checked against an outside reference;
> 6. a movement changes an institution by subtracting a habitat condition;
> 7. agreement inside one evidence pool changes an institution's trajectory with no independent reading and no non-updating ground.
>
> If any holds, I withdraw or narrow the claim. A one-line "this fails because..." is more useful to me than agreement. If you would rather not engage, no reply is needed.
>
> **If it survives, and only then:** here are the public sources. The figures are the authority, not my summary of them.
> - The figures and formula, public repository: https://github.com/IsaiahN/tether/tree/general-agent/figures
> - Full architecture: https://medium.com/@IsaiahNwukor/arrival-of-the-fittest-the-tether-architecture-1de7edf641ec
> - How Intelligence Actually Works: https://adventuresinml.substack.com/p/how-intelligence-actually-works
>
> **If you want to use the method:** the formula is in the figures link. Run it on an institution you know well and can check against a ground. Write down what would refute your result *before* you run it, and label the mode of what you get. The framework's own limit is that a loop cannot check the direction of its own question, so a preference for a favourite mechanism is invisible to the loop until it is written down as an ordinary term. A reported failure is more useful to me than a reported success.
>
> — Isaiah

## Why this version is ordered this way

| the move | figure-grounded reason |
|---|---|
| Output and refuter first, theory second | Fig 10 :52 *"Bring a characterised residual, never the source."* Fig 2 :45 *"Frames range against the ground. They do not negotiate with it."* |
| Residual indexed per site | Fig 1 :19 and :22. "Social change" is an average that hides the live slot. |
| Do not ask for agreement | Fig 2 :35 *"Alignment is triangulation, not negotiation. A mirror cannot correct you."* |
| Union, not a list of four causes | Fig 9 :55, :57. One lever leaves intersections standing. |
| No standing list of good actors | Fig 11 :51. Valence is relative to the residual (Fig 11 :49). |
| Refuter written before sending | Fig 5 :62-66: make the sought-for shape an ordinary term, then it is refusable by the same test. |
| Mode stated on every claim | `THE_FORMULA.md` mode rule. Nothing here is `grounded`. |
| Links only after the verdict | Fig 4 :22-25: send the generator, not a recording of one occasion. Fig 10 :42: a verifier that can reconstruct the claim cannot verify it. |
| No request for trust or prestige | *The Recursive Self-Improvement Trap*: *"Prestige can fund an action. It can give an action a budget to be tried. But it cannot settle an action. Only the ground can settle it."* |
| Offer the formula as a method to reuse | Fig 4 :22 *"Only methods cross up. Only head starts come down."* |
| Ask users to state their own ground and refuter | `THE_FORMULA.md` "Direction"; a frame cannot check the direction of its own question. |

**Two-stage discipline.** Stage 1: the reader checks the shape and its refuter without holding the framework's interior. Record that verdict before showing anything else. Stage 2 is opt-in: only after the verdict is recorded offer the three links, with the note that the figures, not the summary, are the authority. A stage 2 agreement is never pooled as corroboration, because by then the reader has joined the frame being checked (Fig 2 :40).
