## Wider literature that bears on this question

These are the substantive world sources actually read. Full provenance and quotes are in `10_source_ledger.csv`; the WATSON verdict is in `09_watson_answer.md`.

1. **New institutionalism** (Wikipedia, sociology). Explains organizational practice by legitimacy rather than efficiency, and notes that *"actors follow routines because they take a for-granted quality"*. This is the closest disciplinary statement of the residual: routines that are taken for granted are exactly what "everyday practice" means here. It also warns that there is no agreed definition of institution, which is a reason to stay with the residual rather than define the object away.
2. **Historical institutionalism** (Wikipedia, political science). Names gradual change modes: layering, displacement, conversion, drift and exhaustion. These are arrangements over held atoms, not new atoms, which is structurally the same as Fig 12 :117 (same atoms, different bond, different substance). It also records *"widespread noncompliance with the formal rules of an institution, prompting change"*, a direct instance of everyday practice preceding formal change.
3. **Practice theory** (Wikipedia, anthropology and sociology). Bourdieu: structure is *"constantly reproduced and transformed by historical practices"*, and *"social actors are not just shaped by their social world, they shape it as well."* Giddens's structuration has the same two-way form. This corroborates the recursion in Fig 12 :104 but does not supply a figure line; it is same-pool in method, low-overlap in field.
4. **Everyday resistance** (Wikipedia, political anthropology, after James C. Scott 1985). Infrapolitics is *"a dispersed, quiet, seemingly invisible and disguised form of resistance"* requiring *"little or no formal coordination."* This is the strongest corroboration of F3 (change contact quietly) and of the refusal to require a single coordinated lever.
5. **Socio-ecological system / panarchy** (Wikipedia, ecology). The system includes *"associated social actors and institutions"*; the adaptive cycle has cross-level linkages ("remember and revolt"), and knowledge *"often emerges with people's institutions and organisations"* nested across scales. Genuinely low overlap with institutional theory; corroborates the multi-scale recursion, not the institutional fact.
6. **Climate governance / adaptive governance** (Wikipedia, environmental governance). Adaptive governance *"refers to the ways in which institutional arrangements evolve to satisfy the needs and desires of the community in a changing environment"* and takes a *"bottom up" strategy*, with *"bottom up learning, as ideas are passed up from a local to national level."* This is the clearest outside-field witness for the direction of the crossing (Fig 4 :22).
7. **Animal culture** (Wikipedia, animal behaviour). Culture is *"the social transmittance of behavior among peers and between generations"*, and chimpanzee foraging techniques *"spread differentially... with substantial fidelity."* Genuinely low overlap; corroborates that a practice can be transmitted as an arrangement without any formal institution, and that "prestige" bias in who is copied is a real selection pressure. Used for mechanism shape only, not for human institutional claims.

The first four are one broad social-science pool and count as one witness, not four. Sources 5 to 7 are lower-overlap corroborators. The framework essays share the author with the figures and carry no independent witness weight.

## Distribution: offer, do not push

Figure-grounded reasons:

- **Fig 4 :22-25** — *"Only methods cross up. Only head starts come down. A recording carried upward looks like knowledge and is a description of one occasion."* Sending the full architecture before anyone has described a residual sends a recording. Lead with the shape claim and its refuter; offer links after.
- **Fig 10 :42** — *"A verifier that can reconstruct the claim cannot verify it."* A recipient who has absorbed the entire framework before being asked for a verdict has joined the frame being checked.
- **Fig 9 :27-28** — *"describe it before you go looking, or any frame you pick will seem to fit."* Ask what they would expect to see if the shape claim were true before giving them the article.
- **Fig 2 :40** — *"disqualifying for a verdict: agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool"* A send selected for people who already agree produces a convention, not corroboration.

**Recommended order in a reply:** (1) the shape claim and the seven refuters; (2) ask what they would count as a failure at a site they know; (3) record their verdict; (4) only then the three links, with the note that the figures, not the summary, are the authority.

## The two-stage offer

Stage 1 is Fig 10 :52 (*"Bring a characterised residual, never the source."*) and Fig 2 :45 (*"Frames range against the ground. They do not negotiate with it."*): the recipient checks the output without holding the framework's interior. Stage 2 is opt-in by construction, because once a recipient has absorbed the theory their agreement stops being evidence (Fig 2 :40). Stage 3 sends the formula as a method, which is permitted under Fig 4 :22, but each user still needs a ground and a self-written refuter, because `THE_FORMULA.md` "Direction" says a frame *"cannot check the direction of its own question."*

**Hard precondition:** stages 1 and 2 only work if the stage-1 output survives. The output in `01` and `08` is a shape with seven written refuters and a declared gap; it makes no claim to name a local mechanism. If it fails the reader's check, withdraw it rather than defending it.

**Submission constraint:** the AOM AI policy quoted above is the clearest public example of the practical limit on submitting simulated work. The honest move is to offer the shape and refuter as a methods or commentary note through an opt-in call, disclose the simulated status, and let the venue decide.
