## 0. The residual, stated in effect terms

The question asks "how do they reshape institutions." Stated as an effect: institutions at time t+1 differ from t in ways not fully explained by the formal decisions of officials. The residual is the unexplained difference per institution-slot:

> R_s = |Γ(b,a) − o′| at slot s, where s is an institution-site (a workplace, a agency, a commons, a court, a market).

Two constraints follow immediately and are kept for the whole audit:

1. **Index per slot, never aggregate.** Fig 1 :19: *"The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated."* Fig 1 :22: *"Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average."* The phrase social change is an average; the mechanism must be read per site. A global R near zero with one live slot is a legal state (Fig 1 :13).
2. **"Reshape" is ordered, not symmetric.** It is a production or a sequence (Fig 12 :112: *"A plan is a sequence. A goal is a comparison. Progress is a subtraction."*), not a symmetric join of "people + institutions." So the question is about a route, and a vocabulary holding only ingredients *"can name a state and cannot name a route to one"* (Fig 12 :111).

The sought-for shape is made an ordinary term, bound before the run (Direction in `THE_FORMULA.md`): an answer must (i) stay per-slot, (ii) name actors, currency and a non-updating anchor per domain, (iii) explain the crossing from practice to institution, (iv) treat the four actor classes as a union, and (v) carry its own refuter. That term accrues a residual like anything else below.

## 1. R by arity, symmetry and scale

- **Arity:** multi-place and at least four-way at the actor level (ordinary people, officials, firms, movements), plus a habitat, a residual and a time index. Four actor classes in one question is the signature of a union, not a partition (Fig 9 :55: *"A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones."*).
- **Symmetry:** the relation "X reshapes Y" is ordered. There is no symmetric bond that makes people and institutions interchangeable. Valence is relative to the residual (Fig 11 :49): the same actor can help one institution and corrode another. So the question cannot be answered by a standing list of "who are the agents of change."
- **Scale:** at least two strata. Everyday practice is one level; the institution is the next. Fig 12 :103: *"Composition is recursive, and the level decides what is irreducible."* Fig 12 :104: *"A settled molecule becomes an atom for whatever composes over it."* The passage between them is a membrane with a direction (Fig 4 :20: *"Only the actors, the currency and the anchor change."*).

**Partition or union:** union. D1 to D4 all touch the same residual at a site; D5 is the composed result; D6 is the channel that carries readings between them. Verdicts will be kept per domain and never averaged.

**Dependency order (Fig 3 :20: *"The chain is ordered by dependency, not by importance."*):** perception (D6) → vocabulary (can the practice be named as a term?) → objective (what counts as progress at this slot?) → planning (which act gets repeated?) → learn and carry (does it settle into an atom?). The usual break is between vocabulary and objective, a link with few instruments (Fig 3 :27: *"A link with no instrument is not a link that works. It is a link nobody has looked at."*). Fig 12 :114-115 names the error: what looks like a dictionary of affordances *"is a grammar."*

**Habitat enumeration (Fig 11 :48: *"You do not invent the list. You read it off the world, and what you cannot perceive or measure yet is the residual."*):** the habitat for D1 includes, at minimum, family, workplace, school, bureaucratic routine, credit and debt, and the built environment. Contact cascades (Fig 11 :52: *"And contact cascades."*); the residual that matters may be several steps out, so scoping to direct contact misses the change.

## 4. Findings F1 to F9

### F1 — The four actor classes are a union, not a partition (decisive for the question's form)

Fig 9 :55: *"A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones."* Fig 9 :56: *"A single lever clears the region where only one condition holds and leaves the intersections standing, because the second condition still binds."* Fig 9 :57: *"a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either."*

The question names four classes. Ordinary people also work inside firms and movements; officials are also people with everyday practices; firms lobby officials; movements recruit from workplaces. The four classes overlap at the same slots. So "which of the four does the reshaping" is the wrong object: a union compound whose intersections are specific compounds, not harder instances of one class.

**Mode:** `general` for the union structure, `specified` for applying it to these four classes. **Refuter:** a single lever that clears all four conditions and leaves no residue in the intersections, demonstrated at the ground across at least three institution-slots. If one exists, the union claim is false.

### F2 — No standing list of good changemakers (decisive for the actor list)

Fig 11 :49: *"An actor's valence is relative to the residual, not a property it carries."* Fig 11 :50: *"The same actor is a benefactor to one residual and a malfactor to another, so mutualism and parasitism are relations rather than kinds."* Fig 11 :51: *"No standing list of good actors survives a change of residual."*

Whether a firm, an official, a movement or a routine practice helps or harms an institution depends on the residual at that slot, not on the class. A protest that opens one institution can close another. A firm's standard can be a head start in one habitat and a recording of one occasion in another (Fig 4 :25).

**Mode:** `general`. **Refuter:** an invariant valence of one actor class across a documented change of residual at the ground. **Triangulated** across Fig 11 and Fig 2 :39 (*"Correlation has valence."*) with low overlap between the habitat census and the correlation law.

### F3 — Practice changes contact or it changes nothing

Fig 11 :39: *"capability is a property of agent-and-habitat, never of the agent alone."* Fig 11 :40: *"An improvement that does not change contact changes nothing, however much it improves."* Fig 11 :36: *"Where nothing maintains anything, intelligence is a label applied afterwards to a lineage that met its ground. Nothing was intelligent; selection ran."*

Everyday practice is the micro loop. A repeated act changes an institution only if it changes contact at the slot: who pays, what is counted, which rule is invoked, which resource moves. A practice that stays purely expressive, however sincere or numerous, does not move the institution unless it reaches the ground. This is the mechanism behind Scott's infrapolitics (world source W4): dispersed, disguised, low-coordination acts are powerful precisely because they change contact quietly.

**Mode:** `specified`. **Refuter:** an institution that changes reliably at a slot while no practice anywhere changes contact at that slot. **Triangulated** across Fig 11 (habitat) and Fig 7 :35 (selection between rooms), two figures with low overlap.

### F4 — Selection between rooms, not negotiation among rooms

Fig 7 :35: *"A room's agreement is variation, not a verdict. Selection between rooms is where the ground votes."* Fig 7 :36: *"A chain of mirrors is one long room. The next room must differ from the last, and match the residual it surfaced."* Fig 7 :37: *"Progress per handoff has two factors. Zero either and the chain runs in place:"* Fig 2 :35: *"Alignment is triangulation, not negotiation. A mirror cannot correct you."*

The "how" is not that actors agree and then the institution follows. Many practices are variation generated inside rooms; the ground selects between rooms. Progress per handoff needs both the bracket's fidelity across the handoff and the ground's pressure on the room (Fig 7 :38-39). Agreement inside a room is cheap and efficient for a convention (Fig 2 :41) but worthless as a verdict about the institution.

**Mode:** `specified`. **Refuter:** an institution whose trajectory is changed by agreement among frames drawing on one evidence pool, with no independent reading and no non-updating ground. **Triangulated** across Fig 7 and Fig 2, distinct figures.

### F5 — An institution is a settled molecule that becomes an atom

Fig 12 :103: *"Composition is recursive, and the level decides what is irreducible."* Fig 12 :104: *"A settled molecule becomes an atom for whatever composes over it."* Fig 4 :22: *"Only methods cross up. Only head starts come down."* Fig 4 :25: *"A recording carried upward looks like knowledge and is a description of one occasion."*

What crosses from everyday practice to the institution is a reapplicable method, not a recording of one success. A practice settles against the ground, compresses into a routine, and becomes an operand at the next level. The institution is then not a different kind of thing from the practice; it is the same loop at another stratum, with actors, currency and anchor renamed (Fig 4 :20). Where the arrangement is not recorded as a method, *"every lineage pays again"* (Fig 12 :108-110), which is why institutions must be re-taught to each generation.

**Mode:** `general` for the recursion, `specified` for institutions. **Refuter:** show a settled institution cannot serve as an atom for a higher composition, or show a recording of one occasion functioning as a method without re-derivation across a habitat boundary. **Single-source on the figure side** for the strict recursion claim (Fig 12), corroborated structurally by the world source on social-ecological nesting (W5), with low overlap.

### F6 — Official rules can codify or can become verdicts

Fig 10 :47: *"a convention that decides an outcome has stopped being one"* Fig 10 :48: *"a convention nothing can check is a constant the seat authored, carrying the seat's authority"* Fig 2 :33: *"The anchor must not update."* Fig 2 :34: *"If it does, panel 3 turns into panel 1: the anchor becomes another frame, everything drifts together, and the drift is coherent, which is why nobody notices."*

Official rule-making (D2) has a legitimate function: it can codify a method that has settled at the ground. Its failure mode is specific. A rule that merely coordinates a shared meaning is a convention and is fine. A rule that fixes an outcome stops being a convention and becomes a verdict without a ground. If the reference against which the institution is evaluated updates on the institution's own reports, everything drifts together coherently. Fig 11 :63: *"Which is why a managed ecosystem is permanent work rather than a solved state."*

**Mode:** `specified`. **Refuter:** a rule that keeps paying at the ground while its reference is the institution's own report and no external reading is taken. **Triangulated** across Fig 10 (seat) and Fig 2 (anchor), different figures.

### F7 — Movements import; they cannot subtract the habitat

Fig 6 :23: *"No frame escapes its own closure. The bound moves only on contact with a frame whose closure differs."* Fig 8 :51: *"Convergent derivation and adopted import look identical in the contents."* Fig 11 :61: *"Introduction is available; exclusion is not."* Fig 11 :63: *"Which is why a managed ecosystem is permanent work rather than a solved state."*

A movement is a frame whose closure differs from the institution's. It can move the wall only by import, and an import needs a source gate (provenance) and a shadow test (explains a gap). The power is asymmetric: an actor can be added, the habitat cannot be subtracted, because contact is a fact about the world that does not consult anyone. So "reshape" is addition and re-arrangement, never clean removal, and the work is permanent.

**Mode:** `specified`. **Refuter:** a movement that changes an institution by removing a habitat condition rather than by adding or re-arranging, or an import that moves the wall with no provenance and no shadow test. **Triangulated** across Fig 6, Fig 8 and Fig 11, three figures with distinct roles.

### F8 — Isomers, catalysts and cycles describe the composed result

Fig 12 :117: *"Same atoms, different bond, different substance."* Fig 12 :119: *"catalyst. A part that survives its own use."* Fig 12 :121: *"cycle. The output re-enters as input. The sign of the loop decides whether it closes, and a chain cannot close by construction."* Fig 12 :123: *"functional group. A substructure whose presence fixes the outcome. The group says what kind; the qualifier says which member."*

Two institutions built from the same actors, rules and resources but bonded differently are isomers: same atoms, different arrangement, different behaviour. Some parts are catalysts (a norm or office that survives its own use); some are spent. Some arrangements are cycles whose sign decides whether they close: a feedback that reinforces the old institution and one that transforms it share a chain form but differ in sign. The four actor classes are a functional group, not a list of kinds.

**Mode:** `general` for the structural types, `specified` for the institutional reading. **Refuter:** an institutional change produced by adding a genuinely new atom (then it is an import, not a re-arrangement), or two institutions with identical atoms and bonds yet identical behaviour in every slot.

### F9 — No figure names which practice wins: declared gap

Fig 9 :32: *"you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself."* Fig 13 :134: *"Unreachable is a verdict here, not a silence"* Fig 8 :39: *"So a search that finds nothing is never a proof of absence."*

After a genuine re-census, no figure line returns which specific practice, in which habitat, at which threshold, tips a given institution. The figures return the shape of the mechanism and the separator, not the local content. What is owed is a source-discriminating instrument: something that can distinguish a practice that is a method from one that is a recording of one occasion, and distinguish a convention from a verdict. The world source W9 (*The Hard Problem is About Source*) gives the metamerism analogy: an instrument that reads green *"cannot comment on the source."* The gap is reported as `unreached`, not as silence, and not as an invitation to name a favourite mechanism.

**Mode:** `general` for the gap verdict. **Status:** `gap`. **Refuter for the gap itself:** a figure line that does select a practice for a named habitat, or a ground measurement that settles one. Until then the answer stops here.

## 6. Pre-registered refuter

Written before the conclusion, per the standing instruction: *"a falsifier written before the run is a design instrument."* The shape claim is refuted if any of the following holds at the ground:

1. An institution changes reliably at a slot while no practice anywhere changes contact at that slot (breaks F3, Fig 11 :40).
2. A single lever clears all four actor-class conditions and leaves no residue in the intersections, across at least three institution-slots (breaks F1, Fig 9 :55-57).
3. A recording of one occasion crosses the membrane and functions as a reapplicable method without re-derivation (breaks F5, Fig 4 :25).
4. A standing list of actor classes predicts which class is benefactor across a documented change of residual (breaks F2, Fig 11 :51).
5. A convention that decides an outcome still behaves as a convention, checkable against an external reference (breaks F6, Fig 10 :47).
6. A movement changes an institution by subtracting a habitat condition rather than by addition or re-arrangement (breaks F7, Fig 11 :61).
7. Agreement among frames sharing one evidence pool changes the institution's trajectory with no independent reading and no non-updating ground (breaks F4, Fig 2 :40, Fig 7 :35).

If any fires, the shape is withdrawn or narrowed. Until one is run, no part of this may be labelled `grounded`.

## 9. Verdict summary

The shape survives: a union of overlapping actor conditions, indexed per institution-slot, where practice changes contact and is selected against a ground that does not update; what settles becomes an atom at the institutional stratum; addition and re-arrangement are available, subtraction is not; and no standing list of good changemakers holds. The specific local content does not arrive: which practice, where, at what threshold is a declared `gap`. Mode `specified` on the per-domain claims, `general` on the structure, not `grounded`, candidate. After re-census, four strains dissolved, one live, none smoothed over.
