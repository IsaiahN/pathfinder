## What the wider literature bears on the question

1. **The arrangement space is huge, so alternatives are the generic case.** Koonin 2017 and 2009 both state that the standard code is robust but far from optimal, with billions of more robust codes. This is a different evidence pool from the figures and corroborates Fig 13 :115 (closed set, open arrangements). It does **not** make the framework's verdict true; it agrees with it.
2. **Universality is not evidence of optimality.** The 2017 Annual Review attributes universality to frozen accident plus the HGT penalty, and eLife 2018 shows reassignment is tolerated through rescue mechanisms. This corroborates Fig 7 :12-13 (chain of mirrors).
3. **The code partly decides outcomes.** Freeland and Hurst 1998 shows the code is far better than chance on error load, which is why the "pure convention" reading strains against Fig 2 :43.
4. **Alternatives can work, but at a cost that evolution can repair.** Nature Communications 2026 and Science 2013/2022 show recoded organisms are viable yet initially costly, with fitness restored by directed evolution. This is the closest thing to the ground instrument the shape asks for (Fig 5 :42, a bargain paid by what works).
5. **No single rival explanation has won.** The stereochemical evidence is weak (NCBI Bookshelf), coevolution and error minimization remain contested (Koonin 2017, Di Giulio 2024), and frozen accident remains a null hypothesis. This supports treating them as **union components**, not as a winner-take-all contest (Fig 9 :55).

Overlap warning: Koonin 2009, Koonin 2017 and the 2017 Annual Review share an author and an evidence pool. They count as **one witness**, not three. Freeland and Hurst, the recoding groups, and the NCBI Bookshelf chapter are separate pools.

## The two-stage offer

**(1)** Validate the output against the ground, **(2)** only if curious, read the theory that produced it, **(3)** use the formula recursively on other problems.

- **Stage 1 before stage 2** is Fig 10 :52 - *"Bring a characterised residual, never the source"* - and Fig 2 :45 - *"Frames range against the ground. They do not negotiate with it."* The recipient checks the output without holding the framework's interior.
- **Stage 2 is opt-in by construction.** Once a recipient has absorbed the theory, their agreement stops being evidence. So the verdict on stage 1 is recorded **before** any theory is shown, and later agreement is never pooled as corroboration (Fig 2 :40).
- **Stage 3 sends a method, which is permitted.** Fig 4 :22 - *"Only methods cross up. Only head starts come down."* The formula is a generator; giving it away does not give away an answer.
- **Stage 3 still needs a ground per problem.** `THE_FORMULA.md` "Direction": a frame *"cannot check the direction of its own question, because the shape a frame is searching for is not a term it holds."* Each user must write down what would refute their result before running it, and label the mode of what they get.

**Hard precondition:** stages 1 to 3 only work if the stage-1 output survives. As drafted, the claim is a shape, not a verdict; if a reviewer shows that the arrangement-space or convention sub-claim fails, the packet is withdrawn before any theory is offered.
