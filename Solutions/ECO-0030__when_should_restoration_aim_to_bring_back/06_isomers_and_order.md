# Union, functional group and order in the restoration decision

**Mode: `specified`.** Simulated derivation checked against the figures, not a ground reading (`05_what_shape_means.md` section 7).

## RESIDUAL

The question poses two aims as if they were alternatives in one choice. Two structural questions are owed: (1) is the pair a partition or a union, and (2) are historical-fidelity restoration and function restoration *isomers* under Fig 12, or members of a *functional group*, or neither? A third question follows: in what order must the decision be made?

## DOMAIN CENSUS

Atoms held for this question: actors (species and populations), conditions (light, water, temperature, season, terrain), relations (pollination, predation, competition, dependency), functions (nutrient cycling, productivity, resilience) and the historical record. Bonds held: sequence (→), comparison (⋛), conjunction (+), subtraction (−) and negation (¬). No new primitive is available without import.

## DERIVATION

### Q1 Partition or union?

- Fig 9 :4-5: "First: is there a fact here? Disagreements that do not shrink with effort, where each rule keeps working well on a different subset, are not one hard question but several well-formed ones. Split it rather than search."
- Fig 9 :54: "And when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again."
- Fig 9 :55-57: "A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones. A single lever clears the region where only one condition holds and leaves the intersections standing, because the second condition still binds. What remains is smaller and it is also categorically different: a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either."

The pair is a **union**. Three loops are owed: fidelity-only, function-only, and the intersection. The intersection is a specific compound with its own ground, not a compromise between the two.

- **Falsifier, written before the run:** if a census of sites finds an empty intersection (no site where both conditions bind), the union claim fails and the pair is a partition. That would let one lever clear the whole pile.

### Q2 Are the two aims isomers?

- Fig 12 :117: "isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."
- Fig 12 :124: "functional group. A substructure whose presence fixes the outcome. The group says what kind; the qualifier says which member."

As normally posed the two aims do **not** share a full atom set under one bond. A fidelity project is specified by a historical species list plus a reference condition; a function project is specified by a function threshold plus whichever actors deliver it. They share the substrate (actors, conditions, relations) but differ in which atoms are admitted. By Fig 12 :117 they are therefore **not isomers as posed**. They are two members of a **functional group**: both are "state → structure → state" arrangements over the same substrate, with different qualifiers (Fig 12 :124, "the group says what kind; the qualifier says which member").

The stronger form survives. If both aims are written over the full shared atom set {historical species, novel species, conditions, relations, functions}, differing only in which actors are bonded to which conditions and which function each arrangement produces, they **are** isomers: same atoms, different arrangement, different substance. That is the claim worth testing, because it says the difference between the aims is an arrangement, not a different inventory of nature.

- **Falsifier, written before the run:** if either aim requires an atom the other cannot express (a function no historical species can deliver, or a historical species with no function at all), the isomer claim fails and the functional-group reading stands. The retracted stronger claim in `01_figure_grounded_audit.md` retraction log is preserved there.

### Q3 What order governs the decision?

- Fig 3 :20-21: "The chain is ordered by dependency, not by importance. Each link consumes what the one above produces, so effort spent above the break arrives at a step that cannot use it."
- Fig 3 :25-26: "Perception has obvious failures and obvious tests. Planning has obvious failures and obvious tests. The link between them, having terms for what a situation offers and being able to say what would count as progress, has neither, so it attracts the least attention and the fewest instruments."
- Fig 3 :27: "A link with no instrument is not a link that works. It is a link nobody has looked at."

The objective link (link 3) must be settled before either aim is planned. That is the only order the dependency chain licenses. The debate over historical versus future aims is effort landing above a break.

After that:

1. Enumerate the habitat, never compose it: Fig 11 :43 "The habitat is enumerated, never composed." and Fig 11 :44 "List everything in contact with the residual, then what is in contact with those, and outward until the cascade stops mattering."
2. Describe the residual before choosing a frame: Fig 9 :27-28 "describe it before you go looking, or any frame you pick will seem to fit."
3. Test closure: Fig 8 :32 "Closure is idempotent. Composing inside a closed set never leaves it, so no search, however long, adds an atom. This one is definitional: it is what closure means."
4. Import only if the wall must move, with gates: Fig 6 :23 "No frame escapes its own closure. The bound moves only on contact with a frame whose closure differs." and Fig 6 :24 "Import from a live peer is debited against that frame's independence. A wrong atom costs more than starting from primitives."

- **Falsifier:** if a site reaches the correct aim without an instrumented objective, the dependency order is wrong.

### Q4 Is there an analogue that carries over cleanly?

Fig 12 :119: "catalyst. A part that survives its own use. Spent and regenerating parts behave differently across uses, and nothing records which a term is."

An ecosystem engineer (a beaver, a Sphagnum mat, a cordgrass clump) is a candidate catalyst: it survives its own use and changes the cascade for later arrivals. Wikipedia's *Ecosystem engineer* records Jones et al. 1994 and the cordgrass case is cited in Wikipedia's *Novel ecosystem*. But this is an **analogue**, not a figure-derived identification. Strain test: calling every engineer a catalyst would force Fig 12 :119 to govern quantities it does not (a catalyst is a part in a composition, not a species in a habitat). So the analogue is offered as corroboration only; the governing line for the habitat remains Fig 11, not Fig 12.

### Q5 The isolation law and the import law are not in competition

Fig 11 :66-67: "Isolation is not removal of the habitat. It is substitution of one habitat for another. Move the residual and you must reproduce everything its goal required, and you inherit the new habitat's actors, which you did not choose."

Fig 6 :14: "the only operator that moves the wall"

Earlier reading treated these as contradictory: if restoration cannot subtract habitat, how can an import move anything? Re-census dissolves it. Fig 11 governs the **habitat of an actor** moved to a new site; Fig 6 governs the **closure of a frame's library**. Different objects. The import adds an actor to a closure; the isolation law says the actor then meets a habitat you did not author. Both hold at once, and the consequence is that an assisted migration is a permanent work item, not a one-off fix.

- **Falsifier:** an introduced actor that arrives into a habitat fully reproduced from the source, with no inherited actors and no ongoing work, would break the isolation law as applied here.

## CONCLUSION

The pair is a union of three loops, not one question. As posed, historical-fidelity and function restoration are members of a functional group over the same substrate, not isomers; the stronger isomer claim requires both to be written over the full shared atom set and has a stated falsifier. The decision order is: instrument the objective, enumerate the habitat, describe the residual, test closure, import with gates. The catalyst analogue is corroboration only and was strain-tested out of the governing set. One earlier strain (Fig 11 versus Fig 6) was dissolved by re-census.
