## Independent domain census

I redid the census before reading the primary's findings, using the WATSON order: R by arity, symmetry and scale; one line per domain with actors / currency / anchor; partition or union; order by dependency.

- **R (arity / symmetry / scale).** The residual is a decision that does not shrink with effort. Arity: two conditions plus an intersection, so a **union compound**, not one hard question (Fig 9 :54). Symmetry: not a symmetric join; the aims are not swappable and one constrains the other. Scale: first order at species, conditions and relations; second order at function and trajectory; per site (slot), across historical-to-future time.
- **Domain 1, site habitat.** Actors: species, conditions, relations. Currency: contact and persistence. Anchor: measured on-site state, the ground. Question type: enumeration, not composition.
- **Domain 2, objective.** Actors: the seat, the target, the instrument. Currency: progress in the seat's own terms. Anchor: an instrument; absent, this link has no anchor yet. Question type: dependency head.
- **Domain 3, historical reference.** Actors: species list, reference ecosystem, record. Currency: compositional fidelity. Anchor: not the ground; a recording of one occasion. Question type: frame.
- **Domain 4, future function.** Actors: functions, projections, candidate species from other closures. Currency: function and resilience. Anchor: measured function on site, the ground. Question type: union member and import path.
- **Domain 5, verification.** Actors: practitioners and peer readers. Currency: agreement versus residual. Anchor: the ground, never agreement. In this simulated run all seats draw on one evidence pool, so this domain's independence is zero.

Order by dependency: instrument the objective before choosing an aim (Fig 3 :27); enumerate the habitat before selecting actors (Fig 11 :43-44); describe the residual before choosing a frame (Fig 9 :27-28); refuse before mint (formula step 2); settle first order before composing second order (Fig 12 :104).

My census matches the primary's five domains and its union reading. Different starting figures, same boundaries: low overlap between routes, so the agreement is weak but not circular within the framework. It is still one evidence pool.

## Round trip gap R_T

The Symbols table defines R_T as `gap(x, (T_E ∘ T_A)(x))`, the gap between what was sent up and what came back down. Fig 4 :30 requires it to be measured rather than assumed. I ran the round trip on the condensation the folder actually uses.

1. **Sent up (x):** the verbatim figure set, chiefly Fig 9 :54-57 (union), Fig 3 :27 (objective instrument), Fig 11 :49-51, :61-67 (valence, asymmetry, substitution) and Fig 8 :32 (closure).
2. **Abstraction (T_A):** the shorthand carried in `07_the_tether_answer.md`: unbundle the union, instrument the objective, enumerate the habitat, test closure, import when the wall must move, report permanent work.
3. **Concretisation (T_E):** a concrete site prescription: "restore species X and Y because they were here in 1850", or "plant whatever maximises carbon sequestration".

**R_T is nonzero.** Three things the shorthand holds do not survive the trip back down:

- the **per-site index** (Fig 1 :19) is lost when a global rule is handed down;
- the **instrument requirement** (Fig 3 :27) is lost when the aim is chosen before progress is defined;
- the **asymmetry and substitution law** (Fig 11 :61-67) is lost when the prescription assumes a solved state.

Conversely, one thing survives cleanly: actor value is relative to the residual, and the required function either is or is not in the historical pool's closure. That is the residue worth sending. R_T was **not** measured numerically, because no site ground reading exists; it is recorded qualitatively as the enumerated set of dropped constraints. Treating "the formula is the condensed version of the metalaws" as identity would set R_T to zero and would be the dead-reckoning error Fig 4 :30 names.
