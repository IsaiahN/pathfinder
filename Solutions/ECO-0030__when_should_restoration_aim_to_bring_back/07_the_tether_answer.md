# The Tether's answer: when to restore historical species, when to prioritise future function

**Mode: `specified` on the decision procedure, `general` on the structural shape, not `grounded`.** This is a shape and a route, not a site result. Simulated derivation checked for internal consistency against the figures (`05_what_shape_means.md` section 7), not a ground reading.

## 1. The residual it answers

The question does not shrink with argument. Each aim works on a subset of sites and fails on the other. Fig 9 :54 says "And when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again." The residual is a **union compound**, not one hard question, and the per-site signal must not be aggregated (Fig 1 :19, "The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated").

## 2. The answer: a per-site procedure, not a global either/or

The figures do not license "always restore historical species" or "always prioritise future function." They license a decision procedure. Verdicts are kept per domain and never averaged.

### Step 1: bind and instrument the objective

Fig 3 :27: "A link with no instrument is not a link that works. It is a link nobody has looked at." Write down what would count as progress at this site, in the site's own terms, and name the instrument. If there is no instrument, the chain stops here and no aim can be chosen responsibly. Most restoration argument lands above this break.

### Step 2: enumerate the habitat out to cascades

Fig 11 :44: "List everything in contact with the residual, then what is in contact with those, and outward until the cascade stops mattering." Actors, conditions and relations are read off the world, not composed from a preference list. Fig 11 :54 and :65 record why: DDT reached the eagles three steps out, and cane toads and kudzu were "the same move with the cascade unenumerated."

### Step 3: valence is relative to the residual

Fig 11 :51: "No standing list of good actors survives a change of residual." A historical species is not good because it is historical; a novel species is not bad because it is novel. Its value is for *this* residual at *this* site. This is the figure-grounded answer to the origin debate.

### Step 4: run the closure test

- Fig 8 :32: "Closure is idempotent. Composing inside a closed set never leaves it, so no search, however long, adds an atom. This one is definitional: it is what closure means."
- Fig 6 :23: "No frame escapes its own closure. The bound moves only on contact with a frame whose closure differs."

**Aim at historical species** when all of the following hold: the site's residual is compositional continuity (the goal itself is historical fidelity); the required functions are present in the historical species pool under current *and projected* conditions; the historical habitat can be approximately reproduced; and an instrument measures fidelity and contact. The historical list is then a legitimate head start (Fig 4 :22), not a blueprint.

**Prioritise future function** when any of the following holds: the required function is not in the historical closure under projected conditions; the historical habitat cannot be reproduced; the goal is measured functional performance or resilience rather than composition; or the cascade reaches beyond the species list. Here the wall must move, and the move is an import from a frame whose closure differs (assisted migration, functional analogues, species from another region), with provenance, a shadow test and a debit against independence (Fig 6 :24).

**The intersection** (both conditions bind) is its own loop. Fig 9 :57: "a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either." Do not compromise between the two aims; characterise the compound and settle it against the ground.

### Step 5: accept asymmetry and permanent work

Fig 11 :62: "You can add an actor and you cannot subtract the habitat, because contact is a fact about the world and does not consult you." Fig 11 :63-64: "Which is why a managed ecosystem is permanent work rather than a solved state." A restoration is not finished when the planting is done.

## 3. What it explicitly does not say

- Not that historical species are intrinsically better, or that non-native species are intrinsically harmful. Valence is relative to the residual.
- Not that the historical reference is the ground. It is a recording of one occasion (Fig 4 :25-26); the ground is the measured site outcome (Fig 11 :73).
- Not that the framework names the right species for a site. It returns a shape, not a name (Fig 9 :32).
- Not that agreement among practitioners settles the aim. "A room's agreement is variation, not a verdict" (Fig 7 :35).
- Not that the two aims are isomers as usually posed. They are members of a functional group; the stronger isomer claim over the full shared atom set has a stated falsifier (`06_isomers_and_order.md`).
- Not `grounded`. No site measurement was taken.

## 4. What would settle it: the ground

The ground is a measurement at the site, not a further argument. From *The Hard Problem is About Source*: an instrument that reads green "cannot comment on the source"; the spectrometer resolved it. Here the corresponding instruments are:

1. **Closure test:** does the historical species pool, under projected conditions, actually deliver the required function? An experiment or a monitored trial, not a list comparison.
2. **Objective instrument:** what is the measured progress variable, and does it move under each aim?
3. **Cascade instrument:** are second- and third-order effects on the target function detectable before introduction, as the DDT and cane-toad cases require?
4. **Source gate and shadow test:** for any imported actor, where did it come from, what gap does it explain, and what independence was spent? Fig 6 :24: "A wrong atom costs more than starting from primitives."
5. **Standing falsifiers:** a global rule governing all sites; a function supplied by exhaustive search of a pool that lacks it; a site outcome settled by agreement with no ground contact; a managed site reaching a solved state; a demonstration that isolation removes rather than substitutes a habitat.

## 5. The honest stop

**Unreached, not unreachable.** The procedure is a shape; the species names and function thresholds are supplied by the practitioner. "A minimum viable set of six terms is a human's careful guess at reality's compression axes; another sensorium's minimum set need not be those six" (*They're Scanning Books*). Declaring the vocabulary makes the choice visible, not neutral. The figure laws that do the most work here (valence, introduction/exclusion, isolation) are admittedly uncited framework claims, recorded as such in `05_what_shape_means.md` section 8. The answer stops at the limit of the available instruments and records the lineage so the stop is checkable. "Unreachable is a verdict here, not a silence" (Fig 13 :134).

## 6. One-paragraph version for outreach

> The Tether does not pick a side between historical species and future function. It says the question is a union of three cases, not one question: sites where historical fidelity is the only binding condition, sites where future function is the only binding condition, and the intersection where both bind. At each site it asks you to instrument what counts as progress before choosing an aim, enumerate the habitat out to cascades, and test whether the required function is inside the closure of the historical species pool under projected conditions. If it is, historical species are a legitimate head start. If it is not, searching that pool harder cannot supply the function, so function must come from a different closure, with provenance and an independence cost recorded. Species have no standing goodness: their value is relative to the goal. And the site is permanent work, not a solved state. What the framework offers is the shape of a checkable decision and the falsifiers written in advance. The species names are yours to supply and the ground is yours to measure.
