**Use:** one-to-one, or as a cover note attached to a submission through the public venues in `03_public_contact_points.csv`. **Not** for bulk send.

**Hard precondition:** do not send until the three sub-claims in `08_expert_verification_packet.md` have been checked locally. The output must survive validation or the two-stage offer collapses on contact.

**Subject:** One checkable claim about historical species versus future function in restoration

> Hi [name],
>
> I have one specific output I would like you to check against what you already know about restoration. I am not asking you to read the framework first, and I am not asking you to agree with it. If the output does not survive, there is nothing else to look at.
>
> **The output (`specified`, not `grounded`).** The question "should restoration bring back historical species or prioritise future function?" does not shrink with more argument, because each aim works on a different subset of sites. That is a union compound, not one hard question. The figure-grounded procedure that follows from this is:
>
> 1. State what would count as progress at this site, and name the instrument that measures it. Figure 3 :27 puts the failure plainly: "A link with no instrument is not a link that works. It is a link nobody has looked at."
> 2. Enumerate the site's habitat out to second- and third-order contacts, not just direct species lists.
> 3. Test whether the required function is in the closure of the historical species pool under current and projected conditions. If it is, historical fidelity is a legitimate and usually cheaper route. If it is not, searching that pool harder cannot supply it, and function must come from a different closure (for example assisted migration or functional analogues), with provenance and an independence cost recorded.
> 4. Report the site as permanent work, not a solved state.
>
> **What I am not claiming.** I am not claiming that historical species are intrinsically good or that non-native species are intrinsically bad. Their value is relative to the residual: "No standing list of good actors survives a change of residual" (Fig 11 :51). I am not claiming the framework names the right species for your site. It returns a shape, not a name. I am not claiming peer agreement settles the aim: "A room's agreement is variation, not a verdict" (Fig 7 :35).
>
> **What would refute the shape claim:**
> 1. a single global rule (historical-first or function-first) that governs every site with no residual left by aggregation;
> 2. a site where the required function is absent from the historical pool yet emerges from exhaustive search of that pool alone;
> 3. a site outcome correctly settled by practitioner agreement with no ground measurement;
> 4. a managed site shown to have reached a solved state needing no further work.
>
> I wrote these down before sending this. If any one holds, I will retract or repair the procedure. A one-line "this fails because..." is more useful to me than agreement. If you would rather not engage, no reply is needed.
>
> **If it survives, and only then:** the sources are below. The figures are the authority, not my summary of them.
> - Verbatim figure text with line numbers (local extract): `FRAMEWORK/reference/03-FIGURES_TEXT.md`
> - Canonical usage rule (local copy): `FRAMEWORK/reference/FIGURES_README_canonical.md`
> - BES, "Novel ecosystems: the new normal?": https://www.britishecologicalsociety.org/content/novel-ecosystems-the-new-normal
> - Ecology and Society, assisted migration governance (2026): https://ecologyandsociety.org/vol31/iss3/art11
>
> **If you want to use the machinery:** the formula is in the figures link. Run it on a site you know well and can check against a ground. Write down what would refute your result *before* you run it, and state the mode of what you get. A reported failure is more useful to me than a reported success.

## Why this version is ordered this way

| the move | figure-grounded reason |
|---|---|
| Output first, theory second | Fig 10 :52, "Bring a characterised residual, never the source." A recipient who has absorbed the framework before giving a verdict has joined the frame being checked. |
| Do not ask for agreement | Fig 2 :35, "Alignment is triangulation, not negotiation. A mirror cannot correct you." |
| Pre-registered refuter | The formula's "Direction" section: make the sought-for shape an ordinary term so it is refusable by the same test as everything else. |
| Mode stated on every claim | `THE_FORMULA.md`: "Mode. general · specified · grounded. State which." |
| No claim that origin decides value | Fig 11 :49-51, valence is relative to the residual. |
| No claim that the framework names species | Fig 9 :32, "you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself"; figures README, "a shape, not a name". |
| No "bombshell", no "true answer", no "trust" | Fig 10 :42, "A verifier that can reconstruct the claim cannot verify it." |
| Offer the formula as a method to reuse | Fig 4 :22, "Only methods cross up. Only head starts come down." |
| Ask users to state their own ground and refuter | Recursive Self-Improvement Trap: "the truly testable output would need to be derived from the real architecture or working the formula yourself." |
| One-to-one, no ranking by receptiveness | Fig 2 :40. Selecting people for likely agreement manufactures the correlation that makes their agreement worthless. |

## Two-stage offer

**Stage 1:** send the residual, the shape claim and the four refuters above. Ask what the recipient would count as a failure. Record that verdict **before** showing any theory.

**Stage 2, opt-in only:** if they are curious, offer the source links. The figures, not the summary, are the authority. Their later agreement is never pooled as corroboration (Fig 2 :40).

**Stage 3:** offer the formula as a method to reuse, with the instruction that each user writes a refuter and states a mode before running it.
