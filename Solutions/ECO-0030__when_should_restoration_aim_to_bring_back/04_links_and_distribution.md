## Distribution: offer, do not push

Figure-grounded reasons:

- **Fig 10 :52**, "Bring a characterised residual, never the source." Sending the whole framework before a recipient has described a residual sends a recording, not a generator. Lead with the shape claim and its refuters.
- **Fig 10 :42**, "A verifier that can reconstruct the claim cannot verify it." A recipient who has absorbed the framework before giving a verdict has joined the frame being checked.
- **Fig 9 :27-28**, "describe it before you go looking, or any frame you pick will seem to fit". Ask what they would expect to see at a site if the shape claim were true, before giving them the argument.
- **Fig 2 :40**, "disqualifying for a verdict: agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool". A send selected for people likely to agree produces a convention, not corroboration.
- **Fig 4 :22**, "Only methods cross up. Only head starts come down." The formula is a generator, so offering it as a method does not give away an answer.

**Recommended order in a reply:** (1) the shape claim and its four refuters; (2) ask what the recipient would count as a failure and record it; (3) only then the links, with the note that the figures, not this summary, are the authority.

## The two-stage offer

**Stage 1.** Validate the output against a site the recipient knows. No theory shown.

**Stage 2, opt-in only.** If curious, read the sources. Their verdict on stage 1 is already recorded and is never pooled as corroboration (Fig 2 :40).

**Stage 3.** Offer the formula as a method to reuse. Each user writes their own refuter and states their own mode before running it. The Recursive Self-Improvement Trap warns that "the truly testable output would need to be derived from the real architecture or working the formula yourself."

**Hard precondition:** stages 1 to 3 only work if the stage-1 output survives. The four refuters in `08_expert_verification_packet.md` are the test. If one fires, repair before sending.
