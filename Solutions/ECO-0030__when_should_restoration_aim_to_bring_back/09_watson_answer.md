## QUESTION

Decide, in effect terms, what a restoration decision should optimise for at a site: historical compositional fidelity or future ecological function. Option A: a global rule that always prefers historical species. Option B: a global rule that always prefers future function. Option C: a per-site procedure that unbundles the union, instruments the objective, tests closure and imports when the wall must move. What each leads to: A and B each work on a subset and fail on the other, and aggregating over sites hides the live signal; C yields a checkable shape with stated refuters and leaves species names to the practitioner and the ground.

## DOMAIN CENSUS

R by arity, symmetry, scale, then one line per domain with actors / currency / anchor, partition or union, and order by dependency.

- **R (the residual).** A decision that does not shrink with argument: each aim works on a different subset of sites. Arity: two conditions plus an intersection. Symmetry: not a symmetric join; the aims are not interchangeable and one constrains the other. Scale: first order at species, conditions and relations; second order at function, resilience and trajectory; per site, across historical-to-future time. Fig 9 names this a union compound: "a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either."
- **Domain 1, site habitat.** Actors: species, populations, light, water, temperature, season, terrain, pollination, predation, competition, dependency. Currency: contact and persistence. Anchor: measured on-site state, the ground. Type: part of the union; enumerated, never composed.
- **Domain 2, objective.** Actors: the seat (practitioner), the target, the measuring instrument. Currency: progress in the seat's own terms. Anchor: an instrument measuring progress; if none exists, this link has no anchor yet. Type: dependency head; governs the other domains.
- **Domain 3, historical reference frame.** Actors: historical species list, reference ecosystem, paleo and survey record. Currency: compositional fidelity. Anchor: not the ground; a recording of one occasion. Type: a frame.
- **Domain 4, future-function frame.** Actors: functions, services, climate projections, candidate species from other closures. Currency: functional performance and resilience. Anchor: measured function on site, the ground. Type: part of the union; import path.
- **Domain 5, verification / outreach.** Actors: restoration ecologists and practitioners. Currency: agreement versus residual. Anchor: the ground, never agreement. Type: a seat, not a witness.
- **Order by dependency.** Instrument the objective before choosing an aim (Fig 3); enumerate the habitat before selecting actors (Fig 11 :43-44); describe the residual before choosing a frame (Fig 9 :27-28); refuse before mint (formula step 2); settle first-order contact before composing second-order function (Fig 12 :104).

## VERDICT (from the figures)

The answer is a per-site procedure, not a global either/or. The pair of aims is a union compound to be unbundled into three loops (Fig 9 :54). Instrument the objective before choosing an aim (Fig 3 :27). Enumerate the habitat out to cascades (Fig 11 :44). Actor value is relative to the residual, so historical origin is not a valence (Fig 11 :51). If the required function is inside the closure of the historical species pool under projected conditions, historical fidelity is a legitimate head start. If it is not, no amount of searching that pool supplies it (Fig 8 :32), and function must come from a different closure with a source gate and an independence debit (Fig 6 :23-24). Introductions are available; subtraction of the habitat is not; the site is permanent work (Fig 11 :61-64). The framework supplies the shape; the practitioner supplies species and thresholds; the ground settles the outcome. This is `specified`, ungrounded, candidate.

- **cost if wrong:** a restoration budget spent on the wrong aim, plus a chronic cost from an ill-considered introduction, since the seat can add actors but cannot subtract the habitat (Fig 11 :62). Both costs are bounded and locatable because the refuters are written down in advance and the lineage is recorded. The avoidable part is spending an outside reader's independence by sending a conclusion instead of a generator; Fig 2 :42 prices that ("Buy independence in proportion to the cost of being wrong.").
- **undoable:** the decision procedure is fully undoable; revising what counts as progress and re-running the closure test costs little. Introductions are only partly undoable: actors can be added, and in practice removed, but the habitat and its inherited actors cannot be subtracted (Fig 11 :66-67). That asymmetry is the reason the procedure is not a one-off choice.
- **reversed by:** a ground reading, not agreement. A closure trial showing whether the historical pool delivers the function under projected conditions; a measured site outcome under each aim; a demonstrated global rule that leaves no residual at site level; a function supplied by exhaustive search of a pool that lacks it; a managed site reaching a solved state; or a demonstration that isolation removes rather than substitutes a habitat. Until one of these fires, the verdict stands as `candidate`.
