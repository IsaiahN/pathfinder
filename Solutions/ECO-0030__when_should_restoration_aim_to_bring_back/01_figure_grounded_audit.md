## 0. The residual, stated in effect terms

The question asks for a "when": when should a restoration project aim to bring back historical species, and when should it prioritise future ecological function?

Stated as an effect, not a topic: a decision is failing to resolve. Two aims, historical compositional fidelity and future functional performance, each work well on a different subset of sites. More argument between them does not shrink the disagreement. Each rule keeps working on its own subset and fails on the other. That is the signature Fig 9 names: "First: is there a fact here? Disagreements that do not shrink with effort, where each rule keeps working well on a different subset, are not one hard question but several well-formed ones. Split it rather than search." So the residual is a **union compound**, not one hard question.

What an answer would look like, written down and bound as an ordinary term before the run (formula step "Direction"): a per-site procedure that (a) states what would count as progress and instruments it, (b) enumerates the site's habitat out to cascading effects, (c) tests whether the required function is in the closure of the historical species pool under current and projected conditions, and (d) either uses historical species or imports from a different closure, with provenance and a debit against independence. If no figure line governs that procedure after a genuine census, the honest output is `gap`, not a forced fit.

**Scale note.** The decision is indexed per site, not per programme. Fig 1 :19: "The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated." Fig 1 :22: "Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average." A single national rule that averages over sites loses the only signal that says where to look. This is a constraint on the shape of the answer, not an ecological datum.

## 1. Domain census (before choosing any figure line)

**R by arity, symmetry, scale.** Arity: two conditions, historical fidelity and future function, plus the intersection where both bind. Symmetry: not a symmetric join. The two aims are not interchangeable, and choosing one constrains the other; the relation is a comparison or a union, not a conjunction that can be swapped. Scale: first order at the level of species, conditions and relations; second order at the level of function, resilience and trajectory; across time, from a historical reference to a projected future; per site (slot), not aggregated.

One line per domain, with actors / currency / anchor. A domain without a nameable anchor is not a domain yet.

| domain | actors | currency | anchor (nameable?) | type |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| D1 Site habitat | species, populations, light, water, temperature, season, terrain, pollination, predation, competition, dependency | contact and persistence of introduced actors | measured on-site state, the ground | part of the union; enumerated, not composed |
| D2 Objective / what counts as progress | the seat (practitioner), the target, the measuring instrument | progress in the seat's own terms | an instrument that can measure progress; if none, this link has no anchor yet | dependency head; governs the other domains |
| D3 Historical reference frame | historical species list, reference ecosystem, paleo and survey record | compositional fidelity | not the ground; a "description of one occasion" (Fig 4 :25) | a frame, not an anchor |
| D4 Future-function frame | functions, services, climate projections, candidate species from other closures | functional performance and resilience | measured function on site, the ground | part of the union; import path |
| D5 Verification / outreach | restoration ecologists, practitioners, peer reviewers | agreement versus residual | the ground, never agreement | seat, not a witness |

**Partition or union.** Union, not partition. Fig 9 :54: "And when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again." Fig 9 :55-57: "A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones. A single lever clears the region where only one condition holds and leaves the intersections standing, because the second condition still binds. What remains is smaller and it is also categorically different: a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either." The three regions are: historical-fidelity-only, future-function-only, and the intersection (both bind). The intersection is a specific compound with its own loop.

**Order by dependency (Fig 3).** Settle link 3, the objective, before choosing species or functions: "it can say what would count as progress here, in its own terms" (Fig 3 :10). Enumeration precedes selection: "The habitat is enumerated, never composed" (Fig 11 :43). Description precedes frame choice: "describe it before you go looking, or any frame you pick will seem to fit" (Fig 9 :27-28). Refuse before mint (formula step 2). Settle first-order contact before composing second-order function (Fig 12 :104).

## 3. Findings F1 to F10

Each finding carries its verbatim figure line and a refuter. Verdicts stay per domain and are never averaged.

### F1 The question is a union compound, not one hard question (`general`, triangulated)

Fig 9 :54: "And when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again."
Fig 9 :55-57: "A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones. A single lever clears the region where only one condition holds and leaves the intersections standing, because the second condition still binds. What remains is smaller and it is also categorically different: a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either."

The Library of Babel essay asks the same question in its own vocabulary: "Perhaps your problem was a bundle or conflation of several problems masquerading as one?" The world source is secondary; the figure line decides.

- **Domains:** D2, D3, D4.
- **Refuter:** show a single lever that governs every site with no intersection left standing, or a controlled comparison in which one global rule predicts site outcomes with no remaining residual. That would contradict Fig 1 :19 and Fig 9 :54.
- **Status:** triangulated (Fig 9 union rule plus Fig 1 per-slot rule, low overlap between them).

### F2 The decision is per site, not per programme (`general`, triangulated)

Fig 1 :19: "The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated."
Fig 1 :22: "Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average."

The public literature agrees from a different evidence pool: Wikipedia's *Ecological restoration* states "The goal of ecosystem restoration depends on the specific context of each location." Agreement from a different field is corroboration only; it does not decide.

- **Domains:** D1, D2.
- **Refuter:** a site-held residual that disappears under a global rule without loss of predictive power.
- **Status:** triangulated (Fig 1 plus the world source, different evidence pools).

### F3 Historical species have no standing goodness (`specified`, triangulated)

Fig 11 :49: "An actor's valence is relative to the residual, not a property it carries."
Fig 11 :50: "The same actor is a benefactor to one residual and a malfactor to another, so mutualism and parasitism are relations rather than kinds."
Fig 11 :51: "No standing list of good actors survives a change of residual."

Historical origin is a property of an actor's history, not a valence. A species that is a benefactor to a compositional-fidelity residual may be a malfactor to a function residual at the same site. The BES article makes the same point in ecological vocabulary: "Tolerance can be a valid management strategy in cases where: 1. Non-native species invasion is not expected to result in significant deviation from ecosystem operation due to its similar function to the species native to the ecosystem." This corroborates the figure, where function, not origin, decides.

- **Domains:** D1, D3, D4.
- **Refuter:** a species demonstrated to be beneficial for every residual at every site (valence intrinsic), or a standing list of good actors that survives a change of residual.
- **Status:** triangulated (Fig 11 valence rule plus Fig 9 :30-31 "your words are borrowed too, so describe the effect rather than the cause"; different figures).

### F4 The objective link is the unmeasured bottleneck (`specified`, triangulated)

Fig 3 :10: "it can say what would count as progress here, in its own terms"
Fig 3 :25-26: "Perception has obvious failures and obvious tests. Planning has obvious failures and obvious tests. The link between them, having terms for what a situation offers and being able to say what would count as progress, has neither, so it attracts the least attention and the fewest instruments."
Fig 3 :27: "A link with no instrument is not a link that works. It is a link nobody has looked at."

The historical-versus-function debate is mostly an argument at link 3: each side assumes a different objective. Fig 3 :19 says "Optimising a link that already works cannot rescue one that does not." Buying more species or more functions before instrumenting the objective spends budget above the break.

- **Domains:** D2 primarily; governs D1, D3, D4.
- **Refuter:** a project in which progress is measured, the objective link has a working instrument, and failure persists at the same rate. That would show link 3 is not the bottleneck.
- **Status:** triangulated (Fig 3 plus Fig 9 describe-first rule).

### F5 The historical list is a recording, not a generator (`specified`, triangulated)

Fig 4 :24: "downward: something that makes the next search cheaper. Never a replay of what worked once."
Fig 4 :25-26: "A recording carried upward looks like knowledge and is a description of one occasion. Replayed downward, it produces a system that repeats a past success and cannot produce a new one."

A reference ecosystem is a record of one occasion. It is legitimate as a vocabulary and as a head start (Fig 4 :22 "Only methods cross up. Only head starts come down"), and illegitimate as a blueprint whose past success guarantees future function under novel conditions. Wikipedia's *Ecological restoration* names the failure in practice: "myths of restoration such as carbon copy, where a restoration plan, which worked in one area, is applied to another with the same results expected, but not realized."

- **Domains:** D3.
- **Refuter:** a historical assemblage shown to regenerate the required function under novel conditions as a generator rather than as a replay, with the mechanism identified.
- **Status:** triangulated (Fig 4 plus the independent practice literature).

### F6 When the function is not in the historical closure, an import is owed (`specified`, triangulated)

Fig 8 :32: "Closure is idempotent. Composing inside a closed set never leaves it, so no search, however long, adds an atom. This one is definitional: it is what closure means."
Fig 6 :21: "MINT composes inside the closure and can never add an atom."
Fig 6 :23: "No frame escapes its own closure. The bound moves only on contact with a frame whose closure differs."
Fig 6 :14: "the only operator that moves the wall"

If the historical species pool under the site's projected conditions does not contain the required function, no amount of searching that pool manufactures it. The move is an import from a frame whose closure differs (assisted migration, functional analogues, species from another region), gated by provenance and a shadow test and "debited against that frame's independence" (Fig 6 :24). Fig 8 :39: "So a search that finds nothing is never a proof of absence." Absence from the current site is not proof that a species cannot function there; it is a reason to test, not a verdict.

- **Domains:** D3, D4.
- **Refuter:** a site where the required function is absent from the historical pool yet emerges from exhaustive search of that pool without an import; or an import that pays no independence cost.
- **Status:** triangulated (Fig 8 plus Fig 6; two different figures, low overlap).

### F7 Introduction is available, subtraction of habitat is not (`specified`, single-source on the core law)

Fig 11 :61-62: "The seat's power is asymmetric. Introduction is available; exclusion is not. You can add an actor and you cannot subtract the habitat, because contact is a fact about the world and does not consult you."
Fig 11 :63-64: "Which is why a managed ecosystem is permanent work rather than a solved state. And in neither mode is the habitat composed: unmaintained nothing composes it, maintained the seat adds to a habitat it still did not author."
Fig 11 :66-67: "Isolation is not removal of the habitat. It is substitution of one habitat for another. Move the residual and you must reproduce everything its goal required, and you inherit the new habitat's actors, which you did not choose."

Restoration can add actors. It cannot subtract the conditions, the climate, or the contact. Trying to recreate a past habitat is substitution, not removal: you inherit the new habitat's actors whether or not you chose them. So "restoration complete" is the wrong terminal state; a managed site is permanent work.

- **Domains:** D1, D4.
- **Refuter:** a restoration demonstrably subtracting the habitat rather than substituting one; or a managed site reaching a solved state with no further work required.
- **Status:** single-source for the asymmetry law (Fig 11 only). The import branch is triangulated with Fig 6 :23. I keep the core law marked `single-source` rather than borrowing weight.

### F8 Contact cascades; first-order enumeration misses the residual that matters (`specified`, triangulated)

Fig 11 :52-53: "And contact cascades. Scoping the habitat to direct contact is how DDT reached the eagles: first-order enumeration, second-order effect, and the residual that mattered was three steps out."
Fig 11 :65: "Introducing an actor for its cascade is ecosystem engineering, after Jones, Lawton and Shachak, 1994. Cane toads and kudzu are the same move with the cascade unenumerated."

A species-by-species historical list is first-order enumeration. The function residual may be three steps out. Wikipedia's *Biomagnification* independently records the DDT case: "A well known example of the harmful effects of DDT biomagnification is the significant decline in North American populations of predatory birds such as bald eagles and peregrine falcons due to DDT causing eggshell thinning in the 1950s." Wikipedia's *Ecosystem engineer* records the other half: "Kudzu, a leguminous plant introduced to the southeast U.S., changes the distribution and number of animal and bird species in the areas it invades."

- **Domains:** D1, D4.
- **Refuter:** a site where second- and third-order cascades are shown not to matter for the target function.
- **Status:** triangulated (Fig 11 plus two independent ecological sources).

### F9 The ground is measured site outcome, never agreement (`general`, triangulated)

Fig 2 :29-30: "An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks. A measurement, an experiment, a counter, a market, a patient outcome. If it moves when you push on it, it is not an anchor."
Fig 2 :35: "Alignment is triangulation, not negotiation. A mirror cannot correct you."
Fig 7 :35: "A room's agreement is variation, not a verdict. Selection between rooms is where the ground votes."
Fig 11 :73: "The habitat is not the ground. The habitat is where actors come from and it updates; the ground is what settles, and it does not."

A historical reference is a frame; a climate projection is a frame; a practitioner consensus is a frame. Only the measured site outcome settles which aim worked. Fig 2 :40: "disqualifying for a verdict: agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool".

- **Domains:** D5, and the anchor for D1 to D4.
- **Refuter:** a decision correctly settled by practitioner consensus with no ground contact and no measurement.
- **Status:** triangulated (Fig 2, Fig 7, Fig 11, three figures with different subject matter).

### F10 The framework returns a shape, not a site prescription (`general`, triangulated)

Fig 9 :32: "you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself"
Fig 13 :136-137: "It can say the question is not one question and must be split before it is searched again. It can characterise what is missing in effect terms, which is the description that travels to a frame that may hold it."
Fig 13 :134: "Unreachable is a verdict here, not a silence"

What survives is the procedure and the tests. The framework does not name the species list for a given site; that is the ground's work. A site where no figure line closes the residual is recorded as `gap` or `unreachable`, not filled with a plausible answer.

- **Domains:** all.
- **Refuter:** a derivation from the figures alone that names the exact species list for a named site.
- **Status:** triangulated (Fig 9, Fig 13, canonical figures README).

## 4. Pre-registered refuter (written before the conclusion)

Per Fig 2's discipline and the formula's "Direction" note, the sought-for shape is bound as an ordinary term and made refusable before the run. The shape claim is refuted if any of the following holds:

1. A single global rule (historical-first or function-first) is shown to govern all sites, with no residual left by aggregation (breaks F1, F2).
2. The required function is demonstrated to be absent from the historical closure yet supplied by exhaustive search of that closure alone (breaks F6).
3. The two aims are shown to be a partition rather than a union, with an empty intersection at every site (breaks F1).
4. A site outcome is correctly settled by agreement among practitioners with no ground contact (breaks F9).
5. Restoration is shown to subtract the habitat rather than substitute one, or a managed site reaches a solved state needing no permanent work (breaks F7).

If any fires, the procedure is withdrawn or repaired. Until one is run, no part of this may be labelled `grounded`.

## 5. Recommendation

1. **Split before you search.** Treat the question as a union compound and run three loops: historical-fidelity-only, future-function-only, the intersection (F1).
2. **Instrument link 3 first.** Write down what would count as progress at this site and name the instrument that measures it (F4). No instrument, no verdict.
3. **Enumerate the habitat before choosing actors**, out to second- and third-order contact (F8).
4. **Run the closure test.** If the required function is in the historical pool under projected conditions and the historical habitat can be approximately reproduced, historical fidelity is the legitimate, usually cheaper route. If it is not, prioritise function and import from a different closure, with source gate, shadow test and an independence debit (F5, F6, F7).
5. **Keep the verdicts separate.** Do not average a fidelity score and a function score into one "success" number. Fig 1 :19 forbids the aggregation that would hide the live slot.
6. **Report permanent work.** A managed site is not a solved state (F7).
