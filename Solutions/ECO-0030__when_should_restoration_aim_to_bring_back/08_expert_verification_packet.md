# Expert verification packet: the Tether on historical species versus future function

**What this is:** a claim in a form a restoration ecologist or practitioner can check. Mode, derivation and refuters are stated before the conclusion, so a reader knows what to look for and what would settle it.

**Mode: `specified` on the decision procedure, `general` on the structural shape, not `grounded`.** No site measurement is claimed. This is a shape and a route, not a result.

**Status: simulated derivation, not a ground reading.** It cannot settle the question it reports on (`05_what_shape_means.md` section 7).

## 1. The claim, in one paragraph

The question is a union of three cases, not one question: sites where historical compositional fidelity is the only binding condition, sites where future ecological function is the only binding condition, and the intersection where both bind. At each site the decision procedure is: instrument what counts as progress; enumerate the habitat out to second- and third-order contacts; recognise that an actor's value is relative to the residual; test whether the required function is in the closure of the historical species pool under current and projected conditions; use historical species when it is, and import from a different closure with provenance and an independence debit when it is not; report the site as permanent work. Species names and function thresholds are supplied by the practitioner. The framework returns the shape of the decision, not the answer.

## 2. Three checkable sub-claims

### C1 Framework-internal; checkable by reading Figure 9 and Figure 1

- **Statement:** the pair of aims is a union compound to be unbundled into three loops, and the decision must be made per site rather than averaged into a global rule.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 9 :54, "And when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again." Fig 9 :57, "a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either." Fig 1 :19, "The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated."
- **What to check:** are there real sites where both conditions bind, so that an empty intersection would falsify the union reading? Does a global rule actually leave a residual at the site level?
- **Refuter:** a census in which the intersection is empty; or a global rule that predicts site outcomes with no residual left by aggregation. Either breaks the claim.

### C2 Ecology-facing; checkable by a practitioner against a site they know

- **Statement:** historical origin is not a valence. Which species to use depends on the residual, and the required function may or may not lie in the closure of the historical pool under projected conditions.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 11 :51, "No standing list of good actors survives a change of residual." Fig 8 :32, "Closure is idempotent. Composing inside a closed set never leaves it, so no search, however long, adds an atom. This one is definitional: it is what closure means." Fig 6 :23, "No frame escapes its own closure. The bound moves only on contact with a frame whose closure differs."
- **What to check:** at a site you know, can you name a historical species that is beneficial for one goal and harmful for another? Can you name a function required under projected climate that the historical species pool cannot deliver? The BES article *Novel ecosystems: the new normal?* asks the same operational question: "if we can't compare a novel ecosystem's ecological, conservation and/or restoration value by similarities to existing or historical examples, what can we measure?"
- **Refuter:** a species beneficial for every residual at every site; or a site where the required function is absent from the historical pool yet emerges from exhaustive search of that pool without an import.

### C3 Framework-internal but with an ecological consequence; checkable against the record of introductions

- **Statement:** the seat can introduce actors, cannot subtract the habitat, inherits the new habitat's actors, and therefore has permanent work. First-order species lists miss cascades three steps out.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 11 :61-62, "The seat's power is asymmetric. Introduction is available; exclusion is not. You can add an actor and you cannot subtract the habitat, because contact is a fact about the world and does not consult you." Fig 11 :52-53, "And contact cascades. Scoping the habitat to direct contact is how DDT reached the eagles: first-order enumeration, second-order effect, and the residual that mattered was three steps out." Fig 11 :65, "Introducing an actor for its cascade is ecosystem engineering, after Jones, Lawton and Shachak, 1994. Cane toads and kudzu are the same move with the cascade unenumerated."
- **What to check:** does a known introduction (cane toad, kudzu, zebra mussel) fit the description of a cascade that was not enumerated? Is there a managed site that genuinely reached a solved state with no further work?
- **Refuter:** a demonstrable subtraction of the habitat rather than substitution of one; a managed site needing no further work; or a site where second- and third-order cascades are shown not to matter for the target function.

## 3. What is explicitly not claimed

- Not that historical species are intrinsically better, or that non-native species are intrinsically harmful.
- Not that the historical reference is the ground. It is a recording of one occasion (Fig 4 :25-26); measured site outcome is the ground (Fig 11 :73).
- Not that the framework names the species for a site. "you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself" (Fig 9 :32).
- Not that practitioner agreement settles the aim. "A room's agreement is variation, not a verdict. Selection between rooms is where the ground votes" (Fig 7 :35).
- Not that the two aims are isomers as posed. They are members of a functional group; the stronger isomer claim has a falsifier in `06_isomers_and_order.md`.
- Not `grounded`. No measurement was taken in producing this packet.
- Not independent corroboration for the Fig 11 laws. The canonical article lists valence, introduction/exclusion and isolation as claims with no cited prior source (`05_what_shape_means.md` section 8). Treat them as framework claims.

## 5. What would settle it

A ground reading, not more agreement. The instruments are: a closure trial showing whether the historical pool delivers the function under projected conditions; an objective instrument with a measured progress variable; a cascade instrument for second- and third-order effects; and a source gate and shadow test for any import. The standing falsifiers are listed in section 2 and are recorded before the run.

## 6. What an expert can check in twenty minutes

1. Read Fig 9 :54-57, Fig 1 :19 and Fig 11 :49-51. Four short passages.
2. Name one site you know where both conditions bind, or say why the intersection is empty. That decides C1.
3. Name one historical species whose value flips with the goal, and one required function the historical pool cannot deliver under projected climate. That decides C2.
4. Name one introduction where the cascade was not enumerated, or one managed site that reached a solved state. That decides C3.
5. Send the refutation. A failure is more useful than an endorsement.
