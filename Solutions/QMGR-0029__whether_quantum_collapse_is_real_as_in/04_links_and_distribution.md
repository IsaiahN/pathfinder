## What in the wider literature bears on this question

1. **The real-versus-apparent line is drawn in the review literature.** Bassi, Dorato and Ulbricht (2023) write that collapse models are *"different theories and not, strictly speaking, interpretations of quantum mechanics"*, while the Everett view *"changes the metaphysics without changing the physical theory."* That is the figure finding F2 in the field's own language: one side mints a term, the other does not. Cite this when explaining why the binary was split.
2. **The two positions are not merely a convention, but the direct test is far away.** Vaidman (SEP 2026) rejects the in-principle indistinguishability claim while conceding the reversal experiments are strictly gedanken. This is exactly the figure distinction between `unreachable-at-this-budget` and `underivable-in-principle` (Fig 8 :40-43), corroborated from a low-overlap field (philosophy of physics).
3. **The indirect edge is closing.** XENONnT (2026) excludes the original GRW parameter values, and Helou et al. (2016) and Toros and Bassi (2018) supply independent instrument families. Overlap warning: Carlesso, Donadi, Toros and Bassi share the Trieste group and much of the evidence pool with the 2023 review, so their agreement is one pool talking (Fig 2 :40), not several witnesses. Treat XENONnT, LISA Pathfinder and molecular interferometry as the low-overlap instruments.
4. **Carroll (2022) supplies the honest public framing.** The predictions are nearly identical, experiments may yet distinguish them, and the choice matters for theory construction even before it is settled. Use this, not a claim that one side has won.
