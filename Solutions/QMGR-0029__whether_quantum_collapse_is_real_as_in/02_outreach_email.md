**Subject:** One output to check before deciding whether collapse is real or apparent

> Hi [name],
>
> I have one specific output about the quantum measurement question and would like you to check it against what you already know. I am not asking you to read the framework first and I am not asking you to agree with either interpretation. If the output does not survive, there is nothing else to look at.
>
> **The output (`specified`, not `grounded`):** the question "is collapse real or apparent?" is not one binary. It bundles three conditions that need separate verdicts. (1) Is the Schrodinger dynamics exact, or is a stochastic localisation term added? (2) If a term is added, what are its parameters (the rate lambda and length r_C)? (3) Can branching plus decoherence account for definite outcomes with no new term? Averaging these into one answer throws away the only signal that says where to look.
>
> Under the framework's rules, an objective-collapse model is a **mint**: it adds a new term to the dynamics whose standing must be settled by measurement. The many-worlds reading mints no dynamical term; it keeps the same equation and supplies a rule connecting branches to experience. That asymmetry is the content of "real versus apparent." The framework does **not** decide which nature implements. It returns the shape any answer must have and the instrument that would close it.
>
> **The stop, stated honestly:** the direct discriminator, namely interference between macroscopic branches or reversing a measurement, is out of reach at this budget. It is not impossible in principle, and it is not silence: it is a described experiment. Meanwhile the indirect edge is live. Spontaneous radiation, force noise and interferometric contrast measurements are already bounding lambda and r_C, and a 2026 XENONnT result excludes the originally proposed GRW parameter values at 9.1 sigma while leaving coloured and dissipative extensions open.
>
> **What I am not claiming:** that collapse is real, that it is apparent, that this question is a mere convention, or that any exclusion has settled it. I am not claiming the framework names a primitive here. It returns a shape, not a name.
>
> **What would refute the output (written down before sending):**
> 1. a proof that the two readings are strictly empirically indistinguishable in principle, which would make this a convention and not a verdict;
> 2. a confirmed collapse signal at a specific parameter point, or a null result closing the whole viable parameter space including coloured and dissipative variants;
> 3. a working experiment that performs macroscopic branch interference or reverses a measurement, which would end the "unreachable at this budget" reading;
> 4. a re-census showing the binary is a single question rather than a compound.
>
> If any holds, I withdraw the output. A one-line "this fails because..." is more useful to me than agreement. If you would rather not engage, no reply is needed.
>
> **If it survives, and only then:** the sources are below. The figures are the authority, not my summary of them.
> - Verbatim figure text with line numbers (local): `FRAMEWORK/reference/03-FIGURES_TEXT.md`
> - Figure set and formula (public repo, branch `general-agent`): https://github.com/IsaiahN/tether/tree/general-agent/figures
> - Canonical usage rule: https://github.com/IsaiahN/tether/blob/general-agent/figures/README.md
>
> **If you want to use the machinery:** the formula is in the figures link. Run it on a problem you know well and can check against a ground. Write down what would refute your result **before** you run it, and state the mode of what you get. A reported failure is more useful to me than a reported success.
