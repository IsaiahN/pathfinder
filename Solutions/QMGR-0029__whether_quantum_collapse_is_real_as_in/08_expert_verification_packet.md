# Expert verification packet: real collapse or apparent collapse?

**What this is:** a claim in a form a quantum-foundations specialist can check. Mode, derivation and refuters are stated before the conclusion, so a reader knows what to look for and what would settle it.

**Mode: `specified`, not `grounded`.** No measurement at the decisive scale is claimed here; this is a shape, a route and an instrument list.

## 1. The claim, in one paragraph

The question "is collapse real, as in GRW-type models, or apparent, as in many-worlds?" is a union compound, not one binary. It splits into three questions with separate verdicts: is the Schrodinger dynamics exact or modified, what are the parameters if modified, and can branching plus decoherence account for definite outcomes with no new term. Under the figures, an objective-collapse model is a **mint** (a stochastic nonlinear term added to the dynamics whose standing must be settled by measurement), while many-worlds mints no dynamical term and supplies definite records by branching plus decoherence. The many-worlds and collapse positions are not strict isomers, because they do not share the same atom set; they are a functional group, with the qualifier being whether a term is minted. The anchor is an instrument, never agreement. The direct discriminator (macroscopic branch interference or reversal of a measurement) is unreachable at this budget but not impossible in principle, while the indirect instrument edge is live and is already bounding the collapse parameters. The figures return that shape and the instrument list; they do not pick a side.

## 2. Three checkable sub-claims

### C1 - framework-internal; checkable by reading the figures

- **Statement:** the union split and the per-domain verdict separation follow from the figures; the anchor is an instrument, and agreement is not a verdict.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 1 :19 *"The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated."* Fig 2 :29 *"An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks."* Fig 2 :35 *"Alignment is triangulation, not negotiation. A mirror cannot correct you."* Fig 2 :40 *"disqualifying for a verdict: agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool."* Fig 9 :54 *"And when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again."*
- **Refuter:** a proof or replicated result that the two readings are strictly empirically indistinguishable in principle (then the difference is a convention, not a verdict); or a re-census showing the binary is a single question rather than a compound.

### C2 - physics-facing; checkable against the literature by a specialist

- **Statement:** objective collapse mints a new dynamical term with free parameters (lambda, r_C), while many-worlds keeps the Schrodinger equation and adds no term; the two are a functional-group relation, not strict isomers. The direct discriminator is out of reach, the indirect one is live.
- **What to check:** Bassi, Dorato and Ulbricht (Entropy 2023) write that collapse models are *"different theories and not, strictly speaking, interpretations of quantum mechanics"* and that the Everett view *"changes the metaphysics without changing the physical theory."* Vaidman (SEP 2026) states that the claim that many-worlds is in-principle indistinguishable from ideal collapse *"is not the case"*, but the reversal proposals *"remain strictly gedanken experiments, far beyond the reach of current or foreseeable technology."* XENONnT (2026) reports that *"values in the originally proposed parameter ranges are experimentally excluded for the first time"* and that *"A wider parameter range remains to be probed for colored or dissipative model extensions."*
- **Refuter:** showing that many-worlds requires a new dynamical term, or collapse requires none (kills the functional-group reading); or a positive collapse signal at a specific parameter point replicated by low-overlap instruments; or a working macroscopic-interference reversal experiment (ends the "unreachable at this budget" reading).

### C3 - framework-internal derivation

- **Statement:** the isomers are the collapse variants among themselves (GRW, CSL, Diósi-Penrose, dissipative, coloured), not collapse versus no-collapse. Fig 12 :117 *"isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."* The functional-group fallback is Fig 12 :123 *"functional group. A substructure whose presence fixes the outcome. The group says what kind; the qualifier says which member."* The three collapse variants that differ in noise bond are exactly Fig 12 :117 in operation, and the XENONnT result distinguishes them behaviourally.
- **Refuter:** a collapse variant that can be written without the noise atom the others require, or two variants with different bonds producing identical behaviour under every instrument, kills the isomer claim.

## 3. What is explicitly not claimed

- Not that collapse is real, and not that it is apparent. The figures do not decide which nature implements.
- Not that the question is a mere convention. The direct test is out of reach, not impossible; the indirect test is running.
- Not that XENONnT or any other result has settled collapse models. It excluded a Markovian white-noise GRW parameter point; coloured, dissipative and Diósi-Penrose regions remain open.
- Not that majority agreement, prestige or a well-known defender decides anything (Fig 2 :35, :40).
- Not that GRW and many-worlds are isomers (that reading was strained and narrowed).
- Not `grounded`. Nothing here is asserted as settled by a measurement taken in this derivation.
