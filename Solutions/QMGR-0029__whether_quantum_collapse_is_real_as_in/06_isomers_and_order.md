## DERIVATION

### Q1 - Are the collapse models isomers among themselves? ACCEPTED

- Fig 12 :117: *"isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."*
- GRW, CSL, Diósi-Penrose, dissipative CSL and coloured CSL all hold the same atoms: a state, a noise driving localisation, a rate, a length, and an amplification mechanism. They differ in the noise bond (Poisson jumps, Gaussian white noise, Newtonian-potential correlation, finite-temperature dissipation, frequency cutoff).
- The 2019 review confirms the structural point in the field's own words: GRW and CSL are *"the most studied among the collapse models"* and differ in how the noise is modelled; the dissipative and coloured variants modify the same equation. Carlesso and Donadi (2019) write that *"Since these models are phenomenological, the value of their parameters can be bounded and eventually identified only by experiments."*
- ⇒ Within collapse model space the isomer claim is sound: same atoms, different bond, different predicted behaviour. The 2026 XENONnT exclusion of the Markovian white-noise GRW point while leaving *"colored or dissipative model extensions"* open is exactly Fig 12 :117 in operation: same ingredient list, different substance, decisive difference in behaviour.
- **Falsifier, written before the run:** if a proposed collapse variant can be written without the noise atom the others require, or if two variants with different bonds produce identical behaviour under every instrument, the isomer claim fails.

### Q2 - Are objective collapse and many-worlds isomers? REFUSED as written, narrowed

- GRW/CSL adds a stochastic nonlinear term; many-worlds keeps the Schrodinger equation and adds no term. They do not share the same atom set in Fig 12's sense, because the noise atom is present on one side only. The line strains when forced onto this pair.
- Re-census (the README rule: a strained reading has the wrong domain): Fig 12 :117 governs compositions over a held atom set. It therefore governs Q1, not this pair.
- The surviving weaker claim is a **functional group** (Fig 12 :123): *"functional group. A substructure whose presence fixes the outcome. The group says what kind; the qualifier says which member."* Both positions solve the same measurement problem from the same formal starting point; the qualifier is whether a term is minted.
- Operators table: *"a refutation is a negation, which is why a vocabulary without it can only accumulate."* The collapse term negates strict unitarity. Under negation the two are not rearrangeable into one another, which is the structural reason the isomer reading fails.
- **Falsifier:** if many-worlds is shown to require a new dynamical term, or collapse to require none, the functional-group reading fails and the stronger isomer reading must be reconsidered. Triangulated for the refusal (Fig 12 plus Operators table); single-source for the physics characterisation.
