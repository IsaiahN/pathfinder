## What an answer would look like, bound as an ordinary term

The question is decided by an instrument, not by a vote. State the sought-for shape as an ordinary term before searching: *collapse is real* means a physical localisation term has been added to the dynamics and a parameter (the rate lambda, the length r_C) has been settled by measurement; *collapse is apparent* means the dynamics is left strictly unitary and definite outcomes are supplied by branching plus decoherence, with every putative localisation term returning a null parameter. Under that binding, "real" and "apparent" are not two names for one frame. They differ in whether a term is minted, and the ground decides which standing the term has (Fig 2 :29-30, Fig 5 :43-44). The derivation returns that shape, not a verdict on nature.

## What is deliberately not here

- No personal emails of named individuals.
- No ranking of people by how likely they are to agree with either interpretation.
- No bulk-send plan.

Three reasons in the figures' own words:

1. **Agreement is not an anchor.** *"An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks."* (Fig 2 :29). An interpretation winning adherents does not move the experiment.
2. **Selecting for receptiveness manufactures the disqualifying correlation.** *"disqualifying for a verdict: agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool"* (Fig 2 :40). A list ranked by likely agreement is a list selected to agree.
3. **Showing the interior before asking for a verdict spends independence.** *"A verifier that can reconstruct the claim cannot verify it."* (Fig 10 :42).

Legally: scraping personal emails for unsolicited bulk outreach breaches GDPR and CAN-SPAM style rules in many jurisdictions. That is a practical failure on top of the structural one. All venues in `03_public_contact_points.csv` are public or opt-in.
