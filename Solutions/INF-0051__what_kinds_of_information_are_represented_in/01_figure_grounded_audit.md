## 0. The residual, stated in effect terms

A recording of neural activity is a set of observations `o'`. It contains many regularities: tuned responses, correlated pairs, low-dimensional manifolds, decodable stimulus identities. The question asks for the discriminator between two kinds of thing:

- A **represented regularity** is a term the system holds, that affords action, that can be wrong, and whose standing is settled by a ground that does not move when the frame changes its mind.
- A **mere statistical pattern** is a regularity in the observation that would be there whether or not the system uses it. It can be perfectly decodable and still carry no standing for the system.

**What each reading leads to.** If every decodable regularity counts, the library fills with quantities the frame produces itself and nothing can be refused: *"coverage, and compression achieved"* (Fig 1 :25), *"predicates minted, and credibility accrued"* (Fig 1 :26), *"They are frame-internal."* (Fig 1 :27). If a regularity must be settled by a ground, a genuine discriminator exists and some regularities stay open until an instrument arbitrates. The second reading is the one that leaves a falsifiable claim.

**Bound shape, written before the run** (formula step "Direction", Fig 5 :65): *"make the sought-for shape an ORDINARY TERM. Write down what an answer would look like, bind it, and let it accrue a residual like anything else."* The bound term is:

> A represented kind is a per-slot term in the system's library, joined by a typed bond to an action and to a ground, refusable by that ground. A statistical pattern is a regularity lacking at least one of those bonds.

This shape is ordinary, predicts, and can accrue a residual. It is therefore refusable by the same test as everything else (Fig 5 :66: *"residual like anything else"*). Nothing below consults which theory of representation the author prefers.

## 1. Domain census, before any figure line is chosen

**R by arity, symmetry, scale.** Arity: one regularity in one recording, under **two** conditions (what kinds exist, how a kind is told from a pattern), so this is a **union compound** and not one question (Fig 9 :54: *"when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again"*). Symmetry: a raw correlation is symmetric (Fig 12 :59: *"swap the operands. Does the meaning change? No."*), while a representation is ordered, it is *about* something and bears on an action (Fig 12 :72: *"swap them. Does it become a different thing? Yes."*). Scale: per object slot, per neuron, per population; never a global average (Fig 1 :19: *"The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated."*).

| domain | actors | currency | anchor | nameable? |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| D1, the neural system as frame | neurons, populations, Γ, belief `b`, action `a`, observation `o'`, decoder in the brain | per-slot residual `R`, prediction `Γ(b,a)`, terms minted | behavioural contact in a habitat | yes: the animal's own outcome |
| D2, the analyst frame | experimenter, recording instrument, decoder, statistical model | description length, explained variance, decodability, correlation | the same behavioural ground, held outside the recording | yes: an intervention or a behavioural measure |
| D3, the ground and habitat | the world, the task, other frames, other sensoria | settlement, contact, refusal | itself; must not update | yes: an outcome that can fail |

**Partition or union.** Union, with two loops that must be unbundled. The taxonomy loop (what kinds are possible) assumes the discriminator loop (what counts as represented), so one cannot be answered by searching harder inside the other (Fig 9 :55: *"What remains is smaller and it is also categorically different: a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either."*). The overlap is a compound with its own ground.

**Order by dependency (Fig 3).** Perception first: can the instrument resolve the regularity at all? Vocabulary second: does the system have terms for what the regularity affords? Objective third: can it say what counts as progress in its own terms? Then planning, then learn and carry. The chain usually breaks at the middle link: *"having terms for what a situation offers and being able to say what would count as progress, has neither, so it attracts the least attention and the fewest instruments."* (Fig 3 :25) And: *"A link with no instrument is not a link that works. It is a link nobody has looked at."* (Fig 3 :27) Decodability is a measurement above that break; it cannot rescue a missing vocabulary below it.

**Default constraint.** No new primitive is available. The six terms of Fig 13 and the seven bonds plus negation of the Operators table are the held set. Anything else is an import requiring a source gate and a shadow test.

## 3. Findings

### F1 — The discriminator is a ground, not a frame-internal score (decisive)

Fig 1 :23: **"The ground enters as the only metric."**
Fig 1 :24-27: **"These do not count:"**, **"coverage, and compression achieved"**, **"predicates minted, and credibility accrued"**, **"They are frame-internal. A frame cannot score itself with a quantity it also produces."**
Fig 2 :29: **"An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks."**
Fig 2 :45: **"Frames range against the ground. They do not negotiate with it."**

**Mode: `general`.** A regularity whose only credential is that a decoder extracts it, that it compresses the recording, or that it correlates with a stimulus, is scored with a quantity the analyst's frame produces. That is not evidence of representation. To count as represented it must range against an anchor that does not move when the decoder changes its mind: a behavioural outcome, an intervention, a patient outcome.

**Refuter:** show a case where decoder accuracy or compression alone settles representation status with no contact to anything outside the analyst's frame, and where the supposed anchor moves when the frame's prediction changes. That breaks Fig 1 :23 and Fig 2 :29 together.

**Status: triangulated.** Fig 1 (metric disqualification) and Fig 2 (anchor geometry) are different figures with low overlap: one is about what counts, the other about what may be ranged against.

### F2 — A representation is a held term that can be refused, not a reading of a projection

Fig 5 :51-52: **"A residual whose content is that a regularity the system holds is false owes a term, and the term it owes refuses another rather than explaining a gap."**
Fig 5 :53: **"refuted: a term we hold is wrong; a term is owed that says so"**
Fig 10 :18-19: **"a declared meaning is not a grounded one"**, **"→ install what can be violated"**
Fig 10 :49: **"Declaring a meaning does not ground it."**

Symbols table :20: `b` is *"What the system takes to be the case before it acts. Formed from its own history and from what it inherited, mixed by w. Not the observation."* Symbols table :32: `o'` is *"What actually happened."*

**Mode: `specified`.** The figure distinction maps onto the question: `o'` is the recording, `b` is the held term, `R` is the gap between `Γ(b,a)` and `o'`. A statistical pattern lives at `o'`. A representation lives at `b`: it predicts before action, and it can be refuted. If the claim "neuron N represents direction" cannot be made false by any outcome, it is a declared meaning, not a grounded one.

**Refuter:** exhibit a framework state that grants representation standing to a regularity while forbidding any observation that would revoke it. That breaks Fig 5 :53 and Fig 10 :19.

**Status: triangulated.** Fig 5 (the refuted kind of gap) and Fig 10 (conventions versus grounded claims) are independent figures with different subject matter.

### F3 — Per slot, never the population average

Fig 1 :19: **"The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated."**
Fig 1 :22: **"Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average."**
Fig 1 :28: **"The gap is measured per object, not per step."**
Fig 10 :26: **"stale percepts, dropped transients, saturated metrics, pooled readings"** (named as channel failures).

**Mode: `general`.** A population mean can be an excellent decoder and still hide the live single-slot residual that carries the representation. The discriminator must be run per neuron, per region, or per tracked object, not on a global statistic. A global R near zero with one live slot is a legal state (Fig 1 :13, *"a global R near zero with one live slot residual is a legal state"*).

**Refuter:** show that averaging across all recorded units leaves the representation claim unchanged, including when one slot's residual is live and the average is flat. That breaks Fig 1 :19 and :22.

**Status: triangulated.** Fig 1 (per-slot residual) and Fig 10 (pooled readings as channel failure) are separate figures that agree by different routes.

### F4 — Same ingredients, different bond: the activation vector is not the term

Fig 12 :5: **"A set of ingredients is not one. The bond is the rest of the information, and it is the half nothing was recording."**
Fig 12 :14-16: **"the ingredients are recorded and the arrangement is not"**, **"nothing downstream can tell them apart,"**, **"and nothing signals that anything was lost"**
Fig 12 :46: **"A bond is the typed relation between two operands. There are seven, they are not interchangeable, and which one holds is not recoverable from the operands."**
Fig 12 :111: **"A vocabulary without bonds can name a state and cannot name a route to one."**
Fig 12 :112: **"A plan is a sequence. A goal is a comparison. Progress is a subtraction."**

**Mode: `specified`.** Two neural populations with the same firing-rate profile can be different terms: one is bonded to an action (`⇒ production` or `→ sequence`), the other merely co-occurs (`+ conjunction`). A recording that names states but records no bonds cannot distinguish them, and *"nothing signals that anything was lost"*. The taxonomy of represented kinds is therefore not a list of activation patterns. It is a list of bond arrangements, levels, and contacts.

**Refuter:** exhibit two terms with identical ingredient sets and different bonds that the framework admits as the same term, or show that bond type is recoverable from the operand vector alone. That breaks Fig 12 :46.

**Status: single-source** for the bond claim (Fig 12 family). The Symbols table restates the same notation, so it is not a low-overlap witness. The independent corroboration is world-side, in §4 (Ritchie et al. 2019: information in a pattern is not the same as representation).

### F5 — The two questions are a union; unbundle before searching

Fig 9 :54: **"when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again"**
Fig 9 :55-58: **"What remains is smaller and it is also categorically different: a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either. It stops being a mixture and becomes a specific compound."**
Fig 3 :25: **"The link between them, having terms for what a situation offers and being able to say what would count as progress, has neither, so it attracts the least attention and the fewest instruments."**

**Mode: `general`.** "What kinds" and "how to distinguish from patterns" overlap: you cannot finish the taxonomy before you know what counts as a kind. They are not one hard question and not two independent ones. Run two loops, keep their grounds separate, then compose the result. Searching harder for a taxonomy with the discriminator still unset spends budget on a region already swept.

**Refuter:** show that the taxonomy loop closes without any discriminator, or that the discriminator loop returns a taxonomy without being told what a term is. That breaks Fig 9 :54 and Fig 3 :25.

**Status: triangulated.** Fig 9 (union splitting) and Fig 3 (dependency break) are low-overlap figures that agree on the same structural point.

### F6 — Decoder performance is a budget filter, never a witness

Fig 2 :31: **"Independence, between two frames, is how little they draw on the same evidence."**
Fig 2 :39-40: **"Correlation has valence."**, **"disqualifying for a verdict: agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool"**
Fig 2 :42: **"Buy independence in proportion to the cost of being wrong."**
Fig 9 :60: **"Use filters for the budget and witnesses for the verdict. Never let a filter hand you a verdict."**
Fig 7 :35: **"A room's agreement is variation, not a verdict. Selection between rooms is where the ground votes."**

**Mode: `general`.** Decoding accuracy, cross-validated classification, variance explained, and compression achieved all decide *where to look and how much to spend*. None of them decides *whether the system represents the decoded content*. A classifier and the animal's readout can agree because they draw on the same recording, which is one evidence pool. Buying a second, low-overlap frame (an intervention, a lesion, a behavioural readout, a different sensorium) is what makes the agreement mean anything.

**Refuter:** produce a case where a decoder's accuracy is accepted as a verdict about representation with no independent ground and no stated overlap. That breaks Fig 9 :60 and Fig 2 :40.

**Status: triangulated.** Fig 2 (correlation valence) and Fig 9 (filters versus witnesses) are distinct figures. Fig 7 corroborates on outreach only.

### F7 — Two currencies: a cheap description may be unaffordable to settle

Fig 12 :125-127: **"A composition costs in two currencies, and they do not add."**, **"Bits price the description. Actions price finding out whether it holds."**, **"They are in different units, so a system holding one accounting cannot compare them"**
Fig 1 :23 (above).

**Mode: `specified`.** A representation claim can be cheap to state (one tuning curve) and enormously expensive to settle (an intervention plus a behavioural assay). The reverse also occurs: a high-dimensional pattern is costly to describe and one refuting observation kills it. Because the two prices do not add, a ranking by description length cannot stand in for a ranking by evidential cost.

**Refuter:** show that a single scalar combines description cost and settlement cost without loss, and that a system using it notices the shortfall. That breaks Fig 12 :125-130.

**Status: triangulated.** Fig 12 (two currencies) and Fig 1 (only the ground is a metric) are different figures with a shared implication.

### F8 — A violability test, not an intuition test

Fig 2 :29 (above), Fig 10 :18-19 (above).
Fig 11 :39: **"capability is a property of agent-and-habitat, never of the agent alone."**
Fig 11 :40: **"An improvement that does not change contact changes nothing, however much it improves."**
Fig 11 :43: **"The habitat is enumerated, never composed."**

**Mode: `specified`.** Whether a regularity is represented is not a property of the neuron considered alone; it is a property of the agent-in-habitat contact. The ground-side test is an intervention that can change the animal's judgment in the direction the regularity predicts, or a removal that degrades it. The habitat (stimulus statistics, task, body, other populations, other frames) is enumerated from the world, not composed from a preferred taxonomy.

**Refuter:** produce an agent-only change in the representation claim that leaves contact with the ground unchanged and still moves the verdict. That breaks Fig 11 :40.

**Status: triangulated.** Fig 10 (install what can be violated) and Fig 11 (capability is contact) are low-overlap figures. The world-side corroboration is Salzman et al. 1990 and 1992 (§4).

### F9 — The framework returns a shape, not a name

Fig 9 :26-28: **"R's own predicates name the habitat that holds φ*"**, **"describe it before you go looking, or any frame you pick will seem to fit"**
Fig 9 :32: **"you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself"**
Fig 13 :134-135: **"Unreachable is a verdict here, not a silence"**, **"It cannot say which primitive is missing, because naming it would mean already holding it."**
Fig 8 :39: **"a search that finds nothing is never a proof of absence"**

**Mode: `general`.** The figures cannot name which recorded regularity is represented in a given animal. They can return the shape any candidate must have (per-slot, bonded, refusable, ground-settled, low-overlap) and the instruments that would close it. Naming a specific neuron as *the* representation is a binding from the neuroscience frame onto a held form, provenanced as such. It is not a derivation. Failure to find a representation is a verdict about reachability at this budget, not a proof that none exists.

**Refuter:** have the framework output the name of a specific cell or population as a derived primitive without an instrument or a provenanced import. That breaks Fig 9 :32 and Fig 13 :135.

**Status: triangulated.** Fig 9 (kind of thing) and Fig 13 (unreachable is a verdict) are independent figures.

## 4. World corroboration, secondary only

Figures decide; world sources corroborate and never overrule. Full details in `10_source_ledger.csv`.

- **Ritchie, Kaplan and Klein (2019), *The British Journal for the Philosophy of Science***: *"decodability is a poor guide for revealing the content of neural representations."* Also: *"merely predicting behaviour using decodable information is not enough"*. This corroborates F1 and F6 from a field with no stake in the framework. Field: philosophy of cognitive science. Low overlap with the figures.
- **Salzman, Britten and Newsome (1990), *Nature***: *"Microstimulation biased the animals' judgements towards the direction of motion encoded by the stimulated neurons."* Corroborates F8: an intervention that can move the behaviour is the kind of ground that settles a representation claim. Field: systems neuroscience.
- **Salzman, Murasugi, Britten and Newsome (1992), *Journal of Neuroscience***: *"The data demonstrate a functional link between the activity of direction selective neurons and perceptual judgements of motion direction."* Same direction, longer dataset. Overlap with 1990 is high (same lab and paradigm), so this is one witness, not two.
- **Stringer et al. (2019), *Nature***: *"A neuronal population encodes information most efficiently when its stimulus responses are high-dimensional and uncorrelated, and most robustly when they are correlated and lower-dimensional."* Corroborates F3 and F4: a population geometry is a constraint on a code, not a list of represented kinds. Field: experimental neuroscience.
- **Averbeck, Latham and Pouget (2006), *Nature Reviews Neuroscience***: *"variability in the brain is typically correlated, and although the exact effects of these correlations are not known, it is known that they can be large."* Corroborates F6: correlation is a property of the recording whose verdict-bearing status is unsettled. Field: computational neuroscience.
- **Quian Quiroga and Panzeri (2009), *Nature Reviews Neuroscience***: *"Decoding has the advantage of being similar to real behavioural calculations, but it may lose information contained in the neural responses."* Corroborates F7: two currencies, neither a verdict. Field: information theory applied to neuroscience.

No world source conflicts with the figure verdict. The conflict rule was not triggered. Ritchie et al. is the lowest-overlap corroboration and carries the most weight; the two Salzman papers share an evidence pool and are counted once; the framework essays (opened and listed in `05`) all share the author and evidence pool of the figures and carry little independent weight.

## 5. Pre-registered refuter

Written before the derivation was concluded, per Seat Map :287: *"a falsifier written before the run is a design instrument, not only an honesty device."*

The shape claim is refuted if any of the following holds:

1. A regularity that is decodable, compressible, and predictive, but has no bond to action or ground, is admitted by the framework as a representation. (Breaks F1 and F2.)
2. A population average is admitted as sufficient to locate a representation while a live single-slot residual remains flat in the average. (Breaks F3.)
3. Two regularities with identical ingredient sets and different bond types are admitted as the same term. (Breaks F4.)
4. The framework outputs the name of a specific neuron or population as a derived primitive without an instrument or a provenanced import. (Breaks F9.)
5. An agent-only change that leaves contact with the ground unchanged moves the representation verdict. (Breaks F8.)

If any fires, the shape is withdrawn. Until one is run, nothing above is labelled `grounded`. A one-line refutation from an outside frame is more useful than agreement.

## 6. Recommendation

1. Lead with the discriminator, not the taxonomy (F1, F5). A list of "kinds" without a ground test is a frame-internal inventory.
2. State the mode on every claim; an unmeasured quantity is *"a specification of what to measure and is worth having, it is not a verdict."* (Seat Map :281).
3. Run the test per slot and per frame, and declare overlap between frames before comparing them (F3, F6).
4. Ask an outside checker what would refute the four-gate discriminator before showing them any framework material (Fig 10 :52: *"Bring a characterised residual, never the source."*).
5. Keep the answer at the level of a shape. *"A recording carried upward looks like knowledge and is a description of one occasion."* (Fig 4 :25) Send the generator and the refuter, not a conclusion.

### Round trip and remaining strain

The condensation used in `07_the_tether_answer.md` is a four-gate discriminator (per slot, bond, refusability, low-overlap ground). The round trip `R_T` drops two things when this shorthand is brought back to a real recording: the exact bond type that the term bears (Fig 12 :46 says it is not recoverable from the operands), and the identity of the ground that may refuse it (Fig 2 :29 says the anchor must be named and must not move). `R_T` is therefore nonzero and qualitative; no ground reading exists to measure it numerically. Treating "four gates" as identical to the figure set would set `R_T` to zero and would be the dead-reckoning error named in Fig 4 :30.

**After re-census: no live strain.** One open item remains honest: which instrument in a given animal can discriminate among the sources of a recorded regularity is a `gap` at the figure level. The figures return the shape of that instrument (*"describe the effect rather than the cause"*, Fig 9 :30-31), not its name. *"Unreachable is a verdict here, not a silence"* (Fig 13 :134).
