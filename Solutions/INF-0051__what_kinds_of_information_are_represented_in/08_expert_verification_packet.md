# Expert verification packet: the Tether on neural representation

**What this is:** a claim in a form a neural representation researcher can check. Mode, derivation and refuters are stated before the conclusion, so a reader knows what to look for and what would settle it.

**Mode: `specified`, not `grounded`.** No recording, intervention, or behavioural measurement is claimed. This is a shape and a route, not a result.

**Status:** a simulated derivation checked for internal consistency against the figures, not a ground reading. It cannot settle the claim it reports on.

## 1. The claim, in one paragraph

A regularity in neural activity counts as a representation only if it passes four gates: per-slot location, a typed bond to action or to another held term, refusability, and settlement by a ground that does not move with the frame, checked by a second low-overlap frame. Decoding accuracy, compression, and population geometry are filters for where to look and how much to spend, not witnesses for what is represented. Kinds are bond arrangements and levels rather than activation patterns: two equal firing vectors with different bonds are isomers. The framework returns the shape and the falsifiers, not the name of a cell.

## 2. Three checkable sub-claims

### C1 — Ground versus frame-internal score; checkable by anyone who has seen a decoding paper

- **Statement:** decodability, compression, and explained variance cannot confer representation standing on their own. An outside ground must settle it.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 1 :23 *"The ground enters as the only metric."*; Fig 1 :27 *"They are frame-internal. A frame cannot score itself with a quantity it also produces."*; Fig 9 :60 *"Use filters for the budget and witnesses for the verdict. Never let a filter hand you a verdict."*
- **World check:** Ritchie, Kaplan and Klein (2019): *"decodability is a poor guide for revealing the content of neural representations."*
- **Refuter:** exhibit a case where decoder accuracy alone settles representation status with no contact to anything outside the analyst's frame, and where the supposed anchor moves when the frame's prediction changes.

### C2 — Same vector, different bond; checkable against population-recording practice

- **Statement:** two populations with the same firing-rate vector can be different represented kinds if one is bonded to action or to a downstream term and the other merely co-occurs. A clustering of activation vectors would merge them.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 12 :5 *"A set of ingredients is not one. The bond is the rest of the information, and it is the half nothing was recording."*; Fig 12 :46 *"A bond is the typed relation between two operands. There are seven, they are not interchangeable, and which one holds is not recoverable from the operands."*; Fig 12 :117 *"isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."*
- **World check:** Stringer et al. (2019) show that population geometry constrains a code but do not identify which dimension the animal reads out: *"A neuronal population encodes information most efficiently when its stimulus responses are high-dimensional and uncorrelated, and most robustly when they are correlated and lower-dimensional."*
- **Refuter:** recover bond type from the operand vector alone, with no intervention or behavioural observation; or show that the framework admits two different-bond terms with identical ingredients as the same term.

### C3 — Refusability and per-slot location; checkable by reading the figure and any single-unit dataset

- **Statement:** a represented term must be capable of being made false, and the test must be run per slot. A population mean can hide a live single-slot residual.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 5 :53 *"refuted: a term we hold is wrong; a term is owed that says so"*; Fig 10 :19 *"→ install what can be violated"*; Fig 1 :19 *"The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated."*; Fig 1 :22 *"Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average."*
- **World check:** Salzman et al. (1992): *"The data demonstrate a functional link between the activity of direction selective neurons and perceptual judgements of motion direction."* An intervention moved the animal's judgment in the direction encoded by the stimulated cluster.
- **Refuter:** exhibit a framework state that grants representation standing while forbidding any observation that could revoke it; or show that averaging across all units leaves the verdict unchanged when one slot's residual is live and the average is flat.

## 3. What is explicitly not claimed

- Not that decoding accuracy proves representation, and not that failure to decode proves absence.
- Not that the framework can name which cell in a given animal represents what. That is a binding from the neuroscience frame and needs provenance.
- Not that a single scalar combines description cost and settlement cost.
- Not a theory of consciousness and not a solution to Chalmers' hard problem. The source-discrimination reading is about instruments that distinguish causes of a reading, not about qualia.
- Not `grounded`. Nothing here was measured.

## 5. What would settle it

A source-discriminating instrument, not a further argument. From *The Hard Problem is About Source*: a colour instrument *"cannot comment on the source"*, and the case was *"proven wrong not by argument, but by the arrival of a better instrument."*

1. **Intervention:** move the candidate regularity and see whether the judgment moves as predicted.
2. **Removal:** abolish it and see whether behaviour degrades, controlling for the population mean.
3. **Bond discrimination:** separate two populations with equal vectors but different downstream roles.
4. **Independence:** a second frame that did not use the same recording ranges against the same ground.
5. The five falsifiers in `01_figure_grounded_audit.md` §5. Any one firing retracts the shape.

## 6. What an expert can check in twenty minutes

1. Read Fig 1 :23 and :27, Fig 9 :60, Fig 12 :5 and :46, Fig 5 :53 (six short passages).
2. Check C2 against their own population data: have they seen two equal vectors with different behavioural roles? If yes, the clustering taxonomy already fails.
3. Name one intervention that would distinguish a represented term from a decodable pattern in their preparation, or say why none is available.
4. Send the refutation. A failure is more useful than an endorsement.
