## Two things in the wider literature that bear directly on this question

1. **Decodability is not representation.** Ritchie, Kaplan and Klein (2019) show that a classifier can exploit information the brain does not use, that motion can decode better from V1 than from MT even though MT is the motion-specialised region, and that *"merely predicting behaviour using decodable information is not enough"*. This is the strongest low-overlap corroboration for the four-gate discriminator, and it is the check an outside reader is invited to run first.
2. **A geometry is a constraint, not a taxonomy.** Stringer et al. (2019) find a power-law eigenspectrum in mouse V1 and prove that a slower decay would make the code non-smooth. That is a fact about how a population is arranged. It does not name which dimension the animal reads out. Read beside the Salzman microstimulation results, it separates "information present in the pattern" from "signal the animal can be shown to use".

## The two-stage offer, restated

1. **Stage 1:** the four-gate claim, its mode, and the five falsifiers. Ask what the recipient would count as a failure. Record the verdict before showing any framework material.
2. **Stage 2, opt-in only:** if they are curious, offer the three local links. Say plainly that the figures, not this summary, are the authority.
3. **Stage 3:** offer the formula as a method to reuse, with the instruction that each user states a ground and writes a refuter before running it.

**Hard precondition:** stages 1 to 3 only work if the stage-1 claim survives. As written it is `specified` and ungrounded; it is not a claim that any particular cell has been shown to represent anything.
