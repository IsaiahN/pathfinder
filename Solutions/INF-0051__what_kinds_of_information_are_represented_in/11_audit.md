## Round trip gap `R_T`

The Symbols table defines `R_T` as the gap between what was sent up and what came back down, `gap(x, (T_E ∘ T_A)(x))`, measuring what the coarser description cannot hold. Fig 4 :30 requires it to be measured rather than assumed. I ran the round trip on the condensation the folder actually uses.

1. **Sent up (x):** the verbatim figure set, chiefly Fig 1 :23 and :27 (ground versus frame-internal scores), Fig 5 :53 (refusability), Fig 12 :46 and :117 (bond and isomer), Fig 9 :32 (shape, not a name).
2. **Abstraction (T_A):** the shorthand carried in `07_the_tether_answer.md`, the four gates: per slot, bond, refusability, low-overlap ground.
3. **Concretisation (T_E):** a concrete claim about a preparation, for example "population P represents motion direction because its vector decodes motion at 80 percent".

**`R_T` is nonzero.** Three things the shorthand holds do not survive the trip back down:

- the **typed bond** that makes the term a representation rather than a co-occurring pattern (Fig 12 :46 says the bond is not recoverable from the operands);
- the **identity and immovability of the ground** (Fig 2 :29), which the shorthand calls "an outcome" but which a real experiment must name and protect from updating;
- the **per-slot location** (Fig 1 :19), which the shorthand states as a rule but which a population-level analysis quietly drops.

Conversely, one thing survives cleanly: a held regularity must be refusable and must range against a ground that does not move. That is the residue worth sending. `R_T` was **not** measured numerically, because no ground reading exists; it is recorded qualitatively as the enumerated set of dropped constraints. Treating "four gates" as identical to the figure set would set `R_T` to zero and would be the dead-reckoning error Fig 4 :30 names. The honest form is a lossy condensation with a recorded gap, which is what `09_watson_answer.md` states.
