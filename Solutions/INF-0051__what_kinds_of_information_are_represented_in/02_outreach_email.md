# Outreach draft: check the discriminator before you decide about the taxonomy

**Use:** one-to-one, or as a cover letter attached to a submission through the public venues in `03_public_contact_points.csv`. **Not** for bulk send.

**Hard precondition:** do not send until F1, F3, F4 and F9 in `01_figure_grounded_audit.md` are stated in the recipient's own terms. The two-stage offer collapses on contact if the stage-1 output cannot survive a check.

---

**Subject:** One test for telling a neural representation from a statistical pattern

> Hi [name],
>
> I have one specific claim I would like you to check against what you already know. I am not asking you to read any theory first, and I am not asking you to agree. If the claim does not survive, there is nothing else to look at.
>
> **The claim (`specified`, not `grounded`):** a regularity in neural activity counts as a representation only if it passes four gates. (1) **Slot:** it is measured per neuron, per region, or per tracked object, not only in a population average. (2) **Bond:** it has a typed relation to an action or to another held term, not mere co-occurrence with a stimulus. (3) **Refusability:** something can make it false; a claim that no observation could revoke has no standing. (4) **Ground:** its standing is settled by an outcome that does not move when the decoder changes its mind, checked by a second frame that does not draw on the same recording.
>
> A regularity that fails any one of those is a statistical pattern *with respect to that claim*. It can be strongly decodable, highly predictive, and still be a pattern. Decoder accuracy is where to look and how much budget to spend, not whether the animal holds the term.
>
> **What this claim does not say:** that a high decoding accuracy proves representation; that a failure to decode proves absence; that the framework can name which cell in your animal represents what. It returns the shape any such term must have, not the name. Anything more specific is a binding from your field onto that shape and needs its own provenance.
>
> **What would refute it, written down before this message was sent:**
> 1. a decodable, compressible regularity with no bond to action or ground is admitted as a representation by the framework;
> 2. a population average is admitted as sufficient while a live single-slot residual is hidden in the average;
> 3. two regularities with identical ingredient sets and different bond types are admitted as the same term;
> 4. the framework names a specific cell as a derived primitive without an instrument or a sourced import;
> 5. an agent-only improvement that leaves contact with the ground unchanged moves the verdict.
>
> If any holds, I retract the claim. A one-line "this fails because..." is more useful to me than agreement. If you would rather not engage, no reply is needed.
>
> **If it survives, and only then:** here are the sources. The figures, not my summary of them, are the authority.
> - Verbatim figure text with line numbers (local extract): `FRAMEWORK/reference/03-FIGURES_TEXT.md`
> - Canonical usage rule: `FRAMEWORK/reference/FIGURES_README_canonical.md`
> - The eight-step formula and mode rule: `FRAMEWORK/figures/THE_FORMULA.md`
>
> **If you want to use the machinery:** the formula is in the figures link. Run it on a problem you know well and can check against a ground. Write down what would refute your result *before* you run it, and label the mode of what you get. A reported failure is more useful to me than a reported success.
