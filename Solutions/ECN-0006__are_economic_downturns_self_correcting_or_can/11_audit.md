## Independent domain census

I redid the census from the residual line alone, before reading the primary's conclusions, using the WATSON order.

- **R (arity / symmetry / scale).** The gap between predicted and observed spending, output and employment, indexed by slot. Arity: at least two conditions, because the same observed gap has more than one possible source. Symmetry: broken; the slots are not exchangeable, so a representative agent is a modelling assumption, not a reading. Scale: the private adjustment loop closes at one stratum; the policy response lives at another with different actors, currency and anchor.
- **Domain 1, private adjustment.** Actors: firms, households, workers, banks. Currency: prices, wages, rates, inventories. Anchor: realised transactions and employment. Nameable anchor present, so this is a domain.
- **Domain 2, policy / import.** Actors: treasury, central bank, legislature, foreign sector. Currency: fiscal and monetary impulses. Anchor: the same outcomes, from a closure containing a spender not derivable in Domain 1 at the relevant time.
- **Domain 3, measurement.** Actors: statistical agencies, econometricians. Currency: estimates and revisions. Anchor: data, with the caveat that the anchor is partly conventional. Low independence because one macro corpus is shared.
- **Union, not partition.** The two conditions overlap; a balance sheet episode is both a transmission failure and an absence of borrowers.
- **Dependency order.** Per-slot perception, then vocabulary for what the situation affords, then objective, then frame selection, then carry. The middle link has no instrument, which is where the debate stalls.

**How my census was reached differently.** I started from Fig 2's anchor test rather than the primary's residual test. Applying the anchor question ("what settles this, and does it move when a frame thinks about it?") forces the same three domains: an outcome anchor for private adjustment, the same outcome reached from a different closure for policy, and a measurement frame whose anchor is revision-prone. Starting from Fig 9's union/partition test gives the same compound boundary. Different starting figures, same domain boundaries: low overlap between routes inside the framework, so the agreement is weak but not circular within the framework. It is still one evidence pool.

## Round trip gap R_T

The Symbols table defines R_T as the gap between what was sent up and what came back down, measuring what the coarser description cannot hold. Fig 4 :30 requires it to be measured rather than assumed. I ran the round trip on the condensation this folder actually uses.

1. **Sent up (x):** the verbatim figure set, chiefly Fig 1 :19, Fig 6 :23 and :27, Fig 8 :32, Fig 9 :4 and :55, Fig 5 :59.
2. **Abstraction (T_A):** the shorthand carried in `07` and `09`: measure per slot, fix the closure, split depth from absence, bind the horizon, read a trend, refuse a flat residual.
3. **Concretisation (T_E):** a concrete claim about a named episode, for example "the 2008 to 2012 recovery was slow because of debt overhang, so fiscal stimulus was owed."

**R_T is nonzero.** Three things the shorthand holds do not survive the trip back down:

- **which closure is being fixed.** The shorthand says "fix the closure"; the concrete claim fixes it around the private sector, which is a choice, not a figure result. The equations do not select it.
- **the regime label.** The shorthand says "depth or absence"; the concrete claim names debt overhang. That name is an import from economics with provenance but no figure derivation, and it must be tested against the episode's data.
- **the horizon H.** The shorthand says "declared horizon"; the concrete claim silently picks a political or business-cycle horizon. Whether the residual is flat depends on that choice.

Conversely, one thing survives cleanly: a gap measured per slot, with a declared closure and horizon, is a greater discriminating instrument than an aggregate gap, and a flat residual points a refusal at the held term. That is the residue worth sending. R_T was **not** measured numerically, because no ground reading was taken; it is recorded qualitatively as the enumerated set of dropped constraints. Treating the formula as identical to the full figure set would set R_T to zero and commit the dead-reckoning error Fig 4 :30 names.
