## How the wider literature bears on the output

These are corroborations only. A conflict would send the derivation back to the domain census, never flip a figure verdict.

1. **The split is visible in the literature itself.** The IMF explainer states the classical position Keynes overturned ("free markets would automatically provide full employment") and the Keynesian response ("free markets have no self-balancing mechanisms that lead to full employment"). Lumen adds a third possibility that matters for the horizon binding: even if self-correcting, the process can be too slow to matter. Blanchard (JEP 2018) holds the natural-rate hypothesis as the null while giving weight to hysteresis alternatives. Read together, these are three positions on the same compound, which is what Fig 9 :4 predicts: disagreements that do not shrink with effort are several well-formed questions, not one hard one.
2. **The absence branch has a named mechanism.** Koo's balance sheet recession work and the Eggertsson and Egiev review both describe a state in which conventional monetary tools lose traction and discretionary government spending is the prescription that remains. Koo's phrasing, that GDP shrinks when someone saves and no one borrows, is a plain-language statement of F2: internal adjustment cannot add an atom when the private closure contains no willing borrower.
3. **The depth branch has its own defenders.** McGrattan and Prescott argue the 2008 to 2009 downturn is consistent with real-business-cycle models once intangible investment is counted. The RBC systematic review states that business cycles are "not a failure of markets to clear" but efficient responses to real shocks. Stadler, writing from inside that tradition, concedes real shocks explain less than a third of output fluctuations. These sources corroborate that the depth condition has a real literature, not a straw man, and that its own instruments are contested.
4. **A genuinely low-overlap corroboration.** Scheffer et al. (ecology and complex systems) report that recovery rate after small perturbations indicates proximity to a threshold. That is the same quantity F7 names as the empirical form of "self-correcting." Because it comes from a different field and a different evidence pool, it is the strongest non-circular world corroboration here. It still cannot decide which regime a given economy is in.
5. **Overlap warning.** Almost all economics sources above are in one field and share the standard macro corpus. Two papers citing the same Great Recession data are one witness, not two. The ledger records overlap per row. Agreement among them is not corroboration of a figure verdict; the figures decide the shape independently.

## Distribution: offer, do not push

- **Fig 10 :52:** "Bring a characterised residual, never the source." Lead with the split and the refuter.
- **Fig 10 :42:** "A verifier that can reconstruct the claim cannot verify it." Do not send the full framework before asking for a verdict.
- **Fig 9 :27:** "describe it before you go looking, or any frame you pick will seem to fit." Ask what the reader would expect to see in each regime before offering the literature list.
- **Fig 2 :40:** "agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool." Do not select recipients for likely agreement, and do not pool their later agreement as corroboration.
- **Fig 4 :22:** "Only methods cross up. Only head starts come down." The loop is a method; giving it away does not give away an answer.

**Recommended order in a reply:** (1) the two-condition split and the bound term; (2) ask what the reader would count as a failure in each condition; (3) only if curious, the figure extract and the canonical README; (4) the wider literature list, with overlap declared.
