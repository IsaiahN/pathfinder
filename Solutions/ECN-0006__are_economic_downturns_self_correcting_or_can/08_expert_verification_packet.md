# Expert verification packet: the Tether on downturn self-correction

**What this is:** a claim in a form a recession and recovery researcher can check. Mode, derivation and refuters are stated before the conclusion, so a reader knows what to look for and what would settle it.

**Mode: `general` on the shape, `specified` on the two-regime split. Not `grounded`.** No dataset was analysed here. This is a shape and a decision procedure, not an empirical result.

## 1. The claim, in one paragraph

The question "are downturns self-correcting, or can weak demand persist?" is a union compound of two conditions that are indistinguishable from inside the private adjustment frame: a reachability failure, where demand exists inside the frame but its channel is broken, and a genuine absence, where no source of final demand exists inside the frame at any resolution. The residual must be measured per slot, not as an aggregate. Once the private closure is fixed, adjustments inside it cannot create a spender it does not contain; if the spender is absent, an import from a frame whose closure differs is owed. "Self-correcting" becomes answerable only when bound as a term: does the per-slot residual trend to zero within a stated horizon and closure without an import? A flat residual points a refusal at the held term that keeps predicting recovery. The figures do not say which regime the world is in; that is a ground question.

## 2. Three checkable sub-claims

### C1 — framework-internal; checkable by reading Figures 1, 6 and 8
- **Statement:** the residual is per slot; closure is idempotent, so internal adjustment cannot add a missing atom; depth and absence look the same from inside.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 1 :19: "The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated." Fig 8 :32: "Closure is idempotent. Composing inside a closed set never leaves it, so no search, however long, adds an atom." Fig 6 :27: "Which is why some unreachability is depth and some is genuine absence, and from inside the frame the two look the same."
- **Refuter:** (a) all per-slot residuals trend down together while only the aggregate appears flat; (b) a within-closure mechanism is observed creating a new source of final demand after nationwide balance-sheet damage at the zero lower bound, with no public or foreign spender; (c) a single within-frame statistic reliably separates depth from absence before the policy decision. Any one breaks C1.

### C2 — economics-facing; checkable by a recession and recovery researcher
- **Statement:** the two conditions are real and are represented in the literature under different names, so the split is not an invention of this framework. The depth branch includes flexible-price and real-business-cycle accounts; the absence branch includes liquidity-trap and balance-sheet-recession accounts. Blanchard (JEP 2018) keeping the natural-rate hypothesis as null while giving weight to hysteresis is an instance of two-condition reasoning.
- **What to check:** are those characterisations faithful? Is there a single empirical mechanism that closes both conditions in every observed downturn, so the union claim is wrong? Does the literature already have a real-time regime discriminator that this packet ignores?
- **Refuter:** exhibit one mechanism that closes both conditions across downturns, or a validated real-time discriminator that makes the two regimes distinguishable from inside the frame. Either kills the union-indistinguishability claim and leaves the ordinary single-model reading standing.

### C3 — framework-internal derivation with an empirical hook
- **Statement:** the empirical form of "self-correcting" is a trend in the per-slot residual, and recovery rate is a candidate discriminator. Fig 5 :59: "a term whose residual PERSISTS AND DOES NOT TREND DOWN is the one to point the refusal at, which is the same shrinking test the selector already runs, read with the opposite sign." Fig 6 :32: "An instrument is not built from a description. It is improved from a worse instrument already returning something."
- **What to check:** Scheffer et al. (Nature 2009) report that systems near a threshold show "slower recovery from perturbations, increased autocorrelation and increased variance". Is that quantity measurable in macro time series at the frequency policy decisions are made? If not, the empirical hook is a proposal, not a result.
- **Refuter:** show two regimes with indistinguishable recovery-rate profiles that nevertheless require opposite policy responses. Then recovery rate cannot discriminate and C3's preferred ground variable is inadequate.

## 3. What is explicitly not claimed

- Not that markets never self-correct, and not that intervention is always needed.
- Not that the empirical question has been answered. No data were analysed.

## 5. What would settle it

1. A sectoral and regional decomposition of the output and employment gap with stated data vintages, showing which slots carry flat positive mass.
2. A regime discriminator that works before the policy decision, not in hindsight. Recovery rate, autocorrelation and variance are the current candidates; whether they work at policy frequency is unmeasured.
3. A natural experiment in which an imported spender is introduced into one closure and withheld from an otherwise identical one, with outcomes ranging against the ground.

## 6. What an expert can check in twenty minutes

1. Read Fig 1 :19, Fig 8 :32, Fig 6 :27 and Fig 5 :59, four short passages.
2. Check C2 against their own knowledge of the literature: are the two conditions real, and is the union claim faithful?
3. Name one statistic that would separate the conditions in real time, or say why none is available.
4. Send the refutation. A failure is more useful than an endorsement.
