# The Tether's answer on downturns and weak demand

**Mode: `general` on the shape, `specified` on the two-regime split and its refuters. Not `grounded`.** Simulated derivation checked against the figures (`05_what_shape_means.md` §6), not a ground reading. No econometric result was computed here.

## 1. The residual it answers

An economy bets on a path of spending, output and employment; it observes sales, inventories, unemployment and prices. The residual `R_s = |Γ(b,a) − o'|` is indexed by slot: sector, region, cohort of workers, balance sheet. The question is whether that residual trends down after a downturn using only adjustments composable inside the private frame, or whether there is a slot with positive mass that does not trend down and cannot be closed from inside that frame. Fig 9 :4: "Disagreements that do not shrink with effort, where each rule keeps working well on a different subset, are not one hard question but several well-formed ones. Split it rather than search."

## 2. The shape

1. **Measure demand per slot.** Fig 1 :19: "The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated." Fig 1 :22: "Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average." An aggregate "weak demand" is the wrong object; the claim is about at least one slot.
2. **Fix the closure, then the answer becomes conditional.** Fig 8 :32: "Closure is idempotent. Composing inside a closed set never leaves it, so no search, however long, adds an atom." Inside the private frame, wage, price and interest-rate adjustment can reallocate demand but cannot create a spender the frame does not contain. If the needed demand is already inside and merely misrouted, more search is correct. If it is absent, an import from a frame whose closure differs is owed (Fig 6 :23).
3. **Depth and absence look the same from inside.** Fig 6 :27: "Which is why some unreachability is depth and some is genuine absence, and from inside the frame the two look the same." This is why both schools can be coherent and still disagree. The same persistent gap is consistent with a broken transmission channel and with no source of final demand at any resolution.
4. **The question is a union compound, not one question.** Fig 9 :55: "A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones." A single lever ("let markets clear" or "stimulate") clears only the region where one condition holds and leaves the intersection standing.
5. **The answerable form is a trend.** Fig 5 :59 gives the shrinking test: "a term whose residual PERSISTS AND DOES NOT TREND DOWN is the one to point the refusal at, which is the same shrinking test the selector already runs, read with the opposite sign." "Self-correcting" becomes: does `R_s` trend to zero within a declared horizon H without an import? A flat residual points a refusal at the operative term, not at the outcome.
6. **The horizon and closure are declared, not discovered.** A frame cannot check the direction of its own question; the sought-for shape is a preference until it is bound as an ordinary term (Fig 5 :65). Most of the classical versus Keynesian dispute is a silent disagreement about H and the boundary.
7. **Intervention is asymmetric introduction.** Fig 11 :61: "The seat's power is asymmetric. Introduction is available; exclusion is not." A public spender can be introduced; the private habitat cannot be subtracted. Debt, expectations and damaged balance sheets still have to be repaired, so a managed recovery is continuing work (Fig 11 :63).
8. **Persistence is a closing loop.** Fig 12 :121: "cycle. The output re-enters as input. The sign of the loop decides whether it closes, and a chain cannot close by construction." Debt deflation is a negative-sign cycle. A chain-shaped model cannot close it.

## 3. What it explicitly does not say

- Not "markets never self-correct." That would be a `general`-mode verdict on a conditional claim. The depth regime is a real regime with a real literature.
- Not "intervention always works." An import in the depth regime adds an atom that was not needed, which Fig 8 :42 prices above starting from primitives.
- Not that any named mechanism is the true one. Liquidity trap, balance sheet recession and hysteresis are bindings from the economics frame, provenanced in `10_source_ledger.csv`, not names produced by the figures.
- Not that agreement among economists settles anything. Fig 7 :35: "A room's agreement is variation, not a verdict. Selection between rooms is where the ground votes."
- Not grounded. The figures deliver a shape and a decision procedure; they do not report which regime a given economy occupies.

## 4. What would settle it: the ground

The ground is realized outcomes, not argument. Fig 2 :29: "An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks."

- **Per-slot trend test.** Decompose the gap by sector, region and balance sheet. If each slot's residual trends down on a stated vintage of the data, self-correction is occurring in that episode. If one slot carries flat positive mass, persistence is occurring in that slot.
- **Regime discriminator.** Find a within-frame statistic that separates reachability failure from genuine absence before the policy decision. Cross-field corroboration: Scheffer et al. (Nature 2009) report "slower recovery from perturbations, increased autocorrelation and increased variance" near a threshold. That is a measurable candidate for the same quantity. It is secondary and unmeasured here.
- **Horizon binding.** State H explicitly. "Even if the economy is self-correcting, the process can be so slow that the GDP will not close, and employment will not be full for an extended period of time" (Lumen Learning, teaching source, read 2026-11-22). A long H makes almost any downturn self-correcting; a short H makes few of them so. That is a preference, not a discovery.
- **Standing refuters from `01` §4.** A real-time discriminator; a private atom-creating mechanism at the zero lower bound after balance-sheet damage; all slots trending down together; an outcome-blind agreeing group predicting recoveries; one mechanism closing both conditions; recovery-rate statistics that cannot discriminate opposite-response regimes.

## 5. The honest stop

**Unreached, not answered.** The regime discriminator is below the combined horizon of the available instruments at this budget. Fig 8 :39: "So a search that finds nothing is never a proof of absence." Fig 13 :134: "Unreachable is a verdict here, not a silence". This folder reports the shape an answer must have, the refuters written in advance, and exactly what would close it. It stops there and records the lineage so the stop is checkable.

One strain is live and recorded in `01` §6: the economic anchor is partly a maintained convention, since data series are revised and definition-dependent (Fig 10 :18: "a declared meaning is not a grounded one"). This lowers confidence in any single data point, not in the shape.

## 6. One-paragraph version for outreach

> The Tether does not take a side between classical and Keynesian economics, and it does not claim to have measured anything. It says that demand must be measured per slot, because an aggregate hides the signal; that once the private closure is fixed, adjustment inside it cannot create a spender it does not contain; and that the two reasons a gap persists, a transmission channel that has broken and a source of demand that is genuinely absent, look the same from inside the frame. That is why both schools can be coherent and still disagree. The question is a union compound, so one lever cannot clear it. "Self-correcting" only becomes answerable when written as an ordinary term: does the per-slot residual trend to zero within a stated horizon without importing a spender from a different closure? When the residual stays flat, the term to refuse is the one that keeps promising recovery, not the data. What is offered is the shape, the union split, the refuters written in advance, and the instruments that would close it. The empirical verdict stays open until the ground settles it.
