## 0. The question under audit, restated as an effect

The source question offers two options. Restated so each leads somewhere:

- **Option A, replace.** The core commitments of standard rational choice (stable and complete preferences, expected utility maximisation, rational expectations, independence and transitivity) are false as descriptions. A new foundation is owed and the old terms must be refused. Leads to: a refusal at the level of held terms, a new closure with new atoms, and a cost that must be paid for every new atom.
- **Option B, modify.** The standard model is a workable frame whose closure is mostly right. Behavioral findings are anomalies to be re-fit by adding a parameter or a new functional form inside the same frame. Leads to: a rebinding, no new foundation, and a cost equal to the added term plus what remains unexplained.

What each leads to is the effect that matters. A and B are not opposite answers to one measurement; they are different *operations on the library* (Fig 5 :10-14). The figures sort a gap by what is missing or what is wrong before anyone chooses a response.

**Binding the sought-for shape as an ordinary term** (formula "Direction"). The answer this audit looks for is: a per-slot routing of behavioral findings into the figure's five gap kinds; a default to re-fit when the candidate composes inside the standard closure; a named boundary where a new atom is owed; a refusal where a held term is false; and a ground test, held-out predictive success against a non-updating anchor, that decides which binding wins. If the derivation returns anything other than that shape (for example a single global verdict of "replace" or "modify"), the direction discipline has failed and the result must be re-run. I wrote this down before the census.

## 1. The residual in effect terms

**R (arity, symmetry, scale).** R is the set of systematic, replicable departures from the predictions of standard rational choice: the Allais and Ellsberg violations, the endowment effect and loss aversion, present bias and hyperbolic discounting, framing and default effects, other-regarding preferences. Arity: many, not one. Each finding is a separate object slot (Fig 1 :19: "The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated."). Symmetry: an observed choice difference is symmetric with respect to the arrow, since a correlation between a bias manipulation and a choice does not by itself say which causes which; the claimed *mechanism* is ordered. Scale: first order within a decision slot, second order at the level of the model that is supposed to explain many slots. A global "behavioral economics versus rational choice" residual is an aggregate, and Fig 1 :22 warns: "Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average."

**Two immediate consequences, before any figure is chosen.** First, the question's either/or is a request for one verdict over a many-slot residual. Second, an effect size or a count of anomalies is a *filter for the budget*, not a witness for the verdict (Fig 9 :60: "Use filters for the budget and witnesses for the verdict. Never let a filter hand you a verdict.").

**Strain test applied early.** A first reading wants Fig 5's "mechanism broken: a new term is owed" to mean "replace the whole model". That reading strains: Fig 5 sorts a *gap*, not a theory, and the owed term is a single atom, not a new closure. The line governs a slot, so re-census. The correct target for "replace" at the model level is the fifth kind, refuted, where a *held term* is false (Fig 5 :53). This re-census is kept visible here rather than smoothed over.

## 2. Domain census

One line per domain: actors, currency, anchor. No nameable anchor means it is not a domain yet. Partition or union stated. Order by dependency (Fig 3 :20: "The chain is ordered by dependency, not by importance.").

| domain | actors | currency | anchor | question type |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **D1: the standard rational-choice frame** | the representative agent, preference ordering, utility function, belief, the axioms (completeness, transitivity, independence) | prediction of choice; internal consistency | observed choice and market outcomes that do not move when the model's author changes their mind (Fig 2 :29) | Is the frame's closure adequate, or is a held term false? |
| **D2: the behavioral findings frame** | experimental subjects, the manipulations (framing, defaults, stakes, stakes-and-learning), the effect estimates | replicable experimental and field effects | controlled experiments, replication, and field data | Per-slot sort of each gap; is it held, novel, mis-attached, mechanism-broken, or refuted? |
| **D3: the modelling-method frame** | modelers, candidate terms, bounded computation, description length | cost of a term against residual removed; predictive accuracy on held-out data | out-of-sample prediction and a ground that does not update on the model's own report | Replace or modify is an *operation*, governed by the MDL bargain and by where the atom lives (Fig 5 :42-44, Fig 6 :21-23). |
| **D4: the welfare and policy frame** | policymakers, nudges, choice architects, the people whose choices are being steered | welfare claims, consent, reversibility | the person's own judged outcome, which is the right anchor because the question is constitutively about people (Fig 2 :36-38) | Does a correction keep a refusal seat, or does a convention decide an outcome (Fig 10 :47)? |

**Partition or union: union.** D1 to D4 form a union of overlapping conditions, not a partition. A finding can be a rebinding in D3 and a refutation of an axiom in D1 at the same time; the same nudge can be a method in D3 and an outcome-fixing convention in D4. Fig 9 :54: "And when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again." Fig 9 :55-56: "A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones. A single lever clears the region where only one condition holds and leaves the intersections standing, because the second condition still binds." One lever cannot settle all four.

**Order by dependency.** Determine first whether a fact exists in D2 (Fig 9 :4-5); then describe each gap in effect terms before choosing a frame (Fig 9 :27-28); then sort it by kind (Fig 5 :10-14, :53); refuse a held false term before minting a replacement (formula step 2); only then ask whether the candidate is inside closure and whether the ground pays (Fig 5 :33-41, Fig 6 :21-23, Fig 5 step 5). Welfare is last because it composes over a settled descriptive model; a convention that decides an outcome has stopped being a convention (Fig 10 :47). Effort spent above a break cannot rescue a link below it (Fig 3 :19). The link most likely to be broken here is between vocabulary and objective: the field can name biases and can measure effects, but often has no stated term for what would count as a better *model* beyond "fits the anomalies" (Fig 3 :25-27: "A link with no instrument is not a link that works. It is a link nobody has looked at.").

## 4. Findings

### F1: The either/or is a union of conditions, not one hard question (structural, triangulated)

Fig 9 :4-5, quoted from the verbatim text:

> "Disagreements that do not shrink with effort, where each rule keeps working well on a different subset, are not one hard question but several well-formed ones. Split it rather than search."

Fig 9 :54:

> "And when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again."

Fig 9 :55-56:

> "A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones. A single lever clears the region where only one condition holds and leaves the intersections standing, because the second condition still binds."

The literature itself supplies the subsets. Camerer's 2003 Boston Fed paper says "Most of the alternatives are extensions of conventional models which add a parameter or two or suggest new types of functions", while Joffe 2018 frames the same choice as "incremental" versus "selective replacement" and states "It may well be that incremental mode is appropriate in some cases, and selective replacement mode in others." Gigerenzer argues for a selective replacement of rational choice under uncertainty. These are not three opinions about one number; they are three subsets of one union, with different anchors. The union verdict is triangulated across Fig 9's split rule and Fig 5's five-kind sort (low overlap: one governs question structure, the other governs gap routing).

**Refuter:** show that every behavioral finding falls into one kind and shares one anchor, so the split is a partition; or show a single lever that clears all four domains including the welfare intersections. Then F1 fails and a global verdict becomes admissible.

### F2: "Modify" and "replace" are names for two different gap kinds, not two theories (framework-internal, triangulated)

Fig 5 :11-14:

> "held: the model was right, nothing owed"
> "new: extend perception, aim the probe here"
> "mis-attached: refit what you have, do not invent"
> "mechanism broken: a new term is owed"

The amendment, Fig 5 :50 and :53:

> "AMENDMENT · the gap has a fifth kind, and the pipeline has no branch for it."
> "refuted: a term we hold is wrong; a term is owed that says so"

The mapping is exact. An anomaly that is noise or already covered is *held*. A new experimental regime with no prediction is *new*, and owes a probe, not a model. A systematic deviation with the right structure but the wrong fitted object is *mis-attached*, and "refit what you have, do not invent" is literally what "modify" means. A deviation that requires a new mechanism atom is *mechanism broken*. And a demonstration that an axiom we hold is false is *refuted*, where what is owed is "a refusal, not an explanation" (formula step 2). Strong "replace" is the fifth kind, not the fourth: Fig 5 :55 says "What it removes is not a term but a term's standing to be composed over. Nothing leaves the library. What falls is the reach."

Fig 5 :21-22 adds the reachability gate: "can we even build this term? it composes from what we already have." So even a new atom must be reachable from what the frame holds, or be imported.

**Refuter:** exhibit a behavioral finding that cannot be assigned to any of the five kinds without strain, or show that "replace" and "modify" both reduce to the same kind. Then F2 is wrong and the routing is not a law of the gap.

### F3: The choice is a cost bargain, and re-fitting is the default (framework-internal plus method, triangulated)

Fig 5 :42-44:

> "The acceptance test is a bargain, not a threshold."
> "A new term is worth having when stating it, plus what remains unexplained after it, costs less than what was unexplained before."

Fig 5 :26: "and then: does the new term cost less to state than the confusion it removes?"

Fig 12 :125-127:

> "A composition costs in two currencies, and they do not add."
> "Bits price the description. Actions price finding out whether it holds."

So the comparison is not "elegant standard model versus messy behavioral patch". It is cost(term) plus left(R, term) versus cost(R). A one-parameter extension (a probability-weighting function, a present-bias parameter) is cheap in bits and often cheap in actions to test. A wholesale replacement must pay for every new atom it introduces, and pays again in actions if the arrangement is not recorded (Fig 12 :107-110). Fig 6 :24 prices a wrong import above starting from primitives: "Import from a live peer is debited against that frame's independence. A wrong atom costs more than starting from primitives." Hence *modify* is the default, not because the standard model is sacred but because the bargain usually favours the cheaper term. Camerer 1999 makes the same methodological point in world terms: good alternatives should include the rational principle as a mathematical special case, so the added value is measurable. The figures govern the bargain; the world source corroborates the practice. Low overlap, so triangulated.

**Refuter:** produce a case where a cheap re-fit leaves a residual that does not trend down while a costly replacement pays for itself across the same slots; or show that the two currencies add after all. Then the default flips for that slot, which F3 already permits (it is a default, not a veto).

### F4: No amount of searching inside the standard closure manufactures a genuinely new atom (framework-internal, triangulated)

Fig 6 :21-23:

> "MINT composes inside the closure and can never add an atom."
> "NOVEL means novel relative to atoms, not to the world. IMPORT adds an atom, and the added atom is what expands the closure."
> "No frame escapes its own closure. The bound moves only on contact with a frame whose closure differs."

Fig 8 :32:

> "Closure is idempotent. Composing inside a closed set never leaves it, so no search, however long, adds an atom."

Fig 13 :115:

> "The set is closed. The arrangements are not. That gap is why import exists."

Fig 13 :116: "So a frame can search without end and still not arrive: infinite effort inside a finite reach."

Consequence for the question: if a behavioral finding genuinely requires a primitive the standard frame does not hold (for example a reference point that is not a utility argument, or a computational-cost term that is not a preference), then *modification by re-arrangement* cannot reach it. A wall-moving import is owed, with a source gate and a shadow test (Fig 8 :50-52: "Convergent derivation and adopted import look identical in the contents. Only the record of where each came from separates them, and the surplus lives only where the closures differ."). Conversely, if the finding is an arrangement of atoms already in the frame (outcomes, probabilities, a value function, a time index), it is a modification and no import is claimed. This is why the figure verdict is per-slot and cannot be a single word.

**Refuter:** derive a genuinely new behavioral primitive by composition alone from the standard frame's atoms, while keeping the standard closure fixed; or show an import that moves the wall without provenance and a shadow test. Either breaks F4.

### F5: A refutation is a refusal, and the figures do not name which axiom is false (framework-internal plus canonical, single-source on the figures, gap on the name)

Operators table :58: "a refutation is a negation, which is why a vocabulary without it can only accumulate". Fig 5 :53 again: "refuted: a term we hold is wrong; a term is owed that says so". Fig 5 :61: "BUT THE TERM MOST WORTH REFUSING MAY NOT BE IN THE LIBRARY AT ALL."

Fig 13 :133: "what it returns is a shape rather than a name." Fig 13 :134: "Unreachable is a verdict here, not a silence". Fig 9 :32: "you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself".

So the figures license the *shape* of the replacement claim: if a held term is false, a refusal is owed, and the refused term loses its standing to be composed over. They do not license naming independence, or transitivity, or rational expectations as *the* false term. Which term is actually false is a ground question. World sources disagree about it: Rubinstein 2006 says "At the core of most models in Behavioral Economics there are still agents who maximize a preference relation over some space of consequences and the solution in most cases still involves standard equilibrium concepts", which supports the "kept core, changed preferences" reading; Gigerenzer 2018 argues for limiting rational choice to risk and building "a new behavioral economics that can stand on its own feet". That disagreement is a union of subsets, not a contradiction to be averaged.

**Refuter:** a figure line that names a specific axiom as the false term, or a measurement that discriminates the source of a choice pattern and shows which held term fails. Until one is run, the axiom slot stays a declared `gap`, reported as unreached rather than silence (Fig 8 :39: "So a search that finds nothing is never a proof of absence.").

### F6: Findings must be routed per slot, not averaged into a verdict on the field (framework-internal, triangulated)

Fig 1 :19: "The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated." Fig 1 :22: "Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average." Fig 1 :27: "They are frame-internal. A frame cannot score itself with a quantity it also produces."

A paper count, a meta-analytic effect size, or a Nobel count is a frame-internal score or a budget filter. It cannot decide whether a particular deviation is mis-attached, mechanism-broken, or refuted. Fig 2 :42: "Buy independence in proportion to the cost of being wrong." And Fig 11 :51: "No standing list of good actors survives a change of residual." A bias is not a malfactor in itself; its valence is relative to the residual (Fig 11 :49). Loss aversion is a malfactor for a normative expected-utility test and can be a benefactor for a description of how a default option changes savings rates.

**Refuter:** show that a single aggregate statistic routes every slot correctly, including the welfare intersections. Then per-slot verdicts are unnecessary overhead and F6 is refused.

### F7: The anchor is predictive contact, not agreement among economists (framework-internal, triangulated)

Fig 2 :29: "An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks. A measurement, an experiment, a counter, a market, a patient outcome. If it moves when you push on it, it is not an anchor." Fig 2 :35: "Alignment is triangulation, not negotiation. A mirror cannot correct you." Fig 2 :40: "disqualifying for a verdict: agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool". Fig 2 :41: "efficient for a convention: agreement about a shared meaning is the product, not a corruption of it". Fig 2 :45: "Frames range against the ground. They do not negotiate with it."

Fig 10 :24: "the ground does not decay, the channel does". A literature can saturate its metrics and pool its readings while the ground stays put; the output then is "you have lost touch", not a verdict (Fig 10 :27-28). The world sources make the same point in different language. Camerer 1999 asks that new principles include the rational one as a special case so one can measure "how much the new assumption adds". That is a demand for a discriminating instrument, not for endorsement. Gershman, Horvitz and Tenenbaum 2015 (Science, an AI and cognitive-science frame with low overlap with economics) define computational rationality as "identifying decisions with highest expected utility, while taking into consideration the costs of computation in complex real-world problems in which most relevant calculations can only be approximated", and note that "fallacies" may arise as a natural consequence of bounded optimisation. That reframes an anomaly as a term inside a widened objective rather than as evidence for a new psychology. It corroborates per-slot modification from a genuinely different evidence pool.

**Refuter:** produce a case where expert agreement, with a shared evidence pool and no new measurement, correctly names which model is true. Then the anchor clause is wrong for this domain.

### F8: The demand for a "psychologically real mechanism" is a direction the loop cannot check on its own (framework-internal, triangulated)

Fig 5 :62-64:

> "A frame searching for an answer of a particular shape holds that shape as a PREFERENCE rather than as a term. It is not bound, it predicts nothing, and it accrues no residual, so nothing here can see it."

Fig 5 :65: "The remedy is a discipline rather than a branch: make the sought-for shape an ORDINARY TERM."

The debate's recurring demand, that a model must capture the actual mechanism generating choice, is exactly such a preference unless it is written down as a checkable term. The 2024 *Economics and Philosophy* paper "Behavioural and heuristic models are as-if models too" makes the world-side version of this: all decision models in current use are as-if models, so model comparison rests on empirical and morphological desiderata rather than on process-model status. When the sought-for shape (a true process model) is made an ordinary term with a stated refuter, it becomes checkable; when it is held only as a preference, no amount of mechanism-talk tests it. This is why section 0 bound the answer as a routing rule instead of a word.

**Refuter:** show a frame that can certify, from inside itself, that its model is the actual generator of a choice, with a residual that fires if it is not. Then the Direction limit does not apply here.

### F9: Welfare is a separate domain with a different anchor and its own failure mode (framework-internal, single-source on the figures, corroborated in world terms)

Fig 2 :36-38: "An anchor is legitimate when the question is constitutively about it. Human judgment is the right anchor for is this a good answer for a person, because the question is about people. It is only an instrument for is this code correct." So a policy verdict about a nudge is anchored in the person's own outcome, not in the predictive accuracy of the behavioral model alone.

Fig 10 :47: "a convention that decides an outcome has stopped being one". A default option that silently fixes the outcome is no longer a mere coordination convention; it is a verdict and must carry provenance and a refusal seat (Fig 10 :42, :59). Fig 11 :40: "An improvement that does not change contact changes nothing, however much it improves." A nudge that changes a choice but does not change contact with the person's own judged good has not improved welfare.

Gigerenzer 2018 and the ecological-rationality literature supply the world-side conflict: they argue that the "irrationality argument" from biases to paternalism rests on treating heuristics as errors. That conflict returns to the census (it is a D4 anchor disagreement), it does not flip the D1 to D3 routing verdict.

**Refuter:** a welfare rule that can be settled by model fit alone, with no appeal to the person's own outcome and no refusal seat. Then D4 collapses into D3 and F9 fails.

## 5. Pre-registered refuter

Written before the conclusion, per the seat map's rule that "a falsifier written before the run is a design instrument, not only an honesty device." The shape claim is refuted if any of the following holds:

1. The four domains collapse into a partition with one anchor, so one global verdict is admissible (breaks F1).
2. Every behavioral finding can be routed into one of the five gap kinds and the kinds order the response, or a finding resists all five without strain (breaks F2 in either direction).
3. A controlled comparison shows that a costly replacement pays for itself across the same slots while the cheap re-fit leaves a residual that persists and does not trend down, *and* the two currencies add rather than being held apart (breaks F3).
4. A genuinely new behavioral primitive is derived by composition from the standard frame's atoms with the closure fixed, or an import moves the wall with no source gate and no shadow test (breaks F4).
5. A figure line names a specific axiom as the false term, or a source-discriminating instrument shows which held term fails (closes the F5 gap, which would change the answer, not merely fill it).
6. Expert agreement with a shared evidence pool, and no new measurement, correctly selects the true model in a case where predictions are tied (breaks F7).
7. A welfare rule is settled by model fit alone with no appeal to the person's own outcome and no refusal seat (breaks F9).

If any fires, the routing is corrected in this file with the earlier reading preserved. Until one is run, no part of this is labelled `grounded`.

## 6. Recommendation

1. Ask the question as a routing question, not a vote: which gap kind is this finding, and what atom does it need (F1, F2).
2. State the mode on every claim. "Behavioral economics modifies rational choice" is a `specified` default, not a `grounded` verdict (F3).
3. Keep the two currencies apart. A term cheap to state may be expensive to settle; sum them into one score and the choice is made blind (Fig 12 :125-128).
4. Demand provenance for any claimed new primitive, and a shadow test: which gap does it explain that the standard atoms could not (F4).
5. Route welfare through a separate seat with an outcome anchor and a refusal, and record the stake where it cannot be removed (F9, Fig 10 :59-60).
6. Send the generator, not the conclusion. Fig 4 :22: "Only methods cross up. Only head starts come down." A list of anomalies is a recording of one literature; a routing rule with a refuter is a method.
