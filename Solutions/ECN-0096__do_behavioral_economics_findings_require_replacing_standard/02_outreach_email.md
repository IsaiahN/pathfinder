**Subject:** One checkable claim about the "replace or modify" question in behavioral economics

> Hi [name],
>
> I have one specific output I would like you to check against what you already know. I am not asking you to read a theory first, and I am not asking you to agree with either side of the behavioraleconomics debate. If the output does not survive, there is nothing else to look at.
>
> **The output (`specified`, not `grounded`).** The question is usually posed as a choice: replace the standard rational-choice model, or modify it. I claim that is a bundled question, not a two-option partition. A systematic deviation is first routed by what is wrong. In the figure vocabulary there are five kinds: the model was right and nothing is owed; the gap is new and owes a probe; something is mis-attached and owes a re-fit, not a new term; a mechanism is broken and owes one new atom; or a term we hold is false and owes a refusal. "Modify" is the re-fit branch. "Replace" is the refusal branch, or a case where a genuinely new atom is required. Which branch a given finding falls on is decided per finding, not per field.
>
> Two consequences follow. First, re-fitting is the default, not because the standard model is sacred but because the acceptance test is a bargain: stating a term plus what it leaves unexplained must cost less than the confusion it removes. A probability-weighting parameter usually pays; a wholesale replacement must pay for every new atom. Second, no amount of searching inside the standard frame manufactures a genuinely new primitive. If a finding needs one, an import from a frame with a different closure is owed, with a stated source and a check that it explains a gap the old atoms could not. Where a held term is false, a refusal is owed, and the figures do not name which term.
>
> **What I am not claiming.** That behavioral economics has won, that rational choice is dead, that biases are irrational in themselves, or that any particular model (prospect theory, hyperbolic discounting, ecological rationality) is the named winner. The framework returns a shape, not a name. I am also not claiming this is measured. It is a simulated derivation checked for internal consistency against the figures, so it cannot settle the question it reports on.
>
> **What would refute it:**
> 1. Show that all behavioral findings fall into one gap kind with one anchor, so a single global verdict is valid.
> 2. Show a finding that fits none of the five kinds without strain.
> 3. Show a controlled case where a costly replacement pays for itself while a cheap re-fit leaves a residual that persists and does not trend down, with the two costs added rather than held apart.
> 4. Derive a genuinely new behavioral primitive from the standard frame's atoms with the closure fixed, or show an import moving the wall with no provenance and no gap it explains.
> 5. Show that expert agreement with a shared evidence pool, and no new measurement, correctly selects the true model.
>
> I wrote these down before sending this. If any one holds, I will correct the routing and keep the earlier reading visible. A one-line "this fails because..." is more useful to me than agreement. If you would rather not engage, no reply is needed.
>
