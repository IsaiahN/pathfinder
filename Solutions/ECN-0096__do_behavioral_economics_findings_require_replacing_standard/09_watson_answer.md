## QUESTION

Decide, in effect terms, what follows from systematic deviations from standard rational choice. Option A: replace the model, because its core terms are false and a new foundation is owed. Option B: modify it, because the frame is workable and the deviations are anomalies to be re-fit. What each leads to: A owes a refusal at the level of held terms and a new closure whose atoms must be paid for; B owes a cheaper re-fit inside the existing closure and risks importing nothing when a genuinely new primitive is required. The shape this run tests is a per-slot routing with a default to re-fit, a boundary where a new atom or a refusal is owed, and a ground test that decides the binding.

## DOMAIN CENSUS

R by arity, symmetry and scale, then one line per domain giving actors / currency / anchor, partition or union, and order by dependency.

- **R (the residual).** Systematic replicable departures from rational-choice predictions (Allais, Ellsberg, endowment and framing effects, present bias, other-regarding preferences). Arity: many, one per decision slot. Symmetry: a choice difference is symmetric while a claimed mechanism is ordered, so a correlation cannot choose the arrow. Scale: first order within a slot, second order at the model level. Fig 1 :19 "The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated." This is a union, not one hard question.
- **Domain 1, the standard rational-choice frame.** Actors: representative agent, preference ordering, utility function, belief, axioms. Currency: choice prediction and internal consistency. Anchor: observed choice and market outcomes that do not move when the model's author changes their mind (Fig 2 :29). Question type: is the closure adequate, or is a held term false?
- **Domain 2, the behavioral findings frame.** Actors: experimental subjects, manipulations, effect estimates. Currency: replicable experimental and field effects. Anchor: controlled experiments and field data. Question type: per-slot sort of the gap.
- **Domain 3, the modelling-method frame.** Actors: modelers, candidate terms, bounded computation. Currency: description cost against residual removed, and held-out predictive accuracy. Anchor: out-of-sample prediction against a ground that does not update on the model's own report. Question type: replace or modify is an operation governed by the cost bargain and by where the atom lives.
- **Domain 4, the welfare and policy frame.** Actors: policymakers, nudges, the people whose choices are steered. Currency: welfare claims and reversibility. Anchor: the person's own judged outcome, which is right because the question is constitutively about people (Fig 2 :36-38). Question type: does a correction keep a refusal seat, or does a convention decide an outcome?
- **Partition or union: union.** D1 to D4 overlap. A finding can be a rebinding in D3 and a refutation of an axiom in D1 at once. Fig 9 :54 "And when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again."
- **Order by dependency.** Establish the fact, describe the residual in effect terms before naming a frame, sort by gap kind, refuse before mint, check reach and novelty, settle against the ground, then compose a welfare conclusion. Fig 3 :19 "Optimising a link that already works cannot rescue one that does not."

Strain test applied: Fig 5's fourth kind, "mechanism broken: a new term is owed", initially looked like "replace the model". Re-census shows it governs one gap and owes one atom, while model-level replacement is the fifth kind, refuted (Fig 5 :53). Different objects, so no strain remains. See `01_figure_grounded_audit.md` §1 and §8.

## VERDICT (from the figures)

The question is a union, not a choice between two answers. The figures return a per-slot routing: re-fit when the candidate composes inside the standard closure, because the cost bargain favours the cheaper term; import a new atom with provenance and a shadow test where the wall must actually move; and refuse a held term where it is shown false, without naming which axiom. Re-fitting is the default, not a doctrine. Verdicts for the standard frame, the findings, the modelling method and welfare are kept separate and never averaged. Over a fixed atom set the standard and behavioral models are isomers sharing a functional group. No figure line names a winning model or a false axiom; that is a declared `gap`, of the unreached rather than underivable kind. This is a `specified`, ungrounded, candidate result. The world sources corroborate the union and the default-to-modify practice from one broad economics pool and, more weakly but independently, from computational rationality and animal behaviour; the ecological-rationality sources conflict and were returned to the census.

- **cost if wrong:** bounded and locatable. The packet states mode, refuters and provenance before the conclusion, so an error is findable rather than invisible. The real cost is telling a field with policy reach to stop looking for a genuinely new primitive because the cheap re-fit usually wins, or the reverse error of paying for a new foundation when a re-fit would have done. Fig 2 :42 "Buy independence in proportion to the cost of being wrong." That cost is reduced by keeping two low-overlap checkers (computational rationality and animal behaviour) in the witness set, not by adding more agreeing economists.
- **undoable:** yes. The routing is a method, not a primitive. It can be corrected slot by slot without withdrawing the figure set; `07_the_tether_answer.md` and `08_expert_verification_packet.md` already carry the falsifiers. If a discriminator shows that a reference point is a genuinely new atom, the import branch replaces the re-fit branch for that slot and nothing else moves.
- **reversed by:** a ground reading, not agreement. An instrument that discriminates reference dependence from belief distortion from computational cost in a single choice pattern would name the slot; a nested held-out predictive test that makes a costly replacement pay for itself would flip the default for that domain; a controlled refusal test that identifies which axiom fails would close the `gap`; a welfare reading anchored in the person's own outcome with a refusal seat would settle D4. Until one of these fires, the verdict stands as `candidate`.
