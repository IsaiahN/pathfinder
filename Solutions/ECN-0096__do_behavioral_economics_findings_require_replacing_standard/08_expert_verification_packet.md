# Expert verification packet: the Tether on replace versus modify

**What this is:** a claim in a form an economist or decision researcher can check. Mode, derivation and refuters are stated before the conclusion, so a reader knows what to look for and what would settle it.

**Mode: `specified` on the routing and per-domain verdicts, `general` on the union shape, `gap` on the named winner. Not `grounded`.** No measurement at the level of "which model is true" is claimed. Simulated derivation checked for internal consistency against the figures, not a ground reading.

## 1. The claim, in one paragraph

The "replace or modify" question is a bundled union of four conditions, not a two-option partition. A systematic deviation is routed by what is wrong: the model was right (nothing owed), the gap is new (a probe is owed), something is mis-attached (a re-fit is owed, not a new term), a mechanism is broken (one new atom is owed), or a held term is false (a refusal is owed). "Modify" is the re-fit branch; "replace" is the refusal branch or a case where a new atom is genuinely required. Re-fitting is the default because the acceptance test is a cost bargain; no amount of search inside the standard closure manufactures a new primitive, so a real replacement needs an import with provenance and a shadow test; and the figures return a shape, not a name, so no specific axiom or behavioral model is declared the winner. Verdicts are per slot and per domain and are never averaged.

### C3: framework-internal plus world-facing, checkable by an economist against the literature

- **Statement:** the modelling-method default (re-fit) and the welfare-domain anchor (the person's own outcome, with a refusal seat) are different quantities in different domains, and evidence from one cannot decide the other; over a fixed atom set the standard and behavioral models are isomers sharing a functional group.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 12 :117, "isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour." Fig 12 :123, "functional group. A substructure whose presence fixes the outcome. The group says what kind; the qualifier says which member." Fig 2 :36-38, "Human judgment is the right anchor for is this a good answer for a person, because the question is about people." Fig 10 :47, "a convention that decides an outcome has stopped being one". Fig 12 :125-126, "A composition costs in two currencies, and they do not add."
- **What to check:** is expected utility `Σ p_i u(x_i)` and prospect theory `Σ π(p_i) u(x_i − r)` a shared-atom pair differing in bond? Camerer 1999 (PNAS) says alternatives should include the rational principle as a special case so the added value is measurable; Joffe 2018 says incremental and selective replacement are each appropriate in some cases; Gigerenzer 2018 argues for selective replacement. Does that disagreement split by subset and anchor rather than contradict?
- **Refuter:** either model requires an atom the other lacks (which kills the isomer claim and leaves the functional-group claim standing); or a welfare rule is settled by model fit alone with no appeal to the person's outcome and no refusal seat; or the two currencies are shown to add.

## 5. What would settle it

A source-discriminating instrument and held-out prediction, not a further argument. From *The Hard Problem is About Source*: an instrument that reads green "cannot comment on the source"; the spectrometer resolved it. Here:

1. **Discriminator test:** independently manipulate a reference point, a belief distortion and a computational-cost constraint. Which one tracks the choice pattern when the others are held fixed?
2. **Nested predictive test:** does the behavioral term add predictive power on held-out data while containing the rational principle as a special case?
3. **Refusal test:** which held axiom, when refused, lowers total model cost without a loss of ground contact, and does its residual persist and refuse to trend down?
4. **Import test:** for a claimed new primitive, source, shadow test, and independence of the source frame's evidence pool.
5. **Welfare test:** does the person's own judged outcome, with a refusal seat, pay after the channel is checked for saturation?
