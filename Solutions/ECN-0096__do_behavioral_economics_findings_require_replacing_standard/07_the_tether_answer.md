# The Tether's answer: replace or modify the rational-choice model?

**Mode: `specified` on the routing and per-domain verdicts, `general` on the union shape, `gap` on the named winner. Not `grounded`.** This is a shape and a route, not a result. Simulated derivation checked against the figures (`05_what_shape_means.md` §6), not a ground reading.

## 1. The residual it answers

The question asks which of two operations follows from systematic deviations from rational choice. The effect is: a family of per-slot residuals, each with its own anchor, currently bundled into one "replace or modify" verdict. Fig 9 :4-5 says disagreements that do not shrink with effort and where each rule works on a different subset "are not one hard question but several well-formed ones. Split it rather than search." So the question is a union compound with its own loop per domain (Fig 9 :55-57), not one problem with opposite answers.

## 2. The shape

1. **Route by gap kind, not by camp.** Fig 5 :11-14 names five ways a gap can be held, new, mis-attached, mechanism-broken, or refuted; the amendment adds "refuted: a term we hold is wrong; a term is owed that says so" (Fig 5 :53). "Modify" is the re-fit branch (Fig 5 :13: "mis-attached: refit what you have, do not invent"). "Replace" is the refusal branch or a case requiring a new atom (Fig 5 :14, :55).
2. **Re-fitting is the default.** Fig 5 :42-44: "The acceptance test is a bargain, not a threshold. A new term is worth having when stating it, plus what remains unexplained after it, costs less than what was unexplained before." A one-parameter extension usually pays; a wholesale replacement pays for every new atom. This is a `specified` default, not a veto, and it is overridable per slot (F3).
3. **No search inside the standard closure manufactures a new primitive.** Fig 6 :21: "MINT composes inside the closure and can never add an atom." Fig 6 :23: "No frame escapes its own closure. The bound moves only on contact with a frame whose closure differs." Fig 8 :32: "no search, however long, adds an atom." So modification-by-rearrangement reaches only what the standard atoms span. Where a genuinely new primitive is required, an import from a differing closure is owed, with provenance and a shadow test (Fig 8 :50-52).
4. **A false term owes a refusal, and the figures do not name it.** Operators :58: "a refutation is a negation, which is why a vocabulary without it can only accumulate." Fig 13 :133: "what it returns is a shape rather than a name." Whether independence, transitivity, or rational expectations is the false term is a ground question, not a figure answer. That slot is a declared `gap`.
5. **Verdicts are per slot and per domain, never averaged.** Fig 1 :19: "The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated." Fig 1 :22: "Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average." A count of anomalies or an effect size is a filter for the budget, not a witness for the verdict (Fig 9 :60).
6. **Welfare is a separate domain.** Fig 2 :36-38 makes human judgment the right anchor for a question about people. Fig 10 :47: "a convention that decides an outcome has stopped being one." A nudge that fixes an outcome is a verdict and needs provenance and a refusal seat (Fig 10 :42, :59-60).
7. **Neither option is a final theory.** Fig 7 :6: "the limit: no last room". Fig 13 :116-118: "infinite effort inside a finite reach". "The end of rational choice" is the ToE error in a different vocabulary.

## 4. What it explicitly does not say

- Not "behavioral economics wins" and not "rational choice is dead". Both are names where the framework returns a routing.
- Not that any specific model (prospect theory, hyperbolic discounting, ecological rationality) is the named winner. The reference point's status is a live type question (`01` F4).
- Not that biases are irrational in themselves. Fig 11 :49: "An actor's valence is relative to the residual, not a property it carries."
- Not that consensus among economists settles the matter. Fig 2 :35: "Alignment is triangulation, not negotiation. A mirror cannot correct you."
- Not `grounded`. No measurement at the level of "which model is true" was taken.

## 5. What would settle it, the ground

A source-discriminating instrument and held-out prediction, not a further argument. From *The Hard Problem is About Source*, an instrument that reads green "cannot comment on the source"; the spectrometer resolved it. Here:

- **Discriminator test:** does a given choice pattern track a reference point, a belief distortion, or a computational-cost constraint when these are independently manipulated? An instrument that separates these names the slot.
- **Nested predictive test:** does the behavioral term add predictive power on held-out data while containing the rational principle as a special case (Camerer 1999, PNAS)? This is the ground form of the MDL bargain.
- **Refusal test:** which held axiom, when refused, lowers the cost of the remaining model without a compensating loss of contact? A persistent, non-trending residual on one axiom is the candidate (formula step 2, opposite sign).
- **Import test:** for any claimed new primitive, where did it come from, which gap does it explain, and is the source frame independent of the standard frame's evidence pool?
- **Welfare test:** does the person's own judged outcome, with a refusal seat, still pay for the nudge after the channel is checked for saturation (Fig 10 :24-28)?

## 6. The honest stop

**Unreached, not unreachable.** The routing is checkable now, inside the figures and against public literature. The named winner is not reachable at this budget because no discriminating instrument was run and no independent ground reading was taken. Fig 8 :39: "So a search that finds nothing is never a proof of absence." Fig 13 :134: "Unreachable is a verdict here, not a silence". The answer stops at the limit of the available instruments and records the lineage so the stop is checkable. "Indiscernibility is not identity." Prestige or agreement is not such an instrument.

## 7. One-paragraph version for outreach

> The Tether does not take a side in the "replace or modify" debate. It says the debate is a bundled union of at least four conditions, not a two-option partition. A systematic deviation is routed by what is wrong: a re-fit when the model is attached to the wrong object, a new atom when a mechanism is genuinely missing, and a refusal when a held term is false. Re-fitting is the default because a term must cost less than the confusion it removes, but no amount of search inside the standard frame manufactures a new primitive, so a real replacement needs an imported atom with a stated source and a gap it explains. The figures do not name which axiom is false or which behavioral model wins; that is a ground question, and a discriminating instrument rather than a vote is what would settle it. What the answer offers is the routing, the per-domain verdicts, the refuters written in advance, and the instruments that would close the open slot. The named winner stays open until the ground pays.
