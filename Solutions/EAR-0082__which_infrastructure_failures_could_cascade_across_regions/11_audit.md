## Round trip gap R_T

The Symbols table defines R_T as the gap between what was sent up and what came back down, `gap(x, (T_E ∘ T_A)(x))`, measuring what the coarser description cannot hold. Fig 4 :30 requires it to be measured rather than assumed. I ran the round trip on the condensation the folder actually uses.

1. **Sent up (x):** the verbatim figure set, chiefly Fig 11 :43-44, :49, :52-53, :61-67 and Fig 9 :54-58.
2. **Abstraction (T_A):** the shorthand in `07_the_tether_answer.md`: enumerate by contact outward, valence relative to the hazard, unbundle the union, keep cuts reversible, refuse the static list.
3. **Concretisation (T_E):** a named list of critical infrastructure for a named region.

**R_T is nonzero.** Four things the shorthand holds do not survive the trip back down:

- the requirement that the list be **read off the world**, not composed from a vocabulary (Fig 11 :48);
- the **second- and third-order reach**, which a one-hop map drops (Fig 11 :52-53);
- **valence relativity**, which a fixed "critical" label drops (Fig 11 :49-51);
- the **union unbundling**, which a single-lever recommendation drops (Fig 9 :55-58).

Conversely, two things survive cleanly: a structure of contact links must be enumerated, and the sector/region pair is a compound with its own loop. That is the residue worth sending. R_T was **not measured numerically**, because no ground reading exists; it is recorded qualitatively as the enumerated set of dropped constraints. Treating "the figures condensed into a shape" as identity would set R_T to zero and would be the dead-reckoning error Fig 4 :30 names. The honest form is a lossy condensation with a recorded gap, which is what `09_watson_answer.md` states.
