# The Tether's answer: which infrastructure failures could cascade across regions or sectors

**Mode: `general` on the structural shape, `specified` on the per-domain findings. Not `grounded`.** Simulated derivation checked against the figures (`05_what_shape_means.md` §6), not a ground reading. No dependency graph, outage dataset or experiment was read to settle the list.

## 1. The residual it answers

We observe a local failure and a distant outcome. We do not hold the rule that says which links carry the cascade, to what order, or where it stops. The question bundles sectors and regions, and Fig 9 :55 "A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones." So this is a union compound with its own loop, not one hard question and not a catalogue (Fig 9 :57-58 "a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either. It stops being a mixture and becomes a specific compound.").

## 2. The shape, per domain, never averaged

1. **The answer must be an enumeration read off the world, not composed from a vocabulary.** Fig 11 :43 "The habitat is enumerated, never composed." Fig 11 :44 "List everything in contact with the residual, then what is in contact with those, and outward until the cascade stops mattering." A static "critical infrastructure" list is the forbidden object.
2. **First-order scoping is aimed at the wrong quantity.** Fig 11 :52-53 "And contact cascades. Scoping the habitat to direct contact is how DDT reached the eagles: first-order enumeration, second-order effect, and the residual that mattered was three steps out." The effect that matters is second or third order. This is triangulated with Fig 3 :19 "Optimising a link that already works cannot rescue one that does not." (low overlap: habitat web versus dependency chain).
3. **Valence is relative to the hazard, not carried by the asset.** Fig 11 :49 "An actor’s valence is relative to the residual, not a property it carries." Fig 11 :51 "No standing list of good actors survives a change of residual." A buffer in one scenario is a failure amplifier in another.
4. **The sector/region union must be unbundled; cuts stay reversible.** Fig 9 :54 "And when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again." Fig 9 :41 "so keep every cut ranked and reversible." A single lever clears the single-condition region and leaves the intersection standing.
5. **Isolation is substitution, and introduction is available while exclusion is not.** Fig 11 :66 "Isolation is not removal of the habitat. It is substitution of one habitat for another." Fig 11 :61-62 "The seat’s power is asymmetric. Introduction is available; exclusion is not." Decoupling moves the residual into a habitat with unchosen actors; it does not delete contact.
6. **The route needs ordered bonds, not an ingredient list.** Fig 12 :111 "A vocabulary without bonds can name a state and cannot name a route to one." Fig 12 :121 "cycle. The output re-enters as input. The sign of the loop decides whether it closes, and a chain cannot close by construction." The cascade's positive feedback is a cycle, and the bond notation used here is a binding from network science, not a figure derivation.
7. **The specific "which" is a gap, not a silence.** Fig 13 :134 "Unreachable is a verdict here, not a silence," and Fig 13 :135 "It cannot say which primitive is missing, because naming it would mean already holding it." What is missing is the real dependency graph. Fig 8 :39 "So a search that finds nothing is never a proof of absence." forbids reading this as "nothing cascades."

Verdicts by domain: D1 within-network (`specified`, triangulated), D2 cross-sector (`specified`, triangulated), D3 cross-region (`general`, single-source for the isolation claim and triangulated for beyond-direct-contact), D4 verification (`general`, triangulated). These are not averaged into a single score.

## 3. What it explicitly does not say

- Not a named list of critical infrastructures. No asset is labelled intrinsically critical.
- Not a prediction that a specified sector will fail in a specified region. That needs the ground.
- Not grounded. No measurement in this derivation settles left(R, phi).
- Not "no failures cascade." `gap` is a statement about what the figures alone cannot name.
- Not a physical theory of failure propagation. The bond glyphs are notation, not mechanics.
- Not corroboration from a second witness. The auditor in `11_audit.md` shares the primary's evidence pool.

## 4. What would settle it, the ground

An instrument, not more agreement: network data and measured dependency links for a named region, plus a documented case in which the enumeration predicted a second- or third-order effect that was then observed. Concretely:

1. A completed contact enumeration for a region, extended to third order, checked against outage records.
2. A test of the union claim: a single lever applied to a case the analysis calls S ∩ R; if it clears the case, the union reading fails.
3. A test of the ordering claim: a cascade discovered with symmetric propagation where the census declared a directed relation.
4. A source-discriminating check: two independent frames with genuinely different evidence pools ranging against the same measured outage, as required by Fig 2 :35 "Alignment is triangulation, not negotiation. A mirror cannot correct you."

## 5. The honest stop

**Reached in shape, unreached in name.** The figures return the form any answer must have and the method to obtain it. They do not contain the dependency graph of any real region, so the name is `gap` here. The stop is recorded with its lineage so a later reader can locate the error rather than only feel it (Fig 4 :30 "So take the round trip and measure the gap. Without it you are navigating by dead reckoning: confident about a position nobody checked.").

## 6. One-paragraph version for outreach

> The Tether does not hand over a list of critical infrastructure. It says what any correct answer must look like: enumerate the systems in contact with a failure by reading the world, not by composing a vocabulary; carry that enumeration to second and third order, because the effect that matters is usually three steps out; treat the value of each node as relative to the hazard, not as a fixed property; unbundle the sector and regional union before proposing a single lever; and keep every cut reversible, because isolation substitutes a habitat rather than removing one. The sector/region pair is a compound, not a harder case of either, and a chain notation cannot express the feedback loop that makes a cascade a cascade. The figures return this shape, not the name. The actual dependency graph is the ground, and until it is read the honest verdict on "which" is `gap`, not a fabricated catalogue and not a claim that nothing cascades.
