**Subject:** One checkable claim about cascading infrastructure failure, before any theory

> Hi [name],
>
> I have one specific output I would like you to check against what you already know. I am not asking you to read the framework first, and I am not asking you to agree with it. If the output does not survive, there is nothing else to look at.
>
> **The output (`general` on the shape, `specified` on the domain findings, not `grounded`):** the question "which infrastructure failures could cascade across regions or sectors" is a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones. From that it follows that a defensible answer cannot be a static list of critical assets. It has to be an enumeration of contact links read off the world, extended outward until the residual stops registering, with the valuation of each node relative to the hazard being asked about, and with the sector/region union unbundled before search. Scoping to direct contact is exactly how a first-order map misses the effect that matters three steps out. This is a claim about the **shape** of a correct answer, not a measurement and not a named list.
>
> **What I am not claiming:** that any named asset is intrinsically critical; that a particular sector will cascade in a particular region; that this derives the answer. The framework returns a shape, not a name. The actual dependency graph is the ground, and I have not read it here. Where the ground has not settled the list, the honest report is `gap`, not a fabricated catalogue.
>
> **What would refute the shape claim:**
> 1. a cascade travelling through a contact link that a completed enumeration did not list even at third order;
> 2. a cascade whose propagation is symmetric in a domain where the claim says it is ordered;
> 3. a single lever clearing a case the claim calls a union compound;
> 4. two independent frames, drawn from genuinely different evidence pools, disagreeing about the same cascade after each ranges against the ground.
>
> I wrote these down before sending this. If any one holds, I will retract the claim. A one-line "this fails because..." is more useful to me than agreement. If you would rather not engage, no reply is needed.
>
> **What I would count as settling it:** the actual network data, measured dependency links, and a documented case where the enumeration predicted a second- or third-order effect that was then observed. Agreement by itself is not that instrument.
>
> **If it survives, and only then:** the sources are below. The figures are the authority, not my summary of them.
> - Verbatim figure text with line numbers (local extract): `FRAMEWORK/reference/03-FIGURES_TEXT.md`, especially Fig 11 :43-44, :49, :52-53 and Fig 9 :54-58.
> - The interdependent-networks result that motivates the cross-sector case: https://www.nature.com/articles/nature08932
> - The IPCC assessment of cascading impacts across infrastructure and regions: https://www.ipcc.ch/report/ar6/wg2/chapter/chapter-6/
>
> **If you want to use the machinery:** the formula is in the figures link. Run it on a cascade you know well and can check against a ground. Write down what would refute your result *before* you run it, and state the mode of what you get. A reported failure is more useful to me than a reported success.
>
> — [your name]

## The three stages

1. **Stage 1:** send the shape claim and the four refuters. Ask what the recipient would count as a failure. Record their verdict before showing any theory or source.
2. **Stage 2 (opt-in only):** if they are curious, offer the three links, with the note that the figures, not the summary, are the authority. Their later agreement is never pooled as corroboration (Fig 2 :40).
3. **Stage 3:** offer the formula as a method to reuse, with the instruction that each user writes their own refuter and states their own mode.
