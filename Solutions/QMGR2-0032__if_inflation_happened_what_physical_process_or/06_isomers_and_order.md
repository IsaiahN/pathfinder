# Isomers, functional groups, and order among candidate inflation drivers

**Mode: `specified`.** This is a simulated derivation checked against the figures, not a ground reading (see `05_what_shape_means.md` section 6).

## RESIDUAL

The primary audit establishes a shape (a directed, spendable energy difference that persists and ends) and refuses to name the field. Two supporting questions are owed: (1) are the competing candidate drivers **isomers** under Fig 12's definition, and (2) what **order** does the composition impose, given that inflation must precede reheating and reheating must precede radiation domination? A third, smaller question is whether the persistence condition is a catalyst or a cycle, since the two behave differently across uses.

## DERIVATION

### Q1 - Are the candidate drivers isomers?

- Fig 12 :117: **"isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."**
- Fig 12 :124: **"functional group. A substructure whose presence fixes the outcome. The group says what kind; the qualifier says which member."**
- As usually written, the candidates do not share all atoms. Single-field slow-roll reads approximately `{Energy(potential), Gradient(rolling), Time}`; Starobinsky reads `{Space(curvature), Gradient, Time}`; Higgs inflation adds a coupling to curvature. By Fig 12's definition they are **not isomers as written**; they share a **functional group**, the `state - structure - state` shape that F1 and F2 identify. The group says what kind (a directed, spendable difference); the qualifier says which member (which atom fills the state slot and which structure carries the gradient).
- The stronger version: if all candidates are re-written over the **full shared atom set** `{Space, Time, Energy, Gradient, Spectrum, Network}`, differing only in which structure mediates which state pair, then they **are** isomers: same atoms, different arrangement, different substance. That is the claim worth testing, because it predicts that an instrument sensitive only to the arrangement (n_s, r, non-Gaussianity) can distinguish them while an instrument sensitive only to the ingredient list (that some field exists) cannot.
- **Falsifier, written before the run:** if any candidate can be written without an atom the others require (for example a model with no Time gradient, or no Energy-like state), the isomer claim fails and only the functional-group reading stands. Corroboration from the physics frame is low-overlap but not decisive: the Wikipedia inflaton entry lists Higgs inflation and non-minimal coupling as variants, and the JCAP issue page (read this session) carries Starobinsky and EFT reheating work, so multiple bindings genuinely coexist in the literature.

### Q2 - What order does the composition impose?

- Fig 12 :72: **"A then B; B needs A to have happened"** is the sequence bond. Reheating needs the driver to have already acted, so inflation precedes reheating by construction, not by preference.
- Fig 13 :76: **"A gradient over space and one over time are different terms, because which state it is bonded to is part of what it is."** The driver's gradient is over time (a rolling or decaying energy difference), while the observable relics are a gradient over space (the density perturbations). These are different terms; confusing them is a type error in the model comparison.
- Fig 9 :54: **"And when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again."** The source condition C1 and the persistence/end condition C2 are ordered but distinct: first a spendable difference must exist, then it must persist, then it must end.
- The dependency chain (Fig 3 :20, *"The chain is ordered by dependency, not by importance"*) runs: perception (is there a residual in the CMB?) → vocabulary (do we have a directed-spendable-difference term?) → objective (how many e-folds, which n_s and r?) → planning (which instrument discriminates?) → learn and carry. The middle link is the one without an instrument (Fig 3 :27, *"A link with no instrument is not a link that works. It is a link nobody has looked at."*).

### Q3 - Catalyst or cycle?

- Fig 12 :119: **"catalyst. A part that survives its own use. Spent and regenerating parts behave differently across uses, and nothing records which a term is."**
- Fig 12 :121: **"cycle. The output re-enters as input. The sign of the loop decides whether it closes, and a chain cannot close by construction."**
- Slow-roll persistence is the catalyst reading: the potential energy stays roughly constant, so the driver survives its own use across many e-folds. Wikipedia (read this session) states that *"During inflation, the energy density in the inflaton field is roughly constant."*
- Reheating is **not** a cycle. The field's energy is converted into particles; the driver does not re-enter as input. A chain cannot close by construction, and treating reheating as a closed loop would be a misapplied cycle bond. The correct bond is sequence (`→`), followed by a conversion that changes the term.
- **Falsifier:** a model in which the same driver re-enters as input after reheating, with no change of term, would make the cycle reading correct and the catalyst reading wrong. No such model appears in the sources opened for this solution.

### Q4 - Second-order comparison and the operand-type strain

- Fig 12 :104: **"A settled molecule becomes an atom for whatever composes over it. So irreducible is a fact about a level and never about the world."**
- A candidate driver that is settled against the ground (as the slow-roll class partly is, through n_s and r constraints) can serve as an atom for a second-order comparison of models. Their ⋛ comparison is therefore second-order, licensed by the settled-molecule rule rather than by a new primitive.
- **Strain, named not hidden:** the Operators table defines ⋛ as *"a threshold relation between two quantities."* At second order the operands are settled molecules now serving as atoms. Whether an atom counts as a quantity at that level needs an instrument, not an argument. This is strain S1 in `01_figure_grounded_audit.md` section 7.

## CONCLUSION

The candidate drivers share a **functional group** as usually written (a `state - structure - state` directed difference) and are **isomers** only under the stronger re-writing over the full shared atom set. Their order is fixed by sequence: source, persistence, end, reheating, structure. Persistence is the catalyst reading, not the cycle reading. The second-order model comparison is licensed by Fig 12's settled-molecule rule but carries one named type strain on ⋛'s operands.

This rescues what is checkable in the "which field" question without naming a field: the isomer test and the order test need no new primitive, no import that moves the wall, and no claim that the framework returns a name. Which candidate, if any, the ground selects remains the declared gap of F5.
