## 1. A shape is a constraint, not a filler

The canonical figures README states the limit: the framework returns *"what a missing thing would have to look like (a shape, not a name)."* Fig 9 :32: *"you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself."* Fig 13 :135: *"It cannot say which primitive is missing, because naming it would mean already holding it."*

So a **shape** is the narrowed form of an answer you can state before you can name it. It constrains; it does not fill. For this question:

- A shape: "a directed, spendable energy difference that persists and then ends."
- A name: "the inflaton field with a concave potential V(phi)."
- Another name: "Starobinsky R-squared gravity with a scalaron."

The shape is what all three candidate names, if they are genuine answers, must have in common. It is checkable before any of them is chosen.

## 3. What licenses a name

Only two things, both outside the shape itself:

1. **The ground settles it.** Formula step 5: *"the ground settles it. Verified only by what pays."* The canonical README says the framework *"stops at the limit of what the available instruments can distinguish, and records the lineage so the stop is honest."* Here that would be a tensor or non-Gaussianity measurement that discriminates a unique potential.
2. **A provenanced import from a frame whose closure differs** (Fig 6 :23, Fig 8 :51-52), with a source gate and a shadow test. "Inflaton" arrives this way: from the physics frame, with Guth's 1979/1981 proposal and the later variant literature as its provenance. It is a binding onto Energy plus Gradient plus Time, not a new primitive.

The important correction, carried from the exemplar's own §5b and applied here: the "shape, not a name" rule governs a **missing** primitive. Energy, Gradient, and Time are already in the six (Fig 13). Naming a binding onto held atoms is permitted. What is not permitted is presenting the binding as something the figures derived, or claiming it moves the wall (Fig 6 :25: *"An import adds no atom to the world."*).

## 4. Why the CMB reading cannot by itself name the field

The metamerism point from *The Hard Problem is About Source* is the sharpest tool here. Planck 2018 X reports n_s = 0.9649 +/- 0.0042, r < 0.056, and that slow-roll concave potentials are *"increasingly favoured by the data."* That is one green reading. The candidate sources (single-field slow-roll, Higgs inflation, Starobinsky R-squared gravity) can produce near-identical n_s and r values in overlapping parameter ranges. The reading does not determine the source. What is owed is a source-discriminating instrument, not a further argument about which name is more natural.

This is not a claim that the CMB tells us nothing. It narrows the shape (it disfavours some spendable-difference profiles and bounds the tensor amplitude). It is a claim that the narrowing stops short of a name.

## 5. Answer to the question this file exists to settle

**Does the framework license the name "inflaton" as its answer?** No. The framework licenses the shape. The name is a binding from the physics frame, provenanced as such, and the specific field remains a declared `gap`.

**Does the framework forbid naming a held atom in the slot?** No. That was an overstatement in my own earlier reading (see the retraction table in `01_figure_grounded_audit.md` section 7). It forbids inventing a seventh primitive and forbids presenting a binding as a derivation. Both readings are kept visible so neither is quietly lost.

## 6. Two consequences for this question

**Prestige is not action.** From *The Recursive Self-Improvement Trap* (read this session): *"Prestige can fund an action. It can give an action a budget to be tried. But it cannot settle an action. Only the ground can settle it."* A campaign that gathers famous cosmologists to agree that the inflaton is the answer is the named collapse, not corroboration. The legitimate ask is the opposite: give the shape a budget to be tried, let an instrument or a refutation settle it, and record the verdict before any theory is shown.

**This audit is a simulation, not a ground reading.** Same essay: *"LLMs can quickly simulate an approximate answer/output with the formula found in the architecture post... However, the truly testable output would need to be derived from the real architecture or working the formula yourself."* So `01_figure_grounded_audit.md` is a simulated derivation, checked for internal consistency against the figures. It is **not** a test of the claim and must be labelled as such. It cannot settle anything it reports on. The external validator is the ground, not agreement between seats (Fig 2 :35: *"Alignment is triangulation, not negotiation. A mirror cannot correct you."*).
