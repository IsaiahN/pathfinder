# Outreach draft - validate the shape first, then decide about the theory

**Use:** one to one, or as a cover letter attached to a submission through the public venues in `03_public_contact_points.csv`. **Not** for bulk send.

**Audience field:** inflaton field research, early-universe cosmology, CMB inference.

**Hard precondition:** do not send until the five refuters in `01_figure_grounded_audit.md` section 5 are stated in the recipient's own words back to you. The offer only works if the stage-1 claim survives a check by someone who has not seen the framework.

---

**Subject:** Check one shape claim before you decide whether the framework is worth reading

> Hi [name],
>
> I have one specific output I would like you to check against what you already know. I am not asking you to read the theory first, and I am not asking you to agree with it. If the output does not survive, there is nothing else to look at.
>
> **The question, in effect terms:** standard Big Bang evolution fits the CMB only from finely tuned initial conditions, and it supplies no mechanism for the observed perturbations. Something must have driven a brief period of accelerated expansion. I am asking what an answer must be shaped like, not which field it is.
>
> **The output (`specified`, not `grounded`):** the framework's minimum set has six terms in two kinds, states (space, time, energy) and structures (spectrum, gradient, network), with the rule that composition runs across kinds and never within one. Energy is described as *"conserved, finite, directionless alone"* (Fig 13 :14), and the figures say *"Energy and a gradient compose a flow"* (Fig 13 :76). A gradient is *"a difference that can be spent"* (Fig 13 :22) whose defining feature is directedness. From those three lines it follows that any driver of a directed expansion must be a spendable, directed energy difference over time, not merely stored energy. It must also persist across many e-folds and then end: the figures call this *"a part that survives its own use"* (Fig 12 :119).
>
> That is a shape. The framework does not supply the name. It returns *"a kind of thing, never the answer itself"* (Fig 9 :32). So I am not claiming that the framework derives the inflaton, or a scalar potential, or R-squared gravity. "Inflaton" is a name from your field attached to atoms the framework already holds. Convergent derivation and adopted import *"look identical in the contents"* (Fig 8 :51-52), so until provenance is shown the mapping carries zero evidentiary weight.
>
> **What would refute the shape claim, written down before this message:**
> 1. accelerated expansion with no directed, spendable energy difference;
> 2. a driver whose energy density falls as fast as radiation from the outset and still yields sixty e-folds and the observed scalar spectral index;
> 3. a derivation from the six-term set alone that uniquely names a particular field and potential;
> 4. one measurement that closes both the source condition and the persistence-and-end condition with no categorically different residue left over;
> 5. your judgement that the shape is vacuous, that it would fit any proposed driver and predicts nothing.
>
> If any one holds, I will retract the claim. A one-line "this fails because..." is more useful to me than agreement. If you would rather not engage, no reply is needed.
>
> **If it survives, and only then:** the figures are the authority, not my summary of them. The relevant lines are Fig 13 :14, :22, :76; Fig 12 :119; Fig 9 :32; Fig 8 :51-52. The public figure set is at https://github.com/IsaiahN/tether/tree/general-agent/figures . The canonical usage rule is in that folder's README.
>
> **About the specific name:** I am not claiming to have settled which field drove inflation. The Planck 2018 inflation analysis constrains the shape (n_s = 0.9649 +/- 0.0042, r < 0.056 combined with BICEP/Keck BK15, slow-roll concave potentials favoured) but the mechanism remains unidentified in the literature. I am offering the shape and its refuter, not a new name for the field.
