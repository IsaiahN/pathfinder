# The Tether's answer: what drove inflation

**Mode: `specified` - not `grounded`.** This is a shape and a route, not a result. Simulated derivation checked against the figures (`05_what_shape_means.md` section 6), not a ground reading.

## 1. The residual it answers

Standard Big Bang evolution returns the observed CMB only from finely tuned initial conditions (flatness fine-tuned to roughly one part in 10^62), and it provides no mechanism for the observed nearly scale-invariant perturbations. The contact is a **union compound**, not one hard question: a directed source condition C1 and a persistence-and-end condition C2 (Fig 9 :55, *"A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones."*). Each has its own slot and its own ground. R is indexed per observable, never pooled into a single "did inflation happen" number (Fig 1 :19).

## 2. The shape

1. **Energy alone cannot drive a directed flow.** Fig 13 :14 describes energy as *"conserved, finite, directionless alone"*, and Fig 13 :76 says *"Energy and a gradient compose a flow."* So the driver is a state bonded to a structure, not bare energy.
2. **The structure must be a gradient over time.** Fig 13 :22: *"a difference that can be spent"*; Symbols table :97: *"A difference that can be spent. Not merely a distribution: what makes it a gradient is directedness."* Fig 13 :76 distinguishes a gradient over space from one over time. The expansion driver is the time-directed one; the density perturbations are the space-directed relic.
3. **The driver must persist and then end.** Fig 12 :119: *"catalyst. A part that survives its own use."* Slow-roll keeps the driving energy density roughly constant across many e-folds. Reheating is a sequence (`→`, Fig 12 :72: *"A then B; B needs A to have happened"*), not a cycle, because the field converts into particles and does not re-enter unchanged (Fig 12 :121).
4. **The framework returns a shape, not a name.** Fig 9 :32: *"you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself."* So the answer is: a directed, spendable energy difference over time that persists and ends. It is not "the inflaton" as a framework result.
5. **"Inflaton" is a provenanced binding.** It is a physics-frame name attached to atoms the framework already holds (Energy, Gradient, Time). Fig 8 :51-52: *"Convergent derivation and adopted import look identical in the contents. Only the record of where each came from separates them."* The binding adds no atom (Fig 6 :25: *"An import adds no atom to the world."*). It is not a mint, and it is not a derivation.
6. **Candidate names stand as isomers or a functional group.** Over the full shared atom set they are Fig 12's *"isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance"*; as usually written they share only the functional group `state - structure - state` (Fig 12 :124: *"the group says what kind; the qualifier says which member."*). See `06_isomers_and_order.md`.

## 3. What it explicitly does not say

- Not that the framework identifies the inflaton, or any scalar field, or R-squared gravity. Those are bindings from the physics frame.
- Not that the question is a single problem to be solved by naming a field. It is a union of two conditions, kept separate (Fig 9 :54).
- Not that the CMB reading determines the source. *The Hard Problem is About Source*: an instrument *"is speaking only about what it measured, it cannot comment on the source."*
- Not that a period of accelerated expansion is ruled in or out by this derivation. The question asks what a driver would have to be like **if** inflation happened; the frame's answer is conditional on that premise and does not decide it.
- Not grounded. No measurement at the inflationary scale settles `left(R,phi)`.

## 4. What would settle it - the ground

A source-discriminating instrument, not a further argument. From *The Hard Problem is About Source*: *"The answer is unreached until a better instrument exists"*, and it was resolved in the colour case *"not by argument, but by the arrival of a better instrument."* Here:

- **Source Test:** does the tensor-to-scalar ratio r, or a primordial non-Gaussianity signal, discriminate one potential or one class of drivers? Planck 2018 X gives r < 0.056 with BK15 and finds no dynamics beyond slow roll; that narrows the shape without naming the field.
- **Persistence Test:** does the scalar spectral index n_s and a reheating constraint close C2? Planck gives n_s = 0.9649 +/- 0.0042 and favours concave slow-roll potentials. That is a fit to the persistence shape, not a name.
- **Falsifiers from the pre-registered list:** accelerated expansion with no directed, spendable energy difference; a driver whose energy density falls as fast as radiation yet still yields sixty e-folds; a figure derivation that uniquely names a field; one lever that closes both C1 and C2 with no categorically different residue; or the judgement that the shape is vacuous.

## 5. The honest stop

**In reach: the shape. Not in reach at this budget: the name.** Fig 13 :134: *"Unreachable is a verdict here, not a silence,"* and Fig 8 :39: *"So a search that finds nothing is never a proof of absence."* The Six are a visible guess at compression axes, not nature's own list; declaring them makes the choice visible, not neutral. The answer stops where the instruments can no longer distinguish sources, and records the lineage so the stop is checkable. Indiscernibility is not identity.

This is a `specified`, ungrounded, candidate result. It cannot settle which field drove inflation. That is the declared gap, and it stays open.

## 6. One-paragraph version for outreach

> The Tether does not name the field that drove inflation, and it does not claim to settle whether inflation happened. It says that if a directed expansion needs a driver, the driver must be a spendable, directed energy difference over time, because energy is directionless alone and only energy bonded to a gradient composes a flow. That difference must persist across many e-folds like a part that survives its own use, and must then end by conversion into radiation, not by cycling back. The framework returns that shape, not a name: "inflaton", slow-roll potentials and R-squared gravity are bindings from physics onto atoms the framework already holds, and only the provenance record separates a binding from a derivation. What it offers is the shape any candidate answer must have, five refuters written in advance, and the kind of source-discriminating instrument that would close the remaining gap. The field stays unnamed until the ground settles it.
