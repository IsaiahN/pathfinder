## What is deliberately not here

- No personal emails of named individuals.
- No ranking of people by how likely they are to agree with the answer.
- No bulk-send plan.

Three reasons, in the figures' own words:

1. **Agreement is not a verdict when the frames share a pool.** Fig 2 :40: *"disqualifying for a verdict: agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool"*. The well-being literature draws heavily on the same Gallup and World Values Survey instruments. Many administrations of one ladder are one frame wearing many names (Fig 8 :49).
2. **Selecting for likely agreement manufactures the disqualifying correlation.** Fig 2 :41: *"efficient for a convention: agreement about a shared meaning is the product, not a corruption of it"*. A list ranked by receptiveness is a list selected to agree, which produces a convention, not corroboration.
3. **Asking for trust before a check spends the reader's independence.** Fig 10 :42: *"A verifier that can reconstruct the claim cannot verify it."* The first contact carries the residual and its refuter, never the framework's interiors.

Legally, scraping personal emails for unsolicited bulk outreach breaches GDPR and CAN-SPAM style rules in many jurisdictions and would get the sending domain blacklisted. That is a practical failure on top of the structural one.
