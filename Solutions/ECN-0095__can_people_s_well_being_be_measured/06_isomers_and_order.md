# Union, composite and bond: the supporting derivation

**Mode: `specified` on the separators, `general` on the structural claim, `gap` on the weight vector.** This is a simulated derivation checked against the figures, not a ground reading (see `05_what_shape_means.md` §7).

## RESIDUAL

The question offers two forms for one object: one scalar, or five dimensions with equal weight. Three questions are owed: (1) are the five conditions a partition or a union, (2) what kind of bond does "equal weight" assert, and (3) in what order may composition legitimately happen?

## DOMAIN CENSUS

Ingredients named by the question: income/consumption, health, security, relationships, autonomy. Bonds available: the seven of Fig 12 plus negation. Anchors: the person for D1, the decision ground for D2 and D3 (see `01` §1.2). No new primitive is available without import, and none is introduced here.

## DERIVATION

### Q1 — Union or partition?

- Fig 9 :4-5: *"First: is there a fact here? Disagreements that do not shrink with effort, where each rule keeps working well on a different subset, are not one hard question but several well-formed ones. Split it rather than search."*
- Fig 9 :55-56: *"A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones. A single lever clears the region where only one condition holds and leaves the intersections standing, because the second condition still binds."*
- Income works on a subset; the other four work on other subsets; the intersections (sick high-income, insecure high-income, isolated high-income) are bound by the second condition even after the first is satisfied.
- The Library of Babel essay, opened 2026-11-23: *"Perhaps your problem was a bundle or conflation of several problems masquerading as one?"*
- ⇒ **Union.** Five conditions with overlapping intersections, not five points on one line. Fig 9 :54 then applies: *"And when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again."*

**Falsifier, written before the run:** exhibit one instrument that returns independent per-slot residuals for all five conditions and show that the intersections vanish, so one lever clears the whole region. Then the union collapses to a partition and a single scalar may be sufficient.

### Q2 — What kind of bond is "equal weight"?

- Fig 12 :46: *"A bond is the typed relation between two operands. There are seven, they are not interchangeable, and which one holds is not recoverable from the operands."*
- Fig 12 :5: *"A set of ingredients is not one. The bond is the rest of the information, and it is the half nothing was recording."*
- Operators table :11-15: `+` is *"conjunction, both hold at once"*, symmetric, with the test *"swap the operands. Does the meaning change? No."* Operators table :47-51: `⋛` is *"comparison, a threshold relation between two quantities"*, ordered, with the test *"is it about which is larger, rather than both being present? Yes."* Operators table :23-27: `≡` is *"identity, two names, one referent"*, symmetric.
- A weighted sum of five dimensions uses `+` for conjunction and a scalar multiplier for each slot. The multipliers are not bonds in the seven-bond set; they are parameters. A parameter that is not paid for at the ground is a constant authored by whoever set it (Fig 10 :48: *"a convention nothing can check is a constant the seat authored, carrying the seat's authority"*).
- "Equal weight" additionally asserts a symmetry among operands, which is a claim about the bond (Fig 12 :92-93), not a fact about the ingredients. Fig 12 :101: *"A bond that costs nothing is a bond that carries nothing, which is the flat case."*
- The word "needs" in the question is a `⋛` claim: it compares the adequacy of two measures against a threshold. Operators table :51-52: *"a goal is a comparison, which is why a vocabulary without it cannot state one"*. A comparison is not an identity (`≡`) and not a sum (`+`). Income being sufficient for well-being is not the same claim as income plus the other four being sufficient; the bond differs.
- ⇒ **The equal-weight composite is a flat formula recording ingredients without the arrangement.** The priced alternative is a molecule whose bonds are declared, dated and checkable. This is a structural result, not a claim that indices are useless.

**Falsifier:** produce an instrument that pays for each multiplier at the ground and returns all five slots in one currency at one level. If it does, the multipliers are priced bonds rather than authored constants and the flat-form finding fails for that instrument.

### Q3 — Are the five dimensions isomers?

- Fig 12 :117: *"isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."*
- Fig 12 :124: *"functional group. A substructure whose presence fixes the outcome. The group says what kind; the qualifier says which member."*
- Two well-being profiles with the same five readings and different arrangements (one where income mediates health, one where relationships mediate security) are **isomers** under Fig 12 :117: the ingredient list is identical and the behaviour differs. A composite score that lists only the five ingredients cannot tell them apart, and Fig 12 :14-16 says nothing signals that anything was lost.
- Two profiles that share only the shape "a scalar reading composed with several domain readings" are **members of a functional group**, not isomers: the group says what kind, the qualifier says which member. The income-only option and the five-dimension option are in this weaker relation if both are written as `reading → threshold → decision`.
- The isomer claim is the stronger and the more useful one, because it makes the arrangement the thing to measure. It requires the two profiles to be written over the same atom set. That condition is live, not assumed, and is the fixed-atom strain named below.

**Falsifier:** if either profile can be written without an atom the other requires, the isomer claim fails and the functional-group reading stands.

### Q4 — What order is the composition?

- Fig 3 :20-21: *"The chain is ordered by dependency, not by importance. Each link consumes what the one above produces, so effort spent above the break arrives at a step that cannot use it."*
- Fig 12 :104-105: *"A settled molecule becomes an atom for whatever composes over it. So irreducible is a fact about a level and never about the world."* (The full figure line continues *"and there is no bottom"*.)
- Order: (1) each slot returns a reading at first order; (2) each reading settles against the person's judgment through an independent instrument; (3) only then does a second-order composition become legal, because each settled reading is now an atom; (4) the round trip from the composition back down to slot predictions yields R_T, measured not assumed (Fig 4 :30).
- The question as posed reverses the order: it asks which composite to adopt before asking what each slot returns and whether it settled. That is effort above the break. Fig 3 :27: *"A link with no instrument is not a link that works. It is a link nobody has looked at."*
- A composite built from unsettled readings is a molecule whose parts were never admitted. Fig 12 :106: *"Nothing composes over what was never admitted as a part."*

**Falsifier:** show a composite whose per-slot readings have not settled but whose score pays at the ground for the decision it is used for. If that happens, the dependency order is not a constraint for this domain and Q4 fails.

### Q5 — The habitat analogue

- Fig 11 :43-44: *"The habitat is enumerated, never composed."* *"List everything in contact with the residual, then what is in contact with those, and outward until the cascade stops mattering."*
- A fixed five-item list is not the habitat; it is a candidate set assembled before the residual was described. Fig 10 :73-74: *"The residual selects the members, but only from what is available. A candidate set chosen by a participant is an authored room however well the selection is reasoned, so the pool must be unauthored first and examined second."*
- Contact cascades. Fig 11 :52-54 gives the DDT example: first-order enumeration, second-order effect, and the residual that mattered three steps out. For well-being, a change in security may act on relationships two steps out and on health three steps out. A per-slot snapshot misses the cascade.
- ⇒ The enumeration is of the residual's habitat, not of a standing list. Fig 11 :51: *"No standing list of good actors survives a change of residual."*

**Falsifier:** exhibit a five-item list that remains complete and correctly valenced across two residuals with different valence structure.

## CONCLUSION

The five conditions form a union with overlapping intersections, not a partition and not a single scalar (Q1). "Equal weight" is a bond claim, and as currently used it is a free bond, that is, the flat-formula collapse rather than a priced molecule (Q2). Two profiles with identical ingredients and different arrangements are isomers, provided the fixed-atom condition holds; otherwise they are members of a functional group (Q3). Composition must wait until each slot has settled before it is admitted at the next level (Q4). The slot list must be enumerated from the residual's habitat, not assumed from a standing menu (Q5).

What survives is a method: keep slots separate, buy independence with different instruments, settle before composing, declare the weights as a convention, and measure the round trip. What does not survive is the request for a single reliable number or a universal equal-weight vector.
