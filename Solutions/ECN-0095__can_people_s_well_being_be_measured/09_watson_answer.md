## QUESTION

Decide, in effect terms, what the Tether says about the choice between a monetary scalar (income and consumption) and five dimensions with equal weight (income, health, security, relationships, autonomy) as a reliable measure of people's well-being. Option A: assert that the monetary scalar is reliable on its own. Option B: assert that the five dimensions need equal weight. What each leads to: A gives a cheap comparable number that reads material command and is blind to the other slots; because it aggregates, a live slot disappears into the average, and if it decides an outcome a convention becomes a verdict. B gives better coverage but presents a declared weight vector as a measurement result, and a flat sum of ingredients loses the arrangement. The Tether offers a third form: split the union, keep the residual per slot, use the person as anchor, take independent readings, treat weights as a convention with a refusal seat, and report the round trip and the gap.

## DOMAIN CENSUS

R by arity, symmetry and scale, then one line per domain giving actors / currency / anchor, partition or union, and order by dependency.

- **R (the residual).** The gap between what an instrument reports about a person and what settles the question for that person. Arity: five named slots plus whatever else the habitat contains, so a vector, not a scalar. Symmetry: the equal-weight option asserts exchangeability under a symmetric bond, which is a claim about the bond and not recoverable from the operands. Scale: first order per slot and person, second order at the person's overall judgment, third order at a population index used for policy. Fig 1 :19 *"The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated."* Fig 9 :55 *"A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones."*
- **Domain 1, instrument reading.** Actors: market counters, life-satisfaction scales, affect reports, health, security, relationship and autonomy measures. Currency: readings per slot. Anchor: the person's own judgment, because the question is constitutively about a person. This is a union of readings, not one reading.
- **Domain 2, aggregation and index.** Actors: the five ingredients, the weights, the normalisation, the composite score. Currency: bits for description and actions for settling, which do not add. Anchor: the ground for the decision the index is used for. This is a composition, so its bonds must be priced or declared.
- **Domain 3, decision and verification.** Actors: the index, the decision rule, the person measured, the seat holding the anchor. Currency: outcomes. Anchor: a result that does not update on the index or on the reaction to it. This is where a convention becomes a verdict.
- **Partition or union.** Union. A single lever clears the income-only region and leaves the intersections standing.
- **Order by dependency.** Read each slot, then range it against the person's judgment through an independent instrument, then settle, then compose, then measure the round trip. Refuse before minting (Formula step 2). Describe the residual before choosing a frame (Fig 9 :27-28).

## VERDICT (from the figures)

The question's either/or is a union of overlapping conditions. One scalar and one equal-weight vector are both refused as results. The person's judgment is the anchor for a question about a person, and market counters are instruments, so income stays as one slot and cannot settle the whole question. The residual remains indexed per slot; aggregation hides a live slot. A composite is a molecule whose weights are bonds, so equal weight is a declared convention and not a ground-paid result once the index decides an outcome. Independence requires different instruments. No figure line returns a weight vector, and that is recorded as a shape-not-a-name gap with the instrument and decision frame that would close it. The commensurability of the five slots remains a live type strain. This is `specified`, `general` on the shape, ungrounded, candidate.

- **cost if wrong:** bounded and locatable. The answer keeps per-slot readings, states its convention and carries five refuters, so an error is findable. The avoidable cost is spending the independence of outside readers by sending a conclusion instead of a separator. Fig 2 :42 "Buy independence in proportion to the cost of being wrong."
- **undoable:** yes. A declared convention can be revised, a slot added, a weight changed and an instrument replaced without withdrawing the union structure. Nothing here is staked on the permanence of the five-item list.
- **reversed by:** a ground reading, not agreement. A source-discriminating instrument that names which slot drives a score; a commensurate instrument that pays for the weights; a single scalar that leaves no live per-slot residual; a figure line that returns a weight vector; or a demonstration that a composite remains a checkable convention while deciding outcomes. Until one fires, the verdict stands as candidate.
