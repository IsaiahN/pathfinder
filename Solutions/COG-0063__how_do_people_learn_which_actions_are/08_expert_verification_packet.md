# Expert verification packet: the Tether on moral, social and unusual

**What this is:** a claim in a form a researcher in ritual and social learning can check. Mode, derivation and refuters are stated before the conclusion, so a reader knows what to look for and what would settle it.

**Mode: `specified`, not `grounded`.** No experiment was run. This is a shape and a route, not a result.

## 1. The claim, in one paragraph

Learning which actions are morally wrong, socially disapproved or merely unusual is not one loop. It is a union of three loops with different grounds: a consequence record that does not update on opinion for wrongness, peer agreement for disapproval, and a frequency count for unusualness. Agreement is efficient for a convention and disqualifying for a verdict, so the copy mechanism that transmits disapproval cannot by itself ground wrongness. Valence is a relation between an act and a ground, not a property carried by the act. What crosses generations is a re-applicable method for ranging new acts, not a recorded list of prohibitions; ritual is a maintained habitat for that transmission. Revising a category needs a refusal operator, which a vocabulary of bare prohibitions does not have.

## 2. Three checkable sub-claims

### C1 : framework-internal; checkable by reading Figures 2 and 9

- **Statement:** the three categories have different grounds, so agreement has opposite valence in the disapproval and wrongness domains.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 2 :29 "An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks." Fig 2 :40 "disqualifying for a verdict: agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool" Fig 2 :41 "efficient for a convention: agreement about a shared meaning is the product, not a corruption of it" Fig 9 :54 "And when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again."
- **Refuter:** hold the consequence record fixed and vary peer agreement. If authority-independent "wrong" judgments move with peer agreement, C1 fails.

### C2 : framework-internal plus cognition-facing; checkable by a reader of Turiel and of the ritual literature

- **Statement:** valence is relative to the ground a frame ranges against; there is no standing list of wrong acts. Overimitation and high-fidelity ritual transmission are method-convention mechanisms, not verdict mechanisms.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 11 :49 "An actor's valence is relative to the residual, not a property it carries." Fig 11 :50 "The same actor is a benefactor to one residual and a malfactor to another, so mutualism and parasitism are relations rather than kinds." Fig 4 :22 "Only methods cross up. Only head starts come down." Fig 4 :25 "A recording carried upward looks like knowledge and is a description of one occasion."
- **What to check:** does the instrumental record distinguish authority-independent, generalisable judgments from authority-dependent, local ones, as Machery and Stich 2022 report for Turiel's criterion judgments? Does a transmitted prohibition list, with no ranging method, actually classify novel acts?
- **Refuter:** a verbatim prohibition list with no re-applicable method that reliably classifies novel acts against a ground would break C2.

### C3 : framework-internal; checkable by reading Figures 5 and 12

- **Statement:** reclassifying an act requires a refusal operator. A normative vocabulary that lacks it can accumulate exceptions and cannot carry a correction.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 5 :53 "refuted: a term we hold is wrong; a term is owed that says so" Fig 12 :140 "A vocabulary that cannot refuse can only accumulate." Also the Operators table: "a refutation is a negation, which is why a vocabulary without it can only accumulate".
- **Refuter:** a community that retracts a held rule, not merely adds an exception, with no refusal operator in its normative vocabulary, would break C3.

## 3. What is explicitly not claimed

- Not which acts are wrong in any society. Fig 9 :32 "you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself"
- Not that the three categories are universal. World sources conflict and the conflict is recorded, not resolved.
- Not that ritual creates morality or that a ritual authority's verdict is settled. Fig 10 :47 "a convention that decides an outcome has stopped being one"
- Not `grounded`. Nothing here was settled by an experiment.
- Not a claim that any named scholar endorses this. The world sources are corroboration only.

## 5. What would settle it

1. A two-by-two design holding consequences fixed and varying agreement, measuring authority-independent wrongness judgments.
2. A frequency-only condition with harm and authority information removed, looking for a stable wrongness verdict.
3. An ethnographic case of rule retraction with a vocabulary audit for refusal operators.
4. A direct measurement of the vocabulary/objective link before any imitation measure.

## 6. What an expert can check in twenty minutes

1. Read Fig 2 :29, :35, :40-41, Fig 9 :54, Fig 11 :49-50, Fig 4 :22-25, Fig 5 :53 and Fig 12 :140. Ten short passages.
2. Check C2 against their own reading of Turiel, overimitation and ritual transmission. Are those mechanisms being described as verdict mechanisms anywhere? If so, that is the failure to report.
3. Name one instrument that would distinguish a consequence-anchored verdict from a copied convention, or say why none is possible.
4. Send the refutation. A failure is more useful than an endorsement.
