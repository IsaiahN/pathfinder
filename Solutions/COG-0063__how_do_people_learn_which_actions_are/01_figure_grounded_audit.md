## 0. The claim under audit, stated as a residual in effect terms

The question asks for a learning mechanism. Stated in effect terms, a person must assign an action to one of three categories and the mechanism is whatever closes the gap between the assigned category and the category the environment actually returns.

- **R (the residual).** For action slot `a`, `R = |Γ(b,a) - o'|`: the gap between the learner's predicted category (wrong, disapproved, unusual, or none) and the category the social environment returns. The Symbols table: R is "The gap between prediction and observation. The central quantity: everything the system does is driven by it. It is the aim, not only the error signal, and it is always a slice". It is a slice per action, not one global classificatory verdict.
- **What is being decided.** Which ground settles each category, and what each candidate mechanism leads to. Copying peers produces a convention. Ranging an action against consequences produces a verdict. Counting frequency produces "unusual". A mechanism that works for the third does not work for the first.
- **What an answer would look like (bound as an ordinary term before the run).** A *three-loop answer*: three distinguishable grounds, three per-slot residuals, a transmission rule for methods, a refusal operator for revisions, and an explicit statement of what is not settled. This is the sought-for shape made refusable, per `THE_FORMULA.md` "Direction" and Fig 5 :65-66.

**The central candidate claim under audit:** "people learn moral wrongness by learning what their group disapproves of". The audit asks whether the figures license that identification. They do not, and the reason is structural rather than empirical.

## 1. Domain census (before choosing any figure line)

**R by arity, symmetry, scale.** Arity: three conditions (wrong, disapproved, unusual), so a **union compound**, not one hard question. Symmetry: the action-category relation is asymmetric, and the three categories have different grounds, so the three loops are not interchangeable. Scale: first order inside one learner's loop (per action slot), second order across generations when a settled classification becomes an atom for the next learner (Fig 12 :104 "A settled molecule becomes an atom for whatever composes over it.").

**One line per domain. A nameable anchor is required before a domain counts.**

| domain | actors | currency | anchor (nameable?) | question type |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| D1 Individual learner | learner, action, observed reaction | per-slot residual R | yes: the consequence or reaction actually returned, which does not move when peers change their minds | first-order loop, partition of action slots |
| D2 Peer agreement | peers, majority, authority, prestige | agreement, imitation | **no independent anchor inside peer opinion**; if it has one it is D1's anchor imported | convention generator, not a verdict domain |
| D3 Ritual and maintained habitat | ritual specialists, participants, transmitted practices | introduction of actors, high-fidelity transmission | yes: the ground the ritual keeps reachable (consequence, harm record, counted frequency) | maintained mode, seat acts on habitat |
| D4 Cultural transmission | generations, methods, head starts | methods up, head starts down (Fig 4) | yes: the ground, which does not decay | second-order membrane question |

**Partition or union.** Union, not partition. The three categories overlap: an action can be unusual and disapproved, disapproved and wrong, or unusual without either. Fig 9 :55: "A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones." The right move is to unbundle before searching, not to average.

**Order by dependency (Fig 3).** D1 perception and vocabulary first, then objective, then planning and carry; D2 only after D1 has an anchor; D3 after D1 and D2 are separated; D4 last, because a method can only cross after it has settled at least once. Refusal before mint (`THE_FORMULA.md` step 2), and describe the residual before choosing a frame (Fig 9 :27-28).

**Strain test applied during the census.** The initial temptation was to treat "socially disapproved" and "morally wrong" as one domain with a strength parameter. That strains Fig 2 :40-41 and Fig 7 :35, which give agreement opposite valences in the two cases. Re-census: two domains with different anchors. No figure line was forced.

## 2. Findings

### F1 : The question is a union, not one hard question (decisive for the shape)

Fig 9 :5: "Disagreements that do not shrink with effort, where each rule keeps working well on a different subset, are not one hard question but several well-formed ones."
Fig 9 :54: "And when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again."
Fig 1 :19: "The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated."
Fig 1 :22: "Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average."

A single answer of the form "people learn by copying" cannot close R for all three categories because the three categories have different grounds. "Wrong" needs a consequence record that does not update on opinion; "disapproved" is produced by agreement; "unusual" is a frequency count. Blending them produces a global rating in which the live per-action signal disappears.

**Mode: `general` (structural).** Triangulated across Fig 9 (search geometry) and Fig 1 (per-slot measurement), two figures with low overlap.

**Refuter:** a re-census that shows one ground settling all three categories, with no residual left on any slot after one loop, would break F1.

### F2 : Valence is relative to the residual, not carried by the action

Fig 11 :49: "An actor's valence is relative to the residual, not a property it carries."
Fig 11 :50: "The same actor is a benefactor to one residual and a malfactor to another, so mutualism and parasitism are relations rather than kinds."
Fig 11 :51: "No standing list of good actors survives a change of residual."
Fig 9 :31: "your words are borrowed too, so describe the effect rather than the cause"

The same act can be a malfactor to a harm-residual, a neutral item to a frequency-residual, and a benefactor to a cohesion-residual. So there is no standing list of acts that are wrong in themselves. This is **not** a claim that all classifications are arbitrary: it is a claim about what must be measured. The measurement is the relation between the act and the ground a frame ranges against, not an intrinsic property of the act.

World corroboration (secondary, different field): Machery and Stich, *Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy*, 2022, report that Shweder's Indian participants judged a son eating chicken after his father's death to be a serious transgression, and that Turiel and colleagues explained the difference by a difference in **beliefs about harm**, not by a different definition of morality. The effect description is what travels. Overlap with the figures: low (philosophy of mind plus developmental psychology, different author and venue).

**Mode: `specified`.** Triangulated across Fig 11 and Fig 9.

**Refuter:** a demonstration that a fixed intrinsic property of an action (independent of the ground a frame ranges against) predicts the category assignment across all three conditions would break F2.

### F3 : Agreement alone cannot ground a verdict; it can ground a convention

Fig 2 :35: "Alignment is triangulation, not negotiation. A mirror cannot correct you."
Fig 2 :40: "disqualifying for a verdict: agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool"
Fig 2 :41: "efficient for a convention: agreement about a shared meaning is the product, not a corruption of it"
Fig 7 :35: "A room's agreement is variation, not a verdict."
Seat Map :163: "High correlation is disqualifying for a verdict and efficient for a convention."

This is the precise reason the question must be unbundled. When a person learns "this is disapproved" by copying peers, the high correlation between learner and peers is **the product**: a convention has been transmitted. When the same person learns "this is wrong" by the same route, the high correlation is **the collapse**: there is no anchor, so the group converges on whoever had the most influence, and the drift is coherent because nobody notices. "Everyone does it" is evidence for usualness and for disapproval. It is not evidence for wrongness.

World corroboration (secondary, low overlap): Legare and Nielsen 2020 report that "Cultural transmission is made possible by a set of cognitive adaptations that facilitate the acquisition and transmission of information within and across generations, such as biases for conformity and consensus, high-fidelity imitation and teaching". That is a mechanism for convention transmission, and the paper explicitly distinguishes transmitted conventions from functions such as avoiding harm. It does not license promoting the convention to a verdict. Peralta, Kertesz and Iniguez 2022 (physics and society, arXiv) model consensus and polarisation as outcomes of exchange among agents, again showing that agreement is a dynamical state, not a ground.

**Mode: `specified`.** Triangulated across Fig 2 and Fig 7 (different figures, different roles) plus the seat map. World corroboration from two fields with no shared author with the framework.

**Refuter:** a case in which two frames with zero independent evidence between them nevertheless produce a stable, authority-independent wrongness verdict that survives removal of all consequence records would break F3.

### F4 : The anchor must be legitimate and must not update

Fig 2 :29: "An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks."
Fig 2 :30: "If it moves when you push on it, it is not an anchor."
Fig 2 :37: "Human judgment is the right anchor for is this a good answer for a person, because the question is about people."
Fig 2 :33: "The anchor must not update."

For this question, which is constitutively about people, human judgment is a legitimate anchor. But the same line that licenses it warns that an instrument "can be wrong on axes they do not measure" (Fig 2 :37-38). Peer agreement measures coordination; it does not measure harm. Consequence records measure harm; they do not measure coordination. The anchor for "wrong" must be the consequence record that does not move when peers move. The anchor for "disapproved" is the peer reaction itself, which does update, so "disapproved" is a convention reading rather than a verdict.

World corroboration (secondary): Machery and Stich 2022 report Turiel's criterion judgments, that an action judged wrong, **authority independent**, and generalizable to other places and times is classified as moral, while an action judged wrong, **authority dependent**, and not generalizable is classified as conventional. They also report the criticism that Turiel's definition "does not travel well" and the SES finding that lower SES groups moralized more than higher SES groups. So the anchor claim is discriminable in principle, and its cross-cultural scope is genuinely open. The SEP entry additionally warns that authors sometimes "go on to write as though they had established that the judgment is true" after establishing only that it is moral rather than conventional. That is the output-layer failure genus the figures name.

**Mode: `specified`.** Triangulated across Fig 2 and the seat map's anchor test. World corroboration from a different field, with the open cross-cultural question recorded rather than hidden.

**Refuter:** a measurement in which the consequence record is held fixed while peer agreement is varied, and participants' authority-independent "wrong" judgments move with peer agreement, would break F4.

### F5 : The chain breaks at vocabulary and objective, not at perception or planning

Fig 3 :20: "The chain is ordered by dependency, not by importance."
Fig 3 :24: "Why the middle link is where chains usually break."
Fig 3 :27: "A link with no instrument is not a link that works. It is a link nobody has looked at."
Fig 3 :19: "Optimising a link that already works cannot rescue one that does not."

A learner needs terms for what an action affords and a way to say what would count as progress before a route can be planned. Most visible research effort in social learning lands on imitation fidelity (an observable) and on outcomes (also observable), not on this middle link. Fig 3 :25-26 says it "has neither, so it attracts the least attention and the fewest instruments". The prediction is therefore about where evidence is missing, not about what the answer is.

World corroboration (secondary): Nielsen and Tomaselli 2010, *Psychological Science*, report that "From 18 months of age, children will routinely copy even arbitrary and unnecessary actions", and argue that overimitation is fundamental to cultural transmission. Legare and Nielsen 2020 note that "The social conventionality and causal opacity of rituals increase high-fidelity imitation and transmission and inhibit individual-level innovation". Both sources measure the transmission link. Neither measures the middle link. This is corroboration of a *gap in instruments*, not of a conclusion.

**Mode: `specified`.** Single-source within the figures (Fig 3), with the absence of instruments corroborated by two world sources. The corroboration is weak corroboration of a missing measurement, not of a verdict.

**Refuter:** if a well-instrumented study shows that the middle link is already measured and not the binding constraint, F5 fails as a prediction about where evidence is missing.

### F6 : What transmits is a method, not a recording; ritual is a maintained habitat

Fig 4 :22: "Only methods cross up. Only head starts come down."
Fig 4 :25: "A recording carried upward looks like knowledge and is a description of one occasion."
Fig 11 :37: "Where a seat exists, something is doing work, and it is exactly what the seat does: enumerate the habitat, rank by cascade, introduce actors, build instruments, read the ground."
Fig 11 :61: "Introduction is available; exclusion is not."
Fig 10 :47: "a convention that decides an outcome has stopped being one"

A memorised list of forbidden acts is a recording of one occasion. A re-applicable way to range a new act against the consequence record is a method. Ritual is the maintained arrangement that keeps the ground reachable by enacting the classification, and its power is asymmetric: it can introduce actors into the habitat and cannot subtract the habitat. When a ritual's authority actually decides whether an act is wrong, it has stopped being a convention and become a verdict, and the seat that should keep the ground reachable has become a frame.

World corroboration (secondary): McNamara 2024, *KZfSS Kölner Zeitschrift für Soziologie und Sozialpsychologie*, says "Ritual simultaneously cues social learning and can alleviate anxiety by downregulating neurological arousal" and describes ritual as a costly signal that communicates hard-to-fake commitment. Legare and Nielsen 2020 call rituals "ubiquitous socially stipulated conventions" and note that "Ignorance of these behaviours risks ostracism and potentially violent reprisal". Both are consistent with a maintained habitat that transmits methods under cost. Neither settles wrongness.

**Mode: `specified`.** Triangulated across Fig 4, Fig 11 and Fig 10 (three different figures, low overlap). World corroboration from cultural evolution and cognitive anthropology, different authors and venues.

**Refuter:** a demonstration that a verbatim list of prohibitions, with no re-applicable ranging method, reliably classifies novel actions against a ground would break F6.

### F7 : Revising a classification needs a refusal operator

Fig 5 :51: "The four kinds sort a gap by what is missing."
Fig 5 :53: "refuted: a term we hold is wrong; a term is owed that says so"
Fig 12 :140: "A vocabulary that cannot refuse can only accumulate."
Operators table :58: "a refutation is a negation, which is why a vocabulary without it can only accumulate"

Moving an action from "wrong" to "disapproved" or from "disapproved" to "unusual" is not adding a term. It is refusing the standing of the old classification so that nothing can compose over it. Fig 6 :42-43 makes the same point for the reach: "What is recorded only grows. What is reachable is derived, and it can fall." A vocabulary with only "this act is bad" can add exceptions forever and cannot say "that rule is false".

This gives a checkable structural prediction about ritual and social learning systems: look for whether the community's normative vocabulary has a unary refusal (a way to say that a held rule is wrong), as distinct from a list of ever-more-specific exceptions. Where it is absent, the system can correct by refit and cannot carry the correction.

**Mode: `specified`.** Triangulated across Fig 5 (fifth bin), Fig 12 (unary operator), Fig 6 (reach falls) and the Operators table.

**Refuter:** a community that demonstrably retracts a held rule (not merely adds an exception) while its normative vocabulary contains no refusal operator would break F7.

### F8 : Provenance separates convergence from copying

Fig 8 :51: "Convergent derivation and adopted import look identical in the contents. Only the record of where each came from separates them, and the surplus lives only where the closures differ."
Fig 6 :23: "No frame escapes its own closure. The bound moves only on contact with a frame whose closure differs."
Fig 9 :60: "Use filters for the budget and witnesses for the verdict. Never let a filter hand you a verdict."

Two people who each range an act against the consequence record and reach the same verdict have derived it convergently at high independence. Two people who copy each other produce the same content with zero added evidence. Content cannot tell them apart; only the record can. Frequency (Fig 9 :60) is a filter for the budget, not a witness for the verdict. So "everyone agrees" is admissible evidence for a convention and inadmissible evidence for wrongness.

**Mode: `specified`.** Triangulated across Fig 8, Fig 6 and Fig 9.

**Refuter:** a case where the content alone, with no provenance, reliably distinguishes independent derivation from copied adoption would break F8.

### F9 : "Merely unusual" is a count, not a verdict

Fig 1 :28: "The gap is measured per object, not per step."
Fig 9 :32: "you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself"

Frequency is a property of the habitat, not of the ground that settles wrongness. A count can tell you how often an act occurs; it cannot tell you whether it harms. The figures return a kind of thing (a shape for the classification loop), not the name of a particular action. Naming a specific act as wrong is the searcher's contribution and must be recorded as such, with provenance.

World corroboration (secondary): Machery and Stich 2022 report that the "definition of morality" project failed to reach consensus across decades, and that even the criterion judgments used to identify moral judgments were hypothesised rather than defensively established. That is consistent with the framework's shape-not-a-name limit.

**Mode: `specified`.** Single-source within the figures for the count-versus-verdict distinction, corroborated in a different field.

**Refuter:** a demonstration that a frequency count alone, with harm and authority information both removed, produces a stable authority-independent wrongness verdict across independent frames would break F9.

### F10 : Both modes exist: selection without a maintainer, and work by a seat

Fig 11 :36: "Where nothing maintains anything, intelligence is a label applied afterwards to a lineage that met its ground."
Fig 11 :40: "An improvement that does not change contact changes nothing, however much it improves."
Fig 11 :61: "Introduction is available; exclusion is not."

Unmaintained: no seat, pure contact, what survives is what the ground kept. Maintained: a seat exists, enumerates the habitat, ranks by cascade, introduces actors, builds instruments, reads the ground. "People learn" conflates the two. A practice that persists because the ground kept it and a practice that persists because a ritual seat introduced it have different explanations and different failure modes. Capability is a property of agent-and-habitat, never of the agent alone.

**Mode: `general` (structural).** Triangulated across Fig 11 and the seat map's habitat section.

**Refuter:** an agent-only improvement that moves the ground (the standing refuter Fig 11 :71-72 names) would break the capability claim.

### F11 : What is genuinely a gap after re-census

Fig 9 :32: "you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself"
Fig 8 :39: "So a search that finds nothing is never a proof of absence."

After the census, the following are **not** decided by any figure line:

1. Which specific acts are in fact wrong in a given society. No instrument was run, and the figures return a shape rather than a name.
2. Whether the three-category distinction is universal. World sources conflict on scope: Turiel's harm criterion versus Shweder's and Haidt's cross-cultural challenges, with SES effects reported larger than location effects. Per the conflict rule this sends us back to the census, and the census says the three conditions have different grounds. It does not resolve universality.
3. Whether harm, fairness, loyalty, authority and purity are irreducible primitives of the moral domain. That is an empirical domain census of a different object, and this session did not run it.

Verdict for these: `gap`, plainly. Unreachable is a verdict, not a silence.

## 3. Pre-registered refuter (written before the conclusion)

The shape claim is refuted if any of the following holds:

1. **Union refuter.** A re-census shows one ground settling wrongness, disapproval and unusualness, with no slot residual left after one loop (breaks F1).
2. **Anchor refuter.** Holding the consequence record fixed and varying peer agreement moves authority-independent "wrong" judgments (breaks F3 and F4).
3. **Count refuter.** A frequency count alone, with harm and authority information removed, produces a stable wrongness verdict across independent frames (breaks F9).
4. **Refusal refuter.** A community retracts a held rule, not merely adds an exception, while its normative vocabulary has no refusal operator (breaks F7).
5. **Transmission refuter.** A verbatim prohibition list with no re-applicable method reliably classifies novel actions against a ground (breaks F6).

These are written before the synthesis in `07_the_tether_answer.md`. A falsifier written before the run is a design instrument, not only an honesty device (Seat Map :287).

## 4. Recommendation

1. Lead with the union: ask which ground is being used before asking which mechanism is used (F1).
2. Keep the three channels separate in any study design: consequence record, peer reaction, frequency count. Do not pool them into one liking or wrongness rating (F1, F4, F9).
3. Measure the middle link, not only imitation fidelity: what does the act afford, and what would count as progress in classifying it (F5).
4. Record provenance for every norm: independently derived or adopted, because content cannot distinguish them (F8).
5. Look for the refusal operator before claiming that a community "learned" a correction (F7).
6. Do not ask ritual to decide wrongness. A convention that decides an outcome has stopped being one (F6).
7. Label what is not settled as `gap` rather than filling it (F11).

### Updated verdict

The shape survives: a three-condition union with three grounds, per-slot residuals, a method-only transmission rule, a maintained habitat, and a refusal operator for revisions. No specific action, no universal category list, and no cross-cultural verdict survives. No strain after re-census: the apparent Fig 2 versus Fig 7 tension is resolved by assigning agreement to a convention domain and triangulation to a verdict domain.
