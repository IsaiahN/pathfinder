**Subject:** Check one shape claim about moral learning before you decide whether the framework is worth reading

> Hi [name],
>
> I am sending one checkable claim, not a theory. I am not asking you to agree with it, and I am not asking you to read the framework first. If the claim does not survive, there is nothing else to look at.
>
> **The residual, in effect terms.** A person has to assign an action to one of three categories: morally wrong, socially disapproved, or merely unusual. The claim is that this is **one question with one learning mechanism**. The shape claim I derived is that it is **three loops with three different grounds**: a consequence record that does not update for "wrong", peer agreement for "disapproved", and a frequency count for "unusual". A mechanism that closes one does not close the others. This is `specified`, not `grounded`: it says what any answer must look like, not which actions are wrong.
>
> **The three parts you can check:**
> 1. Agreement alone cannot ground a verdict. In the figures, a group that only checks against itself converges on whoever had the most influence, and the drift is coherent precisely because nobody notices. Agreement is described as *efficient for a convention* and *disqualifying for a verdict*. My claim is that "socially disapproved" is the first and "morally wrong" is the second.
> 2. Valence is relative to the residual, not carried by the act. The same act can be a malfunction for a harm record and a neutral item for a frequency count. So there is no standing list of wrong acts; there is a relation between an act and the ground a person ranges against.
> 3. Revising a classification needs a refusal operator. A vocabulary that can only say "this act is bad" can add exceptions without end and cannot say "that rule is false". My claim is that a community retracts a norm only when it has a way to refuse the old classification.
>
> **What would refute it, written before I sent this:**
> 1. Holding the consequence record fixed and varying peer agreement moves authority-independent "wrong" judgments.
> 2. A frequency count alone, with harm and authority information removed, produces a stable wrongness verdict across independent people.
> 3. A community retracts a held rule while its normative vocabulary has no refusal operator.
> 4. One ground settles all three categories with no residual left on any action slot.
>
> If any one holds, I withdraw the claim. A one-line "this fails because..." is more useful to me than agreement. If you would rather not engage, no reply is needed.
>
> **If it survives, and only then:** here are the public sources. The figures, not my summary, are the authority.
> - Figures with line numbers, local extract in the folder I can send: `FRAMEWORK/reference/03-FIGURES_TEXT.md` in the project copy.
> - "Ritual explained: interdisciplinary answers to Tinbergen's four questions", Legare and Nielsen, 2020, open access: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7423255/
> - "The Moral/Conventional Distinction", Machery and Stich, 2022: https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/moral-conventional/
>
> **If you want to use the machinery:** the figures are public. Run the loop on a problem you know well and can check against a ground. Write down what would refute your result *before* you run it, and state whether your output is `general`, `specified`, or `grounded`. A reported failure is more useful to me than a reported success.
