# The Tether's answer on moral, social and unusual

**Mode: `specified`, not `grounded`.** This is a shape and a route, not a result. Simulated derivation checked against the figures (`05_what_shape_means.md` §7), not a ground reading.

## 1. The residual it answers

A person must assign an action to one of three categories: morally wrong, socially disapproved, or merely unusual. The candidate answer "people learn by copying what their group does" treats that as one question. The residual does not close under that answer, because the same copy rule produces a verdict in one domain and a convention in another, and the figures give agreement opposite valences in those two cases (Fig 2 :40-41).

## 2. The shape

1. **It is three loops, not one.** Fig 9 :54 "And when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again." Wrong, disapproved and unusual have different grounds: a consequence record that does not update on opinion, a peer reaction that does, and a frequency count. They overlap, so this is a union compound (Fig 9 :55), not a list of separate questions and not one harder question.
2. **Wrongness needs an anchor that does not move.** Fig 2 :29 "An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks." Fig 2 :35 "Alignment is triangulation, not negotiation. A mirror cannot correct you." A person ranges the act against consequences and against peers **separately**, and neither frame can move the consequence record. Agreement among peers is "efficient for a convention" and "disqualifying for a verdict" (Fig 2 :40-41).
3. **Disapproval is a convention, and that is a legitimate product.** Seat Map :163: "High correlation is disqualifying for a verdict and efficient for a convention." A group that transmits disapproval by imitation is doing exactly what conventions are for. The failure is only when that convention is promoted to a verdict about wrongness.
4. **Unusual is a count, not a verdict.** Fig 9 :60 "Use filters for the budget and witnesses for the verdict. Never let a filter hand you a verdict." Frequency tells you how often an act occurs. It cannot tell you whether it harms.
5. **Valence is a relation, not a property.** Fig 11 :49 "An actor's valence is relative to the residual, not a property it carries." The same act is a malfactor to a harm record and a neutral item to a count. There is no standing list of wrong acts, only a relation between an act and the ground a frame ranges against.
6. **What transmits is a method.** Fig 4 :22 "Only methods cross up. Only head starts come down." A memorised prohibition is "a description of one occasion" (Fig 4 :25). Ritual is the maintained habitat that keeps the ground reachable (Fig 11 :37), but "a convention that decides an outcome has stopped being one" (Fig 10 :47).
7. **Revision needs a refusal operator.** Fig 12 :140 "A vocabulary that cannot refuse can only accumulate." Reclassifying an act from wrong to disapproved is a negation, not an added exception. A system without `¬` can correct and cannot carry the correction.
8. **The middle link is where measurement is missing.** Fig 3 :27 "A link with no instrument is not a link that works. It is a link nobody has looked at." Terms for what an act affords, and a way to say what counts as progress in classifying it, are less measured than imitation fidelity.
9. **Both modes exist.** Fig 11 :36 "Where nothing maintains anything, intelligence is a label applied afterwards to a lineage that met its ground." Unmaintained selection and maintained ritual work are different explanations; conflating them is the error.

## 3. What it explicitly does not say

- Not which actions are wrong, disapproved or unusual in any particular society. The figures return a shape, not a name (Fig 9 :32).
- Not that the three categories are universal. World sources conflict on this and the conflict is recorded, not settled (`01` F11).
- Not that moral judgment is only emotion, only reasoning, only harm, or only culture. The derivation constrains the loop, not the content.
- Not that ritual creates morality. It maintains a habitat and transmits methods under cost; when it decides an outcome it has become a verdict.
- Not `grounded`. No experiment was run in this session.

## 4. What would settle it, the ground

1. **Anchor test.** Hold the consequence record fixed and vary peer agreement. Do authority-independent "wrong" judgments move? A two-by-two design with independent consequence and agreement manipulations is the discriminator.
2. **Count test.** Remove all harm and authority information; present only frequency. Does a stable wrongness verdict appear across independent people? If yes, F9 fails.
3. **Refusal test.** Find a community that retracts a held rule (not merely adds an exception) while its normative vocabulary has no refusal operator. If found, F7 fails.
4. **Middle-link test.** Instrument the vocabulary/objective link directly: can the person say what the act affords and what would count as progress in classifying it, before any imitation measure is taken?
5. **Standing falsifiers.** A derivation of one of the six substrate terms from the other five; an admitted direct bond of two states or two structures that cannot be refused; a measurement of a direct act-to-verdict bond with no instrument in the slot.

## 5. The honest stop

**Unreached, not unreachable.** The three-ground split is a structural result from the figures. The empirical question of which ground a given population actually uses in a given case has not been measured here. *The Library of Babel* warns that a bundle masquerading as one problem must be searched as several. This solution does that much and stops. "So a search that finds nothing is never a proof of absence" (Fig 8 :39). The record of which discriminator was missing is the result.

## 6. One-paragraph version for outreach

> The Tether does not say which actions are wrong. It says that the question is three questions wearing one coat. Wrongness needs a consequence record that does not move when peers change their minds; disapproval is a convention produced by agreement, and that is a legitimate product; unusualness is a frequency count, which is a filter and not a verdict. The same act can occupy different categories depending on which ground a person ranges it against, so there is no standing list of wrong acts. What passes between people is a method for ranging new acts, not a list of verdicts; ritual is the maintained habitat that keeps that method in circulation, but a convention that decides an outcome has stopped being a convention. Revising a category needs a refusal operator, and a vocabulary without one can only accumulate. What the derivation offers is the shape, the refuters written in advance, and the instruments that would close it. It is `specified`, not `grounded`, and the center stays open until a ground settles it.
