## 1. A shape is a constraint you can state before you can name the item

The canonical local README gives the limit: what a missing thing would have to look like, "**a shape, not a name**". Fig 9 :32 "you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself". From *The Library of Babel*: "You can cut your search short if the set of books returned is small and descriptive enough that it provides a general shape of the answer that satisfies your question."

For this question a **shape** is the form of the classification loop, stated before naming any particular action. "Three loops with three grounds, each measured per action slot" is a shape. "Doing such and such is wrong" is a name. The derivation stays with the former.

## 2. How the shape is obtained

- *The Library of Babel*: use the vocabulary you already have that is closest to the thing, look for recurring new terms that overlap or correlate with it, and recurse. "With each new term gained, you are able to narrow the space by definition and deepen your vocabulary."
- *The Library of Babel* on bundles, written before this run: "Perhaps your problem was a bundle or conflation of several problems masquerading as one? You will need to search all of those problems individually to find their answers and then combine them in a satisfactory way." This is the essay-level precedent for F1's unbundling.
- Fig 9 :31 "your words are borrowed too, so describe the effect rather than the cause". Describe the three grounds by their effects: what does not move when peers move, what moves with agreement, what is a count.
- *Decoding Tacit Knowledge*: "When contact isn't possible, the best you can send is a plain description of what you'd expect to see, because anyone can check that against the world." That is why the outreach material leads with a refuter rather than with a theory.
- *The Library of Babel* on stopping: "You will know that you are done because the finish line will also be the starting place". The loop stops when the residual stops arriving, not when a preferred conclusion appears.

## 3. What licenses a name

Only two things, both outside the shape:

1. **The ground settles it.** `THE_FORMULA.md` step 5: "the ground settles it. Verified only by what pays. A gate is not the ground." An experiment that holds consequences fixed and varies peer agreement would name which ground a person actually uses.
2. **A provenanced import from a frame whose closure differs**, with a source gate and a shadow test (Fig 6 :23, Fig 8 :51). A moral rule imported from a ritual authority is admissible content, but its provenance must be recorded and it carries no verdict weight until the ground pays.

*Decoding Tacit Knowledge* sharpens the limit: "and they drift together, so neither notices." And: "Only the ground does that." The essay also warns that "Long, close contact pulls the frames together, so the process that makes communication easy slowly uses up that extra reach." For this question, a tight-knit community is the best transmitter of a convention and the worst verifier of a verdict.

## 4. Why the three-category claim is a shape and not a conclusion

*How Intelligence Actually Works* says "indiscernibility is not identity", and that the question of a substance's source cannot be settled from the projection alone. Applied here: the fact that a person says "wrong" after hearing peers say "wrong" is a projection. It does not tell you whether the person ranged the act against a consequence record or merely adopted a convention. The shape claim names the discriminator that would be needed (an anchor that does not update); it does not report that the discriminator has been used.

*Decoding Tacit Knowledge* describes three parts of contact, context, subtext and pretext, and says "Writing carries the context well and the subtext partly" This is a second reason a transmitted verdict cannot carry its own warrant: the learner's history with harm and authority is rebuilt through contact, not sent with the words.

## 5. Correction to my own reading of the shape limit

I initially wrote that "the framework returns a shape, not a name" forbids naming any of the three categories. That overstates it. The canonical README's phrase governs a **missing** primitive, one not held in the library. Here the categories are not primitives of the six-term substrate; they are conditions of a residual, and naming them is describing the question, not minting a new kind. What the limit actually forbids in this solution is naming **which action** is wrong or which specific content fills a category slot, and treating a convention as if it were a verdict. Both claims are now recorded so neither is quietly lost.

Status: `general` for the limit, `specified` for its application to this question. Triangulated across the canonical README, Fig 9 :32, and *The Library of Babel*.

## 6. Prestige is not action

From *The Recursive Self-Improvement Trap*: "Prestige can fund an action. It can give an action a budget to be tried. But it cannot settle an action. Only the ground can settle it. The Ground Maintainer must be blind to prestige." The essay also says "You may all agree and be wrong."

For this question: a ritual specialist's authority can give a norm a budget to be transmitted. It cannot make the norm wrong. A survey of how many people agree can fund a study; it cannot settle which category the action belongs to. Selecting correspondents because they are likely to agree is the named collapse, not corroboration.

## 7. My own audit is a simulation, not a ground reading

From *The Recursive Self-Improvement Trap*: "LLMs can quickly simulate an approximate answer/output with the formula found in the architecture post." And: "the truly testable output would need to be derived from the real architecture or working the formula yourself." This audit is a simulated derivation checked for internal consistency against the figures. It is **not** a test of the claim and must be labelled as such. It cannot settle anything it reports on.

## 8. One-paragraph version

A shape here is the form of the classification loop, not a list of wrong acts. The figures and the essays opened this session converge on three constraints: the question is a bundle that must be split before it is searched; the grounds of the three parts differ, and only the one that does not update on opinion can carry a verdict; and a transmitted verdict cannot carry its own warrant because the learner's history is rebuilt through contact. Naming a particular act as wrong is the searcher's contribution and is owed provenance. This derivation is a simulation, `specified` rather than `grounded`, and it stops where the instruments stop.
