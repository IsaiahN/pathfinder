Three questions are owed here: (1) are the three loops a partition or a union under Fig 12's structure rules, (2) what is their dependency order under Fig 3, and (3) what do the low-overlap analogues add, and where must they be refused.

## DERIVATION

### Q1 : Partition or union?

- Fig 9 :55 "A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones."
- Fig 9 :57 "What remains is smaller and it is also categorically different: a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either."
- An act can be unusual and disapproved, or disapproved and wrong. The intersection is not a harder case of either condition; it is a compound with its own ground question. **Verdict: union, not partition.** Falsifier: a clean partition in which no action ever satisfies two conditions, measured on a coded corpus of judgments.

### Q2 : Are the three loops isomers under Fig 12?

- Fig 12 :117 "isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."
- Fig 12 :124 "functional group. A substructure whose presence fixes the outcome. The group says what kind; the qualifier says which member."
- All three loops share the same skeleton: perceive an action, predict a category, observe, compute `R`, update. That shared substructure is the **functional group** (Fig 12 :124): it fixes the kind, which is a learning loop.
- They differ in which anchor the loop ranges against. Under Fig 12's definition, they are **isomers only if they are written over the same atom set with different arrangements**. The atoms are not the same: one loop's operand is a consequence record, another's is a peer reaction, another's is a count. So as written they are **not isomers**; they are three members of one functional group with different operands.
- The stronger form: if all three are rewritten over the shared atom set `{action, belief, observation, R, ground}` and differ only in which ground is bonded to which slot, then they **are** isomers, and Fig 12's definition applies. That is the claim worth testing.
- **Falsifier, written before the run:** if any one loop cannot be written over the shared atom set without importing a new primitive, the isomer claim fails and the functional-group reading stands.

### Q3 : What is the dependency order?

- Fig 3 :20 "The chain is ordered by dependency, not by importance."
- Fig 3 :19 "Optimising a link that already works cannot rescue one that does not."
- Order: perception (can the person tell acts apart) → vocabulary (what does the act afford) → objective (what counts as progress in classifying it) → planning (which ground to range against) → learn and carry (name what worked and settle it). A study that measures only imitation fidelity is measuring a link above the break if the vocabulary link is absent.
- `THE_FORMULA.md` step 2 orders refusal before mint. So a revision of a category must be routed through the REFUTED bin before a new category term is minted.
- Fig 9 :27-28 "describe it before you go looking, or any frame you pick will seem to fit." Describe the residual by its three grounds before selecting a field or a method.
- Fig 12 :104 "A settled molecule becomes an atom for whatever composes over it." Each first-order classification, once settled, becomes an atom for the second-order cultural transmission loop. That is why F6 says a method can cross the membrane only after it has settled once.

### Q4 : The alternation of states and structures

- Fig 13 :74 "Everything else is a composition across the two kinds, never within one."
- Fig 13 :75 "Two states never bond directly. It is not space-time but space, a structure, time; and two structures join only through a state."
- A category is not a primitive. It is a composition: an act (state-like item) is ranged through an instrument (a consequence record, a sanction, a count; structure-like) against an outcome (state-like). This is the same alternation the substrate requires. It is a `general` structural reading, not an empirical claim about moral psychology.
- Strain recorded: mapping "instrument" onto the figure's "structure" is an analogy, not a derivation. Fig 13 governs the six substrate terms, not social categories. The mapping is used only to check that the loop does not join two like kinds directly; it is not used as evidence for any category content.

### Q5 : What the low-overlap analogues add, and what must be refused

| analogue | field | what it adds | figure verdict |

| Turiel's criterion judgments (Machery and Stich 2022) | developmental psychology and philosophy | an instrument that distinguishes authority-independent, generalisable judgments from authority-dependent, local ones | corroborates F3 and F4; **conflict on universality is recorded, not resolved** |

**What must be refused:** none of these analogues supplies a name for a wrong action, and none settles whether the three categories are universal. Using them to do so would be echo without shadow: a structure found elsewhere given somewhere to live. Fig 9 :30 "your words are borrowed too, so describe the effect rather than the cause."

## CONCLUSION

The three categories form a **union of one functional group with three different grounds**, not a partition and not, as written, isomers. They are ordered by dependency (Fig 3), with refusal before mint and settlement before transmission. Under the shared-atom rewrite the stronger isomer claim becomes testable, with a falsifier stated before the run. The analogues corroborate the structure from fields with low overlap but do not supply content. Strains after re-census: one, the state/structure mapping in Q4, named and limited rather than hidden.
