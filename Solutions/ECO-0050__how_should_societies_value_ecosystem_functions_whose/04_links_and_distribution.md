## Two findings in the wider literature that bear directly on this question

1. **The commensurability conflict is in the literature, not manufactured by the figures.** The ecosystem-service entry reports a peer-reviewed total of US$16 to $54 trillion per year and, immediately beside it, the claim that "The total value of biodiversity is infinite, so having debate about what is the total value of nature is actually pointless because we can't live without it." One finite number and an infinite number cannot both be the thing being decided. Fig 12 :130 ("Summing them into one figure is the error this names, not the fix for it") explains why: the two readings are in different units. Cite this when explaining why the shape refuses a single headline value.
2. **The discounting problem is structural, not a parameter to be tuned.** The social-discount-rate entry records wide disagreement, no consensus and the observation that a higher rate makes a long-term social project less likely to be funded. The hyperbolic-discounting entry shows the same bias in human and animal choice. Fig 10 :24 ("the ground does not decay, the channel does") names what a high rate actually does: it drops the late arrivals from the channel. Cite this when explaining why undiscounted biophysical trajectories must travel beside discounted money values.
