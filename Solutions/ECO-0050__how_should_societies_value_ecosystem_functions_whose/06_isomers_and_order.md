Two derivations are owed: (1) partition or union, and (2) what order the conditions stand in when a society values them. ## DERIVATION

### Q1: Partition or union? UNION

- Fig 9 :54: "When the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again."
- Fig 9 :55: "A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones."
- Fig 9 :57: "What remains is smaller and it is also categorically different: a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either."

Test the three pairs. A benefit can be **diffuse and long-term** (spread over many future people). It can be **long-term and transboundary** (future generations in other countries). It can be **diffuse and transboundary** (spread over many people in many countries). All three pairwise intersections are non-empty, and the triple intersection is precisely the hard case the question names. So the conditions are a union, giving seven non-empty regions (three singles, three pairwise intersections, one triple). Each region has its own ground and its own loop.

The practical consequence is that a single lever fails. A lower discount rate clears part of the long-term region and leaves the diffuse and transboundary regions standing, because the second condition still binds. Fig 9 :55 to :58 names this exact failure.

- **Falsifier, written before the run:** exhibit one lever that clears all three conditions and their intersections simultaneously, so that unbundling changes nothing. If it exists, the union claim fails and the conditions are a partition or a single condition.

### Q2: What order are the conditions in? DEPENDENCY, NOT IMPORTANCE

Fig 3 :20: "The chain is ordered by dependency, not by importance. Each link consumes what the one above produces, so effort spent above the break arrives at a step that cannot use it." Fig 3 :19: "Optimising a link that already works cannot rescue one that does not."

The five links are perception, vocabulary, objective, planning, learn and carry (Fig 3 :4-15). Applied to valuation:

1. **Perception / slots:** enumerate the habitat and the per-slot contacts before anything is priced (Fig 11 :43-48). This is where "diffuse" bites: if the reading is aggregated, the live slot is lost (Fig 1 :22).
2. **Vocabulary / affordance:** say what the function affords to each slot (Fig 3 :7).
3. **Objective / what counts as progress:** this is the middle link with the fewest instruments (Fig 3 :25-27). "Long-term" bites here, because the horizon decides what counts as progress.
4. **Planning / the valuation method:** choose a frame only after the residual is described (Fig 9 :27-28). "Transboundary" bites here, because the jurisdiction decides who can charge, collect and be heard.
5. **Learn and carry:** report the result with its provenance so the next decision does not re-pay the bill (Fig 12 :107-110).

So the dependency order of the three conditions is **diffuse → long-term → transboundary**: you cannot decide the horizon before you know which slots are in contact, and you cannot decide the jurisdiction before you know which slots and which horizon are at stake. This is a dependency claim, not a ranking of which condition matters most. Fig 3 :18 warns that "Where a given chain stops is a reading, never a property of the chain": which link actually breaks in a given society is a measurement, not something this derivation supplies.

- **Falsifier:** show a case in which the jurisdiction can be fixed before the slots are enumerated and the horizon chosen, with no loss of information, across all seven regions. If so, the order claim fails.

### Q3: Are the three conditions isomers or a functional group? BOTH READINGS, ONE STRONGER

- Fig 12 :117: "isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."
- Fig 12 :124: "functional group. A substructure whose presence fixes the outcome. The group says what kind; the qualifier says which member."

As the question writes them, the three conditions are described by different atoms: diffuse by slots, long-term by the time channel, transboundary by the jurisdiction. They share the anchor and the two currencies only. Under Fig 12's definition they are then a **functional group**: the shared substructure is "benefit not registered by the local present payer," and the qualifiers say which atom is missing from the reading.

The stronger form: write all three over the full shared atom set `{slot, time, jurisdiction, anchor, money reading, biophysical reading}`, differing only in which bond or resolution is absent. Then they are **isomers**: same atoms, different arrangement, different substance, and the difference is decisive in behaviour (one lever clears one region and not another). That is the claim worth testing, because it predicts which policy lever works where.

- **Falsifier, written before the run:** if one condition requires an atom the other two do not (for example, transboundary needs a treaty atom that diffuse and long-term do not), the isomer claim fails and the functional-group reading stands. Both readings are reported so neither is quietly lost.

### Q4: What does the refusal remove? A TERM'S STANDING, NOT A TERM

Formula step 2 names a fifth route: REFUTED, "a term held is false; what is owed is a refusal, not an explanation." Operators table :53-58: "¬ negation / this does not hold / a refutation is a negation, which is why a vocabulary without it can only accumulate." Fig 5 :54-55: a refusing term "pays the same bargain. Stating the refusal, plus what remains unexplained after it, costs less than what was unexplained before. What it removes is not a term but a term's standing to be composed over. Nothing leaves the library. What falls is the reach."

Two held terms are false for these conditions: **"the value is the number returned"** and **"the aggregate is the decision object."** The answer owes a refusal of both. Nothing is deleted from the valuation library: markets, WTP and CBA remain usable for the regions where they hold. What falls is their standing to be composed over into a verdict about diffuse, long-term, transboundary benefits. This is the precise mechanism behind F1 and F6 in `01`.

- **Falsifier:** show a region in which the aggregate is demonstrably the decision object and no per-slot signal is lost, so the refusal has no work to do there.

### Q5: A correction on the habitat mapping (ecology-specific)

Fig 11's "habitat" is not the ecological niche. The essay *Arrival of the Fittest* declines the mapping explicitly: "A niche is defined by an organism's relation to its environment. A habitat here is defined by contact with a *residual*, and the residual moves so the same actors constitute a different habitat when the question changes." So the ordering in Q2 enumerates contacts with the residual, not niches. The ecological cascades cited in `01` F5 are world facts, not figure-derived. The correction is retained visibly, as the exemplar retains its own retractions.

## CONCLUSION

The three conditions are a **union** of seven regions, ordered by **dependency** (diffuse, then long-term, then transboundary), and they are a **functional group** as written but a real **isomer** claim over the full shared atom set. The answer also owes a **refusal** of the held terms "the value is the number" and "the aggregate is the decision object," which removes their standing to be composed over these conditions without deleting them from the library. Each of the four claims carries its falsifier above. Fig 11's habitat is not an ecological niche.
