# Outreach draft: check the bond test before you decide about the theory

**Use:** one to one, or as a cover note attached to a submission through the public venues in `03_public_contact_points.csv`. **Not** for bulk send.

**Hard precondition:** do not send until the union split and the bond typing in `01_figure_grounded_audit.md` are stated without the "either/or" framing. The stage-1 output must survive the test or the two-stage offer collapses on contact.

---

**Subject:** One checkable question about bank loans and deposits

> Hi [name],
>
> I am working on a small, checkable question in monetary economics. I am not asking you to read a theory first, and I am not asking you to agree with anything. If the output below does not survive, there is nothing else to look at.
>
> **The output (`general` on the shape, `specified` on its application):** the two familiar stories, "banks lend out prior deposits" and "lending creates deposits", differ in the typed bond they assert between a loan L and a deposit D. The first asserts sequence: D had to happen first, so `D → L`. The second asserts production: the deposit is absent until the loan fires, so `L ⇒ D`. The framework I am using separates these bonds with a single test, stated verbatim as *"is there a B before A fires? No."* On a bank ledger, when a loan is disbursed the bank credits the borrower's account and records a new liability. The discriminator I would want to see is the origin of that entry, not a quantity aggregate.
>
> **What I am not claiming:** that banks are unconstrained. Creation of the deposit and funding the bank when a payment leaves it are two different bonds in a loop. Solvency, liquidity, the policy rate and credit demand are the real constraints. I am also not claiming the money multiplier is literally false in every regime, or that one jurisdiction's data settles all slots (retail versus wholesale funding, domestic versus cross-border, bank size).
>
> **What would refute the shape claim, written before this message:**
> 1. A ledger trace showing the universal mechanism is a pure transfer of a pre-existing deposit, with no new deposit liability created anywhere when the loan is disbursed.
> 2. Aggregate data showing a stable reserve → deposit → loan multiplier with reserves as the exogenous driver.
> 3. A re-census of the bond tests showing sequence, not production, is the correct bond for loan and deposit.
> 4. An attribution instrument showing the lending share of new deposits is negligible.
>
> If any of these holds, I withdraw the claim. A one-line "this fails because..." is more useful to me than agreement. If you would rather not engage, no reply is needed.
>
> **If it survives, and only then:** the sources are below. The figures are the authority, not my summary of them.
> - Bank of England, "Money creation in the modern economy", Quarterly Bulletin 2014 Q1: https://www.bankofengland.co.uk/quarterly-bulletin/2014/q1/money-creation-in-the-modern-economy
> - Werner 2014, the reported ledger experiment and the three banking theories: https://professorwerner.org/pubs/can-banks-individually-create-money-out-of-nothing-the-theories-and-the-empirical-evidence/
> - Carpenter and Demiralp, the reported empirical test of the multiplier: https://ideas.repec.org/p/fip/fedgfe/2010-41.html
>
> **If you want to reuse the machinery:** the figures and the formula are in the public repository. Run them on a problem you know well and can check against a ground. Write down what would refute your result *before* you run it. A reported failure is more useful to me than a reported success.
