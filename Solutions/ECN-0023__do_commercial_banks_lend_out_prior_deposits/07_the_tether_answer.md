# The Tether's answer: bank lending and deposit creation

**Mode: `general` on the bond shape and the union split, `specified` on the domain applications, `gap` on attribution and constraint mix. Not `grounded`.** Simulated derivation checked for internal consistency against the figures (`05_what_shape_means.md` section 6), not a ground reading.

## 1. The residual it answers

The question forces a choice between two stories. Stated as an effect, it asks which typed bond joins a loan L and a deposit D on a bank's balance sheet. Option A asserts sequence, `D → L`: the deposit is a prior stock that the loan transfers. Option B asserts production, `L ⇒ D`: the deposit is absent until the loan fires. The question also bundles a second issue at a different scale, what constrains total lending, which is where the money multiplier narrative lives. The two issues form a union compound, not one hard question (Fig 9 :4-5). Answering one by averaging the other is the error.

## 2. The shape

1. **The two options are two bonds, not two quantities.** Fig 12 :77: *"A produces B; B did not exist before"*. Fig 12 :79: *"is there a B before A fires? No."* Fig 12 :72: *"A then B; B needs A to have happened"*. The discriminator is a named test on the ledger, not a debate about schools.
2. **At the loan/deposit pair, the production bond is the one that fits the reported ground.** When a bank disburses a loan it credits the borrower's account and books a new liability. The deposit was not waiting in that slot. This is the `specified` core of the answer. It is reported-ground-backed but not taken by this packet.
3. **The forced "or" is malformed.** Existing deposits can be gathered and then lent, and lending can create deposits; both occur. Fig 9 :54: *"And when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again."* Refuse the disjunction on form rather than picking a winner.
4. **The money multiplier is a strained import, not a derivation.** Fig 4 :21: *"The membrane is the boundary between the sizes, and the rule about what may cross it. Not a wall: a filter with a direction."* The single bank's settlement problem does not copy up to the system unchanged, because payments net out. Fig 6 :23: *"No frame escapes its own closure. The bound moves only on contact with a frame whose closure differs."*
5. **Creation and funding are two bonds in a signed loop.** Fig 12 :121: *"The output re-enters as input. The sign of the loop decides whether it closes, and a chain cannot close by construction."* A payment that leaves the bank triggers a reserve acquisition that feeds back into lending capacity. The chain "reserves → deposits → loans" drops the return leg and reverses the first bond.
6. **Constraint is a different quantity from bond.** Fig 12 :125: *"A composition costs in two currencies, and they do not add."* Capital adequacy, liquidity, profitability, the policy rate and credit demand are the constraints. "Lending creates deposits" does not imply "banks are unconstrained".
7. **Attribution and constraint mix are `gap`, not silence.** No instrument in the pool returns the share of deposits attributable to lending at a moment across slots. Fig 8 :39: *"So a search that finds nothing is never a proof of absence."* Fig 13 :134: *"It cannot say which primitive is missing, because naming it would mean already holding it."*

## 4. What it explicitly does not say

- Not that banks are unconstrained. The constraint set is named in point 6.
- Not that the money multiplier is false in every jurisdiction or every regime. Per-slot data are a `gap`.
- Not that the three theories are the same theory with different words. They are isomers: same atoms, different bond, different behaviour.
- Not that agreement among the Bank of England, RePEc records and commentators is three witnesses. They largely share a pool (Fig 2 :40).
- Not `grounded`. No measurement was taken by this packet.

## 5. What would settle it: the ground

An instrument, not another argument. The corresponding questions are:

- **Ledger Test:** can we see the origin code of the deposit entry at the moment a loan is disbursed? That decides sequence versus production.
- **Payment Test:** when the new deposit is spent to another bank, what settles the outflow and in what order? That closes the loop in point 5.
- **Multiplier Test:** does a change in reserve balances precede and predict a proportional change in loans across slots, with deposits in between? That would fire R2.
- **Attribution Instrument:** can we decompose deposit growth into lending, cash inflow, wholesale funding and foreign flows at a moment? That would close the `gap`.
- Standing refuters R1 to R5 from `01` section 0b are repeated here: all five are written before the conclusion.

## 7. One-paragraph version for outreach

> The Tether does not pick a side in "deposits first or lending first". It says the two stories assert different typed bonds between the same two entries, loan and deposit. Sequence says the deposit was already there; production says the deposit is absent until the loan fires, and the framework's own test asks whether there was a deposit before the loan fired. On a bank ledger the deposit is credited as a new liability when the loan is disbursed, so the production bond is the one that fits. But the "or" is malformed: existing-deposit funding and lending-created deposits are both real mechanisms at different scales, and the answer must be split rather than averaged. The money multiplier is a system-level import that strains against the ledger facts and the reported data. Lending creates deposits, yet the bank still needs reserves when a payment leaves it, so creation and funding form a signed loop, and capital, liquidity, the policy rate and credit demand, not prior deposits, are the constraints. The share of deposits attributable to lending at any moment is declared unreached, not denied. What is offered is the bond test, four refuters written in advance, and the instruments that would close the gap.
