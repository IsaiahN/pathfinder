## 1. A shape is a constraint, not a filler

The canonical README states the limit: the framework returns *"what a missing thing would have to look like (a shape, not a name)"*. Fig 9 :32: *"you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself."*

From *The Library of Babel*, opened this session: *"you can cut your search short if the set of books returned is small and descriptive enough that it provides a general shape of the answer that satisfies your question."*

So for ECN-0023 the shape is **not** "credit creation is true". It is the narrower claim: the loan/deposit relation has a typed bond, the two candidate bonds are distinguishable by a named test, the "or" is a union of two real mechanisms at different scales, and the quantities that remain unattributed are reported as gaps. That constrains any acceptable answer without filling in a policy verdict.

## 2. How the shape was obtained

- *Library of Babel*: bootstrap vocabulary by proximity, then recurse. The essay says *"With each new term gained, you narrow the space and deepen your vocabulary."* Here the bootstrap terms were loan, deposit, reserve, production, sequence, settlement and isomer.
- Fig 9 :30-31: *"your words are borrowed too, so describe the effect rather than the cause."* Describing the effect means asking what would be seen on a ledger if each bond held, not asking which school of thought is right.
- *Decoding Tacit Knowledge*, opened this session: *"When contact isn't possible, the best you can send is a plain description of what you'd expect to see, because anyone can check that against the world."* The outreach in `02` sends the bond test, not a theory.
- *Library of Babel* on stopping: the finish line is also the starting place, and the searcher *"composed the answer through the process of locating it."* Here the stop is the ledger discriminator, which is both the beginning of the question and the instrument that closes it.

## 3. What licenses a name

Two things only, both outside the shape itself (formula step 5): the ground settles a minted term, or a provenanced import arrives from a frame whose closure differs (Fig 6 :23, Fig 8 :50-52).

**Correction to an over-strong reading.** I initially treated "shape, not a name" as forbidding the phrase "credit creation". It does not. The README's phrase governs a *missing* primitive, one not held in the library. "Production bond", "sequence bond" and "isomer" are already in the figure vocabulary. Naming the bond is a binding of a held atom to a ledger observation, which is allowed as `specified` with provenance. What is not allowed is inventing a new primitive called "credit" that explains everything, or treating a theory name as a ground settlement. This correction is preserved rather than erased.

*The Hard Problem is About Source*, opened this session, sharpens the point: metamerism means an instrument that reads green *"cannot comment on the source"*. A balance-sheet total reads "deposits up" and cannot say whether the source was lending, cash inflow or wholesale funding. Several sources give the same reading. What is owed is a source-discriminating instrument, not a name. My audit in `01` therefore records the attribution share as `gap`.

## 4. Why the "or" is the wrong frame for a shape

*Library of Babel* again: *"Perhaps your problem was a bundle or conflation of several problems masquerading as one? You will need to search all of those problems individually."* Fig 9 :4-5: *"are not one hard question but several well-formed ones. Split it rather than search."* The shape of the answer to a bundled question is a set of per-domain answers, not a single "yes A" or "yes B". This is why `07` and `09` keep four verdicts separate.

Also from *Decoding Tacit Knowledge*: two frames that check only against each other drift together, and *"High contact builds exactly the shared understanding that makes communication work, but that same factor makes the learner's agreement with the teacher worthless as a check."* The Bank of England papers and the records that cite them are such a high-contact cluster.

## 5. Prestige is not action

From *The Recursive Self-Improvement Trap*, opened this session: *"Prestige must never be allowed to validate an action directly"* and *"Prestige can fund an action. It can give an action a budget to be tried. But it cannot settle an action. Only the ground can settle it."*

A central bank's institutional authority funds a claim by making it worth checking. It does not make the claim true. A famous reviewer's endorsement is a convention, not an anchor. The outreach in `02` therefore asks for a refutation, never for trust, and `03` contains only institutional opt-in channels, no personal emails.

## 6. This audit is a simulation, not a ground reading

Same essay: *"LLMs can quickly simulate an approximate answer/output with the formula found in the architecture post... However, the truly testable output would need to be derived from the real architecture or working the formula yourself."* LLM output is *"simulacra"*.

So `01`, `06`, `07`, `08` and `09` are simulated derivations checked for internal consistency against the figures. They are not tests of the economics. The world sources report ground readings from other people's instruments, and those reports are secondary. Nothing in this folder is labelled `grounded`, and no claim here can settle the question it reports on.
