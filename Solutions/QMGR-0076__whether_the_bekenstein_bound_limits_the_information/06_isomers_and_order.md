## DERIVATION

### Q1 — Union or partition?

- Fig 9 :54: *"And when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again."*
- Fig 9 :57: *"a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either"*
- Fig 9 :58: *"What remains is smaller and it is also categorically different: a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either. It stops being a mixture and becomes a specific"* compound.
- Condition C1: fixed energy E and radius R, weak self-gravity (Bekenstein 1981/2005). Condition C2: energy large enough that the region would collapse, giving the area law (Bousso 2002; 't Hooft 1993; Susskind 1995).
- A region can satisfy C1 without C2. A region at collapse satisfies both, but the area law then binds differently (area rather than `R*E`). The overlap is a compound with its own loop. ⇒ **union, not partition**.
- **Falsifier, written before the run:** if the Bekenstein inequality entails the area law for every region without a separate collapse condition, the two conditions are nested, the union dissolves, and this finding is withdrawn.

### Q2 — What bond is the comparator?

- Fig 12 :87: *"a threshold relation between two quantities"* is the definition of `⋛` (Operators table :49 agrees).
- The inequality states that one quantity does not exceed a threshold set by another. That is a comparison, not a conjunction (both hold at once), not a production (one brings the other into existence), and not a subtraction (absence of an operand).
- Fig 13 :20: *"ordering, so more and less can be said"* supplies the entropy side as a Spectrum structure. Fig 13 :68: *"finiteness in a finite region is what makes the counting terminate"* supplies the energy side.
- ⇒ The bound is a **Spectrum-to-energy budget comparison**, written `S ⋛ (R*E)` in framework notation, with the bond carrying the inequality direction. The bond, not the ingredient list, carries the meaning (Fig 12 :5: *"The bond is the rest of the information, and it is the half nothing was recording."*).
- **Named strain:** the Operators table restricts `⋛` to *"two quantities"*. An entropy and a product `R*E` are quantities, so the type holds, but the coefficient does not follow from the bond. The coefficient is a ground reading, not a composition.
- **Falsifier:** exhibit a bound of this kind whose relation is symmetric or conjunctive, with the inequality direction absent from the bond. Then the comparator finding fails.

### Q3 — How does the bound recurse across scales?

- Fig 12 :103: *"Composition is recursive, and the level decides what is irreducible."*

- Fig 13 :113: *"More compositions never add a member, and no chain reaches outside what the six and their bonds already span."*

- **Falsifier:** show that the bound holds only for one preferred scale and fails for subregions with their own energy. That breaks the recursion claim.

### Q4 — Order of operations, and are the other limits isomers?

- Formula step 2 routes a residual that is *wrong* to a refusal before a mint. The unconditional reading (Reading A in `README.md`) is wrong in effect: it drops the energy condition. Refuse it first, then keep the conditioned shape. Refusing adds a term and removes standing; nothing is deleted.
- Fig 3 :20: *"The chain is ordered by dependency, not by importance."* The order here is: name what is measured (entropy versus information capacity), fix the energy and radius, apply the threshold, then add the collapse condition. A verdict delivered before the first link is a reading below the break.
- Fig 12 :117 defines the isomer test: *"isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."*
- The Bekenstein bound, the Landauer limit and the Bremermann/Margolus-Levitin limit share a **functional group** (Fig 12 :124: *"functional group. A substructure whose presence fixes the outcome. The group says what kind; the qualifier says which member."*): each is a threshold between an information quantity and a physical budget. They do **not** share the same atoms. Bekenstein uses entropy, radius and energy; Landauer uses erasure, temperature and `k_B`; Bremermann uses mass-energy and the uncertainty principle. Under Fig 12's definition they are **not isomers**; they are three members of one functional group.
- The stronger isomer reading would require writing all three over the full shared atom set `{Space, Time, Energy, Spectrum, Gradient, Network}`, differing only in which structure mediates which state pair. That is a checkable structural claim, not a result; it is not asserted here.
- **Falsifier:** exhibit a shared atom set over which the three limits differ only by bond. That would upgrade the functional-group claim to an isomer claim. If no such set exists, the functional-group reading stands.
