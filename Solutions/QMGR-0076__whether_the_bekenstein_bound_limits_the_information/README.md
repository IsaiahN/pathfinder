## What is being decided, in effect terms

Does a bounded region of space have a finite maximum number of distinguishable states, so that information capacity cannot grow without limit? Two readings lead to different places.

- **Reading A, yes without qualification:** the Bekenstein bound, `S <= 2*pi*k*R*E/(hbar*c)`, caps what any region can hold. This leads to finite degrees of freedom, saturation at black holes, and a volume-independent area law.
- **Reading B, the bound is conditional:** the Bekenstein inequality bounds the entropy of a *complete, weakly self-gravitating system of specified energy E and radius R*. With E left free the right-hand side grows without limit, so the inequality alone does not cap a region. An absolute cap needs a second condition, gravitational collapse, which yields the area law `S <= A/(4*l_P^2)`. This leads to a union of two conditions that must be unbundled before the question can be searched.

What an answer would look like, bound as an ordinary term before the run: *a threshold comparison between an ordering of distinguishable states and an energy-and-region budget, with the count terminating because the energy in a finite region is finite.* That is a shape, not the name of a particular inequality.

## What this solution actually concludes

The figures govern the shape (a threshold comparison with a terminating count) and they explicitly name the Bekenstein/holography result as an already-known triangulation case, not a forward-tested derivation. So the Tether can say what any limiting bound must look like and why a finite capacity is possible; it cannot select the numerical bound, fix its coefficient, or settle whether the universe obeys it. The question is a union compound, and the figure-grounded verdict is `general` structural plus `specified` physics reading, `candidate`, ungrounded.
