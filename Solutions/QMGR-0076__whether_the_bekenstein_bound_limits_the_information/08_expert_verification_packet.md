# Expert verification packet: does the Tether's shape govern information capacity in a region?

**What this is:** a claim in a form a physicist or foundations researcher can check. Mode, derivation and refuters are stated before the conclusion, so a reader knows what to look for and what would settle it.

**Mode: `general` structural, `specified` on the physics-facing reading. Not `grounded`.** No direct measurement of a region's information capacity is claimed. This is a shape and a route, not a result.

## 1. The claim, in one paragraph

The question "does the Bekenstein bound limit the information capacity of any region of space?" is a union of two conditions, not one question: a fixed-energy, weakly self-gravitating bound (`S <= 2*pi*k*R*E/(hbar*c)`) and a collapse-limited area bound (`S <= A/(4*l_P^2)`). The Tether's contribution is a shape, not an inequality: any finite capacity must be an ordered threshold comparing an ordering of distinguishable states with an energy-and-region budget, where the count terminates because the energy in a finite region is finite, and where the comparison recurs at every scale. The framework explicitly names Bekenstein and holography as a case already known and not tested forward, so it does not derive the bound or fix its coefficient. What would settle the physics is a ground, not agreement.

### C3 — physics-facing; checkable by a researcher against the published record

- **Statement:** no direct measurement of a finite region's information capacity selects one inequality; the unconditional reading is conditional on energy and weak self-gravity, and the literature contains live scope disputes.
- **What to check:** Bekenstein's own abstract (Found. Phys. 2005, arXiv:quant-ph/0404042) says the bound applies to a *"complete weakly self-gravitating physical system"* in terms of *"circumscribing radius and total gravitating energy"*. Casini 2008 (arXiv:0804.2182) shows that with the right interpretation the bound *"holds automatically"* and *"does not introduce new physical constraints semiclassically."* Bousso 2003 (arXiv:hep-th/0310148) finds it *"both valid and nontrivial if interactions are properly included."* The holographic-principle record notes classical Einstein solutions (*"Wheeler's bags of gold"*) with entropy larger than an area law allows, whose quantum status is not fully understood.

## 3. What is explicitly not claimed

- Not that the Tether derived or predicted the Bekenstein bound. The figure and canonical article record say it is an already-known instance.
- Not that the bound applies unconditionally to any region regardless of energy. The energy condition and the collapse condition are both retained.
- Not that the holographic area law is the true bound, or that the covariant bound is wrong. The framework does not adjudicate among them.
- Not that expert agreement settles anything. Fig 2 :40 *"agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool"*.
- Not `grounded`. Nothing here is asserted as settled by experiment.

A ground, not an argument. Three concrete routes:

1. A measurement in which the information capacity of a finite region at or near the Planck regime selects one inequality and fixes its coefficient.
2. An accepted theorem that removes the current scope ambiguity between the fixed-energy bound, the covariant bound and the area law, including the status of the *"Wheeler's bags of gold"* solutions.
3. A figure re-census that shows the six-term set does not govern information capacity, in which case the honest verdict is `gap` rather than a forced fit.
