# The Tether's answer on information capacity in a region

**Mode: `general` on the structural shape, `specified` on the physics-facing reading. Not `grounded`.** Simulated derivation checked for internal consistency against the figures (`05_what_shape_means.md` section 6), not a ground reading.

## 1. The residual it answers

The question asks whether the Bekenstein bound limits the information capacity of any region of space. Two readings must be kept separate (Fig 9 :54: *"And when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again."*):

1. **Fixed-energy bound, weak self-gravity:** `S <= 2*pi*k*R*E/(hbar*c)`. The bound is conditional on E and R.
2. **Collapse-limited area bound:** `S <= A/(4*l_P^2)`. This is a different condition, reached when the energy in the region would collapse it.

The unconditional question ("any region, no conditions") is a union of these two, not one hard question. Fig 9 :57: *"a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either"*.

## 2. The shape

1. **A finite capacity is possible because the count terminates.** Fig 13 :68: *"finiteness in a finite region is what makes the counting terminate"*. The energy row of the substrate's minimality table is load-bearing for the second law; without a finite energy in a finite region, there is no terminating count to bound. This is `general`, structural.
2. **The bound is an ordered threshold.** Fig 12 :87: *"a threshold relation between two quantities"*, and Fig 13 :20: *"ordering, so more and less can be said"*. The comparator compares an entropy ordering with an energy-and-region budget. It is not a conjunction, a production or a subtraction.
3. **The bound recurs at every scale.** Fig 12 :103: *"Composition is recursive, and the level decides what is irreducible."* Each subregion with its own energy is again bounded; no new member of the six is required.
4. **The framework does not name the inequality or fix the coefficient.** Fig 9 :32: *"you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself"*. Neither `2*pi` nor `1/4` is derivable from the bond type. Choosing between the Bekenstein inequality, the covariant entropy bound and the area law needs the ground.
5. **This case is a retrodiction.** Fig 8 :56-57: *"the triangulation claim fits four cases already known and has not been tested forward: Wiles and Frey, Montgomery and Dyson, Bekenstein and holography, the relativization barriers in complexity theory."* The canonical article's bibliography labels the Bekenstein, Hawking, 't Hooft and Susskind entry *"An instance."* The Tether describes this case; it did not produce it.

## 3. What it explicitly does not say

- Not that the Bekenstein bound applies unconditionally to a region with arbitrary energy. That reading drops the energy condition and is refused before the conditioned shape is kept.
- Not that the Tether derived, predicted or selected the Bekenstein bound. The figure record says the opposite.
- Not that the holographic area law is the true bound. The physics record contains scope disputes and unresolved classical counterexamples (*"Wheeler's bags of gold"*).
- Not that agreement among physicists settles the question. Fig 2 :40: *"agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool"*.
- Not `grounded`. No measurement of a finite region's information capacity is claimed.

## 4. What would settle it, and the pre-registered refuter

The ground, not more agreement. Fig 2 :29: *"An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks."*

Written before the run:

1. **Termination failure:** a finite-energy system in a finite region with infinitely many distinguishable states not rescued by interactions (breaks Fig 13 :68).
2. **Comparator failure:** a bound of this kind that is symmetric or conjunctive rather than an ordered threshold (breaks Fig 12 :87).
3. **Ground selection:** an experiment or accepted theorem that selects one inequality and fixes its coefficient for all regions with no scope split. This would confirm the physics side but reverse the "framework cannot name the bound" finding.
4. **Census failure:** a re-census showing the six-term set does not govern information capacity at all. Then the honest verdict is `gap`, not a forced fit (Fig 8 :39: *"So a search that finds nothing is never a proof of absence."*).
5. **Provenance reversal:** a dated forward derivation of a numerical entropy bound from the framework preceding the physics result (reverses the retrodiction finding).

Existing instruments that could sharpen the scopes: black hole thermodynamics, the generalized second law, QFT relative entropy (Casini 2008), and the bound-state analysis of Bousso 2003. None of these is a direct measurement of a finite region's capacity.

## 5. The honest stop

**Unreached, not unreachable.** The shape stops at the limit of what the available sources distinguish. There is theorem-ground (Casini's positivity result) and strong evidence (Bousso 2002), but the unconditional reading has no registering instrument. Fig 6 :32: *"An instrument is not built from a description. It is improved from a worse instrument already returning something."* There is something already returning: the black-hole results. What is owed is a discriminator of scopes, not another argument. The lineage is recorded so the stop is checkable. *"Indiscernibility is not identity."*

## 6. One-paragraph version for outreach

> The Tether does not derive the Bekenstein bound and does not choose between it, the covariant entropy bound and the holographic area law. It says that any finite information capacity must have the shape of an ordered threshold: an ordering of distinguishable states compared with an energy-and-region budget, with the count terminating because energy in a finite region is finite. The bound recurs at every scale, and the question as asked is really two conditions, a fixed-energy bound and a collapse-limited area bound, that must be unbundled before either is searched. The framework's own record names Bekenstein and holography as a case already known and not tested forward, so this is a retrodicted shape with a provenance rule, not a prediction. What would settle the physics is a ground: an experiment or an accepted theorem that fixes the coefficient and the scope. What the framework offers is the shape, the union split, four refuters written in advance, and the honest statement that it cannot name the inequality.
