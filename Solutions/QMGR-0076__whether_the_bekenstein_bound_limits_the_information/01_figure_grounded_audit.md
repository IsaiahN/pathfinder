## 0. The residual, stated in effect terms

Let `R` be the question residual: *does a finite region of space have a finite maximum information capacity?*

- **Arity.** The comparison has two operands: something to be bounded (entropy, Shannon information, or number of distinguishable states) and a budget (the product `R*E` in Bekenstein's inequality, or the area `A` in the holographic area law). The question as worded hides a second arity: the inequality needs both a radius and an energy. The compound therefore has two conditions, not one.
- **Symmetry.** The comparator is an ordered threshold, not a symmetric join. Fig 12 :87 names `⋛` as *"a threshold relation between two quantities"*. Swapping the operands changes an upper bound into a lower one, so the relation is not symmetric.
- **Scale.** The bound is scale-relative: any subregion with its own energy is again bounded. Fig 12 :103: *"Composition is recursive, and the level decides what is irreducible."*

**Option A and its consequence.** Yes, unconditionally: information capacity is finite in every region; the count terminates; black holes saturate it; an area law replaces a volume law. This is the popular reading of the question.

**Option B and its consequence.** The Bekenstein inequality conditions the bound on a *complete, weakly self-gravitating system of specified energy*. Let the energy grow and the right-hand side grows. So the inequality alone does not cap a region; an absolute cap needs the extra condition of gravitational collapse, giving the area law. This is a union compound and must be split before it is searched (Fig 9 :54).

**Binding the sought-for shape as an ordinary term (Formula, "Direction").** Before searching, write down what an answer would look like: *an ordered threshold comparing a spectrum of distinguishable states against an energy-in-a-region budget, where finiteness of energy in a finite region makes the count terminate.* That shape is now refusable like any other term. It does not name which inequality is correct.

## 3. Findings

Each finding keeps its verdict per domain. A verdict is marked `triangulated` only when two figures with low overlap govern it; otherwise `single-source`.

### F1 — The question is a union compound, not one hard question (framing, general)

Fig 9 :54: *"And when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again."* Fig 9 :57: *"a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either"*.

- **D1 verdict:** the Bekenstein inequality bounds a *complete, weakly self-gravitating system* in terms of *circumscribing radius and total gravitating energy* (Bekenstein, arXiv:quant-ph/0404042, abstract, opened 2026-11-23). Holding a region fixed while letting energy grow removes the cap. The area law is a different condition, reached at collapse. `specified`.
- **D2 verdict:** the figure governs the *form* of the split (union, not partition), not the physics content. `general`.
- **D3 verdict:** asking one question of one expert invites an averaged answer that hides the compound. `general`.
- **Status:** `triangulated` across Fig 9's union rule and Fig 1 :19 (*"The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated."*) with low overlap between search topology and per-slot residual indexing.
- **Refuter:** show that the two conditions are nested rather than overlapping (that the Bekenstein inequality already entails the area law for all regions without a collapse assumption) and the union finding collapses into a single question.

### F2 — A finite information capacity is possible because finite energy in a finite region terminates the count (shape, general)

Fig 13, minimality table, energy row (Fig 13 :68): *"finiteness in a finite region is what makes the counting terminate"*. Network row (Fig 13 :59): *"no relation between systems, and no configurations to count"*. Spectrum (Fig 13 :20): *"ordering, so more and less can be said"*.

Read together, these give the shape: information capacity is an ordering over the configurations of a finite system; the count terminates because the energy in a finite region is finite; the comparator is a threshold. The substrate's own argument for why finiteness is load-bearing (the second law row) is the figure-grounded reason any bound of this kind is possible at all.

- **D2 verdict:** `general`, structural. The figures assert the shape, not the coefficient.
- **D1 verdict:** corroborated by Bekenstein's own account (arXiv:quant-ph/0404042): the bound applies to a *complete weakly self-gravitating physical system* and can be written as a bound on information capacity. `specified`.
- **Status:** `triangulated` across Fig 13's minimality table and Fig 8's Bekenstein/holography case (Fig 8 :56-57), which have low overlap: one is the substrate's energy row, the other is the union-surplus claim about an already-known case.
- **Refuter:** exhibit a finite-energy system in a finite region with infinitely many distinguishable states not rescued by interactions. That breaks the termination claim at the ground.

### F3 — The bound is an ordered threshold, not a symmetric join (comparator, general)

Fig 12 :87: *"a threshold relation between two quantities"*. Operators table :49 gives the same definition. Fig 13 :20: *"ordering, so more and less can be said"*.

The inequality `S <= 2*pi*k*R*E/(hbar*c)` compares an entropy ordering with a budget. The comparator is `⋛` in the framework's notation: ordered, threshold, not a conjunction. This is a much cleaner fit than the exemplar's use of `⋛` for a quantum-gravity comparison, because a bound *is* a threshold relation.

- **D2 verdict:** `general`, triangulated across Fig 12 and the Operators table (low overlap: arrangement semantics versus operator notation).
- **D1 verdict:** `specified`, corroborated by Bousso 2002 (arXiv:hep-th/0203101): *"There is strong evidence that the area of any surface limits the information content of adjacent spacetime regions, at 10^(69) bits per square meter."*
- **Named strain, not hidden:** the Operators table defines `⋛` as a threshold between *two quantities*. Here one operand is an entropy (a logarithm of a count) and the other is `R*E` or an area. Both are quantities, so the type is permitted, but the numerical coefficient (`2*pi` versus `1/4`) is not derivable from the bond type. The framework fixes the shape of the comparison, not its constant.

## 5. Pre-registered refuter (written before the conclusion)

"a falsifier written before the run is a design instrument." The shape claim *finite information capacity is an ordered threshold over a terminating count, and the framework does not name the inequality* is refuted if any of the following fires:

1. **Termination failure:** a finite-energy system in a finite region is shown to have infinitely many distinguishable states not rescued by interactions (breaks Fig 13 :68).
2. **Comparator failure:** the bound is shown to be a symmetric or conjunctive relation rather than an ordered threshold (breaks Fig 12 :87 and the Operators table).
3. **Ground selection:** an experiment or accepted theorem selects one inequality and fixes its coefficient for all regions, with no scope split (reverses F5, confirms the physics side of F2).
4. **Census failure:** a re-census shows the six-term set does not govern information capacity at all, so no figure line governs and the honest verdict is `gap` (Fig 8 :39: *"So a search that finds nothing is never a proof of absence."*).
5. **Provenance reversal:** a dated forward derivation of a numerical entropy bound from the framework appears before the corresponding physics result (reverses F4).

If any fires, the affected finding is withdrawn and the retraction log below is updated rather than silently rewritten.
