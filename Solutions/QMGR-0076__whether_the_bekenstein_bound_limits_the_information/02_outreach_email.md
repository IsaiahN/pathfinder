**Subject:** Check one shape claim before you decide whether the framework is worth reading

> Hi [name],
>
> I have one specific output I would like you to check against what you already know. I am not asking you to read the framework first, and I am not asking you to agree with it. If the output does not survive, there is nothing else to look at.
>
> **The question in effect terms.** Does a finite region of space have a finite maximum information capacity? The popular reading is that the Bekenstein bound answers yes unconditionally. The careful reading is that the inequality bounds the entropy of a complete, weakly self-gravitating system of specified energy E and radius R. Let E grow and the right-hand side grows, so the inequality alone does not cap a region. An absolute cap needs a second condition, gravitational collapse, which gives the area law. I wrote this down as two conditions, not one, before searching.
>
> **The output (`general` structural, `specified` on the physics reading, not `grounded`):** the framework says that any such bound must be an ordered threshold comparing an ordering of distinguishable states with an energy-and-region budget, and that the count terminates because energy in a finite region is finite. It returns that shape, not the name of an inequality. It does not fix `2*pi` or `1/4`. Its own record names Bekenstein and holography as a case already known and not tested forward, so I am not presenting this as a derivation of the bound.
>
> **What I am not claiming:** that the Tether derived the Bekenstein bound, that it selects the holographic area law over the covariant entropy bound, or that it settles the scope disputes in the QFT literature. Those are imports with provenance, not outputs of the loop. Agreement from people who share the same evidence pool is not a second instrument.
>
> **What would refute the shape claim:**
> 1. a finite-energy system in a finite region with infinitely many distinguishable states not rescued by interactions;
> 2. a bound that is symmetric or conjunctive rather than an ordered threshold;
> 3. an experiment or accepted theorem that selects one inequality and fixes its coefficient for all regions with no scope split;
> 4. a forward derivation of a numerical entropy bound from the framework with dated provenance preceding the physics result.
>
> I wrote these down before sending this. If any one holds, I withdraw the corresponding claim. A one-line *"this fails because..."* is more useful to me than agreement. If you would rather not engage, no reply is needed.
>

> - Canonical usage rule (local): `FRAMEWORK/reference/FIGURES_README_canonical.md`
