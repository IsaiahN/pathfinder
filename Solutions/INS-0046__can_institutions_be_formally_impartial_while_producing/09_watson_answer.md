## QUESTION

Decide, in effect terms, what the Tether says about the claim that an institution can be formally impartial and still produce systematically unequal outcomes. Option A: treat the two properties as one quantity with one anchor and answer yes or no directly. Option B: split them into rule form, habitat and outcome distribution, give each its own loop, and answer the composability question. What each leads to: A promotes a convention to a verdict and averages away the live per-slot signal; B returns a checkable shape, an instrument list and four refuters written in advance.

## DOMAIN CENSUS

R by arity, symmetry and scale, then one line per domain with actors / currency / anchor, partition or union, and order by dependency.

- **R (the residual).** The mismatch between a rule described as symmetric and a distribution that is persistently asymmetric. Arity: three, not two; the habitat is the omitted third term. Symmetry: mixed, the rule is swap-invariant, the distribution is an ordered comparison of magnitudes. Scale: the rule acts at the step or seat scale, the distribution at the cycle or lineage scale. Fig 9 :5 "are not one hard question but several well-formed ones. Split it rather than search." Fig 8 :53 "Residual first, frame second."
- **Domain 1, rule form (seat convention).** Actors: decision rule, adjudicator, blind categories. Currency: decisions per case, form of the criterion. Anchor: the institution's declared criterion, which is nameable but declared, not grounded. Question type: partition boundary, which compositions are admitted.
- **Domain 2, habitat (field of contact).** Actors: entrants, starting positions, conditions, relations. Currency: contact, capability, cascade. Anchor: the ground, what actually reaches the rule. Question type: enumeration, not composition.
- **Domain 3, outcome distribution (ground).** Actors: per-group slots. Currency: per-slot residual, selection rate. Anchor: measurement, counter, audit. Question type: ground reading.
- **Domain 4, verification / outreach.** Actors: outside frames. Currency: agreement and residual. Anchor: the ground, never agreement. In this simulated run all seats draw on one evidence pool, so independence is near zero and agreement is not a witness.
- **Union.** D1 intersect D2 produces the compound "an impartial rule over an unequal habitat", whose own ground is the outcome distribution. Fig 9 :54 "And when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again."
- **Order by dependency (Fig 3).** Refuse the single-property framing, enumerate contact, measure per slot, check anchor independence, then name a source with provenance.

Strain test applied: Fig 10 :47 initially seemed to forbid the co-existence. Re-census shows it governs the misuse of a convention as a verdict, not co-existence. Different quantities, so no strain remains. The comparison bond type is constrained to real quantities; group-level tallies satisfy it, abstract properties do not.

## VERDICT (from the figures)

Yes, and the two properties are not in tension under the figures. "Formally impartial" is a symmetric convention at the scale of one decision; "systematically unequal outcomes" is a per-slot ground reading at the scale of many decisions, generated by the habitat of entrants with unequal starting positions. Because these have different anchors, the rule's form does not constrain the distribution's equality. Fig 11 :40 makes the co-existence mechanical: an improvement that does not change contact changes nothing. Fig 1 :13 makes the aggregation legal: a global neutral reading with one live unequal slot is a legal state. The contradiction appears only if the convention is promoted to a verdict about distribution, which Fig 10 :47 forbids. The normative question of what an institution ought to do about the gap is a `gap`; the empirical prevalence of the pattern is a world claim, not a figure claim. This is a `specified`, ungrounded, candidate result.

- **cost if wrong:** bounded and locatable. If the answer were no, we would be treating a convention as a verdict (Fig 10 :47) and averaging away the live slot (Fig 1 :22). The real cost is a false anchor: an institution's own impartiality score accepted as ground, so the source-discriminating instrument is never built. Fig 2 :42 "Buy independence in proportion to the cost of being wrong." That cost is avoidable by leading with the per-slot claim and its refuter.
- **undoable:** yes. The union is unbundled into three domains without withdrawing any primitive or revising any figure line. `07_the_tether_answer.md` and `08_expert_verification_packet.md` already state the corrected form. A sent claim can be superseded by the corrected packet.
- **reversed by:** a ground reading, not agreement. A measurement in which all per-slot residuals are near zero once contact is enumerated would empty the inequality claim; a controlled case where changing only the rule's form moves the distribution with contact fixed would refute Fig 11 :40 here; a rating that moves with outcomes would show "impartial" was never an anchor; a figure re-census that admits rule form and distribution in one domain with one anchor would break the union finding. Until one of these fires, the verdict stands as `candidate`.
