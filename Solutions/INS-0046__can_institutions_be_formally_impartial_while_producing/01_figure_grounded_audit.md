## 0. The claim under audit

> "Can institutions be formally impartial while producing systematically unequal outcomes?"
>
> Source: `institutional questions.txt` line 57. Section 5, Power, inequality, and elite influence.

Restated in effect terms: an institution applies one criterion to all cases, blind to the group label of the case (formally symmetric), and the stream of decisions it issues displays a persistent skew across groups (systematically unequal). What failed is not a rule and not a number but a **census**: the sentence puts a property of a *procedure* and a property of a *distribution* into one frame and asks whether they can both be true, as if they ranged against the same anchor.

Fields named first, frame second is the inversion Fig 9 warns about:

> Fig 9 :27-28 "describe it before you go looking" / "any frame you pick will seem to fit"

So this audit describes R before choosing the legal, philosophical or sociological frame.

---

## 1. Residual in effect terms

R = the mismatch between a self-description ("we apply the same rule to all") and an observed distribution ("the outcomes are persistently skewed"). Three quantities are entangled in that sentence:

- **Rule form**: a symmetry property of the decision procedure. Swap the group label, the decision is unchanged.
- **Habitat**: the set of actors, conditions and relations that supply cases to the rule, with unequal starting positions.
- **Outcome distribution**: the per-group tally of decisions, which is ground-visible.

The question "can A while B" is a composability question about these three, not an empirical survey. The empirical question "does this happen, and where" belongs to the world and is handled in `09_watson_answer.md`. The normative question "ought an institution to answer for B" is a gap: no figure line decides a value.

## 2. Domain census (before any figure line is chosen)

### 2a. R by arity, symmetry, scale (Fig 9 :22)

- **Arity:** three, not two. The sentence looks binary (impartiality versus inequality) but the mechanism that connects them needs a third term: the field of contact that delivers cases with unequal starting positions. "One field with two readers is a seam"; here the seam is between the rule and the distribution.
- **Symmetry:** the rule's claimed symmetry is a *swap test*: exchange the group labels and the decision should not change. The outcome's asymmetry is a *comparison* between group-level quantities (Fig 12 :85-88, the comparison bond ⋛: "a threshold relation between two quantities"). One is a symmetry of notation; the other is an ordered relation between measured magnitudes. They are not the same kind of property.
- **Scale:** the rule acts at the step or seat scale (one decision). The distribution exists at the cycle or lineage scale (many decisions compounding). Fig 4 :20 "Only the actors, the currency and the anchor change." A step-level property need not survive the trip to the lineage level.

### 2b. One line per domain: actors / currency / anchor

| domain | actors | currency | anchor | nameable anchor? |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| D1 The rule's form (seat convention) | decision rule, adjudicator, the categories the rule is blind to | decisions per case; the symmetric form of the criterion | the institution's own declared criterion | yes, but it is a *declared* anchor: see F2 |
| D2 The habitat (field of contact) | applicants, citizens, employees, their starting positions, conditions, relations | contact, capability, cascade | the ground: who actually enters and what survives | yes, the ground |
| D3 The outcome distribution (ground) | per-group outcome slots | per-slot residual, selection rate | measurement, counter, audit | yes, the ground |
| D4 Verification / outreach | outside frames (researchers, auditors, affected parties) | agreement and residual | the ground, never agreement | yes, the ground |

D1 has an anchor only in a special sense: the criterion is declared, and a declared meaning is not a grounded one (Fig 10 :18). It is kept as a domain because the anchor is *nameable*, not because it is immovable. Fig 11 capability is agent-and-habitat, so D2 is not reducible to D1.

### 2c. Partition or union (Fig 9 :54-58)

Not a partition. D1 and D3 overlap in the compound **"an impartial rule run over an unequal habitat"**, which is its own domain with its own loop and its own ground (the outcome distribution). D2 is upstream of that compound. Fig 9 :5 "are not one hard question but several well-formed ones. Split it rather than search." The question as posed is a "bundle or conflation of several problems masquerading as one" (*The Library of Babel*, Isaiah N, 2026).

### 2d. Order by dependency (Fig 3)

1. Recognise before comparing: separate rule form from outcome distribution (Fig 9 :43-45).
2. Enumerate habitat contact before scoring the rule (Fig 11 :43 "The habitat is enumerated, never composed.").
3. Measure the per-slot residual, not the aggregate (Fig 1 :19).
4. Check anchor independence: can the institution's "impartiality" rating move when outcomes change? (Fig 2 :29-30).
5. Only then decide whether the compound is legitimate and what would reverse it.

Effort spent at step 5 before steps 2 and 3 arrives at a link that cannot use it (Fig 3 :20-23).

## 4. Findings

### F1 - The question is a union compound, not one hard question (structural, triangulated)

Fig 9 :5 "are not one hard question but several well-formed ones. Split it rather than search."
Fig 9 :54 "And when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again."
*The Library of Babel* (opened this session): "Perhaps your problem was a bundle or conflation of several problems masquerading as one? You will need to search all of those problems individually to find their answers and then combine them in a satisfactory way."

The rule's form (D1) and the outcome distribution (D3) are not two readings of one quantity. One is a symmetry of the notation, the other a comparison of measured magnitudes. The habitat (D2) is the third term that makes them composable at all. Answering "yes" or "no" without splitting answers a question nobody asked.

- **Refuter:** show that rule form and outcome distribution range against one anchor, so that a change in one is a change in the other by construction. Then the union collapses to a partition and this finding falls.

### F2 - "Formally impartial" is a convention, not a ground fact (decisive against the naive reading)

Fig 10 :18 "a declared meaning is not a grounded one"
Fig 10 :49 "Declaring a meaning does not ground it."
Fig 10 :47 "a convention that decides an outcome has stopped being one"

An institution's claim that it is impartial is a declaration about the form of its rule. It becomes a false or dangerous claim only when it is *used as a verdict about the distribution* ("our rule is impartial, therefore the outcomes are fair"). At that moment it decides an outcome and has stopped being a convention. Kept as a claim about form, it is a legitimate convention; promoted to a claim about distribution, it is a frame certifying itself.

This is the single most important line in the audit: the apparent contradiction in the question exists only if "impartial" is smuggled across the boundary from convention to verdict.

- **Refuter:** show an institution whose impartiality rating changes when you push on outcomes. By Fig 2 :29-30 "An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks", a rating that moves with outcomes was never an anchor, and the convention/verdict distinction collapses.

### F3 - A global "impartial" is compatible with a live per-slot unequal residual (triangulated)

Fig 1 :13 "a global R near zero with one live slot residual is a legal state"
Fig 1 :19 "The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated."
Fig 1 :22 "Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average."

The institution that reports a single fairness score near zero while one group's slot carries a live residual is not merely being dishonest. Under Fig 1 that is a *legal state*, and the aggregate is precisely what hides the signal. "Systematically unequal outcomes" is a per-slot finding; "formally impartial" is typically a global one. They can both be true because they are measured at different resolutions.

- **Refuter:** produce a measurement in which every per-slot residual is at or near zero once habitat contact is enumerated. Then the inequality claim has no live slot and F3 is empty.

### F4 - The institution scores itself with a quantity it also produces (frame-internal)

Fig 1 :27 "They are frame-internal. A frame cannot score itself with a quantity it also produces."
Fig 1 :23 "The ground enters as the only metric."
Fig 2 :29-30 "An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks. A measurement, an experiment, a counter, a market, a patient outcome. If it moves when you push on it, it is not an anchor."

An "impartiality index" published by the institution is a frame-internal quantity. The outcome distribution, measured by an outside counter (audit study, selection-rate table, court record), is the ground. The question asks whether the frame-internal quantity can coexist with the ground reading. It can, because they do not range against the same thing.

- **Refuter:** show an impartiality index that does not co-vary with the institution's own decisions. Then it is not frame-internal and F4 must be withdrawn.

### F5 - Changing the rule's form without changing contact changes nothing (triangulated)

Fig 11 :40 "An improvement that does not change contact changes nothing, however much it improves."
Fig 11 :39 "So the answerable question is not what intelligence is, but what makes a system capable. And capability is a property of agent-and-habitat, never of the agent alone."
Fig 4 :20 "it or a thousand. Only the actors, the currency and the anchor change."

Making a rule blind to group labels is an improvement to the procedure *in isolation*. If the habitat is unchanged, the same actors arrive with the same starting positions, and the outcome distribution does not move. This is the engine of the answer: form and outcome are decoupled by contact, not by intention.

- **Refuter:** a controlled case where the rule's form changes, habitat contact is held fixed, and the outcome distribution changes substantially. That would refute Fig 11 :40 as applied here and force a re-census of D2.

### F6 - The habitat is asymmetric, and exclusion is not available (triangulated)

Fig 11 :49 "An actor’s valence is relative to the residual, not a property it carries."
Fig 11 :61 "Introduction is available; exclusion is not." (quote from "The seat’s power is asymmetric. Introduction is available; exclusion is not.")
Fig 11 :43 "The habitat is enumerated, never composed."

The same actor is an advantage in one residual and a neutral fact in another. What produces skew is not the label itself but the cascade of positions and relations that reaches the rule's door. Because contact is a fact about the world and does not consult the seat, a formally blind rule cannot simply subtract inequality from its inputs.

- **Refuter:** a habitat in which every actor's starting position is identical with respect to the residual. Then valence is not relative here, F6 is empty, and unequal outcomes must be explained by the rule itself.

### F7 - The measurement that would name the source is an import with a source gate, not a new label (single-source for the figure, corroborated by two fields)

Fig 8 :53 "Residual first, frame second."
Fig 6 :23 "No frame escapes its own closure. The bound moves only on contact with a frame whose closure differs."
Fig 12 :85 "a threshold relation between two quantities" (Operators table :48, same wording)

Calling a disparity "discrimination" is a binding from the legal frame onto the measured reading. The instrument (audit study, disparity statistic, regression with confounders) names what the rule's form cannot: whether the source of the reading is the rule or the habitat. *The Hard Problem is About Source* (opened this session): "If several paths create the same behavior, then behavior is a projection with lossy information (it cannot define consciousness in full)." And: "An instrument that can’t distinguish these cases demonstrates its own inadequacy, it cannot claim anything substantial about what is being measured or what causes it."

- **Refuter:** a source-discriminating instrument that shows the disparity vanishes once the formally neutral criterion is applied, with habitat held fixed. Then the binding "habitat" is the wrong name.

**Strain note:** the comparison bond ⋛ is defined as a threshold relation between *two quantities*. Applying it to per-group outcome slots is legal because those are quantities. Applying it to "impartiality versus inequality" as abstract properties is a type error. This is named, not hidden.

### F8 - Agreement cannot settle this; only an outside anchor can (triangulated)

Fig 2 :35 "Alignment is triangulation, not negotiation. A mirror cannot correct you."
Fig 2 :40 "disqualifying for a verdict: agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool"
Fig 7 :35 "A room’s agreement is variation, not a verdict. Selection between rooms is where the ground votes."
Fig 10 :42 "A verifier that can reconstruct the claim cannot verify it."

A panel selected for agreeing that institutions are (or are not) impartial produces a convention, not corroboration. The outside frame must be given the residual and the refuter, not the framework's interiors, and its verdict must be recorded before any theory is shown.

- **Refuter:** two frames with genuinely low overlap, each ranging separately against a measured outcome distribution, that return different verdicts and can be arbitrated by the ground. That would show agreement is not the only channel here.

## 5. Pre-registered refuter

Written here before the conclusion, per the standing rule that "a falsifier written before the run is a design instrument." The shape claim is refuted if any of the following holds:

1. A change in an institution's impartiality rating tracks a change in its outcome distribution, so "impartial" is not a stable convention but an updating frame (breaks F2, anchor test).
2. Every per-slot outcome residual is at or near zero once habitat contact is enumerated (breaks F3 and F4).
3. Changing only the rule's form, with habitat contact held fixed, reliably changes the outcome distribution (breaks F5).
4. A figure re-census places rule form and outcome distribution in one domain with one anchor, so the union reading is wrong (breaks F1).
5. A source-discriminating instrument attributes the disparity to the rule rather than the habitat in a case where the rule is formally symmetric (breaks F6 and F7).

If any fires, the shape is withdrawn or re-censused. Until one is run, no part of this may be labelled `grounded`.

## 6. Recommendation

1. Split the question before answering it (F1). Do not accept the yes/no framing.
2. Keep "impartial" in D1 as a convention and refuse to let it become a verdict about D3 (F2).
3. Report outcomes per slot, never as a single fairness score (F3, F4).
4. Enumerate habitat contact before scoring the rule (F5, F6).
5. Treat "discrimination" as a binding from the legal frame, provenanced as such, and ask for the instrument that discriminates source (F7).
6. Lead outreach with the residual and the refuter, never with the framework (F8, Fig 10 :52).

### Seven-question recursive derivation (summary)

1. **One question or several?** Several, a union compound. Fig 9 :5 plus *Library of Babel* on bundles. `general`.
2. **What is "impartial"?** A symmetric bond on the rule's form: swap group labels, decision unchanged. Fig 12 :59. A convention, not a ground fact.
3. **What is "unequal outcomes"?** An ordered comparison of per-group quantities, measured at the ground. Fig 12 :85; Fig 1 :19.
4. **What connects them?** The habitat, enumerated by contact. Fig 11 :43, :40. The third term the yes/no framing omitted.
5. **Can they compose?** Yes, in the compound domain: D1 over D2 produces D3. No contradiction arises because there is no shared anchor.
6. **What would name the source?** A source-discriminating instrument, not a further argument. Fig 6 :23; *Hard Problem is About Source*.
7. **Is the answer settled?** No. The shape is `specified`; the empirical prevalence and the normative "ought" remain open. "Unreachable is a verdict here, not a silence" (Fig 13 :134).

## 9. Updated verdict

The two properties can co-exist, and the figures say why: they are not two readings of one quantity. "Formally impartial" is a symmetric convention at the seat level (D1); "systematically unequal outcomes" is a per-slot ground reading (D3) generated by an asymmetric habitat (D2). A convention that does not change contact does not change the distribution (Fig 11 :40), and a global impartiality near zero with one live unequal slot is a legal state (Fig 1 :13). The contradiction exists only if the convention is promoted to a verdict about distribution, at which point Fig 10 :47 applies and the claim was never a convention anymore.

Triangulation: the structural claim is triangulated across Fig 10, Fig 11 and Fig 1 (three low-overlap figure groups). The metamerism/source point is single-source in the figures and corroborated by two independent world fields, which is not the same as two witnesses for the figure verdict. The normative question is a `gap`.

After re-census: one live type constraint (⋛ operands), one declared gap (the "ought"), no hidden strains. The shape survives; the naive yes/no framing does not.
