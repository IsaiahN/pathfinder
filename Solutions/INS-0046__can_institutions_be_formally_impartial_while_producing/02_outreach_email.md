**Use:** one to one, or as a cover letter attached to a submission through the public venues in `03_public_contact_points.csv`. **Not** for bulk send.

**Subject:** One checkable claim about impartial rules and unequal outcomes

> Hi [name],
>
> I have one specific output I would like you to check against what you already know about institutions. I am not asking you to read a theory first, and I am not asking you to agree with anything. If the output does not survive, there is nothing else to look at.
>
> **The output (`specified`, not `grounded`):**
>
> A rule can be formally impartial, in the precise sense that swapping the group label of a case does not change the decision, and the distribution of outcomes it produces can still be systematically unequal. That is not a contradiction. The rule's form is a convention at the level of one decision. The outcome distribution is a measurement at the level of many decisions. Between them sits the habitat: who actually reaches the rule, with what starting positions and relations.
>
> Three consequences follow, each checkable:
>
> 1. A single fairness score near zero can coexist with one group's slot carrying a live residual. Averaging across slots is how the signal disappears.
> 2. Changing the rule's form while leaving the habitat untouched will not, on this claim, change the outcome distribution. Contact is what has to change.
> 3. Calling the disparity "impartial" because the rule is symmetric promotes a convention into a verdict about distribution. At that point it has stopped being a convention.
>
> **What I am not claiming:** that any particular institution is unjust; that unequal outcomes are always caused by bias; that impartiality is worthless; or that a normative "ought" follows. Those need a ground reading and a value choice I am not making here. This is a shape and a route, not a result, and I have not measured an institution.
>
> **What would refute it, written before this message:**
>
> 1. An impartiality rating that changes when outcomes change, so it was never a stable convention.
> 2. A measurement in which every per-slot outcome residual is at or near zero once who reaches the rule is enumerated.
> 3. A controlled case where only the rule's form changes, habitat is held fixed, and the distribution changes substantially.
> 4. An instrument that shows the disparity comes from the rule itself even though the rule is symmetric.
>
> A one-line "this fails because..." is more useful to me than agreement. If you would rather not engage, no reply is needed. **If you do reply, please give me your verdict on this output before I send anything else.**
