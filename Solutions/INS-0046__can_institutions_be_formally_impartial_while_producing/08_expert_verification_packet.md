# Expert verification packet - INS-0046

**What this is:** a claim in a form a researcher of institutions can check. Mode, derivation and refuters are stated before the conclusion, so a reader knows what to look for and what would settle it.

**Mode: `specified` on the structure, `general` on the shape. Not `grounded`.** No institution was measured. Simulated derivation checked for internal consistency against the figures, not a ground reading.

## 1. The claim, in one paragraph

A rule can be formally impartial, in the sense that swapping a group label does not change the decision, while the distribution of outcomes it produces is systematically unequal. These are not contradictory, because they are different quantities with different anchors. The rule's form is a symmetric convention at the scale of one decision. The outcome distribution is a per-slot ground reading at the scale of many decisions. Between them sits the habitat: who reaches the rule, with what starting positions and relations. A convention that does not change contact does not change the distribution. A convention promoted into a verdict about the distribution has stopped being a convention. The framework returns this split and the instrument list that would close it; it does not name a guilty institution and it does not decide a normative "ought".

## 2. Three checkable sub-claims

### C1 - framework-internal; checkable by reading Figures 10, 12 and 1

- **Statement:** rule form and outcome distribution use different bonds and different anchors, so they cannot be one term under one verdict.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 12 :59 "swap the operands. Does the meaning change? No." (the symmetric test, satisfied by a formally impartial rule). Fig 12 :85 "a threshold relation between two quantities" (the ordered comparison bond, which the outcome distribution uses). Fig 10 :18 "a declared meaning is not a grounded one". Fig 10 :47 "a convention that decides an outcome has stopped being one". Fig 1 :27 "They are frame-internal. A frame cannot score itself with a quantity it also produces."
- **Refuter:** show an impartiality rating that moves when outcomes move (so it is not a stable convention), or show that rule form and outcome distribution range against one anchor by construction. Either breaks the split.

### C2 - domain-facing; checkable by a researcher against real institutions

- **Statement:** changing the rule's form while holding habitat contact fixed will not reliably change the outcome distribution. Contact is the variable that matters.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 11 :40 "An improvement that does not change contact changes nothing, however much it improves." Fig 11 :39 "So the answerable question is not what intelligence is, but what makes a system capable. And capability is a property of agent-and-habitat, never of the agent alone." Fig 11 :61 "Introduction is available; exclusion is not."
- **What to check:** find a case where a formal rule was changed (blind review, standardized score, neutral criterion) while the population reaching it stayed the same. Did the group-level distribution move? The world literature already contains the instrument classes: disparate-impact statistics, audit studies, confounder-controlled regression, and the UC Berkeley department-level reversal in Simpson's paradox.
- **Refuter:** a controlled case where the form change alone substantially changes the distribution with contact held fixed. That refutes Fig 11 :40 as applied here and forces a re-census of the habitat domain.

### C3 - measurement-facing; checkable against any outcome table

- **Statement:** a single institution-level fairness score can be near zero while one group's slot carries a live residual, and averaging is how the signal disappears.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 1 :13 "a global R near zero with one live slot residual is a legal state". Fig 1 :19 "The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated." Fig 1 :22 "Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average."
- **What to check:** take any published institutional fairness or selection table. Recompute the residual per group rather than the pooled score. Does a live slot appear that the pooled figure hid?
- **Refuter:** a table where every per-slot residual is at or near zero once contact is enumerated. Then C3 is empty for that case.

## 3. What is explicitly not claimed

- Not that any named institution is unjust. No institution was measured.
- Not that unequal outcomes are always caused by bias, or that impartiality is worthless.
- Not a normative "ought". The figures decide what can be composed, not what should be done. This is a declared `gap`.
- Not `grounded`. Nothing here is asserted as settled by experiment.
- Not an isomer claim between the rule and the distribution as posed. They share no atom set (`06` Q3).
- Not that "discrimination" is derived by the framework. It is a binding from the legal frame, provenanced as such, and it requires a source-discriminating instrument before it is attached to a case.

## 5. What would settle it

A source-discriminating instrument, not a further argument. From *The Hard Problem is About Source*: "An instrument that can’t distinguish these cases demonstrates its own inadequacy, it cannot claim anything substantial about what is being measured or what causes it." Here:

1. **Per-slot test:** enumerate entrants and starting positions, then measure outcome residual per group. All slots near zero, the inequality claim has no live residual.
2. **Contact test:** change habitat contact with the rule fixed. Does the distribution move?
3. **Form test:** change the rule's form with contact fixed. A large move refutes Fig 11 :40 for this domain.
4. **Anchor test:** push on outcomes and see whether the impartiality rating moves. A move means it was never an anchor.
