## Two findings in the wider literature that bear directly

1. **The law already separates form from effect.** *Griggs* does not say a formally neutral test is a lie; it says a practice "fair in form, but discriminatory in operation" is actionable when it is not job related. That is exactly the D1/D3 split, with the business-necessity defence functioning as the source gate from F7. Use this to explain why the question is not a paradox.
2. **The instrument, not the argument, decides the source.** The *Disparate impact* article records the 80 percent rule, statistical significance tests, and regression with confounders as the devices used to separate a real disparity from noise or confounding. That is the ground-side counterpart of Fig 6 :23: only contact with a frame whose closure differs moves the wall. Simpson's paradox shows why a single pooled "fairness" score is the wrong instrument (Fig 1 :22).

## Distribution: offer, do not push

- **Fig 4 :22** "Only methods cross up. Only head starts come down." Sending the full architecture before anyone has described a residual sends a recording, not a generator. Lead with the shape claim and its refuter; offer the links after.
- **Fig 10 :42** "A verifier that can reconstruct the claim cannot verify it." A recipient who has absorbed the framework before being asked for a verdict has joined the frame being checked.
- **Fig 9 :27** "describe it before you go looking" Ask what they would expect to see if the shape claim were true before giving them the article.
- **Fig 2 :40** "disqualifying for a verdict: agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool" A send selected for people who already agree produces a convention, not corroboration.
- **Fig 2 :42** "Buy independence in proportion to the cost of being wrong." The cost here is a false anchor: an institution's own impartiality score accepted as ground. That is avoidable by buying an outside frame.

**Recommended order in a reply:** (1) the shape claim and its refuter; (2) ask what they would count as a failure, and record the answer; (3) only then the links, with the note that the figures, not the summary, are the authority.

## The two-stage offer

1. **Stage 1.** Send the residual and the four refuters from `01` section 5. Ask for a verdict on that alone. Nothing about the framework is shown.
2. **Stage 2, opt-in only.** If they are curious and have recorded a verdict, offer the three framework links. Their later agreement is never pooled as corroboration.
3. **Stage 3.** Offer the formula as a method to reuse. Each user writes their own refuter and states their own mode before running it. The Medium article's own caveat applies: outputs "could never be verified if the solutions presented were not contrived to fit the formula."

**Hard precondition:** stages 1 to 3 only work if the stage-1 output survives. As written in the question, it does not: "impartiality" and "outcome" are treated as one quantity with one anchor (F1, F2). Split before sending.
