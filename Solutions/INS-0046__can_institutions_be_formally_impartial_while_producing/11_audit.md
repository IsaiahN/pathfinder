## Round trip gap R_T

The Symbols table defines R_T as `gap(x, (T_E ∘ T_A)(x))`, measuring what the coarser description cannot hold. Fig 4 :30 requires it to be measured rather than assumed. I ran the round trip on the condensation this folder actually uses.

1. **Sent up (x):** the verbatim figure set, chiefly Fig 9 :5 (split the union), Fig 10 :18 and :47 (convention versus verdict), Fig 1 :13 and :22 (per-slot residual, aggregation), Fig 11 :40 (contact).
2. **Abstraction (T_A):** the shorthand carried in `01` section 9 and `07` section 2: form and outcome are different quantities with different anchors; a symmetric rule over an asymmetric habitat can yield an asymmetric distribution; a global neutral score with a live slot is legal.
3. **Concretisation (T_E):** the original question's concrete framing: can one institution be both impartial and unequal, yes or no?

**R_T is nonzero.** Three things the shorthand holds do not survive the trip back down to the yes/no form:

- the **union structure** (three terms, one overlap), which the question's binary phrasing erases;
- the **bond-type distinction** (swap-invariant form versus ordered comparison), which the question treats as one property;
- the **convention/verdict boundary**, which the question crosses by asking whether a self-description can be reconciled with a distribution.

Conversely, one thing survives cleanly: a symmetric convention does not constrain the distribution's equality when contact is held fixed. That is the residue worth sending. R_T was **not** measured numerically, because no ground reading exists; it is recorded qualitatively as the enumerated set of dropped constraints. Treating "the formula is the condensed version of the metalaws" as identity would set R_T to zero and would be the dead-reckoning error Fig 4 :30 names. The honest form is a lossy condensation with a measured gap, which is what `09_watson_answer.md` states.
