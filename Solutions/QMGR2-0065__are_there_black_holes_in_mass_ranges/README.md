## Restatement as an effect

An observer has a mass estimate for a compact object and a population model predicts a low or zero density of objects at that mass. The question asks whether the objects are there. The options lead to different places: if the ranges are genuinely empty, the models are right and the task is to explain why collapse skips those masses; if they are sparsely populated, the "gap" is a resolution effect or a second formation channel and the task is to identify the channel and the instrument that separates the sources. The figures do not return a mass number. They return the shape any answer must have, and they refuse to turn a reading into a source.

## What an answer would look like

A bound ordinary term: for each mass slot, name the actors, name the anchor that would settle the slot, say whether the slot is depth (instrument cannot resolve) or genuine absence (no channel), and write the refuter before checking. The sought-for shape is bound as a term so it can accrue a residual like anything else (Formula, "Direction").

## What is deliberately not here

No personal emails of named individuals. No ranking of people by how likely they are to believe the claim. No bulk-send plan. Three reasons, in the figures' own words:

1. **A room does not upgrade the label.** Fig 7 :13, *"agreement compounds, evidence does not"*, and Fig 2 :35, *"Alignment is triangulation, not negotiation. A mirror cannot correct you."* Selecting recipients for receptiveness manufactures the correlation that would make their agreement worthless.
2. **The ground, not agreement, decides.** Fig 2 :29: *"An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks."* A population model and ten papers citing it are one witness, not eleven.
3. **A verifier who holds the interiors cannot verify.** Fig 10 :42: *"A verifier that can reconstruct the claim cannot verify it."* The first contact carries the residual and the refuter, never the framework's interior.

Legally: scraping personal emails for unsolicited bulk outreach breaches GDPR and CAN-SPAM style rules in many jurisdictions. Public opt-in institutional channels avoid that failure as well as the structural one.
