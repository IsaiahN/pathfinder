# Outreach draft: check one output before you decide about the framework

**Use:** one-to-one, or as a cover note attached to a submission through a public venue in `03_public_contact_points.csv`. **Not** for bulk send.

**Hard precondition:** do not send until the referee has confirmed that the stage-1 output below is stated as a `general` or `specified` shape, not as a measurement. If that distinction is lost, the two-stage offer collapses on contact (Fig 10 :42).

---

**Subject:** What would count as a failure of this claim about black hole mass gaps?

> Hi [name],
>
> I have one checkable output about black hole mass ranges and I would like you to test it against what you already know. I am not asking you to read a theory first, and I am not asking you to agree with anything. If the output does not survive, there is nothing else to look at.
>
> **The output.** "Are there black holes in mass ranges where current models predict few or none?" is not one question. It is a union of at least three conditions with different actors and different anchors: the lower gap near 2 to 5 solar masses, the pair-instability gap near 50 to 130 solar masses, and the intermediate-mass range near 100 to 100,000 solar masses. A single lever aimed at all three clears the intersection and leaves the other regions standing. Each slot needs its own verdict, and those verdicts must not be averaged.
>
> The shape I am offering is this: a mass reading is a projection that cannot comment on its source, so "few or none" must be split into **depth** (the instrument cannot resolve the objects) and **absence** (no channel makes them). From inside one population frame those two look the same. An answer has to name, for each slot, the actors, the anchor that could settle it, and the instrument that would separate the sources. It does not return a mass number and it does not name a formation channel without provenance.
>
> Applied to the published record, this yields three separate verdicts, each with its own mode:
>
> 1. Lower gap: `specified`. Objects exist there (GW190814 at 2.50 to 2.67 solar masses, GW230529 at about 3.6), but whether the primary in each case is a neutron star or a black hole is still `unreached` because the classification depends on population priors and the neutron-star equation of state.
> 2. Upper pair-instability gap: `specified`. The gap is real as a cutoff for first-generation collapse and is populated by a second channel, consistent with the 2026 spin and nuclear-rate results. Same atoms, different bond, different substance.
> 3. Intermediate-mass range: `gap` of the unreached kind. There are few observational constraints between 100 and 10,000 solar masses and no solid detections in globular clusters. That is not a claim that none exist.
>
> **What is explicitly not claimed:** that any specific event's object is a black hole; that the figures derive a mass number; that the framework says which formation channel is right; that model agreement settles any slot. A mass reading is not a source. Agreement among reviews that share an evidence pool is one witness, not several.
>
> **What would refute the shape claim.** I wrote these down before building the rest of the package:
> 1. one mechanism with one boundary and one instrument accounts for all three ranges;
> 2. a complete volume-limited sample with pinned equation of state shows every lower-gap object is a neutron star and no black hole exists between 2 and 5 solar masses;
> 3. in-gap objects above 50 solar masses are shown to have low spin and no hierarchical history while the nuclear rate is pinned high enough to forbid single-star remnants there;
> 4. a controlled census with known selection function returns zero intermediate-mass black holes where instruments would have detected them;
> 5. a seventh primitive is derived from the six, or a direct state-state or structure-structure composition is admitted that cannot be refused.
>
> If any one holds, the corresponding claim is withdrawn. A one-line "this fails because..." is more useful to me than agreement. If you would rather not engage, no reply is needed.
>
> **If it survives, and only then:** here are the three public sources. The figures, not my summary, are the authority.
> - LIGO science summary for GW230529: http://ligo.org/science-summaries/GW230529
> - Tong, Fishbach, Thrane et al., "Evidence of the pair-instability gap from black-hole masses", Nature 652, 874 (2026): https://www.nature.com/articles/s41586-026-10359-0
> - Greene, Strader and Ho, "Intermediate-Mass Black Holes", Annual Review of Astronomy and Astrophysics 58, 257 (2020): https://www.annualreviews.org/content/journals/10.1146/annurev-astro-032620-021835
>
> **If you want to reuse the method:** the loop is public in the figures link below. Run it on a problem you know well and can check against a ground. Write down what would refute your result *before* you run it, and state the mode of what you get. A reported failure is more useful to me than a reported success.
