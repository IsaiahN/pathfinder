## 1. A shape is a constraint, not a filler

The canonical figures README states the limit: the framework returns *"what a missing thing would have to look like (a shape, not a name)."* Fig 9 :32: *"you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself."* Fig 13 :135: *"It cannot say which primitive is missing, because naming it would mean already holding it."*

From *The Library of Babel*: *"You can cut your search short if the set of books returned is small and descriptive enough that it provides a general shape of the answer that satisfies your question."*

So a **shape** is the narrowed form of an answer you can state before you can name the source. It constrains; it does not fill.

For this question, the shape is not a mass interval and not a formation channel. It is the following constraint: *any answer must say which mass slot it is about, which anchor could settle that slot, whether the slot is depth or absence, and which instrument would separate the sources.* That is a `general` statement about the form of an answer. The three per-domain verdicts in `01` section 3 are `specified` applications of it.

## 2. How the shape is obtained for this question

- *Library of Babel*: start from the vocabulary you already have that is closest to the thing, then let recurring new terms narrow the space. *"With each new term gained, you are able to narrow the space and deepen your vocabulary."* Here the starting vocabulary is "mass gap", "compact object", "spectrum", "reading" and "source"; the new terms that narrow it are "fallback", "pair instability", "effective spin", "S-factor", "selection function", "lensing", "ringdown".
- Fig 6 :30: *"The description travels. Fails by being sharpened into terms only we can read. Cast it in effect terms."* Fig 9 :30-31: *"your words are borrowed too, so describe the effect rather than the cause."* So the question is carried to other frames as "few or none at which mass, under which selection function", not as "the PISN gap" or "the lower mass gap", which are names already bound to particular mechanisms.
- *Decoding Tacit Knowledge*: *"When contact isn't possible, the best you can send is a plain description of what you'd expect to see, because anyone can check that against the world."* In practice: a lower-gap object with a tidal deformability above neutron-star limits, or an upper-gap object with isotropic spin, is something anyone can check.
- *Library of Babel* on bundling: if the exhaustive search fails, *"Perhaps your problem was a bundle or conflation of several problems masquerading as one? You will need to search all of those problems individually."* This is the direct essay-level warrant for splitting D1, D2 and D3.

## 3. What licenses a name

Only two things, both outside the shape itself:

1. **The ground settles it.** Formula step 5: *"SETTLE — the ground settles it. Verified only by what pays. A gate is not the ground."* A dynamically measured mass, a tidal deformability, a spin distribution that separates channels, a nuclear rate: these are ground readings.
2. **A provenanced import from a frame whose closure differs** (Fig 6 :23), with a source gate and a shadow test. The 2026 spin result is such an import into the mass-fit frame.

*The Hard Problem is About Source* gives the failure mode. A color instrument reads green but *"cannot comment on the source"*: one green light, or yellow plus blue, or RGB, or CMYK. *"If several paths create the same behavior, then behavior is a projection with lossy information."* The spectrometer resolved it, not an argument. Here a mass reading is green; the source could be a neutron star, a fallback black hole, a first-generation remnant, a merger remnant or a primordial black hole. Naming the source from the reading is the metamerism error. The 2026 spin result is our spectrometer for the upper gap; for the lower gap it is not yet available at the needed precision; for the intermediate range there is no equivalent reading with a controlled selection function.

## 4. Why the model frame's own gap is the forbidden error

Fig 8 :34: *"A frame cannot certify its own limit."* A population model that assumes a notch returns a notch. A review that cites only models with a notch reports consensus. Neither is an anchor. Fig 2 :29: *"An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks."*

This is also why the outreach material never asks a recipient to agree. *The Recursive Self-Improvement Trap* gives the rule: *"Prestige must never be allowed to validate an action directly... Prestige can fund an action. It can give an action a budget to be tried. But it cannot settle an action. Only the ground can settle it."*

## 5. Q: does "shape, not a name" forbid naming a mass range or a channel?

**Answer: no, with a preserved correction.** The README's phrase is *"what a **missing** thing would have to look like (a shape, not a name)."* Its subject is a primitive not held in the library. Here no primitive is missing: mass ordering is the spectrum term (Fig 13 :20), and a gap is an empty region of an arrangement (Fig 13 :43-44). Naming a mass slot is therefore permitted.

Two limits survive:

- Naming the **source** of a reading without an instrument that discriminates sources is the metamerism error and is not permitted as evidence.
- Naming a **channel** as if the framework derived it is an unprovenanced import. The figures return the shape (a second bond over a shared atom set is possible); the world supplies the name (hierarchical merger), with provenance and a shadow test.

### 5b. Correction to my own section 3

I initially wrote that *The Hard Problem is About Source* forbids naming any source at all from a reading. That overstates it. The essay's claim is that the answer is *"unreached until a better instrument exists"*, not that no instrument can ever exist. The correct form here is: the lower-gap source is unreached at current precision; the upper-gap source is partly reached by spin orientation; the intermediate-range source is unreached. Both the original metamerism point and this narrowing are preserved so neither is quietly lost.
