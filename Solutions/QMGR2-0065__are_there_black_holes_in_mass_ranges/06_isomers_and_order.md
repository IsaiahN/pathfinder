## DERIVATION

### Q1. What is a gap? SPECTRUM, NOT A SEVENTH TERM

- Fig 13 :20 defines spectrum as *"ordering, so more and less can be said."*
- Fig 13 :43-44: *"A continuum was the seventh and was removed for failing exactly this: it is a spectrum without gaps, or smoothness over space and time."*
- A mass distribution orders objects as more and less massive. It is therefore a spectrum. A gap is a region of that ordering with no members. It is a property of the arrangement, not an added primitive and not a new kind of thing.
- Fig 13 :74: *"Everything else is a composition across the two kinds, never within one."* A mass value alone is a state-like reading; to become a distribution it must be composed with a structure (ordering) over a state (a population in space and time). The gap sits in that composition.
- Operators table :48: *"a threshold relation between two quantities."* The edges of a gap have the grammar of a comparison, ⋛, which is ordered rather than symmetric. Swapping "upper edge" and "lower edge" does not leave the same claim.
- **Strain avoided:** Fig 13 :115 uses the word "gap" for the difference between a closed set and open arrangements. That is a homonym, not evidence for mass gaps. Recorded in `01` section 6 rather than used here.

**Falsifier Q1:** derive a seventh primitive from the six, or show that a mass distribution cannot be written as ordering over a population without adding a new kind. Then the spectrum reading fails.

### Q2. Are first-generation and hierarchical upper-gap black holes isomers?

- Fig 12 :117: *"isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."*
- Fig 12 :124: *"functional group. A substructure whose presence fixes the outcome. The group says what kind; the qualifier says which member."*
- Fig 12 :94: *"The arrangement is the substance."*
- The two upper-gap candidates share the atom set: mass, collapse, spin, a dense environment, general relativity. They differ in the bond. A first-generation remnant is the endpoint of one collapse; a hierarchical remnant is the output of a merger that re-enters as input. That is Fig 12 :121: *"cycle. The output re-enters as input. The sign of the loop decides whether it closes, and a chain cannot close by construction."*
- Therefore they are **isomers over the shared atom set**, with a decisive behavioural discriminator: spin magnitude and orientation. The 2026 Nature Astronomy result finds a low-spin group with no black holes above the gap and an isotropic high-spin group across it, which is exactly the behavioural difference an isomer claim predicts (world source, `grounded`; the figure mapping is `specified`).
- If the atom sets are allowed to differ, for example one channel requires a cluster and the other does not, the weaker claim is that they are members of one **functional group** of compact-remnant formation, distinguished by the qualifier "first generation" versus "hierarchical."
- The 2021 ApJ exclusion-region test supplies a second discriminator: a black hole above 50 solar masses with spin below about 0.2 cannot be made by hierarchical merger without fine-tuning. A detection of such an object would be the isomer's predicted failure mode.

**Falsifier Q2:** if in-gap objects are low-spin with no dynamical history while the nuclear rate is pinned so that single-star remnants can reach those masses, the isomer claim fails and the two-channel reading collapses to one population. Written before the world verdicts in `01` F6 were finalized.

### Q3. Is the lower gap an isomer question?

- No. The lower-gap ambiguity is not two arrangements over one atom set; it is **two different source assignments for one reading**. A neutron star and a black hole at 3.6 solar masses differ in which atom occupies the mass slot, not in the bond between fixed atoms.
- Fig 2 :29: *"An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks."* The anchor here is a source-discriminating instrument, not a mass fit.
- Therefore the lower gap is a metamerism problem, and its verdict is `unreached` on classification, not an isomer claim. Keeping this separate from Q2 is the union discipline of Fig 9 :54.

**Falsifier Q3:** if a tidal or electromagnetic reading labels the lower-gap object unambiguously, the metamerism claim for that event is settled and the event moves to D1's populated side. The figure shape is unchanged.

### Q4. What order do the operations run in?

Dependency order, not importance order (Fig 3 :20: *"The chain is ordered by dependency, not by importance."*):

1. **Describe the residual before choosing a frame.** Fig 9 :27-28: *"describe it before you go looking, or any frame you pick will seem to fit."* Say "few or none at which mass, under which selection function" before saying "PISN gap" or "IMBH desert."
2. **Split the union.** Fig 9 :54. A single lever on three conditions leaves the intersections standing.
3. **Refuse before mint.** Formula step 2 routes a held term that is false to REFUTED; a refusal is a negation, and *"a vocabulary without it can only accumulate"* (Operators table :58). The held term here is "a mass reading names a source." It is refused before any channel is asserted.
4. **Bind the sought-for shape as an ordinary term** (Formula, "Direction"), so it can accrue a residual and be refuted by the five conditions in `01` section 4.
5. **Settle the single object before aggregating the population.** Fig 1 :19: *"The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated."*
6. **Range each frame separately against the ground.** Fig 2 :45: *"Frames range against the ground. They do not negotiate with it."*
7. **Only then mint or import a channel name, with provenance.** Fig 6 :23: *"No frame escapes its own closure. The bound moves only on contact with a frame whose closure differs."*

**Falsifier Q4:** if a channel name can be derived from the six-term set alone, without a source-discriminating ground reading, then steps 3 to 6 are unnecessary and the order claim is wrong.

### Q5. What analogue does the low-overlap field supply?

The mathematical **mass gap** in quantum field theory is defined as the energy difference between the vacuum and the next lowest excited state (Wikipedia, "Mass gap", fetched 2026-11-23). It is a property of a Hamiltonian's spectrum, proved on a lattice in some theories and an open Clay Millennium problem in Yang-Mills. The analogy is structural and deliberately limited:

| feature | mathematical mass gap | black hole mass gap |
|---|---|---|
| what is ordered | energy eigenvalues | compact-object masses |
| what a gap is | no states between vacuum and first excitation | few or no objects in a mass interval |
| what settles it | a proof or lattice computation | an instrument with a known selection function |
| what it is not | an extra particle | an extra primitive |

The analogue corroborates Q1's shape only. It says nothing about which astrophysical channel populates which slot, and it carries zero verdict weight for D1, D2 or D3 (Fig 9 :60: *"Use filters for the budget and witnesses for the verdict. Never let a filter hand you a verdict."*).

## CONCLUSION

A mass gap is a spectrum feature, not a seventh term. The upper gap contains two arrangements over a shared atom set whose bond differs (single collapse versus merger cycle), with spin orientation as the behavioural discriminator; that is an isomer claim with a stated falsifier. The lower gap is a source-assignment problem, not an isomer claim, and stays `unreached` on classification. The intermediate range is unreached at the instrument edge. The order of operations is describe, split, refuse, bind, settle per slot, range frames, then name with provenance.

No figure line returns a mass number or a channel name. The world supplies those, and the mapping from world to figure vocabulary is this simulation's own contribution.
