## 0. The residual in effect terms

Put the question as an effect before choosing any figure line.

An astronomer obtains a **reading**: a mass estimate for a compact object, from a gravitational-wave waveform, an X-ray binary orbit, a dynamical measurement, an accretion signature, or a lensing event. A **model** predicts a low or zero density of objects at that mass. The residual `R` is the gap between the observed mass distribution and the model-predicted mass distribution, **indexed per mass slot and per object**, not aggregated into one global density (Fig 1 :19, Fig 1 :22).

Three consequences follow immediately and govern the whole audit:

1. **A mass reading is not a source.** An instrument that returns a mass cannot say whether the object is a neutron star, a first-generation black hole, a merger remnant, a primordial black hole, or an accreting object whose mass was mismeasured. This is the metamerism point from *The Hard Problem is About Source*: *"the instrument is speaking only about what it measured, it cannot comment on the source."* The world literature says the same thing in its own vocabulary: Mali and Essick (2026) find that the probability a lower-gap object is a neutron star swings from 1 percent to 67 percent for GW230529 depending on population priors.
2. **"Few or none" mixes two different facts.** Fig 6 :27: *"Which is why some unreachability is depth and some is genuine absence, and from inside the frame the two look the same."* A sparse range may be sparse because the detector cannot resolve the objects, or because no channel makes them. These issue different instructions and must not be averaged.
3. **The ranges are not one gap.** The question bundles at least three mass slots with different actors and different anchors: the lower gap near 2 to 5 solar masses, the upper pair-instability gap near 50 to 130 solar masses, and the intermediate-mass range near 100 to 100,000 solar masses. Fig 9 :4-5: *"Disagreements that do not shrink with effort, where each rule keeps working well on a different subset, are not one hard question but several well-formed ones. Split it rather than search."*

**Binding the sought-for shape as an ordinary term (Formula, "Direction").** The answer shape we commit to before searching is: *for each mass slot, identify the actors, name the anchor that could settle it, classify the slot as depth or absence, and state the instrument that would separate the sources.* Once written down, this shape can be refuted by the five conditions in section 3. Without that binding, any mass range we look at would seem to fit (Fig 9 :27-28).

## 1. Domain census before choosing any figure line

Describe `R` by arity, symmetry and scale, then one line per domain with actors, currency and anchor. No nameable anchor means it is not a domain yet.

**R by arity, symmetry, scale.** Arity: three or more conditions (the mass slots), so a **union compound**, not one hard question (Fig 9 :54, Fig 9 :57-58). Symmetry: a gap is a **threshold relation**, an ordered comparison of an upper edge against a lower edge, not a symmetric join; the Operators table :48 defines comparison as *"a threshold relation between two quantities."* Scale: first order for a single object's mass reading, second order for a population distribution over many objects, third order when the population model itself is corrected by an instrument. The three scales are not interchangeable (Fig 12 :103, :104).

| domain | actors | currency | anchor | partition or union | dependency |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **D1, lower gap (about 2 to 5 solar masses)** | neutron stars, fallback black holes, neutron-star mergers, gravitational-wave detectors, equation-of-state models | mass and spin posteriors, compactness, merger rate | GW190814 secondary (2.50 to 2.67), GW230529 primary (about 3.6), Gaia and X-ray dynamical masses, plus a source-discriminating observation | union: NS or BH classification depends on pairing and spin priors as well as mass | first; settle classification before counting |

Partition or union: the whole is a **union**, not a partition, because the same object can sit in two conditions at once. A lower-gap object can be both "in the model gap" and "a black hole by fallback"; an upper-gap object can be both "forbidden to first-generation collapse" and "produced by a previous merger" (Fig 9 :54, :55-58). Order by dependency: describe each slot's residual in effect terms before picking a frame (Fig 9 :27-28); refuse before mint (Formula step 2); settle a single object before aggregating a population (Fig 1 :19); keep the model frame's agreement out of the verdict (Fig 2 :35, Fig 9 :60).

**Strain test applied during census.** Fig 13 :115 says *"The set is closed. The arrangements are not. That gap is why import exists."* The word "gap" there means the difference between a closed generating set and an open set of arrangements. It does **not** govern an astrophysical mass gap. Using it as a figure citation for this question would be a strain, so it is re-censused and recorded in section 6 instead of being used as evidence. ## 3. Findings F1 to F10

Mode is stated on each. Every figure quote is verbatim from the local figure-text extract. Each finding carries a refuter.

### F1. The question is a union of at least three mass slots, not one gap (`general`, triangulated)

Fig 9 :4-5: *"Disagreements that do not shrink with effort, where each rule keeps working well on a different subset, are not one hard question but several well-formed ones. Split it rather than search."* Fig 9 :54: *"And when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again."* Fig 9 :57-58: *"a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either. It stops being a mixture and becomes a specific compound."*

World corroboration: the lower gap is debated because of neutron-star maximum mass and population priors (LIGO GW230529 science summary, 2024; Mali and Essick 2026); the upper gap is a pair-instability prediction tied to a nuclear rate (Tong, Fishbach, Thrane et al. 2026; Antonini, Romero-Shaw et al. 2026); the intermediate range is an instrument-coverage problem (Greene, Strader and Ho 2020). Three different levers, three different grounds. A single lever applied to all three would clear the intersection and leave the other regions standing.

**Refuter:** if one mechanism with one boundary and one instrument accounts for all three ranges, the union claim fails and the question collapses to one gap.

### F2. A mass reading cannot comment on its source (`general`, triangulated)

Fig 2 :29: *"An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks."* Fig 9 :30-31: *"your words are borrowed too, so describe the effect rather than the cause."* Fig 13 :135: *"It cannot say which primitive is missing, because naming it would mean already holding it."*

The metamerism essay supplies the mechanism: the spectrometer, not an argument, resolved green from yellow plus blue. Here the analogue is a spin measurement, a tidal deformability, an electromagnetic counterpart or a host-environment measurement. The LIGO GW190814 detection page says the secondary *"could be either a low-mass black hole or a heavy neutron star."* Mali and Essick (2026) show that even the classification probability depends on pairing priors and spin tilts. The reading stays the same while the source assignment changes.

**Refuter:** an instrument that returns an unambiguous source label at 2 to 5 solar masses, for example a tidal deformability or a confirmed counterpart, closes the classification part of D1. That would not break the figure claim, which is about readings, not about this particular instrument; it would settle one slot.

### F3. Depth and absence look the same from inside one frame (`general`, triangulated)

Fig 6 :27: *"Which is why some unreachability is depth and some is genuine absence, and from inside the frame the two look the same."* Fig 8 :39: *"So a search that finds nothing is never a proof of absence."* Fig 9 :44: *"Only a sealed room can be searched to the end, and no frame is a sealed room. So searching and finding nothing proves nothing."*

The lower gap was originally inferred from an absence of X-ray counterparts (LIGO Caltech news release, 2020: before gravitational waves, masses *"fell into two distinct ranges with a gap between them from about 2 to 5 times the mass of our Sun"*). Gravitational-wave instruments then returned objects inside it. The IMBH range has not yet had the equivalent arrival. Absence of detections in one channel is not absence of objects.

**Refuter:** a complete volume-limited census with a known selection function that returns zero events where the instruments would have detected them converts "unreached" to "absent" for that slot. That is a ground reading, not an argument.

### F4. A mass gap is a feature of a spectrum, not a new primitive (`general`, single-source on the figure definition, with a low-overlap analogue)

Fig 13 :20: *"ordering, so more and less can be said."* Fig 13 :43-44: *"A continuum was the seventh and was removed for failing exactly this: it is a spectrum without gaps, or smoothness over space and time."* Fig 13 :74: *"Everything else is a composition across the two kinds, never within one."*

The mass distribution is a spectrum over a quantity: it orders objects as more and less massive. A gap is a region of that ordering with no members. It needs no seventh primitive and no new kind of thing; it is a property of the arrangement. The low-overlap analogue is the mathematical **mass gap** in quantum field theory, defined on the Wikipedia "Mass gap" page as *"the difference in energy between the lowest energy state, the vacuum, and the next lowest energy state."* There too a gap is a property of a spectrum, not an additional particle. That analogue corroborates the shape only. It carries no weight for which astrophysical channel fills which slot.

**Refuter:** derive a seventh primitive from the six, or show that a mass distribution requires a state or structure not in the minimum set. Then the spectrum reading fails.

### F5. The lower gap is being populated, but the occupants are not yet classified (`specified`, triangulated)

Fig 1 :19: *"The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated."* Fig 1 :22: *"Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average."* Fig 8 :50: *"Two frames can hold the same atom by convergence, reaching it separately, which is corroboration at maximum independence."*

World readings: GW190814's secondary is 2.50 to 2.67 solar masses (LIGO DCC P190814, 2020), *"either the lightest black hole or the heaviest neutron star ever discovered"*; GW230529's primary is about 3.6 solar masses and is *"the first binary candidate with the primary component in the mass gap"* (LIGO science summary, 2024). The LIGO summary also states that a nuclear-physics-based neutron-star probability is *"small but non-zero, and under certain assumptions it can even reach a few percent."* Mali and Essick (2026) push that range much higher under different population priors. Population fits shift the inferred minimum black-hole mass from about 6.04 to about 3.36 solar masses when GW230529 is included.

**Verdict:** the model prediction of "few" is weakened at the ground; the prediction of "none" is refuted for at least one object if it is a black hole; whether it is one is still `unreached`. Verdicts are not averaged with D2 or D3.

**Refuter:** 150 neutron-star-black-hole events with pinned equation of state and no in-gap black hole would restore a sharp lower gap. This is the scale the IOPscience 2022 population study says would constrain the maximum neutron-star mass to plus or minus 0.02 solar masses.

### F6. The upper gap is real for first-generation collapse and populated by a second channel (`specified`, triangulated)

Fig 12 :104: *"A settled molecule becomes an atom for whatever composes over it. So irreducible is a fact about a level and never about the world."* Fig 12 :117: *"isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."* Fig 12 :121: *"cycle. The output re-enters as input. The sign of the loop decides whether it closes, and a chain cannot close by construction."*

World readings: Woosley and Heger (2021) find that momentary pair instability leaves no remant above a limiting mass and note that recent LIGO observations show black holes with individual masses above the old lower edge. Edelman, Doctor and Farr (2021) find *"mild evidence favoring an upper mass gap"* with bounds near 56 and 104 solar masses under one mass model and no preference under another, so the result is prior-sensitive. Abbott et al. (2020, GW190521) report a primary at 85 solar masses *"within the gap produced by (pulsational) pair-instability supernova processes."* The 2026 Nature and Nature Astronomy papers independently find a lower gap edge near 44 solar masses and a high-spin, isotropic population that occupies the gap, which both interpret as hierarchical mergers. Tong et al. (2026) add a nuclear constraint: the gap location constrains the 12C(alpha,gamma)16O S-factor to about 260 keV barns with wide errors.

So the same mass range contains two substances over a shared atom set: a first-generation remnant is forbidden there, and a merger remnant is allowed there. That is Fig 12's isomer, not a contradiction and not a single population with a wide tail. The 2026 spin result is a genuine second witness because it does not draw on the mass-fit evidence pool alone.

**Refuter:** in-gap objects with low spin and no dynamical history, plus a nuclear rate pinned so high that no single-star remnant can exceed the lowest observed in-gap mass, would kill the isomer reading and leave one population. This is close to the exclusion-region test proposed by the 2021 ApJ paper on high mass and low spin.

### F7. The intermediate-mass range is unreached, not named empty (`general` shape with a single-source figure basis; `grounded` world measurement)

Fig 6 :34-35: *"The question is not whether a sensor could exist. It is whether anything, at any resolution, is already returning something that fails to resolve."* Fig 8 :39: *"So a search that finds nothing is never a proof of absence."* Fig 13 :134: *"Unreachable is a verdict here, not a silence."*

World readings: Greene, Strader and Ho (2020) report *"no solid detections of black holes in globular clusters"* and *"few observational constraints on black holes in any environment"* between 100 and 10,000 solar masses. Askar, Baldassare and Mezcua (2024) say whether intermediate-mass black holes are an independent category or merely the heaviest stellar mass and lightest supermassive ones *"is still unclear, mostly owing to the lack of an observational smoking gun."* Fujii et al. (2024) simulate globular-cluster formation producing black holes above 1,000 solar masses, which is a model result, not an anchor. Lensing statistics and ringdowns are already returning something that fails to resolve, so this is depth with instruments under development, not a sensor that would have to be specified from nothing.

**Verdict:** `gap` of the unreached kind, reported as a verdict. It does not say there are none. It says the current instrument set cannot return a source-discriminating reading in this range.

**Refuter:** a dynamically confirmed intermediate-mass black hole, a lensing inference with a controlled selection function, or a ringdown component mass in this range would settle that the objects exist. It would change the world verdict, not the figure shape.

### F8. The model frame cannot certify its own gap (`general`, triangulated)

Fig 8 :34: *"A frame cannot certify its own limit."* Fig 9 :44: *"Only a sealed room can be searched to the end, and no frame is a sealed room."* Fig 2 :35: *"Alignment is triangulation, not negotiation. A mirror cannot correct you."*

A population model that assumes a notch produces a notch. F1's three domains each have a model frame that can generate its own gap. The way out is not another model vote but an instrument that bears on the residual: spin orientation for D2 (Antonini et al. 2026), nuclear reaction rates for D2 (Tong et al. 2026), tidal deformability and counterparts for D1, lensing and dynamics for D3. Fig 8 :50 permits genuine corroboration when channels converge at maximum independence: *"Two frames can hold the same atom by convergence, reaching it separately, which is corroboration at maximum independence."* Agreement from one evidence pool is one witness (Fig 7 :13, Fig 2 :40).

**Refuter:** show a model frame that proves its own completeness without importing an anchor. That would overturn the barrier reading and is a figure-internal test.

### F9. The budget cannot hand you the verdict (`general`, triangulated)

Fig 9 :60: *"Use filters for the budget and witnesses for the verdict. Never let a filter hand you a verdict."* Fig 5 :48: *"Its own output is never a measure of success. A system can be enthusiastically curious and produce nothing."*

Catalog size, signal-to-noise, event count and citation count are budget filters. Adding more events inside the same waveform model and the same prior family searches a sealed room more thoroughly; it does not import a new atom (Fig 8 :32: *"Closure is idempotent. Composing inside a closed set never leaves it, so no search, however long, adds an atom."*). The 2026 result is interesting precisely because spin and nuclear rate are different instruments, not because the catalog grew.

**Refuter:** demonstrate that a verdict changed solely because of more of the same observations with no new instrument or channel. Under the figure this is impossible in principle, so this refuter is a consistency check on the method, not an observable claim about astrophysics.

## 4. Pre-registered refuter

Required before any outreach: a falsifier written before the run is a design instrument. The shape claim is refuted if any of the following holds.

1. **Union fails.** One mechanism with one boundary and one instrument accounts for all three mass ranges.
2. **D1 fails.** A complete volume-limited sample with pinned equation of state shows that every lower-gap object is a neutron star and no black hole exists between 2 and 5 solar masses.
3. **D2 fails.** In-gap objects above 50 solar masses are shown to have low spin and no hierarchical history, while the 12C(alpha,gamma)16O rate is pinned high enough that no single-star remnant can land in the observed range.
4. **D3 fails in the opposite direction from the one expected.** A controlled census with known selection function returns zero intermediate-mass black holes in a volume where the instruments would have detected them, converting "unreached" into "absent."
5. **Substrate fails.** A seventh primitive is derived from the six, or a direct state-state or structure-structure composition is admitted that cannot be refused.

If any fires, the corresponding claim is withdrawn. Until one is run, no part of this derivation may be labelled `grounded`. These conditions were written before the per-domain verdicts in section 3 and before the synthesis in `07_the_tether_answer.md`.
