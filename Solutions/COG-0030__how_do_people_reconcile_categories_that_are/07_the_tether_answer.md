# The Tether's answer on everyday versus scientific or bureaucratic categories

**Mode: `general` on the structural shape, `specified` on the three per-domain verdicts, nothing `grounded`.** Simulated derivation checked against the figures (`05_what_shape_means.md` section 6), not a ground reading.

## 1. The residual it answers

The same object needs different terms in different settings. "Which category is right" bundles three questions with three different grounds. Fig 2 :36: *"An anchor is legitimate when the question is constitutively about it."* The request is for the shape any successful reconciliation must have, not for the cognitive mechanism that produces it.

## 2. The shape

1. **Do not merge the frames; bind each term to a question.** Fig 12 :5 *"A set of ingredients is not one. The bond is the rest of the information, and it is the half nothing was recording."* A flat list cannot say which term is in force. The reconciled term carries a scope index.
2. **Keep the two legitimate grounds separate.** For the question whether a category is good for a person, the anchor is human judgment, because the question is about people (Fig 2 :37-38). For the question whether a category tracks the world, the anchor is a measurement (Fig 2 :29-30). Neither settles the other.
3. **Read agreement with valence.** Fig 2 :39 *"Correlation has valence."* Fig 2 :41 *"efficient for a convention: agreement about a shared meaning is the product, not a corruption of it"*; Fig 2 :40 *"disqualifying for a verdict: agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool"*. Everyday agreement is useful for the everyday term and worthless as evidence about the scientific one.
4. **A bureaucratic classification is a convention until it decides an outcome.** Fig 2 :43 *"Guard, from Figure 10: a convention that decides an outcome has stopped being one."* Fig 10 :49 *"Declaring a meaning does not ground it."* Once a rule assigns a tax, a benefit or a meal credit, it must be checked against what the decision is about, and it must be installed so it can be violated.
5. **Refit before mint.** Fig 5 :13 *"mis-attached: refit what you have, do not invent"*. Most conflicts are mis-attached bindings. A third public category is permitted only if it pays the MDL bargain and passes all three guards as a product.
6. **Unbundle the union.** Fig 9 :57 *"a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either."* Over the shared atom set, the everyday and official systems are Fig 12 :117 *"isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."*
7. **Provenance is the whole issue between frames.** Fig 8 :50-52 *"Convergent derivation and adopted import look identical in the contents. Only the record of where each came from separates them, and the surplus lives only where the closures differ."*
8. **The mechanism stays open.** Fig 9 :32 *"you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself"*. The figures give the shape, not the cognitive process.

## 3. What it explicitly does not say

- Not that the everyday categories are false, or that the scientific ones are more real in every setting.
- Not that official classifications are stupid. They are legitimate conventions until they decide an outcome.
- Not that people actually use this shape. No measurement of frame switching was taken.
- Not that the framework returns a psychological mechanism. That is a `gap`, unreached rather than unreachable.
- Not `grounded`. No instrument discriminated among candidate mechanisms.

## 4. What would settle it

The ground, not agreement. From *The Hard Problem is About Source*, a spectrometer rather than an argument discriminated among sources that gave the same reading. Here the corresponding instrument would:

1. Present the same object under conflicting category demands and record which term a person uses, with each demand's anchor stated in advance. Does a scope index predict the switch better than a flat-feature account?
2. Test whether agreement from a shared evidence pool changes the answer to a question whose anchor is a measurement held fixed.
3. Test whether a bureaucratic rule that decides an outcome remains correct when the thing the decision is about changes underneath it.
4. Test refuter 2: can a flat vocabulary with no context marker select the right term in every setting?

## 5. The honest stop

Fig 13 :134 *"Unreachable is a verdict here, not a silence"* and Fig 8 :39 *"So a search that finds nothing is never a proof of absence."* The figures stop at the limit of what they can distinguish. The lineage is recorded so the stop is checkable. If a figure line were found that names the routing mechanism, the gap would close. Until then, the answer is a shape and a route, not a result.

## 6. One-paragraph version for outreach

> The Tether does not say which category is right. It says that one word hides three questions with three different grounds: what works for a person, what the world contains, and what an official decision is for. Human judgment settles the first, a measurement settles the second, and a rule that decides an outcome must be checked against the thing the decision is about rather than taken as a verdict. So a reconciliation is not a merge. It is a binding of each term to a question and a context, with provenance recorded and agreement read with valence, useful for shared meanings and worthless as evidence about the world when the agreeing people learned from one pool. The framework returns that shape, not the cognitive mechanism that produces it. Four refuters were written in advance, and the instrument that would close the gap is named but was not run.
