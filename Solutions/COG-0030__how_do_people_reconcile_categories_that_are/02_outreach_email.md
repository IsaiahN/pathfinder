# Outreach draft: check one shape claim before you decide whether the framework is worth reading

**Use:** one-to-one, or as a cover letter attached to a submission through the public venues in `03_public_contact_points.csv`. **Not** for bulk send.

**Hard precondition:** do not send until the recipient can be shown the residual and the refuter without any framework material. If the stage-one claim does not survive, there is nothing else to look at.

---

**Subject:** One check about everyday categories versus official ones, before any theory

> Hi [name],
>
> I have one specific shape claim I would like you to check against what you already know. I am not asking you to read a theory first, and I am not asking you to agree with it. If the claim does not survive, there is nothing else to look at.
>
> **The residual.** The same object gets put in different places by an everyday category and by a scientific or official one. A tomato is a fruit to a botanist and a vegetable in a tariff or a kitchen. The object did not change. The question "which category is right" hides at least three different questions: what works for a person here, what the world contains, and what an official rule is for. Each has a different thing that could settle it.
>
> **The shape claim (`general`, not `grounded`):** a successful reconciliation does not merge the two systems. It binds each term to a question and a context, keeps the three grounds distinct, and records where each term came from. It does not average them and it does not let a rule decide what the world contains. This is a shape a successful answer must have, not a measurement of how you or anyone else actually switches categories.
>
> **What would refute it, written down before I sent this:**
> 1. One ground settles both "good for this person" and "true of the world" without loss on either side.
> 2. Someone can show a flat vocabulary, with no context marker, that reliably selects the correct term in every setting.
> 3. An official rule that decides an outcome, is unchecked against what the decision is about, and still tracks the ground as well as a measurement does.
> 4. Agreement among people who learned from the same source changes a measurement of the world with the instrument held fixed.
>
> If any one holds, the shape is wrong and I will retract it. A one-line "this fails because..." is more useful to me than agreement. If you would rather not engage, no reply is needed.
>
> **What I am explicitly not claiming:** that the framework explains the cognitive mechanism of switching categories. It does not. It gives the shape a successful switch would have, and stops there. I am also not claiming everyday categories are false, or that official classifications are stupid. Both are legitimate in the question they are about.
>
> **If it survives, and only then:** here are the sources. The figures are the authority, not my summary of them.
> - Verbatim figure text with line numbers (local extract in this project): `FRAMEWORK/reference/03-FIGURES_TEXT.md`
> - Canonical usage rule for the figures: https://github.com/IsaiahN/tether/blob/general-agent/figures/README.md
> - Full architecture (Medium, 2026-08-19): https://medium.com/@IsaiahNwukor/arrival-of-the-fittest-the-tether-architecture-1de7edf641ec
> - Worked narrative: https://adventuresinml.substack.com/p/how-intelligence-actually-works
>
> **If you want to use the machinery:** the formula is in the figures link. Run it on a problem you know well and can check against a ground. Write down what would refute your result *before* you run it, and state whether your output is general, specified or grounded. A reported failure is more useful to me than a reported success.
>
> — Isaiah

## Standing of this draft

This email offers a shape and four refuters. It does not offer a finding about how people actually reconcile categories. That would require the instrument described in `01_figure_grounded_audit.md` section 6, which was not run here.
