# Expert verification packet: the Tether on everyday versus official categories

**What this is:** a claim in a form a researcher on concepts, categorization or classification can check. Mode, derivation and refuters are stated before the conclusion.

**Mode: `general` on the shape, `specified` on the three domain verdicts, not `grounded`.** No measurement of actual frame switching is claimed.

## 1. The claim, in one paragraph

One word, "reconcile", covers three questions with three legitimate grounds: what works for a person, what the world contains, and what an official decision is for. A successful reconciliation does not merge the systems. It binds each term to a question and a context, keeps the grounds distinct, records provenance between frames, and reads agreement with valence. A bureaucratic classification is a convention until it decides an outcome, at which point it must be checked against the thing the decision is about. The framework returns this shape and four refuters. It does not return the cognitive mechanism by which a person switches terms, and no such mechanism was measured here.

## 2. Three checkable sub-claims

### C1 — framework-internal, checkable against Fig 2

- **Statement:** the two grounds are different in kind and neither settles the other. Human judgment is legitimate for a question constitutively about people; a measurement is legitimate for a question about the world. Agreement is productive for a shared meaning and disqualifying as evidence about the world when the agreeing frames share a pool.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 2 :36 *"An anchor is legitimate when the question is constitutively about it."* Fig 2 :37-38 *"Human judgment is the right anchor for is this a good answer for a person, because the question is about people. It is only an instrument for is this code correct, and instruments can be wrong on axes they do not measure."* Fig 2 :29-30 *"An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks."* Fig 2 :39 *"Correlation has valence."* Fig 2 :41 *"efficient for a convention: agreement about a shared meaning is the product, not a corruption of it"* and Fig 2 :40 *"disqualifying for a verdict: agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool"*.
- **Refuter:** one ground settles both questions without loss on either; or agreement from a shared pool changes a measurement with the instrument held fixed.

### C2 — checkable against Fig 12 and a classification task

- **Statement:** the reconciled term is a molecule with a scope relation, not a flat list of features. Over the shared atom set the everyday and official systems stand as isomers: same atoms, different bond to a ground, different behaviour in a form versus a kitchen.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 12 :5 *"A set of ingredients is not one. The bond is the rest of the information, and it is the half nothing was recording."* Fig 12 :94 *"The arrangement is the substance."* Fig 12 :117 *"isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."*
- **What to check:** can a flat vocabulary `{object, everyday term, official term}` select the correct term in every context without a scope marker? Does the IEP case of the mathematics book shelved differently by purpose (teachers' monograph versus children's textbook) behave as a scope-bonded binding rather than a failed partition?
- **Refuter:** a flat list selects correctly in all contexts with no scope marker and no loss; or the two systems share no atom, which kills the isomer claim and leaves only the weaker functional-group reading.

### C3 — checkable against Fig 10 and a legal or administrative case

- **Statement:** a classification rule that decides an outcome has stopped being a convention and must be checked against what the decision is about. Declaring a meaning does not ground it.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 2 :43 *"Guard, from Figure 10: a convention that decides an outcome has stopped being one."* Fig 10 :47 *"a convention that decides an outcome has stopped being one"* Fig 10 :49 *"Declaring a meaning does not ground it."* Fig 10 :18-19 *"a declared meaning is not a grounded one"* and *"→ install what can be violated"*.
- **What to check:** Nix v. Hedden (149 U.S. 304, 1893) classified tomatoes as vegetables for a tariff using the ordinary meaning rather than the botanical one. The holding is about the tariff, not botany. Does the rule remain correct when what the decision is about changes? The 1981 pickle-relish proposal and the 2011 tomato-paste rule are further cases.
- **Refuter:** a rule that decides an outcome, is unchecked against what the decision is about, and still tracks the ground as well as a measurement does.

## 3. What is explicitly not claimed

- Not that everyday categories are false or official ones are correct.
- Not a derivation of the botanical classification from the figures. That is world fact, cited with provenance.
- Not that the framework names a cognitive mechanism. It returns a shape, not a name.
- Not that the shape describes any particular population. No experiment was run.
- Not `grounded`. Every substantive claim is `general` or `specified`.
- Not that agreement from people who share a pool corroborates the claim. It does not.

## 5. What would settle it

A source-discriminating instrument, not a further argument. The spectrometer in *The Hard Problem is About Source* resolved a reading that several sources could produce. Here:

1. Same object, conflicting category demands, record which term is used, with each demand's anchor stated in advance.
2. Hold a measurement instrument fixed and vary whether the agreeing people share an evidence pool.
3. Change what an official decision is about while holding the rule fixed and see whether the rule still pays.
4. Test the flat-list refuter directly.

## 6. What an expert can check in twenty minutes

1. Read Fig 2 :29-30, :36-43 and Fig 10 :47-49. Four short passages.
2. Check C2 against a classification task you know: does a scope index appear in practice, or is a flat list enough?
3. Check C3 against one legal or administrative case you know. Was the rule being used as a claim about the world, or as a convention for a decision?
4. Name one instrument that would distinguish two mechanisms that produce the same term-switching behavior, or say why none is possible.
5. Send the refutation. A failure is more useful than an endorsement.
