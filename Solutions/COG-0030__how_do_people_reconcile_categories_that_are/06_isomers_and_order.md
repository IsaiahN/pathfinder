# Structure, order and analogues in the reconciliation of categories

**Mode: `general` on the structure, `specified` on the ordering claims. Simulated derivation, not a ground reading** (`05_what_shape_means.md` section 6).

## RESIDUAL

The same object requires a different term in a different setting, and the word "reconcile" covers three operations with three different grounds. Two questions are owed: (1) what is the structure of the reconciled term, and (2) in what order must the operations run, and is the conflict a partition or a union?

## DOMAIN CENSUS

Atoms held: the object, the everyday term, the official term, the context, the anchor of each frame. Bonds held: conjunction, identity, sequence, comparison, subtraction, negation (Operators table; Fig 12). No new primitive is available without import. Order is by dependency per Fig 3, not by importance.

## DERIVATION

### Q1 — Is the reconciled term a molecule or a list?

- Fig 12 :5: *"A set of ingredients is not one. The bond is the rest of the information, and it is the half nothing was recording."*
- Fig 12 :94: *"The arrangement is the substance."*
- A flat list `{tomato, fruit, vegetable}` records the ingredients and not the question each term answers. Nothing downstream can tell them apart.
- Write the reconciled term as `object [scope: everyday / scientific / official] term`, where the bracket is a typed scope relation, analogous to the comparison bond's role as a threshold between two quantities.
- **Falsifier, written before the run:** if a flat ingredient list selects the correct term in every context with no scope marker and no loss, the molecule claim fails.

### Q2 — Which bond carries the scope?

- Operators table: identity `≡` says two names, one referent. That is false here: "fruit" and "vegetable" are not one referent under one description; they are two bindings of one object to two questions.
- Conjunction `+` says both hold at once. That is also wrong for a form that requires exactly one legal category.
- Comparison `⋛` is *"a threshold relation between two quantities"* (Operators table). The conflict is not which is larger; it is which question is being asked.
- The scope relation is a typed index that the figures describe structurally (Fig 12 :44 *"the arrangement is priced or it is free"*) but do not name among the seven bonds. So it is a **binding from the question's structure onto the bond vocabulary**, provenanced as such. It is not a new atom (Fig 6 :21 *"MINT composes inside the closure and can never add an atom."*).
- **Strain, named not hidden:** the scope index is not one of the seven named bonds. Under Fig 12 :90 *"A relation that fails all six is not a bond you are missing. It is a claim that has not been made yet."* I record it as an imported relation with a source gate, not as a figure-derived eighth bond. Re-census does not resolve this; it is a genuine strain.

### Q3 — What is the order of operations?

- Fig 9 :27-28: *"describe it before you go looking, or any frame you pick will seem to fit"* Residual first.
- Formula step 2: refuse before mint. The "fruit versus vegetable" route is REFUTED as a single question, not NOVEL. What is owed is a refusal of the bundled question plus a rebinding per slot.
- Fig 3 :19: *"Optimising a link that already works cannot rescue one that does not."* Vocabulary before objective before rule.
- Fig 5 :13: *"mis-attached: refit what you have, do not invent"* Refit the binding before minting a third public category.
- Order: (1) describe the per-object residual, (2) name the question each term answers, (3) assign the legitimate anchor for each, (4) bind the term with a scope index, (5) only then consider an institutional rule, (6) check the rule against the thing the decision is about.
- **Falsifier:** if a rule can be written before steps 1 to 4 and still pay in all contexts, the dependency order is wrong.

### Q4 — Partition, union, or isomer?

- Fig 9 :57: *"a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either."* The everyday and scientific terms overlap on the object but keep different anchors. Union.
- Fig 12 :117: *"isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."* Are the two category systems isomers? Over the shared atom set {object, term, context, anchor} they are: same atoms, different bond to anchor, different behaviour in a form versus a kitchen. As commonly written they share only the object, so the weaker reading is Fig 12 :123 *"functional group. A substructure whose presence fixes the outcome. The group says what kind; the qualifier says which member."*
- The world analogue supports this. The IEP article records that a mathematics book appears in different Dewey classes depending on whether it is a teachers' monograph or a children's textbook. Same object, different bond to a purpose, different substance for retrieval. That is an isomer at the level of classification practice.
- **Falsifier:** if the two systems can be written without a shared atom (for example, a scientific classification with no object and no context), the isomer claim fails and only the functional-group reading stands.

### Q5 — Does the same structure hold at institutional scale?

- Fig 4 :19-20: *"The kernel is the part that does not change with size: the same loop and the same rule for naming what worked, whether one agent is running it or a thousand. Only the actors, the currency and the anchor change."*
- At one person: currency is fluent action, anchor is usefulness to that person. At an institution: currency is an authorized decision, anchor should be what the decision is about. Same loop, different cast.
- Fig 4 :25: *"A recording carried upward looks like knowledge and is a description of one occasion."* An institution that freezes one successful binding into a rule has carried up a recording. It still looks like knowledge.
- **Falsifier:** show an institutional rule that keeps its contact with the ground with no maintained channel. That would break the asymmetry.

## CONCLUSION

The reconciled term is a molecule with a scope bond, not a list. The operations are ordered by dependency, with refusal before mint and description before frame selection. The conflict is a union, and over a shared atom set the two category systems are isomers in the figure's sense. The scope relation is **not** one of the seven named bonds and is recorded as an imported relation with a source gate; that is the one live strain. None of this names the cognitive mechanism by which a person performs the switch.
