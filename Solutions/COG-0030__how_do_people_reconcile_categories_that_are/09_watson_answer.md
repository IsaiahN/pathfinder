## QUESTION

Decide, in effect terms, what the Tether says when an everyday category and a scientific or bureaucratic classification put the same object in different places. Option A: treat this as one hard question and ask which classification is really right. Option B: unbundle it into three questions with three grounds, keep the frames separate, and return the shape a successful reconciliation must have while refusing to name a cognitive mechanism. What each leads to: A averages different grounds and lets a convention decide an outcome; B sends four pre-registered refuters, a scope-bond shape, and an honest stop at the mechanism gap.

## DOMAIN CENSUS

- **R (the residual).** The same object requires a different term in a different setting. Arity: at least three, usually four (object, everyday term, official term, selecting context). Symmetry: the frames are asymmetric in ground, not in vocabulary. Scale: one person and one institution are the same kernel at two sizes. Union, not partition: the conditions overlap on the object and keep different anchors.
- **D1, everyday categorization.** Actors: a person, familiar objects, basic-level terms. Currency: fluent action and graded typicality. Anchor: human judgment about what works for a person, legitimate because the question is constitutively about people. Union member. Depends on the observer being able to tell the objects apart.
- **D2, scientific classification.** Actors: taxa, measurements, theories. Currency: prediction. Anchor: a measurement that does not update on opinion. Union member. Owed where the question is about the world.
- **D3, bureaucratic classification.** Actors: statutes, forms, officials, categories. Currency: an authorized decision. Anchor: none as written; a convention that decides an outcome has stopped being one. Union member. Must follow the statement of the rule's purpose, not precede it.
- **D4, the reconciler.** Actors: the same person moving between contexts. Currency: per-slot residual and context switching. Anchor: none of the three alone; the person routes each slot to the frame whose closure predicts its shape. This is the union frame and it must be unbundled before search.
- **Order by dependency.** Describe the residual before choosing a frame (Fig 9 :27-28), refuse the bundled question before minting (formula step 2), settle each first-order domain before any institutional rule (Fig 3 :19).

Strain test applied: Fig 2 :40 and Fig 2 :41 appear to disagree about agreement. Re-census resolves it: :40 governs verdicts, :41 governs conventions, and :39 says correlation has valence. Different objects, so no strain after re-census. The one live strain is the type of the scope relation, recorded in section TETHER D4 and in `06_isomers_and_order.md` Q2.

## VERDICT (from the figures)

The shape survives; the mechanism is refused. Keep the everyday term for the question about a person, keep the scientific term for the question about the world, and treat a bureaucratic classification as a convention until it decides an outcome, at which point it must be checked against what the decision is about. Reconciliation is a scope-bonded binding of each term to a question and a context, with provenance recorded and agreement read with valence. The two systems stand to each other as isomers over a shared atom set. What is not delivered is a cognitive mechanism: the figures return a shape, not a name, and no instrument was run. This is `general` on the shape and `specified` on the three domain verdicts. Nothing here is `grounded`.

- **cost if wrong:** bounded and locatable. The packet states four refuters before the conclusion and records the lineage, so an error is findable rather than invisible. The real cost is in option A: averaging three grounds and letting a convention decide an outcome. Fig 2 :42 "Buy independence in proportion to the cost of being wrong." That cost is avoidable by leading with the refuters and never pooling later agreement as corroboration.
- **undoable:** yes. The bundling is removable without withdrawing any domain verdict. `07_the_tether_answer.md` and `08_expert_verification_packet.md` already remove it. A sent claim can be superseded by a corrected packet, and no primitive has to be withdrawn. The scope relation is recorded as an import, so it can be withdrawn without touching the domain verdicts.
- **reversed by:** a ground reading, not agreement. A task that presents the same object under conflicting category demands and discriminates among mechanisms would name what the framework leaves open. A flat-list result with no scope marker would break the bond claim. A rule that decides an outcome and tracks what the decision is about without any check would break the convention/verdict boundary. A figure line naming the routing mechanism would close the gap. Until one of these fires, the verdict stands as `candidate`.
