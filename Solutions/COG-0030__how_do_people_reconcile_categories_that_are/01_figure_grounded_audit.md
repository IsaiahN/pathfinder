## 0. The question restated as an effect, and the pre-bound answer shape

> How do people reconcile categories that are useful in everyday life but conflict with scientific or bureaucratic classifications?

In effect terms: a person has a term that pays in one setting and fails in another. The object is the same, the term differs, and the context selects which term is required. Nothing in the question asks which term is *true* in general. It asks what makes the two usable without erasing either.

**Answer shape, written down before the run** (formula step "Direction": make the sought-for shape an ordinary term so it can accrue a residual):

> A reconciliation is a per-object binding of a term to a frame, with a scope index that says which question the term is answering, and with the anchor of each frame kept distinct. It does not merge the frames, does not average their verdicts, and does not let a convention decide an outcome.

This is the hypothesis under audit, not a conclusion. Its refuter is in section 5.

## 1. Residual

- **Arity.** At least three, usually four: the object, the everyday term, the official term, and the context that selects which term is required. Because the two conditions overlap on the same object, this is a **union compound**, not one hard question. Fig 2 :40-41 gives the two valence cases that the single word "agree" conflates.
- **Symmetry.** The frames are asymmetric in ground, not in vocabulary. The everyday term is settled by usefulness to a person. The scientific term is settled by a measurement. The bureaucratic term is settled by a rule, which is not an anchor once it decides an outcome. The comparison is therefore not a symmetric join of two equal operands.
- **Scale.** One person reconciling two terms, and an institution reconciling two terminologies, are the same kernel at two sizes. Fig 4 :19-20: *"The kernel is the part that does not change with size: the same loop and the same rule for naming what worked, whether one agent is running it or a thousand. Only the actors, the currency and the anchor change."*
- **Slot structure.** Fig 1 :19: *"The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated."* Fig 1 :22: *"Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average."* So "which classification is right overall" is the wrong measurement. The residual lives on each object and each context.

## 4. Findings

### F1 — There are two legitimate anchors, and the question does not say which one it is asking (decisive)

Fig 2 :36: *"An anchor is legitimate when the question is constitutively about it."*

Fig 2 :37-38: *"Human judgment is the right anchor for is this a good answer for a person, because the question is about people. It is only an instrument for is this code correct, and instruments can be wrong on axes they do not measure."*

Fig 2 :29-30: *"An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks."*

So "is this a useful category for a person here" is settled by human judgment about people, and "does this category track the structure of the world" is settled by a measurement. A conflict between them is not a contradiction. It is two questions with two grounds that have been bundled under one word. The everyday term is not refuted by the scientific term, and the scientific term is not made more true by everyday agreement.

**Triangulated** across Fig 2 :36-38 (anchor legitimacy) and Fig 2 :29-30 (anchor definition), low overlap between them. **Refuter:** show one anchor that settles both questions without loss on either, that is, a measurement that also fixes what is good for a person, or a human judgment that also fixes what the world contains. If that exists, F1 is false.

### F2 — Agreement has valence, so "people agree on the everyday term" is not evidence about the scientific term

Fig 2 :39: *"Correlation has valence."*

Fig 2 :40: *"disqualifying for a verdict: agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool"*

Fig 2 :41: *"efficient for a convention: agreement about a shared meaning is the product, not a corruption of it"*

This is the figure that stops the reader from treating the two kinds of agreement as one quantity. Agreement on the everyday term is the product, because the everyday term *is* a shared meaning. Agreement on the scientific term from people who learned the same textbook is one evidence pool talking, not corroboration.

**Triangulated** across Fig 2 :39-41 and Fig 8 :48-49 (two frames that overlap almost completely are one frame wearing two names). **Refuter:** exhibit a case where agreement among members of one culture changes a measurement of the world, with the measurement instrument held fixed. That would collapse the valence distinction.

### F3 — A bureaucratic classification is a convention until it decides an outcome, then it is a verdict without a ground

Fig 2 :43: *"Guard, from Figure 10: a convention that decides an outcome has stopped being one."*

Fig 10 :47: *"a convention that decides an outcome has stopped being one"*

Fig 10 :49: *"Declaring a meaning does not ground it."*

Fig 10 :18-19: *"a declared meaning is not a grounded one"* and *"→ install what can be violated"*

The Nix v. Hedden case is the clean instance: the Court used the ordinary meaning of fruit and vegetable for a tariff rather than the botanical meaning, because the question was constitutively about the tariff. A classification that assigns a tax, a benefit or a meal credit has crossed from convention into verdict. Under F1 its anchor must then be the thing the decision is about, and the rule must be installed so it can be violated. The ketchup and pizza episodes show what happens when the rule is treated as a claim about nature instead.

**Triangulated** across Fig 2 :43 and Fig 10 :47, :49, which are two separate figures with different subject matter. **Refuter:** show a bureaucratic rule that decides an outcome, is not checked against anything the decision is about, and still tracks the ground as well as a measurement does. If so, the convention/verdict boundary is wrong.

### F4 — The reconciled term carries a scope bond; a flat list of features cannot do the work

Fig 12 :5: *"A set of ingredients is not one. The bond is the rest of the information, and it is the half nothing was recording."*

Fig 12 :94: *"The arrangement is the substance."*

Fig 12 :111: *"A vocabulary without bonds can name a state and cannot name a route to one."*

Fig 12 :112: *"A plan is a sequence. A goal is a comparison. Progress is a subtraction."*

The everyday term and the official term share ingredients. What differs is the bond to a question and a context. A vocabulary that records only the ingredients, "tomato, fruit, vegetable", cannot record which term is in force. So reconciliation is a bonding operation, not a new ingredient and not an average.

**Triangulated** across Fig 12 :5, :94 and Fig 4 :19-20 (only the cast changes with size). **Refuter:** show a vocabulary of bare ingredients that reliably selects the correct term in every context without any scope marker. If it can, the bond claim is false.

### F5 — This is a union, so unbundle before searching; do not treat it as one harder question

Fig 9 :54: *"And when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again."*

Fig 9 :57: *"a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either."*

Fig 9 :60: *"Use filters for the budget and witnesses for the verdict. Never let a filter hand you a verdict."*

D1, D2 and D3 overlap on the same object but keep different anchors. Trying to solve "the category conflict" as one problem is the bundled error. What survives unbundling is categorically different: three per-slot questions with three grounds.

**Triangulated** across Fig 9 :54-57 and the domain census's anchor test. **Refuter:** show that one lever resolves all three conditions, including the institutional decision, without changing any of the three anchors. That would make it a partition, not a union.

### F6 — The route for a conflict is refit or refuse before mint; a new category is not automatically owed

Fig 5 :13: *"mis-attached: refit what you have, do not invent"*

Fig 5 :41: *"Any one of them at zero stops the mint entirely. Which is why a system that is minting nothing may have nothing wrong with its minting."*

Fig 5 :26: *"does the new term cost less to state than the confusion it removes?"*

Most everyday/official conflicts are mis-attached bindings, not missing mechanisms. The term exists in both frames; what is wrong is which frame it is bound to in which context. Minting a third public category is a possible answer but must pay the MDL bargain and pass all three guards as a product.

**Single-source for the bin assignment** (Fig 5 and the formula ROUTE step share an evidence pool). **Refuter:** show a conflict where no rebinding of existing terms pays and a new term is required by the ground. That routes it to BROKEN-mechanism rather than BROKEN-rebinding.

### F7 — An import between frames needs a source gate and a shadow test

Fig 6 :23: *"No frame escapes its own closure. The bound moves only on contact with a frame whose closure differs."*

Fig 6 :30: *"The description travels. Fails by being sharpened into terms only we can read. Cast it in effect terms."*

Fig 8 :50-52: *"Convergent derivation and adopted import look identical in the contents. Only the record of where each came from separates them, and the surplus lives only where the closures differ."*

When a scientific term enters everyday language, or a bureaucratic term enters a scientific report, the record of where it came from is the only thing that separates an import from a convergence. The agreeable surface of the term is not evidence.

**Triangulated** across Fig 6 and Fig 8. **Refuter:** show two frames with identical closures producing a surplus term, or an import with no provenance that is still distinguishable from convergence by its content alone.

### F8 — What the framework cannot give is the cognitive mechanism

Fig 9 :32: *"you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself"*

Fig 13 :133: *"A system with a closure can answer that before it searches, and what it returns is a shape rather than a name."*

Fig 13 :134: *"Unreachable is a verdict here, not a silence"*

Fig 8 :39: *"So a search that finds nothing is never a proof of absence."*

No figure line states which psychological process a person uses to switch frames. Code switching, theory change, prototype adjustment and accommodation are world hypotheses. The figures constrain the shape any successful process must have: separate anchors, per-slot residual, scope bond, recorded provenance. They do not select among the candidate processes. This is an honest `gap` at the ground level, **unreached, not unreachable**. It is not a failure to be hidden.

**Single-source for the limit**, triangulated weakly with the canonical figures README's phrase *"a shape, not a name"* (same author, so one pool). **Refuter:** a figure line that names a mechanism for frame routing would settle this and close the gap.

### F9 — The same kernel runs at one person and at a thousand, but a frozen rule is only a recording

Fig 4 :22: *"Only methods cross up. Only head starts come down."*

Fig 4 :25: *"A recording carried upward looks like knowledge and is a description of one occasion."*

Fig 11 :40: *"An improvement that does not change contact changes nothing, however much it improves."*

An institution that copies one person's successful classification into a rule has carried up a recording. It looks like knowledge and is a description of one occasion. The channel to the thing the rule is about then decays, and nothing internal reports it. This is why a reconciliation that only changes vocabulary changes nothing.

**Triangulated** across Fig 4 and Fig 11. **Refuter:** show a recording carried upward that keeps its contact with the ground without any maintained channel, which would break the asymmetry.

### F10 — Filters are not witnesses

Fig 2 :42: *"Buy independence in proportion to the cost of being wrong."*

Fig 10 :42: *"A verifier that can reconstruct the claim cannot verify it."*

Fig 10 :52: *"Bring a characterised residual, never the source."*

Outreach on this question must ask for a refutation of the per-slot shape, not for agreement with the framework. A recipient who has absorbed the framework before giving a verdict has joined the frame being checked.

**Triangulated** across Fig 2 and Fig 10. **Refuter:** show that prior agreement from a shared pool can substitute for an independent check of the shape claim.

## 5. Pre-registered refuter

Written before the conclusion, per Seat Map :287: *"a falsifier written before the run is a design instrument, not only an honesty device."*

The shape claim in section 0 is refuted if any of the following holds:

1. One anchor is shown to settle "good for this person" and "true of the world" without loss on either (breaks F1, Fig 2 :36-38).
2. A person is shown to fuse two category systems into one flat term that pays in every context with no per-slot residual and no scope marker (breaks F4 and Fig 1 :19).
3. A bureaucratic rule is shown to decide an outcome, to be unchecked against anything the decision is about, and still to track the ground as well as a measurement (breaks F3, Fig 10 :49).
4. Agreement from one shared evidence pool is shown to settle a scientific classification with the instrument held fixed (breaks F2, Fig 2 :40).
5. A figure line is found that names the cognitive mechanism of frame routing (closes F8, which would be welcome rather than a defeat).

If any of 1 to 4 fires, the shape is wrong and is withdrawn. Until one is run, no part of this may be labelled `grounded`.

## 6. Route check, asked in the formula's order

- **SEARCHED (cheapest, asked first).** Is the answer already in the closure of the everyday, scientific and bureaucratic vocabularies held here? Yes at the level of shape: Fig 2, Fig 9 and Fig 12 already yield separate anchors, unbundling and a scope bond. No at the level of mechanism: F8. So more search of the same figures will not close the mechanism question, and importing a psychology textbook is not automatically owed.
- **INWARD.** Extend the instrument. The instrument here would be a task that presents the same object under conflicting category demands and records which term the person uses, with the anchor for each demand stated in advance. No such measurement was taken in this run. Fig 6 :32: an instrument is improved from a worse instrument already returning something.
- **OUTWARD.** Import. Characterise R in effect terms first, then find a frame whose closure predicts its shape. Candidate external frames are concept theory, folk taxonomy, legal classification and library classification, all read in `09_watson_answer.md`. They corroborate the shape and none supplies the cognitive mechanism. They are world sources, so they cannot overrule the figures.

The three are kept distinct. No filter was allowed to hand over a verdict.
