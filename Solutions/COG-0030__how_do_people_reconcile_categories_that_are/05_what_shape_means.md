## 1. A shape is a constraint, not a filler

From *The Library of Babel*: *"This means that you can cut your search short if the set of books returned is small and descriptive enough that it provides a general shape of the answer that satisfies your question."*

So a **shape** is the narrowed form of an answer you can state before you can name the mechanism. For this question the shape is: separate anchors, per-object residual, a scope bond, recorded provenance. That is a set of constraints. It is not an answer to "which cognitive process does a person use".

`SPACE(???)TIME` was the exemplar's shape. The analogue here is `everyday term [scope: ?] official term`, with the slot to be filled by a ground or a provenanced import, not by the framework.

## 2. How the shape was obtained

- *Library of Babel*: describe the residual in effect terms, use the vocabulary already held that is closest, and bootstrap by proximity. With each new term the space narrows.
- Fig 6 :30 *"The description travels. Fails by being sharpened into terms only we can read. Cast it in effect terms."* I described the conflict as a per-object failure of a prediction, not as a dispute over the word "vegetable".
- Fig 9 :30-31 *"your words are borrowed too, so describe the effect rather than the cause"* The effect is: the same object needs a different term in a different setting.
- *Library of Babel* on bundling: *"Perhaps your problem was a bundle or conflation of several problems masquerading as one? You will need to search all of those problems individually to find their answers and then combine them in a satisfactory way."* This is the direct warrant for F5 in `01_figure_grounded_audit.md`.

## 3. What licenses a name

Only two things, both outside the shape itself:

1. **The ground settles it.** `THE_FORMULA.md` step 5: *"the ground settles it. Verified only by what pays. A gate is not the ground."* Here the ground would be a task that presents the same object under conflicting category demands and records which term the person uses, with each demand's anchor stated in advance. No such task was run.
2. **A provenanced import from a frame whose closure differs**, with a source gate and a shadow test (Fig 6 :23, Fig 8 :50-52). A theory from cognitive science can be imported as a candidate mechanism, but it must be recorded as an import, not presented as a figure derivation.

*The Hard Problem is About Source* sharpens the second point. Metamerism: an instrument reads green and *"it cannot comment on the source"*. Several sources can give the same reading. The reading does not determine the source, and *"An instrument that can't distinguish these cases demonstrates its own inadequacy"*. Applied here: observing that a person *uses* the everyday term in a kitchen and the scientific term in a lab does not tell you *why* they switched. Several mechanisms could produce that behavior. What is owed is a discriminating instrument, not a name for the mechanism.

## 4. The specific error this question invites

The question uses one verb, "reconcile", for three different operations:

| operation | ground | figure verdict |
|---|---|---|
| keep a useful everyday term | human judgment about a person | efficient convention, Fig 2 :41 |
| decide what the world contains | measurement | verdict, Fig 2 :40 |
| make an official decision | the thing the decision is about, not the rule itself | convention that has crossed into verdict, Fig 2 :43 |

*Decoding Tacit Knowledge* states the asymmetry directly: *"Agreement is the whole point when you're settling what words mean. It's worthless for settling what's true when both sides learned from the same sources."* So the error is not choosing the wrong category. It is using one kind of agreement to settle a question of the other kind. This is the metamerism error at the level of grounds: one word, three sources, and an instrument that reads them all as "agreement".

## 5. What shape means for this question, answered

**What licenses a particular reconciliation mechanism?** Nothing in the framework. The framework returns a shape. A particular mechanism is licensed only by a measurement that discriminates among mechanisms, or by a provenanced import with a shadow test. Until one of those, the mechanism is the searcher's own contribution and must be recorded as such.

**Does this solution commit the error the README forbids?** It does not name a mechanism. It does bind three grounds that are already in the figure set, which is permitted: Fig 2 :36-38 licenses human judgment for a question about people, and Fig 2 :29-30 licenses a measurement for a question about the world. What remains unproven is whether any particular population actually uses the scope-bond shape. That is why the answer is `general` on the shape and `specified` only on the three per-domain verdicts.

## 5b. Correction to my own section 3

I first wrote that naming any component of the reconciliation is forbidden by "shape, not a name". That is too broad. The README's phrase governs a *missing* primitive. The three grounds here are not new primitives; they are already distinguished by Fig 2. Naming them is a binding from the figure set, not the invention of a new term. What remains forbidden is naming the cognitive mechanism, which is genuinely missing from Γ. Both readings are recorded so neither is quietly lost.

## 6. Two consequences for outreach

**Prestige is not action.** From *The Recursive Self-Improvement Trap*: *"Prestige can fund an action. It can give an action a budget to be tried. But it cannot settle an action. Only the ground can settle it."* A campaign that gathers famous names to agree that the shape is elegant is the named collapse. The legitimate ask is a budget to try the shape against a ground.

**My own audit is a simulation, not a ground reading.** Same essay: *"the output is only as good as the validator. The agent, or the tool, or the frame, or the theory, cannot validate itself."* And: *"the truly testable output would need to be derived from the real architecture or working the formula yourself."* So `01_figure_grounded_audit.md` is a simulated derivation checked for internal consistency against the figures. It is not a test of the claim, and it cannot settle anything it reports on.
