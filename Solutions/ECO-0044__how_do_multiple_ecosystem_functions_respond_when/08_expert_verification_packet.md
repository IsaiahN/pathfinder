# Expert verification packet - multiple ecosystem functions under biodiversity decline

**What this is:** a claim in a form an ecologist or biodiversity scientist can check. Mode, derivation and refuters are stated before the conclusion, so a reader knows what to look for and what would settle it.

**Mode: `specified` on the shape claims, `gap` on the universal response curve. Not `grounded`.** No per-function measurement is claimed. This is a shape and a route, not a result. **Standing: a simulated derivation checked for internal consistency against the figures, not a ground reading. It cannot settle the claim it reports on.**

## 1. The claim, in one paragraph

When biodiversity declines, multiple ecosystem functions do not respond as one quantity. Each function is its own residual slot with its own non-updating measured rate, and the per-function verdicts stay separate and are never averaged into a single multifunctionality score. There is no standing cross-ecosystem list of robust and sensitive functions, because an actor's effect is relative to the function being tracked. The response is a union of overlapping conditions, including species identities, interaction bonds, habitat conditions and third-order cascades. The interaction arrangement carries information the species list does not, so two communities with the same species and different links are isomers with different function rates. The decline-to-response arrow is ordered while a pooled richness association is symmetric, and management can add but not subtract habitat contact. The universal response curve is a declared gap.

## 2. Three checkable sub-claims

### C1 - per-slot indexing; checkable by reading Fig 1 and by inspecting a multifunctionality dataset

- **Statement:** the response must be read per function. A pooled index cannot preserve what happens to each slot at the same resolution.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 1 :19 - *"The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated."* Fig 1 :22 - *"Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average."* Fig 1 :28 - *"The gap is measured per object, not per step. A system that averages across objects has thrown away the only signal that says where to look."*
- **What to check:** in a dataset you know, did the multifunctionality index and the individual functions move in the same direction? Bradford et al. 2014 (PNAS 111: 14478-14483) is a worked case where the indices rose consistently while three of five processes were neutral or negative. If your data behave that way, the per-slot claim survives. If the index preserves sign, ranking and causal direction of every slot at the same resolution, it does not.
- **Refuter (R1):** a single index shown to preserve the sign, ranking and causal direction of every per-function reading at the same resolution.

### C2 - union structure and residual-relative valence; checkable by reading Fig 9 and Fig 11

- **Statement:** the functions form a union of overlapping conditions with an unassignable shared fraction, and no standing list of sensitive functions survives a change of the tracked function.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 9 :55 - *"A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones."* Fig 9 :57 - *"a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either"* Fig 11 :49 - *"An actor's valence is relative to the residual, not a property it carries."* Fig 11 :51 - *"No standing list of good actors survives a change of residual."*
- **What to check:** does a single lever clear the response in your system, or do intersections remain? Hector and Bagchi 2007 (Nature 448: 188-190) found only 0.2 to 0.5 proportional overlap among the species influencing different functions. Soliveres et al. 2016 (Nature 536: 456-459) found on average three trophic groups influencing each service. Does any proposed ranking of functions stay fixed when you change which function you track?
- **Refuters (R2, R4):** a cross-ecosystem standing list derived from the figures alone and surviving a change of residual; or a positive index-level effect with no neutral or negative slot anywhere.

### C3 - bonds and order; checkable by reading Fig 12 and the Operators table

- **Statement:** the interaction arrangement is the bond that carries the information a species list omits, and the response arrow is ordered while a symmetric richness association cannot choose it.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 12 :5 - *"A set of ingredients is not one. The bond is the rest of the information, and it is the half nothing was recording."* Fig 12 :94 - *"The arrangement is the substance."* Fig 12 :117 - *"isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."* Operators table :34 - *"the only bond a flat chain can express, so every term today is a sequence whether or not it should be"*
- **What to check:** do two communities with the same species list but different interaction networks show different function rates? Albert et al. 2026 (Nature Communications 17: 5787) found network structure often had stronger direct effects than tree species richness and affected 73.8% of network-index and function combinations. Does a pooled cross-sectional association in your data establish the direction of decline, or only co-occurrence?
- **Refuter (R3):** species identities alone predict each function's response at least as well as a model including the bonds. A related live type question: if only symmetric associations are available, the ordered causal form is refused and only the shape stands.

## 3. What is explicitly not claimed

- Not a universal response curve, slope, threshold diversity value or cross-ecosystem ordering of functions. That is a `gap` (Fig 9 :32; Fig 13 :134).
- Not that biodiversity loss always reduces every function. The sign of each slot is a ground reading, not a figure output.
- Not that a multifunctionality index is useless. It is a filter for the budget, not a witness for the verdict (Fig 9 :60).
- Not a standing list of sensitive functions (Fig 11 :51).
- Not that the six-term physical substrate is a list of ecosystem functions.
- Not `grounded`. This is a simulated derivation and cannot settle the claim it reports on.
- Not an empirical result from any of the cited studies. Those studies are secondary corroboration only.

## 5. What would settle it

A per-function, source-discriminating instrument, not a further argument. From *The Hard Problem is About Source*: a reading that cannot comment on its source *"demonstrates its own inadequacy"*. Here:

1. For each function, is the response read separately with its own rate and uncertainty, rather than through an index?
2. Does the design distinguish identity, bond arrangement, habitat condition and third-order cascade as sources of the same reading?
3. Is there an ordered instrument (before/after, exogenous decline) so the arrow is observed rather than assumed from a symmetric association?
4. Does a threshold-like collapse appear at a function slot, or only in the aggregate?
5. Does any proposed function ordering survive changing which function is tracked?

## 6. What an expert can check in twenty minutes

1. Read Fig 1 :19, :22 and Fig 12 :5, :94 - four short passages.
2. Name one dataset you know where the index and an individual function moved in different directions, or say that you know of none.
3. Say whether your system's response needs a bond term beyond the species list, and what instrument would show it.
4. Name one function whose apparent sensitivity changes when you switch to a different function as the tracked residual.
5. Send the refutation. A failure is more useful than an endorsement. The two-stage rule applies: this packet is stage 1, and framework material is offered only after a verdict is recorded.
