**Subject:** One checkable claim about how several ecosystem functions respond to diversity loss

> Hi [name],
>
> I have one specific output I would like you to check against what you already know. I am not asking you to read the framework first, and I am not asking you to agree with it. If the output does not survive, there is nothing else to look at.
>
> **The output (`specified`, not `grounded`):** when biodiversity declines, the figures return a per-function shape, not a single response. Each ecosystem function is its own residual slot with its own measured rate, and the verdicts stay separate. Fig 1 :19 says *"The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated."* Fig 1 :22 says *"Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average."* The response is a union of overlapping conditions (species identities, interaction bonds, habitat conditions, cascades), not a partition into a resilient set and a fragile set. And there is no standing list of functions that respond first: Fig 11 :51 says *"No standing list of good actors survives a change of residual."*
>
> **What I am not claiming:** I am not claiming a universal response curve, a threshold diversity value, or an ordering of functions that holds across ecosystems. I am not claiming that any particular function is always the sensitive one. I am not claiming that a pooled multifunctionality index is useless: it is an efficient filter for deciding where to look. I am claiming it is the wrong instrument for deciding what happened to each function.
>
> **What would refute the shape claim:**
> 1. Show that a single multifunctionality index preserves the sign, the ranking and the causal direction of every per-function reading at the same resolution as those readings.
> 2. Produce a cross-ecosystem standing list of robust and sensitive functions that survives a change of which function is being tracked.
> 3. Show that species identities alone, with no interaction-arrangement term, predict each function's response at least as well as a model that includes the bonds.
> 4. Show that the positive index-level diversity effect holds in the same direction for every function, with no neutral or negative slot anywhere in the data.
>
> I wrote these down before sending this. If any one holds, the shape claim is wrong and I will retract it. A one-line "this fails because..." is more useful to me than agreement. If you would rather not engage, no reply is needed.
>
> **If it survives, and only then:** sources are below. The figures are the authority, not my summary of them.
> - Hector and Bagchi 2007, *Biodiversity and ecosystem multifunctionality*, Nature 448: 188-190: https://www.nature.com/articles/nature05947
> - Bradford et al. 2014, *Discontinuity in the responses of ecosystem processes and multifunctionality*, PNAS 111: 14478-14483, open text: https://europepmc.org/articles/PMC4210050
> - Manning et al. 2018, *Redefining ecosystem multifunctionality*, Nature Ecology & Evolution 2: 427-436: https://www.nature.com/articles/s41559-017-0461-7
