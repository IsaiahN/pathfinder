# Isomers, functional groups and the order of the response

**Mode: `specified` on the structural claims, `general` on the analogy. This is a simulated derivation checked against the figures, not a ground reading (see `05_what_shape_means.md` section 7).**

## RESIDUAL

The question asks how multiple functions respond. Three supporting questions are owed: (1) are two communities with the same species set but different interaction bonds isomers, (2) is a function a functional group, and (3) is the response arrow ordered or symmetric? A fourth concerns the two currencies in which the answer is priced.

## DOMAIN CENSUS

Atoms held: the function slots enumerated for a site (for example primary productivity, nutrient cycling, litter decomposition, herbivory, as in Albert et al. 2026), the species and functional groups present, and the habitat conditions. Bonds held: the seven, especially + (symmetric), → (sequence), ⇒ (production) and ⋛ (comparison). No new primitive is available without import from a trait, network or experimental frame. No figure line returns a response curve.

## DERIVATION

### Q1 - Are two communities with the same species set but different bonds isomers?

- Fig 12 :5: *"A set of ingredients is not one. The bond is the rest of the information, and it is the half nothing was recording."*
- Fig 12 :94: *"The arrangement is the substance."*
- Fig 12 :117: **"isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."**
- Two communities over the identical species list whose pollination, predation and competition links differ therefore satisfy the figure's definition: same atoms, different bond, different function rates. The species list alone cannot tell them apart, exactly as the flat formula cannot tell melt from freeze (Fig 12 :12-17).
- World check: Albert et al. 2026, opened 2026-11-23, found that *"ecosystem service provisioning does not only rely on biodiversity across trophic levels, but to a similar degree on how species interact"* and that network structure frequently had stronger direct effects than species richness. `specified`, triangulated for the figure definition; world-facing model comparison still unrun.
- **Falsifier, written before the run:** if a community difference that appears to be a bond difference can always be restated as a change in the atom set (a species added, lost or replaced, or a new function measured), then the isomer claim fails and the relation is composition plus import instead. The claim stands only over a fixed atom set at a fixed site and time.

### Q2 - Is a function a functional group?

- Fig 12 :123: **"functional group. A substructure whose presence fixes the outcome. The group says what kind; the qualifier says which member."**
- A function such as pollination or decomposition is the group: a substructure whose presence fixes an outcome class. The species performing it are the members; the qualifier says which member. This is why a species can be a member of several groups and why the loss of one member does not necessarily remove the function.
- It also explains the redundancy result without turning it into a standing list. Hector and Bagchi 2007 found only 0.2 to 0.5 overlap among the species sets affecting different functions; that is a result about members across groups, not about a fixed rank of groups.
- Fig 11 :51: *"No standing list of good actors survives a change of residual."* The group membership is relative to the function tracked.
- **Falsifier:** if across several ecosystems a named function has no recurrent substructure, with entirely different mechanisms and no shared qualifier, the functional-group reading fails and the function must be treated as a site-specific label rather than a kind.

### Q3 - Is the response arrow ordered, and can a pooled association choose it?

- Operators table :34: *"the only bond a flat chain can express, so every term today is a sequence whether or not it should be"*
- Fig 12 :71-74: sequence is the ordered bond whose test is *"swap them. Does it become a different thing? Yes."* Conjunction + is symmetric: *"swap the operands. Does the meaning change? No."*
- A cross-sectional association between species richness and a multifunctionality index is a symmetric co-occurrence, +. The question's wording *"respond when biodiversity declines"* is ordered: → or ⇒. No symmetric reading of the same data can choose the direction. What is owed is an instrument that supplies the order: a before/after measurement, an exogenous diversity manipulation, or a design where the decline precedes the function reading.
- *How Intelligence Actually Works* (CARRIED) makes the same admission at the level of the six-term derivation: *"we cannot say which state composed first, and we don't know if the order mattered."* The response here is likewise a route, not a state.
- Bradford et al. 2014 is the cautionary case: the index effect and the per-process effects had different signs, so the pooled association did not even preserve the ranking, let alone the arrow.
- **Falsifier:** if a study supplies only symmetric associations and no ordered instrument, the causal form of the claim is refused and only the shape survives. This is a live type question, not a hidden one (strain S5 in `01`).

### Q4 - The two currencies: a cheap index and costly per-function settlement

- Fig 12 :125: *"A composition costs in two currencies, and they do not add."*
- Fig 12 :126: *"Bits price the description. Actions price finding out whether it holds."*
- A multifunctionality index is cheap in bits: one number, easy to state and compare. Settling each function's response is expensive in actions: one instrument and one ground reading per slot. Summing the two into one score is the error the figure names, not the fix.
- Fig 12 :128-129: *"A long chain of cheap parts is expensive to describe and may cost one action to refute. A short arrangement over a costly operand is cheap to state and may take a whole budget to settle."* An index over 34 functions is cheap to state and can take a whole campaign to settle slot by slot.
- World check: Albert et al. 2026 combined 34 function measures into a Hill-number index that accounts for correlations between functions, which is a deliberate choice of description cost. Manning et al. 2018 argues for two levels, function and service, which is two objects each with its own price.
- **Falsifier:** if a single number can be shown to settle every slot at the same resolution and cost, the two-currency claim fails. This is refuter R1 in `01`.

### Q5 - Is insurance a new term or a time-channel composition?

- Fig 13 :28-32: the remainder of each turn re-enters the next; *"a remainder compounding through a chain that amplifies is chaos, and the detail a crossing discards reappears above it as noise."*
- Asynchrony and temporal complementarity are a time-channel composition of the same function slots, not a seventh primitive. Fig 13 :115: *"The set is closed. The arrangements are not."*
- World check: Albert et al. 2026 discusses niche overlap enabling species to *"offset performance decreases of other species occupying similar niches (functional similarity leading to insurance effects)"* and asynchrony as temporal complementarity. `specified`, single-source on the framework side for the composition, with world corroboration.
- **Falsifier:** if asynchrony can be derived from a species count alone with no time-channel composition and no interaction term, the insurance reading collapses into a richness proxy and the figure adds nothing.

## CONCLUSION

Over a fixed atom set, two communities with the same species list but different interactions are Fig 12 isomers: same atoms, different bond, different substance. A function is a Fig 12 functional group whose members are species, and that membership is relative to the tracked function. The response itself is ordered while a pooled richness association is symmetric, so an ordered instrument is owed and a pooled association cannot supply it. The index is cheap in bits and per-function settlement is expensive in actions, and the two costs do not add. Insurance is a time-channel composition, not a new primitive. Every claim here carries a falsifier; none is `grounded`.
