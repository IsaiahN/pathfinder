## 0. The question under audit, stated as an effect

The source question is: *"How do multiple ecosystem functions respond when biodiversity declines?"* (ecology.txt line 55, section 5, Ecosystem functions and services).

Stated in effect terms: after a decline in the actor set and the interaction arrangement of a community, what changes in the measured rate of each function, which slots carry the residual, and does one lever clear the response or several overlapping conditions? The two candidate readings are:

- **A (aggregate reading):** the functions respond together as one multifunctionality quantity; one curve, one slope, one verdict.
- **B (per-slot reading):** each function is its own residual slot with its own anchor; the response is a union of overlapping conditions, and there is no standing list of which functions respond first.

What each leads to: A produces a single number that is cheap to state and easy to communicate, but it discards the sign and ranking of the individual slots (Fig 1 :22; Bradford et al. 2014). B produces a vector of per-function verdicts, a union structure, and a declared gap where no figure line governs. B is more expensive to settle and is the only one the figures license as a `specified` claim.

**Direction discipline.** The shape being searched for is made an ordinary term before the run, as `THE_FORMULA.md` "Direction" requires. The sought-for shape is: *a per-slot vector of function residuals, indexed by function, with a union of overlapping conditions, no standing ordering, and a refuter*. It is bound here and will accrue a residual like anything else. This is the discipline the exemplar uses when it writes `SPACE(???)TIME` instead of silently wanting an answer of a particular form.

**R by arity, symmetry, scale** (Fig 9 :22 - *"arity · symmetry · scale"*):

- **Arity:** many. "Multiple" is not decoration. The residual is indexed by function slot s, not summed (Fig 1 :19).
- **Symmetry:** the response relation is ordered. A decline in diversity precedes or produces a change in a function; a correlation between richness and an index is symmetric. A pooled association cannot choose the arrow, because + is symmetric while → and ⇒ are not (Fig 12 :71-74; Operators table :34).
- **Scale:** within a plot and a trophic group (first order), across trophic levels and habitats through cascades (second order), and across seasons and years through asynchrony and insurance (Fig 4 :19-20; Fig 11 :52-54). Each scale has its own anchor; a verdict at one scale is not a verdict at another.

**Partition or union.** D1, D2 and D3 are not a partition of one question. They are a **union of overlapping conditions** with an unassignable shared fraction: the same measured rate is read in D1, produced in D2 and valued in D3. Fig 9 :55: *"A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones."* Fig 9 :57: *"a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either"* it is a specific compound. Within D2 the conditions themselves form a union: a function can be influenced by plant richness, soil fauna and land use at once, and one lever clears the region where only one condition holds while leaving the intersections standing (Soliveres et al. 2016; Albert et al. 2026).

**Order by dependency** (Fig 3 :20 - *"The chain is ordered by dependency, not by importance"*):

1. Perceive each function with an instrument (D1 perception link).
2. Have terms for what each function affords and say what counts as progress in it (D1 vocabulary and objective). This is the middle link Fig 3 :25-27 names as the one with no instrument.
3. Index the residual by function slot and refuse to average (Fig 1 :19, :22).
4. Enumerate the habitat, including second-order and third-order contact (D2; Fig 11 :43-44, :52-54).
5. Build the union compound per function, keeping verdicts separate (Fig 9 :54-57).
6. Only then let D3 act, by introduction rather than subtraction (Fig 11 :61-63).

Effort spent above the break cannot rescue a link below it (Fig 3 :19). A better multifunctionality index cannot rescue a missing per-function instrument.

## 4. Findings

### F1 - The response is indexed by function, not by one average (decisive, `specified`, triangulated)

Fig 1 :19: *"The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated."* Fig 1 :22: *"Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average."* Fig 1 :28: *"The gap is measured per object, not per step. A system that averages across objects has thrown away the only signal that says where to look."*

Symbols table :38-40: *"It is the aim, not only the error signal, and it is always a slice: what cannot be perceived yet is residual and simply cannot be read."*

The effect reading is therefore: each ecosystem function is a slot s with its own residual R_s = |prediction_s - observation_s|. A global index near zero with one live function slot is a legal state, exactly as Fig 1 :20 permits for object slots. Asking "how do multiple functions respond" and answering with one number is the named error: it throws away the signal that says where to look.

**Strain test.** Fig 7 :14 *"blending: novelty swamped to the mean"* and Fig 10 :25 *"stale percepts, dropped transients, saturated metrics, pooled readings"* were tried against the same quantity. They fit without strain: an index is a pooled reading. No re-census needed.

**World corroboration (low overlap).** Hector and Bagchi 2007 (Nature 448: 188-190), opened 2026-11-23: *"appreciable differences in the sets of species influencing different ecosystem functions, with average proportional overlap of about 0.2 to 0.5."* Bradford et al. 2014 (PNAS 111: 14478-14483), abstract via Europe PMC, opened 2026-11-23: *"only two of the five ecosystem processes also responded positively to increasing complexity, whereas the other three responded neutrally or negatively."* The statistical analogue, Simpson's paradox, opened via Wikipedia 2026-11-23: *"a trend appears in several groups of data but disappears or reverses when the groups are combined."*

**Refuter (R1).** A single index preserves sign, ranking and causal direction of every per-function reading at the same resolution. Not run here.

**Verdict:** `specified`, **triangulated** across Fig 1 (framework primary) and two independently produced low-overlap witnesses (empirical ecology and statistics). The framework and the ecology share no evidence pool, so the agreement carries weight; the two ecology sources share the BEF literature and count as one pool against the statistics source.

### F2 - No standing list of robust or sensitive functions (`specified`, triangulated)

Fig 11 :49: *"An actor's valence is relative to the residual, not a property it carries."* Fig 11 :50: *"The same actor is a benefactor to one residual and a malfactor to another, so mutualism and parasitism are relations rather than kinds."* Fig 11 :51: *"No standing list of good actors survives a change of residual."*

Transfer to this question: a species, trait or functional group is not "good for multifunctionality" in itself. Its valence depends on which function is the residual. A grazers' effect on primary productivity and its effect on nutrient turnover are two different readings of the same actor. The question "which functions respond first" therefore cannot be answered with a standing ranking that survives a change of tracked function. Bradford et al. 2014 put the same point in ecology's own terms: *"species that are redundant for one ecosystem process under a given set of environmental conditions play a distinct role under different conditions or in the provision of another ecosystem process."*

**Strain test.** Fig 12 :123 *"functional group. A substructure whose presence fixes the outcome. The group says what kind; the qualifier says which member."* was tried against F2. At first it looked like a standing category. Re-census: Fig 11 governs actor valence relative to a residual; Fig 12 governs arrangement kinds. Different objects. The group says what kind of function is present, not which member species is universally beneficial. No strain after re-census; see S2.

**Refuter (R2).** A cross-ecosystem standing list of robust and sensitive functions derived from the figures alone, surviving a change of residual. Not produced.

**Verdict:** `specified`, **triangulated** (Fig 11 plus the Hector and Bagchi 2007 overlap result, low overlap between framework and ecology).

### F3 - The response is a union of overlapping conditions, not a partition (`specified`, triangulated)

Fig 9 :4-5: *"Disagreements that do not shrink with effort, where each rule keeps working well on a different subset, are not one hard question but several well-formed ones. Split it rather than search."* Fig 9 :54: *"when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again"* Fig 9 :55: *"A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones."* Fig 9 :57: *"a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either"*

Fig 11 :45-47 supplies the census items: *"actors: what acts on it, and what it acts on"*, *"conditions: light, water, temperature, season, terrain"*, *"relations: pollination, predation, competition, dependency"*. These overlap. A single lever, for example adding a dominant species, clears the region where only one condition holds and leaves the intersections standing because the second condition still binds.

World corroboration: Soliveres et al. 2016 (Nature 536: 456-459), opened 2026-11-23: *"On average, three trophic groups influenced each ecosystem service, with each trophic group influencing at least one service."* Albert et al. 2026 (Nature Communications 17: 5787), opened 2026-11-23: *"Most indices of network structure had consistently stronger direct effects on ecosystem multifunctionality than tree species richness."*

**Refuter (R4).** A positive index-level effect with no neutral or negative slot anywhere. The Bradford 2014 result already fires against this: three of five processes were neutral or negative. The refuter is live and not fired in the sense that would defeat the claim; it is evidence for it.

**Verdict:** `specified`, **triangulated** across Fig 9 and Fig 11 (two figures with low content overlap: one about question structure, one about habitat contact), plus ecology.

### F4 - Bonds carry what counts do not (`specified`, triangulated)

Fig 12 :5: *"A set of ingredients is not one. The bond is the rest of the information, and it is the half nothing was recording."* Fig 12 :94: *"The arrangement is the substance."* Fig 12 :111: *"A vocabulary without bonds can name a state and cannot name a route to one."*

A species list names a state of the community; it cannot name the route from that state to a function rate. The interaction arrangement is the bond. Fig 13 :115 *"The set is closed. The arrangements are not."* explains why adding more species counts cannot substitute for the arrangement.

World corroboration, low overlap with the framework: Albert et al. 2026 abstract, opened 2026-11-23: *"These findings emphasize that ecosystem service provisioning does not only rely on biodiversity across trophic levels, but to a similar degree on how species interact."* The same paper reports that network structure, not just network size, mediated tree-richness effects, and that *"the structure of individual network types was consistently associated with individual functions"*.

Isomer reading (developed in `06_isomers_and_order.md`): two communities over the same species set but with different interactions are Fig 12 :117 isomers, *"same atoms, different bond, different substance."* The falsifier is stated there.

**Refuter (R3).** Species identities alone predict each function at least as well as a model including bonds. Not run here; the Albert et al. result points the other way but a model comparison on the same data would settle it.

**Verdict:** `specified`, **triangulated** (Fig 12 plus an independent network-ecology result).

### F5 - A pooled index severs the channel while the ground does not decay (`specified`, triangulated)

Fig 10 :24: *"the ground does not decay, the channel does"* Fig 10 :25: *"stale percepts, dropped transients, saturated metrics, pooled readings"* Fig 10 :46: *"Each frame is corrected by it separately, and what makes the correction sound is that neither frame can move it."*

In this domain the ground is the per-function measured rate. It does not decay because an index was recomputed. What decays is the channel: a pooled reading, a saturated metric, a transient that is dropped because it did not move the average. Fig 1 :27: *"They are frame-internal. A frame cannot score itself with a quantity it also produces."* A multifunctionality index computed from the same function rates it is used to explain cannot validate the claim about those rates.

World corroboration: Bradford et al. 2014 described the positive index effect as *"a statistical fallacy analogous to Simpson's paradox (where aggregating data obscures causal relationships)"*; the reply title, recorded in Europe PMC metadata, is *"Aggregation can obscure understanding of ecosystem multifunctionality"*. Manning et al. 2018 (Nature Ecology & Evolution 2: 427-436), opened 2026-11-23, reports *"the redefinition of multifunctionality as a property that exists at two levels: ecosystem function multifunctionality and ecosystem service multifunctionality."* Two levels with two currencies is exactly what Fig 12 :125 *"A composition costs in two currencies, and they do not add."* would predict; the function rate and the service valuation do not add.

**Refuter (R1, shared with F1).** An index that preserves sign, ranking and direction at the same resolution. Not shown.

**Verdict:** `specified`, **triangulated** across Fig 10, Fig 1 and the ecology/statistics sources.

### F6 - Contact cascades: the residual may be three steps out (`specified`, triangulated)

Fig 11 :52-54: *"Scoping the habitat to direct contact is how DDT reached the eagles: first-order enumeration, second-order effect, and the residual that mattered was three steps out."* Fig 11 :44: *"List everything in contact with the residual, then what is in contact with those, and outward until the cascade stops mattering."*

A biodiversity decline acting on a predator can surface three steps away in nutrient dynamics or decomposition. Scoping the habitat to direct species-to-function links is first-order enumeration and will miss it. Fig 11 :39: *"capability is a property of agent-and-habitat, never of the agent alone"* and :40: *"An improvement that does not change contact changes nothing, however much it improves."* Adding species that never change contact with a function does not change that function.

World corroboration: Albert et al. 2026, opened 2026-11-23, states that the index accounts for *"effects of species interactions that cascade to functions via indirect pathways through higher-order interactions"*; Soliveres et al. 2016 found multitrophic richness more important than the richness of any single group.

**Refuter.** A result showing that direct species-to-function links account for all per-function residual mass, with zero second- or third-order contact contribution. Not shown.

**Verdict:** `specified`, **triangulated** (Fig 11 plus network ecology).

### F7 - Management is asymmetric and permanent work (`specified`, single-source on the framework side, with world corroboration)

Fig 11 :61: *"Introduction is available; exclusion is not."* Fig 11 :63: *"Which is why a managed ecosystem is permanent work rather than a solved state."* Fig 11 :66: *"Isolation is not removal of the habitat. It is substitution of one habitat for another."*

For D3 this means restoration and management can add actors, instruments and conditions but cannot subtract the habitat; the contact remains a fact about the world that does not consult the manager. A multifunctionality target is therefore a maintained state with a seat doing work, not a solved equilibrium. Manning et al. 2018 distinguishes ecosystem function multifunctionality from ecosystem service multifunctionality, which corroborates the two-level reading but does not derive the asymmetry.

**Refuter.** A management regime that demonstrates genuine subtraction of a habitat rather than substitution of one habitat for another, with the per-function contact reproduced unchanged. Not shown; Fig 11 :66 makes the predicted form of any apparent subtraction a substitution.

**Verdict:** `specified`, **single-source** on the framework side (Fig 11 alone governs the asymmetry), with world corroboration from Manning et al. 2018 for the two-level distinction only. Marked single-source, not upgraded.

### F8 - The universal response curve is a `gap`, not silence (`general` shape, `gap` on the curve)

No figure line returns a slope, a threshold diversity value, a critical diversity number, or an ordering of functions across ecosystems. Fig 9 :32: *"you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself"* Fig 13 :134: *"Unreachable is a verdict here, not a silence"* Fig 8 :39: *"So a search that finds nothing is never a proof of absence."*

What can be said is the shape: per-slot residuals, union structure, no standing list, bonds carry the arrangement, cascades go third order, and the management frame is asymmetric. What cannot be said is the numerical form. An empirical literature can supply a number for a given site and set of functions; that number is a binding from the world frame with provenance, not a figure-derived universal.
