# The Tether's answer: which species or ecological relationships are essential?

**Mode: `specified` on the per-domain verdicts, `general` on the structural shape, `gap` on the literal standing list. Not `grounded`.** This is a shape and a route, not a result. Simulated derivation checked against the figures (`05_what_shape_means.md` §6), not a ground reading. No ecological measurement was taken.

## 1. The residual it answers

The question asks which species or ecological relationships are essential for maintaining ecosystem functions. The residual is not "we lack a list." It is that different removal rules work on different functions and habitats, so the disagreement does not shrink with effort. Fig 9 :4-5 "First: is there a fact here? Disagreements that do not shrink with effort, where each rule keeps working well on a different subset, are not one hard question but several well-formed ones. Split it rather than search." The question is a union compound with its own slots and its own ground, not one hard problem (Fig 9 :55, :57).

## 2. The shape, per domain

**D1, the function slot.** The residual is indexed per function, never aggregated. Fig 1 :19 "The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated." Fig 1 :22 "Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average." Primary production, nutrient cycling, pollination, decomposition, carbon storage and resilience are different slots with different anchors. The ground for each is an independent measurement that does not move when a frame thinks otherwise (Fig 2 :29). Verdict: `specified`, triangulated (Fig 1 + Fig 2).

**D2, the habitat.** The habitat is enumerated, never composed. Fig 11 :42 "The habitat is enumerated, never composed." Fig 11 :46 "conditions: light, water, temperature, season, terrain." Fig 11 :47 "relations: pollination, predation, competition, dependency." The candidate set is read off the world, not derived from the question (Fig 11 :48), and a species selected before the function is described will seem to fit by construction (Fig 9 :27-28). Verdict: `specified`, triangulated (Fig 11 + Fig 9).

**D3, valence and the standing list.** Fig 11 :49 "An actor's valence is relative to the residual, not a property it carries." Fig 11 :51 "No standing list of good actors survives a change of residual." A free-floating list of essential species is a category error. "Essential" is always relative to a named function in a named habitat. Verdict: `specified` at the structural level, triangulated (Fig 11 + Fig 2 :36). The literal list is a `gap` (see section 4).

**D4, the cascade.** Fig 11 :52-53 "And contact cascades. Scoping the habitat to direct contact is how DDT reached the eagles: first-order enumeration, second-order effect, and the residual that mattered was three steps out." The essential relation can be several steps away; enumeration runs outward until the cascade stops mattering. Verdict: `specified`, triangulated (Fig 11 + Fig 3).

**D5, the test.** "Essential" is an ordered relation, not a correlation. Operators table: subtraction is removal, "A with B removed or absent"; comparison is a threshold, "a threshold relation between two quantities." A symmetric presence-function conjunction cannot choose the arrow. The evidence that licenses the word is a knock-out or exclusion result plus a stated threshold. Verdict: `specified`, single-source on the figure side (Operators table), corroborated in the world by Paine's removal experiment and by the engineering notion of a single point of failure, from low-overlap fields. Those sources are secondary; they do not raise the figure status to triangulated.

**D6, the intervention.** Fig 11 :61-62 "The seat's power is asymmetric. Introduction is available; exclusion is not. You can add an actor and you cannot subtract the habitat, because contact is a fact about the world and does not consult you." Fig 11 :68-69 "what you failed to reproduce is invisible until the goal fails" and "what you unintentionally introduced is invisible until it acts." Fig 11 :63-64 "Which is why a managed ecosystem is permanent work rather than a solved state." The answer must carry the two silent failure modes. Verdict: `specified`, triangulated (Fig 11 + Fig 6 import gate).

## 3. What the answer is, in one sequence

For each function slot: name the function and its non-updating measurement; enumerate the habitat outward (actors, relations, conditions); apply a removal or exclusion test with a threshold; trace the cascade at least two steps; record the two silent failure modes; keep the verdict for that slot; write the failure condition before the run. Over a fixed species set, communities with different bonds are isomers (Fig 12 :117), a pollinator or nitrogen-fixing module is a functional group (Fig 12 :123), and a persisting modifier is catalyst-like (Fig 12 :119). These are shapes any answer must have.

## 4. What it explicitly does not say

- Not a universal list of essential species. The framework returns a shape, not a name; the literal list is a `gap` (Fig 9 :32, Fig 13 :134).
- Not that charismatic or abundant species are essential. Conservation-bias sources show charisma funds attention without guaranteeing recovery; Fig 11 :51 refuses the standing list structurally.
- Not that any one species is essential "in itself." Fig 11 :39 "capability is a property of agent-and-habitat, never of the agent alone."
- Not that diversity itself is the answer. Under the insurance hypothesis a redundant species pool can reduce variance, so which species is essential depends on whether the slot is the mean or the variance.
- Not that a consensus of reviewers settles the question. Fig 2 :40 "agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool."
- Not `grounded`. No removal result or function measurement was taken in this derivation.

## 5. What would settle it - the ground

A removal or exclusion with a function measurement that does not update on what anyone thinks. The discriminating questions:

- Does the function drop below a stated threshold when the candidate actor or relation is removed, against a control in the same habitat?
- Does the same removal produce the same result for a different function? If not, the valence-relative verdict is confirmed at the ground.
- Is the effect first order, or does it appear three steps out as in the DDT case?
- Does the result survive substituting a habitat, and which actors came with the substitution?
- **Standing falsifiers from `01` section 4:** an invariant list across functions; intrinsic valence; no cascade; a correlation that determines essentialness; an agent-only improvement that moves the ground; a subtractable habitat.

What would move the verdict to `grounded` is an actual removal result plus a non-updating anchor, not more papers agreeing about a name.

## 6. The honest stop

**Unreached, not unreachable.** The per-function separator is deliverable now. The species names are not: naming requires enumeration and a removal result in a specific habitat, and no figure line supplies either (Fig 13 :134 "Unreachable is a verdict here, not a silence"). The habitat updates while the ground does not (Fig 11 :73), so a result in one habitat is a provenanced binding, not a universal law. The answer stops at the limit of the available instruments and records the lineage so the stop is checkable. "Indiscernibility is not identity."

## 7. One-paragraph version for outreach

> The Tether does not return a standing list of essential species. It says the question is a union of overlapping functions, so "essential" must be indexed per function and per habitat; that the habitat is enumerated from the world, never composed from a preferred shortlist; that valence is relative to the function, so the same actor can help one function and harm another; that "essential" means a removal and a threshold, not a correlation, and that the decisive effect can be three steps out; and that managing the answer is permanent work because you can add an actor but cannot subtract a habitat, with two silent failures. What it offers is the shape any answer must have, a failure condition written in advance, and the instruments that would close each slot. The species names stay open until a removal result at the ground settles them.
