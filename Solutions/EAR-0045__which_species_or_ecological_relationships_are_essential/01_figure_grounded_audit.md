## 0. The residual, in effect terms

The question asks which species or ecological relationships are essential for maintaining ecosystem functions. Restated as an effect, the question asks: for each function we care about, which removals cause that function to fail, so that we know what to protect? The word "essential" is a knock-out or subtraction relation, not a prestige ranking: if X is removed and function F stays, X was not essential for F under those conditions. The word "functions" is plural, so there are several slots, not one.

What an answer would look like, bound as an ordinary term per the formula's "Direction" step: a function-indexed list. For each function slot s, a set of actors and relations whose removal drops a measured function below a stated threshold, with the cascade traced outward from direct contact and the intervention's two silent failure modes named. The shape is a relation (a removal result), not a standing list of good species.

Option A (answer with a universal list of essential species) leads to a convention that survives a change of function and a change of habitat, which the figures forbid. Option B (answer with a function-indexed, habitat-enumerated, removal-tested separator) leads to a checkable claim with a pre-registered failure condition. The derivation below chooses B and records what it gives up.

## 1. Domain census (before any figure line is chosen)

**R by arity, symmetry, scale.** Arity: plural functions, so multiple slots. Fig 1 :19 "The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated" and Fig 1 :22 "Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average." A single "ecosystem health" score would hide the very signal the question seeks. Symmetry: asymmetric. A species essential for one function can be a malfactor for another (Fig 11 :49, :50). Correlation between presence and function is symmetric; "essential" is ordered. Scale: the local habitat, with influence cascading several steps out (Fig 11 :52-53) and across the scale membrane (Fig 4).

**One line per domain: actors, currency, anchor.** A domain needs a nameable anchor; where none can be named, it is not yet a domain.

| domain | actors | currency | anchor | question type |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| D1 The functions (the residual definition) | function slots: primary production, nutrient cycling, pollination, decomposition, carbon storage, resilience | a measured function level (a quantity) | an independent measurement that does not move when a frame changes its mind (Fig 2 :29) | per-slot partition into functions; the anchor is nameable per slot |
| D2 The habitat (actors and relations) | species, relations (pollination, predation, competition, dependency), conditions (light, water, temperature, season, terrain) | contact and effect on the function | the habitat read off the world, enumerated not composed (Fig 11 :42-48) | union of overlapping conditions (Fig 9 :55) |
| D3 The cascade (scale and indirect effect) | second- and third-order effects | influence travelling through relations | the same per-slot ground, reached indirectly | dependency chain outward (Fig 3) |
| D4 The intervention (management seat) | managers, restorers, engineers | introduction, never exclusion | the ground still decides (Fig 11 :20-22) | asymmetric seat (Fig 11 :61-62) |

**Partition or union:** union, not partition. "Species" and "relationships" overlap: a species matters through its relations, and a relation needs actors. "Functions" overlap: one species can support several functions and one function can need several species. Fig 9 :55 "A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones. A single lever clears the region where only one condition holds and leaves the intersections standing." Fig 9 :57 "a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either. It stops being a mixture and becomes a specific compound." So one lever (protect one iconic species) clears only the single-condition region and leaves the compound standing.

**Order by dependency (Fig 3):** D1 (name the function and its anchor) before D2 (enumerate the habitat) before D3 (trace the cascade) before D4 (choose an intervention). Fig 3 :19 "Optimising a link that already works cannot rescue one that does not." The chain usually breaks between vocabulary and objective: having terms for what a situation affords and being able to say what would count as progress. Fig 3 :27 "A link with no instrument is not a link that works. It is a link nobody has looked at." In this field the objective link is often unmeasured, so effort lands above the break (more species inventories) while "what counts as functioning" has no instrument.

## 3. Findings F1 to F10

### F1 - The question is a union, not a partition; "essential" is per function slot (triangulated)

Fig 9 :55 "A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones. A single lever clears the region where only one condition holds and leaves the intersections standing." Fig 9 :57 "a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either. It stops being a mixture and becomes a specific compound." Fig 1 :19 "The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated." Fig 11 :49 "An actor's valence is relative to the residual, not a property it carries."

A species essential for pollination need not be essential for carbon storage. Asking for one list collapses several slots into one average and loses the signal. **Verdict:** there is no single answer; there is one verdict per function, kept separate and never averaged. Triangulated across Fig 9, Fig 1 and Fig 11 (low overlap between union logic, per-slot indexing and valence).

**Refuter:** a list of essential species is shown to be invariant across independent functions and habitats, with the same members and the same removal results. That would make the average legitimate and dissolve the union.

### F2 - The habitat is enumerated, never composed; no a priori essential list (triangulated)

Fig 11 :42 "The habitat is enumerated, never composed." Fig 11 :43 "List everything in contact with the residual, then what is in contact with those, and outward until the cascade stops mattering." Fig 11 :47 "relations: pollination, predation, competition, dependency." Fig 11 :48 "You do not invent the list. You read it off the world, and what you cannot perceive or measure yet is the residual."

The candidate set of "essential" things is read off the world, not derived from the question. This is the mirror of Fig 9 :27-28 "describe it before you go looking, or any frame you pick will seem to fit": if we pick charismatic species first and then look for functions they serve, any species will seem to fit. **Verdict:** the enumeration, not the favourite list, governs. Triangulated across Fig 11 and Fig 9.

**Refuter:** a complete candidate set is produced by derivation from the question alone, with no world enumeration, and it passes removal tests on an independent function.

### F3 - Valence is relative to the residual; "essential" is a relation, not a species property (triangulated)

Fig 11 :49 "An actor's valence is relative to the residual, not a property it carries." Fig 11 :50 "The same actor is a benefactor to one residual and a malfactor to another, so mutualism and parasitism are relations rather than kinds." Fig 11 :51 "No standing list of good actors survives a change of residual."

This is the structural refusal of the question as literally posed. There is no standing list of essential species, because valence flips when the residual changes. A nitrogen fixer can be a benefactor to primary production and a malfactor to a low-nutrient specialist community. **Verdict:** "essential" must always be indexed to a named function and a named habitat; a free-floating list is a category error. Triangulated across Fig 11 and Fig 2 (an anchor is constitutively about a question, Fig 2 :36).

**Refuter:** an actor is shown to carry intrinsic "essentialness" that predicts removal failure across residues and habitats without reference to a function. That would refute valence-relativity.

### F4 - Contact cascades; direct-contact enumeration misses the residual three steps out (triangulated)

Fig 11 :52-53 "And contact cascades. Scoping the habitat to direct contact is how DDT reached the eagles: first-order enumeration, second-order effect, and the residual that mattered was three steps out." Fig 11 :54 "Carson, Silent Spring, 1962. The mechanism, biomagnification through the food chain to eggshell thinning, was established over the following decade." Fig 3 :20 "The chain is ordered by dependency, not by importance."

The essential relation may be two or three steps away from the function. Scoping to direct contact is how the important actor is missed. **Verdict:** enumerate outward until the cascade stops mattering, and trace several orders, not one. Triangulated across Fig 11, Fig 3 and the Carson case that Fig 11 itself cites (world corroboration in `10_source_ledger.csv`).

**Refuter:** a function is shown to be fully determined by direct contacts, with every removal effect at first order and no second- or third-order effect, across multiple habitats.

### F5 - The ground is a measured function, not agreement among ecologists (triangulated)

Fig 2 :29 "An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks." Fig 2 :35 "Alignment is triangulation, not negotiation. A mirror cannot correct you." Fig 2 :40 "agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool." Fig 9 :60 "Use filters for the budget and witnesses for the verdict. Never let a filter hand you a verdict."

A consensus that starfish are "keystone" is agreement about a shared meaning, efficient for a convention but worthless as a verdict about a function (Fig 2 :41). The anchor is the measured function, which does not move when reviewers change their minds. Effect sizes and citation counts are filters for the budget, never witnesses. **Verdict:** require an independent function measurement for each slot. Triangulated across Fig 2 and Fig 9.

**Refuter:** a function measurement is shown to move with the frame's expectation (a non-updating anchor cannot be found), in which case no ground exists for that slot and the verdict must be recorded as `gap`, not as consensus.

### F6 - "Essential" is a subtraction and a threshold, not a correlation (single-source on the figure side)

Operators table: subtraction is "A with B removed or absent" with the test "is the second operand's absence the point? Yes," and "progress is a subtraction, which is why a vocabulary without it cannot say how far along it is." Comparison is "a threshold relation between two quantities" with the test "is it about which is larger, rather than both being present? Yes," and "a goal is a comparison, which is why a vocabulary without it cannot state one."

Correlation between a species' presence and a function level is a symmetric conjunction (Operators "+"), so it cannot choose the arrow. "Essential" needs a removal (subtraction) and a threshold below which the function has failed (comparison). **Verdict:** the evidence that licenses "essential" is a knock-out or exclusion result plus a stated threshold, not a co-occurrence. Single-source on the figure side (Operators table), corroborated in the world by Paine's removal experiment and the engineering notion of a single point of failure (both in `10_source_ledger.csv`), but those are secondary only.

**Refuter:** a symmetric presence-function correlation is shown to determine essentialness without any removal or threshold, so the ordered reading is unnecessary.

### F7 - Managing is introduction, not exclusion; isolation substitutes habitat (triangulated)

Fig 11 :61-62 "The seat's power is asymmetric. Introduction is available; exclusion is not. You can add an actor and you cannot subtract the habitat, because contact is a fact about the world and does not consult you." Fig 11 :63-64 "Which is why a managed ecosystem is permanent work rather than a solved state." Fig 11 :65 "Introducing an actor for its cascade is ecosystem engineering, after Jones, Lawton and Shachak, 1994. Cane toads and kudzu are the same move with the cascade unenumerated." Fig 11 :66 "Isolation is not removal of the habitat. It is substitution of one habitat for another." Fig 11 :68-69 "what you failed to reproduce is invisible until the goal fails" and "what you unintentionally introduced is invisible until it acts."

Once you act on the answer, you can add an actor but you cannot subtract a habitat. Moving a species to a new reserve substitutes a habitat and inherits actors you did not choose. **Verdict:** the answer must include the intervention's two silent failure modes, and restoration is permanent work, not a solved state. Triangulated across Fig 11 and Fig 6 (an import needs a source gate and shadow test; Fig 6 :23).

**Refuter:** a habitat is shown to be subtractable at will (contact removable by decree), or an introduced actor's cascade is shown to be fully enumerable in advance in a novel habitat.

### F8 - Capability is agent-and-habitat, never of a species alone (triangulated)

Fig 11 :39 "capability is a property of agent-and-habitat, never of the agent alone." Fig 11 :40 "A system cannot be made more capable by improving the agent in isolation. An improvement that does not change contact changes nothing, however much it improves." Fig 11 :73 "The habitat is not the ground. The habitat is where actors come from and it updates; the ground is what settles, and it does not."

A species is not "essential in itself"; it is essential in a habitat, and the habitat updates while the function ground does not. **Verdict:** the unit of the answer is species-in-habitat-for-function, not a species. Triangulated across Fig 11 and Fig 4 (the same machine at two sizes; only actors, currency and anchor change).

**Refuter:** an agent-only improvement is shown to move the function ground, which would refute the capability claim (Fig 11 :71-72 names this as the standing refuter).

### F9 - The objective link has no instrument; split rather than search (triangulated)

Fig 9 :4-5 "First: is there a fact here? Disagreements that do not shrink with effort, where each rule keeps working well on a different subset, are not one hard question but several well-formed ones. Split it rather than search." Fig 3 :27 "A link with no instrument is not a link that works. It is a link nobody has looked at." Fig 1 :23 "The ground enters as the only metric."

Much of the "which species are essential" literature disagrees without shrinking, because different rules work on different functions and habitats. That is the signature of a union, not one hard question. **Verdict:** split by function before searching; owe an instrument for the objective link. Triangulated across Fig 9, Fig 3 and Fig 1.

**Refuter:** the apparent disagreements are shown to shrink with effort on a single well-defined function, so the question was one hard question after all.

### F10 - What survives: a separator, and a `gap` for the literal list (triangulated)

The shape that survives is: for each function slot, enumerate the habitat outward, apply a removal and threshold test against an independent measurement, keep the verdict per slot, name the cascade and the intervention's silent failures, and write the failure condition before the run. No figure line returns a universal list of essential species. Fig 9 :32 "you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself." Fig 13 :134 "Unreachable is a verdict here, not a silence." Fig 8 :39 "So a search that finds nothing is never a proof of absence."

**Verdict:** the literal standing list is a `gap` of the shape-not-a-name kind and is unreached at this budget, reported as a verdict rather than silence. The separator is `specified` and triangulated. Naming a particular species as essential is a binding from the ecology frame with provenance (a removal result in a named habitat), not a framework derivation.

**Refuter:** a figure line is produced that selects a particular species for a function from the per-slot residual alone, with no world enumeration. None was found after a genuine re-census across Fig 1 to Fig 13 and both tables.

## 4. Pre-registered refuter (written before the conclusion)

A falsifier written before the run is a design instrument. The shape claim is refuted if any of the following holds:

1. A standing list of essential species or relations is shown to survive a change of focal function and habitat with the same members and the same removal results (breaks Fig 11 :51, F1 and F3).
2. An actor's valence is shown to be intrinsic, predicting removal failure across residues without reference to a function (breaks Fig 11 :49-50).
3. A function is shown to be fully determined by direct contacts with no second- or third-order effect across multiple habitats (breaks Fig 11 :52-53 and F4).
4. A symmetric presence-function correlation is shown to determine essentialness with no removal and no threshold (breaks F6 and the Operators table).
5. An agent-only improvement is shown to move the function ground (breaks Fig 11 :72's capability claim).
6. A habitat is shown to be subtractable at will, or an introduced actor's cascade fully enumerable in a novel habitat (breaks Fig 11 :61-66).

If any fires, the corresponding finding is withdrawn and the shape is revised. Until one is run, no part of this may be labelled `grounded`.

## 5. Recommendation

1. Do not answer with a universal list. Answer with a per-function separator and say plainly that the literal list is a `gap` (F1, F3, F10).
2. Name the function and its independent measurement before naming any species (F5, Fig 9 :27-28).
3. Enumerate the habitat outward from the residual, including conditions and relations, not just a charismatic species shortlist (F2, F4).
4. Require a removal or exclusion result and a stated threshold; treat correlations as budget filters only (F6, Fig 9 :60).
5. Include the cascade and the two silent failure modes of intervention; treat restoration as permanent work (F7).
6. Send the generator (the residual, the shape and the refuter), not a recording of one successful reserve (Fig 4 :22-25 "Only methods cross up. Only head starts come down.").
