# Outreach draft - check one output before you decide whether the claim is worth reading

**Use:** one to one, or as a cover note attached to a submission through the public venues in `03_public_contact_points.csv`. **Not** for bulk send.

**Audience:** a ground-visible researcher in ecology and ecosystem services research (functional ecology, biodiversity and ecosystem functioning, restoration, or conservation prioritization), reachable through an opt-in or public channel. Chosen for the public, checkable record of contact with a ground, not for likely agreement.

**Hard precondition:** do not send until the shape claim and its refuter below survive your own check against the figures. The output must survive validation or the two-stage offer collapses on contact.

---

**Subject:** Check one separator before you decide whether the claim is worth reading

> Hi [name],
>
> I have one specific output I would like you to check against what you already know about ecosystem functions. I am not asking you to read a theory first, and I am not asking you to agree with anything. If the output does not survive, there is nothing else to look at.
>
> **The output (`specified`, not `grounded`):** the question "which species or ecological relationships are essential for maintaining ecosystem functions?" does not return a standing list. Figure 11 of the framework I am using says "No standing list of good actors survives a change of residual" and "An actor's valence is relative to the residual, not a property it carries." Figure 1 says "The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated." Figure 9 says a bundled problem is "a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones."
>
> What the figures do license is a separator, stated before any species is named:
>
> 1. Name one function and its independent measurement (primary production, nutrient cycling, pollination, decomposition, carbon storage, or resilience). The measurement must not move when a reviewer changes their mind.
> 2. Enumerate the habitat outward from that function: actors, relations such as pollination, predation, competition and dependency, and conditions such as light, water, temperature, season and terrain. Figure 11 says "The habitat is enumerated, never composed" and "You do not invent the list. You read it off the world."
> 3. Apply a removal or exclusion test with a stated threshold. A correlation between presence and function is symmetric; "essential" is ordered, so a knock-out and a threshold are what license the word.
> 4. Trace the cascade at least two steps out. Figure 11 notes that scoping to direct contact is how DDT reached the eagles, with "the residual that mattered was three steps out."
> 5. Name the two silent failures of intervention: what you failed to reproduce and what you unintentionally introduced. Figure 11 says "Which is why a managed ecosystem is permanent work rather than a solved state."
>
> **What I am not claiming:** that any particular species is essential in general. Naming one is a binding from your field onto a measured removal result in a named habitat, with provenance, not something the framework derives. The framework returns a shape, not a name. I am also not claiming that a large consensus among ecologists makes a species essential. Figure 2 says "agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool."
>
> **What would refute the output, written before sending this:**
> 1. a list of essential species that is invariant across independent functions and habitats, with the same removal results;
> 2. an actor whose "essentialness" predicts failure across residues without reference to a function;
> 3. a function fully determined by direct contacts, with no second- or third-order effect, across several habitats;
> 4. a symmetric presence-function correlation that determines essentialness with no removal and no threshold;
> 5. an agent-only improvement that moves the function ground.
>
> If any of these holds, I will retract the claim. A one-line "this fails because..." is more useful to me than agreement. If you would rather not engage, no reply is needed.
>
> **If it survives, and only then:** I can send the three public sources below. The figures are the authority, not my summary of them.
> - Verbatim figure text with line numbers: local framework file `FRAMEWORK/reference/03-FIGURES_TEXT.md` (I can supply a public link on request).
> - The canonical figures README and formula: https://github.com/IsaiahN/tether/tree/general-agent/figures
> - The ecology and ecosystem services sources behind the removal and enumeration points are listed in the packet, not hidden in a reading list.
>
> **If you want to use the machinery:** the formula is in the figures link. Run it on a function you can measure independently and can check against a ground. Write down what would refute your result before you run it, and state the mode of what you get. A reported failure is more useful to me than a reported success.
