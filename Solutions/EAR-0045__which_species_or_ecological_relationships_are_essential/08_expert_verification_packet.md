# Expert verification packet - the Tether on essential species and relationships

**What this is:** a claim in a form an ecologist or ecosystem-services researcher can check. Mode, derivation and refuters are stated before the conclusion, so a reader knows what to look for and what would settle it.

**Mode: `specified` on the per-domain verdicts, `general` on the structural shape, `gap` on the literal standing list. Not `grounded`.** No ecological measurement is claimed. This is a shape and a route, not a result.

**Standing:** a simulated derivation checked for internal consistency against the figures, not a ground reading. It cannot settle the question it reports on.

## 1. The claim, in one paragraph

"Which species or ecological relationships are essential for maintaining ecosystem functions?" does not license a standing list. It is a union of overlapping function slots, so an essentialness verdict is relative to a named function in a named habitat. The habitat is enumerated from the world, actors carry valence relative to the residual, and "essential" is a removal plus a threshold rather than a correlation. The decisive relation may be several steps out, and intervention can add actors but cannot subtract the habitat, with two silent failure modes. The framework returns this shape, not the species names; the names require a provenanced removal result and a non-updating function measurement. What is offered is the shape, a pre-registered failure condition, and the instruments that would close each slot.

## 2. Three checkable sub-claims

### C1 - framework-internal plus field-facing; checkable by reading Figures 1, 9 and 11

- **Statement:** the question is a union, not a partition; "essential" is per function, and there is no standing list that survives a change of residual.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 9 :55 "A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones. A single lever clears the region where only one condition holds and leaves the intersections standing." Fig 1 :19 "The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated." Fig 11 :51 "No standing list of good actors survives a change of residual." Supporting: Fig 11 :49 "An actor's valence is relative to the residual, not a property it carries."
- **What to check:** is the function you work on a single slot, or several? Does the identity of the "essential" actor change when you change the function (for example, mean productivity versus interannual stability)? If it does, the union and valence verdicts are confirmed at the ground.
- **Refuter:** a list of essential species that is invariant across independent functions and habitats, with the same removal results. That would make the average legitimate and dissolve the union.

### C2 - framework-internal, on the meaning of "essential"; checkable by reading the Operators table and Figure 11

- **Statement:** "essential" is an ordered relation (removal plus threshold), not a symmetric presence-function correlation; the decisive relation can be indirect.
- **Source, verbatim:** the Operators table defines subtraction as "A with B removed or absent" with the test "is the second operand's absence the point? Yes," and comparison as "a threshold relation between two quantities" with the test "is it about which is larger, rather than both being present? Yes." Fig 11 :52-53 "And contact cascades. Scoping the habitat to direct contact is how DDT reached the eagles: first-order enumeration, second-order effect, and the residual that mattered was three steps out."
- **What to check:** in your system, is "essential" asserted from co-occurrence, or from a removal or exclusion with a threshold? Is the effect first order, or does it appear several steps out? Paine's starfish removal and Jones, Lawton and Shachak's ecosystem engineers are world instances of the removal and indirect-effect reading, but they are corroboration only.
- **Refuter:** a symmetric presence-function correlation that determines essentialness without any removal and without a threshold, in which case the ordered reading is unnecessary and this sub-claim fails.

### C3 - intervention and habitat; checkable by reading Figure 11 and Figure 6

- **Statement:** the answer must be indexed to a habitat; intervention can introduce but cannot subtract the habitat, and substitution inherits actors and hides two failures.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 11 :61-62 "The seat's power is asymmetric. Introduction is available; exclusion is not. You can add an actor and you cannot subtract the habitat, because contact is a fact about the world and does not consult you." Fig 11 :68-69 "what you failed to reproduce is invisible until the goal fails" and "what you unintentionally introduced is invisible until it acts." Fig 11 :73 "The habitat is not the ground. The habitat is where actors come from and it updates; the ground is what settles, and it does not." Also Fig 6 :23 "No frame escapes its own closure. The bound moves only on contact with a frame whose closure differs."
- **What to check:** in a restoration or reintroduction you have seen, which actors arrived with the substituted habitat unchosen, and which required functions failed silently? Does adding a single actor reliably reproduce the function, or is the result permanent work?
- **Refuter:** a habitat shown to be subtractable at will, or a novel habitat in which an introduced actor's cascade is fully enumerable in advance, which would break the asymmetry and substitution claims.

## 3. What is explicitly not claimed

- Not a universal list of essential species; that is a declared `gap` (Fig 9 :32, Fig 13 :134).
- Not that charismatic, abundant or well-funded species are essential. Charisma funds attention, not recovery; the standing-list refusal is structural.
- Not that a species is essential "in itself." Capability is agent-and-habitat (Fig 11 :39).
- Not that diversity per se is the answer; which actor matters depends on whether the slot is a mean or a variance.
- Not that reviewer consensus settles the question (Fig 2 :40).
- Not `grounded`. No removal result or function measurement is asserted here.
- Not a derivation of a species name. A name is a provenanced binding from the ecology frame, licensed by a removal result in a named habitat.

## 5. What would settle it

A removal or exclusion with a function measurement that does not update on opinion. From *The Hard Problem is About Source* (carried quote): an instrument that reads green "cannot comment on the source"; a spectrometer resolved it. Here:

1. Does the function drop below a stated threshold when the candidate is removed, against a control?
2. Does the same removal change its result when the function changes? That would confirm valence-relative essentialness at the ground.
3. Is the effect first order, or three steps out?
4. Does the result survive habitat substitution, and which actors came with it?

Pre-registered refuter (written before the conclusion, repeated from `01` section 4):

1. an invariant essential list across functions and habitats;
2. intrinsic valence independent of a function;
3. no second- or third-order effect across several habitats;
4. a correlation that determines essentialness without removal or threshold;
5. an agent-only improvement that moves the ground;
6. a subtractable habitat or a fully enumerable cascade in a novel habitat.

## 6. What an expert can check in twenty minutes

1. Read Fig 9 :55, Fig 1 :19, Fig 11 :49-51 and :52-53, and the Operators entries for subtraction and comparison. Six short passages.
2. Check C1 against one function you know: does the "essential" actor change when the function changes?
3. Check C2 against one result you know: was essentialness asserted from correlation or from removal plus threshold, and was the effect direct?
4. Check C3 against one restoration you know: which actors came with the habitat, and what failed silently?
5. Send the refutation. A failure is more useful than an endorsement. If you would rather not engage, no reply is needed.
