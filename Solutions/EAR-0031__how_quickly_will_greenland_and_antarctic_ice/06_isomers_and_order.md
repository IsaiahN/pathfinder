# Isomers and order: the structure of the ice-sheet sea-level union

**Mode: `general` on the bond typing, `specified` on the physical arrangement. This is a simulated derivation checked against the figures, not a ground reading (see `05_what_shape_means.md` §7).**

## RESIDUAL

The audit leaves two structural questions owed. First, given two sheets and two loss processes, are Greenland and Antarctica **isomers** under Fig 12, or merely members of one functional group? Second, what is the **order** of the union, and which bonds carry it? A related question is whether the instability tail is a threshold comparison or a smooth gradient. These are supporting derivations: they constrain the shape, not the number.

## DOMAIN CENSUS

Atoms are not invented here. The world frame supplies the inventory: ice, ocean heat, atmosphere, ice shelves, grounding lines, bedrock, surface melt, dynamical discharge, time, and the sea-level fingerprint. The six-term substrate is the composition rule above them, not a replacement for them (see the retraction in `01` §6). Bonds held are the seven typed relations plus negation: `+`, `∥`, `≡`, `→`, `⇒`, `−`, `⋛`, `¬`. No new primitive is available without a provenanced import.

## Q1 : Are the two sheets isomers over a shared atom set?

Fig 12 :117: **"isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."**

Read the two sheets over the shared atom set `{ocean heat, atmosphere, ice, ice shelf, grounding line, bedrock, surface melt, dynamical discharge, time}`:

- Greenland: roughly half the cumulative loss from reduced surface mass balance (meltwater runoff) and half from dynamic imbalance; the dynamic share is declining as marine margins retreat and ocean forcing diminishes. The dominant atmosphere-coupled path is `atmosphere → surface melt → mass flux`.
- Antarctica: loss dominated by West Antarctic outlet glaciers forced by ocean-driven ice-shelf basal melt and grounding-line retreat; East Antarctica uncertain and partly gaining. The dominant ocean-coupled path is `ocean heat → ice shelf thinning → grounding-line retreat → mass flux`.

Same atoms, different bonds and different relative weights, different behaviour and different response time. By Fig 12's definition they are **isomers**, over that shared set. That is a second, independent structural reason not to average them: an average is blind to exactly the arrangement that decides the behaviour.

**Falsifier, written before the run:** if either sheet can be written without an atom the other requires (for example, Antarctica with no bedrock-control term, or Greenland with no surface-melt term), the isomer claim fails and the weaker functional-group reading below stands. **Status:** `specified`, single-source on the figure definition (Fig 12), with the physical arrangement corroborated by the IMBIE and IPCC sources in `10_source_ledger.csv`. Low overlap between the figure rule and the glaciology, so the figure verdict is not flipped by the world; the world supplies the arrangement.

## Q2 : Surface mass balance versus dynamical discharge: isomer or functional group?

Fig 12 :123: **"functional group. A substructure whose presence fixes the outcome. The group says what kind; the qualifier says which member."**

Both are members of "mass loss" and both produce sea-level equivalent, but they are not arranged variants of one molecule under one instrument. They have different forcings (atmosphere versus ocean), different instruments (regional climate models and runoff gauges versus altimetry and ice-flow/grounding-line observations), and different reversibility. They are a **functional group** whose qualifier says which member. The group fixes the kind (mass loss); the qualifier fixes the mechanism.

**Falsifier:** if a single instrument and a single anchor were shown to measure both processes as one flux with no loss of source information, the functional-group split would collapse to a single term. That would remove one of the four conditions in the union and change the shape. **Status:** `specified`, triangulated (Fig 12 functional-group rule, Fig 1 per-slot rule, and the world's separate instrument practices).

## Q3 : What order is the union?

Fig 3 :20: **"The chain is ordered by dependency, not by importance. Each link consumes what the one above produces, so effort spent above the break arrives at a step that cannot use it."**

The dependency chain for this question is:

```
warming pathway (forcing)
  → atmosphere / ocean response (heat content, circulation)
    → ice-shelf basal melt or surface melt (the two processes)
      → grounding-line retreat / discharge response (dynamics)
        → mass flux per sheet (the rate slot)
          → gravitational, rotational, deformational fingerprint
            → regional sea level at a coast
```

This is a `→` sequence: *"A then B; B needs A to have happened"* (Fig 12 :72). Operators :34 warns: *"the only bond a flat chain can express, so every term today is a sequence whether or not it should be."* Writing it as a flat sequence is a notation choice, and the figure warns that it may hide a cycle. Fig 12 :121: *"cycle. The output re-enters as input. The sign of the loop decides whether it closes, and a chain cannot close by construction."* Ice loss changes the solid Earth and the ocean circulation that forced it, so the feedback is real; the sign decides whether it closes. The figure says a chain cannot express that, so the sequence must be annotated as a loop with a stated sign, not silently flattened.

A rate is a subtraction over this chain. Operators :46: **"progress is a subtraction, which is why a vocabulary without it cannot say how far along it is."** "How quickly" is therefore well formed only after the two endpoints and the horizon are named.

**Falsifier:** if the feedback sign is such that the loop never closes and output never re-enters as input, the cycle reading is unnecessary and the flat chain is adequate. The figure's warning would then be a notation caution only, not a structural claim. **Status:** `general` for the bond typing, `specified` for which feedbacks actually close; the closure question needs an instrument, not an argument.

## Q4 : Is the instability tail a threshold comparison or a smooth gradient?

Fig 12 :87: **"a threshold relation between two quantities"** defines ⋛, with the test *"is it about which is larger, rather than both being present? Yes."* Operators :52: **"a goal is a comparison, which is why a vocabulary without it cannot state one."**

Marine ice sheet instability and ice cliff failure are threshold processes in the world sources: a grounding line retreats past a bedrock threshold, or a cliff height exceeds a stability limit, and the response becomes self-sustaining. Structurally that is a `⋛` comparison, not a `+` conjunction of ingredients. Fig 13 :115: **"The set is closed. The arrangements are not. That gap is why import exists."** And Fig 8 :39: **"So a search that finds nothing is never a proof of absence."**

The operand type is a live strain. Operators :49 restricts ⋛ to *"a threshold relation between two quantities."* Here one operand is a warming level and the other is a geometric or mechanical limit. Whether they count as quantities at the same level is a typing question the figures do not settle. It is named, not hidden.

**Falsifier:** an observation showing the response is smooth in warming with no threshold at the relevant scale would retire the comparison bond; the tail would be a gradient and the deep-uncertainty framing would need re-census. **Status:** `specified`, single-source on the figure type, strained on the operand type, corroborated structurally (not numerically) by the world sources.

## Q5 : The committed total as second-order composition

Fig 12 :104: **"A settled molecule becomes an atom for whatever composes over it. So irreducible is a fact about a level and never about the world, and there is no bottom"**

The 2100 rate and the observed mass-balance record are settled against the ground. Each can therefore serve as an atom at the next level. The multi-century committed total composes over them plus the slow-response atoms (deep ocean heat uptake, bedrock adjustment, ice-sheet equilibration). This is recursion, not a new primitive, and it is legal only because the first-order terms settled. Fig 4 :30 then requires the round trip gap to be measured: **"So take the round trip and measure the gap. Without it you are navigating by dead reckoning: confident about a position nobody checked."**

**Falsifier:** if the committed total is a lossless time derivative of the 2100 rate, then `R_T = 0` and the second-order reading is unnecessary. This is the same failure condition already pre-registered in `01` §4, condition 3. **Status:** `general` for the recursion rule, `specified` for the physical composition.

## CONCLUSION

Over the shared atom set the two sheets are **isomers**: same atoms, different bond, different behaviour, which is why averaging them discards the deciding structure (Fig 12 :117). Surface mass balance and dynamical discharge are a **functional group**, not isomers: the group says mass loss, the qualifier says which mechanism (Fig 12 :123). The union is a `→` dependency chain with a real feedback loop whose sign must be stated; it cannot be silently flattened (Fig 12 :72, :121; Operators :34, :46). The instability tail is a `⋛` threshold comparison with a live operand-type strain (Fig 12 :87; Operators :49), and the committed total is a second-order composition over settled first-order rates with a nonzero round-trip gap (Fig 12 :104; Fig 4 :30).

None of this returns a number. It rescues the part of the question that is well formed: the arrangement, the order, the bond types, and the falsifiers for each.
