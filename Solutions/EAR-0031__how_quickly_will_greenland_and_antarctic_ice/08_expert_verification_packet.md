# Expert verification packet : the Tether on ice-sheet sea-level rise

**What this is:** a claim in a form an ice-sheet dynamicist or sea-level scientist can check. Mode, derivation and refuters are stated before the conclusion, so a reader knows what to look for and what would settle it.

**Mode: `general` on the shape, `specified` on the per-domain physical verdicts, `gap` on the literal number. Not `grounded` as a forecast.** This packet is a simulated derivation checked for internal consistency against the figures, not a ground reading. No ice-sheet measurement was taken by this run.

## 1. The claim, in one paragraph

The question "how quickly will Greenland and Antarctic ice loss raise sea levels under different warming pathways" is a union of four overlapping conditions, not one rate. The residual must stay indexed per sheet and per process: Greenland's recent loss is roughly half surface mass balance and half dynamic imbalance, while Antarctica's is dominated by ocean-forced ice-shelf thinning and grounding-line retreat. Over a shared atom set the two sheets are isomers, same atoms arranged differently, with different behaviour, so averaging them discards the deciding structure. "How quickly" is a subtraction over a named horizon, so a 2100 rate and a committed multi-century total are different quantities across a lossy transform. The anchor is the observation record, not model agreement; frames that share forcing, ancestry and calibration contribute one witness. The marine-instability tail is unreached at this budget, which is a verdict and not a silence, and committed loss is not subtractable on a planning horizon. The figures return this shape, not a number.

## 2. Three checkable sub-claims

### C1 : framework-internal; checkable by reading Figures 1, 9 and 12

- **Statement:** the question is a union that must be unbundled, the residual is per slot, and the two sheets are isomers over a shared atom set rather than instances of one process.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 9 :54 : *"And when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again."* Fig 9 :57 : *"a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either."* Fig 1 :19 : *"The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated."* Fig 12 :117 : *"isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."*
- **Refuter:** show a figure rule that licenses a single global slot and demonstrates that the aggregate predicts each sheet as well as the per-sheet treatment; or show that one sheet can be written without an atom the other requires, which kills the isomer claim and leaves the functional-group reading (Fig 12 :123).

### C2 : physics-facing; checkable by a glaciologist against the two sheets and the two processes

- **Statement:** over the world inventory `{ocean heat, atmosphere, ice, ice shelf, grounding line, bedrock, surface melt, dynamical discharge, time}`, the two sheets are different arrangements with different dominant processes and different response times; surface mass balance and dynamical discharge are a functional group of mass-loss members with different instruments and anchors, not one flux. The 2100 rate and the committed total are different quantities.
- **What to check:** is the characterization faithful? Is Greenland's loss now mostly surface melt with a declining dynamic share, and Antarctica's mostly ocean-forced West Antarctic discharge with an uncertain East Antarctic contribution? Do the two processes have genuinely different instruments? Does the assessed 2100 range sit alongside, rather than inside, the committed multi-millennial rise?
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 12 :123 : *"functional group. A substructure whose presence fixes the outcome. The group says what kind; the qualifier says which member."* Operators :46 : *"progress is a subtraction, which is why a vocabulary without it cannot say how far along it is."* Fig 4 :30 : *"So take the round trip and measure the gap. Without it you are navigating by dead reckoning: confident about a position nobody checked."*
- **Refuter:** a single instrument and a single anchor shown to measure both loss processes as one flux with no source information lost; or a committed millennial contribution shown to be a lossless derivative of a 2100 rate, which sets the round-trip gap to zero.

### C3 : method-facing; checkable by reading Figures 2, 3, 8, 10 and 11

- **Statement:** the anchor is observation; model consensus from a shared evidence pool is not a witness; the deep-uncertainty tail is `gap`, unreached rather than absent; independence must be bought in proportion to the cost of being wrong; committed loss is not subtractable on a planning horizon.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 1 :23 : *"The ground enters as the only metric."* Fig 2 :29 : *"An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks."* Fig 2 :35 : *"Alignment is triangulation, not negotiation. A mirror cannot correct you."* Fig 3 :27 : *"A link with no instrument is not a link that works. - **Refuter:** a model consensus that moves the observed mass-balance anchor and survives an independent instrument; a process-resolving observation that closes the tail into a tight settled band; or an observation that committed ice-sheet contribution can be removed on a planning timescale by a real non-substitutive operation.

## 3. The six pre-registered refuters, in one place

Written before the conclusion, per Seat Map :287: *"a falsifier written before the run is a design instrument, not only an honesty device."*

1. A figure rule licenses a single global slot and the aggregate predicts each sheet as well as the per-sheet treatment.
2. A model consensus moves the observed mass-balance anchor rather than ranging against it, and survives an independent instrument.
3. A committed millennial contribution is a lossless derivative of a 2100 rate.
4. A process-resolving observation closes the instability tail into a tight settled band.
5. Two frames with disjoint evidence and no shared calibration are shown to add no surplus.
6. A numerical ice-sheet rate is derived from the six-term substrate alone with no imported observation.

If any fires, the corresponding finding is withdrawn. Until one is run, no forecast here is `grounded`.

## 4. What is explicitly not claimed

- Not a number, not a Gt, not a millimetre per year, and not a single rate across pathways.
- Not that model agreement is an anchor. Agreement is a frame property; the ground settles.
- Not that the high-end storyline is the true tail, and not that its exclusion from *likely* ranges proves it cannot happen.
- Not a derivation of ice physics from the six-term set. That first-pass mapping was retracted in `01` §6.
- Not `grounded`. This is a simulated derivation checked for internal consistency, not a ground reading, and it cannot settle the claim it reports on.
- Not that stabilising temperature rapidly reverses committed mass loss.

## 6. What would settle it

1. A process-discriminating observation separating surface-mass-balance loss from dynamical discharge at the slot.
2. A grounding-line or ice-shelf observation that resolves the threshold tail into a settled band.
3. New altimetry and gravimetry series that move the measured rate, with independent instruments agreeing separately against the same anchor.
4. Any one of the six refuters above firing.

## 7. What an expert can check in twenty minutes

1. Read Fig 1 :19, :22 and Fig 9 :54, :57. Do you accept that the two sheets and the two processes are separate slots of a union? If not, say which figure permits a single aggregate.
2. Check C2 against your own knowledge: is the per-sheet process characterization faithful, and is the rate versus commitment distinction real in the assessed literature? One line is enough.
3. Name one instrument that would separate the two loss processes at the slot, or say why none exists. That is the discriminator the shape owes.
4. Send the refutation. A failure is more useful than an endorsement, and later agreement will not be pooled as corroboration.
