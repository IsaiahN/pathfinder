## 0. The question under audit, stated as a residual in effect terms

> How quickly will Greenland and Antarctic ice loss raise sea levels under different warming pathways?

Read as an effect, R is the gap between a prediction and an observation per slot. The slots are not just two. They are Greenland, West Antarctica, East Antarctica, and within each, surface mass balance (snowfall minus meltwater runoff) and dynamical discharge (ice flow across the grounding line, modulated by ice-shelf buttressing). The currency is mass flux (Gt per year) translated to sea-level equivalent (mm per year). The scale is triple: a decadal observed rate, a century-scale scenario-conditioned projection to 2100, and a multi-century committed rise that continues after temperatures stabilise.

The residual is indexed by slot, not by a global total. Fig 1 :19: *"The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated."* Fig 1 :22: *"Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average."* A single global "ice loss rate" is the aggregation the figure forbids, because Greenland's mass loss is now dominated by surface melt while West Antarctica's is dominated by ocean-driven ice-shelf thinning and grounding-line retreat. These have different actors, different currencies of forcing, different instruments, and different anchors. They are separate loops.

The sought-for shape, written down and bound as an ordinary term before any conclusion (THE_FORMULA, step "Direction"): **a per-sheet, per-pathway, per-horizon rate with an uncertainty band set by process understanding, plus a separately stated committed total and a separately stated deep-uncertainty tail.** Binding it makes the frame's preference for one number refusable by the ordinary test.

## 1. Domain census before choosing any figure line

### 1.1 R by arity, symmetry, scale

- **Arity:** multiple slots. At minimum six (two sheets times two processes, plus the ocean forcing and the gravitational/rotational/deformational fingerprint that converts mass loss into regional sea level). This is a union, not a scalar.
- **Symmetry:** asymmetric and path-dependent. Grounding-line retreat on a retrograde bed is hysteretic; ice-shelf hydrofracture and marine ice cliff failure are threshold processes. A comparison bond ⋛ is ordered (*"is it about which is larger, rather than both being present? Yes."*, Operators :50), and a pathway is a sequence (*"A then B; B needs A to have happened"*, Fig 12 :72). A rate is a subtraction of one state from another (*"progress is a subtraction, which is why a vocabulary without it cannot say how far along it is"*, Operators :46). None of these are symmetric conjunctions.
- **Scale:** decadal observation, centennial projection, millennial commitment. The transform between them is lossy. Fig 4 :30: *"So take the round trip and measure the gap. Without it you are navigating by dead reckoning: confident about a position nobody checked."*

### 1.2 One line per domain: actors, currency, anchor

| domain | actors | currency | anchor | no anchor? |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| D1, the physical cryosphere system | ice, ice shelves, grounding lines, bedrock, ocean heat, atmosphere, meltwater | mass flux (Gt/yr), sea-level equivalent (mm/yr), heat content | the observing system: altimetry (ICESat, ICESat-2, CryoSat-2), gravimetry (GRACE, GRACE-FO), tide gauges, in situ section data | has a nameable anchor |
| D2, the modelling and scenario frame | emissions pathways (SSPs/RCPs), climate models, ice-sheet models (ISMIP6, LARMIP-2), emulators | probabilities, *likely* ranges, confidence language | the observed record plus process understanding, which the model ranges against | has a nameable anchor |

| D4, the verification and outreach frame | expert readers, ice-sheet dynamicists | agreement and residual | the ground, never agreement | has a nameable anchor |

All four have nameable anchors, so all four are domains. If any lacked one it would not yet be a domain and would be treated as an owed atom.

### 1.3 Partition or union

**Union.** Fig 9 :54: *"And when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again."* Fig 9 :57: *"a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either."* The question bundles at least four overlapping conditions: sheet identity, process (surface versus dynamic), horizon (rate versus commitment), and pathway (forcing scenario). A single lever clears the region where only one condition holds and leaves the intersections standing. Verdicts are therefore kept per domain and never averaged.

### 1.4 Order by dependency (Fig 3)

1. Observe current mass balance per sheet (perception, link 1). This link works and has instruments.
2. Have terms for what the processes afford: buttressing, grounding-line retreat, hydrofracture, surface melt (vocabulary, link 2). Partly works; the deep-uncertainty processes are thinly instrumented.
3. Say what would count as progress: constrain a per-slot, per-pathway rate with a stated band (objective, link 3). This is the broken middle link. Fig 3 :27: *"A link with no instrument is not a link that works. It is a link nobody has looked at."*
4. Plan: choose models, scenarios, observations (link 4).
5. Learn and carry: update the projections as new altimetry and gravimetry arrive (link 5).

Fig 3 :20: *"The chain is ordered by dependency, not by importance."* Effort spent above the break (more model ensembles) cannot rescue link 3.

## 3. Findings

### F1 : The question is a union compound; a single rate is the wrong object (decisive, `general`)

Fig 9 :54: *"And when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again."* Fig 9 :57: *"a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either."* Fig 1 :19: *"The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated."* Fig 1 :22: *"Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average."*

Greenland and Antarctica are not two instances of one process. The world evidence says Greenland's recent loss is roughly half surface melt and half dynamic imbalance, while Antarctica's is dominated by West Antarctic outlet glaciers forced by ocean-driven ice-shelf basal melt. A single combined rate discards the distinction that predicts which lever works. The correct object is a vector of per-sheet, per-process rates.

- **Mode:** `general` for the union shape; the physical distinction between the sheets is `specified` from the world sources.
- **Refuter:** show that the two sheets are a partition with a common anchor and a common mechanism such that their aggregation preserves the live per-slot signal. If a combined number predicts each sheet's contribution as well as the per-sheet numbers, the union verdict fails.
- **Status:** triangulated (Fig 9 union rule, low overlap with Fig 1 per-slot rule).

### F2 : The ground is observation; agreement among models is not an anchor (`general`)

Fig 1 :23: *"The ground enters as the only metric."* Fig 2 :29: *"An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks."* Fig 2 :33: *"The anchor must not update."* Fig 2 :35: *"Alignment is triangulation, not negotiation. A mirror cannot correct you."* Fig 2 :45: *"Frames range against the ground. They do not negotiate with it."*

A trajectory ensemble is a set of frames. If the frames share forcing data, model ancestry, and calibration targets, their agreement is one evidence pool talking. Fig 2 :40: *"agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool."* Fig 8 :51: *"Convergent derivation and adopted import look identical in the contents. Only the record of where each came from separates them"* The assessed ranges in the world literature are calibrated statements about a pool, not a new instrument.

- **Mode:** `general`.
- **Refuter:** demonstrate a model consensus that shifts the observed mass-balance record rather than ranging against it, and that survives an independent instrument. If the consensus legitimately moves the anchor, the anchor was misidentified.
- **Status:** triangulated (Fig 1, Fig 2, Fig 8, low overlap between the metric rule and the provenance rule).

### F3 : "How quickly" conflates a rate with a commitment (`general` / `specified`)

Fig 12 :112: *"A plan is a sequence. A goal is a comparison. Progress is a subtraction. A refutation is a negation, and that one is not a bond."* Operators :46: *"progress is a subtraction, which is why a vocabulary without it cannot say how far along it is"* Fig 4 :30: *"So take the round trip and measure the gap. Without it you are navigating by dead reckoning: confident about a position nobody checked."*

A rate over 2100 and the committed rise over 2000 years are different quantities with different anchors. The world evidence gives them separately: assessed GMSL rise by 2100 versus committed GMSL rise over two millennia at a given peak warming. Compressing the second into the first sets the round-trip gap to zero and is the dead-reckoning error Fig 4 names. "How quickly" is a term the vocabulary can write only as a subtraction over a chosen horizon, so the horizon must be stated.

- **Mode:** `general` for the bond typing, `specified` for the two-horizon distinction.
- **Refuter:** show that the committed millennial rise is a single time derivative of the 2100 rate with no lossy transform, so that carrying one up and back down returns the other exactly.
- **Status:** triangulated (Fig 12 bond typing, Fig 4 round trip, Operators subtraction).

### F4 : The deep-uncertainty tail is unreached at this budget, not absent (`specified`)

Fig 3 :27: *"A link with no instrument is not a link that works. It is a link nobody has looked at."* Fig 6 :32: *"An instrument is not built from a description. It is improved from a worse instrument already returning something."* Fig 8 :39: *"So a search that finds nothing is never a proof of absence."* Fig 9 :44: *"Only a sealed room can be searched to the end, and no frame is a sealed room. So searching and finding nothing proves nothing."*

The instability processes (marine ice sheet instability, marine ice cliff instability, hydrofracture of buttressing shelves) are links with thin instruments. The world literature calls them deep uncertainty and low-likelihood, high-impact storylines. The correct verdict is `gap` of the unreachable-at-this-budget kind, not underivable in principle. They have registered in paleo constraints and in observed grounding-line retreat, so there is an edge to extend from; this is not a sensor for something that has never registered (Fig 6 :34-35).

- **Mode:** `specified`.
- **Refuter:** a process-resolving observation that closes the tail into a tight, well-settled band, showing the link had an adequate instrument all along. Then the `gap` collapses and the tail moves to `specified` or `grounded`.
- **Status:** triangulated (Fig 3 instrument rule, Fig 6 instrument rule, Fig 8 proof-of-absence rule).

### F5 : Independence is debited, so more agreeing models are one witness (`general`)

Fig 7 :12-13: *"same closure, handed along; agreement compounds, evidence does not"* Fig 7 :35: *"A room's agreement is variation, not a verdict. Selection between rooms is where the ground votes."* Fig 8 :48: *"How much two frames draw on the same evidence decides both whether their agreement means anything and whether their union has any surplus."* Fig 2 :42: *"Buy independence in proportion to the cost of being wrong."*

Multi-model intercomparison is valuable precisely where closures differ (different ice-sheet physics, different ocean forcing, different initialisation). Where they share data and lineage, their spread is not independent corroboration. Fig 9 :60: *"Use filters for the budget and witnesses for the verdict. Never let a filter hand you a verdict."* A *likely* range is a filter for the budget; it is not a witness.

- **Mode:** `general`.
- **Refuter:** exhibit two model frames with genuinely disjoint evidence, different instruments and no shared calibration, whose agreement nevertheless adds no surplus. That would falsify the independence rule here.
- **Status:** triangulated (Fig 7, Fig 8, Fig 2).

### F6 : The cost if wrong is high, so the independence purchase is mandatory (`general`)

Fig 2 :42 again: *"Buy independence in proportion to the cost of being wrong."* The cost of a wrong ice-sheet rate is borne by coastal defence design, zoning, managed retreat and infrastructure lifetime, all of which commit capital for decades. The figures therefore do not permit a cheap consensus here. This is a design instruction, not an empirical claim.

- **Mode:** `general`.
- **Refuter:** show a decision domain with the same structure where being wrong is cheap enough that a single agreeing frame is sufficient. That narrows the rule's scope rather than flipping it.
- **Status:** single-source on the figure side (Fig 2), with the real cost of coastal commitment corroborated by the world sources.

### F7 : Committed loss is not undoable on the horizon that matters (`general`)

Fig 11 :61: *"You can add an actor and you cannot subtract the habitat, because contact is a fact about the world and does not consult you."* Fig 11 :66: *"Isolation is not removal of the habitat. It is substitution of one habitat for another."* Fig 11 :68-69: *"what you failed to reproduce is invisible until the goal fails"* and *"what you unintentionally introduced is invisible until it acts"*

Once ice is discharged and grounding lines retreat past a threshold, the sea-level contribution is committed on centuries to millennia. Stabilising temperature does not subtract the habitat. The world evidence explicitly distinguishes rates that could slow from committed rise that continues. This matters to the verdict because an "undoable" assumption would make the high-end tail cheap to ignore.

- **Mode:** `general` for the asymmetry law, `specified` for its application to ice sheets.
- **Refuter:** an observation that a committed ice-sheet contribution can be removed on a planning timescale by a real, non-substitutive operation. None of the world sources reports one.
- **Status:** triangulated (Fig 11 asymmetry, world process understanding).

### F8 : The framework returns a shape, not a number: the literal rate is a `gap` (`general`)

Fig 9 :32: *"you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself"* Fig 13 :134: *"So the system can report that it lacks something rather than producing an answer anyway. Unreachable is a verdict here, not a silence,"* Fig 13 :135: *"It cannot say which primitive is missing, because naming it would mean already holding it."*

No figure line contains a rate, a millimetre, a Gt, or a warming pathway. Deriving the number from the six-term substrate would require an import with a source gate and a shadow test. The import is available (the observations), but the figure cannot name it; the world evidence supplies it as secondary corroboration. So the honest report is: the shape is figure-governed, the number is ground-governed and comes from outside the figures, and this run does not manufacture one.

- **Mode:** `general`.
- **Refuter:** a derivation of a numerical ice-sheet rate from the six-term set alone, with no imported observation and no borrowed physics, that matches the measured record. Until one is shown, the number stays a declared gap.
- **Status:** triangulated (Fig 9, Fig 13, canonical figures README).

## 4. Pre-registered refuter (written before the conclusion)

Per Seat Map :287: *"a falsifier written before the run is a design instrument, not only an honesty device."*

The shape verdict is refuted if any of the following holds:

1. A figure line is produced that licenses aggregating Greenland and Antarctica into one residual slot and shows the aggregate predicts each sheet as well as the per-sheet treatment (breaks F1, Fig 1 :19, Fig 9 :54).
2. A model consensus is shown to move the observed mass-balance anchor rather than range against it, while surviving an independent instrument (breaks F2, Fig 2 :33).
3. A committed millennial contribution is shown to be a lossless time derivative of a 2100 rate (breaks F3, Fig 4 :30).
4. A process-resolving instrument closes the deep-uncertainty tail into a tight settled band (retires F4 to `specified`).
5. Two frames with disjoint evidence and no shared calibration are shown to add no surplus (breaks F5, Fig 8 :48).
6. A numerical ice-sheet rate is derived from the six-term substrate alone with no imported observation (breaks F8).

If any fires, the corresponding finding is withdrawn and the verdict is recomputed. Until one is run, no forecast claim may be labelled `grounded`.

## 5. Recommendation

1. Lead with the union unbundling and the per-slot rule (F1), not with a number.
2. State the horizon with every rate; never compress commitment into 2100 (F3).
3. Name the ground (altimetry, gravimetry, tide gauges) and deny model consensus the status of anchor (F2, F5).
4. Report the instability tail as a `gap`, with the instrument that would close it, not as a fake precision (F4).
5. Price the cost if wrong before buying independence (F6), and price irreversibility (F7).
6. Put the derivation before the import, with dates, so provenance is checkable (Fig 8 :51).
7. Send the generator, the shape and the refuter, not a recording of one projection. Fig 4 :22: *"Only methods cross up. Only head starts come down."*
