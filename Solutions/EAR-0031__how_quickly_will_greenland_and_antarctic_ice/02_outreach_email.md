# Outreach draft : check the shape before you decide whether the method is worth reading

**Use:** one-to-one, or as a cover note attached to a submission through the public venues in `03_public_contact_points.csv`. **Not** for bulk send.

**Hard precondition:** do not send until the refuter in `01` section 4 has been checked and the shape claim in `08` survives validation. The offer collapses on contact if the output does not survive.

**Subject:** One checkable claim about ice-sheet sea-level rates, before any theory

> Hi [name],
>
> I have one specific output I would like you to check against what you already know. I am not asking you to read a framework first, and I am not asking you to agree with anything. If the output does not survive, there is nothing else to look at.
>
> **The output (`general` on the shape, `specified` on the physical distinction, not `grounded` as a forecast):** the question "how quickly will Greenland and Antarctic ice loss raise sea levels under different warming pathways" is a union of at least four overlapping conditions, not one rate. It bundles sheet identity, surface mass balance versus dynamical discharge, a 2100 rate versus a multi-century committed total, and the choice of warming pathway. The residual must stay indexed per slot. Aggregating the two sheets into one number throws away the signal that tells you which lever works, because Greenland's recent loss is largely a surface-melt story while West Antarctica's is largely an ocean-forced ice-shelf and grounding-line story.
>
> A second point follows from that. "How quickly" names a subtraction over a chosen horizon. A rate to 2100 and the committed rise over two millennia are different quantities with different anchors, and compressing the second into the first loses the distinction the ground actually measures. The honest object is a per-sheet, per-pathway, per-horizon rate with a band set by process understanding, a separately stated committed total, and a separately stated deep-uncertainty tail for marine ice sheet instability and ice cliff failure.
>
> **What I am not claiming:** that any framework returns a millimetre-per-year number. It returns a shape, not a name and not a rate. I am not claiming that model agreement is an anchor; where models share forcing, ancestry and calibration, their agreement is one evidence pool talking. The anchor is the observation record: altimetry, gravimetry and tide gauges. I am not claiming the instability tail is absent; it is unreached at this budget, which is a verdict and not a silence. And I am not claiming this email settles anything. It is a simulated derivation checked for internal consistency, not a ground reading.
>
> **What would refute the shape claim, written before I sent this:**
> 1. a figure rule that licenses aggregating the two sheets and shows the aggregate predicts each as well as the per-sheet treatment;
> 2. a model consensus that moves the observed mass-balance record rather than ranging against it, and survives an independent instrument;
> 3. a committed millennial contribution shown to be a lossless derivative of a 2100 rate;
> 4. a process-resolving observation that closes the instabilities into a tight settled band;
> 5. two frames with genuinely disjoint evidence and no shared calibration whose agreement adds no surplus;
> 6. a numerical rate derived from the six-term substrate alone with no imported observation.
>
> If any one holds, I withdraw the corresponding claim. A one-line "this fails because..." is more useful to me than agreement. If you would rather not engage, no reply is needed.
>
> **If it survives, and only then:** the sources are below. The figures are the authority, not my summary of them.
> - Canonical figure text with line numbers (local extract): `FRAMEWORK/reference/03-FIGURES_TEXT.md`
> - Axes and citation rule: https://github.com/IsaiahN/tether/blob/general-agent/figures/README.md
> - The assessed physical basis the numbers come from: https://www.ipcc.ch/report/ar6/wg1/chapter/chapter-9/

Run it on a problem you know well and can check against a ground, write down what would refute your result *before* you run it, and state the mode of what you get. A reported failure is more useful to me than a reported success.

## Distribution rule

Stage 1: send the shape claim and the six refuters. Ask what the recipient would count as a failure. Record their verdict before showing any theory. Stage 2, opt-in only: offer the three links, with the note that the figures, not the summary, are the authority. Stage 3: offer the formula as a method, with the instruction that each user writes their own refuter and states their own mode. Never pool later agreement as corroboration (Fig 2 :40).
