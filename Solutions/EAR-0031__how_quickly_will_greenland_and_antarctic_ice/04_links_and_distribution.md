## Three things in the world sources that bear directly on this question

1. **The sheets are different domains.** IMBIE Greenland attributes roughly half its loss to surface mass balance and half to dynamic imbalance; IMBIE Antarctic attributes its loss mainly to West Antarctic outlet glaciers forced by ocean-driven ice-shelf basal melt. This is the physical reason the per-slot rule (Fig 1 :19) is not a stylistic preference. It changes which instrument and which lever matter.
2. **Rate and commitment are different quantities.** IPCC AR6 gives an assessed 2100 range and a separate committed rise over two millennia at a stated peak warming; DeConto and Pollard give a high-emissions tail to 2500. Compressing these into one "how quickly" number loses the distinction the measurement actually makes. This is the cross-scale round trip of Fig 4 :30.
3. **The tail is deep uncertainty, not absence.** IPCC explicitly excludes the marine ice sheet and marine ice cliff instability processes from its *likely* ranges because of limited process understanding and evaluation data, and gives them a low-likelihood high-impact storyline. That is exactly the `unreached at this budget` verdict of Fig 3 :27 and Fig 8 :39. Do not present the storyline as a settled prediction, and do not present its exclusion as proof it cannot happen.

## Distribution: offer, do not push

Figure-grounded reasons:

- **Fig 4 :22-25** : *"Only methods cross up. Only head starts come down. A recording carried upward looks like knowledge and is a description of one occasion."* Send the shape and the refuter first; offer the projection only after the recipient has described what they would count as failure.
- **Fig 10 :42** : *"A verifier that can reconstruct the claim cannot verify it."* A recipient who has absorbed the whole framework before being asked for a verdict has joined the frame being checked.
- **Fig 9 :27-28** : *"describe it before you go looking, or any frame you pick will seem to fit."* Ask what the recipient would expect to see if the per-slot shape claim were true before giving them the article.
- **Fig 2 :40** : *"agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool"* Never pool later agreement as corroboration. Most glaciology sources share observations and model ancestry; that is one pool.

**Recommended order in a reply:** (1) the per-sheet shape claim and the six refuters; (2) ask what they would count as a failure; (3) only then the three links, with the note that the figures, not the summary, are the authority.
