**Use:** one-to-one, or as a cover note attached to a submission through the public venues in `03_public_contact_points.csv`. **Not** for bulk send.

**Subject:** One checkable claim about the matter-antimatter asymmetry, before any theory

> Hi [name],
>
> I have one specific output I would like you to check against what you already know. I am not asking you to read a theory first, and I am not asking you to agree with anything. If the output does not survive, there is nothing else to look at.
>
> **The output (`specified`, not `grounded`):** the observed baryon asymmetry, eta about 6.1 x 10^-10, is one live slot in an otherwise successful frame. The Standard Model plus the standard hot thermal history does not produce it. The figure framework I am using says that a closed frame cannot generate a quantity its atoms do not reach, and that only an import from a frame whose closure differs can move that boundary. So the framework owes an import to the particle-physics frame. It does **not** name the import, and it does not claim that the framework itself has discovered new physics.
>
> The shape the import must have is five properties, stated before this message was written: baryon number violation, C and CP violation, operation out of thermal equilibrium, a prediction of eta inside the measured band, and at least one signature an existing or upcoming instrument could refuse. The first three are the Sakharov conditions. The framework treats them as one compound condition, not three independent problems: clearing one does not close the residual.
>
> Two mappings are offered with their status marked, not as derivations. C and CP violation resembles the framework's negation operator, a refusal of an invariance claim, and out-of-equilibrium supplies the directedness the framework calls a gradient, a difference that can be spent. Both are bindings from the physics frame onto held vocabulary. There is no figure term for broken baryon-number symmetry; I record that as a gap rather than dressing it as an answer.
>
> **What I am not claiming:** that new physics is confirmed, that a particular model is right, that the framework can tell you which heavy neutrino mass or Higgs sector nature chose, or that this settles the question. The framework returns a shape, not a name. "New physics" here means the particle-physics frame must gain access to a primitive it does not hold. Whether such a primitive exists in the world is for an instrument to decide.
>
> **What would refute the shape claim, written before this message:**
> 1. a Standard Model calculation, with no imported degrees of freedom, that produces eta in the measured band and is confirmed by both BBN and the CMB;
> 2. a confirmed mechanism that produces eta with one Sakharov condition demonstrably absent;
> 3. a measurement of eta consistent with zero at BBN and CMB precision;
> 4. all candidate frames excluded by EDM, Higgs, gravitational-wave and collider data to the point where the question is unreachable at this budget, which is a different fact from underivable.
>
> A one-line "this fails because..." is more useful to me than agreement. If you would rather not engage, no reply is needed.
>

## Two-stage offer, explicit

1. **Stage 1.** Send the residual, the five-part shape and the four refuters. Ask what the recipient would count as a failure. Record that verdict **before** any theory is shown.
2. **Stage 2, opt-in only.** If curious, offer the three links. The figures, not the summary, are the authority. A later agreement is never pooled as corroboration: Fig 2 :40 "disqualifying for a verdict: agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool".
3. **Stage 3.** Offer the formula as a method to reuse, with the instruction that each user states their own ground and writes their own refuter. The source article warns that solutions "could never be verified if the solutions presented were not contrived to fit the formula"; that warning travels with the method.
