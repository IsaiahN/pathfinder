## What an answer would look like

Stated as an ordinary term before the run (Formula step "Direction"): a **discriminator** is a procedure that, per event and per object slot, returns "pays at the ground / does not pay", with (i) a stated anchor, (ii) at least one independent second reading or a time comparison, (iii) a cost in both bits and actions, and (iv) a written refuter. A bare pathway name is not a discriminator; it is a candidate that still has to be checked at the ground.

## What is deliberately not here

- No personal emails of named individuals.
- No ranking of people by how likely they are to agree.
- No bulk-send plan.
- No claim that the figures name a molecular mechanism.

Three figure-grounded reasons:

1. **A room is not an anchor.** Fig 7 :35 *"A room's agreement is variation, not a verdict. Selection between rooms is where the ground votes."* Agreement from a list selected for receptiveness is a convention, not corroboration.
2. **Correlation disqualifies a verdict.** Fig 2 :40 *"disqualifying for a verdict: agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool"*. Two readers of the same review are one witness.
3. **Do not hand the interior to the checker.** Fig 10 :42 *"A verifier that can reconstruct the claim cannot verify it."* The first contact carries the residual and its refuter, never the framework.

Legally: unsolicited bulk email scraped from personal pages breaches GDPR and CAN-SPAM style rules in many jurisdictions. Public institutional and opt-in channels avoid that failure as well as the structural one.
