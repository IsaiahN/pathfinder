## Round trip gap R_T

The Symbols table defines `R_T` as the gap between what was sent up and what came back down, `gap(x, (T_E ∘ T_A)(x))`, measuring what the coarser description cannot hold. Fig 4 :30 requires it to be measured rather than assumed. I ran the round trip on the condensation the folder actually uses.

1. **Sent up (x):** the verbatim figure set, chiefly Fig 5 :55, Fig 6 :22 and :42-43, Fig 11 :39 and :49, Fig 9 :55.
2. **Abstraction (T_A):** the shorthand in `01` §3: constrain is fall by addition, enable is rise by import, valence is relative, capability is habitat-bound, rule needs grounding.
3. **Concretisation (T_E):** a concrete claim about a named institution, for example "WTO rules constrain a domestic subsidy" or "trade agreements enable export policy."

**R_T is nonzero.** Four things the shorthand holds do not survive the trip back down:

- the **identity of the added term** (ruling, damages exposure, condition or domestic political cause), which the shorthand leaves as "a refusal";
- the **identity of the imported primitive** (access, finance, commitment device, coordination axis), which the shorthand leaves as "an atom";
- the **slot specification** without which "valence is relative" cannot be applied to any actual policy;
- the **enforcement channel** required to turn a declared rule into a grounded one.

Conversely, two things survive cleanly: a reach can fall by addition without deletion, and a reach can rise on contact with a different closure. That is the residue worth sending. `R_T` was **not** measured numerically, because no ground reading exists. It is recorded qualitatively as the enumerated set of dropped constraints. Treating "the formula is the condensed version of the figures" as identity would set `R_T` to zero and would be the dead-reckoning error Fig 4 :30 names. `09_watson_answer.md` now states this explicitly.
