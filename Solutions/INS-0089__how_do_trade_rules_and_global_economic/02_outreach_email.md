**Subject:** One refutable claim about "policy space", checked before any theory

> Hi [name],
>
> I have one specific claim I would like you to check against what you already know. I am not asking you to agree with it, and I am not asking you to read the framework first. If the claim does not survive, there is nothing else to look at.
>
> **The claim (`general` on the shape, `specified` on the mechanism, not `grounded`):** the question "do trade rules constrain or enable domestic policy?" does not have one answer. It has one answer per policy slot. Two different operations are hiding inside that question:
>
> 1. **Constrain:** a domestic instrument is legislated, but its predicted effect does not arrive. Nothing is deleted from the statute book. A refusing term was added (an obligation, a ruling, a damages exposure, a condition), and the added term removes the standing of that instrument to be relied on. The set of reachable policies falls because something was added.
> 2. **Enable:** a move that the domestic frame could not even compose becomes reachable after contact with a different frame. A primitive was imported (market access, finance, a credible commitment device, a coordination rule), and that added primitive expands what the frame can reach.
>
> A third claim connects them: the valence of an institution is relative to the policy slot, not a property of the institution. The same agreement can enable export access and constrain tariff autonomy at once. So "constrain or enable" is a bundled question that must be split before it is searched.
>
> **What I am not claiming:** that any named institution is always constraining or always enabling; that any named country's sovereignty has been lost or preserved; that the framework predicts magnitudes; or that this is a measurement. It is a shape and a mechanism. Where the figures do not govern, I record `gap`.
>
> **What would refute it (written before this message):**
> 1. a domestic policy whose reach falls with no term added from another frame;
> 2. a policy becoming reachable with no imported primitive;
> 3. the same institution showing identical valence across two policy slots that differ in the relevant respect;
> 4. a global average over policy slots that preserves the location of a live slot residual;
> 5. an agency-only reform that changes the reachable policy set with no change in contact with the institutional habitat.
>
> If any holds, I withdraw the relevant part. A one-line "this fails because..." is more useful to me than agreement. If you would rather not engage, no reply is needed.
>
> **If it survives, and only then:** here are the sources. The figures are the authority, not my summary of them.
> - Verbatim figure text with line numbers (local framework file): `FRAMEWORK/reference/03-FIGURES_TEXT.md`
> - Figures folder, branch `general-agent`: https://github.com/IsaiahN/tether/tree/general-agent/figures
> - Canonical usage rule: https://github.com/IsaiahN/tether/blob/general-agent/figures/README.md

> **If you want to use the machinery:** the formula is in the figures link. Run it on a problem you can check against a ground, write down what would refute your result *before* you run it, and state the mode of what you get. A reported failure is more useful to me than a reported success.
