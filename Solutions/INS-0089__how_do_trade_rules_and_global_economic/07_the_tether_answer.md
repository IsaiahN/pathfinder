# The Tether's answer: trade rules and domestic policy choice

**Mode: `general` on the shape, `specified` on the mechanisms, not `grounded`.** Simulated derivation checked against the figures, not a ground reading (`05_what_shape_means.md` §6). No country, treaty or magnitude is asserted.

## 1. The residual it answers

A domestic frame predicts that a legislated instrument returns its intended effect. The world often returns something else. The residual is per policy slot: Fig 1 :19 "The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated." The question "constrain or enable?" is a union of two operations on different slots, so it must be unbundled before it can be answered (Fig 9 :55: "A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones").

## 2. The shape

1. **Constrain is fall by addition.** The statute is not deleted. A refusing term is added, and that term removes the standing of the instrument to be relied on. Fig 5 :55: "What it removes is not a term but a term's standing to be composed over." Fig 6 :43: "something was added rather than because anything was deleted." A panel finding, a damages exposure, or a condition does the work; the reach falls though nothing was struck out.

2. **Enable is rise by import.** A move the domestic frame could not compose becomes reachable when a frame with a different closure supplies a primitive. Fig 6 :22: "IMPORT adds an atom, and the added atom is what expands the closure." Fig 8 :45: "Two frames together reach what neither reaches alone." Market access, finance, a commitment device, or a coordination axis is such a primitive.

3. **Valence is relative to the slot.** Fig 11 :49: "An actor's valence is relative to the residual, not a property it carries." The same agreement can open export access and close tariff autonomy at once. There is no standing answer to "is this institution constraining or enabling?" There is an answer per slot.

4. **Capability belongs to government and habitat.** Fig 11 :39: "capability is a property of agent-and-habitat, never of the agent alone." Agency-only reform does not enlarge the choice set unless it changes contact. Withdrawal is substitution of one habitat for another, not subtraction (Fig 11 :66).

5. **A rule must be grounded to constrain.** Fig 10 :49: "Declaring a meaning does not ground it." A treaty text is a declared meaning; the panel, tribunal, disbursement or market reaction is the ground. An institution whose finding moves when the domestic frame pushes on it is not an anchor.

6. **The compound is structured, not flat.** The domestic/external bond is ordered and productive (an obligation is produced that did not exist before), and the objective "policy autonomy" is a comparison. See `06_isomers_and_order.md`. Two regimes with identical instruments can therefore be isomers: same atoms, different arrangement, different reachable set.

## 3. What it explicitly does not say

- Not that any named institution is always constraining or always enabling. Valence is relative (§2.3).
- Not that a country's sovereignty has been lost or preserved. That is an aggregate over slots, and the aggregate is exactly what Fig 1 disqualifies.
- Not a magnitude, a forecast, or a country-specific prediction. The figures govern the shape, not the fill.
- Not that a declared commitment is ineffective. It is a declared meaning until an enforcement channel grounds it (§2.5).
- Not `grounded`. Nothing here was settled by a dataset, a ruling, or a market observation.
- Not a claim that the six-term physical substrate maps onto legislatures and trade panels. That mapping was refused as a strain in `06_isomers_and_order.md`.

## 4. What would settle it: the ground

A source-discriminating instrument, not another argument. *The Hard Problem is About Source* (read 2026-11-23): an instrument that reads green "cannot comment on the source," and the case was settled "not by argument, but by the arrival of a better instrument." Observing that a subsidy was withdrawn does not tell us which mechanism fired.

Concretely:

1. **For constrain:** a final adverse ruling plus the compliance record, showing that reliance on the measure ended while the measure stayed on the books (or was withdrawn in response to the ruling and not to a domestic cause).
2. **For enable:** a documented case where a policy became composable only after an external primitive (access, finance, commitment device, coordination rule) arrived.
3. **For relative valence:** the same institution observed on two slots with opposite effects, with the residual for each described independently.
4. **For habitat-bound capability:** a comparison of agency-only reform against contact-changing reform, holding the habitat fixed in the first and changed in the second.
5. **For grounding:** an explicit enforcement channel with an observable verdict, as distinct from a signed text.

Fig 2 :45: "Frames range against the ground. They do not negotiate with it." Fig 7 :35: "A room's agreement is variation, not a verdict. Selection between rooms is where the ground votes."

## 5. The honest stop

**Verdict on shape, `gap` on specifics.** The figures settle the form of the relation and refuse the unqualified question. They do not settle which policy, which institution, or how much. Fig 13 :134: "Unreachable is a verdict here, not a silence." Fig 8 :39: "So a search that finds nothing is never a proof of absence."

This run stops at the limit of what the available instruments distinguish and records the lineage so the stop is checkable. The five refuters in `01` §4 were written before this conclusion. Fig 4 :30 requires the round-trip gap to be measured, not assumed: "So take the round trip and measure the gap. Without it you are navigating by dead reckoning: confident about a position nobody checked." `11_audit.md` records that gap qualitatively; no numeric `R_T` is claimed because no ground reading was taken.

## 6. One-paragraph version for outreach

> The Tether does not answer "do trade rules constrain or enable domestic policy?" with a single verdict, because that question bundles two different operations over different policy slots. Constrain is a fall in the reachable policy set caused by an added term: a ruling, a damages exposure, or a condition removes a measure's standing without deleting it from the statute book. Enable is a rise in reach caused by an imported primitive: market access, finance, a commitment device, or a coordination axis that the domestic frame could not compose on its own. Valence is relative to the slot, so the same agreement can open export access and close tariff autonomy at the same time, and capability belongs to the government and its institutional habitat rather than to the government alone. A signed rule is a declared meaning until an enforcement channel grounds it. The framework returns that shape and stops there: which policy, which institution, and by how much are a `gap` until a ruling, a dataset, or a market observation closes them. That is a report of what the figures govern and what they do not, with the refuters written in advance, not a measurement and not a final theory.
