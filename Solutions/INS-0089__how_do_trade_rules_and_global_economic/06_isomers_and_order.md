## RESIDUAL

Three questions are owed by `01`: (1) what *kind* of bond joins a domestic policy move to an external rule, (2) can two policy regimes be **isomers** under Fig 12, and (3) is an institution a catalyst, a functional group, a cycle, or a one-off chain? These are structure questions. They decide whether the shape in `07` is a molecule or just a list of ingredients.

- **Bonds held:** the seven, plus negation. Of these, `→` (sequence), `⇒` (production), `⋛` (comparison) and `¬` (refusal) do the work here.

## Q1. What bond joins a domestic move to an external rule?

- Symmetric bonds (`+`, `∥`, `≡`) cannot be right. A symmetric bond means swapping operands changes nothing. Swapping them does change things: a panel ruling flowing to a domestic frame is not the same as domestic non-compliance flowing back. So the relation is **ordered**.
- Operators table :30: "A then B; B needs A to have happened" defines `→` sequence. A measure followed by a review is sequence.
- Operators table :36: "A produces B; B did not exist before" defines `⇒` production. An adverse ruling **produces** an obligation that did not exist before the measure was challenged. That is production, not mere sequence. F1 in `01` is therefore a `⇒` bond with a `¬` refusal attached, which is why the reach falls by addition.
- Operators table :49 defines `⋛` as "a threshold relation between two quantities." "Policy autonomy" is exactly such a comparison: what the frame can compose against what the habitat permits. Fig 12 :112: "A plan is a sequence. A goal is a comparison. Progress is a subtraction." Autonomy is a goal, hence a comparison, not a stock of sovereignty.

**Result:** the domestic/external relation is `⇒` (production of an obligation) composed with `¬` (refusal of the instrument's standing), and the objective towards which it is measured is `⋛` (a comparison). Treating the relation as `+` (both just hold) is the flat-formula error Fig 12 names.

**Falsifier:** show that swapping domestic and external operands leaves the outcome unchanged, or that the obligation existed before the ruling. Either collapses the relation to a symmetric bond and removes the basis for F1.

## Q2. Are two policy regimes isomers?

Fig 12 :117: "Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."

Take two regimes with the **same** atoms: one tariff, one subsidy, one scheduled commitment, one enforcement channel.

- Regime A: commitment bonded to market access by `⇒` (access produced by the commitment), subsidy bonded to the tariff by `+` (both held at once).
- Regime B: the same four atoms, but the commitment bonded to the subsidy by `⇒` and to market access only by `→`.

Same ingredients, different arrangement, different reachable set. Under Fig 12 these are **isomers**, not variants of one policy, and the difference is "invisible in the formula" of a policy platform that lists only instruments. This is the structural reason two countries with identical stated policies can have different policy spaces.

The weaker reading is also available: both regimes share a **functional group**, Fig 12 :123: "functional group. A substructure whose presence fixes the outcome. The group says what kind; the qualifier says which member." The presence of an enforcement channel fixes whether a commitment is a constraint; the qualifier says which institution holds it.

**Falsifier:** if swapping the bonds leaves the reachable policy set unchanged, the atoms carry no arrangement-specific information and the isomer claim fails; only the functional-group claim survives.

## Q3. Is an institution a catalyst or a functional group?

Fig 12 :119: "catalyst. A part that survives its own use. Spent and regenerating parts behave differently across uses, and nothing records which a term is."

- A standing dispute body is catalyst-like: it survives its own use across cases.
- A one-off conditionality tranche is spent: it does not regenerate unless renewed.

The difference matters for F6. A convention that decides an outcome has "stopped being one" (Fig 10 :47), but a catalyst that survives its use can keep deciding outcomes across slots. Recording which a term is, is part of the provenance obligation.

Fig 12 :123 (functional group) applies to the *enforcement channel*: its presence fixes whether a declared rule is grounded. Its qualifier names the body. This is the structural version of F6.

**Falsifier:** a body that is spent by its first use yet continues to determine outcomes across later slots. That would break the catalyst/spent distinction and weaken F6.

## Q4. Is rule-following a cycle or a chain?

Fig 12 :121: "cycle. The output re-enters as input. The sign of the loop decides whether it closes, and a chain cannot close by construction."

Domestic measure → external challenge → ruling → domestic compliance or redesign → new measure. The output re-enters as input, so this is a **cycle**. A chain model ("rules imposed, policy adjusted, end") cannot close by construction and therefore cannot represent the recurring character of trade disputes. The sign of the loop decides whether it closes: a loop that returns to the same slot with an unchanged residual is closed in the bad sense (motion, no progress, Fig 7 :13 "agreement compounds, evidence does not"); a loop that returns with the slot re-described is progress.

**Falsifier:** if the sequence demonstrably terminates without re-entering (one measure, one ruling, no redesign), the cycle reading fails and the chain reading stands. This is an empirical question, not a figure question, so it is recorded as `gap` until a ruling series is read.

## Q5. Order across levels

Fig 12 :104: "A settled molecule becomes an atom for whatever composes over it. So irreducible is a fact about a level and never about the world."

A domestic policy settled against its own ground (implemented, tested, working) becomes an atom at the next level, where it is composed with an external rule. This is why F1 and F2 are second-order and need no new primitive. Fig 4 :30 requires the round trip to be measured: "So take the round trip and measure the gap. Without it you are navigating by dead reckoning: confident about a position nobody checked." The level-1 description "a subsidy works at home" and the level-2 description "a subsidy is scheduled internationally" are not the same object; the loss between them is `R_T` and must be reported, not assumed zero.

**Falsifier:** a settled domestic policy that cannot serve as an operand for an external composition would break the recursion and force a new primitive.

## CONCLUSION

The two-frame relation is ordered and productive (`⇒` plus `¬`), not a symmetric co-presence; the objective is a comparison (`⋛`). Two regimes with identical instruments can be Fig 12 isomers when the bonds differ, which explains why a list of policies is not a policy space. A standing institution is catalyst-like, an enforcement channel is a functional group, and rule-following is a cycle rather than a chain. All of this is `specified`, unmeasured, and independent of the refused Fig 13 mapping.
