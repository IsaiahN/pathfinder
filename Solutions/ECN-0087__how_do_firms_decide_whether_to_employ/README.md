# Solution ECN-0087  -  figure-grounded package

**Question:** How do firms decide whether to employ workers directly, use contractors, or outsource production?

**Source:** economics questions.txt line 115 (question 87 in the source section)

**Section:** 9. Firms, markets, and business power

**Field for public contact points:** firm boundary and employment research

**Mode:** `specified` on the applied firm-boundary shape, `general` on the structural laws, not `grounded`. No measurement of a specific firm's total cost under each governance mode is claimed here, so nothing in this folder is `grounded`.

**Status:** done

**Registry status:** pending

**Standing of this work:** a simulated derivation checked for internal consistency against the figures, not a ground reading. In the words of *The Recursive Self-Improvement Trap*, "the truly testable output would need to be derived from the real architecture or working the formula yourself." This package cannot settle the question it reports on. It states the shape any answer must have, the refuters written in advance, and the instruments that would close it.

---

## The question restated as an effect

For one activity inside one firm, which governance frame (direct employment, a contractor, or an outside supplier) keeps the activity reachable, that is, keeps the productive atoms composable, at the lowest total of two costs that do not add: the cost of describing the task in advance and the cost of finding out afterwards whether it was done. The market is named as an anchor in Fig 2 :29, so realized total cost and quality are the ground. The choice is indexed per activity, not per firm average (Fig 1 :19). A firm-wide outsourcing percentage hides exactly the live slot that matters (Fig 1 :22).

An answer would look like a per-activity rule with a stated ground and a stated falsifier. That sought-for shape is bound as an ordinary term in `01`, so it can accrue a residual like anything else (THE_FORMULA, "Direction").

## What is deliberately not here

- No personal emails of named individuals.
- No ranking of people by how likely they are to believe the shape claim.
- No bulk-send plan.
- No claim that the figures hand down a universal numeric threshold for make or buy. That is declared as a `gap` in `01` F7.

Three reasons, in the framework's own words:

1. **A room does not upgrade the label.** Seat Map :283: "A room reaching a conclusion without the ground is agreement, and agreement among frames is a weighted average of influence. Adding people raises the vocabulary and does not add an anchor." A list of sympathetic economists produces a convention, not a verdict.
2. **Correlation disqualifies a verdict.** Seat Map :163: "High correlation is disqualifying for a verdict and efficient for a convention." Fig 2 :40: "agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool." Selecting recipients for likely agreement manufactures the correlation that would make their agreement worthless.
3. **Asking for trust before checking spends independence.** Fig 10 :42: "A verifier that can reconstruct the claim cannot verify it." The first contact carries the residual and its refuter, never the framework's interior.

Legally: unsolicited bulk email to scraped personal addresses breaches GDPR and CAN-SPAM style rules in many jurisdictions. All public routes in `03` are institutional and opt-in. The Academy of Management also states that submitted work must be created by the authors and not be the product of AI tools unless appropriate to the research question and properly cited, which is a further reason to disclose this package's simulated status rather than pass it off as a human field study.
