**Subject:** One checkable output on the make / buy / employ boundary

> Hi [name],
>
> I have one specific output I would like you to check against what you already know about firm boundaries. I am not asking you to read the framework first, and I am not asking you to agree with it. If the output does not survive, there is nothing else to look at.
>
> **The output (`specified`, not `grounded`).** The decision of whether to employ a worker, use a contractor, or outsource an activity is a per-activity choice, not a firm-wide ratio. Three claims follow from that:
>
> 1. A firm internalizes an activity when its own closure cannot reach the atoms the activity needs; a supplier or contractor is the frame whose closure differs and whose contact moves the boundary. The boundary is where internal composition stops paying.
> 2. Overlap decides the governance mode. When the activity is standard and the firm can switch partners easily, the market price is a clean anchor and an arms-length contract works. When the assets are relationship-specific and the parties are mutually dependent, the two sides behave like one frame under two names, and authority inside one boundary becomes the cheaper way to resolve conflict.
> 3. The choice costs in two currencies that do not add: the bits needed to describe the task in advance, and the actions needed to find out afterwards whether it was done. A single all-in cost number hides the distinction, and the distinction is where the decision lives.
>
> A contractor is not a point between "employ" and "outsource." It is a compound: a hierarchy inside the contractor and a market relation at the interface, so it needs its own check rather than being averaged into a make-or-buy line.
>
> **What I am not claiming.** I am not claiming a universal numeric threshold, a best practice that fits every firm, or that market price alone settles the question. Price is the anchor for cost and an imperfect instrument for quality and specificity. The numeric boundary is declared a gap in my notes, not filled with an invented number.
>
> **What would refute this:**
> 1. a firm renewing the reach of an activity by internal composition alone, adding a productive atom it did not hold, with no contact with a differing closure;
> 2. a relationship-specific, mutually dependent activity repeatedly run by a clean arms-length contract with no hold-up or renegotiation cost, checked against realized total cost;
> 3. a single scalar that predicts the governance choice across contexts without losing the distinction between describing the task and verifying it;
> 4. a firm-level average that predicts per-activity governance as well as a per-activity measurement.
>
> I wrote these down before contacting you. If any one holds, I will retract the part it breaks. A one-line "this fails because..." is more useful to me than agreement. If you would rather not engage, no reply is needed.
>
> **If it survives, and only then:** here are the working notes and the source material. The figures, not my summary, are the authority.
> - Full derivation and retraction log: the `01_figure_grounded_audit.md` file in this folder.
> - Canonical figures and formula, branch `general-agent`: https://github.com/IsaiahN/tether/tree/general-agent/figures
> - The transaction-cost and contract-theory sources I read: https://www.nobelprize.org/prizes/economic-sciences/2009/popular-information/ and https://www.nobelprize.org/prizes/economic-sciences/2016/popular-information/
>
> **If you want to use the method:** run it on an activity you know well and can check against a ground, and write down what would refute your result *before* you run it. A reported failure is more useful to me than a reported success.

## Two-stage rule for the reply

1. **Stage 1.** Record the reader's verdict on the four refuters and on the three claims, in writing, before any framework material is shown.
2. **Stage 2, opt-in only.** If they are curious, send the figure and source links. Their later agreement is never pooled as corroboration, because by then they hold the framework's interior (Fig 2 :40; Seat Map :163).
3. **Stage 3, opt-in only.** Offer the formula as a method to reuse, with the instruction that each user writes their own refuter and states their own mode.
