# The Tether's answer on employ / contract / outsource

**Mode: `specified` on the firm application, `general` on the structural laws, not `grounded`.** This is a shape and a route, not a result. Simulated derivation checked against the figures (`05_what_shape_means.md` §6), not a ground reading.

## 1. The residual it answers

For one activity, the firm must place it in a governance frame: its own hierarchy, a contractor, or an outside supplier. What fails to resolve is which frame keeps the activity reachable at the lowest total of two costs that do not add, while the realized cost and quality ground stays in view. The residual is per activity slot, not per firm (Fig 1 :19: "The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated"). It is a **union compound**, not one hard question, because contracting is simultaneously hierarchy and market (Fig 9 :55: "A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones"). It has its own loop and its own ground.

## 2. The shape

1. **Decide per activity, not per firm average.** Fig 1 :22: "Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average." Different activities in the same firm belong in different frames simultaneously.
2. **The boundary is the reach of the firm's closure.** Fig 6 :23: "No frame escapes its own closure. The bound moves only on contact with a frame whose closure differs." Internalize when the firm's closure already holds the atoms the activity needs; go outside when the needed atom lives in a frame whose closure differs. Fig 8 :45: "Two frames together reach what neither reaches alone."
3. **Overlap decides the governance mode.** Fig 8 :49: "Two frames that overlap almost completely are one frame wearing two names." Where the activity is standard and partners are switchable, frames are independent and the market price is a clean anchor. Where assets are relationship-specific and the parties are mutually dependent, the two sides are one frame under two names, haggling replaces triangulation, and authority inside a boundary becomes the cheaper conflict resolver. Fig 2 :42: "Buy independence in proportion to the cost of being wrong."
4. **Two costs, in different units, held apart.** Fig 12 :125: "A composition costs in two currencies, and they do not add." Fig 12 :126: "Bits price the description. Actions price finding out whether it holds." A contract that cannot specify future states must allocate decision rights; a firm cannot see monitoring it does not measure. The all-in price is a projection, not the decision.
5. **The three modes are isomers, and contracting is a compound.** Fig 12 :117: "Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour." Contracting is a union of internal hierarchy and external price, so it must be checked on both conditions, not averaged into a make-or-buy line.
6. **The ground is realized cost and quality, not agreement.** Fig 2 :29 names a market as an anchor; Fig 2 :35 says "Alignment is triangulation, not negotiation. A mirror cannot correct you." Industry consensus about best practice is not a settlement.
7. **No universal number.** Fig 9 :32: "you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself." The figures constrain the shape of a boundary rule; they do not supply its parameter values. That is declared `gap` in `01` F7.

## 3. What it explicitly does not say

- Not a universal make-or-buy threshold, and not a claim that any figure line yields one.
- Not "outsourcing is always cheaper" or "employment is always safer." The choice is per slot and per measured ground.
- Not that market price settles the whole question. Price is the anchor for cost and an instrument, wrong on axes it does not measure, for quality and specificity.
- Not that a contractor is a midpoint between employee and supplier. It is a different bond over the same atoms and a union of two conditions.
- Not a derivation of Coase, Williamson, Hart or Holmström from the figures. Those are imports with provenance; they corroborate the shape after it was derived, and they never overrule a figure.
- Not `grounded`. No firm-level measurement is reported here.

## 4. What would settle it  -  the ground

An instrument that separates the two currencies and measures realized total cost and quality per activity against a market benchmark. Fig 8 :39: "So a search that finds nothing is never a proof of absence." The specific tests are:

- **Per-slot test:** does a per-activity description predict the governance choice better than a firm-wide average? (breaks F1 if not)
- **Specificity test:** does a relationship-specific, mutually dependent activity repeatedly do fine under a clean arms-length contract with no hold-up cost? (breaks F3 if yes)
- **Two-currency test:** do two activities with identical predicted all-in cost but opposite specifiability and observability still get correctly governed by a single scalar? (breaks F4 if yes)
- **Union test:** is a contractor arrangement fully described by one condition alone, with no internal hierarchy or no switchable external partner? (breaks F5's compound reading if yes)
- **Channel test:** is the realized cost ground still reachable, or have the metrics saturated, dropped transients, or pooled readings across a changed activity? (Fig 10 :25-26)

A source with a genuinely different evidence pool could also reverse the verdict. The DOL economic-reality rule, the biology analogue of symbiosis, and Simon's bounded rationality are such pools, but each corroborates a mechanism, not the firm-level fact.

## 5. The honest stop

**Unreached, not unreachable.** The numeric boundary is unreachable from the figures as currently held; it lives in the ground. Fig 13 :134: "Unreachable is a verdict here, not a silence." *They're Scanning Books* records the limit honestly: "A minimum viable set of six terms is a human's careful guess at reality's compression axes; another sensorium's minimum set need not be those six." The shape stops where the instruments stop, and the lineage is recorded so the stop is checkable. *How Intelligence Actually Works*: "The recursion converges toward indiscernibility, and indiscernibility is not identity."

## 6. One-paragraph version for outreach

> The Tether does not hand down a universal make-or-buy rule. It says that the choice belongs to each activity, not to a firm-wide average; that a firm internalizes an activity until its own closure stops reaching the atoms that activity needs, and goes outside when a different closure already holds them; that low overlap between firm and supplier favors a market while high overlap pushes toward authority inside one boundary; and that the decision costs in two currencies that do not add, the bits needed to describe the task and the actions needed to verify it. Employment, contracting and outsourcing are the same productive atoms under different governance bonds, and contracting is a union of hierarchy and market, not a point between them. The figures give this shape and four refuters written in advance. They do not give the number. That stays open until a ground, a measured total cost and quality per activity, settles it.
