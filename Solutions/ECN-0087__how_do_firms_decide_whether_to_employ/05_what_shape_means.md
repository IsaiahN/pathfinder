## 1. A shape is a constraint, not a filler

*The Library of Babel*: "you can cut your search short if the set of books returned is small and descriptive enough that it provides a general shape of the answer that satisfies your question." Fig 9 :32: "you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself."

For this question, a **shape** is the set of constraints any correct make / buy / employ answer must satisfy, stated before the numeric or firm-specific content is available:

- the decision is per activity slot, not per firm average (Fig 1 :19);
- the boundary is where internal composition stops reaching an atom (Fig 6 :23);
- overlap between the two frames decides whether the market or hierarchy governs (Fig 8 :49);
- the two costs, description and verification, are in different units and do not add (Fig 12 :125);
- contracting is a union compound, not a third point on a line (Fig 9 :55);
- the ground is realized economic outcome, not agreement (Fig 2 :29).

"Employ / contract / outsource by slot, with two costs held apart and a measured ground" is a shape. "Specificity above 0.7 means outsource" is a name, and the framework does not supply it.

## 2. How the shape is obtained: proximity, then effect terms

- *Library of Babel*: "With each new term gained, you narrow the space and deepen your vocabulary." The vocabulary closest to the residual here is transaction cost, asset specificity, residual control rights, and monitoring. Recursing on those terms narrows the search to the firm-boundary literature.
- Fig 6 :30: "outward: does another frame already hold it? The description travels. Fails by being sharpened into terms only we can read. Cast it in effect terms." Fig 9 :30-31: describe "the effect rather than the cause." So the residual is described as "an activity whose needed atoms are or are not reachable inside the current frame and whose two costs are or are not separable," not as "a governance mode chosen by a manager."
- *Decoding Tacit Knowledge*: "When contact isn't possible, the best you can send is a plain description of what you'd expect to see, because anyone can check that against the world." That is why `02_outreach_email.md` leads with four refuters a practitioner can check without accepting the framework.
- *Arrival of the Fittest*: "a frame searching for an answer of a given SHAPE holds that shape as a PREFERENCE, not a term." The remedy is the one used in `01`: write the sought-for shape down as an ordinary term so it can accrue a residual.

## 3. What licenses a name, and the correction to my own first reading

Two things, both outside the shape itself:

1. **The ground settles it.** THE_FORMULA step 5: "the ground settles it. Verified only by what pays." A measured total-cost comparison under each governance mode, for the same activity, against a market benchmark, names the choice.
2. **A provenanced import from a frame whose closure differs** (Fig 6 :23, Fig 8 :45-46), with a source gate and a shadow test. Coase, Williamson, Hart and the DOL classification test are such imports; they are recorded with provenance in `10_source_ledger.csv`.

**Correction to §1 of my own reading.** I initially treated "shape, not a name" as forbidding any concrete governance recommendation at all. That overstates it. The canonical phrase governs a **missing** thing, a primitive or parameter not held in the frame. "Firm," "market," "contractor," "hierarchy," and "price" are already held in the economics frame; binding an activity to one of them is an ordinary import, not the invention of a seventh kind. What remains forbidden is claiming that the framework itself names the numeric threshold, or that an import that adds no atom moves the wall. *They're Scanning Books*: "Declaring the primitives does not make them neutral. It makes the choice visible." Both the original claim and this narrowing are kept here so neither is quietly lost.

## 4. Why the numeric threshold is a source error, not just an absent number

*The Hard Problem is About Source* gives the mechanism. An instrument that reads green "cannot comment on the source." Several paths can create the same behavior, so "behavior is a projection with lossy information." The reading does not determine the source. "The answer is unreached until a better instrument exists."

Applied here: two activities can both read as "contractor is cheaper" while one is cheap because the task is standardizable (low description bits, many alternative suppliers) and the other is cheap because the firm has suppressed the true monitoring cost (an unread channel). The reading cannot comment on the source. What is owed is a source-discriminating instrument that separates the two currencies, not a better rule of thumb. Fig 10 :25-26 names the failure: "stale percepts, dropped transients, saturated metrics, pooled readings."

*How Intelligence Actually Works*: "The recursion converges toward indiscernibility, and indiscernibility is not identity." A total-cost figure indiscernible from the true governance cost is still a projection, which is why the lineage has to be kept (Fig 13 :127-130).

## 5. The contractor question is a bundle, not one question

*Library of Babel*: "Perhaps your problem was a bundle or conflation of several problems masquerading as one?" Fig 9 :55: "A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones." *How a Society of Agents Would Work* adds the sharp operational form: "Use the success rate per try, not the total," because whatever gets tried most piles up votes. That is Fig 1 :22 applied to learning rather than to firms. For this question, averaging across activity slots or across the two conditions inside contracting is exactly the error. Each condition needs its own loop.

## 6. Two consequences for outreach

**Prestige is not action.** *The Recursive Self-Improvement Trap*: "Prestige can fund an action. It can give an action a budget to be tried. But it cannot settle an action. Only the ground can settle it." A campaign that gathers famous economists to agree with the shape claim is the named collapse. The legitimate ask gives the claim a budget to be tried and lets the ground settle it. *Decoding Tacit Knowledge* puts the same rule differently: "Sounding right isn't the same as being checked against the world."

**My own audit is a simulation, not a ground reading.** *The Recursive Self-Improvement Trap*: "the truly testable output would need to be derived from the real architecture or working the formula yourself." *Arrival of the Fittest*: LLM outputs "must be seen as simulacra," and the demonstration "could never be verified if the solutions presented were not contrived to fit the formula." So `01` is a simulated derivation checked for internal consistency against the figures. It is not evidence that the shape claim is true about real firms, and it cannot settle anything it reports on.

## 7. Scope limit, stated honestly

*They're Scanning Books*: "A minimum viable set of six terms is a human's careful guess at reality's compression axes; another sensorium's minimum set need not be those six." The framework's shape is a visible guess about how systems compose, not a discovery of nature's own list of governance modes. An economics frame that has never met the figure set can reject the mapping entirely; that rejection is a ground-facing correction, not a failure to agree. This solution does not claim otherwise.
