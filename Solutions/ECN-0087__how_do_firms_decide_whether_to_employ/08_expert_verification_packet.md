# Expert verification packet  -  the Tether on the make / buy / employ boundary

**What this is:** a claim in a form an economist, organizational scholar, or employment lawyer can check. Mode, derivation and refuters are stated before the conclusion, so a reader knows what to look for and what would settle it.

**Mode: `specified` on the firm application, `general` on the structural laws, not `grounded`.** No firm-level measurement is claimed. This is a shape and a route, not a result.

**Standing:** a simulated derivation checked for internal consistency against the figures, not a ground reading. It cannot settle the question it reports on.

## 1. The claim, in one paragraph

For one activity, the governance choice (employee, contractor, or outside supplier) is a per-slot decision, not a firm-wide ratio. A firm internalizes an activity until its own closure stops reaching the productive atoms that activity needs; a supplier is the frame whose closure differs and whose contact moves the boundary. Overlap between the two frames decides the mode: low overlap and switchable partners favor a market, while relationship-specific assets and mutual dependence push toward authority inside one boundary. The choice costs in two currencies that do not add, the bits needed to describe the task in advance and the actions needed to verify it afterwards, so a single all-in price hides the deciding quantity. Employment, contracting and outsourcing are the same atoms under different governance bonds, and contracting is a union of internal hierarchy and external price, not a midpoint on a line. The figures supply this shape and four refuters, and explicitly refuse to supply a universal numeric threshold.

## 2. Three checkable sub-claims

### C1  -  per-activity indexing and the closure boundary
- **Statement:** decide per activity slot. The boundary sits where composition inside the firm's closure stops reaching a needed atom; contact with a differing closure is the only way that bound moves.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 1 :19 "The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated." Fig 1 :22 "Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average." Fig 6 :23 "No frame escapes its own closure. The bound moves only on contact with a frame whose closure differs." Fig 8 :45 "Two frames together reach what neither reaches alone."
- **What to check:** in a firm you know, do the governance choices differ across activity slots in ways a firm-wide ratio hides? Is there an activity the firm cannot make internally for lack of an atom a supplier holds?
- **Refuter:** a firm-level average predicts per-activity governance as well as a per-activity measurement, or a firm renews the reach of an activity by internal composition alone with no external contact.

### C2  -  overlap decides market versus hierarchy
- **Statement:** asset specificity is overlap between the firm's frame and the supplier's frame. Low overlap gives the market a surplus and a clean price anchor; high overlap makes the pair "one frame wearing two names," and authority inside a boundary becomes the cheaper conflict resolver. Buy independence in proportion to the cost of being wrong.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 8 :49 "Two frames that overlap almost completely are one frame wearing two names." Fig 2 :42 "Buy independence in proportion to the cost of being wrong." Fig 2 :35 "Alignment is triangulation, not negotiation. A mirror cannot correct you."
- **What to check:** does a relationship-specific activity with no alternative trading partner stay under a clean arms-length contract? Does a standard activity with many partners get integrated anyway? Williamson's coal-mine and power-plant study (Nobel 2009 popular information, read 2026-11-23) is one public test case: "a coal-burning power plant that is located next to a coal mine is about six times more likely to be fully integrated than is any other coal-burning power plant."
- **Refuter:** a relationship-specific, mutually dependent activity repeatedly governed by an arms-length contract with no hold-up or renegotiation cost, checked against realized total cost.

### C3  -  two currencies and the contracting compound
- **Statement:** description and verification are different currencies and do not add. Contracting is a union of hierarchy (inside the contractor) and market (at the interface), so it needs both conditions checked, not an average.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 12 :125 "A composition costs in two currencies, and they do not add." Fig 12 :126 "Bits price the description. Actions price finding out whether it holds." Fig 12 :130 "Summing them into one figure is the error this names, not the fix for it." Fig 9 :55 "A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones." Fig 9 :57 "a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either"
- **What to check:** can you find two activities with the same predicted all-in cost but opposite rankings on specifiability and observability? Does any contractor arrangement you know have no internal authority and no switchable external partner? Coase's 1991 lecture (read 2026-11-23) names both currencies: "contracts have to be drawn up, inspections have to be made." The DOL economic-reality test (Fact Sheet 13, read 2026-11-23) says "No single factor determines a worker's status" and requires "the totality of the circumstances."
- **Refuter:** a single scalar that fully predicts the governance choice across contexts with no loss of the description/verification distinction, or a contractor described by one condition alone.

## 3. What is explicitly not claimed

- Not a universal numeric threshold. The threshold is declared `gap` in `01` F7; no figure line yields it.
- Not that outsourcing is always cheaper, employment always safer, or contracting always a hybrid compromise.
- Not that market price settles the whole choice. Price is the anchor for cost and an instrument, wrong on axes it does not measure, for quality and specificity.
- Not that the figures derive Coase, Williamson, Hart, Holmström, or the DOL test. Those are imports with provenance, secondary corroboration only.
- Not `grounded`. Nothing here is asserted as settled by a measurement of a real firm.
- Not a simulation-independent result. The derivation shares the framework's evidence pool; its agreement with the essays is internal consistency, not corroboration.

## 5. What would settle it

An instrument that separates the two currencies and measures realized total cost and quality per activity against a market benchmark. Specifically: a per-slot comparison beating a firm average; a hold-up test on relationship-specific assets; a two-currency discrimination test; a union test on contractors; and a channel test on the cost metrics (Fig 10 :25-26, "stale percepts, dropped transients, saturated metrics, pooled readings"). A refutation from a genuinely different evidence pool is copied into `11_audit.md` with provenance and re-evaluated by the auditor seat, as if it had come from any other source.

## 6. What an expert can check in twenty minutes

1. Read Fig 1 :19, Fig 6 :23, Fig 8 :49, Fig 12 :125-126, and Fig 9 :55. Five short passages.
2. Check C1 against one firm you know: are governance choices per-activity, and is there an activity whose atoms the firm cannot compose?
3. Check C2 and C3 against one contract you know: how specific are the assets, and can you name both the description cost and the verification cost separately?
4. Name one instrument that would separate those two costs for that activity, or say why none is possible.
5. Send the refutation. A failure is more useful than an endorsement.
