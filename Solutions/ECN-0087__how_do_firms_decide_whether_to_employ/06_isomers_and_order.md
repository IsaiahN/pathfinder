# Are employ, contract, and outsource isomers? Order, cost currencies, and analogues

**Mode: `specified`, not `grounded`.** This is a simulated derivation checked against the figures, not a ground reading (see `05_what_shape_means.md` §6).

## RESIDUAL

The main audit leaves two things unresolved: (1) whether direct employment, contracting and outsourcing are genuinely the same productive atoms arranged differently, or only a loose analogy, and (2) whether there is a meaningful order among the three, so that one is a precursor of another. A third question follows: what exactly is being priced when a firm compares them.

## DOMAIN CENSUS

Atoms held (from the economics frame, as imports with provenance): labor, capital, equipment, knowledge, risk, and decision rights. Bonds held: command (inside a firm), specified obligation plus residual control rights (a contract), and price (a market transaction), plus the seven figure bonds. No new productive atom is needed to describe any of the three modes. Frames held: D1 firm, D2 market, D3 the contracting compound, D4 the measurement channel (see `01` §1.2). Order by dependency: describe the activity, name the two costs, locate the frame, choose, measure.

## DERIVATION

### Q1  -  Are the three modes isomers?

- Fig 12 :117: "Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."
- Fig 12 :123: "functional group. A substructure whose presence fixes the outcome. The group says what kind; the qualifier says which member."
- Write the modes over the shared atom set {labor, capital, equipment, knowledge, risk, decision rights}. The atoms are the same in each case; what differs is the bond that carries them: command, contract, price. The behavioral difference is decisive: incentives, who absorbs residual risk, who can re-decide when the description runs out, and what the firm can learn from the activity.
- Fig 12 :94 (also Fig 12's Operators context): "The arrangement is the substance." So the modes are **isomers** under the figure's own definition when written over the full shared set.
- Weaker form: if a firm writes employment with atoms {labor, capital, equipment} and outsourcing with {labor, knowledge, decision rights}, they share only labor and are not isomers; they are two members of a **functional group** (Fig 12 :123), "the group says what kind; the qualifier says which member."
- **Falsifier, written before the run:** if one governance mode requires a productive atom the others do not, so the atom sets are not the same, the isomer claim fails and only the functional-group claim stands.
- Status: `single-source` for the figure definition (Fig 12), unmeasured at the firm level.

### Q2  -  What is being priced: two currencies, not one

- Fig 12 :125: "A composition costs in two currencies, and they do not add." Fig 12 :126: "Bits price the description. Actions price finding out whether it holds." Fig 12 :130: "Summing them into one figure is the error this names, not the fix for it."
- **Description bits:** how completely the activity can be stated before work begins. Where it cannot be stated, Hart and Holmström's result applies: the contract "must instead specify who has the right to decide what to do when the parties cannot agree" (Nobel 2016 popular background, read 2026-11-23). That is why decision rights are among the atoms.
- **Settling actions:** inspection, monitoring, dispute resolution, and the measurement that tells the firm whether the activity actually paid. Coase's own list (1991 Nobel lecture, read 2026-11-23) names both: "contracts have to be drawn up, inspections have to be made." These are different activities in different units.
- Consequence: a firm that minimizes only the quoted price of a contractor lowers description cost while raising unread settling cost. The all-in number looks better while the ground signal degrades. Fig 10 :24: "the ground does not decay, the channel does." Fig 10 :25-26: "stale percepts, dropped transients, saturated metrics, pooled readings."
- **Falsifier:** two activities with identical predicted total cost but opposite rankings on specifiability and observability, where a single scalar still selects the correct governance mode in both cases. If that holds, the two-currency claim fails.
- Status: `triangulated` (Fig 12 :125-130 and Fig 3 :25-27, the middle link with no instrument).

### Q3  -  Is there an order among the three modes?

- Fig 12 :72 (Operators table :30): "A then B; B needs A to have happened" is the sequence bond →. Applying it to governance would claim that employment precedes contracting precedes outsourcing as a developmental ladder. The figures give no such order.
- What the figures do give is a **dependency order of the decision**, not an order of the modes (Fig 3 :20: "The chain is ordered by dependency, not by importance"). Describe the activity, state what counts as progress, locate the frame, choose, measure. A mode is selected, not promoted from a lower rung.
- The biology analogue says something similar about ordering. *Symbiogenesis* (Wikipedia, read 2026-11-23): "multi-membraned eukaryotic organelles such as mitochondria and chloroplasts are endosymbionts descended from formerly free-living prokaryotes," and "the loss of autonomy and integration of the endosymbiont with its host" proceeds by gene transfer. There is a direction (free living toward integrated organelle), but it is a change of relation under consequence, not a universal ladder that every firm climbs. It is an analogue from a different pool, not a firm-level measurement.
- **Falsifier:** a documented, repeated sequence in which every firm that outsources an activity first employed it and then contracted it, with the transition order predicting total cost better than the per-slot atom and cost description. Until such a sequence is measured, the modes are a set, not a chain.
- Status: `single-source` for the negative claim (no figure line orders the modes), `gap` for the empirical sequence.

### Q4  -  Contracting is a union compound, so it needs its own loop

- Fig 9 :55: "A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones." Fig 9 :57: "a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either"
- A contractor is a hierarchy seen from inside (its own employees, its own closure) and a market counterparty seen from outside (a price, a contract, a switchable alternative). A single lever aimed at "contractors" clears the region where only one condition holds and leaves the intersection standing. The intersection is where hold-up, renegotiation, and residual control rights live.
- Fig 6 :23: "No frame escapes its own closure. The bound moves only on contact with a frame whose closure differs." Fig 8 :45: "Two frames together reach what neither reaches alone." The contractor exists because the union of the client's closure and the contractor's closure reaches what neither alone reaches. The surplus lives only where the closures differ, which is exactly why high overlap (relationship-specific assets) erodes the benefit of contracting.
- **Falsifier:** a contractor arrangement with no internal hierarchy (no authority relation inside the supplier) and no market interface (no switchable alternative, no price comparison), so that one condition alone describes it.
- Status: `triangulated` (Fig 9 union rule and Fig 6/Fig 8 closure rules, distinct figures). Corroborated from a different field by the DOL economic-reality test, which says "No single factor determines a worker's status" and requires "the totality of the circumstances" (read 2026-11-23). That is corroboration, not a witness for the verdict.

## CONCLUSION

The three governance modes are **isomers over a shared atom set**: same productive ingredients, different governance bonds, decisive behavioral difference. This is a structural claim with a stated falsifier, not a measurement. The modes are **not ordered as a ladder**; only the decision procedure is ordered by dependency. The choice costs in **two currencies that do not add**, so a single all-in number hides the very thing that decides it. Contracting is a **union compound** with its own loop, not a midpoint between employment and outsourcing. The flawed forms of each claim are preserved above rather than replaced.
