## 0. The residual, stated in effect terms

A firm holds an activity: a task, a production stage, a service. It must place that activity in one of three governance frames.

- **Employ directly.** The activity enters the firm's closure. The currency is command and salary. The firm composes it with what it already holds.
- **Use a contractor.** The activity stays in a different firm's closure and is reached through a contract. This is a compound: inside the contractor there is hierarchy, between contractor and client there is a market relation.
- **Outsource production.** The activity leaves entirely for a supplier whose closure differs from the firm's.

The residual R for one activity slot s is: which frame keeps the activity reachable (that is, keeps the productive atoms composable) at the lowest total of two costs that do not add, namely the cost of describing the task in advance and the cost of finding out afterwards whether it was done, while keeping the ground reachable and unmovable.

R is indexed per activity slot. Fig 1 :19: "The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated." Fig 1 :22: "Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average." A firm-wide "we outsource 30 percent" is not an answer; it is an average thrown over the live slots.

An anchor is available and is legitimate for this question. Fig 2 :29: "An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks. A measurement, an experiment, a counter, a market, a patient outcome." The market is named in the figure. Realized total cost and realized quality are the anchor. The firm's own accounting is not the anchor; it is a channel to it, and that channel decays (Fig 10 :24, :25-26).

**What an answer would look like (the sought-for shape, bound as an ordinary term).** A per-activity rule that (a) states which frame's closure holds the atoms the activity needs, (b) states the two costs separately, (c) names the ground that would settle the choice, and (d) carries a refuter written in advance. Binding this shape now lets it accrue a residual like anything else, which is the remedy THE_FORMULA "Direction" prescribes for a frame that cannot check its own search direction.

## 1. Domain census (before choosing any figure line)

### 1.1 R by arity, symmetry, scale

- **Arity.** Three named options, but structurally a two-place relation (inside against outside) with a hybrid middle. The hybrid is not a third point on a line; it is a union of two conditions, which is why Fig 9 :55 warns that a bundled problem is "a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones."
- **Symmetry.** The three options are not interchangeable. Their governance bond differs, so Fig 12 :117 applies: "Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour." The relevant comparison is a threshold (a cost crosses a rival cost), not a symmetric join of two equal operands.
- **Scale.** First order is the single activity slot. Second order is the firm-wide boundary. Third order is the market or population of firms that prices the result. The transform between scales is lossy; a per-activity saving does not sum cleanly into a firm-level saving (Fig 4 :30).

### 1.2 One line per domain: actors, currency, anchor

| domain | actors | currency | anchor | status |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| D1 Firm frame (employment) | managers, employees, routines | commands, salaries, internal allocation | realized total cost and quality, ranged against a market | admitted |
| D2 Market frame (outsourcing) | suppliers, customers, rival firms | prices, contracts, competition | the market price and the ability to switch trading partner | admitted |
| D3 Contracting frame (hybrid compound) | client firm, contractor firm, the contract | specified obligations, residual control rights | realized performance under the contract | admitted, but unbundled: part D1 inside the contractor, part D2 at the interface |
| D4 Measurement channel | accounting, audits, market prices, benchmarks | readings of cost and quality | the ground itself, which does not update | admitted; the channel can decay while the ground does not |
| X1 "There is one universal make-or-buy rule" | none nameable | none | none nameable | not a domain yet; rejected for lack of an anchor |
| X2 "Market price alone settles the whole choice" | the price | price | price | absorbed into D2 and D4; it is the anchor for cost and an instrument, wrong on axes it does not measure, for quality and specificity |

No nameable anchor means not a domain yet. X1 is out. X2 is not separate; it is a collapse of D4 into D2 that the anchor rule forbids (Fig 2 :29 with Fig 2 :36-37).

### 1.3 Partition or union

**Union, not partition.** D3 overlaps D1 (the contractor has its own hierarchy) and D2 (the client buys a service). A single lever applied to "contractors" therefore clears the region where only one condition holds and leaves the intersection standing. Fig 9 :55-57: "A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones" and "a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either." Unbundle before searching again.

### 1.4 Order by dependency

Fig 3 :20: "The chain is ordered by dependency, not by importance." The order for this question is:

1. Perceive the activity and what it affords (what atoms does it need, what can be specified, what can be observed).
2. Have terms for the governance affordances (employ, contract, buy, and the hybrid).
3. State what counts as progress: a fall in the two-cost total against a measured ground.
4. Decide which frame's closure holds the atoms.
5. Measure and carry the result to the next activity.

The middle link is the one with no instrument in most firms. Fig 3 :27: "A link with no instrument is not a link that works. It is a link nobody has looked at." A reading taken below that break is not a weak reading; it is a reading of nothing.

## 3. Findings

### F1  -  Decide per activity slot, never per firm average

- **Verbatim:** Fig 1 :19 "The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated." Fig 1 :22 "Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average."
- **Reading:** the governance choice is a per-slot decision. A firm may employ its engineers, contract its cleaners, and outsource its payroll simultaneously, and nothing in the aggregate predicts which slot is live. Mode `general` (structural).
- **Refuter:** a firm-level average (for example, an aggregate make-or-buy ratio) that predicts the correct governance mode for each activity as well as a per-activity measurement does.
- **Status:** `triangulated`. Fig 1 (per-slot residual) and Fig 9 :55-57 (union unbundling) are distinct figures with low overlap: one is about the agent loop, the other about leaving and arriving.

### F2  -  The boundary is where internal composition stops reaching an atom

- **Verbatim:** Fig 6 :21 "MINT composes inside the closure and can never add an atom." Fig 6 :23 "No frame escapes its own closure. The bound moves only on contact with a frame whose closure differs." Fig 8 :32 "Closure is idempotent. Composing inside a closed set never leaves it, so no search, however long, adds an atom." Fig 13 :115 "The set is closed. The arrangements are not. That gap is why import exists."
- **Reading:** a firm internalizes an activity when the atoms it needs cannot be composed from what it already holds and the activity must be reached from a closure that differs (a supplier, a contractor). It keeps an activity inside when its own closure already holds the atoms and internal composition pays. This is the figure-side form of the Coase/Williamson boundary: the firm expands until organizing the next transaction internally costs more than obtaining it through the market, and the factor that tips it is mutual dependence on relationship-specific assets. Mode `general` for the closure law, `specified` for the firm mapping.
- **Refuter:** a firm renews the reach of an activity by internal composition alone, adding a productive atom it did not hold, with no contact with a differing closure, repeatedly and measurably.
- **Status:** `triangulated`. Fig 6 (what moves the wall), Fig 8 (why nothing else can), and Fig 13 (closed set, open arrangements) are three distinct roles. Agreement from a shared evidence pool is one witness; these are different figure functions, so the triangulation is structural, not statistical.

### F3  -  Low overlap buys a market; high overlap forces hierarchy

- **Verbatim:** Fig 8 :47 "Independence is one quantity read twice." Fig 8 :49 "Two frames that overlap almost completely are one frame wearing two names." Fig 2 :42 "Buy independence in proportion to the cost of being wrong." Fig 2 :35 "Alignment is triangulation, not negotiation. A mirror cannot correct you."
- **Reading:** asset specificity is overlap between the firm's frame and the supplier's frame. When the two draw on little shared evidence (many alternative trading partners, standard task), the market price is a clean anchor and an arms-length contract works; the union has surplus (Fig 8 :45). When overlap is high (a supplier's asset is worthless outside the relationship), the two frames are "one frame wearing two names," haggling replaces triangulation, and authority inside one frame becomes the cheaper conflict resolver. Independence is bought in proportion to the cost of a hold-up failure. Mode `specified`.
- **Refuter:** a relationship-specific, mutually dependent activity repeatedly governed by a clean arms-length market contract with no renegotiation or hold-up cost, verified against realized total cost.
- **Status:** `triangulated`. Fig 8 (surplus and overlap) and Fig 2 (correlation valence and buying independence) are distinct figures. The world literature (Williamson) is secondary and arrives after this reading; it does not hand the verdict to the figures.

### F4  -  The choice costs in two currencies that do not add

- **Verbatim:** Fig 12 :125 "A composition costs in two currencies, and they do not add." Fig 12 :126 "Bits price the description. Actions price finding out whether it holds." Fig 12 :130 "Summing them into one figure is the error this names, not the fix for it."
- **Reading:** one currency is the bits needed to *describe* the task in advance (how completely can the contract be written; what cannot be specified is what Hart calls non-contractible). The other is the actions needed to *find out* whether the description held (inspection, monitoring, dispute settlement, verification). A production cost figure in dollars prices one projection of these; it does not add them. Coase's own list, "contracts have to be drawn up, inspections have to be made," is already two currencies. Mode `general` for the two-currency law, `specified` for the firm application.
- **Refuter:** a single scalar that fully predicts the governance choice across contexts without losing the distinction between specification difficulty and monitoring difficulty (for example, two activities with identical predicted total cost but opposite rankings on specifiability and observability, where the scalar still picks correctly in both).
- **Status:** `triangulated`. Fig 12 :125-130 (two currencies) and Fig 3 :25-27 (the middle link has no instrument) are distinct figures; the DOL "economic reality" test is a different-pool world corroboration, not a figure.

### F5  -  Employment, contracting, and outsourcing are isomers; contracting is a compound

- **Verbatim:** Fig 12 :117 "Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour." Fig 9 :55 "A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones." Fig 9 :57 "a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either"
- **Reading:** the three modes rearrange the same productive atoms (labor, capital, knowledge, equipment, risk) with different governance bonds: command (inside employment), specified obligation plus residual control rights (contracting), and price (outsourcing). Same ingredients, different arrangement, decisive behavioral difference in incentives and cost. Contracting cannot be treated as a point on a make/buy line because it is a union of hierarchy and market; see `06_isomers_and_order.md`. Mode `specified`.
- **Refuter:** the three modes use different productive atoms such that they are not the same atoms differently bonded, or contracting is shown to be a clean partition with no internal hierarchy and no interface market relation.
- **Status:** `single-source` for the isomer definition (Fig 12 alone defines isomerism), `triangulated` for the union reading (Fig 9 plus the DOL multi-factor test in a different field). Kept separate, never averaged.

### F6  -  The ground is realized economic outcome, not agreement

- **Verbatim:** Fig 2 :29 "An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks. A measurement, an experiment, a counter, a market, a patient outcome." Fig 2 :35 "Alignment is triangulation, not negotiation. A mirror cannot correct you." Fig 2 :40 "agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool" Fig 10 :24 "the ground does not decay, the channel does" Fig 10 :25-26 "stale percepts, dropped transients, saturated metrics, pooled readings"
- **Reading:** the choice is settled by realized total cost and quality measured against a market, not by industry consensus, consultant fashion, or internal agreement. Accounting metrics are a channel and can saturate or pool readings across changed conditions. Seat Map :163 adds the selection rule: "High correlation is disqualifying for a verdict and efficient for a convention." Mode `general`.
- **Refuter:** a firm whose governance choice is validated by consensus while its realized total cost rises against a stable market benchmark and no correction occurs.
- **Status:** `triangulated`. Fig 2 (anchor and correlation) and Fig 10 (channel decay) are distinct figures.

### F7  -  The numeric boundary is a gap, not a hidden answer

- **Verbatim:** Fig 9 :32 "you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself" Fig 13 :134 "Unreachable is a verdict here, not a silence" Fig 8 :39 "So a search that finds nothing is never a proof of absence."
- **Reading:** after a genuine re-census, no figure line yields the numeric threshold at which, say, an asset specificity index or a transaction frequency crosses over from market to hierarchy. Figures constrain the *shape* of a boundary rule; they do not hand down its parameter values. This is declared `gap`, not filled with an invented number. The remedy follows THE_FORMULA step 7, in order: **SEARCHED** first (is the threshold already in the local management literature and merely unfound? that is cheapest), then **INWARD** (measure realized total cost per activity with an instrument that separates the two currencies), then **OUTWARD** (import transaction cost economics or contract theory with a source gate and a shadow test, debited against independence). INWARD and OUTWARD are opposed and are never read against each other.
- **Refuter:** a figure line, found after re-census, that directly yields a universal numeric make-or-buy threshold. Until one is shown, the gap stands.
- **Status:** `gap`. This is a verdict, not a silence. Importing a number against a budget limit would add an atom that was not needed; a wrong atom costs more than starting from primitives (Fig 6 amendment, Fig 8 :40-43).

### F8  -  Describe the activity first; then choose the frame

- **Verbatim:** Fig 9 :27-28 "describe it before you go looking, or any frame you pick will seem to fit" Fig 9 :43 "The verdict tells you to leave. The lookup tells you where to go. Elimination is what you do after you arrive." Fig 6 :30 "outward: does another frame already hold it? The description travels. Fails by being sharpened into terms only we can read. Cast it in effect terms."
- **Reading:** the sequence is residual first, frame second. Name the activity's atoms, the two costs, and the ground in effect terms before asking whether employment, a contractor, or a supplier fits. Picking the frame first makes any frame seem to fit. "Leave" is decided by the verdict on the current frame; "where to go" is a one-pass lookup against a frame whose closure differs. Mode `general` for the method, `specified` for the firm.
- **Refuter:** a universal decision procedure that selects the governance frame correctly without first describing the activity's atoms and costs in effect terms.
- **Status:** `triangulated`. Fig 9 (leave/arrive/describe) and Fig 6 :30 (cast in effect terms) are distinct figures.

## 4. Pre-registered refuter (written before the conclusion)

Per Seat Map :287: "a falsifier written before the run is a design instrument, not only an honesty device." The shape claim is refuted if any of the following holds:

1. A firm renews the reach of an activity by internal composition alone, adding a productive atom it did not hold, with no contact with a differing closure (breaks F2).
2. A relationship-specific, mutually dependent activity is repeatedly governed by a clean arms-length market contract with no hold-up or renegotiation cost, verified against realized total cost (breaks F3).
3. A single scalar fully predicts the governance choice across contexts with no loss of the specification/monitoring distinction (breaks F4).
4. A firm-level average predicts per-activity governance as well as per-slot measurement (breaks F1).
5. A figure line is found that directly yields a universal numeric boundary (converts F7 from `gap` to governed, and would force a re-census of the whole derivation).

If any fires, the affected finding is withdrawn and the reach falls because something was added, not because anything was deleted (Fig 6 amendment). Until one is run, no part of this may be labelled `grounded`.

## 5. Recommendation

1. Decide per activity slot, and state which frame's closure holds the atoms (F1, F2).
2. State the two costs separately. Do not add bits and actions into one management metric (F4).
3. Treat contractors as a union compound, not a point on a make-or-buy line (F5).
4. Buy independence in proportion to the cost of being wrong: low-overlap activities go to the market, high-overlap activities toward hierarchy (F3).
5. Keep the ground reachable. Fig 4 :30: "So take the round trip and measure the gap. Without it you are navigating by dead reckoning: confident about a position nobody checked." Fig 10 :52: "Bring a characterised residual, never the source."
6. Do not manufacture the numeric threshold. Declare the gap and measure (F7).
7. Send the generator, the per-slot method and its refuter, not a list of conclusions (Fig 4 :22, "Only methods cross up. Only head starts come down").

## 8. Round trip note (to be expanded in `11_audit.md`)

The condensation used in this folder is: per-slot residual, closure wall, low overlap means market, high overlap means hierarchy, two non-additive costs, union compound, numeric threshold unreachable. Carrying that shorthand back down to a specific firm drops at least three constraints: the two-currency distinction (F4), the union nature of contracting (F5), and the ground-instrument distinction (F6). The round-trip gap R_T is therefore nonzero and qualitative here; it is enumerated in `11_audit.md`. Treating "the formula is a condensed version of the metalaws" as identity would set R_T to zero and would be the dead-reckoning error Fig 4 :30 names.
