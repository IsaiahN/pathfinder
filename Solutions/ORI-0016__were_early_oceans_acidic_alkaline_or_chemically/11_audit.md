## Independent domain census

I redid the census from the residual line alone, before reading the primary's conclusions, using the WATSON order: R by arity, symmetry and scale; one line per domain with actors, currency and anchor; partition or union; order by dependency.

- **R, arity / symmetry / scale.** The residual is the mismatch between the question's request for one global label and observations that are already indexed by slot. Arity: at least three settings and two time windows, so a union compound. Symmetry: pH is an ordering and the class split is a threshold comparison, not a symmetric join. Scale: molecular speciation through vent chimney and basin to globe, with a lossy transform between scales.
- **Domain 1, bulk ocean and atmosphere frame.** Actors: pCO2, silicate and seafloor weathering, carbonate burial, dissolved inorganic carbon. Currency: alkalinity and bulk pH. Anchor: paleosols, carbonates, banded iron formations and isotopes. These do not update on the models.
- **Domain 2, local setting and habitat frame.** Actors: hydrothermal fluids, serpentinization, vent chimneys, pore waters, shallow ponds, impact ejecta. Currency: local pH, proton motive force, redox disequilibrium. Anchor: direct fluid measurement and mineral assemblages.
- **Domain 3, archive and instrument frame.** Actors: boron and oxygen isotopes, carbonate rarity, greenalite, zircons. Currency: contact with a past state. Anchor: the rock.
- **Domain 4, question frame.** Actors: the three offered labels. Currency: class labels. Anchor: none until the third is bound to a per-slot distribution; then it is the union of domains 1 to 3.
- **X1, a single global pH.** No nameable anchor. Not a domain. This is the aggregation error.
- **Union and order.** Union, because domains 1 and 2 overlap in the carbon cycle and domain 3 reads both. Dependency order: archive first, then slot vocabulary, then objective, then the two frames, then the label.

## Round trip gap R_T

The Symbols table defines R_T as the gap between what was sent up and what came back down, `gap(x, (T_E ∘ T_A)(x))`, measuring what the coarser description cannot hold. Fig 4 :30 requires it to be measured rather than assumed. I ran the round trip on the condensation this folder actually uses.

1. **Sent up, x:** the verbatim figure set, chiefly Fig 1 :19, Fig 9 :55 and :57, Fig 10 :26, Fig 12 :117 and :123, Fig 13 :22 and :134.
2. **Abstraction, T_A:** the shorthand carried in files 05 to 09: the answer is per slot; the bulk acidic and alkaline values are abstractions; the settings form a union; the number is a gap; a gradient is spendable.
3. **Concretisation, T_E:** the concrete claim a reader would take away: the early ocean had one bulk pH of around 6.5 to 7, or alternatively 10, and local variation is a detail.

**R_T is nonzero.** Three things the shorthand holds do not survive the trip back down:

- the **slot index itself**, which collapses to a single number if the answer is used as an input to another argument;
- the **provenance requirement**, since a bulk number carried downward loses the frame, source gate and shadow test that made it a legitimate slot value;
- the **gradient structure**, because a distribution of values cannot hold directedness, which Fig 13 :22 says is what makes a gradient a gradient.

One thing survives cleanly: a bulk curve is an abstraction over slot readings and is second order. That is the residue worth sending. R_T was **not** measured numerically because no ground reading exists; it is recorded qualitatively as the enumerated set of dropped constraints. Treating the shorthand as identical to the figures would set R_T to zero and would be the dead-reckoning error Fig 4 :30 names.
