# Are the acidic and alkaline readings isomers or a functional group?

**Mode: `specified` on the structural claim, `gap` on any numeric resolution.** This supporting derivation is a simulation checked against the figures, not a ground reading (see `05_what_shape_means.md` section 6).

## RESIDUAL

Two bulk readings of early ocean chemistry disagree in the world literature: weakly acidic to circumneutral, around pH 6.5 to 7 from carbon-cycle and proxy work, and alkaline around pH 10 from thermodynamic models of a reducing prebiotic ocean. Two questions are owed. First, under the figure's definition, are these readings isomers, a functional group, or something weaker? Second, in what dependency order must the slots, the archive and the label be settled?

## DOMAIN CENSUS

Atoms in play as world frames currently write them: CO2 and carbonate speciation, alkalinity flux, silicate and seafloor weathering, water-rock interaction, banded-iron formation mineral assemblages, boron and rare-earth proxies, time window, and local setting. Bonds in play: the carbon-cycle buffering relation and the serpentinization or hydrothermal alteration relation. No new primitive is available without an import. The two readings are bulk abstractions, not slot readings, so this census stays inside D1 of file 01.

## DERIVATION

### Q1: Isomer test for the two readings

Fig 12 :117: "isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."

Fig 12 :123: "functional group. A substructure whose presence fixes the outcome. The group says what kind; the qualifier says which member."

- As the two bulk frames currently write them, the acidic reading requires the CO2 carbonate equilibrium and carbon-cycle buffering, while the alkaline reading sets CO2 virtually absent and puts pH under serpentinization and ferrous-iron hydroxide control. They do not share a full atom set under the same bonds, so in the strict figure sense they are **not isomers**.
- What they do share is a substructure: a bulk pH estimate inferred from a proxy-constrained thermodynamic or carbon-cycle model. That substructure fixes the kind of thing being produced. The qualifier, acidic or alkaline, says which member. By Fig 12 :123 that is a **functional group**, not an isomer pair.
- The stronger claim is available but not established: if both readings were rewritten over the full shared atom set, carbon cycle plus alkalinity plus water-rock interaction plus local setting plus time window, differing only in which bond mediates which pair, then they would be isomers in Fig 12's sense, same atoms arranged differently. This is the claim worth testing, not a result.
- **Falsifier, written before the run:** if either reading requires an atom the other cannot express without import, the isomer claim fails and the functional-group reading stands. If both can be written over the same atom set with only the bond changed, the functional-group reading is downgraded and the isomer claim survives.

This matters because the question's three offered options are often read as if acidic and alkaline were rival members of one list and varied were a third. Under Fig 12 the acidic and alkaline numbers are two members of a functional group of bulk abstractions, while varied is a different kind of statement about slot structure. That is the Fig 9 union result reached by a second route.

### Q2: Dependency order

Fig 3 :27: "A link with no instrument is not a link that works. It is a link nobody has looked at."

Fig 3 :20: "The chain is ordered by dependency, not by importance. Each link consumes what the one above produces, so effort spent above the break arrives at a step"

The order for this question is:

1. **Archive and instrument.** Can the slot be read at all? The Hadean rock record is sparse, and the Hadean Wikipedia synthesis says Hadean rocks are very rare, largely from one locality. Hao et al. 2026 say direct constraints on Hadean ocean chemistry are sparse and contentious. This link is weak and is where effort must go first.
2. **Vocabulary for what a slot affords.** A fluid at a vent wall affords a gradient; a carbonate or paleosol affords a contact with past seawater; a global curve affords only an average.
3. **Objective.** What counts as a slot value, and what counts as a failure of the per-slot claim. The five refuters in file 01 section 4 are written here, before the run.
4. **Frames.** D1 bulk carbon-cycle frame, then D2 local settings frame. Neither is promoted to the ground.
5. **Label.** Varied is the figure-licensed form. Acidic and alkaline are retained as slot values with provenance.

Optimising a global pH curve while the archive link is weak is effort above the break. It cannot rescue the label.

### Q3: Order across scales

Fig 12 :104: "A settled molecule becomes an atom for whatever composes over it. So irreducible is a fact about a level and never about the world"

Fig 4 :27: "The transform between scales loses information, and that is the point."

A slot reading, once settled against a rock or a fluid, becomes an operand for a bulk description. The bulk curve is therefore second order, not a more fundamental first-order fact. The transform down from the bulk curve to slots is lossy, so the round-trip gap R_T is nonzero and must be reported rather than assumed zero (Fig 4 :30). This is the same recursion the figures use elsewhere: composition crosses levels, and the level decides what counts as irreducible.

Fig 13 :134: "Unreachable is a verdict here, not a silence,"

No figure line yields the pH of a named slot. That field stays `gap` until an instrument closes it. The absence of a preserved carbonate is not evidence of acidity everywhere, because Fig 8 :39 says "So a search that finds nothing is never a proof of absence."

## CONCLUSION

The acidic and alkaline bulk readings are, as currently written, two members of a functional group of proxy-constrained bulk pH estimates, not isomers under Fig 12's definition. The isomer reading is a real but untested stronger claim, with a stated falsifier. Varied is a different kind of term: the per-slot form that the other two presuppose. Dependency order puts the archive link first and the label last, and the bulk abstraction is second order over settled slot readings with a nonzero R_T. No new primitive, no global-pH term and no single cause are licensed.
