## 1. The claim in one paragraph

An early-warning signal is a channel between a system and an observer, not an anchor. The realised crossing, or a stable control, is what settles whether the reading was correct. Signals fail when that channel loses ground contact while the indicator keeps producing readings, and the failure states are four: a window chosen after the outcome is visible; a fast excursion missed by sampling; a statistic saturated by noise or detrending; and an average across systems that erases the live slot. The question "how often" is an enumeration over a named habitat and a filter for a monitoring budget, not a universal percentage and not a verdict for any one system. The four classic indicators are isomers of one reading and form two functional groups, critical slowing down and flickering, which is why they disagree.

## 2. Three checkable sub-claims

### C1 - framework-internal; checkable by reading Figure 10 and Figure 2

- **Statement:** an indicator is a frame, not an anchor; a signal fails when the channel decays, and the four named channel states exhaust the failures the framework permits.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 10 :24 *"the ground does not decay, the channel does"*; Fig 10 :25 *"stale percepts, dropped transients, saturated metrics, pooled readings"*; Fig 2 :29 *"An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks."*
- **Refuter:** show a failure that is none of the four channel states and not a vocabulary gap; or show that a pre-registered detector tested on replicated systems separates approaching bifurcations from stable systems at stated error bounds. If either holds, C1 is narrowed or withdrawn.

### C2 - empirical-facing; checkable against the published record

- **Statement:** the four classic indicators are not independent witnesses. They are the same detrended series under different summaries (Fig 12 :117 *"Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."*), and their agreement is one evidence pool.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 2 :40 *"disqualifying for a verdict: agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool"*; Fig 12 :124 *"functional group. A substructure whose presence fixes the outcome. The group says what kind; the qualifier says which member."*
- **What to check:** Gsell et al. 2016 report AR(1) and SD increasing together in only 5 of 16 lake series and only three of 64 trend tests significant in more than half of surrogate comparisons. Contosta et al. 2023 report *"Temporal trends in critical slowing down and flickering were neither coherent across ecosystem state variables, nor were they unambiguous."* O'Brien et al. 2023 report most indicators no better than chance across nine lakes. Are those characterisations faithful?
- **Refuter:** demonstrate that two indicators from the same series are independent evidence, or that a study's stable controls remove the low agreement. Either refutes C2 for that case.

### C3 - frequency; checkable as a union-versus-average question

- **Statement:** a universal frequency is not derivable and must not be averaged across habitats. The count is a filter for the budget, and the decision is per slot.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 9 :60 *"Use filters for the budget and witnesses for the verdict. Never let a filter hand you a verdict."*; Fig 1 :22 *"Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average."*; Fig 11 :43 *"The habitat is enumerated, never composed."*; Fig 13 :134 *"Unreachable is a verdict here, not a silence"*.
- **What to check:** the four opened enumerations differ in habitat and definition: 44 percent of indicator tests failed in five lakes (Gsell et al. 2016); about 58 percent of signals suggested declining resilience in one forest (Contosta et al. 2023); few critical transitions and chance-level indicators in nine lakes (O'Brien et al. 2023); signals 10 to 30 years early of the flickering kind in one lake (Wang et al. 2012). Do these refer to the same quantity? If not, no average is legitimate.
- **Refuter:** one protocol, applied to an enumerated habitat with signal and ground defined in advance, returning a stable frequency across independent enumerations. That closes the gap.

## 3. What is explicitly not claimed

- Not that signals never work. Several studies find them, sometimes years ahead.
- Not that there is a universal percentage. Frequency is a filter, not a witness.
- Not that slowing down is specific to a catastrophic bifurcation (Kéfi et al. 2013).
- Not that four indicators are four witnesses, and not that more indicators of the same series increase ground contact (Fig 11 :40).
- Not `grounded`. The empirical counts belong to the studies that measured them.
- Not a ranking of experts by likely agreement, and no personal emails. Fig 2 :40 disqualifies a verdict built from a shared pool.

## 5. What would settle it

A ground reading, not agreement. The needed instrument is a replicated comparison with the detector, window, variable and threshold fixed before outcomes are examined, plus a discriminator between an approaching bifurcation, a chance excursion, flickering and a detrending artefact. *The Hard Problem is About Source* (opened 2026-11-22): *"If several paths create the same behavior, then behavior is a projection with lossy information"*; *"An instrument that can't distinguish these cases demonstrates its own inadequacy"*.

## 6. What an expert can check in twenty minutes

1. Read Fig 10 :24-25, Fig 2 :29 and :40, Fig 9 :60, Fig 1 :22, Fig 11 :43 and Fig 13 :134. Eight short passages.
2. Check C2 against one dataset you know: do the indicators disagree the way claimed, and do they draw on one series?
3. Look for a control or surrogate in one paper you know well. Was the window chosen before or after the outcome?
4. Name the instrument that would distinguish a fold approach from a chance excursion, or say why none exists.
5. Send the refutation. A failure is more useful than an endorsement.
