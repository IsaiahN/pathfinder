# Outreach draft: check the failure taxonomy before you decide whether the framework is worth reading

**Use:** one-to-one, or as a cover note attached to a submission through a public venue in `03_public_contact_points.csv`. **Not** for bulk send. No personal emails are used; the contact form in `03` is institutional and opt-in.

**Hard precondition:** do not send until the four figure quotes in `08_expert_verification_packet.md` have been checked against the canonical figure text again on the day of sending. A failure found at contact is more useful than a claim that survives only because the recipient could not check it.

**Subject:** One falsifiable claim about early-warning signals, before any theory

> Hello [name or team],
>
> I have one specific, checkable claim about early-warning signals for tipping points. I am not asking you to read a framework first, and I am not asking you to agree. If the claim does not survive, there is nothing else to look at.
>
> **The residual (`specified`, not `grounded` by this message):** for a given system, an early-warning indicator is a reading made from a time series (rising lag-1 autocorrelation, rising variance, skewness, flickering). It is not the transition. The transition, or the counterfactual no transition, is what settles whether the reading was correct. So there are three different questions, and they must be kept separate: did the signal precede a real crossing; did it fire when nothing crossed; did it miss a crossing? A single "the signals work X percent of the time" answer mixes them and is not a verdict about any particular system.
>
> **The failure claim I want checked:** signals fail when the channel between the system and the observer loses ground contact while the indicator keeps producing readings. Four named failure states cover what I have found: a window chosen after the collapse is visible (stale percepts); a fast excursion missed by the sampling interval (dropped transients); a variance or autocorrelation statistic that cannot discriminate because of window length, detrending or noise (saturated metrics); and averaging across variables, taxa, lakes or systems so that the one live signal disappears (pooled readings). These are not statistical footnotes. They are the whole failure taxonomy I am offering.
>
> **What would refute it, written down before this message:**
> 1. a pre-registered detector, tested on replicated bifurcation and replicated stable systems, that separates them at false-positive and false-negative rates fixed before the run;
> 2. a demonstration that two indicators computed from the same time series are genuinely independent evidence, not one shared pool;
> 3. an instrument that reads the transition mechanism off the pre-transition series alone, so that slowing down is known to mean a bifurcation rather than general instability;
> 4. one protocol, enumerated over a stated habitat, that returns a stable success frequency across independent enumerations.
>
> If any of these holds, I will withdraw the corresponding claim. A one-line "this fails because..." is more useful to me than agreement. If you would rather not engage, no reply is needed.
>
> **What I am not claiming:** that early-warning signals never work. Published empirical studies find them in some systems, sometimes years ahead. I am also not claiming a universal failure rate. Enumerations differ: in one set of five lakes a quarter of the 64 indicator tests were significant in most comparisons, in one forest about 58 percent of signals suggested declining resilience, and in nine lakes most indicators did no better than chance. Those are different habitats and different definitions, not one estimate with noise. I will not average them.
>
> **If it survives, and only then:** here are the sources. The figures, not my summary, are the authority.
> - Verbatim figure text with line numbers (local framework extract): `FRAMEWORK/reference/03-FIGURES_TEXT.md`
> - Canonical usage rule: `FRAMEWORK/reference/FIGURES_README_canonical.md`
> - The empirical papers that bear on the claim: Gsell et al. 2016, Boettiger and Hastings 2012, Contosta et al. 2023, O'Brien et al. 2023 (full links in the companion file)
>
> **If you want to reuse the machinery:** the formula is in the framework files. Run it on a problem you know well and can check against a ground. Write down what would refute your result *before* you run it, and state the mode of what you get. A reported failure is more useful to me than a reported success.
>
> Thank you for your time,
> [name]

## Stage order

1. **Stage 1:** send the residual, the failure taxonomy and the four refuters. Ask what the recipient would count as a failure. Record the verdict before showing the framework.
2. **Stage 2, opt-in only:** if curious, send the three links, noting that the figures are the authority.
3. **Stage 3:** offer the formula as a method to reuse, with the instruction that each user writes their own refuter and states their own mode.

Later agreement from a stage 2 or stage 3 reader is never pooled as corroboration. Fig 2 :40 disqualifies it.
