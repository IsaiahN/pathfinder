## Wider literature that bears on the question, read in the sources above

1. **Sequences are already the operative unit in the field.** Powner and Sutherland describe "sequences of conditions" and warn against rigidly invoking one set or sequence; Ritson et al. report an uninterrupted flow sequence and ask whether such a sequence could occur under one condition set. The figures' route-versus-state distinction is not an external imposition on this literature; the field uses it already.
2. **Separate validation is the known failure mode.** Hirakawa et al. frame the interstep problem directly: steps validated separately may not work together in natural geological settings. That is Fig 1 :19 and Fig 3 :19 applied to a real experimental programme.
3. **The substitution is the methodological problem.** Richert asks experimenters to report interventions and discontinuities; Eiby and Hassenkam say the laboratory bears little resemblance to the early Earth and that boundaries and parameters are set by the experimenter. These are the two silent failure modes of Fig 11 written in laboratory vocabulary.
4. **A low-overlap analogue corroborates the shape, not the chemistry.** Fowler's integration-test distinction comes from software engineering. It says individually verified modules do not establish the behaviour of the connected system. It is an analogue from a different closure and is labelled as such; it is not a chemical source and carries no chemical verdict weight.

## Overlap warning

The first five world sources all sit in origins-of-life research and cite one another. Powner and Sutherland is cited by Richert and by Eiby and Hassenkam; Hirakawa et al. cites the same interstep literature. Their agreement with the figure verdict is therefore **one evidence pool**, not five independent witnesses (Fig 2 :40). The Fowler source is genuinely low-overlap in field and instrument, but it is an analogy. Independence is bought in proportion to the cost of being wrong (Fig 2 :42), and here the cost is a wasted submission rather than a settled scientific claim.
