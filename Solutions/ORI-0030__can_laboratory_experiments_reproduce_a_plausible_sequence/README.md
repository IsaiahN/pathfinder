# Solution ORI-0030

**Question:** Can laboratory experiments reproduce a plausible sequence of natural conditions rather than a single isolated reaction?

**Source:** origins of life.txt line 40

**Section:** 3. Prebiotic ingredients and chemical supply

**Mode:** `general` on the structural shape, `specified` on the per-domain claims. Nothing here is `grounded`: no experiment was run in this derivation. This is a simulated derivation checked for internal consistency against the figures, not a ground reading.

**Status:** done

**Registry status:** pending

**Field for public contact points:** prebiotic simulation research.

---

## What is being decided, in effect terms

Option A: treat a successful single isolated reaction (one feedstock set, one fixed condition, one product measured) as an answer to the sequence question. It leads to a claim about a **state**: something can form.

Option B: require a chained run of conditions in which the product of one step is the feedstock of the next, with the residual read per step and the difference between the laboratory habitat and the proposed natural habitat measured. It leads to a claim about a **route**: something could have run through. Fig 12 :111 says *"A vocabulary without bonds can name a state and cannot name a route to one."*

The answer binds the shape of Option B and refuses Option A as an answer to this question. Whether any particular experimental run achieves the shape is a ground question for that run, not something this folder settles.

## What is deliberately not here

- No personal emails of named individuals. The ISSOL contact page publishes individual addresses; they are not used here. The public society, journal and conference routes are used instead.
- No ranking of people by how likely they are to agree with the claim.
- No bulk-send plan.
- No claim that this derivation settles the question. A simulated derivation checked against the figures is not a measurement (Fig 2 :29 names an experiment, not an agreement, as an anchor).
