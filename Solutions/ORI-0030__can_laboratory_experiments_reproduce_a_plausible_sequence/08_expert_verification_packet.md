# Expert verification packet : the Tether on sequencing prebiotic experiments

**What this is:** a claim in a form a chemist, geochemist or methodologist can check. Mode, derivation and refuters are stated before the conclusion, so a reader knows what to look for and what would settle it.

**Mode: `general` on the shape, `specified` on the laboratory and union claims. Not `grounded`.** No multi-step experiment was run by this derivation.

## 1. The claim, in one paragraph

A single isolated reaction and a plausible sequence of natural conditions are different kinds of claim. A reaction returns a state; a sequence returns a route. The figure-grounded shape of a sequence claim is: conditions joined by **sequence**, whose test is that swapping two steps changes the outcome; the product of each step carried into the next and measured at its actual concentration; the residual read per step and per carry-over rather than as one pathway average; the laboratory habitat declared as a substitution, with what it failed to reproduce and what it brought with it enumerated; the lab-to-nature transform bracketed with a measured round-trip gap; and the verdict returned by an experiment, not by agreement among groups sharing protocol and feedstocks. The framework returns this shape. It does not name a pathway, and this derivation does not settle any particular pathway.

## 2. Three checkable sub-claims

### C1 : framework-internal; checkable by reading Figures 12 and 5

- **Statement:** a single isolated reaction is a state term, not a route term, and cannot by itself answer a sequence question.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 12 :111 *"A vocabulary without bonds can name a state and cannot name a route to one."* Fig 12 :112 *"A plan is a sequence. A goal is a comparison. Progress is a subtraction."* Fig 5 :44 *"fails this, and so does a term that explains one occasion perfectly."* The Operators table gives the test for sequence: *"swap them. Does it become a different thing? Yes."*
- **Refuter:** show that a single-isolation result determines the behaviour of the full condition sequence with no added condition, carry-over or intervention; or show that swapping the order of two conditions never changes the outcome for a claimed pathway across all permutations (then the bond is conjunction, not sequence); or show that the bond distinction does not apply to experimental descriptions.

### C2 : chemistry-facing; checkable against any published multi-step prebiotic protocol

- **Statement:** the laboratory is a substituted habitat, so a bench sequence proves the wiring of the bench, not the capability of the natural sequence; the substitution and its gap must be reported.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 11 :66 *"Isolation is not removal of the habitat. It is substitution of one habitat for another."* Fig 11 :70 *"A test harness is a substituted habitat. Which is why a synthetic solve proves wiring and never capability."* Fig 11 :68 *"what you failed to reproduce is invisible until the goal fails"* Fig 4 :30 *"So take the round trip and measure the gap. Without it you are navigating by dead reckoning: confident about a position nobody checked."*
- **What to check:** does the protocol state the number of condition changes and manual interventions? Does it say what was added and what was left out relative to the proposed natural setting? Richert 2018 asks for the intervention count explicitly; Eiby and Hassenkam 2025 state that *"Laboratory simulations of prebiotic chemistry are inherently biased, since their boundaries and parameters are set by the experimenter."* If these are reported, the substitution gap can be estimated. If they are not, the claim "reproduces natural conditions" is not yet checkable.
- **Refuter:** demonstrate a laboratory sequence for which every condition and actor required by the proposed natural sequence has been reproduced with measured equivalence, so the gap is zero at every step; or show that a substituted habitat can establish natural capability without reproducing what the goal required.

### C3 : methodology-facing; checkable against the residual definition

- **Statement:** the residual for a pathway belongs to each step and each interstep hand-off, and the question is a union of three conditions that must be unbundled before search.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 1 :19 *"The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated."* Fig 1 :22 *"Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average."* Fig 9 :55 *"A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones."* Fig 9 :57 *"a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either"*
- **What to check:** does the protocol report a yield per step and a carry-over per interstep, or only a pathway total? Are chemical tractability, geological coherence and habitat fidelity assessed separately? Hirakawa et al. 2025 frame the interstep question directly: models have "many steps, each separately validated by laboratory experiments", and the challenge is whether the steps "work together in natural geological environments, absent human intervention."
- **Refuter:** show that the steps of a pathway are exchangeable so a global statistic is sufficient; or show that the three conditions collapse to one, making the union reading unnecessary.

## 3. What is explicitly not claimed

- Not that a sequence cannot be reproduced. Fig 8 :39: *"So a search that finds nothing is never a proof of absence."*
- Not that single-reaction experiments are worthless. They are the correct anchor for a single-step question (Fig 2 :29 names an experiment as an anchor).
- Not that the bench protocol is the natural habitat. Fig 11 :67 says the residual must be reproduced and unwanted actors are inherited.
- Not that better analytics alone settle the question. That addresses one condition of the union.
- Not that agreement among laboratories constitutes corroboration. Fig 2 :40: *"agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool"*.
- Not a named pathway, a geochemical scenario or a new primitive.
- Not `grounded`. This is a simulated derivation checked against the figures, not a ground reading.

## 5. What would settle it

1. **Order test:** swap two consecutive conditions. Does the measured intermediate or product set change? That decides sequence versus conjunction (Fig 12 :70 to :74).
2. **Carry-over test:** show the product of step i is the actual feedstock of step i+1 at measured concentration and with its real by-products.
3. **Cycle test:** if the protocol cycles, show the output re-enters as input and the loop closes; otherwise it is a repeated chain, which Fig 12 :121 says cannot close.
4. **Substitution ledger and gap:** state interventions, condition changes, what was added and what was omitted; measure the round-trip gap (Fig 4 :30).
5. **Ground:** an independent experiment or measurement, not a vote among groups using the same protocol.

## 6. What an expert can check in twenty minutes

1. Read four short passages: Fig 12 :111 and :112, Fig 11 :66 and :70, Fig 1 :19, Fig 9 :55.
2. Take one published multi-step prebiotic protocol you know. Does it report per-step residuals, condition changes and interventions? If it reports only a final yield, C2 and C3 are unsupported for that protocol.
3. Ask whether swapping two of its conditions would change the outcome. A yes makes it a route; a universal no makes it a conjunction.
4. Name one measurement that would discriminate the natural sequence from the bench substitution, or say why none is possible.
5. Send the refutation. A failure is more useful than an endorsement.
