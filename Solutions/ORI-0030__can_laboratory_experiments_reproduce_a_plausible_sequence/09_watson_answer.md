## QUESTION

Decide, in effect terms, what the Tether says about the claim that laboratory experiments can reproduce a plausible sequence of natural conditions rather than a single isolated reaction. Option A: accept a successful single isolated reaction as an answer to the sequence question. Option B: require an ordered chain of conditions, carried products between steps, per-step residuals, a declared habitat substitution with a measured gap, and ground settlement. What each leads to: A returns a state term and is the one-occasion explanation the acceptance bargain refuses; B returns a route term with checkable sub-claims and an honest stop at the measurement that would settle any specific pathway.

## DOMAIN CENSUS

R by arity, symmetry and scale, then one line per domain with actors, currency and anchor, followed by partition or union and order by dependency.

- **R (the residual).** Arity: n condition steps plus n-1 interstep carry-overs, indexed per slot. Symmetry: ordered, the sequence bond whose test is "swap them. Does it become a different thing? Yes" (Operators table :33), not the symmetric conjunction whose test is "swap the operands. Does the meaning change? No." Scale: first order inside one condition, second order across the chain where a settled product becomes feedstock, and habitat-substitution scale where the bench replaces the natural setting.
- **Domain 1, chemical reaction frame.** Actors: feedstocks, intermediates, products, energy inputs, mineral surfaces. Currency: yield and selectivity at one condition. Anchor: the analytical measurement. Fig 2 :29 names an experiment as an anchor. This is where a single isolated reaction lives.
- **Domain 2, geochemical and natural-habitat frame.** Actors: environments, changing conditions, cycles, fluxes, meteorite inputs. Currency: compatibility of a condition order with geological constraints. Anchor: the geological and planetary record, which does not update on what is convenient in a laboratory.
- **Domain 3, experimental simulation frame.** Actors: protocol, apparatus, operator as maintainer, numbered interventions. Currency: reproduced conditions, observed products, count of discontinuities. Anchor: the experiment, but only when aimed at the right residual. This is where the question bites, because a laboratory is a substituted habitat.
- **Domain 4, verification and interpretation frame.** Actors: reviewers, replicate groups. Currency: agreement and residual. Anchor: the ground, never the agreement. In this simulated run all four domains draw on one evidence pool, so this domain's independence is zero.
- **Partition or union:** union compound. Chemical tractability, geological coherence and habitat fidelity overlap and bind jointly. A case in three conditions is not a harder case of any one.
- **Order by dependency:** perception, vocabulary, objective, planning, learn and carry (Fig 3). Refuse the state term before minting a route term (Formula step 2). Describe the residual before choosing a frame (Fig 9 :27).

Strain test applied: the acceptance-bargain refusal in Fig 5 and the null-search warning in Fig 8 are not in conflict. Fig 5 governs a claim offered as an explanation; Fig 8 governs the inference from a search that found nothing. Different quantities, re-census rather than softening either line.

## VERDICT (from the figures)

Yes in principle, with the figures specifying what a yes must look like; no if the offered evidence is a single isolated reaction; and not settled here for any particular pathway. A sequence claim must be an ordered route whose per-step residuals and interstep carry-overs are reported, whose laboratory substitution is enumerated with a measured round-trip gap, whose condition order is shown to matter by the swap test, and whose verdict is returned by an experiment rather than by agreement inside a shared evidence pool. The framework returns this shape, not a named geochemical scenario, and this derivation is a `specified`, ungrounded result. The strongest single figure point is the habitat rule: a test harness proves wiring and never capability.

- **cost if wrong:** bounded and locatable. The packet states its mode, refuters and lineage before the conclusion, so an error is findable. The real cost is spending the independence of outside readers by sending a conclusion before a characterisation, which Fig 2 :42 prices as "Buy independence in proportion to the cost of being wrong." That cost is avoidable by leading with the shape claim and its refuter.
- **undoable:** yes. The four refuters in `01` §5 let the claim be repaired without withdrawing any primitive. A protocol that reports its interventions and per-step residuals simply moves from unsupported to checkable; no atom or figure has to change.
- **reversed by:** a ground reading, never agreement. A swap test showing condition order changes nothing for a claimed pathway would collapse sequence into conjunction; a laboratory sequence with a measured zero substitution gap would discharge the habitat burden; a global residual shown to locate failure as well as per-step residuals would break the per-slot claim; a demonstration that the question is a partition rather than a union would break the unbundling claim; and an independent experiment settling a specific pathway would move that pathway from `candidate` to `grounded`. Until one of these fires, the verdict stands as `candidate`.
