## 1. A shape is a constraint you can state before you can name the thing

The canonical usage rule: the framework returns *"what a missing thing would have to look like (a shape, not a name)."* Fig 9 :32: *"you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself."*

From *The Library of Babel*: *"You can cut your search short if the set of books returned is small and descriptive enough that it provides a general shape of the answer that satisfies your question."*

For this question the shape is not a named prebiotic pathway and not a named laboratory protocol. It is a set of constraints any candidate answer must satisfy:

- it is ordered (a route, not a state);
- it carries products between consecutive conditions rather than treating steps as independent;
- it reads the residual per step, not as one pathway average;
- it states what the laboratory substituted for the natural habitat and measures the gap;
- it is settled by an experiment or other ground, not by agreement among sympathetic readers.

A shape constrains. It does not fill. "A sequence of conditions" is a shape. "The cyanosulfidic scenario in a post-impact evaporite basin" is a name, and the framework does not supply it.

## 2. How the shape is obtained: proximity and recursion

*The Library of Babel*: *"With each new term gained, you narrow the space and deepen your vocabulary."* It advises using the vocabulary you already have that is closest to what you are trying to describe, then looking for recurring new terms that overlap or correlate with it.

Applied here, the starting vocabulary is ordinary experimental language: step, yield, condition change, carry-over, work-up, intervention. The nearby terms that narrow the space are "interstep compatibility", "uninterrupted synthesis", "number of discontinuities", "substituted habitat", and "per-slot residual". Each one cuts the space of possible answers without yet naming a pathway. That is the bootstrapping described in the essay.

The essay also gives the stop rule: the answer is reached when *"the finish line will also be the starting place"*, because by then *"you have composed the answer through the process of locating it."* For a methodological question like this one, that means the stop comes when further searching returns only finer vocabulary for the same four or five constraints. The answer to "can experiments reproduce a sequence" is a criterion set, not a recipe.

And the bundle warning: if an exhaustive search fails, one possibility the essay names is that *"your problem was a bundle or conflation of several problems masquerading as one."* That is Fig 9 :54 to :58 in essay vocabulary, and it is why the census in `01` returns a union of chemical, geological and substitution conditions rather than one question.

## 3. What licenses a name

Two things, both outside the shape itself:

1. **The ground settles it.** `THE_FORMULA.md` step 5: *"the ground settles it. Verified only by what pays. A gate is not the ground."* Fig 2 :29 calls an experiment an anchor. For this question a real multi-step experiment is exactly the anchor that would license saying a particular sequence is reproducible.
2. **A provenanced import from a frame whose closure differs** (Fig 6 :23, Fig 8 :50 to :52), with a source gate and a shadow test.

*The Hard Problem is About Source* gives the discriminator rule: *"If several paths create the same behavior, then behavior is a projection with lossy information"*. An analytical reading of the final product of a pathway does not say which pathway produced it. Several condition sequences can give the same product mixture. What is owed is a source-discriminating instrument (the essay's spectrometer), not a name assigned from a projection. In this question the equivalent confusion is reading "the product formed in the flask" as "this natural sequence ran." Fig 4 :30 requires the round trip and the measured gap instead.

## 4. Why the outreach must lead with an effect description

*Decoding Tacit Knowledge*: *"When contact isn't possible, the best you can send is a plain description of what you'd expect to see, because anyone can check that against the world."* The same essay warns that two people who check only against each other drift together, and that *"High contact builds exactly the shared understanding that makes communication work, but that same factor makes the learner's agreement with the teacher worthless as a check."*

This is the direct reason the first contact in `02_outreach_email.md` carries the residual and the refuter, not the framework. A recipient who has absorbed the architecture before being asked for a verdict has joined the frame being checked (Fig 10 :42: *"A verifier that can reconstruct the claim cannot verify it."*), and their agreement is worthless as a check. Stage 2 is opt-in for the same reason.

Fig 6 :30: *"The description travels. Fails by being sharpened into terms only we can read. Cast it in effect terms."* Fig 9 :30 to :31: *"your words are borrowed too, so describe the effect rather than the cause."* So the claim is stated as "here is what you would measure if the sequence claim were true, and here is what would refute it", not as "here is the Tether's account of prebiotic chemistry."

## 5. Why this answer cannot settle the question it reports on

*The Recursive Self-Improvement Trap*: *"the output is only as good as the validator. The agent, or the tool, or the frame, or the theory, cannot validate itself."* And explicitly about this kind of output: *"LLMs can quickly simulate an approximate answer/output"*, while *"the truly testable output would need to be derived from the real architecture or working the formula yourself."*

So `01_figure_grounded_audit.md`, `06_isomers_and_order.md`, `07_the_tether_answer.md`, `08_expert_verification_packet.md` and `09_watson_answer.md` are simulated derivations checked for internal consistency against the verbatim figure text. No flask was run, no spectrum was read, no geological constraint was measured. They are **not** a ground reading and cannot settle whether laboratory experiments reproduce a plausible prebiotic sequence. Only a real experiment, ranged against a stated condition sequence with its substitution gap declared, can do that.

The honest stopping point is the shape. Fig 8 :39: *"So a search that finds nothing is never a proof of absence."* This answer does not say the sequence cannot be reproduced. It says what a reproduction claim would have to look like in order to count.

## 6. A correction carried forward from the exemplar

The QMGR exemplar initially treated "shape, not a name" as a blanket ban on naming anything, then narrowed it in its own §5b: the phrase governs a **missing** primitive, not a binding onto an atom already held. For this question the same narrowing matters. The framework does not name which prebiotic pathway is correct, and that is the relevant limit here. But it can name the *criteria* a pathway must meet, because those criteria are derived from figure lines, not imported from a laboratory. Claiming the framework supplies a specific geochemical scenario would exceed what it returns; declining to state the criteria would fall short of what the figures do govern.

Both statements are kept: the framework returns a shape, not a name of a pathway; and the shape itself is nameable, checkable and refutable.
