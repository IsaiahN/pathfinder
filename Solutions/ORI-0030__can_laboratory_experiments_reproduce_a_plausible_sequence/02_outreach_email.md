**Subject:** One checkable claim about sequencing prebiotic experiments, no theory required

> Hi [name],
>
> I am writing about a narrow methodological claim, not about a theory. I am not asking you to agree with anything and I am not asking you to read a framework first. If the claim fails, there is nothing else to look at.
>
> **The claim (`general` on the shape, `specified` on the laboratory part, not `grounded`):** a single isolated reaction and a sequence of natural conditions are different kinds of claim. A single reaction returns a state, something can form here. A sequence returns a route, given this order of conditions with the product of each step carried into the next, the chain runs or it fails at step k. The framework's bond grammar makes the distinction explicitly: a vocabulary without bonds can name a state and cannot name a route to one. So a successful one-pot or one-condition result does not, by itself, answer a question about a plausible sequence.
>
> Three consequences follow, and each is checkable on its own:
>
> 1. The residual for a proposed pathway belongs to each step and each interstep hand-off, not to the pathway averaged. A mean yield across steps hides the one live slot.
> 2. A laboratory run of a condition sequence is a substituted habitat, not the natural habitat with a piece removed. It proves that the apparatus is wired correctly. What the substitution failed to reproduce, and what it brought with it, stay invisible until the goal fails. Those two things should be enumerated and the lab-to-nature gap measured, not assumed to be zero.
> 3. The question is a union of at least three conditions: each step chemically tractable, the condition order compatible with geological constraints, and the substitution faithful. These bind jointly. A single lever clears only the region where one condition is missing.
>
> **What would refute the claim, written down before this message:**
> 1. A single-isolation result that determines the behaviour of the full sequence with no added condition, carry-over or intervention.
> 2. A laboratory sequence whose gap to the proposed natural habitat is measured as zero at every step.
> 3. A demonstration that per-step residuals add no diagnostic power over one global pathway statistic.
> 4. A sequence claim settled by agreement among groups using the same protocol, feedstocks and analytical conventions, with no independent ground.
>
> A one-line failure is more useful to me than agreement. If you would rather not engage, no reply is needed.
>
> **If it survives, and only then:** here are the sources. The figures are the authority, not my summary of them.
> - Verbatim figure text with line numbers (local framework extract): Fig 12 on state versus route, Fig 11 on substituted habitats, Fig 1 on per-slot residuals, Fig 4 on the round-trip gap, Fig 9 on unions.
> - ISSOL, the international society for origin-of-life research: https://issol.org/
> - Discover Life, the open-access successor to Origins of Life and Evolution of Biospheres: https://link.springer.com/journal/11084
> - Astrobiology, the peer-reviewed journal covering prebiotic chemistry: https://home.liebertpub.com/publications/astrobiology/99/overview
>
> **If you want to reuse the method:** the formula is in the figures. Run it on a problem you know well and can check against a ground. Write down what would refute your result before you run it, and state whether the result is `general`, `specified` or `grounded`. A reported failure is more useful to me than a reported success.
>
> [name]

## Two-stage sequence

1. **Stage 1.** Send the claim, its three consequences and the four refuters. Ask what the recipient would count as a failure. Record that verdict before any framework material is shown.
2. **Stage 2, opt-in only.** If they are curious, send the figure links and the world sources. Their later agreement is never pooled as corroboration, because once they hold the interior they have joined the frame being checked (Fig 10 :42).

A third stage, offering the formula as a reusable method, is permitted because a generator can be given away without giving away an answer. Each user must write their own refuter and state their own mode.
