# Structure, order and analogues for the education question

**Mode: `specified`.** This is a simulated derivation checked against the figures, not a ground reading. See `05_what_shape_means.md` section 6.

## RESIDUAL

The audit in `01` found that the question bundles two conditions (preserve, question). Two structural questions remain: (1) is the bundle a union or a partition, and (2) what is the dependency order of the loops, so that effort is not spent above a break that cannot use it? A third question is asked as an analogue check: are the two conditions isomers of one another, or different kinds of operation?

## DERIVATION

### Q1 - Union or partition?

- Fig 9 :54: **"And when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again."**
- Fig 9 :57: **"a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either."**
- Preserve operates on head starts and conventions. Question operates on refusals and independent grounds. They have different currencies (cheap search versus agreement about meaning, versus settlement by what pays) and different anchors. A partition requires a single common anchor for the subproblems. There is none.
- **Conclusion:** union. Two loops, never averaged. A single "tradition versus inquiry" score is the Fig 1 averaging error.
- **Falsifier:** show that both conditions share one anchor and one currency in every educational system, so one loop governs both. Until then, the union reading stands.

### Q2 - Are preservation and questioning isomers?

- Fig 12 :117: **"isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."**
- Preservation and questioning share some atoms (a claim, a ground, a learner) but do not share the same full atom set in the same arrangement. Preservation composes `settled claim → method → next learner` (Fig 4 head start). Questioning composes `held claim + ¬ → refusal → next learner` (Fig 5 refuted kind). The bonds differ: one is a cross-scale transfer (`→` with a membrane), the other is a unary negation followed by an addition to the record.
- **Conclusion:** not isomers under Fig 12's definition. They are closer to Fig 12 :123's functional group: **"functional group. A substructure whose presence fixes the outcome. The group says what kind; the qualifier says which member."** Both contain a ground relation; the qualifier (preserve versus refuse) decides which member. Claiming they are isomers would overcharge the bond term by treating a transfer and a negation as the same operator.
- **Falsifier:** write both conditions as molecules over the identical atom set with the same bonds and show that only the spelling differs. If that can be done while keeping the ground relation in both, the isomer claim is restored. As written it cannot be.

### Q3 - What is the dependency order?

- Fig 3 :20: **"The chain is ordered by dependency, not by importance. Each link consumes what the one above produces, so effort spent above the break arrives at a step that cannot use it."**
- Order for this question:
  1. **Perceive:** name a residual per idea, not a global average (Fig 1 :19).
  2. **Vocabulary:** have terms for what an idea affords, a head start, a convention, a refusal (Fig 3 :7).
  3. **Objective:** say what would count as progress, namely that the idea's residual stops arriving or is rightly refused (Fig 3 :10).
  4. **Planning:** choose which ideas to teach as methods, which as conventions, which as targets of question.
  5. **Learn and carry:** carry only the method up, send only a head start down (Fig 4 :22).
- **Where the chain usually breaks:** between vocabulary and objective. Schools have abundant terms for inherited content and few instruments for what would count as a corrected idea. Fig 3 :27: **"A link with no instrument is not a link that works. It is a link nobody has looked at."**
- **Falsifier:** demonstrate an educational system where the objective link is measured directly (a ground reading of which ideas are owed a refusal) while the vocabulary link is absent. That would reverse the dependency and break this order.

### Q4 - What do the analogues actually carry?

| analogue | figure | what carries | what does not carry |
|---|---|---|---|
| Curriculum as membrane | Fig 4 :22 | methods up, head starts down, never a recording | the recording of a single successful occasion |
| Classroom as room | Fig 7 :35 | variation inside, selection between, ground votes | the room's agreement as a verdict |
| Shared store as habitat | Fig 11 :39 | capability is agent-and-habitat | improvement of the learner in isolation |
| Curriculum as convention set | Fig 2 :41, Fig 10 :47 | shared meaning, efficient product | a convention that decides an outcome |

- Fig 8 :50 to :52: **"Convergent derivation and adopted import look identical in the contents. Only the record of where each came from separates them."** Each analogue arrives from a different field. Their agreement with the figure is retrodiction, not forward prediction. The record of which figure was chosen first is kept in `01`.
- **Falsifier:** show an analogue whose contents match the figure but whose closure was derived first and the figure imported later without a source gate. That would be echo without shadow, and its agreement would carry no weight.

## CONCLUSION

The question is a union of two conditions, not a partition and not a single balance. Preservation and questioning are not isomers; they are members of one functional group distinguished by whether the operation is a cross-scale head start or a negation added to the record. The dependency order runs from a per-slot residual to a vocabulary of affordances to an objective, and the likely break is the middle link, where schools have content vocabulary but no instrument for what a corrected idea would look like. Three falsifiers are stated. The strongest one is the union/partition test, because it decides whether the rest of the derivation needs two loops at all.
