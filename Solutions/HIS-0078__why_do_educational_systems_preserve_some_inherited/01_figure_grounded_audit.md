## 0. The residual, stated in effect terms

The question asks for the cause of a differential. A learner arrives with a belief `b` that is *"Formed from its own history and from what it inherited, mixed by w"* (Symbols table, `b`). The weighting `w` is *"How far the belief leans on private history versus inherited prior. w_A + w_B = 1, and w is learnable from the outcomes of past weightings"* (Symbols table, `w`). In effect: what is inherited is already part of the starting belief, and the mix is learnable, so an educational system that changes the mix changes what the learner can predict.

R is the gap between prediction and observation, per slot. The system must decide, for each inherited idea, whether the idea is transferred (nothing owed), refitted, or refused. The effect to explain is why some inherited ideas are carried forward as given and others are made objects of question. Option A (all inherited ideas preserved) leads to a system that can only accumulate. Option B (all inherited ideas questioned) destroys the head start and makes each new learner re-pay the cost of everything prior. The observed middle path is the thing to derive.

**R by arity, symmetry, scale.** Arity: one residual per idea, not one global verdict. Fig 1 :19: **"The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated."** Symmetry: asymmetric. Preservation transfers a head start across a scale boundary (Fig 4), while questioning adds a refusal (negation, a unary operator, Operators :70 to :78). They are not two symmetric moves on one substance. Scale: one learner (one agent) versus a community and its generations (population), joined by the membrane of Fig 4.

## 1. Domain census (before any figure line is chosen)

One line per domain with actors / currency / anchor. No nameable anchor means it is not a domain yet.

- **Domain 1, the learner's belief formation.** Actors: the learner, the teacher, the belief `b`, the weighting `w`. Currency: per-slot residual `R`, updated by contact. Anchor: the ground, what pays, which does not change because the learner thinks otherwise. Nameable. This is a partition boundary question within one agent: which held terms are load-bearing and which are owed a refusal.
- **Domain 2, the convention and shared-meaning layer.** Actors: members of a community reading each other against a shared backdrop. Currency: agreement about shared meaning. Anchor: the convention itself, which is a declared meaning with no truth value until tested (Fig 10 :46). Nameable. Question type: a convention is not a claim about the world, so it is preserved for coordination and must never decide a verdict.
- **Domain 3, the verification layer.** Actors: claims, instruments, refuters, independent frames. Currency: settlement by what pays. Anchor: the ground, pinned (Fig 2 :24 to :27). Nameable. Question type: correlation from a shared evidence pool is disqualifying here.
- **Domain 4, the transmission membrane.** Actors: generations, methods, recordings, new learners. Currency: methods and head starts versus recordings and replays. Anchor: the round-trip gap `R_T` (Fig 4 :30). Nameable. Question type: what may cross between one agent and a population.

**Partition or union.** The question bundles two conditions: preserve, and question. Fig 9 :54: **"And when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again."** Fig 9 :57: **"a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either."** So this is a union compound with its own loops. Domain 1 and Domain 3 are separate loops. Domain 2 and Domain 4 are separate loops. Their verdicts are kept apart and never averaged.

**Order by dependency (Fig 3).** The chain is ordered by dependency, not importance: perception of the residual, vocabulary for what an idea affords, objective (what counts as progress), planning, then learn and carry. Refuse before mint (Formula step 2). Describe the residual before choosing a frame (Fig 9 :27 to :28). Settle a method before it crosses the membrane (Fig 4 :8).

## 3. Findings F1 to F9, with verbatim quotes and refuters

### F1 - Preservation is the head-start rule across the membrane (governs D4)

Fig 4 :22: **"Only methods cross up. Only head starts come down."** Fig 4 :11: **"a head start, never a recording."** Fig 4 :24: **"downward: something that makes the next search cheaper. Never a replay of what worked once."** Fig 4 :25 to :26: **"A recording carried upward looks like knowledge and is a description of one occasion. Replayed downward, it produces a system that repeats a past success and cannot produce a new one."**

An inherited idea is preserved when it functions as a head start: settled, compressed to its essentials, and able to make the next learner's search cheaper. This is why education transmits a curriculum at all. The price of losing it is stated in Fig 12 :107: **"Settling compresses the description and not what it cost to establish"**, and Fig 12 :108: **"where the arrangement is recorded, re-deriving it is a lookup and the actions were spent once."** The record lets the next learner skip the bill. What must not cross is the recording of one occasion, because replay produces repetition, not a new result.

A textbook is an output, not a generator. *They're Scanning Books When They Should Be Studying Nature*: *"A written sentence is a lossy compression of the reasoning that produced it."* So a textbook taught as a script to replay is the Fig 4 failure. Taught as a method to reapply with a ground, it is the Fig 4 success. This is not a strain in the figure; it is a prediction the figure makes about two ways to use the same book.

- **Mode:** `general` (structural). Which specific idea is a head start is `specified` and needs a measurement.
- **Refuter:** show an inherited idea preserved with no head-start function (it does not make any subsequent search cheaper) and no convention function (F2), preserved instead only because a prestigious source first held it. That breaks Fig 4 as the governing line for D4.
- **Triangulation:** triangulated across Fig 4 and Fig 12 (two figures with low overlap: membrane transport versus cost of re-derivation).

### F2 - Preservation is also the convention rule (governs D2)

Fig 2 :41: **"efficient for a convention: agreement about a shared meaning is the product, not a corruption of it."** Fig 2 :40: **"disqualifying for a verdict: agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool."**

These are two different quantities, not a contradiction. Shared language, notation, and basic assumptions are conventions. Preserving them is efficient because agreement about meaning is the product a community needs in order to range against the world together. But the same agreement is worthless for deciding what is true when the agreeing frames learned from one another.

The guard is Fig 10 :18: **"a declared meaning is not a grounded one"**, followed by Fig 10 :19: **"→ install what can be violated."** And Fig 10 :47: **"a convention that decides an outcome has stopped being one."** So an educational system preserves conventions correctly only while they remain conventions: shared meanings that can be checked and violated, not verdicts that settle a question by authority.

- **Mode:** `general`.
- **Refuter:** show a community in which a shared meaning is preserved but cannot be violated or checked, and where it decides an outcome. That is the Fig 10 failure genus, not a refutation of the two-quantity distinction; it refutes the claim that all preservation in D2 is legitimate convention preservation.
- **Triangulation:** triangulated across Fig 2 and Fig 10 (correlation valence versus convention versus verdict).

### F3 - Questioning requires a refusal operation, not just new content (governs D1)

Fig 12 :140: **"A vocabulary that cannot refuse can only accumulate."** Operators :58: **"a refutation is a negation, which is why a vocabulary without it can only accumulate."** Fig 5 :53: **"refuted: a term we hold is wrong; a term is owed that says so."**

To question an inherited claim is to add a term that refuses it. Negation is unary and is not one of the seven bonds (Operators :70 to :78), so a system that only composes new terms can add content around an error but cannot carry the correction. Formula step 3 makes the asymmetry explicit: *"NOTHING IS EVER DELETED, and the reach falls because something was ADDED."* Questioning therefore adds a record, and the reach falls because a term that was composed over is no longer available for composition. This is why the correction must be taught, not merely discovered: without the operator, the learner cannot carry it.

- **Mode:** `general` for the operator requirement; `specified` for the claim that a particular curriculum teaches it.
- **Refuter:** exhibit a system that carries a correction of an inherited claim with no negation-type operation, only accumulation, and that nevertheless stops composing over the refuted term. That breaks Fig 12 :140 and Operators :58.
- **Triangulation:** triangulated across Fig 12, Operators, Fig 5, and Formula step 3.

### F4 - Questioning is required by independence, not by a preference for novelty (governs D3)

Fig 2 :35: **"Alignment is triangulation, not negotiation. A mirror cannot correct you."** Fig 2 :45: **"Frames range against the ground. They do not negotiate with it."** Fig 7 :35: **"A room's agreement is variation, not a verdict. Selection between rooms is where the ground votes."**

A student who accepts an inherited idea because the teacher and the textbook agree has joined a mirror chain. The class's agreement is variation inside a room, not a verdict. The ground votes only between rooms whose closures differ and whose frames are each corrected separately (Fig 2 :46). Questioning is the practice that keeps the learner a separate frame rather than a copy of the frame that taught them.

Fig 2 :42: **"Buy independence in proportion to the cost of being wrong."** So the amount of questioning a system should encourage is not a cultural preference. It is a function of what an error costs. Education that never buys independence is under-buying it relative to the stakes of being wrong.

- **Mode:** `general`.
- **Refuter:** show, with a measurement, that learners who only check against the same shared source produce verdicts as reliable as learners who check against an independent ground. That breaks Fig 2 :40 and Fig 7 :35 in this domain.
- **Triangulation:** triangulated across Fig 2 and Fig 7 (alignment geometry and room selection).

### F5 - Why *some* and not all: the per-slot selector

Fig 1 :19: **"The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated."** Fig 1 :22: **"Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average."** Formula step 2 names the selection quantity: **"a term whose residual PERSISTS AND DOES NOT TREND DOWN is the one to point the refusal at"** (Fig 5 :58 to :59 carries the same shrinking test read with the opposite sign).

Each inherited idea is a slot. A slot whose residual against the ground stays at zero is transferred (Fig 5 :11, *"held: the model was right, nothing owed"*). A slot whose residual persists and does not trend down is the one to refuse. The differential is therefore not a policy about tradition versus progress. It is the selector running per slot. The ideas that survive are the ones still paying: settled methods (F1), load-bearing conventions (F2). The ideas questioned are the ones whose residual keeps arriving (F3, F4).

This is the finding that dissolves the question as posed. There is no single answer of the form "education preserves X percent and questions Y percent." There is a per-slot rule and a ground that decides each slot.

- **Mode:** `general` for the rule; `gap` for the empirical allocation of which ideas in which real systems, because no figure line supplies that number.
- **Refuter:** exhibit an inherited idea whose residual persists and does not trend down, yet is preserved with no ground contact that pays for it and no convention or head-start function. That breaks the selector claim.
- **Triangulation:** triangulated across Fig 1 and Formula step 2 / Fig 5.

### F6 - The question is a union, not a single hard problem

Fig 9 :54: **"And when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again."** Fig 9 :57: **"a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either."** The Library of Babel essay states the same rule in prose: *"Perhaps your problem was a bundle or conflation of several problems masquerading as one? You will need to search all of those problems individually."*

Preservation (F1, F2) and questioning (F3, F4) are different conditions with different loops, different currencies, and different anchors. Aggregating them into one "balance between tradition and inquiry" score reproduces the Fig 1 error: a live signal disappears into an average.

- **Mode:** `general`.
- **Refuter:** show that the two conditions share one anchor and one currency in every case, so one loop governs both. That would collapse the union into a partition and remove the need for separate verdicts.
- **Triangulation:** triangulated across Fig 9 and Fig 1.

### F7 - The learning environment is part of the mechanism

Fig 11 :39: **"capability is a property of agent-and-habitat, never of the agent alone."** Fig 11 :40: **"An improvement that does not change contact changes nothing, however much it improves."** Fig 11 :61: **"the seat's power is asymmetric. Introduction is available; exclusion is not."**

A school is a habitat, not a container. What it introduces (methods, instruments, grounds, other frames) changes what the learner can contact. What it cannot do is subtract the habitat: contact is a fact about the world and does not consult the school. This is why "encouraging questioning" is not a slogan but an act of introduction. It introduces a second frame and an unmovable ground. The *How a Society of Agents Would Work* essay makes the institutional form explicit: the shared store holds *"methods, never recordings"*, verdicts come *"from outcomes, not popularity"*, and *"things fade, so they can be tried again"* while *"the record is never deleted."*

- **Mode:** `general` for the habitat claim; `specified` for any particular institution.
- **Refuter:** show an agent-only improvement in learning that demonstrably changes no contact and still moves the ground. The figure names this as the standing refuter of the capability claim (Fig 11 :71 to :72).
- **Triangulation:** triangulated across Fig 11 and Fig 4.

### F8 - Reach can fall when an inherited idea is refused

Fig 6 :42: **"What is recorded only grows. What is reachable is derived, and it can fall."** The Formula record-and-reach section: a refusal *"removes nothing"* from the record and the reach falls *"because something was ADDED rather than because anything was struck out."*

When education questions an inherited idea, it does not delete it from the curriculum's history. It adds a refusal. Everything composed over the refused term loses standing, so the learner's reachable set shrinks even though the library grew. This explains an observed feature of education: a corrected idea is still taught as a known error, and the record of the error is kept precisely so a later learner can locate it. Fig 10 :63 to :64: *"Not so the ruling can be appealed, but so a later reader can locate the error rather than only feel it."*

- **Mode:** `general`.
- **Refuter:** show a system whose record grows by a refusal while the derivable set stays identical, in a case where the refused term had been composed over. That breaks the fall claim in Fig 6.
- **Triangulation:** triangulated across Fig 6 and Formula record-and-reach.

## 4. Pre-registered refuter (written before the conclusion)

Per the seat rule, *"a falsifier written before the run is a design instrument, not only an honesty device."* The shape claim in this folder is refuted if any of the following holds:

1. An inherited idea is shown to be preserved with no head-start function (it does not cheapen any subsequent search) and no convention function (it is not shared meaning), preserved only by prestige. Breaks F1 and F2.
2. A system is shown to carry a correction of an inherited claim with no negation-type operation, only accumulation, and still stop composing over the refused term. Breaks F3.
3. A measurement shows learners who check only against the same shared source reach verdicts as reliable as learners who check against an independent ground. Breaks F4.
4. An inherited idea whose residual persists and does not trend down is preserved with no ground contact that pays for it. Breaks F5.
5. The two conditions (preserve, question) are shown to share one anchor and one currency in every case, so one loop governs both. Breaks F6.

Until one is run, no part of this answer may be labelled `grounded`.

## 5. Recommendation

1. State the residual per idea, never as a single tradition-versus-inquiry score (F5, F6).
2. Treat preservation as two distinct legitimate functions: head start (F1) and convention (F2). Guard the second with Fig 10 :47.
3. Teach questioning as an operator: a refusal added to the record, with the history kept (F3, F8).
4. Buy independence deliberately, in proportion to the cost of being wrong (F4).
5. Design the habitat by introduction, not subtraction, and measure the round trip (F7, Fig 4 :30).
6. Report the empirical allocation as a `gap` until a ground reading supplies it (F9).
