# Expert verification packet - inherited ideas and questioning in education

**What this is:** a claim in a form a researcher in education or knowledge transmission can check. Mode, derivation, and refuters are stated before the conclusion, so a reader knows what to look for and what would settle it.

**Mode: `specified` on the structural claim, `general` on the shape. Not `grounded`.** No classroom or curriculum is claimed to have been measured. This is a simulated derivation checked for internal consistency against the figures, not a ground reading.

## 1. The claim, in one paragraph

The question is a union of two conditions, not one balance. Preservation runs on two functions: a settled inherited idea is a head start that cheapens the next learner's search, and a shared meaning is a convention whose agreement is the product, so long as the convention never decides a verdict. Questioning runs on two other mechanisms: a correction must be added as a refusal because a vocabulary without negation can only accumulate, and independence must be bought from an unmovable ground because agreement among frames that share an evidence pool is worthless as a check. The differential is per idea, selected by the residual that persists and does not trend down. The framework returns that shape, not the allocation of which ideas in which system.

## 2. Three checkable sub-claims

### C1 - framework-internal, checkable by reading Figures 4, 2 and 10

- **Statement:** preservation has two legitimate functions, head start and convention, and the convention must not decide an outcome.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 4 :22, *"Only methods cross up. Only head starts come down."* Fig 4 :24, *"downward: something that makes the next search cheaper. - **Refuter:** show an inherited idea preserved with no head-start function and no convention function, kept only because a prestigious source first held it; or show a shared meaning that is preserved but cannot be violated and that decides an outcome.

### C2 - framework-internal, checkable by reading Figures 12, 6 and 2

- **Statement:** questioning requires a negation added to the record, and independent grounds, not a change of mind inside one frame.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 12 :140, *"A vocabulary that cannot refuse can only accumulate."* Operators :58, *"a refutation is a negation, which is why a vocabulary without it can only accumulate."* Fig 6 :42, *"What is recorded only grows. What is reachable is derived, and it can fall."* Fig 2 :35, *"Alignment is triangulation, not negotiation. A mirror cannot correct you."*
- **Refuter:** exhibit a system that carries a correction with no negation-type operation and still stops composing over the refused term; or a measurement showing learners checking only against the same shared source reach verdicts as reliable as learners checking against an independent ground.

### C3 - framework-internal derivation, checkable by reading Figures 1, 9 and Formula step 2

- **Statement:** the bundle is a union of two conditions and the selected idea is the one with a persistent, non-descending residual, per slot.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 9 :54, *"And when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again."* Fig 1 :19, *"The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated."* Formula step 2, *"a term whose residual PERSISTS AND DOES NOT TREND DOWN is the one to point the refusal at."*

## 3. What is explicitly not claimed

- Not a theory of education, and not a curriculum design.
- Not `grounded`. No measurement, no dataset, no classroom observation.
- Not that preservation is good in itself or questioning is good in itself. Fig 2 :42 prices independence by the cost of error.
- Not that any particular inherited idea is correctly preserved or correctly questioned. The framework returns a shape, not a name.
- Not that the two conditions can be averaged into one tradition-versus-inquiry score.
- Not that the six independent world sources in `09_watson_answer.md` prove the claim. They corroborate from other fields; the figures decide.

## 5. What would settle it

A ground reading, not agreement. The instruments owed are:

1. **Head-start test:** does the inherited idea cheapen a subsequent novel search? Compare learners with and without it on a task it does not directly answer.
2. **Convention test:** can the shared meaning be violated and checked without deciding an outcome?
3. **Refusal and independence tests:** can the learner carry the correction into a novel problem, and do learners with an independent ground outperform learners checking only against the shared source at a stated cost of error?
4. **Per-slot test:** compare the list of ideas with persistent, non-descending residuals with the ideas actually questioned.
5. **Allocation measurement:** the fraction attributable to head start, convention, and prestige respectively. This is an open `gap` in this solution.
