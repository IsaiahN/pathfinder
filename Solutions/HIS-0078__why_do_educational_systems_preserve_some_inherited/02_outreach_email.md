**Use:** one-to-one, or as a cover note attached to a submission through the public venues in `03_public_contact_points.csv`. **Not** for bulk send.

**Hard precondition:** send only after the five refuter conditions in `01_figure_grounded_audit.md` section 4 are stated in the note. The offer collapses on contact if the refuter is withheld.

**Subject:** One checkable claim about inherited ideas in education, before any theory

> Hi [name],
>
> I have one specific output I would like you to check against what you already know about education and knowledge transmission. I am not asking you to read a theory first, and I am not asking you to agree. If the output does not survive, there is nothing else to look at.
>
> **The output (`specified`, not `grounded`):** the question "why do educational systems preserve some inherited ideas while questioning others" is not one question. It is two conditions with different currencies and different grounds. Preservation runs on two distinct functions: an inherited idea that is already settled is a head start that makes the next learner's search cheaper, and a shared meaning is a convention whose agreement is the product rather than a corruption. Questioning runs on two other things: a refusal that must be added to the record because a vocabulary without it can only accumulate, and an independent ground, because agreement among frames that learned from one another is worthless as a verdict. The differential is per idea, not a global balance. The idea selected for questioning is the one whose residual keeps arriving and does not trend down.
>
> That is a **shape**, not a measurement. It does not say which fraction of ideas in which school is preserved for which reason. I am not claiming to have measured that.
>
> **What I am not claiming:** that all preservation is good (a convention that decides an outcome has stopped being a convention), that all questioning is good (independence is bought in proportion to the cost of being wrong), or that a real classroom has been observed. Nothing here is `grounded`.
>
> **What would refute the shape claim, written before sending this:**
> 1. an inherited idea preserved with no head-start function and no convention function, kept only because a prestigious source first held it;
> 2. a system that carries a correction with no negation-type operation and still stops composing over the refused term;
> 3. a measurement showing that learners who check only against the same shared source reach verdicts as reliable as learners who check against an independent ground;
> 4. an idea with a persistent, non-descending residual preserved with no ground contact that pays for it;
> 5. a demonstration that preservation and questioning share one anchor and one currency, so one loop governs both.
>
> A one-line "this fails because..." is more useful to me than agreement. If you would rather not engage, no reply is needed.
>
