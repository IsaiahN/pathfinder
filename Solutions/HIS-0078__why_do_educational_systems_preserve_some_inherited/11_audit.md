## Round trip gap R_T

The Symbols table defines R_T as the gap between what was sent up and what came back down, `gap(x, (T_E ∘ T_A)(x))`, measuring what the coarser description cannot hold. Fig 4 :30 requires it to be measured rather than assumed. I ran the round trip on the condensation this folder actually uses.

1. **Sent up (x):** the verbatim figure lines, chiefly Fig 4 :22 to :25 (head start, never recording), Fig 2 :40 and :41 (two valences of agreement), Fig 12 :140 (refusal), Fig 1 :19 (per-slot residual).
2. **Abstraction (T_A):** the shorthand in `07`, *"preserve head starts and conventions, question with refusals and independent grounds, decide per idea by the residual."*
3. **Concretisation (T_E):** an answer to the original question, for example *"schools keep the multiplication table because it is a head start, keep the school language as a convention, and teach students to refute claims that keep failing against evidence."*

**R_T is nonzero.** Three things the shorthand holds do not survive the trip back down:

- the **per-slot indexing**, which the concretisation tends to replace with a list of examples, losing Fig 1 :19;
- the **guard on conventions**, that a convention must never decide an outcome (Fig 10 :47), which a concretisation easily drops when it calls something a shared assumption;
- the **record-and-reach asymmetry**, that a refusal is added and reach falls, which the shorthand compresses into "correct the idea" as if the old term were deleted.

Conversely, one thing survives cleanly: two conditions with different currencies and anchors, and a head-start function for preservation. That is the residue worth sending. R_T was **not** measured numerically, because no ground reading exists; it is recorded qualitatively as the enumerated set of dropped constraints. Treating "the formula is the condensed version of the metalaws" as identity would set R_T to zero and would be the dead-reckoning error Fig 4 :30 names.
