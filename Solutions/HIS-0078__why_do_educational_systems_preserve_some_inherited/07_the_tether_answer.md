# The Tether's answer on inherited ideas and questioning

**Mode: `specified` on the structural claim, `general` on the shape. Not `grounded`.** Simulated derivation checked against the figures (`05_what_shape_means.md` section 6), not a ground reading.

## 1. The residual it answers

A learner starts with inherited belief mixed with private history (`w`). The system must decide per idea whether the idea is transferred, refitted, or refused. The effect to explain is the differential. It is a union compound with two conditions, not one hard question: preservation and questioning have different currencies and different anchors (Fig 9 :54, :57).

## 2. The shape

1. **Preservation is a head start across a membrane.** Fig 4 :22: *"Only methods cross up. Only head starts come down."* Fig 4 :24: *"downward: something that makes the next search cheaper. Never a replay of what worked once."* Inherited ideas that are already settled are kept because the bill was paid once and the next learner can skip it (Fig 12 :107 to :108). What must not cross is the recording of one occasion.
2. **Preservation is also a convention.** Fig 2 :41: *"efficient for a convention: agreement about a shared meaning is the product, not a corruption of it."* Shared language, notation, and assumptions are preserved for coordination. The guard is Fig 10 :47: *"a convention that decides an outcome has stopped being one."*
3. **Questioning is a refusal added to the record.** Fig 12 :140: *"A vocabulary that cannot refuse can only accumulate."* Operators :58: *"a refutation is a negation, which is why a vocabulary without it can only accumulate."* Questioning adds a term that refuses; it does not delete. Fig 6 :42: *"What is recorded only grows. What is reachable is derived, and it can fall."*
4. **Questioning buys independence against the ground.** Fig 2 :35: *"Alignment is triangulation, not negotiation. A mirror cannot correct you."* Fig 7 :35: *"A room's agreement is variation, not a verdict. Selection between rooms is where the ground votes."* A class that accepts an inherited idea because teacher and textbook agree is a mirror chain. Independence is bought *"in proportion to the cost of being wrong"* (Fig 2 :42).
5. **The differential is per idea.** Fig 1 :19: *"The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated."* The idea selected for questioning is *"a term whose residual PERSISTS AND DOES NOT TREND DOWN"* (Formula step 2). A slot with no residual is transferred, nothing owed. There is no single tradition-versus-inquiry balance.
6. **The school is a habitat.** Fig 11 :39: *"capability is a property of agent-and-habitat, never of the agent alone."* Questioning is an act of introduction: a second frame and an unmovable ground. Subtraction is not available (Fig 11 :61).

## 3. What it explicitly does not say

- Not that preservation is good in itself. A convention that decides an outcome has stopped being a convention.
- Not that questioning is good in itself. Independence is priced by the cost of error.
- Not that any real classroom, curriculum, or historical period has been measured. Nothing here is `grounded`.
- Not which particular inherited idea belongs in which slot. The framework returns a shape, not a name.
- Not that the two conditions can be averaged into one score. That is the Fig 1 error.
- Not that the whole framework is a theory of everything. There is no last room (Fig 7 :6).

## 4. What would settle it - the ground

A measurement, not a further argument. The instruments owed are:

- **Head-start test:** does the inherited idea make a subsequent novel search cheaper? Compare learners with and without the idea on a task the idea does not directly answer. If there is no cost difference, F1 fails.
- **Convention test:** can the shared meaning be violated and checked without deciding an outcome? If it decides an outcome, Fig 10 :47 fires.
- **Refusal test:** can the learner carry the correction, that is, add a term that stops composing over the refused idea? A novel-problem probe that requires the correction settles this.
- **Independence test:** do learners who check against an independent ground outperform learners who check only against the same shared source, at a stated cost of error? If not, Fig 2 :40 and Fig 7 :35 fail here.
- **Per-slot test:** list ideas with persistent, non-descending residuals and compare them with the ideas actually questioned. If the sets do not track, the selector claim fails.
- **Allocation measurement:** the fraction of preserved ideas attributable to head start versus convention versus prestige. This is a `gap` in this solution. Only a ground reading can fill it.

## 5. The honest stop

**Unreached, not unreachable.** The eight essays are all by the framework's author, so their agreement with the figures is one evidence pool. The six independent world sources in `09` corroborate from different fields but are secondary. No classroom was measured. The answer stops at the limit of the available instruments, and the lineage is recorded so the stop is checkable. Indiscernibility is not identity.

The one live strain: locating the boundary where a convention becomes a verdict needs an instrument in a specific institution. The figures give the rule and stop. It is named, not hidden.

## 6. One-paragraph version for outreach

> The Tether does not say "preserve tradition" or "question everything." It says the question is two questions. Inherited ideas are preserved for two distinct reasons: a settled idea is a head start that makes the next learner's search cheaper, and a shared meaning is a convention whose agreement is the product, provided the convention never decides a verdict. Students are taught to question for two other reasons: a correction has to be added as a refusal because a vocabulary that cannot refuse can only accumulate, and agreement among people who learned from the same source is worthless as a check against the world, so an independent ground is needed. Which idea gets which treatment is decided per idea by the residual that keeps arriving, not by a single balance between tradition and inquiry. The framework returns that shape, not a list of which ideas to keep. Whether a real school does this, and at what cost, is a measurement this answer does not claim to have made.
