## What the wider literature contributes (secondary only)

1. **The intervention test.** Both SEP entries and the Wikipedia methodology page independently state the difference between association and causation in terms of what happens when a cause is changed or set. This is a strong corroboration of Fig 10 :40 (*"An improvement that does not change contact changes nothing, however much it improves."*) from fields that did not read the figures.
2. **Common cause and confounding.** The cognitive-science page gives the ice-cream and drowning case, which is Fig 2's "collapse 2: common cause" in a textbook example.
3. **Pluralism and triangulation.** Vandenbroucke, Broadbent and Pearce (2016) argue against reducing all causal evidence to one design and explicitly defend triangulation. That is Fig 7 :35 (*"A room's agreement is variation, not a verdict. Selection between rooms is where the ground votes."*) arrived at independently.
4. **A real conflict, handled by re-census.** The same paper quotes Pearl: *"Surely we have causation without manipulation."* Read naively, this conflicts with the framework's emphasis on intervention. Re-census resolves it: the figures require a change of **contact** with the ground (Fig 10, Fig 11), not a human act. An unmanipulable cause can still be settled by an instrument that changes what the ground returns. The conflict sent us back to the domain census; it did not flip the verdict. The figure lines govern; the world source only corroborates or, here, sharpens the reading.

## Distribution: offer, do not push

- Fig 4 :22 to :25: *"Only methods cross up. Only head starts come down."* Sending the full architecture before a reader has described a residual sends a recording, not a generator. Lead with the shape claim and the refuter.
- Fig 10 :42: *"A verifier that can reconstruct the claim cannot verify it."* A recipient who has absorbed the framework before ruling has joined the frame being checked.
- Fig 9 :27 to :28: *"describe it before you go looking, or any frame you pick will seem to fit."* Ask what the reader would expect to see if the shape claim were true before giving them the sources.
- Seat Map :163: *"High correlation is disqualifying for a verdict and efficient for a convention."* A mass send selected for agreement produces a convention, not corroboration.
- Fig 2 :40: *"disqualifying for a verdict: agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool"* (full line begins *"disqualifying for a verdict:"*). Later agreement from a reader who has seen the sources is one pool talking.

**Recommended order in a reply:** (1) residual and four refuters, (2) ask what the reader would count as a failure, record the verdict, (3) only then the links, with the note that the figures, not this summary, are the authority, (4) optionally the formula as a method for the reader's own checked problem.
