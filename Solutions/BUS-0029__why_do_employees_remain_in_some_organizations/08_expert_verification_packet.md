## 1. The claim, in one paragraph

For one employee-slot, `R_s = |Γ(b,a) − o'|`: the gap between what the employee's frame predicts and the revealed stay or leave event. Retention is a property of the employee and the habitat together, not of pay or of the person alone. Pay is an actor whose valence is relative to `R_s`, not a fixed good, so increasing an atom already held in the frame is search inside a closed frame and cannot move the wall when what is missing is a bond or an outside comparison. "Stay despite poor conditions" and "leave despite attractive pay" are one union compound read two ways, not one puzzle or two. Same atoms under different typed bonds are different substances, which is why two employees with the same pay and conditions can diverge. The framework returns this shape, not a name; which bond wins in a slot stays open until an instrument that discriminates bond types exists.

## 2. Three checkable sub-claims

### C1 : framework-internal, checkable by reading Figures 11 and 8

- **Statement:** retention is a property of employee-and-habitat; changing an already-held atom (pay) in isolation does not change the decision where the missing thing is a bond or a differing frame.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 11 :39 *"So the answerable question is not what intelligence is, but what makes a system capable. And capability is a property of agent-and-habitat, never of the agent alone."* Fig 11 :40 *"An improvement that does not change contact changes nothing, however much it improves."* Fig 8 :32 *"Closure is idempotent. Composing inside a closed set never leaves it, so no search, however long, adds an atom. This one is definitional: it is what closure means."*
- **Refuter:** a within-person record in which stay/leave and tenure are invariant to changes in ties, alternatives and channel updates while pay varies systematically with the residual not trending down; or a repeated reversal of a quit by increasing an already-held atom with no contact with a differing frame.

### C2 : psychology-facing, checkable against the commitment and embeddedness literature

- **Statement:** the three commitment components and job embeddedness are typed bonds and atom sets over the same employee, not quantities of one stuff; two employees over the same atoms and different bonds are isomers in the sense of Fig 12.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 12 :117 *"isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."* Fig 12 :124 *"functional group. A substructure whose presence fixes the outcome. The group says what kind; the qualifier says which member."* Fig 12 :5 *"A set of ingredients is not one. The bond is the rest of the information, and it is the half nothing was recording."*
- **What to check:** QIC-WD's organizational commitment summary defines the components as desire, obligation and perceived cost of leaving, and says they *"can be experienced by the same individual simultaneously."* QIC-WD's job embeddedness summary lists links, fit and sacrifice. Are these bond-type distinctions, as claimed, or do they collapse to one factor with no incremental prediction of the observed quit?
- **Refuter:** the three components collapse to one factor with no incremental prediction of the observed quit; or two employees with identical atom sets and identical bond types still diverge at the ground with no unmeasured atom.

### C3 : method-facing, checkable by anyone who has run a turnover study

- **Statement:** the two halves of the question are a union compound, indexed per slot; an aggregated turnover rate is a budget filter, never a verdict; the decision can run on a decayed channel while the ground does not decay.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 9 :55 *"A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones."* Fig 1 :19 *"The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated."* Fig 10 :24 *"the ground does not decay, the channel does"* and Fig 10 :26 *"saturated metrics, pooled readings"*.
- **What to check:** does a single lever reliably clear both surface readings with no categorically different residue? Does updated, unpooled information flip a decision with history and ties held constant? Is the revealed event being used as the ground, or is an intention or exit-interview reason standing in for it?
- **Refuter:** one lever restores both conditions with no leftover compound; or decisions track only current readings with no history effect; or a low-overlap evidence pool shows per-employee retention fully predicted by pay averaged across slots.

## 3. What is explicitly not claimed

- Not that pay never retains anyone. Pay may be the live atom in a particular slot; a global negative would break the per-slot rule (Fig 1 :13).
- Not that commitment is a single cause of retention, or that "they stayed because they were committed" is an explanation. As the field defines it, that is the outcome renamed.
- Not that any specific driver is derived by the framework. Affective commitment, normative commitment, continuance commitment, embeddedness and fairness are bindings from the psychology frame with provenance recorded and zero verdict weight until a ground settles them.
- Not a theory of exit interviews. A report generated inside the frame under check is not a ground reading.
- Not `grounded`. Nothing here is asserted as settled by an experiment.

## 5. What would settle it

A source-discriminating instrument, not a further argument. *The Hard Problem is About Source*: an instrument that reads green *"cannot comment on the source"*, and *"The answer is unreached until a better instrument exists."*

1. **Bond-type test:** measure `want to`, `ought to` and `have to` independently of the stated reason and of the outcome, and see which predicts the revealed event per slot.
2. **Contact test:** hold pay constant, introduce an outside offer or a differing team, and observe whether the decision reverses at the ground.
3. **Channel test:** hold history and ties constant, update the reading of current conditions, and observe whether the decision flips.
4. **Habitat test:** check whether a better-paid move reproduces the goal-relevant actors without inheriting unchosen ones.
