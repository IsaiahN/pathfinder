# Atoms, bonds and order in the retention molecule

**Mode: `specified`.** This is a simulated derivation checked against the figures, not a ground reading (see `05_what_shape_means.md` section 6).

## RESIDUAL

The question asks why two surface readings (stay in poor conditions, leave despite pay) coexist. The residual is `R_s = |Γ(b,a) − o'|` per employee-slot. Two supporting questions are owed: (1) can the stay and leave decisions be written as molecules over a shared atom set, differing in bond rather than in ingredients, and (2) what is the dependency order, so that effort is not spent above a break.

## DOMAIN CENSUS

Atoms held in the retention frame: pay, working conditions, tenure or sunk investment, peers and manager, outside alternatives, community and family ties, and the shock event. Bonds held: the seven typed relations plus negation, which is a unary operator and not a bond (Operators table :70-78). No new primitive is available without import. D1 to D4 are the domains in `01_figure_grounded_audit.md` section 1; X1 to X3 were re-censused out.

## DERIVATION

### Q1 : What is the atom and what is the bond?

Fig 12 :5 *"A set of ingredients is not one. The bond is the rest of the information, and it is the half nothing was recording."*

- **Atoms:** pay, conditions, tenure, peers, alternatives, community ties, shock. These are the ingredients a pay-and-conditions survey records.
- **Bonds:** `want to`, `ought to`, `have to` are the three typed relations the psychology frame calls affective, normative and continuance commitment (QIC-WD organizational commitment, opened 2026-11-22). They are not amounts of one stuff. Fig 12 :46 *"A bond is the typed relation between two operands. There are seven, they are not interchangeable, and which one holds is not recoverable from the operands."*
- Fig 12 :94 *"The arrangement is the substance."*

The field's own three-factor model is therefore a compositional claim: same employee, same job, different bond, different behaviour. QIC-WD reports that the components *"can be experienced by the same individual simultaneously"* and that *"varying combinations of the three types of commitment will result in different attitudinal and behavioral outcomes"*. That is a bond claim, not an atom claim.

**Falsifier, written before the run:** if the three components collapse to one factor with no incremental prediction of the observed quit, the typed-bond claim fails and the flat ingredient reading stands.

### Q2 : Are the two decisions isomers?

Fig 12 :117 *"isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."*

Fig 12 :92 *"Symmetry is what decides whether an arrangement is a new thing."*

Write the "stay despite poor conditions" molecule over the full shared atom set `{pay, conditions, tenure, peers, alternatives, community, shock}` with the bond `have to` dominating: a `⋛` comparison in which the cost of leaving exceeds the attraction of the current conditions, a `−` subtraction of what would be lost, and a `→` sequence from past investment to present obligation. Fig 12 :112 *"A plan is a sequence. A goal is a comparison. Progress is a subtraction. A refutation is a negation, and that one is not a bond."* The "have to stay" molecule is literally a comparison, a subtraction and a sequence. That is why a dictionary of conditions cannot write it.

Write the "leave despite attractive pay" molecule over the **same** atom set with the bond `want to` failing and a `¬` refusal of the stay term, often triggered by a shock or by an outside offer that crosses from D3. The refusal is a negation, not a bond, so it does not enter the bond price (Operators table :77 *"negation is not one of them, so it does not enter that count"*).

- As written with the full shared atom set, the two are **isomers**: same atoms, different bonds, different substances.
- If each is written with only its locally salient atoms (poor conditions plus tenure versus good pay plus shock), they share few atoms and are instead members of a **functional group**, Fig 12 :124 *"functional group. A substructure whose presence fixes the outcome. The group says what kind; the qualifier says which member."*
- The isomer reading is the stronger, checkable claim. The functional-group reading is the weaker one that survives if the atom sets cannot be matched.

**Falsifier, written before the run:** if either decision can be written without an atom the other requires (for example, a leave case with no alternatives and no shock, fully predicted by conditions alone), the isomer claim fails and the functional-group reading stands.

### Q3 : What is the order, and where is the break?

Fig 3 :20 *"The chain is ordered by dependency, not by importance."* Fig 3 :27 *"A link with no instrument is not a link that works. It is a link nobody has looked at."*

1. **Perception:** can the employee tell current conditions from past conditions? Often no. Fig 10 :26 *"saturated metrics, pooled readings"*. The grebe study (Kloskowski 2021) is the low-overlap instance: last year's cue drove this year's settlement.
2. **Vocabulary:** does the employee have terms for what the organization affords? Fig 12 :115 *"What can be said is that having terms for what a situation affords was taken for a dictionary and it is a grammar."* Pay is a dictionary term. The bond set is the grammar.
3. **Objective:** what would count as progress? Fig 12 :112 again: *"A goal is a comparison."* The employee compares alternatives against the current bond.
4. **Planning:** the action, stay or leave.
5. **Learn and carry:** the event is settled against the ground and becomes an atom for the next decision. Fig 12 :104 *"A settled molecule becomes an atom for whatever composes over it. So irreducible is a fact about a level and never about the world"*

The likely break is between links 2 and 3, the middle link that Fig 3 :25-27 says attracts the least attention and the fewest instruments. A retention programme that buys better perception instruments (engagement surveys) while the vocabulary or objective is missing is spending effort above the break (Fig 3 :19 *"Optimising a link that already works cannot rescue one that does not."*).

### Q4 : What do the analogues add, and where do they stop?

- **Ecology (low overlap).** The ecological-trap definition opened this session: *"Ecological traps are scenarios in which rapid environmental change leads organisms to prefer to settle in poor-quality habitats."* This is a structure match for "stay in poor conditions when the cue decouples from quality." It stops at the species level: it does not explain a person's normative obligation.
- **Animal behaviour (low overlap).** Kloskowski 2021: *"grebes lured to low-quality ponds were more likely to shift territories than birds nesting on high-quality ponds."* This is a partial match, and honestly a partial *conflict* with the literal stay-in-poor-conditions reading. Returned to the census (Fig 2 :35 *"Alignment is triangulation, not negotiation. A mirror cannot correct you."*): what it corroborates is the mechanism, cue-based and history-dependent settlement under change, and the trap for stayers on formerly good ponds. It does not flip the employee-level verdict because it is not the same domain.
- **Engineering and physics (low overlap).** IEEE: *"Hysteresis is the property of a physical system in which its current state depends on the history of past inputs, not solely on the present input."* This matches the subtraction and sequence in the `have to` molecule. It stops at the analogy: a magnet has no frame and no anchor.

Fig 13 :24 *"that entities relate, and influence travels"* (network) is the substrate structure nearest to the habitat. It does not by itself derive any particular bond.

## CONCLUSION

The two decisions can be written as isomers over a shared atom set with different bonds, or, more weakly, as members of one functional group. The `have to stay` molecule is literally a comparison, a subtraction and a sequence; the `leave` molecule adds a negation, which is not a bond. The dependency chain most likely breaks at vocabulary or objective, not at perception or planning. The analogues corroborate the mechanism shape from low-overlap pools and are stopped at the domain boundary where they conflict. `have to` versus `want to` remains a binding from the psychology frame until a source-discriminating instrument settles it.

## Falsifier for this supporting derivation
