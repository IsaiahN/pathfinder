## Two things in the wider literature that bear directly on this claim

1. **The UNESCO Box 2.2 caution is a live constraint, not a footnote.** The UN World Water Development Report 2025 has a box titled *"Caution against applying the 'peak water' concept in water resources policy"*. It says the idealized peak water trend *"weakens as the size of the basin increases where the basin outflow is further downstream from the glaciers"* and that claims about downstream reliance on glaciers are *"often mischaracterized"*. The IPCC SROCC FAQ gives the mechanism: effects are strong close to the glaciers and tend to become small or negligible far away, though meltwater still matters in hot and dry years. So a stage-1 claim that says "peak water affects all glacier-fed communities equally" fails before it is sent. The shape must be basin- and distance-specified. This is the re-census R2 in `01_figure_grounded_audit.md` section 6.
2. **The glaciology and water-policy sources share an evidence pool.** Huss and Hock 2018 is cited by the IPCC assessments and by the WWDR; ETH News is written by one of the paper's authors. Three web pages quoting one study are one witness, not three (Fig 2 :40, *"agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool"*). The genuinely low-overlap witness is Milly et al. 2008, which comes from water-resource engineering and makes the same structural point about a frame that cannot derive change from inside its own assumptions. It corroborates the method, not the basin-level forecast.

**Recommended order in a reply:** (1) the shape claim and its refuter; (2) ask what they would count as a failure in their own basin; (3) record that verdict; (4) then the links, with the note that the figures, not this summary, are the authority.

## The two-stage offer

- **Stage 1 before stage 2** is Fig 10 :52 - *"Bring a characterised residual, never the source"* - and Fig 2 :45 - *"Frames range against the ground. They do not negotiate with it."* The recipient checks the output without holding the framework's interior.
- **Stage 2 is opt-in by construction.** Once a recipient has absorbed the theory, their agreement stops being evidence. So the stage-1 verdict must be recorded *before* any theory is shown, and later agreement is never pooled as corroboration (Fig 2 :40).
- **Stage 3 sends a method, which is permitted.** Fig 4 :22 - *"Only methods cross up. Only head starts come down."* The formula is a generator, so giving it away does not give away an answer.
- **Stage 3 still needs a ground per basin.** `THE_FORMULA.md` "Direction": a frame *"cannot check the direction of its own question, because the shape a frame is searching for is not a term it holds."* Each user must write down what would refute their result before running it, and label the mode of what they get.

**Hard precondition:** stages 1 to 3 only work if the stage-1 output survives. As drafted, the public literature already forbids two tempting shortcuts: treating peak water as a uniform date, and aggregating basins. Keep both refusals in the stage-1 text.

## AI-policy and venue caveat

The Cryosphere submission page states that authors must describe the use of AI tools in the Methods or Acknowledgements, and disclose proprietary information, modifications such as training data, competing interests and ownership. The Journal of Glaciology requires an institutional submission route and ORCID for corresponding authors. Since this entire folder is a **simulated derivation, not a ground reading**, it is not submitted anywhere by this agent. A human author who chooses to submit must say plainly what was simulated, what was measured, and who takes responsibility. Presenting this package as an empirical result would fail both the venue rules and Fig 1 :27: *"They are frame-internal. A frame cannot score itself with a quantity it also produces."*
