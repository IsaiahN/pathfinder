## Independent domain census

I redid the census before reading the primary's conclusions, using the WATSON order: R by arity, symmetry and scale; one line per domain with actors / currency / anchor; partition or union; order by dependency.

- **R (arity / symmetry / scale).** The residual is the gap between a planned yield and delivered water in a glacier-fed basin. Arity: a four-place relation (glacier state, forcing, routing, human use) indexed by basin and ordered by season and year. It carries an ordered bond, because accumulation precedes peak water and peak water precedes decline, and a comparison bond, because adequacy is a threshold against demand. Symmetry: asymmetric in time and relative to demand. Scale: glacier, basin, managed system, downstream cascade; the membrane between scales is lossy (Fig 4).
- **Domain 1, glacier mass balance.** Actors: accumulation, ablation, ice, snow. Currency: mass and energy. Anchor: measured mass balance, archived by a public service. Question type: a sequence to be read, not a trend to be assumed.
- **Domain 2, basin hydrology.** Actors: glacier, snow, rain, groundwater, reservoirs. Currency: discharge and timing. Anchor: gauged streamflow per basin. Question type: union with D1, because the glacier is part of the source.
- **Domain 3, water management and agriculture.** Actors: managers, irrigators, crops, communities. Currency: reliability and yield. Anchor: delivered water and harvested crop. Question type: a binding onto an imported atom, not a new primitive.
- **Domain 4, the monitoring channel.** Actors: gauges, remote sensing, records, models. Currency: contact with the ground. Anchor: the ground, which does not decay. Independence in this simulated run is zero, because every domain is read from the same evidence pool.
- **Excluded X1 "global water supply":** no anchor independent of per-basin sums. **Absorbed X2 "climate sole cause":** already the forcing inside D1. **Absorbed X3 "water wars":** a cascade to enumerate, not an anchor.

Order by dependency: measure the glacier state, read the hydrograph and its timing, state what counts as adequate, choose the response, carry what worked (Fig 3). The break is at vocabulary and objective, because the stationarity closure has no term for non-stationarity (Fig 3 :27; Milly et al. 2008).

My census matches the primary's four domains and its union reading. Where the primary derived from Fig 1 indexing and Fig 9 union rules, I derived from Fig 3 dependency order and Fig 12 bond types. ## Round trip gap R_T

The Symbols table defines R_T as the gap between what was sent up and what came back down, `gap(x, (T_E ∘ T_A)(x))`, measuring what the coarser description cannot hold. Fig 4 :30 requires it to be measured rather than assumed. I ran the round trip on the condensation the folder actually uses.

**R_T is nonzero.** Three things the shorthand holds do not survive the trip back down:

- the **basin index and the distance-from-glacier condition**, which the shorthand preserves as a rule but cannot populate without per-basin measurements;
- the **source-discrimination requirement**, namely that a low-flow reading cannot by itself name glacier retreat as the source, which the basin-level statement silently drops;
- the **date and magnitude of peak water**, which are properties of a glacier model and a climate scenario, not of the figure bonds.

Conversely, one thing survives cleanly: the residual must be indexed per basin, the response has an order, adequacy is a threshold, and the same glacier can change valence. That is the residue worth sending. R_T was **not** measured numerically, because no per-basin ground reading was taken here; it is recorded qualitatively as the enumerated set of dropped constraints. Treating "the formula is the condensed version of the metalaws" as identity would set R_T to zero and would be the dead-reckoning error Fig 4 :30 names. The honest form is a lossy condensation with a recorded gap, which is what `09_watson_answer.md` now states.
