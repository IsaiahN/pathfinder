## DERIVATION

### Q1 - What order does the figure license for the glacier response?

- Fig 12 :72, sequence: **"A then B; B needs A to have happened"**. The test is *"swap them. Does it become a different thing? Yes."* (Fig 12 :74).
- The physical account in the world literature: glacier stores water as ice; warming releases it; runoff rises; the shrinking mass eventually cannot sustain the release; runoff declines. UNESCO WWDR 2025: *"annual runoff initially increases within glacier-fed basins and rivers. After some years or decades, a certain point is reached ... after which runoff from glacial meltwater declines"*.
- ⇒ The licensed form is a **sequence** `accumulate → release increases → peak → decline`, not a monotone subtraction `supply − glacier`. Alpine and Andean observations provide the world witness; Fig 12 provides the bond type. Triangulated for order, `specified` and unmeasured for any basin's dates.
- **Falsifier, written before the run:** a documented glacier-fed basin whose annual runoff declines monotonically from the onset of warming with no intervening increase, or whose increase never peaks before the ice disappears. Either breaks the general sequence.

### Q2 - What kind of bond is "supply meets demand"?

- Operators table :48-51 and Fig 12 :87: **"a threshold relation between two quantities"**, with test *"is it about which is larger, rather than both being present? Yes."*
- Fig 12 Operators :52: **"a goal is a comparison, which is why a vocabulary without it cannot state one"**.
- ⇒ "Adequate water supply" is a `⋛` threshold between delivered water and demand, not an absolute stock. This is why a constant annual total can yield opposite agricultural outcomes: if the water arrives before the growing season, the comparison fails even though magnitude is unchanged. The WWDR reports exactly this: *"a shift in timing towards an earlier spring snow-melt peak has been observed. This poses challenges to farmers to accurately predict the timing of water intake for irrigation systems and to manage spring crop planting schedules."*
- **Falsifier:** a water system whose "adequacy" is demonstrably independent of demand and of timing, i.e. a fixed magnitude above which supply is always adequate. If such a system exists in the field, the comparison reading is wrong for it.

### Q3 - Are the three conditions isomers, a functional group, or a union?

- Fig 12 :117: **"isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."**
- Fig 12 :124: **"functional group. A substructure whose presence fixes the outcome. The group says what kind; the qualifier says which member."**
- Fig 9 :57: **"a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either."**
- The three conditions C1 magnitude/phase, C2 timing and C3 variability/hazard **share the same atoms** (glacier mass, meltwater, a basin, a demand) but differ in which bond governs and which instrument reads them. Under Fig 12's definition they are **isomers**: same atoms, different bond, different behaviour. Two basins with identical ice mass and identical annual runoff can have opposite agricultural outcomes because the timing bond and the threshold bond are arranged differently. This is the isomer claim worth testing.
- The weaker reading is that they are members of a **functional group** (`state → structure → threshold`) with different qualifiers. Both readings are recorded; the isomer reading is stronger and is the one with a falsifier.
- **Falsifier:** if C1, C2 and C3 cannot be measured independently in any basin, or if a change in one always forces a proportional change in the others so that no basin can fail one and pass another, the isomer claim fails and they collapse into one condition. This is also the refuter F5 in `01_figure_grounded_audit.md`.

### Q4 - What is the order of the union?

- Fig 12 :104: **"A settled molecule becomes an atom for whatever composes over it. So irreducible is a fact about a level and never about the world."**
- Fig 3 :20: **"The chain is ordered by dependency, not by importance."**
- Order by dependency, not by importance: (1) measure the glacier state, (2) read the basin hydrograph and its timing, (3) state what counts as adequate supply against demand, (4) choose the allocation and infrastructure response, (5) carry what worked. This matches the five links of Fig 3. The characteristic break is at links 2 and 3, the vocabulary and objective of non-stationarity, because that is where the stationarity closure has no terms.
- Each settled measurement becomes an atom for the next level: the mass-balance series becomes an atom for the runoff model; the runoff model becomes an atom for the reservoir rule; the rule becomes an atom for the crop decision. This is Fig 12's recursion and needs no new primitive.
- **Caveat, named:** the recursion is structural. It does not say that the resulting forecast is correct. Whether a settled mass-balance term can be composed over into a dependable-yield term is a fact about the coupling, and the coupling is measured, not argued (Fig 5 :32, *"then check it against the ground, then carry it up"*).

### Q5 - Does the cycle close, and with what sign?

- Fig 12 :121: **"cycle. The output re-enters as input. The sign of the loop decides whether it closes, and a chain cannot close by construction."**
- Two cycles are live. The **natural** cycle: meltwater recharges soil moisture and groundwater, which sustain baseflow, which affects the next season's water availability. The **managed** cycle: supply reliability informs infrastructure investment, which changes the hydrograph that is later measured. The sign of the managed loop decides whether it stabilises the system or locks it into a failing closure (Fig 7 :36).
- **Falsifier:** a managed basin whose investment loop re-enters as input with the opposite sign, i.e. investment that made the system less able to read the residual, yet supply improves. That would break the cycle-sign reading.

## CONCLUSION

The peak-water response is a **sequence** under Fig 12's `→`, and adequacy is a **threshold** under `⋛`. The three conditions of the union are best read as **isomers** over a shared atom set, with the functional-group reading retained as the weaker fallback. The union is ordered by dependency, not importance, and breaks where the planning vocabulary has no term for non-stationarity. The managed cycle can close with either sign. All of this is structural and `specified`: it names no basin, no peak year, no crop. `metric tensor = Gradient` analogue has no counterpart here; the bindings of figure bonds to hydrological quantities are imports with provenance owed, not derivations.
