# Expert verification packet - the Tether on glacier retreat and water supply

**What this is:** a claim in a form a glaciologist, hydrologist or water-resource researcher can check. Mode, derivation and refuters are stated before the conclusion, so a reader knows what to look for and what would settle it.

**Mode: `specified` - not `grounded`.** No basin-level forecast is claimed. This is a shape and a route, not a result. It is a simulated derivation checked for internal consistency against the figures, not a ground reading.

## 1. The claim, in one paragraph

Glacier retreat does not produce one water outcome but a **union of three conditions indexed per basin**. The magnitude/phase condition depends on where the basin sits in the sequence accumulation, increased melt, peak water, decline. The seasonal-timing condition governs whether a given annual total helps or hurts irrigation. The variability/hazard condition governs whether the glacier acts as a drought buffer or a flood and GLOF source. Adequacy is a threshold relation between delivered water and demand, not an absolute stock. Water systems lose contact with the ground where a stationarity-based planning closure has no atom for the glacier sequence and where the monitoring channel has degraded, not because the ground changed its mind. The framework returns this shape, the instruments that would close it per basin, and its refuters. It names no basin, no peak year and no crop.

## 2. Three checkable sub-claims

### C1 - indexing and union; checkable by reading Fig 1, Fig 9 and Fig 12

- **Statement:** the residual is per basin and per season, never a global aggregate; the three conditions are a union of overlapping isomers over a shared atom set, not one trend. Adequacy is a threshold against demand.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 1 :19 - *"The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated."* Fig 9 :57 - *"What remains is smaller and it is also categorically different: a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either."* Fig 12 :117 - *"isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."* Fig 12 :87 - *"a threshold relation between two quantities"*.
- **What to check:** do the three conditions admit independent measurement in a real basin, or does a change in one always force proportional changes in the others? Is "adequacy" in your practice a threshold against demand, or a fixed magnitude?
- **Refuter:** a global or continental statistic that predicts prospectively and per named basin which basin peaks first and by how much, with no basin-level inputs; or a demonstration that the three conditions cannot be measured independently in any basin, so that they collapse into one.

### C2 - the ordered sequence and the anchor; checkable against the glacier record

- **Statement:** the general runoff response is a sequence, accumulation → peak → decline, not a monotone subtraction. The anchor is measured mass balance and gauged streamflow, not model agreement.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 12 :72 - *"A then B; B needs A to have happened"*. Fig 2 :29 - *"An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks. A measurement, an experiment, a counter, a market, a patient outcome."* Fig 10 :24 - *"the ground does not decay, the channel does"*.
- **What to check:** is the sequence faithful to the glacier record you know? Does the public mass-balance and streamflow record for a basin you know actually read the peak-water phase, or is the phase only a model output? Where are the gauges thin or the record stale?
- **Refuter:** a documented glacier-fed basin whose annual runoff declines monotonically from the onset of warming with no intervening increase, or whose increase never peaks before disappearance; or a "measurement" that changes when a planner changes the model.

### C3 - closure and channel; checkable against planning practice

- **Statement:** a stationarity-based frame cannot derive glacier change from inside its own closure; it needs an import from glacier physics. Loss of contact is a channel failure (stale percepts, pooled readings, dropped gauges), not a decay of the ground.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 6 :23 - *"No frame escapes its own closure. The bound moves only on contact with a frame whose closure differs."* Fig 8 :32 - *"Closure is idempotent. Composing inside a closed set never leaves it, so no search, however long, adds an atom. This one is definitional: it is what closure means."* Fig 10 :26 - *"stale percepts, dropped transients, saturated metrics, pooled readings"*.
- **What to check:** can your planning model predict the timing and magnitude of peak water for a named basin from historical streamflow alone? If not, what atom did you have to import? Is the local monitoring record maintained and re-read, or is it inherited?
- **Refuter:** a planning model that predicts peak-water timing and magnitude from its own historical record alone, with no glacier-physics import, and passes the ground test prospectively; or a basin where the channel measures high fidelity and high ground pressure yet supply fails for reasons internal to the model's own terms.

## 3. What is explicitly not claimed

- Not a forecast for any named basin, region, year or crop.
- Not "less water everywhere". Roughly half of the 56 studied basins are still before peak water; for about two thirds of the world's large rivers the glacier is too small to affect continental runoff materially (Huss and Hock 2018; ETH News 2018).
- Not that peak water is a policy date. UN World Water Development Report 2025 Box 2.2 is titled *"Caution against applying the 'peak water' concept in water resources policy"* and says the idealized trend weakens with basin size and that downstream reliance is often mischaracterised. This is a governing constraint, not a footnote.
- Not that glacier retreat is the sole source of low summer flow. Rainfall, snowmelt, groundwater and diversion also contribute. An instrument that reads the flow cannot by itself name the source.
- Not that more storage is the general fix. One lever clears the region where only one condition holds and leaves the intersections standing.
- Not `grounded`. No per-basin measurement is claimed by this agent.

## 5. What would settle it

A maintained per-basin record, not a further argument.

1. **Sequence test:** does the basin's annual runoff show accumulation → peak → decline, and where is it now?
2. **Timing test:** has the seasonal centre of melt shifted earlier, and does the supply-demand threshold now fail in the growing season at constant annual total?
3. **Source-discrimination test:** after a low-flow reading, can glacier melt, snowmelt, rain, groundwater and diversion be separated? If not, the instrument is inadequate.
4. The falsifiers in C1 to C3 and the seven in `01_figure_grounded_audit.md` section 4.

## 6. What an expert can check in twenty minutes

1. Read Fig 1 :19, Fig 9 :57, Fig 12 :72, :87, :117, Fig 2 :29 and Fig 10 :24. Eight short passages.
2. Check C1 against your own basin: can the conditions be measured independently, and is adequacy a threshold?
3. Check C2 against the mass-balance and streamflow record you know: is the sequence faithful, and is the anchor maintained?
4. Name one instrument that would separate glacier melt from rain, snowmelt and groundwater in a low-flow reading, or say why none is available.
5. Send the refutation. A failure is more useful than an endorsement.
