# The Tether's answer on the observer's role in computation

**Mode: `specified`  -  not `grounded`.** This is a shape and a route, not a result. Simulated derivation checked against the figures (`05_what_shape_means.md` §6), not a ground reading.

## 1. The residual it answers

A physical system is described physically; a computational description assigns states, transitions, inputs and outputs. A mapping between them is proposed. The question asks what the observer contributes to that assignment. The residual is a **union compound** (Fig 9 :54-58): one condition is the mapping the observer declares, another is whether the physical system supports that mapping. They must be unbundled before the question is searched. Fig 9 :57-58: *"a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either. It stops being a mixture and becomes a specific compound."*

## 2. The shape

1. **The observer authors a convention, not a verdict.** The observer supplies the coarse-graining: which physical state types map to which computational states, which count as inputs and outputs. That mapping is a convention. Fig 10 :18: *"a declared meaning is not a grounded one"* and Fig 10 :49: *"Declaring a meaning does not ground it."*
2. **The observer makes the residual statable.** Fig 10 :16-17: *"the convention does not fix the bug"*, *"it makes the bug statable"*. Without a declared mapping there is no gap to measure. The convention has a legitimate product: Fig 2 :41: *"efficient for a convention: agreement about a shared meaning is the product, not a corruption of it."*
3. **The observer cannot be the anchor.** Fig 2 :29-30: *"An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks."* An observer's assignment moves when the observer changes. Fig 2 :30: *"If it moves when you push on it, it is not an anchor."* What settles whether the system supports the mapping is a measurement or controlled intervention.
4. **If the declaration decides the outcome, it is a failure.** Fig 10 :47: *"a convention that decides an outcome has stopped being one"*. That is the observer-relativity collapse: every sufficiently complex system computes everything, so nothing is explained. The world literature names this *"unlimited pancomputationalism"* and records its descriptive and explanatory failures.
5. **The observer selects the frame after describing the residual.** Fig 8 :53: *"Residual first, frame second."* Fig 9 :27-28: *"describe it before you go looking, or any frame you pick will seem to fit."* The observer's act is downstream of the gap, not a free choice made in advance.
6. **The mapping is a typed bond.** Fig 12 :94: *"The arrangement is the substance."* Two labellings of the same physical inventory are isomers in Fig 12's sense: same atoms, different bond, different computational substance. The observer authors the bond; the system supplies the atoms; the ground settles whether the bond holds.
7. **Which specific states count is a gap from the figures.** Fig 9 :32: *"you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself."* The figures give the office of the observer, not a state list for any particular system. That needs an instrument that discriminates among candidate mappings.

## 3. What it explicitly does not say

- Not that the observer decides which physical states count. That is the failure named by Fig 10 :47.
- Not that the observer is irrelevant. It authors the convention that makes the residual sayable (Fig 10 :16-17).
- Not that the mapping is read off the physics alone. The atoms are physical; the bond is declared (Fig 12 :5).
- Not that agreement among observers makes a mapping true. Fig 2 :40: *"disqualifying for a verdict: agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool."*
- Not a specific state list for any named system. That is a `gap`.
- Not grounded. No measurement that discriminates among candidate mappings was taken.

## 4. What would settle it  -  the ground

An instrument that discriminates among candidate mappings, not a further argument. From *The Hard Problem is About Source* (opened 2026-11-23): an instrument reads green and *"cannot comment on the source"*; the spectrometer resolved it. Here the corresponding questions are:

- **Contrast test:** under two competing labellings of the same system, does an intervention produce the different outputs the labellings predict? If both labellings retrodict every outcome, the mapping lacks empirical content (the SEP names *"Unwarranted retrodictive success"* as one failure of unlimited pancomputationalism).
- **Anchor test:** does the proposed assignment change when the observer is changed while the system is held fixed? If yes, it is a convention, not a ground.
- **Instrument test:** is there a measurement that identifies which grouping of microphysical states the system actually realizes, as a spectrometer identifies a source? If none exists, the state list stays `unreachable` at this budget, not disproved. Fig 8 :39: *"So a search that finds nothing is never a proof of absence."*
- Standing falsifiers from `01_figure_grounded_audit.md` §6: an observer assignment that is a legitimate anchor; a frame certifying its own assignment without a ground; the two conditions shown to be one question.

## 5. The honest stop

**Office reached, state list unreached.** The office of the observer is reachable from Fig 10 and Fig 2. The specific states that count, for any system, are not reachable from the figures. Fig 13: *"Unreachable is a verdict here, not a silence"*, and Fig 9 :32: *"you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself."* The answer stops at the limit of the available instruments and records the lineage so the stop is checkable. *"Indiscernibility is not identity."*

## 6. One-paragraph version for outreach

> The figures do not tell us which physical states are computational states in any particular system. They tell us the office of the observer. The observer authors the convention that groups physical states into computational states and makes the gap statable; it selects the descriptive frame after the residual is described, not before; and it ranges that frame against a measurement it cannot author. Its contribution is real but conventional. A convention that decides an outcome has stopped being a convention, so if the observer's say-so settles which states count, the explanation has collapsed into the claim that everything computes everything. The mapping itself is a typed bond over physical and computational states, so two labellings of the same system can be different computational substances, same atoms and different arrangement. What the framework returns is that shape, plus refuters written in advance and the instrument that would close the remaining gap. The state list stays open until a ground settles it.
