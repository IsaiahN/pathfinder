## Independent domain census

I redid the census from the residual line: "what role does the observer play in deciding which physical states count as computational states" - using the WATSON order. R by arity, symmetry and scale; one line per domain with actors, currency and anchor; partition or union; order by dependency.

- **R (arity / symmetry / scale).** The residual is the gap between a physical state description and a computational state description. Arity: a typed relation between two inventories, so not a one-place property of either. Symmetry: the assignment changes with the observer, which is the whole point of the question, so the observer's contribution is visible as a transformation rather than a constant. Scale: microphysical states to computational macro-states, a membrane crossing. Question type: a **union compound** (Fig 9 :54-58), because the observer's declaration and the system's physical support can vary independently.
- **Domain 1, physical ground.** Actors: physical states, microphysical states, transitions, energy. Currency: physical prediction. Anchor: measurement or controlled intervention (Fig 2 :29-30). This is a domain with a nameable anchor.
- **Domain 2, computational description.** Actors: computational states, transition function, labelling scheme. Currency: description length and the typed bond. Anchor: a correctness check, which is an instrument for this question rather than its anchor (Fig 2 :36-38). Nameable but bounded.
- **Domain 3, observer / seat.** Actors: describer, assigning frame, instrument builder. Currency: conventions, declared meanings, labels. Anchor: the ground it ranges against; it must not author the verdict (Fig 10 :43-44). Nameable.
- **Order by dependency.** Perceive states, build vocabulary, state objective, plan the mapping, verify and carry (Fig 3 :4-15). Describe the residual before choosing the frame (Fig 8 :53). Refuse before mint (Formula step 2).

My census matches the primary's three domains and its union reading.
