## What the wider literature actually does to the claim

1. **It corroborates the union reading.** Curtis-Trudel makes implementation a three-place relation (system, model, labelling scheme) and argues that non-arbitrary grounds for a labelling scheme come from engineering and scientific context, not from free interpretation. This is the world's version of F1 and F6: the observer supplies the scheme; the ground and the investigative context constrain which scheme is worth using.
2. **It corroborates the gap, not a state list.** The SEP entry records that there is no consensus on where to draw the boundary between computational and non-computational processes. That is exactly F9's `gap`. A figure-grounded solution must not pretend the world literature has produced the specific answer.
3. **It supplies a live conflict, recorded rather than hidden.** White disputes Searle's inference from intensionality to observer-relativity, and Chrisley distinguishes interpretation from interpretability. Both resist the strong Option A in §0 of `01_figure_grounded_audit.md`. They do not overrule the figure verdict; they are secondary witnesses that the observer's say-so is not sufficient.
4. **It does not settle the question.** Agreement across these sources is partly one evidence pool (the Putnam and Searle triviality literature). Fig 2 :40: *"disqualifying for a verdict: agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool."* The verdict in `09_watson_answer.md` comes from the figures.

## What is not sent

No attachment of the full framework, no bulk list, no personal emails, no request for trust or endorsement, and no claim that a famous reviewer has approved the argument. Prestige can fund a trial, it cannot settle one. From *The Recursive Self-Improvement Trap*: *"Prestige can fund an action. It can give an action a budget to be tried. But it cannot settle an action. Only the ground can settle it."*
