# The mapping as a bond, and the order of the question

**Mode: `specified`.** This is a simulated derivation checked against the figures, not a ground reading (see `05_what_shape_means.md` §6).

## RESIDUAL

The observer assigns computational states to physical states. Two questions are owed beyond the main audit: (1) is that assignment a new ingredient or a typed bond over ingredients already present, and (2) in what order must the question be worked, so that a claim is not made at a link whose input never arrives?

## DOMAIN CENSUS

Atoms held: physical states (equivalence classes of microphysical states), computational states, inputs, outputs. Bonds held: the seven, including identity `≡`, sequence `→`, comparison `⋛`, and the unary negation `¬`. The mapping itself is a typed relation between the two inventories; it is not a ninth physical atom. Fig 6 :32: **"MINT composes inside the closure and can never add an atom."**

## DERIVATION

### Q1  -  Is the mapping an ingredient or a bond?

- Fig 12 :5: **"A set of ingredients is not one. The bond is the rest of the information, and it is the half nothing was recording."**
- Fig 12 :94: **"The arrangement is the substance."**
- Fig 12 :46: *"A bond is the typed relation between two operands. There are seven, they are not interchangeable, and which one holds is not recoverable from the operands."*
- ⇒ The mapping between physical state types and computational states is a bond, not a new state. Two systems with the same physical state inventory can implement different computations under different typed relations, and nothing in the inventory tells you which relation holds.
- **Why this matters for the observer question:** if the mapping were an ingredient, the observer would be discovering a new physical thing. Because it is a bond, the observer is declaring a relation among things already there. That is what makes its contribution conventional in the specific sense of Fig 10 :16-17 (*"the convention does not fix the bug"*, *"it makes the bug statable"*) without making it a verdict.
- **Falsifier, written before the run:** if a candidate mapping adds a physical atom to the system (a new field, a new conserved quantity), then it is not a bond and this derivation fails. The observer would then be an import source, not a convention author.

### Q2  -  Are two different mappings isomers?

- Fig 12 :117: **"isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."**
- Take one physical system with a fixed inventory of state types. Under labelling scheme L1, states group one way and compute function f. Under L2, they group another way and compute function g. Same atoms, different bond, different computational substance. Fig 12 :117 governs this directly.
- Curtis-Trudel's circuit example is the world-facing instance: a bistable circuit *"computes logical AND; under the reverse assignment, it computes logical OR."* Same voltages, different assignment, different function. That is an isomer claim in the figure's sense, with the assignment playing the role of the bond.
- **Falsifier:** if two different labellings of the same system always yield the same input-output behaviour under every intervention, the isomer claim is empty for that system. The mapping would be a redescription, not a different substance. This is the case the SEP calls (*"no empirical discoveries"*) under unlimited pancomputationalism.

### Q3  -  What is the order of the question?

Fig 3 gives a dependency order, not an importance order. Fig 3 :19-20: **"Optimising a link that already works cannot rescue one that does not. The chain is ordered by dependency, not by importance."**

| link | Fig 3 lines | what is required here | failure if skipped |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 perception | :4-5 | distinguish the physical states and tell them apart over time | no residual can be read; the observer has nothing to map |
| 2 vocabulary | :6-7 | have terms for what the states afford (group, label, input, output) | states cannot be grouped into computational state types |
| 3 objective | :9-10 | say what would count as progress in the observer's own terms | the mapping is chosen by taste; no refuter |
| 4 planning | :12-13 | choose a route to the objective under incomplete information | the observer jumps to a state list |
| 5 learn and carry | :14-15 | name what worked, verify it, carry it to the next system | agreement compounds and evidence does not (Fig 7 :35) |

The observer's office sits at links 2 and 3: it supplies the vocabulary and states the objective. It does not sit at link 1 (the physical states) or at settlement (the ground). Fig 3 :25-27: *"The link between them, having terms for what a situation offers and being able to say what would count as progress, has neither [obvious failures and tests], so it attracts the least attention and the fewest instruments."* That is precisely why the observer question is hard: the middle link is where the convention is authored, and it has no instrument of its own until one is built.

**Falsifier:** if a worked case shows the observer deciding the outcome at link 1 (changing which physical states exist by changing the description), this order is wrong and the observer is a ground, not a seat. Fig 10 :49: *"Declaring a meaning does not ground it."*

### Q4  -  Is a refused mapping a new bond?

- Operators table :58: *"a refutation is a negation, which is why a vocabulary without it can only accumulate"*. Fig 12 amendment :140: *"A vocabulary that cannot refuse can only accumulate."* The mapping stays in the record and loses its reach.
- A wrong mapping therefore needs `¬` (refusal), not another mapping piled on top. In the formula's language (step 3), the refusal *"removes not a term but a term's standing to be composed over"*: the mapping stays in the record and loses its reach.
- **Falsifier:** if a discipline can correct a computational-state assignment without any operation that says "this assignment is false", the negation claim does not apply to that discipline and the analogue is strained.

## CONCLUSION

The mapping is a typed bond over physical and computational state inventories, so two labellings of the same system are isomers in Fig 12's sense: same atoms, different bond, different computational substance. The observer authors that bond at the vocabulary and objective links of Fig 3, where there is no instrument of its own, and it reports the result to a ground that must settle it separately. A failed mapping is refused with negation, not replaced by accumulation. The falsifiers are stated above and none has fired in this simulated run.
