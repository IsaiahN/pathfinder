# Expert verification packet  -  the Tether on the observer's role in computation

**What this is:** a claim in a form a philosopher of computation or cognitive scientist can check. Mode, derivation and refuters are stated before the conclusion, so a reader knows what to look for and what would settle it.

**Mode: `specified`, not `grounded`.** No measurement discriminating among candidate mappings is claimed. This is a shape and a route, not a result.

**Status of this packet:** a simulated derivation checked for internal consistency against the figures, not a ground reading. It cannot settle the question it reports on.

## 1. The claim, in one paragraph

The question "what role does the observer play in deciding which physical states count as computational states" is a union of two conditions: the mapping the observer declares and the physical support the system provides. Under the figures, the observer authors the convention that maps physical state types to computational states, makes the residual statable, and ranges the chosen frame against a ground it cannot author. It does not decide the outcome. If its declaration settles which states count, a convention has become a verdict, which is the named failure and is indistinguishable in the world literature from unlimited pancomputationalism. The mapping is a typed bond, so two labellings of the same physical inventory can be different computational substances. Which specific states count, for any given system, is a gap from the figures and needs a source-discriminating instrument.

## 2. Three checkable sub-claims

### C1  -  framework-internal; checkable by reading Figure 10 and Figure 2
- **Statement:** the observer's state assignment is a convention, not an anchor. Its legitimate work is to make the gap statable; deciding the outcome is the failure mode.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 10 :18  -  *"a declared meaning is not a grounded one"*; Fig 10 :49  -  *"Declaring a meaning does not ground it."*; Fig 10 :16-17  -  *"the convention does not fix the bug"* / *"it makes the bug statable"*; Fig 2 :29-30  -  *"An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks."* / *"If it moves when you push on it, it is not an anchor."*; Fig 10 :47  -  *"a convention that decides an outcome has stopped being one"*.
- **Refuter:** show an observer's assignment that does not move when the observer is changed and that settles the question without a measurement; or show that the framework permits a convention to decide an outcome without contradicting Fig 10 :47.

### C2  -  physics and computation facing; checkable against the implementation literature
- **Statement:** the mapping is a typed bond over physical and computational state inventories, so two labellings of the same system are computationally different in the figure's sense (Fig 12 :117: *"isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."*). The observer authors the bond; the ground settles whether it holds.
- **What to check:** is the three-place structure real in the literature? Curtis-Trudel: *"implementation is a three-place relation between a physical system, computational model, and a labelling scheme."* Searle: *"computation is not discovered in the physics, it is assigned to it."* Chrisley: *"That an object is interpreted by someone as being C is a deeply observer-relative fact; that an object is interpretable by someone as being C need not be observer-relative, if enough constraints are put on the conditions of interpretation."* Do these three, from different subfields, support a conventional bond with grounded constraints?
- **Refuter:** exhibit a case where the physical state inventory alone fixes a unique computational state assignment with no descriptive convention under any description, in a way that makes the bond redundant; or show that different labellings never yield different predictions under any intervention, so the isomer claim is empty.

### C3  -  framework-internal derivation
- **Statement:** the question is a union compound; the observer's convention condition and the system's physical support condition must be kept separate and each ranged against its own ground. The observer selects the frame after the residual is described.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 9 :54  -  *"And when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again."*; Fig 9 :57-58  -  *"a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either. It stops being a mixture and becomes a specific compound."*; Fig 8 :53  -  *"Residual first, frame second."*; Fig 9 :30-31  -  *"your words are borrowed too, so describe the effect rather than the cause."*
- **Refuter:** show the two conditions are one question and cannot be satisfied separately; or show a successful practice that chooses the computational frame first and still gets a ground-settled state assignment.

## 3. What is explicitly not claimed

- Not that the observer decides which physical states count. That is the Fig 10 :47 failure.
- Not that the observer is irrelevant; it authors the convention that makes the gap statable.
- Not a state list for any named system. That is a `gap`, and Fig 9 :32 says *"you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself."*
- Not that agreement among observers validates the mapping (Fig 2 :40).
- Not that the world sources agree with each other or with the figures from an independent evidence pool. The SEP entries, White, Leupold, Chrisley and Curtis-Trudel overlap heavily on Putnam and Searle. Their agreement is one pool talking.
- Not `grounded`. Nothing here is asserted as settled by measurement.

## 5. What would settle it

A source-discriminating instrument, not a further argument. From *The Hard Problem is About Source*: an instrument that reads green *"cannot comment on the source"*; the spectrometer resolved it.

1. **Contrast test:** under two competing labellings of one system, does an intervention produce the different outputs each labelling predicts? If all labellings retrodict every outcome, the mapping is empirically empty.
2. **Anchor test:** hold the system fixed and change the observer. Does the assignment change? If it does, the assignment is a convention, not a ground.
3. **Instrument test:** is there a measurement that identifies which grouping of microphysical states the system realises? If none exists, the state list is `unreachable` at this budget, not disproved (Fig 8 :39).

## 6. What an expert can check in twenty minutes

1. Read Fig 10 :16-18, :47, :49 and Fig 2 :29-30, :41. Five short passages.
2. Check C2 against their own knowledge of the implementation debate. Is the three-place structure (system, model, labelling scheme) a fair description of the current literature?
3. Name one intervention that would distinguish two competing labellings of a fixed system, or say why none is possible.
4. Send the refutation. A failure is more useful than an endorsement.
