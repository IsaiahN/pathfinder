## Distribution: offer, do not push

Figure grounded reasons:

- **Fig 10 :52**, *"Bring a characterised residual, never the source."* Send the shape and the refuter first. The recipient checks the output without holding the framework interior.
- **Fig 2 :35**, *"Alignment is triangulation, not negotiation. A mirror cannot correct you."* Do not ask whether they agree. Ask what they would count as a failure.
- **Fig 9 :27 to 28**, *"describe it before you go looking, or any frame you pick will seem to fit"* and *"you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself"*. Elicit their expected signature before naming a mechanism.
- **Fig 4 :22**, *"Only methods cross up. Only head starts come down."* The formula is a generator, so offering it later gives away a method, not an answer.
- **Fig 2 :40**, *"agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool."* A reply that arrives after the recipient has read the framework is a conversation, never corroboration.

**Recommended order in a reply:** (1) the shape claim and its four refuters, (2) ask what they would expect to see if it were true, (3) record their verdict, (4) only then offer the packet, with the note that the figures, not the summary, are the authority.

## The two stage offer

1. **Stage 1.** Send the residual, the shape claim and the pre registered refuter. No theory.
2. **Stage 2, opt in.** If they are curious, offer the verification packet and the figure links. The figures are the authority.
3. **Stage 3.** Offer the formula as a method to reuse, with the instruction that each user writes their own refuter and states their own mode.

The verdict from stage 1 is recorded before any theory is shown, and later agreement is never pooled as corroboration. This is the exemplar's two stage offer, adapted to this question.
