**Mode: `specified` on the shape claims, `general` on the structural facts, not `grounded`.** This document answers the two questions the primary audit left as supporting work: in what sense a strand is one object, and what follows from the fact that the 2',5' and 3',5' linkages are isomers rather than two spellings of one term. It is a simulated derivation checked for internal consistency against the figures, not a ground reading, so it cannot settle which prebiotic route ran (see `05_what_shape_means.md` section 7).

## RESIDUAL

The primary audit closes at a shape: activation, a persistent catalyst, an orienting functional group, and a union of closures, yielding a distribution of chains. Two supporting questions remain.

1. **Structure.** If a strand is not a new kind of object, what is the exact contribution of the bond, and why does listing nucleotides fail to predict the product?
2. **Order.** The phosphodiester link is directed, 5' to 3', and two linkages are possible. Are those two linkages one term under a symmetric bond, or two substances under an ordered one? What does the answer do to the claim that a long strand is a name for one product?

Both are questions about the composition, not about which mineral or activating group won on early Earth. Answering them does not license a chemical name.

## DERIVATION

### Q1. A strand is a chain, and a chain is not a new kind of object

Fig 12 :48: "A molecule is a bonded arrangement. A chain is the degenerate case: one bond type, no branching. Nothing about a chain is a different kind of object."
Fig 12 :5: "A set of ingredients is not one. The bond is the rest of the information, and it is the half nothing was recording."
Fig 12 :46: "A bond is the typed relation between two operands. There are seven, they are not interchangeable, and which one holds is not recoverable from the operands."

A strand is the degenerate molecule: one bond type, repeated, no branching. That makes the question "how could nucleotides link into long strands" a composition question, not a new-kind question. The residual splits cleanly into two sub questions: how the typed relation is formed at all, and how the same relation is reapplied to extend the chain. An inventory of mononucleotides answers neither, because the information that would answer them is exactly the information the inventory omits. Fig 8 :32: "Closure is idempotent. Composing inside a closed set never leaves it, so no search, however long, adds an atom." A nucleotide only frame cannot compose a bond it does not hold, however long it is searched.

**Status:** triangulated across Fig 12 (composition notation) and Fig 8 (closure), two figures with different subject matter and low overlap. **Mode: `general`** for the structural claim, `specified` for its application to this residual.

### Q2. The bond is ordered, so order is substance

Fig 12 :72: "A then B; B needs A to have happened."
Fig 12 :74: "swap them. Does it become a different thing? Yes."
Fig 12 :92: "Symmetry is what decides whether an arrangement is a new thing."

The phosphodiester link joins the 3' (or 2') hydroxyl of one nucleotide to the 5' phosphate of the next. Swapping the operands does not produce the same molecule under a different spelling. It produces a different thing. Under a symmetric bond, k operands would have k! spellings and one term; here the bond is ordered, so different orders are different terms and charging for the sequence is correct. The Operators table states the consequence for flat notations: "the only bond a flat chain can express, so every term today is a sequence whether or not it should be" (Operators table :34). That is a warning about the notation, not evidence that every chain is a sequence. The chemistry decides which bond holds, and the bond is not recoverable from the ingredients.

**Status:** `single-source` for the bond test in Fig 12, with the Operators table as a second, related statement rather than an independent witness. **Mode: `general`** for the symmetry rule.

### Q3. The two linkages are isomers, not variants of one substance

Fig 12 :117: "isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."
Fig 13 :124: "the same ingredients under a different bond are a different substance."
Fig 12 :97: "This is not an analogy. Chemistry hit it first: silver fulminate and silver cyanate have the same composition and different properties, which is what forced structure into the notation."

The 2',5' and 3',5' phosphodiester linkages have identical ingredient sets and different bonds. They are isomers in the figure's own sense, and the chemical precedent is the figure's own example, not an analogy imported for this question. The behavioural difference is measured, not assumed. Kaddour and Sahai 2014 report that "The products of non-enzymatic oligomerization of RNA contained a mixture of 2',5' and 3',5' linkages." The Szostak Lab page states that "The resulting complementary strand will therefore contain a mixture of 2′-5′- and 3′-5′-linkages" and that "RNA duplexes can buffer local structural changes caused by 2′-5′-linkages, resulting in a minimally altered global structure." A long strand is therefore not a name for one product. It is a distribution over linkages, and the distribution is part of what any discriminating instrument must resolve.

Consequence for the shape claim: a prebiotic route is not validated by producing "RNA-like polymer" as a single reading. Several routes produce the same reading, and metamerism means the reading cannot comment on the source. The linkage distribution is one of the few places where different sources leave different traces, which is why it is worth measuring.

**Status:** triangulated across Fig 12 and Fig 13 (definition plus chemical instance), corroborated by two independent chemistry pools. **Mode: `specified`** for the prediction of a mixture, `general` for the isomer definition.

### Q4. The functional group is the local determinant, and it needs an instrument

Fig 12 :123: "functional group. A substructure whose presence fixes the outcome. The group says what kind; the qualifier says which member."
Fig 3 :27: "A link with no instrument is not a link that works. It is a link nobody has looked at."

The reacting pair is a functional group in this sense. The presence of a free 2' or 3' hydroxyl and an activated 5' phosphate fixes whether condensation can occur; that is the group, and it says what kind of reaction is possible. The activating group (imidazole, 2-aminoimidazole, cyclic phosphate, 1-methyladenine) is the qualifier that says which member of the class actually reacts. Orientation is the middle link of Fig 3's chain: neither bare perception nor bare planning, with no obvious instrument in the abstract, and yet decisive for whether the chain forms at all.

The world literature is largely an effort to build that instrument. Zhang et al. 2017 describe a bound intermediate "with the primer 3ʹ-hydroxyl oriented to attack the 5ʹ-phosphate of the adjacent G residue", and report a 4.1 angstrom distance and a 126 degree attack angle for the GpppG analog. Ferris 2006 states that "It is proposed that the metal ions serve as catalysts, because they bind to the activated monomer and growing polymer so that the phosphorimidazolide group of the activated monomer is proximal to the 2′ or 3′-hydroxyl on the 3′-end of the oligomer." These are measurements of orientation, not arguments about it.

**Status:** `single-source` for the figure definition, corroborated by two independent instrument-level sources. **Mode: `specified`**.

### Q5. Persistence across uses, recursion, and why copying is not a property of the chain

Fig 12 :119: "catalyst. A part that survives its own use. Spent and regenerating parts behave differently across uses, and nothing records which a term is."
Fig 12 :121: "cycle. The output re-enters as input. The sign of the loop decides whether it closes, and a chain cannot close by construction."
Fig 12 :104: "A settled molecule becomes an atom for whatever composes over it. So irreducible is a fact about a level and never about the world"
Fig 12 :106: "Nothing composes over what was never admitted as a part."

Enzymes are one occupant of the catalyst role, and the figure defines that role by persistence across uses rather than by being a protein. Removing enzymes removes an occupant, not the role. Mineral surfaces, metal ions, lipid matrices and short peptides are candidate occupants drawn from the world frame, recorded as bindings with provenance rather than derived names. Kaddour and Sahai 2014: "At least three different catalysts have been found to enhance the non-enzymatic polymerization of RNA, namely montmorillonite surfaces, lipid surfaces and, more recently, a peptide." Ferris 2006: "The formation of biopolymers from monomers in aqueous solution required the presence of catalysts."

Once an oligomer survives hydrolysis long enough to be measured and reused, it has settled against the ground and can serve as an atom for the next round. The bond forming loop is the kernel across scale: Fig 4 :19 to 20, "The kernel is the part that does not change with size: the same loop and the same rule for naming what worked, whether one agent is running it or a thousand. Only the actors, the currency and the anchor change." No new primitive is needed for elongation, only repeated application of the same loop to a different operand. Copying is a cycle layered on top of a chain, not something a chain can do by construction.

**Status:** triangulated across Fig 12 and Fig 4. **Mode: `specified`** for the role claim, `general` for the recursion.

### Q6. Order of operations, and the two currencies that must not be added

Fig 9 :54: "And when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again."
Fig 12 :125: "A composition costs in two currencies, and they do not add."
Fig 12 :126: "Bits price the description. Actions price finding out whether it holds."
Fig 12 :128: "The two readings come apart in both directions. A long chain of cheap parts is expensive to describe and may cost one action to refute."

The dependency order is activate, orient and concentrate, form the bond, elongate, then copy. Each condition is a closure whose overlap with the others is the surplus none has alone; Fig 8 :45: "Two frames together reach what neither reaches alone." A lever that clears only activation leaves the orientation intersection standing, which is why the conditions are held as a union and never averaged into one score.

The figures also refuse a single accounting. A proposed route can be cheap to state and expensive to settle, or expensive to state and cheap to refute. The figure names this as a two-currency problem and explicitly says summing is the error, not the fix. The figures do not supply the price of a prebiotic step in either currency. That is a **gap** in what the framework returns, not a hidden result: it requires an instrument and a budget, not a further reading of Fig 12.

**Status:** triangulated across Fig 9, Fig 8 and Fig 12 for the union and order; `gap` for the prices themselves. **Mode: `specified`**.

## FALSIFIER, written before the conclusion

Restated from `01_figure_grounded_audit.md` section 5, where it was pre-registered. The supporting derivation is rebuilt or withdrawn if any of the following holds:

1. Long phosphodiester strands form reliably from unactivated nucleotides in water with no catalyst, surface or energy cycle under plausible conditions. That removes the import requirement and Q1 with it.
2. A route is demonstrated in which no part survives its own use, so the catalyst role in Q5 is empty.
3. A prebiotic route produces a single phosphodiester linkage type rather than a mixture. That breaks the isomer shape in Q3.
4. Random collision of activated groups gives the same yield and regiochemistry as surface or template assisted alignment. That breaks the orientation claim in Q4.
5. The six term substrate rule is shown to govern chemical monomers, so Fig 13 rather than Fig 12 governs Q2 and Q3. That would send this derivation back to census.

Until one of these is run, no claim here is labelled `grounded`.

## CONCLUSION

The supporting work does not add a mechanism. It sharpens what any mechanism must satisfy. A strand is the degenerate chain case of a molecule, so the bond is the omitted information rather than a detail. The bond is ordered, so the two linkages are substances, not spellings: the product of a prebiotic route is an isomer distribution. The reacting hydroxyl and activated phosphate form a functional group whose orientation is a middle link needing an instrument. Persistence across uses is a role, not a protein, and elongation is recursion over a settled oligomer rather than a new primitive. Activation, orientation, bonding and copying form a union of closures with a dependency order, and the two currencies of cost stay separate. The framework returns that structure and refuses to name the occupant.
