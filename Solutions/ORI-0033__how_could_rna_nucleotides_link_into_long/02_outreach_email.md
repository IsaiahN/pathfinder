# Outreach draft: check the output first, then decide about the theory

**Use:** one to one, or as a cover letter attached to a submission through the public venues in `03_public_contact_points.csv`. **Not** for bulk send.

**Hard precondition:** the shape claim in `08_expert_verification_packet.md` must survive the three checkable sub claims before this is sent. The offer collapses on contact if the output does not hold.

---

**Subject:** Check one shape claim about nonenzymatic RNA polymerization before we discuss theory

> Hi [name],
>
> I have one specific output I would like you to check against what you already know. I am not asking you to read the framework first, and I am not asking you to agree with it. If the output fails, there is nothing else to look at.
>
> **The output (`specified`, not `grounded`):** nucleotides in water do not contain their own bond. A strand is a chain, which the figures call the degenerate case of a molecule, one bond type with no branching. From that, any route to long strands without enzymes must supply four things, and the framework says what each must look like but not which chemical it is:
>
> 1. an activation step that pays for the bond, because condensation in water is uphill;
> 2. a catalyst that survives its own use, which is the figure's own definition of a catalyst, so removing enzymes removes one occupant of the role and not the role;
> 3. a functional group that orients the reacting groups, because the bond is a typed relation not recoverable from the ingredients;
> 4. a union of closures, because a nucleotide only frame cannot compose what it does not hold.
>
> The product should not be one substance. Nonenzymatic chemistry gives a mixture of 2',5' and 3',5' phosphodiester linkages. Same ingredients, different bond, different substance. That is a predicted shape, not a measurement, and I am not claiming it settles which route ran on early Earth.
>
> **What I am not claiming:** that montmorillonite, 2-aminoimidazole activation, wet dry cycling, or any specific laboratory system is *the* answer. The framework returns a kind of thing, never the answer itself. Those are bindings from the chemistry frame onto the shape, and until their provenance is shown they carry no evidentiary weight. I am also not claiming this is a final theory or that it is the only one.
>
> **What would refute the shape claim:** I wrote these down before sending this.
> 1. Long phosphodiester strands forming reliably from unactivated nucleotides in water with no catalyst, no surface and no energy cycle under plausible conditions. That would remove the import requirement.
> 2. A route in which no part survives its own use, that is, a purely one shot condensation with no catalytic role at all. That would break the catalyst shape.
> 3. A product with a single linkage type rather than an isomer mixture. That would break the isomer shape.
> 4. A re census showing the framework's six term substrate rule governs chemical monomers, not just the physical primitives. That would send my derivation back to the drawing board.
>
> If any one holds, I will retract or rebuild the claim. A one line "this fails because..." is more useful to me than agreement. If you would rather not engage, no reply is needed.
>
> **If it survives, and only then:** the sources are in the attached packet. The figures are the authority, not my summary of them. I am happy to hear that the shape is wrong, that is the useful outcome.
>
> **If you want to use the machinery:** run it on a problem you know well and can check against a ground. Write down what would refute your result *before* you run it, and state the mode of what you get. A reported failure helps me more than a reported success.
>
> Best regards,
> [name]
