# The Tether's answer: how could RNA nucleotides link into long strands without enzymes?

**Mode: `specified` on the shape claims, `general` on the structural facts, not `grounded`.** This is a shape and a route, not a result. No measurement in this simulated derivation settled which route ran or whether it ran on early Earth. It is checked for internal consistency against the figures (`05_what_shape_means.md` section 7), and it cannot settle the question it reports on.

## 1. The residual it answers

Modern cells solve the problem with polymerases and nucleotide triphosphates, whose activation pays for the bond. The question removes the enzyme and asks what else can close the same gap. Stated as an effect: what supplies the free energy to form a phosphodiester bond in water, the orientation of two reacting groups, a catalytic role that persists across uses, and net elongation against hydrolysis, when no protein enzyme is present?

The residual is a union, not a single hard problem. Fig 9 :55: "A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones." A lever that clears only activation leaves the orientation and persistence conditions standing. Per Fig 1 :19, "The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated", so the four conditions carry separate verdicts and are never averaged into one score.

## 2. The shape, stated before any mechanism

1. **A strand is a chain, and the bond is the omitted information.** Fig 12 :48: "A molecule is a bonded arrangement. A chain is the degenerate case: one bond type, no branching. Nothing about a chain is a different kind of object." Fig 12 :5: "A set of ingredients is not one. The bond is the rest of the information, and it is the half nothing was recording." **Mode: `general`.** The question is therefore how the typed relation appears and how it is reapplied, not which ingredients are present.

2. **Activation is owed before bonding.** Condensation in water is uphill, so an activated nucleotide or an equivalent energy source must pay for the bond. Fig 12 :46: "A bond is the typed relation between two operands. There are seven, they are not interchangeable, and which one holds is not recoverable from the operands." The activation step is the one that makes the relation expressible at all. **Mode: `specified`** for this residual, `general` for the bond rule.

3. **Removing enzymes removes an occupant of the catalyst role, not the role.** Fig 12 :119: "catalyst. A part that survives its own use. Spent and regenerating parts behave differently across uses, and nothing records which a term is." The figure defines the role by persistence across uses, not by being a protein. What the prebiotic habitat must supply is some part that survives its own use. Which part is a binding from the chemistry frame, provenanced as such. **Mode: `specified`**.

4. **An orienting functional group is the middle link that needs an instrument.** Fig 12 :123: "functional group. A substructure whose presence fixes the outcome. ## 4. What would settle it, the ground

The anchor is an experiment that measures chain length, linkage distribution, persistence of the catalyst across uses and copying fidelity under plausible conditions. Fig 2 :29: "An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks." Fig 2 :35: "Alignment is triangulation, not negotiation. A mirror cannot correct you." Consensus cannot substitute for the measurement.

The discriminating tests are the refuters already pre-registered in `01_figure_grounded_audit.md` section 5, repeated in `08_expert_verification_packet.md`:

1. Long strands from unactivated nucleotides in water with no catalyst, no surface and no energy cycle. That would close the residual inside nucleotide chemistry and remove the import requirement.
2. A route with no part surviving its own use. That would empty the catalyst role.
3. A single linkage type under plausible conditions. That would break the isomer shape.
4. Random collision matching surface assisted yield and regiochemistry. That would break the orientation claim.
5. A re-census showing Fig 13's six term substrate governs chemical monomers, so the wrong governing figure was used.

A measurement that fires any of these is the desired failure. Instruments already return something: oligomers of tens of units under montmorillonite catalysis (Ferris 2006), an identified imidazolium bridged intermediate with a measured binding geometry (Zhang et al. 2017), and a measured tolerance of 2',5' links in duplexes (Szostak Lab page). This is not a case where nothing has ever registered, so the verdict is open, not unreachable.
