## 0. The residual, stated in effect terms

Modern cells solve this with polymerases and nucleotide triphosphates. The question removes the enzyme and asks what else can close the same gap. Stated as an effect: what supplies (a) the free energy to form a phosphodiester bond in water, (b) the orientation of two reacting groups, (c) a catalytic role that persists across uses, and (d) net elongation against hydrolysis, when no protein enzyme is present?

**Arity.** Four coupled conditions, not one. By Fig 9 :54, *"And when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again."* And Fig 9 :55, *"A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones."* A lever that clears only the activation condition leaves the orientation intersection standing.

**Symmetry.** The phosphodiester bond is ordered, 5' to 3', and the product is not one substance. Two linkages are possible, 2',5' and 3',5'. Under a symmetric bond different spellings would collapse to one term; here they do not. Fig 12 :92: *"Symmetry is what decides whether an arrangement is a new thing."*

**Scale.** Monomer, activated monomer, oligomer, strand, template. Each settled product can become an operand at the next level. Fig 12 :104: *"A settled molecule becomes an atom for whatever composes over it. So irreducible is a fact about a level and never about the world."*

**What an answer would look like.** Not a chemical name. A shape: the set of constraints any mechanism must satisfy, written down and made refusable before a mechanism is picked. This binds the sought for shape as an ordinary term, which `THE_FORMULA.md` "Direction" requires, because a frame cannot check the direction of its own question.

## 3. Findings

### F1. The residual is the bond, not an ingredient (decisive for the shape)

Fig 12 :5: *"A set of ingredients is not one. The bond is the rest of the information, and it is the half nothing was recording."*
Fig 12 :46: *"A bond is the typed relation between two operands. There are seven, they are not interchangeable, and which one holds is not recoverable from the operands."*
Fig 12 :48: *"A molecule is a bonded arrangement. A chain is the degenerate case: one bond type, no branching. Nothing about a chain is a different kind of object."*

A strand is not a new kind of object. It is the degenerate case of a molecule: one bond type, no branching. The question "how could nucleotides link into long strands" therefore reduces to two sub questions: how is the bond formed, and how is the chain extended. Listing nucleotides does not answer either, because the bond is the information the list omits.

Triangulation: Fig 8 :32, *"Closure is idempotent. Composing inside a closed set never leaves it, so no search, however long, adds an atom."* A nucleotide only frame cannot compose a bond it does not hold, however long it is searched. Two figures with low overlap (composition notation versus closure), so **triangulated**.

**Refuter:** show a long phosphodiester strand forming reliably from unactivated nucleotides in water with no added catalyst, surface or energy cycle. If that holds, the bond is reachable inside D1 and this finding fails.

### F2. Removing enzymes removes an occupant of a role, not the role itself

Fig 12 :119: *"catalyst. A part that survives its own use. Spent and regenerating parts behave differently across uses, and nothing records which a term is."*

The question is phrased "without enzymes". Enzymes are one occupant of the catalyst role. The figure defines that role by persistence across uses, not by being a protein. So the honest restatement is: what in the prebiotic habitat survives its own use and can therefore fill the role? Mineral surfaces, metal ions, lipid matrices and short peptides are candidate occupants from the world frame, recorded as bindings, not derived names.

World corroboration, independent evidence pool: Kaddour and Sahai 2014 report that *"At least three different catalysts have been found to enhance the non-enzymatic polymerization of RNA, namely montmorillonite surfaces, lipid surfaces and, more recently, a peptide."* Ferris 2006 writes that *"The formation of biopolymers from monomers in aqueous solution required the presence of catalysts."*

**Status:** the role definition is **single source** in the figures (Fig 12 :119), corroborated by two chemistry sources that do not draw on the framework.

**Refuter:** demonstrate a route with no persistent part at all, a one shot condensation in which nothing survives its own use and no catalysis occurs. That breaks the catalyst shape.

### F3. The functional group is the middle link that needs an instrument

Fig 12 :123: *"functional group. A substructure whose presence fixes the outcome. The group says what kind; the qualifier says which member."*
Fig 3 :27: *"A link with no instrument is not a link that works. It is a link nobody has looked at."*

The 2' or 3' hydroxyl and the activated 5' phosphate are a functional group in this sense: their presence fixes whether condensation can occur, and the activating group (imidazole, 2 aminoimidazole, cyclic phosphate) is the qualifier that says which member of the class reacts. Orientation is the middle link of Fig 3: neither pure perception nor pure planning, it has no obvious instrument in the abstract and yet decides whether the chain works. The mineral surface literature is largely an effort to build that instrument.

**Status:** **single source** for the figure definition, with a specific world prediction that surfaces orient rather than merely concentrate. Corroborated by the Szostak lab page, which reports that *"RNA duplexes can buffer local structural changes caused by 2′-5′-linkages, resulting in a minimally altered global structure"*, a measured consequence of group placement.

**Refuter:** show that orientation does not matter, that random collision of activated groups gives the same yield and regiochemistry as surface bound alignment.

### F4. The mechanism is a union of closures, not one reaction

Fig 6 :14: *"the only operator that moves the wall"* (import).
Fig 6 :23: *"No frame escapes its own closure. The bound moves only on contact with a frame whose closure differs."*
Fig 8 :45: *"Two frames together reach what neither reaches alone."*
Fig 8 :46: *"Composition crosses the boundary between them, and that surplus is where imports live."*
Fig 9 :55: *"A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones."*

Activation, orientation, catalysis and templating are not four versions of one reaction. They are four closures whose intersection has a surplus none of them has alone. Nucleotide chemistry plus activation chemistry plus a surface frame plus an energy cycle produce oligomers; adding a template frame produces copying. No single frame inside D1 supplies the polymer, and more searching inside D1 does not help (F1). Import is the only operator that moves the wall.

**Status:** **triangulated** across Fig 6, Fig 8 and Fig 9, three figures with different subject matter (wall, union surplus, split).

**Refuter:** close the residual inside D1 alone, by showing that unassisted nucleotide chemistry contains all four conditions. Same refuter as F1 by construction, which is why the union finding is reported as one verdict, not four independent ones.

### F5. The product is an isomer distribution, not one substance

Fig 12 :117: *"isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."*
Fig 13 :123 to 124: *"Two chains built from different links may compose functionally identical terms, and the converse holds too: the same ingredients under a different bond are a different substance."*

The 2',5' and 3',5' phosphodiester linkages are isomers in exactly this sense: identical ingredient sets, different bond, different behaviour. So "a long strand" is not a name for one product. It is a distribution over linkages. The two essays reinforce this: *"If several paths create the same behavior, then behavior is a projection with lossy information"* (*The Hard Problem is About Source*), and *"indiscernibility is not identity"* (*How Intelligence Actually Works*; also the figures README).

World corroboration, independent pool: Kaddour and Sahai 2014: *"The products of non-enzymatic oligomerization of RNA contained a mixture of 2',5' and 3',5' linkages."* The Szostak Lab page: *"The resulting complementary strand will therefore contain a mixture of 2′-5′- and 3′-5′-linkages."* Ferris 2006 reports both bond types and selectivity depending on the base.

**Status:** **triangulated** across Fig 12 and Fig 13 (definition plus chemical instance) and corroborated by three independent chemistry sources.

**Refuter:** a prebiotic route yielding a single linkage type under plausible conditions. That breaks the isomer shape but not necessarily the rest of the derivation.

### F6. The habitat is enumerated, never composed, and valence is relative to the residual

Fig 11 :43: *"The habitat is enumerated, never composed."*
Fig 11 :44: *"List everything in contact with the residual, then what is in contact with those, and outward until the cascade stops mattering."*
Fig 11 :46: *"conditions: light, water, temperature, season, terrain."*
Fig 11 :49: *"An actor's valence is relative to the residual, not a property it carries."*
Fig 11 :39: *"capability is a property of agent-and-habitat, never of the agent alone."*

Enumerating the habitat of this residual gives: water, nucleotides, pH, Mg2+ and other cations, volcanic ash and clay minerals, metal sulfides, lipids, short peptides, UV, heat, wet dry cycles, eutectic ice, concentration by evaporation. The list is read off the chemistry literature, not invented. Water has dual valence: solvent required for chemistry and hydrolyser that caps chain length. Fig 11 :40: *"A system cannot be made more capable by improving the agent in isolation. An improvement that does not change contact changes nothing, however much it improves."* A better catalyst that never contacts the monomers changes nothing.

**Status:** **triangulated** across Fig 11 and Fig 8 (habitat enumeration versus union surplus), with the world literature supplying the actual list.

**Refuter:** identify a condition in contact with the residual that changes chain length when removed, which the enumeration omitted. The enumeration is corrigible, not closed.

### F7. Elongation is recursion, not a new primitive

Fig 12 :104: *"A settled molecule becomes an atom for whatever composes over it."*
Fig 4 :19 to 20: *"The kernel is the part that does not change with size: the same loop and the same rule for naming what worked, whether one agent is running it or a thousand. Only the actors, the currency and the anchor change."*
Fig 12 :106: *"Nothing composes over what was never admitted as a part."*

An oligomer that has risen above the hydrolysis threshold is settled against the ground and can serve as a primer or template for the next round. The bond forming loop is the kernel; only actors, currency and anchor change when we move from dimer to 50 mer. No new primitive is needed for elongation, only repeated application of the same loop with a different operand. Fig 12 :121: *"cycle. The output re-enters as input. The sign of the loop decides whether it closes, and a chain cannot close by construction."* A linear chain cannot close by construction; copying is a cycle built on top of it, not a property of the chain.

**Status:** **triangulated** across Fig 12 and Fig 4.

**Refuter:** show that reaching a copyable length requires a qualitatively new primitive rather than repeated application of the same bond forming loop. That would break the recursion claim.

### F8. The framework returns a shape, not the name of a mechanism

Fig 9 :32: *"you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself."*
Canonical figures README: the framework returns *"what a missing thing would have to look like (a shape, not a name)"* and stops where instruments cannot distinguish, recording the lineage. *"Indiscernibility is not identity."*

Naming montmorillonite, 2 aminoimidazole activation or wet dry cycling as *the* answer is a binding from the chemistry frame onto an already held shape. It is not a derivation of a new primitive and not an import that moves the wall, because those atoms are already in the world literature. The record of where the binding came from is the whole difference. Fig 8 :50 to 52: *"Convergent derivation and adopted import look identical in the contents. Only the record of where each came from separates them."*

**Status:** **triangulated** across Fig 9 and the canonical README, which are different documents with different purposes.

**Refuter:** produce a figure line from which a specific mineral or activating group follows as a derivation rather than a binding. None was found after the census below.

### F9. The ground is the experiment, not agreement

Fig 2 :29 to 30: *"An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks. A measurement, an experiment, a counter, a market, a patient outcome."*
Fig 2 :35: *"Alignment is triangulation, not negotiation. A mirror cannot correct you."*
Fig 2 :40: *"agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool."*
Fig 7 :35: *"A room's agreement is variation, not a verdict. Selection between rooms is where the ground votes."*

The anchor here is an experiment that measures chain length, linkage distribution and template copying. Agreement among reviewed papers is variation unless the underlying instruments are independent. In this simulated run the second seat reads the same evidence pool, so its agreement is one pool talking and carries no independent weight.

**Status:** **single source** in Fig 2 for the anchor rule, but it governs all four domains and is not averaged with them.

**Refuter:** show that a verdict on which prebiotic route ran can be settled by consensus of experts without a discriminating measurement. That would contradict the anchor rule.

### F10. The status is open, not unreachable

Fig 8 :39: *"So a search that finds nothing is never a proof of absence."*
Fig 8 :40 to 43: underivable in principle and unreachable at budget are different facts with different instructions. Fig 8 :42: *"already in the closure and was not found, so the remedy is more search, not another atom."*
Fig 6 :32: *"An instrument is not built from a description. It is improved from a worse instrument already returning something."*
Fig 6 :33: *"Nobody specified a telescope from an account of Mars. A wandering point in the sky was already a reading, and every sharper instrument was built on the residual the last one produced."*

Instruments already return something: oligomers up to 30 to 50 units under montmorillonite catalysis (Ferris 2006), primer extension products with defined kinetics and an identified imidazolium bridged intermediate (Zhang et al. 2017). This is not a case where nothing has ever registered. The honest verdict is that the ground has not settled which route ran and whether it ran on early Earth, and that more of the same search will not add the discriminating atom. OUTWARD import from independent fields is available; INWARD sharpening of the linkage and length instruments is available. They are opposed and must not be read against each other.

**Status:** **triangulated** across Fig 8 and Fig 6.

**Refuter:** a discriminating measurement that settles the route. That would move the verdict from candidate to grounded, which is the desired failure.

## 4. Strain test and re census

**Strain encountered and withdrawn.** An earlier reading applied Fig 13 :75, *"Two states never bond directly. It is not space-time but space, a structure, time; and two structures join only through a state."*, to nucleotides, arguing that a nucleotide "state" needs a "structure" between it and another nucleotide. This only fits with a strain. Nucleotides are not Fig 13 states, which are space, time and energy. The line governs the six physical primitives, not chemical monomers. Per the canonical README, *"a reading that makes one contradict another has misidentified its domain, not found a flaw."* The reading was sent back to census. Fig 12 is the governing composition figure for chemical bonds. The strain is recorded here, not silently erased.

**Second strain, narrowed rather than dropped.** Fig 9 :32 (*"you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself"*) was first read as a ban on naming any mechanism at all. The canonical README narrows it: the limit governs a *missing* primitive, one not held in Γ. Montmorillonite, metal ions and activating groups are already held in the chemistry frame, so naming one as a candidate occupant of the catalyst role is a binding, which is allowed if provenanced. Claiming the framework itself selects that occupant is still forbidden. Both forms are recorded.

**Third strain, corrected.** The phrase "open challenge" in the chemistry literature was first recorded as Tether `unreachable`. Fig 8 :40 to 43 forbids that slide. Instruments return oligomers, so this is neither underivable in principle nor unreachable at budget. It is a ground that has not yet discriminated among routes. Corrected to `open, candidate` everywhere.

**After re census:** no live strain. All three were either withdrawn or narrowed and recorded.

## 5. Pre registered refuter

Written before the conclusion, per the standing instruction that a falsifier written before the run is a design instrument.

The shape claim is refuted, rebuilt or withdrawn if any of the following holds:

1. **R1, import fails.** Long phosphodiester strands form reliably from unactivated nucleotides in water with no catalyst, no surface and no energy cycle under plausible prebiotic conditions.
2. **R2, catalyst role fails.** A route is demonstrated in which no part survives its own use, with no catalytic role at all.
3. **R3, isomer shape fails.** A prebiotic route produces a single phosphodiester linkage type rather than a 2',5' and 3',5' mixture.
4. **R4, re census fails.** The six term substrate rule is shown to govern chemical monomers, not only the physical primitives, so this derivation used the wrong governing figure.
5. **R5, orientation fails.** Random collision of activated groups gives the same yield and regiochemistry as surface or template assisted alignment.

If any fires, the finding it touches is retracted and the shape is rebuilt from the residual. Until one is run, no claim in this folder may be labelled `grounded`.

## 8. Verdict of this audit

The shape survives: a strand is the degenerate chain case of a molecule; the bond is the omitted information; any enzyme free route must supply activation, a persistent catalyst, an orienting functional group and a union of closures; the product is an isomer distribution; elongation is recursion over a settled oligomer; the framework returns that shape and does not name a mechanism. The specific chemical bindings remain unproven as answers: montmorillonite, imidazole activation, wet dry cycling and helper oligos are world frame bindings recorded as provenance. No figure line derives any of them. Status `specified`, ungrounded, candidate. Not `grounded`, because no measurement in this simulated derivation settled which route ran or whether it ran on early Earth.
