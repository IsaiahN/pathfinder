# Bond type, order, catalyst and isomer readings for INS-0083

The question lists institution, coalitions, trust and taxes as if a flat list were a mechanism. Two questions are owed: (1) which bond joins the three effects, and (2) does the dependency chain currently stop at a link with no instrument? A third, weaker question asks whether competing institutional designs are isomers under Fig 12.

## DERIVATION

### Q1 : Which bond joins institution, trust and taxes?

Fig 12 :5 "A set of ingredients is not one. The bond is the rest of the information, and it is the half nothing was recording." Fig 12 :46 "A bond is the typed relation between two operands." Fig 12 :111 "A vocabulary without bonds can name a state and cannot name a route to one."

Four readings of the same three ingredients:

| reading | bond | figure test | prediction here |
|---|---|---|---|
| institution, then trust, then tax compliance | → sequence (Fig 12 :70 "A then B; B needs A to have happened") | "swap them. Does it become a different thing? Yes" (Fig 12 :74) | changing the order of exposure should change the outcome |
| institution, trust and compliance all present | + conjunction (Fig 12 :55 "both hold at once") | "swap the operands. Does the meaning change? No" (Fig 12 :59) | order is irrelevant; only co-presence matters |
| institutional quality produces both trust and compliance | ⇒ production (Fig 12 :76 "A produces B; B did not exist before") | "is there a B before A fires? No" (Fig 12 :79) | removing the common cause should remove both effects |
| trust and fairness compare against a threshold before compliance | ⋛ comparison (Fig 12 :87 "a threshold relation between two quantities") | "is it about which is larger, rather than both being present? Yes" (Fig 12 :89) | a kink, not a slope |

A flat list cannot distinguish any of these. The bond must be stated and then checked against an instrument. World corroboration: the tax-evasion entry records both a "tax money is appropriately used" channel and the exchange relationship hypothesis, while Our World in Data notes the trust-growth link is bidirectional. The bidirectional finding in particular blocks a naive sequence.

- **Status:** `specified`, unmeasured, single-source for the bond definitions (Fig 12 and Operators table), corroborated on the existence of competing readings by two low-overlap world sources.
- **Falsifier, written before the run:** name one instrument that cannot discriminate sequence from common-cause production for these three quantities. If such an instrument is all that is available, the bond question is unreachable at this budget and must be reported as such, not settled by wording.

### Q2 : Where does the dependency chain stop?

Fig 3 :20 "The chain is ordered by dependency, not by importance." Fig 3 :26 "having terms for what a situation offers and being able to say what would count as progress, has neither, so it attracts the least attention and the fewest instruments." Fig 3 :27 "A link with no instrument is not a link that works. It is a link nobody has looked at."

Chain for this question:

```
1 perception    can we measure each slot separately?                yes, partially
2 vocabulary    do we have terms for what an institution affords?   weak: "regime" is an import
3 objective     can we say what counts as progress in each slot?    weak: no per-slot criterion
4 planning      which lever to move?                              not reached
5 learn/carry   replicate across habitats                          not reached
```

The break is between 2 and 3. There is no shared, per-slot criterion for "trust improved" that survives a change of instrument, and no criterion for "coalition changed" that is independent of the researcher's favoured typology. Effort spent on more sophisticated estimation (step 4 and above) arrives at a step that cannot use it: Fig 3 :19 "Optimising a link that already works cannot rescue one that does not."

- **Status:** `general` for the chain rule, `specified` for the location of this break.
- **Falsifier:** produce a per-slot objective that two researchers using different instruments would accept as the same criterion. If that exists, the break moves up and the planning step becomes assessable.

### Q3 : Does a welfare institution act as a catalyst?

Fig 12 :119 "catalyst. A part that survives its own use." Fig 12 :120 "Spent and regenerating parts behave differently across uses, and nothing records which a term is."

A universal benefit that creates a permanent beneficiary organisation is a candidate catalyst in the coalition slot: the organisation survives the original legislative moment and regenerates coalition effort in later rounds. A one-off transfer that produces no lasting organisation is a spent part. The distinction is structural and is invisible in a spending total, which records neither survival nor regeneration.

- **Status:** `specified`, unmeasured, single-source for the catalyst definition.
- **Falsifier:** show a coalition effect that leaves no organisation, rule or routine capable of acting again, yet persists at the same strength across later rounds. The catalyst reading would then be unnecessary.

### Q4 : Are institutional designs isomers?

Fig 12 :117 "isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour." Fig 12 :123 "functional group. A substructure whose presence fixes the outcome. The group says what kind; the qualifier says which member."

The strong claim: universal and targeted designs are isomers over a shared atom set {eligibility rule, contribution rule, benefit level, administrative contact, financing source}, differing in which bond joins eligibility to contribution. They would then have the same ingredients and different behaviour, with the difference invisible in a spending total.

This is a **strained reading** and is recorded as such. Fig 12's isomers are molecules of the six substrate terms; institutional designs are not composed from those six in any figure line, and calling them isomers imports the chemical analogue without a source gate (Fig 6 :23). The weaker, defensible claim is the functional-group one: universal and targeted designs are two members of a family (a rule that allocates a benefit) with different qualifiers. Fig 13 :34 "And within a kind the members are distinct too, which is what makes them separate frames."

- **Status:** `specified`, single-source for the figure definition; the isomer form is strained, the functional-group form is the one retained.
- **Falsifier:** exhibit two designs with identical atom sets and identical behaviour on all three slots under an instrument that discriminates the bond. If behaviour is identical, the isomer claim fails.

### Q5 : What order is the full answer?

Fig 12 :103 "Composition is recursive, and the level decides what is irreducible." Fig 12 :104 "A settled molecule becomes an atom for whatever composes over it. So irreducible is a fact about a level and never about the world."

Each settled slot result becomes an atom for the next level: a coalition finding settled against election records becomes an operand for a trust finding, and so on. This is recursion, not import, and needs no new primitive. But settlement is "necessary for a term to be composed over" and not sufficient (Fig 5, amendment :69-72), so a well-supported coalition finding may still fail to compose with a trust finding if the bond type at the next level is wrong.

- **Status:** `general`, single-source for the recursion rule (Fig 12), corroborated by `60_FIGURES_AS_METALAWS.md` as a metalaw.

## CONCLUSION

The bonds matter more than the ingredient list; the chain stops at the vocabulary/objective link; a welfare institution can be a catalyst in the coalition slot; and the isomer reading is available only in its weaker functional-group form after a re-census. This rescues what is defensible in a flat "institutions, coalitions, trust, taxes" claim without importing a seventh term or naming a winning design. The bond question and the catalyst question remain `specified` and unmeasured.
