# Expert verification packet : the Tether on welfare institutions

**What this is:** a claim in a form a researcher in welfare institutions can check. Mode, derivation and refuters are stated before the conclusion, so a reader knows what to look for and what would settle it.

**Mode: `general` on the shape, `specified` on the three-slot prediction, `gap` on the empirical verdict.** No measurement is claimed. This is a shape and a route, not a result. Simulated derivation checked for internal consistency against the figures, not a ground reading.

## 1. The claim, in one paragraph

The question "how do welfare institutions change political coalitions, social trust, and willingness to pay taxes?" is a union of three overlapping conditions, not one question with one answer. Each slot needs its own anchor: election records and coalition agreements for coalitions; a trust measure whose meaning (reported or behavioural) is stated explicitly; filed returns and audit settlements for tax behaviour. A single welfare-performance index that lets one slot cancel another is disallowed. The habitat is enumerated per slot, with valence relative to the slot rather than carried by a standing actor list. The bond between institution, trust and taxes must be typed, because sequence, conjunction and common-cause production have the same ingredients and different substances. The framework does not name a winning design and does not assert an empirical sign. It returns that shape, the refuters written in advance, and a `gap` verdict on the ground question.

## 2. Three checkable sub-claims

### C1 : framework-internal; checkable by reading Figures 1 and 9

- **Statement:** the three effects must be kept as separate slots, and the compound cases need their own loop.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 1 :19 "The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated." Fig 9 :54 "And when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again." Fig 9 :57 "a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either."
- **Refuter:** exhibit a single aggregate measure that leaves zero residual on all three slots at once with no trade-off hidden inside it, or show that the effects form a clean partition with no compound case.

### C2 : field-facing; checkable by a welfare researcher against their own instruments

- **Statement:** each slot requires an anchor that does not change its answer when a frame changes its interpretation, and the reported-versus-behavioural distinction must be stated for trust.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 2 :29 "An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks." Fig 2 :36 "An anchor is legitimate when the question is constitutively about it." Fig 2 :37-38 "instruments can be wrong on axes they do not measure."
- **What to check:** for each slot, can you name the counter that would settle a disagreement? If the trust anchor is a survey item, is the question constitutively about reported trust? If not, what behavioural instrument discriminates trust from trustworthiness?
- **Refuter:** name an anchor for one slot that moves when a researcher or a government adopts a different reading of it. If every candidate does, the slot is not yet a domain and must be reported as such.

### C3 : framework-internal with a field-facing consequence; checkable by reading Figure 12

- **Statement:** the relation among institution, trust and tax willingness is a typed bond, and a flat ingredient list cannot record it.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 12 :5 "A set of ingredients is not one. The bond is the rest of the information, and it is the half nothing was recording." Fig 12 :46 "A bond is the typed relation between two operands." Fig 12 :111 "A vocabulary without bonds can name a state and cannot name a route to one."
- **What to check:** can you design an observation that distinguishes "institution then trust then compliance" from "institutional quality produces both"? If not, the bond question is unreachable at this budget and should be reported as a gap, not settled by wording.
- **Refuter:** show that two different bonds yield identical predictions under every available instrument. The bond claim then carries no information and is withdrawn.

## 3. What is explicitly not claimed

- Not that universal, targeted, contributory or conditional provision is the answer.
- Not that the welfare state raises or lowers any of the three effects. No ground reading was taken.
- Not that the three effects always move together, or that they are independent. They are a union with intersections.
- Not that agreement among studies is corroboration. Most share a survey pool (Fig 2 :40).
- Not that a macro association is a mechanism (Fig 4 :30).
- Not `grounded`. Every empirical claim is `candidate`.

## 5. What would settle it

1. Exogenous variation in an institution (phased rollout, eligibility threshold, court ruling) measured against the coalition anchor, with a catalyst test: did a durable organisation or routine appear?
2. A restated trust question with a stated anchor: repeated survey item for reported trust, or an incentivised behavioural measure that discriminates trust from trustworthiness.
3. Tax compliance from filed returns and audit settlements, not self-report alone.
4. A bond-discriminating instrument for the sequence versus common-cause question.
5. A measured round-trip gap `R_T` for any macro-to-micro condensation.

No amount of additional agreement among studies using the same pool counts as any of these.

## 6. What an expert can check in twenty minutes

1. Read Fig 1 :19, Fig 9 :54 and Fig 2 :29, three short passages.
2. For each of the three slots, write down the counter you would accept as an anchor, or say that none exists.
3. Say whether "reported trust" or "behavioural trust" is the intended object, and name the instrument that would settle it.
4. Name one observation that would distinguish sequence from common-cause production, or say why none is available.
5. Send the refutation. A failure is more useful than an endorsement.
