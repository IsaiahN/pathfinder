# Outreach draft : check the output before you decide about the frame

**Use:** one-to-one, or as a cover note attached to a submission through a venue in `03_public_contact_points.csv`. **Not** for bulk send. No personal emails are used anywhere in this folder.

**Ordering rule:** the residual and the refuter come first. The verdict on stage 1 is recorded before any theory is shown. Links come only after. This follows Fig 10 :52 "Bring a characterised residual, never the source." and Fig 9 :27-28 "describe it before you go looking, or any frame you pick will seem to fit".

---

**Subject:** One checkable output on welfare institutions, and what would refute it

> Hello,
>
> I have one specific output I would like you to check against what you already know about welfare institutions. I am not asking you to read a framework first, and I am not asking you to agree with anything. If the output does not survive, there is nothing else to look at.
>
> **The output (`general` on the shape, `specified` on the prediction, `gap` on the empirical verdict):**
> A welfare institution is a bet about entitlement, financing and administrative contact. The question "how do welfare institutions change political coalitions, social trust, and willingness to pay taxes?" is not one question. It is a union of three overlapping conditions with a fourth case where two or three bind together. So:
>
> 1. Keep three separate slots. Political coalitions, social trust and tax willingness are indexed separately, never folded into a single welfare-performance score. A gain in one must be allowed to cancel with a loss in another and still be visible.
> 2. Give each slot its own anchor. Election records and coalition agreements for coalitions. Filed returns and audit settlements for tax behaviour. For social trust, say explicitly whether you mean reported trust (then the repeated survey item is the anchor) or behavioural trust (then the survey is only an instrument and a behavioural measure is owed).
> 3. Enumerate the habitat rather than carrying a standing actor list. Eligibility rules, conditionality, benefit level, financing, administrative burden, case-worker discretion, union and employer organisation, perceived fairness, informal economy size. The same actor can help one slot and hurt another. Effects can sit three steps out: rule, administrative encounter, perceived respect, compliance.
> 4. State the bond between institution, trust and taxes. "Institutions then trust then taxes" is a sequence. "Institution produces both trust and compliance" is a production claim. These have the same ingredients and different substances. A flat list cannot tell them apart.
>
> **What I am not claiming:** that any particular institutional design (universal, targeted, contributory, conditional) is the answer. The figures do not name designs. They constrain the shape an answer must have and the tests it must pass. I am also not claiming a measured effect size or direction, and I am not claiming that existing cross-national agreement is evidence. Most of it draws on the same small pool of surveys.
>
> **What would refute the output, written down before sending this:**
> 1. A single aggregate measure that leaves zero residual on all three slots at once, with no trade-off hidden inside it.
> 2. A demonstration that the three effects form a clean partition with no cross-slot interaction and no compound case.
> 3. Two supposedly independent corroborations that share a dataset or survey designer and are still counted as two witnesses.
> 4. A derivation of the sign of any institutional effect from the six-term substrate alone, with no empirical import. That would kill my `gap` verdict.
>
> If any of these holds, I will retract the corresponding claim. A one-line "this fails because..." is more useful to me than agreement. If you would rather not engage, no reply is needed.
>
> **If it survives, and only then:** here are the sources. The figures are the authority, not my summary of them.
> - Verbatim figure text with line numbers (local extract in this project): `FRAMEWORK/reference/03-FIGURES_TEXT.md`
> - Canonical usage rule (local): `FRAMEWORK/reference/FIGURES_README_canonical.md`
> - Our World in Data, "Trust": https://ourworldindata.org/trust
> - Wikipedia, "Welfare state": https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Welfare_state
>
> **If you want to reuse the machinery:** the formula is in the figures link. Run it on a problem you know well and can check against a ground. Write down what would refute your result *before* you run it, and state the mode of what you get. A reported failure is more useful to me than a reported success.

## Why this version is ordered this way

| the move | figure-grounded reason |
|---|---|
| Output before theory | Fig 10 :52 "Bring a characterised residual, never the source." |
| Do not ask for agreement | Fig 2 :35 "Alignment is triangulation, not negotiation. A mirror cannot correct you." |
| Pre-registered refuter | Seat Map :287, "a falsifier written before the run is a design instrument, not only an honesty device." |
| Mode on every claim | `THE_FORMULA.md`, "Mode. general · specified · grounded. State which." |
| Three slots, no aggregate | Fig 1 :19 "The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated." |
| Union, not a list | Fig 9 :54 "And when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again." |
| Anchor per slot | Fig 2 :29 "An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks." |
| Habitat enumerated, valence relative to residual | Fig 11 :43 "The habitat is enumerated, never composed." and Fig 11 :49 "An actor's valence is relative to the residual, not a property it carries." |
| Bond type stated | Fig 12 :5 "A set of ingredients is not one. The bond is the rest of the information, and it is the half nothing was recording." |
| Empirical answer withheld | Fig 13 :134 "Unreachable is a verdict here, not a silence" |
| No prestige ask | *The Recursive Self-Improvement Trap*: "Prestige can fund an action. It can give an action a budget to be tried. But it cannot settle an action. Only the ground can settle it." |
| Offer the formula as a method | Fig 4 :22 "Only methods cross up. Only head starts come down." |

## Hard precondition

Do not send stage 2 until the recipient has recorded a verdict on stage 1. Once a person has absorbed the framework before giving a verdict, their agreement stops being evidence (Fig 10 :42 "A verifier that can reconstruct the claim cannot verify it."). Later agreement is never pooled as corroboration (Fig 2 :40).
