## 0. The residual, stated in effect terms

A welfare institution W is a bet about entitlement, financing and administrative contact. Let Γ(b,a) be what a frame predicts will happen in three object slots when W is enacted, and o' what is actually observed. The residual is a vector, not a scalar:

```
R = ( R_s1 , R_s2 , R_s3 )
s1 = political coalitions       (who aligns with whom, whose voice is represented)
s2 = social trust               (generalised trust, trust in institutions)
s3 = willingness to pay taxes   (compliance, accepted burden, tax morale)
```

Fig 1 :19 "The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated." Fig 1 :22 "Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average." So the residual is not "does the welfare state work". It is: for each slot, what failed to resolve under this design, and what would count as a resolution.

**Arity:** three. **Symmetry:** the slots are not interchangeable; the same institution can move one and leave another standing. **Scale:** W is enacted at polity scale, experienced at case-worker and household scale, and registered at survey or administrative scale. The membrane governs what crosses (Fig 4). **Anchor question:** each slot needs its own anchor, and an anchor is "whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks" (Fig 2 :29). A survey instrument is not automatically an anchor; "instruments can be wrong on axes they do not measure" (Fig 2 :37-38).

**Question type:** union, not partition. Fig 9 :55 "A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones." The three effects overlap because the same actors (unions, employers, beneficiaries, taxpayers) appear in more than one slot, and the same administrative encounter can feed all three.

## 1. Domain census

R described by arity, symmetry and scale is given in section 0. One line per domain follows, giving actors, currency and anchor. A domain without a nameable anchor is not a domain yet.

| domain | actors | currency | anchor | type |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| D1 institutional design | legislators, administrators, eligibility workers, financing rules | rules, entitlements, administrative contacts, budgets | enacted statute and administrative case records (an auditable counter) | intervention, not an effect slot |
| D2 coalition formation | parties, unions, employer bodies, beneficiary groups, voters | votes, seats, coalition agreements, membership | election results and signed coalition agreements | one slot of the union |
| D3 reported trust | respondents, survey instruments (WVS, ISSP, national surveys) | survey responses to the generalised trust item | the recorded response distribution, an instrument rather than the ground (strain named in F3) | one slot of the union |
| D4 tax behaviour | taxpayers, firms, revenue authority | filed returns, assessments, audit outcomes, tax morale items | tax records and audit settlements (money actually paid) | one slot of the union |
| D5 derivation frame | the six terms, seven bonds, negation | composition inside closure(Γ) | the figure text as metalaw; no empirical ground | governs shape only |

D5 has an anchor in the metalaw sense (the figures decide design questions, per `60_FIGURES_AS_METALAWS.md`) but not an empirical ground. Its verdicts are about reachable shape, never about the sign of an effect.

**Partition or union.** The three effect slots are a union with nonempty intersections. Fig 9 :54 "And when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again." Fig 9 :56 "A single lever clears the region where only one condition holds and leaves the intersections standing, because the second condition still binds." So we run one loop per slot and a separate loop for the compound cases where two or three conditions bind together.

**Order by dependency (Fig 3 :20 "The chain is ordered by dependency, not by importance."):** perception (a measure exists for each slot) -> vocabulary (terms for coalition change, trust change, tax morale) -> objective (what counts as progress in each slot) -> planning (which institutional lever to move) -> learn and carry (replication across habitats). The likely break is between perception and vocabulary, because "having terms for what a situation offers and being able to say what would count as progress, has neither, so it attracts the least attention and the fewest instruments" (Fig 3 :26). Fig 3 :27 "A link with no instrument is not a link that works. It is a link nobody has looked at." Effort spent on more regression specifications sits above the break and cannot rescue a missing objective.

**Strain test applied before concluding.** An early draft of this audit mapped universal benefits onto the network bond and targeted benefits onto subtraction. That mapping only fits if "network" and "subtraction" are made to mean something the figures do not say. It is a strain, so by the rule "a reading that makes one contradict another has misidentified its domain" (`FIGURES_README_canonical.md`) it governs a different quantity, not welfare design. It is retracted in section 7 and not used as a finding.

## 3. Findings

### F1 : The three effects must not be averaged into one index

Fig 1 :19 "The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated." Fig 1 :23 "The ground enters as the only metric." Fig 1 :27 "They are frame-internal. A frame cannot score itself with a quantity it also produces."

A single "welfare state performance" score (spending plus trust plus compliance folded into one number) lets a gain in trust cancel a loss in coalition representation while the aggregate still looks stable. That is exactly the aggregation failure the figure names. The world literature supplies a corroborating warning: the decommodification index built by Esping-Andersen aggregates qualitative and quantitative variables, and "Menahem has concerns regarding the validity of the index, and its potential for replication" (Wikipedia, *Gøsta Esping-Andersen*, 2026).

- **Mode:** `general` (structural), triangulated (Fig 1 plus an independent social-science source with no figure-pool overlap).
- **Refuter:** exhibit a measure whose per-slot residuals for coalition, trust and tax are all zero simultaneously, with no trade-off hidden between them, or show that a rise in one slot cannot mask a fall in another under the same measure.

### F2 : This is a union of overlapping conditions, not three independent questions

Fig 9 :54 "And when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again." Fig 9 :57 "a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either." Fig 9 :56 "A single lever clears the region where only one condition holds and leaves the intersections standing, because the second condition still binds."

Run three loops, then a fourth on the compound cases (for example, a targeted benefit that simultaneously changes coalition alignment, perceived fairness and reported income). The compound is categorically different, not just more difficult.

- **Mode:** `general`, triangulated (Fig 9 plus the documented trust-to-tax channel in the world sources; see F3).
- **Refuter:** demonstrate zero cross-slot residual covariance across designs and show a lever that clears all intersections at once; that would collapse the union back into a partition.

### F3 : Each slot needs an anchor that does not update on what frames think

Fig 2 :29 "An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks." Fig 2 :36 "An anchor is legitimate when the question is constitutively about it." Fig 2 :37-38 "It is only an instrument for is this code correct, and instruments can be wrong on axes they do not measure."

Coalitions: election records and signed agreements. Tax: filed returns and audit settlements. Trust: here is the strain. "Social trust" is partly a reported attitude and partly a behaviour. If the question is constitutively about reported trust, the repeated survey item is the legitimate anchor. If it is about behavioural trust, the survey is only an instrument, and an incentivised behavioural measure is owed. Do not let the name decide which.

- **Mode:** `general` plus `specified` for the trust-slot distinction, triangulated (Fig 2 plus the measurement discussion at Our World in Data, which notes that attitudinal trust items are weak predictors of incentivised trusting behaviour).
- **Refuter:** name an anchor for one slot that changes its answer when a researcher or a government adopts a different interpretation of it.

### F4 : Agreement across studies may be one evidence pool

Fig 2 :40 "agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool." Fig 2 :42 "Buy independence in proportion to the cost of being wrong." Fig 7 :12-13 "same closure, handed along; agreement compounds, evidence does not."

Most welfare/trust/tax work draws on the same small set of cross-national surveys and the same family of specifications. Ten papers agreeing is one room, not ten witnesses. The WVS site itself lists a webinar, "Economic Inequality and Unfairness Evaluations of Income Distribution Negatively Predict Political and Social Trust: Evidence From Latin America Over 23 Years", which shares the WVS pool with the trust measurement used elsewhere here.

- **Mode:** `general`, triangulated (Fig 2 and Fig 7 are low-overlap figure lines, both primary).
- **Refuter:** produce two independent instruments, for example audit-file compliance data and an incentivised trust game, that converge on the same slot without sharing a dataset or a survey designer.

### F5 : Enumerate the habitat; do not keep a standing list of good actors

Fig 11 :43 "The habitat is enumerated, never composed." Fig 11 :49 "An actor's valence is relative to the residual, not a property it carries." Fig 11 :52-54 "Scoping the habitat to direct contact is how DDT reached the eagles: first-order enumeration, second-order effect, and the residual that mattered was three steps out."

Enumerate for each slot: eligibility rules, conditionality, benefit level, financing source, administrative burden, case-worker discretion, trade-union density, employer organisation, pre-existing trust, perceived fairness of taxation, informal economy size, and the media system. The same actor reverses valence across slots: corporatist insurance was "initially designed to create divisions among the working class and thereby undermine the power of organised labour, as well as to tie the individual closer to the state" (Wikipedia, *Welfare state*, 2026), which is a coalition effect, while its universality may raise perceived fairness in the tax slot. Effects three steps out (institution -> administrative encounter -> perceived respect -> compliance) are the ones a first-order model misses.

- **Mode:** `general`, triangulated (Fig 11 plus the independent historical record on coalition design).
- **Refuter:** exhibit a fixed list of actors with the same sign for all three residuals across a change of residual, or show a first-order-only model that captures a documented three-step effect.

### F6 : Macro associations are recordings, not mechanisms

Fig 4 :19 "Only the actors, the currency and the anchor change." Fig 4 :22 "Only methods cross up. Only head starts come down." Fig 4 :30 "So take the round trip and measure the gap. Without it you are navigating by dead reckoning: confident about a position nobody checked."

A cross-national correlation between social spending and reported trust is a description at one scale. Replaying it as an individual mechanism is a recording carried downward, which the figure forbids. What may cross up is a method (for example, a regression-discontinuity design around an eligibility threshold) that can be reapplied in another habitat; what comes down is a head start, never the previous result.

- **Mode:** `general`, single-source for the figure rule (Fig 4), with the lineage requirement corroborated by `60_FIGURES_AS_METALAWS.md`.
- **Refuter:** show a macro association that predicts individual-level change in a new habitat with a measured round-trip gap `R_T = 0`.

### F7 : The bond between institution, trust and taxes is information the flat formula loses

Fig 12 :5 "A set of ingredients is not one. The bond is the rest of the information, and it is the half nothing was recording." Fig 12 :46 "A bond is the typed relation between two operands." Fig 12 :111 "A vocabulary without bonds can name a state and cannot name a route to one."

Writing "institutions -> trust -> taxes" uses the sequence bond (→: A then B). Writing "institutions + trust + taxes" uses conjunction (+: both hold). Writing "institutional quality produces both trust and compliance" uses production (⇒). These are different substances with the same ingredients, and a flat list cannot tell them apart. The world literature explicitly contains both the "tax money is appropriately used" channel and the alternative that both are downstream of institutional quality; choosing between them is a bond question, not a coefficient question.

- **Mode:** `specified` (candidate structural claim), single-source for the bond definitions (Fig 12 and the Operators table), unmeasured.
- **Refuter:** name an instrument that cannot distinguish sequence from common-cause production for these three quantities.

### F8 : Two currencies: bits and actions

Fig 12 :125 "A composition costs in two currencies, and they do not add." Fig 12 :126 "Bits price the description. Actions price finding out whether it holds."

A cross-country typology is cheap to state (few bits) and expensive to settle (many actions in the field). A local randomised rollout is expensive to state and may be cheap to settle. Summing the two into one "evidence quality" score is the named error.

- **Mode:** `general`, single-source (Fig 12).
- **Refuter:** exhibit a design cheap in both currencies that settles all three slots in more than one habitat.

### F9 : The empirical sign and size are unreachable from these figures

Fig 8 :32 "no search, however long, adds an atom." Fig 8 :39 "So a search that finds nothing is never a proof of absence." Fig 13 :134 "Unreachable is a verdict here, not a silence" and Fig 13 :136 "It can say the question is not one question and must be split before it is searched again." Fig 9 :44 "Only a sealed room can be searched to the end, and no frame is a sealed room."

The six-term substrate does not contain "universal versus targeted benefit", "decommodification", "tax morale" or "generalised trust" as primitives. Nor can it derive which design raises which slot. Those atoms live in empirical frames and must be imported with a source gate and a shadow test. Until that import is settled against a ground, the empirical verdict is `gap`: unreachable from the figures at this budget, and underivable from a domain-agnostic metalaw in principle because it is a contact question, not a grammar question. Fig 11 :40 "An improvement that does not change contact changes nothing, however much it improves."

- **Mode:** `general` on reach, `specified` on this question, triangulated (Fig 6, Fig 8 and Fig 13 are distinct lines with the same instruction).
- **Refuter:** derive the sign of the effect of universalism on reported trust from the six-term set alone, with no empirical import. If that succeeds, the gap verdict is false.

### F10 : Mode discipline

A statement such as "welfare states erode trust" is a verdict form with an unspecified mode. From `THE_FORMULA.md`: "Mode. general · specified · grounded. State which. Accepted means the ground settled it; otherwise candidate." The honest form is: "Under a union reading with per-slot anchors, this design is predicted to move this slot in this direction, `specified`, awaiting a ground reading." Operators table :58 "a refutation is a negation, which is why a vocabulary without it can only accumulate" also requires that a failed slot be refused, not averaged away.

- **Mode:** `general` (a rule about claims), single-source for the mode rule but corroborated by the metalaw note.
- **Refuter:** a grounded measurement naming its anchor for each slot.

## 4. Step 7 kept distinct: SEARCHED, INWARD, OUTWARD

`THE_FORMULA.md` step 7 asks three questions, not one.

- **SEARCHED (asked first).** Is the empirical answer already inside the empirical frame and merely unfound? Likely yes in part: there are many studies of welfare regimes, trust and compliance. More search of the same survey pool adds "same atoms, same wall, more of the space actually visited" (Fig 6 :49). This is a budget filter, not a witness: "Use filters for the budget and witnesses for the verdict. Never let a filter hand you a verdict" (Fig 9 :60).
- **INWARD.** Sharpen our own instrument: define each slot, name the bond type, measure `R_T` for the macro-to-micro condensation. This extends representation from an instrument already returning something (Fig 6 :32 "An instrument is not built from a description. It is improved from a worse instrument already returning something.").
- **OUTWARD.** Import empirical atoms from a frame whose closure differs: administrative register data, audit records, incentivised behavioural measures, natural experiments around eligibility thresholds. Fig 6 :23 "No frame escapes its own closure. The bound moves only on contact with a frame whose closure differs." Each import needs provenance and a shadow test, and "Import from a live peer is debited against that frame's independence" (Fig 6 :24).

INWARD and OUTWARD are opposed and never read against one another. Underivable-in-principle (the metalaw cannot compose an empirical sign) and unreachable-at-this-budget (specific studies not yet found) are different facts with different instructions. Importing against a budget limit would add an atom that was not needed.

## 5. Pre-registered refuter (written before the conclusion)

The shape claim is that this is a three-slot union requiring per-slot anchors, an enumerated habitat, an explicit bond type, and a `gap` verdict on the empirical sign. It is refuted if any of the following fires:

1. A single aggregate measure is demonstrated to leave zero residual on all three slots simultaneously, with no off-setting trade-off (breaks F1).
2. The three effects are shown to be a clean partition with zero cross-slot interaction and no compound case (breaks F2).
3. Two supposedly independent corroborations are shown to share an instrument, dataset or survey designer and are still counted as two witnesses (breaks F4).
4. The sign and magnitude of an institutional effect are derived from the six-term set alone, with no empirical import (breaks F9, the gap verdict).
5. A measure of `R_T` comes back at zero for a macro-to-micro condensation (breaks F6).

Any one of these is recorded in `11_audit.md` with provenance and re-evaluated by the second seat. A failure is more useful than an endorsement.

## 6. Recommendation

1. Split the question into four loops: coalition, trust, tax, and the compound intersections. Do not report a single "welfare effect" (F1, F2).
2. Name an anchor per slot before reviewing literature. Treat the trust survey as an instrument unless the question is restated as being about reported trust (F3).
3. Enumerate the habitat per slot, including second- and third-order contacts. Do not carry a standing "pro-welfare actor" list across slots (F5).
4. Write the bond explicitly: sequence, conjunction, production or comparison. State which instrument could distinguish them (F7).
5. Keep bits and actions in separate columns when comparing a cross-national index with a local experiment (F8).
6. Report the empirical verdict as `gap`. What would close it is exogenous institutional variation measured against each anchor, not more agreement (F9).
7. State mode on every substantive sentence (F10).
