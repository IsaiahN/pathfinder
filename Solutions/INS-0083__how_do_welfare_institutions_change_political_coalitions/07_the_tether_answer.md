# The Tether's answer on welfare institutions and their three effects

**Mode: `general` on the shape, `specified` on the three-slot prediction, `gap` on the empirical verdict. Nothing here is `grounded`.** Simulated derivation checked against the figures (`05_what_shape_means.md` §7), not a ground reading.

## 1. The residual it answers

The question names one intervention and three outcomes. That is a union compound, not one hard problem: Fig 9 :57 "a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either." The residual is a vector R = (coalition, trust, tax willingness), indexed by slot, never averaged (Fig 1 :19 "The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated."). What is owed is a per-slot route from design to observable effect, plus a separate loop for cases where two or three slots bind together.

## 2. The shape, per domain

Verdicts are kept separate and never averaged.

**Domain 1, institutional design.** The institution is the intervention, not an effect slot. Its anchor is the enacted rule and administrative record. Fig 4 :19 "Only the actors, the currency and the anchor change." No figure line names a design as the winner. Verdict: `gap` on which design; `general` on the requirement that the design be stated as an auditable rule.

**Domain 2, coalition formation.** Anchor: election results and signed coalition agreements. Valence is relative to this residual: an actor that helps coalition formation can hurt another slot (Fig 11 :49 "An actor's valence is relative to the residual, not a property it carries."). A welfare institution may be a catalyst here, a part that survives its own use (Fig 12 :119). Verdict: `specified` that the coalition slot needs its own loop and its own catalyst test; `gap` on sign and size.

**Domain 3, social trust.** Anchor conditional on the question's meaning. If the question is about reported trust, the repeated survey item is legitimate, Fig 2 :36 "An anchor is legitimate when the question is constitutively about it." If it is about behavioural trust, the survey is only an instrument and "instruments can be wrong on axes they do not measure" (Fig 2 :37-38). Verdict: `specified`, with the anchor condition named as a live strain; `gap` on the effect.

**Domain 4, tax behaviour.** Anchor: filed returns and audit settlements. This is the strongest of the three anchors because it is a money counter that does not update on what a frame thinks. Verdict: `specified` on per-slot treatment; `gap` on magnitude.

**Domain 5, derivation frame.** The figures decide the shape and the loop, not the empirical sign. Fig 8 :32 "no search, however long, adds an atom." Fig 13 :134 "Unreachable is a verdict here, not a silence". Verdict: `general` on reach; the empirical verdict is `gap`.

## 3. What it explicitly does not say

- Not that any welfare design (universal, targeted, contributory, conditional) is the answer. The framework returns a shape, not a name.
- Not that welfare raises or erodes trust, coalitions or compliance. No measurement was taken.
- Not that the three effects move together. They are a union with intersections, and a gain in one can mask a loss in another.
- Not that agreement among studies is evidence. Most share a survey pool. Fig 2 :40 "agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool."
- Not that a macro correlation is a mechanism. Fig 4 :30 "So take the round trip and measure the gap. Without it you are navigating by dead reckoning: confident about a position nobody checked."
- Not `grounded`. The status of every empirical claim is `candidate`.

## 4. What would settle it : the ground

An anchor per slot plus exogenous variation in the institution:

1. **Coalition slot:** a phased or threshold-based change in eligibility, with coalition agreements and party positions recorded before and after. Does a persistent organisation appear that did not exist before (the catalyst test)?
2. **Trust slot:** restate the question first. For reported trust, a repeated item with a stable instrument. For behavioural trust, an incentivised measure that discriminates trust from trustworthiness, of the kind developed in the experimental literature cited at Our World in Data.
3. **Tax slot:** filed returns and audit settlements, not self-reported willingness alone. The tax-evasion entry records that voluntarily reported compliance in the US is about 85 percent of tax due, so the counter exists.
4. **Bond test:** an instrument that separates sequence from common-cause production (institution -> trust -> tax versus institutional quality -> both). This is the test named in `06_isomers_and_order.md` Q1.
5. **Round trip:** measure `R_T` for any macro-to-micro condensation instead of assuming zero.

## 5. The honest stop

**Unreachable from the figures; reachable in principle from a ground.** Fig 13 :136 "It can say the question is not one question and must be split before it is searched again." That is exactly what this answer does. Fig 9 :44 "Only a sealed room can be searched to the end, and no frame is a sealed room." The empirical frame is not sealed, so further study can add atoms; the metalaw frame cannot. The lineage is recorded so the stop is checkable. Indiscernibility from inside the figure frame is not identity with an empirical answer.

The strongest single figure-grounded point is not about welfare at all: it is the union rule. A question that arrives as "how do welfare institutions change coalitions, trust and taxes" is already a bundled question. Stopping at a single verdict would be the named failure.

## 6. One-paragraph version for outreach

> The Tether does not say whether welfare states raise or lower trust, coalitions or tax compliance, and it names no winning design. It says the question is a bundle of three overlapping effects that must be split before it is searched: political coalitions, social trust and tax willingness each need their own anchor and their own loop, with a fourth loop for cases where two or three bind together. Election records, a trust measure whose meaning is stated, and filed tax returns are different anchors, and agreement across studies that share one survey pool is one witness, not several. The bond between institution, trust and taxes must be stated rather than assumed, because sequence, conjunction and common-cause production have the same ingredients and different substances. The framework returns that shape and its refuters, then stops. The empirical sign stays open until exogenous institutional variation is measured against each anchor. That is a `gap` verdict, not silence.
