## Wider literature that bears on this question

- Kenneth Arrow, "Gifts and Exchanges" (1972), quoted at Our World in Data: "virtually every commercial transaction has within itself an element of trust, certainly any transaction conducted over a period of time." Useful for the claim that trust is a transaction cost variable, not a mood.
- Algan and Cahuc, "Trust and growth", Annual Review of Economics 5(1), 2013, cited at Our World in Data for a causal reading of the trust-growth link and for the warning that the relationship is bidirectional. Relevant because it blocks a one-way "institution -> trust" sequence.
- Glaeser, Laibson, Scheinkman and Soutter, "Measuring Trust", Quarterly Journal of Economics, 2000, cited at Our World in Data: attitudinal items and past behaviour are both weak predictors of incentivised trust choices. This is the instrument-versus-anchor distinction in F3.
- Esping-Andersen, *The Three Worlds of Welfare Capitalism*, 1990, and "The Three Political Economies of the Welfare State", International Journal of Sociology 20(3), 1990. The regime typology is a candidate frame for the coalition slot, but it is an import, not a figure derivation.
- Ashraf, Bohnet and Piankov, "Decomposing trust and trustworthiness", Experimental Economics 9, 2006, cited at Our World in Data. A source-discriminating instrument in the sense used by F3 and F9.

None of these was read as a full text this session. They are recorded here as leads named inside sources that were opened, and no quote is attributed to them that was not present in an opened page. They are not counted as world sources in `10_source_ledger.csv` for that reason.

## Distribution rules: offer, do not push

- Fig 4 :22 "Only methods cross up. Only head starts come down." Sending the full framework before a recipient has described a residual sends a recording, not a generator. Lead with the three-slot output and its refuter.
- Fig 10 :42 "A verifier that can reconstruct the claim cannot verify it." A reader who has absorbed the architecture before giving a verdict has joined the frame being checked.
- Fig 9 :27-28 "describe it before you go looking, or any frame you pick will seem to fit." Ask what they would count as a failure in each slot before giving the links.
- Fig 2 :40 "agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool." Do not pool later agreement as corroboration.
- Seat Map :163 "High correlation is disqualifying for a verdict and efficient for a convention." Do not select recipients by likely agreement.

**Recommended order in a reply:** (1) the three-slot shape claim and the pre-registered refuter; (2) ask what they would count as a failure in each slot and record the answer; (3) only then the links, with the note that the figures, not the summary, are the authority.

## Two-stage offer

**Stage 1.** Validate the output against the ground: does the three-slot split survive, does each slot have a non-updating anchor, and does the bond type change the prediction?

**Stage 2, opt-in only.** If curious, read the framework that produced the output. The verdict on stage 1 must be recorded before stage 2 is shown, and later agreement is never counted as a witness.

**Stage 3.** Offer the formula as a method to reuse. `THE_FORMULA.md` "Direction" says a frame cannot check the direction of its own question, so each user writes their own refuter and states their own mode before running it.
