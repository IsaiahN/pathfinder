## 1. A shape is a constraint, not a filler

The canonical limit: the framework returns "what a missing thing would have to look like (a shape, not a name)". Fig 9 :32: "you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself." Fig 13 :135: "It cannot say which primitive is missing, because naming it would mean already holding it."

From *The Library of Babel*, opened 2026-11-23: "You can cut your search short if the set of books returned is small and descriptive enough that it provides a general shape of the answer that satisfies your question."

So a **shape** is the narrowed form of an answer you can state before you can name it. It constrains; it does not fill. For this question the shape is: the predicate must be declared, the frequency is anchor-owned, importance is relative to a named outcome, the habitat is enumerated, and rocky peers are isomers over a shared atom set. A number such as "22 percent" is not the shape; it is a reading that belongs to one predicate and one instrument.

## 2. How a shape is obtained

- *Library of Babel*: use the vocabulary you already have that is closest to the thing, look for recurring new terms that overlap or correlate with it, recurse. "With each new term gained, you are able to narrow the space by definition and deepen your vocabulary."
- Fig 6 :30: "The description travels. Fails by being sharpened into terms only we can read. Cast it in effect terms." Fig 9 :30: "your words are borrowed too, so describe the effect rather than the cause."
- *Decoding Tacit Knowledge*, opened 2026-11-23: "When contact isn't possible, the best you can send is a plain description of what you'd expect to see, because anyone can check that against the world."
- *Library of Babel* on stopping: "You will know that you are done because the finish line will also be the starting place, as there is nowhere else to search and nothing else that can describe the answer better."

Applied here: the reader does not need to be told the number in advance. They need a description of what they would see if the shape were true, so they can check it against the exoplanet record.

## 3. The central shape for this question

The question's "or" is a union masquerading as a dichotomy. *Library of Babel* names this failure directly: "Perhaps your problem was a bundle or conflation of several problems masquerading as one? You will need to search all of those problems individually to find their answers and then combine them in a satisfactory way." Fig 9 :54 says the same thing: "And when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again."

The two unbundled shapes:

1. **Occurrence.** A count over a declared predicate, settled by an anchor. The framework supplies the demand for a declared predicate and the name of the anchor (a counter), not the count. This is a `gap` in the figure library and an import from measurement.
2. **Discrimination.** An enumerated habitat in which importance is relative to a named outcome; rocky worlds figure as isomers and functional-group members over a shared atom set. Which features are load-bearing remains unreached because we have one inhabited instance and no source-discriminating instrument for Earth twins.

## 4. Why the source question governs the second half

*The Hard Problem is About Source*, opened 2026-11-23, gives the mechanism. An instrument reads green and "cannot comment on the source": the same reading can come from one green light, yellow plus blue, RGB, CMYK, or a scheme not yet discovered. "If several paths create the same behavior, then behavior is a projection with lossy information." The spectrometer, not an argument, resolved it.

A planet radius and an insolation are the same kind of reading. They cannot comment on the source: surface state, composition, atmosphere, life. Kreidberg and Stevenson 2025 report that "no atmospheres have been definitively detected" on rocky worlds and that Earth analogs around Sun-like stars are not accessible to JWST. What is owed is the discriminating instrument, not a name. Fig 6 :32: "An instrument is not built from a description. It is improved from a worse instrument already returning something." The edge already exists in M-dwarf spectra, so this is unreachable-at-this-budget rather than underivable in principle.

## 5. What licenses a name here

Only two things, both outside the shape itself:

1. **The ground settles it.** Formula step 5: "the ground settles it. Verified only by what pays." A measurement of occurrence over a declared sample settles the count; a source-discriminating measurement would settle a feature's role.
2. **A provenanced import from a frame whose closure differs** (Fig 6 :23, Fig 8 :50), with a source gate and a shadow test. The Kepler occurrence result is such an import; it must be recorded with its predicate, instrument and confidence interval.

A name that is neither settled nor provenanced is the searcher's own contribution and must be recorded as such. Calling a 1.6 Earth-radius planet "Earth-like" is a binding from the observation frame, not a derivation.

## 6. Direction: the question's outcome is a preference, not a term

`THE_FORMULA.md` "Direction": a frame "cannot check the direction of its own question, because the shape a frame is searching for is not a term it holds." Fig 5 :65: "make the sought-for shape an ORDINARY TERM. Write down what an answer would look like, bind it, and let it accrue a residual like anything else."

The question asks which of Earth's properties are "important" but never names important *for what*. The honest move is to bind the outcome (microbial life, complex life, technology, habitability over a billion years) as an ordinary term and let it accrue a residual. Fig 11 :49: "An actor's valence is relative to the residual, not a property it carries." Without that binding, "unusual in important ways" is a preference masquerading as a term, and the search can only return whatever frame the reader picked.

## 7. Prestige is not action

*The Recursive Self-Improvement Trap*, opened 2026-11-23: "Prestige must never be allowed to validate an action directly." "Prestige can fund an action. It can give an action a budget to be tried. But it cannot settle an action. Only the ground can settle it. The Ground Maintainer must be blind to prestige."

A campaign that gathers famous astrobiologists to agree that Earth is rare is the named collapse, not corroboration. The legitimate ask is the opposite: give the shape a budget to be tried, let an outside reader say what would refute it, and let the ground settle it.

## 8. Correction to my own §4 and §5

I initially treated "shape, not a name" as a ban on naming any term in the slot. The canonical phrase is "what a **missing** thing would have to look like (a shape, not a name)". It governs a primitive not held in the library. Naming a held atom or a measured quantity is a binding, not the invention of a new primitive. What remains wrong is calling such a binding a derivation, or letting the binding move the wall (Fig 6 :25: "An import adds no atom to the world."). Both the earlier and the narrowed reading are recorded so neither is quietly lost.

## 9. My own audit is a simulation, not a ground reading

*The Recursive Self-Improvement Trap*: "LLMs can quickly simulate an approximate answer/output with the formula found in the architecture post. It will often be better than the generic outputs that you will usually get otherwise too." But "the truly testable output would need to be derived from the real architecture or working the formula yourself." So `01` is a simulated derivation checked for internal consistency against the figures. It is not a test of the question and must be labelled as such. It cannot settle anything it reports on.
