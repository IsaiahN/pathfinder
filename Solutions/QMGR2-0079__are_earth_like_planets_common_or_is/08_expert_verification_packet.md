# Expert verification packet: the Tether on Earth-like planets

**What this is:** a claim in a form an exoplanet researcher or astrobiologist can check. Mode, derivation and refuters are stated before the conclusion, so a reader knows what to look for and what would settle it.

**Mode: `general` on the shape and the union split, `specified` on the per-domain findings and the gap, `candidate`, not `grounded`.** No occurrence rate is asserted as a figure result. This is a simulated derivation checked for internal consistency against the verbatim figure text, not a ground reading. It cannot settle the question it reports on.

## 1. The claim, in one paragraph

The question is a union compound, not a forced choice: a count and a feature discrimination can both be true. For the count, the figure library yields a shape and an anchor requirement, not a number, so the honest verdict is a `gap` owned by measurement; the published rate varies from 5.8 to about 50 percent for the same planets under different habitable-zone definitions, which is exactly the predicate-dependence the framework predicts. For the features, importance is a habitat enumeration relative to a named outcome, Earth and its rocky peers are isomers over a shared atom set, and a radius or insolation reading cannot determine surface habitability. Which features are load-bearing stays unreached with one inhabited instance and no source-discriminating instrument for Earth twins.

### C2 - framework-internal and observation-facing; checkable by reading Figure 11 and the occurrence record

- **Statement:** "Earth-like" is a predicate whose thresholds decide the count; "important ways" is a habitat enumeration; importance is relative to the outcome named.

- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 12 :117 - "isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour." ## 3. What is explicitly not claimed

- Not an occurrence rate as a figure result. The number is ground-owned and imported with provenance if used at all.
- Not that Earth is unique, or typical. Neither half is settled.
- Not that any feature is necessary for life. Importance is relative to a named residual, and there is one inhabited instance.
- Not that a non-detection of an atmosphere means no atmosphere. Fig 8 :39: "So a search that finds nothing is never a proof of absence."
- Not that radius and insolation determine habitability. The source is underdetermined by the reading.
- Not a derivation of the Rare Earth feature list from the six-term set. It is an enumeration read off the world.
- Not grounded. No measurement was taken in this folder.

## 5. What would settle it

A ground reading, not agreement:

1. An occurrence rate over a declared radius, insolation and stellar-type box with injection-recovery completeness and confidence interval. Independent instruments converging on that rate would count as corroboration; reviews sharing the Kepler pool would not.
2. A source-discriminating measurement distinguishing composition and surface state at a given radius, currently unavailable for Earth twins around Sun-like stars (Kreidberg and Stevenson 2025: "Studying the atmospheres of true Earth analogs around Sun-like (FGK) stars is not possible with JWST").
3. At least two comparison cases with different feature configurations and a measured outcome, so that a feature's valence can be tested against a named residual.

## 6. What an expert can check in twenty minutes

1. Read Fig 9 :54, Fig 9 :27, Fig 11 :43, Fig 11 :49 and Fig 12 :117 - five short passages.
2. Check C2 against the occurrence record: does the 5.8 to about 50 percent spread under different zone definitions hold? Barbato et al. 2018 is the quickest revision to inspect.
3. Name one feature whose importance you could rank without first naming the outcome it affects. If you cannot, the valence-relativity limit stands.
4. Name one instrument that would distinguish an Earth twin from a bare rock with the same radius around a Sun-like star, or say why none is currently possible.
5. Send the refutation. A failure is more useful than an endorsement.
