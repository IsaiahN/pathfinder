## Three findings in the wider literature that bear directly on this question

1. **The count moves with the predicate.** Petigura et al. 2013 Table 1 spans 5.8 to about 50 percent for the same planets under different habitable-zone models, and Barbato et al. 2018 revise the radius-dependent estimate with Gaia parallaxes. This is the empirical reason to bind "Earth-like" as an ordinary term before searching (Formula "Direction"). Cite it when explaining why a single "how often" number is not the answer.
2. **The two halves are answered by different instruments.** Kepler counts planets; JWST and future direct-imaging missions discriminate atmospheres. Kreidberg and Stevenson: "Studying the atmospheres of true Earth analogs around Sun-like (FGK) stars is not possible with JWST." A count from one instrument cannot settle a feature claim that needs the other. Keep the domain verdicts separate.
3. **The rarity debate is a conflict in the second half only.** Conway Morris predicts convergence; Ward and Brownlee predict a long improbable conjunction. Waltham 2019 finds no individual property with convincing anthropic bias but notes similar habitability timescales. Both sides accept that planets are common. This is a conflict about which configuration matters, not about the count, and it is unresolved with one inhabited instance.
