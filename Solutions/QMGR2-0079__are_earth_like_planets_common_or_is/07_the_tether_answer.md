# The Tether's answer: are Earth-like planets common, or is Earth unusual?

**Mode: `general` on the structural shape and the union split, `specified` on the per-domain findings and the gap, `candidate` throughout, not `grounded`.** This is a shape and a route, not a result. Simulated derivation checked against the figures (`05_what_shape_means.md` section 9), not a ground reading. No measurement was taken.

## 1. The residual it answers

The question asks for a frequency and a discrimination under one "or". They are not alternatives: Earth-size planets can be common while Earth's configuration is unusual. This is a union compound (Fig 9 :54: "And when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again."), so the verdicts stay per domain and are never averaged.

## 2. The shape

1. **The predicate must be declared before anything is counted.** "Earth-like" can mean radius, composition, insolation, a feature list, or inhabited. Fig 9 :27: "describe it before you go looking, or any frame you pick will seem to fit." The published occurrence rate is 5.8, 8.6, 22, 26 or about 50 percent depending only on the habitable-zone definition used. The number is a function of the predicate and instrument.
2. **The frequency half is a `gap` in the figure library.** A frequency is a counter, the anchor case named in Fig 2 :29. The framework returns "a kind of thing, never the answer itself" (Fig 9 :32), so it cannot produce the rate. "Unreachable is a verdict here, not a silence" (Fig 13 :134). The import instruction is to measure occurrence over a declared box, publish the completeness correction and confidence interval, and treat non-detections as bounds (Fig 8 :39).
3. **"Important ways" is a habitat enumeration, not a derivation.** Fig 11 :43: "The habitat is enumerated, never composed." The Rare Earth feature list is read off the world, which is exactly what the figure requires; no figure line derives it. And Fig 11 :49: "An actor's valence is relative to the residual, not a property it carries." Nothing is "important" until the outcome is named.
4. **Earth and its rocky peers are isomers over a shared atom set.** Fig 12 :117: "isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour." The weaker form is a functional group (Fig 12 :124): "functional group. A substructure whose presence fixes the outcome. The group says what kind; the qualifier says which member." "Earth is unusual" means a different arrangement, not a different substance.
5. **Radius does not determine habitability.** A radius reading cannot comment on its source (the metamerism argument in *The Hard Problem is About Source*). Fig 6 :32: "An instrument is not built from a description. It is improved from a worse instrument already returning something." The source-discriminating instrument exists for M-dwarf planets and not yet for Earth twins, so this is unreachable at this budget, not underivable in principle.
6. **The question's outcome is a preference, not a term.** The question never says important for what. Fig 5 :65: "make the sought-for shape an ORDINARY TERM. Write down what an answer would look like, bind it, and let it accrue a residual like anything else." Until the outcome is bound, the feature ranking is a search preference and cannot be checked.

## 3. What it explicitly does not say

- Not a number. No occurrence rate is asserted as the framework's answer.
- Not a verdict that Earth is unique or typical. Both halves remain open in their different ways.
- Not that any feature is necessary for life. Importance is relative to a named residual, and we have one inhabited instance.
- Not that a non-detection is an absence. Fig 8 :39: "So a search that finds nothing is never a proof of absence."
- Not that radius or insolation determines habitability.
- Not grounded. Nothing here is settled by a measurement taken in this folder.

## 4. What would settle it

The anchor, not more argument (Fig 2 :45: "Frames range against the ground. They do not negotiate with it."):

- **For the count:** an occurrence rate over a declared radius, insolation and stellar-type box, with injection-recovery completeness, confidence interval and date. Independent instruments converging on the same rate would be real corroboration (Fig 8 :50); agreeing reviews of the same Kepler pool would not be.
- **For the features:** a source-discriminating measurement that distinguishes composition and surface state at a given radius, plus at least two inhabited or uninhabited comparison cases with different feature configurations. With one instance, feature importance cannot be ranked.
- **Standing falsifiers from `01` section 5:** a strain-free figure line yielding the number; one stable predicate across instrument revisions; feature importance shown independent of the named outcome; radius plus insolation shown to determine habitability; two same-atom planets with different arrangements shown to have identical habitability outcomes.

## 5. The honest stop

**Unreached, not unreachable.** The count is ground-owned and simply not in the figure library. The feature ranking is blocked by one inhabited instance and a missing instrument for Earth twins. The framework stops where the instruments stop distinguishing and records the lineage so the stop is checkable. Fig 13 :134: unreachable is a verdict, not a silence. Prestige or agreement is not an instrument (*The Recursive Self-Improvement Trap*: "Prestige can fund an action. It can give an action a budget to be tried. But it cannot settle an action.").

## 6. One-paragraph version for outreach

> The Tether does not give a number for how common Earth-like planets are, and it does not declare Earth unique or typical. It says the question is two questions under one "or", not a forced choice: a count and a feature judgment, each with its own anchor. The count depends on which predicate you declare, and published estimates range from about 6 to about 50 percent for the same planets under different habitable-zone definitions, so the framework's honest verdict there is a gap owned by measurement, not a failure of search. For the second half it gives a shape: importance is a habitat enumeration relative to an outcome you must name, Earth and its rocky peers are isomers over a shared atom set, and a radius reading cannot tell you the surface condition. What it offers is the shape any answer must have, five refuters written in advance, and the instruments that would close it. Both halves stay open until the ground settles them.
