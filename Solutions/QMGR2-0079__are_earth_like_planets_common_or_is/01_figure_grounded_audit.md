## 0. The residual, stated in effect terms before any figure line is chosen

**R.** A verdict is requested on two things bundled under one "or": (a) a frequency, the occurrence of a predicate called "Earth-like"; (b) a discrimination, which of Earth's properties are load-bearing for an outcome the question never names. Arity: two conditions, so a union compound rather than one hard question (Fig 9 :54). Symmetry: the common versus unusual judgment is a threshold comparison against a reference distribution (a ⋛ relation), not a symmetric combine of two co-present quantities; the important-ways judgment asks which features are load-bearing, which is an isomer and functional-group question (Fig 12 :117, :124). Scale: a population count, a single object's feature list, and deep-time habitability.

**What an answer would look like, bound as an ordinary term.** A declared predicate set (radius, mass, insolation, composition, feature list, inhabited), an occurrence rate with a confidence bound over a declared stellar and radial box, and an enumerated habitat in which each Earth property is tagged necessary, contingent or irrelevant to a named outcome, each tag settled by measurement. Binding this shape lets the preference accrue a residual and be refused. Fig 5 :65: "make the sought-for shape an ORDINARY TERM. Write down what an answer would look like, bind it, and let it accrue a residual like anything else."

**The "or" is invalid as a forced choice.** "Common" and "unusual in important ways" are not exhaustive alternatives. Earth-size planets can be common in radius while Earth's feature configuration is unusual. Fig 9 :54: "And when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again." Fig 9 :55: "A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones." This is a union, not a partition. Verdicts are kept per domain and never averaged.

## 3. Findings

### F1: The question is a union compound and the predicate must be declared before counting (decisive for the framing)

Fig 9 :54: "And when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again."
Fig 9 :27: "describe it before you go looking, or any frame you pick will seem to fit"

"Earth-like" is a bundled predicate. It can mean (i) Earth-size radius, (ii) rocky composition, (iii) habitable-zone insolation, (iv) possession of Earth's feature list, or (v) inhabited. These are different conditions with different frequencies. Petigura, Howard and Marcy 2013, opened 2026-11-23, report that the occurrence of Earth-size planets in the habitable zone is 22 percent under a simple flux definition, 5.8 percent under Kasting 1993, 8.6 percent under Kopparapu et al. 2013, 26 percent under Zsom et al. 2013 and about 50 percent under Pierrehumbert and Gaidos 2011. The same paper notes that earlier estimates ranged from 1 percent to 34 percent. The number is a function of the declared predicate and instrument, not a property of the world alone. That is exactly why the predicate has to be bound as an ordinary term before the search (Formula "Direction").

The "or" is therefore a false forced choice: "common" (a count over one predicate) and "unusual" (a rarity judgment over a different feature set) can both be true. Mode `general`. Triangulated: Fig 9 plus the measured spread of occurrence estimates from an independent astronomy evidence pool.

**Refuter:** show a single figure-grounded predicate for "Earth-like" that yields one stable number across instrument revisions and across the published habitable-zone definitions. That would collapse the union into a partition and restore the forced choice.

### F2: The frequency half is a counter, so it is a `gap` in the figure library (decisive for D1)

Fig 2 :29: "An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks. A measurement, an experiment, a counter, a market, a patient outcome."
Fig 9 :32: "you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself"
Fig 13 :134: "Unreachable is a verdict here, not a silence"

A frequency is literally a count over a sample. It is the anchor case named by Fig 2. No line in the verbatim figure extract returns an occurrence rate, an exponent or a confidence interval. `THE_FORMULA.md` step 7 separates the cases: underivable-in-principle and unreachable-at-this-budget are different facts with different instructions. The frequency is not underivable in principle; it is reachable by measurement and unreachable from the figure library. Fig 8 :30: "underivable, and searchable."

**Verdict for D1: `gap` in the figure framework.** This is not silence. The honest report: the figures constrain the shape of an answer and name the anchor that would settle it; they do not produce the number. Claiming otherwise would be a `general`-mode verdict with no ground, the defect the seat map names. Mode `specified` on the gap claim. The import instruction is: measure occurrence over a declared radius and insolation box and stellar type, publish the completeness correction and confidence interval, and report non-detections as bounds (Fig 8 :39).

**Refuter:** show a figure line, applied without strain, that determines a numerical occurrence rate for a declared Earth-like predicate; or produce a ground measurement that settles the rate for a declared sample. Either closes the gap and replaces the verdict.

### F3: "Important ways" is a habitat-enumeration question, and importance is relative to a residual (governing figure for D2)

Fig 11 :43: "The habitat is enumerated, never composed."
Fig 11 :48: "You do not invent the list. You read it off the world, and what you cannot perceive or measure yet is the residual."
Fig 11 :49: "An actor's valence is relative to the residual, not a property it carries."

Ward and Brownlee's Rare Earth hypothesis, as summarised by EBSCO 2022 and the Astronomy.com feature (fetch failed, see section 8, so cited through the EBSCO and University of Washington pages actually read), enumerates a habitat: right galaxy, right star, liquid water, stable orbit, mild tilt, gas giants, large Moon, molten core and magnetic field, oxygen, plate tectonics. That list is exactly what Fig 11 asks for: an enumeration read off the world. It is not a composition from the six-term set, and no figure line derives it.

Crucially, "important" has no verdict until the outcome is named. Fig 11 :50: "The same actor is a benefactor to one residual and a malfactor to another, so mutualism and parasitism are relations rather than kinds." A large Moon that stabilises obliquity for complex life may be irrelevant to microbial life; oxygen is a waste product to anaerobes and a prerequisite to animals. Waltham 2019, opened 2026-11-23, an independent geology source, reports that "None of the individual properties considered show convincing evidence for anthropic bias" while the habitability timescales are "surprisingly similar," giving only "tentative support for the view that Earth may be special." That is the correct status: a shape, not a ranking.

**Strain, named.** Obliquity stabilisation by the Moon looks like a maintainer seat. Re-census: the relevant system has no seat choosing outcomes; it is unmaintained contact (Fig 11 :56: "Unmaintained. No maintainer and no live frame in the seat. The habitat is pure contact, nothing selects, and what survives is what the ground kept."). The Moon is a habitat actor in a contact-driven flow, not an author of a verdict. The unmaintained reading survives with that caveat recorded.

Mode `general` on the structure, `specified` on the literature reading, which remains unreached because we have one inhabited instance. Triangulated across Fig 11 and an independent geology and planetary-science pool.

**Refuter:** show that a feature's importance is intrinsic to the feature and independent of the outcome named, across at least two different residuals; or show that the Rare Earth feature list is derivable from the six-term set without enumeration from the world.

### F4: Earth-likeness is a source-discrimination problem, and the discriminating instrument does not yet exist for Earth twins

Fig 6 :32: "An instrument is not built from a description. It is improved from a worse instrument already returning something."
Fig 9 :30: "your words are borrowed too, so describe the effect rather than the cause"

A radius of one to two Earth radii and an insolation do not determine surface conditions, composition or life. The NASA Science news release on the Petigura result, opened 2026-11-23, quotes the team: "Earth-size planets in Earth-size orbits are not necessarily hospitable to life"; "Some may have thick atmospheres, making it so hot at the surface that DNA-like molecules would not survive." Kreidberg and Stevenson 2025, opened 2026-11-23, report that "Studying the atmospheres of true Earth analogs around Sun-like (FGK) stars is not possible with JWST" and that "no atmospheres have been definitively detected." Seager et al. 2025, opened 2026-11-23, compute a transmission signal of about 1 ppm for an Earth twin against a 30 ppm noise floor. The reading underdetermines the source: many compositions give the same radius. This is the metamerism error described in the essay *The Hard Problem is About Source* (framework author, high overlap).

Importantly, this is unreachable-at-this-budget, not underivable-in-principle. The edge already exists: JWST returns spectra for M-dwarf rocky planets, and instruments are improved from worse instruments already returning something (Fig 6 :34: "The question is not whether a sensor could exist. It is whether anything, at any resolution, is already returning something that fails to resolve."). Mode `specified`.

**Refuter:** demonstrate an instrument for which a radius and insolation reading uniquely determines surface habitability without an atmospheric or source-discriminating measurement.

### F5: "Common or unusual" is a threshold comparison that needs a declared reference class, and the two halves must not be averaged

Fig 12 :86: "a threshold relation between two quantities"
Fig 1 :27: "They are frame-internal. A frame cannot score itself with a quantity it also produces."
Fig 1 :19: "The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated."

To say "Earth is unusual" is to compare Earth's properties to a distribution. The comparison is a ⋛ relation, and the reference class must be declared or the verdict is a frame-internal preference. The framework's own rule is that a frame cannot score itself with a quantity it also produces. And the signal lives per object: a single averaged occurrence fraction destroys the slot index that says which planets and which features drive the answer. So D1 and D2 are separate slots; a high occurrence rate does not convert an unusual configuration into a common one, and vice versa. Mode `general`.

**Refuter:** show that the frequency and the feature-rarity judgment are the same quantity, so that averaging them loses nothing, or that "unusual" can be settled without a reference class.

### F6: Rocky planets over a shared atom set are isomers, and "unusual" means a different arrangement

Fig 12 :117: "isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."
Fig 12 :124: "functional group. A substructure whose presence fixes the outcome. The group says what kind; the qualifier says which member."

Earth, Venus and Mars share rock, metal, volatiles, an atmosphere and an energy flux. They are figure-isomers: the same atom set under a different arrangement, with decisive behavioural difference. So the honest structural form of "Earth is unusual" is not a different substance but a different arrangement over a shared set. The weaker form is that Earth belongs to a functional group (rocky, temperate, volatile-bearing) whose qualifier is its particular configuration. The carbon-silicate thermostat is a cycle (Fig 12 :121: "cycle. The output re-enters as input. The sign of the loop decides whether it closes, and a chain cannot close by construction."); plate tectonics is a catalyst-like persistent part (Fig 12 :119: "catalyst. A part that survives its own use."). Mode `specified`, unmeasured. See `06_isomers_and_order.md`.

**Falsifier, written before the run:** if a feature invoked to make Earth unusual introduces an atom absent from the comparison class (a large Moon as a qualitatively new operand, not a different bond over the same atoms), the isomer claim fails and the comparison becomes a different composition; the claim is withdrawn rather than stretched.

### F7: Convergence versus rarity is a real low-overlap conflict in the world, and it is about the second half only

Conway Morris 2010, *Philosophical Transactions of the Royal Society B*, opened 2026-11-23, a genuinely low-overlap biology source, argues that "evolution is remarkably predictable" and that convergence is ubiquitous, predicting that comparable outcomes recur wherever a suitable planet exists. The Rare Earth sources predict that a long conjunction of improbable conditions is required. Ward and Brownlee 2000 and Waltham 2019 are not in the figure library, so this is world-level material, secondary only.

A conflict sends us back to the census, never flips the figure verdict. Re-census: both sides accept that planets are common; they disagree about whether a particular configuration is necessary for an outcome. That is Fig 11 :49 valence relativity plus a missing ground (one inhabited instance), not a conflict about the figure-derived shape. So the D2 feature ranking remains unreached, and the convergence material is recorded as a conflict in the second half, not as a refutation of the framework. Mode `specified`.

**Refuter:** a figure line that ranks Earth's features by importance independent of a named residual, which would dissolve the valence-relativity limit and let the convergence-versus-rarity dispute be decided from the framework.

### F8: Verification is not corroboration, and sources must be counted by evidence pool

Fig 2 :35: "Alignment is triangulation, not negotiation. A mirror cannot correct you."
Fig 2 :40: "disqualifying for a verdict: agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool"
Fig 2 :42: "Buy independence in proportion to the cost of being wrong."

The Kepler occurrence papers, the Berkeley and NASA news releases, and the M-dwarf occurrence follow-ups all draw on one Kepler and Keck evidence pool; they are one witness for the count, not several. The JWST atmosphere papers share the JWST pool. Waltham 2019 (geology) and Conway Morris 2010 (evolutionary biology) are genuinely low overlap with the astronomy pool, but they cannot settle a count either. Fig 8 :50: "Two frames can hold the same atom by convergence, reaching it separately, which is corroboration at maximum independence." That condition is not met here for the frequency. Mode `specified`.

**Refuter:** a case where two low-overlap frames agree on the occurrence rate by derivation from unrelated evidence and the ground later disagrees, which would show the independence purchase was mispriced.

## 5. Pre-registered refuter (written before the conclusion)

A falsifier written before the run is a design instrument. The shape claim is refuted if any of the following holds:

1. A figure line, applied without strain, determines a numerical occurrence rate for a declared Earth-like predicate. That closes the D1 gap.
2. A single figure-grounded predicate for "Earth-like" is derived that yields one stable number across instrument revisions and published habitable-zone definitions. That collapses the union into a partition and breaks F1.
3. A feature's importance is shown to be intrinsic to the feature and independent of the outcome named, across two different residuals. That breaks F3.
4. An instrument is demonstrated for which radius and insolation alone uniquely determine surface habitability. That breaks F4.
5. Two planets with the same atom set and different feature arrangements are shown to have identical habitability outcomes, collapsing the isomer claim; or a required feature is shown to introduce a new atom and the isomer reading is withdrawn rather than stretched. That breaks F6.

If any fires, the corresponding finding is withdrawn and the audit is rerun from the census. Until one is run, no part of this may be labelled `grounded`.

## 9. Verdict

The question is a union compound and must be unbundled. The frequency half is a counter, so it is a `gap` in the figure library: the figures give the shape of the anchor and the rule for declaring the predicate, not a number. The "important ways" half survives as a shape: importance is a habitat-enumeration question relative to a named outcome, Earth and its peers are isomers over a shared atom set, and "common" versus "unusual" is a threshold comparison requiring a declared reference class. Which features are actually load-bearing stays unreached with one inhabited instance and no source-discriminating instrument for Earth twins. This is `specified` and `candidate`, never `grounded`. Strongest single figure-grounded point: Fig 11, the habitat is enumerated, never composed, and what you cannot measure yet is the residual.
