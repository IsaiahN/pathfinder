# Outreach draft: check one output before you decide whether the framing is worth reading

**Use:** one to one, or as a cover letter attached to a submission through the public venues in `03_public_contact_points.csv`. **Not** for bulk send.

**Hard precondition:** do not send until the predicate in F1 and the refuter in section 5 of `01_figure_grounded_audit.md` are stated in the message. Output before theory or the two-stage offer collapses on contact.

**Subject:** Check one output before you decide whether the framing is worth reading

> Hi [name],
>
> I have one specific output I would like you to check against what you already know. I am not asking you to read the framework first, and I am not asking you to agree with it. If the output does not survive, there is nothing else to look at.
>
> **The output (`general` on the shape, `specified` on the gap, not `grounded`):** the question "are Earth-like planets common, or is Earth unusual in important ways?" is two questions bundled under an "or", and they are not alternatives. Both can be true. The framework's rule for a union like this is to unbundle before searching. "Earth-like" can mean a radius, a composition, an insolation, a feature list, or inhabited, and the published occurrence rate is 22 percent, 5.8 percent, 8.6 percent, 26 percent or about 50 percent depending only on which definition is used. So the number is a function of the declared predicate, not a property of the world alone.
>
> Two consequences follow. First, the frequency is a counter. It is settled by an observed sample, not by any frame, and the framework returns a shape and an anchor requirement, not a number. My honest result for that half is a `gap`: I can say what must be measured and what would settle it, but I cannot produce the rate. Second, "important ways" is a habitat-enumeration question. Which of Earth's properties matter is relative to the outcome you name: a large Moon matters differently for complex life than for microbial life. Earth and its rocky peers stand to one another as isomers, the same atoms under a different arrangement, not different substances.
>
> **What I am not claiming:** that I can tell you the occurrence rate; that any particular feature is necessary for life; that Earth is unique, or typical; that a non-detection of an atmosphere means no atmosphere; or that radius alone tells you habitability. Those are bindings from the observation frame onto terms the framework holds, not derivations, and the source-discriminating instrument for an Earth twin around a Sun-like star does not exist yet.
>
> **What would refute the shape claim:**
> 1. a figure line, applied without strain, that yields a numerical occurrence rate for a declared Earth-like predicate;
> 2. a single predicate that gives one stable rate across instrument revisions and the published habitable-zone definitions;
> 3. a demonstration that a feature's importance is intrinsic to it and independent of the outcome named;
> 4. an instrument for which radius and insolation alone determine surface habitability;
> 5. two planets with the same atom set and different arrangements shown to have identical habitability outcomes, which would collapse the isomer claim.
>
> I wrote these down before sending this. If any one holds, the corresponding claim is withdrawn. A one-line "this fails because..." is more useful to me than agreement. If you would rather not engage, no reply is needed.
>
> **If it survives, and only then:** the sources are below. The figures are the authority, not my summary of them.
> - Verbatim figure text with line numbers (local framework file): `FRAMEWORK/reference/03-FIGURES_TEXT.md`
> - Canonical usage rule (local framework file): `FRAMEWORK/reference/FIGURES_README_canonical.md`
> - The eight-step formula (local framework file): `FRAMEWORK/figures/THE_FORMULA.md`
>
> **If you want to use the machinery:** the formula is in the local framework files. Run it on a problem you know well and can check against a ground. Write down what would refute your result *before* you run it, because a loop cannot check the direction of its own question. A reported failure is more useful to me than a reported success.
