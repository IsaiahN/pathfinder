# Housing, income, health and services as a union: structure, order and analogues

**Mode: `specified`.** This is a simulated derivation checked against the figures, not a ground reading (see `05_what_shape_means.md` §7).

## RESIDUAL

The per-slot harm distribution `D_s` is not explained by a list of four factors. Two questions are owed: (1) are there cases where two slots share the same set of conditions yet bear differently, which would make the conditions **isomers** under Fig 12's definition; and (2) what is the dependency order of the union, so an intervention is aimed at the link that is actually broken.

## DOMAIN CENSUS

Atoms held for this derivation: the four observed conditions (housing, income, health, service access), each as a condition or relation in the habitat, plus the hazard and the measured outcome. Bonds held: the seven, including `+` conjunction, `→` sequence, `⋛` comparison and `−` subtraction. No new primitive is available without import. ## DERIVATION

### Q1 - Can two slots share the same four conditions and bear differently?

- Fig 12 :117: **"isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."**
- Fig 12 :95: **"Two entries with an identical ingredient set are not variants of one thing. They are different things whose notation cannot tell them apart, and the difference is carried entirely by the bond."**

An index records the ingredient set. It cannot record which condition bound to which state (housing tenure to flood exposure, health status to service reach, income to evacuation option). Two neighbourhoods with identical index scores can therefore be different substances with respect to `D_s`. This is the isomer claim in effect terms: **the same four conditions under different bonds are different bearing structures, and the index cannot tell them apart.**

The world match is Mah et al. 2023: two households below the poverty line, one with strong social ties and one isolated, are *"two distinct tiers of social vulnerability not captured by examining household income alone."* Same income atom, different bonds, different outcome. Corroboration only; same evidence-pool caution as elsewhere.

### Q2 - Are the four a functional group or a standing list?

- Fig 12 :123: **"functional group. A substructure whose presence fixes the outcome. The group says what kind; the qualifier says which member."**
- Fig 11 :51: **"No standing list of good actors survives a change of residual."**
- Fig 11 :50: **"The same actor is a benefactor to one residual and a malfactor to another, so mutualism and parasitism are relations rather than kinds."**

The four conditions form a **functional group**: each says what kind of bearing structure is present, not which member will bind. A seawall (housing/physical condition) protects against surge and can trap heat; income buys evacuation and cannot buy dialysis if the clinic is closed. The qualifier, that is, the specific hazard and outcome, decides the member. This is why `07_the_tether_answer.md` refuses to rank the four.

### Q3 - What is the dependency order of the union?

- Fig 9 :55: **"A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones."**
- Fig 9 :56: **"A single lever clears the region where only one condition holds and leaves the intersections standing, because the second condition still binds."**
- Fig 3 :20: **"The chain is ordered by dependency, not by importance."**

Order, by dependency rather than importance:

1. **Perception** (D3): can the per-slot harm be read at all, or is it pooled (Fig 10 :26)?
2. **Vocabulary** (D2): are there terms for what housing, income, health and service access afford a household in this specific hazard (Fig 3 :7)?
3. **Objective** (D1/D4): can we say what would count as reducing unequal bearing, per slot (Fig 3 :10)?
4. **Planning** (D4): which overlapping condition is being targeted, and which intersection will remain (Fig 9 :56)?
5. **Learn and carry** (D1): is the result settled against the ground and carried as a method, not a recording (Fig 3 :15; Fig 4 :22)?

The break is expected at link 2 or 3, where there is no instrument (Fig 3 :27). Most adaptation finance lands above the break, where metrics already exist. IPCC WGII Chapter 6 corroborates at the policy stratum: *"The greatest gaps between policy and action are in failures to manage adaptation of social infrastructure (community facilities, services and networks)"*.

### Q4 - Catalysts, cycles and the duration of bearing

- Fig 12 :119: **"catalyst. A part that survives its own use. Spent and regenerating parts behave differently across uses, and nothing records which a term is."**
- Fig 12 :121: **"cycle. The output re-enters as input. The sign of the loop decides whether it closes, and a chain cannot close by construction."**

A resource that regenerates across events (insurance that pays and is renewed, a social network that is rebuilt) is a catalyst; one that is spent (savings, a single evacuation) is not. Nothing in the resource's description records which. And recovery is a cycle whose sign decides whether it closes: a loan that cannot be repaid re-enters as deeper debt rather than as recovery. This is the structural reading of F7 in `01`: duration is a chain of handoffs that can fail at the middle link, and the two factors of Fig 7 :37-39 decide whether the loop closes.

### Q5 - Is a settled per-slot result an atom for the next level?

- Fig 12 :104: **"A settled molecule becomes an atom for whatever composes over it. So irreducible is a fact about a level and never about the world, and there is no bottom"**

If a per-slot claim is settled against the ground, it can serve as an operand for city-level or national-level composition. Until then, aggregating unsettled per-slot claims upward is dead reckoning (Fig 4 :30). This licenses cross-scale transfer only after settlement, and it is why the honest answer stops at the shape here.

## FALSIFIER, WRITTEN BEFORE THE RUN

If either of the following holds, the isomer and union claims fail:

1. A single condition can be written without any of the others and still fully predict `D_s` in every case, which collapses the union into a partition (breaks Q1 and Q3, Fig 12 :117, Fig 9 :57).
2. A condition's valence is invariant across hazards and outcomes, which makes it a standing good or bad actor and breaks Fig 11 :50-51 (breaks Q2).

A third, softer falsifier: if a single lever reduces harm equally in all intersections with no categorically different residue, the dependency order in Q3 is not a real union and the intervention advice in `07` is wrong.

## CONCLUSION

The four conditions are a **functional group**, not a ranking and not an index. Two slots can share the same ingredient set and bear differently because the bonds differ, which is Fig 12's isomer claim applied to habitat conditions. The union has a dependency order: perception, vocabulary, objective, planning, carry, with the break expected where no instrument exists. Duration adds catalysts and cycles, whose sign decides whether recovery closes. All of this is `specified` and ungrounded; the source-discriminating instrument that would name the binding condition in a given case is the declared gap.
