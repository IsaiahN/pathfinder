# WATSON answer, EAR-0085

**Mode: `specified`, not `grounded`.** Figures are primary and decide. World sources are secondary, corroborate but never overrule, and a conflict returns the derivation to the domain census. This is a simulated derivation checked for internal consistency against the figures, not a ground reading.

## QUESTION

How do housing, income, health and access to services shape who bears climate impacts?

Restated as an effect: why does `R = |Γ(b,a) - o′|` stay small in some household slots and grow large in others after the same hazard, and how do the four habitat conditions bear on that difference? R is indexed per slot, never aggregated (Fig 1 :19). Anchor: the measured per-slot human outcome, legitimate because the question is constitutively about people (Fig 2 :29, :36-37). Option A: treat the four factors as an additive index and report a regional vulnerability score. Option B: enumerate them as habitat, keep the union and the per-slot distribution, and stop at a shape until a source-discriminating instrument exists. A yields a filter presented as a verdict and loses the signal that says where to look. B yields a checkable shape, written refuters and a declared gap.

## DOMAIN CENSUS

R by arity, symmetry and scale, then one line per domain with actors, currency and anchor; partition or union; order by dependency.

- **R (the residual).** Arity: multi-place, bearer slot, hazard, action, outcome, indexed by time (shock, response, recovery); carries an ordered bond and a comparison bond. Symmetry: asymmetric, bearing is a threshold relative to a baseline and to other slots. Scale: household, community, city and region, plus the time channel; membrane between scales is lossy (Fig 4 :21-22).
- **D1 Household capability loop.** Actors: household members, Γ, actions. Currency: actions and residual reduction. Anchor: realised outcome per household. Admitted.
- **D2 Habitat of vulnerability.** Actors: housing stock and tenure, income and assets, health status, service access. Currency: contact and valence relative to the residual. Anchor: the measured harm distribution; habitat updates, ground settles (Fig 11 :73). Admitted.
- **D3 Measurement frame.** Actors: surveys, sensors, indices, records, analysts. Currency: contact versus pooled or saturated readings. Anchor: the ground, kept reachable (Fig 10 :29). Admitted.
- **D4 Union compound.** Actors: intersections of the four conditions. Currency: which condition binds at the intersection. Anchor: per-slot outcome at the intersection. Admitted as a compound with its own loop.
- **X1 Climate vulnerability as a free-floating property of a place.** No anchor independent of measured outcomes; a frame-internal score (Fig 1 :24-27). Not a domain yet.
- **X2 Adaptive capacity as one stock.** Absorbed into D1 and D2.

Union, not partition: D1/D2 and D2/D4 overlap, and D3 overlaps all three (Fig 9 :54-57). Order by dependency: perception, vocabulary, objective, planning, learn and carry (Fig 3 :20-27). Strain test: an attempt to map Fig 13's six physical terms onto the four factors only fit by renaming tenure as space and illness as gradient; retracted (strain S1). Fig 13 retained for the honest-stop role only.

## VERDICT (from the figures)

The shape survives: **who bears climate impacts is a per-slot distribution produced by a household-in-habitat relation over an enumerated set of housing, income, health and service conditions; the conditions have valence relative to the specific residual, overlap as a union whose intersections need different levers, and often act through cascades several steps from direct contact; the per-slot signal is lost when readings are pooled, and the binding condition in a named place stays unnamed until a source-discriminating instrument exists.** Verdicts are kept separate per domain and never averaged. Mode `specified`, ungrounded. The asserted causal weight of any one factor is a declared `gap`, not a hidden answer. The world sources corroborate the shape after re-mapping, and no world source flipped a verdict.

- **Cost if wrong:** high and partly irreversible. A union treated as a list sends one lever into intersections where the second condition still binds, and adaptation finance is misallocated toward households whose readings are already visible while the ones lost to pooled data are missed. The real cost is spending the independence of outside readers by sending a score instead of a distribution. Fig 2 :42 prices the remedy: *"Buy independence in proportion to the cost of being wrong."* The cost is reducible by leading with the shape claim and its refuter.
- **Undoable:** yes, in the derivation. No term is deleted if the claim fails; a refutation is added and the reach falls because *"something was added rather than because anything was deleted"* (Fig 6 :42-43). The shape and its nine falsifiers are recorded in a provenance trail so a later reader can locate the error (Fig 10 :62-64). Real-world misallocation is less easily undone, which is why the cost is high.
- **Reversed by:** a ground reading, not agreement. A per-slot outcome series that discriminates the four conditions would name the binding factor; a natural experiment whose single-condition change moves the distribution would re-census the union; a cascade trace showing all material harm is first order would break F3; an aggregate that predicts per named household would break F1; a factor with invariant valence would break F2 and F11-style valence rules; a check settled by a shared evidence pool would break F8. Until one of these fires, the verdict stands as `candidate`.
