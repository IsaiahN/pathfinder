**Subject:** One checkable claim about who bears climate harm

> Hi [name],
>
> I have one specific output I would like you to check against what you already know. I am not asking you to read the framework first, and I am not asking you to agree with it. If the output does not survive, there is nothing else to look at.
>
> **The output (`specified`, not `grounded`):** the question "who bears climate impacts" is a per-slot distribution, not an average. Housing, income, health and access to services are best read as an enumerated habitat of conditions and relations in contact with that harm, not as ingredients in a single vulnerability score. Three consequences follow. Their valence is relative to the specific hazard and outcome, so the same condition can protect in one case and harm in another. The four overlap as a union, and the intersections are categorically different cases, not harder versions of one case. Material harm often arrives through cascades several steps out from direct contact, so a first-order exposure map misses the people it matters most to see.
>
> The anchor for all of this is measured per-slot human outcome, mortality, illness, displacement, property and income loss, service interruption. That is legitimate here because the question is constitutively about people. A regional mean or a composite index is an instrument, and instruments can be wrong on the axes they do not measure.
>
> **What I am not claiming:** that any one of the four factors is *the* binding condition, that a vulnerability index cannot be useful, or that this is a settled result. The framework returns a shape, not a name. Which factor binds in a named place, and with what weight, is something I am calling a gap until a source-discriminating instrument exists.
>
> **What would refute the shape claim:**
> 1. an aggregate statistic that predicts, prospectively and per named household, who will bear the next shock with no per-slot reading;
> 2. a factor whose measured valence, protects versus harms, is invariant across hazards and outcomes;
> 3. a documented set of events in which all material harm is first order and no second-order cascade matters;
> 4. an agent-only intervention that moves the harm distribution without changing any contact variable;
> 5. a single lever that reduces harm equally in all intersections, leaving no categorically different residue.
>
> I wrote these down before sending this. If any one holds, the shape is wrong and I will retract it. A one-line "this fails because..." is more useful to me than agreement. If you would rather not engage, no reply is needed.
>
> **If it survives, and only then:** the sources are below. The figures, not my summary of them, are the authority.
> - The figures and formula, public repository: https://github.com/IsaiahN/tether/tree/general-agent/figures
> - The architecture and the citation list: https://medium.com/@IsaiahNwukor/arrival-of-the-fittest-the-tether-architecture-1de7edf641ec
> - The worked narrative: https://adventuresinml.substack.com/p/how-intelligence-actually-works
>
> **If you want to use the machinery:** the formula is in the figures link. Run it on a problem you know well and can check against a ground. Write down what would refute your result *before* you run it, and state the mode of what you get. A reported failure is more useful to me than a reported success.
