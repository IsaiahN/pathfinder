# The Tether's answer: can transparent decision-making increase trust under unpopular outcomes?

**Mode: `specified` on the conditional shape, `general` on the structural rules, `gap` on the magnitude. Not `grounded`.** This is a simulated derivation checked against the verbatim figures (`05_what_shape_means.md` §7), not a ground reading. No measurement of institutional trust was taken.

## 1. The residual it answers

Trust is not a population average. It is R_s = |Γ(b,a) - o'|, the gap for each affected group between what the institution's decision process predicts citizens will accept and what those citizens actually register. "The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated." (Fig 1 :19). "Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average." (Fig 1 :22). An unpopular outcome makes the affected slots live. The question asks which intervention reduces that residual.

## 2. The shape

1. **Human judgment is the legitimate anchor, per slot.** Fig 2 :36: "An anchor is legitimate when the question is constitutively about it." Fig 2 :37: "Human judgment is the right anchor for is this a good answer for a person, because the question is about people." So a trust question is answered by people, not by an institution's KPI. But a survey reading is an instrument that can miss axes it does not measure, so the per-slot judgment must be anchored in contact, not just attitude.

2. **The unpopular case is a union compound, not one hard question.** Fig 9 :57: "What remains is smaller and it is also categorically different: a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either." There is an unfavorable outcome and a process the citizen cannot range against their ground. One lever, publish more, clears only the region where one condition holds.

3. **Disclosure must bear on the residual, not on the maker.** Fig 9 :51: "evidence bearing on the residual is admissible; evidence bearing only on its maker is a stake-inducing channel, and the verifier is denied it." A narrative of internal deliberation is maker evidence. Evidence about whether the decision ranged against the affected group's ground is admissible. Fig 10 :59: "an interest in the outcome is unavoidable, an interest in the reading is disqualifying." The institution that authors both decision and disclosure has a disqualifying interest in the reading.

4. **Transparency is a channel, and more is not monotone.** Fig 10 :24: "the ground does not decay, the channel does." Fig 10 :26: "stale percepts, dropped transients, saturated metrics, pooled readings." Fig 4 :30: "So take the round trip and measure the gap. Without it you are navigating by dead reckoning: confident about a position nobody checked." A published summary is a lossy condensation of the decision, not the decision itself.

5. **The change must change contact.** Fig 11 :40: "An improvement that does not change contact changes nothing, however much it improves." Fig 11 :49: "An actor's valence is relative to the residual, not a property it carries." Transparency has no standing positive valence. Under a repairable misreading, residual-bearing disclosure can help. Under a genuine break in contact, maker-only disclosure makes the break visible without repairing it.

6. **Agreement cannot certify the result.** Fig 10 :42: "A verifier that can reconstruct the claim cannot verify it." Fig 2 :31: "Independence, between two frames, is how little they draw on the same evidence." Fig 2 :42: "Buy independence in proportion to the cost of being wrong." Many readers of the same publication are one evidence pool, not corroboration.

## 3. Verdict in one sentence

**Yes, conditionally, and not by the mechanism usually assumed.** Transparency can increase trust when an unpopular outcome is in play only where it changes the institution's contact with the affected group's ground, by putting residual-bearing evidence before an outside verifier who can refuse the institution's account; it cannot do so merely by publishing more of the institution's own reasoning, by saturating or pooling the channel, or by reporting trust as a single aggregate. Mode `specified`. Magnitude `gap`.

## 4. What it explicitly does not say

- Not that transparency always helps, or that it always harms. Valence is relative to the residual (Fig 11 :49).
- Not that any given design works in all fields. No world result is promoted to a verdict.
- Not that a pooled trust score is false. It is a budget filter, not a witness (Fig 9 :60).
- Not that this settles the question. Fig 9 :32: "you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself."
- Not a number. The world meta-analysis average of 0.13 is secondary corroboration for a conditional effect, and an average over slots is the Fig 1 :22 error.
- Not that the framework names which disclosure works. That is a provenanced import or a ground measurement, and neither has been supplied here.

## 5. What would settle it, the ground

A source-discriminating instrument, not a further argument. *The Hard Problem is About Source* ( essay opened 2026-11-23): an instrument that reads green "cannot comment on the source"; the spectrometer resolved it. Here:

- **Slot instrument.** Does the affected group's judgment and compliance move, measured per slot before and after disclosure, with the aggregate reported separately? An instrument that cannot separate contact from attitude is the metamerism case.
- **Admissibility test.** Does the disclosure carry evidence bearing on the decision's contact with the group, or only maker reasoning? Can an outside verifier refuse the account on that evidence?
- **Round-trip test.** Measure the gap between the decision and its published summary. A zero gap would dissolve F3.
- **Valence test.** Does the same disclosure have the same effect across a documented change of residual with contact held constant? A fixed effect would break Fig 11 :49-51.
- **Standing falsifiers from `01`:** any of the five pre-registered refuters firing withdraws the shape.

## 6. The honest stop

**Unreached, not unreachable.** The magnitude and the specific instrument are below this run's horizon. Fig 13 :134: "Unreachable is a verdict here, not a silence." Fig 8 :39: "So a search that finds nothing is never a proof of absence." Fig 3 :27: "A link with no instrument is not a link that works. It is a link nobody has looked at."

The essays call this the union horizon: a report that nobody has reached the discrimination yet, not a settled verdict. What is owed is a description of the instrument in effect terms, carried to a frame whose closure differs. Prestige, a large response count, or a satisfied stakeholder group is not such an instrument.

## 7. One-paragraph version for outreach

> The figures do not answer yes or no to transparency. They say to measure trust per affected group rather than as one population average, because an average hides the group that actually stopped trusting. They say human judgment is the legitimate anchor for a question about people, but a survey taken right after a disclosure is a reading that cannot tell where the change came from. They say an unpopular outcome is two conditions, not one harder case, so one lever called publish more will leave the intersection standing. They say disclosure counts only when it carries evidence about the decision's contact with the affected group, not the institution's own reasoning, and only when someone outside the institution can refuse the account. More information can saturate or pool the channel. Transparency has no standing good valence; it helps only where it changes contact. The framework returns this conditional shape, its five written refuters, and the instrument still owed. It does not return a coefficient, and it does not claim to have settled the question.
