## Question restated as an effect

An institution runs a decision process whose library Γ predicts the observation it expects from citizens. The residual is R = |Γ(b,a) - o'| per citizen-slot s, indexed per slot and never averaged (Fig 1 :19, :22). When the outcome is unpopular, o' diverges from Γ(b,a): the affected group does not accept the decision. The question asks which change to the institution's channel moves that per-slot residual toward zero: (A) publishing more of the decision process, or (B) changing the decision's contact with the affected group's ground. Option A can add agreement without changing contact; option B changes contact. What an answer would look like, bound as an ordinary term before the run: a conditional rule stating which disclosure conditions reduce R in which slots, with a named instrument and a pre-registered refuter, so the rule is itself refusable rather than a preference (formula step "Direction").

## What is deliberately not here

- No personal emails of named individuals.
- No ranking of people by how likely they are to agree that transparency helps.
- No bulk-send plan.

Three figure-grounded reasons:

1. A room does not upgrade the label. Many readers of the same disclosure share one evidence pool, so their agreement is the pool talking, not corroboration (Fig 2 :40, Fig 7 :13).
2. Selecting recipients for likely agreement manufactures the disqualifying correlation (Fig 2 :31, Fig 13 :38).
3. A verifier that has already absorbed the institution's framing cannot verify its effect (Fig 10 :42). The first contact carries the residual and its refuter, never the framework's interior.

Legally: unsolicited bulk email to scraped addresses breaches GDPR and CAN-SPAM style rules in many jurisdictions. Public opt-in venues avoid that failure as well as the structural one.
