## Wider literature that bears on the question

1. **The average effect is positive but conditional.** Wang and Guan find a positive overall effect of 0.13 with decision-making transparency as a negative moderator. This is corroboration for a conditional shape, not a verdict, and the average is exactly the pooled quantity Fig 1 :22 warns about.
2. **More detail can lower trust.** Prajod and colleagues find trust and subscription falling under detailed disclosure while source checking rises. That is a trust-versus-vigilance trade-off: a citizen can trust less and check more, which is closer to Fig 10 :19 "install what can be violated" than to a simple disclosure-increases-trust story.
3. **Transparency is calibration, not a monotone good.** Zerilli and colleagues frame both overtrust and distrust as failures and propose vigilance as the midpoint. This corroborates the figure-grounded claim that the quantity to reduce is the per-slot residual, not an attitude score that can rise for the wrong reason.
4. **What is tracked matters more than what is observable.** Bernstein's factory kept performance tracked while hiding work activity. That is an organizational instance of Fig 9 :51: admissible evidence bears on the residual; evidence bearing on the maker is a different channel.
5. **A genuine conflict.** Schaap and Saarikkomaki report that procedural justice theory blurs process and outcome in practice and is poorly equipped for structural inequality. This conflicts with a clean process/outcome split. It returned the derivation to the census and did not flip the verdict, but the owed instrument is now sharper: one that can still discriminate contact evidence from maker evidence under conditions where they are empirically entangled.

No world source sets the verdict. The essays by the framework author share an evidence pool with the figures and carry no independent corroboration weight.

## Distribution: offer, do not push

- **Fig 10 :52** "Bring a characterised residual, never the source." Lead with the per-slot residual and the five refuters. Send the figures only after a verdict is recorded.
- **Fig 9 :27-28** "describe it before you go looking, or any frame you pick will seem to fit." Ask what the recipient would expect to see if the shape were true before showing the framework.
- **Fig 4 :22** "Only methods cross up. Only head starts come down." The formula is a method, so giving it away does not give away an answer.
- **Fig 2 :40** "agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool." A list selected for receptiveness is one pool wearing many names. Do not rank recipients by likely agreement.
- **Fig 10 :42** "A verifier that can reconstruct the claim cannot verify it." A recipient who has absorbed the whole framework before giving a verdict has joined the frame being checked.

**Recommended order in a reply:** (1) the residual and the shape claim, (2) the five refuters and the request for a failure in the recipient's own terms, (3) record that verdict, (4) only then the local source links, with the note that the figures, not the summary, are the authority, (5) offer the formula as a reusable method with each user's own refuter written first.

**Stage 2 is opt-in by construction.** Later agreement is never pooled as corroboration (Fig 2 :31). A Track B reply is a conversation, never a witness.
