# Expert verification packet: the Tether on transparency and trust under unpopular outcomes

**What this is:** a claim in a form a transparency or institutional-trust researcher can check. Mode, derivation and refuters are stated before the conclusion.

**Mode: `specified` on the shape, `general` on the structural rules, `gap` on the magnitude. Not `grounded`.** No measurement is claimed. This is a simulated derivation checked for internal consistency against the verbatim figures, not a ground reading. It cannot settle the question it reports on.

## 1. The claim, in one paragraph

Trust should be treated as a per-slot residual, not a population average: the gap between what an institution's decision process predicts affected citizens will accept and what those citizens actually register. Under an unpopular outcome the case is a union of two conditions, an unfavorable outcome and a process the citizen cannot range against their ground, so one lever called publish more leaves the intersection standing. Disclosure can reduce the residual only where it changes the institution's contact with that ground, by putting evidence about the decision's contact into the hands of an outside verifier who can refuse the institution's account. Disclosure that shows only the institution's own reasoning is maker evidence, not admissible evidence for the verifier. Transparency has no standing positive valence; its effect depends on the residual. The magnitude of any trust gain is a declared gap, not a result.

## 2. Three checkable sub-claims

### C1: Measurable by a trust researcher

- **Statement:** if transparency works, the effect must be visible per affected group, and a population average can hide it.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 1 :19, "The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated." Also Fig 1 :22, "Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average." And Fig 2 :37, "Human judgment is the right anchor for is this a good answer for a person, because the question is about people."
- **What to check:** is there a design in which the population mean rises while a specific affected group's trust and compliance stay flat or fall? If such a case exists, the warning is empirically live. If no such case exists under a source-discriminating instrument, this sub-claim fails.
- **Refuter:** a single aggregate trust reading that fully predicts compliance in every affected slot with no hidden slot residual.

### C2: Measurable by a transparency or public-administration researcher

- **Statement:** maker-process disclosure and residual-bearing disclosure are different substances over the same atoms. Only the second is admissible to a verifier.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 9 :51, "evidence bearing on the residual is admissible; evidence bearing only on its maker is a stake-inducing channel, and the verifier is denied it." Also Fig 10 :59, "an interest in the outcome is unavoidable, an interest in the reading is disqualifying." And Fig 12 :117, "isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."
- **What to check:** do the two disclosure kinds produce different per-slot trajectories when their content is independently classified? A known empirical tension: the Wang and Guan meta-analysis reports that decision-making transparency negatively moderates the trust effect, and Schaap and Saarikkomaki report that process and outcome are blurred in practice. The second is a genuine conflict with the clean split; the packet keeps it visible rather than smoothing it.
- **Refuter:** maker-only disclosure, with no outside verifier and no residual-bearing evidence, reducing the live per-slot residual across a change of contact conditions.

### C3: Measurable by an organizational or human-factors researcher

- **Statement:** transparency is a channel that can saturate, go stale or pool readings, and its summary has a nonzero round-trip gap relative to the decision. Increasing the amount published is not monotone in reducing the trust gap.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 10 :24, "the ground does not decay, the channel does." Fig 10 :26, "stale percepts, dropped transients, saturated metrics, pooled readings." Fig 4 :30, "So take the round trip and measure the gap. Without it you are navigating by dead reckoning: confident about a position nobody checked." Corroborating world evidence: detailed AI disclosures lowered trust while raising source checking (Prajod and others, 2026); explanations can backfire and transparency should not be equated with explainability (Zerilli, Bhatt and Weller, 2022); a factory kept performance tracked while hiding work activity (Bernstein, 2012).
- **Refuter:** a disclosure with a measured round-trip gap of zero at every resolution, including per-slot residuals and the violation path, with no saturation or pooling, that still fails to move trust for reasons other than a contact failure.

## 3. What is explicitly not claimed

- Not that transparency is always good, or always harmful. Valence is relative to the residual (Fig 11 :49).
- Not a coefficient. The 0.13 average in the meta-analysis is world evidence and a pooled quantity, not the figure verdict.
- Not that this is grounded. No measurement was taken, and the framework's own rule says a gate is not the ground.
- Not that the framework names which disclosure works. It returns a shape (Fig 9 :32: "you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself").
- Not that agreement from many readers certifies the claim. Fig 2 :40: "agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool."
- Not a theory of institutions and not a policy recommendation. It is a checkable conditional with a written refusal.

## 5. What would settle it

1. A per-slot instrument that discriminates changed contact from changed attitude, with results reported slot by slot.
2. An independently classified test of maker-process versus residual-bearing disclosure with an outside verifier able to refuse the account.
3. A measured round-trip gap between the decision and its published account.
4. A valence test across a documented change of residual with contact held constant.
5. Any of the five refuters in `01_figure_grounded_audit.md` §4.

## 6. What an expert can check in twenty minutes

1. Read Fig 1 :19, Fig 9 :51, Fig 10 :24 and :59, Fig 11 :49 and Fig 12 :117. Six short passages.
2. Say whether C1 and C2 are faithful to your field's own findings. Name the result you would use against them.
3. Say what instrument would distinguish contact from attitude in your setting, or why none is possible.
4. Send the refutation. A failure is more useful than an endorsement. No theory needs to be read first, and no agreement is requested.
