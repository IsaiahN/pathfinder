# Isomers and order in the transparency and trust question

**Mode: `specified`.** This is a simulated derivation checked against the verbatim figures, not a ground reading (see `05_what_shape_means.md` §7).

## RESIDUAL

The main audit says the unpopular-outcome case is a union of two conditions and that disclosure's valence is relative to the residual. Two supporting questions are owed: (1) what is the dependency order of the chain from disclosure to trust, and (2) are maker-process disclosure and residual-bearing disclosure isomers under Fig 12, or merely the same thing with a different name? A third question is the round-trip gap R_T between the decision and its published account.

## DOMAIN CENSUS (supporting)

Atoms held: institution Γ, decision, disclosure act a, citizen slot s, observation o', ground. Bonds held: → sequence, ⇒ production, ⋛ comparison, ¬ negation. No new primitive is available without import. The two disclosure kinds share every atom; only the bond between disclosure and the decision differs.

## DERIVATION

### Q1: What order does the disclosure chain have?

Fig 12 :30: "A then B; B needs A to have happened." The chain is ordered, not symmetric. Fig 3 states the dependency order: perception, vocabulary, objective, planning, learn and carry. Applied here:

1. **Perception.** The institution can tell what is in front of it: the affected group's observation o'. Fig 3 :5: "the system can tell what is in front of it, and tell it apart from what was there before".
2. **Vocabulary.** It has terms for what the decision affords: what can be refused, revised, explained.
3. **Objective.** It can say what counts as progress in its own terms: R_s falling, not agreement rising. This is the middle link Fig 3 :24-27 says usually breaks because it has no instrument.
4. **Planning.** A disclosure route chosen under incomplete information.
5. **Learn and carry.** Name what worked, verify it against the ground, carry it to the next decision.

Fig 3 :19: "Optimising a link that already works cannot rescue one that does not." Effort spent on step 4 (more web pages, more PDFs) arrives at a step that cannot use it if step 3 has no instrument. This is the structural reason "publish more" is not the lever.

**Falsifier, written before the run:** a case where steps 1 to 3 all measure high and non-declining, yet disclosure still fails to move R_s. That would put the break at planning or carrying and reverse this ordering.

### Q2: Are the two disclosure kinds isomers?

Fig 12 :117: "isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."

Write both over the same atom set {institution, decision, disclosure, citizen, ground}:

- **Maker-process disclosure.** institution → disclosure → citizen, where the disclosure's content is the institution's reasoning. The bond runs from maker to audience. Fig 9 :51 applies: "evidence bearing on the residual is admissible; evidence bearing only on its maker is a stake-inducing channel, and the verifier is denied it."
- **Residual-bearing disclosure.** institution → disclosure → citizen, but the content is the decision's contact with the group's ground, installed so an outside verifier can refuse it. Fig 10 :19: "install what can be violated."

Same atoms, different bond type and different substance. Under Fig 12 they are isomers, not variants of one thing. A policy that counts "amount of information published" treats them as the same formula and therefore cannot see the difference, which is exactly Fig 12 :14-16: the ingredients are recorded and the arrangement is not.

**Status `triangulated`** across Fig 12 (isomer definition) and Fig 9 :51 (admissibility), two figures with low overlap (composition notation versus verification admission).

**Falsifier:** a case where maker-process disclosure and residual-bearing disclosure produce the same per-slot trust trajectory across a change of residual, with an instrument that discriminates their content. That would collapse the isomer claim; they would be one substance with two spellings.

### Q3: Sequence and production are not the same bond

Fig 12 :34-40 distinguishes → (A then B; B needs A to have happened) from ⇒ (A produces B; B did not exist before). A disclosure can *precede* a trust judgment without *producing* the change in contact. The question's wording, "can transparent decision-making increase trust," silently assumes ⇒. The figures give only → by default. Fig 12 :111: "A vocabulary without bonds can name a state and cannot name a route to one." So the claim that disclosure produces trust must be earned, not assumed from temporal order.

**Falsifier:** a controlled case where the disclosure is withheld until after the trust judgment is recorded, and the same trust trajectory appears. That would show → without ⇒.

### Q4: The round trip gap R_T

Fig 4 :30: "So take the round trip and measure the gap. Without it you are navigating by dead reckoning: confident about a position nobody checked."

1. **Sent up (x):** the decision as it actually ranged against the affected group's ground, with its residual per slot.
2. **Abstraction (T_A):** the published account, a compressed summary of reasons.
3. **Concretisation (T_E):** what a citizen or outside verifier reconstructs from that account about the decision's contact.

**R_T is nonzero in structure.** At least three things the decision carries do not survive the trip: the per-slot indexing (Fig 1 :19), the maker versus residual distinction (Fig 9 :51), and the violation path that would let the account be refused (Fig 10 :19). Conversely, the bare fact of a decision and its stated reasons usually survives. R_T is **not** measured numerically here because no ground reading was taken; it is recorded qualitatively as the enumerated set of dropped constraints. Treating the published account as identical to the decision would set R_T to zero and commit the dead-reckoning error.

**Falsifier:** a disclosure record with a measured round-trip gap of zero at every resolution, including per-slot residuals and the violation path. Then the condensation would not be lossy and this finding fails.

### Q5: Negation is required

Formula step 2 (local) says a REFUTED term owes "a refusal, not an explanation." Fig 12 amendment: "A vocabulary that cannot refuse can only accumulate." A transparency regime that can only add documents and never carry a refusal is structurally unable to correct a maker-only account. So the violation path in Q2 is not an optional design nicety; it is the negation operator the vocabulary needs.

**Falsifier:** a disclosure regime that carries a refusal without a unary negation operator, and whose refusal is nonetheless composable and reversible. That would contradict the operator table (local).

## CONCLUSION

The disclosure chain is ordered (perception to objective to planning to learn), and its middle link, what counts as progress, has no instrument in this derivation. Maker-process disclosure and residual-bearing disclosure are isomers over the same atoms with a different bond and a different substance; counting the amount of information published cannot see the difference. Disclosure precedes a trust judgment by → but does not thereby produce it by ⇒. The decision to its published account has a structural round-trip gap R_T that is enumerated but not measured. A refusal operator is required, not optional.

This framing keeps what is defensible without claiming a measured effect: the arrangement, not the amount of disclosure, is the substance.
