# Outreach draft : check the shape before you decide which missions matter

**Use:** one-to-one, or as a cover letter attached to a submission through a public venue in `03_public_contact_points.csv`. **Not** for bulk send.

**Hard precondition:** do not send until the shape claim and its seven refuters in `01_figure_grounded_audit.md` §4 are intact in the text below. The offer collapses on contact if the stage-1 claim cannot survive a refuter.

---

**Subject:** Check one selection rule before you decide which cosmology observations are worth prioritizing

> Hi [name],
>
> I have one specific output I would like you to check against what you already know. I am not asking you to read the framework first, and I am not asking you to agree with it. If the output does not survive, there is nothing else to look at.
>
> **The question in effect terms:** which upcoming observations will *resolve* a major cosmology debate rather than just add more data? The output is a selection rule, not a mission ranking.
>
> **The output (`specified`, not `grounded`):** an observation resolves a debate only if it can act as a pinned anchor at which at least two low-overlap frames make different predictions, it is sharpened from an instrument already returning a reading that fails to resolve, and it can refuse a held term or settle a new one. An observation whose effect is only more photons, smaller error bars or another combined fit spends precision inside a closure that is already returning the same disagreement. Under the framework, that is coverage, and coverage is scored by the frame itself, so it cannot be the verdict. The rule also says that debates which do not shrink with added data are not one hard question but several well-formed ones, so they must be split before anything is ranked.
>
> **What this does not say:** it does not name the best mission, and it does not claim that a discovery is required. A sufficiently deep upper limit on the tensor-to-scalar ratio can refuse whole inflation model classes, which changes the reach without detecting anything. It also does not say that dark-ages 21 cm is a top priority: that window has not registered at any resolution yet, so under the instrument rule it is unreachable at this budget, which is a verdict and not a silence.
>
> **What would refute the shape claim, written before this message:**
> 1. an observation that only increases precision within one closure settling a debate at the ground without refusing a term;
> 2. a debate that does not shrink with added data turning out to be one well-formed question;
> 3. an allegedly resolving observation moving when a theory frame pushes on it;
> 4. two supposedly independent observations turning out to share one evidence pool;
> 5. an upper limit leaving the held model set unchanged;
> 6. a never-registered sensor outranking an already-returning one;
> 7. a figure line that ranks specific missions.
>
> I wrote these down before sending this. A one-line "this fails because..." is more useful to me than agreement. If you would rather not engage, no reply is needed.
>
> **If it survives, and only then:** the sources are below. The figures, not my summary, are the authority.
> - Verbatim figure text with line numbers: `FRAMEWORK/reference/03-FIGURES_TEXT.md` in the public Tether repository (branch `general-agent`).
> - The canonical usage rule (figures README, same repository).
> - The two stage-1 world checks: the LIGO standard-siren explainer and the CMB-S4 inflation page.
>
> **If you want to use the machinery:** the formula is in the figures link. Run it on a problem you know well and can check against a ground. Write down what would refute your result *before* you run it, and state the mode of what you get. A reported failure is more useful to me than a reported success.
