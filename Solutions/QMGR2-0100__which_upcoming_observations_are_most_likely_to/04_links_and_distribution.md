## Wider literature that bears on the selection rule

- **Survey statistics, not cosmology:** the design effect and effective sample size show that multiplying correlated observations does not multiply independent information. This is Fig 2 :44's point made in another field: the effective-sample-size correction for correlated observations is standard in survey statistics and meta-analysis. It is a genuine low-overlap analogue, not a second witness from the cosmology pool.
- **Instrument history:** Fig 6 :33 says "Nobody specified a telescope from an account of Mars." The sequence from a wandering point to a sharper instrument is the normal history of CMB polarization and 21 cm limits, and it is why an already-returning instrument is a better shortlist pick than a never-registered one.
- **Blinding as channel maintenance:** DESI's catalog-level blinding and the CMB foreground/lensing separation problem are the practical form of Fig 2 :25-27. They keep the anchor from updating on what the analysts expect. They do not themselves settle anything.
- **A caution about tension metrics:** a reduction in a dataset tension is not by itself evidence for a model. This matches the figure's rule that a tension is a signal about where to look, not a verdict, and it is recorded in `01_figure_grounded_audit.md` F2 and F8.

## The two-stage offer (and the optional third stage)

1. **Stage 1:** validate the output against the ground. The output is the selection rule and the per-domain verdicts, with modes.
2. **Stage 2, opt-in only:** if curious, read the framework that produced it. The stage-1 verdict is already recorded, so later agreement is never pooled as corroboration (Fig 2 :40).
