# The Tether's answer: which upcoming observations resolve, and which only add data

**Mode: `specified` on the shape and on every per-domain prediction; `general` on the structural selection rule. Not `grounded`.** Simulated derivation checked for internal consistency against the figures (`05_what_shape_means.md` §6), not a ground reading. No em dashes.

## 1. The residual it answers

Cosmology currently holds several live disagreements at once: the Hubble constant, the S8/growth mismatch, the dark-energy equation of state, the inflation model class, dark matter and neutrino mass. The question asks which upcoming observations will *resolve* these rather than merely add data. Fig 1 :19: *"The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated."* There is no single global "tension score" whose reduction would answer this question. There are slots, and each is a union compound (Fig 9 :54).

## 2. The shape

1. **A resolving observation is a pinned anchor at which low-overlap frames differ.** Fig 2 :29: *"An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks."* Fig 2 :31: *"Independence, between two frames, is how little they draw on the same evidence."* An observation that both camps can absorb into their own fit is not an anchor.
2. **It changes reach, not library size.** Fig 8 :32: *"Closure is idempotent. Composing inside a closed set never leaves it, so no search, however long, adds an atom."* Fig 13 :113: *"The array is unbounded and the reach is not."* More data inside one closure is coverage; Fig 1 :27: *"They are frame-internal. A frame cannot score itself with a quantity it also produces."*
3. **It can refuse a term.** Fig 5 :53: *"refuted: a term we hold is wrong; a term is owed that says so"*. A deep upper limit on the tensor-to-scalar ratio that rules out plateau inflation models is a resolution without a detection. Fig 9 :32: *"you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself"*.
4. **It is sharpened from an instrument already returning a failing reading.** Fig 6 :32: *"An instrument is not built from a description. It is improved from a worse instrument already returning something."* Fig 6 :34-35: *"The question is not whether a sensor could exist. It is whether anything, at any resolution, is already returning something that fails to resolve."*
5. **It is applied per slot, after the union is split.** Fig 9 :4-5: *"Disagreements that do not shrink with effort, where each rule keeps working well on a different subset, are not one hard question but several well-formed ones. Split it rather than search."*

## 4. What it explicitly does not say

- Not a forecast. No mission is predicted to succeed; the figures return a shape, not a name.
- Not "bigger is better". Survey volume is bits; settlement is actions (Fig 12 :125-127).
- Not "5σ or nothing". A refusal can resolve without a detection (Fig 5 :53-54).
- Not that all "tensions" are new physics. Tension reduction is a filter, not a witness (Fig 9 :60).
- Not that the framework is a theory of cosmology. It is a generator of the shape of a resolving observation, and it stops where the instruments stop.
- Not `grounded`. No source in this package reports an observation that has settled a debate.

## 5. What would settle it : the ground

A ground reading of "resolved", not more agreement. In this field that means: an anchor whose reading is stable under changes of analysis frame and at which two previously contending frames made different predictions, followed by a decision recorded with provenance. *The Hard Problem is About Source* supplies the instrument criterion: "An instrument that can't distinguish these cases demonstrates its own inadequacy". The spectrometer, not an argument, resolved metamerism. Here the corresponding questions are:

- Does a third-anchor H0 measurement land outside both camps, or inside one, with a channel that survives a blind re-analysis?
- Does a B-mode result detect r or push an upper limit through a model-class boundary?
- Do lensing, BAO and supernova probes still disagree after their systematics are independently characterised, or was the disagreement a pooled reading?

## 6. The honest stop

**Unreached, not unreachable, for most slots; unreachable at this budget for dark-ages 21 cm.** The six-term substrate is a human guess at compression axes, and a future instrument could change what counts as a discriminator. The answer stops at the limit of the available instruments and records the lineage so the stop is checkable. Fig 13 :134: *"Unreachable is a verdict here, not a silence"*. Indiscernibility is not identity.

## 7. One-paragraph version for outreach

> The Tether does not name a best next telescope. It says what a resolving observation must look like: a pinned anchor at which competing cosmology frames make different predictions, sharpened from an instrument already returning a reading that fails to resolve, and able to refuse a held term rather than just refine it. More photons, smaller error bars and another combined fit spend precision inside a closure that is already disagreeing, so by the framework's own metric they cannot be the verdict. The major debates are union compounds, so they must be split before anything is ranked: standard sirens are an independent H0 anchor, a deep B-mode upper limit could refuse whole inflation classes, and weak lensing plus BAO plus supernovae only gain reach where their closures differ. Dark-ages 21 cm has not registered at any resolution, so it is a genuine stop, not a weak candidate. What is offered is the shape, the seven refuters written in advance, and the observation that would close each slot. The sky decides.
