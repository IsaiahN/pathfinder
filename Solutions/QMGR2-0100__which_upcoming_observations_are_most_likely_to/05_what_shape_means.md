## 1. A shape is a constraint, not a filler

The canonical figures README says the framework returns *"what a missing thing would have to look like (a shape, not a name)"*. Fig 9 :32: *"you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself"*. Fig 13 :133: *"A system with a closure can answer that before it searches, and what it returns is a shape rather than a name."*

From *The Library of Babel*: "you can cut your search short if the set of books returned is small and descriptive enough that it provides a general shape of the answer that satisfies your question."

So for this question a **shape** is the narrowed form of a resolving observation that you can state before you can name a mission. It constrains; it does not pick. "An anchor, low overlap, already returning, capable of refusal" is a shape. "CMB-S4 is the answer" is a name.

## 2. How the shape is obtained

- *Library of Babel*: start with the vocabulary closest to the thing, correlate it with recurring new terms, recurse. "With each new term gained, you narrow the space and deepen your vocabulary."
- Fig 6 :30: *"The description travels. Fails by being sharpened into terms only we can read. Cast it in effect terms."* Fig 9 :30-31: *"your words are borrowed too, so describe the effect rather than the cause"*. So the question is posed as an effect: what does an observation *do* to a disagreement, not what instrument is it.
- *Decoding Tacit Knowledge*: "When contact isn't possible, the best you can send is a plain description of what you'd expect to see, because anyone can check that against the world." A selection rule stated in effect terms is checkable by a cosmologist who never reads the framework.
- *Library of Babel* on stopping: "the finish line will also be the starting place", because by then "you have already composed the answer through the process of locating it."

## 3. The decisive essay point for this question: source discrimination

*The Hard Problem is About Source* is the closest essay to the observation-selection question. Its mechanism is metamerism: an instrument that reads green "cannot comment on the source". Several different sources can produce the same reading, so "If several paths create the same behavior, then behavior is a projection with lossy information". The resolution came "not by argument, but by the arrival of a better instrument": the spectrometer. And "An instrument that can't distinguish these cases demonstrates its own inadequacy".

Mapped to this question: two cosmology frames can produce the same fitted parameter while disagreeing on its source (calibration versus sound horizon, mass versus dark-energy model). More readings from the same kind of instrument reproduce the projection. What is owed is a source-discriminating observation, that is an anchor whose closure predicts a different reading for each source. The essay's phrase "The answer is unreached until a better instrument exists" is the shape of the whole answer in one line.

This is not retracted and it is not merely rhetorical. Fig 2 :29 says *"An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks."* Fig 11 :40 says *"An improvement that does not change contact changes nothing, however much it improves."*

## 4. What licenses naming a particular mission

Only two things, both outside the shape itself:

1. **The ground settles it.** `THE_FORMULA.md` step 5: *"SETTLE : the ground settles it. Verified only by what pays. A gate is not the ground."* No upcoming observation has yet paid for a cosmology debate in this folder's record.
2. **A provenanced binding from the world frame** onto the shape, with its own sources and its own residual. Fig 8 :50-52: *"Convergent derivation and adopted import look identical in the contents. Only the record of where each came from separates them"*.

*Library of Babel* also gives the failure mode for a shortlist assembled too early: "Perhaps your problem was a bundle or conflation of several problems masquerading as one? You will need to search all of those problems individually". That is Fig 9 :4-5 in plain language. A single "best next observatory" answer assumes the debates are one problem.

## 5. Answer to the only question that needs answering here

**What does the framework say a resolving observation must be?** Nothing in the framework names a mission. It returns a shape: pinned anchor, low overlap with the contending frames, sharpened from an instrument already returning a failing reading, and able to refuse a held term or settle a new one, applied per slot after the union is split. A particular mission is licensed only by the ground settling it, or by a provenanced world-frame binding. Until then it is the searcher's own contribution and must be recorded as such.

**Does the folder commit the forbidden error?** It does not rank missions as figure verdicts. Where it names CMB-S4, standard sirens, DESI, Euclid or 21 cm, it does so as world-frame bindings with sources and modes, never as the framework's answer. This is the correction the exemplar had to make for "Gradient": the limit governs missing primitives, not the mention of things already in the world frame.

## 6. Two consequences for outreach

**Prestige is not action.** *The Recursive Self-Improvement Trap*: "Prestige can fund an action. It can give an action a budget to be tried. But it cannot settle an action. Only the ground can settle it. The Ground Maintainer must be blind to prestige." A list of famous observatories is a budget, not a verdict. The legitimate ask is: give the candidate observation a budget to be tried, and let the ground settle it.

**My own audit is a simulation, not a ground reading.** Same essay: "the output is only as good as the validator. The agent, or the tool, or the frame, or the theory, cannot validate itself." And: LLMs "can quickly simulate an approximate answer/output" but "the truly testable output would need to be derived from the real architecture or working the formula yourself." So `01` is a simulated derivation, checked for internal consistency against the figures. It is **not** a forecast validated by the sky and must be labelled as such. It cannot settle which observation will actually resolve anything.
