**Question:** Which upcoming observations are most likely to resolve major cosmology debates, rather than merely add more data?

**Source:** QM & GR Questions.txt line 269

**Section:** J. Methods, evidence, and competing explanations

**Mode:** `specified` on the shape and on every per-domain prediction; `general` on the structural selection rule. Nothing here is `grounded`, because no observation in scope has settled a cosmology debate at the ground.

**Status:** done

**Registry status:** pending (registry files are outside this folder and were not touched)

**Boundary.** Work was confined to this folder. No file outside it was modified. ## What is deliberately not here

- No personal emails of named individuals.
- No ranking of instruments by how likely a community is to agree that they are important.
- No bulk-send plan, and no pooling of later agreement as corroboration.

The reason is structural, not courtesy. Fig 2 :35 says *"Alignment is triangulation, not negotiation. A mirror cannot correct you."* Fig 9 :60 says *"Use filters for the budget and witnesses for the verdict. Never let a filter hand you a verdict."* A shortlist of fashionable missions is a budget filter. It is not a witness for which observation will settle anything.

## Honest standing

This is a **simulated derivation checked for internal consistency against the figures**, not a ground reading. From *The Recursive Self-Improvement Trap*: LLMs "can quickly simulate an approximate answer/output" and "the truly testable output would need to be derived from the real architecture or working the formula yourself." This package cannot settle the question it reports on. It can say what shape a resolving observation must have, what each live debate is a compound of, and what would reverse the claim.
