## Restating the question as an effect

Two frames (a person, a group, a discipline) each hold a library Γ: terms for what is the case (factual beliefs), terms for what counts as a goal or a harm (values), and a weighting of private history against inherited prior (interests, Fig 2 and Symbols :24-26). Each produces a moral judgment about one act, o′. The residual is the persistent gap between the two judgments after each has ranged against what it takes to be relevant:

> R_s = |Γ(b,a) − o′| per slot s, indexed by frame and by contested act, never averaged (Fig 1 :19, :22).

The question asks which term in the disagreement is doing the work. The three offered answers (values, facts, interests) are not three values of one variable; the derive-first census below returns **a union of two anchored domains plus one collapse condition**, so the verdicts are kept separate and never averaged.

**Binding the sought-for shape as an ordinary term (Formula, "Direction"):** an answer would look like a separator that, given a described residual, tells you (i) whether a non-updating anchor exists for the contested quantity, (ii) whether the disagreement shrinks when both frames range against it, (iii) whether the frames draw on independent evidence, and (iv) what would refute the attribution. That written-down shape is then refusable by the same test as anything else (Fig 5 :65-66).

## What is deliberately not here

- No personal emails of named individuals.
- No ranking of people by how likely they are to agree with the separator.
- No bulk-send plan, and no pooling of later agreement as corroboration (Fig 2 :40, Fig 2 :35).

Three figure-grounded reasons: (1) a room does not upgrade the label, *"Adding people raises the vocabulary and does not add an anchor"* (Seat Map :283); (2) selecting recipients for receptiveness manufactures exactly the disqualifying correlation, *"High correlation is disqualifying for a verdict and efficient for a convention"* (Seat Map :163); (3) showing the theory before asking for a verdict spends the reader's independence, *"A verifier that can reconstruct the claim cannot verify it"* (Fig 10 :42).
