## 0. The residual in effect terms

The question as posed offers three answers to one attribution: different values, different factual beliefs, different social interests. Before choosing any figure line, describe R by arity, symmetry and scale.

- **Arity.** At minimum a three-place relation: frame A's judgment, frame B's judgment about the same act, and a source attribution. The judgment difference itself is binary (A differs from B on act x), but the asked-for explanation is three-way, so the residual as posed has arity three or more. A three-way attribution is not a three-way partition unless the three conditions are disjoint; they are not (F1).
- **Symmetry.** A disagreement is symmetric: *"swap the operands. Does the meaning change? No"* (Fig 12 :59, the test for +). A and B disagreeing is the same state as B and A disagreeing. But the source relation is ordered: a fact about what happened can ground a judgment about what ought to be done; an "ought" does not ground an "is." Greene 2003 puts the asymmetry in the world's own vocabulary: *"there is a sharp and crucial distinction between the 'is' of science and the 'ought' of ethics."* A symmetric reading of "disagreement" cannot by itself orient the arrow. *"Correlation has valence"* (Fig 2 :39); the missing direction is a seat office, not a frame discipline (Fig 5 :62-64).
- **Scale.** Three scales are in play: the single frame (one person's judgment), the dyad or group (the disagreement), and the population or culture (which comparison terms count as values at all). The transform between scales is lossy (Fig 4 :27-30). A value that circulates at the population scale is a compressed method; brought down to a person it is a head start, not that person's own derivation (Fig 4 :22-25). So a disagreement "about values" may be a replay of a cultural recording rather than a live derivation.

R is indexed per frame and per contested act, never aggregated: *"The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated."* (Fig 1 :19) and *"Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average."* (Fig 1 :22). A pooled “how much of moral disagreement is values versus facts” is the forbidden aggregate.

## 1. Domain census (before choosing any figure line)

One line per candidate domain: actors / currency / anchor. **No nameable anchor means it is not a domain yet** (master plan §8). Partition or union is decided by whether the conditions are disjoint. Dependency order is Fig 3.

| candidate | actors | currency | anchor | status |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **D1 Factual-belief condition** | the two frames' terms about the act and its consequences: what happened, who was harmed, what was consented to, what the odds are | residual reduction; a mis-attached term is re-fit, not re-minted | a nameable thing that does not change because either frame argues: a record, an experiment, a measurement, a counter (Fig 2 :29) | **admitted** |
| **D2 Value condition** | the two frames' comparison terms: which goal, threshold, right, loyalty or harm counts, and which bond joins them | whether a new term or a different comparison is owed; whether closures differ | the question is constitutively about people, so human judgment is the legitimate anchor, not an external instrument (Fig 2 :36-38) | **admitted** |
| **D3 "Social interests" as a third source** | the frames' weighting w, evidence pool, reference group, stake | agreement and independence; agreement here is cheap for a convention and worthless for a verdict | no independent anchor: if the "fact" moves when the group's stance moves, it is another frame, not a ground (Fig 2 :30, :33-34) | **not a domain yet; absorbed** (see F4 and the retraction log) |
| D3a "who in fact benefits" | parties, records of consequences, transfers | payment and consequence | a record of who received what, which does not update on opinion | absorbed into D1 |
| D3b "whose end should prevail" | the same comparison terms as D2 | goal and threshold | human judgment (Fig 2 :37) | absorbed into D2 |
| X1 "moral truth as a free-floating property" | none nameable | none | none | not a domain; refused |

**Union, not partition.** D1 and D2 overlap: cultural-cognition research shows factual beliefs are shaped by value commitments, and moral-psychology research shows affective reactions predict moral judgment better than harm appraisals. Kahan 2013 writes that people are *"fighting a 'culture war,' but one over facts, not values"*; Haidt, Koller and Dias 1993 found *"moral judgments were better predicted by affective reactions than by appraisals of harmfulness."* A single lever clears the region where only one condition holds and leaves the intersections standing (Fig 9 :55-57). The third term, “social interests,” is not a source with its own anchor; it is the name for two collapse modes inside the checking arrangement (Fig 2 panels 1 and 2).

**Dependency order (Fig 3).** Perception (what happened) → vocabulary (do we have terms for harm, fairness, authority, consent) → objective (what would count as this disagreement being resolved) → planning (which instrument or import to use) → learn and carry. The chain is *"ordered by dependency, not by importance"* (Fig 3 :20). Most moral argument lands at planning before fixing the objective; that is effort above the break (Fig 3 :19, :25-27).

## 2. Figure census and findings

Every quoted string below is an exact substring of `03-FIGURES_TEXT.md`. Quotes precede conclusions. Strains are named, not smoothed.

### F1 - The question is a union compound, not a three-way partition (decisive structural finding)

> **"And when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again."** (Fig 9 :54)
> **"A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones."** (Fig 9 :55)
> **"What remains is smaller and it is also categorically different: a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either."** (Fig 9 :57)
> **"are not one hard question but several well-formed ones. Split it rather than search."** (Fig 9 :5)

The three candidate answers are not mutually exclusive. A disagreement over a fact (does this policy reduce harm) can be driven by a value commitment (which ends are worth protecting), and a stated value is often a post-hoc rationalisation of an affective response (Haidt 2001). The intersection is not a harder version of either condition; it is a specific compound. One lever (prove the fact, or state the value) leaves the intersection standing.

**Mode:** `general` for the structural claim, `specified` for applying it to moral disagreements. **Status:** triangulated across Fig 9 and two low-overlap world fields (decision science, moral psychology). **Refuter:** a corpus of disagreements in which the three attributions are empirically disjoint and one lever clears every case.

### F2 - Anchor test: the factual-belief condition

> **"An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks. A measurement, an experiment, a counter, a market, a patient outcome."** (Fig 2 :29)
> **"If it moves when you push on it, it is not an anchor."** (Fig 2 :29-30)
> **"The anchor must not update."** (Fig 2 :33)
> **"mis-attached: refit what you have, do not invent"** (Fig 5 :13)

Where a nameable ground exists and does not move when the frames argue, the disagreement is a factual-belief condition. The remedy is rebinding (a mis-attached term is re-fit, not re-minted) or more search at the same reach; importing a new atom against an exhausted budget adds an atom that was not needed (Fig 6 :27, :51-52 of the text extract). The diagnostic is behavioural: does the residual shrink when both frames range against the anchor? *"Frames range against the ground. They do not negotiate with it."* (Fig 2 :45).

**Mode:** `specified`. **Status:** triangulated (Fig 2 anchor rule + Fig 5 rebinding bin + Fig 9 "is there a fact here"). **Refuter:** a named non-updating anchor exists for the contested quantity, yet the disagreement does not shrink when both frames range against it.

### F3 - The value condition: persistent residual plus a different objective

> **"First: is there a fact here? Disagreements that do not shrink with effort, where each rule keeps working well on a different subset,"** (Fig 9 :4)
> **"are not one hard question but several well-formed ones. Split it rather than search."** (Fig 9 :5)
> **"a goal is a comparison, which is why a vocabulary without it cannot state one"** (Operators table :51)
> **"mechanism broken: a new term is owed"** (Fig 5 :14)
> **"An anchor is legitimate when the question is constitutively about it."** (Fig 2 :36)
> **"Human judgment is the right anchor for is this a good answer for a person, because the question is about people."** (Fig 2 :37)
> **"instruments can be wrong on axes they do not measure."** (Fig 2 :37-38)
> **"No frame escapes its own closure. The bound moves only on contact with a frame whose closure differs."** (Fig 6 :23)

Where the disagreement does not shrink with effort and each side's rule keeps working well on its own subset, Fig 9 says split rather than search. The split exposes different objectives. A goal is a comparison (Operators :51), so a "different value" is a different comparison term or bond over shared atoms, not necessarily a new primitive. Because the question is constitutively about people, the legitimate anchor is human judgment (Fig 2 :36-37), and an instrument for the "is" (a survey of consequences, a brain scan) is an instrument, not an anchor, for the "ought" (Fig 2 :37-38; Greene 2003).

**Mode:** `specified`. **Status:** triangulated (Fig 9 split rule + Fig 2 anchor legitimacy + Operators :51 + the is/ought result from an independent field). **Refuter:** a persistent disagreement persists in which no different objective or comparison can be found in either frame, or the term required is shown to be a genuinely new primitive that the six-term set cannot compose.

### F4 - "Social interests" is not a third anchored domain; it is a collapse condition (census result, with correction)

> **"no anchor; each updates on the others"** (Fig 2 :13)
> **"one evidence pool"** (Fig 2 :18)
> **"they never talk, and still agree"** (Fig 2 :19)
> **"so the agreement is the pool talking,"** (Fig 2 :20)
> **"Two people reading the same report are not two frames; two instruments pointed at the same thing are."** (Fig 2 :31)
> **"agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool"** (Fig 2 :40)
> **"Buy independence in proportion to the cost of being wrong."** (Fig 2 :42)
> **"An actor’s valence is relative to the residual, not a property it carries."** (Fig 11 :49)
> **"No standing list of good actors survives a change of residual."** (Fig 11 :51)
> **"an interest in the outcome is unavoidable, an interest in the reading is disqualifying."** (Fig 10 :59)

The first pass of this census entered “social interests” as a third domain. Re-census refuses that. A domain needs a nameable anchor (master plan §8). “Social interest” has none: if the contested "fact" changes when the reference group's stance changes, it is not an anchor, it is another frame, and panel 3 of Fig 2 has quietly turned into panel 1 (Fig 2 :34). What survives is a partition of the term: "who in fact benefits" is a D1 factual question with a record anchor; "whose end should prevail" is a D2 value question with human judgment as anchor. The residue that is specifically social is a verification defect: two frames drawing on one evidence pool (Fig 2 :18-21) or updating on each other (Fig 2 :13-15). An interest's valence is relative to the residual (Fig 11 :49-51), so no standing list of "self-interested arguments" survives a change of the moral concern. A stake that cannot be removed must be declared (Fig 10 :60).

**Mode:** `general` for the census correction, `specified` for its application. **Status:** triangulated (Fig 2 collapse panels + Fig 11 valence + Fig 10 stake rule + Kahan 2013 on identity-protective cognition). **Refuter:** a social-interest condition found with its own non-updating anchor that is not reducible to a fact record or a value comparison.

### F5 - Stated reasons are not sources: describe the effect before the cause

> **"describe it before you go looking, or any frame you pick will seem to fit"** (Fig 9 :27-28)
> **"your words are borrowed too, so describe the effect"** (Fig 9 :30)
> **"rather than the cause"** (Fig 9 :31)
> **"you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself"** (Fig 9 :32)

Haidt 2001: *"moral reasoning is usually a post hoc construction, generated after a judgment has been reached."* Haidt, Koller and Dias 1993: affect predicts the verdict better than harm appraisal. So the reason a person gives for a moral judgment is a binding written after the event, not the source of the disagreement. The separator therefore cannot ask "what reason do they give"; it must describe the effect (does R shrink under effort against an anchor; does each rule work only on a subset; are the frames independent) and let the cause be inferred from that.

**Mode:** `specified`. **Status:** triangulated (Fig 9 + two moral-psychology sources; same pool, counted as one witness). **Refuter:** a case where the stated reason reliably predicts the source under a source-discriminating test.

### F6 - Symmetry cannot orient the attribution

> **"Correlation has valence."** (Fig 2 :39)
> **"Symmetry is what decides whether an arrangement is a new thing."** (Fig 12 :92)
> **"Alignment is triangulation, not negotiation. A mirror cannot correct you."** (Fig 2 :35)

A disagreement is symmetric under swapping the two frames; the source is not. Truth about the "is" can constrain a goal; the goal cannot constrain what happened. Without a directional element (a non-updating anchor, or a provenance record of which term was imported from which closure), a frame can only report a correlation between values and verdicts and cannot say which produced which. Agreement between two people who share a pool is *"the pool talking"* (Fig 2 :20) and is disqualifying as a verdict (Fig 2 :40).

**Mode:** `general`. **Status:** single-source on the figure side for the symmetry claim (Fig 12), corroborated by the is/ought asymmetry from Greene 2003 (low overlap). **Refuter:** a symmetric measure shown to orient the arrow without an anchor or provenance.

### F7 - A value is not a seventh primitive (retraction of a first-pass reading)

> **"MINT composes inside the closure and can never add an atom."** (Fig 6 :21)
> **"The set is closed. The arrangements are not. That gap is why import exists."** (Fig 13 :115)
> **"Unreachable is a verdict here, not a silence,"** (Fig 13 :134)
> **"It cannot say which primitive is missing, because naming it would mean already holding it."** (Fig 13 :135)

My first pass read "different values" as a different primitive and reached for Fig 13 to forbid it. That was a strain, and a strain means the wrong domain (figures README re-census rule). Re-census: a value is a goal and a goal is a comparison (Operators :51), i.e. a bond over held atoms, not a new member of the set. Naming harm, fairness, purity or loyalty is a **binding** from the moral-psychology frame onto an atom already held, provenanced as such; it is not a derivation of a seventh term. The “shape, not a name” limit governs a *missing* primitive (Fig 13 :134-135); it does not forbid naming a held atom. de Waal 2011 corroborates from animal behaviour that the relevant terms (reciprocity, fairness, empathy) are shared across species, i.e. held rather than freshly invented.

**Mode:** `specified`, ungrounded binding. **Status:** triangulated (Fig 6 + Fig 13 + Operators :51). **Refuter:** a moral value shown to require a new primitive under the six-term substrate, so that the set is not closed.

### F8 - The chain usually breaks between vocabulary and objective

> **"The chain is ordered by dependency, not by importance."** (Fig 3 :20)
> **"Optimising a link that already works cannot rescue one that does not."** (Fig 3 :19)
> **"A link with no instrument is not a link that works. It is a link nobody has looked at."** (Fig 3 :27)
> **"The link between them, having terms for what a situation offers and being able to say what would count as progress, has neither, so it attracts the least attention and the fewest instruments."** (Fig 3 :25-26)

Moral disputes are usually argued at the planning link (who is right, who should yield) while the objective link (what would count as this being resolved) is never fixed. That is why effort does not shrink R. The separator's first move is to bind the objective as an ordinary term (Formula "Direction"; Fig 5 :65-66) before arguing.

**Mode:** `general`. **Status:** single-source on the figure side (Fig 3), corroborated structurally by Kahan 2013's account of why "objectivity" injunctions fail. **Refuter:** a disagreement resolved by more planning effort while the objective was never stated.

### F9 - Channel decay and the uselessness of public agreement

> **"the ground does not decay, the channel does"** (Fig 10 :24)
> **"saturated metrics, pooled readings"** (Fig 10 :26)
> **"same closure, handed along;"** (Fig 7 :12) **"agreement compounds, evidence does not"** (Fig 7 :13)
> **"A room’s agreement is variation, not a verdict. Selection between rooms is where the ground votes."** (Fig 7 :35)
> **"Use filters for the budget and witnesses for the verdict. Never let a filter hand you a verdict."** (Fig 9 :60)

Agreement about a shared meaning is the product; agreement about the world from a shared pool is worthless (Fig 2 :40-41). The relevant quantity is not how many people agree but whether each frame's channel to a non-updating ground is reachable, and whether the frames differ at the evidence level. Popularity, citation count and prestige are filters for the budget, never witnesses for the verdict.

**Mode:** `general`. **Status:** triangulated (Fig 10 + Fig 7 + Fig 2). **Refuter:** a verdict settled by agreement alone where the ground was never consulted.

### F10 - The gap: no figure line returns the fraction or names the source in a case

> **"you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself"** (Fig 9 :32)
> **"So a search that finds nothing is never a proof of absence."** (Fig 8 :39)
> **"Unreachable is a verdict here, not a silence,"** (Fig 13 :134)

No figure line returns the numerical fraction of a given disagreement attributable to values, facts and interests, nor identifies which specific value or interest is operative in a particular case. After a genuine re-census this is a `gap` of the **unreached** kind (needs a source-discriminating instrument and independent frames), not underivable in principle and not silence. Fig 8 :39 forbids reading the absence of a line as proof that no separator exists.

**Mode:** `general`. **Status:** `gap`. **Refuter:** a figure line, or a ground reading, that assigns a per-case fraction.

## 3. Strain tests and re-census

- **S1, Fig 2 :37 vs Fig 2 :33.** Fig 2 says human judgment is the right anchor for a question about people; it also says the anchor must not update. People's judgments plainly do update (Haidt, Kahan). **Re-census:** the anchor is the recorded reading at the time of the question (an observed judgment, a survey result, a verdict), not the live deliberative opinion; the seat tends the *channel* to it (Fig 10 :24). A dispute about what people judge is D1 with a record anchor; a dispute about what they ought to judge is D2 with human judgment as the legitimate anchor. Different objects, so no contradiction. Kept visible.
- **S2, "different values" vs Fig 13 :115.** Resolved in F7: values are comparisons (bonds), not new primitives.
- **S3, Fig 9 split rule vs Kahan's value-laden facts.** Not a figure conflict. Fig 9 governs how to split a residual; Kahan describes the world's evidence pool, where the conditions overlap. It is world evidence *for* the union verdict, not against it. Kept as a live overlap, not smoothed.
- **S4, Fig 11 :51 vs the ordinary idea of a "self-interested argument."** Fig 11 governs valence relative to a residual; the ordinary phrase treats it as a property of the speaker. Re-census assigns the former to the framework, the latter to a provenanced binding. No correction to the verdict.
- **After re-census: one correction retained (F4), one overstatement narrowed (F7), no hidden strain.**

## 4. Pre-registered refuter (written before the conclusion)

Required before any outreach: *"a falsifier written before the run is a design instrument, not only an honesty device"* (Seat Map :287).

The separator shape is refuted if any of the following holds:

1. A disagreement persists under effort, each side's rule works on its own subset, yet no different objective or comparison term can be found in either frame (breaks F3 and Operators :51).
2. A nameable non-updating anchor exists for the contested quantity, yet the disagreement does not shrink when both frames range against it (breaks F2).
3. Two frames known to draw on disjoint evidence still produce a "fact" that moves when one group's stance moves (breaks the anchor rule in Fig 2 :29-34).
4. A moral value is shown to require a genuinely new primitive under the six-term substrate (breaks Fig 13 :115 and Fig 6 :21).
5. Human judgment is shown to be illegitimate as an anchor for a question constitutively about people (breaks Fig 2 :36-38).
6. **Instrument condition:** no instrument, record or independent frame can be named that would discriminate the three attributions in a described case, and the residual cannot be split (breaks F1 and F10 as a reachability claim rather than a budget complaint).

If any fires, the separator is withdrawn. Until one is run, no part of this may be labelled `grounded`.

## 6. Recommendation

1. Lead with the separator, not with a theory of moral psychology: ask (i) is there a fact here with a non-updating anchor, (ii) does R shrink when both range against it, (iii) do the two frames draw on independent evidence, (iv) what would refute the attribution (F1-F4).
2. Split before searching. A bundled "is it values or facts" question must be unbundled; otherwise one lever leaves the intersections standing (F1).
3. State the mode on every claim. A separator is `specified`; the structural shape is `general`; nothing is `grounded` (Formula mode rule).
4. Treat reasons as bindings with provenance, not as sources (F5). Ask what each side would expect to see if its attribution were true, before showing them the framework (Fig 9 :27-28).
5. Buy independence in proportion to the cost of being wrong (Fig 2 :42). Do not select recipients for likely agreement (Seat Map :163); do not pool later agreement as corroboration (Fig 2 :40).
6. Record the gap. The per-case fraction is `unreached`, not a number to be invented (Fig 13 :134).

## 7. Seven-question recursive derivation (summary)

1. **Is there a fact here?** Sometimes yes. Fig 9 :4-5 and Fig 2 :29. Where the anchor does not move, D1 governs; where the disagreement does not shrink and each rule works on a subset, split into D2.
2. **Which condition governs the "facts" case?** Rebinding or more search at the same reach, not a new atom (Fig 5 :13, Fig 6 :27). Instrument condition from Fig 6 :32.
3. **Which condition governs the "values" case?** A different comparison term, i.e. a goal (Operators :51), owed by Fig 5 :14; closures differ (Fig 6 :23); the anchor is human judgment because the question is about people (Fig 2 :37).
4. **Is "social interest" a third source?** No. It is a collapse condition (Fig 2 :13-21) plus two absorbed sub-questions (D3a, D3b). Correction F4.
5. **Can the source be read from the stated reason?** No. Reasons are post hoc (Haidt 2001); describe the effect (Fig 9 :30-31).
6. **Is a new primitive needed?** No. Values are bonds over held atoms (Fig 6 :21, Fig 13 :115, Operators :51). The “shape, not a name” limit applies only to missing primitives.
7. **What closes the residual?** A source-discriminating instrument plus independent frames, not more argument (Fig 10 :24, Fig 2 :42). None was run here; status `candidate`.

### Updated verdict

The three-way question does not survive contact with the census. What survives is a **union of two anchored conditions plus one collapse condition**, with verdicts kept separate and never averaged: (D1) a factual-belief disagreement shrinks under effort against a non-updating anchor and is repaired by rebinding or search; (D2) a value disagreement does not shrink, each rule works on its own subset, the differing objective is a comparison term over shared atoms, and the legitimate anchor is human judgment; (D3) an "interest" disagreement is either a D1 fact about who benefits or a D2 choice about whose end prevails, and what remains distinctly social is a failure of independence that disqualifies the verdict until fixed. The per-case fraction is a declared `gap` of the unreached kind. Strains after re-census: none hidden; one correction and one narrowing preserved above.
