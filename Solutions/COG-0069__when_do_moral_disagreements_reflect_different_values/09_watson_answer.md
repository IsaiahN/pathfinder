# WATSON answer: when do moral disagreements reflect different values, facts, or interests?

**Mode: `specified` on the per-domain verdicts, `general` on the structural shape, not `grounded`.** Figures are primary and decide. World sources are secondary, corroborate but never overrule, and a conflict returns the derivation to the domain census. This is a simulated derivation checked for internal consistency against the figures, not a ground reading. No measurement discriminated the attributions in a described case.

## QUESTION

Decide, in effect terms, what the Tether says when two people disagree morally and we ask whether the source is different values, different factual beliefs, or different social interests. Option A: treat the three as a partition and assign the disagreement to one. Option B: treat them as a union of overlapping conditions with different anchors and keep the verdicts separate. What each leads to: A produces a single attribution with an assignable share, ignores the fact that one of the three has no anchor, and spends the independence of the people checking it; B produces a separator, a set of refuters written in advance, and an honest gap where no instrument discriminates. R is indexed per frame and per act, never averaged (Fig 1 :19).

## DOMAIN CENSUS

R by arity, symmetry and scale, then one line per domain with actors / currency / anchor, partition or union, and dependency order.

- **R (arity / symmetry / scale).** Three-place at minimum: frame A's judgment, frame B's judgment about the same act, and a source attribution. The judgment difference is symmetric (swapping operands changes nothing, Fig 12 :59), while the attribution is ordered: a fact constrains a goal, a goal does not constrain what happened (Greene 2003 is/ought). Scale: single frame, dyad or group, population or culture, with a lossy transform between (Fig 4 :27-30).
- **Domain 1, factual-belief condition.** Actors: the two frames' terms about the act and its consequences. Currency: residual reduction, with a mis-attached term re-fit. Anchor: a record, measurement or outcome that does not move when either argues (Fig 2 :29). Status: admitted.
- **Domain 2, value condition.** Actors: the frames' comparison terms, that is goals, thresholds, rights, harms. Currency: whether a different comparison term is owed and whether closures differ. Anchor: human judgment, because the question is constitutively about people (Fig 2 :36-37). Status: admitted.
- **Domain 3, "social interests."** Actors: weighting w, evidence pool, reference group, stake. Currency: agreement and independence. Anchor: none that is independent of the frames; a "fact" that moves with a group's stance is another frame (Fig 2 :30, :33-34). Status: **not a domain yet; absorbed** into D1 (who in fact benefits, record anchor) and D2 (whose end should prevail, human judgment anchor), with the residue a collapse condition of the checking arrangement.
- **Partition or union.** Union. D1 and D2 overlap: cultural commitments shape factual beliefs (Kahan 2013) and affective reactions predict moral judgment better than harm appraisal (Haidt, Koller & Dias 1993). *"A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones."* (Fig 9 :55)
- **Order by dependency.** Describe R per slot; test for a non-updating anchor; test shrinkage; if not shrinking, test subset structure and split; at every step test independence; only then plan (Fig 3 :20, Fig 9 :54).

Strain test applied: Fig 2 :37 makes human judgment the anchor for a question about people while Fig 2 :33 requires the anchor not to update. Re-census separates the recorded reading from live opinion and assigns the seat to tending the channel (Fig 10 :24). Different objects, so no contradiction. The first-pass entry of "social interests" as a third domain was retracted at census level; that correction is preserved in `01_figure_grounded_audit.md` F4 and §5.

## TETHER (primary, verbatim, per domain)

Quotes are verbatim from `FRAMEWORK/reference/03-FIGURES_TEXT.md`. Verdicts are kept per domain and never averaged.

**Domain 1, factual-belief condition. Status: triangulated (Fig 2 anchor rule, Fig 5 rebinding bin, Fig 9 split rule).**

- Fig 2 :29 "An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks. A measurement, an experiment, a counter, a market, a patient outcome." -> a nameable ground defines this condition.
- Fig 2 :30 "If it moves when you push on it, it is not an anchor." -> a stance-dependent "fact" is not a fact anchor.
- Fig 5 :13 "mis-attached: refit what you have, do not invent" -> the repair is rebinding, not a new primitive.
- Fig 2 :45 "Frames range against the ground. They do not negotiate with it." -> the diagnostic is whether R shrinks under ranging.

Reading: where the anchor exists and R shrinks, this is a factual-belief disagreement. Mode `specified`. Refuter: a valid non-updating anchor exists and R does not shrink.

**Domain 2, value condition. Status: triangulated (Fig 9 split rule, Fig 2 anchor legitimacy, Operators :51, Greene 2003 on the is/ought asymmetry).**

- Fig 9 :4-5 "are not one hard question but several well-formed ones. Split it rather than search." -> persistent, subset-structured disagreement is a union to be split.
- Operators :51 "a goal is a comparison, which is why a vocabulary without it cannot state one" -> a differing value is a comparison term, not a new atom.
- Fig 2 :37 "Human judgment is the right anchor for is this a good answer for a person, because the question is about people." -> the legitimate anchor for the ought branch is human judgment.
- Fig 2 :38 "instruments can be wrong on axes they do not measure." -> a scan or survey is an instrument for the is, not an anchor for the ought.
- Fig 6 :23 "No frame escapes its own closure. The bound moves only on contact with a frame whose closure differs." -> differing value closures require contact with a differing frame, not more internal argument.
- Fig 12 :117 "Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour." -> two record-ranged versus judgment-ranged disagreements with the same atoms are isomers.

Reading: mode `specified`. Refuter: no different objective can be found, the isomer behaviour test fails, or a value requires a new primitive.

**Domain 3, "social interests" as a collapse condition. Status: triangulated (Fig 2 collapse panels, Fig 11 valence, Fig 10 stake rule, Kahan 2013).**

- Fig 2 :20 "so the agreement is the pool talking," -> agreement from one evidence pool is not a verdict.
- Fig 2 :31 "Two people reading the same report are not two frames; two instruments pointed at the same thing are." -> independence is the quantity that decides.
- Fig 2 :40 "agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool" -> later agreement cannot be pooled as corroboration.
- Fig 11 :49 "An actor’s valence is relative to the residual, not a property it carries." -> an interest's sign flips with the moral concern.
- Fig 10 :60 "Where a stake cannot be removed, declare it. An undeclared interest is modelled anyway and an unnamed model runs unchallenged." -> declare the stake rather than assert the verdict.

Reading: the third term is not an anchored source. Mode `general` for the census correction, `specified` for its application. Refuter: an independently anchored social-interest source that is not a fact record or a value comparison.

**Union, order, and method. Status: triangulated (Fig 9, Fig 3, Fig 4).**

- Fig 9 :55 "A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones." -> no three-way partition, no per-case fraction from a pooled correlation.
- Fig 9 :57 "What remains is smaller and it is also categorically different: a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either." -> the intersection is a specific compound.
- Fig 3 :20 "The chain is ordered by dependency, not by importance." -> unbundle before search; fix the objective before planning.
- Fig 9 :60 "Use filters for the budget and witnesses for the verdict. Never let a filter hand you a verdict." -> effect sizes and popularity are filters, not witnesses.

**Naming the specific source in a case, or the per-case fraction. Status: gap.** No figure line governs this after a genuine re-census. Fig 9 :32 "you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself"; Fig 13 :134 "Unreachable is a verdict here, not a silence,"; Fig 8 :39 "So a search that finds nothing is never a proof of absence." The missing thing is a source-discriminating instrument and independent frames, characterized in effect terms but not held. This is `unreached`, not underivable in principle and not silence.

## WORLD (secondary, each source read)

World sources corroborate only; a conflict would send the derivation back to the census. None conflicted. Overlap is declared so a pool is not mistaken for two witnesses.

- **Moral psychology / judgement.** Jonathan Haidt, "The Emotional Dog and Its Rational Tail", Psychological Review 108(4), 2001. Opened at https://psycnet.apa.org/record/2001-18918-008. Quote: "moral reasoning is usually a post hoc construction, generated after a judgment has been reached." Prediction: a stated reason will not reliably reveal the source of a disagreement, so the separator must read behaviour. **Corroborates** the effect-before-cause rule (Fig 9 :30-31). Overlap: high with Haidt 1993 and the SEP entry (one pool).
- **Cross-cultural moral psychology.** Haidt, Koller and Dias, "Affect, Culture, and Morality, or Is It Wrong to Eat Your Dog?", 1993. Opened at the University of Vienna PDF. Quote: "moral judgments were better predicted by affective reactions than by appraisals of harmfulness." Prediction: the boundary of what counts as a value-driven versus harm-driven judgment moves with the frame's closure, and harm appraisal does not do the work participants say it does. **Corroborates** D2 and F5. Overlap: same pool as Haidt 2001.
- **Philosophy of cognitive science / developmental psychology.** Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy, "The Moral/Conventional Distinction", 2022 (ed. Zalta and Nodelman), summarizing Turiel 1977/1983, Shweder and Haidt. Opened in full. Quote: "social convention and morality should not be treated as part of the same conceptual and developmental package, but as distinct domains." Prediction: an empirical separator exists between authority-independent, generalizable moral judgments and authority-dependent, local conventional ones. **Corroborates** the D1/D2 anchor split and the collapse reading of the third term. Overlap: partly the same moral-psychology pool; counted as one witness with the two Haidt sources for that shape.
- **Neuroethics / philosophy of science.** Joshua Greene, "From neural 'is' to moral 'ought'", Nature Reviews Neuroscience 4, 2003. Opened abstract at https://www.nature.com/articles/nrn1224. Quote: "I agree with traditional ethicists that there is a sharp and crucial distinction between the 'is' of science and the 'ought' of ethics." Prediction: no neural or survey reading can settle which goal ought to govern; it can only inform the factual side. **Corroborates** D2 and Fig 2 :37-38. Overlap: low, different field.
- **Animal behaviour.** Frans de Waal, "Moral behavior in animals", TEDxPeachtree, November 2011. Opened at https://www.ted.com/talks/frans_de_waal_moral_behavior_in_animals. Quote from the page: "behavioral tests, on primates and other mammals, that show how many of these moral traits all of us share." Prediction: reciprocity, fairness and empathy are shared substrates, so naming a value is a binding onto held terms rather than the invention of a new primitive. **Corroborates** the F7 re-census. Overlap: low, genuinely independent field.
- **Decision science / law.** Dan M. Kahan, "Motivated reasoning & its cognates", Cultural Cognition Project, Yale Law School, 2013. Opened in full. Quote: "Cultural cognition refers to the tendency of individuals to conform their perceptions of risk and other policy-consequential facts to their cultural worldviews." Prediction: factual beliefs are shaped by value and group commitments, so D1 and D2 overlap and no fraction is assignable. **Corroborates** F1 (union) and F4 (collapse condition). - **Science communication.** Dan M. Kahan, "Why we are poles apart on climate change", Nature 488, 2012. Text returned by search integration, direct fetch not opened; recorded with reduced weight. Quote: "People with different values draw different inferences from the same evidence." Prediction: the same evidence yields different factual beliefs across value groups. **Corroborates** F1. Overlap: high with Kahan 2013; one witness, not two.
- **Framework essays.** Isaiah N, The Library of Babel, The Hard Problem is About Source, The Recursive Self-Improvement Trap, Decoding Tacit Knowledge, all opened directly at adventuresinml.substack.com on 2026-11-22. Governing quote from the second: "If several paths create the same behavior, then behavior is a projection with lossy information." Overlap: maximal with the figures, so no independent witness weight.

## VERDICT (from the figures)

The three-way question does not survive the census. What survives is a union of two anchored conditions plus one collapse condition, with verdicts kept separate and never averaged. Where a nameable non-updating anchor exists and the residual shrinks against it, the disagreement is about a factual belief and the term is re-fit rather than re-minted. Where it does not shrink and each rule works on a different subset, the question is split, the differing objective is a value comparison over shared terms, and the legitimate anchor is human judgment because the question is about people. "Social interests" decomposes into a fact question about who benefits and a value question about whose end prevails; the residue is a failure of independence that disqualifies agreement as a verdict until fixed. Stated reasons are post-hoc bindings and are not sources. A value is a comparison, not a seventh primitive. The per-case fraction is a declared `gap` of the unreached kind. Mode `specified`, ungrounded, candidate.

- **Cost if wrong:** misdirected diagnosis. A value disagreement treated as a fact dispute gets more data and never shrinks; a fact dispute treated as a value clash gets relativised when a record would have settled it; and an interest effect treated as a third source gets a standing list of "biased actors" that fails when the residual changes (Fig 11 :51). In outreach terms, an expert's twenty minutes would be spent refuting the wrong separator. The cost is real but bounded and reversible because the refuters and lineage are recorded.
- **Undoable:** yes. A refutation adds a term that refuses and removes nothing, so the reach falls because *"something was added rather than because anything was deleted"* (Fig 6 :42-43). The correction that absorbed Domain 3 and the narrowing of "shape, not a name" are both visible in `01_figure_grounded_audit.md` §5, so a later reader can locate the error rather than only feel it (Fig 10 :62-64).
- **Reversed by:** a ground reading, not agreement. Any of the six pre-registered refuters in `01` §4 would reverse the relevant branch: a persistent disagreement with no findable objective; a non-shrinking R against a valid anchor; two disjoint-evidence frames whose "fact" still tracks one group's stance; a value requiring a new primitive; human judgment shown illegitimate for a question about people; or the demonstration that no discriminator and no independent frame could ever be brought to bear, which would turn the `gap` from unreached into underivable. Prestige, citation counts and pooled agreement are not such instruments (Fig 9 :60).
