# Outreach draft - check the separator before you decide about the framework

**Use:** one-to-one, or as a cover letter attached to a submission through the public venues in `03_public_contact_points.csv`. **Not** for bulk send.

**Hard precondition:** do not send until the separator in `01_figure_grounded_audit.md` F1-F4 is intact and the refuter below is attached. The offer collapses on contact if the stage-1 output cannot survive.

---

**Subject:** Check one separator before you decide whether the theory is worth reading

> Hi [name],
>
> I have one specific output I would like you to check against what you already know. I am not asking you to read the theory first, and I am not asking you to agree with it. If the output does not survive, there is nothing else to look at.
>
> **The question, restated as an effect.** Two people disagree about an act. The residual is the gap between their judgments, tracked per person and per act, never averaged: R_s = |Γ(b,a) − o′|. The question asks which term is doing the work: a different factual belief, a different value, or a social interest.
>
> **The output (`specified`, not `grounded`).** The three answers are not a three-way partition. Two of them have a nameable anchor and one does not, so the right object is a union of two anchored conditions plus a verification failure:
>
> 1. **Factual-belief condition.** If there is something that settles the contested point and does not change because either person argues (a record, a measurement, an outcome), and the disagreement shrinks when both range against it, then a term is mis-attached and should be re-fit, not re-minted. The framework's own test is: if it moves when you push on it, it is not an anchor.
> 2. **Value condition.** If the disagreement does not shrink with effort and each person's rule keeps working well on a different subset, it is not one hard question but several well-formed ones. Split it. The differing objective is a comparison term, a goal, over terms both sides already hold. Because the question is constitutively about people, the legitimate anchor here is human judgment, not an external instrument.
> 3. **Interest condition.** This is not a third source with its own ground. "Who in fact benefits" is a factual question with a record; "whose end should prevail" is a value question. What remains distinct is a failure of independence: two people drawing on one evidence pool, or updating on each other, so their agreement is the pool talking and cannot count as a verdict.
>
> What I am **not** claiming: that any particular value is the right one, that a brain scan or a survey of consequences can settle the "ought," that a stated reason reveals its own source, or that anyone's agreement settles the attribution. Reasons are written after the judgment; the separator reads the behaviour of the disagreement instead.
>
> **What would refute it:** (1) a persistent disagreement in which no different objective or comparison can be found in either frame; (2) a named non-updating anchor that both range against, with the disagreement still not shrinking; (3) two frames on genuinely disjoint evidence whose "fact" still tracks one group's stance; (4) a moral value that requires a genuinely new primitive under the framework's six-term substrate; (5) a case where no record, instrument or independent frame could discriminate the three attributions. I wrote these down before sending this. A one-line "this fails because..." is more useful to me than agreement. If you would rather not engage, no reply is needed.
>
> **If it survives, and only then:** the sources are below. The figures are the authority, not my summary of them.
> - Figures and formula (local canonical files): `FRAMEWORK/reference/03-FIGURES_TEXT.md`, `FRAMEWORK/figures/THE_FORMULA.md`.
> - The canonical usage rule: `FRAMEWORK/reference/FIGURES_README_canonical.md`.
> - The checking arrangement: `FRAMEWORK/figures/THE_SEAT_MAP_Field_Guide_amended.md`.
>
> **If you want to use the machinery:** the formula is in the figures link. Run it on a disagreement you know well and can check against a ground. Write down what would refute your result *before* you run it: the framework's own rule is that a loop cannot check the direction of its own question. A reported failure is more useful to me than a reported success.
>
> - [author name]

## Two-stage offer, stated explicitly

1. **Stage 1:** the residual, the separator and the refuter. Ask what the recipient would count as a failure. Record that verdict **before** showing any theory.
2. **Stage 2, opt-in only:** if they are curious, offer the four links. The figures, not this summary, are the authority. Their later agreement is never pooled as corroboration (Fig 2 :40).
3. **Stage 3:** offer the formula as a method to reuse, with the instruction that each user writes their own refuter and states their own mode.
