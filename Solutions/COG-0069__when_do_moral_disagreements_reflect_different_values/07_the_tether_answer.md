# The Tether's answer: values, facts, and interests in moral disagreement

**Mode: `specified` on the per-domain verdicts, `general` on the structural shape, not `grounded`.** This is a shape and a separator, not a result. Simulated derivation checked for internal consistency against the figures (`05_what_shape_means.md` §6), not a ground reading.

## 1. The residual it answers

Two frames disagree about an act. The residual is the gap between their judgments, indexed per frame and per act, never averaged: R_s = |Γ(b,a) − o′| (Fig 1 :19, :22). The question asks which term is doing the work: a different value, a different factual belief, or a social interest. The ordinary question hides a union: the three candidates do not have the same anchor, and one of them has none (audit F1, F4).

## 2. The shape

1. **The question must be split before it is answered.** Fig 9 :55: *"A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones."* Fig 9 :57: *"a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either."* A single lever clears one region and leaves the intersections standing. There is no three-way partition and no percentage to report.
2. **First ask whether there is a fact here.** Fig 2 :29: *"An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks."* If the contested point has a record, measurement or outcome that does not move when either argues, and the residual shrinks when both range against it, the condition is a **factual-belief** disagreement. The repair is rebinding: *"mis-attached: refit what you have, do not invent"* (Fig 5 :13), or more search at the same reach (Fig 6 :27). Not a new primitive.
3. **If the residual does not shrink, split on the objective.** Fig 9 :4-5: disagreements that *"do not shrink with effort, where each rule keeps working well on a different subset, are not one hard question but several well-formed ones. Split it rather than search."* The differing objective is a **value**: a goal, and *"a goal is a comparison, which is why a vocabulary without it cannot state one"* (Operators :51). Two frames over the same atoms ranged against different things are isomers (Fig 12 :117): same atoms, different bond, different behaviour.
4. **The anchor for the value condition is human judgment, not an instrument.** Fig 2 :36-37: *"An anchor is legitimate when the question is constitutively about it"* and *"Human judgment is the right anchor for is this a good answer for a person, because the question is about people."* An instrument for "is" *"can be wrong on axes they do not measure"* (Fig 2 :38). Greene 2003 states the same asymmetry in the world's terms. So a brain scan or a consequence survey cannot settle which goal ought to govern; it can only inform the "is" side.
5. **"Social interest" is not a third source.** It is either a D1 question about who in fact benefits (record anchor) or a D2 question about whose end should prevail (human judgment anchor). The residue that is distinctly social is a failure of independence: *"so the agreement is the pool talking"* (Fig 2 :20); *"Two people reading the same report are not two frames; two instruments pointed at the same thing are."* (Fig 2 :31). Until the frames are independent, their agreement is disqualifying as a verdict (Fig 2 :40). *"Buy independence in proportion to the cost of being wrong."* (Fig 2 :42).
6. **The stated reason is not the source.** Haidt 2001: reasoning is *"usually a post hoc construction, generated after a judgment has been reached."* Describe the effect, not the cause (Fig 9 :30-31). Ask what each side would expect to see if its attribution were true, before showing any theory.
7. **A value is a binding, not a seventh primitive.** Fig 6 :21: *"MINT composes inside the closure and can never add an atom."* Naming harm, fairness, purity or loyalty is a provenanced binding from the moral-psychology frame onto held terms, not a derivation. The "shape, not a name" limit governs *missing* primitives (Fig 13 :134-135). de Waal 2011 corroborates that the underlying terms are shared across species.

## 3. What it explicitly does not say

- Not a three-way partition, not a universal formula, and not a ratio of values to facts to interests.
- Not that any particular value is correct, or that a survey, a scan or a majority settles the "ought."
- Not that a disagreement is "really" about interests whenever it is inconvenient. An interest's valence is relative to the residual (Fig 11 :49-51), and no standing list of self-interested arguments survives a change of the moral concern (Fig 11 :51). A stake must be declared (Fig 10 :60), not used as a verdict.
- Not that reasons are irrelevant to deliberation. They are relevant to reaching a shared meaning, where agreement is the product (Fig 2 :41). They are not evidence of the source.
- Not `grounded`. No instrument discriminated the attributions in a described case.

## 4. What would settle it - the ground

A source-discriminating instrument plus independent frames, not a further argument. *The Hard Problem is About Source*: a reading of green *"cannot comment on the source"*; *"If several paths create the same behavior, then behavior is a projection with lossy information"*; *"The answer is unreached until a better instrument exists."* Concretely:

- **Shrinkage test:** a record is named for the contested quantity and both frames range against it; R either does or does not fall. This settles the D1 branch (F2 refuter).
- **Subset test:** each rule is shown to keep working on a different subset; the disagreement splits and the two objectives are stated. This settles the D2 branch (F3 refuter).
- **Independence test:** two instruments or raters with genuinely disjoint evidence are shown to agree or disagree; if the "fact" tracks one group's stance, the anchor fails (F4 refuter).
- **Discriminator:** an instrument that distinguishes a value comparison from a factual belief from an interest-driven reading of the same verdict. None was run here.
- **Standing falsifiers:** the six pre-registered conditions in `01_figure_grounded_audit.md` §4, especially a persistent disagreement with no findable objective, a non-shrinking R against a valid anchor, a value requiring a new primitive, or the absence of any possible discriminator (the reachability condition).

## 5. The honest stop

**Unreached, not unreachable.** Fig 13 :134: *"Unreachable is a verdict here, not a silence,"* and Fig 8 :39: *"So a search that finds nothing is never a proof of absence."* No figure line assigns the per-case fraction or names the specific operative source. The stop is recorded with its lineage: the census, the re-census that absorbed the third domain, the correction that narrowed "shape, not a name," and the six refuters written before the conclusion. *"Indiscernibility is not identity."* Another field's minimum set need not be these six; a culture's value vocabulary is a visible guess until the ground pays for it.

## 6. One-paragraph version for outreach

> The three answers to "is this disagreement about values, facts, or interests" are not three values of one variable. Two of them have an anchor and one does not. Where something settles the contested point without moving when either person argues, and the disagreement shrinks against it, you have a factual-belief dispute and the fix is to re-fit the mis-attached belief. Where the disagreement will not shrink and each side's rule keeps working on a different subset, it is several well-formed questions, not one hard one; split it, and the differing objective is a value, a comparison term over things both sides already hold. Because the question is about people, the legitimate anchor for that branch is human judgment, not a brain scan or a survey. "Social interest" is not a third source: who benefits is a fact question, whose end should prevail is a value question, and what remains is a failure of independence, where two people sharing one evidence pool agree and call it a verdict. Stated reasons are written after the judgment, so the separator reads the behaviour of the disagreement instead. What it offers is the shape any attribution must have, refuters written in advance, and the discriminator still owed. The per-case fraction stays open until an instrument and independent frames are actually brought to bear.
