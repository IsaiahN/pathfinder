# Expert verification packet - the Tether on moral disagreement

**What this is:** a separator in a form a moral psychologist or decision scientist can check. Mode, derivation and refuters are stated before the conclusion so a reader knows what to look for and what would settle it.

**Mode: `specified` on the sub-claims, `general` on the structure, not `grounded`.** No measurement discriminated the three attributions in a described case. This is a shape and a route, not a result. Simulated derivation checked against the figures, not a ground reading.

## 1. The claim, in one paragraph

A moral disagreement is not the sort of thing that can be assigned to values, facts or interests from its content. The three candidates have different anchors. Where a non-updating record exists and the residual shrinks against it, the dispute is about a factual belief and the repair is rebinding. Where the residual does not shrink and each rule works on its own subset, the dispute is several well-formed questions: split it, and the differing objective is a value, a comparison term over shared atoms, with human judgment as the legitimate anchor because the question is about people. "Social interest" is not a third source; it is either a fact about who benefits or a value about whose end prevails, plus a failure of independence when the two frames share an evidence pool. The per-case fraction is unreached, not silence.

## 2. Three checkable sub-claims

### C1 - framework-internal; checkable by reading Figures 2, 5 and 9

- **Statement:** the anchor test separates the factual-belief condition. If the contested quantity has a ground that does not move when either frame argues, and R shrinks when both range against it, the term is mis-attached and should be re-fit, not re-minted.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 2 :29 - *"An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks. A measurement, an experiment, a counter, a market, a patient outcome."* Fig 2 :30 - *"If it moves when you push on it, it is not an anchor."* Fig 5 :13 - *"mis-attached: refit what you have, do not invent"*. Fig 2 :45 - *"Frames range against the ground. They do not negotiate with it."*
- **What to check:** can a ground be named for the contested quantity that would not change if the two parties argued differently? Does the disagreement actually shrink when both are corrected by it?
- **Refuter:** a named non-updating anchor exists, both frames range against it, and R does not shrink. This breaks the D1 branch; the claim is withdrawn.

### C2 - framework-internal plus a field check; checkable by reading Figure 9 and the Operators table

- **Statement:** a disagreement that does not shrink with effort, where each rule keeps working on a different subset, is not one hard question but several; split it, and the differing objective is a comparison term (a goal) over terms both frames hold. Two such frames with the same atoms ranged against different anchors are isomers under Fig 12 :117 - *"Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."*
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 9 :4-5 - *"are not one hard question but several well-formed ones. Split it rather than search."* Operators :51 - *"a goal is a comparison, which is why a vocabulary without it cannot state one"*. Fig 2 :37 - *"Human judgment is the right anchor for is this a good answer for a person, because the question is about people."* Fig 2 :38 - *"instruments can be wrong on axes they do not measure."* Greene 2003, Nature Reviews Neuroscience: *"there is a sharp and crucial distinction between the 'is' of science and the 'ought' of ethics."*
- **What to check:** in a case you know, can each side's rule be shown to work on a different subset? Can the two objectives be stated as comparisons? Is there any reason to think a neural or survey reading settles which objective ought to govern?
- **Refuter:** no different objective or comparison can be found in either frame, or two disagreements with the same atoms and different stated bonds show identical residual behaviour under effort (breaks the isomer claim of `06_isomers_and_order.md` Q1b), or a moral value requires a genuinely new primitive (breaks Fig 6 :21 and Fig 13 :115).

### C3 - verification-frame claim; checkable from Figure 2 and Figure 11

- **Statement:** "social interest" is not a third anchored source. It decomposes into a fact question (who benefits, record anchor) and a value question (whose end prevails, human judgment anchor). What is left is a failure of independence: agreement from a shared evidence pool is the pool talking, not a verdict.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 2 :20 - *"so the agreement is the pool talking,"* Fig 2 :31 - *"Two people reading the same report are not two frames; two instruments pointed at the same thing are."* Fig 2 :40 - *"agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool"*. Fig 11 :49 - *"An actor’s valence is relative to the residual, not a property it carries."* Fig 10 :60 - *"Where a stake cannot be removed, declare it. An undeclared interest is modelled anyway and an unnamed model runs unchallenged."*
- **What to check:** in the literature you know, are "self-interest" effects better read as a record of consequences, as a competing goal, or as a shared-pool artefact? Does the valence of a given interest flip when the moral concern changes?
- **Refuter:** a social-interest condition is found with its own non-updating anchor that is not reducible to a consequence record or a value comparison; or two frames on genuinely disjoint evidence still produce a "fact" that tracks one group's stance (breaks the anchor rule).

## 3. What is explicitly not claimed

- Not a three-way partition, not a universal formula, not a ratio or percentage of values versus facts versus interests.
- Not that any particular value is correct, or that a scan, survey, majority or prestige settles the "ought."
- Not that reasons reveal their own source; they are post-hoc bindings (Haidt 2001), so the separator reads behaviour instead.
- Not that "interest" is a standing property of a person or group. Valence is relative to the residual (Fig 11 :49-51).
- Not `grounded`, and not a claim that the framework's figures are true. This is a checkable shape, not a settled attribution.
- Not a claim that the per-case fraction can be computed from a pooled correlation; that is a filter for the budget, not a witness for the verdict (Fig 9 :60).

## 5. What would settle it

A source-discriminating instrument plus independent frames, not a further argument. From *The Hard Problem is About Source*: a reading of green *"cannot comment on the source"*; *"If several paths create the same behavior, then behavior is a projection with lossy information"*; the answer stays *"unreached until a better instrument exists."*

1. **Shrinkage test:** name the record for a contested quantity; R falls or does not when both range against it.
2. **Subset test:** show each rule working on a different subset; split and state the two objectives.
3. **Independence test:** use two instruments or raters with disjoint evidence; see whether the "fact" tracks group stance.
4. **Discriminator:** an instrument that separates a value comparison from a factual belief from an interest-driven reading of the same verdict. None was run.

## 6. What an expert can check in twenty minutes

1. Read Fig 2 :29-31, :37-40 and Fig 9 :4-5, :54-57. Six short passages.
2. Check C2 against a case you know: can the two objectives be stated, and does each rule work on a different subset?
3. Name one instrument that would distinguish a value comparison from a factual belief in that case, or say why none is possible.
4. Send the refutation. A failure is more useful than an endorsement.
