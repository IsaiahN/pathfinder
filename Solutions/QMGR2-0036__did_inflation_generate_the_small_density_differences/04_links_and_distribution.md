## Three findings in the wider literature that bear on this question

1. **The inverse-problem framing.** Baumann's TASI lectures name the move from CMB fluctuations back to inflationary-era information an *"inverse problem"*. That is the source-attribution residual in the physics literature's own words. It supports treating "which source" as a separate loop with its own ground, not as a footnote to "what pattern".
2. **Pattern consistency is not source identity.** The Planck papers report consistency with adiabatic, near scale-invariant perturbations, and the Wikipedia inflation entry reports both that the particle physics mechanism is unknown and that some scientists dissent. So even in the world literature, the reading is far more settled than the writer.
3. **Low-overlap corroboration for the shape limit.** The philosophy source (contrastive underdetermination) and the colour-science source (metamerism) are from fields outside cosmology. Both say that a finite reading underdetermines its source. They corroborate the figure verdict but are secondary: they do not decide it, and they are not the same as a figure line.

Overlap warning: Baumann, both Planck papers and the two Wikipedia cosmology pages all draw on the same CMB and Planck evidence pool. Their agreement is one witness restated several ways, not independent corroboration. The SEP and metamerism sources are the genuinely low-overlap ones.
