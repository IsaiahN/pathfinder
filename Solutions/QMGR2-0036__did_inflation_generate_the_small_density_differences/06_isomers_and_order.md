## DERIVATION

### Q1 — Isomers or functional-group variants?

Fig 12 :117: *"isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."*

Fig 12 :124: *"functional group. A substructure whose presence fixes the outcome. The group says what kind; the qualifier says which member."*

Fig 13 :121: *"Different routes can arrive at the same substance."*

- As written in the literature, single-field slow-roll inflation and, say, an ekpyrotic or curvaton alternative share the observed spectral atoms (scalar index, adiabaticity, Gaussianity bound) but differ in the route that produced them. Under Fig 13 :121 they are different routes arriving at the same measured substance.
- If we hold the **full** atom set fixed (spectral index, amplitude, isocurvature fraction, tensor ratio, non-Gaussianity) and differ only in which mechanism occupies the production slot, they satisfy Fig 12's isomer definition: same atoms, different bond, different underlying substance, with the difference invisible in the spectral formula and decisive in the early-universe history.
- If we hold only the **minimal** shared set (the observed spectrum), they share a functional group, *"the group says what kind; the qualifier says which member"*: both are scale-invariant-seed producers, qualified by different mechanisms.
- Fig 12 :104: *"A settled molecule becomes an atom for whatever composes over it."* The settled perturbation spectrum becomes an atom for structure formation. That is why the downstream galaxy pattern is the same regardless of which writer produced the seed: the demotion to atom discards the route.

**Falsifier for Q1:** if an alternative is shown to require an observational atom that inflation cannot produce, or vice versa, the isomer claim fails and the two are different substances already at first order, not different routes to one substance.

### Q2 — What order does the inference run in?

Fig 3 :20-21: *"The chain is ordered by dependency, not by importance. Each link consumes what the one above produces, so effort spent above the break arrives at a step that cannot use it."*

1. **Perception:** measure the perturbation pattern. This link works; several instruments return consistent spectra.
2. **Vocabulary:** have terms for what a source could afford (inflaton potential, bounce, defect network, curvaton). This link also works; the model literature is large.
3. **Objective:** say what would count as progress, in our own terms, namely a discriminator whose value differs across candidate writers. This is the usual break. Fig 3 :27: *"A link with no instrument is not a link that works. It is a link nobody has looked at."*
4. **Planning and carry:** choose the observable and run it, then name what worked and carry it.

Effort spent refining the pattern (link 1) cannot rescue a missing discriminator (link 3). This is why the honest answer stops at a shape and an owed instrument.

### Q3 — Analogue: metamerism as a cross-field check

The Hard Problem is About Source (read 2026-11-23) gives the analogue directly: an instrument reads green but *"cannot comment on the source"*, because several underlying mixtures give the same reading. The Wikipedia metamerism entry (read) confirms this is a standard colour-science fact: *"metamerism is a perceived matching of colors with different (nonmatching) spectral power distributions."*

The analogy is structural, not physical. CMB temperature and polarization are the tristimulus reading; the candidate early-universe mechanisms are the spectral power distributions. A better instrument (here, a tensor or non-Gaussianity or isocurvature discriminator) is what separates them. Fig 6 :32: *"An instrument is not built from a description. It is improved from a worse instrument already returning something."* We already have the scalar index and the bounds; the discriminator is an improvement on those, not a device specified from nothing.

### Q4 — Round trip from spectral shape to source name

Fig 4 :30: *"So take the round trip and measure the gap. Without it you are navigating by dead reckoning: confident about a position nobody checked."*

- Sent up: the measured spectral constraints (abstraction).
- Brought down: the claim "inflation generated the perturbations" (concretisation).
- The gap `R_T` contains at least the route, the tensor sector and the non-Gaussianity information that the coarse spectral summary discards. Treating the summary as if it named the source sets `R_T` to zero and is the dead-reckoning error. The honest report is a lossy condensation with a nonzero, enumerated gap.

## FALSIFIER (written before the conclusion)

The convergence claim is refuted if either:

1. a candidate source is shown to be unable to compose the observed spectrum, so it drops out of the isomer set and the route is no longer many-to-one; or
2. an observable is found and measured that separates the candidates at the ground, closing `R_T` to zero on the source question.

If either fires, `07` and `09` must be corrected to name the surviving writer.

## CONCLUSION

Inflation and its alternatives are best read as **different routes to one measured substance**, with a genuine isomer claim when the full observational atom set is held fixed and a weaker functional-group claim when only the minimal spectrum is shared. The dependency order breaks at the objective link: we have the pattern and the vocabulary, but not yet the discriminator. The metamerism analogue from a low-overlap field corroborates the shape limit, and the round-trip gap `R_T` records what is lost when a spectral summary is mistaken for a source name.
