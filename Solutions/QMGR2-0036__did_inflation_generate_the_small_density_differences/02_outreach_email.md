# Outreach draft: check the output first, then decide about the source claim

**Use:** one-to-one, or as a cover letter attached to a submission through the public venues in `03_public_contact_points.csv`. **Not** for bulk send.

**Hard precondition:** do not send until the refuter in `01_figure_grounded_audit.md` §4 is stated first in your own words and you are willing to have it fired. The offer collapses on contact if the output does not survive.

---

**Subject:** One checkable claim about primordial perturbations, before any theory

> Hi [name],
>
> I have one specific output I would like you to check against what you already know. I am not asking you to read a framework first, and I am not asking you to agree with anything. If the output does not survive, there is nothing else to look at.
>
> **The output (`specified`, not `grounded`).** The primordial density perturbations are a settled reading: nearly scale invariant, adiabatic, nearly Gaussian, with a measured scalar index and bounds on the tensor-to-scalar ratio and non-Gaussianity. The question "did inflation generate them" is not a question about that pattern. It is a question about the **source** of the pattern. The structural claim is that a reading does not recover the route that produced it. Different routes can arrive at the same substance, and what separates them is the record of how each was reached, not the pattern itself. So the honest deliverable is a shape: the constraints any writer must satisfy, plus a source-discriminating observable that is still owed. It is not the name "inflation".
>
> **What I am explicitly not claiming:** that the CMB pattern uniquely identifies inflation; that a failure to discriminate so far proves no discriminator exists; or that this is a `grounded` result. Several independent instruments agree on the pattern, but they draw on overlapping evidence pools, and agreement among frames is not an anchor.
>
> **What would refute the output, written before this message:**
> 1. A source-discriminating observable is measured that uniquely selects inflation, or uniquely excludes it. The slot can then be named.
> 2. It is shown that only one candidate source can compose the observed statistic, so different routes cannot arrive at the same substance here.
> 3. A measured statistic falls outside the inflationary prediction space, so the inflation binding is a term whose residual persists and does not trend down.
> 4. A reading is shown to recover its producing route deterministically.
>
> I wrote these down before contacting you. If any one holds, I retract the shape claim. A one-line "this fails because..." is more useful to me than agreement. If you would rather not engage, no reply is needed.
>
> **What I would like from you:** what would you count as a failure of this claim? And is there a discriminator you would expect to see if it were true? I am asking for your verdict on that, not for endorsement.
>
> **If it survives, and only then:** the sources are below. The figures are the authority, not my summary of them.
> - Canonical figure text with line numbers (local extract): `FRAMEWORK/reference/03-FIGURES_TEXT.md`, cited as `Fig N :line`.
> - Baumann, "TASI Lectures on Inflation", arXiv:0907.5424, especially the passage on the inverse problem of extracting information on the inflationary era from CMB fluctuations: https://arxiv.org/abs/0907.5424
> - Planck Collaboration, "Planck 2015 results. XX. Constraints on inflation", arXiv:1502.02114: https://arxiv.org/abs/1502.02114
>
> **If you want to use the machinery:** the formula is in the figures link. Run it on a problem you know well and can check against a ground. Write down what would refute your result *before* you run it. A reported failure is more useful to me than a reported success.
>
> Thanks,
> [name]
