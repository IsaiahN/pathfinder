# Expert verification packet: inflation and the primordial density differences

**What this is:** a claim in a form a cosmologist can check. Mode, derivation and refuters are stated before the conclusion, so a reader knows what to look for and what would settle it.

**Mode: `specified` on the structural claims, `general` on the shape limit. Not `grounded`.** No new measurement is claimed. This is a shape and a route, not a result.

## 1. The claim, in one paragraph

The primordial density perturbation pattern is settled by instruments (near scale-invariant, adiabatic, nearly Gaussian, measured amplitude, bounded tensor and non-Gaussianity sectors). The question of which process wrote that pattern is a separate, source-attribution question. A reading does not recover its producing route: different routes can arrive at the same substance, and only the record separates them. Inflation is the leading candidate binding in the physics literature, recorded as an import with provenance, not as a figure derivation and not as a `grounded` verdict. What the framework returns is a shape (the constraints any writer must satisfy) plus an owed discriminator. A source-discriminating measurement, not further agreement about the pattern, would name the writer.

## 2. Three checkable sub-claims

### C1 — framework-internal; checkable by reading Figure 13

- **Statement:** a composed term's reading does not encode which route produced it; the lineage is what carries that information.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 13 :121 — *"Different routes can arrive at the same substance."* Fig 13 :128 — *"Nothing about a composed term tells you, from looking at it, which route produced it or whether that route was available."* Fig 13 :129 — *"It is recoverable from the lineage."*
- **Refuter:** exhibit a reading from which the producing route is deterministically recoverable, or show that exactly one candidate source can compose the observed statistic.

### C2 — physics-facing; checkable against the inflation and alternatives literature

- **Statement:** single-field slow-roll inflation and its main alternatives can each be written so as to reproduce the same observed spectral atoms (scalar index, adiabaticity, Gaussianity bound, amplitude). They are therefore different routes to one measured substance, or isomers when the full atom set is held fixed. This is a structural claim about overlap, not a probability ranking.
- **What to check:** is that faithful to the literature? Baumann's TASI lectures describe inflation as transforming *"microscopic quantum fluctuations into macroscopic seeds for cosmological structure formation"* and call the reverse move *"the inverse problem of extracting information on the inflationary era from observations of cosmic microwave background fluctuations."* Planck 2015 and 2018 report consistency with adiabatic, near scale-invariant perturbations. Do the alternative models quoted in the sources reproduce those same atoms?
- **Refuter:** show that a named alternative cannot compose the observed spectrum, or that a measured statistic falls outside the inflationary prediction space, so the inflation binding is refused.

### C3 — framework-internal derivation; checkable by reading Figures 6, 3 and 9

- **Statement:** what is owed is a source-discriminating instrument, and the inference must describe the residual before choosing a frame.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 6 :32 — *"An instrument is not built from a description. It is improved from a worse instrument already returning something."* Fig 3 :27 — *"A link with no instrument is not a link that works. It is a link nobody has looked at."* Fig 9 :27-28 — *"describe it before you go looking, or any frame you pick will seem to fit"*
- **What to check:** are the scalar index and the bounds on r, f_NL and isocurvature genuinely the "worse instruments already returning something"? A specialist can say whether any of them already separates the candidates, in which case the owed instrument is already in hand and the claim must be updated.
- **Refuter:** specify a discriminator purely from the pattern, build and run it without any prior instrument returning a signal, and close the source question.

## 3. What is explicitly not claimed

- Not `grounded`. Nothing here is asserted as settled by a new measurement.
- Not "inflation definitely did it" and not "inflation did not do it".
- Not that the perturbation pattern is in doubt. The pattern is settled; the source is not.
- Not that alternatives are equally probable. Probability ranking belongs to the physics frame.
- Not a derivation of inflation from the six-term substrate. Inflation is an import from physics, provenanced as such.
- Not a claim that no discriminator can exist. The stop is unreached, not unreachable (Fig 8 :39, *"So a search that finds nothing is never a proof of absence."*).

## 5. What would settle it

A source-discriminating measurement, not a further argument. The Hard Problem is About Source (read) reports that the spectrometer, not a debate, resolved the analogous case: *"the answer is unreached until a better instrument exists."* Concretely:

1. A tensor signal at a specified r, or a specified non-Gaussianity shape, or a primordial isocurvature component, measured yes or no.
2. A demonstration that only one candidate source can compose the full observed atom set.
3. A measured statistic outside the inflationary prediction space, which refuses the inflation term.

## 6. What an expert can check in twenty minutes

1. Read Fig 13 :121 and :128, Fig 6 :32, Fig 9 :27-28, Fig 8 :39. Five short passages.
2. Check C2 against your own knowledge: do inflation and at least one alternative reproduce the same observed spectral atoms? Name one that does not.
3. Name one observable that would separate the candidates, or say why the current bounds already do.
4. Send the refutation. A failure is more useful than an endorsement.
