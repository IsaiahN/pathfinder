## Independent domain census

I redid the census from the residual line in effect terms, using the WATSON order: R by arity, symmetry and scale; one line per domain with actors, currency and anchor; partition or union; order by dependency.

- **R (arity / symmetry / scale).** The observed variance in whom an accountability body sanctions. Arity: two target slots per body (insiders, opponents) plus the ground (misconduct). Symmetry: asymmetric, because the coalition controls appointment, budget and removal and the opponent does not. Valence is relative to this residual (Fig 11 :49). Scale: meso, the institution as checker. Question type: a union compound, because under-enforcement of insiders and over-enforcement of opponents co-occur and intersect. Fig 9 :57: "a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either."
- **Domain 1, checking seat.** Actors: checker, coalition, ground. Currency: verdicts. Anchor: misconduct facts and law, which must not update on coalition preference. Partition: which configurations keep the checker outside the slice it grades.
- **Domain 2, constitution of the checker.** Actors: selector, pool, occupants. Currency: selection, removal, budget, information access. Anchor: an unauthored pool. Partition: authored room versus unauthored pool.
- **Domain 3, habitat.** Actors: coalition, opponents, donors, other oversight bodies, civil society. Currency: power and standing. Anchor: none of its own; it updates and feeds actors into D1 and D2. Enumeration, not composition.
- **Order.** Ground reachable, then seat outside the slice, then unauthored membership, then habitat enumeration. Fig 3 :20: "The chain is ordered by dependency, not by importance."

I reached the same three domains and the same union reading as the primary, but by a different route: I started from Fig 9's union/partition split and Fig 12's isomer definition, whereas the primary started from Fig 2's anchor theory and Fig 10's seat theory. Low overlap between routes within a shared framework, so the agreement is weak but not mechanically circular. It is still one evidence pool.

## Round trip gap R_T

The Symbols table defines R_T as `gap(x, (T_E ∘ T_A)(x))`, and Fig 4 :30 requires it to be measured rather than assumed. I ran the round trip on the condensation this folder actually uses.

1. **Sent up (x):** the verbatim figure set, chiefly Fig 2 :33 and :45 (anchor must not update; range against the ground), Fig 10 :86 and :73-74 (outside the slice; unauthored pool), Fig 9 :55-57 (union), Fig 12 :117 (isomer).
2. **Abstraction (T_A):** the shorthand in `07` and `08`: an independent accountability body constrains power when the seat is outside the slice, the ground is reachable, the stake is not in the reading, membership is unauthored, and the two outcomes are a union compound.
3. **Concretisation (T_E):** the field claim a reader would actually act on, for example "create an independent anti-corruption commission with a protected budget and it will constrain the coalition."

**R_T is nonzero.** Four things the shorthand holds do not survive the trip back down:

- independence is **positional**, not attitudinal; a protected budget without removal protection leaves the seat inside the slice;
- the residual is **per slot**; an overall drop in corruption can conceal increased targeting of opponents;
- the union compound needs **two loops and a named intersection**, not one lever;
- refresh requires a frame with a **different closure**; adding a second lookalike body is a mirror chain.

One thing survives cleanly: a checker cannot own the reading of the slice it grades, and the ground must not move when the party checked pushes on it. That is the residue worth sending. R_T was **not** measured numerically, because no ground reading exists; it is recorded qualitatively as the enumerated set of dropped constraints. Treating "the formula is the condensed version of the metalaws" as identity would set R_T to zero and commit the dead-reckoning error Fig 4 :30 names.
