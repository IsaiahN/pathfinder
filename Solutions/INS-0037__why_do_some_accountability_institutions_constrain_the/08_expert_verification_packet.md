# Expert verification packet: the Tether on accountability institutions

**What this is:** a claim in a form a researcher of accountability institutions can check. Mode, derivation and refuters are stated before the conclusion, so a reader knows what to look for and what would settle it.

**Mode: `specified`, not `grounded`.** No dataset or natural experiment is claimed. This is a shape and a route, not a result.

**Status: simulated derivation checked for internal consistency against the figures, not a ground reading.** It cannot settle the claim it reports on.

## 1. The claim, in one paragraph

An accountability body constrains the ruling coalition when it occupies a checking seat outside the slice it grades, ranges against misconduct and law that do not move when the coalition pushes, gains nothing from which reading the ground returns, and draws its occupants from a pool the party it checks did not author. It mainly polices opponents when those conditions are inverted. Insider impunity and opponent targeting are two conditions of one union compound, with a third region, case choreography, that is categorically different from either. The bodies are isomers over the same formal atom set: same atoms, different bond, different substance. Declared independence is a convention that makes the defect statable, not a settlement that fixes it. The framework returns a shape, not a country name.

## 2. Three checkable sub-claims

### C1 — framework-internal; checkable by reading Figures 2, 9 and 10

- **Statement:** the governing variable is the position of the checker and what it ranges against, not the formal mandate. A checker cannot own the reading of the slice it is graded on; evidence about its maker is inadmissible.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 10 :86, "Outside the slice being graded. Whoever is measured by a reading cannot own it." Fig 2 :33, "The anchor must not update." Fig 9 :51, "what the verifier may see: evidence bearing on the residual is admissible; evidence bearing only on its maker is a stake-inducing channel, and the verifier is denied it."
- **Triangulation status:** triangulated across Fig 10 (seat theory) and Fig 2 (anchor theory), two figures with low overlap. Seat Map :304 and Seat Map :201 are the same law restated in the seat guide, not additional witnesses.
- **Refuter:** targeting is statistically independent of coalition connection after controlling for misconduct, in a body the coalition can demonstrably move on appointment or budget.

### C2 — field-facing; checkable by a researcher against real institutions

- **Statement:** the two outcomes are a union compound with two loops and a compound intersection, and the institutions are isomers over the same formal atoms. Over-enforcement of opponents is not the mirror image of under-enforcement of insiders.
- **What to check:** do cases against coalition insiders fail through non-opening, non-collection, delay or budget starvation (channel loop), while cases against opponents are opened selectively on evidence about loyalty or faction (authored-room and stake loop)? Is the intersection a timed prosecution rather than either loop alone? Do the two bodies hold the same formal atoms (mandate, investigators, budget, sanction power) and differ only in controlling relation?
- **Figure line:** Fig 12 :117, "isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour." Fig 9 :57, "a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either."
- **Refuter:** one lever clears both conditions with no residue; or the bodies differ by a missing atom rather than a different bond, which would make this a missing-atom finding instead of an isomer claim.

### C3 — design-facing; checkable against institutional rules

- **Statement:** selection, removal, budget and information access work conjunctively; a declared independence clause alone does not. Independence is positional and decays with use, so refresh requires a checker with a different closure.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 10 :73-74, "A candidate set chosen by a participant is an authored room however well the selection is reasoned, so the pool must be unauthored first and examined second." Fig 10 :49, "Declaring a meaning does not ground it." Fig 10 :81, "no amount of use makes them less alike and replacement is the only refresh."
- **World corroboration, secondary:** Melton and Ginsburg (2014) find that selection and removal rules are the only de jure protections that enhance judicial independence, working conjunctively; Nyuon (2024) reports that formal independence without fiscal protection is weak. These are different fields from the figures, so they are genuine corroboration, but they do not decide the verdict.
- **Refuter:** formal protections alone predict de facto enforcement independence as well as the conjunctive seat account; or a checker's measured independence rises with repeated use against the same target without rising correlation.

## 3. What is explicitly not claimed

- Not a country name, a reform blueprint or a causal coefficient.
- Not that formal rules are irrelevant. Selection and removal rules matter, in combination with budget and information rights.
- Not "captured" as a moral judgement. A seat with no stake at all does not exist (Fig 10 :58); the claim is about position and gain from the reading.
- Not that more oversight bodies help. Seat Map :283, "Adding people raises the vocabulary and does not add an anchor."
- Not `grounded`. Nothing here was settled by data.

## 5. What would settle it

The verdict is settled by a ground reading, not by agreement:

1. **Distribution test.** Targeting distribution per slot against an independent misconduct measure.
2. **Selection test.** A natural experiment on appointment and removal rules with budgets held comparable.
3. **Channel test.** Whether insider-misconduct information reaches the checker at all.
4. **Union test.** Whether a lever clearing insider impunity leaves opponent targeting standing.

Agreement from a list selected for agreement is disqualifying for a verdict (Seat Map :163: "High correlation is disqualifying for a verdict and efficient for a convention").

## 6. What an expert can check in twenty minutes

1. Read Fig 10 :73-74, :86, :49 and Fig 2 :33, Fig 9 :51, Fig 12 :117. Six short passages.
2. Check C2 against one institution you know: which loop actually produces its targeting, and what is in the intersection?
3. Check C3 against the appointment, removal and budget rules of that institution. Are they conjunctive or is one doing all the work?
4. Name one instrument that would discriminate impartial enforcement from selective enforcement, or say why none exists.
5. Send the refutation. A failure is more useful than an endorsement.
