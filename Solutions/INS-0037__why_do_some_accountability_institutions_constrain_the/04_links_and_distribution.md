## Distribution rules: offer, do not push

Figure-grounded reasons:

- **Fig 4 :22**:"Only methods cross up. Only head starts come down." Sending the full architecture before anyone has described a residual sends a recording, not a generator. Lead with the shape claim and its refuter; offer links after.
- **Fig 10 :42**:"A verifier that can reconstruct the claim cannot verify it." A recipient who has absorbed the whole framework before being asked for a verdict has joined the frame being checked.
- **Fig 9 :27-28**:"describe it before you go looking, or any frame you pick will seem to fit." Ask what they would count as a failure before giving them the framework.
- **Seat Map :163**:"High correlation is disqualifying for a verdict and efficient for a convention." A mass send selected for people who already agree produces a convention, not corroboration.
- **Seat Map :283**:"Adding people raises the vocabulary and does not add an anchor."

**Recommended order in a reply:** (1) the shape claim and its four refuter tests; (2) ask what they would count as a failure; (3) record that verdict; (4) only then the links, with the note that the figures, not the summary, are the authority.

## The two-stage offer

- **Stage 1.** Residual, shape claim, pre-registered refuter. The recipient checks the output without holding the framework's interior. Verdict recorded now.
- **Stage 2, opt-in only.** If curious, the local figure extract and the canonical usage rule. Later agreement is never pooled as corroboration (Fig 2 :40: "agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool").
- **Stage 3.** Offer the formula as a method to reuse. Each user writes their own refuter, states their own ground, and labels their own mode. The framework's own "Direction" note says a loop cannot check the direction of its own question.
