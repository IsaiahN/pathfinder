## 0. The residual, in effect terms

The question asks why, given two institutions with the same nominal mandate (a court, an auditor, an anti-corruption commission, a parliament), one sanctions the coalition that appointed and funds it while the other concentrates its sanctions on that coalition's rivals.

State the failure as a prediction gap, not as a topic. The prediction "formal mandate determines who is policed" fails. R is the difference between the observed targeting distribution and the distribution the mandate predicts, measured per institution against the ground.

- **Arity.** Two target slots per institution: coalition insiders and opponents. There is a third term, the ground: actual misconduct, independent of who committed it. The residual is indexed by institution and target, never averaged across institutions. Fig 1 :19: "The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated." Fig 1 :22: "Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average." A global statement like "accountability works" throws away the only signal that says where to look.
- **Symmetry.** The two slots are not interchangeable. The coalition controls appointment, budget, removal and information; the opponent does not. An actor's valence is relative to this residual: Fig 11 :49: "An actor's valence is relative to the residual, not a property it carries." The same prosecutor is a constraint on the coalition in one configuration and a weapon against opponents in another. No standing list of "good institutions" survives a change of this residual.
- **Scale.** Meso, the institution as a checking arrangement. The micro scale (an occupant's incentives) and the macro scale (the political settlement) are adjacent and they are orders, not substitutes: Fig 10 :68: "Different actors, a different currency, a different ground, and a lossy transform between."

**What an answer would look like, bound as an ordinary term (Formula step "Direction").** An answer is a configuration of the checking seat: where the checker sits relative to the party it grades, what it ranges against, what it gains from a particular reading, and how its occupants were selected. Written down before the run so it can accrue a residual like anything else. The sought-for shape is a structural description, not a country name and not a reform slogan.

**Question type.** A union, not a partition. Under-enforcement of insiders and over-enforcement of opponents are two conditions that co-occur and intersect. Fig 9 :57: "a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either. It stops being a mixture and becomes a specific compound." See `06_isomers_and_order.md` for the unbundling.

## 1. Domain census (before choosing any figure line)

One line per domain: actors, currency, anchor. No nameable anchor means it is not a domain yet.

| domain | actors | currency | anchor | question type |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **D1, the checking seat** (the institution as checker) | checker (occupants), checked (coalition and its agencies), the ground (facts of misconduct and the law) | verdicts: investigate, prosecute, sanction, drop, delay, report | the ground: what actually happened and what the law says. It must not move when the coalition pushes. "The anchor must not update." (Fig 2 :33) | partition: which configurations keep the checker outside the slice it grades |
| **D2, the constitution of the checker** (selection, tenure, budget, removal) | appointing authority, selector, candidate pool, occupants | selection rules, removal rules, funding, information access | an unauthored pool: a lottery, everyone qualified, or "a set assembled before the question existed" (Fig 10 :78) | partition: authored room versus unauthored pool |
| **D3, the habitat** (the political settlement, donors, party competition, civil society) | coalition, opponents, donors, oversight bodies, monitors | power, resources, standing, reputational cost | no anchor of its own. The habitat updates and is where actors come from; D1 supplies the ground. "The habitat is not the ground." (Fig 11 :73) | enumeration: read actors off the world, never compose them |

**Partition or union.** D1 and D2 are partitions with their own loops. D3 is a habitat, enumerated, and it feeds actors into both. The residual itself is a union compound, so D1 carries two loops (insider impunity, opponent targeting) and their intersection (case choreography).

**Dependency order (Fig 3).** The chain is ordered by dependency, not importance: Fig 3 :20: "The chain is ordered by dependency, not by importance. Each link consumes what the one above produces." Here:

1. The ground must be reachable (D1); otherwise nothing can range against it.
2. The checker must be outside the slice it grades (D1); otherwise it is measured by the reading it owns.
3. Occupants must come from an unauthored pool with protected selection and removal (D2); otherwise the room is stacked before the first case.
4. The habitat is then enumerated (D3) to locate what acts on those conditions.

Effort spent at step 4 cannot rescue a broken step 2: "Optimising a link that already works cannot rescue one that does not." (Fig 3 :19) A reading taken below the break is a reading of nothing.

## 3. Findings

### F1 — The institution is a checker, and the governing variable is whether it ranges against the ground or negotiates with the coalition

Fig 2 :45: "Frames range against the ground. They do not negotiate with it."

Fig 2 :29: "An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks." Fig 2 :33: "The anchor must not update." Fig 2 :34: "If it does, panel 3 turns into panel 1: the anchor becomes another frame, everything drifts together, and the drift is coherent, which is why nobody notices."

For an accountability institution the ground is the fact of misconduct and the law, not the coalition's preference and not the institution's own activity report. When the institution's verdicts move when the coalition pushes, the anchor has become another frame. That is the coherent-drift collapse: it looks like an independent institution because the paperwork, the mandate and the annual report are all intact.

**Mode:** `specified`. **Status:** triangulated across Fig 2 (anchor theory) and Fig 10 (seat theory), two figures with low conceptual overlap.

**Refuter:** an enforcement-distribution study, using misconduct measures independent of the institution, in which verdicts track the ground and are statistically independent of a target's coalition connection, while the institution is demonstrably movable by the coalition on appointment or budget. If the channel exists but is not used, F1's claim that the channel is decisive fails.

### F2 — The decisive condition is position: outside the slice being graded

Fig 10 :86: "Outside the slice being graded. Whoever is measured by a reading cannot own it." Fig 10 :93: "A ground with a reputation to protect is a ground someone benefits from, and its keeper is thereby disqualified."

Seat Map :201: "outside the slice being graded, whoever is measured by a reading cannot own it." Seat Map :304: "Independence is not a virtue the occupant supplies; it is a fact about position, the same way the right vocabulary is a fact about agent-and-habitat rather than about the agent."

An auditor removable by the agency it audits, or a commission whose members are drawn from the coalition it must investigate, is inside the slice. Its reading of the coalition is a reading it owns, and a seat that owns a reading cannot range against it. This is not a claim about the virtue of the occupants. It is a claim about the position.

**Mode:** `specified`. **Status:** triangulated across Fig 10 and Fig 2 (an anchor listed as a party has become a frame).

**Refuter:** a case in which the checker is formally outside the coalition's appointment, funding and removal chain, yet enforcement still systematically spares insiders, with no detectable information channel or career incentive linking occupants to the coalition. Position alone would then be insufficient and the census would need a fourth domain.

### F3 — An interest in the reading, not an interest in the outcome, is disqualifying

Fig 10 :58-59: "A seat with no stake at all does not exist." The condition is that "an interest in the outcome is unavoidable, an interest in the reading is disqualifying." Fig 10 :60: "Where a stake cannot be removed, declare it. An undeclared interest is modelled anyway and an unnamed model runs unchallenged."

Fig 9 :51: "what the verifier may see: evidence bearing on the residual is admissible; evidence bearing only on its maker is a stake-inducing channel, and the verifier is denied it."

An institution may legitimately care about the outcome (stability, development, order). It becomes a weapon when it gains from *which* reading the ground returns: rivals prosecuted, insiders spared. Evidence of loyalty to the appointing coalition is evidence about the maker, not about the residual, and a checking arrangement must deny it to the verifier. Declaring the stake is the minimum honest move; removing the stake from the reading is the structural one.

**Mode:** `specified`. **Status:** triangulated across Fig 10 (stake rule) and Fig 9 (evidence admissibility), low overlap.

**Refuter:** an institution whose occupants demonstrably gain from a particular reading (career advancement tied to conviction counts, for example) yet whose case selection is independent of target identity after controlling for misconduct. The stake would then be real but inert, and F3 would overreach.

### F4 — Authored room versus unauthored pool: selection, tenure and budget

Fig 10 :73-74: "The residual selects the members, but only from what is available. A candidate set chosen by a participant is an authored room however well the selection is reasoned, so the pool must be unauthored first and examined second." Fig 10 :76-79 names the mechanisms: "a lottery", "everyone who qualifies", "a set assembled before the question existed", "or the habitat itself, enumerated rather than composed."

Seat Map :213: "A conscientious selection is still a selection, so unauthored needs a mechanism."

World corroboration, secondary only: Melton and Ginsburg (2014), opened 2026-11-23, find that "rules governing the selection and removal of judges are the only de jure protections that actually enhance judicial independence in practice and that they work conjunctively." Nyuon (2024) reports that anti-corruption bodies with "broad rhetoric but limited coercive independence" permit selective action while preserving executive discretion, and that "formal independence without fiscal protection is weak."

**Mode:** `specified`. **Status:** triangulated across Fig 10 and Fig 9 (residual-first selection), with independent world corroboration from a different field (law and economics).

**Refuter:** a natural experiment in which selection and removal rules are strong and unauthored and budgets are protected, yet selective enforcement persists with no detectable channel. That would show the conjunctive seat account is incomplete. The weaker result, that selection alone is insufficient, is already predicted and is not a refutation.

### F5 — The residual is a union compound, so there are two loops and one intersection

Fig 9 :54: "And when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again." Fig 9 :57: "a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either. It stops being a mixture and becomes a specific compound." Fig 9 :55: "A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones."

- **Loop 1, insider impunity.** A channel-starvation problem: cases are not opened, evidence is not collected, budgets are withheld, reports are delayed. Fig 10 :93: a channel dies and "the internal instruments stay green because they read through the channel."
- **Loop 2, opponent targeting.** An authored-room and stake problem: cases are opened selectively, evidence about the maker substitutes for evidence about the residual. Fig 9 :51.
- **The intersection, case choreography.** High-profile prosecutions timed to factional contestation. Nyuon (2024) names this mechanism directly. It is categorically different from either loop alone, and a single lever aimed at only one leaves it standing.

**Mode:** `specified`. **Status:** single-source for the union rule (Fig 9), corroborated by the two-loop structure in the world source.

**Refuter:** a single lever that clears both conditions with no residue, for example one budget reform that eliminates insider impunity and opponent targeting together with no compound left over. Then the union reading fails and the question is one hard problem after all.

### F6 — Declared independence is a convention, not a ground

Fig 10 :49: "Declaring a meaning does not ground it." Fig 10 :47: "a convention that decides an outcome has stopped being one." Fig 10 :48: "a convention nothing can check is a constant the seat authored, carrying the seat's authority."

A law that declares an institution independent makes the defect statable, as Fig 10 :16 says: "the convention does not fix the bug" and "it makes the bug statable" The world literature agrees from a different direction: the de jure and de facto independence of courts can diverge substantially, and Melton and Ginsburg find that formal protections work only conjunctively, not in isolation.

**Mode:** `specified`. **Status:** triangulated across Fig 10 (conventions) and Fig 2 (instruments can be wrong on axes they do not measure), with world corroboration.

**Refuter:** a multi-country comparison in which formal protections alone predict enforcement independence as well as the conjunctive seat account. That conflicts with the world source and would send us back to the census, by the rule that a conflict never flips a verdict.

### F7 — Independence is positional and decays with use

Fig 10 :80-81: "divergence regenerates unevenly. Frames whose independence is renewed by separate experience can be maintained; frames whose correlation is fixed at origin can only be rotated, because no amount of use makes them less alike and replacement is the only refresh." Seat Map :233: "Every correction raises correlation between the corrector and the corrected."

Seat Map :235: "The room composition is a function of the question. When the question changes, the room should change. A room that stays fixed while the question moves is the failure; the correlation is only its symptom."

A watchdog that has investigated the same coalition for years is more correlated with it, not less. Refresh comes from a frame with a different closure: a different institution, a court, a legislature, a civil-society monitor, a rotating panel. Adding a fifth copy of the same body is motion, not refresh: Fig 7 :36: "A chain of mirrors is one long room. The next room must differ from the last, and match the residual it surfaced."

**Mode:** `specified`. **Status:** single-source for the decay law (Fig 10 plus Seat Map, same framework family); the room-composition claim is corroborated by Fig 7, which is a genuinely different figure.

**Refuter:** a checker whose measured independence increases with repeated use against the same target and whose correlation does not rise, under a design that reports both. That would refute the shelf-life claim.

### F8 — The ground for the verdict is the distribution of enforcement, not the volume of activity

Fig 1 :19: "The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated." Fig 1 :24-27 disqualifies frame-internal quantities: "coverage, and compression achieved", "predicates minted, and credibility accrued", "They are frame-internal. A frame cannot score itself with a quantity it also produces."

A rise in audits, arrests or reports is a quantity the institution produces. It cannot score the institution. The admissible reading is the distribution of cases across coalition insiders and opponents, compared against independent misconduct data, so that the two slots stay separate.

World corroboration: Nyuon (2024) reports that donor programmes "measure activity instead of impartiality" and recommends monitoring "enforcement distribution and case outcomes, not only capacity metrics." The Brennan Center (Roth, 2026) reports falling report counts but also documents dismissal, budget and staffing changes that bear on position, not just volume.

**Mode:** `specified`. **Status:** triangulated across Fig 1 (per-slot residual) and Fig 9 (filters are not witnesses), with world corroboration.

**Refuter:** an institution whose own activity metric reliably predicts targeting distribution across contexts, so that the frame-internal quantity is admissible after all. Then Fig 1's disqualification was aimed at the wrong quantity.

## 4. Pre-registered refuter (written before the conclusion)

"a falsifier written before the run is a design instrument, not only an honesty device" (Seat Map :287). The shape claim is refuted if any of the following holds:

1. **Channel test.** A controlled comparison finds targeting statistically independent of coalition connection after controlling for misconduct, in an institution that is demonstrably movable by the coalition on appointment, budget or removal. (Breaks F1 and F2.)
2. **Position test.** Unauthored selection, protected tenure and a protected budget coexist with systematic insider protection and there is no detectable information or career channel. (Breaks F4 and F7.)
3. **Union test.** One lever clears insider impunity and opponent targeting with no specific compound left standing. (Breaks F5.)
4. **Formal-rule test.** Formal protections alone predict de facto enforcement independence as well as the conjunctive seat account. (Breaks F6 and conflicts with the world source; conflict returns the question to the census.)
5. **Stake test.** Occupants gain from a particular reading and case selection is still independent of target identity. (Breaks F3.)

If any fires, the shape is withdrawn or re-censused. Until one is run, none of the above is labelled `grounded`.

## 5. Recommendation

1. Stop treating the mandate as the answer. A mandate names powers; it does not name position. Fig 12 :5: "A set of ingredients is not one. The bond is the rest of the information, and it is the half nothing was recording."
2. Name the ground and keep it reachable: misconduct data and the law, not the institution's own output. (F1, F8)
3. Move the checker outside the slice: independent selection and removal, protected budget, information rights that do not depend on the agency under review. (F2, F4)
4. Deny the verifier evidence about its maker: loyalty, party service and career sponsorship are not evidence about the residual. (F3)
5. Unbundle the two conditions and measure the compound separately. (F5)
6. Refresh by a frame whose closure differs, not by adding another copy. (F7)
7. Offer the shape and the refuter first, the links only after a verdict is recorded. Fig 10 :52: "Bring a characterised residual, never the source."

## 7. Strains, round trip and what is owed

- **Strain, live:** D3 has no anchor of its own. It is a habitat, enumerated, and it supplies actors to D1 and D2. Calling it a "domain with an anchor" would force Fig 11 and Fig 2 to contradict each other. Re-census keeps D3 as habitat. Honest and unresolved: the political-settlement literature supplies content here, not figure authority.
- **Round trip gap R_T:** the abstraction "independent accountability institution constrains power" loses three constraints on the way back down: (a) independence is positional, not attitudinal; (b) the residual is per slot, not aggregate; (c) the union compound needs two loops. Measured qualitatively in `11_audit.md`; no ground reading exists, so R_T is not a number.
- **Owed:** an enforcement-distribution study with independent misconduct measures, and a natural experiment on selection and removal rules. Until those exist, verdict is `specified`, candidate.
