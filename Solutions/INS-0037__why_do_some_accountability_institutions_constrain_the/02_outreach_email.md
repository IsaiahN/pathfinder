# Outreach draft: check one output before you decide whether the framework is worth reading

**Use:** one-to-one, or as a cover letter attached to a submission through a public venue in `03_public_contact_points.csv`. **Not** for bulk send.

**Hard precondition:** do not send until the shape claim and its refuter in `01_figure_grounded_audit.md` §3 and §4 survive your own check. The output must stand on its own or the two-stage offer collapses on contact.

---

**Subject:** One checkable claim about why accountability bodies pick different targets

> Hi [name],
>
> I have one specific output I would like you to check against what you already know. I am not asking you to read the framework first, and I am not asking you to agree with anything. If the output does not survive, there is nothing else to look at.
>
> **The residual (`specified`, not `grounded`):** two accountability bodies with the same formal mandate behave differently. One sanctions the coalition that appointed and funds it; the other mainly sanctions that coalition's opponents. The prediction "mandate determines targeting" fails. That failure is the question.
>
> **The shape claim:** an accountability body constrains the coalition when it occupies a checking seat that is outside the slice it grades, ranges against a ground (the facts of misconduct and the law) that does not move when the coalition pushes, gains nothing from which reading the ground returns, and draws its occupants from a pool that the party it checks did not author. It mainly polices opponents when those conditions are inverted: the checker is inside the slice, shares the coalition's evidence, gains from a particular reading, and is staffed from a room the coalition authored. This is a structural description, not a country name and not a reform slogan.
>
> **What would refute it, written down before this message:**
> 1. Targeting is independent of coalition connection once misconduct is controlled for, in a body the coalition can demonstrably move on appointment or budget.
> 2. Independent selection, protected tenure and a protected budget coexist with systematic insider protection and no detectable information or career channel.
> 3. One lever clears both insider impunity and opponent targeting with no compound left over.
> 4. Formal protections alone predict de facto enforcement independence as well as the positional account.
>
> If any holds, I withdraw or re-census. A one-line "this fails because..." is more useful to me than agreement. If you would rather not engage, no reply is needed.
>
> **What I am not claiming:** that any named country is the answer; that a particular reform package is the answer; that the framework can tell you the causal weight of each condition; that this is grounded. The framework returns a shape, not a name. Convergent derivation and adopted import look identical in the contents and only the record separates them, so nothing here is offered as corroboration.
>
> **If it survives, and only then:** the sources are below. The figures are the authority, not my summary of them.
> - Figures with line numbers (local extract): `FRAMEWORK/reference/03-FIGURES_TEXT.md`
> - Canonical usage rule (local): `FRAMEWORK/reference/FIGURES_README_canonical.md`
> - The eight essays, indexed in `05_what_shape_means.md`
>
> **If you want to use the machinery:** the formula is in the figures. Run it on a problem you know well and can check against a ground. Write down what would refute your result *before* you run it, and state the mode of what you get. A reported failure is more useful to me than a reported success.
