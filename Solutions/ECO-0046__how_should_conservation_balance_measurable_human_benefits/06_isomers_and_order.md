# Isomers, thresholds and order in a conservation balance

**Mode: `specified`.** This is a simulated derivation checked against the figures, not a ground reading (see `05_what_shape_means.md` section 6).

## RESIDUAL

The question supplies the word 'balance' without a structure. Three sub-questions are owed: (1) which bond composes a priced benefit with a protected ecological value, (2) in what order must the decision procedure run, and (3) can two plans with the same ingredients be different things? Each has its own falsifier.

## Q1: Which bond composes P and U?

- Operators table: comparison ⋛ is "a threshold relation between two quantities". The identifying test is "is it about which is larger, rather than both being present? Yes."
- Fig 12 :112: "A plan is a sequence. A goal is a comparison. Progress is a subtraction."
- The symmetric bond + (conjunction, "both hold at once") would say P and U are both present and can be added. That is the single-total move, and it fails F1 of `01_figure_grounded_audit.md`: the two are in different units.
- ⋛ does not add. It sets a threshold relation. That is the correct grammar for a protected value: U is not a weight to be traded down; it is a condition the action must meet or a limit the action must not cross. A sacred or taboo value (Daw et al. 2015) behaves as ⋛ or as a refusal (negation, ¬), not as +.
- Where U is genuinely commensurable (a service with a market proxy), + is allowed inside P's domain. The ban is on crossing domains with +.
- **Falsifier, written before the run:** if a protected value is shown to be compensable at the margin without changing what the protecting community protects, the ⋛/refusal mapping fails and + stands.

Verdict: `specified`, single-source on the figure side (Operators table plus Fig 12), corroborated by Daw et al. 2015 from an independent field.

## Q2: What order must the procedure run?

Fig 3 gives the dependency chain, and the order is by dependency, not importance.

1. **Perception.** Can the frame register U at all? Fig 3 :5: 'the system can tell what is in front of it, and tell it apart from what was there before'. A cost-benefit frame with no term for existence value fails here.
2. **Vocabulary.** Does it have terms for what the situation affords? Fig 3 :7: 'it has terms for what things afford: what can be moved, opened, joined, blocked'. Without terms for bequest, identity, and intrinsic value, the frame cannot even state the residual.
3. **Objective.** Can each domain say what counts as progress in its own terms? Fig 3 :10: 'it can say what would count as progress here, in its own terms'. Note 'in its own terms': this is where the plural requirement enters, not a single common objective.
4. **Planning.** A route under incomplete information (Fig 3 :13).
5. **Learn and carry.** Fig 3 :15: 'name what worked, verify it, and carry it to the next problem'.

- Fig 3 :19: "Optimising a link that already works cannot rescue one that does not."
- Fig 3 :27: "A link with no instrument is not a link that works. It is a link nobody has looked at."
- Most conservation effort lands above the break, at planning and optimisation, because that is where the measurements exist. A weighting exercise is work at link 4 or 5. If the break is at link 2 (no vocabulary for U), the weighting is work on a chain that cannot use it. A reading taken below the break is a reading of nothing (Fig 3 :22 to :23).
- **Falsifier:** if a decision procedure that never names U still protects U as reliably as one that does, the vocabulary link is not load-bearing and this ordering claim fails.

Verdict: `general` structural, triangulated across Fig 3 and Fig 9's 'describe before looking'.

## Q3: Can two plans with the same ingredients be different things?

Fig 12 :117: "isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."

Take two plans over the same atoms:

- Plan A: protect the core first, then restore the corridor. Atoms: {core protection, corridor restoration}. Bond: sequence (→, 'A then B; B needs A to have happened', Operators table).
- Plan B: restore the corridor first, then relax protection in the core. Same two atoms, different sequence.

Under → these are **different terms**, not spellings of one plan. Under a symmetric bond they would be one. The bond is charged for what it could have been (Fig 12 :99), so the order must be recorded, not flattened into a list of actions. This is Fig 12 :95 to :96: "Two entries with an identical ingredient set are not variants of one thing. They are different things whose notation cannot tell them apart, and the difference is carried entirely by the bond."

- A related structure is the **catalyst** (Fig 12 :119): 'A part that survives its own use.' A monitoring program or a trust that persists across decisions is a catalyst, not a one-off cost. Treating it as a one-off is a different substance with the same ingredient list.
- **Falsifier, written before the run:** if outcomes are invariant to the order of the two actions across all cases of interest (that is, the bond is actually symmetric +), the isomer claim fails and the two plans are variants of one thing.

Verdict: `specified`, single-source on the figure definition, with a stated field test.

## Q4: What is lost when the balance is condensed into an index?

Fig 4 :30: "So take the round trip and measure the gap. Without it you are navigating by dead reckoning: confident about a position nobody checked."
Fig 4 :31: "The gap is not an error to eliminate. It is the measurement of what the coarser description cannot hold"

- Sent up (x): the full, heterogeneous value set with per-slot residuals.
- Abstraction (T_A): a single index, for example total economic value or a composite biodiversity score.
- Concretisation (T_E): applying that index to choose an action.

Three things routinely fail to survive the trip back down: the identity of the slot that carried the live loss (Fig 1 :19 and :22); the protected standing of a sacred value (Q1); the order of actions (Q3). Conversely, the rough ranking of actions often does survive. R_T is the enumerated set of dropped constraints. It is not measured numerically here, because no ground reading exists; it is recorded qualitatively. Treating the index as identical to the value set would set R_T to zero and is the dead-reckoning error the figure names.

**Falsifier:** an index whose T_E composed with T_A returns the original value set with zero gap.

## CONCLUSION

The bond that composes a priced benefit with an unpriced protected value is ⋛ (threshold) or ¬ (refusal), not + (sum). The procedure runs in Fig 3 dependency order, and the load-bearing break is usually at vocabulary, not planning. Two plans with the same ingredients but different action order are isomers, so the sequence must be recorded. Any single index carries a nonzero round-trip gap that must be published. Each claim has a falsifier written in advance.
