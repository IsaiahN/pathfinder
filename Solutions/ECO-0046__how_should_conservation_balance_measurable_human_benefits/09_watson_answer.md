## QUESTION

Decide, in effect terms, what the Tether says about the request to 'balance' measurable human benefits against ecological values difficult to quantify. Option A: treat it as one scalar optimisation and find the right weight or total. Option B: treat it as a union compound and specify a procedure that keeps the currencies apart. What each leads to: A produces a self-consistent number inside one accounting, silently drops the unpriced residual, and spends the independence of the people checking it; B produces a checkable shape, a per-slot rule, visible conventions, a threshold for protected values, a published round-trip gap, and a refuter written in advance.

## DOMAIN CENSUS

R by arity, symmetry and scale, then one line per domain with actors / currency / anchor, partition or union, order by dependency.

- **R (the residual).** A decision procedure holding only a priced accounting cannot register a value with no unit in that accounting; the omission generates no error signal inside the frame. Arity: two conditions. Symmetry: asymmetric, P is orderable in money, U is not. Scale: first order inside each domain, second order at the decision, cascades three steps out (Fig 11 :52 to :53). Fig 9 :57: "a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either". Union, not partition.
- **Domain 1, market valuation.** Actors: analysts, prices, willingness-to-pay surveys, discount rates. Currency: money. Anchor: the measured biophysical flow being priced, not the price, because the price updates on what frames think. Type: one reading, not the verdict.
- **Domain 2, ecological state.** Actors: species, populations, processes, habitats. Currency: biophysical state and trajectory. Anchor: survey, plot, experiment, which does not move when a valuation frame objects. Type: the ground for 'did it work', not for 'what should be valued'.
- **Domain 3, plural and held values.** Actors: communities, indigenous and local knowledge, ethicists, cultural identity. Currency: reasons, narratives, principles, protected values. Anchor: documented, deliberated human judgment, legitimate because the question is constitutively about people (Fig 2 :36). Type: incommensurable with D1.
- **Domain 4, decision and governance.** Actors: boards, regulators, managers, conventions. Currency: a choice among alternatives with reversibility and cost of being wrong. Anchor: follow-up monitoring of whether the action conserved what it claimed. Type: composes D1 to D3.
- **Order by dependency (Fig 3).** Perception, vocabulary, objective, planning, learn and carry. The vocabulary-to-objective link in D3 is the likely break, because it has no instrument (Fig 3 :27). Unbundle the union before searching (Fig 9 :54). Describe the residual before naming a frame (Fig 9 :27 to :28).

Strain test: the Fig 12 two-currency rule names bits and actions; applying it to money and ecological value is a level-recursive application, not a verbatim naming. Fig 12 :103 makes the recursion legitimate. Recorded as `general` rule plus `specified` application, corroborated by world sources. No hidden strain after re-census.

## VERDICT (from the figures)

The shape survives; a single balance number does not. The question is a union compound and must be unbundled before it is searched. The priced and the unpriced currencies cannot be added in one accounting, so summing them is the named error, not the fix. Residuals stay per species, place and group. The habitat is enumerated and its cascades followed, never composed from a fixed service list. Measured ecological state and documented human judgment are two independent anchors; a market price is only an instrument. Discount rates and weights are visible, violable conventions; protected values get threshold or refusal standing; any index carries a published round-trip gap; the procedure carries a pre-registered refuter and a declared stake. This is a `general` shape and a `specified` procedure, ungrounded and a candidate. The strongest corroboration is external: Daw et al. show the aggregate failure empirically, and IPBES shows the incommensurability is already a policy finding.

- **cost if wrong:** high and partly irreversible. A single-total procedure can conclude that conservation pays while the hard-to-quantify value is already gone; ecological loss often cannot be undone, which is why Fig 11 :61 says introduction is available and exclusion is not. The avoidable part of the cost is spending the independence of outside readers by sending a conclusion instead of a generator. Fig 2 :42 prices that: 'Buy independence in proportion to the cost of being wrong.'
- **undoable:** partly. The procedure is removable and revisable without touching any ecological fact; a bad decision grounded in an irreversible loss is not. The asymmetry is Fig 11 :61 to :63: you can add an actor and you cannot subtract the habitat. So the verdict is undoable as a method and not as an outcome.
- **reversed by:** a ground reading, not agreement. A commensuration preserving all per-slot residuals with R_T = 0; an anchor that settles the question while updating under a frame's opinion; a case set in which a single-number procedure protects hard-to-quantify values at least as well as a plural one; a demonstration that a protected value is compensable at the margin without changing what it protects; or a re-census showing the two-currency rule does not recurse to valuation. Until one of these fires, the verdict stands as `candidate`.
