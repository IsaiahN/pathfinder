## Two things in the opened sources that bear directly on the answer

1. **The terminological finding is itself evidence for the categorical distinction.** Smith-Patten et al. insist that *extirpation* and *extinction* are different categories and that collapsing them causes public misunderstanding. The figures reach the same distinction by a different route (source availability, Fig 6 :25-26 and Fig 8 :32). Two low-overlap routes to one boundary: the world source is genuine corroboration here, not a second spelling of the same pool.
2. **The rediscovery record is the epistemic edge, not a nuisance.** The same paper reports frequent rediscovery of species classified extinct. That is the world-side twin of Fig 8 :39, *"So a search that finds nothing is never a proof of absence."* It is why the "global" label is `candidate` in this folder.

## Distribution: offer, do not push

Figure-grounded reasons:

- **Fig 4 :22** — *"Only methods cross up. Only head starts come down."* Sending the full architecture before anyone has described a residual sends a recording, not a generator. Lead with the shape claim and its refuter; offer links after.
- **Fig 10 :42** — *"A verifier that can reconstruct the claim cannot verify it."* A recipient who has absorbed the whole framework before being asked for a verdict has joined the frame being checked.
- **Fig 9 :27-28** — *"describe it before you go looking, or any frame you pick will seem to fit."* Ask what they would expect to see if the shape claim were true before giving them the four sources.
- **Fig 2 :42** — *"Buy independence in proportion to the cost of being wrong."* One-to-one contact to people whose public work touches the residual; no list ranked by likely agreement.
- **Fig 2 :40** — *"disqualifying for a verdict: agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool."* A later agreement with the framework is never pooled as corroboration of the stage-1 verdict.

**Recommended order in a reply:** (1) the shape claim and its five refuters; (2) ask what they would count as a failure; (3) record that verdict; (4) only then the four links, with the note that the figures, not the summary, are the authority; (5) offer the formula as a method to reuse, with the instruction that each user write their own refuter before running it.

## The two-stage offer

- **Stage 1 before stage 2.** Fig 10 :52, *"Bring a characterised residual, never the source,"* and Fig 2 :45, *"Frames range against the ground. They do not negotiate with it."* The recipient checks the output without holding the framework's interior.
- **Stage 2 is opt-in by construction.** A recipient's later agreement is not evidence once they hold the theory, so the stage-1 verdict is recorded first and never pooled afterwards.
- **Stage 3 sends a method, which is permitted.** Fig 4 :22: *"Only methods cross up. Only head starts come down."* The formula is a generator; giving it away does not give away an answer.
- **Stage 3 still needs a ground per problem.** `THE_FORMULA.md` "Direction": a frame *"cannot check the direction of its own question, because the shape a frame is searching for is not a term it holds."* Each user must write down what would refute their result before running it, and label the mode of what they get.

**Hard precondition:** stages 1 to 3 work only if the stage-1 output survives. The shape claim in `01_figure_grounded_audit.md` §4 is the output to test. Send the generator, not the conclusion.
