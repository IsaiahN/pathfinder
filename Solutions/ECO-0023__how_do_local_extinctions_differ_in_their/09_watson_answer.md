## QUESTION

Decide, in effect terms, what distinguishes a local extinction (a species vanishes from one habitat while surviving elsewhere) from a global extinction (it vanishes everywhere), and what ecological consequences follow each. Option A: treat them as the same event at different scales, differing only in magnitude. Option B: treat them as categorically different, because the local case retains a source that the global case removes. What each leads to: A produces a scalar ranking and a consequence study that measures only the local cascade; B produces a categorical break, a per-slot consequence vector, five refuters written in advance, and a `candidate` label on the global claim.

## VERDICT (from the figures)

Local and global extinction differ categorically, not by degree. Local extinction is a removal from one habitat with a live external source: recolonization is an import from a closure that already holds the actor, and the cascade can be bounded at that habitat. Global extinction removes the source itself: nothing can be imported, every habitat's dependency chain is engaged, and repair is habitat substitution rather than reversal. Consequences are a vector over habitat, function and time, never a scalar, and the global label stays `candidate` because no survey is a sealed room. The operator-level form is subtraction versus negation (`06_isomers_and_order.md`). The figures deliver that shape and no ecological measurement, so the which-function, how-much, how-fast question remains a stated gap. This is `general` plus `specified`, ungrounded, candidate.

- **cost if wrong:** bounded and locatable. If the categorical break fails, the failure is in the closure reading of source availability (D2), which is the one `specified` cross-level extension. The packet names it and supplies a refuter before the conclusion, so the error is findable rather than invisible. The avoidable cost is spending an outside reader's independence by sending a theory before a residual, which Fig 2 :42 prices: *"Buy independence in proportion to the cost of being wrong."*
- **undoable:** yes. The categorical claim rests on figure lines that can be re-censused without touching the world sources. A documented natural restoration of a globally extinct species from a still-held closure, or a closed-world survey procedure, would collapse D2 to a scale difference. Nothing in the derivation has to be deleted to make that correction; the refusal is added to the record and the reach falls.
- **reversed by:** a ground reading, not agreement. A matched local-global experiment showing cascades of identical bounded scope would break D1. A closed, finite enumeration of all habitats would make "all habitats minus S" a finished composition and collapse the subtraction/negation distinction. A context-free species-loss ranking surviving a change of function would break D1's valence rule. A source-discriminating instrument naming which function a species actually performs would close the stated gap. Until one of these fires, the verdict stands as `candidate`.
