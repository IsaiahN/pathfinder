# Expert verification packet — the Tether on local versus global extinction

**What this is:** a claim in a form an ecologist or conservation biologist can check. Mode, derivation and refuters are stated before the conclusion, so a reader knows what to look for and what would settle it.

**Mode: `general` on the shape, `specified` on the ecological predictions, not `grounded`.** No measurement is claimed. This is a shape and a route, not a result.

## 1. The claim, in one paragraph

Local and global extinction differ categorically, not by degree. A local extinction (extirpation) is a removal from one habitat with a live external source: the species still exists elsewhere, so recolonization is an import from a closure that already holds the actor, and the cascade can terminate at that habitat's boundary. A global extinction removes the source itself: no closure holds the actor, no import can restore it, and every habitat's dependency chain is engaged at once. Consequences are a vector indexed by habitat, function and time, never a scalar, and the "globally extinct" label is a fallible world claim because no survey is a sealed room. The framework returns that shape, not the name of any function that will change.

## 2. Three checkable sub-claims

### C1 — reversibility and source availability (the categorical break)

- **Statement:** L is repairable by import from a population that still holds the actor; G is not, because the source is gone.
- **Source, verbatim:**
  - Fig 6 :25, *"An import adds no atom to the world."*
  - Fig 8 :32, *"Closure is idempotent. Composing inside a closed set never leaves it, so no search, however long, adds an atom."*
  - Fig 11 :61-62, *"The seat's power is asymmetric. Introduction is available; exclusion is not. You can add an actor and you cannot subtract the habitat, because contact is a fact about the world and does not consult you."*
- **World check:** the "Local extinction" reference records populations that *"may disappear from one habitat but be re-established due to individuals migrating from nearby populations."*
- **Refuter:** a documented case of a globally extinct species restored by natural immigration from a population still inside the same world closure. If that happens, G was really L. Also: a closed, finite enumeration of all habitats making "all habitats minus S" a finished composition.

### C2 — cascade scope and per-slot consequences

- **Statement:** consequences must be measured per habitat, function and time. A global loss engages dependency links beyond the first habitat; a local loss can be bounded there. There is no context-free "worst species" ranking.
- **Source, verbatim:**
  - Fig 11 :44, *"List everything in contact with the residual, then what is in contact with those, and outward until the cascade stops mattering."*
  - Fig 11 :52-53, *"And contact cascades. Scoping the habitat to direct contact is how DDT reached the eagles: first-order enumeration, second-order effect, and the residual that mattered was three steps out."*
  - Fig 1 :19, *"The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated."*
  - Fig 11 :49, *"An actor's valence is relative to the residual, not a property it carries."*
- **World check:** "Functional extinction" defines the function slot: *"the reduced population no longer plays a significant role in ecosystem function."*
- **Refuter:** a measured global loss whose cascade is bounded exactly as a single local loss, with no additional dependencies engaged; or a context-free ranking of species whose loss is always worse, surviving a change of function and habitat.

### C3 — fallibility of the global label

- **Statement:** "globally extinct" is `candidate`, never `grounded`, because no survey is a sealed room.
- **Source, verbatim:**
  - Fig 8 :39, *"So a search that finds nothing is never a proof of absence."*
  - Fig 9 :44, *"Only a sealed room can be searched to the end, and no frame is a sealed room. So searching and finding nothing proves nothing."*
- **World check:** Smith-Patten et al. 2015 report that more than a third of mammal species classified extinct or possibly extinct have been rediscovered, and give the glossary distinction *"Extinction: irreversible species death"* versus *"Extirpation: loss of a species in a specific area."*
- **Refuter:** a closed-world survey procedure whose negative result entails logical absence rather than failure to observe.

## 3. What is explicitly not claimed

- Not that global extinction has a larger scalar consequence. The consequence is a vector.
- Not that any species, function or habitat is named by the framework. It returns a shape, not a name (Fig 9 :32).
- Not that global is local at a bigger scale, or that the two are isomers (negation is not a bond; `06_isomers_and_order.md`).
- Not that local extinction is harmless or easily reversed in practice.
- Not that a failed survey proves absence.
- Not `grounded`. Nothing here is asserted as settled by measurement.

## 5. What would settle it

A source-discriminating measurement, not a further argument. From *The Hard Problem is About Source*: an instrument reading green *"cannot comment on the source"*; *"The answer is unreached until a better instrument exists."*

1. **Source check:** is there a population elsewhere still holding the actor at the moment of local loss?
2. **Cascade check:** in matched local and global losses, are additional dependency links engaged three or more steps out in the global case?
3. **Function check:** per named function, is the role removed, or taken up by another actor with the same valence? Measured per slot, never averaged.
4. **Debt and credit check:** is the local debt paid by immigration credit when the source is intact, and unpaid when it is gone?

## 6. What an expert can check in twenty minutes

1. Read Fig 6 :25, Fig 8 :32 and Fig 8 :39 (three short passages).
2. Check C1 against a system you know: was the "global" event actually the loss of the last source, or was there a population elsewhere?
3. Name one measurement that would distinguish a bounded cascade from an unbounded one across habitats, or say why none is possible.
4. Send the refutation. A failure is more useful than an endorsement.
