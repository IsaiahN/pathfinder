## Round trip gap R_T

The Symbols table defines R_T as the gap between what was sent up and what came back down, `gap(x, (T_E ∘ T_A)(x))`, measuring what the coarser description cannot hold. Fig 4 :30 requires it to be measured rather than assumed. I ran the round trip on the condensation this folder actually uses.

1. **Sent up (x):** the verbatim figure set, chiefly Fig 11 :44, :52-53, :61-62 (habitat enumeration, cascade, asymmetry), Fig 6 :25 and Fig 8 :32 (import and idempotent closure), Fig 8 :39 (fallibility), Fig 1 :19 (per-slot residual).
2. **Abstraction (T_A):** the shorthand in `07_the_tether_answer.md`: local is removal with a source; global is removal of the source; consequences are a vector; the global label is candidate.
3. **Concretisation (T_E):** a concrete ecological claim about a named species, habitat and function.

**R_T is nonzero.** Four things the shorthand holds do not survive the trip back down:

- the **identity of the source population** at the time of local loss (the shorthand says "a source exists"; the ground needs a census of a named population);
- the **scope of the cascade** (the shorthand says "bounded versus all habitats"; the ground needs the dependency links actually present three steps out);
- the **function that is lost** (the shorthand keeps the slot empty; the ground must name and measure it);
- the **operator-level form** (subtraction versus negation) depends on the closure of the habitat enumeration, which the world does not supply.

Conversely, one thing survives cleanly: the categorical distinction between removal with a source and removal of the source. That is the residue worth sending. R_T was **not** measured numerically, because no ground reading exists; it is recorded qualitatively as the enumerated set of dropped constraints. Treating the formula as identical to the figures would set R_T to zero and would be the dead-reckoning error Fig 4 :30 names. The honest form is a lossy condensation with a recorded gap.
