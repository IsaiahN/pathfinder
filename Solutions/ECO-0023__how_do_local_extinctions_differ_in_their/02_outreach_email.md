# Outreach draft — check the shape before you decide about the framework

**Use:** one-to-one, or as a cover letter attached to a submission through the public venues in `03_public_contact_points.csv`. **Not** for bulk send.

**Hard precondition:** do not send until the shape claim and its refuter in `01_figure_grounded_audit.md` §4 are stated in the recipient's own terms. The output must survive the check or the two-stage offer collapses on contact.

---

**Subject:** One checkable claim about local versus global extinction, before any theory

> Hi [name],
>
> I have one specific output I would like you to check against what you already know. I am not asking you to read a theory first, and I am not asking you to agree with it. If the output does not survive, there is nothing else to look at.
>
> **The question in effect terms.** A species disappears from one habitat while surviving elsewhere (local extinction, extirpation), or it disappears everywhere (global extinction). The claim is about the *kind* of difference, not the amount.
>
> **The output (`specified`, not `grounded`):** the two events differ categorically, because global extinction removes the source that local extinction leaves intact.
>
> 1. A local loss is a removal from one habitat with a live external source. Recolonization imports the actor from a population that still holds it. That import adds no atom to the world, which is why it is cheap and repeatable.
> 2. A global loss removes the source itself. No closure holds the actor, so no import can restore it, and search inside what remains cannot manufacture it.
> 3. Consequences are not one number. They are a vector indexed by habitat, by function and by time. An actor's valence is relative to the function you track; the same species can be a benefactor to one function and a malfactor to another.
> 4. The "globally extinct" label is a fallible world claim. No survey is a sealed room, so a failed search is not proof of absence.
>
> This is a **shape**, not an ecological measurement. I am not claiming to know which functions change, or by how much, in any named system. That needs a ground.
>
> **What would refute it (written before this message):**
> 1. a globally extinct species restored by natural immigration from a population still inside the same world closure;
> 2. a global loss whose cascade is bounded exactly as a single local loss, with no additional dependencies engaged;
> 3. a context-free ranking of species whose loss is always worse, surviving a change of function and habitat;
> 4. a closed-world survey whose negative result entails logical absence;
> 5. a full restore of a globally extinct species with no inherited actors and no hidden failures.
>
> If any holds, I will retract the claim. A one-line "this fails because..." is more useful to me than agreement. If you would rather not engage, no reply is needed.
>
> **If it survives, and only then:** here are the sources. The figures, not my summary, are the authority.
> - Local extinction (definition and recolonization): https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Local_extinction
> - Is extinction forever? (terminology and rediscovery): https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC4404403
> - Extinction debt (delay and immigration credit): https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Extinction_debt
> - Functional extinction (function as the tracked slot): https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Functional_extinction
>
> **If you want to reuse the machinery:** the figures and formula are public at https://github.com/IsaiahN/tether/tree/general-agent/figures . Run the loop on a problem you know well and can check against a ground, write down what would refute your result *before* you run it, and label the mode of what you get. A reported failure is more useful to me than a reported success.
>
> — [name]
