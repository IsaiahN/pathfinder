# The Tether's answer: local versus global extinction

**Mode: `general` on the structural shape, `specified` on the ecological predictions, not `grounded`.** Simulated derivation checked against the figures (`05_what_shape_means.md` §6), not a ground reading.

## 1. The residual it answers

A species disappears. In the local case it vanishes from H1 while surviving elsewhere; in the global case it vanishes everywhere. Question: what kind of difference separates the two, and what ecological consequences attach to each? Fig 9 :54 unbundles the compound before answering: the removal event and the source-availability condition are separate conditions, and a lever on one does not clear the other.

## 2. One loop per domain, verdicts kept separate

### D1, habitat and contact web, verdict: `triangulated`

The habitat is enumerated, never composed: *"List everything in contact with the residual, then what is in contact with those, and outward until the cascade stops mattering"* (Fig 11 :44). Consequences are indexed per slot, not averaged (Fig 1 :19, :22). The slots are habitat, function and time (Fig 11 :46-47).

The difference in this domain is **cascade scope**. A local loss has a cascade that can terminate at H1's boundary, because other habitats still supply the actor's function. A global loss has no such boundary: every habitat's dependency chain is engaged at once, and the effect that matters can be three steps out (Fig 11 :52-53, the DDT example). Valence is relative to the tracked function, so there is no standing list of species whose loss is always worse (Fig 11 :49-51).

### D2, source availability across closures, verdict: `triangulated`

This is the categorical break. Fig 6 :25: *"An import adds no atom to the world."* Fig 8 :32: *"Closure is idempotent. Composing inside a closed set never leaves it, so no search, however long, adds an atom."*

- **L:** the actor still exists elsewhere. Recolonization imports it into H1's closure without adding a new atom to the world. Cheap and repeatable.
- **G:** no closure holds the actor. There is nothing to import and nothing inside the remaining system that can compose to it.

Repair follows the same asymmetry. Fig 11 :61-62: *"Introduction is available; exclusion is not."* Reintroducing after L adds an actor to a habitat that is still there. Restoring after G is habitat substitution (Fig 11 :66-69), with failures that are invisible until the goal fails or the introduced actors act. At the operator level, L is a subtraction and G is a negation (`06_isomers_and_order.md`).

### D3, verification, verdict: no ground reading, `single-source` in this simulation

All seats here draw on one evidence pool. Fig 2 :40: *"agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool."* The world sources corroborate the shape, but no measurement was taken, and "global" remains `candidate` because no survey is a sealed room (Fig 8 :39, Fig 9 :44). This domain contributes no verdict of its own.

The three verdicts are not averaged. D2 supplies the categorical difference; D1 supplies the scope of consequences; D3 supplies the honesty condition.

## 3. What is explicitly not claimed

- Not that global extinction has a larger *scalar* consequence than local. The consequence is a vector, and which entries matter depends on the function tracked.
- Not that any particular species, function or habitat is named by the framework. It returns a shape, not a name (Fig 9 :32).
- Not that a global extinction is a local extinction at a bigger scale. Union, not scale (Fig 9 :54); subtraction versus negation (`06_isomers_and_order.md`).
- Not that "globally extinct" is proven by a failed survey. *"So a search that finds nothing is never a proof of absence"* (Fig 8 :39); the rediscovery record is real.
- Not that local extinction is harmless or easily reversed in practice. Reversibility is a structural possibility, not a guarantee; habitat destruction can remove the source (extinction debt and the loss of the insurance effect).
- Not `grounded`. Nothing here is asserted as settled by ecological measurement.

## 4. What would settle it — the ground

A source-discriminating measurement, not a further argument. From *The Hard Problem is About Source*: an instrument that reads green *"cannot comment on the source"*; *"The answer is unreached until a better instrument exists."* The corresponding checks here are:

1. **Cascade scope:** in matched local and global losses, does the global case engage additional dependency links three or more steps out, or is its cascade bounded exactly as the local one?
2. **Source availability:** is there a population elsewhere that still holds the actor at the time of the local loss? If not, the event was global in disguise.
3. **Function slots:** for each named function, does the loss remove the role, or does another actor with the same valence take it up? Measured per slot, never averaged.
4. **Debt and credit:** is the local debt paid back by immigration credit when the source is intact, and does it fail to be paid when the source is gone?
5. **Standing falsifiers:** a globally extinct species restored by natural immigration from a still-held closure; a context-free consequence ranking surviving a change of function; a closed-world survey entailing logical absence; a full restore with no inherited actors. Any one breaks the shape.

## 5. The honest stop

**Reached in shape, unreached in content.** The figures deliver the categorical structure: removal with a source versus removal of the source. They do not deliver which function declines, in which habitat, by how much, or after what delay. That is the gap in audit F9, stated plainly. *"Unreachable is a verdict, not a silence"* (Fig 13 :134). The world sources supply ecological content and corroborate the shape, but they cannot make this derivation a ground reading. The lineage is recorded so a later reader can locate the error rather than only feel it.

## 6. One-paragraph version for outreach

> The Tether does not rank extinctions by size. It says the difference is categorical: a local extinction removes a species from one habitat while other habitats still hold it, so recolonization is an import from a source that already exists and the cascade can stop at that habitat's boundary; a global extinction removes the source itself, so no import is possible and every habitat's dependency chain is engaged at once. Consequences are not one score but a vector over habitat, function and time, and the label "globally extinct" stays a candidate claim because no survey is a sealed room. What the framework offers is that shape, five falsifiers written in advance, and the measurements that would close it. The function-level content stays open until the ground settles it.
