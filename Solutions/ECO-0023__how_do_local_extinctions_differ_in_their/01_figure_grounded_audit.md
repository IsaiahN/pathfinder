## 0. The residual, stated in effect terms

An actor (a species) disappears from a habitat. Two removal operators are compared:

- **L, local extinction (extirpation):** the actor vanishes from habitat H1 while remaining in H2, H3, and so on.
- **G, global extinction:** the actor vanishes from every habitat at once.

What is being decided is *not* "how much" consequence follows, but what kind of difference separates L from G, and which consequences attach to each. An answer is binding if it (a) says what recolonization can and cannot repair, (b) says over which slots the consequences must be measured, and (c) states what would refute it.

**Direction, bound as an ordinary term.** The sought-for shape is written down here before any conclusion, per the Figures README's warning that a frame searching for a shape of answer holds that shape as a *preference* that can accrue no residual, and per `THE_FORMULA.md` "Direction": make the sought-for shape an *ordinary term*. Bound shape: **the difference is categorical and lives in the presence or absence of an external source that still holds the actor; consequences are a vector indexed by habitat, function and time, not a scalar.** It is refusable at §4.

## 1. Domain census, before choosing any figure line

### 1.1 R by arity, symmetry, scale

- **Arity:** two removal operators over a habitat-plus-actor structure. The comparison is not between two values of one quantity, because G is L performed in every habitat *with the source condition removed*. That is a **union compound** (Fig 9 :54), not a single hard question and not a partition.
- **Symmetry:** L and G are not interchangeable. Swapping them changes whether any source remains, which changes reversibility. The removal bond is ordered, not symmetric (Fig 12 :71-74, `→` sequence: *"A then B; B needs A to have happened"*).
- **Scale:** actor at population scale, currency at interaction scale, consequence at community scale, with a time delay (extinction debt) between removal and full effect. Three scales, so a membrane and round trip are owed (Fig 4 :30).

### 1.2 One line per domain: actors, currency, anchor

| domain | actors | currency | anchor | nameable? |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| D1 habitat and contact web | species populations; conditions (light, water, temperature, season, terrain); relations (pollination, predation, competition, dependency) | contact and cascade; valence relative to the residual; **per slot** | the ground, as measured function and persistence in a named habitat | yes, Fig 11 :45-47 |
| D2 source availability across closures | the actor as an atom still held in some habitats and absent in others; the removal operators L and G | presence or absence of a source from which an import can occur | whether any habitat still holds the actor (a world fact, not a frame opinion) | yes, Fig 6 :25-26, Fig 8 :32 |
| D3 verification and outreach | ecologists, experiments, long-term plots, museum and survey records | agreement and residual | the ground (measurement), never agreement | yes, Fig 2 :29 |

No domain is admitted without a nameable anchor. D1's anchor is a function measurement in a habitat. D2's anchor is the world fact of whether any individual of the species remains. D3's anchor is measurement. All three pass.

### 1.3 Partition or union

**Union.** G contains L everywhere plus the loss of the source condition. Fig 9 :54: *"And when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again."* The compound unbundles into (a) the local removal event and (b) the source-availability condition. A single lever on (a) leaves (b) standing. This is why "local and global are the same thing at different scales" fails: it conflates a removal with the destruction of the only source of replacement.

### 1.4 Dependency order (Fig 3)

1. Enumerate the habitat and its relations (perception and vocabulary). A reading below this break is a reading of nothing (Fig 3 :22-23).
2. Determine whether any source closure still holds the actor (Fig 6, Fig 8). This decides reversibility.
3. Only then assess consequences per function and per habitat (Fig 1 :19).
4. Verify against the ground (Fig 2 :45, *"Frames range against the ground. They do not negotiate with it."*).

Fig 1 :19-22 governs the measurement rule:

> **"The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated."**
> **"Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average."**

So "the ecological consequence of extinction" is not one number. It is a vector over (habitat, function, time).

## 2. Findings

### F1 — The habitat must be enumerated, never composed (`general`, triangulated)

Fig 11 :44-47:

> **"List everything in contact with the residual, then what is in contact with those, and outward until the cascade stops mattering."**
> **"actors: what acts on it, and what it acts on"**
> **"relations: pollination, predation, competition, dependency"**

Fig 11 :43: **"The habitat is enumerated, never composed."**

The list is read off the world, not invented (Fig 11 :48: *"You do not invent the list. You read it off the world, and what you cannot perceive or measure yet is the residual."*). For L, the list is bounded to H1 and its second-order contacts. For G, the same list must be made for every habitat, because the actor is gone from all of them. **Refuter:** show that the consequence set for either operator is composable from a fixed recipe independent of which actors and relations are actually in contact. That breaks enumeration.

### F2 — Contact cascades, so first-order counting is not the measurement (`specified`, triangulated)

Fig 11 :52-53:

> **"And contact cascades. Scoping the habitat to direct contact is how DDT reached the eagles: first-order enumeration, second-order effect, and the residual that mattered was three steps out."**

Fig 11 :73: **"The habitat is not the ground. The habitat is where actors come from and it updates; the ground is what settles, and it does not."**

A local loss can look harmless at first order and decisive three steps out; the DDT case is the figure's own example. **The difference between L and G in D1 is cascade scope:** for L the cascade can terminate at the boundary of H1, with other habitats still supplying the actor's function; for G there is no such boundary, so every habitat's dependency chain is hit at once. The consequence is not smaller or larger by a scalar, it is *structurally different in scope*. **Refuter:** a measured case where a global loss produces a cascade bounded exactly as a local loss in one habitat, with no additional dependencies engaged.

### F3 — Valence is relative to the residual; there is no fixed "good species" (`general`, triangulated)

Fig 11 :49-51:

> **"An actor's valence is relative to the residual, not a property it carries."**
> **"The same actor is a benefactor to one residual and a malfactor to another, so mutualism and parasitism are relations rather than kinds."**
> **"No standing list of good actors survives a change of residual."**

Combined with Fig 1 :19, this kills the question's hidden assumption that an extinction has one consequence. Whether a loss is a malfunction depends on which function is tracked. **Refuter:** produce a context-free ranking of species whose loss is always worse, independent of residual and habitat, that survives a change of residual.

### F4 — Reversibility is the categorical difference (`specified`, triangulated)

Fig 6 :25-26:

> **"An import adds no atom to the world."**
> **"The world does not gain primitives; a frame gains access."**

Fig 8 :32: **"Closure is idempotent. Composing inside a closed set never leaves it, so no search, however long, adds an atom."**

For **L**, the actor still exists elsewhere. Recolonization or reintroduction imports it into H1's closure. That import adds no atom to the world (Fig 6 :25), which is exactly why it is cheap and repeatable. The world source "Local extinction" states the corresponding ecological fact: populations *"may disappear from one habitat but be re-established due to individuals migrating from nearby populations."*

For **G**, no habitat holds the actor. There is no closure to import from, and search inside the remaining system cannot manufacture the atom (Fig 8 :32). **This is the categorical break: L is a change in one habitat with a live external source; G is the loss of the source itself.** **Refuter:** a documented case of a globally extinct species being restored by natural immigration from a population that was still within the same world closure. If that happens, G was misclassified and was really L.

### F5 — Introduction is available, exclusion is not (`general`, triangulated)

Fig 11 :61-62:

> **"The seat's power is asymmetric. Introduction is available; exclusion is not. You can add an actor and you cannot subtract the habitat, because contact is a fact about the world and does not consult you."**

This is the repair asymmetry. A local loss can be repaired by *adding* the actor back, because the habitat and its relations are still there. There is no symmetric operation that "un-removes" a global loss while leaving the rest of the world untouched: the only candidate is de-extinction, which is a **substituted habitat**, not a reversal. Fig 11 :66-69:

> **"Isolation is not removal of the habitat. It is substitution of one habitat for another."**
> **"Move the residual and you must reproduce everything its goal required, and you inherit the new habitat's actors, which you did not choose."**
> **"what you failed to reproduce is invisible until the goal fails"**
> **"what you unintentionally introduced is invisible until it acts"**

The world source "Local extinction" records the ecological side: *"Local extinctions can be reversed, in some cases artificially. Wolves are a species that have been reintroduced into parts of their historical range."* **Refuter:** a demonstrated global restoration that reproduces the full goal-required habitat with no inherited actors and no hidden failures. Until one exists, G's repair is categorically different from L's.

### F6 — The shape is per slot; averaging destroys the signal (`general`, single-source for the figure, corroborated by world)

Fig 1 :22: **"Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average."** Fig 1 :28: **"The gap is measured per object, not per step. A system that averages across objects has thrown away the only signal that says where to look."**

Fig 11 :46-47 gives the slots for ecology: conditions and relations. The world source "Functional extinction" defines the relevant slot explicitly: a species is functionally extinct when *"the reduced population no longer plays a significant role in ecosystem function."* So a species can be globally extant and locally functionally extinct, or locally present and functionally absent. **Refuter:** demonstrate that a single aggregate biodiversity metric is sufficient to rank the consequences of L and G for every function in every habitat.

### F7 — The distinction has an epistemic edge: global absence is never proved from inside (`specified`, triangulated)

Fig 8 :39: **"So a search that finds nothing is never a proof of absence."**

Fig 9 :44: **"Only a sealed room can be searched to the end, and no frame is a sealed room. So searching and finding nothing proves nothing."**

"Global extinction" is a claim about the whole world's closure. No survey is a sealed room, so the claim is always fallible. That is not a reason to deny global extinction; it is a reason to mark the classification as `candidate` and to keep the lineage. The world source Smith-Patten et al. 2015 documents exactly this failure: *"A substantial proportion (more than a third) of mammal species that have been classified as extinct or possibly extinct, or flagged as missing, have been rediscovered"* (quoted in that paper). Their own glossary makes the category distinction the audit needs: *"Extinction: irreversible species death / Extirpation: loss of a species in a specific area."* **Refuter:** a closed-world survey procedure whose negative result entails logical absence, not merely failure to observe.

### F8 — Debt and credit: consequences are delayed and can be reversed from the source side (`specified`, single-source for the figure family, corroborated by world)

Fig 11 :52-53 establishes the multi-step cascade. The world source "Extinction debt" adds the time structure: *"Extinction debt occurs because of time delays between impacts on a species, such as destruction of habitat, and the species' ultimate disappearance."* And the source can reverse it: *"Immigration credit is the corollary to extinction debt. It refers to the number of species likely to migrate to an area after an event such as the restoration of an ecosystem."*

So L can carry an extinction debt in H1 that is paid only if immigration credit fails; G has no immigration credit available. **Refuter:** a measured extinction debt in a habitat that is paid back at the same rate with and without a source population elsewhere.

## 3. Strain test and re-census

Three figure lines strained. None was smoothed over.

1. **Fig 11 :66, *"Isolation is not removal of the habitat. It is substitution of one habitat for another."*** Forced onto the extinction event itself, this strains: G removes an actor from all habitats; it does not substitute a habitat. **Re-census:** the line governs the *repair* operator (reintroduction, translocation, de-extinction), not the removal operator. It is filed under F5, not F1. After re-census there is no strain.
2. **Fig 6 :23, *"No frame escapes its own closure. The bound moves only on contact with a frame whose closure differs."*** A habitat is defined in the Symbols table as *"the field of contact"* and a frame as *"any bounded system with a library."* Calling a source population a "frame" strains the vocabulary. **Re-census:** the line is applied at the level of *closure*, the set of what a place can supply, which is licensed by Fig 4 :19-20: *"Only the actors, the currency and the anchor change."* The actors are populations, the currency is presence of the actor, the anchor is the world fact of survival. Recorded as a `specified` cross-level extension, not a `grounded` identification. The cleaner form of F4 uses Fig 6 :25-26 and Fig 8 :32, which need no frame vocabulary at all.
3. **Fig 8 :39 versus the ecological definition of global extinction.** The IUCN style definition requires exhaustive surveys to fail before a taxon is presumed extinct; Fig 8 :39 says a failed search proves nothing. **Re-census:** the figure governs what can be *proved from inside a closure*, not what the world is. Global extinction remains a fallible world claim. Recorded as F7, with the rediscovery literature as corroboration, not as a contradiction.

No figure line was made to govern a quantity it does not govern. The gap in F9 is stated plainly: the figures return a shape, not an ecological measurement. *"Unreachable is a verdict, not a silence"* (Fig 13 :134).

## 4. Pre-registered refuter

Written before any conclusion, per the standing rule that a falsifier written before the run is a design instrument. The shape claim is refuted if any of the following holds:

1. A globally extinct species is restored by natural immigration from a population still inside the same world closure (breaks F4, the categorical break).
2. A global loss produces a cascade bounded exactly as a local loss in a single habitat, with no additional dependencies engaged (breaks F2).
3. A context-free consequence ranking survives a change of residual and a change of habitat (breaks F3 and F1).
4. A closed-world survey procedure is produced whose negative result entails logical absence (breaks F7).
5. The full goal-required habitat is reproduced for a globally extinct species with no inherited actors and no hidden failures (breaks F5).

If any fires, the shape is withdrawn. Until one is run, nothing in this folder is `grounded`.

## 7. Verdict of the audit

The shape survives; the ecological measurement does not. Local and global extinctions differ **categorically, not by degree**: L is a removal from one habitat with a live external source, so its cascade can be bounded and its loss can be repaired by import; G removes the source itself, so every habitat's dependency chain is engaged and no import can restore the actor. Consequences are a vector over habitat, function and time (Fig 1 :19), and "global" is a fallible world claim (Fig 8 :39). After re-census, **one live strain remains and it is named**: the closure-level reading of source availability in F4 is a `specified` cross-level extension of Fig 6 :23, not a `grounded` identification. Strains resolved by re-census: three. Gaps: one (which functions change, and by how much, in a named system). Mode of everything here: `general` for shape, `specified` for prediction, never `grounded`.
