## 1. A shape is a constraint, not a filler

The figures README states the limit: the framework returns *"what a missing thing would have to look like (a shape, not a name)."* Fig 9 :32: *"you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself."* Fig 13: *"It can say the answer is out of reach with the library as it stands... It cannot say which primitive is missing, because naming it would mean already holding it."*

From *The Library of Babel* (read directly this session): *"you can cut your search short if the set of books returned is small and descriptive enough that it provides a general shape of the answer that satisfies your question."*

So a **shape** is the narrowed form of an answer you can state before you can name it. It constrains; it does not fill. For this question:

- A shape: "redundancy buys independence, sized to the cost of being wrong, indexed per slot, staged as reversible cuts."
- A name: "hold six weeks of inventory and two suppliers, one in Malaysia."

The first is checkable before the ground speaks. The second is a verdict stated in `general` mode against a ground that has not moved (Fig 2 :29).

## 2. How a shape is obtained

- *Library of Babel*: use the vocabulary you already have that is *closest* to the thing, look for recurring new terms that overlap or correlate with it, recurse. *"With each new term gained, you are able to narrow the space by definition and deepen your vocabulary."*
- Fig 6 :30: *"The description travels. Fails by being sharpened into terms only we can read. Cast it in effect terms."* Fig 9 :30-31: *"your words are borrowed too, so describe the effect rather than the cause."*
- *Decoding Tacit Knowledge*: *"When contact isn't possible, the best you can send is a plain description of what you'd expect to see, because anyone can check that against the world."*
- *Library of Babel* on stopping: *"you will know that you are done because the finish line will also be the starting place"*, and the paradox is that by then you have *"composed the answer through the process of locating it."*

Applying this to BUS-0074: the closest available vocabulary is "spare capacity," "buffer inventory," "dual sourcing," "reserve capacity," "common-cause failure," "response diversity." The shape is obtained by narrowing on the effect each of these actually produces (independence per slot, not duplication of a number).

## 3. The bundled question is the specific trap here

*The Library of Babel* names this exact failure, in the context of an exhaustive search that fails: *"Perhaps your problem was a bundle or conflation of several problems masquerading as one? You will need to search all of those problems individually to find their answers and then combine them in a satisfactory way."*

Fig 9 :54-57 gives the figure form: *"And when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again."* and *"A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones."* and *"What remains is smaller and it is also categorically different: a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either."*

"How much redundancy is worth paying for" is such a bundle: it conflates (a) which slot is live, (b) whether the channel to the ground is maintained, (c) whether reach is renewed by a differing closure, and (d) whether the cut is reversible. Searching for one number across all four is the missing-library error. This is why the answer is a shape over the union, not a scalar.

## 4. What licenses a name

Only two things, both outside the shape itself:

1. **The ground settles it.** Formula step 5: *"the ground settles it. Verified only by what pays."* The README: the framework *"stops at the limit of what the available instruments can distinguish, and records the lineage so the stop is honest."* For BUS-0074 the ground is a disruption that has not yet occurred, so this route is currently closed.
2. **A provenanced import from a frame whose closure differs** (Fig 6 :23, Fig 8 :50-52), with a source gate and a shadow test. A specific supplier, a specific geography, a specific buffer level is a binding from the supply frame. Fig 8 :50-52: *"Convergent derivation and adopted import look identical in the contents. Only the record of where each came from separates them."*

*They're Scanning Books* sharpens this: *"The real import is not a new term in the existing space. It is a new dimension of the space."* And: *"A minimum viable set of six terms is a human's careful guess at reality's compression axes; another sensorium's minimum set need not be those six."* Also: *"Declaring the primitives does not make them neutral. It makes the choice visible."*

The practical consequence: a firm's "risk map" is a visible guess, not nature's own list. Naming one supplier as "the redundant one" turns a visible choice into an invisible one unless the provenance is recorded.

## 5. Why "no disruptions yet" is the metamerism error

*The Hard Problem is About Source* gives the mechanism. Metamerism: an instrument reads green and *"cannot comment on the source"* - one green light, or yellow+blue, or RGB, or CMYK. The reading does not determine the source. *"If several paths create the same behavior, then behavior is a projection with lossy information."* And: *"An instrument that can't distinguish these cases demonstrates its own inadequacy."* It was the spectrometer, not an argument, that resolved it.

For BUS-0074 the clean-history reading is a metamerism error: a quiet period is compatible with (a) genuinely low hazard, (b) an unrecorded sub-tier failure that was absorbed, (c) a near miss nobody logged, (d) a hazard that simply has not arrived yet. The reading does not determine the source. What is owed is a better instrument (visibility several tiers out), not the name "low risk."

*The Recursive Self-Improvement Trap* (read directly this session): *"the output is only as good as the validator. The agent, or the tool, or the frame, or the theory, cannot validate itself. An external validator, or ground, is always needed to tether the veracity of the output to reality."*

## 6. Correction to my own reading of "shape, not a name"

I initially read the rule as forbidding the naming of any specific redundancy arrangement. That was too strong. The README's phrase is *"what a **missing** thing would have to look like (a shape, not a name)."* It governs a primitive not held in Gamma. "Independent closure," "per-slot residual," "reversible cut" are not missing primitives; they are bindings onto held structural terms (Fig 11 habitat, Fig 1 slot, Fig 9 cut). Naming them is permitted. What remains forbidden is naming the *missing* thing: the specific source or buffer level that the ground has not settled and that is not derivable from what the firm holds. Both the correct and the overstated forms are recorded here so neither is quietly lost.

## 7. Prestige is not action, and this audit is a simulation

**Prestige is not action.** From *The Recursive Self-Improvement Trap*: *"Prestige must never be allowed to validate an action directly."* And: *"Prestige can fund an action. It can give an action a budget to be tried. But it cannot settle an action. Only the ground can settle it. The Ground Maintainer must be blind to prestige."*

A list of well-known supply-chain academics who agree with the shape is the named collapse, not corroboration. The legitimate ask gives the claim a budget to be tried and lets the ground, a disruption or a controlled stress test, settle it.

**My own audit is a simulation, not a ground reading.** Same essay: *"LLMs can quickly simulate an approximate answer/output with the formula found in the architecture post... However, the truly testable output would need to be derived from the real architecture or working the formula yourself."* So `01` is a simulated derivation, checked for internal consistency against the figures. It is **not** a test of the claim and must be labelled as such. It cannot settle anything it reports on.
