# Common-cause twins, independent closures, and the two-currency price

**Mode: `specified`.** This is a simulated derivation checked against the figures, not a ground reading (see `05_what_shape_means.md` section 7).

## RESIDUAL

Two "redundant" sources are counted as two in a firm's risk model, yet they fail together in a disruption. Two questions are owed: (1) under Fig 12's bond definitions, are the two sources isomers (same atoms, different bond, different substance) or are they one term with two spellings, and (2) what is the dependency order that determines which redundancy investment can actually pay?

## DERIVATION

### Q1 - Are two shared-cause sources one term or two?

- Fig 2 :31-32: *"Independence, between two frames, is how little they draw on the same evidence. Two people reading the same report are not two frames; two instruments pointed at the same thing are."*
- Fig 8 :48-49: *"How much two frames draw on the same evidence decides both whether their agreement means anything and whether their union has any surplus. Two frames that overlap almost completely are one frame wearing two names."*
- Under a symmetric bond, Fig 12 :92: *"Symmetry is what decides whether an arrangement is a new thing."* Two names for one referent is the identity bond: Operators table :27: *"substitute one for the other. Is anything lost? No."*

Result: two sources that share a port, a sub-tier supplier, an infrastructure base and a maintenance regime are bonded by identity (≡) in the risk model even though the model records them as separate atoms. Fig 12 :96: *"Two entries with an identical ingredient set are not variants of one thing. They are different things whose notation cannot tell them apart, and the difference is carried entirely by the bond."* Here the converse bites: the notation records two things that are one thing, because the bond (shared cause) was not recorded. This is Fig 12 :5: *"A set of ingredients is not one. The bond is the rest of the information, and it is the half nothing was recording."*

**Falsifier, written before the run:** if two sources with a documented shared cause fail independently in a real event, the identity reading is refuted and they must be counted as two frames. This is refuter 1 in `01` section 4.

Status: `triangulated` (Fig 2 and Fig 8, low overlap), corroborated at the ground by the NASA/RAMS 2025 common-cause paper.

### Q2 - What would make them genuine isomers (two substances, not one)?

- Fig 12 :117: *"isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."*
- Fig 12 :124: *"functional group. A substructure whose presence fixes the outcome. The group says what kind; the qualifier says which member."*

If two candidate sources are written over the same functional role (both can supply the component) but sit in **differing closures** (different geography, different sub-tier base, different infrastructure, different regulatory regime), they are isomers in the figure's sense: same role, different arrangement, different failure behaviour. Their union has a surplus because the closures differ (Fig 8 :45: *"Two frames together reach what neither reaches alone."*).

If they share the closure, they are the same substance under two names (Q1), and the union adds nothing: Fig 8 :14: *"their union adds nothing"* and Fig 8 :49: *"one frame wearing two names."*

Status: `triangulated` for the definition (Fig 12 and Fig 8); `specified`, unmeasured for any particular pair of suppliers. The business supply-base check is a binding, not a derivation.

**Falsifier:** if adding a source from a demonstrably differing closure does not expand the reachable set in a real event, the isomer-surplus reading fails. This is refuter 2 in `01` section 4.

### Q3 - Why expected value is the flat-formula error

- Fig 12 :125: *"A composition costs in two currencies, and they do not add."*
- Fig 12 :126: *"Bits price the description. Actions price finding out whether it holds."*
- Fig 12 :130: *"Summing them into one figure is the error this names, not the fix for it. A price in bits and a price in actions are two readings, held apart."*

The expected-loss form p times C multiplies a probability of an event by the cost of the event and treats the product as "the" answer. Under Fig 12 that is the flat formula: it records ingredients and loses the arrangement. It also conflates two different costs that the figure prices in different units:

| reading | unit | question it answers |
|---|---|---|
| cost of stating the risk (description) | bits | how much of the network's state can the model hold? |
| cost of finding out whether a source is independent (action) | budget | what does it cost to audit a sub-tier, run a stress test, qualify a new source? |

Fig 12 :6-8 names the collapse: *"collapse 6: the flat formula"* where *"the ingredients are recorded and the arrangement is not"* and *"nothing downstream can tell them apart, and nothing signals that anything was lost."*

Status: `triangulated` (Fig 12 two-currency rule and the Operators pricing rule). The business expected-value literature conflicts; the conflict was returned to the census and did not flip the verdict.

**Falsifier:** a figure derivation licensing one currency for this decision. This is refuter 4 in `01` section 4.

### Q4 - What is the order of the union? Dependency, not importance

- Fig 3 :20-21: *"The chain is ordered by dependency, not by importance. Each link consumes what the one above produces, so effort spent above the break arrives at a step that cannot use it."*
- Fig 3 :24: *"Why the middle link is where chains usually break."* Fig 3 :25-26: perception and planning have obvious tests; the link between them (terms for what a situation affords, and what would count as progress) has neither.

Applied here, the dependency order is:

1. **Perception:** can the firm see the slot at all, including tier two and tier three? (Fig 11 :43-53)
2. **Vocabulary:** does it have terms for what a disruption affords (what can fail, be buffered, rerouted, substituted)? This is the middle link with no instrument (Fig 3 :27).
3. **Objective:** what counts as progress, stated in its own terms, e.g. "the slot's R stays small under a documented disruption" rather than "we bought two suppliers."
4. **Planning:** the redundancy spend, chosen under incomplete information.
5. **Learn and carry:** name what worked, verify it, carry it to the next slot. (Fig 3 :15)

Most redundancy money is spent at step 4 while step 2 is unmeasured. Fig 3 :19: *"Optimising a link that already works cannot rescue one that does not."* A reading taken below the break is a reading of nothing (Fig 3 :22-23).

**Falsifier:** a case where the spend at step 4 pays without steps 1 to 3, with measured R staying small per slot through a documented disruption. If shown, the dependency order is wrong for this domain.

### Q5 - Is the cut reversible?

- Fig 9 :40-41: *"a wrong cut removes the answer and speeds up"* / *"so keep every cut ranked and reversible"*.
- Fig 11 :61: *"Introduction is available; exclusion is not. You can add an actor and you cannot subtract the habitat, because contact is a fact about the world and does not consult you."*

A redundancy investment is a cut in the space of possible supply configurations. Because the ground has not spoken, the cut must be ranked and reversible where possible. It is only partly undoable: adding a second supplier introduces an actor and inherits its habitat; the firm cannot subtract the contact. So the honest report is "largely undoable, with a residual commitment," not "fully reversible."

**Falsifier:** a redundancy cut that is fully reversible with no inherited habitat and no residual commitment. If common, the undoability caveat is relaxed.

## CONCLUSION

Two "redundant" sources sharing a closure are an identity bond wearing two names, not Fig 12 isomers. They become genuine isomers only when the closures differ, and that difference is what creates the union surplus. The expected-value product is the flat-formula error because it sums two currencies the figure holds apart. The dependency order puts visibility and vocabulary before the spend, and the cut must be staged as ranked and reversible, with the honest caveat that an introduced source brings a habitat that cannot be subtracted. This derivation rescues the checkable structural content without naming a specific redundancy number or a specific supplier.
