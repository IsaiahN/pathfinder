## What the wider literature bears on the question

The following were opened 2026-11-22. They are secondary corroboration only and never overrule a figure line. Full provenance and quotes are in `10_source_ledger.csv` and the WORLD section of `09_watson_answer.md`.

1. **Business / supply chain.** Sunil Chopra and ManMohan S. Sodhi, *"Reducing the Risk of Supply Chain Disruptions,"* MIT Sloan Management Review, Spring 2014. The article says underestimating disruption likelihood is "far more expensive in the long run than overestimating the likelihood," and that "the goal of this thought exercise is to identify not the probability of disruption but the potential sources of disruption." Predicts: size to the cost of being wrong and map sources, not probabilities. Corroborates F4, F7 and F9 in effect. Overlap with the figure verdict is low (different field), but the conclusion is decision-theoretic, not a figure line.
2. **Reliability engineering.** *"Common Cause Failures Dominate and Defeat Redundancy,"* NASA NTRS / Annual Reliability and Maintainability Symposium (RAMS), 2025. Abstract: "No amount of redundancy can reduce the total failure probability below the common cause failure probability. Common cause failures defeat redundancy." Predicts: identical redundant units share a failure floor, so headcount overstates independence. Corroborates F3 and F6 at the ground. This is independent of the framework and is the strongest low-overlap witness.
3. **Reliability engineering (tutorial).** RePyability, *"When redundancy disappoints,"* 2025, https://derrynknife.github.io/RePyability/learn/dependence/. "Independence flatters redundancy" and the beta-factor model give a shared-cause floor. Corroborates F3. Overlaps with source 2 in evidence pool (both reliability engineering), so these two count as one witness.
4. **Ecology.** Biggs et al., *"Does functional redundancy affect ecological stability and resilience? A review and meta-analysis,"* Ecosphere 11(7), 2020. Mean effect of functional redundancy on stability/resilience was positive but modest (mean r = 0.18, 95% CI -0.02 to 0.37) and evidence was thin (32 studies). Predicts: functional overlap buffers against loss but the empirical link is weak. Corroborates F3 and F10 in shape and refuses a strong numerical claim. Independent field.
5. **Human cognition.** Amos Tversky and Daniel Kahneman, *"Judgment under Uncertainty: Heuristics and Biases,"* Science 185(4157), 1974. "the risk involved in an undertaking may be grossly underestimated if some possible dangers are either difficult to conceive of, or simply do not come to mind." Predicts: managers underweight unimagined disruptions, so a clean history is unreliable. Corroborates F7. Independent field.
6. **Decision theory / mathematics.** DNB Working Paper 487, *"Decision making in times of uncertainty: An info-gap perspective,"* 2016. "Frank Knight (1921) distinguished between 'risk' (for which probability distributions are known) and 'true uncertainty' (for which probability distributions are not known)." Predicts: when p is unknown, expected-value optimisation is unavailable and robust-satisficing or min-max is used. Corroborates F1 and F4 and conflicts with the expected-value form in F5. Independent field.
7. **Resilience engineering.** Erik Hollnagel, *"The Four Cornerstones of Resilience Engineering,"* in Resilience Engineering Perspectives Volume 2, CRC Press, 2009, and David Woods, 2006. Resilience is the ability to adjust functioning before, during and after the unexpected; Woods: effective organizations "create safety through proactive resilient processes rather than through reactive barriers and defenses." Predicts: resilience is maintained adaptive work, not a purchased buffer. Corroborates F10. Independent field.

**Conflict handling.** Source 6 and the standard business expected-value literature conflict with F5 as written. Per the WATSON rule, the conflict was returned to the domain census (two currencies, Fig 12 :125-130) and did not flip the verdict. Sources 2 and 3 overlap in evidence pool and count as one witness. Sources 1 and 4 and 5 and 6 and 7 are low-overlap fields and corroborate the mechanism, not the business-specific fact.

## Distribution: offer, do not push

Figure-grounded reasons:

- **Fig 4 :22-25** - *"Only methods cross up. Only head starts come down. A recording carried upward looks like knowledge and is a description of one occasion."* Sending the full architecture before anyone has described a residual sends a recording, not a generator. Lead with the shape claim and the refuter; offer the links after.
- **Fig 10 :42** - *"A verifier that can reconstruct the claim cannot verify it."* A recipient who has absorbed the entire framework before being asked for a verdict has joined the frame being checked.
- **Fig 9 :27-28** - *"describe it before you go looking, or any frame you pick will seem to fit."* Ask what they would expect to see if the shape claim were true before giving them the article.
- **Seat Map :163** - *"High correlation is disqualifying for a verdict and efficient for a convention."* A mass send selected for people who already agree produces a convention, not corroboration.

**Recommended order in a reply:** (1) the shape claim and its four refuters; (2) ask what they would count as a failure, including whether they have seen common-cause twins fail independently; (3) then the framework links, with the note that the figures, not the summary, are the authority.

## The two-stage offer

1. **Stage 1:** validate the shape claim and the refuter against the ground, with no theory shown. Record the verdict before stage 2.
2. **Stage 2, opt-in only:** if curious, offer the three framework links. Later agreement is never pooled as corroboration (Fig 2 :40 - *"disqualifying for a verdict: agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool"*).
3. **Stage 3:** offer the formula as a method to reuse, with the instruction that each user states their own ground and writes their own refuter before running it (`THE_FORMULA.md` "Direction").

**Hard precondition:** stages 1 to 3 only work if the stage-1 output survives. It is a shape and a decision rule, and it must not be smuggled out as a number. If a recipient reads it as "the framework says hold two suppliers," the output has failed and the packet must be corrected before any further send.
