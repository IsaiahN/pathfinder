# The Tether's answer on redundancy under uncertainty

**Mode: `specified` - not `grounded`.** This is a shape and a route, not a result. Simulated derivation checked against the figures (`05_what_shape_means.md` section 7), not a ground reading.

## 1. The residual it answers

A firm's operating model predicts continuity Gamma(b,a). A future disruption returns o-prime, an interruption. R = |Gamma(b,a) - o'| is indexed per slot (supplier, component, route, region, tier), never averaged (Fig 1 :19, Fig 1 :22). The question of how much redundancy is worth paying for asks for a decision before the ground (the disruption) has returned a reading, with both p and C uncertain.

It is a **union compound**, not one hard question. Fig 9 :55: *"A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones."* The four conditions are: which slots are live; whether the channel to the ground is maintained; whether reach is renewed by a differing closure; whether the cut is reversible. It has its own loop and its own ground.

## 2. The shape

1. **No number is settled.** The ground is a future disruption and has not spoken. Fig 2 :29: *"An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks."* Any specific "hold X weeks of inventory" is a `general`-mode verdict with no ground reading. The framework returns a shape, not a name (Fig 9 :32).

2. **Index per slot, never as a portfolio average.** Fig 1 :22: *"Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average."* The single-source component in one port is a positive-mass slot; an expected-loss average across the network hides exactly that slot.

3. **Redundancy buys independence, not headcount.** Fig 2 :31-32: *"Independence, between two frames, is how little they draw on the same evidence."* Fig 8 :49: *"Two frames that overlap almost completely are one frame wearing two names."* A second supplier sharing a port, sub-tier source, infrastructure and maintenance regime is one failure mode with two labels. Count independent closures, not vendors.

4. **Size the independence to the cost of being wrong, not to a point estimate of p.** Fig 2 :42: *"Buy independence in proportion to the cost of being wrong."* This is the only figure line that functions directly as a decision rule, and it deliberately is not p times C. A catastrophic, unbracketable hazard warrants substantial independence even if a clean history offers no probability estimate. Fig 8 :39: *"So a search that finds nothing is never a proof of absence."*

5. **Hold probability and cost as two readings.** Fig 12 :125: *"A composition costs in two currencies, and they do not add."* Fig 12 :130: *"Summing them into one figure is the error this names, not the fix for it."* The expected-loss product is the flat-formula error: it records ingredients and loses the arrangement.

6. **Stage the spend as ranked, reversible cuts.** Fig 9 :41: *"so keep every cut ranked and reversible."* Because the ground has not spoken, preserve the ability to revise. The cut is only partly undoable: an introduced source brings a habitat that cannot be subtracted (Fig 11 :61).

7. **Enumerate the habitat outward.** Fig 11 :43: *"The habitat is enumerated, never composed."* Fig 11 :52-53: the material residual is often *"three steps out."* First-tier maps are first-order enumeration. Fig 11 :64: *"a managed ecosystem is permanent work rather than a solved state."* Resilience is maintained work, not a purchased state.

## 3. What it explicitly does not say

- Not an optimal number. No weeks of inventory, no count of suppliers, no premium percentage is asserted.
- Not that the framework names which supplier to dual-source. That is a binding from the supply frame onto a held slot; it needs a provenance gate and a shadow test (Fig 8 :50-52).
- Not that expected value is useless as an accounting convenience. It is refused as a *verdict-generating* rule, not as a budget filter. Fig 9 :60: *"Use filters for the budget and witnesses for the verdict. Never let a filter hand you a verdict."*
- Not that "no disruptions yet" means low risk (F7), and not that all redundancy is justified.
- Not grounded. No disruption has settled `left(R,phi)` for any specific spend.

## 4. What would settle it - the ground

A disruption, or a controlled stress test that returns a real reading, not a further argument. *The Hard Problem is About Source*: an instrument that reads green *"cannot comment on the source"*; the spectrometer resolved it. Here the corresponding questions are:

- Do two "independent" sources share a hidden cause? A real event in which common-cause twins fail independently would falsify the common-cause reading (refuter 1).
- Does adding a source from a demonstrably differing closure expand the reachable set when a disruption hits (refuter 2)?
- Is a clean history a sufficient statistic for p in this class of systems (refuter 5)?
- Can the two currencies be added under the figure's own rule (refuter 4)?
- Standing falsifiers from `01` section 4: a figure proof that a number is settled without a ground reading; a proof that an identical duplicate expands the closure; a proof that one lever clears all four union conditions with a non-categorical residue.

## 5. The honest stop

**Unreached, not unreachable.** The ground will eventually speak: a disruption will occur, and when it does the bracket can be settled per slot. Until then the verdict is `candidate`. The six-term substrate and the figures are *"a human's careful guess at reality's compression axes"*; declaring them makes the choice visible, not neutral. The answer stops at the limit of the available instruments and records the lineage so the stop is checkable. *"Indiscernibility is not identity."*

## 6. One-paragraph version for outreach

> The Tether does not give a number for how much redundancy to buy, because the ground, a future disruption, has not returned a reading and a number would be a verdict in the wrong mode. Instead it gives a shape. The gap between predicted and actual supply is indexed per supplier, component, route, region and tier, so a portfolio average hides the live slot. Redundancy buys independence, not headcount: a second source sharing a port or a sub-tier supplier is one failure mode with two names. Buy that independence in proportion to the cost of being wrong, not to a point estimate of disruption probability, since a clean history is a search that found nothing and is not proof of a low probability. Hold probability and cost as two readings rather than multiplying them into one expected loss, map dependencies several tiers out, and stage the spend as ranked, reversible cuts. The result is a checkable shape with refuters written in advance, held as a candidate until the ground settles it.
