## 1. The residual, in effect terms

The question asks what separates two regimes of the same ingredients. In effect terms: given many local interactions, does the **macro-slot residual** trend down and stay down, so a large-scale order persists, or does the remainder re-enter and amplify, so the macro state keeps changing? The thing to be decided is not "which mechanism is famous" but what **conditions** make the first outcome obtain.

Write the sought-for shape as an ordinary term before searching (`THE_FORMULA.md` "Direction"): an answer would look like a set of conditions C such that, when C holds, the residual on the macro slot trends down toward a stable value, and when C fails, it persists or grows. Binding C to a critical temperature or a coupling constant is a name, not a shape, and would require a ground reading. This is recorded here so it can accrue a residual like anything else and be refused by the same test.

**Arity / symmetry / scale (Fig 9 :22):**

- **Arity:** many-to-one. N local interactions compose into one or a few macro order parameters. The question is about a composition, not a sum.
- **Symmetry:** local rules are typically exchange- and translation-symmetric (the same rule for every agent and site). A stable large-scale order is a **symmetry breaking**: the many symmetric spellings collapse to one ordered term. Fig 12 :92 governs this: under a symmetric bond, k operands have k! spellings and one term.
- **Scale:** at least two, the local step and the macro level, with a membrane and a crossing between them (Fig 4). The question is a *cross-scale* question, not a single-scale one.

## 4. Findings

### F1 - Randomness and order come from the same alternation (decisive, triangulated)

Fig 13 :28:

> **"They alternate, and the alternation is where both the recursion and the randomness come from."**

Fig 13 :31-32:

> **"a remainder compounding through a chain that amplifies is chaos, and the detail a crossing discards reappears above it as noise."**

This is the closest the figures come to the question's own terms. Order and fluctuation are not two different stuffs. They are two regimes of one cross-kind alternation, distinguished by **what happens to the remainder at each turn**. If the remainder is discarded or compressed so that the macro residual trends down, the chain closes into order. If the remainder compounds through an amplifying chain, the same alternation yields chaos, and what a crossing discards reappears above it as noise (fluctuation).

The condition is therefore about the **gain on the remainder across the crossing**, not about a substance. This finding is triangulated: Fig 13 supplies the substrate mechanism and Fig 4 supplies the cross-scale loss rule (F2). The world corroborates independently: *Self-organization* (Wikipedia) says order is *"often triggered by seemingly random fluctuations, amplified by positive feedback"*; *Dissipative system* (Wikipedia) says *"If the system is driven beyond a certain threshold, oscillations are no longer damped out, but may be amplified."*

### F2 - Stable order is a derivative, and the round trip must be measured (triangulated)

Fig 4 :22: *"Only methods cross up. Only head starts come down."*
Fig 4 :30:

> **"So take the round trip and measure the gap. Without it you are navigating by dead reckoning: confident about a position nobody checked."**

Fig 4 :34-35:

> **"The level a method lands on remakes it. A seed carries no small tree: it carries what will grow one, and the conditions it lands in decide what it becomes."**

A stable large-scale order is not a primitive that sits at the top. It is what a local method grows into under the conditions it lands in. The transform from local to macro is lossy, and the loss is the point: going up throws away detail, which is what makes the result re-applicable; coming back down does not reproduce what was sent. So any claim of stable order owes a round-trip gap R_T, measured rather than assumed zero. Treating "the macro pattern is just the sum of local rules" sets R_T to zero and is the dead-reckoning error Fig 4 :30 names.

World corroboration: *Swarm behaviour* (Wikipedia) reports that from the modeller's view it is *"an emergent behaviour arising from simple rules that are followed by individuals and does not involve any central coordination"*; the boids result is that order emerges *"even though there is no centralized coordination, and even though the neighbours for each particle constantly change over time."* The macro state is a derivative, not an ingredient.

### F3 - The condition is a property of agent-and-habitat, not of the agents alone (triangulated)

Fig 11 :39:

> **"capability is a property of agent-and-habitat, never of the agent alone."**

Fig 11 :40:

> **"An improvement that does not change contact changes nothing, however much it improves."**

Fig 11 :36:

> **"Where nothing maintains anything, intelligence is a label applied afterwards to a lineage that met its ground."**

Applied here: "the local rules produce order" is the wrong unit of analysis. The conditions that decide order versus fluctuation live in the **agent-and-habitat**: the coupling, the noise, the density, the network topology, the dimensionality, the availability of energy. Changing only the agents while leaving contact with these conditions unchanged does not move the regime. Where nothing maintains anything, "the system self-organizes" is a label applied afterwards to a lineage that met its ground; *"nothing selects; what survives is what the ground kept"* (Fig 11 :14-15).

World corroboration: *Ising model* (Wikipedia) has the *same* local rule (nearest-neighbour spins) and *"no phase transition in one dimension"*, but *"undergoes a phase transition between an ordered and a disordered phase in 2 dimensions or more."* Dimensionality, a habitat property, decides the regime. *Self-organized criticality* (Wikipedia): *"to date there is no known set of general characteristics that guarantee a system will display SOC."* The literature itself refuses a single name, matching the shape-not-a-name limit.

### F4 - Persistence requires variation inside and selection between (triangulated)

Fig 7 :33: **"variation inside, selection between"**
Fig 7 :35:

> **"A room's agreement is variation, not a verdict. Selection between rooms is where the ground votes."**

Fig 11 :14-15: **"nothing selects; what survives is what the ground kept"**

A single pattern agreeing with itself is variation, not a verdict about stability. To say an order is *stable* is to say it survived selection against competing patterns under the same conditions, and that the ground, not popularity, kept it. So a condition for stable order is a **pool of competing patterns with low overlap** plus a selecting ground. Fig 2 :31: *"Independence, between two frames, is how little they draw on the same evidence."* Ten copies of one pattern are one pattern under ten names.

World corroboration: *Synchronization* (Wikipedia) reports that *"an abrupt transition from unsynchronized to full synchronization takes place when the coupling strength exceeds a critical threshold"* (the Kuramoto transition). The framework essay *How a Society of Agents Would Work* gives the direct complex-systems analogue: *"A group is only worth having while its members stay different"* and, on collapse, *"everything drifts together, and the drift is coherent, which is why nobody notices."*

### F5 - Grounded order needs an anchor that does not update (triangulated)

Fig 2 :33: **"The anchor must not update."**
Fig 2 :35: **"Alignment is triangulation, not negotiation. A mirror cannot correct you."**

A common failure is to call mutual alignment "order": agents update on each other, converge, and the macro state looks stable. Fig 2 names this collapse 1 (mutual update, coherent drift): they converge on *"whoever had the most influence."* That is not stable large-scale order in the grounded sense; it is a mirror chain. A condition on any answer is that the apparent order range against something that does not move when the agents move. Fig 7 :13: *"agreement compounds, evidence does not."*

World corroboration: *The Hard Problem is About Source* (Isaiah N) gives the discriminator principle: *"If several paths create the same behavior, then behavior is a projection with lossy information"*, and the resolution came *"not by argument, but by the arrival of a better instrument."* An instrument that reads order but cannot discriminate the source cannot tell grounded order from drift.

### F6 - The sign of the loop decides whether order closes (triangulated)

Fig 12 :121:

> **"cycle. The output re-enters as input. The sign of the loop decides whether it closes, and a chain cannot close by construction."**

Fig 12 :119: *"catalyst. A part that survives its own use."*
Fig 12 :125: *"A composition costs in two currencies, and they do not add."*

Local interactions produce order through **feedback loops whose output re-enters as input** (cycles), not through open chains. The sign of the loop is decisive: positive feedback amplifies a fluctuation into a pattern; negative feedback damps it and restores the fixed point (F1). A catalyst, a part that survives its own use, is why a small local trace (a pheromone trail, a stigmergic mark) can stabilise a large pattern. World corroboration: *Swarm behaviour* defines stigmergy as *"the trace left in the environment by an action stimulates the performance of a next action, by the same or a different agent"*, producing *"the spontaneous emergence of coherent, apparently systematic activity."* The trace is the catalyst; the loop sign decides the regime.

### F7 - Measure the residual per slot, never as a global average (single-source within the framework)

Fig 1 :19: *"The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated."*
Fig 1 :22: **"Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average."**

"Stable large-scale order" is tempting to measure as one global order parameter. The figures forbid averaging away the slots: a global residual near zero with one live slot is a legal state (Fig 1 :13). A fluctuation that is dying in most places and growing in one is invisible to a global average, and it is exactly the live slot that decides whether order holds. Marked **single-source** within the framework (Fig 1) but corroborated in method by the essay *How a Society of Agents Would Work*: *"Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average."*

### F8 - The framework returns a shape, not a critical number (triangulated)

Canonical usage rule: the framework reports *"what a missing thing would have to look like (a shape, not a name)"*, then *"stops at the limit of what the available instruments can distinguish, and records the lineage so the stop is honest. Indiscernibility is not identity."*
Fig 9 :32: *"you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself."*

So this audit will not name the temperature or coupling at which a given system orders. It gives the structural conditions any such answer must satisfy and the instruments that would bind them. The world literature agrees that no general guarantee is known (*Self-organized criticality*: *"no known set of general characteristics that guarantee a system will display SOC"*; *Synchronization*: transition at a threshold whose value is system-specific). Naming a universal number would be a name where the framework returns a shape, and a verdict in `general` mode.

## 5. Pre-registered refuter

Required before the conclusion and before any outreach: the falsifier is a design instrument. Written here in advance.

The condition-shape is refuted if any of the following holds:

1. A case is shown of stable large-scale order from local interactions with **no cross-kind composition** (a pure same-kind aggregation with no structure in the slot), and the framework must admit it (breaks Fig 13 :74-75).
2. A case is shown where the remainder **amplifies** through the scale-crossing chain (the chaos regime) yet the macro residual still settles durably to stable order (breaks Fig 13 :31-32).
3. A case is shown where a stable order persists with **no selection between competing patterns and no non-updating ground**, and pure coherent drift counts as grounded order (breaks Fig 7 :35 and Fig 2 :33).
4. A case is shown where changing only the agents, with **contact with the habitat conditions unchanged**, moves the macro regime (breaks Fig 11 :40).
5. A **single general theorem naming the exact critical conditions** for an arbitrary system is derived and settled from the figures with no ground reading (breaks the shape-not-a-name limit, F8).

If any fires, the condition-shape is wrong or overclaimed and is withdrawn or narrowed accordingly. Until one is run, no part of this may be labelled `grounded`.
