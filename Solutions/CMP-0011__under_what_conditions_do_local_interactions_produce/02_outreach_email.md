**Subject:** Check one output about order versus fluctuation before you decide whether the shape is worth reading

> Hi [name],
>
> I'm writing about one specific output, not about the framework behind it. I am not asking you to read the framework first, and I am not asking you to agree. If the output does not survive, there is nothing else to look at.
>
> **The output (`specified`, not `grounded`):** local interactions do not come in two different stuffs. Order and fluctuation are two regimes of one cross-kind alternation. What separates them is what happens to the remainder at each scale crossing. If the remainder is damped or discarded so the residual on the macro slot trends down, order settles. If it compounds through an amplifying chain, the same alternation yields chaos, and the detail a crossing discards reappears above it as noise. In the figures' words: *"a remainder compounding through a chain that amplifies is chaos, and the detail a crossing discards reappears above it as noise."*
>
> A second part of the shape is that the conditions are not properties of the agents alone. They are properties of the agent-and-habitat: coupling, noise, density, network structure, dimensionality, energy throughput. The figures put it as *"capability is a property of agent-and-habitat, never of the agent alone."* An apparent order that is just agents updating on each other is not grounded order; it needs an anchor that does not move when they move.
>
> **What I am not claiming:** a universal critical temperature or coupling constant; a single mechanism that explains every self-organizing system; that the local rules alone determine the regime; that agreement among researchers settles any of this. The framework returns a shape, not a name. It says what any answer must have, not which parameter value is the answer.
>
> **What would refute the shape claim:**
> 1. a stable large-scale order from purely same-kind local interactions, with no structure in the slot and no cross-kind composition;
> 2. a case where the remainder amplifies across the crossing yet the macro residual still settles durably;
> 3. a stable order that persists with no selection between competing patterns and no non-updating ground, where coherent drift counts as grounded order;
> 4. a case where changing only the agents, with habitat contact unchanged, moves the regime.
>
> I wrote these down before sending this. If any one holds, the shape is wrong or overclaimed and I will narrow or retract it. A one-line "this fails because..." is more useful to me than agreement. If you'd rather not engage, no reply is needed.
>
> **If it survives, and only then:** the sources are below. The figures are the authority, not my summary of them.
> - Verbatim figure text with line numbers (primary): `FRAMEWORK/reference/03-FIGURES_TEXT.md` in the public figures repo, branch `general-agent`.
> - Canonical usage rule: the `figures/README.md` on that branch.
> - Wider literature I actually read: the Wikipedia entries on self-organization, self-organized criticality, dissipative system, swarm behaviour, synchronization and the Ising model, all linked in the file.
>
> **If you want to use the machinery:** the formula is in the figures link. Run it on a problem you know well and can check against a ground. Write down what would refute your result *before* you run it, and label the mode. The framework's own rule is that a loop cannot check the direction of its own question. A reported failure is more useful to me than a reported success.
