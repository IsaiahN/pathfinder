## Wider literature that bears on the question

1. **The dimensionality result (Ising) is the sharpest world analogue.** The *same* local rule gives order in two or more dimensions and no phase transition in one. That is a world-level demonstration that the condition lives in the habitat (connectivity, dimensionality), not in the local rule alone, matching Fig 11 :39-40.
2. **The threshold result (Kuramoto, via Synchronization) matches ⋛ as a threshold relation.** Order appears when coupling exceeds a critical threshold. The figures define comparison as *"a threshold relation between two quantities"*; the exact threshold is system-specific, which is why the framework gives a shape, not a number.
3. **The damping/amplification result (Dissipative system) matches Fig 13 :31-32 directly.** Near equilibrium, fluctuations are damped; beyond a threshold they are amplified into limit cycles or spatial order. The sign of the feedback decides.
4. **The "no general guarantee" result (Self-organized criticality) matches the shape-not-a-name limit.** Thirty-plus years after BTW, the literature still reports no general rule deciding whether an arbitrary local algorithm displays SOC. That is exactly the kind of number the framework refuses to name.
5. **Stigmergy (Swarm behaviour) is the catalyst analogue.** A trace that survives its own use lets simple local actions build coherent global order (Fig 12 :119).

## Distribution: offer, do not push

Figure-grounded reasons:

- **Fig 4 :22** - *"Only methods cross up. Only head starts come down."* Sending the full architecture before anyone has described a residual sends a recording, not a generator. Lead with the condition-shape and its refuter; offer links after.
- **Fig 10 :42** - *"A verifier that can reconstruct the claim cannot verify it."* A recipient who has absorbed the whole framework before being asked for a verdict has joined the frame being checked.
- **Fig 9 :27-28** - *"describe it before you go looking, or any frame you pick will seem to fit."* Ask what they would expect to see if the shape claim were true before giving them the article.
- **Fig 7 :13** - *"agreement compounds, evidence does not."* A send selected for people who already agree produces a convention, not corroboration.

**Recommended order in a reply:** (1) the condition-shape and its four refuters; (2) ask what they would count as a failure; (3) only then the links, with the note that the figures, not the summary, are the authority.

## The two-stage offer

- **Stage 1 before stage 2** is Fig 10 :52 - *"Bring a characterised residual, never the source"* - and Fig 2 :45 - *"Frames range against the ground. They do not negotiate with it."* The recipient checks the output without holding the framework's interior.
- **Stage 2 is opt-in by construction.** Once a recipient has absorbed the theory, their agreement stops being evidence. The stage-1 verdict must be recorded *before* any theory is shown, and later agreement is never pooled as corroboration (Fig 2 :40 - *"agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool"*).
- **Stage 3 sends a method, which is permitted.** Fig 4 :22 - *"Only methods cross up. Only head starts come down."* The formula is a generator; giving it away does not give away an answer.
- **Stage 3 still needs a ground per problem.** `THE_FORMULA.md` "Direction": a frame *"cannot check the direction of its own question."* Each user must write down what would refute their result before running it, and label the mode.

**Hard precondition:** stages 1 to 3 work only if the stage-1 output survives. As drafted, the output is a shape with four stated refuters, not a measurement (`01_figure_grounded_audit.md` F9). Keep the refuters in front of the links.
