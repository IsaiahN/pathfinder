## RESIDUAL

The question asks for *conditions* distinguishing stable order from fluctuation. Three supporting questions are owed: (1) what is the **order** of the composition that produces a macro pattern, (2) can the **same local rules** yield different regimes by arrangement alone, and (3) what does the **sign of the feedback loop** decide? A falsifier is written before each conclusion.

## DERIVATION

### Q1 - What is the order of the composition?

- Fig 13 :74: *"Everything else is a composition across the two kinds, never within one."*
- Fig 13 :75: *"Two states never bond directly. It is not space-time but space, a structure, time; and two structures join only through a state."*
- A first-order macro pattern is therefore `state – structure – state`, one crossing. The local interaction supplies the structure; the macro order is the settled molecule at the next level.
- Fig 12 :104: *"A settled molecule becomes an atom for whatever composes over it."* Once a local pattern settles against the ground, it can serve as an atom for the next scale; that is how a local cluster becomes a node of a larger order. This is recursion, not a new primitive.
- **Falsifier, written before the run:** if a stable large-scale order can be shown to be a pure same-kind aggregation with no structure in the slot, and the framework must admit it, then the first-order composition claim fails.

### Q2 - Can the same local rules yield different regimes by arrangement alone?

- Fig 12 :117: **"Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."**
- Fig 12 :124: **"functional group. A substructure whose presence fixes the outcome. The group says what kind; the qualifier says which member."**
- The world supplies two clean instances of the figure's isomer idea applied to *regimes*, with the local rule held fixed:
  - **Ising:** the same nearest-neighbour spin rule gives *"no phase transition in one dimension"* but order *"in 2 dimensions or more"* (Wikipedia, *Ising model*). Dimensionality is the bond.
  - **Voter/flocking/oscillator networks:** arXiv nlin.AO lists *"Localized active phase in the coevolving voter model with fixed links"* and *"Flocking Time and Graph Volume in Nonlinear Cucker-Smale Models with Dynamic Networks"* (fetched listing). Topology is the bond.
- So the framework's isomer definition maps cleanly: **same local rules, different arrangement (bond/topology/dimensionality), different regime**. The macro regime is not written in the local rule; it is carried by the arrangement, which is exactly what a flat formula fails to record (Fig 12 :14-16: *"the ingredients are recorded and the arrangement is not"*).
- **Falsifier, written before the run:** if the same local rule always yields the same regime regardless of topology, dimensionality or connectivity, the arrangement/isomer claim fails and the regime is a property of the rule alone.

### Q3 - What does the sign of the feedback loop decide?

- Fig 12 :121: **"cycle. The output re-enters as input. The sign of the loop decides whether it closes, and a chain cannot close by construction."**
- Fig 12 :119: *"catalyst. A part that survives its own use."*
- Fig 13 :31-32: *"a remainder compounding through a chain that amplifies is chaos, and the detail a crossing discards reappears above it as noise."*
- A loop whose output re-enters as input is a cycle, and order requires a cycle: an open chain cannot close. The **sign** of the loop decides the regime: positive feedback amplifies a fluctuation into an ordered pattern (world: *Self-organization* - *"fluctuations, amplified by positive feedback"*; *Dissipative system* - beyond a threshold oscillations *"are no longer damped out, but may be amplified"*); negative feedback damps it back to the fixed point. A catalyst, a part that survives its own use, is what lets a small local trace persist long enough to stabilise the loop (world: stigmergy, *Swarm behaviour*).
- This triangulates F1 and F6 of `01_figure_grounded_audit.md`: the same alternation yields order or chaos according to the loop sign and the gain on the remainder.
- **Falsifier, written before the run:** if a chain with no re-entrant output settles into a durable macro order, the cycle claim fails; if the loop sign can be reversed with no change of regime, the sign claim fails.

### Q4 - What order is the union of "compose" and "persist"?

- `01` §2 census found the question is a union: composition (D2) overlaps persistence (D3) at the macro pattern.
- Fig 9 :54: *"And when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again."*
- Fig 9 :57: *"a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either"* - it is a specific compound with its own loop.
- Therefore the union is not "one hard question about order". It is two ordered loops joined at the macro pattern: a composition loop (what can form) and a selection loop (what persists). Each has its own ground: the composition loop is settled by whether the macro term composes at all; the selection loop is settled by what the ground keeps (Fig 11 :14-15: *"nothing selects; what survives is what the ground kept"*).
- **Falsifier, written before the run:** if composition and persistence are settled by a single measurement on a single ground and cannot come apart, the unbundling is unnecessary and this derivation over-splits.

## CONCLUSION

A stable large-scale order is a **first-order cross-kind molecule** (`state – structure – state`) that has settled and become an atom for larger scales. Its regime is not in the local rule: **same local rules, different arrangement, different regime** is the figure's isomer claim applied to order. The **sign of the feedback loop** and the gain on the remainder decide whether the alternation closes into order or amplifies into fluctuation. The question's union of "compose" and "persist" must be unbundled into two loops joined at the macro pattern. No new primitive, no universal critical number and no famous mechanism is derived. All four claims are `specified`, with their falsifiers stated in advance.
