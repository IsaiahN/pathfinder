## DERIVATION

### Q1 - What structure is the adaptive loop?

- Fig 12 :121: **"cycle. The output re-enters as input. The sign of the loop decides whether it closes, and a chain cannot close by construction."**
- The output of one generation (the evolved trait distribution) re-enters as the input to the next generation's selection. The loop closes only if the sign is right: the response must reduce the gradient between trait and optimum. A positive feedback in the wrong direction (response pushes trait further from optimum) is a chain that diverges, not a cycle that closes.
- Fig 12 :119: **"catalyst. A part that survives its own use."** Standing genetic variation is the catalyst-like part: it is spent and regenerated by mutation and recombination across generations. Schiffers et al. 2013 show what happens when regeneration is removed: with mutation rate set to zero, *"populations inevitably died, because standing genetic variation alone did not provide enough scope for full adaptation to new conditions."*
- **Falsifier:** if a population's adaptive response can be shown to persist across generations with no regenerating variation and no surviving catalytic part, the catalyst reading fails.

### Q2 - What is ordered, and what is produced?

- Fig 12 lists → as **sequence** (A then B; B needs A to have happened) and ⇒ as **production** (A produces B; B did not exist before).
- Climate forcing is a sequence: the optimum moves, then selection acts. The new optimum is not produced by the population; it arrives from outside the population's closure.
- The adaptive response is production: a new trait distribution exists afterward that did not exist before (Fig 12 :77: *"A produces B; B did not exist before"*, Operators table :39: *"is there a B before A fires? No."*).
- Fig 12 :111: **"A vocabulary without bonds can name a state and cannot name a route to one."** A list of variables (variance, heritability, selection, population size) cannot say what follows what. The shape is the route, not the list.
- **Falsifier:** if the response can be shown to be a simultaneous equilibrium with no ordered dependence on the preceding environmental change, the sequence/production distinction is not governing here.

### Q3 - Union or partition of the two conditions?

- Fig 9 :55: *"A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones."* Fig 1 :13: *"a global R near zero with one live slot residual is a legal state."*
- The adaptive-capacity condition and the collapse-threshold condition overlap: a population with high variance but a very fast-moving optimum, and a population with a slow optimum but no variance, fail for different reasons, while the case with both binds differently. So the compound is a union, and a single lever clears only the region where one condition holds (Fig 9 :56).
- **Falsifier:** if the two conditions can be shown to be independent and additive across all measured slots, the union reading fails and a partition is correct.

### Q4 - Are two populations isomers?

- Fig 12 :117: **"isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."**
- Two populations with the same atom set (same species, same standing variance, same climate optimum) but different bond arrangements (one with high gene flow across heterogeneous habitat, one isolated) can have the same ingredients and different outcomes: full rescue in one, partial or failed rescue in the other. Schiffers et al. 2013 report exactly this: *"the likelihood of evolutionary rescue was strongly reduced or even hindered for a range of dispersal distances, for which rapid adaptation would have been possible without local adaptation."*
- By Fig 12's definition these are **isomers**: same atoms, different arrangement, different substance. The arrangement is the substance (Fig 12 :94).
- The weaker reading is a **functional group** (Fig 12 :123: *"a substructure whose presence fixes the outcome"*): both populations share the two-factor motif (transmission times selection) even where they differ in atoms.
- **Falsifier:** if two populations with identical ingredients and different arrangements are shown to have identical rescue outcomes across the measured range, the isomer claim fails and only the functional-group claim stands.

### Q5 - What order is the union?

- Fig 12 :104: **"A settled molecule becomes an atom for whatever composes over it. So irreducible is a fact about a level and never about the world."**
- A measured per-population rescue outcome, once settled against the ground, becomes an atom for the community-level composition. The community claim (did the ecosystem collapse?) is second order, built over settled per-population terms. It is not a first-order property of the species list.
- This is the recursion Fig 12 names, not an import: no new primitive is needed to go from population outcomes to a community statement, only the settled outcomes and the interaction bonds.
- Type caveat, still open: the community term is built over a network of interactions (Fig 13 :24 *"that entities relate, and influence travels"*), and whether a network of settled population outcomes counts as one quantity at the next level needs an instrument, not an argument. Recorded as a strain, not smoothed.

## CONCLUSION

The adaptive loop is a **cycle** with a catalytic part (standing variation) and a sign condition (response must reduce the gradient). The environmental forcing is a **sequence** that produces a new optimum outside the population's closure. The two conditions form a **union**, not a partition. Two populations with the same ingredients arranged differently are **isomers** under Fig 12's definition, with the weaker functional-group reading available if the ingredients are not shared. The community-level collapse claim is **second order**, built over settled per-population terms.

This framing rescues what is checkable in the question without turning it into a universal rate: the order, the cycle, the union and the isomer test need no new primitive and no critical warming constant.
