## 0. The question under audit

> Can evolution occur quickly enough to prevent ecosystem collapse under rapid climate change?

Stated as an effect: the environment moves an adaptive optimum at some rate; a population's trait distribution tracks it at some rate; if the gap persists until absolute fitness is negative long enough, that population is lost, and because contact cascades (Fig 11), its loss changes the residuals of other populations. The question asks whether the second rate exceeds the first before the threshold is crossed.

Two hunches are refused at the door. The first is that the answer is a single number (a critical rate of warming). The second is that the answer is a property of a species (short generation time means "yes"). Both are filters, not witnesses (Fig 9 :60).

## 1. Residual and domain census

### 1.1 R by arity, symmetry and scale

- **Arity:** two quantities in a threshold relation (rate of environmental change, rate of adaptive response), plus a cascade over slots. Not a one-term quantity.
- **Symmetry:** a comparison, not a symmetric join. The Operators table defines comparison as *"a threshold relation between two quantities"* and names it *"a goal is a comparison, which is why a vocabulary without it cannot state one."* Swapping the two operands does not give the same claim; the question is which is larger.
- **Scale:** micro-evolutionary (generations, within a population) and macro-ecological (population, community, climate over decades to centuries). Two scales, with a membrane between them (Fig 4 :22: *"Only methods cross up. Only head starts come down."*).

### 1.2 One line per domain: actors, currency, anchor

| domain | actors | currency | anchor | question type |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| D1 evolutionary ground | individuals, alleles, standing variation, new mutation, selection, demography | heritability, additive genetic variance, population size, mutation rate, generation time | measured persistence or extinction of the population | threshold comparison (⋛) of two rates |
| D2 ecological ground | species, pollinators, predators, competitors, habitat, climate | abundances, interaction strengths, ranges, ecosystem functions | measured ecosystem state and function | union compound; per-slot cascade |
| D3 research / checking frame | evolutionary rescue researchers, experimental evolution lines, long-term field studies, models | predictions, data, residuals | the ground, never agreement | checking arrangement |
| D4 application / management frame | conservation managers, translocation and gene-flow practitioners | introductions and contact changes | the ground; managed work is permanent | maintained mode of the habitat |

D2 is a **union compound**, not a partition: adaptive capacity and the collapse threshold bind at once, and a case in both conditions is categorically different from a case in either (Fig 9 :54: *"When the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again."*). D3 and D1 or D2 overlap only in the ground; their agreement is not a second witness.

### 1.3 Dependency order (Fig 3)

Perception (measure the residual per species) precedes vocabulary (terms for what a population affords: heritable variance, plasticity, dispersal), which precedes objective (what counts as rescue: positive growth, retained function), which precedes planning (which interventions), which precedes learn and carry. Fig 3 :19: *"Optimising a link that already works cannot rescue one that does not."* Fig 3 :27: *"A link with no instrument is not a link that works. It is a link nobody has looked at."* The middle link, having terms for what a population affords, is where this literature is weakest: the papers read here measure rates and variances well, but the mapping from those to what a population affords is still a grammar treated as a dictionary.

## 3. Findings

### F1 - The question is a union compound and a per-slot residual (decisive for the shape)

Fig 1 :19: **"The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated."** Fig 1 :22: **"Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average."** Fig 11 :52 records the cascade in the figures' own example: scoping a habitat to direct contact is how DDT reached the eagles, with the residual three steps out.

"Ecosystem collapse" is therefore not one slot with one clock. It is a set of per-population residuals coupled by relations. One population with a live positive residual while the global average looks fine is a legal state, exactly the state Fig 1 :20 describes. A single global yes or no would aggregate away the only signal that says where to look.

Fig 9 :54: **"When the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again."** The adaptive-capacity condition and the collapse-threshold condition must be separated and then recombined as a compound with its own loop.

**Verdict:** the question as posed is malformed. It must be unbundled before it is searched. Status: `triangulated` across Fig 1 (slot rule), Fig 11 (cascade) and Fig 9 (union rule), three figures with low overlap in subject matter.

### F2 - The deciding quantity is a threshold comparison between two rates

The Operators table calls comparison *"a threshold relation between two quantities"* and *"a goal is a comparison, which is why a vocabulary without it cannot state one."* Fig 12 :94: **"The arrangement is the substance."** The question is not "is evolution fast" but "is the adaptive response rate larger than the rate of environmental change for this slot, and by when."

World corroboration (secondary): Alexander et al. 2014 write *"Declining populations face a 'race' between adaptation and extinction"* and *"evolutionary adaptation occurring on the same timescale as demographic dynamics determines whether populations survive severe environmental change."* The Wikipedia entry for evolutionary rescue states that under continual change the system appears as *"a stable lag of the mean trait value behind a moving environmental optimum, where the rate of evolution and change in the environment are equal."*

**Verdict:** the shape is a comparison of two rates at a threshold, settled per slot by measurement. Status: `triangulated` (figures plus two independent literature sources), mode `specified`.

### F3 - Adaptive progress has two factors and either at zero stops it

Fig 7 :37: **"Progress per handoff has two factors. Zero either and the chain runs in place:"** Fig 7 :38: **"the bracket's fidelity across the handoff"** and Fig 7 :39: **"the ground's pressure on the room."**

Map from the figure to the biology: a handoff is a generation; bracket fidelity is the fidelity with which the adaptive signal is transmitted (heritability, effective additive genetic variance); ground pressure is selection imposed by the changed environment. If transmission is zero, no amount of selection accumulates a response. If selection is zero, no amount of heritable variance produces directional change. The product stops.

World corroboration (secondary): Schiffers et al. 2013 write *"the rate of adaptation is predominantly driven by the amount of available additive genetic variance and the strength of environmental selection."* Alexander et al. 2014 identify the same determinants: *"severity of environmental change, population size, and genetic variance or mutation rate."*

**Verdict:** a `general` structural constraint, `specified` when instantiated for a species. Not a speed limit, because the figures supply no constants; they supply the two-factor shape. Status: `triangulated` (Fig 7, Fig 13's spendable-gradient structure, and the world literature).

### F4 - In the unmaintained mode nobody steers; selection simply ran

Fig 11 :36: **"Where nothing maintains anything, intelligence is a label applied afterwards to a lineage that met its ground."** The sentence continues *"Nothing was intelligent; selection ran."* Fig 11 :56 (same panel): *"Unmaintained. No maintainer and no live frame in the seat. The habitat is pure contact, nothing selects, and what survives is what the ground kept."*

Evolution is the unmaintained mode of the habitat. The framework grants it no preference for survival and no guarantee of sufficiency. Asking "can evolution occur quickly enough" is asking whether an unmaintained process happens to meet the ground before the ground moves again.

The essays make the same point about natural selection as an instance rather than the whole code: *"Natural selection is not the whole answer. It is a special case"* (*The Recursive Self-Improvement Trap*).

**Verdict:** the role of evolution in the shape is as the unmaintained selector, not as an agent with a speed dial. Status: `single-source` for the figure reading (Fig 11), corroborated by a framework essay from the same evidence pool, so weak corroboration only.

### F5 - Evolvability is a property of population and habitat, never of the population alone

Fig 11 :39: **"capability is a property of agent-and-habitat, never of the agent alone."** Fig 11 :40: **"A system cannot be made more capable by improving the agent in isolation. An improvement that does not change contact changes nothing, however much it improves."**

Applied: the capacity to track a moving optimum belongs to the population together with its habitat (population size, standing variance, gene flow, spatial heterogeneity, other species). A short generation time in isolation does not licence "yes". This is also the figures' own stated refuter for the capability claim: Fig 11 :72 says *"an agent-only improvement that moved the ground would refute the capability claim."*

World corroboration (secondary): Schiffers et al. 2013 find that dispersal helps tracking of temporal change but hurts under local adaptation and habitat heterogeneity, that highest survival occurred at intermediate dispersal, and that *"even when evolutionary rescue saves a population from extinction, its spatial range following climate change may be strongly narrowed, that is, the rescue is only partial."* Their closing warning is direct: *"neglecting the impact of non-climatic, local adaptation might lead to a considerable overestimation of a population's evolvability under rapid environmental change."*

**Verdict:** "can evolution occur quickly enough" is a property of a population-habitat pair, not of a taxon. Status: `triangulated` (Fig 11 plus a model-and-data paper with a different method).

### F6 - The applied lever is contact, and the work is permanent

Fig 11 :61: **"Introduction is available; exclusion is not."** Fig 11 :63: **"Which is why a managed ecosystem is permanent work rather than a solved state."** The figure's reason is that contact is a fact about the world and does not consult a manager.

In the maintained mode, a conservation seat can add actors or head starts (assisted gene flow, corridors, reduction of non-climate stressors) but cannot subtract the habitat. This is a shape, not a prescription: the figures do not say which actor to introduce. The idea of "speeding up evolution" as an isolated intervention is the error F5 names.

**Verdict:** for the applied reading of the question, management acts on the habitat and the contact structure, and must keep acting. Status: `single-source` (Fig 11) for the rule, with the world literature silent on the rule itself rather than conflicting with it.

### F7 - The ground settles the per-slot question; this derivation cannot

Fig 2 :29: **"An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks."** Fig 7 :35: **"A room's agreement is variation, not a verdict. Selection between rooms is where the ground votes."** Fig 10 :42: **"A verifier that can reconstruct the claim cannot verify it."**

No figure line returns a measured persistence for a named species under a named warming rate. The anchor is the experiment, the long-term plot, the field record. Agreement among modellers, especially where they cite the same reviews, is one evidence pool talking (Fig 2 :40: *"agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool"*).

**Verdict:** `gap` on the empirical universal; reachable in principle for each named slot. "Unreachable is a verdict, not a silence" (Fig 13 :134). Status: `triangulated` for the rule (Fig 2, Fig 7, Fig 10).

### F8 - Failure to find rescue proves nothing about impossibility

Fig 8 :39: **"So a search that finds nothing is never a proof of absence."** Fig 9 :44: **"Only a sealed room can be searched to the end, and no frame is a sealed room."** Fig 6 :51: **"Figure 8 separates underivable-in-principle from unreachable-at-this-budget."**

World corroboration (secondary, low overlap with the rescue literature): the Wikipedia entry on the rate of evolution quotes Williams and Gould that *"Long term rates of change, even in lineages of unusual rapid evolution, are almost always far slower than they theoretically could be."* So a handful of documented fast responses cannot be generalised, and a handful of failures cannot close the possibility.

**Verdict:** report "unreached at this budget" for any single system, never "evolution is too slow in principle." Status: `triangulated` (Fig 8, Fig 9, Fig 6, plus a palaeontological source).

### F9 - Filters are not witnesses

Fig 9 :60: **"Use filters for the budget and witnesses for the verdict. Never let a filter hand you a verdict."** Generation time, heritability and the rate of climate change are filters deciding which species to measure first. They are not the verdict. The verdict is the ground's answer for that slot.

Status: `single-source` (Fig 9), corroborated by the metalaws note that thresholds are filters, never the decision.

### F10 - Two rates are priced in different units and both must be read

Fig 12 :126: *"Bits price the description. Actions price finding out whether it holds."* The rate of environmental change and the rate of adaptive response are measured with different instruments and different uncertainties; summing them into one "pace mismatch score" is the error Fig 12 names. Each must be read, and the choice made blind otherwise.

Status: `single-source` (Fig 12), applied as a caution on method rather than as a claim about the biology.

## 4. Pre-registered refuter

Required before the conclusion: Seat Map :287 says *"a falsifier written before the run is a design instrument, not only an honesty device."*

The shape claim is refuted if any of the following holds:

1. A single rate comparison is shown to decide all slots at once, with no per-slot residual and no cascade (breaks F1, Fig 1 :19 and Fig 9 :54).
2. Adaptive progress is demonstrated with one of the two factors at zero: heritable transmission zero, or selection zero (breaks F3, Fig 7 :37-39).
3. A population is made more capable by an improvement that leaves its contact unchanged (breaks F5, Fig 11 :40; this is the framework's own named refuter).
4. A ground reading for a named species under a named rate of change returns a value the shape cannot accommodate, for example rescue with a negative response rate or stable function with a live positive residual at the relevant slot.
5. The two rates are shown to be commensurable in one currency such that reading both separately is unnecessary (breaks F10, Fig 12 :126).

If any fires, the shape claim is withdrawn. Until one is run, no part of this may be labelled `grounded`.

## 5. Recommendation

1. Answer the question in its unbundled form: per species, per habitat, per interaction (F1).
2. Lead with the threshold comparison and the two factors, not with a global speed claim (F2, F3).
3. Report measured evolvability as a property of the population-habitat pair, citing the dispersal and heterogeneity result (F5).
4. Keep filters (generation time, variance) out of the verdict column (F9).
5. Say `gap` for the universal and `specified, unreached` for any single system; never convert an unfound rescue into a proof of impossibility (F7, F8).
6. Send the generator, that is the residual, the shape and the refuter, before any theory (Fig 4 :22: *"Only methods cross up. Only head starts come down."*).

## 6. Canonical usage check and strains

### Strains encountered and re-censused

- **Initial reading:** Fig 7's diagram text *"Progress per handoff: R = h² × S"* was taken as a formula giving a universal rate of adaptation. **Re-census:** the surrounding lines define the two factors (bracket fidelity, ground pressure) and Fig 7 :35 says selection between rooms is where the ground votes. The formula is a per-handoff progress statement, not a universal constant. The "universal rate" reading is retracted; the two-factor shape is kept.
- **Initial reading:** world papers reporting rapid evolution were treated as corroborating a "yes". **Re-census:** those papers give rates, conditions and partial rescues, not a universal. Fig 9 :60 forbids a filter handing the verdict. The world cannot hand the verdict; the retraction is recorded.
- **Initial reading:** a specific critical warming value from one simulation was considered as an answer. **Re-census:** it is a parameter of one model with stated assumptions (mutation rate, carrying capacity, dispersal). It is a filter for the budget, not a witness. Retracted as an answer, retained as a worked example.
- **Apparent conflict:** Fig 11 says natural selection is the unmaintained mode, while the world literature treats evolutionary rescue as a process managers can influence (assisted gene flow). **Re-census:** no conflict. Fig 11 :58-64 distinguishes unmaintained from maintained mode and says the seat adds to a habitat it did not author. Different domains, not a contradiction.

After re-census: **one live strain, recorded not hidden.** The Operators table defines comparison as a threshold relation between two quantities; here the two operands are rates measured with different instruments and error structures (F10). Fig 12 :126 says the two prices are in different units and both must be read. Whether a single comparison bond can carry both without a lossy transform is not settled by the figures; it needs a measuring instrument, not an argument.
