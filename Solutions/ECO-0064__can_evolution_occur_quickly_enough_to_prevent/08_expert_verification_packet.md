# Expert verification packet - the Tether on evolutionary rescue and collapse

**What this is:** a claim in a form an evolutionary ecologist can check. Mode, derivation and refuters are stated before the conclusion, so a reader knows what to look for and what would settle it.

**Mode: `specified` on the structural claims, `general` on the shape, `gap` on the universal empirical question. Not `grounded`.** No measurement is claimed here.

## 1. The claim, in one paragraph

"Can evolution occur quickly enough to prevent ecosystem collapse?" should be answered per population, not per ecosystem average, and as a threshold comparison between two rates rather than as a global speed. Adaptive progress per generation has two factors, transmission fidelity and selection pressure, and either at zero stops progress. Evolvability belongs to a population together with its habitat, not to a species alone. Evolution is the unmaintained mode of the habitat, with no guarantee of sufficiency; where a manager intervenes, introduction is available and exclusion is not, so the work is permanent. A community collapse claim is second order, built over per-population outcomes that have settled against the ground. The framework supplies the shape and the refuters, not a critical warming rate or a name for the saving actor.

## 2. Three checkable sub-claims

### C1 - framework-internal; checkable by reading Figures 1 and 7

- **Statement:** the residual is per population, and adaptive progress needs two factors, either at zero stopping it.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 1 :19 - *"The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated."* Fig 7 :37 - *"Progress per handoff has two factors. Zero either and the chain runs in place:"* with Fig 7 :38 *"the bracket's fidelity across the handoff"* and Fig 7 :39 *"the ground's pressure on the room."*
- **Refuter:** a single rate comparison that decides all slots at once with no per-population residual, or adaptive progress demonstrated with transmission zero or selection zero.

### C2 - biology-facing; checkable by an evolutionary ecologist against the rescue literature

- **Statement:** evolvability is a property of population and habitat together, so the comparative claim must include habitat structure (dispersal, heterogeneity, gene flow), not just taxon-level traits such as generation time.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 11 :39 - *"capability is a property of agent-and-habitat, never of the agent alone."* Fig 11 :40 - *"An improvement that does not change contact changes nothing, however much it improves."*
- **What to check:** do the data support habitat-dependence? Schiffers et al. 2013 found the highest rescue probability at intermediate dispersal, a strong reduction under the combination of dispersal and habitat heterogeneity, and frequent partial rescue. Alexander et al. 2014 identify severity of change, population size and genetic variance or mutation rate as the common determinants across conservation and medicine.
- **Refuter:** a population made more capable by an improvement that leaves its contact unchanged, or a demonstration that a taxon-level trait alone predicts rescue across habitats with no interaction term.

### C3 - framework-internal derivation, with a live type strain

- **Statement:** collapse is a second-order composition over settled per-population outcomes, not a first-order property of a species list; and the comparison's two operands are rates measured with different instruments, so both must be read separately.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 12 :104 - *"A settled molecule becomes an atom for whatever composes over it. So irreducible is a fact about a level and never about the world."* Fig 12 :126 - *"Bits price the description. Actions price finding out whether it holds."*
- **Refuter:** show that a community outcome can be derived from a species list without settling any population-level term, or show that the two rates are commensurable in one currency so that reading both separately is unnecessary. The operand-type question is live and is named, not hidden.

## 3. What is explicitly not claimed

- Not a universal yes or no; the question as posed is a `gap`.
- Not a critical rate of warming, and not a number taken from any simulation as an answer.
- Not a claim that short generation time or high heritability guarantees rescue.
- Not a claim that any named species was or will be rescued here.
- Not a name for the source of persistence: plasticity, range shift, demographic rescue and genetic adaptation can produce the same reading.
- Not a prescription for which conservation actor to introduce.
- Not `grounded`. Nothing here is asserted as settled by experiment.

## 5. What would settle it

A source-discriminating instrument and a per-slot time series, not a further argument. From *The Hard Problem is About Source*: the spectrometer resolved an ambiguous colour reading, not a better argument about colour.

1. **Rate test:** measured movement of the local optimum against measured change in the trait mean, with the two error structures reported separately.
2. **Two-factor test:** effective additive genetic variance (transmission fidelity) and selection differential (ground pressure), per generation.
3. **Source test:** genomic or common-garden evidence separating genetic adaptation from plasticity, range shift and demographic rescue.
4. **Cascade test:** for a population crossing the threshold, which interaction partners change residual, and at what order of contact.

## 6. What an expert can check in twenty minutes

1. Read Fig 1 :19 and :22, Fig 7 :37-39, Fig 11 :39-40 and Fig 12 :104. Five short passages.
2. Check C2 against their own reading of the rescue literature: does dispersal-by-heterogeneity evidence support the habitat-dependence claim?
3. Name one instrument that would distinguish genetic adaptation from plasticity in a field population, or say why none is available.
4. Send the refutation. A failure is more useful than an endorsement.
