## DOMAIN CENSUS

R by arity, symmetry and scale, then one line per domain with actors, currency and anchor; partition or union; order by dependency.

- **R (the residual).** The gap between a moving adaptive optimum and a population's current trait distribution, coupled across populations by interaction. Arity: two quantities (two rates) in a threshold relation, plus a cascade. Symmetry: comparison, not a symmetric join; swapping operands changes the claim. Scale: generational within a population, decadal to centennial at the community; two scales with a membrane. Fig 9 names the two conditions a union compound: a case in both is not a harder case of either.
- **Domain 1, the evolutionary ground.** Actors: individuals, alleles, standing variation, new mutation, selection, demography. Currency: heritability, additive genetic variance, population size, mutation rate, generation time. Anchor: measured persistence or extinction. Question type: threshold comparison of two rates.
- **Domain 2, the ecological ground.** Actors: species, pollinators, predators, competitors, habitat, climate. Currency: abundances, interaction strengths, ranges, functions. Anchor: measured ecosystem state and function. Question type: union compound, per-slot cascade.
- **Domain 3, the research and checking frame.** Actors: evolutionary rescue researchers, experimental evolution lines, long-term field studies, models. Currency: predictions, data, residuals. Anchor: the ground, never agreement. In this simulated run every seat draws on one evidence pool, so its agreement is one pool talking, not corroboration.
- **Domain 4, the application frame.** Actors: conservation managers and gene-flow practitioners. Currency: introductions and contact changes. Anchor: the ground; managed work is permanent. Question type: maintained mode of the habitat.
- **Order by dependency.** Measure the residual per slot before vocabulary; vocabulary before objective (what counts as rescue); objective before planning an intervention; refuse the universal question before minting an answer (Formula step 2); describe the residual before choosing a frame (Fig 9 :27).

## VERDICT (from the figures)

The universal question is a `gap`: it bundles a per-slot threshold comparison with a community-level cascade, so no single yes or no is reachable as posed. The shape that survives is fourfold: score per population; compare the two rates at a threshold; require both transmission fidelity and selection pressure; treat evolvability as population-and-habitat, with the managed lever on contact and permanent work. Evolution is the unmaintained mode and carries no guarantee; collapse is second order over settled population outcomes; the ground decides each slot. This is `specified` on structure, `general` on shape, `gap` on the universal, and not `grounded`.

- **cost if wrong:** bounded and locatable. The packet states its mode, refuters and comparison before any conclusion, so an error is findable. The real cost is spending the independence of outside readers by sending a universal claim instead of a per-slot generator, which Fig 2 :42 prices: "Buy independence in proportion to the cost of being wrong." That cost is avoidable by leading with the shape and its refuter.
- **undoable:** yes. The universal claim is withdrawn without touching the per-slot derivation; `07_the_tether_answer.md` and `08_expert_verification_packet.md` already remove it. No primitive has to be abandoned, and a sent claim can be superseded by the corrected packet.
- **reversed by:** a ground reading, not agreement. Per-slot rate series with separate error structures, a two-factor measurement, and a source-discriminating test separating genetic adaptation from plasticity, range shift and demographic rescue would close a slot. A single rate comparison deciding all slots, progress with a factor at zero, or a capability gain with unchanged contact would break the shape. Until one of these fires, the verdict stands as candidate.
