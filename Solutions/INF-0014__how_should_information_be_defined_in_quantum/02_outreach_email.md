**Subject:** Check one claim about quantum information before you decide whether the framing is worth reading

> Hi [name],
>
> I have one specific output I would like you to check against what you already know. I am not asking you to read the framework first, and I am not asking you to agree with it. If the output does not survive, there is nothing else to look at.
>
> **The question in effect terms:** how should information be defined in a quantum system when the outcome you get depends on which compatible measurements you perform together? Option A identifies information with the read-out of the chosen context. Option B identifies it with what does not change when the context changes, and treats the context as the pose that renders a read-out.
>
> **The output (`general` on the shape, `specified` on the binding, not `grounded`):**
>
> 1. The question is a union compound, not one hard problem. The rule that works inside a fixed context and the rule that works across contexts do not shrink into one question with effort, so they need separate loops and separate grounds.
> 2. A definition whose value moves with the measurement context has no anchor. An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks. The chosen basis is a frame; if it decides what the word denotes, the definition has no ground.
> 3. The outcome needs a pose. The map from the invariant quantity to the read-out cannot run without specifying one. That is the figure's structural reading of contextuality: the state did not change, the pose did.
> 4. The information is carried by the system-context relation, not by the system alone. Dropping the context from the description drops information, not noise.
> 5. The framework refuses to name what occupies the invariant slot. The standard candidate from quantum information theory is the von Neumann entropy, which is invariant under change of basis. I record that as a binding from the physics frame, not as a derivation.
>
> **What I am explicitly not claiming:** that Shannon entropy of a measurement outcome is the definition of information in the system (it is indexed by a specified random variable, so it moves with the context); that a noncontextual assignment of definite values to all observables can be recovered (the Kochen-Specker result rules it out in three or more dimensions); that this settles any interpretational question; or that the framework is grounded. Nothing here is a measurement.
>
> **What would refute it, written down before this message:**
> 1. a definition whose value changes with the context and is still claimed as the anchor;
> 2. a noncontextual definite-value assignment reproducing all quantum predictions in dimension three or above;
> 3. a context that can be removed from the definition with no change in any outcome distribution;
> 4. a round trip from invariant to read-out and back that is lossless for every state.
>
> If any one holds, I retract the corresponding claim. A one-line "this fails because..." is more useful to me than agreement. If you would rather not engage, no reply is needed.
>
> **If it survives, and only then:** the sources are below. The figures are the authority, not my summary of them.
> - Verbatim figure text with line numbers (local extract): `FRAMEWORK/reference/03-FIGURES_TEXT.md`
> - Canonical usage rule: `https://github.com/IsaiahN/tether/blob/general-agent/figures/README.md`
> - Kochen-Specker contextuality review: `https://arxiv.org/html/2102.13036v5`
> - A short introduction to classical and quantum information: `https://arxiv.org/html/1805.11965v5`
>
> **If you want to reuse the machinery:** the formula is in the figures link. Run it on a problem you know well and can check against a ground. Write down what would refute your result before you run it, and label the mode of what you get. A reported failure is more useful to me than a reported success.
