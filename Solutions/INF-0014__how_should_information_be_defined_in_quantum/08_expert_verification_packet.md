# Expert verification packet: the Tether on information in quantum systems

**What this is:** a claim in a form a physicist or philosopher of physics can check. Mode, derivation and refuters are stated before the conclusion, so a reader knows what to look for and what would settle it.

**Mode: `general` on the shape, `specified` on the binding, not `grounded`.** No measurement is claimed to have settled which functional of the state is information. This is a shape and a route, not a result.

## 1. The claim, in one paragraph

The question is a union compound: the rule that works inside a fixed measurement context and the rule that works across contexts do not shrink into one question, so they need separate loops and separate grounds. A definition of quantum information whose value changes with the measurement context has no anchor, because an anchor is whatever settles a question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks. The context is the pose that the frame transform needs in order to render an outcome from the invariant quantity, and the information is carried by the system-context bond, not by the system alone. The framework returns this shape and refuses to name the occupant of the invariant slot. Quantum information theory offers the von Neumann entropy as the occupant, invariant under unitary change of basis; that is a provenanced binding from the physics frame, not a derivation. Shannon outcome entropy is refused as the anchor because it is indexed by a chosen random variable.

## 2. Three checkable sub-claims

### C1 - framework-internal; checkable by reading Figure 2 and the Symbols table

- **Statement:** a definition that moves with the measurement context is not an anchor. The context is a frame, and an anchor must not move when a frame changes.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 2 :29-30, *"An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks. A measurement, an experiment, a counter, a market, a patient outcome. If it moves when you push on it, it is not an anchor."* Symbols table lines 102 to 106: *"Whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks. It is not a frame and has no vantage to update from."*
- **Refuter:** exhibit a quantity that changes with the measurement context and is nonetheless the thing the ground settles as information in the system. By the anchor test that is a contradiction; a real case would break Fig 2.

### C2 - framework-internal plus physics-facing; checkable by reading Figure 12 and the Symbols table, then checking the physics

- **Statement:** the context is the pose required to render an outcome, and the system-context bond carries information the system alone does not. Two contexts over the same atom set, differing only in which compatible observables are grouped, are Fig 12 isomers; as ordinarily phrased they are members of a functional group.
- **Source, verbatim:** Symbols table `F`, *"The map between allocentric and egocentric at one scale. Distinct from T_A and T_E, which run between scales. Cannot be run without a pose."* Fig 12 :5, *"A set of ingredients is not one. The bond is the rest of the information, and it is the half nothing was recording."* Fig 12 :117, *"Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."*
- **What to check:** is a measurement outcome really a function of a compatible-measurement set as well as of the state? Does dropping the context leave the distribution unchanged for any state? Kochen-Specker contextuality (Budroni et al., Rev. Mod. Phys. 94, 045007, 2022) is the relevant physics; the packet does not claim to derive it.
- **Refuter:** a context removable from the definition with no change in any outcome distribution, or a context-free outcome. Either breaks the bond claim or the pose claim.

### C3 - framework-internal derivation

- **Statement:** the question is a union compound and must be split; the state estimate is second order over settled first-order read-outs. The von Neumann entropy is a provenanced binding, not a figure derivation.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 9 :4-5, *"Disagreements that do not shrink with effort, where each rule keeps working well on a different subset, are not one hard question but several well-formed ones. Split it rather than search."* Fig 12 :104, *"A settled molecule becomes an atom for whatever composes over it."* Fig 9 :32, *"you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself."*
- **Refuter:** a single rule resolving both subsets with no remainder (breaks the union finding); a state estimate composed before the read-outs are settled (breaks the dependency order); a derivation of `S(ρ)` from the six-term set without physics input (would promote the binding to figure-derived, changing the claim rather than refuting the shape).
- **Named strain, not hidden:** applying a context is a pose-parameterised frame transform `F`. It is not one of the seven bonds, and the price of such an operator is open in the same way the price of negation is open (Operators table :77-78). Show that `F` is just ⋛ or → and the strain dissolves.

## 3. What is explicitly not claimed

- Not that Shannon entropy of a measurement outcome is information in the system. It is context-relative.
- Not that a noncontextual assignment of definite values to all observables can be recovered. Kochen-Specker rules it out in three or more dimensions.
- Not that the framework settles which functional of the state is the definition. The slot is open.
- Not that this resolves an interpretation of quantum mechanics, or that it is `grounded`.
- Not an import that moves the wall: the state and entropy are already held in the physics frame. Fig 6, *"IMPORT adds an atom"*; this binding adds none.

## 5. What would settle it

An instrument, not agreement. The questions that would fire:

1. **Invariant test:** does the proposed quantity stay fixed under every change of measurement context for the same preparation?
2. **Pose test:** can an outcome be registered with no compatible-measurement set specified?
3. **Bond test:** does removing the context change nothing in the outcome distribution for every state?
4. **KS test:** does a noncontextual definite-value model reproduce all predictions in dimension at least three?
5. **Framework tests:** derive one of the six from the other five; admit an unrefusable state-state or structure-structure composition; show a lossless round trip for all states.

## 6. What an expert can check in twenty minutes

1. Read Fig 2 :29-30, Fig 9 :4-5 and :32, Fig 12 :5 and :104, and the Symbols table entry for `F`. Six short passages.
2. Check C2 against their own knowledge of contextuality: is an outcome a function of the compatible-measurement set as well as the state?
3. Check whether the von Neumann entropy really is invariant under change of basis, and whether the Shannon entropy of a read-out is not.
4. Name one instrument that would distinguish an invariant quantity from a context-relative read-out, or say why none is possible.
5. Send the refutation. A failure is more useful than an endorsement.
