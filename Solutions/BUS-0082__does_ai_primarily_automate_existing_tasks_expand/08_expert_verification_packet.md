# Expert verification packet - the Tether on AI, tasks and occupations

**What this is:** a claim in a form a labor economist or empirically minded practitioner can check. Mode, derivation and refuters are stated before the conclusion, so a reader knows what to look for and what would settle it.

**Mode: `specified` - not `grounded`.** No prospective per-occupation task measurement is claimed. This is a shape and a route, not a result. It is a simulated derivation checked for internal consistency against the figures, not a ground reading (`05_what_shape_means.md` section 7).

## 1. The claim, in one paragraph

The three things people mean by AI automating, expanding and creating work are three different operators, not three degrees of one effect. Automation removes the human operand from a task composition that already closes the gap between predicted and actual outcome, so it is subtraction and needs no new element. Expansion produces a term that was not in the worker's library before, but composes it from elements already in reach, often borrowed from a more able colleague. A genuinely new form of work is an import: it needs an element from a closure that differs, a new tool, market, institution or client, and no amount of rearranging the old elements supplies it. Which operator fires is a property of the occupation-task slot, not of the tool, which is why the balance varies by occupation. Exposure indices score perceived potential and are instruments, not outcomes; the ground is realized task time, quality, employment and pay. The claim is a shape any candidate answer must have; it does not name which occupation converts next.

## 2. Three checkable sub-claims

### C1 - Framework-internal; checkable by reading Figures 6, 8, 12 and the Operators table

- **Statement:** automation is subtraction, expansion is production inside the closure, and new forms of work are imports; the third is not an extreme version of the second.
- **Source, verbatim:**
  - Fig 12 :112 - *"Progress is a subtraction."* Operators table - *"A with B removed or absent"*.
  - Fig 12 :77 - *"A produces B; B did not exist before"*.
  - Fig 6 :21 - *"MINT composes inside the closure and can never add an atom."*
  - Fig 6 :14 - *"the only operator that moves the wall"*; Fig 8 :32 - *"Closure is idempotent. Composing inside a closed set never leaves it, so no search, however long, adds an atom."*
  - Fig 9 :57 - *"What remains is smaller and it is also categorically different: a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either."*
- **Refuter:** a genuinely new form of work generated entirely by composition inside an unchanged reach, with no atom entering from a differing closure; or a ground measurement that cannot tell a removed worker from an extended worker. Either fires against C1.

### C2 - Slot dependence and the instrument rule; checkable by reading Figures 11, 10 and 2, plus one empirical check

- **Statement:** the tool's valence is relative to the residual at the slot, capability is worker-and-habitat, and exposure indices are instruments that cannot be read as outcomes.
- **Source, verbatim:**
  - Fig 11 :49 - *"An actor’s valence is relative to the residual, not a property it carries."* Fig 11 :50 - *"The same actor is a benefactor to one residual and a malfactor to another, so mutualism and parasitism are relations rather than kinds."*
  - Fig 11 :39 - *"So the answerable question is not what intelligence is, but what makes a system capable. And capability is a property of agent-and-habitat, never of the agent alone."*
  - Fig 10 :24 - *"the ground does not decay, the channel does"*; Fig 10 :26 - *"stale percepts, dropped transients, saturated metrics, pooled readings"*
  - Fig 2 :29 - *"An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks."* The same line names *"a measurement"* and *"a market"* as instances.
- **What a researcher can check against their own knowledge:** Brynjolfsson, Li and Raymond (NBER 31161, 2023) report *"a 34% improvement for novice and low-skilled workers but with minimal impact on experienced and highly skilled workers"*. Noy and Zhang (Science 2023) report *"The average time taken decreased by 40% and output quality rose by 18%. Inequality between workers decreased."* Both are short-horizon task readings. Do they license a claim about the operator in a named occupation, or only about the instrument in a task? A "no" to the second question would be a real refutation of the mapping.
- **Refuter:** a task where the tool's valence is invariant across a documented change of residual and across worker skill; or a measured improvement that survives removing the tool from the worker's habitat; or an exposure index that predicts the realized operator prospectively per occupation with no ground measurement. Any one fires against C2.

### C3 - Structure and competing explanations

- **Statement:** the question is a union, not one number; two occupations with identical task atoms but different bonds are isomers, so a task inventory cannot predict the balance; imports need a source gate and a shadow test.
- **Source, verbatim:**
  - Fig 9 :57 - *"What remains is smaller and it is also categorically different: a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either."*
  - Fig 12 :117 - *"isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."*
  - Fig 12 :124 - *"functional group. A substructure whose presence fixes the outcome. The group says what kind; the qualifier says which member."*
  - Fig 6 :23 - *"No frame escapes its own closure. The bound moves only on contact with a frame whose closure differs."*
- **What a researcher can check:** Felten, Raj and Seamans (arXiv 2303.01157, 2023) find *"a positive correlation between wages and exposure to AI language modeling"*, while the ILO 2025 report finds *"As most occupations consist of tasks that require human input, transformation of jobs is the most likely impact of GenAI."* Are these two readings of the same instrument measuring the same thing? If neither a task list nor an exposure score fixes the bond, the isomer claim has work to do. A demonstration that a task list alone does fix the operator would refute it.
- **Refuter:** one intervention that produces all three operators in all three failure types with no categorically different residue; or two occupations with identical atoms and identical bonds whose balance diverges with no difference in ground contact or imported atoms.

## 3. What is explicitly not claimed

- Not `grounded`. No measurement has paid for any part of this.
- Not a theory of everything about work, and no final or unique theory claim.
- Not an average. No claim about the percentage of tasks or jobs automated, augmented or created economy-wide.
- Not that the tool, the worker or the task is irrelevant. They are Γ and habitat; the claim is that they do not settle the operator on their own.
- Not that exposure studies are wrong. They measure potential; reading them as outcomes is the error.
- Not that the labor literature independently corroborates this. Brynjolfsson et al., Acemoglu and Restrepo, Acemoglu, Autor and Felten et al. share one evidence pool. The ILO report and the organizational field experiment are lower-overlap, and they corroborate the mechanism shape, not a named occupation.
- Not that any specific occupation is the augmented or automated one. That is a `gap` at the instrument boundary, not a hidden answer.
- Not that this packet, produced with an LLM, is submittable or publishable as authored work. See the AI-policy note in section 6.

## 5. What would settle it

A prospective, per-occupation study that assigns the operator, records bonds as well as atoms, and sources every import. Concretely:

1. **Outcome test:** is the task outcome readable at all, or saturated and pooled? Indicators: stale percepts, saturated metrics, pooled readings (Fig 10 :25-26).
2. **Subtraction test:** was the human operand removed from a composition that otherwise still closes R, with no new atom? Fig 12 :112.
3. **Production test:** did a term appear that was not in the worker's library, composed from elements already in reach, including a colleague's practice? Fig 6 :23.
4. **Import test:** did an element enter from a closure that differs, and can its source gate and shadow test be shown? Fig 8 :9-13. If not, the "new work" claim has not been demonstrated.
5. **Source-discriminating instrument:** can the study distinguish a removed worker from an extended reach from an imported atom when all three produce the same productivity reading? The source essay says a reading alone *"cannot comment on the source"*.
