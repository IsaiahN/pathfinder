**Mode: `specified`, not `grounded`.** Figures are primary and decide. World sources are secondary, corroborate but never overrule, and a conflict returns the derivation to the domain census. This is a simulated derivation checked for internal consistency against the figures, not a ground reading.

## QUESTION

Does AI primarily automate existing tasks, expand what workers can do, or create new forms of work, and how does the balance vary by occupation? Restated as an effect: why does inserting one general-purpose tool close R = |Γ(b,a) - o'| by removing the worker in some occupation-slots, by extending the worker's reach in others, and by adding a slot that was not in the library in still others? R is indexed per occupation-task and never averaged (Fig 1 :19). Anchor: realized task outcome and employment, named in Fig 2 :29. The sought-for shape was bound before the run as an operator assignment per occupation plus an instrument that discriminates subtraction, production and import at the ground.

## DOMAIN CENSUS

Arity: a three-place relation among worker library, action and task outcome, indexed by occupation-task slot and ordered by time; carries an ordered bond and a threshold comparison bond, not symmetric ones. Symmetry: tool valence is relative to the slot (Fig 11 :49). Scale: worker step loop, population of workers, occupation structure across economies; the membrane is lossy (Fig 4).

## VERDICT (from the figures)

The figure-derived verdict: AI changes work through three different operators. Automation is subtraction of the human operand from an existing composition; expansion is production of a new term inside the worker's reach; genuinely new forms of work require an atom imported from a closure that differs, so the third is categorically different from the second. Which operator fires is a property of the occupation-task slot, not of the tool. The balance must be read per occupation against realized task outcomes; exposure indices measure potential and are instruments, not ground. Mode `specified`. Pool A corroborates the shape but counts as one witness. The two low-overlap sources corroborate mechanism, not a named occupation. Which occupation converts next is a declared `gap`, not a hidden answer.

- **cost if wrong:** misdirected diagnosis. A slot failing C3 would be given more C2 instruments, or a slot failing C1 would be told to import more, and a single lever would be applied to a union that needs three. In outreach terms, an expert's twenty minutes would be spent refuting a shape that was never the right shape. The cost is real but bounded and largely reversible.
- **undoable:** yes. No term is deleted if the claim fails; a refutation is added and the reach falls because *"something was added rather than because anything was deleted"* (Fig 6 :42-43). The shape and its six falsifiers are recorded in a provenance trail so a later reader can locate the error (Fig 10 :62-64).
- **reversed by:** any of the six pre-registered falsifiers, in particular (1) a new form of work produced with no import, (2) invariant tool valence across a change of residual, (3) an improvement that survives removing the tool from the habitat, (4) an exposure index that predicts the operator per occupation with no ground measurement, (5) an average that predicts the named slot and survives habitat substitution, or (6) one intervention restoring all three operators with no categorically different residue. A source with a genuinely different evidence pool showing that per-occupation operator assignment is fully predicted by averaged industry effects would also reverse it.
