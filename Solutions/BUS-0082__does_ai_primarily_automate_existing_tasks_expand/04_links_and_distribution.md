## 2. What the wider literature bears on the question

**One evidence pool, one witness.** Brynjolfsson et al., Acemoglu and Restrepo, Acemoglu, Autor and Felten et al. all work in the task-based labor economics frame, cite one another and reuse O*NET-style task taxonomies and exposure scores. Per Fig 2 :31, *"Independence, between two frames, is how little they draw on the same evidence."* Their agreement is one witness, not five. Fig 2 :40: *"disqualifying for a verdict: agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool"*.

- **Brynjolfsson, Li and Raymond (NBER 31161, QJE 2025)** find *"a 34% improvement for novice and low-skilled workers but with minimal impact on experienced and highly skilled workers"*. Prediction: expansion is concentrated in low-skill slots. **Corroborates** F2 and F3, the slot-dependence of the operator.
- **Acemoglu and Restrepo (NBER 24196, 2018)** identify *"the displacement effect"*, a productivity effect, and *"the creation of new labor-intensive tasks"*. Prediction: three distinct channels. **Corroborates** F1, but from the same pool, so it adds no independent verdict weight.
- **Autor (JEP 2015)** writes that *"the interplay between machine and human comparative advantage allows computers to substitute for workers in performing routine, codifiable tasks while amplifying the comparative advantage of workers in supplying problem-solving skills, adaptability, and creativity."* Prediction: two operators select by task content. **Corroborates** F1 and F2.
- **Acemoglu (NBER 32487, 2024)** warns that *"some of the new tasks created by AI may have negative social value (such as design of algorithms for online manipulation)"*. Prediction: the third operator is not a uniform good and is a distinct category. **Corroborates** F1 and refuses a naive "more new work is always better" reading.
- **Felten, Raj and Seamans (arXiv 2303.01157, 2023)** find *"a positive correlation between wages and exposure to AI language modeling"*. Prediction on its face: exposure tracks high wages, which **conflicts** with a simple "AI hits low skill first" story. The conflict was returned to the census (re-census R1 in `01_figure_grounded_audit.md`): exposure is an instrument, not an outcome, and the correlation is evidence that a task list does not fix the bond. Verdict unchanged.
- **Noy and Zhang (Science 2023, record via Stanford SCALE)** report *"The average time taken decreased by 40% and output quality rose by 18%. Inequality between workers decreased."* Prediction: expansion and compression of within-task inequality in a short experiment. **Corroborates** F2 and F4 at the task stratum; the writing-task setting is an instrument, not the employment ground.

**Low-overlap witnesses** from different fields and evidence pools:

1. **ILO / NASK, Working Paper 140 (2025)** builds exposure from 29,753 Polish tasks, a survey of 1,640 workers and expert review, and concludes that *"As most occupations consist of tasks that require human input, transformation of jobs is the most likely impact of GenAI."* This is an institutional, tripartite evidence pool, not the US NBER pool. It **corroborates** the expansion/transformation reading and the per-occupation indexing of F5. It still measures *potential*, so it remains an instrument under F4.
2. **Organizational field experiment (Dell'Acqua et al., NBER 33641, 2025)** reports that *"individuals with AI matched the performance of teams without AI"* and that professionals using AI produced balanced solutions regardless of background. Different method, different unit (team rather than occupation), **corroborates** F3 at the team stratum and Fig 6 :23 at the colleague stratum.

**Framework-internal essays** are used for the canonical reading of "shape" in `05_what_shape_means.md` only. They were opened this session but their author wrote the figures, so they carry no independent world witness weight.

## 3. Distribution rules

Figure-grounded reasons for how material is offered:

- **Fig 4 :22:** *"Only methods cross up. Only head starts come down."* Send the generator, the residual and the refuter, not the conclusion. A recording carried upward *"looks like knowledge and is a description of one occasion"* (Fig 4 :25).
- **Fig 10 :42:** *"A verifier that can reconstruct the claim cannot verify it."* A recipient who has absorbed the framework before being asked for a verdict has joined the frame being checked.
- **Fig 9 :27-28:** *"describe it before you go looking, or any frame you pick will seem to fit."* Ask what they would expect to see if the operator shape were true before showing the source.
- **Fig 2 :40:** agreement from a shared pool is worthless as a verdict. Later agreement about the framework is never pooled as corroboration.
- **Fig 10 :59:** *"an interest in the outcome is unavoidable, an interest in the reading is disqualifying."* Declare the stake; do not ask for belief.
- **Seat Map:** *"High correlation is disqualifying for a verdict and efficient for a convention."* A list selected for people likely to agree produces a convention, not a check.

**A hard practical constraint.** The AOM Publishing page states: *"Artificial Intelligence: Work submitted to AOM must be created by the authors and not the product of artificial intelligence tools unless appropriate to the research question and properly cited."* This solution is an LLM-assisted simulated derivation and is labelled as such everywhere. It must not be submitted as authored work. The legitimate use is as a private checking instrument: post the residual and the refuter, invite a human to run the check, and let that human author whatever they conclude, with disclosure as required by the venue.

## 4. The two-stage offer, compressed

**Stage 1.** Send `08_expert_verification_packet.md` sections 1 to 3 only: the one-paragraph claim, the three checkable sub-claims with their verbatim figure lines and refuters, and the non-claims. No provenance bundle, no essay links.

**Stage 2, opt-in only.** If the recipient is curious and has already recorded a verdict on stage 1, offer the three public links: the figure text, the formula and usage rule, and the essays. Their later agreement is a conversation, not corroboration (Fig 2 :40).

**Stage 3.** Offer the formula as a method, never as an answer (Fig 4 :22). Each user writes a refuter before running it and states the mode of what they get.

**Stop rule.** If stage 1 fails, the theory is not offered. A failure is more useful than an endorsement.

**Hosting.** Nothing here is hosted or sent by this agent. The human operator hosts the packet on an opt-in page and sends one to one. Prefer a download page that lets a recipient decline contact over an attachment pushed to a list.
