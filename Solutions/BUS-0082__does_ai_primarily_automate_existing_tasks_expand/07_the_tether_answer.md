# The Tether's answer on AI, tasks and occupations

**Mode: `specified` - not `grounded`.** This is a shape and a route, not a result. Simulated derivation checked against the figures (`05_what_shape_means.md` section 7), not a ground reading. No per-occupation task measurement pays for any claim here.

## 1. The residual it answers

An answer would explain why inserting the same general-purpose tool closes the gap between what a worker's routines predict a task will return and what it actually returns in three different ways: by removing the worker, by extending the worker's reach, and by adding a slot that was not in the worker's library. R is indexed per occupation-task and never averaged (Fig 1 :19, :22). The question is about the **operator**, not about the level of productivity.

The union must be unbundled before it is searched (Fig 9 :54). It is three conditions, not one hard question:

- **C1:** the task outcome stays readable as a ground reading, so the slot can be graded at all.
- **C2:** the worker's reach is composed inside the closure already held, which covers subtraction and production.
- **C3:** a new atom enters from a closure that differs, which covers new forms of work.

## 2. The shape

1. **Automation is subtraction, not a stronger form of augmentation.** The human operand is removed from a composition that already closes R. Fig 12 :112: *"Progress is a subtraction."* No new atom is needed. Operators table: *"A with B removed or absent"*.
2. **Expansion is production inside the reach.** A term appears that was not in the worker's library, but it composes from what the library and reachable peers already hold. Operators table: *"A produces B; B did not exist before"*. Fig 6 :21: *"MINT composes inside the closure and can never add an atom."*
3. **New work is an import, not a mint.** Fig 6 :14: *"the only operator that moves the wall"*. Fig 8 :32: *"Closure is idempotent. Composing inside a closed set never leaves it, so no search, however long, adds an atom."* A genuinely new form of work needs a new atom from a differing closure: a new tool, a new market, a new institution, a new client. It is categorically different from the second option (Fig 9 :57).
4. **Which operator fires is a property of the slot.** Fig 11 :49: *"An actor’s valence is relative to the residual, not a property it carries."* The same assistant substitutes in one task and complements in another. This is why the balance varies by occupation; that clause is the locus of the answer, not a qualifier.
5. **Capability is worker-and-habitat, not AI alone.** Fig 11 :39: *"capability is a property of agent-and-habitat, never of the agent alone."* Fig 11 :40: *"An improvement that does not change contact changes nothing, however much it improves."* A percentage gain without a task, a colleague set and a measurement is an agent-only reading.
6. **Exposure indices are instruments, not the ground.** Fig 10 :24: *"the ground does not decay, the channel does"*; :26: *"stale percepts, dropped transients, saturated metrics, pooled readings"*. AIOE and the ILO Global Index score perceived potential from task descriptions and expert judgment. They are Γ, not o'.
7. **The ground is the realized task outcome, not agreement.** Fig 2 :29 names *"a measurement, an experiment, a counter, a market, a patient outcome"* as anchors. Time per task, independently scored quality, employment and wage are the anchors here. Analyst consensus and index rankings are instruments.
8. **Two occupations with the same task atoms can still differ.** Under Fig 12 :117 they are isomers if the bonds differ: *"same atoms, different bond, different substance."* If they share only a `worker → tool → outcome` pattern, they are a functional group (Fig 12 :124). A task inventory alone cannot predict the operator.

## 3. What it explicitly does not say

- Not `grounded`. No prospective per-occupation measurement has paid for this assignment.
- Not a name. It does not say which occupation converts to which operator next. Fig 9 :32: *"you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself."*
- Not an average. It makes no claim about what fraction of tasks or jobs is "automated" economy-wide. Such numbers are filters for budget, not witnesses (Fig 9 :60).
- Not that the tool, the worker or the task is irrelevant. They are Γ and habitat. The claim is that Γ and the tool do not settle the operator on their own (Fig 11 :40).
- Not that exposure studies are wrong. They are instruments built to measure potential; the error would be to read them as outcomes (Fig 2 :37-38).
- Not corroboration by agreement. Brynjolfsson et al., Acemoglu and Restrepo, Acemoglu, Autor and Felten et al. share the task-based labor-economics evidence pool (Fig 2 :31, :40). The ILO report and the organizational field experiment are lower-overlap witnesses, and they corroborate mechanism, not a named occupation.
- Not a theory of everything about work. It stops where the instrument stops.

## 4. What would settle it - the ground

A source-discriminating instrument, not a further argument. The source essay's metamerism rule: a reading *"cannot comment on the source"*, and *"The answer is unreached until a better instrument exists."* Here the readings are task time, quality, employment and wage; the candidate sources are a removed human operand, an extended reach, and an imported atom.

The falsifiers, pre-registered in `01` and repeated in `08`:

1. a genuinely new form of work produced entirely by rearranging existing tasks with no import;
2. a tool whose valence is invariant across a documented change of residual and across worker skill;
3. a measured improvement that survives removing the tool from the worker's habitat;
4. an exposure index that predicts the realized operator prospectively and per occupation with no ground measurement;
5. an economy-wide average that predicts which named occupation changes next and survives a habitat substitution;
6. one intervention that produces all three operators in all three failure types with nothing categorically different left over.

A prospective study that assigns the operator per occupation, records bonds as well as task atoms, sources every import, and reports which condition failed before a change in employment would move this from `specified` toward `grounded`.

## 5. The honest stop

**Unreached, not unreachable.** Fig 13 :134: *"Unreachable is a verdict here, not a silence,"* and Fig 8 :39: *"So a search that finds nothing is never a proof of absence."* The shape stops at the limit of the available instruments and records the lineage so the stop is checkable. The missing thing is a discriminator of operators at the task level, and it is owed. Naming a winning occupation from a productivity reading would be the metamerism error the source essay forbids. The three conditions in section 2 are a shape; the fourth thing, which named occupation gets which operator next, is not held and is not pretended.

## 6. One-paragraph version for outreach

> The Tether does not give a single percentage for AI and work. It says that the three things people mean are three different operators. Automation removes the worker from a task composition that already works and needs no new element. Expansion produces something the worker could not do before out of elements already in reach, often borrowed from a more able colleague. A genuinely new form of work is different in kind: it needs an element imported from outside that reach, a new tool, market, institution or client, and no amount of rearranging the old elements supplies it. Which operator fires is a property of the occupation-task slot, not of the tool, which is why the balance varies by occupation. Exposure scores measure a perceived possibility, not the realized outcome; the ground is what actually happens to time, quality, employment and pay. What is offered is that shape, six falsifiers written in advance, and the instruments that would close it. Which named occupation converts next stays open until a source-discriminating instrument exists, and the stop is recorded rather than filled.
