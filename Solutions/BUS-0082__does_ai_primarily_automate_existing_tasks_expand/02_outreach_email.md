# Outreach draft - validate the output first, then decide about the theory

**Use:** one to one, or as a cover note attached to a submission through a public venue in `03_public_contact_points.csv`. **Not** for bulk send. No personal emails are used. The recipient must have an opt-in or public institutional route.

**Hard precondition:** the stage-1 output must survive the recipient's check before any theory is shown. The output here is a `specified` shape with no per-occupation ground measurement, so the legitimate ask is a refutation of the shape, never endorsement of it. If the recipient cannot be shown a failure condition they can actually test, do not send.

---

**Subject:** Check one claim about how AI changes tasks before you decide whether the framework is worth reading

> Hi [name],
>
> I have one narrow claim about AI and work that I would like you to check against what you already know. I am not asking you to read a theory first, and I am not asking you to agree with anything. I am asking for a failure. If the claim does not survive, there is nothing else to look at.
>
> **The claim (`specified`, not `grounded`):** the three things people mean by "AI automates", "AI expands", and "AI creates new work" are three different operators, not three degrees of one effect.
>
> 1. **Automation is subtraction.** It removes the human operand from a task composition that already closes the gap between what the worker expected and what the task returned. No new element is needed.
> 2. **Expansion is production inside the worker's reach.** The worker can do something that was not in their library before, but every element of it was already available to them or to a colleague whose practice they can import.
> 3. **A new form of work is an import.** It needs an element that was not in that reach at all: a new tool, a new market, a new institution, a new client. No amount of rearranging the old elements supplies it.
>
> The further claim is that which of the three fires is a property of the occupation-task slot, not of the tool. The same assistant substitutes in one slot and complements in another. That is why the balance varies by occupation; it is not a side observation.
>
> **What I am not claiming:** that any particular occupation is a case in point, that I can name which one converts next, that this is settled by evidence, or that it is the only account of AI and work. I am offering a shape any candidate answer would have to have. A shape is not a name. I am also not treating exposure scores as outcomes: they are instruments that measure a perceived possibility, and a reading of an instrument is not a reading of the ground.
>
> **What would refute it, written down before I sent this:**
> 1. a genuinely new form of work produced entirely by rearranging a worker's existing tasks, with nothing new entering from outside that reach;
> 2. a task where the tool's effect is invariant across a documented change in what the task requires and across worker skill, that is, always substitution or always complementarity;
> 3. a measured improvement that survives removing the tool from the worker's whole habitat, including colleagues and workflow;
> 4. an exposure index that predicts, prospectively and per occupation, which of the three operators fires, with no measurement of time, quality or employment;
> 5. an economy-wide average that predicts which named occupation changes next and survives a change of habitat in that occupation;
> 6. one intervention that produces all three kinds of change in all three failure types and leaves nothing categorically different behind.
>
> If any one holds, I withdraw the claim. A single line saying which one holds and why is more useful to me than agreement. If you would rather not engage, no reply is needed.
>
> **If it survives, and only then:** I can send the three public sources below. The figures, not my summary of them, are the authority.
>
> - Figure text with line numbers: the local canonical figure text in this solution folder.
> - Formula and usage rule: the `figures` folder on the public `general-agent` branch of the `tether` repository.
> - The essays on source, search shape and the prestige/action separation: my public Substack archive.
>
> I will send URLs only after you tell me what you would count as a failure.
>
> **If you want to use the machinery:** the formula is public. Run it on an occupation you know well and can check against a real outcome. Write down what would refute your result *before* you run it, and state the mode of what you get. A reported failure is more useful to me than a reported success.
>
> Thanks,
> [name]

## Why this version is ordered this way

| the move | figure-grounded reason |
|---|---|
| Residual and refuter first, theory last | *"Bring a characterised residual, never the source."* (Fig 10 :52). The recipient checks the claim without holding the framework's interior |
| Do not ask for agreement | *"Alignment is triangulation, not negotiation. A mirror cannot correct you."* (Fig 2 :35) |
| Three operators kept separate | *"What remains is smaller and it is also categorically different: a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either."* (Fig 9 :57); a blended impact score is a pooled reading (Fig 10 :26) |
| Pre-registered refuter | Seat Map: *"And a falsifier written before the run is a design instrument, not only an honesty device."* |
| Mode stated on the claim | *"Mode. general · specified · grounded. State which."* (`THE_FORMULA.md`) |
| Exposure scores not called outcomes | *"the ground does not decay, the channel does"* (Fig 10 :24); instruments can be wrong on axes they do not measure (Fig 2 :37-38) |
| No name for the winning occupations | *"you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself"* (Fig 9 :32) |
| Links only after a verdict | Fig 10 :42, *"A verifier that can reconstruct the claim cannot verify it."* Once the recipient has the framework, their later agreement is not corroboration (Fig 2 :40) |
| Prestige kept out of the ask | *The Recursive Self-Improvement Trap*: *"Prestige can fund an action. It can give an action a budget to be tried. But it cannot settle an action. Only the ground can settle it."* |
| Offer the formula as a method, not an answer | *"Only methods cross up. Only head starts come down."* (Fig 4 :22) |
| Ask the user for their own refuter | Formula, "Direction": a frame cannot check the direction of its own question; make the sought-for shape an ordinary term |

## Sending rules

- One recipient at a time. No distribution lists, no mail merge, no forwarding chain.
- Use only a public institutional contact, a call for submissions, or an address the person has published themselves. Record the source and date.
- A reply is a conversation, never corroboration. If a recipient returns a refutation, copy it into `11_audit.md` with provenance and have the auditor seat re-evaluate it as if it came from any other source (Fig 2 :40).
- Do not follow up with the theory after a "no". *"an interest in the outcome is unavoidable, an interest in the reading is disqualifying"* (Fig 10 :59).
- The AOM publishing page states: *"Artificial Intelligence: Work submitted to AOM must be created by the authors and not the product of artificial intelligence tools unless appropriate to the research question and properly cited."* This packet is an LLM-assisted simulated derivation. It must not be submitted as authored work. A human researcher who runs the check owns and writes whatever is published, with disclosure as the venue requires.
