# Expert verification packet: the Tether on the MMT versus mainstream disagreement

**What this is:** a claim in a form a monetary theorist or economic methodologist can check. Mode, derivation and refuters are stated before the conclusion, so the reader knows what to look for and what would settle it.

**Mode: `specified`, not `grounded`.** No measurement of the disagreement is claimed. This is a shape and a route.

## 1. The claim, in one paragraph

The question "how much of the MMT versus mainstream disagreement is mechanisms, and how much is policy priorities and risk judgments?" is not well formed. A disagreement that does not shrink with effort and where each side works well on a different subset is several well-formed questions and must be split. Mechanism claims are about the world and settle against data and model comparison; policy priorities are about people and settle by judgment about values; risk judgments are a mixed compound of a probability claim and an acceptability claim and need a further split. Since the two parts have different anchors and different currencies, there is no single proportion that apportions them. The correct output is a decomposition with an anchor and a verdict per part, never an average.

## 2. Three checkable sub-claims

### C1 - The split is required (framework-internal; checkable by reading Figure 9)

- **Statement:** a non-shrinking disagreement with each side working on a different subset must be split, and a bundled problem with overlapping conditions is a union, not a partition.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 9 :4-5 - *"Disagreements that do not shrink with effort, where each rule keeps working well on a different subset, are not one hard question but several well-formed ones. Split it rather than search."* Fig 9 :55 - *"A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones."* Fig 9 :57-58 - *"a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either."*
- **World corroboration:** the Library of Babel essay: *"Perhaps your problem was a bundle or conflation of several problems masquerading as one? You will need to search all of those problems individually to find their answers and then combine them in a satisfactory way."*
- **Refuter:** show that the disagreement does shrink with effort against one ground, or exhibit a dispute census in which every point lives in a single condition.

### C2 - The two parts have different anchors (framework-internal; checkable by reading Figure 2)

- **Statement:** mechanism claims settle on an empirical ground; policy priorities settle on human judgment because they are constitutively about people. Data is an instrument in the second domain, not an anchor.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 2 :36 - *"An anchor is legitimate when the question is constitutively about it."* Fig 2 :37-38 - *"Human judgment is the right anchor for is this a good answer for a person, because the question is about people. It is only an instrument for is this code correct, and instruments can be wrong on axes they do not measure."* Fig 2 :29-30 - *"An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks."*
- **World corroboration:** the Wikipedia article on positive and normative economics distinguishes positive statements, which are *"potentially falsifiable and may be contradicted by evidence"*, from a normative statement, which *"reflects value judgments"*.
- **Refuter:** show that a policy priority is constitutively about the world rather than about people, or produce an instrument that settles an "ought" without importing a value.

### C3 - A single proportion mixes two currencies that do not add (framework-internal; checkable by reading Figures 12 and 1)

- **Statement:** the "how much" number adds an empirical residual to a value choice in one figure. The figures name that sum as the error.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 12 :125 - *"A composition costs in two currencies, and they do not add."* Fig 12 :130 - *"Summing them into one figure is the error this names, not the fix for it."* Fig 1 :19 - *"The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated."* Fig 1 :22 - *"Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average."*
- **World corroboration:** risk perception is defined as *"the subjective judgement that people make about the characteristics and severity of a risk"* and *"Risk perceptions often differ from statistical assessments of risk"*, so the components of a risk judgment are not one currency.
- **Refuter:** exhibit a single currency that prices both a mechanism claim and a policy priority without loss, and name the instrument that reads it.

## 3. What is explicitly not claimed

- Not a defence or critique of MMT. No claim that either school is right.
- Not a fraction, not "mostly mechanisms" and not "mostly policy."
- Not a claim that the mechanism questions are already settled.
- Not a claim that policy priorities are arbitrary. They have a legitimate anchor in human judgment.
- Not `grounded`. No survey, dataset or dispute census was taken.

## 5. What would settle it

A ground reading, not agreement. In order of cost:

1. **A dispute census** that assigns each live point of disagreement to a condition under an explicit protocol. The Caplan and Miller reduction claim makes this necessary work, not decoration.
2. **A shrinking test** showing mechanism disputes fall under one empirical protocol while value disputes do not.
3. **A currency test** exhibiting one measure for both parts, with the round trip gap measured (Fig 4 :30).
4. **A no-value instrument test** settling an "ought" without importing a value.
5. Any of the four pre-registered refuters in `01` section 3 firing.

## 6. What an expert can check in twenty minutes

1. Read Fig 9 :4-5 and :55, Fig 2 :36-38, and Fig 12 :125 and :130. Five short passages.
2. Name one live MMT dispute that is purely a world claim and one that is purely a value claim. If you cannot, the split is in trouble.
3. Take the dispute you think is "mostly policy" and check whether it reduces to a fact dispute (the Caplan and Miller challenge). Say which anchor settles it.
4. Try to state a single currency for both. If you cannot, say why that is a limitation of the instrument rather than a limitation of the question.
5. Send the refutation. A failure is more useful than an endorsement.
