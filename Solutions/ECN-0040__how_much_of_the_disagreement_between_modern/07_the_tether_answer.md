# The Tether's answer: how much of the MMT versus mainstream disagreement is mechanisms, and how much is policy priorities and risk judgments?

**Mode: `specified`, not `grounded`.** This is a shape and a split, not a measurement. Simulated derivation checked against the figures (`05_what_shape_means.md` section 6), not a ground reading.

## 1. The residual it answers

The question asks for a proportion between two parts of a disagreement. The residual is not "what is the true fraction." It is: the held term "one disagreement with two measurable parts" does not survive the figures' first test. Fig 9 :4-5: *"Disagreements that do not shrink with effort, where each rule keeps working well on a different subset, are not one hard question but several well-formed ones. Split it rather than search."* The answer owed is a refusal of the framing followed by a decomposed shape, not a number.

## 2. The shape

1. **The disagreement is a union compound, not one sum.** Fig 9 :55: *"A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones."* Risk judgments overlap both mechanisms and policy, so they are a mixed compound requiring their own split.
2. **The mechanism part has an empirical ground.** World claims about money creation, deficit financing and inflation settle against data and model comparison. Fig 2 :29-30: *"An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks."* Palley's *"MMT lacks a plausible theory of inflation"* and the 2024 model comparison are claims of this kind.
3. **The policy part has a different anchor.** Fig 2 :37-38: *"Human judgment is the right anchor for is this a good answer for a person, because the question is about people. It is only an instrument for is this code correct, and instruments can be wrong on axes they do not measure."* Data is an instrument here, not an anchor.
4. **Risk judgments split into probability and acceptability.** The probability of inflation belongs to the empirical part; the acceptable level of inflation belongs to the value part. The risk-perception source confirms that judged severity comes apart from statistical assessment.
5. **The two parts do not add.** Fig 12 :125: *"A composition costs in two currencies, and they do not add."* Fig 12 :130: *"Summing them into one figure is the error this names, not the fix for it."* Fig 1 :22: *"Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average."* So no honest percentage can be reported.
6. **The refusal comes before any mint.** Formula step 2: *"REFUTED: a term held is false; what is owed is a refusal, not an explanation"*. The term "one measurable disagreement with two commensurable parts" is refused.
7. **The output is a per-domain verdict map.** For each named dispute: its condition, its anchor, and a verdict that is never averaged with the others. Fig 9 :60: *"Use filters for the budget and witnesses for the verdict. Never let a filter hand you a verdict."*

## 3. What it explicitly does not say

- Not a defence of MMT and not a critique of it. No claim that either school is right on any substantive point.
- Not a claim that the disagreement is mostly mechanisms or mostly policy. No fraction is asserted.
- Not a claim that mainstream economics has settled the mechanism questions. Several remain open and empirical.
- Not a claim that policy priorities are arbitrary. They are anchored in human judgment, which is a legitimate anchor for a question constitutively about people (Fig 2 :36).
- Not `grounded`. No survey, no dataset, no census of the actual disputes was taken here.

## 4. What would settle it, the ground

A ground reading, not more argument. Concretely:

1. **A dispute census.** Enumerate the live points of disagreement and assign each to a condition by using an explicit protocol. The Caplan and Miller conflict in `01` F8 says this classification is empirical work, not a stipulation.
2. **A shrinking test.** Track whether mechanism disputes fall under one empirical protocol while value disputes do not. If all disputes shrink under one ground, the split claim fails.
3. **A currency test.** Exhibit an instrument that prices a mechanism claim and a policy priority in one currency without loss. Fig 4 :30 requires measuring the round-trip gap rather than assuming it is zero.
4. **A no-value instrument test.** Produce an instrument that settles an "ought" claim without importing a value. If one exists, D2 gains the empirical anchor this answer says it lacks.
5. **Standing refuters from `01`:** the four pre-registered conditions. If any fires, the shape is withdrawn.

## 5. The honest stop

**Unreached, not unreachable.** A census of the disputes is possible; it simply was not run here. The framework stops at the limit of what the available instruments distinguish and records the lineage. *The Recursive Self-Improvement Trap*: *"You may all agree and be wrong."* Agreement among economists, including agreement with this shape, is not a measurement. Fig 8 :39: *"A search that finds nothing is never a proof of absence."*

## 6. One-paragraph version for outreach

> The Tether does not say what fraction of the MMT versus mainstream disagreement is mechanisms and what fraction is policy. It says the question is not well formed. A disagreement that does not shrink with effort, where each side keeps working well on a different subset, is several questions and must be split. Mechanism claims settle against data; policy priorities settle by judgment about values; risk judgments mix a probability claim with an acceptability claim and must be split again. Because the two parts have different anchors and different currencies, a single percentage adds things the figures say do not add. What the Tether offers is the shape a correct answer must have: a decomposition, a named ground per part, per-domain verdicts kept separate, and four refuters written in advance. The "how much" number stays open until a ground settles it.
