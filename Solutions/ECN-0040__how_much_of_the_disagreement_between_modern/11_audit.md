## 1. Independent domain census

I redid the census before reading the primary's conclusions, using the WATSON order: R by arity, symmetry and scale; one line per domain with actors, currency and anchor; partition or union; order by dependency.

- **R (arity / symmetry / scale).** The residual is the request to apportion the MMT versus mainstream disagreement. Arity: at least two conditions, with risk a mixed third. Symmetry: the parts do not range against the same anchor, so the relation is asymmetric. Scale: first order for claims about money and inflation, second order for the schools. My first figure was Fig 9 :4-5, the split test, reached from the shape of the word "how much" rather than from the primary's figure list.
- **Domain 1, mechanism claims.** Actors: models, data, estimators. Currency: prediction minus observation. Anchor: empirical ground. Nameable, so a domain.
- **Domain 2, policy priorities.** Actors: governments, central banks, voters. Currency: value and preference. Anchor: human judgment, because the question is constitutively about people. Nameable, so a domain.
- **Domain 3, risk judgments.** Actors: the same plus modelers. Currency: probability mixed with weighting. Anchor: none single. A compound until split.
- **Domain 4, the proportion.** No actors, no currency, no anchor. Not a domain.
- **Union, not partition.** D3 overlaps D1 and D2.
- **Order by dependency.** Describe, split, anchor, settle, compose. A verdict on D2 cannot be composed before D1's ground is named, because the input never arrived.

My census matches the primary's four-way structure and its union reading. I reached it from Fig 9's split test and the anchor definition in Fig 2, rather than from Fig 12's currency rule, which the primary led with in F3. ## 4. Round trip gap R_T

The Symbols table defines R_T as the gap between what was sent up and what came back down, `gap(x, (T_E ∘ T_A)(x))`, measuring what the coarser description cannot hold. Fig 4 :30 requires it to be measured rather than assumed. I ran the round trip on the condensation the folder actually uses.

1. **Sent up (x):** the verbatim figure set, chiefly Fig 9 :4-5 (split rule), Fig 2 :36-38 (anchor rule), Fig 12 :125-130 (two currencies), Fig 1 :19-22 (no aggregation), Fig 9 :60 (filters are not witnesses).
2. **Abstraction (T_A):** the shorthand carried in `01`, `07` and `09`: the disagreement is a union, the parts have different anchors, the currencies do not add, and the answer is a split rather than a proportion.
3. **Concretisation (T_E):** the original question as asked, "how much of the disagreement is mechanisms and how much is policy priorities and risk judgments?", plus the tempting answer of a single percentage.

**R_T is nonzero.** Four things the shorthand holds do not survive the trip back down:

- the **classification of particular disputes**, which the Caplan and Miller conflict shows cannot be stipulated (F8);
- the **split of risk judgments** into probability and acceptability, which the shorthand compresses into one compound;
- the **asymmetry of the two anchors**, which a proportion silently makes symmetric by placing both parts on one scale;
- the **order of refusal before mint**, which the question reverses by asking for a calculation before testing the held term.

Conversely, one thing survives cleanly: the disagreement must be split, and each part has a nameable anchor except D4, which has none. That is the residue worth sending. R_T was **not** measured numerically, because no ground reading exists; it is recorded qualitatively as the enumerated set of dropped constraints. Treating "the answer is just a split" as identical to the figure set would set R_T to zero and would be the dead-reckoning error Fig 4 :30 names. The honest form is a lossy condensation with a recorded gap, which is what `09_watson_answer.md` states.
