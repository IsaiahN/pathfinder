# Outreach draft: check the shape claim before deciding whether the framing is worth keeping

**Use:** one-to-one, or as a cover note attached to a submission through the public venues in `03_public_contact_points.csv`. **Not** for bulk send.

**Hard precondition:** do not send until the audit findings F1 to F3 in `01_figure_grounded_audit.md` have survived your own check. The output must stand on its own or the two-stage offer collapses on contact.

---

**Subject:** A claim about the MMT debate that you can refute in one line

> Hi [name],
>
> I have one specific output I would like you to check against what you already know. I am not asking you to agree with it, and I am not asking you to read a theory first. If the output does not survive, there is nothing else to look at.
>
> **The output (`specified`, not `grounded`):** the question "how much of the MMT versus mainstream disagreement is mechanisms, and how much is policy priorities and risk judgments?" asks for a proportion. The figures I work from require asking first whether this is one question or several. Their rule is that a disagreement which does not shrink with effort, where each side keeps working well on a different subset, is several well-formed questions and should be split, not searched. Mechanism claims ("does money-financed spending at full employment cause inflation?") are about the world and settle against data. Policy priorities ("should full employment be purchased at a higher inflation tolerance?") are about people and settle by judgment about values. Risk judgments mix the two: the probability is a world claim, the accepted level is a value claim. Since the two parts have different anchors and different currencies, there is no single number that apportions them. A percentage would mix two currencies the figures say do not add.
>
> **What the claim is not:** it is not a defence of MMT, not a critique of it, not a claim that one side is right, and not a claim that the disagreement is small or large. It does not say that mainstream economics has settled the mechanism questions, and it does not say that MMT's policy goals are wrong. It says only that the question as framed is not well formed, and that a checkable answer is a split with a named ground for each part rather than a ratio.
>
> **What would refute it, written before sending this:**
> 1. the disagreement is shown to shrink with effort against one ground, so it really is one question;
> 2. a single currency is exhibited that prices mechanism claims and policy priorities without loss;
> 3. an instrument is produced that settles a policy priority without importing a value;
> 4. a census of the actual disputes shows every one lives in a single condition.
>
> If any holds, I withdraw the claim. A one-line "this fails because..." is more useful to me than agreement. If you would rather not engage, no reply is needed.
>
> **If it survives, and only then:** here are the sources. The figures are the authority, not my summary of them.
> - Figures text with line numbers (local extract): `FRAMEWORK/reference/03-FIGURES_TEXT.md`
> - Canonical usage rule: https://github.com/IsaiahN/tether/blob/general-agent/figures/README.md
> - The wider reading that bears on this: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Positive_and_normative_economics and https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Modern_Monetary_Theory
>
> **If you want to reuse the method:** the eight-step loop is in the figures link. Run it on a problem you know well and can check against a ground, and write down what would refute your result *before* you run it. A reported failure is more useful to me than a reported success.
>
> Thanks,
> [name]
