## QUESTION

Decide, in effect terms, what the Tether says about the request to apportion the MMT versus mainstream disagreement into an economic-mechanisms part and a policy-priorities-and-risk-judgments part. Option A: treat it as one measurable disagreement and report a proportion. Option B: test whether it is one question or several, then give a per-part verdict with a named anchor. What each leads to: A produces a number that mixes two currencies and has no instrument to settle it; B produces a decomposed, checkable shape whose refusal can be tested in one line.

## DOMAIN CENSUS

R by arity, symmetry and scale, then one line per domain with actors, currency and anchor; partition or union; order by dependency.

- **R, the residual.** The persisted MMT versus mainstream disagreement. Arity: at least two conditions, with risk itself mixed. Symmetry: the parts do not range against the same anchor, so the relation is not symmetric. Scale: first order for individual claims about money, deficits and inflation, second order for the schools themselves. Fig 9 :4-5 supplies the split test and Fig 9 :57-58 the union reading.
- **Domain 1, mechanism claims.** Actors: competing models, data, estimators. Currency: prediction minus observation, R per slot. Anchor: the empirical ground, a measurement that pays. This is a domain; the anchor is nameable.
- **Domain 2, policy priorities.** Actors: elected governments, central banks, voters, distributional stakeholders. Currency: preference and value. Anchor: human judgment, because the question is constitutively about people. This is a domain with a different anchor.
- **Domain 3, risk judgments.** Actors: the same, plus modelers. Currency: probability estimates mixed with value weighting. Anchor: none single, because probability belongs to D1 and acceptability to D2. A compound, not a domain until split.
- **Domain 4, the "how much" score.** No actors that can settle it, no common currency, no anchor. Not a domain.
- **Partition or union.** Union, not partition. D3 overlaps D1 and D2.
- **Order by dependency.** Describe the residual, split the union, name an anchor per part, settle mechanism claims on their ground, then compose the policy verdict. Fig 3 :22-23 says a step whose input never arrives is a reading of nothing.

Strain test applied: Fig 2 :36-38 could be read as making human judgment the anchor for all human affairs, including mechanism claims made by people. Re-census resolves it: the anchor rule is about what a question is constitutively about, and a claim about the world remains a world question even when people make it. Different objects, so no strain after re-census. The Caplan and Miller reduction claim in the world section is a genuine strain about classification and is recorded, not hidden.

## VERDICT (from the figures)

The question as asked is refused. The MMT versus mainstream disagreement is a union compound, not one measurable sum. Mechanism claims have a landable empirical ground; policy priorities have a different, legitimate anchor in human judgment; risk judgments are a mixed compound that must be split again; and the requested proportion has no anchor and no common currency. What survives is the shape of a correct answer: decompose the disputes, name an anchor per part, keep the verdicts separate, do not add them, and record the round-trip gap. This is a `specified`, ungrounded result. The strongest single figure-grounded point is Fig 9 :4-5: the disagreement does not shrink and each side works on a different subset, so split it.

- **cost if wrong:** bounded and locatable. If the split is wrong, the error shows up as a dispute that was assigned to one anchor but is actually settled by the other, and the Caplan and Miller conflict gives a concrete way to find it. The larger cost is spending the independence of an outside reader by sending a conclusion instead of a refuter. Fig 2 :42 - "Buy independence in proportion to the cost of being wrong." That cost is avoidable by leading with the shape claim and its four refuters.
- **undoable:** yes. A dispute can be reassigned to a different condition without withdrawing the split method; a failed classificaton is a correction on the census, not a collapse of the framework. No primitive and no school-level claim has to be retracted. Fig 9 :40-41 - "keep every cut ranked and reversible" applies to the classification cuts.
- **reversed by:** a ground reading, not agreement. A dispute census showing every point lives in one condition would break the union claim; an instrument that settles an "ought" without importing a value would give D2 an empirical anchor and break the anchor rule; a single currency that prices both parts without loss would make a proportion well formed and break Fig 12 :125; a shrinking series under one empirical protocol would break Fig 9 :4-5. Until one of these fires, the verdict stands as `candidate`.
