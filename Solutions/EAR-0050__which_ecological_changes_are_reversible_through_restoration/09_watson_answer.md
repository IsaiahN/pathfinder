## QUESTION

Decide, in effect terms, which ecological changes a restoration seat can reverse and which require different expectations or adaptation. Option A: treat "restoration potential" as one property of a site and rank sites on it. Option B: decide per slot, by reachability of the target from the current state using available atoms and imports, and keep four verdicts separate. What each leads to: A aggregates live signals into an average and can either waste a budget on a closed reach or abandon a reachable change; B yields a checkable shape, per-slot monitoring requirements and five falsifiers written in advance, and stops honestly at a `gap` for any specific site.

## VERDICT (from the figures)

The shape survives; the site-specific answer is not reachable here. Reversibility is a per-slot reachability property of a seat acting on a habitat: the missing atom must be held in a donor frame, the recipient conditions must still hold, and the cascade must be enumerated before acting. Unreachable splits into at-budget (more search and resources) and in-principle (new target, measured gap), and ongoing change makes a union compound that requires an adjusted reference. Every recovery remains maintained work, and the anchor is per-slot measurement, not agreement. This is `specified`, ungrounded and `candidate`; no named site is declared reversible or doomed.

- **cost if wrong:** two opposite errors are priced. Spending a restoration budget on a change whose atom is gone or whose habitat was substituted wastes the budget and, per Fig 6 :24, a wrong atom costs more than starting from primitives. Abandoning a change that was merely unreachable at this budget forfeits a reachable recovery, and Fig 8 :39 forbids treating a failed search as proof of absence. The cost is bounded and locatable because each verdict is per slot and carries a refuter, but it is paid at the site, not in this folder. Fig 2 :42: "Buy independence in proportion to the cost of being wrong."
- **undoable:** the decision is undoable, the ecosystem change often is not. A classification can be superseded by a donor census, a hysteresis test or a budget change; no primitive in this analysis has to be withdrawn. At the site, a lost atom or a substituted habitat is not restored by changing the label. Fig 11 :63 makes maintenance the ongoing cost of the parts that were reversible.
- **reversed by:** a ground reading, not agreement. Per-slot monitoring against an adjusted reference (survival, recruitment, soil function, hydrology, composition, external exchanges); a donor-frame census with source provenance; a hysteresis test at a threshold site; a measured round trip gap R_T; a maintenance test across one full turnover of the relevant organisms. Any of the five pre-registered refuters in `01_figure_grounded_audit.md` section 4 firing retracts the affected claim. Prestige and practitioner agreement are not such instruments.
