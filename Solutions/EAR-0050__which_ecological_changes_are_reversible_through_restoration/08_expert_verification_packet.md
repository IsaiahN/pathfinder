# Expert verification packet - the Tether on restoration reversibility

**What this is:** a claim in a form a restoration scientist can check. Mode, derivation and refuters are stated before the conclusion, so a reader knows what to look for and what would settle it.

**Mode: `specified` - not `grounded`.** No site measurement is claimed. This is a shape and a route, not a result. Simulated derivation checked for internal consistency against the figures, not a ground reading; it cannot settle the question it reports on.

## 1. The claim, in one paragraph

Reversibility through restoration is a per-slot reachability property, not a property of an ecosystem in bulk. A change is reversible when the missing atom (a population, propagule, process or condition) is held in a donor frame whose closure differs, the recipient habitat still holds the conditions that make the addition work, and the cascade of contacts has been enumerated outward before acting. Two other cases must be kept separate: unreachable at this budget, where the atom exists and search or resources are short; and unreachable in principle, where no donor exists or a cross-scale transform has discarded what the return would need. Ongoing global change makes a union compound with degradation, so the target must be adjusted rather than frozen. In all cases recovery is maintained work, and the verdict is settled by per-slot measurement against a reference model, never by agreement among practitioners. The framework returns this shape, not a species list.

## 2. Three checkable sub-claims

### C1 - Framework-internal plus practice-facing; checkable by reading Fig 11 and comparing with restoration practice

- **Statement:** a seat can add actors and conditions and cannot subtract the habitat; recovery therefore depends on the recipient conditions and on an enumerated cascade, and the recovered state stays managed.
- **Sources, verbatim:** Fig 11 :61-62 - "The seat's power is asymmetric. Introduction is available; exclusion is not. You can add an actor and you cannot subtract the habitat, because contact is a fact about the world and does not consult you." Fig 11 :43-44 - "The habitat is enumerated, never composed." and "List everything in contact with the residual, then what is in contact with those, and outward until the cascade stops mattering." Fig 11 :63 - "Which is why a managed ecosystem is permanent work rather than a solved state."
- **What to check:** is the introduction/not-exclusion asymmetry a fair description of the actual move set at a site you know? Can you name a case where a single-lever introduction failed because a second- or third-order contact was not enumerated? Does the site require maintenance after the nominal target is reached?
- **Refuter:** a change we classify as reversible returns to its adjusted target and then persists without any maintenance, monitoring or renewed introduction for a full turnover of the relevant organisms; or a site is reversed by subtracting the habitat itself rather than by adding to a still-present habitat. Either breaks this sub-claim.

### C2 - Reachability; checkable by a donor census and a hysteresis test

- **Statement:** missing atoms must be imported from a frame whose closure differs; one failed search is not proof of absence; crossing a threshold can make return require more than restoring the old driver.
- **Sources, verbatim:** Fig 6 :21 - "MINT composes inside the closure and can never add an atom." Fig 6 :23 - "No frame escapes its own closure. The bound moves only on contact with a frame whose closure differs." Fig 8 :39 - "So a search that finds nothing is never a proof of absence." Fig 4 :28-29 - "Going up throws away detail; that is what makes the result reapplicable. But it means the trip is not reversible, and something carried up and brought back down will not match what was sent."
- **What to check:** for a site you know that failed to return to reference, was there a donor frame holding the missing atom that nobody searched? Did the system show hysteresis, that is, did returning the driver to its former value fail to return the state? If yes to the first, our unreachable-in-principle label was premature; if no to the second, the lossy-transform argument does not govern that case.
- **Refuter:** a change labelled unreachable in principle is reversed by an introduction from a donor frame we did not know about, with the same habitat and adjusted target; or a state that crossed a threshold returns to its historical reference simply by restoring the old driver value, with no import and no hysteresis. Either breaks this sub-claim.

### C3 - Verification; checkable from a monitoring design

- **Statement:** verdicts must be per slot and anchored in measurement; agreement among practitioners is disqualified for a verdict; a recovered-looking projection does not comment on its source.
- **Sources, verbatim:** Fig 1 :19 - "The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated." Fig 1 :22 - "Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average." Fig 2 :40 - "disqualifying for a verdict: agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool". Fig 4 :30 - "So take the round trip and measure the gap. Without it you are navigating by dead reckoning: confident about a position nobody checked."
- **What to check:** can you design a monitoring set where canopy cover recovers while recruitment or soil function remains short? If so, an aggregate recovery index would hide the live signal, as the figure says. Can two practitioners agree that a site "looks recovered" while a per-slot measurement contradicts them?
- **Refuter:** a site is reported as restored on a single aggregate index while a measured slot remains short of its adjusted reference and no further action is planned for it; or per-slot residuals can be shown to aggregate without loss for the decision at hand. Either breaks this sub-claim.

All three sub-claims are `specified` and ungrounded. Verification here means checking the shape and its discriminators, not confirming a site result.

## 3. What is explicitly not claimed

- Not a species list, a recovery timetable, or a verdict on any named site or ecosystem type.
- Not that a failed restoration proves irreversibility.
- Not that a recovered-looking site is the historical system; indiscernibility is not identity.
- Not that "novel ecosystem" is a framework term. It is a world-frame classification; the figure term is substituted habitat (Fig 11 :66-67).
- Not that practitioner agreement, publication count or reviewer prestige settles anything.
- Not `grounded`. No measurement was taken.

## 5. What would settle it

A ground reading, not agreement. In order of cost:

1. Per-slot monitoring against an adjusted reference model, with survival, recruitment, soil function, hydrology, composition and external exchanges recorded separately.
2. A donor-frame census with source provenance, run before any "unreachable in principle" label is applied.
3. A hysteresis test at any site suspected of a threshold crossing.
4. A measured round trip gap R_T describing what the coarse degraded-state description dropped about the target.
5. A maintenance test across one full turnover of the relevant organisms.

## 6. What an expert can check in twenty minutes

1. Read Fig 11 :43-44, :61-63 and Fig 1 :19, :22 (four short passages).
2. Check C1 against one restoration project you know: what was added, what cascade was missed, and is maintenance still happening?
3. Check C2 by naming a donor frame for a local extinction, or saying why none exists, and by saying whether hysteresis was ever tested at your site.
4. Check C3 by naming one slot an aggregate index would hide.
5. Send the refutation, not the endorsement. A failure is more useful than agreement, and agreement from a shared evidence pool is disqualified by Fig 2 :40.
