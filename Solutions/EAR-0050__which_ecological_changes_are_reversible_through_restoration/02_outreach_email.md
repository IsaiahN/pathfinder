# Outreach draft - check one classification before you decide whether the framing is worth reading

**Use:** one-to-one, or as a cover note attached to a submission through the public venues in `03_public_contact_points.csv`. **Not** for bulk send.

**Hard precondition:** do not send until the pre-registered refuter in `01_figure_grounded_audit.md` section 4 has been copied verbatim into the message and the mode line is intact. The output must survive validation or the two-stage offer collapses on contact.

---

**Subject:** One classification to check: reversible by restoration versus adaptation

> Hi [name],
>
> I have one specific output I would like you to check against what you already know from restoration practice. I am not asking you to read the framework first, and I am not asking you to agree with it. If the output does not survive, there is nothing else to look at.
>
> **The output (`specified`, not `grounded`):** reversibility should be decided per slot, not per ecosystem. A change is a candidate for reversal through restoration when closing the gap needs only adding an actor or condition whose source still exists in another frame, when the cascade of contacts around the change has been enumerated outward, and when the habitat still holds the conditions that make the addition work. Two cases are *not* the same and should not be merged: the atom is gone or a threshold was crossed, which calls for different expectations or adaptation, and the atom exists elsewhere and only budget, knowledge or logistics are missing, which calls for more search and resources. A recovered state is maintained work, not a finished object. This is a shape and a route, not a field result.
>
> **What I am not claiming:** I am not claiming that any particular species or site is recoverable. The framework returns a shape, not a name. I am not claiming that a site which fails one attempt is irreversible; a search that finds nothing is not proof of absence. I am not claiming that a restored site is done. Managed ecosystems stay managed.
>
> **What would refute the shape claim (written before this run):**
> 1. A site reported as restored on a single aggregate index while a measured slot such as soil function, recruitment or external exchange remains short of its reference and no further action is planned for it.
> 2. A change labelled unreachable in principle reversed by an introduction from a donor frame we did not know about, with the same habitat and adjusted target.
> 3. A state that crossed a threshold returning to its historical reference simply by restoring the old driver value, with no import and no hysteresis.
> 4. A change labelled reversible returning to its target and persisting without any maintenance, monitoring or renewed introduction for a full turnover of the relevant organisms.
> 5. A project under ongoing climate change reaching and holding its historical reference without any adjustment of the target.
>
> If any holds, I retract the affected claim. A one-line "this fails because..." is more useful to me than agreement. If you would rather not engage, no reply is needed.
>
> **If it survives, and only then:** the sources are below. The figures are the authority, not my summary of them.
> - Verbatim figure text with line numbers (local extract): `FRAMEWORK/reference/03-FIGURES_TEXT.md`. Fig 11 :61-63, Fig 6 :21-25, Fig 8 :39-43, Fig 4 :28-32 are the relevant passages.
> - Canonical usage rule (local): `FRAMEWORK/reference/FIGURES_README_canonical.md`.
> - Public SER restoration standards and definitions: https://www.ser.org/page/SERstandards and https://ser-rrc.org/what-is-ecological-restoration
>
> **If you want to use the machinery:** the formula is in the figures material. Run it on a restoration problem you know well and can check against a ground. Write down what would refute your result *before* you run it: the framework's own rule is that a loop cannot check the direction of its own question, and the essays warn that outputs can be contrived to fit the formula. A reported failure is more useful to me than a reported success.
>
> - [your name]

## Two-stage discipline

1. **Stage 1:** send the residual, the shape claim and the five refuters. Ask what the recipient would count as a failure. Record their verdict *before* showing any framework material.
2. **Stage 2, opt-in only:** if they are curious, send the local figure passages and the SER standards link. Their later agreement is never pooled as corroboration, because Fig 2 :40 says "disqualifying for a verdict: agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool."
3. **Stage 3, optional and only after stage 1 is recorded:** offer the formula as a method to reuse, with the instruction that each user writes their own refuter and states their own mode.

The first contact is one-to-one. No bulk send, no personal addresses harvested from directories, no ranking by likely agreement.
