# Are the change classes isomers? Bonds, order and analogues in restoration

**Mode: `specified`.** Simulated derivation checked against the figures, not a ground reading (see `05_what_shape_means.md` section 7).

## RESIDUAL

The four outcomes in `07_the_tether_answer.md` (reversible, unreachable at budget, unreachable in principle, adaptation under a moving target) look like a flat list of ecosystem conditions. Two questions are owed:

1. Are two changed sites with the same species list the same kind of change, or are they different substances whose ingredient lists cannot tell them apart?
2. What bond does a restoration action actually use: a sequence (→), a production (⇒), or something else? A recipe that lists only ingredients cannot name a route.

## DERIVATION

### Q1 - Same species list, different substance

Fig 12 :14-16 describes the failure of a flat formula: "the ingredients are recorded and the arrangement is not / nothing downstream can tell them apart, / and nothing signals that anything was lost". Fig 12 :117:

> **"isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."**

Two sites can share a native tree list. At site A the trees were planted into soil whose mycorrhizal network and seed predators are intact; recruitment is occurring and the missing bond is a production step that has already fired. At site B the same trees were planted into enriched, compacted soil with no dispersers; no recruitment occurs. The species list is the same. The recovery prospects are not. By Fig 12 :117 they are **isomers**, not severities of one condition. Reporting both as "planted native woodland, recovering" is the flat formula error.

This is a structural claim, not a field result. It is checkable by measuring recruitment, soil biota and dispersers per slot.

### Q2 - The bond of a restoration action: production, not mere sequence

Fig 12 :70-72 and the Operators table define → (sequence) as "A then B; B needs A to have happened", and ⇒ (production) as "A produces B; B did not exist before", with the test "is there a B before A fires? No."

A reintroduction is a **production** bond: before the action there is no breeding population of that species on the site, afterwards there is. Earthworks that precede hydrological reconnection are a **sequence** bond: the flow needs the channel to have been cut. Conflating them is not pedantry. If you write a reintroduction as a sequence, you assume the missing thing was already present and merely waiting, which is exactly the assumption that fails for extinct or locally extirpated atoms.

Fig 12 :111-112:

> **"A vocabulary without bonds can name a state and cannot name a route to one."**
> **"A plan is a sequence. A goal is a comparison. Progress is a subtraction. A refutation is a negation, and that one is not a bond."**

So a restoration plan needs bonds. The goal is a comparison (current slot against adjusted reference). Progress is a subtraction (the shrinking residual per slot). A refusal, when a proposed intervention is rejected because the donor cascade was not enumerated, is a negation and is not a bond. A vocabulary with only ingredient lists can write "plant trees, add wolves, reconnect river" and cannot say which comes first or which produces what.

The subtraction bond needs a caution. Fig 11 :61-62 denies subtraction of the habitat to a seat. Fig 12 :112 says progress is a subtraction. These govern different quantities (bookkeeping inside a frame versus physical removal from a field of contact), as recorded in `01_figure_grounded_audit.md` section 5.

### Q3 - Three structures that decide the outcome

- **Catalyst.** Fig 12 :119: "catalyst. A part that survives its own use. Spent and regenerating parts behave differently across uses, and nothing records which a term is." Mycorrhizal inoculum, a beaver population, a repeated prescribed-fire regime: each survives its own use and changes the recovery trajectory. A one-off application and a self-regenerating one are different terms even with the same starting ingredients. Recording which is a measurement obligation.
- **Cycle.** Fig 12 :121: "cycle. The output re-enters as input. The sign of the loop decides whether it closes, and a chain cannot close by construction." Recovery is often a negative feedback loop (more vegetation stabilises soil, which supports more vegetation). Degradation can be a positive feedback loop that locks an alternative state (turbidity suppresses plants, which keeps water turbid). The regime-shift literature read for `01` gives the world-side form: Kéfi et al. 2016 write that "a positive feedback is a necessary (but not sufficient) condition for systems to have alternative stable states." A chain model of restoration cannot close either loop, which is why a one-off intervention can fail to hold.
- **Functional group.** Fig 12 :123: "functional group. A substructure whose presence fixes the outcome. The group says what kind; the qualifier says which member." A reference ecosystem is a functional group: it fixes the kind of target (structure, composition, function, external exchanges). The site-specific adjusted model is the qualifier that says which member. SER's 2019 standards make the same distinction in world terms by separating the reference model from the project targets and goals.

### Q4 - Are the four verdict classes isomers?

No. They are a **union**, not a list of severities of one thing. Fig 9 :55-57:

> **"A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones. A single lever clears the region where only one condition holds and leaves the intersections standing, because the second condition still binds."**

The classes differ both in atoms and in bonds:

| class | atom status | governing bond / transform |
|---|---|---|
| reversible | missing atom available in a donor frame; habitat holds conditions | production ⇒ plus an enumerated cascade |
| unreachable at this budget | atom exists in a donor frame; search or resource shortfall | more SEARCHED at the same reach (Fig 6 :49) |
| unreachable in principle | atom absent from all available frames, or conditions substituted | closure failure; import needed but none exists (Fig 6 :21, :23) |
| adaptation under moving target | union of degradation and ongoing external change | substituted habitat plus a second binding condition (Fig 9 :55-57) |

Treating them as points on one "recoverability" axis is the blend Fig 8 :4-6 names: "collapse 4: the blend → vocabulary, never an atom." More vocabulary about recoverability does not add any atom to a donor frame.

### Q5 - What analogues are and are not licensed

The architecture essay (*Arrival of the Fittest*, 2026, fetched this session) explicitly declines the habitat-as-niche mapping: "A niche is defined by an organism's relation to its environment. A habitat here is defined by contact with a residual, and the residual moves so the same actors constitute a different habitat when the question changes." The resemblance is real and the definitions do not align. So ecological niche, alternative stable state and hysteresis are used here as **world corroboration**, not as translations of Fig 11 or Fig 4. When they conflict, the figures decide.

## CONCLUSION

The two changed sites with the same species list are Fig 12 isomers: same atoms, different bonds, different behaviour (Q1). A restoration action is primarily a production bond ⇒, with sequence → for preparatory steps, comparison ⋛ for the target, subtraction − for per-slot progress and negation ¬ for refusals (Q2). Catalyst, cycle and functional group are the structures that decide whether a recovered state holds (Q3). The four verdict classes are a union of overlapping conditions, not severities on one scale (Q4). Ecological analogues corroborate but do not translate the figure terms (Q5). All of this is `specified` and ungrounded; the falsifier at the top has not been run.
