# The Tether's answer: reversibility through restoration

**Mode: `specified` - not `grounded`.** This is a shape and a route, not a field result. Simulated derivation checked against the figures (`05_what_shape_means.md` section 7), not a ground reading. No site was assessed.

## 1. The residual it answers

Given a changed ecosystem C and a target T (a reference model adjusted for changed and predicted conditions), decide per slot which changes restoration can reverse and which need different expectations or adaptation. The options lead to different actions: treating a change as reversible commits a budget to introductions and maintenance; treating it as unreachable in principle commits to a new target and to adaptation; confusing the two either wastes scarce restoration effort on a closed reach or abandons a change that was reachable all along. Fig 1 :19: "The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated."

## 2. The shape: four separate verdicts, never averaged

**Verdict 1 - Reversible through restoration.** A change is a candidate for reversal when all of the following hold: the missing thing is an atom held by a frame whose closure differs (a donor population, seed bank or process), so an import can cross the wall; the recipient habitat still holds the conditions that make the addition work (Fig 4 :34-35, "A seed carries no small tree: it carries what will grow one, and the conditions it lands in decide what it becomes"); the cascade of contacts has been enumerated outward (Fig 11 :43-44) so the intervention is not a cane-toad move; and the cross-scale record has not been so thinned that the target cannot be specified. The work is addition, not subtraction: Fig 11 :61-62, "The seat's power is asymmetric. Introduction is available; exclusion is not." The recovered state is maintained: Fig 11 :63, "Which is why a managed ecosystem is permanent work rather than a solved state."

**Verdict 2 - Unreachable at this budget, not unreachable in principle.** The atom exists somewhere; donor, knowledge, taxonomy or funding are short. Fig 8 :40-43: "Underivable: go outside. Unreachable-at-budget: the term is already in the closure and was not found, so the remedy is more search, not another atom." The instruction is SEARCHED first, distributed and independent, then more resources. Crucially, Fig 8 :39: "So a search that finds nothing is never a proof of absence." One failed project does not license verdict 3.

**Verdict 3 - Unreachable in principle: different expectations.** No available frame holds the atom (extinction, loss of a soil biome beyond recovery), or a threshold was crossed and the return path requires going further back than the forward collapse. Fig 4 :28-29: "Going up throws away detail; that is what makes the result reapplicable. But it means the trip is not reversible, and something carried up and brought back down will not match what was sent." World evidence for the physical form of this: Scheffer et al. 2001 describe hysteresis, where restoring the old driver value is insufficient to return to the old state. Fig 13 makes this an answer rather than an omission: "Unreachable is a verdict here, not a silence,". The honest product is a new target and a measured gap R_T, not a claim of failure.

**Verdict 4 - Adaptation under a moving target.** Restoration and ongoing global change form a union compound. Fig 9 :55-57: "a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either". Clearing degradation alone leaves the intersection standing, because the second condition still binds. The reference model must be adjusted for changed and predicted conditions (Gann et al. 2019, SER Standards). The verb "restore" still applies to the parts that are reachable; the expectation attached to them changes. The world literature calls some of these novel ecosystems; the framework's own term is a substituted habitat (Fig 11 :66-67), and the mapping between them is corroboration, not translation.

Every verdict carries a per-slot residual vector. No averaging across slots. Fig 1 :22: "Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average."

## 3. What it explicitly does not say

- Not that any named species, site or ecosystem type is recoverable or doomed. The framework returns a shape, not a name (Fig 9 :32: "you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself").
- Not that a failed restoration proves irreversibility (Fig 8 :39).
- Not that a recovered-looking site is the historical system. Indiscernibility is not identity.
- Not that "novel ecosystem" is a figure term. It is a world-frame classification.
- Not that maintenance is optional. Fig 11 :63.
- Not that agreement among restoration practitioners settles any of this. Fig 2 :35: "Alignment is triangulation, not negotiation. A mirror cannot correct you."
- Not `grounded`. No measurement was taken; all verdicts are `candidate`.

## 4. What would settle it - the ground

1. **Per-slot monitoring against the adjusted reference:** survival, recruitment, soil function, hydrology, species composition, external exchanges. Fig 2 :29-30: "A measurement, an experiment, a counter, a market, a patient outcome." The measured slot is the anchor.
2. **A donor-frame census:** which frame actually holds the missing atom, with a recorded source gate. If none is found, verdict 3 is still only `candidate`, because Fig 8 :39 forbids reading absence of search as absence of donor.
3. **A measured round trip gap R_T:** what the coarser description of the degraded state cannot hold about the reference (Fig 4 :30, "So take the round trip and measure the gap. Without it you are navigating by dead reckoning: confident about a position nobody checked.").
4. **A hysteresis test:** does returning the driver to its old value return the system, or must it go further back? This is the discriminator between verdict 1 and verdict 3 at a threshold site. The regime-shift literature supplies the instrument; the figures supply the shape.
5. **A maintenance test:** does the recovered slot persist across a full turnover of the relevant organisms without renewed intervention? This is the discriminator for verdict 1's obligation.

The five pre-registered refuters in `01_figure_grounded_audit.md` section 4 were written before this conclusion. Any one firing retracts the affected claim with provenance.

## 5. The honest stop

**Instance-level answers are a `gap` here.** Which specific change at a specific site is reversible needs field measurements, donor frames and a budget, none of which is in this folder. That is a verdict, not a silence: Fig 13: "Unreachable is a verdict here, not a silence,". The stop is at the limit of what could be distinguished with the available instruments, and the lineage is recorded so the stop is checkable. What was unreachable is named in effect terms; what was imported is marked with provenance; what would reverse it is listed above. Fig 8 :54: "underivable is not unknowable."

## 6. One-paragraph version for outreach

> Reversibility is not a property of an ecosystem in bulk. It is a per-slot reachability question. A change is reversible through restoration when the missing thing still exists in a donor frame, the habitat still holds the conditions that make the addition work, and the cascade of contacts around the change has been enumerated before acting. Two other cases are not the same: the atom may exist and only knowledge, search or funding may be short, in which case more search and resources are the remedy and one failed project proves nothing; or the atom may be gone and the cross-scale record thinned, in which case the old state is unreachable and a new target with a measured gap is the honest product. Where ongoing climate or land-use change is still moving the target, restoration forms a union compound with it, and the expectation must change with the reference model. In every case the recovered state is maintained work, not a finished object, and the verdict is settled by per-slot measurement against an anchor, never by agreement among practitioners. This is a shape and a route, offered with five falsifiers written in advance; the site-specific answer is a gap until the ground is read.
