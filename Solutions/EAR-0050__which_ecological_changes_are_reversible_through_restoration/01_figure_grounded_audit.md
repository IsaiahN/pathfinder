## 0. The residual under audit

> Which ecological changes are reversible through restoration, and which require different expectations or adaptation?

Restate in effect terms before choosing any figure. Let C be the current state of an ecosystem and T the target state. Let s index the *slots* of that state: species composition, community structure, soil and water conditions, ecosystem function, external exchanges, and the disturbance regime. Let R_s = the difference between C_s and T_s that a restoration action is meant to close. Restoration is not a generic "improvement": it is a **seat** acting on a **habitat**, and its power has a specific shape:

- it can **introduce** an actor, a condition or a process (propagules, reintroduced animals, hydrological reconnection, prescribed fire);
- it cannot **subtract the habitat**, nor the contact relations that define the system;
- it supplies conditions under which the organisms do the recovering themselves.

So the decision is not "is this ecosystem degraded?" It is, per slot: **is T_s reachable from C_s with the atoms the frame holds or can import, with an enumerable cascade, before the transform that produced C discarded what the return would need?** If yes: reversible through restoration, with the honest addition that the recovered state is maintained work, not a finished object. If no, split the verdict:

| unreachable kind | meaning | instruction |
|---|---|---|
| in principle | the atom is gone from every available frame (extinction), or a threshold was crossed and a new habitat now holds (Fig 11 :66-67) | different expectations, adaptation, or a new target |
| at this budget | the atom exists in a donor frame; knowledge, logistics or funding are missing | more search, a source gate, more resources (Fig 8 :40-43) |

The two must never be averaged into one "recovery potential" number, because Fig 1 :19 says "The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated." and Fig 1 :22 says "Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average."

## 1. Domain census (before any figure line is chosen)

R by arity, symmetry and scale:

- **Arity:** many slots, not one. A restored ecosystem is a vector of per-slot residuals, never a single score.
- **Symmetry:** asymmetric. Degradation and recovery are not inverse operations. Fig 12 :112: "A plan is a sequence. A goal is a comparison. Progress is a subtraction. A refutation is a negation, and that one is not a bond." Reversing the sign of a change does not reconstruct the route (Fig 4 :28-29).
- **Scale:** local site up to landscape and biome; cross-scale transforms are lossy (Fig 4), so a result at one scale does not travel to another without a measured gap.

One line per domain, giving actors, currency and anchor. No nameable anchor means it is not a domain yet.

| domain | actors | currency | anchor (nameable?) | partition or union |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **D1. Practice / seat frame** | restoration practitioners (the seat), habitat actors and conditions, reference model | introductions, conditions, actions, monitoring hours | measured recovery against the reference model on a site (Fig 2 :29-30) | partition boundary: what a seat can add |
| **D2. Ecosystem dynamics / ground frame** | species, propagules, soil biota, hydrology, disturbance, climate | matter and energy, demographic rates, viability | the system itself, the ground that does not update (Fig 11 :73; Fig 10 :24) | union with D4 when the external frame moves |
| **D3. Verification / expectation frame** | monitoring indicators, reference sites, success criteria, stakeholders | residuals per slot; agreement is *not* currency | measurement, never agreement (Fig 2 :35, :40) | partition: ground versus agreement |
| **D4. Ongoing global change / novel conditions** | changed climate, land use, novel species combinations, altered disturbance | a moving reference condition | the ground, which keeps returning different values | union compound with D2 (Fig 9 :55-57) |

D4 is not a fifth "type of degradation." It is the second condition in a compound, and Fig 9 :57 says "a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either." It stops being a mixture and becomes a specific compound, with its own loop.

**Dependency order (Fig 3 :20, "The chain is ordered by dependency, not by importance"):**

1. Perceive the slots (Fig 1 :11), which change actually registered.
2. Enumerate the habitat (Fig 11 :43-44), everything in contact with the residual, outward until the cascade stops mattering.
3. State the objective per slot (Fig 3 :9-11): what would count as progress here, in this frame's own terms.
4. Plan a route (Fig 12 :111, "A vocabulary without bonds can name a state and cannot name a route to one").
5. Act, then settle against the ground (Fig 2 :45, "Frames range against the ground. They do not negotiate with it.").
6. Carry methods upward, never recordings (Fig 4 :22-23).

Effort spent above a broken link cannot rescue it: Fig 3 :19, "Optimising a link that already works cannot rescue one that does not." Planting more seedlings cannot rescue a missing hydrological link if water no longer arrives.

## 3. Findings F1 to F9

### F1 - The governing figure is the habitat, and its power is asymmetric (decisive for D1)

Fig 11 :61-62:

> **"The seat's power is asymmetric. Introduction is available; exclusion is not. You can add an actor and you cannot subtract the habitat, because contact is a fact about the world and does not consult you."**

Fig 11 :63:

> **"Which is why a managed ecosystem is permanent work rather than a solved state."**

A restoration project is a seat entering the maintained mode (Fig 11 :56, :58-59). Its effective moves are additions: propagules, reintroduced consumers, reconnected flows, reinstated disturbance. This gives a positive test for reversibility, stated in effect terms: the change is a candidate for reversal if closing R_s requires only adding an actor or condition whose source exists in a frame whose closure differs, and if the recipient habitat still holds the conditions that make the addition work. Fig 4 :34-35 states the same limitation in the language of the seed: "A seed carries no small tree: it carries what will grow one, and the conditions it lands in decide what it becomes."

The negative half is equally explicit. Fig 11 :66-67:

> **"Isolation is not removal of the habitat. It is substitution of one habitat for another."**
> **"Move the residual and you must reproduce everything its goal required, and you inherit the new habitat's actors, which you did not choose."**

So "we removed the stressor and waited" is not a restoration operation in general: if the stressor is the climate or the surrounding land use, there is no subtraction available. Replacing a site with a controlled analogue (a nursery, a fenced plot, a test harness) substitutes a habitat and inherits actors you did not choose. Fig 11 :70 names the same failure for experiments: "A test harness is a substituted habitat. Which is why a synthetic solve proves wiring and never capability."

**Important correction to my own first reading (see section 6):** I initially wrote that exclusion is unavailable, full stop, which would forbid weed control or predator removal. Re-census fixes the scope: what cannot be subtracted is the *habitat* and the *contact*, not an individual incumbent actor. Removing an invasive plant is itself an introduction of competitors, shading, or control agents into a still-present habitat. The asymmetry is about the field of contact, not about every manipulation. The finding is retained with that narrowed scope.

**Mode:** `specified`, triangulated across Fig 11 and Fig 4 (different figures, different quantities: seat power versus lossy transform). World corroboration (secondary): SER Restoration Resource Center, fetched 2026-11-22, "Restoration practitioners do not carry out the actual work of ecosystem recovery. Rather, they create the conditions needed for recovery so the plants, animals, and microorganisms can carry out the work of recovery themselves."

### F2 - Reachability, not effort, separates reversible from unreachable (governs D2)

Fig 6 :21 and :23:

> **"MINT composes inside the closure and can never add an atom."**
> **"No frame escapes its own closure. The bound moves only on contact with a frame whose closure differs."**

Fig 6 :25: "An import adds no atom to the world."

Fig 8 :40-43:

> **"Underivable: go outside. Unreachable-at-budget: the term is already in the closure and was not found, so the remedy is more search, not another atom."**

Applied to restoration in effect terms: an extinct species is an atom that no composition of the remaining biota can mint. Reversing that slot requires an import from a frame that still holds the atom (a donor population, a seed bank, a captive lineage). Where no such frame exists, the change is unreachable in principle and adaptation is the honest verdict. Where the donor exists but funding, taxonomy, transport or knowledge are missing, the change is unreachable at this budget: the remedy is search and resources.

Fig 8 :39: "So a search that finds nothing is never a proof of absence." A failed restoration attempt is therefore not evidence of irreversibility. Conversely Fig 8 :44: "Only a sealed room can be searched to the end, and no frame is a sealed room. So searching and finding nothing proves nothing." The verdict "adaptation needed" must be earned by a closure argument (no donor exists / the conditions were substituted), not by one failed project.

Fig 6 :24 prices the import: "Import from a live peer is debited against that frame's independence. A wrong atom costs more than starting from primitives." Fig 11 :65 makes the ecological cost concrete: "Introducing an actor for its cascade is ecosystem engineering, after Jones, Lawton and Shachak, 1994. Cane toads and kudzu are the same move with the cascade unenumerated." A wrong introduction is not a neutral failure; it creates a new residual.

**Mode:** `specified`, triangulated across Fig 6 and Fig 8 (closure arithmetic versus scope of the verdict). World corroboration: Hobbs, Higgs and Harris 2009 and Perring, Standish and Hobbs 2013 (novel ecosystems and thresholds), read 2026-11-22.

### F3 - Lossy cross-scale transforms mark the hard boundary (governs D2, round trip)

Fig 4 :28-29:

> **"Going up throws away detail; that is what makes the result reapplicable. But it means the trip is not reversible, and something carried up and brought back down will not match what was sent."**

Fig 4 :31-32:

> **"The gap is not an error to eliminate. It is the measurement of what the coarser description cannot hold, and it is the only honest report of how much was given up."**

A degraded ecosystem is often a coarser description of a former one: complex age structure becomes an even-aged stand, a seed bank becomes a depleted topsoil, a food web becomes a simplified trophic chain, a soil biome becomes compacted substrate. The transform that produced that coarse state discarded detail. Reconstructing from the coarse state cannot recover the detail by composition; at best it produces a facsimile with a measurable round trip gap R_T. This is why full historical fidelity is not a realistic target once the record has been dropped, and why a measured gap is the honest report rather than a claim of failure or success.

World corroboration (secondary, low overlap with the framework). *The Recursive Self-Improvement Trap*, fetched 2026-11-22: "You cannot recreate humanity. The exact lineage of events is lost. You would need to know every natural event and interaction with every other species, all the way back... At best you will have a facsimile, but it will never be aligned with humanity." The essay is about minds, not forests, which is exactly why it is a low-overlap witness for the same structural claim.

Ecological corroboration: Scheffer et al. 2001 (Nature) give the physical form of the same boundary. In the full text (read via the Gatsby PDF copy) they write that "to induce a switch back to the upper branch, it is not sufficient to restore the environmental conditions of before the collapse (F1). Instead, one needs to go back further, beyond the other switch point (F2), where the system recovers by shifting back to the upper branch. This pattern, in which the forward and backward switches occur at different critical conditions, is known as hysteresis." Returning the driver to its old value is not returning to the old state.

**Mode:** `specified`, triangulated across Fig 4 and the regime-shift literature (low overlap).

### F4 - Verdicts are per slot, and "self-organization" is not a global pass (governs D3)

Fig 1 :19 and :22:

> **"The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated."**
> **"Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average."**

A site can recover canopy cover while losing soil function, or regain a plant list while lacking recruitment, or recover a predator while the prey it needed is absent. The practical anchor is a per-slot vector of measured residuals. The SER Restoration Resource Center agrees in world terms: "When full recovery is the goal, an important benchmark is when the ecosystem demonstrates self-organization. At this stage, if unexpected barriers or lack of particular species or processes take recovery off course, further restoration actions may be required to ensure that the trajectory ultimately continues toward full recovery." Self-organization is a benchmark on a trajectory, not a final certificate.

Fig 2 :29-30:

> **"An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks. A measurement, an experiment, a counter, a market, a patient outcome. If it moves when you push on it, it is not an anchor."**

Monitoring data and reference sites are the anchor. Agreement among practitioners about "the site looks better" is a frame-internal reading, and Fig 2 :40 disqualifies it for a verdict: "disqualifying for a verdict: agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool."

**Mode:** `specified`, triangulated across Fig 1 and Fig 2.

### F5 - Cascades must be enumerated, not assumed (governs all domains)

Fig 11 :43-44:

> **"The habitat is enumerated, never composed."**
> **"List everything in contact with the residual, then what is in contact with those, and outward until the cascade stops mattering."**

Fig 11 :52-53:

> **"And contact cascades. Scoping the habitat to direct contact is how DDT reached the eagles: first-order enumeration, second-order effect, and the residual that mattered was three steps out."**

Reversibility is a property of the enumerated cascade, not of the target organism. A single-lever intervention clears the region where only one condition holds and leaves the intersections standing (Fig 9 :55-56). Fig 11 :49-51 adds that actor valence is relative to the residual: "An actor's valence is relative to the residual, not a property it carries." The same species is a restorer in one slot and a malfactor in another, so no standing list of "good" or "bad" actors survives a change of residual.

The Yellowstone wolf reintroduction literature is a low-overlap world witness. Beschta and Ripple 2016 (Biological Conservation), read 2026-11-22: "Some of the riparian plant communities in Yellowstone's northern range are in an early phase of recovery relative to the previous decades of intensive elk herbivory and 'full recovery' of these highly altered riparian ecosystems, which currently represent a range of alternative states, will require time." Ripple and Beschta 2012: "the reintroduction of wolves restored a trophic cascade with woody browse species growing taller and canopy cover increasing in some, but not all places." An introduction (Fig 11 :61) worked through a cascade, and the per-place variation is exactly F4's per-slot verdict.

**Mode:** `specified`, triangulated across Fig 11 and Fig 9.

### F6 - Ongoing global change makes a union compound, not a harder version of the same problem (governs D4)

Fig 9 :55-57:

> **"A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones. A single lever clears the region where only one condition holds and leaves the intersections standing, because the second condition still binds."**
> **"a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either"**

Restoration under a moving climate is restoration (condition one) plus continued change in the target conditions (condition two). Clearing degradation alone leaves the intersection standing. Fig 11 :66-67 says the same from the habitat side: you substituted a habitat and inherited actors you did not choose. The honest consequence is a different target: the reference model is adjusted for changed and predicted conditions rather than frozen at a historical date. Gann et al. 2019 (SER Standards), read 2026-11-22: "appropriate reference models for ecological restoration are not based on immobilizing an ecological community at some past point in time, but rather increasing potential for native species and communities to recover and continue to reassemble, adapt, and evolve."

Hobbs, Higgs and Harris 2009 (Trends in Ecology and Evolution), read 2026-11-22: "When retention or restoration of historical ecosystems is no longer possible, or at least no longer feasible given anything short of heroic action and intensive manipulation and management, what other options are there that could be considered as valid conservation and/or restoration goals?" Perring et al. 2013 (Ecological Processes, open access, fetched this session): "In some instances, potentially irreversible abiotic and/or biotic changes have led to the crossing of thresholds and thus the formation of 'novel ecosystems.' Their widespread emergence challenges a traditional restoration goal of restoring an historical ecosystem."

**Mode:** `specified`, triangulated across Fig 9 and Fig 11 (union logic versus habitat substitution). World sources corroborate but note that "novel ecosystem" is a world-frame term, not a figure line.

### F7 - "Reversible" and "finished" are different predicates (governs the expectation half of the answer)

Fig 11 :63: "Which is why a managed ecosystem is permanent work rather than a solved state." Fig 11 :73: "The habitat is not the ground. The habitat is where actors come from and it updates; the ground is what settles, and it does not." Fig 10 :24: "the ground does not decay, the channel does."

An ecosystem that has been returned to a target trajectory remains in the maintained mode: the seat keeps choosing instruments, distilling impactful actors, and introducing what is missing. Fig 11 :40: "A system cannot be made more capable by improving the agent in isolation. An improvement that does not change contact changes nothing, however much it improves." More funding for nursery stock does not help if the contact (mycorrhizae, hydrology, herbivory regime, seed dispersers) was never restored. The expectation that follows is not "we failed"; it is "maintenance is the product."

**Mode:** `specified`, triangulated across Fig 11 and Fig 10.

### F8 - The framework returns a shape, not a species list (limit on what this solution can claim)

Fig 9 :32: "you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself." The canonical figures README (local copy, read this session) says the framework returns "what a missing thing would have to look like (a shape, not a name)." Applied here, the deliverable is a per-slot reachability test (actors, imports, closure, cascade, lossy gap), not a table saying which named species will recover on which named site. That requires site measurements, reference sites, donor frames and a budget, none of which is in this folder.

A genuine `gap` is therefore recorded at the **instance level**: which specific change at a specific site is reversible is unreachable from the figures alone. Fig 13 makes this a verdict rather than an omission: "Unreachable is a verdict here, not a silence,". The essay *Decoding Tacit Knowledge* (read 2026-11-22) gives the practical form: "When contact isn't possible, the best you can send is a plain description of what you'd expect to see, because anyone can check that against the world."

**Mode:** `general` on the limit, `specified` on the per-slot test. Single-source for the figure reading, triangulated with the canonical README and the essay.

## 4. Pre-registered refuter (written before the conclusion, per the method)

Required by the standing instruction: "A falsifier written before the run is a design instrument." Written 2026-11-22, before sections 4 to 6 were completed.

The shape claim is refuted if any of the following holds:

1. **Per-slot falsifier.** A site is reported as "restored" on the strength of a single aggregate index while at least one slot (soil function, recruitment, or external exchange) remains measurably short of its adjusted reference, and no further action is planned for that slot. This breaks F4 and Fig 1 :19.
2. **Import falsifier.** A change we classify as unreachable in principle (no donor frame holds the atom) is reversed by an introduction from a frame we did not know about, with the same habitat and the same adjusted target. This breaks F2 and Fig 6 :23.
3. **Lossy-transform falsifier.** A state that crossed a threshold and was classified as adaptation-only returns to its historical reference simply by restoring the previous driver value, with no further import and no hysteresis. This breaks F3 and Fig 4 :28-29.
4. **Maintenance falsifier.** A change classified as reversible returns to its adjusted target and then persists without any maintenance, monitoring or renewed introduction for a full turnover of the relevant organisms. This breaks F7 and Fig 11 :63.
5. **Compound falsifier.** A restoration project under ongoing climate change reaches and holds its *historical* reference without any adjustment of the target. This breaks F6 and Fig 9 :55-57.

If any fires, the affected finding is retracted in the log below with provenance, not quietly edited. None has fired in this simulated run, because no site measurement was taken.

## 6. Recommendation

1. Lead with the per-slot residual and the four domain verdicts, not with a list of "recoverable" ecosystem types. Fig 1 :19.
2. For each site, enumerate the habitat outward before choosing an intervention (Fig 11 :43-44), and describe the residual before selecting a reference frame (Fig 9 :27-28).
3. For each slot, ask the closure question: which atom is missing, which frame holds it, and what is the source gate (Fig 6 :23; Fig 8 :9-13).
4. Measure R_T rather than claiming fidelity. "So take the round trip and measure the gap. Without it you are navigating by dead reckoning: confident about a position nobody checked." (Fig 4 :30).
5. Where the driver is still moving, change the expectation and the target rather than spending budget above the break (Fig 9 :55-57; Fig 3 :19).
6. Send the generator, not the conclusion: Fig 4 :22-23, "Only methods cross up. Only head starts come down." The two-stage offer in `02_outreach_email.md` follows this.

### Step 7 questions kept distinct

- **SEARCHED:** for a site classified adaptation-only, was the donor frame actually searched? Fig 8 :39 forbids treating one failed attempt as proof of absence. This run did no site search, so the classification remains `candidate`.
- **INWARD:** sharpen the monitoring instrument (per-slot indicators, early warning indicators such as critical slowing down in the regime-shift literature). Built from a worse instrument already returning something, Fig 6 :34-35.
- **OUTWARD:** find the frame whose closure predicts the missing atom (a donor population, a seed bank, a different reference ecosystem). Fig 9 :27-28: "describe it before you go looking, or any frame you pick will seem to fit."

These were not read against one another. INWARD would not substitute for OUTWARD here, because an extinct atom cannot be sharpened into existence.

### Updated verdict after canonical check

The shape survives: reversibility is a per-slot reachability property of a seat acting on a habitat, bounded by closure, by lossy cross-scale transforms and by an enumerable cascade, and it always carries a maintenance obligation. The two unreachable kinds are kept separate, and the instance-level answer is an honest `gap` pending ground data. Strains after re-census: **none hidden**; one named overlap with a world term (novel ecosystem versus substituted habitat) and one open measurement (R_T at a real site).
