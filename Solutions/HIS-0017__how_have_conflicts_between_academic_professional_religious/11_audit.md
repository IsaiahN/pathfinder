## Round trip gap R_T

The Symbols table defines R_T as `gap(x, (T_E ∘ T_A)(x))`: what the coarser description cannot hold. Fig 4 :30 requires it to be measured rather than assumed. I ran the round trip on the condensation this folder actually uses.

1. **Sent up (x):** the verbatim figure set, chiefly Fig 2 :29, :36, :47 (anchor and legitimacy), Fig 9 :4-5, :57 (split and union), Fig 5 :55 (refusal), Fig 10 :24 (channel decay).
2. **Abstraction (T_A):** the shorthand in `07_the_tether_answer.md`, namely "a conflict closes when a subquestion is ranged against a legitimate, non-updating anchor; the alternatives are unsplit union, truce, refusal, and decayed record."
3. **Concretisation (T_E):** the concrete historical claim a reader might draw, "the 1633 sentence was resolved in 1992," or "the school board lost because science won."

**R_T is nonzero.** Three constraints dropped on the trip down:

- the requirement that the anchor be legitimate for the *specific subquestion*, which the shorthand carries but the concrete version replaces with "who won";
- the distinction between refusal and truce, which collapses when only the verdict is recorded;
- the channel condition, which drops out when a ruling is cited as a headline. A headline carries the verdict and not the reasons.

One thing survives cleanly: a structure must be ranged against an anchor that does not move, and a bundle must be split before it is searched. That is the residue worth sending. R_T was not measured numerically because no ground reading exists for the class of all cases; it is recorded qualitatively as the enumerated set of dropped constraints. Treating the shorthand as identical to the figures would set R_T to zero and commit the dead-reckoning error Fig 4 :30 names. The honest form is a lossy condensation with a recorded gap, which is what `09_watson_answer.md` states.
