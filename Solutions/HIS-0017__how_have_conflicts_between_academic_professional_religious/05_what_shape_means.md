# What "shape" means for this question

Canonical reading from the eight essays, read this session where quoted. Source list at the end. The figures govern: "what a missing thing would have to look like (a shape, not a name)" is the canonical limit in `FIGURES_README_canonical.md`, and Fig 9 :32 says "you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself."

## 1. A shape is a constraint, not a filler

From *The Library of Babel: Locating The Book of Books*: "you can cut your search short if the set of books returned is small and descriptive enough that it provides a general shape of the answer that satisfies your question."

For HIS-0017 the shape is not a list of settlements and not a verdict for or against any authority. It is the form a resolution must have: a subquestion ranged against an anchor legitimate for that subquestion, which does not move when any party pushes on it. `SPACE(???)TIME` was a shape in the exemplar; here the analogous open form is `CONFLICT(?anchor, ?subquestion)`, with the two slots left for a case to fill.

A shape constrains. It does not fill. It lets a historian say what any adequate account of a resolution must look like before naming the particular ruling, council, court, or commission.

## 2. How the shape is obtained

- *Library of Babel*: use the vocabulary already held that is closest to the thing, then recurse. "With each new term gained, you narrow the space and deepen your vocabulary." Applied here: start with the ordinary terms "settled", "compromise", "lost jurisdiction", "reopened", and let the figures narrow them into four distinct mechanisms rather than one continuum.
- Fig 6 :30: "The description travels. Fails by being sharpened into terms only we can read. Cast it in effect terms." Fig 9 :30-31: "your words are borrowed too, so describe the effect rather than the cause." So we do not ask which authority was "right"; we ask what changed in what could be composed and what was ranged against what.
- *Decoding Tacit Knowledge*: "When contact isn't possible, the best you can send is a plain description of what you'd expect to see, because anyone can check that against the world." That is why the outreach packet leads with a checkable effect statement, not with the framework.
- *Library of Babel* on the bundle: "Perhaps your problem was a bundle or conflation of several problems masquerading as one? You will need to search all of those problems individually to find their answers and then combine them in a satisfactory way." This is Fig 9 :54-58 in another register, and it is the central move of this solution.

## 3. Why the particular settlement cannot be read off the outcome

*The Hard Problem is About Source* gives the mechanism. A reading does not determine its source: "If several paths create the same behavior, then behavior is a projection with lossy information". The color instrument "cannot comment on the source"; several underlying causes produce the same green. And: "An instrument that can't distinguish these cases demonstrates its own inadequacy, it cannot claim anything substantial about what is being measured or what causes it."

Applied here, "the conflict was resolved" is the green reading. At least four distinct sources can produce it: triangulation against a legitimate anchor, a split into subquestions, a refusal that removes standing, and a truce with no new ground contact. A chronicle that records only the outcome cannot distinguish them. That is exactly the metamerism error, and it is why F4 and F6 in `01_figure_grounded_audit.md` are separate findings rather than one. What is owed is a better discriminator, here provenance plus a low-overlap witness, not a name for the winner.

## 4. Q2 answered: what licenses a particular authority in the anchor slot?

Nothing in the framework. The framework returns a shape. A particular anchor is licensed only when the subquestion is constitutively about it (Fig 2 :36) or when the ground settles a minted term. Until one of those, the choice is the historian's own contribution and must be recorded as such. This is why the solution refuses to rank academic, professional, religious, and traditional authority globally, and why it records the author's institutional stake in §0 of `01`.

## 5. Correction to my own §3 and §4

I initially overstated the "shape, not a name" rule, following the exemplar's own correction pattern. The canonical phrase governs a **missing** primitive, one not held in the library. Here no primitive is missing. The four authority frames and the anchor function are all already in the figure set (Fig 4 :19-20, Fig 10 :68). So naming a particular court, council, or commission as the anchor of a case is not forbidden; it is a grounding from the historical record, not an invention of a new figure term.

What remains wrong if a chronicle simply names the winner as the cause of settlement is the metamerism point in §3, now stated correctly: the defect is the missing discriminator among several sources of one reading, not a ban on naming held things. Both claims are kept so neither is quietly lost.

## 6. Prestige is not action

From *The Recursive Self-Improvement Trap*: "Prestige must never be allowed to validate an action directly." And: "Prestige can fund an action. It can give an action a budget to be tried. But it cannot settle an action. Only the ground can settle it. The Ground Maintainer must be blind to prestige."

A famous council, a prestigious journal, or a century of acceptance funds an inquiry and cannot settle it. This is the specific danger for HIS-0017 because the question is about authority itself: it is tempting to explain a resolution by the standing of the institution that announced it. That is the prestige loop, and the figures forbid it (Fig 2 :35: "Alignment is triangulation, not negotiation. A mirror cannot correct you.").
