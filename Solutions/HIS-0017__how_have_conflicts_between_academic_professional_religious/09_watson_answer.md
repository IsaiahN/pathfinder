## QUESTION

Decide, in effect terms, what the Tether says about the question: how have conflicts between academic, professional, religious, and traditional authorities been resolved, or left unresolved? Option A: treat "resolved" and "unresolved" as two points on one scale of social agreement, so a well-endorsed settlement counts as resolved. Option B: treat them as different mechanisms, so a conflict is resolved only when a subquestion is ranged against a legitimate, non-updating anchor, and everything else is an unsplit union, a truce, a refusal, or a decayed channel. What each leads to: A makes agreement the verdict and cannot distinguish a court ruling from a compromise; B gives a checkable four-way discriminator, written refuters, and tests that a historian can apply case by case.

## DOMAIN CENSUS

R by arity, symmetry, scale; one line per domain with actors, currency, anchor; partition or union; order by dependency.

- **R (the residual).** Four authority frames making claims over the same world, with three possible outcomes: closed, left open, or declared closed while the disagreement continues. Arity: four frames, so at least two. Symmetry: asymmetric, because actors, currency, and anchor differ; two "authorities" that draw on the same evidence pool are one frame wearing two names. Scale: a single case, a recurring cycle, an institutional level, and a lineage of handed-down precedents, with the same loop at each scale (Fig 4 :19-20).
- **Domain 1, academic authority.** Actors: scholars, universities, journals, reviewers, instrument makers. Currency: citations, credentials, peer review, prediction. Anchor: "preestablished impersonal criteria" (Merton 1942, quoted in Anderson et al. 2010) and experiment or data for empirical subquestions (Fig 2 :29). Legitimate when the subquestion is constitutively about empirical or argumentative ground.
- **Domain 2, professional authority.** Actors: practitioners, licensing boards, clients. Currency: credentials, licences, outcomes. Anchor: the outcome, for example "a patient outcome" (Fig 2 :29). Legitimate for practice efficacy.
- **Domain 3, religious authority.** Actors: clergy, councils, scriptures, denominations. Currency: doctrine, interpretation, spiritual standing. Anchor: revelation as interpreted, legitimate for subquestions constitutively about faith; an instrument, and a fallible one, for empirical subquestions (Fig 2 :37-38).
- **Domain 4, traditional authority.** Actors: elders, local practitioners, communities. Currency: custom, continuity, multi-generational practice. Anchor: community continuity and long contact with a local ground; capability is agent-and-habitat (Fig 11 :39-40).
- **Domain 5, the record.** Actors: historians, archives, documents, later communities. Currency: provenance and testimony. Anchor: the historical record, which is itself a channel that can decay (Fig 10 :24). This domain carries the live strain noted in the Tether and Verdict sections.
- **Union, not partition.** In the hard cases the four frames are overlapping conditions of one compound: empirical, normative, and identity-bearing claims at once (Fig 9 :57). The compound gets its own loop and its own ground. A single-predicate empirical dispute is the exceptional partition case.
- **Order by dependency (Fig 3).** Split the question, decide which anchor is legitimate, range each frame separately, then decide, refuse, or mint, then record with provenance. The middle link, anchor legitimacy, has the least instrumentation (Fig 3 :27).

Strain test applied before choosing lines: Fig 9's split rule governs the question; Fig 2's anchor rule governs the ground. Different quantities, so no strain between them. The one live strain is the record domain, reported below.

## VERDICT (from the figures)

The Tether returns a shape, not a ranking. A conflict closes when a subquestion is ranged against an anchor legitimate for that subquestion and non-updating. Long-running conflicts are typically unsplit unions, and the durable resolution is to split them. Truces are conventions, not verdicts and cannot decide an outcome. Some conflicts genuinely close by refusal, which removes an authority's standing while keeping its old claims in the record. Some close by import from a frame whose closure differs. Some are declared closed but the channel carrying the reasons decayed, so a later generation reopens them. Verdicts are kept separate by domain; the answer is `specified` and ungrounded.

- **cost if wrong:** bounded and locatable. The packet states four mechanisms, four refuters, and provenance, so an error is findable rather than invisible. The real cost is treating a truce as a verdict and thereby importing a prestige-driven "settlement" into a record that later historians rely on. Fig 2 :42 prices the remedy: "Buy independence in proportion to the cost of being wrong."
- **undoable:** yes. The four-way distinction can be applied to a case without touching any institution, and a wrongly labelled settlement can be relabelled with its provenance intact. If a refusal was misread as a synthesis, the record already holds the term, so nothing must be deleted.
- **reversed by:** a ground reading, not more agreement. A case audit showing that the losing claim stayed composable, the reasons survived, and the parties' agreement did not grow would collapse three of the four mechanisms into one; a stable cross-domain authority ranking that beats the question-relative rule would refute the legitimacy claim; a chain that handed down a ruling with its derivation intact and no growth in mere agreement would refute the channel-decay finding; a single-ground conflict that resists honest search would force a re-census of the union claim. Until one fires, the verdict stands as `candidate`.
