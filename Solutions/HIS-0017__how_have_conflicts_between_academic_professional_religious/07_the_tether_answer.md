# The Tether's answer on authority conflicts

**Mode: `specified`, not `grounded`.** This is a shape and a route, not a historical verdict. Simulated derivation checked against the figures (`05_what_shape_means.md` §7), not a ground reading.

## 1. The residual it answers

Four authority types make claims over the same world. Some conflicts close, some persist for centuries, and some are announced as closed while the underlying disagreement continues. What is being decided is the difference between those outcomes. The question is a union compound, not one hard problem (Fig 9 :57: "a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either. It stops being a mixture and becomes a specific compound"). It has its own loop and its own ground.

## 2. The shape

1. **A conflict closes when a subquestion is ranged against a legitimate, non-updating anchor.** Fig 2 :29: "An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks." Fig 2 :36: "An anchor is legitimate when the question is constitutively about it." No authority type is globally supreme. Each is the right anchor for what is constitutively about its domain and a fallible instrument elsewhere (Fig 2 :37-38).
2. **Most long-running conflicts are unions that were never split.** Each authority keeps working well on a different subset, so extra effort does not shrink the disagreement (Fig 9 :4-5: "are not one hard question but several well-formed ones. Split it rather than search"). The empirical part, the normative part, and the identity-bearing part go to different anchors. Unbundling is the resolution, or the precondition for one.
3. **Agreement is not resolution.** Fig 2 :35: "Alignment is triangulation, not negotiation. A mirror cannot correct you." Fig 7 :13: "agreement compounds, evidence does not." A truce in which each authority keeps its claim and merely respects the others is efficient as a convention, but Fig 2 :43 says "a convention that decides an outcome has stopped being one."
4. **Some conflicts close by refusal, not by synthesis.** Fig 5 :55: "What it removes is not a term but a term's standing to be composed over." The old claim stays in the record with provenance, but it can no longer be used as a premise for new rulings in that domain. The reach falls because something was added, not because anything was deleted (Fig 6 :42).
5. **Import across frames can move the wall.** Fig 6 :23: "No frame escapes its own closure. The bound moves only on contact with a frame whose closure differs." The surplus exists only where closures differ (Fig 8 :45). Assimilation that erases the other frame's validation criteria destroys the surplus; the TEK and AEK case in Ludwig and Poliseli 2018 shows both the gain and the loss.
6. **A handed-down settlement is a recording, and the channel decays.** Fig 10 :24: "the ground does not decay, the channel does." Fig 4 :22: "Only methods cross up. Only head starts come down." What crosses a chain of institutions is a head start, not the derivation, so a later generation can inherit the verdict and lose the reason for it.

## 3. What it explicitly does not say

- Not that any one of the four authorities is more trustworthy in general. Valence is relative to the residual (Fig 11 :49-51), and "No standing list of good actors survives a change of residual" (Fig 11 :51).
- Not that a court, council, or commission settled a metaphysical question when it only settled a jurisdictional one. Kitzmiller is the worked example: the court declined to take a position on the truth of the claim (Vile 2023).
- Not that a long-accepted settlement is correct. A ruling with clean provenance is still to be corrected by the ground (Fig 10 :65-66).
- Not a catalogue of cases and not a new historical thesis. No individual disestablishment, trial, or commission was audited case by case in this run.
- Not `grounded`. The mode is `specified` throughout.

## 4. What would settle it, the ground

A case-by-case record with a discriminator that separates the four sources of the reading "resolved." Concretely, for a named conflict:

- **Split test:** can the dispute be written as two subquestions with different legitimate anchors? If yes, a single "who won" answer is malformed.
- **Anchor test:** name the thing appealed to and ask whether it would return the same answer if the winning frame changed its mind. If it would move, it was a party, not an anchor (Fig 2 :47).
- **Refusal test:** after the ruling, can the losing claim still be composed into new rulings in the same domain? If no, a refusal fired; if yes, it was a truce.
- **Channel test:** do the reasons for the ruling survive in the record, or only the verdict? A missing reason is a lost channel, not a settled question (Fig 10 :24-28).
- Standing falsifiers from `01`: a verdict no party could range against that stays closed; a single-ground conflict that resists honest search; a fixed authority ranking that predicts closure better; a chain with intact reasons and no growth in mere agreement.

## 5. The honest stop

**Unreached, not unreachable.** The historical record exists, so this is reachable in principle. It is unreached at this budget because this run audited no named case end to end. There is also a live strain: the ground for a history question is constitutively human judgment (Fig 2 :36), and the record through which the historian must range is itself a channel that can decay (Fig 10 :24). The answer stops at the limit of the available instruments and records the lineage so the stop is checkable. Indiscernibility is not identity.

## 6. One-paragraph version for outreach

> The Tether does not rank academic, professional, religious, and traditional authority and does not say who won a given dispute. It says that a conflict closes only when a subquestion is ranged against an anchor legitimate for that subquestion and does not move when any party pushes on it; that most long-running conflicts are bundles of different questions that were never split; that agreement between the parties is a truce, not evidence; that some conflicts end by a refusal which removes one authority's standing while leaving its old claims in the record; and that a settlement handed down a chain is a head start, not a derivation, so its record decays. What it offers is the shape any adequate account of a resolution must have, four refuters written in advance, and the tests that would close it case by case. The record stays open until the ground settles each case.
