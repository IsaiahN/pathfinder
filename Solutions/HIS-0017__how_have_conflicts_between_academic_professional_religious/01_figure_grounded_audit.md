## 0. The question in effect terms

The question asks how conflicts among four named authority types get resolved or left unresolved. Restated as an effect: a claim is advanced inside frame A and refused inside frame B, both frames are internally coherent, and either (i) something settles which claim stands, (ii) the parties agree to disagree while each keeps its claim, or (iii) the disagreement persists across generations. The three outcomes are not the same thing. We are asked for the shape of the difference between them.

What an answer would look like, bound as an ordinary term before the run: **a resolution is a subquestion ranged against an anchor that is legitimate for that subquestion and does not move when any frame pushes on it; anything else is a truce, a refusal, or a channel failure.** Binding this in advance makes it refusable (Formula step "Direction"; Fig 5 :65: "make the sought-for shape an ORDINARY TERM"). The refuter is written in §4 before the conclusion.

**Stake declaration.** The four named authorities are live institutions and this question is constitutively about people and their institutions. Fig 2 :36: "An anchor is legitimate when the question is constitutively about it." So human judgment is part of the ground here, not an impurity to be removed. But Fig 2 :37 warns that an instrument "can be wrong on axes they do not measure," and Fig 10 :58 says "A seat with no stake at all does not exist." The author's own institutional position is declared: the derivation is written from inside a research institution, with academic authority as the default frame, and that is an interest in the reading to be watched, not a credential.

## 1. Residual, domain census, order

### 1a. R by arity, symmetry, scale (Fig 9 :22, :27-28)

- **Arity:** four named frames (academic, professional, religious, traditional), so at least two and typically four. A two-party "science versus religion" telling is already a compression of a multi-frame residual.
- **Symmetry:** the frames are not interchangeable. They differ in actors, currency, and anchor. Two of them can be "both authority" and still be one frame wearing two names if they draw on the same evidence (Fig 8 :48-49). Symmetry is therefore a question to test, not an assumption.
- **Scale:** step (a single ruling or benchmark), cycle (a recurring dispute in one field), level (institutional jurisdiction), lineage (precedent handed down across generations, Fig 7). The same loop runs at each scale; only actors, currency, and anchor change (Fig 4 :19-20).

### 1b. One line per domain: actors / currency / anchor

A domain needs a nameable anchor. Where none can be named, it is not yet a domain.

| domain | actors | currency | anchor (nameable?) | status |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| D1 Academic authority | scholars, universities, journals, reviewers, instrument makers | citations, credentials, peer review, prediction | impersonal criteria plus experiment or data when the question is empirical (Merton 1942, quoted in Anderson et al. 2010); Fig 2 :29 names "a measurement, an experiment, a counter, a market, a patient outcome" | domain, anchor nameable |
| D2 Professional authority | practitioners, licensing boards, professional societies, clients | credentials, licences, outcomes, expertise | the outcome, for example "a patient outcome" (Fig 2 :29); practical efficacy | domain, anchor nameable |
| D3 Religious authority | clergy, councils, scriptures, denominations, believers | doctrine, spiritual standing, continuity of interpretation | revelation and its interpretation, legitimate for questions constitutively about faith (Fig 2 :36); an instrument, and a fallible one, for empirical questions (Fig 2 :37) | domain, anchor nameable but scope-relative |
| D4 Traditional authority | elders, local practitioners, communities, oral tradition | custom, continuity, multi-generational practice, relational obligation | community continuity and long contact with a local ground; capability is agent-and-habitat (Fig 11 :39-40) | domain, anchor nameable but local |
| D5 The verification/historical record | historians, archives, documents, rulings, subsequent communities | provenance, records, testimony, publication | the historical record and its instruments, itself a channel that can decay (Fig 10 :24) | domain, with a live strain (§3 F9) |

### 1c. Partition or union

The four authorities do not partition a single question. In the hard cases they form a **union of overlapping conditions**: a claim can be simultaneously empirical (does the earth move, does the drug work), normative (what may be taught or done), and identity bearing (what a community may hold sacred). Fig 9 :57: "a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either. It stops being a mixture and becomes a specific compound." Fig 9 :54: "And when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again." So the compound gets its own loop and its own ground; it is not one mega-dispute with four parties.

When the conflict really is only about one empirical claim, it is a partition question and a single legitimate anchor can close it. The census must decide which case it is before any figure line is chosen.

### 1d. Order by dependency (Fig 3)

1. Is there a fact here, and is it one question or several? (Fig 9 :4-5). Upstream.
2. Which subquestion is at issue, and which anchor is legitimate for it? (Fig 2 :36). This is the middle link, the one with no instrument and therefore the one nobody looks at (Fig 3 :25-27).
3. Range each frame separately against that anchor. (Fig 2 :45: "Frames range against the ground. They do not negotiate with it.")
4. Only then decide, refuse, or mint. Refusal precedes minting (Formula step 2).
5. Record the ruling with provenance so a later reader can locate the error (Fig 10 :62).

Optimising step 4 cannot rescue a break at step 2 (Fig 3 :19: "Optimising a link that already works cannot rescue one that does not.").

### 1e. Strain test applied before concluding

The Fig 9 split rule governs the *question* (one or several). The Fig 2 anchor rule governs the *ground* (legitimate or instrumental for that subquestion). Different quantities, so no strain between them. The one live strain is F9 in §3.

## 3. Findings, with verbatim quotes and refuters

Mode is `specified` on every finding. Each one says what would have to be observed, not what was measured.

### F1, A conflict closes only against an anchor that does not move (decisive)

Fig 2 :29: **"An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks."**
Fig 2 :30: **"you push on it, it is not an anchor."**
Fig 2 :33: **"The anchor must not update."**
Fig 2 :47: **"A ground listed as one of the parties has already become a frame."**

A "resolution" in which the religious authority adjudicates whether its own scripture is literally correct is panel 1 of Fig 2, not panel 3: the anchor became a party and drifted with it. A "resolution" in which the academy certifies its own competence over a question equally about communal meaning is the same failure from the other side. The test is not which institution won. It is whether the thing appealed to would return the same answer if the winning frame changed its mind.

Worked cases from the sources read this session:
- Kitzmiller v. Dover (Vile 2023) is a case where an external court, not either contending frame, was the anchor for the *jurisdictional* question (what may be taught in a public science class). The court's own test was empirical: Vile quotes the prior cases' conclusion that creation science "was not empirical and that it rested on assumptions that were 'neither testable nor falsifiable.'" The court did not settle whether Intelligent Design is metaphysically true; Vile records Jones: "while ID arguments may be true, a proposition on which the Court takes no position, ID is not science." That is an anchor scoped to the question, which is exactly Fig 2 :36.
- Galileo is the negative case. The 1633 sentence was a ruling by one of the parties about the interpretation of another party's text. The 1992 address does not pretend otherwise; John Paul II records that the sentence "was not irreformable" and that the debate was closed in 1820 with the imprimatur given to Settele, an institutional act, before the 1992 commission. The resolution took centuries because the anchor that could settle the physical question (observation and prediction) was not allowed to be the ground for the doctrinal question until the frames were separated (F2).

**Verdict:** triangulated across Fig 2 (:29, :33, :47) and Fig 10 (:24, ground does not decay). Refuter: exhibit a conflict that closed by a verdict which both sides could move by changing their own minds, and which nonetheless stayed closed without a channel failure. If found, F1 fails.

### F2, Anchor legitimacy is question-relative, so no authority type is globally supreme

Fig 2 :36: **"An anchor is legitimate when the question is constitutively about it."**
Fig 2 :37: **"Human judgment is the right anchor for is this a good answer for a person, because the question is about people."**
Fig 2 :37-38: an instrument "can be wrong on axes they do not measure."

The four authorities are not ranked. Each is a legitimate anchor for the subquestion constitutively about its own domain and an instrument, possibly wrong, elsewhere. Academic method is the right anchor for whether a measurement replicates; religious interpretation is the right anchor for what a community is obliged to hold sacred; professional outcome is the right anchor for whether a treatment works in practice; traditional continuity is the right anchor for what a local community has sustained over generations. The error is not having an anchor. It is using an anchor for a question that is not constitutively about it.

John Paul II (1992) performs exactly this split in the Galileo case: "the different branches of knowledge call for different methods" and "There exist two realms of knowledge, one which has its source in Revelation and one which reason can discover by its own power." That is a historical actor arriving at the Fig 2 :36 rule from inside one of the frames. It is corroboration from a different field (religion and history of science), not a figure line.

Fig 11 :49 independently supports the same thing: **"An actor's valence is relative to the residual, not a property it carries."** There is no standing list on which academia outranks tradition or faith outranks expertise (Fig 11 :51: "No standing list of good actors survives a change of residual.").

**Verdict:** triangulated across Fig 2 (:36-38) and Fig 11 (:49, :51); world corroboration from John Paul II 1992 and Ludwig and Poliseli 2018. Refuter: show a fixed cross-domain ordering of the four authority types that predicts which conflicts close better than the question-relative rule. If it does, F2 fails.

### F3, Most long-running conflicts are unions that were never split

Fig 9 :4: **"First: is there a fact here? Disagreements that do not shrink with effort, where each rule keeps working well on a different subset,"**
Fig 9 :5: **"are not one hard question but several well-formed ones. Split it rather than search."**
Fig 9 :54: **"And when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again."**
Fig 9 :57: **"a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either. It stops being a mixture and becomes a specific compound."**

Each authority "keeps working well on a different subset," which is the exact signature Fig 9 names. Extra effort does not shrink the disagreement because the parties are answering different questions with the same words. The resolution is to unbundle: the empirical subquestion to an empirical anchor, the normative subquestion to the community that must live under the norm, the jurisdictional subquestion to a body whose authority is constitutively about jurisdiction.

Oberheim and Hoyningen-Huene (SEP 2025), writing on Kuhn and Feyerabend, reported this session, supply the low-overlap corroboration: where taxonomies differ, "it is neither necessary nor possible to translate between them. Rather, one must become bilingual, learning to use (and keep separate) the incongruently structured lexical taxonomies underwriting different laws and theories." Keeping separate is the Fig 9 split applied to vocabularies. The Galileo address independently does the same with "the different branches of knowledge call for different methods."

**Verdict:** triangulated across Fig 9 (:4-5, :54, :57) and Fig 2 (:36); world corroboration from Oberheim and Hoyningen-Huene 2025 and John Paul II 1992. Refuter: demonstrate a four-authority conflict that is genuinely one question (single predicate, single ground, no subset structure) and still fails to shrink after a honest search. Then it is a hard question, not a union, and F3 fails for that case.

### F4, Agreement between the frames is not resolution

Fig 2 :35: **"Alignment is triangulation, not negotiation. A mirror cannot correct you."**
Fig 2 :40: **"agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool"**
Fig 7 :13: **"agreement compounds, evidence does not"**
Fig 7 :35: **"A room's agreement is variation, not a verdict. Selection between rooms is where the ground votes."**
Fig 2 :43: **"Guard, from Figure 10: a convention that decides an outcome has stopped being one."**

A negotiated truce in which each authority keeps its claim and agrees to "mutual respect" is a convention. That is fine for coordination. It becomes a defect the moment the convention decides an empirical or jurisdictional outcome, because then it has stopped being a convention and has started doing the work of a verdict without a ground. Seat Map :163 says "High correlation is disqualifying for a verdict and efficient for a convention," which is the same rule stated for the checking arrangement.

Leonard (SEP 2023) supplies a different-field witness. His entry defines authoritative testimony by Preemption: "The fact that an authority… [testifies] that p is a reason for me to believe that p which replaces my other reasons relevant to p and is not simply added to them" (quoting Zagzebski 2012: 107). Deference replaces evidence rather than adding it. That predicts a truce built on deference carries no independent corroboration, which matches Fig 2 :40.

Collins (Physics Today 2021) is a second, lower-overlap witness: "In an area of serious dispute, you can't tell who is right simply by repeating the experiments because scientists disagree about when replication has been done properly... It's more like a social agreement than a transfer of information or a formula." He describes scientists settling a boundary by broad agreement when no anchor is reachable. That is the mirror chain, even inside the academic frame.

**Verdict:** triangulated across Fig 2 (:35, :40), Fig 7 (:13, :35), Fig 10 (:47); world corroboration from Leonard 2023 and Collins 2021. Refuter: show a truce that added new ground contact, not merely new votes, and that closed a conflict without any frame ranging against an external anchor. If so, F4 is too strong.

### F5, Some conflicts close by refusal, not by a new settlement

Fig 5 :53: **"refuted: a term we hold is wrong; a term is owed that says so"**
Fig 5 :55: **"What it removes is not a term but a term's standing to be composed over. Nothing leaves the library. What falls is the reach."**
Fig 6 :42: **"What is recorded only grows. What is reachable is derived, and it can fall."**

Historical conflicts often end because one authority loses standing to compose over the question, not because a synthesis was minted. Disestablishment, the transfer of marriage or education from clerical to civil jurisdiction, and the removal of a professional body's licensing power are refusals in this sense. The old claim stays in the record, with provenance (Fig 10 :62: "A ruling carries provenance like an atom."), but it can no longer be used as a premise for new rulings in that domain. The reach falls because something was added, not because anything was deleted (Fig 6 :42-43).

This is why "left unresolved" and "resolved by refusal" are different. A refusal is a real change in what can be composed, and it is reversible only by new growth, not by repeating the old claim (Fig 6 :44-45).

**Verdict:** triangulated across Fig 5 (:53, :55) and Fig 6 (:42-43); no direct world source was opened for a specific disestablishment case, so the historical mapping is `specified` and ungrounded. Refuter: show a case where an authority lost standing yet its older rulings remained composable as premises for new rulings in the same domain. Then the refusal did not fire and F5 fails.

### F6, "Resolution" handed down a chain is a recording, and the channel decays

Fig 4 :22: **"Only methods cross up. Only head starts come down."**
Fig 7 :12: **"same closure, handed along;"**
Fig 7 :13: **"agreement compounds, evidence does not"**
Fig 10 :24: **"the ground does not decay, the channel does"**
Fig 10 :28: **"you have lost touch"**

A settlement repeated by institutions over generations is a recording passed down. Fig 4 says only a method can cross up and only a head start can come down, so a handed-down verdict produces a system that repeats a past success and cannot produce a new one. Agreement along the chain compounds; evidence does not.

The channel then decays: Fig 10 :25 names "stale percepts, dropped transients, saturated metrics, pooled readings." Applied here, the record of why a conflict was settled is replaced by the mere fact that it was settled, and a later generation reopens it, or inherits a myth about it. The 1992 Galileo address is an explicit instance: John Paul II says the case became "a sort of ‘myth’, in which the image fabricated out of the events was quite far removed from reality." The ground did not change. The channel had.

**Verdict:** triangulated across Fig 7 (:12-13), Fig 10 (:24-28), and Fig 4 (:22-25); world corroboration from John Paul II 1992. Refuter: exhibit a chain of institutions handing down a settlement with no loss of the reasons for it and no increase in mere agreement. If that is the normal case, F6 fails.

### F7, Import across frames can move the wall; assimilation destroys the surplus

Fig 6 :23: **"No frame escapes its own closure. The bound moves only on contact with a frame whose closure differs."**
Fig 8 :45: **"Two frames together reach what neither reaches alone."**
Fig 8 :53: **"Residual first, frame second."**
Fig 11 :40: **"An improvement that does not change contact changes nothing, however much it improves."**

A conflict can close because one frame imports an atom from another: statistics into professional practice, an external court into a doctrinal dispute, an ecological mechanism into a traditional management system. The import must have provenance and a shadow test (Fig 6 source gate, Fig 8 :51 "Convergent derivation and adopted import look identical in the contents. Only the record of where each came from separates them").

Ludwig and Poliseli (Biology and Philosophy 2018) supply the lower-overlap witness for the failure mode. They write that "the line between integration and assimilation is often far from clear," and warn that "emphasis on the scientific character of TEK can lead to a loss of ‘the distinctiveness of other ways of knowing, and, also, their epistemic value in terms of their own validation criteria.’" Their Balinese water-temple case is a positive import: academic ecology recognized a mechanism that traditional management had engineered for centuries. Their Green Revolution case is assimilation: academic authority replaced the temple system and lost both the mechanism and the crop. That is Fig 8's surplus destroyed, plus Fig 11 :40 (an improvement that did not change contact in the right way).

**Verdict:** triangulated across Fig 6 (:23), Fig 8 (:45, :53), Fig 11 (:40); world corroboration from Ludwig and Poliseli 2018. Refuter: show a case where one frame absorbed another's claims without changing its own closure and nonetheless gained the other's predictive reach. Then import did not move the wall and F7 fails.

### F8, Which frame wins is decided by the residual, not by a permanent hierarchy

This is F2 stated at the level of outcomes and recorded separately so it cannot be averaged away. Fig 11 :49 and :51 are the grounds; Ludwig and Poliseli 2018 show the empirical shape: in Bali the traditional frame was the one carrying the working mechanism, in the doctrinal cases the academic or civil frame carried the empirical anchor. The same authority type is benefactor in one residual and malfactor in another. No verdict of the form "religious authority is unreliable" or "traditional knowledge is superior" survives changing the residual.

**Verdict:** single-source for the figure rule (Fig 11 :49-51), with one world witness (Ludwig and Poliseli 2018). Refuter: a stable cross-case ranking of authority types by reliability. None was found in the sources read; finding one would force a re-census of D1 to D4 as one domain rather than four.

### F9, The ground for a history question is itself a channel (live strain)

Fig 2 :36 makes human judgment legitimate when the question is constitutively about people. This question is about human institutions, so human and historical judgment belongs in the ground. But Fig 10 :24 says the channel decays, and Fig 10 :62-66 says provenance shows a ruling was reached legitimately and never shows it was right. The historical record through which a historian must range is produced by the very authorities whose conflicts are being studied, and it comes with the stake of the recorder (Fig 10 :59).

This is a genuine strain, not a flaw to be smoothed over. It does not reverse F1 to F8. It limits the mode: the answer can be `specified` (a testable shape), not `grounded` (a settled description of all cases). The honest response is the one the figures prescribe: name the stake, keep provenance, use witnesses with low overlap and independent evidence pools, and let the ground settle each case (Fig 2 :42: "Buy independence in proportion to the cost of being wrong.").

**Verdict:** single-source for the figure rule (Fig 10 :24, :59, :62-66); strain recorded, not hidden. Refuter: a historical source that is outside every institution it describes and whose channel does not decay. If one exists, the strain dissolves.

## 4. Pre-registered refuter (written before the conclusion)

Required before any outreach: Seat Map :287: "And a falsifier written before the run is a design instrument, not only an honesty device." The shape claim is refuted if any of the following holds:

1. A conflict closes by a verdict that no frame could range against, and it stays closed without any channel failure (refutes F1 and F4).
2. A multi-authority conflict is shown to be one question with one ground, yet resists honest search (refutes F3).
3. A fixed ordering of the four authority types predicts which conflicts close better than the question-relative anchor rule (refutes F2 and F8).
4. A settlement is handed down unchanged with its reasons intact and no growth in mere agreement (refutes F6).
5. A frame absorbs another's claims without changing its closure and gains the other's reach (refutes F7).

If any fires, the shape is withdrawn or narrowed. Until one is run, no part of this may be labelled `grounded`.

## 5. Recommendation

1. Lead with the split, not with a verdict (F3). Ask which subquestion is actually in dispute before asking who had authority.
2. Apply the anchor test, not the winner test (F1, F2). Ask what would have to be true for the thing appealed to change its answer.
3. Treat "resolved" as four distinct mechanisms: triangulation against a legitimate anchor; split into subquestions each with its own anchor; refusal that removes standing; and import that moves a wall. Treat "unresolved" as four distinct failures: unsplit union, mirror truce, lost standing still asserted, and channel decay. Never average them.
4. Demand provenance for any ruling offered as precedent (F6, Fig 10 :62). A ruling with a clean record is still to be corrected by the ground.
5. For outreach, send the shape claim and its refuter first, then the public venues in `03_public_contact_points.csv`, then, only if asked, the links in `04_links_and_distribution.md`. Never ask for agreement.
