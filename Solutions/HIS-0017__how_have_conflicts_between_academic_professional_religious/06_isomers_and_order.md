# Frame separation, dependency order, and isomers of resolution

**Mode: `specified`.** A simulated derivation checked against the figures, not a ground reading (see `05_what_shape_means.md` §7).

## RESIDUAL

`01_figure_grounded_audit.md` established that the four authority frames are not interchangeable and that an "unresolved" conflict is usually a union that was never split. Two supporting questions remain: (1) are the four authorities genuinely four frames or fewer names for one frame, and (2) what is the order of dependence among the operations that close a conflict, and what does a resolution become when it is reused?

## Five-line seat-reasoning header

```
DECIDES:    If two authority frames share an evidence pool -> treat as one frame wearing two names, no surplus.
            If their closures differ -> treat as two frames, import possible, record provenance. A = B? No:
            one case has surplus, the other has only extra spelling (Fig 8 :48-49).
HOURS:      Derivation only, no instrument time; the check is a short reading plus a case audit, and the
            reading cannot itself settle a case (Fig 12 :126-130 keeps bits and actions apart).
PRIOR CASE: QMGR-0037 used Fig 12's settled-molecule rule to make GR and QM atoms at second order; same
            rule is reused here for a handed-down ruling, with the same caveat about recording vs method.
ANALOGUE:   A court and a commission are both seats: they author conventions and transmit verdicts, and a
            verifier that could reconstruct their ruling could not check it (Fig 10 :42).
LIMIT:      Across 1 case or 10,000 cases the split rule is constant; what changes is whether the record
            survives the handoff. A chain of agreeing chronicles adds no evidence (Fig 7 :13).
```

## DOMAIN CENSUS (recap, not repeated derivation)

Actors: the four authority frames plus the record. Currency: credentials, outcomes, doctrine, continuity, provenance. Anchor: legitimately one of these only for the subquestion constitutively about it (Fig 2 :36). Union: empirical plus normative plus identity-bearing claims form a compound (Fig 9 :57). Order: split, then anchor legitimacy, then settlement, then refusal or mint, then record (Fig 3).

## DERIVATION

### Q1, Four frames, or one frame with four names?

- Fig 13 :34: **"And within a kind the members are distinct too, which is what makes them separate frames."** Being "an authority" does not make the four one frame.
- Fig 8 :48: **"How much two frames draw on the same evidence decides both whether their agreement means anything and whether their union has any surplus."** The test is evidence overlap, not institutional label.
- Fig 8 :49: **"two frames that overlap almost completely are one frame wearing two names."** (The figure text wraps this across :48-49; the quoted sentence is verbatim.)
- Oberheim and Hoyningen-Huene (SEP 2025) report Kuhn's later view that neighbouring specialities with incompatible lexical taxonomies cannot be merged: "one must become bilingual, learning to use (and keep separate) the incongruently structured lexical taxonomies underwriting different laws and theories." A separate-field witness for keeping frames separate.
- **Ruling:** academic and professional authority are separate frames when they range against different anchors (impersonal evidence versus outcome); they collapse into one frame when both simply cite the same institutional reputation. Religious and traditional authority are separate frames when their validation criteria differ (scriptural interpretation versus community continuity); they collapse when tradition is treated merely as a weaker form of academic evidence. Verdict **triangulated** (Fig 13 + Fig 8), with one world witness.
- **Falsifier:** show two authority types that draw on separate evidence yet whose union produces no new reach and no new disagreement. Then they were one frame all along.

### Q2, What is the order of dependence, and where does the chain break?

- Fig 3 :20: **"The chain is ordered by dependency, not by importance. Each link consumes what the one above produces, so effort spent above the break arrives at a step"** that cannot use it.
- Fig 3 :27: **"A link with no instrument is not a link that works. It is a link nobody has looked at."**
- The four links, in order: (1) is there a fact here and is it one question (Fig 9 :4-5); (2) which anchor is legitimate for that subquestion (Fig 2 :36); (3) range each frame separately against it (Fig 2 :45); (4) decide, refuse, or mint.
- Link 2 is the middle link with no instrument. Histories of these conflicts usually report link 4, the decision, and skip link 2, the question of what the decision was constitutively about. That is why more archival detail about a ruling does not resolve the conflict: it lands above the break.
- **Ruling:** a conflict cannot be diagnosed by reading the verdict; it can only be diagnosed by reading the subquestion and its anchor. Verdict **triangulated** (Fig 3 + Fig 2).
- **Falsifier:** demonstrate a conflict that was correctly diagnosed from its verdict alone, with no information about which subquestion it answered or what anchor it ranged against.

### Q3, Are different resolutions isomers?

- Fig 12 :117: **"isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."**
- Fig 12 :123: **"functional group. A substructure whose presence fixes the outcome. The group says what kind; the qualifier says which member."**
- Take two resolutions with the same atoms: an external court, a written record, and a split of the original question into an empirical and a jurisdictional part. In case A the court **refuses** one frame's standing to decide the empirical part (Fig 5 :53, "refuted: a term we hold is wrong; a term is owed that says so"); in case B the court supplies the **anchor** for the jurisdictional part while each frame ranges separately against its own empirical ground. Same atoms, different bond, different subsequent behaviour: in A the old claims cannot be composed into new rulings (Fig 5 :55), in B they can still be used inside their own frames. They are isomers under Fig 12 :117.
- All genuine resolutions share a **functional group**: `{subquestion, legitimate anchor, ground contact, provenance}`. The group says what kind, the qualifier says which member (Fig 12 :123). A truce lacks ground contact and is therefore not in the group, whatever it is called.
- **Ruling:** the isomer claim is a real, checkable structural claim, `specified` and ungrounded. Verdict **single-source** for the figure definition (Fig 12), with no world source opened specifically for an isomer pair.
- **Falsifier:** show two resolutions with identical atoms, identical bonds, and different downstream reach. Then the bond is not carrying the difference and the isomer reading fails.

### Q4, What does a resolution become when it is reused?

- Fig 12 :103: **"Composition is recursive, and the level decides what is irreducible."**
- Fig 12 :104: **"A settled molecule becomes an atom for whatever composes over it. So irreducible is a fact about a level and never about the world, and there is no bottom"** (the line continues; this is the verbatim first portion).
- A ruling settled against the ground can serve as an atom in a later dispute. That is a precedent, and it is legal only because the earlier ruling was settled. But Fig 4 :22 says "Only methods cross up. Only head starts come down," and Fig 7 :13 says "agreement compounds, evidence does not." A precedent handed down as a recording gives the next generation a head start, not the derivation. If the record drops the reasons, every lineage pays again (Fig 12 :107-110).
- **Ruling:** reuse is legal at the next level, but a ruling carried as a headline rather than as a method is a recording, and its reach is narrower than it looks. Verdict **triangulated** (Fig 12 + Fig 7 + Fig 4).
- **Falsifier:** exhibit a chain of institutions that hands down a ruling with its derivation intact, its reasons undisputed, and no increase in mere agreement. Then the recording/head-start distinction does not bite for that case.

## CONCLUSION

The four authorities are four frames when their closures and evidence pools differ, and fewer when they do not. The chain of resolution runs split, anchor legitimacy, separate ranging, then decision or refusal; its unexamined middle link is anchor legitimacy. Two resolutions with the same atoms and different bonds are genuine isomers, and every genuine resolution shares a functional group that a truce lacks. A settled ruling becomes an atom for the next dispute, but what crosses down a chain is a head start, not the derivation.

This rescues what is defensible in a plain chronicle of "resolved and unresolved" cases without flattening them: the order and the isomer test need no new frame, and the refusal is a real change in reach rather than a change of wording.
