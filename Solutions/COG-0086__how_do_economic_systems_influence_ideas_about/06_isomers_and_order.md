# Four ideas, four bonds: the isomer and order derivation

**Mode: `specified` on the bond assignments, `general` on the structural claim. Not `grounded`.** This is a simulated derivation checked against the figures, not a ground reading (see `05_what_shape_means.md` section 6).

## RESIDUAL

The four idea slots are not interchangeable, and the question does not say how they differ. Two things are owed: (1) can choice, obligation, ownership and success be written as different bond arrangements over the same held atoms, and (2) what order, if any, does the dependency chain license among them? A third thing is owed by the union finding in `01`: are these a partition or a union, and what follows for the search?

## DERIVATION

### Q1: Partition or union?

- Fig 9 :54 "when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again."
- A case in two conditions "stops being a mixture and becomes a specific compound" (Fig 9 :57-58). Ownership and obligation overlap: a property claim carries duties. Choice and success overlap: a merit ideology joins them.
- Therefore the four slots are a **union of overlapping conditions**, to be unbundled into per-slot loops. A single lever clears the region where only one condition holds and leaves the intersections standing.
- **Falsifier:** show that the four slots form a partition, that is, that each idea has a predicate no other slot uses and no overlap survives any change of regime.

### Q2: What bond does each slot use?

- Fig 12 :112 "A plan is a sequence. A goal is a comparison. Progress is a subtraction. A refutation is a negation". Operators table :51 "a goal is a comparison, which is why a vocabulary without it cannot state one."
- **Success** is a goal, therefore a **comparison** ⋛: a threshold relation between two quantities, a rank or income against a standard. Operators table :49 "a threshold relation between two quantities."
- **Obligation** is what was given and what must be returned. The return did not exist before the gift fired, so the bond is **production** ⇒ ("A produces B; B did not exist before", Operators table :37), with an ordered **sequence** → where timing rather than creation is the point (Kula and potlatch schedules).
- **Ownership** is a bond between a state (a resource in space or time) and a structure (a network of recognised claims). Its spelling changes the substance: alienable commodity versus inalienable possession. Fig 12 :117 "Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."
- **Choice** is a **disjunction** ∥ over options ("either suffices; not both required", Operators table :19) and, where options are ranked, a ⋛ comparison. Under a market regime the spectrum widens; under a gift regime the disjunction may be over kin obligations rather than goods.
- **Assignment status:** `specified`, not `grounded`. The figure licenses the bond vocabulary; which bond a given culture actually uses is an empirical reading.
- **Falsifier:** show that two slots use the same bond and yet behave differently with identical atoms, or that a bond's type cannot be recovered by the six tests of Fig 12.

### Q3: Are the four slots isomers?

- Fig 12 :117 "Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."
- Written over the full shared atom set {Space, Time, Energy, Spectrum, Gradient, Network} plus the habitat atoms {resource, claim, exchanger, institution}, the four slots **are** isomers: same atoms, different arrangement, different substance.
- As field descriptions they are not isomers, because each uses a different subset of atoms. The weaker claim is that they share a **functional group**: Fig 12 :123 "a substructure whose presence fixes the outcome. The group says what kind; the qualifier says which member." The common substructure is `state → structure → state` over a resource.
- This mirrors the exemplar's own re-census: atoms held versus arrangement chosen. No new primitive is needed either way. Triangulated across Fig 12 and the Operators table (low overlap: definition versus identification test).
- **Falsifier:** either slot requires an atom no other slot can even express (kills the isomer claim, leaves the functional-group claim), or an arrangement is found whose substance is fully determined by its atoms alone (kills the isomer claim outright).

### Q4: What order does the dependency chain license?

- Fig 3 :20 "The chain is ordered by dependency, not by importance. Each link consumes what the one above produces, so effort spent above the break arrives at a step that cannot use it."
- Ordered links: perception of the regime and the slot; vocabulary for what it affords; objective (what counts as progress); planning; learn and carry.
- Fig 3 :26-27: the middle link has "neither" obvious failures nor obvious tests, "so it attracts the least attention and the fewest instruments. A link with no instrument is not a link that works. It is a link nobody has looked at."
- Therefore the order is **per slot**, not across the four ideas. There is no licensed sequence "choice first, then ownership, then success". Asking for such a sequence would be composing within one kind and adding an ordering the figure does not supply, the same error the exemplar found when it wrote `→` over a set the source wrote with commas.
- **Falsifier:** an instrument that measures a stable cross-slot order (for example obligation always changes before ownership across all observed regime shifts) with no per-slot confound.

### Q5: What crosses scales, and is it a recording?

- Fig 4 :22 "Only methods cross up. Only head starts come down." Fig 4 :25 "A recording carried upward looks like knowledge and is a description of one occasion."
- Across the population-to-person membrane, what arrives is a cheapened search: which payoffs count, which claims are already settled, which options are thinkable. It is not a copy of a conclusion. Replayed downward, a recording produces a person who repeats a past success and cannot produce a new one.
- A cross-system import is different: Fig 6 :23 "No frame escapes its own closure. The bound moves only on contact with a frame whose closure differs." It adds an atom, is priced (Fig 6 :24 "A wrong atom costs more than starting from primitives."), and needs a provenance record (Fig 8 :50 "Two frames can hold the same atom by convergence, reaching it separately, which is corroboration at maximum independence.").
- **Falsifier:** a case where exact answers, not methods, transfer across a regime boundary and are sufficient to reproduce the new culture's judgments without any change in the recipient's search space.

### Q6: Does the union claim survive the isomer claim?

- Yes, and the two are different claims. The union is about **coverage**: four overlapping conditions must be unbundled. The isomer claim is about **composition**: over the shared atoms, arrangement makes the substance.
- A lever that clears one slot changes the bond in that slot and leaves the intersections standing. This is why the world literature is split: economic anthropology emphasizes gift versus commodity (the ownership bond); moral-economy history emphasizes obligation and fairness; the overjustification literature emphasizes the changed search objective (the success bond). Each is a single-slot frame, and none is a verdict over all four.
- **Falsifier:** a single intervention that re-bonds all four slots simultaneously, with no residue in the intersections.

## CONCLUSION

The four slots form a **union**, not a partition, and must be looped separately. Over the shared substrate and habitat atoms they are an **isomer** claim in the stronger reading and a **functional group** claim in the weaker one. Success is a comparison, obligation a production or sequence, ownership a bond over a resource and a recognised claim, choice a disjunction or comparison. Dependency order is per slot, with the objective link as the likely break. What crosses scales is a head start; what moves the wall is an import with gates and a price. None of this names which economic system causes which idea.
