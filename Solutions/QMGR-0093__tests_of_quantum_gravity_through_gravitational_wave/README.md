## Restatement in effect terms

The detector returns a strain time series that matches classical general relativity to high precision. Candidate quantum-gravity models predict deviations (a dispersion term, a graviton mass, a Lorentz-violating coefficient, a near-horizon echo, a quantum-optical signature). The residual is the gap between those predictions and what the instrument can register. The decision is between: (A) more precision will eventually register a quantum-gravity effect, and (B) current high-precision gravitational-wave observations constrain phenomenological deviations but do not reach the Planck-scale theory, so the honest report is a bound plus a statement of what would have to be imported. Option A spends the audience's independence on a verdict the figures cannot deliver; option B sends a checkable shape, written refuters, and a named instrument gap.

## What is deliberately not here

- No personal emails of named individuals.
- No ranking of people by how likely they are to agree with the claim.
- No bulk-send plan.
- No claim that this simulated derivation settles whether quantum gravity can be tested with gravitational waves.

The reasons are the figures' own. Fig 2 :35 says *"Alignment is triangulation, not negotiation. A mirror cannot correct you."* Fig 2 :40 says *"disqualifying for a verdict: agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool"*. Fig 10 :42 says *"A verifier that can reconstruct the claim cannot verify it."* Selecting recipients for receptiveness manufactures the correlation that would make their agreement worthless, and showing them the framework before asking for a verdict spends the only independence they contribute.
