### Q1 - What bond is the test itself?

Fig 12 :117: **"isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."** The Operators table defines ⋛ as *"a threshold relation between two quantities"*, with the test *"is it about which is larger, rather than both being present?"*

So the test is a ⋛ comparison, and this is a **clean first-order type**, not the strained second-order case that afflicts other QM/GR bindings. The two operands are quantities; ⋛ applies without demoting a settled molecule to an atom.

### Q2 - What is the first-order molecule that the waveform sits in?

Fig 13 :74: **"Everything else is a composition across the two kinds, never within one."** A first-order molecule is `state → structure → state`, one crossing. An interferometer measures a directed difference in arm length over ordered time, which reads:

```
Space(state) -- Gradient(structure) --> Time(state)
```

Fig 13 itself licenses more than one such molecule: *"A gradient over space and one over time are different terms, because which state it is bonded to is part of what it is."* A dispersion modification deforms the spectrum (ordering) of the waveform rather than its spatial gradient, giving a parallel first-order reading `Space → Spectrum → Time`. Both are legal compositions.

- **Mode:** `specified`, ungrounded. The binding of "gradient" to strain and "spectrum" to the frequency-domain waveform is a mapping from the physics frame onto held atoms, not a derivation from the six. It is provenance-bearing, and provenance is recorded.
- **Falsifier:** if the strain signal cannot be written as a cross-kind composition at first order (for example if it required two states bonded directly), this reading fails and Fig 13 :75 governs instead.

### Q3 - Are the two classic bounds isomers?

Fig 12 :117 defines isomer; Fig 12 :124: **"functional group. A substructure whose presence fixes the outcome. The group says what kind; the qualifier says which member."**

- **As actually reported**, GW170817 uses arrival-time difference between gravitational and electromagnetic messengers, while GW150914 uses the phasing of a binary-black-hole waveform and a graviton-mass assumption. They share only Time and the detector atom set. Under Fig 12's definition they are **not** isomers as written; they are two members of the same functional group (a comparison against a detector ground) with different observable slots.
- **The stronger form:** write both over the full shared atom set `{Space, Time, Energy, Gradient, Spectrum, Network}`, differing only in which structure mediates which state pair. Then they **are** isomers: same atoms, different arrangement, different substance. This is the claim worth testing, because it says the two bounds are not independent confirmations of one another; they are different substances with the same ingredients.
- **Falsifier, written before the run:** if either bound can be written without an atom the other requires, the isomer claim fails and the functional-group reading stands instead. The source ledger's overlap column is the check: if both draw on the same LVK data pool, they are one witness wearing two names, which is a different failure from being isomers.

### Q4 - What is the order of the union?

The order is dependency order, not importance (Fig 3). From `01`: perception (instrument already returning something failing to resolve) precedes vocabulary (deviation stated in effect terms) precedes objective (epsilon fixed) precedes planning precedes learn-and-carry. Optimising a link that already works cannot rescue one that does not.

Fig 3 :19: **"Optimising a link that already works cannot rescue one that does not."** In this field the detector link largely works and attracts most of the effort. The vocabulary link (a QG model predicting a specific, falsifiable waveform deformation) is the one with fewest instruments. Giddings's abstract in `04_links_and_distribution.md` names exactly that asymmetry when it calls for more precise predictions from quantum-modification scenarios before data analysis can improve constraints.

For the reach question, Formula step 7 imposes a second order: **SEARCHED first** (is the deviation already in the closure and unfound?), then **INWARD** (extend the instrument from one already returning something) and **OUTWARD** (import a frame that already predicts a deviation at reachable scales) as *opposed* tracks never read against each other. Underivable-in-principle and unreachable-at-this-budget are different facts with different instructions.

### Q5 - What does the round trip cost?

Fig 4 :30: **"So take the round trip and measure the gap. Without it you are navigating by dead reckoning: confident about a position nobody checked."** The shorthand *"a test is a deviation, an observable, a ground and a bound"* abstracts away the union structure, the filter/witness distinction and the null-is-not-absence rule. Bringing it back down to *"more precision tests quantum gravity"* loses all three. That loss is `R_T`, enumerated rather than numerically measured in `01` §F8. It is not an error to eliminate; it is the honest report of what the coarser description cannot hold.

## CONCLUSION

The test is a first-order ⋛ comparison of two quantities, which is a clean type (Q1). The waveform sits in a legal cross-kind first-order molecule, `specified` as a binding rather than a derivation (Q2). The two classic bounds are a functional group as written and a genuine isomer pair only over the full shared atom set; they are not independent corroboration (Q3). The union is ordered by dependency, with the vocabulary link, not detector precision, as the likely break (Q4), and the figure-to-question condensation carries a nonempty round-trip gap (Q5).

This rescues what is defensible in the question without importing a new primitive: the test shape needs no new kind, no claim that null equals absence, and no naming of a winning QG theory.
