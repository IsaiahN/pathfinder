# Outreach draft: check the shape of the test before deciding about the theory

**Use:** one to one, or as a cover letter attached to a submission through the public venues in `03_public_contact_points.csv`. **Not** for bulk send.

**Hard precondition:** do not send until the shape claim in `01_figure_grounded_audit.md` survives the reader's own check. If the output fails on contact, the two-stage offer collapses.

**Subject:** One checkable output on gravitational-wave tests of quantum gravity

> Hi [name],
>
> I have one specific output I would like you to check against what you already know. I am not asking you to read the framework first, and I am not asking you to agree with it. I am also not asking you to trust me. If the output does not survive, there is nothing else to look at.
>
> **The residual, in effect terms.** A detector returns a strain time series. General relativity predicts it to within waveform uncertainty. Candidate quantum-gravity models predict a deviation: a dispersion term, a graviton mass, a Lorentz-violating coefficient, a near-horizon echo, a quantum-optical signature. The question is whether more precision closes the gap between those predictions and what the instrument can register.
>
> **The output (`specified`, not `grounded`).** A high-precision gravitational-wave test of quantum gravity is a three-part compound, not one measurement. It needs (1) a deviation stated as an ordinary term with a mapping to an observable, (2) an observable channel, and (3) a ground that can be read independently. I write the sought-for shape as `TEST_QG_GW(d, O, G, epsilon)`: state `d`, range the observable `O` against the detector ground `G`, and return either a measured nonzero `d` or a bound `|d| < epsilon`. That bound is a constraint, not a proof of absence.
>
> Four things follow, and these are what I want checked:
>
> 1. The detector is the ground. A model's internal coherence cannot settle it, because that is a quantity the model's own frame produces and scores.
> 2. Precision is a budget filter, not a witness. Better strain sensitivity narrows the bound; it does not make a deviation real.
> 3. The scale gap is a reach gap. Today's searches are already returning things that fail to resolve at the Planck scale, so this is a live search inside the current reach, not a hunt for a hypothetical sensor. Underivable in principle and unreachable at this budget are different facts with different instructions.
> 4. A null result is a bound. "We found nothing" is not "there is nothing there", and an aggregate "quantum gravity excluded" number throws away the per-channel readings that say where to look.
>
> **What would refute the shape claim.** I wrote these down before sending this:
>
> - A high-precision observation returns a deviation uniquely attributable to a quantum-gravity effect and settles a candidate model against the detector ground. That extends the reach and kills the "unreachable at this budget" claim.
> - The three conditions are a partition rather than a union, so one loop closes all of them.
> - The detector ground is shown to update on the theory frame's predictions, for example an analysis tuned until a desired bound appears with the tuning unrecorded.
> - A single aggregate exclusion with no per-channel attribution is admitted as a legitimate test.
>
> If any holds, I will withdraw or re-cense the claim. A one-line "this fails because..." is more useful to me than agreement. If you would rather not engage, no reply is needed.
>
> **What I am not claiming.** Not that any particular quantum-gravity theory is right. Not that the framework names which deviation will show up. Not that a null result excludes a theory. Not that this email is a ground reading; it is a simulated derivation checked for internal consistency against the figures, and it cannot settle the question it reports on.
>
> **If it survives, and only then:** the sources are below. The figures are the authority, not my summary of them.
> - Canonical figures text with line numbers: `FRAMEWORK/reference/03-FIGURES_TEXT.md` (local file in this repository)
> - Figures and formula for direct download (branch `general-agent`): https://github.com/IsaiahN/tether/tree/general-agent/figures
> - The architecture article: https://medium.com/@IsaiahNwukor/arrival-of-the-fittest-the-tether-architecture-1de7edf641ec
> - Public gravitational-wave data and community forum: https://gwosc.org/ and https://ask.igwn.org
>
> **If you want to use the machinery:** the formula is in the figures link. Run it on a problem you know well and can check against a ground. Write down what would refute your result *before* you run it and state the mode of what you get. A reported failure is more useful to me than a reported success.
>
> Thanks for reading,
> [name]
