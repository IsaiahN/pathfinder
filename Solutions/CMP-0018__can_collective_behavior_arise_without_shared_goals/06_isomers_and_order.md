## DERIVATION

### Q1 : Union or partition?

- Fig 9 :54 "And when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again."
- Fig 9 :55-57 "A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones. A single lever clears the region where only one condition holds and leaves the intersections standing, because the second condition still binds."
- Fig 9 :58 "a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either."
- *Library of Babel*: "Perhaps your problem was a bundle or conflation of several problems masquerading as one?"

The three prerequisites overlap without reducing to one another. A shared medium can serve both as "common information" (a pheromone trail) and as a substitute for "central control" (no one authored the trail). That intersection is a compound with its own loop: the medium itself has to be enumerated as an actor in the habitat (Fig 11 :43-44 "The habitat is enumerated, never composed."). Removing all three prerequisites at once does not leave a harder version of any single one; it leaves a different case.

**Falsifier:** derive central control from shared goals plus shared information using the seven bonds, so the union collapses to a partition. If that derivation succeeds, Q1 fails and the three loops must be merged.

### Q3 : Are the mechanisms isomers or a functional group?

- Fig 12 :117 "isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."
- Fig 12 :124 "functional group. A substructure whose presence fixes the outcome. The group says what kind; the qualifier says which member."

The four world mechanisms read as follows:

| mechanism | atoms it uses | bond that closes the loop | what it shows |
|---|---|---|---|
| Boids alignment | agents, local heading, neighbor radius | → sequence repeated per step | order from three local rules |
| Stigmergy | agents, environment trace, positive feedback | catalyst, "a part that survives its own use" (Fig 12 :119) | coordination without planning or direct talk |
| Vicsek alignment with noise | self-propelled particles, average neighbor heading, noise | cycle, "The output re-enters as input" (Fig 12 :121) | phase transition to collective motion |
| Spontaneous order | individuals, price or convention, local adjustment | ⋛ comparison, "a threshold relation between two quantities" | large-scale coordination without design |

As written, they do **not** share a full atom set, so by Fig 12's definition they are not isomers. They share a **functional group**: local interaction plus a loop plus ground contact. "The group says what kind; the qualifier says which member." The stronger form would write all four over the full shared substrate and claim they differ only in which structure mediates which state pair. That is a real isomer claim, but it is `specified` and ungrounded here because it has not been checked against the four systems.

**Falsifier:** show that one mechanism requires an atom none of the others can do without, for example that stigmergy cannot function unless the environment retains a trace while the other three work with no persistent medium. That kills the isomer claim and leaves the functional-group claim standing.

### Q4 : What crosses between one field and another?

Fig 4 :22 "Only methods cross up. Only head starts come down." Fig 4 :25-26 "A recording carried upward looks like knowledge and is a description of one occasion. Replayed downward, it produces a system that repeats a past success and cannot produce a new one."

What crosses from collective behavior research into the figure set is not "flocking" or "termite nests" as recordings. It is the method: describe the residual, then find the frame whose closure predicts its shape (Fig 8 :53-54). Boids, stigmergy, Vicsek, and spontaneous order are four independent arrivals at a shared functional group. They are corroboration at high independence because the fields do not share an evidence pool. Fig 8 :50-52 "Convergent derivation and adopted import look identical in the contents. Only the record of where each came from separates them."

**Falsifier:** show a single field's recording being carried into another field and working there as a generator without re-derivation. That would make the membrane rule false.

## CONCLUSION

The question is a union, and the loops have a fixed dependency order with the objective link as the usual break point. The four cross-field mechanisms are a functional group rather than isomers as written, with a stated falsifier for the stronger isomer reading. The membrane permits only the method to cross, and the round trip carries a nonzero gap that has been enumerated rather than assumed to be zero.
