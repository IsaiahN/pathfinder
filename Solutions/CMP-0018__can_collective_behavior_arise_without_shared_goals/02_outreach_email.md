# Outreach draft: check the shape before you decide about the theory

**Use:** one to one, or as a cover note attached to a submission through a public venue in `03_public_contact_points.csv`. **Not** for bulk send.

**Hard precondition:** do not send until the reader is being asked for a verdict on stage 1 only. The links and the framework stay below the fold until their verdict on the shape is recorded.

---

**Subject:** One shape claim about collective behavior, with the refuters written down first

> Hi [name],
>
> I work on figure-grounded derivations in complexity science. I have one specific output about collective behavior I would like you to check against what you already know. I am not asking you to read a theory first, and I am not asking you to agree. If the output does not survive, there is nothing else to look at.
>
> **The output (`general` on the shape, nothing `grounded`):** the question "can collective behavior arise without shared goals, central control, or common information?" is not one question. It is a union of three non-swappable conditions plus one verdict question. Working each one separately against a figure set gives this shape:
>
> 1. **Shared goals: not necessary.** Where nothing maintains anything, selection does the work that purpose appears to do. A goal is a comparison, a specific operator, not a precondition for order.
> 2. **Central control: not necessary.** Actors updating on each other converge without an anchor. The convergence is real order, but it is drift rather than correction unless something immovable settles it.
> 3. **Common information: not necessary as a communicated plan.** Agents who never talk can still agree because one cause produced both readings. What cannot be removed is contact: actors in a habitat, and a ground that does not move when they change their minds.
> 4. **The verdict question:** agreement, however large, is not truth. The figure set assigns one valence to a convention and the opposite to a verdict. A coordinated group can be wrong together.
>
> So the answer I am offering is a shape, not a named mechanism. It says which prerequisites are not necessary and what the residue is. It does not say which of alignment, stigmergy, selection, or quorum sensing produced any particular flock or market. That is for an instrument, not a derivation.
>
> **What I am not claiming:** that no real collective system ever has a leader, that evolution is the only mechanism, or that this settles the empirical question. I am claiming only that none of the three named prerequisites is individually necessary under the figure set, and that contact is.
>
> **The refuters, written before this message:**
> 1. Show a collective pattern maintained with no agent-agent contact and no contact with a shared ground. That breaks the contact clause.
> 2. Show that removing every shared comparison leaves the same pattern at the same rate and that the only remaining explanation is a shared goal. That breaks the goal verdict.
> 3. Show that agreement drawn from one evidence pool constitutes a verdict about the world. That breaks the verdict clause.
> 4. Derive a collective order that needs a seventh substrate primitive, or show the local loop changes with size. That breaks the shape.
> 5. Show that the record cannot in principle separate two agents who reached the same rule independently from two who copied it. That breaks the provenance claim.
>
> If any one holds, I withdraw the corresponding clause. A one-line "this fails because..." is more useful to me than agreement. If you would rather not engage, no reply is needed.
>
> **If it survives, and only then:** here are the framework essays. The figures themselves are the authority, not my summary of them.
> - How a Society of Agents Would Work: https://adventuresinml.substack.com/p/how-a-society-of-agents-would-work
> - The Library of Babel: Locating the Book of Books: https://adventuresinml.substack.com/p/the-library-of-babel-locating-the
> - The Recursive Self-Improvement Trap: https://adventuresinml.substack.com/p/the-recursive-self-improvement-trap
>
> **If you want to use the method:** the eight-step loop is in the figures. Run it on a system you know and can check against a ground. Write down what would refute your result *before* you run it and state the mode of what you get. A reported failure is more useful to me than a reported success.
>
> Thanks for reading,
> [name]

## Distribution rule

One recipient at a time. Record their verdict on stage 1 before the links are shown. Do not pool later agreement as corroboration: Fig 2 :39-40 "agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool". Do not rank recipients by receptiveness: Fig 9 :60 "Use filters for the budget and witnesses for the verdict. Never let a filter hand you a verdict."
