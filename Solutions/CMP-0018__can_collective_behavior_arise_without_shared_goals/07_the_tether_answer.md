# The Tether's answer: can collective behavior arise without shared goals, central control, or common information?

**Mode: `general` on the shape, `specified` and ungrounded on any binding to a particular system, nothing `grounded`.** This is a simulated derivation checked against the figures (`05_what_shape_means.md` §7), not a ground reading.

## 1. The residual it answers

Coordinated order is observed at the group level. The question asks whether three named prerequisites are individually necessary. It is a union of three non-swappable conditions plus a verdict question (Fig 9 :54 "And when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again."). Each loop gets its own verdict; they are never averaged into a single yes or no (Fig 1 :19 "The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated.").

## 2. The shape, per domain

1. **Shared goals: not necessary.** Fig 11 :36 "Where nothing maintains anything, intelligence is a label applied afterwards to a lineage that met its ground. Nothing was intelligent; selection ran." A goal is a comparison, a specific operator, not a precondition for order. Where no one maintains the habitat, what survives is what the ground kept.
2. **Central control: not necessary.** Fig 2 :13-15 "no anchor; each updates on the others / they converge, and what they converge on is whoever had the most influence." Actors updating on each other converge without a controller. The caveat is load-bearing: Fig 2 :34 "the anchor becomes another frame, everything drifts together, and the drift is coherent, which is why nobody notices." Order without a ground is drift, not correction.
3. **Common information: not necessary as a communicated plan.** Fig 2 :18-21 "one evidence pool / they never talk, and still agree / so the agreement is the pool talking, not two frames confirming each other." Agents that never communicate can agree because one cause produced both readings. The residue that cannot be removed is contact: a shared medium or a shared ground, not a shared intention.
4. **Contact is necessary.** Fig 11 :40 "An improvement that does not change contact changes nothing, however much it improves." Fig 10 :24 "the ground does not decay, the channel does." Capability belongs to agent and habitat together, never to the agent alone.
5. **Agreement is not truth.** Fig 7 :35 "A room's agreement is variation, not a verdict. Selection between rooms is where the ground votes." Fig 2 :39-40 "agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool". A coordinated group can be wrong together; the figure assigns one valence to a convention and the opposite to a verdict.
6. **The mechanism is a shape, not a name.** Fig 9 :32 "you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself." The figure set licenses network, cycle, and surviving trace (Fig 13 :23-24 "network / that entities relate, and influence travels"; Fig 12 :121 "cycle. The output re-enters as input. The sign of the loop decides whether it closes, and a chain cannot close by construction."; Fig 12 :119 "catalyst. A part that survives its own use."). It does not say which of these produced a given flock.

## 3. What it explicitly does not say

- Not that real flocks, nests, or markets never have leaders. The verdict is about individual necessity under the figure set, not an empirical universal (Fig 8 :39 "So a search that finds nothing is never a proof of absence.").
- Not that "emergence" is an explanation. Naming the label is not naming a mechanism; the label is applied afterwards to a lineage that met its ground.
- Not that a shared evidence pool is absent. It is often present; it is just not a communicated plan, and its presence does not make the agreement a verdict.
- Not that selection is the only mechanism. Fig 2's mutual update and common cause are two others, and the figures govern all three at once.
- Not that any mechanism has been identified for a particular system. That is `gap`, to be closed by an instrument.
- Not `grounded`. No measurement was taken.

## 4. What would settle it, the ground

An instrument, not a longer argument. The five pre-registered refuters are in `01` §3 and `08` §2. Short form:

1. A collective pattern maintained under complete isolation, with no agent-agent contact and no shared ground contact, breaks the contact clause.
2. A controlled removal of all shared comparisons that still yields the same pattern, with a shared goal as the only remaining explanation, breaks the goal verdict.
3. A demonstration that one-pool agreement constitutes a verdict about the world breaks the verdict clause.
4. A collective order requiring a seventh substrate member, or a kernel that changes with size, breaks the shape.
5. A proof that the record cannot in principle separate convergent derivation from adopted import breaks the provenance distinction (Fig 8 :50-52 "Convergent derivation and adopted import look identical in the contents. Only the record of where each came from separates them").

## 5. The honest stop

**Unreached, not unreachable.** The shape stops at the limit of what the figures can distinguish: which prerequisites are not necessary, what is necessary, and what agreement can and cannot do. It does not name the mechanism for a given case, and the README forbids pretending otherwise: a shape, not a name. "Indiscernibility is not identity." The lineage is recorded so the stop is checkable. Nothing here is settled: agreement or prestige is not an instrument.

## 6. One-paragraph version for outreach

> The Tether does not say that collective behavior needs a hidden designer, and it does not say that "emergence" explains anything. It says that the question is a union of three separate conditions, and that none of shared goals, central control, or a communicated plan is individually necessary for coordinated order. Actors updating on each other converge without a controller; agents that never talk can agree from one common cause; and where nothing maintains anything, selection does the work that purpose appears to do. What cannot be removed is contact: actors in a habitat, and a ground that does not move when they change their minds. The order so produced is real, but it is a convention or a variation, not a verdict, so a group can be coordinated and still be wrong together. The framework returns this shape and five refuters written in advance, and it stops short of naming which mechanism produced any particular flock. That is for an instrument, not a derivation.
