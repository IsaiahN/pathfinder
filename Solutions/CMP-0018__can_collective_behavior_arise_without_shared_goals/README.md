## The question restated as an effect

The observed effect is coordinated order at the group level: flocks, trails, nests, markets, consensus decisions. Three candidate prerequisites are named: shared goals, central control, common information. The decision is whether each is individually necessary. Option A says collective order requires at least one of them. Option B says none is individually necessary, but contact with a habitat and a ground is. A leads to a search for a designer or a shared plan behind every pattern; B leads to a search for local rules, loops, traces, and the ground that settles them. What an answer would look like is bound in advance as an ordinary term (formula step "Direction"): three slots each marked `necessary` or `not necessary`, one contact clause, and one verdict clause separating agreement from truth. That binding is then refusable by the same test as anything else.

## What is deliberately not here

- No personal emails of named individuals.
- No ranking of people by how likely they are to agree.
- No bulk-send plan.

The figures give three reasons. Fig 2 :35: "Alignment is triangulation, not negotiation. A mirror cannot correct you." Fig 2 :39-40: "agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool". Fig 10 :42: "A verifier that can reconstruct the claim cannot verify it." Selecting recipients for receptiveness manufactures the very correlation that makes their agreement worthless, and sending the architecture before asking for a verdict turns a checker into a member of the frame being checked.
