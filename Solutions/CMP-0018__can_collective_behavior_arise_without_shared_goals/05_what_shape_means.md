## 1. A shape is a constraint, not a filler

The README states the limit: the framework returns "what a missing thing would have to look like (a shape, not a name)". Fig 9 :32 "you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself." Fig 13 :138-139 "you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself." Fig 13 :134 "So the system can report that it lacks something rather than producing an answer anyway. Unreachable is a verdict here, not a silence," and Fig 13 :135 "It cannot say which primitive is missing, because naming it would mean already holding it."

From *The Library of Babel*: "This means that you can cut your search short if the set of books returned is small and descriptive enough that it provides a general shape of the answer that satisfies your question."

So a **shape** is the narrowed form of an answer you can state before you can name it. It constrains; it does not fill. For this question the shape is four clauses: goals not necessary, control not necessary, communicated information not necessary, contact necessary, agreement not truth. A named mechanism for a particular flock ("this flock used a quorum rule") is a name, not a shape.

## 2. How a shape is obtained

- *Library of Babel*: use the vocabulary you already have that is closest to the thing, look for recurring new terms that overlap or correlate with it, recurse. "With each new term gained, you narrow the space by definition and deepen your vocabulary."
- **The bundle passage is directly on point here**: "Perhaps your problem was a bundle or conflation of several problems masquerading as one? You will need to search all of those problems individually to find their answers and then combine them in a satisfactory way." This is the essay form of Fig 9 :54-58. Our question is such a bundle.
- Fig 9 :30-31 "your words are borrowed too, so describe the effect rather than the cause." The three prerequisites have to be described by their effects (a comparison, a seat, an evidence pool), not by the words "goal", "control", "information", which carry field-specific baggage.
- *Decoding Tacit Knowledge*: "When contact isn't possible, the best you can send is a plain description of what you'd expect to see, because anyone can check that against the world." This is the reason the outreach file leads with a refuter: a description of what failure would look like can be checked without the recipient joining the frame.
- *Library of Babel* on stopping: "You will know that you are done because the finish line will also be the starting place, as there is nowhere else to search and nothing else that can describe the answer better." The stop here is at the three verdicts plus the contact clause, not at a named mechanism.

## 3. What licenses a name

Two things, both outside the shape itself:

1. **The ground settles it.** Formula step 5: "SETTLE the ground settles it. Verified only by what pays. A gate is not the ground." The README: the framework "stops at the limit of what the available instruments can distinguish, and records the lineage so the stop is honest."
2. **A provenanced import from a frame whose closure differs** (Fig 6 :23, Fig 8 :50-52), with a source gate and a shadow test.

*They're Scanning Books* sharpens the limit: "The real import is not a new term in the existing space. It is a new dimension of the space." And: "A minimum viable set of six terms is a human's careful guess at reality's compression axes; another sensorium's minimum set need not be those six." Also: "Declaring the primitives does not make them neutral. It makes the choice visible."

The six are a visible guess, not a discovery of nature's own list. Naming one as *the* mechanism behind a flock turns a visible choice into an invisible one.

## 4. Why "collective behavior" is itself a post-hoc label

*Arrival of the Fittest* opens with the claim that the mapping is the intelligence and "we are not the holders of that mapping, we are the mapping, the same way a seed is the seedline." Fig 11 :36 "Where nothing maintains anything, intelligence is a label applied afterwards to a lineage that met its ground. Nothing was intelligent; selection ran."

Apply the same move: "collective behavior" is a label applied afterwards to a set of local interactions that met a ground. It is not an entity with its own goals. *How a Society of Agents Would Work* says it directly: "The group's agreement is never the verdict." And: "capability is a property of agent-and-habitat, never of the agent alone."

This is why the question's framing is partly an effect question and partly a category mistake. The effect (coordinated order) is real; the reification (a collective *that wants* something) is the label applied afterwards. The figure does not deny the order; it refuses to promote the label to an actor.

## 5. Why "shared information" is the hardest slot

*The Hard Problem is About Source* gives the mechanism. Metamerism: an instrument reads green and "cannot comment on the source"; one green light, or yellow plus blue, or RGB, or CMYK, or a scheme not yet discovered. "If several paths create the same behavior, then behavior is a projection with lossy information." And: "An instrument that can't distinguish these cases demonstrates its own inadequacy." It was the spectrometer, not an argument, that resolved it.

The same structure appears in *How Intelligence Actually Works*: a video frame and a photograph can be indistinguishable as artifacts, and "nothing in the image records which". "Provenance loss" is the thing the framework is built around.

Applied here: two agents who reach the same rule independently and two who copied it from one source produce the same observable coordination. Fig 8 :50-52 "Convergent derivation and adopted import look identical in the contents. Only the record of where each came from separates them." So "did they share information?" is not answerable from the behavior. It is answerable from the lineage, if the lineage was kept.

## 6. Correction to my own §1 and §3

I initially wrote that "shape, not a name" forbids naming any mechanism for collective behavior at all. That overstates the rule. The README's phrase is "what a **missing** thing would have to look like". Its subject is a primitive not held in Γ. Network, cycle, catalyst, and selection are already in the figure vocabulary (Fig 13 :23-24, Fig 12 :121, :119, Fig 11 :36). Naming them is a binding from the research frame onto atoms already held, not the invention of a new primitive.

What remains forbidden is different: naming *which* held mechanism produced a particular observed flock without a source gate and a shadow test, or claiming the binding moves the wall (Fig 6: "IMPORT adds an atom" is false here, it adds none). Both claims are now recorded so neither is quietly lost. The correction does not change the four-clause shape; it narrows where the refusal applies.

## 7. Prestige, self-validation, and my own standing

*The Recursive Self-Improvement Trap*: "Prestige must never be allowed to validate an action directly... Prestige can fund an action. It can give an action a budget to be tried. But it cannot settle an action. Only the ground can settle it. The Ground Maintainer must be blind to prestige."

Same essay: "the output is only as good as the validator. The agent, or the tool, or the frame, or the theory, cannot validate itself." And: "You may all agree and be wrong. You may set your clock to someone else's, who was setting your clock to yours, and be off by many deviations over time. You need ground truth to define where you stand."

So `01_figure_grounded_audit.md` is a simulated derivation, checked for internal consistency against the figures. It is **not** a test of the question and must be labelled as such. It cannot settle anything it reports on. An expert who agrees after reading the essays has joined a frame; their agreement is not corroboration (Fig 2 :39-40).
