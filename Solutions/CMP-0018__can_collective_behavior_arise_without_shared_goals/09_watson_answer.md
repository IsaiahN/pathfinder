## QUESTION

Decide, in effect terms, whether coordinated order at the group level requires shared goals, central control, or common information. Option A says at least one is individually necessary. Option B says none is individually necessary, but contact with a habitat and a ground is, and the order so produced is a convention or a variation rather than a verdict. What each leads to: A sends the search looking for a designer or a shared plan behind every pattern; B sends it looking for local rules, loops, traces, and the ground that settles them. ## VERDICT (from the figures)

None of shared goals, central control, or a communicated plan is individually necessary for collective order. What is necessary is contact: actors in a habitat and a ground that does not move when frames think. The order so produced is a convention or a variation, not a verdict, so coordination never settles truth. The mechanism shape (network, cycle, surviving trace, scale-invariant kernel) is licensed; a named mechanism for any particular system is `gap`. This is a `general`, ungrounded result, not a `grounded` one.

- **cost if wrong:** bounded and locatable. The packet states mode, refuters, and lineage before its conclusion, so an error is findable rather than invisible. The real cost is spending an outside reader's independence by sending a recording instead of a generator, which Fig 2 :42 prices: "Buy independence in proportion to the cost of being wrong." That cost is avoidable by leading with the shape claim and its refuter.
- **undoable:** yes. The system-level bindings are removable without touching the shape; `07_the_tether_answer.md` and `08_expert_verification_packet.md` already remove them. A sent claim can be superseded by a corrected packet, and no primitive has to be withdrawn.
- **reversed by:** a ground reading, not agreement. A controlled isolation experiment showing maintained coordination with no agent-agent contact and no shared ground breaks the contact clause; a goal-removal experiment with unchanged outcome breaks the goal verdict; an instrument that distinguishes a shared plan from a common cause in a specific system closes the provenance gap; a derivation needing a seventh substrate member breaks the substrate shape. Until one of these fires, the verdict remains `candidate`.
