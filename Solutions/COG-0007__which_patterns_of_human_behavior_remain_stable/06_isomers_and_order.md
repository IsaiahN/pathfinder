## DERIVATION

### Q1: What kind of thing is a stable behavior pattern?

- Fig 12 :124: "functional group. A substructure whose presence fixes the outcome. The group says what kind; the qualifier says which member."
- Fig 12 :117: "isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."
- A recurring pattern across periods is a **functional group**: a substructure (state, structure, state; perceive, name; contact, import) whose presence fixes the outcome. The qualifier, not the group, is what changes between cultures and periods.
- A pattern that differs only in which condition is contacted, or which qualifier fills the slot, is an **isomer** over the same held atoms. It looks like a different custom from the outside and is the same functional group with a different bond.
- So "stable" names the group and "changing" names the isomer. This is a structural distinction, not a ranking.
- **Mode:** `specified`. **Triangulated** for the group/isomer definitions (Fig 12 plus Fig 13's closed set). `single-source` for the application to human behavior. **Falsifier:** show a behavior pattern that recurs across every condition examined and cannot be written as an instance of any held functional group. That would break the identification and leave F2 standing without this structure.

### Q2: What order does the comparison have?

- Operators table :48 defines ⋛ as "a threshold relation between two quantities." The test is "is it about which is larger, rather than both being present?"
- Stability across periods is therefore an **ordered** comparison, not a symmetric conjunction. `pattern at t` → `pattern at t+1`, then ⋛ on persistence. Swapping the operands does not produce the same claim: recurrence forward in time is not recurrence backward, because the habitat has changed in between.
- Fig 12 :33 names the symmetric bonds as +, ∥, ≡ and the ordered ones as →, ⇒, −, ⋛. Writing stability as "both periods hold" (+) would erase the time direction and make the question unanswerable by construction.
- **Mode:** `general` on the bond type. **Triangulated** across the Operators table and Fig 12 :49-89. **Falsifier:** show that the stable/changing comparison is symmetric under time reversal, that is that a pattern recurring from t to t+1 is the same claim as recurring from t+1 to t with the habitat held fixed. If so, ⋛ is the wrong operator and → is not required.

### Q3: Do recurrences compound, and does that change the level?

- Fig 12 :104: "A settled molecule becomes an atom for whatever composes over it. So irreducible is a fact about a level and never about the world."
- A behavior pattern that recurs and is settled against the ground becomes an atom at the next level. Traditions therefore do not accumulate as a flat list; they become operands for higher compositions (institutions, norms, identities).
- Fig 4 :22: "Only methods cross up. Only head starts come down." What crosses between generations is the method, compressed and re-applicable, not the recording of a particular occasion.
- **Mode:** `specified`, triangulated across Fig 12 and Fig 4. **Falsifier:** show a recurring pattern that cannot serve as an operand for any next-level composition. That would break the level claim.

### Q4: Do low-overlap fields arrive at the same shape?

- Cultural Evolution Society, opened this session: culture is information passed by social learning, and evolution is variation, inheritance and selection applied to it. Its chid/chided example is a stable mechanism with a changing form over 200 years. That is the functional group and its isomer, observed in language.
- Cultural evolution (Wikipedia), opened this session: "Cultural evolution is the change of this information over time," and the field's own pages describe "diversification" into different forms, broadly tree-like. Tree-like diversification is the isomer set unfolding.
- Animal culture (Wikipedia), opened this session: socially transmitted behavioral traditions in non-human animals, with "regional variations that are independent of genetic or ecological factors." Same capacity, different forms, no human language. This is the highest-independence corroboration available in this run.
- The Civilizing Process (Wikipedia), opened this session: Elias documents "a profound change in human behaviour" across roughly 800 to 1900 AD while the human capacity to learn and transmit is not in question. Changing cast, stable kernel.
- **Verdict:** **corroborates** at the world level. The fields are genuinely different (linguistics, evolutionary biology, primatology, historical sociology), so their convergence is not one evidence pool wearing several names. **Mode:** `specified`. **Falsifier:** a low-overlap field showing a recurring behavior with no stable transmitting kernel, or a changing behavior with no variation/inheritance/selection structure. That would conflict and send us back to the census.

## CONCLUSION

A stable behavior pattern is a functional group, a recurring substructure whose qualifier may change. A changing pattern is an isomer over the same atoms: same capacities, different bond to the condition, different substance. The comparison is ordered (→ then ⋛), not symmetric, because the habitat changes between periods. Settled recurrences become atoms at the next level, which is why cultural change compounds rather than merely accumulating. Low-overlap fields (historical linguistics, cultural evolution, animal culture, historical sociology) independently arrive at the same kernel/cast shape. This rescues what is defensible in the question without producing the list it asks for: the invariant grammar and the changing arrangements, with a falsifier for each step.
