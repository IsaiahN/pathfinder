## 0. The residual in effect terms

A pattern B is observed under social conditions C in period t. We are asked to sort B into "stable across historical periods" or "changes when social conditions change." The residual is not the list. The residual is the missing rule that would tell us what makes B persist or move when C changes.

- **Option A, intrinsic kind.** Stability is carried by B. Some patterns are universals, others are variables, and the deliverable is two lists. This is the reading the question's grammar invites.
- **Option B, contact relation.** Stability is a relation between B and the habitat that contacts it. The deliverable is an invariant kernel plus a changing cast, with the boundary located by contact.
- **Option C, union.** The question is not one partition. "Stable" and "changing" are valences relative to different conditions, so one pattern can be stable on one axis and changing on another. The deliverable is an unbundling before any list.

What an answer would look like, written down before the run as an ordinary term: (i) the invariant quantities, (ii) the contact-varying quantities, (iii) a decision whether (i) and (ii) partition the space or overlap, and (iv) the instrument that would move an item from one to the other. This is the shape claim that §4 refutes in advance.

## 1. Domain census (before choosing any figure line)

### R by arity, symmetry and scale

- **Arity:** two. The operands are a pattern and a condition. "Stable" is a binary relation, not a monadic property carried by B. The question's phrasing ("which patterns are stable") hides this by dropping the second operand.
- **Symmetry:** ordered and comparative, not a symmetric join. The stable/changing split is a persistence threshold over time, which is the comparison bond's shape. Operators table :48 defines ⋛ as "a threshold relation between two quantities." Treating stability as a symmetric conjunction (+, "both hold at once") is a type error.
- **Scale:** three levels. The loop runs at the individual scale, composes to a population through a membrane, and composes again across historical periods. Fig 10 :68: "Different actors, a different currency, a different ground, and a lossy transform between." So a single list mixing levels is a dead-reckoning error (Fig 4 :30).

### One line per domain (actors, currency, anchor)

No nameable anchor means it is not a domain yet. All four below have one.

1. **D1, the individual learning loop.** Actors: one agent, its library Γ, its bets and actions. Currency: per-slot residual R and the two prices (bits and actions). Anchor: the perceptual or experimental ground. Nameable: yes.
2. **D2, population and transmission.** Actors: many agents, a shared library, and something that ranks what they produce (Fig 4 :5). Currency: methods crossing up, head starts crossing down. Anchor: ground-visible reproduction, that is whether a method survives renewed contact. Nameable: yes.
3. **D3, the historical habitat (social conditions).** Actors: conditions such as institutions, subsistence, technology and climate, and relations such as kinship, trade and competition. Currency: contact. Introduction is available, exclusion is not (Fig 11 :61). Anchor: the ground, since what survives is what the ground kept. Nameable: yes.
4. **D4, the measurement and comparison frame.** Actors: researchers and instruments, including cross-cultural datasets and longitudinal records. Currency: independence between frames (Fig 2 :31). Anchor: a measurement that does not update on what any frame thinks (Fig 2 :29). Nameable in principle; in this field the channel is partly stale, because much of the evidence pool is drawn from a narrow set of societies.

### Partition or union

The question asks for a partition. The census says union. A behavior pattern's stability is a valence relative to a residual, so the same pattern can be stable with respect to one condition and changing with respect to another. Fig 9 :54: "And when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again." Fig 9 :57: "a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either." The two bins are not two kinds of pattern. They are two readings of one pattern under different contact.

### Dependency order (Fig 3)

Fig 3 :20: "The chain is ordered by dependency, not by importance." The order here is D1 (perception and vocabulary) before D2 (learn and carry) before D3 (the habitat that supplies conditions) before D4 (a measurement frame that can compare them). The middle link, "what counts as stability," is the least instrumented link. Fig 3 :27: "A link with no instrument is not a link that works. It is a link nobody has looked at." Most effort in this debate lands above the break, on ever finer description of patterns, while the objective "stable with respect to what" is assumed rather than measured.

## 3. Findings

### F1: Stability is a relation to a habitat, not a property of a pattern (decisive against Option A)

Fig 11 :49: "An actor's valence is relative to the residual, not a property it carries."
Fig 11 :50: "The same actor is a benefactor to one residual and a malfactor to another, so mutualism and parasitism are relations rather than kinds."
Fig 11 :51: "No standing list of good actors survives a change of residual."

A behavior pattern is in the same position as the figure's actor. Its stability is a valence relative to the residual under examination, not a label it carries into every habitat. A standing list of stable behaviors does not survive a change of residual. This is the direct figure answer to Option A.

Fig 11 :39 supplies the positive form: "capability is a property of agent-and-habitat, never of the agent alone." And Fig 11 :40: "An improvement that does not change contact changes nothing, however much it improves." A behavior that persists does so because its contact is maintained, not because it is intrinsically stable.

**Mode:** `general` (structural), triangulated across Fig 11 :49-51 and Fig 4 :19-20 (low overlap: habitat valence versus membrane invariance). Per-domain verdict: D1 and D3. **Refuter:** exhibit a pattern whose stability is invariant across every habitable condition and whose valence does not change with the residual, with the instrument named. If such a pattern is measured, F1 is false for it and Option A is partially restored.

### F2: The kernel is invariant; the cast changes (the stable/changing partition the question should have asked)

Fig 4 :19-20: "The kernel is the part that does not change with size: the same loop and the same rule for naming what worked, whether one agent is running it or a thousand. Only the actors, the currency and the anchor change."

This is the figure line that governs the question most directly. Translated without strain: across any change of scale, period or social condition, what stays stable is the loop and the rule for naming what worked; what changes is the actors, the currency and the anchor. So the honest partition is not between individual behaviors. It is between the kernel (D1's loop and its acceptance rule) and the cast (D2/D3's actors, currencies and anchors).

Fig 13 :3 confirms the inventory form: "No system functions without any of them, none is built from the others, and everything else is a composition across the two kinds." Fig 13 :4-5 adds: "There is no order of rank here. The two kinds are in lockstep, a crossing is a change of role rather than a step upward, and no member of the pair is senior to the other." There is no privileged universal and no privileged variable, only a stable grammar and changing arrangements over it.

**Mode:** `general` on the kernel/cast distinction, `specified` on applying it to human behavior. Triangulated across Fig 4 and Fig 13. **Refuter:** show a society in which the loop or the naming rule does not operate, or show an invariant cast with a changing kernel. Either breaks the partition.

### F3: The set is closed, the arrangements are not

Fig 13 :115: "The set is closed. The arrangements are not. That gap is why import exists."
Fig 12 :117: "isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."
Fig 12 :124: "functional group. A substructure whose presence fixes the outcome. The group says what kind; the qualifier says which member."

Under the figures, the invariant is the set of kinds and the recurring functional groups (state, structure, state; perceive, name; import, settle). The variable is the arrangement, the isomers. A behavior that recurs across periods is typically the same functional group with a different qualifier, not the same molecule. This is why a list of stable behaviors looks universal from a distance and changes under inspection.

**Mode:** `specified`, single-source on the figure definition (Fig 12), with Fig 13 as a second, low-overlap figure for the closed-set half. **Refuter:** exhibit a stable human pattern that is not an instance of a recurring functional group over the held kinds, or show the set of kinds growing by arrangement alone.

### F4: The question is a union masquerading as a partition

Fig 9 :4-5: "First: is there a fact here? Disagreements that do not shrink with effort, where each rule keeps working well on a different subset, are not one hard question but several well-formed ones. Split it rather than search."
Fig 9 :55: "A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones."
Fig 9 :54: "And when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again."

The stable/changing question has exactly the signature Fig 9 names: each candidate rule works well on a different subset. A rule about shared biology works for some patterns and fails for others; a rule about institutions works for a different subset; neither shrinks the disagreement under more effort within its own subset. The correct move is to split, not to keep searching for the master list. Seat Map :211 repeats the law: "And when a split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before searching again."

**Mode:** `specified`, triangulated across Fig 9 and Seat Map :211 (the field guide derives its union law from Fig 9, so overlap is moderate, not zero). **Refuter:** demonstrate a single condition such that every behavior pattern falls into exactly one of the two bins under that condition, with no pattern changing bin under any other condition tested. That would make the partition genuine and this union reading false.

### F5: Recordings do not transmit; methods do

Fig 4 :22: "Only methods cross up. Only head starts come down."
Fig 4 :25: "A recording carried upward looks like knowledge and is a description of one occasion."

A behavior observed in one period and copied in the next is a recording, not a generator. It can survive as an artifact while the method that produced it is lost. This distinguishes "the pattern recurred" from "the pattern was stably transmitted," a distinction the question conflates. It also explains why surface similarity across cultures is weak evidence for a shared mechanism.

The Library of Babel, opened this session, states the search-side consequence: "This means that you can cut your search short if the set of books returned is small and descriptive enough that it provides a general shape of the answer that satisfies your question." We are owed the shape of the invariant, not a census of occasions.

**Mode:** `specified`, triangulated across Fig 4 and the essay (different fields, low overlap). **Refuter:** show a case where a recorded behavioral form, with no access to the generating method, reliably regenerates the behavior under a changed habitat. That would collapse the recording/method distinction for this case.

### F6: The evidence is pooled, so the agreement is not corroboration

Fig 2 :31: "Independence, between two frames, is how little they draw on the same evidence."
Fig 2 :40: "agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool."
Fig 1 :19: "The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated."
Fig 1 :22: "Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average."

The field's own record names the channel defect. The WEIRDest People in the World entry quotes Daniel Dennett: "we must stop assuming that our ways are 'universal.'" It also records a genuine conflict: Pauline Grosjean noted in *Science* that "marriage age has varied over time" and that 19th century migrants to the US and Australia married younger, which she offers as evidence against Henrich's specific marriage-prohibition account. That conflict is on a specific historical binding, not on the figure verdict, so it does not flip anything; it is recorded as a reason not to treat that binding as grounded.

**Mode:** `specified` on the field, `general` on the independence rule. Triangulated across Fig 2 and Fig 1. **Refuter:** produce two genuinely low-overlap instruments (different evidence pools, different measurement axes) that return the same stable/changing partition per slot. That would be one witness or two? Under Fig 2 it is two only if the pools do not overlap.

### F7: No last list, and no proof of absence from a null result

Fig 7 :12-13: "same closure, handed along; agreement compounds, evidence does not"
Fig 7 :36: "A chain of mirrors is one long room. The next room must differ from the last, and match the residual it surfaced."
Fig 8 :39: "So a search that finds nothing is never a proof of absence."
Fig 13 :90: "One chain is one route through a space that has no last route."

Any answer that presents a final list of stable patterns is a terminal room, which the figures forbid. A study that finds no universal is not proof that none exists; it is a report about that instrument at that budget. Underivable in principle and unreachable at this budget are different facts with different instructions. The honest verdict on the empirical list is `gap`, which is a verdict and not a silence.

**Mode:** `general`, triangulated across Fig 7, Fig 8 and Fig 13. **Refuter:** produce a closed, sealed room in which the question can be searched to the end, and complete the search. Fig 9 :44 says no frame is a sealed room, so this refuter is expected to be unmet; that is precisely why `unreachable` must be reported rather than hidden.

### F8: Condition change moves the wall by import, not by inner search

Fig 6 :23: "No frame escapes its own closure. The bound moves only on contact with a frame whose closure differs."
Fig 8 :32: "no search, however long, adds an atom."

A culture's behavioral repertoire is a closure. Searching harder inside it visits more of the same space. What changes when social conditions change is contact: a new actor, institution or instrument arrives from a frame whose closure differs, and that import is the only operator that adds an atom. The Cultural Evolution Society states the field-side version: "We define culture as any information that is passed from one individual to another via social learning, rather than genetically." Transmission is contact, and its pathways are multiple.

**Mode:** `specified`, triangulated across Fig 6 and Fig 8 plus the field source. **Refuter:** exhibit a repertoire that gains a genuinely new behavioral atom with no contact with a differing closure, at a fixed instrument and budget. That would falsify the import law for this case.

### F9: The framework returns a shape, not the list

Fig 9 :32: "you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself"
Fig 9 :27-28: "describe it before you go looking, or any frame you pick will seem to fit"

The deliverable is the shape any answer must have: an invariant kernel, a contact-varying cast, a union unbundled before search, and a per-slot instrument. It is not a table of which behaviors are universal. Any such table is a binding from the world frame onto the shape, and under Fig 8 :50-52 "Convergent derivation and adopted import look identical in the contents. Only the record of where each came from separates them." It therefore needs provenance and a shadow test before it carries weight.

**Mode:** `general` on the limit, triangulated across Fig 9 and the canonical README. **Refuter:** show the framework naming a specific primitive it did not already hold and did not import, with the derivation recorded. Until then, a named universal is the searcher's contribution.

### F10: What is stable is a visible choice, not a discovery of nature's list

The Hard Problem is About Source, opened this session: an instrument that reads green "cannot comment on the source." Cultural universal (Wikipedia) records the non-nativist caution: "The observation of the same or similar behavior in different cultures does not prove that they are the results of a common underlying psychological mechanism." Human nature (Wikipedia) records that the existence of an "invariable and metaphysical human nature is subject of much historical debate."

A cross-cultural regularity is a reading, not a source. Several sources can produce the same behavioral reading, and the reading does not discriminate among them. What is owed is a source-discriminating instrument, not a more confident list.

**Mode:** `specified`, triangulated across the source essay (cognition/instrumentation) and the two encyclopedic sources (field record), with the caveat that all are broad secondary summaries. **Refuter:** show an instrument that discriminates among the sources of a given cross-temporal regularity and returns a single source. That would grounded-name one slot.

## 4. Pre-registered refuter (written before the conclusion)

Per Seat Map :287: "And a falsifier written before the run is a design instrument, not only an honesty device." Written before the findings were concluded:

1. **Habitat refuter.** If a behavior pattern's stability is shown to be intrinsic, invariant under every condition examined and unchanged in valence when the residual changes, F1 is false and Option A is restored for that pattern.
2. **Kernel refuter.** If a society is found in which the loop and the naming rule do not operate, or in which the cast is invariant while the kernel changes, F2 is false.
3. **Partition refuter.** If a single condition is exhibited under which every behavior falls into exactly one bin and no pattern changes bin under any other condition tested, F4's union reading is false.
4. **Independence refuter.** If two instruments with demonstrably non-overlapping evidence pools return the same per-slot partition, the empirical part moves from one witness to two. That is not a refutation of the structure but it does refute this solution's `single-source` label for the empirical list.
5. **Shape refuter.** If the framework is shown naming a primitive it neither held nor imported, F9 is false.

If 1, 2, 3 or 5 fires, the corresponding finding is withdrawn. Until an instrument fires on the empirical list, no part of the answer is `grounded`.

## 6. Where the derivation stops

The figures decide the structure: stability is a contact relation; the invariant is a kernel and a closed set of kinds; the variable is the cast and the arrangements; the question is a union; the framework returns a shape and an instrument, not a list. They do not decide which specific behaviors are stable. That needs a source-discriminating instrument with independence between frames, and none was available at this budget. The answer stops there and records the lineage, which is the honest stop the canonical rule requires.
