# Outreach draft: check the shape before you decide about the list

**Use:** one-to-one, or as a cover letter attached to a submission through the public venues in `03_public_contact_points.csv`. **Not** for bulk send.

**Hard precondition:** do not send until the mode labels and the refuter in `01_figure_grounded_audit.md` are intact. The offer collapses on contact if the first-stage claim cannot survive a twenty-minute check.

---

**Subject:** One checkable claim about "stable" versus "changing" human behavior

> Hi [name],
>
> I have one specific claim I would like you to check against what you already know. I am not asking you to read a theory first, and I am not asking you to agree with me. If the claim does not survive, there is nothing else to look at.
>
> **The claim (`general` on the structure, `specified` on the field reading, not `grounded`):** "stable across historical periods" and "changes when social conditions change" are not two kinds of behavior. They are two valences of the same pattern under different contact. What stays stable is the kernel, the loop and the rule for naming what worked; what changes is the cast, the actors, the currency and the anchor. The figures put it this way: "The kernel is the part that does not change with size: the same loop and the same rule for naming what worked, whether one agent is running it or a thousand. Only the actors, the currency and the anchor change."
>
> A second figure makes the stronger point: "An actor's valence is relative to the residual, not a property it carries." Applied here, a behavior pattern carries no stability of its own. A standing list of stable behaviors does not survive a change of residual. So the question as usually asked is a union masquerading as a partition, and it needs unbundling before any list is attempted.
>
> **What I am not claiming:** that any particular behavior is a universal, that any particular behavior is purely cultural, that this produces the final list, or that a null result proves no universal exists. I am not claiming the framework can name the items. It returns a shape, not a name. Specific bindings from the cultural-evolution literature are bindings, with provenance, not derivations.
>
> **What would refute it, written before this message:**
> 1. A behavior pattern whose stability is intrinsic, invariant under every condition examined, with a valence that does not change when the residual changes.
> 2. A society in which the loop and the naming rule do not operate, or in which the cast is invariant while the kernel changes.
> 3. A single condition under which every behavior falls into exactly one bin, and no pattern changes bin under any other condition tested.
> 4. Two instruments with demonstrably non-overlapping evidence pools returning the same per-slot partition (this would not refute the structure, only my `single-source` label on the empirical part).
>
> If any of the first three holds, I withdraw the corresponding claim. A one-line "this fails because..." is more useful to me than agreement. If you would rather not engage, no reply is needed.
>
> **What I would count as settling the empirical part:** a source-discriminating instrument, not another argument. Same behavior reading from several different sources is a projection with lossy information. What is missing is the instrument that tells the sources apart, plus independence between the frames using it.
>
> **If it survives, and only then:** the sources are below. The figures are the authority, not my summary of them.
> - Canonical figure text with line numbers (local in this project): `FRAMEWORK/reference/03-FIGURES_TEXT.md`
> - Cultural Evolution Society, "What is cultural evolution?": https://culturalevolutionsociety.org/about-cultural-evolution/what-is-cultural-evolution/
> - The Library of Babel: Locating The Book of Books: https://adventuresinml.substack.com/p/the-library-of-babel-locating-the
>
> **If you want to reuse the method:** derive the shape on a problem you know well and can check against a ground. Write down what would refute your result *before* you run it. Report failures, they are more useful than successes.
