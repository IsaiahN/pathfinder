# Expert verification packet: the Tether on stable and changing human behavior

**What this is:** a claim in a form a cultural-evolution researcher or historical psychologist can check. Mode, derivation and refuters are stated before the conclusion, so a reader knows what to look for and what would settle it.

**Mode: `general` on the structure, `specified` on the field reading, not `grounded`.** No claim is made about which specific behavior is universal. This is a shape and a route, not a list.

**Status:** a simulated derivation checked for internal consistency against the figures. It cannot settle the question it reports on.

## 1. The claim, in one paragraph

"Stable across historical periods" and "changes when social conditions change" are not two kinds of behavior. They are two valences of the same pattern under different contact. What stays stable is the kernel, the loop and the rule for naming what worked; what changes is the cast, the actors, the currency and the anchor. A recurring pattern is a functional group with a changing qualifier; change enters a repertoire by import from a differing closure. The question is a union of overlapping conditions and must be unbundled before any list is attempted. What the framework returns is that shape, a set of pre-registered refuters, and the source-discriminating instrument owed for each slot. It does not return the list.

## 2. Three checkable sub-claims

### C1: framework-internal; checkable by reading Figures 11 and 4

- **Statement:** stability is a contact relation, not a property carried by a pattern. A standing list of stable patterns does not survive a change of residual.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 11 :49 "An actor's valence is relative to the residual, not a property it carries." Fig 11 :51 "No standing list of good actors survives a change of residual." Fig 4 :19-20 "The kernel is the part that does not change with size: the same loop and the same rule for naming what worked, whether one agent is running it or a thousand. Only the actors, the currency and the anchor change."
- **Status:** `triangulated`. Fig 11 governs habitat valence; Fig 4 governs scale membrane invariance. The two figures have low overlap and agree.
- **Refuter:** exhibit a behavior pattern whose stability is intrinsic, invariant under every condition examined, with a valence that does not change when the residual changes. Or show a society in which the loop and the naming rule do not operate.

### C2: union versus partition; checkable against the question's own structure

- **Statement:** the stable/changing question is a union of overlapping conditions, not a partition of behaviors into two kinds. Each candidate rule works well on a different subset.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 9 :4-5 "First: is there a fact here? Disagreements that do not shrink with effort, where each rule keeps working well on a different subset, are not one hard question but several well-formed ones. Split it rather than search." Fig 9 :54 "And when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again."
- **Status:** `triangulated`. Fig 9 plus Seat Map :211.
- **Refuter:** exhibit a single condition under which every behavior falls into exactly one bin, with no pattern changing bin under any other condition tested. That makes the partition genuine and this sub-claim false.

### C3: world-facing; checkable against cultural-evolution and historical evidence

- **Statement:** the kernel/cast shape is independently arrived at in historical linguistics, cultural evolution, animal culture and historical sociology, with genuinely different evidence pools. A null result on a universal is not proof of absence, and the empirical list remains `gap`.
- **What to check:** is the chid/chided case (Cultural Evolution Society) a real instance of a stable mechanism with a changing form? Do Whiten's chimpanzee traditions show the same capacity with different local forms? Does Elias document changing conduct without a change in the underlying human capacity? Are the evidence pools of those four fields actually non-overlapping?
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 8 :39 "So a search that finds nothing is never a proof of absence." Fig 2 :31 "Independence, between two frames, is how little they draw on the same evidence." Fig 2 :40 "agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool."
- **Status:** `triangulated` on the figure rules; `single-source` on each individual world example until its own source is read by the checker. The examples are real pages opened in this run, listed in `10_source_ledger.csv`.
- **Refuter:** a low-overlap field showing a recurring behavior with no stable transmitting kernel, or a changing behavior with no variation and selection structure. Or two instruments with demonstrably non-overlapping pools returning a per-slot partition that conflicts with the kernel/cast shape.

## 3. What is explicitly not claimed

- Not a list of universals or a list of variables.
- Not that no behavior is stable. The kernel is stable; the claim is about where stability lives.
- Not that culture is free-floating. Capability is a property of agent and habitat (Fig 11 :39).
- Not a final answer. There is no last room (Fig 7 :6) and no last route (Fig 13 :90).
- Not grounded. No source-discriminating instrument was run.
- Not that any named scholar's list is correct. Such lists are bindings from the world frame and need provenance and a shadow test.

## 5. What would settle it

A source-discriminating instrument, not another argument. Three concrete needs:

1. Does an instrument distinguish shared biology, shared environment and independent invention as sources of the same recurring behavioral reading?
2. Are there two frames with non-overlapping evidence pools that return the same per-slot partition?
3. Does a per-slot residual persist and fail to trend down after the kernel/cast account is applied?

Until these fire, the empirical list is `gap` and the verdict is `candidate`.

## 6. What an expert can check in twenty minutes

1. Read Fig 11 :49-51 and Fig 4 :19-20. Four short passages. Decide whether a behavior's stability is a carried property or a contact valence.
2. Decide whether the question is a union or a partition. Ask whether you know a pattern that is stable on one axis and changing on another. If you do, C2 survives.
3. Check one world example in C3 against your own field knowledge. Is the chid/chided case really stable mechanism with changing form? Are the four fields' evidence pools actually independent?
4. Name one instrument that would discriminate sources for one recurring behavior, or say why none is possible.
5. Send the refutation. A failure is more useful than an endorsement.
