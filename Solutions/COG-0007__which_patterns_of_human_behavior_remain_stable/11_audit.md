I redid the census from the residual line alone, before using the primary's conclusions, in the WATSON order: R by arity, symmetry and scale; one line per domain with actors, currency and anchor; partition or union; order by dependency.

Arity: two operands, pattern and condition, so stability is a relation and the question's grammar is misleading. Symmetry: ordered and comparative, a threshold over time, not a symmetric conjunction; the Operators table gives ⋛ as the comparison bond. Scale: three levels with lossy transforms between, individual, population, historical. This is a union compound (Fig 9: a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either), not one hard question.
- **Domain 1, individual loop.** Actors: one agent, Γ, bets and actions. Currency: per-slot residual, priced in bits and actions. Anchor: the ground. Question type: which compositions cross the two kinds.
- **Domain 2, transmission.** Actors: many agents and a shared library. Currency: methods up, head starts down. Anchor: ground-visible reproduction. Question type: settlement and promotion.
- **Domain 3, historical habitat.** Actors: conditions and relations. Currency: contact, introduction available and exclusion not. Anchor: the ground. Question type: which valence the residual assigns.
- **Domain 4, measurement.** Actors: instruments and researchers. Currency: independence. Anchor: a measurement that does not update on frames. Question type: partition versus union, and per-slot residuals. Independence in this simulated run is effectively zero because all four domains draw on one evidence pool.

Order by dependency: settle the individual loop before the population comparison (Fig 12 :104); unbundle the union before naming a frame (Fig 9 :27-28); keep per-slot residuals unaggregated (Fig 1 :19).

## Round trip gap R_T

The Symbols table defines R_T as the gap between what was sent up and what came back down, `gap(x, (T_E ∘ T_A)(x))`, measuring what the coarser description cannot hold. Fig 4 :30 requires it to be measured rather than assumed. I ran the round trip on the condensation this folder actually uses.

1. **Sent up (x):** the verbatim figure set, chiefly Fig 11 :49-51 (valence relative to residual), Fig 4 :19-20 (kernel versus cast), Fig 9 :4-5 and :54 (union unbundling), Fig 6 :23 (import), Fig 8 :39 (unreachable is not absent).
2. **Abstraction (T_A):** the shorthand carried in `07` and `08`: stability is a contact relation; the invariant is the kernel; the variable is the cast; the question is a union; the deliverable is a shape plus an instrument, not a list.
3. **Concretisation (T_E):** a concrete field claim of the kind the question invites, for example "behavior X is a universal, behavior Y is culturally variable, and here is the two-column table."

**R_T is nonzero.** Three things the shorthand holds do not survive the trip back down:

- The **second operand** of "stable." The shorthand says "relative to contact"; the concrete table silently drops the condition and presents stability as monadic. This is the exact error Fig 9 :4-5 names.
- The **union unbundling**. The shorthand says the question is a union; the concrete table reinstates a two-bin partition. Fig 9 :54 is lost.
- The **source-discrimination requirement**. The shorthand says a source-discriminating instrument is owed at each slot; the concrete table names sources from readings. The Hard Problem is About Source calls this the metamerism error.

Conversely, two things survive cleanly: a kernel/cast distinction holds at every level examined, and change arrives through contact and import rather than inner search. That residue is what should be sent. R_T was **not** measured numerically, because no ground reading exists; it is recorded qualitatively as the enumerated set of dropped constraints. Treating the formula as identical to the figure set would set R_T to zero and would be the dead-reckoning error Fig 4 :30 names. The honest form is a lossy condensation with a measured gap, which is what `09_watson_answer.md` states.
