# Expert verification packet: the Tether on efficiency versus resilience

**What this is:** a claim in a form an operations or supply chain researcher can check. Mode, derivation and refuters are stated before the conclusion, so a reader knows what to look for and what would settle it.

**Mode: `specified`, not `grounded`.** No firm-level measurement is claimed. This is a shape and a decision order, not a result. It is a simulated derivation checked for internal consistency against the figures, not a ground reading.

## 1. The claim, in one paragraph

The question is bundled, not scalar: routine cost and disruption survival are two overlapping conditions with different grounds, so they must be kept in two ledgers. Capability is a property of the firm and its supply habitat, not of the firm's slack policy, and the habitat must be enumerated outward through tiers because the residual that matters can be three steps out. The operational choice is a bond between atoms, not a count of atoms: the same suppliers under `→` versus `∥` are isomers, invisible in a bill of materials and decisive under disruption. A capability that needs a supplier or region the current closure lacks requires an import from a differing closure, with source gate and shadow test. Independence is bought in proportion to the cost of being wrong. A stress test is a substituted habitat and proves wiring, not capability. The specific numeric balance is a `gap`, settled by a disruption or a source-discriminating instrument, never by internal agreement.

## 2. Three checkable sub-claims

### C1. Structural, framework-internal; checkable by reading Figure 9 and Figure 12

- **Statement:** the "balance" is a union compound; two ledgers must be kept apart and no scalar optimum is licensed.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 9 :57 "What remains is smaller and it is also categorically different: a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either." Fig 12 :125 "A composition costs in two currencies, and they do not add." Fig 12 :130 "Summing them into one figure is the error this names, not the fix for it. A price in bits and a price in actions are two readings, held apart."
- **What to check:** is the mapping strain honest? Fig 12's "bits" and "actions" are not literally dollars and recovery time; this packet uses the line only for the keep-apart instruction, not for a unit conversion. That strain is recorded in `01_figure_grounded_audit.md` §6.
- **Refuter:** one scalar index that predicts both routine cost and disruption survival with no remaining per-slot residual, across firms, breaks C1. So does showing that the two readings share one evidence pool entirely.

### C2. Operations-facing; checkable against any real supply network

- **Statement:** the relevant unit is the firm and its habitat, enumerated outward; the arrangement is a bond, so identical supplier lists can behave differently.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 11 :40 "A system cannot be made more capable by improving the agent in isolation. An improvement that does not change contact changes nothing, however much it improves." Fig 11 :52 "And contact cascades. Scoping the habitat to direct contact is how DDT reached the eagles: first-order enumeration, second-order effect, and the residual that mattered was three steps out." Fig 12 :117 "isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."
- **What to check:** take a component you know. Does its disruption outcome depend on tier-2 or tier-3 conditions that a tier-1 map omits? Do two configurations with the same supplier list but different bonds (single-source `→` versus qualified alternate `∥`) actually have the same recovery profile?
- **Refuter:** a documented disruption fully determined by tier-1 suppliers with zero measured tier-2-and-beyond effect breaks the cascade application. Identical atoms and bond records with different behaviour, or different bond records with identical behaviour across every disruption that reaches them, breaks the isomer claim.

### C3. Method, framework-internal derivation

- **Statement:** a new capability that the current supplier set cannot hold needs contact with a differing closure (an import), and a stress test cannot substitute for settlement in the real habitat.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 6 :23 "No frame escapes its own closure. The bound moves only on contact with a frame whose closure differs." Fig 8 :32 "Closure is idempotent. Composing inside a closed set never leaves it, so no search, however long, adds an atom. This one is definitional: it is what closure means." Fig 11 :70 "A test harness is a substituted habitat. Which is why a synthetic solve proves wiring and never capability."
- **What to check:** distinguish adding a second supplier of an already-known type (a bond change, an isomer) from qualifying a source or region never held (a new atom, an import). Does the qualification record say which one happened, and does it explain a named gap?
- **Refuter:** a disruption-surviving reach produced by internal optimisation alone with no new atom and no differing closure breaks C3. So does a synthetic stress test that transfers to full real-habitat capability with no residual.

## 3. What is explicitly not claimed

- Not a universal buffer size, safety-stock level, or supplier count.
- Not that slack is worthless, that stress tests are useless, or that every firm should dual source. Fig 2 :42 "Buy independence in proportion to the cost of being wrong."
- Not that reshoring removes risk. Fig 11 :66 "Isolation is not removal of the habitat. It is substitution of one habitat for another."
- Not that this framework can validate itself, and not `grounded`. No measurement was taken.
- Not that a prestigious endorsement makes the claim true. *The Recursive Self-Improvement Trap*: "Prestige can fund an action. It can give an action a budget to be tried. But it cannot settle an action. Only the ground can settle it."
- Not a claim that adding a supplier of a known type moves the wall. Fig 6 :32 "MINT composes inside the closure and can never add an atom."

## 5. What would settle it

1. A per-slot recovery instrument that distinguishes a robust configuration from one not yet hit. From *The Hard Problem is About Source*: an instrument that reads green *"cannot comment on the source"*, and *"The answer is unreached until a better instrument exists."*
2. A tier-k disruption trace with measured effects beyond tier 1.
3. An isomer test: identical atoms and bonds compared under a representative disruption set, or a case where removing one of two bonded suppliers never matters.
4. An import test: same firm, with and without contact with a differing closure, showing whether an atom was added or a bond changed.
5. Any of the eight refuters in `01` §4 firing.

## 6. What an expert can check in twenty minutes

1. Read Fig 9 :57, Fig 12 :125 and :130, Fig 11 :40 and :52, Fig 6 :23 and Fig 8 :32. Seven short passages.
2. Name one component whose disruption outcome depends on a tier-2 condition. If you cannot, say so; a search that finds nothing is not proof of absence (Fig 8 :39 "So a search that finds nothing is never a proof of absence.").
3. Pick a real pair of configurations with the same supplier list but different bonds. Do you expect the same recovery profile? Why or why not?
4. Name one instrument that would distinguish robust from untested, or say why none is possible.
5. Send the refutation. A failure is more useful than an endorsement.
