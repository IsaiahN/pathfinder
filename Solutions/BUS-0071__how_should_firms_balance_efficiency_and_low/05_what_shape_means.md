## 1. A shape is a constraint you can state before you can name the filler

The figures README gives the limit: the framework returns *"what a missing thing would have to look like (**a shape, not a name**)."* Fig 9 :32: "you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself." Fig 13: *"It can say the answer is out of reach with the library as it stands... It cannot say which primitive is missing, because naming it would mean already holding it."*

From *The Library of Babel*: *"You can cut your search short if the set of books returned is small and descriptive enough that it provides a general shape of the answer that satisfies your question."*

For BUS-0071 the shape is the list of conditions a configuration must satisfy, stated in effect terms: two ledgers kept apart, capability in the firm-and-habitat, the habitat enumerated outward, the bond recorded rather than just the ingredient list, an import from a differing closure when a new capability is needed, independence bought in proportion to the cost of being wrong, the channel kept reachable per slot, and stress tests treated as wiring checks. The forbidden name is a number: "hold N days of inventory" or "use M suppliers" as a universal answer.

## 2. How the shape is obtained, and why the question itself had to be re-described

*Library of Babel* on vocabulary bootstrapping: *"With each new term gained, you narrow the space and deepen your vocabulary."* And on steering: the returned books must be *"tested or ranged against the problem you want answered in order to steer the direction of the next search."*

Fig 6 :30: "The description travels. Fails by being sharpened into terms only we can read. Cast it in effect terms." Fig 9 :30-31: "your words are borrowed too, so describe the effect rather than the cause."

*Decoding Tacit Knowledge*: *"When contact isn't possible, the best you can send is a plain description of what you'd expect to see, because anyone can check that against the world."*

The original question, "how should firms balance efficiency and low cost against spare capacity and resilience", is a bundle. *Library of Babel* names the failure directly: *"Perhaps your problem was a bundle or conflation of several problems masquerading as one? You will need to search all of those problems individually to find their answers and then combine them in a satisfactory way."* This is the essay-level counterpart of Fig 9 :55-57. Re-describing the residual in effect terms (per-slot gap between planned and realized recovery) was a precondition of choosing any figure line, not a stylistic rewrite.

## 3. What would license a name, and the metamerism trap for this question

Only two things, both outside the shape itself:

1. **The ground settles it.** Formula step 5: *"the ground settles it. Verified only by what pays."* Here that means a real disruption or a source-discriminating instrument, not a planning meeting.
2. **A provenanced import from a frame whose closure differs** (Fig 6 :23, Fig 8 :50-52), with a source gate and a shadow test.

*The Hard Problem is About Source* gives the mechanism. Metamerism: a color instrument reads green and *"it cannot comment on the source."* *"If several paths create the same behavior, then behavior is a projection with lossy information (it cannot define consciousness in full)."* And: *"The answer is unreached until a better instrument exists."* *"An instrument that can't distinguish these cases demonstrates its own inadequacy, it cannot claim anything substantial about what is being measured or what causes it."*

For a firm the analogue is exact in structure: a high aggregate service level is a reading, not a source. It is consistent with a genuinely robust network and with a single-source network that has not yet been hit. Naming the source ("resilient") from the reading is the metamerism error. What is owed is a discriminator: a per-slot recovery signal, a tier-k deviation, a near miss that was allowed to register.

**Correction to my own first reading.** I initially treated "shape, not a name" as if it forbade naming any operational choice. That overstates it. The README's phrase governs a **missing** thing, one not held in Γ. Buffers, dual sourcing and regional alternatives are ordinary bindings from the operations frame onto terms already in the library; naming them is not inventing a seventh primitive. What is forbidden is (a) presenting such a binding as figure-derived, (b) claiming it moves the wall when it only changes a bond (Fig 6 :32, "MINT composes inside the closure and can never add an atom"), and (c) claiming the framework sets the quantity. The `gap` is the ground-settled number, not the existence of a buffer.

## 4. Imports here change an axis, not just a supplier count

*They're Scanning Books*: *"The real import is not a new term in the existing space. It is a new dimension of the space. Importing a term extends what can be said; importing an axis extends what can be perceived at all."* Also: *"A minimum viable set of six terms is a human's careful guess at reality's compression axes; another sensorium's minimum set need not be those six."* And: *"Declaring the primitives does not make them neutral. It makes the choice visible."*

A second supplier of the same type in the same region is a new bond, hence an isomer (Fig 12 :117: "Same atoms, different bond, different substance"). A qualified source in a different region, on a different technology, or a different logistics route is closer to a new axis: it lets the firm perceive and survive a failure its old closure could not represent. The distinction matters for cost accounting: a bill of materials records the first kind invisibly and cannot record the second at all.

## 5. Why this derivation cannot validate itself

*The Recursive Self-Improvement Trap*: *"The catch is that the output is only as good as the validator. The agent, or the tool, or the frame, or the theory, cannot validate itself."* And: *"Metatheories cannot validate themselves."* On simulated output: *"LLMs can quickly simulate an approximate answer/output with the formula found in the architecture post."* However, *"the truly testable output would need to be derived from the real architecture or working the formula yourself."*

So `01_figure_grounded_audit.md`, `07_the_tether_answer.md` and this file are simulated derivations checked for internal consistency against the figures. They are **not** a measurement of any firm, and they cannot settle the question they report on. Mode remains `specified`, never `grounded`.

## 6. Prestige is not action, and independence is not agreement

From the same essay: *"Prestige must never be allowed to validate an action directly."* *"Prestige can fund an action. It can give an action a budget to be tried. But it cannot settle an action. Only the ground can settle it. The Ground Maintainer must be blind to prestige."*

For outreach this means a well-known operations scholar's endorsement gives the shape a budget to be tried. It does not make the shape true. Fig 2 :42: "Buy independence in proportion to the cost of being wrong." Fig 2 :40: "agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool." A list of recipients chosen because they are likely to agree manufactures the disqualifying correlation; it does not test the claim.

## 7. One-paragraph reading

For this question, a **shape** is the set of structural conditions that any acceptable answer must satisfy, statable before anyone can name the right number of days of buffer or the right count of suppliers. It is obtained by re-describing the question as a per-slot residual, splitting its bundled conditions, and ranging candidate figure lines against that residual until only the ones that fit without strain remain. It is not filled by the framework: a specific quantity is licensed only by a real disruption, a source-discriminating instrument, or a provenanced import from a differing closure. Because this document is a simulated derivation, the shape is `specified`, ungrounded, and offered with falsifiers written in advance. The number stays a `gap`, which is a verdict, not a silence.
