# Outreach draft: check one operational claim before you decide whether the framework is worth reading

**Use:** one-to-one, or as a cover letter attached to a submission through the public venues in `03_public_contact_points.csv`. **Not** for bulk send.

**Hard precondition:** do not send until the two strains in `01_figure_grounded_audit.md` §6 are understood and the mode labels are intact. The claim must survive on its own before any theory is offered.

---

**Subject:** One claim about lean supply chains that you can refute in five minutes

> Hi [name],
>
> I have one specific claim I would like you to check against what you already know. I am not asking you to read any framework first, and I am not asking you to agree. If the claim does not survive, there is nothing else to look at.
>
> **The claim (`specified`, not `grounded`):** "balance efficiency against resilience" is not one dial with two ends. It is two overlapping conditions with different grounds. Routine cost is settled by a cost ledger; disruption survival is settled by realized recovery. A single lever, for example adding buffer everywhere, clears the region where only one condition binds and leaves the overlap standing. So the useful unit of analysis is not the firm's inventory policy. It is the firm plus its supply habitat, enumerated outward through tiers, with particular bonds between particular suppliers that a bill of materials cannot show. Two firms can hold the same suppliers under different bonds and behave completely differently when a tier-two source fails.
>
> Three things follow that are easy to check:
> 1. Adding slack inside the firm without changing its contact with the disruption ground changes the firm, not its resilience to that ground.
> 2. Optimising harder inside the current supplier set cannot produce a capability that needs a supplier or region the set does not contain. That takes contact with a different closure, with a provenance record and a check that it explains a real gap.
> 3. A stress test is a substituted habitat. It proves the wiring responds. It does not prove capability in the real habitat.
>
> **What I am not claiming:** that slack is worthless, that stress tests are useless, that every firm should dual source, or that there is a universal number of days of inventory. Independence should be bought in proportion to the cost of being wrong, which for many components means buying none. The framework returns a shape, not a name, and no figure line sets a numeric buffer. The number stays open until a ground settles it.
>
> **What would refute this, written down before I sent it:** (1) one scalar index that predicts routine cost and disruption survival with no per-slot residual left; (2) changing the firm's habitat while holding its internal policy fixed leaves disruption outcome unchanged; (3) a documented disruption with zero tier-two-and-beyond effect; (4) a disruption-surviving reach produced by internal optimisation alone with no new source or region contacted; (5) a synthetic stress test that transfers to full real-habitat capability with no residual. If any of these holds, I withdraw the corresponding part. A one-line "this fails because..." is more useful to me than agreement. If you would rather not engage, no reply is needed.
>
> **If it survives, and only then:** here are the sources. The figures are the authority, not my summary of them.
> - Verbatim figure text with line numbers (local extract): `FRAMEWORK/reference/03-FIGURES_TEXT.md`
> - Canonical usage rule (local): `FRAMEWORK/reference/FIGURES_README_canonical.md`
> - Figures and formula (public repository): https://github.com/IsaiahN/tether/tree/general-agent/figures
>
> **If you want to reuse the machinery:** the formula is in the figures link. Run it on a problem you know well and can check against a ground. Write down what would refute your result *before* you run it, and state the mode of what you get. A reported failure is more useful to me than a reported success.
