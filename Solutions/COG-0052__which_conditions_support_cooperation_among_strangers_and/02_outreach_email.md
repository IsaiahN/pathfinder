**Subject:** One checkable claim about cooperation among strangers, and what would refute it

> Hi [name],
>
> I have one specific output I would like you to check against work you already know. I am not asking you to read the framework first, and I am not asking you to agree with anything. If the output does not survive, there is nothing else to look at.
>
> **The output (`specified`, not `grounded`):** asking "which conditions support cooperation among strangers" is asking for a list, and a list is the wrong object. Cooperation is a property of agents in a habitat, not of agents in isolation. So the conditions are the habitat of a specifically described cooperation problem: what acts on it, what conditions it sits in, what relations carry influence, enumerated outward until the cascade stops mattering. The same condition changes valence with the problem. A sanction, a reputation system, or a dense network can support cooperation in one setting and suppress it in another. It follows that no standing list of cooperation-supporting conditions transfers unchanged across societies, and that cross-society variation is the measurement to be made, not noise to be averaged away.
>
> This also means the second half of the question, "how do those conditions vary across societies", is not an appendix. It is the same enumeration re-run per slot, because the residual changed. A pooled cross-society average keeps the universal part and erases the varying part, which is the part the question asks about.
>
> **What I am not claiming:** that I can name the condition that explains cooperation in any particular society. The framework returns a shape, not a name. The candidate mechanisms (repetition, reputation, network clustering, group structure, punishment, user-made rules) come from published experiments and field studies; they are imports with provenance, not things this derivation produced, and they carry no weight here until checked. I am also not claiming that agreement among researchers is evidence for a causal claim. Shared norms are a convention, where agreement is the product. Shared evidence in a causal test is a defect.
>
> **What would refute the shape claim:**
> 1. one condition shown to raise cooperation across every cooperation problem and every society, with no sign reversal;
> 2. cooperation shown to be fully determined by individual disposition with the habitat held constant;
> 3. a global cross-society average shown to identify the right intervention in every society;
> 4. two teams drawing on the entire same evidence pool shown to give each other genuine correction on a world fact.
>
> I wrote these down before sending this. If any one holds, I will retract the claim. A one-line "this fails because..." is more useful to me than agreement. If you would rather not engage, no reply is needed.
>
> **If it survives, and only then:** the sources are below. The figures are the authority, not my summary of them.
> - Canonical figures and formula for direct download (branch `general-agent`): https://github.com/IsaiahN/tether/tree/general-agent/figures

>
> **If you want to use the machinery:** the formula is in the figures link. Run it on a cooperation problem you know well and can check against a ground. Write down what would refute your result *before* you run it, and state the mode of what you get. A reported failure is more useful to me than a reported success.
