# Expert verification packet — the Tether on cooperation among strangers

**What this is:** a claim in a form an experimental social scientist can check. Mode, derivation and refuters are stated before the conclusion, so a reader knows what to look for and what would settle it.

**Mode: `specified` on the shape and the cross-society prediction, `general` on the structural claims, not `grounded`.** No measurement in this packet claims to name the condition operating in any society.

## 1. The claim, in one paragraph

Asking which conditions support cooperation among strangers is asking for the wrong object. Cooperation is a property of agents in a habitat, so the conditions are the habitat of a specifically described cooperation residual, enumerated rather than composed. An actor's valence is relative to that residual, so no standing list of cooperation-supporting conditions transfers unchanged across societies, and cross-society variation is the measurement to be made rather than noise to average away. The conditions also form a union of overlapping compounds, so a single lever clears only the regions where one condition holds and leaves the intersections standing. The framework returns this shape, not the name of the condition in any society. Candidate mechanisms from published experiments enter as provenanced imports, with four refuters written in advance.

## 2. Three checkable sub-claims

### C1 — framework-internal; checkable by reading Figure 11 and Figure 1
- **Statement:** conditions are the habitat of a described residual; their valence is relative to it; residuals are per slot and never aggregated.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 11 :39 **"capability is a property of agent-and-habitat, never of the agent alone."** Fig 11 :43 **"The habitat is enumerated, never composed."** Fig 11 :49 **"An actor's valence is relative to the residual, not a property it carries."** Fig 11 :51 **"No standing list of good actors survives a change of residual."** Fig 1 :19 **"The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated."** Fig 1 :22 **"Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average."**
- **What to check:** are these lines present verbatim, and does the shape claim follow from them without importing an empirical premise?
- **Refuter:** exhibit one condition that raises cooperation across every enumerated cooperation residual and every society; or show cooperation fully determined by individual disposition with the habitat fixed; or show a global average that locates the right intervention in every slot.

### C2 — field-facing; checkable by an experimental social scientist against the cooperation literature
- **Statement:** the candidate conditions overlap as a union, and their magnitude and sign vary across societies; the world literature already reports both the universal part and the varying part in the same datasets.
- **Source, verbatim figure line:** Fig 9 :55 **"A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones."** Fig 9 :56 **"A single lever clears the region where only one condition holds and leaves the intersections standing, because the second condition still binds."**
- **World check:** Nowak 2006 (Science, author manuscript in PMC) derives five mechanism rules and then writes *"Punishment can enhance the level of cooperation that is achieved in such models."* Henrich et al. 2006 (Science, abstract) report from 15 populations that *"the magnitude of this punishment varies substantially across populations"* and that it *"positively covaries with altruistic behavior across populations."* Fehr and Gächter 2002 (Nature) report *"cooperation flourishes if altruistic punishment is possible, and breaks down if it is ruled out."*
- **Refuter:** show the mechanisms partition the residual (each cooperation failure in exactly one condition), or show the cross-population magnitude is constant once measurement error is removed.

### C3 — framework-internal derivation with a field-facing check
- **Statement:** the condition set is a molecule, not a list, so two societies with the same condition atoms in different bonds are different substances; where they do not share one atom set, the weaker functional-group claim holds.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 12 :5 **"A set of ingredients is not one. The bond is the rest of the information, and it is the half nothing was recording."** Fig 12 :117 **"isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."** Fig 12 :123 **"functional group. A substructure whose presence fixes the outcome. The group says what kind; the qualifier says which member."**
- **What to check:** do two societies in your data share the condition atoms while differing in arrangement? If yes, does the outcome differ in the same residual? If no, does the group and qualifier reading fit?
- **Refuter:** show that two societies with identical condition atoms in different arrangements return the same cooperation outcome in the same residual, and that no group/qualifier distinction is needed.

## 3. What is explicitly not claimed

- Not a universal list of conditions for stranger cooperation.
- Not an averaged cross-society effect used to pick an intervention.
- Not a derivation of Nowak's rules, Ostrom's principles or the punishment result from the six-term substrate. They are provenanced imports with their own ground support in their own fields, and this derivation adds no atom (Fig 6 :23).
- Not a claim that agreement among researchers corroborates a causal verdict. Fig 2 :40 **"disqualifying for a verdict: agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool."**
- Not a claim that shared norms are worthless. Fig 2 :41 gives the opposite valence for conventions: *"agreement about a shared meaning is the product, not a corruption of it."*
- Not `grounded`. Nothing here is asserted as settled by a measurement in this folder.
- Not a claim that the framework names which condition operates in any society. It returns a shape.

## 5. What would settle it

A per-slot, source-discriminating measurement, not a further argument. From *The Hard Problem is About Source*, a reading *"cannot comment on the source"*; the spectrometer resolved the analogous case. Here:

1. For each society and cooperation problem, enumerate the habitat and measure the outcome in that slot, with the predicted direction registered before data collection.
2. Test Nowak's per-site thresholds (`w`, `q`, `k`, `m`) against the per-slot outcome rather than a pooled mean.
3. Replicate across independent teams with different evidence pools; report the universal part and the magnitude variation together, as Henrich et al. did.
4. Run any of the four refuters in `01` §4. A fired refuter ends the shape claim.

## 6. What an expert can check in twenty minutes

1. Read Fig 11 :39, :43, :49, :51 and Fig 1 :19, :22 (six short passages).
2. Check C2 against your own knowledge: do the three cited world sources actually say what this packet attributes to them?
3. Name one per-slot instrument that would discriminate reputation from sanction as the live condition in a given society, or say why none is possible.
4. Send the refutation. A failure is more useful than an endorsement.
