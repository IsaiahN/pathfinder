# The Tether's answer: conditions for cooperation among strangers

**Mode: `specified` on the shape and the cross-society prediction, `general` on the structural claims, not `grounded`.** This is a shape and a route, not a result. Simulated derivation checked against the figures (`05_what_shape_means.md` §6), not a ground reading.

## 1. The residual it answers

A no-mechanism frame predicts that a stranger with no kinship, no repeated encounter and no enforcer will not be helped. Observation returns cooperation with genetically unrelated strangers, in large groups, with people never met again, sometimes with little reputational gain (Fehr and Gächter, 2002); and the magnitude of the relevant behaviour varies substantially across populations (Henrich et al., 2006). R is the gap between that prediction and that observation, plus the gap between a universal list and its observed variation. It is a **union compound**, not one hard problem: Fig 9 :57 **"a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either. It stops being a mixture and becomes a specific compound."**

## 2. The shape

1. **The conditions are the habitat of a described cooperation residual.** Cooperation is not a property of the lone individual. Fig 11 :39 **"capability is a property of agent-and-habitat, never of the agent alone."** Fig 11 :43 **"The habitat is enumerated, never composed."** So the answer to "which conditions" is an enumeration: actors, conditions, relations, outward until the cascade stops mattering (Fig 11 :44-48).
2. **No condition carries its valence as a property.** Fig 11 :49 **"An actor's valence is relative to the residual, not a property it carries."** Fig 11 :51 **"No standing list of good actors survives a change of residual."** A sanction or a reputation system can support cooperation in one setting and suppress it in another. This is the structural answer to "how do those conditions vary across societies": the residual changed, so the enumeration must be re-run per society. Fig 4 :34-35 supplies the second route: **"A seed carries no small tree: it carries what will grow one, and the conditions it lands in decide what it becomes."**
3. **The conditions form a union of overlapping compounds.** Fig 9 :55 **"A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones."** Fig 9 :56 **"A single lever clears the region where only one condition holds and leaves the intersections standing, because the second condition still binds."** Nowak's five rules and Ostrom's conjoint design principles corroborate the overlap from different fields. The intersections are the actual cases in stranger cooperation.
4. **Cross-society results are per slot, never averaged.** Fig 1 :19 **"The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated."** Fig 1 :22 **"Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average."** Henrich et al. show the universal part and the varying part in the same dataset. A pooled mean would keep the first and erase the second, which is the part the question asks about.
5. **Shared meaning and shared evidence have opposite valences.** Fig 2 :41 **"efficient for a convention: agreement about a shared meaning is the product, not a corruption of it"**; Fig 2 :40 **"disqualifying for a verdict: agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool."** A shared norm is the product of cooperation; a shared evidence pool in a causal test is a defect. Fig 2 :35 **"Alignment is triangulation, not negotiation. A mirror cannot correct you."**
6. **The structure is a molecule, not a list.** Fig 12 :5 **"A set of ingredients is not one. The bond is the rest of the information, and it is the half nothing was recording."** Reputation before helping is a sequence, sanction after defection is a production following a subtraction, a surviving norm is a catalyst, and two societies with the same atoms in different bonds are isomers (Fig 12 :117). The full derivation is in `06_isomers_and_order.md`.
7. **The name is owed to the ground.** Fig 9 :32 **"you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself."** The framework returns a shape. Candidate mechanisms are provenanced imports, not derivations (Fig 6 :23). Which candidate fills which slot in which society is a measurement question.

## 3. What it explicitly does not say

- Not a universal condition list. No mechanism is asserted to raise cooperation in every society.
- Not an averaged cross-society score. No pooled effect is allowed to choose an intervention.
- Not a derivation of Nowak's rules, Ostrom's principles, or the punishment result from the six-term substrate. They are world candidates with their own evidence and their own provenance debt.
- Not a claim that agreement among researchers is corroboration for a causal verdict (Fig 2 :40).
- Not a claim that the framework's shapes are new empirical findings. They are constraints on what any answer must look like.
- Not `grounded`. No measurement in this folder settles any society's slot.

## 4. What would settle it — the ground

A per-slot, source-discriminating measurement, not a further argument. From *The Hard Problem is About Source*: an instrument that reads green *"cannot comment on the source"*; the spectrometer resolved it. A pooled cooperation mean is the same metamerism: it cannot say whether reputation, sanction, network closure or a boundary rule produced it. The settling instruments here are:

- **Per-slot enumeration plus outcome.** For each society and cooperation problem, list what acts on the residual and measure the outcome in that slot. Register the list and the predicted direction before collecting data (OSF; APS Registered Replication Reports).
- **Threshold checks.** Nowak's rules give checkable quantities: probability of another encounter `w`, probability of knowing reputation `q`, average neighbours `k`, group count `m`. Measure them per site and test the predicted threshold rather than the pooled mean.
- **Cross-society covariance.** Henrich et al.'s design, all populations and magnitude variation reported together, is the model. Replicate with independent teams drawing on different evidence pools (Fig 2 :31).
- **Standing falsifiers** from `01` §4: a condition that raises cooperation everywhere; individual disposition sufficient with habitat fixed; a global average that locates every intervention; two frames with rho approximately 1 correcting each other; two same-atom different-bond societies with identical outcomes.

## 5. The honest stop

**Reached in shape, unreached in name.** The enumeration rule, the relative-valence rule, the union rule and the per-slot rule are figure-derived and internally consistent. The empirical naming is not. Fig 13 :138: **"you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself"** and *"narrowing gets you close while the ground decides."* The six-term substrate is *"a human's careful guess at reality's compression axes"*; declaring it makes the choice visible, not neutral. The answer stops where the available instruments stop discriminating among sources, and the lineage is recorded so the stop is checkable. *"Indiscernibility is not identity."*

## 6. One-paragraph version for outreach

> The Tether does not give a universal list of conditions for cooperation among strangers. It says that cooperation is a property of agents in a habitat, so the conditions are that habitat, enumerated for a specifically described cooperation problem: what acts on it, what it sits in, what relations carry influence. The same condition changes valence with the problem, so no list transfers unchanged across societies; cross-society variation is the measurement to be made, not noise to be averaged away. The conditions also overlap as a union, so a single lever clears only the single-condition cases, and their arrangement carries information their ingredient list does not. Reputation, repetition, network clustering, sanctions and user-made rules enter as provenanced candidates from published experiments, not as things the framework derived. The framework returns a shape, not a name. What it offers is the shape any answer must have, four refuters written in advance, and the per-slot instruments that would close it. The slot stays open until the ground settles it.
