## DOMAIN CENSUS

R by arity, symmetry and scale, then one line per domain with actors / currency / anchor, partition or union, and order by dependency.

- **R (the residual).** The no-mechanism prediction says a stranger with no kinship, no repeated encounter and no enforcer will not be helped. Observation returns cooperation with genetically unrelated strangers, in large groups, with people never met again, sometimes with little reputational gain (Fehr and Gächter, 2002), and substantial magnitude variation across populations (Henrich et al., 2006). Arity: multiple overlapping conditions, so a union compound, not one hard question. Symmetry: conditions are not interchangeable; reputation must be known before helping, a norm must be breached before sanction, a boundary must be drawn before exclusion, so ordered bonds rather than a symmetric pile. Scale: encounter, group, society, cross-society comparison, with different actors, currency and anchor at each (Fig 10 :68). Slot indexing: the specific cooperation problem in the specific place, never an aggregate (Fig 1 :19).
- **Domain 1, the encounter.** Actors: donor, recipient, observer, stranger. Currency: cost c, benefit b, reputation, payoff. Anchor: the observed cooperation rate in a one-shot or repeated interaction. Question type: union of mechanisms, not partition.
- **Domain 2, the local habitat and institution.** Actors: resource users, monitors, sanctioners, norm carriers; conditions such as repeat probability w, reputation knowledge q, network degree k, group count m, boundary and rule enforcement. Currency: decisions, rule enforcement, conflict resolution, information about reputation. Anchor: the field outcome, whether the commons or group persists, measured. Question type: habitat enumerated, never composed (Fig 11 :43).
- **Domain 3, cross-society comparison.** Actors: populations, field sites, experimental labs. Currency: measured magnitude of punishment, altruism and cooperation. Anchor: the cross-population measurement itself. Question type: per-slot residuals, explicitly not averaged (Fig 1 :19, :22).
- **Domain 4, the verification frame.** Actors: experimenters, replicators, preregistration venues, outside readers. Currency: agreement and residual, replication outcome. Anchor: the ground, never agreement (Fig 2 :35). In this simulated run all seats draw on one evidence pool, so its independence is zero.
- **Order by dependency.** Perception of who counts as a stranger and what counts as cooperation, then vocabulary for what conditions afford, then the objective of what counts as progress in that society, then planning an intervention, then carrying a method to the next society (Fig 3 :4-15). Fig 3 :20 "The chain is ordered by dependency, not by importance." The second to third link has no agreed cross-society instrument; that is an operational gap, not a figure conflict.

Strain test applied: reading Fig 2 :40 as a ban on norm agreement was re-censused using Fig 2 :41, which gives the opposite valence for conventions. Different objects, no strain after re-census (see `01_figure_grounded_audit.md` §6).

## VERDICT (from the figures)

The shape survives; a universal list does not. Conditions are the habitat of a specifically described cooperation residual, enumerated, with valence relative to that residual. They form a union of overlapping compounds whose arrangement carries information their ingredient list does not, and cross-society residuals are indexed per slot and never averaged. The variation across societies follows from relative valence and from the conditions the seed lands in, and is the measurement to be made. Candidate mechanisms from Nowak, Fehr and Gächter, Henrich et al. and Ostrom are provenanced imports with their own ground support in their own fields; which candidate fills which slot in which society is not settled here. This is a `specified`, ungrounded, candidate result. The strongest single figure point is Fig 11 :51: no standing list of good actors survives a change of residual.

- **cost if wrong:** bounded and locatable. The packet is mode labelled, states four refuters before the conclusion and records provenance, so an error is findable rather than invisible. The real cost is spending the independence of outside readers by sending a universal list instead of a generator, and erasing the varying signal by pooling. Fig 2 :42 says "Buy independence in proportion to the cost of being wrong." Both costs are avoidable by leading with the shape and the refuter and reporting per slot.
- **undoable:** yes. The candidate list is removable without touching the derivation; `07_the_tether_answer.md` and `08_expert_verification_packet.md` already remove it. A sent shape claim can be superseded by a corrected one, and no primitive has to be withdrawn.
- **reversed by:** a ground reading, not agreement. A per-slot instrument that discriminates reputation from sanction as the live condition would name a slot; a condition shown to raise cooperation across every residual and every society, individual disposition sufficient with the habitat fixed, a global average that locates every intervention, two shared-pool frames giving each other genuine correction, or two same-atom different-bond societies with identical outcomes would each break the shape claim. Until one of these fires, the verdict stands as `candidate`.
