## The answer in one line

Cooperation among strangers is a property of agents in a habitat, not of agents in isolation, so the conditions that support it must be enumerated against a specifically described cooperation residual and their valence is relative to that residual; consequently no standing list of conditions transfers unchanged across societies, and cross-society variation is the measurement to be made rather than noise to be averaged away (Fig 11 :39, :49, :51; Fig 1 :19).
