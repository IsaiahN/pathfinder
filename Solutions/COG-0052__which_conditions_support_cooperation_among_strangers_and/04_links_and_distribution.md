## Two things in the literature that bear directly on the claim

1. **The cross-society result is both universal and varying in the same dataset.** Henrich et al. (2006) report that all 15 populations show some costly punishment, that its magnitude varies substantially, and that it covaries with altruistic behaviour. That is exactly the shape claim: a universal-looking mechanism whose valence and magnitude are relative to the local habitat. It is **not** a single pooled effect, and it must not be presented as one.
2. **The mechanism list is a union, not five independent levers.** Nowak (2006) derives one simple rule per mechanism (`r > c/b`, `w > c/b`, `q > c/b`, `b/c > k`, `b/c > 1 + n/m`) and then notes that punishment *"can enhance the level of cooperation that is achieved in such models"* rather than being a sixth independent mechanism. This corroborates the Fig 9 union reading. It does not supply a name for any society's slot.

## Distribution: offer, do not push

Figure-grounded reasons:

- **Fig 10 :52** — **"Bring a characterised residual, never the source."** Sending the whole architecture before anyone has described a cooperation failure sends a recording, not a generator. Lead with the shape claim and the four refuters; offer the links after.
- **Fig 4 :22** — **"Only methods cross up. Only head starts come down."** The enumeration procedure is a method and may be given away. A list of named conditions is a recording and is withheld until it pays against a ground.
- **Fig 9 :27-28** — **"describe it before you go looking, or any frame you pick will seem to fit"** Ask what they would expect to see if the shape claim were true before showing any framework material.
- **Fig 2 :40 and :41** — agreement about shared meaning is efficient for a convention but disqualifying for a verdict. Do not select recipients for likely agreement and do not pool later agreement as corroboration.
- **Fig 7 :35** — *"A room's agreement is variation, not a verdict. Selection between rooms is where the ground votes."* Seek low-overlap rooms (experimental economists, cross-cultural fieldworkers, replication specialists), not a single agreeable community.

**Recommended order in a reply:** (1) the shape claim and its four refuters; (2) ask what they would count as a failure and which per-slot measurement could settle it; (3) only then the three links, with the note that the figures, not the summary, are the authority.

## The two-stage offer

1. **Stage 1, before any theory:** send the residual in effect terms, `SHAPE_COOP`, and the pre-registered refuter. Record the recipient's verdict on this stage first.
2. **Stage 2, opt-in only:** if they are curious, offer the figures, the formula and the article. Their later agreement is never added to the evidence for stage 1 (Fig 2 :40).
3. **Stage 3:** offer the formula as a method to reuse. Each user writes their own refuter and states their own mode before running it, because a frame cannot check the direction of its own question (`THE_FORMULA.md`, "Direction").

**Hard precondition:** stages 1 to 3 only work if the stage-1 output survives. The refuters in `01_figure_grounded_audit.md` §4 are written down in advance precisely so that this can be checked quickly. A failure is more useful than an endorsement.
