## 1. A shape is a constraint, not a filler

The canonical README states the limit: the framework returns *"what a missing thing would have to look like (a shape, not a name)."* Fig 9 :32: **"you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself."** Fig 13 :134-135: *"It can say the answer is out of reach with the library as it stands... It cannot say which primitive is missing, because naming it would mean already holding it."*

From *The Library of Babel*: *"You can cut your search short if the set of books returned is small and descriptive enough that it provides a general shape of the answer that satisfies your question."*

For COG-0052 the distinction is operational. A shape answer says: the conditions are the habitat of a described cooperation residual, enumerated, with relative valence, forming a union, indexed per society, and the variation is a measurement rather than a nuisance. A name answer would say: reputation and punishment are what make strangers cooperate, everywhere. The first constrains and can be refused (§4 of `01_figure_grounded_audit.md`); the second fills the slot and cannot be refused without a new measurement.

## 2. How a shape is obtained

- *Library of Babel*: use the vocabulary you already have that is *closest* to the thing, look for recurring new terms that overlap or correlate with it, recurse. *"With each new term gained, you narrow the space and deepen your vocabulary."*
- Fig 6 :30: **"The description travels. Fails by being sharpened into terms only we can read. Cast it in effect terms."** Fig 9 :30-31: **"your words are borrowed too, so describe the effect rather than the cause."**
- *Decoding Tacit Knowledge*: *"When contact isn't possible, the best you can send is a plain description of what you'd expect to see, because anyone can check that against the world."*
- Fig 9 :26: **"R's own predicates name the habitat that holds φ*"** — this is the sentence that licenses starting from the cooperation residual and reading the habitat off it, rather than starting from a favourite institution.

Applied here: describe the failure (a stranger is not helped when the no-mechanism prediction says they will not be), then enumerate what contacts it (repeat encounters, observers, network position, group boundaries, sanctions, norms), then ask which of those is live in this slot. Fig 8 :53: **"Residual first, frame second."**

## 3. What licenses a name

Only two things, both outside the shape itself:

1. **The ground settles it.** Formula step 5: *"the ground settles it. Verified only by what pays."* The README: the framework *"stops at the limit of what the available instruments can distinguish, and records the lineage so the stop is honest."*
2. **A provenanced import from a frame whose closure differs** (Fig 6 :23, Fig 8 :50-52), with a source gate and a shadow test.

*They're Scanning Books* sharpens this: *"The real import is not a new term in the existing space. It is a new dimension of the space."* And: *"A minimum viable set of six terms is a human's careful guess at reality's compression axes; another sensorium's minimum set need not be those six."* Also: *"Declaring the primitives does not make them neutral. It makes the choice visible."*

For this question, the candidate mechanisms are exactly such imports: Nowak's five rules from evolutionary game theory, Fehr and Gächter's punishment result from experimental economics, Ostrom's design principles from institutional field studies, Henrich et al.'s 15-population measurement from cross-cultural experimental economics. Naming any of them as *the* condition for a given society is licensed only by a measurement in that society, or by a provenanced import with a shadow test. This derivation has the imports and the provenance; it does not have the per-slot settlement.

## 4. Why an averaged cross-society answer is the specific forbidden error

*The Hard Problem is About Source* gives the mechanism. Metamerism: an instrument reads green and *"cannot comment on the source"* — one green light, or yellow plus blue, or RGB, or CMYK, or a scheme not yet discovered. The reading does not determine the source. *"If several paths create the same behavior, then behavior is a projection with lossy information."* And: *"An instrument that can't distinguish these cases demonstrates its own inadequacy."* It was the **spectrometer**, not an argument, that resolved it.

A pooled cooperation rate is the same metamerism error. Two societies can return the same mean cooperation while one is sustained by reputation and the other by sanction, or while one has a high mean with wide variance and the other a moderate mean with almost none. The reading does not determine the source, and it cannot comment on which condition is live. What is owed is a per-slot instrument that discriminates sources, not a better average.

*The Recursive Self-Improvement Trap*: *"the output is only as good as the validator. The agent, or the tool, or the frame, or the theory, cannot validate itself."*

## 5. Q: does "shape, not a name" forbid naming reputation or punishment? ANSWERED

**No, with a condition.** The README's phrase governs a **missing** primitive, one not held in Γ. Reputation, sanction, network and boundary are not primitives of the six-term substrate; they are compositions (a reputation is a spectrum over a network, a sanction is a comparison plus a production, a boundary is a subtraction over space). So naming them is not inventing a seventh member. What is forbidden is claiming that naming one settles which condition operates in a given society, or claiming the name was derived here rather than imported.

- The shape survives: enumerate the habitat per residual, keep valence relative, keep the union unbundled, keep slots separate.
- The name is a binding from a world frame, provenanced in `10_source_ledger.csv`, and still unproven per society.
- This corrects an over-reading I made while drafting: I initially treated "shape, not a name" as a blanket ban on mentioning mechanisms. Re-census against the README and Fig 13 narrows it to missing primitives and to the settlement requirement. The correction is kept in `01_figure_grounded_audit.md` §6.

## 6. Two consequences for outreach

**Prestige is not action.** From *The Recursive Self-Improvement Trap*: *"Prestige must never be allowed to validate an action directly... Someone let prestige stand in for proof... Prestige can fund an action. It can give an action a budget to be tried. But it cannot settle an action. Only the ground can settle it. The Ground Maintainer must be blind to prestige."*

A campaign that gathers well-known names to agree with the shape claim is the named collapse, not corroboration. The legitimate ask gives the claim a budget to be tried: a per-slot experiment or a replication, with a refuter written in advance.

**My own audit is a simulation, not a ground reading.** Same essay: *"LLMs can quickly simulate an approximate answer/output... However, the truly testable output would need to be derived from the real architecture or working the formula yourself."* LLM output is *"simulacra."* So `01` is a simulated derivation checked for internal consistency against the figures. It is not a test of the claim and must be labelled as such. It cannot settle which conditions support cooperation in any society.

## 7. What "shape" means here, in one paragraph

A shape is the narrowed form of an answer you can state before you can name it. For COG-0052 it is the claim that conditions are the habitat of a described cooperation residual (Fig 11 :39, :43), that valence is relative to that residual so no list transfers unchanged (Fig 11 :49, :51), that the conditions overlap as a union to be unbundled (Fig 9 :54-57), and that cross-society residuals are indexed per slot and never averaged (Fig 1 :19, :22). It constrains any candidate answer without supplying the name. The name is owed to a ground that discriminates sources: per-slot, preregistered, replicated measurement. Until that ground pays, the result is `specified` and `candidate`.
