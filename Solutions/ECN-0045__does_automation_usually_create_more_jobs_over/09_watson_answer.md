## QUESTION

Does automation usually create more jobs over time than it eliminates, and how does the answer vary by occupation? Restated as an effect: R = |Γ(b,a) − o′| per occupational slot s, where b is what the slot expects to be paid for, a is technology adoption, Γ(b,a) is the substitution-or-compensation prediction, and o′ is what the market actually returns in employment, wages and task content. Option A: return a universal net sign and a standing list of safe occupations. Option B: return a per-slot separator with different anchors, a task-level discriminator, and a declared gap for the sign and the list. A sends a `general` verdict that aggregates away the signal and spends the independence of the readers checking it; B sends a checkable shape with refuters and an open slot until the ground pays.

## DOMAIN CENSUS

R by arity, symmetry and scale, then one line per domain with actors / currency / anchor, partition or union, and dependency order.

- **R (the residual).** Two rival predictions at one occupational slot: substitution removes the labor performing task t; compensation lowers cost, raises output and raises demand in the remaining tasks. Arity: three-place (slot Γ, action, market return), indexed by slot and ordered by time, carrying an ordered bond and a comparison bond. Symmetry: "more jobs than" is a threshold (Fig 12 :87), not a symmetric sum, and losers and winners are not interchangeable. Scale: single slot, population of slots, time channel across decades.
- **D1 Occupational task slot.** Actors: workers, tasks, tools, the slot's Γ. Currency: task performance and residual reduction. Anchor: realized employment and wage the market pays for that slot. Admitted.
- **D2 Labor-market habitat.** Actors: firms, workers, entrants, regulators, customers. Currency: hiring, survival, wages. Anchor: the market, explicitly named in Fig 2 :29. Admitted.
- **D3 Task library.** Actors: task atoms and bonds. Currency: reach, |φ| cost. Anchor: settlement against the ground (Fig 5 :32). Admitted. Recombination is an isomer.
- **D4 Time and measurement channel.** Actors: BLS OEWS, Employment Projections, O*NET, metrics, percepts. Currency: contact with the ground. Anchor: the ground, which does not decay (Fig 10 :24). Admitted.
- **X1 Automation as a free-floating agent with a fixed job count.** No anchor independent of the market's verdict. Not a domain yet. X2 technology as a single cause is absorbed into D2 and D3.
- **Union, not partition.** D1/D3 overlap (Γ inside the slot) and D2/D4 overlap (contact inside the habitat). Unbundled conditions: C1 maintained channel to a paying ground (displacement) and C2 renewed reach via a differing closure (creation). A slot failing one condition is not a milder case of a slot failing the other (Fig 9 :57).
- **Dependency order.** Perception, vocabulary, objective, planning, learn and carry (Fig 3). The middle link, having terms for what the changed task set affords and saying what progress would count as, has no instrument (Fig 3 :27).

Strain test: an early reading mapped Fig 13's six physical terms onto task kinds by calling a routine a kind of energy. That is a strain, so Fig 13 was re-censused to the honest-stop role only. No unresolved strain after re-census.

## VERDICT (from the figures)

The figure-derived verdict: the question as posed asks for the wrong object. Per occupational slot, displacement is a failure of the channel to a ground that still pays, creation is an import of new task atoms from a closure that differs, and a surviving occupation is an isomer over a held task set whose valence flips with the residual, so no standing list of safe occupations survives a change of residual. The labor market, not a poll or projection, is the anchor. Two conditions are kept separate per domain and never averaged into a national net sign. The universal sign and the winning-occupation list are a declared `gap`. Mode `specified` on the per-domain claims, `general` on the structural shape, not `grounded`. The labor-economics pool corroborates the two-condition shape only after being read as two conditions; Moravec's paradox corroborates the mechanism shape at a different stratum. Conflicts returned to the census, never flipped the verdict.

- **cost if wrong:** misdirected diagnosis. A slot failing C1 would be sold import policies, and a slot failing C2 would be sold channel instruments; a single lever would be applied to a union that needs two. In outreach terms, an expert's twenty minutes would be spent refuting a shape that was never the right shape. The cost is real, bounded and largely reversible. Sending a recording of the theory before a verdict would additionally spend the reader's independence, which Fig 2 :42 prices: *"Buy independence in proportion to the cost of being wrong."*
- **undoable:** yes. No term is deleted if the claim fails; a refutation is added and the reach falls because *"something was added rather than because anything was deleted"* (Fig 6 :42). The shape and its pre-registered refuters are recorded with provenance so a later reader can locate the error (Fig 10 :62-64).
- **reversed by:** a ground reading, not agreement. Any of the seven refuters in `01` section 4, in particular a prospective per-occupation statistic that survives a habitat substitution, an occupation losing work while both handoff factors measure high, a two-decade renewal with no import, or a safe-occupation list whose valence is invariant across a residual change. A genuinely independent second frame ranged against the market anchor would also change the verdict. Until one fires, this stays `candidate`.
