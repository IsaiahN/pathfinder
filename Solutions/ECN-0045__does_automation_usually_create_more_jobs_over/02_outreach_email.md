**Use:** one-to-one, or as a cover note attached to a submission through a public venue in `03_public_contact_points.csv`. **Not** for bulk send.

**Hard precondition:** do not send until the retraction table in `01_figure_grounded_audit.md` section 6 is applied. The stage-1 output must survive validation or the two-stage offer collapses on contact.

**Subject:** One check on the "automation creates more jobs than it destroys" question

> Hi [name],
>
> I have one specific output I would like you to check against what you already know about labor markets. I am not asking you to read a theory first, and I am not asking you to agree. If the output does not survive, there is nothing else to look at.
>
> **The output (`specified`, not `grounded`).** The question as posed asks for one net sign and then for a variation by occupation. Those are two different objects. The framework's rule is that the residual is indexed by object slot, never aggregated. A national net jobs figure is a legitimate way to decide where to spend a research budget. It is not a witness about which occupation is losing contact with what pays.
>
> What survives a derivation is a separator with two conditions, not a sign:
>
> 1. **Displacement at a slot.** An occupation loses work when its channel to a ground that still pays degrades while the market keeps paying someone else. The named failure modes are stale readings, saturated metrics and pooled readings. The ground does not decay; the channel does.
> 2. **Creation at a slot.** A new task is an import from a closure that differs from the slot's own task set. Composing inside a closed task set never adds a task atom, however long the search runs. An import needs a provenance record and a shadow test, otherwise it is a name for something that was already there.
>
> **How the answer varies by occupation.** There is no standing list of safe occupations. An actor's valence is relative to the residual, not a property it carries. Before and after automation, an occupation is an isomer over the same task atoms with a different bond, and the bond carries the information the atom list does not. The same technology is a benefactor to one residual and a malfactor to another.
>
> **What I am not claiming.** I am not claiming a universal "yes, automation usually creates more jobs" or "no, it destroys them". I am not claiming a percentage of jobs at risk, a safe-occupation list, or that any named task is *the* new work. A projection or a poll is not the anchor here; the labor market is. The framework returns a shape, not a name.
>
> **What would refute this, written before sending:**
> 1. a prospective, per-occupation statistic that predicts which named slots gain and lose next and survives a change in who pays for their tasks;
> 2. an occupation that loses work while both the fidelity of its reading and the market's pressure on it independently measure high and are not declining;
> 3. an occupation that keeps producing ground-verified new tasks for two decades with no import from a differing closure;
> 4. a standing safe-occupation list whose valence is invariant across a documented change of residual.
>
> If any one holds, I will retract the claim. A one-line "this fails because..." is more useful to me than agreement. If you would rather not engage, no reply is needed.
>
> **If it survives, and only then:** the sources are below. The figures are the authority, not my summary of them.
> - Verbatim figure text with line numbers (local extract): `FRAMEWORK/reference/03-FIGURES_TEXT.md`
> - Canonical usage rule (local): `FRAMEWORK/reference/FIGURES_README_canonical.md`
> - "Why Are There Still So Many Jobs? The History and Future of Workplace Automation", David H. Autor, Journal of Economic Perspectives 29(3), 2015: https://www.aeaweb.org/articles?id=10.1257/jep.29.3.3
> - "Robots and Jobs: Evidence from US Labor Markets", Daron Acemoglu and Pascual Restrepo, NBER Working Paper 23285: https://www.nber.org/papers/w23285
>

Write down what would refute your result *before* you run it, and state the mode of what you get. A reported failure is more useful to me than a reported success.

## Two-stage discipline

**Stage 1.** Send only the residual, the separator and the four refuters. Ask what the recipient would count as a failure and record their verdict **before** showing any framework material.

**Stage 2, opt-in only.** If they are curious, offer the four links. Their stage-1 verdict is never pooled as corroboration, because once they have absorbed the frame they no longer stand outside it (Fig 10 :42: *"A verifier that can reconstruct the claim cannot verify it."*). Later agreement is a convention, not a second witness (Fig 2 :40).

No stage 3 is offered by this agent. Hosting and sending are yours to do.
