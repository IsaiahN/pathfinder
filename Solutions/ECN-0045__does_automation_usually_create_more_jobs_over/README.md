## Question and provenance

- **Question:** Does automation usually create more jobs over time than it eliminates, and how does the answer vary by occupation?
- **Source:** `Questions/economics questions.txt` line 61, question 45.
- **Section:** 5. Economic growth, productivity, and technology.

## The residual in effect terms

R = |Γ(b,a) − o′| per slot s (Formula step 1; Symbols table). Here **b** is what an occupational slot believes will be demanded and paid for, **a** is the adoption of a technology, **Γ(b,a)** is the prediction that automation substitutes tasks or creates demand, **o′** is what the labor market actually returns in employment, wages and task content, and **s** is the occupation, read per slot and per time. The question asks for a net sign ("more jobs than it eliminates") and a variation across slots. The answer returned is not a net sign: it is a separator between two conditions with different anchors, a shape, and a declared `gap` for the universal count.

## What is deliberately not here

- No personal emails of named individuals. All venues in `03` are public institutional submission or contact routes.
- No ranking of people by how likely they are to agree. Selecting for agreement manufactures the correlation that disqualifies a verdict (Fig 2 :40: *"disqualifying for a verdict: agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool"*).
- No bulk-send plan, and no claim that a forecast, a poll or a consensus settles the question. The anchor is the market, not an agreement (Fig 2 :29, :35).

## The answer in one line

The net sign is the wrong object. Displacement is a failure of contact at an occupational slot, creation is an import of new task atoms from a differing closure, and an occupation that survives is an isomer over a held task set whose valence flips with the residual, so there is no standing list of safe occupations. Verdicts stay per slot and are never averaged. Mode `specified`, ungrounded, candidate; the universal net count is a declared `gap`.

## Honest limits

This package is a `specified` shape derived from the figures and checked for internal consistency only. The world sources in `09` and `10` are secondary corroboration, never the verdict, and most of the economics sources share one evidence pool. The second seat in `11` is simulated and shares the primary's evidence pool, so its agreement is internal consistency, not corroboration. A real labor-market measurement per occupation is owed before any claim here becomes `grounded`.
