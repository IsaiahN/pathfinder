# Expert verification packet: ECN-0045, automation and occupational employment

**What this is:** a claim in a form a labor economist or labor statistician can check. Mode, derivation and refuters are stated before the conclusion, so a reader knows what to look for and what would settle it.

**Mode: `specified` on the per-domain sub-claims, `general` on the structural shape, not `grounded`.** No per-occupation measurement is claimed by this run. This is a separator and a route, not a result.

## 1. The claim, in one paragraph

The net sign asked for is the wrong object. Per occupational slot, displacement is a failure of the channel to a ground that still pays for the slot's tasks, while creation is an import of new task atoms from a closure that differs from the slot's own. A slot that survives is an isomer over a held task set (same atoms, different bond, different substance), and the value of any task or skill is relative to the current residual, so no standing list of safe occupations survives a change of residual. The two conditions are kept separate and never averaged into a national net jobs figure. The universal sign and the winning-occupation list are a declared `gap`, not a hidden answer.

## 2. Three checkable sub-claims

### C1: method-facing, checkable by a labor statistician against the data design

- **Statement:** a national net jobs figure cannot settle a per-occupation question. The residual must be read per slot; a pooled figure is a filter for the budget, not a witness.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 1 :19, *"The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated."* Fig 1 :22, *"Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average."* Fig 9 :60, *"Use filters for the budget and witnesses for the verdict. Never let a filter hand you a verdict."*
- **Refuter:** a prospective, per-occupation statistic that predicts which named slots gain and lose next, and that survives a habitat substitution changing who pays for those tasks. If such a statistic exists, C1 is false.

### C2: economics-facing, checkable against the Autor and Acemoglu-Restrepo results

- **Statement:** displacement and creation are two conditions of a union with different anchors, not opposite answers to one question. Measured local displacement is real, and complementarity is real; they apply to different conditions and need different instruments.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 9 :57, *"What remains is smaller and it is also categorically different: a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either."* Fig 6 :23, *"No frame escapes its own closure. The bound moves only on contact with a frame whose closure differs."* Fig 8 :32, *"Closure is idempotent. Composing inside a closed set never leaves it, so no search, however long, adds an atom. This one is definitional: it is what closure means."*
- **What to check:** Acemoglu and Restrepo, *"we estimate large and robust negative effects of robots on employment and wages across commuting zones"* (NBER WP 23285), is a local displacement reading. Autor 2015, *"automation also complements labor, raises output in ways that leads to higher demand for labor, and interacts with adjustments in labor supply"* (JEP 29(3)), is a mechanism for the other condition. Are they being treated as two conditions here, rather than as a single net sign?
- **Refuter:** a single lever that restores both conditions in both failure types with no categorically different residue, or a slot that renews its task set for two decades purely by internal composition with no import and still passes the ground test.

### C3: occupational facing, checkable against within-occupation task data

- **Statement:** occupational variation is not a standing safe list. Before and after are isomers over held task atoms, and valence flips with the residual. The within-occupation task shift is the measurable signature.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 11 :51, *"No standing list of good actors survives a change of residual."* Fig 12 :117, *"isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."* Fig 11 :49, *"An actor's valence is relative to the residual, not a property it carries."*
- **What to check:** Autor, Levy and Murnane report *"Changes in task content within nominally identical occupations explain more than half of the overall demand shift induced by computerization"* (NBER WP 8337, later QJE 118(4), 2003). Does the observed change stay within nominal occupation labels, as the isomer claim predicts? O*NET task taxonomies and BLS OEWS employment series are the instruments to check it against.
- **Refuter:** a standing list of automation-safe occupations whose valence is invariant across a documented change of residual, or two slots with identical task atoms, identical bonds and identical residual that nonetheless diverge systematically.

## 3. What is explicitly not claimed

- Not a net-sign verdict in either direction, not a forecast, not a percentage of jobs at risk.
- Not a list of safe occupations, and not a named "job of the future".
- Not that reskilling, a robot tax, or any other lever is the answer. Those are candidate bindings with provenance, not figure outputs.
- Not that the six-term physical substrate names task kinds. That reading was strained and re-censused to the honest-stop role.
- Not `grounded`. Nothing here was settled by a labor-market measurement taken in this run.

## 5. What would settle it

A task-level discriminator, not a further aggregate argument. The rule from *The Hard Problem is About Source*: an instrument that reads green *"cannot comment on the source"*; several paths can produce the same reading, so a discriminator is owed. Here an employment count is the projection and the three candidate sources are task removal, task transformation and task import.

1. Can O*NET task data plus BLS OEWS series separate those three sources at a named occupation, prospectively?
2. Do bracket fidelity and the market's pressure on the slot both measure high while an occupation still loses work? If both are high, the channel-failure claim is wrong.
3. Does a new task cross in from a closure that differs, with a provenance record and a shadow test? If yes, the creation condition is met at the ground.
