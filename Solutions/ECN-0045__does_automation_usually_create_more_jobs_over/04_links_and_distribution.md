## What in the wider literature bears on this question

1. **The two conditions are already separate in the literature, even when authors treat them as opposing answers.** Autor 2015 supplies the complementarity mechanism; Acemoglu and Restrepo supply the measured local displacement effect. Under Fig 9 :57 they are two conditions of a union, *"a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either"*. Do not present either as a refutation of the other.
2. **The resolution has to be within occupations.** Autor, Levy and Murnane find that most of the computerization-induced demand shift is *"within nominally identical occupations"*, which is exactly the isomer reading: the occupation label stays, the bond changes. A top-line occupation count cannot see it.
3. **The aggregate "on balance" claim is a filter.** The World Bank position as reported on the *Technological unemployment* page and the Luddite-fallacy compensation argument are aggregate statements. They are useful for where to look and useless as a per-slot verdict (Fig 9 :60).
4. **Opinion splits are not measurements.** The Pew 48/52 result is two rooms of opinion sharing an evidence pool. Under Fig 2 :40 it is disqualified as a verdict.
5. **Task automability is not a fixed difficulty ladder.** Moravec's paradox is the low-overlap corroborator: the tasks that felt hard (codifiable reasoning) automated first, and the tasks that feel effortless (perception, mobility) did not. This matches residual-relative valence (Fig 11 :49-51) rather than a standing safe-task list.
6. **The instrument exists.** O*NET supplies the task taxonomy, BLS OEWS the per-occupation employment and wage reading, and Employment Projections the forward instrument. What this run did not do is use them. That is why the verdict stays `specified` and the net sign stays a `gap`.

## Distribution: offer, do not push

Figure-grounded reasons:

- **Fig 4 :22** - *"Only methods cross up. Only head starts come down."* Sending the full framework before anyone has described a residual sends a recording, not a generator. Lead with the separator and its refuter.
- **Fig 10 :42** - *"A verifier that can reconstruct the claim cannot verify it."* A recipient who has absorbed the framework before giving a stage-1 verdict has joined the frame being checked.
- **Fig 9 :27-28** - *"describe it before you go looking, or any frame you pick will seem to fit"*. Ask what they would expect to see if the separator were true before giving them the links.
- **Fig 2 :40** - *"disqualifying for a verdict: agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool"*. A list selected for people likely to agree produces a convention, not corroboration.

Recommended order in a reply: (1) the residual and the two-condition separator, (2) ask what they would count as a failure and record the answer, (3) only then the links, with the note that the figures, not the summary, are the authority.

## The two-stage offer

**Stage 1.** Residual, separator, four refuters. No theory, no links, no request for agreement. Record the verdict first.
