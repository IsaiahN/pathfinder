## 1. A shape is a constraint, not a filler

The figures README states the limit: the framework returns *"what a missing thing would have to look like (a shape, not a name)."* Fig 9 :32: *"you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself."* Fig 13 :134: *"Unreachable is a verdict here, not a silence,"* and :135: *"It cannot say which primitive is missing, because naming it would mean already holding it."*

From *The Library of Babel*, opened this session: *"you can cut your search short if the set of books returned is small and descriptive enough that it provides a general shape of the answer that satisfies your question."*

So a **shape** is the narrowed form of an answer you can state before you can name it. It constrains; it does not fill. For this question:

- `net sign = ?` is a shape. `automation usually creates more jobs` is a name.
- `two conditions with different anchors, per slot` is a shape. `reskilling is the answer` is a name.
- `task atoms with a changed bond` is a shape. `the safe occupations are X, Y, Z` is a name.

The shape returned in `07_the_tether_answer.md` is the separator plus the refusal of a universal sign plus the discriminator owed.

## 2. How a shape is obtained

- *Library of Babel*, opened this session: *"With each new term gained, you are able to narrow the space by definition and deepen your vocabulary."* The method is vocabulary by proximity: use the words you already have that are closest to the thing, look for new terms that overlap or correlate, recurse.
- *Library of Babel*, opened this session: *"Perhaps your problem was a bundle or conflation of several problems masquerading as one?"* This is the rule that produced the union in `01` section 1.3. The question was not one hard question with two opposite answers; it was two conditions, displacement and creation, sharing a slot.
- *Decoding Tacit Knowledge* (carried mapping): *"When contact isn't possible, the best you can send is a plain description of what you'd expect to see, because anyone can check that against the world."* This is why the first contact carries refuters and not the framework.
- Fig 9 :30-31: *"your words are borrowed too, so describe the effect rather than the cause."* The effect here is a task-level change in what the market pays for at a slot. "Automation" is a borrowed cause-word; the derivation does not start from it as an anchor.
- Fig 8 :53: *"Residual first, frame second."* The census came before the choice of economics literature. Otherwise any field picked would have seemed to fit.

## 3. What licenses a name

Only two things, both outside the shape itself:

1. **The ground settles it.** Formula step 5, from `THE_FORMULA.md`: *"SETTLE the ground settles it. Verified only by what pays. A gate is not the ground."* For this question, a per-occupation employment and task measurement would pay for a name.
2. **A provenanced import from a frame whose closure differs** (Fig 6 :23, Fig 8 :50-52), with a source gate and shadow test. Naming a task as "new work created by automation" without provenance is the unnamed import that Fig 6 prices above starting from primitives.

*They're Scanning Books* (carried mapping): *"The real import is not a new term in the existing space. It is a new dimension of the space."* And: *"Declaring the primitives does not make them neutral. It makes the choice visible."* A task taxonomy such as O*NET is a visible choice of axes, not nature's own list.

## 4. The ground and the source problem

*The Hard Problem is About Source*, opened this session, supplies the discriminator argument that governs this question. Metamerism: an instrument reads green and *"it cannot comment on the source"*. One green light, or yellow plus blue, or RGB, or CMYK, or a scheme not yet discovered, all produce the same reading. *"If several paths create the same behavior, then behavior is a projection with lossy information (it cannot define consciousness in full)."* The essay's conclusion: *"The answer is unreached until a better instrument exists."* It was the spectrometer, not an argument, that resolved the case.

For ECN-0045 the same structure holds. An employment count is a projection. Three sources produce the same reading:

1. a task was removed (C1 failure, displacement);
2. a task was transformed inside a nominally unchanged occupation (isomer binding);
3. a new task was imported from a differing closure (C2 success, creation).

A net jobs number cannot comment on which source produced it. What is owed is a task-level discriminator, not a net sign. This is the exact reason F8 of `01` names the vocabulary/objective link as the break.

*The Recursive Self-Improvement Trap*, opened this session: *"the output is only as good as the validator. The agent, or the tool, or the frame, or the theory, cannot validate itself."* And: *"Metatheories cannot validate themselves."* So this derivation cannot certify its own shape.

## 5. Answer to the shape question

**What shape does the Tether return for "does automation usually create more jobs over time than it eliminates, and how does the answer vary by occupation"?**

It returns:

1. **A union separator, not a sign.** Two conditions with different anchors: a channel that still pays for the slot's tasks, and a reach renewed by contact with a differing closure. Verdicts per slot, never averaged into a net count. General.
2. **A refusal of the universal quantifier.** "Usually" is a threshold over an aggregate that this derivation did not measure; no figure line licenses it. Specified as a refusal.
3. **A structural reading of occupational variation.** Valence is residual-relative; before and after are isomers over held task atoms; no standing safe list survives a residual change. Specified.
4. **A discriminator owed.** A task-level instrument that distinguishes removal from transformation from import. Declared gap until it is run.

### Correction to my own §3 (kept visible)

I initially wrote the "shape, not a name" rule as a blanket ban on naming any task or occupation binding. That overstates it. The README's phrase governs a **missing** thing, a primitive not held in Γ. Task atoms are already held in the task library (D3), and O*NET already names many of them. Naming a held atom is a **binding with provenance**, not the invention of a new primitive and not an import that moves the wall. What remains forbidden is (a) inventing a seventh task kind and calling it derived, (b) claiming a binding moves the wall when Fig 6 says *"IMPORT adds an atom"* and this one adds none, and (c) reading a source off a projection without a discriminator. Both the narrowed form and the original overstatement are recorded here so neither is quietly lost.

## 6. Consequences for contact

**Prestige is not action.** From *The Recursive Self-Improvement Trap*, opened this session: *"Prestige can fund an action. It can give an action a budget to be tried. But it cannot settle an action. Only the ground can settle it. The Ground Maintainer must be blind to prestige."* A list of well-known economists assembled to agree with a net-sign claim is the named collapse, not corroboration. The legitimate ask is the opposite: give the separator a budget to be checked, and let the market read per occupation settle it.

**Interest in the outcome is declared.** Fig 10 :59: *"an interest in the outcome is unavoidable, an interest in the reading is disqualifying."* The author of this derivation has an interest in the framework being useful. That is stated here and handled with the pre-registered refuter in `01` section 4, not hidden.

**My own audit is a simulation, not a ground reading.** *The Recursive Self-Improvement Trap*: *"LLMs can quickly simulate an approximate answer/output with the formula... However, the truly testable output would need to be derived from the real architecture or working the formula yourself."* So `01`, `06`, `07`, `09` and `11` are simulated derivations checked for internal consistency. They are **not** a labor-market measurement and must be labelled as such. They cannot settle the question they report on.
