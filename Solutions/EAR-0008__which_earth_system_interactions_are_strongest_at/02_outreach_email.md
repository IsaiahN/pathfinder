# Outreach draft: check the separator before you decide whether the scale question is well posed

**Use:** one to one, or as a cover letter attached to a submission through the public venues in `03_public_contact_points.csv`. **Not** for bulk send.

**Hard precondition:** do not send a ranking. The output below is a separator that takes a named residual and returns what a checkable answer would have to look like. If you find yourself writing a list of "the strongest interactions" before naming the residual and the currency, stop (`Fig 9 :27-28`).

---

**Subject:** One checkable separator for the strongest-interactions-at-each-scale question

> Hi [name],
>
> I have one specific output I would like you to check against what you already know about Earth-system interactions. I am not asking you to read a theory first, and I am not asking you to agree with anything. If the output does not survive, there is nothing else to look at.
>
> **The output (`specified`, not `grounded`).** The question "which interactions are strongest at local, regional, continental or global scales" asks for a standing list. I think that object is not well formed. Three things in the figures decide that:
>
> 1. A residual is indexed per object slot, not aggregated (`Fig 1 :19`). A global average near zero can sit beside one live slot (`Fig 1 :13`). So "strongest globally" is not where the signal lives.
> 2. An actor's valence is relative to the residual, not a property it carries (`Fig 11 :49`). There is *"No standing list of good actors survives a change of residual."* (`Fig 11 :51`). An interaction that is strong for carbon storage need not be strong for flood risk.
> 3. Scale is a frame parameter. The kernel does not change with size; only actors, currency and anchor change (`Fig 4 :19-20`). Cross-scale influence travels through a lossy membrane that needs a round trip (`Fig 4 :30`), and the effect that matters at the receiving scale is often two or three steps out (`Fig 11 :52-54`).
>
> So what the figures return is a shape, not a list: for a named scale, name the slot and residual; enumerate the habitat around it including second and third-order contacts; range an independent measurement that does not move when your frame pushes on it (`Fig 2 :29`); state the currency and compare (`Fig 12 :87`); and measure the round-trip gap across the scale boundary (`Fig 4 :30`). That is the whole output. It returns a kind of thing, never the answer itself (`Fig 9 :32`).
>
> **What I am not claiming.** I am not claiming a universal ranking, not that global interactions are categorically stronger than local ones, not that ocean-atmosphere coupling or any other named pair wins at any scale, and not that the ranking is settled by experiment. Strength is an ordered comparison under a declared currency, and the currencies do not add (`Fig 12 :125`). The world literature does produce conditional, per-outcome effect sizes, and those are useful; the claim is only that they do not concatenate into one standing list.
>
> **What would refute the output.** I wrote these down before sending this:
> 1. A figure line gives an ordering of interactions by local, regional, continental or global scale independent of a named residual.
> 2. One interaction ranks first for every residual and every currency, stated before measurement, with the rank stable across scales.
> 3. A global aggregate predicts the live slot residuals as well as slot-level measurement on a named outcome.
> 4. A local-to-global-to-local round trip returns the original values exactly, so the gap is zero.
> 5. Two independent frames using different aggregation units and different non-updating grounds return incompatible orderings for the same fixed residual.
>
> If any holds, I retract the separator. A one-line "this fails because..." is more useful to me than agreement. If you would rather not engage, no reply is needed.
>
> **If it survives, and only then:** the sources are below. The figures are the authority, not my summary of them.
> - Verbatim figure text with line numbers (local framework file): `FRAMEWORK/reference/03-FIGURES_TEXT.md`.
> - Canonical usage rule (local framework file): `FRAMEWORK/reference/FIGURES_README_canonical.md`.
> - Figures and formula for direct download (public repo, branch `general-agent`): https://github.com/IsaiahN/tether/tree/general-agent/figures
>
> **If you want to use the method:** the formula is in the figures link. Run it on an Earth-system problem you know well and can check against a ground. Write down what would refute your result *before* you run it, and label the mode of what you get. A reported failure is more useful to me than a reported success.
>
> [your name]

## Contact discipline

One message per person, through a public institutional or opt-in channel. No scraped addresses, no bulk send, no ranking recipients by likely agreement. Every message carries an opt-out line. A reply is a conversation, never corroboration; if a recipient returns a refutation, it is copied into `11_audit.md` with provenance and re-evaluated as if it came from any other source.
