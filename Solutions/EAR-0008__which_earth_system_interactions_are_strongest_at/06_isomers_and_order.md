## DERIVATION

### Q1. Are two scale configurations isomers?

- `Fig 12 :117` **"Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."**
- `Fig 12 :92` **"Symmetry is what decides whether an arrangement is a new thing."**
- `Fig 12 :94` **"The arrangement is the substance."**
- As the question is usually posed, an interaction is a pair: land and water locally, ocean and atmosphere globally. Two such pairs share at most one atom. By `Fig 12`'s definition they are **not isomers**; they are two members of the same **functional group**, where `Fig 12 :123` says *"A substructure whose presence fixes the outcome. The group says what kind; the qualifier says which member."* The group is "component pair coupled to a residual."
- The stronger version: write both configurations over the **full shared atom set** `{atmosphere, ocean, cryosphere, land, biosphere, human}` and let them differ only in which structure mediates which pair. Then they **are** isomers: same atoms, different arrangement, different substance. This is the checkable structural claim worth putting to a referee.
- **Falsifier, written before the run:** if either configuration necessarily requires an atom the other does not, the isomer claim fails and the relation is composition plus import (`Fig 6 :23`), not isomerism. A second falsifier: if two arrangements over the full shared set are observationally identical for every residual, the "different substance" half fails and the distinction is notation only.

### Q2. What order is the comparison?

- `Fig 12 :87` defines `⋛` as **"a threshold relation between two quantities"**; the Operators table test is *"is it about which is larger, rather than both being present? Yes."* So "stronger than" is ordered, not symmetric.
- `Fig 13 :20` supplies the ordering axis: **"ordering, so more and less can be said"** (spectrum). A ranking is a spectral arrangement over a comparison bond.
- `Fig 12 :104` **"A settled molecule becomes an atom for whatever composes over it. So irreducible is a fact about a level and never about the world"** permits a per-slot strength reading, once settled, to become an operand at the next scale. That is the recursion, not a new primitive.
- `THE_FORMULA.md` step 2 and `Fig 3 :20` order the operations: refuse the standing list before minting a rank, settle each first-order reading before the cross-scale comparison, and describe the residual before naming a frame (`Fig 9 :27-28`). The chain is ordered by dependency, not importance.
- **Strain, named:** at the second-order comparison the operands are settled readings now serving as atoms. Whether they count as *quantities* at that level needs an instrument. This is the same live type question S3 carried from `01`, not a correction.

### Q3. Are the four scales a sequence?

- `Fig 13 :4-5` **"The two kinds are in lockstep, a crossing is a change of role rather than a step upward, and no member of the pair is senior to the other."**
- `Fig 4 :33` **"Which is why there is no top and no infinite regress."**
- `Fig 12 :48` calls a chain *"the degenerate case: one bond type, no branching."* Treating local, regional, continental and global as a chain imposes one bond type and a direction of rank that the figures do not license. They are four observation frames of the same loop with changed actors, currency and anchor (`Fig 4 :20`).
- A comparison between scales is therefore a `⋛` at second order over settled first-order readings, not a step up a ladder. The global label does not make a reading senior to the local one.
- **Falsifier:** derive an ordering of the four scales from the six-term set, or show a figure line that makes one scale senior to another. Either would reverse Q3.

### Q4. Is a tipping threshold the same as a strength comparison? (live type question)

- The climate literature read this session defines a tipping point as *"a critical threshold that, when crossed, leads to large, accelerating and often irreversible changes in the climate system"*, and names nine global core and seven regional impact elements.
- `Fig 12 :87` defines `⋛` as a threshold relation between **two quantities**. A tipping threshold is a critical value of a control parameter in a dynamical system, not obviously a relation between two quantities.
- The two may be the same operator seen at different levels, or different operators. Figure 12 :90 says **"Six tests, and they separate the seven bonds. Seven is a count of bonds, not of operators."** A relation that fails all six is *"a claim that has not been made yet."* Deciding whether tipping fits the six needs an instrument and an outside frame, not an argument.
- **Falsifier:** a formal demonstration that tipping thresholds satisfy all six bond tests and preserve the ordered comparison semantics would close the type question; a demonstration that they fail the tests would make it a separate operator and force a re-census of the D4 domain.

## CONCLUSION

The two scale configurations are a **functional group** as usually reported (one component pair each) and a real **isomer claim** when both are written over the full shared atom set. Strength is an ordered spectrum under a comparison bond, and the operations that produce it are dependency-ordered: refuse before rank, settle before compare, describe before look. The four scales are **not** a sequence with a senior member; they are four frames of one loop. The isomorphism between a tipping threshold and a strength comparison remains a **live type strain**, named rather than hidden.

This framing rescues what is checkable in the question without the part that fails: the arrangement and the order are figure-derived, while the specific ordering of named interactions is a `gap` until a ground pays for it.
