## Independent domain census

I redid the census before reading the primary's conclusions, using the WATSON order: R by arity, symmetry and scale; one line per domain with actors, currency and anchor; partition or union; order by dependency.

- **R (arity / symmetry / scale).** The residual is the unchosen meaning of "strongest". Arity: four scales plus a definition question, and the residual is per slot. Symmetry: "stronger than" is ordered, so a pooled symmetric correlation cannot choose it. Scale: the four labels are observation frames, not a rank ladder, because the kernel does not change with size. This is a union compound with an unassignable shared fraction: the definition question binds all four scale questions.
- **Domain 1, observation.** Actors: instruments, reanalyses, model tiles. Currency: residual per slot. Anchor: a measurement that does not move when a frame pushes on it. Nameable, so a domain.
- **Domain 2, mechanism.** Actors: atmosphere, ocean, ice, land, ecosystems and their relations. Currency: contact and cross-kind composition. Anchor: experiment or paleo record. Nameable.
- **Domain 3, cross-scale.** Actors: the four scale frames and the membrane. Currency: methods up, head starts down, contact cascades. Anchor: the round-trip gap. Nameable.
- **Domain 4, ranking.** Actors: interactions as relations. Currency: ordered comparison on a spectrum. Anchor: the named residual. Nameable only after the residual is written down; this is the domain most likely to be skipped.
- **Domain 5, human.** Actors: land and water use, emissions. Currency: introduction, not subtraction. Anchor: observed altered state. Nameable.
- **Union, not partition.** D1 with D2, D2 with D3, D4 dependent on D1, D5 overlapping both. Verdicts must be kept per domain.
- **Order by dependency.** Residual first, then habitat enumeration, then comparison, then round trip, then human introductions.

My census matches the primary's five domains and its union reading. Where the primary derived from Fig 1 and Fig 11, I derived from Fig 9's union or partition split and Fig 3's dependency order.
