- Fig 5 :50-53 names a fifth kind: *"The four kinds sort a gap by what is missing. None of them sorts a gap by what is wrong. A residual whose content is that a regularity the system holds is false owes a term, and the term it owes refuses another rather than explaining a gap."*

### Q2 — What is the order of the three conditions?

- Fig 3 :20: *"The chain is ordered by dependency, not by importance."* Fig 3 :21: each link consumes what the one above produces.
- Order derived: (a) a residual indexed per slot, because without a signal there is nothing to range (Fig 1 :19); (b) an anchor that does not update, because a signal ranged against a moving frame is negotiation (Fig 2 :33); (c) a visible lineage and an outside checker, because a ranged signal can still be misattributed if the provenance is lost and the checker holds the interiors (Fig 8 :51, Fig 10 :42).

### Q3 — Are the three conditions a checklist or a product?

- Fig 5 :33: *"The three guards are a product, not a checklist."* Fig 5 :41: *"Any one of them at zero stops the mint entirely."*
- The discriminator's conditions have the same form. If the residual is aggregated away, if the anchor updates on the model, or if the checker can reconstruct the claim, the arrangement conceals regardless of how strong the other two are. There is no compensation between them.
- Consequence: a model cannot be "mostly extending." That phrase averages across slots or conditions, which Fig 1 :22 warns against. The verdict is per condition and per slot, then recorded, never collapsed to a single score.
- **Falsifier:** exhibit a model with one condition at zero that nevertheless extends reasoning against the ground as judged by an independent checker. Then the conditions are weights, not a product, and Q3 falls.

### Q4 — Are extension and concealment isomers?

- Fig 12 :117: *"Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."*
- The two regimes share the atom set: model, output, checker, ground, lineage. They differ in the bond. Extension uses triangulation: two frames range against one unmoved anchor (Fig 2 :45-46). Concealment uses substitution: a frame-internal proxy is bonded where the ground should be (Fig 1 :27, Fig 5 :63).
- So they are a real isomer claim: same atoms, different arrangement, different behaviour. The distinction is invisible in a list of ingredients (you cannot tell from a list containing "a model, a metric and a checker" which regime you have) and decisive in behaviour (whether a refused term changes contact).
- This is stronger than saying they are opposites on a scale. A scale would allow averaging. Isomers do not.
- **Falsifier:** if concealment requires an atom that extension does not, or vice versa, the isomer claim fails and the two regimes are different substances with different inventories, not different arrangements. Mark `specified`, unmeasured.

### Q5 — The two currencies explain why complexity is attractive

- Fig 12 :125: *"A composition costs in two currencies, and they do not add."* Fig 12 :126: *"Bits price the description. Actions price finding out whether it holds."*
- Fig 12 :127: *"They are in different units, so a system holding one accounting cannot compare them and will not notice the shortfall. It proposes arrangements it cannot afford to test, and the budget is what decides how much of the space is reached at all."*
- Complexity is a price in bits: a longer description, more parameters, more layers. Settlement is a price in actions: an experiment, a measurement, an independent check. A field that reports only bits cannot see that it has proposed an arrangement it cannot afford to test. That is the structural reason a proxy feels like progress.
- Fig 12 :130: *"Summing them into one figure is the error this names, not the fix for it. A price in bits and a price in actions are two readings, held apart."* So the recommendation to report reach separately from parameter count is a figure requirement, not a stylistic preference.
- **Falsifier:** exhibit a model and a field that price description and settlement in one number without losing the distinction between them, and where the combined number correctly predicts whether an independent checker can range the output against the ground. Then Q5 falls.

## CONCLUSION

Concealment is not a missing mechanism and not a false held term. It is a channel and provenance failure, re-censused out of Fig 5's fifth bin into Fig 10. The three conditions bind in dependency order and as a product, so no condition can compensate for another. Extension and concealment are isomers over the same atom set with different bonds, which forbids averaging them into a single score. The pull of complexity is explained by two incommensurable currencies: bits price the description, actions price the settling, and a system holding one accounting cannot see the shortfall.

This rescues what is checkable in the question without naming a winning architecture. `metric equals meaning` is not claimed; `extension and concealment are isomeric arrangements of one atom set with a stated falsifier` is a checkable structural claim.
