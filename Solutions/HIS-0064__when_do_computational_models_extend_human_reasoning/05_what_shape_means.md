## 1. A shape is a constraint, not a filler

The canonical README states the limit: the framework returns *"what a missing thing would have to look like (a shape, not a name)."* Fig 9 :32: *"you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself."* Fig 13 :134: *"Unreachable is a verdict here, not a silence."*

From *The Library of Babel*: *"You can cut your search short if the set of books returned is small and descriptive enough that it provides a general shape of the answer that satisfies your question."*

So for this question, a **shape** is the narrowed form of a discriminator you can state before you can rank any particular model. It constrains; it does not fill. The shape is:

> a model extends reasoning when its output preserves a per-slot residual that an independent frame can range against an unmoved ground through a visible lineage; it conceals assumptions when a frame-internal proxy replaces any one of those three conditions.

That is a shape. "Transformer models extend reasoning and recurrent models conceal assumptions" is a name, and the framework does not return it.

## 2. How the shape was obtained

- *Library of Babel*: use the vocabulary you already have closest to the thing, then look for recurring new terms that overlap or correlate with it. *"With each new term gained, you narrow the space and deepen your vocabulary."*
- Fig 6 :30: *"The description travels. Fails by being sharpened into terms only we can read. Cast it in effect terms."* Fig 9 :30-31: *"your words are borrowed too, so describe the effect rather than the cause."*
- *Decoding Tacit Knowledge*: *"When contact isn't possible, the best you can send is a plain description of what you'd expect to see, because anyone can check that against the world."* That is why stage 1 of the outreach asks what a checker would expect to see if the discriminator were wrong.
- *Library of Babel* on stopping: *"the finish line will also be the starting place."* The paradox is that by then the searcher has composed the answer through locating it. Here the searcher composes the discriminator by describing the residual, not by listing models.

## 3. What licenses a name

Only two things, both outside the shape itself:

1. **The ground settles it.** `THE_FORMULA.md` step 5: *"the ground settles it. Verified only by what pays. A gate is not the ground."* The canonical README says the framework *"stops at the limit of what the available instruments can distinguish, and records the lineage so the stop is honest."*
2. **A provenanced import from a frame whose closure differs** (Fig 6 :23, Fig 8 :50-52), with a source gate and a shadow test.

*They're Scanning Books* sharpens the second point from a different direction: *"A corpus is the compressed output of human minds; the capacity that produced it is a generator, and outputs do not contain their generator."* And: *"Declaring the primitives does not make them neutral. It makes the choice visible."* Applying the discriminator to a named model is a binding from the modeling frame onto the shape, provenanced as such. It is not a derivation of the model's quality.

*How Intelligence Actually Works* gives the mechanism of provenance loss: *"often, very little about a composed term tells you, from looking at it alone, which route produced it."* That is the source of F3 and F4, and it is exactly why the shape includes a visible lineage as a condition rather than an afterthought.

## 4. What "shape" means specifically for "conceal assumptions behind complexity"

The word "complexity" in the question is the searcher's borrowed vocabulary. The essays say what to do with borrowed vocabulary: cast it in effect terms. Re-census gives three figure quantities underneath it.

| borrowed word | figure quantity | where |
|---|---|---|
| "complexity" as size | library growth without reach growth | Fig 5 :69, Fig 13 :118 |
| "complexity" as opacity | the bond and the arrangement are not recorded | Fig 12 :5, :94 |
| "complexity" as uncheckability | import indistinguishable from derivation; checker holds the interiors | Fig 8 :51, Fig 10 :42 |

*They're Scanning Books* names the deeper version of the third line: *"The real import is not a new term in the existing space. It is a new dimension of the space."* A model can hide an assumption by adding a term inside its space, or by silently changing the space itself. Only the second changes what can be perceived at all, and it is the harder case to see from inside.

*The Hard Problem is About Source* gives the instrument test that would discriminate: *"If several paths create the same behavior, then behavior is a projection with lossy information"* and *"An instrument that can't distinguish these cases demonstrates its own inadequacy."* The spectrometer, not an argument, resolved the color case. So the honest answer to "when do they conceal" is not a catalogue of architectures; it is the absence of a discriminating instrument, which is an unreached state, not a silence.

*How a Society of Agents Would Work* adds the social version: *"The group's agreement is never the verdict"* and *"A group is only worth having while its members stay different."* A large team of modelers who share one evidence pool are one frame wearing many names, so their agreement cannot certify that the model extends reasoning.

## 5. Correction to my own §3 (kept visible)

I initially wrote that the canonical phrase *"a shape, not a name"* forbids naming any modeling property at all. That overstates it. The README's phrase governs a **missing** primitive, one not held in Γ. The discriminator's three conditions (per-slot residual, unmoved ground, visible lineage) are not missing primitives; they are relations among things the figures already hold. Naming them is a binding from the framework onto the modeling question, not the invention of a seventh term.

What remains forbidden is claiming the framework selects a particular architecture as the extender. That would be a name where the framework returns a shape. Both readings are recorded so neither is quietly lost. This matches the independent correction in the exemplar's `05_what_shape_means.md` §5b, reached here from this question's census rather than copied from that file.

## 6. Two consequences for outreach

**Prestige is not action.** From *The Recursive Self-Improvement Trap*: *"Prestige can fund an action. It can give an action a budget to be tried. But it cannot settle an action. Only the ground can settle it. The Ground Maintainer must be blind to prestige."* A campaign that gathers famous names to agree that a model extends reasoning is the named collapse. The legitimate ask gives the claim a budget to be tried and lets a checker who cannot reconstruct it range against the ground.

**My own audit is a simulation, not a ground reading.** Same essay: *"LLMs can quickly simulate an approximate answer/output"* and *"the truly testable output would need to be derived from the real architecture or working the formula yourself."* *Arrival of the Fittest* adds the warning that outputs *"could never be verified if the solutions presented were not contrived to fit the formula"* and that simulated outputs *"must be seen as simulacra."* So `01` is a simulated derivation checked for internal consistency against the figures. It is not a test of any model and must be labelled as such. It cannot settle what it reports on.
