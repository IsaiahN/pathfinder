## 0. The residual in effect terms

The question is not "are models good." It is a discriminating question: under what conditions does a model plus a human checker increase what can be checked against the ground, and under what conditions does the same arrangement make the model's assumptions uncheckable because they sit inside its complexity?

A model is a frame. The Symbols table :111 says: *"A frame is any bounded system with a library: an agent, a person, a discipline, a model."* So the two sides of the question are two frames in a checking arrangement, not two properties of a piece of software.

**R (the residual).** Does an output of the model remain discriminable from the model's own assumptions by an outside frame that ranges against the same ground? Arity: one relation with two possible routings (extension and concealment), not two independent causes. Symmetry: asymmetric, because the anchor must not update (Fig 2 :33). Scale: the model is one frame at one scale; human plus model plus ground is a membrane crossing (Fig 4).

**What an answer would look like, bound as an ordinary term (formula step "Direction").** A candidate discriminator φ: *a model extends reasoning when its output carries a per-slot residual that an independent checker can range against an unmoved ground through a visible lineage; it conceals assumptions when a frame-internal proxy replaces any one of those three conditions.* Written down before the run so it can accrue a residual and be refused (Fig 5 :65: *"make the sought-for shape an ORDINARY TERM"*). This is the shape the derivation tests, not a conclusion.

**Option A and Option B, with what each leads to.** A: treat model complexity as evidence of extension (more parameters, more layers, more score). This substitutes a quantity the frame produces for the metric (Fig 1 :27) and sends a recorder where a generator was owed (Fig 4 :25). B: treat checkability, not complexity, as the discriminator. This preserves the independence of the checker and records the lineage. This audit derives B from the figures and states what would refute it.

## 1. Domain census (before choosing any figure line)

R by arity, symmetry and scale is stated in section 0. One line per domain gives actors, currency and anchor. A domain with no nameable anchor is not a domain yet and is dropped.

| domain | actors | currency | anchor | nameable? |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| D1 the model as frame | Γ (library, parameters), atoms, bonds, R per slot, candidate φ | bits for the description and actions for settling (Fig 12 :125-126) | the ground: data, experiment, instrument | yes |
| D2 the human reasoner as frame | belief b, weighting w, the sought-for shape held as a preference | ability to refuse a term and to re-aim the question | the ground: the problem the human is actually trying to solve | yes |
| D3 the checking arrangement | checker, provenance record, channel, importer | evidence bearing on the residual versus evidence bearing only on its maker (Fig 9 :51) | the ground, never agreement | yes |
| D4 the habitat of deployment | habitat, instruments, conditions, introduced actors | contact (Fig 11 :39-40) | the ground | yes |

**Partition or union.** The four domains are a **union compound** over one residual, not a partition of independent questions. They overlap: the same model output is an object in D1, a claim in D2, a record in D3 and an intervention in D4. Fig 9 :57 says a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either, and unbundling is owed before search. The two regimes, extension and concealment, are two routings of R per domain (formula step 2), not two substances. Domain verdicts are therefore kept separate and never averaged.

**Order by dependency (Fig 3 :20).** Fig 3 :20: *"The chain is ordered by dependency, not by importance."* Order: (1) is the anchor unmoved and is R indexed per slot (D1, Fig 2 :29-30); (2) does the reach grow or only the library (D1, Fig 5 :69); (3) can the human refuse a term and is the direction an ordinary term (D2, Fig 5 :62-65); (4) can the checker verify without reconstructing the claim, and is the lineage visible (D3, Fig 10 :42); (5) did contact change in deployment (D4, Fig 11 :40). Fig 3 :19: *"Optimising a link that already works cannot rescue one that does not."*

**Strain test.** "Complexity" is not a figure term. On a first pass I tried to map it to the fourth bin of Fig 5 (mechanism broken). That strains: the fourth bin sorts *what is missing*, while concealment is *what is wrong with the channel* (Fig 5 :50-56 names a fifth kind for what is wrong). The re-census sends concealment to D3 (provenance and independence), and maps "complexity" to three figure quantities: library growth without reach growth (Fig 5 :69, Fig 13 :118), unrecorded arrangement (Fig 12 :5), and frame-internal self-scoring (Fig 1 :27). No strain survives the re-census. This retraction is kept visible in section 6.

## 3. Findings

### F1 — Extension begins with a per-slot residual that the ground can settle, not with a global score

Fig 1 :19: *"The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated."*
Fig 1 :22: *"Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average."*
Fig 1 :27: *"They are frame-internal. A frame cannot score itself with a quantity it also produces."*
Fig 1 :23: *"The ground enters as the only metric."*

A model extends reasoning when its output preserves the slot-level gap between what the library predicted and what the ground returned. A single accuracy number, a loss curve or a benchmark rank aggregates the signal away and lets the frame score itself with a quantity it produces. Fig 1 :25 and Fig 1 :26 name the two most common substitutes directly: *"coverage, and compression achieved"* and *"predicates minted, and credibility accrued."*

**Refuter.** Exhibit a model whose global score improves while a named slot residual persists and is unfunded, and show that decisions are still correctly routed to that slot. Or show that a frame scoring itself with its own produced quantity can be a verdict. If either holds, F1 falls.

**Mode:** `specified`. **Status:** triangulated across Fig 1 (per-slot signal), Fig 5 :48 (output is never a measure of success) and Fig 13 :134 (the ground decides). Low overlap: signal indexing, MDL bargain and decidability are different quantities.

### F2 — Extension means reach grows, not that the library grows

Fig 5 :69: *"a library that grows is not a reach that grows."*
Fig 6 :21: *"MINT composes inside the closure and can never add an atom."*
Fig 6 :23: *"No frame escapes its own closure. The bound moves only on contact with a frame whose closure differs."*
Fig 13 :118: *"infinite effort inside a finite reach."*

Adding parameters, data or layers enlarges Γ. It does not by itself change what is composable against the ground. Fig 6 :14 calls import *"the only operator that moves the wall"*, with two gates (Fig 6 :12-13): *"source gate: provenance"* and *"shadow test: explains a gap."* A model that merely searches longer in a fixed closure is in the position Fig 13 :118 names: infinite effort inside a finite reach. This is the figure-grounded reason why scale is a budget filter and never a witness.

**Refuter.** Exhibit a model in which adding parameters increases the set of composable, settled terms without any import from a closure that differs and without new ground contact. Then library growth is reach growth and F2 falls.

**Mode:** `specified`. **Status:** triangulated across Fig 5, Fig 6 and Fig 13.

### F3 — Concealment mechanism 1: the arrangement is not recorded, so the assumption carried by the bond disappears

Fig 12 :5: *"A set of ingredients is not one. The bond is the rest of the information, and it is the half nothing was recording."*
Fig 12 :94: *"The arrangement is the substance."*
Fig 12 :47: *"A recipe that names only its ingredients has recorded half of itself."*
Fig 12 :16: *"nothing signals that anything was lost"*

A computational model is a molecule of assumptions: inputs, parameters, objectives, priors and the typed relations between them. If only the ingredients are published and the bonds are not, downstream readers cannot tell which model they have, and nothing in the output signals the loss. This is the figure's own warning about notation, carried into modeling practice. The world literature corroborates from a different pool: the black-box entry notes that *"multiple internal mechanisms can produce identical input-output behavior"* and quotes Shannon's result that any external behaviour can be realised by indefinitely many internal structures (Wikipedia, Black box, 2026, accessed 2026-11-23).

**Refuter.** Show a model output that records every bond and every import with provenance, and two competent readers still cannot reconstruct which assumption produced a given reading, with no instrument able to discriminate them. Then the loss is not in the bond record and F3 falls.

**Mode:** `specified`. **Status:** triangulated inside the figures across Fig 12 and Fig 13 :127-130; single-source for the Shannon/Ashby parallelism in the world source.

### F4 — Concealment mechanism 2: an imported assumption looks like a derivation

Fig 8 :51: *"Convergent derivation and adopted import look identical in the contents. Only the record of where each came from separates them"*
Fig 10 :65: *"Provenance shows a ruling was reached legitimately. It never shows it was right: the ground does that, and a wrong ruling stays wrong however clean its record."*
Fig 6 :12: *"source gate: provenance"*
Fig 6 :13: *"shadow test: explains a gap"*

A prior, a loss function or a training corpus is an import from a frame whose closure differs. When the record is dropped, the import is indistinguishable from something the model derived. When the record is kept but mistaken for settlement, a clean provenance trail is treated as proof. Both errors hide assumptions. The first is the provenance error Fig 8 names; the second is the output-layer error Fig 10 :65 names. The map-territory literature corroborates from outside the framework: Korzybski's point, quoted in the entry, is that *"the map is not the territory it represents, but, if correct, it has a similar structure to the territory, which accounts for its usefulness."*

**Refuter.** Exhibit a case where a recorded import is indistinguishable in the output from a convergent derivation and no audit of the record can tell them apart, or show that provenance alone settles a claim against the ground. Then F4 falls.

**Mode:** `specified`. **Status:** triangulated across Fig 8, Fig 10 and Fig 6.

### F5 — Concealment mechanism 3: the loop checks its mechanism, not the direction of the question

Fig 5 :63: *"Every check above runs on the MECHANISM and none runs on the DIRECTION OF THE QUESTION"*
Fig 5 :65: *"make the sought-for shape an ORDINARY TERM"*
Fig 5 :66: *"Committing a prediction before a run is this, already working."*

The shape a searcher wants is held as a preference, not as a term. It is unbound, predicts nothing and accrues no residual, so no internal check can see it. A model can therefore be perfectly validated on all its bound terms while the question it was built to answer has been quietly replaced by a proxy it can optimise. The human side does the same thing: the attribute-substitution entry states that a judgment about a target attribute that is *"computationally complex"* is replaced by a *"more easily calculated heuristic attribute"*, and the subject does not notice. Goodhart's law, as quoted by Strathern, adds the institutional version: *"When a measure becomes a target, it ceases to be a good measure."* The remedy in the figures is a discipline, not a step: write the sought-for shape down as an ordinary term before the run and let it accrue a residual.

**Refuter.** Show a model that checks the direction of its own question from inside its own closure, so that its aim is a bound, predicting, residuating term without an external seat. Then the limit in Fig 5 :62-68 is wrong and F5 falls.

**Mode:** `specified`. **Status:** single-source in the figures (Fig 5 Direction amendment) with two independent world corroborations from human cognition and from economics/accountability. Kept separate, never averaged.

### F6 — A gate is not the ground, and settled is necessary but not sufficient

Fig 5 :70: *"A term is settled when the ground pays for it. A term is usable when something can compose over it."*
Fig 5 :69: *"a library that grows is not a reach that grows."*
Fig 10 :18: *"a declared meaning is not a grounded one"*

Cross-validation, a held-out set and a passing benchmark are gates. They price a term; they do not settle it against the ground of the problem the model is meant to help with. Fig 10 :26 names the channel failure directly: *"saturated metrics, pooled readings."* The formal-verification literature gives a clean independent instance: a proof checker has a *"soundness bug"* when it declares a false statement proven, and the field's response is to make the core small enough to hand-check and to use multiple independent checkers (Wikipedia, Proof assistant, 2026, accessed 2026-11-23). That is the figure's verifier rule expressed in an engineering discipline.

**Refuter.** Show that passing a declared gate guarantees ground settlement for the problem the model was built for, with no further contact required. Or show a settled term that cannot be composed over is still a term the model can use. If either holds, F6 falls.

**Mode:** `specified`. **Status:** triangulated across Fig 5, Fig 10 and THE_FORMULA step 5.

### F7 — Independence spent: a checker who reconstructs the claim cannot verify it

Fig 10 :42: *"A verifier that can reconstruct the claim cannot verify it."*
Fig 10 :43: *"Soundness requires the checker to be unable to produce what it is checking."*
Fig 7 :35: *"A room's agreement is variation, not a verdict. Selection between rooms is where the ground votes."*
Fig 9 :51: *"evidence bearing on the residual is admissible; evidence bearing only on its maker is a stake-inducing channel, and the verifier is denied it"*
Fig 9 :60: *"Use filters for the budget and witnesses for the verdict. Never let a filter hand you a verdict."*

A model extends reasoning only while an outside frame can still range against the ground separately. Show the checker the full interiors and its later agreement is the pool talking (Fig 2 :40: *"agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool"*). Fig 10 :59 adds the honest condition: *"an interest in the outcome is unavoidable, an interest in the reading is disqualifying."* Publication venue, citation count and parameter count are budget filters. They decide what gets tried, not whether the model extends reasoning.

**Refuter.** Show that a checker given full access to the model's interiors and author still returns a verdict that is independent in the Fig 2 sense, measured by low overlap of evidence. Then Fig 10 :42 is wrong for this domain and F7 falls.

**Mode:** `specified`. **Status:** triangulated across Fig 10, Fig 7, Fig 9 and Fig 2.

### F8 — Deployment: contact, not capability in isolation

Fig 11 :40: *"An improvement that does not change contact changes nothing, however much it improves."*
Fig 11 :39: *"capability is a property of agent-and-habitat, never of the agent alone."*
Fig 11 :70: *"A test harness is a substituted habitat. Which is why a synthetic solve proves wiring and never capability."*

A model that performs on a benchmark is a model in a substituted habitat. The losses are invisible until the goal fails (Fig 11 :68-69). Extension in the real question requires that the model change the human's contact with the ground: a new measurement, a discriminated source, a refused assumption. A model that only makes the old answer faster is a head start, which Fig 4 permits to travel downward, not a method that raises what the pair can check.

**Refuter.** The figure states its own falsifier: an agent-only improvement that moves the ground would refute the capability claim (Fig 11 :71). Exhibit one and F8 falls.

**Mode:** `specified`. **Status:** single-source in the figures (Fig 11), corroborated by the model-selection and overfitting literature and by the falsifier the figure itself publishes.

### F9 — The honest stop: the framework returns a discriminator shape, not a verdict about any named model

Fig 9 :32: *"you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself"*
Fig 13 :134: *"Unreachable is a verdict here, not a silence"*
Fig 9 :44: *"Only a sealed room can be searched to the end, and no frame is a sealed room. So searching and finding nothing proves nothing."*

No figure line names a particular architecture, field or technique as the extender and another as the concealer. The figures license the *shape* of the discriminator, not a ranking of models. Applying it requires a ground and a checker in each case. This is a stop at the limit of the instruments, with the lineage recorded, not an evasion.

**Refuter.** Produce a figure line that directly names a specific modeling technique as sufficient for extension or concealment, independent of ground, lineage and checker. No such line was found in a full census of the 13 figures and both tables. If one is produced, F9 falls.

**Mode:** `general` on the shape. **Status:** gap in the specific sense, honestly named: the figures do not decide which named model extends reasoning. The general discriminator is triangulated across Figs 1, 8, 10 and 11.

## 4. Pre-registered refuter (written before the conclusion)

Required by Fig 10's discipline and by Seat Map :287: *"a falsifier written before the run is a design instrument, not only an honesty device."*

The discriminator is refuted if any of the following holds:

1. A frame can score itself with a quantity it also produces and that score is admitted as a verdict (breaks Fig 1 :27).
2. Growth in Γ alone increases the composable, settled reach without import or new ground contact (breaks Fig 5 :69, Fig 6 :21).
3. Two readers of the same fully provenanced output derive different sources where no instrument can discriminate them, and the lineage is nevertheless called white box (breaks Fig 8 :51, Fig 13 :141).
4. A checker who can reconstruct the claim returns a verdict that is independent in the Fig 2 sense (breaks Fig 10 :42).
5. An agent-only improvement changes the model's contact with the ground (breaks Fig 11 :40).
6. A model checks the direction of its own question from inside its closure, with its aim a bound predicting term (breaks Fig 5 :62-68).
7. A figure line is produced that names a specific technique as sufficient independent of ground, lineage and checker (breaks F9).

If any fires, the discriminator is withdrawn or narrowed as the finding requires. Until one is run, no part of this audit may be labelled `grounded`.

## 5. Recommendation

1. Lead with the discriminator and its refuter, not with the framework. Fig 10 :52: *"Bring a characterised residual, never the source."*
2. Require per-slot residuals against a named ground; reject a single global score as a verdict (F1).
3. Report reach and settlement separately from parameter count and benchmark score (F2, F6).
4. Publish the bonds and the imports, with source gate and shadow test, so convergent derivation is distinguishable from adopted import (F3, F4).
5. Write the sought-for shape down as an ordinary term before the run, and commit the prediction (F5).
6. Keep at least one checker who cannot reconstruct the claim; record their verdict before showing any theory (F7).
7. Test in the habitat, not only in a harness; name what contact changed (F8).
8. Do not name a winner. The framework returns a shape, and the ground decides (F9).
9. Fig 4 :25: *"A recording carried upward looks like knowledge and is a description of one occasion."* Send the generator, meaning the residual, the discriminator and the refuter, not a conclusion.

## 7. Seven-question recursive derivation (summary)

1. **Is a model a frame?** Yes. Symbols table :111 names a model as a bounded system with a library. This sets the census, not the verdict.
2. **What is the metric?** The per-slot residual against the ground (Fig 1 :19/:23), not coverage, compression, predicates minted or credibility accrued (Fig 1 :25-27).
3. **Does more machinery extend?** Only if reach grows. MINT cannot add an atom (Fig 6 :21); import moves the wall (Fig 6 :14) and requires two gates (Fig 6 :12-13).
4. **Where do assumptions hide?** In the unrecorded bond (Fig 12 :5), in an import mistaken for a derivation (Fig 8 :51), and in a preference that was never bound as a term (Fig 5 :62-64).
5. **Can the model check this itself?** No for direction (Fig 5 :62-68) and no for its own output as a measure of success (Fig 5 :48). An outside frame is required, and it must not hold the interiors (Fig 10 :42).
6. **What settles it?** The ground, not agreement (Fig 2 :45, Fig 9 :42). A gate is not the ground (THE_FORMULA step 5).
7. **Route.** Describe R first (Fig 9 :27-28), keep the four domains separate, use filters only for the budget (Fig 9 :60), stop at the shape and record the lineage (Fig 13 :134-141).

After re-census: one narrowed reading (self-metrics as filters), one re-census (concealment moved from Fig 5 to D3), one honest gap (no named technique). Strains hidden: none.
