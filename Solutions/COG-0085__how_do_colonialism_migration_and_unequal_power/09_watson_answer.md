## QUESTION

Decide, in effect terms, what shapes the survival of a knowledge item in a contact zone. Option A: treat colonialism, migration and unequal power as three independent causes of a culture-wide outcome and report which knowledge was preserved or destroyed. Option B: keep the residual indexed per knowledge slot, treat the three conditions as a union, and return a per-domain separator plus a declared `gap` for the inventory. What each leads to: A aggregates away the signal about where the loss occurred, averages different anchors, and manufactures a verdict no instrument in this run can settle; B returns a checkable shape, per-domain verdicts with refuters, and an instrument-shaped stop.

## DOMAIN CENSUS

R by arity, symmetry and scale, then one line per domain with actors / currency / anchor, partition or union, and order by dependency.

- **R (the residual).** At a knowledge slot, the record predicts the item will still be there and still be usable; observation gives continued guidance, inert inscription, or departure from transmission. Arity: three overlapping conditions, so a union compound (Fig 9 :57, "a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either"). Symmetry: preservation is continued settlement, dismissal is a refusal (¬), so they are not inverse operations (Operators :58). Scale: first order within a community of use, second order when a record crosses an institutional boundary.
- **Domain 1, certification and power.** Actors: institutions, colleges, journals, funders, the dominant language. Currency: credibility and the authority to certify. Anchor: whether the knowledge still pays at its slot to what it is about. Question type: partition boundary, does the reference update on the frame or range against a ground.
- **Domain 2, movement and habitat.** Actors: migrants, elders, families, teachers, languages, rituals, curricula. Currency: contact. Anchor: whether the receiving habitat reproduced what the knowledge's goal required. Question type: union with D1 and D3.
- **Domain 3, closure and import.** Actors: knowers and their frames, held atoms and bonds, the seven operators. Currency: atoms and bonds. Anchor: the ground settling a minted term. Question type: partition at the wall, what composes inside a closure and what only import can move.
- **Domain 4, record and lineage.** Actors: archives, catalogues, curricula, citation chains. Currency: provenance. Anchor: can a later reader locate the route and the error. Question type: union with D1, a record can be both method and credential.
- **Order by dependency.** State R per slot; secure a term for the bond; determine the anchor in D1 and D2; only then settle verdicts. Fig 3 :20: "The chain is ordered by dependency, not by importance. Each link consumes what the one above produces, so effort spent above the break arrives at a step that cannot use it." The likely break is the objective link, where a field can name an item and has no instrument for what its survival would be.

Strain test: the Fig 13 six-term substrate governs physical primitives; knowledge items are terms in a social frame's library. Assigning each figure to its proper object removes the apparent contradiction. Re-census recorded in `01` section 7.

## VERDICT (from the figures)

The shape survives; the inventory does not exist in this run. Unequal power removes the reference that does not update on the certifying frame, so the record drifts toward influence and its consensus is variation rather than a verdict. Migration substitutes a habitat, and survival is reproduction of what the knowledge's goal required, with two silent failures. Extraction carries a recording while the method and the typed arrangement are left behind, so each lineage re-derives what it appears to have inherited. Dismissal is a negation of a held classification, and a record without ¬ can only accumulate. Which forms survived at a named place is a `gap` (Fig 9 :32, Fig 13 :134), closed by a source-discriminating reading and independent frames, not by more agreement. This is `specified`, ungrounded, candidate.

- **cost if wrong:** bounded and locatable but not trivial. If the missing-anchor reading is wrong, a researcher could treat an institution's consensus as evidence and miss the divergence the ground would show. If the method/recording reading is wrong, a revival could be dismissed as a replay when it actually carried the arrangement. The larger avoidable cost is spending an outside reader's independence by sending the framework before the refuter (Fig 10 :42); Fig 2 :42 prices that ("Buy independence in proportion to the cost of being wrong."). The packet in `08` keeps the cost low by leading with the shape and its refuters.
- **undoable:** yes. The four domain verdicts are stated separately and can be retracted one at a time without touching the others. A refutation of the fixed-atom isomer analog in `06` leaves the union, anchor, habitat and bond claims intact. No primitive has to be withdrawn, and no historical verdict was issued that would need correction in the field.
- **reversed by:** a ground reading, not agreement. A source-discriminating instrument at a named slot showing that the knowledge still guides action independently of the certifier would settle the anchor question; evidence that a receiving habitat reproduced none of the goal's conditions and the knowledge still settled would break the habitat verdict; a recording shown to carry its typed arrangement with no re-derivation would break the method verdict; a verbatim figure line naming a surviving form, or a ground reading of the inventory, would close the `gap`. Prestige, consensus and a clean provenance are not such instruments (Fig 10 :65-66, Fig 2 :40).
