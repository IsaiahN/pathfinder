# Union, bond and order in knowledge survival

**Mode: `specified` on the structural claims, `general` on the analogy, not `grounded`.** Simulated derivation checked for internal consistency against the figures, not a ground reading (see `05_what_shape_means.md` section 7).

## RESIDUAL

The audit returned a four-condition shape and one `gap`. Two questions remain. First, what joins the conditions: a partition of independent causes, or a union with its own compound cases? Second, what is the unit that travels when knowledge survives: an item in a list, or a bonded arrangement with an order and a route? This file answers those, then states a falsifier for the analogy.

## DOMAIN CENSUS (recap, not a replacement)

Atoms held in a social frame's library: knowledge items as terms. Bonds held: the seven typed relations plus negation. Currency: description bits and actions (Fig 12 :125-126). Anchor: the ground at a slot. The physical six-term substrate of Fig 13 is not remapped onto social knowledge; that earlier reading was retracted in `01` section 6. No new primitive is available without import, and import adds no atom to the world (Fig 6 :25).

## Q1 — Partition or union?

Fig 9 :54: "And when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again." Fig 9 :55-58: "A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones. A single lever clears the region where only one condition holds and leaves the intersections standing, because the second condition still binds. What remains is smaller and it is also categorically different: a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either."

Colonialism, migration and unequal power overlap in the same slot. A displaced speaker in a former colony is not in three boxes; she is in a compound whose anchor must be determined separately. The practical consequence is that a policy lever aimed at one condition (say, adding a language to a curriculum) clears the region where only certification binds and leaves the habitat and bond intersections standing, because those conditions still bind. The residue after such a lever is not a smaller version of the same problem; it is a specific compound.

**Falsifier for Q1:** show that the three conditions define disjoint populations with no shared slots, and that one lever clears all regions including intersections. The union claim would then fail.

## Q2 — Ingredient or bond?

Fig 12 :5: "A set of ingredients is not one. The bond is the rest of the information, and it is the half nothing was recording." Fig 12 :46: "A bond is the typed relation between two operands. There are seven, they are not interchangeable, and which one holds is not recoverable from the operands." Fig 12 :94: "The arrangement is the substance."

A list of preserved items (a language, a ritual, a text) records the ingredients and not the relation that made them work. Fig 12 :111: "A vocabulary without bonds can name a state and cannot name a route to one." Fig 12 :112: "A plan is a sequence. A goal is a comparison. Progress is a subtraction." Knowledge survival is a route, so a bond vocabulary is required. Fig 4 :25 supplies the failure mode: "A recording carried upward looks like knowledge and is a description of one occasion."

The relevant bonds here are `→` sequence (a curriculum is ordered), `⇒` production (teaching produces a new knower), `−` subtraction (a practice is removed from a programme) and `¬` negation (a held classification is refused). Fig 12 :112 and Operators :58: "a refutation is a negation, which is why a vocabulary without it can only accumulate." A canon that holds only `+` and `−` can list and remove items and cannot record that its own account of an item is false. That is the structural reason dismissals are silent in records that are otherwise meticulous.

**Falsifier for Q2:** show a knowledge item whose survival is fully determined by its ingredient set, with the bond recoverable from the operands and no route required. The bond claim would then be idle for this question.

## Q3 — Are two knowledge systems isomers?

Fig 12 :117: "isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour." Fig 12 :123: "functional group. A substructure whose presence fixes the outcome. The group says what kind; the qualifier says which member."

Take a fixed set of knowledge items held in two communities. If both communities hold the same items but arrange them under different bonds, for example one teaching a practice as a sequence of steps and the other as a comparison against a living standard, then over that fixed atom set the two are **isomers**: same atoms, different bond, different substance, with the difference decisive in behaviour even though an inventory of items cannot see it. If the two communities hold different items but the same `state → structure → state` teaching form, they share a **functional group** and are not isomers.

This is the strongest structural claim the figures allow here, and it comes with a live type strain. Migration and colonial contact add and drop atoms: new actors, new media, new institutions. Fig 11 :61: "The seat's power is asymmetric. Introduction is available; exclusion is not." So the fixed-set condition is rarely met in a real contact zone. The isomer claim is therefore conditional and must be stated as such: *over a fixed atom set, two arrangements of the same knowledge items are isomers*. Where the atom set has changed, the relation is composition plus import, not isomerism.

**Falsifier for Q3, written before the run:** if either system can be written without an atom the other requires, the isomer claim fails and the functional-group reading stands instead. If the bond is recoverable from the operands, the difference is not a bond difference and the claim fails for that reason too. This is the same test the exemplar uses when it refuses to call two physical theories isomers over unequal atom sets.

## Q4 — What order, and where does the chain break?

Fig 3 :20: "The chain is ordered by dependency, not by importance. Each link consumes what the one above produces, so effort spent above the break arrives at a step that cannot use it." Fig 3 :24-27: "Why the middle link is where chains usually break. Perception has obvious failures and obvious tests. Planning has obvious failures and obvious tests. The link between them, having terms for what a situation offers and being able to say what would count as progress, has neither, so it attracts the least attention and the fewest instruments. A link with no instrument is not a link that works. It is a link nobody has looked at."

For knowledge survival the likely break is between naming an item (perception / vocabulary) and saying what would count as its survival (objective). A programme can list a language and have no instrument for whether speakers still use it to solve new problems. Effort spent adding more listed items lands above the break and cannot be used by the missing objective. Fig 3 :22-23: "A step whose input never arrives cannot be diagnosed, only its predecessor can. Which is why a reading taken below the break is not a weak reading. It is a reading of nothing."

Order at the second level: Fig 12 :103: "Composition is recursive, and the level decides what is irreducible." Fig 12 :104: "A settled molecule becomes an atom for whatever composes over it." A knowledge practice that still settles at the ground becomes an atom for the next level (a curriculum, a legal recognition, a diaspora institution). One that never settled cannot be composed over, no matter how often it is cited: Fig 12 :106: "Which is why a system that cannot settle has one level however large its library. Nothing composes over what was never admitted as a part."

Two structures worth watching for: Fig 12 :119 "catalyst. A part that survives its own use." A teacher or an elder can be a catalyst, and a spent part that is no longer regenerating behaves differently across uses while the inventory records both as "present". Fig 12 :121 "cycle. The output re-enters as input." A transmission chain in which each cohort teaches the next is a cycle; a chain cannot close by construction, so a one-off documentation project cannot substitute for it.

**Falsifier for Q4:** show an instrument at the objective link that already returns a reading of survival independent of the item list, or show a cited but unsettled practice serving successfully as an atom at the next level. Either would move the break and require re-census.

## Q5 — Can a revival be a replay?

Fig 4 :23: "upward: a method that can be reapplied. Never the recording of a particular success." Fig 4 :26: "Replayed downward, it produces a system that repeats a past success and cannot produce a new one." Fig 11 :66: "Isolation is not removal of the habitat. It is substitution of one habitat for another." Fig 11 :67: "Move the residual and you must reproduce everything its goal required, and you inherit the new habitat's actors, which you did not choose."

A revival that installs the recorded form into a substituted habitat without reproducing the conditions the goal required is a replay. It will look like preservation in the record and will fail when the goal is tested. Fig 11 :68-69 names the two silent failures: "what you failed to reproduce is invisible until the goal fails" and "what you unintentionally introduced is invisible until it acts." This is not an argument against revival. It is a specification of what a revival must reproduce and what it must watch for.

**Falsifier for Q5:** show a revival that installs a recorded form, reproduces none of the goal's conditions, and still settles at the ground across changing conditions. That would break the method/recording distinction for this domain.

## CONCLUSION

The three conditions form a union, so verdicts stay per slot and one lever leaves the intersections standing. What travels is a bond, not an ingredient list. Over a fixed atom set, two arrangements of the same knowledge items are isomers; with a changed atom set the relation is composition plus import, and the fixed-set condition is a live strain. The chain likely breaks between naming an item and having an instrument for what its survival would be, so more inventories land above the break. A revived form is a method only if the receiving habitat reproduces what the goal required. Every structural claim here carries a falsifier, and none depends on a new primitive.
