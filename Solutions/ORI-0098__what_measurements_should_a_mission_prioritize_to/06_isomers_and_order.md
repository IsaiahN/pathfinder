## DERIVATION

### Q1 - Is "past or present life" a partition or a union?

- Fig 9 :54: *"And when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again."*
- Fig 9 :56-58: a bundled problem is "a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones"; a single lever "leaves the intersections standing, because the second condition still binds"; *"a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either. It stops being a mixture and becomes a specific compound."*
- Present life produces traces that persist after death, so "present" overlaps "past." Abiotic processes and forward contamination can mimic either. The two conditions overlap and bind one another.
- ⇒ The headline is a **union compound**, not two separable questions and not one hard question. It must be unbundled before search. This is also what *The Library of Babel* warns about: *"Perhaps your problem was a bundle or conflation of several problems masquerading as one?"*
- **Falsifier:** if a single measurement class has the same false-positive structure, the same expected-detection condition and the same null meaning under both past-life and present-life hypotheses, the split is unnecessary and the union reading is refuted. Mark `specified`, not measured.

### Q2 - What order do the measurement roles take?

- Fig 3 :4-5: link 1, perception: *"the system can tell what is in front of it, and tell it apart from what was there before."*
- Fig 3 :6-7: link 2, vocabulary: *"it has terms for what things afford: what can be moved, opened, joined, blocked."*
- Fig 3 :9-10: link 3, objective: *"it can say what would count as progress here, in its own terms."*
- Fig 3 :19-21: *"Optimising a link that already works cannot rescue one that does not. The chain is ordered by dependency, not by importance. Each link consumes what the one above produces, so effort spent above the break arrives at a step that cannot use it."*
- Fig 3 :26-27: *"A link with no instrument is not a link that works. It is a link nobody has looked at."*
- ⇒ The order is **perception, then vocabulary, then objective, then planning** (instrument selection as planning). It is not an importance ranking. The middle links (vocabulary and objective) have the fewest instruments, so they are the first priority by dependency, not the last.
- This contradicts the usual mission-development order, where the payload is chosen first and the science traceability matrix is written backwards from it. Under Fig 3 that is effort above the break.
- **Falsifier:** a mission that selects its instrument suite first and only afterwards states context and disconfirmation criteria, yet whose result bears on the ground. If one can be shown, dependency order is not mandatory.

### Q3 - Are the three structural axes isomers or one frame?

- Fig 13 :35-37: *"Space, time and energy are not three readings of one quantity. Neither are spectrum, gradient and network. Each answers a question the other two cannot"*; :20 *"ordering, so more and less can be said"*; :22 *"a difference that can be spent"*; :24 *"that entities relate, and influence travels."*
- Fig 12 :117: *"isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."*
- Fig 12 :124: *"functional group. A substructure whose presence fixes the outcome. The group says what kind; the qualifier says which member."*
- Reading the three measurement axes over the full shared atom set (Space, Time, Energy, Spectrum, Gradient, Network):
  - **Spectrum over Energy**: mass distributions, isotopic ratios, size-frequency spectra. The structure is ordering.
  - **Gradient over Space**: redox disequilibrium, pH, chemical or thermal potential across distance. The structure is spendable difference.
  - **Network over Time**: co-occurrence, correlation and influence through a sequence of samples. The structure is relation.
- Same six atoms, different bonds, different measurement substances. Under Fig 12 :117 they are **isomers** of the substrate. The weaker form, that all three share the motif *state -> structure -> state*, makes them one **functional group** (Fig 12 :124). The stronger isomer claim is the one worth testing.
- **Falsifier, written before the run:** if any one axis can be derived from another under a declared code with no additional instrument, for example if network structure is fully recoverable from a spectrum alone, the isomer claim fails and only the functional-group reading stands. The figure definition decides; the physics is unmeasured here. Status `specified`, triangulated for the figure definition, single-source for the mission mapping.

### Q4 - What does the order cost in two currencies?

- Fig 12 :125-126: *"A composition costs in two currencies, and they do not add. Bits price the description. Actions price finding out whether it holds."*
- Fig 12 :127: *"They are in different units, so a system holding one accounting cannot compare them"*
- A measurement suite pays **bits** for distinct axes, resolution and the description of competing abiotic nulls. It pays **actions** for sample acquisition, drilling, imaging dwell time, calibration repeats and return. A suite with many bits and no actions cannot reach a buried or transient target. A suite with deep access and one bit cannot discriminate a source.
- The two readings come apart in both directions (Fig 12 :128-129): a long chain of cheap parts is expensive to describe and may cost one action to refute; a short arrangement over a costly operand is cheap to state and may take a whole budget to settle. Summing them into one "science value" is the named error, not the fix.
- **Falsifier:** a common unit in which bits and actions are commensurable without loss, preserving both orderings. If exhibited, this finding is withdrawn.

### Q5 - Analogues from the world literature (secondary only)

1. **The Ladder's rungs** (NASA Neveu et al. 2018, via NASA public pages) are an ordering by decreasing strength of evidence and increasing ease of measurement, with explicit false-positive, false-negative and specificity columns. That is a world analogue of Fig 3's dependency ordering plus Fig 2's independence, arrived at in mission-design practice. It corroborates; it does not decide.
2. **The paleogeology absence case** (Brewer 2025) supplies an analogue for Q1 and for null handling: geologists unbundle the presence question into auxiliary hypotheses about preservation, and treat absence as evidence of absence only when evidence was expected. This is Fig 9's unbundling plus Fig 8 :39's null rule, restated in a historical science. Low overlap with the framework, so useful corroboration.
3. **Assembly theory** (Sharma et al. 2023) supplies a physical instance of Q3's isomer claim: many identical copies of a high-assembly object are evidence of selection, not merely the ingredient list. It corroborates the bond-over-atom reading.

## CONCLUSION

The headline question is a union compound and must be split. The measurement roles are ordered by dependency (perception, vocabulary, objective, then planning), not by importance, and the least-instrumented links are the first priority. The three structural axes are isomers over the shared six-term substrate, which is what makes them independent witnesses rather than redundant spellings. The whole ordering is priced in two non-additive currencies. Each sub-claim has a falsifier above.

This rescues what is defensible in the ordinary "prioritise the best biosignature" framing without accepting it: there is a real ordering, but it is an ordering of roles and dependencies, not of named signifiers.
