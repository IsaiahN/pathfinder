# The Tether's answer: what measurements a mission should prioritise

**Mode: `specified` on the ordering, `general` on the shape. Not `grounded`.** This is a dependency rule and a set of falsifiers, not a measurement and not a mission design. Simulated derivation checked for internal consistency against the figures (`05_what_shape_means.md` §7), not a ground reading.

## 1. The residual it answers

A mission has finite mass, power, time and sample access. It must order candidate measurements so that the result, positive or negative, can bear on the question of past or present life. The residual is the gap between what a closed instrument set can represent and what would settle that question at the ground. It is a **union compound**, because "past" and "present" overlap and bind one another (Fig 9 :54, :58-59), not one hard question.

## 2. The shape and the ordering

1. **Describe the residual before naming instruments.** Fig 9 :27-28: *"describe it before you go looking, or any frame you pick will seem to fit"*; Fig 10 :52: *"Bring a characterised residual, never the source."* State which trace, at what scale, under which abiotic null, at which sample slot. A payload list that precedes this is the inverted order.

2. **Split the union.** Past-life and present-life inquiries have different false-positive structures and different null meanings. Fig 9 :54: *"And when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again."*

3. **Secure the lower dependency links first.** Fig 3 :20-21: *"The chain is ordered by dependency, not by importance. Each link consumes what the one above produces, so effort spent above the break arrives at a step that cannot use it."* The order is perception (tell what is in front of the sensor and distinguish it from prior state), vocabulary (what the environment affords: water, gradient, energy, preservation), objective (what would count as progress, stated as a comparison), then planning (instrument selection). Fig 3 :26-27: *"A link with no instrument is not a link that works. It is a link nobody has looked at."* The middle links are the first priority.

4. **Buy independence between measurements, not agreement among interpreters.** Fig 2 :31-32: *"Independence, between two frames, is how little they draw on the same evidence. Two people reading the same report are not two frames; two instruments pointed at the same thing are."* Fig 2 :35: *"Alignment is triangulation, not negotiation. A mirror cannot correct you."* Fig 2 :40: *"disqualifying for a verdict: agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool"* Fig 2 :42: *"Buy independence in proportion to the cost of being wrong."* Because a false life claim is costly, buy more independent closures.

5. **Use the three structural axes, which are independent by construction.** Fig 13 :35-37: *"Space, time and energy are not three readings of one quantity. Neither are spectrum, gradient and network. Each answers a question the other two cannot"* So: ordering (mass/isotopic/size spectra), spendable difference (redox, pH, thermal or chemical gradients), and relation (co-occurrence and influence across samples). Three variants of one spectrometer are one axis.

6. **Price bonds over ingredients.** Fig 12 :5: *"A set of ingredients is not one. The bond is the rest of the information, and it is the half nothing was recording."* Fig 12 :117: *"isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."* Enantiomeric excess across several amino acids, fractionation departing from equilibrium, a polymer with repeating charge, and correlated distributions carry information an ingredient inventory does not. This is the metamerism point of `05_what_shape_means.md` §4: a reading cannot comment on its source.

7. **Fund the channel.** Fig 10 :24: *"the ground does not decay, the channel does"* Fig 10 :27-28: *"the output is not a verdict, it is you have lost touch"* Fig 11 :61: *"Introduction is available; exclusion is not. You can add an actor and you cannot subtract the habitat, because contact is a fact about the world and does not consult you."* Negative controls, calibration, sample preservation, and forward-contamination control are first-order science. A lost channel is not a weak verdict, it is the loss of the anchor.

8. **Sharpen what already fails to resolve.** Fig 6 :32: *"An instrument is not built from a description. It is improved from a worse instrument already returning something."* Fig 6 :34-35: *"The question is not whether a sensor could exist. It is whether anything, at any resolution, is already returning something that fails to resolve."* If a signature has never registered at any resolution, that is a proto-instrument search, a different and cheaper inquiry.

9. **Report nulls as unreached.** Fig 8 :39: *"So a search that finds nothing is never a proof of absence."* Fig 13 :134: *"Unreachable is a verdict here, not a silence,"* The honest null names the kind of thing that would have been needed, in effect terms. World sources sharpen the condition: a null carries weight only when evidence was highly expected (Brewer 2025), so the instrument set's expected detection must be stated.

10. **Keep two budgets.** Fig 12 :125-126: *"A composition costs in two currencies, and they do not add. Bits price the description. Actions price finding out whether it holds."* Do not merge them into one science-value score.

11. **Report per slot.** Fig 1 :19: *"The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated."* Fig 1 :22: *"Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average."* One unresolved sample must remain visible when every other slot is quiet.

## 3. What it explicitly does not say

- Not a ranking of named instruments, molecules or targets. The framework returns a shape, not a name. Which molecule occupies a slot needs a ground reading or a provenanced import.
- Not a claim that one biosignature is decisive. No single reading can comment on its own source (metamerism).
- Not that "no life detected" means "no life." A null is a statement about a closed instrument set.
- Not that agreement among reviewers, mission boards or published papers is corroboration. Agreement inside one evidence pool is one witness.
- Not a mission design, not a mass/power budget, and not a target-selection recommendation.
- Not `grounded`. No measurement in this folder pays for any claim.

## 4. What would settle it: the ground

A source-discriminating comparison, not a further argument. From *The Hard Problem is About Source*: an instrument that reads green *"cannot comment on the source"*; the spectrometer resolved it. Here the corresponding tests are:

1. **Source separation:** can the measurement set distinguish biology from abiotic synthesis and from terrestrial contamination for the same reading, under declared competing nulls? A result that cannot is a projection, not a detection.
2. **Independence audit:** do the three axes draw on different physical processes? If two collapse to one under a declared code, the independence claim fails.
3. **Expected-detection statement:** for each null, what would the instrument have returned if the target were present? Without this, a null carries no weight (Brewer 2025; MacKenzie 2011).
4. **Channel check:** did calibration, blanks and preservation hold throughout? A saturated or pooled reading is loss of contact, not a verdict (Fig 10 :25-28).
5. Standing falsifiers: any of the six in `01_figure_grounded_audit.md` §5.

## 5. The honest stop

**Unreached, not unreachable.** The ordering is the shape any answer must have. It does not tell us which instrument will work at a particular target, because that depends on a ground reading we do not have. The six-term substrate is a visible guess, not nature's own list; declaring it makes the choice visible, not neutral. The answer stops at the limit of the available instruments and records the lineage so the stop is checkable. *"Indiscernibility is not identity."*

## 6. One-paragraph version for outreach

> The Tether does not name the best biosignature for a life detection mission. It orders the measurement roles. Describe the residual first and split past life from present life, because they are two conditions with different false-positive structures. Secure context, vocabulary and a stated disconfirmation criterion before any high-rung signifier, since the chain is ordered by dependency and the least-instrumented links are the ones to look at first. Buy independence between measurements rather than agreement among interpreters: ordering (spectra), spendable difference (gradients) and relation (networks) are three structurally different axes, not three readings of one quantity. Price arrangement above ingredient presence, because the same atoms under a different bond are a different substance. Fund contamination control, calibration and preservation, because the ground does not decay but the channel does. Sharpen instruments already returning something that fails to resolve, and report a null as unreached with a named gap rather than as absence. That is a shape and a dependency rule, not a payload. The ground decides which instrument fills it.
