## DERIVATION

### Q1 - Are UV, lightning, heat and chemical gradients isomers? NO

- Fig 12 :117 "isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."
- Isomerism requires the same atoms under different bonds. The four candidates do not share an atom set: a photon, an electrical discharge, geothermal heat and a pH or redox gradient are different world operands. One of them, the gradient, is a structure while the others are energy inputs (Fig 13 :14, :22).
- ⇒ The four are **not isomers**. Calling them "alternative energy sources" is the flat formula of Fig 12 :5, not an isomer claim.
- **Falsifier, written before the run:** show that all four reduce to the same atom, for example a common electrochemical potential difference, with only the bond or arrangement differing. If that reduction succeeds, the isomer claim becomes live and this finding fails.

### Q2 - Do they form a functional group? YES, weakly

- Fig 12 :124 "functional group. A substructure whose presence fixes the outcome. The group says what kind; the qualifier says which member."
- All four share the functional role of supplying work to a non-equilibrium chemical system. The group says "energy input"; the qualifier distinguishes "photonic", "electrical", "thermal" and "gradient".
- But the group does not equalise them. Fig 13 :76 "Energy and a gradient compose a flow." The qualifier "gradient" names the spendable structure, while the other three name inputs that may or may not be coupled into one. So the functional-group reading is correct but insufficient for the ranking.
- **Falsifier:** show that one of the four cannot be expressed as an energy input to any prebiotic transition (for example, that a gradient only names a state and never supplies work). The group then loses a member.

### Q3 - Are two prebiotic pathways isomers? YES, as a testable structural claim

The literature offers at least two route families that use overlapping feedstocks:

- A UV-driven cyanosulfidic network, described in Rimmer et al. (2021) and in Todd, Szostak and Sasselov (2021), using HCN, sulfite or sulfide, phosphate and light.
- An alkaline-vent redox and pH-gradient route, described in Westall et al. (2018) and criticised in Jackson (2017), using H2, CO2, Fe(Ni)S minerals and natural gradients.

Both use one-carbon feedstocks, reduced sulfur or iron, and phosphate chemistry. Over the shared atom set {H, C, N, O, S, P, Fe, Ni} they are candidates for Fig 12's isomer relation: same atoms, different bond and arrangement, different substance and different downstream products. Fig 13 :121 "Different routes can arrive at the same substance." That line licenses the possibility; it does not assert that these two routes do.

- **Falsifier, written before the run:** if either route requires an atom the other cannot in principle use (for example, a pathway that needs a photon as an external operand and one in which photons are destructive and unusable), the isomer claim fails and the weaker functional-group reading stands. Todd et al. (2021) already supply evidence that UV is destructive for some intermediates, which is a live threat to this claim, not a settled refutation.
- Status: `specified`, ungrounded, single-source for each route, triangulated for the legality of the isomer form.

### Q4 - What order does the question imply? Dependency, not chronology

- Fig 12 :75 "A then B; B needs A to have happened" is the sequence bond. Writing "UV, then lightning, then heat, then gradients" would commit to a chronology the figures and the sources do not give. Fig 13 :75 "Two states never bond directly. It is not space-time but space, a structure, time; and two structures join only through a state." The order in the substrate is cross-kind, not a list of stacked inputs.
- The legitimate order is dependency. Fig 3 :20 "The chain is ordered by dependency, not by importance." Fig 3 :19 "Optimising a link that already works cannot rescue one that does not." For prebiotic chemistry the dependency chain is: supply of feedstock, concentration and activation, synthesis of monomers, polymerisation, compartment formation, then a coupling that lets the system persist. An energy input matters at the link it supplies.
- **Falsifier:** if the transitions are shown to be independent, so that monomer synthesis, activation and compartment formation can each be driven in isolation without ordering, the dependency claim fails and a simultaneous ranking becomes possible.
- The literature supports dependency without settling chronology. Westall et al. (2018) list concentration, stabilisation and complexification as three critical steps. Deamer and Weber (2010) separate monomer synthesis, condensation and metabolic energy flow into three kinds of energy use. Neither establishes a single historical sequence.

### Q5 - Cycle, catalyst and the persistence of a gradient

- Fig 12 :119 "catalyst. A part that survives its own use." Mineral surfaces and Fe(Ni)S barriers play this role in the vent literature; Jackson (2017) disputes whether the proposed inorganic membrane can do the work claimed.
- Fig 12 :121 "cycle. The output re-enters as input. The sign of the loop decides whether it closes, and a chain cannot close by construction." Spitzer, Pielak and Poolman (2015) argue that diurnal cycling of solar radiation, temperature and water activity keeps mixtures "repeatedly stoked with energy". Westall et al. (2018) describe wet-dry and tidal settings.
- A chemical gradient is not a one-shot input. It is maintained, so it behaves closer to the catalyst or cycle structures than to a consumed photon. This is the structural reason a single ranking against photons is a category error.
- **Falsifier:** show that the gradient in question is consumed in one step and not maintained, in which case it should be ranked as a one-shot input alongside photons and the structural distinction weakens.

## CONCLUSION

The four candidates are not isomers and are only weakly a functional group. Two full prebiotic route families are a real, untested isomer claim over a shared atom set, with a written falsifier. The order implicit in the question is dependency order, not chronology, and it is the dependency link that decides an input's importance. Gradients behave structurally like maintained cycles or catalysts rather than consumed energy packets, which reinforces F1 of `01_figure_grounded_audit.md`: the question has put a structure in a list of states.
