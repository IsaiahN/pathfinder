## 1. A shape is a constraint, not a filler

The canonical figures README states the limit: the framework returns "what a missing thing would have to look like (a shape, not a name)". Fig 9 :32: "you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself". Fig 13 says it "can say the answer is out of reach with the library as it stands... It cannot say which primitive is missing, because naming it would mean already holding it."

From *The Library of Babel*: "you can cut your search short if the set of books returned is small and descriptive enough that it provides a general shape of the answer that satisfies your question."

So a **shape** is the narrowed form of an answer you can state before you can name it. It constrains; it does not fill. For this question, "a licensed substitution between two readings" is a shape. "Use reference material X, protocol Y and tolerance Z" is a name.

## 2. How the shape is obtained

- *The Library of Babel*: use the vocabulary you already have that is closest to the thing, look for recurring new terms that overlap or correlate with it, recurse. "With each new term gained, you narrow the space and deepen your vocabulary."
- Fig 6 :30: "The description travels. Fails by being sharpened into terms only we can read. Cast it in effect terms." Fig 9 :30-31: "your words are borrowed too, so describe the effect rather than the cause."
- *Decoding Tacit Knowledge*: "When contact isn't possible, the best you can send is a plain description of what you'd expect to see, because anyone can check that against the world."
- *The Library of Babel* on stopping: the answer is reached when "the finish line will also be the starting place", and the paradox is that by then you have "composed the answer through the process of locating it."

For this question the effect description comes first: what decision the number must support, at what scale, for which object slot, under which perturbation a wrong substitution would be detected. The reference material is selected after that description, not before (Fig 9 :26-28).

## 3. What licenses a name

Only two things, both outside the shape itself:

1. **The ground settles it.** `THE_FORMULA.md` step 5: "the ground settles it. Verified only by what pays. A gate is not the ground." The figures README: the framework "stops at the limit of what the available instruments can distinguish, and records the lineage so the stop is honest."
2. **A provenanced import from a frame whose closure differs** (Fig 6 :23, Fig 8 :50-52), with a source gate and a shadow test.

*They're Scanning Books When They Should* sharpens the second: "The real import is not a new term in the existing space. It is a new dimension of the space." And: "A minimum viable set of six terms is a human's careful guess at reality's compression axes; another sensorium's minimum set need not be those six." Also: "Declaring the primitives does not make them neutral. It makes the choice visible."

A shared unit or protocol is a visible choice. Declaring it does not make it a ground. Naming one as *the* answer to comparability turns a visible choice into an invisible one.

## 4. Why two equal numbers need not be the same measurement

*The Hard Problem is About Source* gives the mechanism. Metamerism: an instrument reads green and "cannot comment on the source" (one green light, or yellow plus blue, or RGB, or CMYK, or a scheme not yet discovered). "If several paths create the same behavior, then behavior is a projection with lossy information." And: "An instrument that can't distinguish these cases demonstrates its own inadequacy, it cannot claim anything substantial about what is being measured or what causes it." It was the spectrometer, not an argument, that resolved it.

*How Intelligence Actually Works* adds the video-frame-versus-photograph case: "The frame and the photograph are indistinguishable as artifacts, and they carry different warrants... And nothing in the image records which." Provenance loss is the central issue, and "indiscernibility is not identity." The lab-grown versus mined diamond contrast is the other half: isotope ratios are a real trace, so a tool can recover the distinction "because a trace existed, and not because a better tool was created." Some comparability questions will close with a discriminator; others will only stay open. That is a case-dependent fact, not a verdict about all measurements.

This is the exact error a bare comparison of readings makes: it reads the projection and names the source. What is owed is a source-discriminating instrument or a recorded lineage, not a vote on the number.

## 5. Q: For this question, does "shape, not a name" forbid naming a standard?

**ANSWERED.** Nothing in the framework names a universal reference artifact, protocol or tolerance. The framework licenses only the shape: non-updating anchor, independence between frames, a convention that makes the disagreement statable without deciding it, a round trip with a measured gap R_T, and a lineage that travels with the number. A particular standard is licensed only by a ground settling a minted term, or by a provenanced import with a shadow test. Until one of those, it is the searcher's own contribution and must be recorded as such.

**Does naming a standard commit the forbidden error?** Yes, if it is presented as the framework's answer. The canonical README says the framework returns "a shape, not a name". Fig 9 :32 says "you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself." A named standard is drawn from the metrology frame, not derived from the figures.

## 5b. Correction to my own §3 and §5

I initially overstated the rule as "the framework can never name anything." The README's phrase is "what a **missing** thing would have to look like (a shape, not a name)." It governs a primitive not held in Gamma. A unit, a protocol or a reference material is not a missing primitive of the six-term substrate; it is a binding or an import from the metrology frame. What remains wrong is calling such a binding figure-derived, or claiming it moves the wall (Fig 6: "IMPORT adds an atom" - a binding adds none). My §4 metamerism point is **not** retracted: naming the source of a reading without a discriminator is still the error. The reason is the missing discriminator, not a blanket ban on names. Both claims are recorded so neither is quietly lost.

## 6. Two consequences for this question

**Prestige is not action.** From *The Recursive Self-Improvement Trap*: "Prestige must never be allowed to validate an action directly." And: "Prestige can fund an action. It can give an action a budget to be tried. But it cannot settle an action. Only the ground can settle it. The Ground Maintainer must be blind to prestige." A coalition that gathers prestigious laboratories to agree that readings are comparable is the named collapse, not corroboration. The legitimate ask is the opposite: give the shape a budget to be tried, let an anchor settle it.

**My own audit is a simulation, not a ground reading.** Same essay: "LLMs can quickly simulate an approximate answer/output with the formula found in the architecture post... However, the truly testable output would need to be derived from the real architecture or working the formula yourself." LLM output is "simulacra." *Arrival of the Fittest* adds the warning that solutions may be "contrived to fit the formula." So `01_figure_grounded_audit.md` is a simulated derivation, checked for internal consistency against the figures. It is **not** a test of the shape claim and must be labelled as such. It cannot settle anything it reports on.
