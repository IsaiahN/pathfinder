# Outreach draft: check the output first, then decide about the shape

**Use:** one-to-one, or as a cover letter attached to a submission through a public venue in `03_public_contact_points.csv`. **Not** for bulk send.

**Hard precondition:** do not send until the shape claim and its refuter in `01_figure_grounded_audit.md` survive your own check. The two-stage offer collapses on contact if stage 1 does not hold.

**Mode:** `specified` on the shape claim, `general` on the structural rule. Not `grounded`.

---

**Subject:** One checkable claim about cross-team measurement comparability

> Hi [name],
>
> I work on a figure-grounded framework for hard methodological questions. I have one output I would like you to check against what you already know. I am not asking you to read the framework first, and I am not asking you to agree with it. If the output fails, there is nothing else to look at.
>
> **The residual, in effect terms.** Two teams use different instruments and different assumptions and each returns a number for what each claims is the same quantity. The wanted service is substitutability: a reading from team A can stand in for a reading from team B in a downstream decision without loss. The residual is the failure of that substitution to be licensed. It is indexed per measurand, time and scale, not averaged over the two teams.
>
> **The output (`specified`, not `grounded`).** Comparability is not agreement between teams. It is a five-part shape:
>
> 1. A reference that neither team can move. If it moves when a team pushes on it, it is not an anchor.
> 2. Independence between the teams' evidence until both stage-1 verdicts are recorded. Different instruments pointed at the same thing are the independence, not the defect.
> 3. The smallest convention that makes the disagreement statable across the seam, stopping short of deciding which value the ground returns.
> 4. A round trip: carry a reference item through A's pipeline, across to B's frame and back, then report the gap rather than hiding it.
> 5. A lineage that travels with each number, because two readings can be equal in value and different in what they mean.
>
> This is a shape, not a universal standard. I am not naming a reference material, protocol or software package as the answer.
>
> **What I am not claiming.** That more teams reading the same report corroborate anything. That a shared protocol settles which value is correct. That two equal numbers are the same measurement. That the figures license a specific standard. A convention that decides an outcome has stopped being a convention, and a filter for the budget is not a witness for the verdict.
>
> **What would refute the shape claim.** I wrote these down before sending this:
>
> 1. a team's instrument can be shown to correct itself against a quantity it also produces;
> 2. two teams with genuinely disjoint evidence agree, yet diverge under a perturbation that the ground resolves;
> 3. a convention can be shown to decide which value the ground returns and still detect ground-level disagreement;
> 4. a cross-team transform has zero round-trip gap across every distinguishable condition;
> 5. two readings with different routes are interchangeable for every downstream decision, so the lineage carries no information.
>
> If any one holds, I will narrow or retract the claim. A one-line "this fails because..." is far more useful to me than agreement. If you would rather not engage, no reply is needed.
>
> **What I would count as your stage-1 verdict:** your answer to "what would you expect to see if this shape claim were true, and what would make you reject it?" recorded before any framework material is shown.
>
> **If it survives, and only then:** the public figures and formula are at the links below. The figures are the authority, not my summary of them.
> - Figures with line numbers (local framework extract): `FRAMEWORK/reference/03-FIGURES_TEXT.md`
> - Canonical usage rule: `FRAMEWORK/reference/FIGURES_README_canonical.md`
> - SI definitions and key comparisons (metrology ground): https://www.nist.gov/si-redefinition/definitions-si-base-units and https://www.bipm.org/en/toolbox-cipm-mra
>
> **If you want to reuse the method:** the formula is in the figures link. Run it on a comparability problem you know well and can check against a ground. Write down what would refute your result before you run it, and state the mode of what you get. A reported failure is more useful to me than a reported success.
