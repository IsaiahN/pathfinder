## The residual in effect terms

Team A and team B each point an instrument at what they take to be the same quantity, under different assumptions. Each returns a number. The service wanted is: a number from A can be substituted for a number from B in a downstream decision without loss. The residual is the failure of that substitution to be licensed. In effect terms the question is not "are the numbers close" but "what would have to be true for a substitution to be allowed". The answer the figures give is a shape, not a standard: a non-updating anchor, independence between frames, a convention that makes the disagreement statable without deciding it, a transform whose round-trip gap is measured, and a lineage that travels with the number.

## What is deliberately not here

- No personal emails of named individuals.
- No ranking of people by how likely they are to agree with the shape claim.
- No bulk-send plan.
- No concrete universal standard named as *the* answer. The figures return a shape, not a name (Fig 9 :32). Naming a specific reference material or protocol would be an import with a source gate and shadow test still owed.

Three reasons, in the figures' own words:

1. **Agreement is not a verdict.** Fig 2 :35: "Alignment is triangulation, not negotiation. A mirror cannot correct you."
2. **Correlation disqualifies a verdict.** Seat Map :163: "High correlation is disqualifying for a verdict and efficient for a convention." Selecting recipients for receptiveness manufactures the correlation that makes their agreement worthless.
3. **A verifier must not hold the interiors.** Fig 10 :42: "A verifier that can reconstruct the claim cannot verify it."

Legally: scraping personal emails for unsolicited bulk outreach breaches GDPR and CAN-SPAM style rules in many jurisdictions. Public institutional and opt-in channels avoid that failure as well as the structural one.
