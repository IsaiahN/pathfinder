## 0. The residual under audit, stated in effect terms

Two teams, A and B, use different instruments and different assumptions. Each produces a number for what each claims is the same quantity. The wanted service is substitutability: a reading from A may stand in for a reading from B in a downstream decision without loss. The residual R is the failure of that substitution to be licensed, registered per slot (object, measurand, time, scale) and not averaged across the two teams (Fig 1 :19: "The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated.").

- **Arity.** At least four places: team A, team B, an anchor, and a transform between A and B. A two-place "do the numbers agree" question is already the wrong arity; it drops the anchor and the transform.
- **Symmetry.** Swapping A and B should not change the verdict if the frames are genuinely independent and range against the same anchor. If it does, one frame has been privileged and independence is lost. This is a test of the arrangement, not of the numbers.
- **Scale.** Two scales are in play: within a team (its prediction against its own observation) and across teams (the round trip from A's frame to the shared reference and back into B's frame). Fig 4 requires the gap between them to be measured, not assumed zero.

**What an answer would look like, bound as an ordinary term.** The sought-for shape is written down before the search, per the formula's "Direction": an answer is a licensed substitution, meaning a stated anchor that neither team can move, independence between the teams' evidence, a shared convention that makes a disagreement statable without deciding it, a transform with a measured round-trip gap R_T, and a lineage that travels with each number. Binding this shape lets it accrue a residual and be refused like any other claim, rather than remaining an invisible preference.

**Question under audit, option form.** Option A: make the teams agree on one protocol and one number, then call the measurements comparable. Option B: keep the teams independent, range each separately against a common anchor, author a convention that makes the disagreement statable, measure the transform gap, and record the route. A leads to a convention that has decided an outcome, which Fig 10 forbids. B leads to a checkable shape. The derivation below tests B.

## 1. Domain census

Domains are described before any figure line is chosen. A domain needs actors, a currency and a nameable anchor. No nameable anchor means it is not yet a domain, only a channel.

| domain | actors | currency | anchor | domain yet? |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| D1, each team's instrument frame | instrument, operator, local library Gamma_A, prediction Gamma_A(b,a) | per-slot residual R_A between prediction and observation | none inside the team | no, a channel only. A frame cannot score itself with a quantity it also produces (Fig 1 :27) |
| D2, the anchor or ground | the reference: a measurement, experiment, counter, market, outcome, or an invariant of nature | the value returned, which does not update on opinion | itself | yes. Fig 2 :29: "An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks." |
| D3, the seam and convention | units, protocols, shared reference items, the ground-maintainer seat | shared meaning, that is agreement about a convention, not about the world | the ground, which the convention makes statable but does not settle | yes. Fig 10 :16-17: "the convention does not fix the bug it makes the bug statable" |
| D4, comparison and lineage | the two readings, the transform, and the record of each route | provenance: what was converged, what was imported, what was given up | the ground plus the record | yes. Fig 8 :50-52: "Convergent derivation and adopted import look identical in the contents. Only the record of where each came from separates them" |

**Partition or union.** Union, not partition. Comparability bundles at least four conditions: same referent, same unit and ordering, independent evidence, and a recorded route. A single lever that fixes units clears the region where only the unit condition fails and leaves the intersections standing, because the other conditions still bind (Fig 9 :54-58). It must be unbundled before search, not treated as one hard problem.

**Order by dependency (Fig 3 :20).** The chain is ordered by dependency, not by importance.

1. **Perception.** Is each instrument already returning something that fails to resolve? Fig 6 :34-35: "Where nothing is, there is no edge to extend from, and the search for a proto-instrument is a search for one that is already there."
2. **Vocabulary and convention.** Do the teams have terms for what the shared reference affords? Without a convention the disagreement cannot even be stated (Fig 10 :16-17).
3. **Objective.** Can each team say, in its own terms, what would count as progress on its own residual?
4. **Anchor registration.** Can each frame be corrected separately by the same non-updating anchor (Fig 2 :46)?
5. **Transform and round trip.** Only now the cross-team transform, with bracket fidelity and ground pressure as the two factors (Fig 7 :38-39).

Optimising step 5 statistics cannot rescue a missing step 4 anchor or a missing step 2 convention (Fig 3 :19: "Optimising a link that already works cannot rescue one that does not."). A reading taken below the break is not a weak reading, it is a reading of nothing (Fig 3 :22-23).

## 3. Findings F1 to F10

### F1: Comparability is triangulation, not negotiation (decisive)

Fig 2 :35: "Alignment is triangulation, not negotiation. A mirror cannot correct you."
Fig 2 :45: "Frames range against the ground. They do not negotiate with it."
Fig 2 :46: "Each frame is corrected by it separately, and what makes the correction sound is that neither frame can move it."

A workshop in which the two teams agree on a number is panel 1 of Fig 2 (mutual update) or panel 2 (common cause), not panel 3 (an anchor). Fig 2 :33: "The anchor must not update." Fig 2 :34: "everything drifts together, and the drift is coherent, which is why nobody notices." The effect term is simple: if the shared reference moves when a team pushes on it, it is not an anchor and the correction is not sound.

**Mode:** `general` for the structural claim, `specified` for the prediction that a negotiation-only regimen will fail to improve ground contact.
**Status:** `triangulated`. Fig 2's anchor rule and Fig 8's triangulation-of-low-overlap-frames derivation are distinct routes; both are framework-internal, so this is low overlap within one evidence pool, not two independent witnesses.
**Refuter:** exhibit a case where two teams that negotiated a number, with no non-updating anchor, are corrected by the ground as well as two teams that ranged separately, and where the correction persists under a perturbation that moves the negotiated number.

### F2: Different instruments are the independence, not the defect

Fig 2 :31: "Independence, between two frames, is how little they draw on the same evidence. Two people reading the same report are not two frames; two instruments pointed at the same thing are."
Fig 2 :40: "agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool"
Fig 2 :42: "Buy independence in proportion to the cost of being wrong."

The question says the teams use different instruments and assumptions. Under Fig 2 that is the resource, not the obstacle. What would destroy comparability is not the difference but the teams drawing on one another's readings, one report, one calibration pool or one prior, before each verdict is recorded. Fig 6 :53: "searchers drawing on the same evidence are one searcher wearing many names."

**Mode:** `general`.
**Status:** `triangulated`, Fig 2 independence definition plus Fig 6 :53 plus Fig 8 :48-49, with world corroboration from Province and Borecki (2013) that hidden dependencies inflate false positives. The world source is secondary; the figure line decides.
**Refuter:** show two instruments with genuinely disjoint evidence producing the same residual under a perturbation that the ground resolves in opposite directions for the two readings, so agreement survives ground contact.

### F3: A shared convention is needed, but declared meaning is not grounded meaning

Fig 10 :16-17: "the convention does not fix the bug it makes the bug statable"
Fig 10 :18: "a declared meaning is not a grounded one"
Fig 10 :47: "a convention that decides an outcome has stopped being one"
Fig 10 :49: "Declaring a meaning does not ground it."

Comparability requires a convention: units, a protocol, a reference item, a rule for what counts as the same slot. But the convention's office is to make the disagreement statable across the seam. If it decides which value the ground returns, it has become a verdict and the seam is closed. Fig 2 :41: "efficient for a convention: agreement about a shared meaning is the product, not a corruption of it." Seat Map :163: "High correlation is disqualifying for a verdict and efficient for a convention."

**Mode:** `general` for the office of a convention, `specified` for the prediction that a protocol which fixes the outcome will stop detecting disagreement.
**Status:** `triangulated` across Fig 10's seam derivation and Fig 2 :41's correlation-valence derivation, low overlap between the two routes, still one framework evidence pool.
**Refuter:** exhibit a convention that determines which value the ground returns and still reliably detects a ground-level disagreement about that value.

### F4: Identical numbers are not identical measurements; the record is the term

Fig 12 :94: "The arrangement is the substance."
Fig 12 :95-96: "Two entries with an identical ingredient set are not variants of one thing. They are different things whose notation cannot tell them apart, and the difference is carried entirely by the bond."
Fig 12 :15-16: "nothing downstream can tell them apart, and nothing signals that anything was lost"
Fig 13 :129: "It is recoverable from the lineage."

A bare number is a flat formula: ingredients recorded, arrangement not. Two readings can be equal in value and inverse in what they mean. Fig 12 :117: "Same atoms, different bond, different substance." The transform and its provenance are part of the measurement, not documentation added afterwards. This is why a comparability claim must travel with the route by which each team reached its value.

**Mode:** `general`.
**Status:** `triangulated`. Fig 12's bond and isomer derivation and Fig 13's lineage-as-proof derivation have low overlap.
**Refuter:** show a case where two readings reached by demonstrably different routes, under different assumptions, are interchangeable for every downstream decision and where the route carries no information, so the lineage is superfluous.

### F5: The round-trip gap must be measured, not assumed zero

Fig 4 :30: "So take the round trip and measure the gap. Without it you are navigating by dead reckoning: confident about a position nobody checked."
Fig 4 :31-32: "The gap is not an error to eliminate. It is the measurement of what the coarser description cannot hold, and it is the only honest report of how much was given up."
Symbols table: R_T is `gap(x, (T_E ∘ T_A)(x))`.

Carry a reference item (or a common set of items) through A's pipeline, across to B's frame, and back; compare what went out with what came back. The gap is not noise to be tuned away. It is the report of what the coarser shared description cannot hold. Fig 10 :68: "Different actors, a different currency, a different ground, and a lossy transform between." The two instruments are different actors with possibly different currencies; a lossy transform is expected, and its size is the answer.

**Mode:** `specified`: a round trip is owed and its gap is nonzero in general; how large is unmeasured.
**Status:** `single-source` at figure level (Fig 4 plus the Symbols table, same derivation), with secondary corroboration from Bauer and Hussong's common-item and chained-item designs (see `04_links_and_distribution.md`). World corroboration does not change the figure verdict.
**Refuter:** exhibit a cross-team transform whose round-trip gap is zero across every distinguishable condition, including changes of instrument, operator and assumption.

### F6: Describe the residual before choosing the shared standard

Fig 9 :26-28: "R's own predicates name the habitat that holds φ* describe it before you go looking, or any frame you pick will seem to fit"
Fig 9 :30-31: "your words are borrowed too, so describe the effect rather than the cause"
Fig 8 :53: "Residual first, frame second."

Written first: what decision must the number support, at what scale, with what tolerance, for which object slot, and under which perturbation would a wrong substitution be detected? Only then pick the reference material or protocol. If the standard is chosen first, any pair of teams can be made to look comparable under its vocabulary. Fig 9 :32: "you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself."

**Mode:** `general`.
**Status:** `triangulated`, Fig 9's describe-before-looking rule plus Fig 8's residual-first rule plus the tacit-knowledge essay's effect-description point as secondary corroboration.
**Refuter:** choose the standard first and show the comparability verdict is invariant under a counterfactual residual that changes the decision the number must support.

### F7: Filters for the budget, witnesses for the verdict

Fig 9 :60: "Use filters for the budget and witnesses for the verdict. Never let a filter hand you a verdict."
Fig 2 :44: "The effective-sample-size correction for correlated observations is standard in survey statistics and meta-analysis; the collapse is the same arithmetic read as a warning."

A protocol, an uncertainty budget and a preregistered tolerance are filters: they decide which comparisons get run and how much effort is spent. They are not witnesses that the measurements agree. The ground settles. Fig 1 :23: "The ground enters as the only metric." Fig 1 :27: "A frame cannot score itself with a quantity it also produces."

**Mode:** `general`.
**Status:** `triangulated`, Fig 9's budget-verdict split plus Fig 2 :44's arithmetic warning plus Fig 1's only-metric rule.
**Refuter:** exhibit a filter that returns a verdict about the world without any ground reading, and show that the verdict survives removal of the filter.

### F8: More teams reading the same report add no anchor

Fig 7 :13: "same closure, handed along; agreement compounds, evidence does not"
Fig 7 :35: "A room's agreement is variation, not a verdict. Selection between rooms is where the ground votes."

A coalition of teams is not a bigger instrument. If they share the closure, their agreement is a weighted average of influence. Fig 7 :36: "A chain of mirrors is one long room. The next room must differ from the last, and match the residual it surfaced." Selection between different closures is where the ground votes, not accumulation of agreement inside one closure.

**Mode:** `general`.
**Status:** `single-source` at figure level (Fig 7), corroborated secondarily by the correlated-meta-analysis literature.
**Refuter:** show a case where adding teams that all draw on one report improves contact with the ground, measured by a correction that persists under an independent perturbation.

### F9: Same label, different meaning is a genuine difference, not a wording issue

Fig 13 :16: "it names what is being measured"
Fig 13 :26: "it decides what the measurement would mean"
Fig 13 :36: "their agreement would carry no information, and one of them would be redundant rather than minimal"
Fig 12 :117: "Same atoms, different bond, different substance."

Two teams can both report "the same quantity" while one produces an ordering (spectrum-like, more and less) and the other a directedness (gradient-like, something that can be spent). These answer different questions and are not two readings of one fact (Fig 13 :35). Under Fig 12 they are not variants of one measurement; they are different substances whose shared notation hides the difference. The remedy is a source-discriminating instrument, not a terminology vote. The Hard Problem is About Source: "An instrument that can't distinguish these cases demonstrates its own inadequacy, it cannot claim anything substantial about what is being measured or what causes it."

**Mode:** `specified` for the prediction that same-named readings can fail substitutability; the general rule is `general`.
**Status:** `triangulated` for the measured-versus-meant distinction (Fig 13 states and structures) and `single-source` for the specific isomer framing (Fig 12), with the metamerism essay as secondary corroboration.
**Refuter:** demonstrate with a discriminator that the two readings are the same quantity at the same level, so the difference is label-only and carries no behavioural consequence.

### F10: The honest stop returns a shape, not a standard

Fig 9 :32: "you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself"
Fig 8 :39: "So a search that finds nothing is never a proof of absence."
Fig 13 :134: "Unreachable is a verdict here, not a silence,"

The figures license the shape of a comparability regimen, not the name of a universal reference artifact, protocol or software package. Naming one is an import: Fig 6 :12-13 requires a "source gate: provenance" and a "shadow test: explains a gap", and Fig 6 :24 adds "A wrong atom costs more than starting from primitives." If no figure line governs a proposed standard after re-census, the honest report is `gap`, not silence. The figures README states the limit as "a shape, not a name".

**Mode:** `general`.
**Status:** `triangulated`, Fig 9 plus Fig 8 plus Fig 13 plus the canonical figures README.
**Refuter:** derive a specific universal standard from the figures alone, with its source gate and shadow test, such that no import from a world frame is required.

## 4. Pre-registered refuter

Written before the conclusion, per Seat Map :287: a falsifier written before the run is a design instrument. The shape claim is refused if any of the following holds:

1. A team's instrument is shown to correct itself against a quantity it also produces, so Fig 1 :27 does not bar its self-score.
2. Two teams whose readings agree are shown to draw on genuinely disjoint evidence yet diverge under an invariant perturbation that the ground resolves, so triangulation fails (breaks F1 and F2).
3. A shared convention is shown to decide which value the ground returns, so Fig 10 :47 is false (breaks F3).
4. A cross-team transform is shown to have zero round-trip gap R_T across all distinguishable conditions (breaks F5).
5. Two readings with different routes and different assumptions are shown interchangeable for every downstream decision, with no provenance-bearing difference (breaks F4 and F9).
6. A specific universal standard is derived from the figures alone with source gate and shadow test, so the shape-not-a-name limit is exceeded in a licensed way (breaks F10).

If any fires, the shape claim is withdrawn or narrowed and the retraction is recorded here, not erased.

## 5. Strains, re-census and corrections

**Strain 1: shared reference items versus independence.** Metrology compares instruments through common transfer standards, which looks like overlap, while Fig 2 :31 says independence is low shared evidence. Re-census: the common item represents the anchor, not a frame's evidence. Fig 2 :46: "Each frame is corrected by it separately, and what makes the correction sound is that neither frame can move it." The anchor may be shared; the frames' evidence and verdicts may not be. Different quantities, no strain after re-census.

**Strain 2: the seat authors conventions but the anchor must not update.** Fig 10 lets the maintainer author conventions because nothing else can see across a seam; Fig 2 forbids a moving anchor. Re-census: a convention makes a disagreement statable and carries no truth value (Fig 10 :46), while the anchor settles and carries no frame's opinion. Different offices, no strain after re-census.

**Strain 3: a convention that decides an outcome.** This is not a strain but a named failure. It is the transition Fig 10 :47 forbids and Seat Map :105 says is silent. It is kept as a live test, not resolved away.

**Correction to my own first reading.** I first wrote that comparability requires a universally shared protocol that determines the agreed value. Retracted: that is a convention that decides an outcome (F3). The protocol is a filter for the budget, not a witness for the verdict (F7).

**Correction to my own second reading.** I first treated "different instruments and assumptions" as noise to be removed. Re-censused: different instruments pointed at the same thing are the definition of independence (F2). What is removed is shared evidence between frames, not difference of instrument.

## 6. Recommendation

1. State the residual in effect terms per slot before selecting any standard or protocol (F6).
2. Install a non-updating anchor and make each team range against it separately. Do not negotiate the number (F1).
3. Keep the teams' evidence disjoint until both stage-1 verdicts are recorded. Buy independence in proportion to the cost of being wrong (F2).
4. Author the smallest convention that makes the disagreement statable. Stop before it decides the value (F3).
5. Run a round trip with common items and measure R_T. Report the gap as the result, not as an error to hide (F5).
6. Ship the number with its lineage: instrument, assumptions, route, imports and gap (F4).
7. Use protocols and tolerances as filters for the budget only. Never let a filter hand you a verdict (F7).
8. If no figure line governs after re-census, say `gap`. Do not name a universal standard that is not derived (F10).

Fig 4 :25: "A recording carried upward looks like knowledge and is a description of one occasion." Send the generator, the shape claim plus its refuter plus the round-trip method, not a conclusion.
