# Expert verification packet: the Tether on cross-team measurement comparability

**What this is:** a claim in a form a metrologist, measurement scientist, psychometrician or methodologist can check. Mode, derivation and refuters are stated before the conclusion, so a reader knows what to look for and what would settle it.

**Mode: `specified` on the shape, `general` on the structural rules, not `grounded`.** No measurement is claimed. This is a shape and a route, not a result.

**Status:** a simulated derivation checked for internal consistency against the figures, not a ground reading. It cannot settle the question it reports on.

## 1. The claim, in one paragraph

Comparability between independent teams is not agreement and is not a shared protocol. It is a five-part shape: a reference neither team can move, with each frame corrected by it separately; independence between the teams' evidence until both stage-1 verdicts are recorded; the smallest convention that makes the disagreement statable without deciding which value the ground returns; a round trip through both pipelines with its gap R_T reported; and a lineage that travels with each number so two equal values reached by different routes can be distinguished. The question is a union of four conditions, not one hard problem, and the tolerances used to test it are filters for the budget, never witnesses for the verdict. The framework returns this shape, not a universal standard.

## 2. Three checkable sub-claims

### C1: the anchor and independence claims
- **Statement.** Comparability requires each team to be corrected separately by a reference that does not move when a team pushes on it. Teams drawing on one report or one calibration pool are one frame wearing two names, whatever their instruments.
- **Source, verbatim.** Fig 2 :35: "Alignment is triangulation, not negotiation. A mirror cannot correct you." Fig 2 :46: "Each frame is corrected by it separately, and what makes the correction sound is that neither frame can move it." Fig 2 :31: "Independence, between two frames, is how little they draw on the same evidence. Two people reading the same report are not two frames; two instruments pointed at the same thing are."
- **Refuter.** Exhibit a case where two teams that negotiated a number, with no non-updating anchor, are corrected by the ground as well as two teams that ranged separately, and where the correction persists under a perturbation that moves the negotiated number. Or exhibit disjoint-evidence agreement that diverges under a perturbation the ground resolves.

### C2: the convention and round-trip claims
- **Statement.** A shared unit or protocol makes a disagreement statable but must not decide which value is correct. A common item must be carried through both pipelines and back, and the round-trip gap reported as a result rather than hidden as error.
- **Source, verbatim.** Fig 10 :16-17: "the convention does not fix the bug it makes the bug statable" and Fig 10 :47: "a convention that decides an outcome has stopped being one." Fig 4 :30: "So take the round trip and measure the gap. Without it you are navigating by dead reckoning: confident about a position nobody checked."
- **Refuter.** Exhibit a convention that determines which value the ground returns and still reliably detects a ground-level disagreement about that value. Or exhibit a cross-team transform whose round-trip gap is zero across every distinguishable condition of instrument, operator and assumption.

### C3: the record and substitution claims
- **Statement.** A bare number is not the whole measurement. Equal values reached by different routes can be different substances, and substitutability has to be earned by showing that nothing is lost when one reading replaces the other.
- **Source, verbatim.** Fig 12 :94: "The arrangement is the substance." Fig 12 :95-96: "Two entries with an identical ingredient set are not variants of one thing. They are different things whose notation cannot tell them apart, and the difference is carried entirely by the bond." Fig 13 :129: "It is recoverable from the lineage." The Operators table defines identity as "two names, one referent" with the test "substitute one for the other. Is anything lost? No."
- **Refuter.** Show two readings reached by demonstrably different routes under different assumptions that are interchangeable for every downstream decision, with no provenance-bearing difference, so the lineage carries no information.

## 3. What is explicitly not claimed

- Not a universal reference material, protocol or tolerance. Fig 9 :32: "you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself."
- Not that more teams or more agreement add evidence. Fig 7 :35: "A room's agreement is variation, not a verdict. Selection between rooms is where the ground votes."
- Not that equal numbers are the same measurement, or that same-named constructs are comparable. Fig 12 :117: "Same atoms, different bond, different substance."
- Not that a preregistered tolerance settles a disagreement about the world. Fig 9 :60: "Use filters for the budget and witnesses for the verdict. Never let a filter hand you a verdict."
- Not `grounded`, and not a derivation of any particular SI unit, key comparison or psychometric invariance rule. Those are world instances, cited as secondary corroboration only.
- Not a claim that this simulated derivation has been executed against a real pair of teams.

## 5. What would settle it

A source-discriminating instrument and a recorded round trip, not a further argument.

1. **Discriminator test.** Can an instrument distinguish an ordering from a directedness in the two readings? An instrument that cannot distinguish cases "demonstrates its own inadequacy" (*The Hard Problem is About Source*).
2. **Round-trip test.** What is R_T when a common item passes through both pipelines and back? Zero across all distinguishable conditions would remove the round-trip requirement.
3. **Anchor test.** Does the reference move when either team pushes on it? If yes, it is not an anchor.
4. **Substitution test.** Put each reading into the downstream decision. Is anything lost? The identity test from the Operators table supplies the criterion.

## 6. What an expert can check in twenty minutes

1. Read Fig 2 :31, :35, :46 and Fig 10 :16-17, :47. Five short passages.
2. Check C1 against a real inter-laboratory comparison you know: were the labs corrected separately against a reference they could not move, or did they negotiate a consensus value?
3. Check C2 against a common-item or key-comparison design you know: is the common item a representative of the reference, or did it become the decision?
4. Check C3 against a case where two studies used the same label for different constructs. Did the label hide a real difference in meaning?
5. Name one instrument that would distinguish the two readings' source, or say why none is available.
6. Send the refutation. A failure is more useful than an endorsement, and a Track B reply is a conversation, never corroboration.
