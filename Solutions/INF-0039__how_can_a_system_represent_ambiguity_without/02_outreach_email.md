**Subject:** One checkable claim about holding multiple interpretations

> Hi [name],
>
> I am working on one narrow question in information and interpretation: how can a system hold several readings of the same signal without committing to one too early? I have one specific output I would like you to check against what you already know. I am not asking you to read a theory first, and I am not asking you to agree with it. If the output does not survive, there is nothing else to look at.
>
> **The output (`specified`, not `grounded`):** ambiguity is not one reading with a probability attached. It is a set of candidate readings indexed per object slot, each carrying its own prediction error, each a distinct arrangement of the same held atoms rather than a spelling of one reading. Two figures carry most of the weight. The first says the error is indexed by object slot and that aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears. The second says same atoms, different bond, different substance, and that the difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour. So what a system must preserve is the bond and the per-slot error, not just the list of ingredients or one global confidence number.
>
> The part I would most value you checking: agreement between readings is useful for a shared meaning and disqualifying for a verdict about the world. A system can hold several readings under a convention that makes the disagreement statable, and only let the ground, a measurement or a discriminating instrument, settle one. A declared meaning does not ground it, and a convention that decides an outcome has stopped being a convention. Narrowing is allowed, but only with every cut kept ranked and reversible. When a reading is refused, the refusal is added to the record, not subtracted, so the system can still tell never-held from held-and-given-up.
>
> **What I am not claiming:** that this returns an algorithm, a holding time, a probability threshold, or a universal encoding for ambiguity. It returns a shape. No figure line I can find selects those numbers, so I record that as a gap, not as silence and not as a result. I am also not claiming that the framework settles the question. It cannot validate itself, and this derivation is a simulation checked for internal consistency, not a ground reading.
>
> **What would refute the shape:**
> 1. two readings on one slot both carry live error and the correct ground-verified behaviour is to report exactly one with no ranked remainder;
> 2. two readings with identical atoms and different bonds are behaviourally identical under every ground test, so the bond carries nothing;
> 3. agreement among frames drawing on one evidence pool legitimately settles a reading about the world;
> 4. a system ranges multiple readings correctly with no shared convention making the disagreement statable;
> 5. a reading is dropped without any refusal operator and the record still distinguishes never-held from held-and-given-up;
> 6. a narrowing cut is irreversible and a later ground reading shows the kept reading was wrong;
> 7. a new reading from the same evidence pool buys as much as one from a genuinely differing closure.
>
> I wrote these down before sending this. A one-line "this fails because..." is more useful to me than agreement. If you would rather not engage, no reply is needed.
>
