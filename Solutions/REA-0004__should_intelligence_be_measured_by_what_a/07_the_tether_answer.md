# The Tether's answer: output, learning, or adaptation?

**Mode: `specified` — not `grounded`.** This is a shape and a decision procedure, not a result. Simulated derivation checked against the figures (`05_what_shape_means.md` section 5), not a ground reading.

## 1. The residual it answers

The question offers three bases for measuring intelligence: what a system can do, how it learns, how flexibly it adapts. The residual is not "which of the three is right." It is that the subject term is wrong (Fig 11 :35) and that all three candidates are agent-internal readings of a property that is a relation (Fig 11 :39). A decision that picks one, or weights all three, leaves the real gap untouched. The sought-for shape was bound as an ordinary term in `01` section 2, so it accrues a residual and is refusable.

## 2. The shape

1. **The question is refused and replaced.** Fig 11 :35: *"The wrong question, and this figure is the replacement for it."* The answerable question is what makes a system capable, not what intelligence is (Fig 11 :39).
2. **Capability is agent-and-habitat.** Fig 11 :39: *"capability is a property of agent-and-habitat, never of the agent alone."* Fig 11 :40: *"An improvement that does not change contact changes nothing, however much it improves."* Contact is the settling quantity.
3. **Output alone is disqualified.** A harness is a substituted habitat; a synthetic solve proves wiring, never capability (Fig 11 :70). A frame cannot score itself with a quantity it also produces (Fig 1 :27). A behaviour is a lossy projection that cannot comment on its source (essay *The Hard Problem is About Source*).
4. **Learning is a mechanism reading, necessary but not sufficient.** It is legible only when a correction signal arrives from outside the frame (Fig 1 :6, :23). A learning curve cannot tell which link of the Fig 3 chain stops the system (Fig 3 :29-30).
5. **Flexible adaptation is closest but still partial.** It must be read as agent-and-habitat (Fig 11 :39) and per slot (Fig 1 :19, :22). Behavioural flexibility by itself is not evidence of a cognitive mechanism (Schnell et al. 2021, secondary).
6. **Verdicts stay per domain, never averaged.** D1 assessment, D2 learning and D3 habitat are a union of overlapping conditions (Fig 9 :54-58), each with its own anchor and its own refuter. A weighted sum mixes bond types and currencies (Fig 12 :125-127).
7. **No universal index is returned.** Fig 9 :32: *"you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself."* The gap is declared, not filled (Fig 13 :134).

## 3. What it explicitly does not say

- Not that output, learning or adaptation are worthless. Each becomes admissible when it is an external ground reading rather than the frame's own score.
- Not that a universal intelligence number exists, or that one is merely waiting to be computed. No figure line returns one.
- Not that "the environment" is a single anchor. The habitat is enumerated, never composed (Fig 11 :43-44), and the anchor is whatever settles the question and does not move on the score (Fig 2 :29).
- Not a derivation from one of the three to the others, and not a new primitive.
- Not `grounded`. No measurement in this folder settles the shape; the world sources are secondary and share no anchor with the figures.

## 4. What would settle it — the ground

A source-discriminating instrument, not a further argument. From *The Hard Problem is About Source*: an instrument that reads green *"cannot comment on the source"*; the spectrometer resolved it. Here the corresponding questions are:

- **Contact test:** does a proposed measure's rise coincide with a change in contact with a ground that does not update on the measure? If not, it is frame-internal (Fig 1 :27, Fig 11 :40).
- **Harness test:** under an instrument that can discriminate sources, does a synthetic solve carry the same warrant as a solve in the contacted habitat? Fig 11 :70 says no; a discriminator would confirm or break it.
- **Slot test:** when the global score is decomposed into per-slot residuals, is any live slot hidden by the average (Fig 1 :13, :19, :22)?
- **Governing falsifier, from the figure itself:** Fig 11 :72, *"an agent-only improvement that moved the ground would refute the capability claim."*
- **Standing refuters from `01` section 4:** subject-term, harness, aggregation, union and gap falsifiers. Any one fires a retraction.

## 5. The honest stop

**Unreached, not unreachable.** The figures fix the shape of an admissible measure and refuse the question's subject term, but they do not name an index, a battery or a weighting. That is a `gap` of the shape-not-a-name kind (Fig 9 :32, Fig 13 :134), reported as a verdict and not as silence. A search that finds nothing is never a proof of absence (Fig 8 :39). The answer stops at the limit of the available discriminating instruments and records the lineage so the stop is checkable.

## 6. One-paragraph version for outreach

> The Tether does not pick one of the three measures and does not average them. It says the question is the wrong one: "intelligence" is a label applied afterwards to a lineage that met its ground, so the answerable question is what makes a system capable, and capability is a property of agent and habitat together, never of the agent alone. From that, output alone is disqualified, because a test harness is a substituted habitat and a synthetic solve proves wiring, not capability; learning is a mechanism reading that is blind without an external correction; flexible adaptation is the closest of the three but still has to be read per slot and as a relation. Verdicts are kept separate for assessment, learning and habitat, never blended into one number. What is offered is the shape any admissible measure must have, a falsifier written before the run, and the instruments that would close it. No universal index is returned. That is a declared gap, not silence, and nothing here is claimed as settled by experiment.
