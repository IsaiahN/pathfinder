# Are the three readings isomers, a functional group, or a chain?

**Mode: `specified`.** This is a simulated derivation checked against the figures, not a ground reading (see `05_what_shape_means.md` section 5).

The question offers three candidate measures as if they were interchangeable spellings of one quantity. Two questions are owed: (1) under Fig 12, are output, learning and adaptation **isomers** (same atoms, different bond, different substance), or merely members of a **functional group** (same kind, different members), or something else; and (2) what is their **dependency order** under Fig 3, so that a measure is not read at a link whose input never arrives.

## DERIVATION

### Q1 — Are they isomers?

- Fig 12 :117: **"isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."**
- Fig 12 :124: **"functional group. A substructure whose presence fixes the outcome. The group says what kind; the qualifier says which member."**
- As usually measured, the three readings do **not** share a full atom set. An output score uses {system, harness, score}; a learning curve uses {system, history, correction signal, time}; an adaptation test uses {system, habitat, change, contact}. They share roughly "system" and a comparison operation, so under Fig 12's definition they are **not isomers**. They are three members of a **functional group** whose common substructure is "a reading of a system against something," with different qualifiers in the slots.
- The stronger form: if all three are rewritten over the full shared set {system, habitat, ground, residual, slot, score, correction signal}, differing only in which bond connects which atoms, then they **are** isomers: same atoms, different arrangement, different substance. That is a checkable structural claim worth making, with the falsifier below.
- **Falsifier, written before the run:** if any one reading requires an atom that the others do not (for example a learning curve that needs no habitat, or an output score that needs no ground), the isomer claim fails and the functional-group reading stands instead.

### Q2 — What bonds do they use?

- A **score** is a threshold relation between the observed result and a criterion: Operators :51, *"a goal is a comparison, which is why a vocabulary without it cannot state one."* So an output reading is built with ⋛ (comparison).
- **Learning progress** is a subtraction: Fig 12 :112, *"Progress is a subtraction."* Operators :46: *"progress is a subtraction, which is why a vocabulary without it cannot say how far along it is."* So a learning reading is built with − (subtraction) over a time-ordered sequence →.
- **Adaptation** joins system to habitat across change: Fig 13 :101, *"that entities relate, and influence travels between them"* (network), plus → sequence across the change. So an adaptation reading is built with → and network.
- Three different bonds, not three spellings of one. Fig 12 :125-127: *"A composition costs in two currencies, and they do not add."* A weighted sum of output, learning and adaptation prices in mixed bonds and currencies; it is not a single figure (Fig 9 :54-58).

### Q3 — What is the dependency order?

Fig 3 :4-15 names the chain: perception, vocabulary, objective, planning, learn and carry. Fig 3 :20: *"The chain is ordered by dependency, not by importance."*

- **Perception first:** can the system tell what is in front of it and tell it apart from what was there before (Fig 3 :4-5)? An output score presupposes this.
- **Vocabulary second:** does it have terms for what things afford (Fig 3 :6-8, with Fig 11 affordance echoes)? A learning curve presupposes a vocabulary in which progress can be named.
- **Objective third:** can it say what would count as progress in its own terms (Fig 3 :9-11)? This is the middle link where chains usually break (Fig 3 :24-27). A flexibility test that does not name its objective is a reading of nothing.
- **Planning and learn/carry last:** a route under incomplete information, then name what worked, verify it, carry it (Fig 3 :12-15).
- **Consequence for the three readings:** the output reading is downstream of the whole chain; the learning reading sits at the last link; the adaptation reading questions whether the chain is even the right room after the question moved (Fig 10 :72: *"A room that stays fixed while the question moves is the failure; the correlation is only its symptom."*). Because the chain is ordered by dependency, not importance, the measurement effort should go to the objective link first, which is where the instrument is missing (Fig 3 :27: *"A link with no instrument is not a link that works. It is a link nobody has looked at."*).

### Q4 — Where does the bond comparison sit in the measurement?

Fig 12 :89 (comparison): *"is it about which is larger, rather than both being present? Yes."* A capability claim is a threshold relation: does the system's contact with the ground exceed what the previous reach could hold? So the natural bond for the *verdict* is ⋛, applied to per-slot residuals, and the natural bond for the *claim about improvement* is − (progress as subtraction). A single scalar "intelligence" collapses a comparison and a subtraction into one operator, which Fig 12 :40 (*"same two atoms, three substances"*) says cannot be done without losing information.

- **Strain, named:** ⋛ is defined at Operators :48 as *"a threshold relation between two quantities."* At the capability level the operands are per-slot residuals and habitat contact, which are quantities under the declared code; if they are not, the comparison is mistyped. This needs an instrument, not an argument. Kept as a live type question.

## CONCLUSION

The three readings are **not isomers as normally measured**; they are a functional group sharing "a reading of a system against something," with different bonds in the slots (Fig 12 :117, :124). Under the stronger rewrite over one shared atom set they become a genuine isomer claim with a stated falsifier. Their bonds differ (⋛ for a score, − plus → for learning progress, → plus network for adaptation), so a weighted sum is a category error under Fig 12 :125-127. Their dependency order is the Fig 3 chain, so the objective link, not the output score, is where a measure must first be made legible. No new primitive is required for any of this; the three readings are arrangements of held atoms and bonds, not wall-moving imports (Fig 6 : *"IMPORT adds an atom"*).
