# Outreach draft — check the separator before deciding about the theory

**Use:** one to one, or as a cover note attached to a submission through a public venue in `03_public_contact_points.csv`. **Not** for bulk send.

**Hard precondition:** send only the claim and refuter in stage 1. The theory and links go out only after a verdict on stage 1 is recorded. If the separator does not survive, nothing else is worth reading.

---

**Subject:** Check one separator before you decide whether the measurement claim is worth reading

> Hi [name],
>
> I have one specific claim I would like you to check against what you already know. I am not asking you to read a theory first, and I am not asking you to agree. If the claim does not survive, there is nothing else to look at.
>
> **The residual.** The question asks whether intelligence should be measured by what a system can do, how it learns, or how flexibly it adapts. The problem is the subject term: a figure in the framework I am using says of that kind of question, *"The wrong question, and this figure is the replacement for it."* The replacement is that the answerable question is what makes a system capable, and capability is a property of agent and habitat together, never of the agent alone.
>
> **The claim (`specified`, not `grounded`).** No agent-internal reading is a sufficient measure. A score that rises without a change in contact with a ground that does not move on the score has not measured capability. A test harness is a substituted habitat, so a synthetic solve proves wiring, not capability. Any admissible reading must be per-slot, because a global average is how the signal that says where to look disappears.
>
> **What I am not claiming.** Not that output, learning or flexibility are useless. Output is admissible when it is an external ground reading rather than the frame's own score. Not that a universal index exists. The framework returns a shape, not a name, and no figure line gives a weighting or a test battery. That is a declared gap, reported as unreachable rather than as silence. Not that this is settled by experiment. It is a simulated derivation checked against the figures, not a ground reading.
>
> **What would refute the claim (written before this message):**
> 1. an agent-only improvement that moves the ground without changing habitat contact;
> 2. a reading in which intelligence predicts ground contact per slot with no habitat reference;
> 3. a synthetic solve shown by a source-discriminating instrument to be capability, not merely wiring;
> 4. a single global score that preserves every live slot residual against the ground;
> 5. the three candidates shown to be a clean partition with no overlapping conditions.
>
> If any holds, I retract the claim. A one-line "this fails because..." is more useful to me than agreement. If you would rather not engage, no reply is needed.
>
> **If it survives, and only then:** the figures are the authority, not my summary of them. The relevant lines are Fig 11 :35, :39, :40, :70 and Fig 1 :19, :22, :27. I can send the public figure text and the essay it comes from.
>
> **If you want to use the method:** write down what would refute your own result before you run it, and state whether your claim is general, specified or grounded. A reported failure is more useful to me than a reported success.

## Stage order

1. **Stage 1:** residual, claim, refuter. Ask what they would count as a failure. Record their verdict before showing any theory.
2. **Stage 2, opt-in only:** if they are curious, send the three source links with the note that the figures, not this summary, are the authority.
3. **Stage 3:** offer the loop as a method to reuse, with the instruction that each user writes their own refuter and states their own mode and ground.

A Track B reply, if a publicly published institutional address is ever used, is a conversation and never corroboration; it carries zero verdict weight.
