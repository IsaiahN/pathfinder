## 0. The question under audit

> Should intelligence be measured by what a system can do, how it learns, or how flexibly it adapts?

Stated as an effect, three candidate measures are on the table:

- **Output reading (what it can do):** credit the observable behaviour or task score. Leads to benchmarks, test harnesses, and a score that can rise while the underlying capability does not.
- **Process reading (how it learns):** credit the learning mechanism and its improvement over time. Leads to training curves, sample efficiency, and a process metric that can be studied in isolation from any contacting environment.
- **Transfer reading (how flexibly it adapts):** credit performance under change and in new conditions. Leads to out-of-distribution tests, transfer tasks, and habitat-sensitive evaluation.

What an answer must decide: which of these is admissible as a *measure*, at what scale, against what anchor, and what would refute the decision. A decision that returns "all three, weighted" is not an answer yet; it is a union that has not been unbundled (Fig 9 :54-57). The sought-for shape is made an ordinary term in section 2 so it can accrue a residual like anything else.

## 1. Domain census (before choosing any figure line)

**R by arity, symmetry, scale.**

- **Arity.** The question presents three candidates, but they are not three readings of one quantity. Under Fig 11 they are a compound of agent and habitat/contact, so the residual is at least binary. The deeper finding (F1) is that the question's own subject term is wrong, so arity is not three options but a category error followed by a replacement.
- **Symmetry.** The three candidates are not symmetric spellings of one thing. Output is a reading of a projection (source essay *The Hard Problem is About Source*); process is a mechanism trace; transfer is a contact event. They draw on different evidence, so their agreement is not corroboration (Fig 2 :31-32, :40).
- **Scale.** A single system is one scale; capability spans agent-and-habitat and crosses scales through the membrane (Fig 4). A measurement taken at the agent scale cannot see contact, just as a reading below a broken link is a reading of nothing (Fig 3 :20-23).

**One line per domain: actors / currency / anchor.**

| domain | actors | currency | anchor | no anchor? |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| D1 Assessment frame | system under test, test harness, metric, scorer | scores, pass rates, task success | *claimed* ground = the environment; *actual* proxy = a substituted habitat (Fig 11 :70) | proxy can be gamed, so the anchor is not legitimate unless the question is constitutively about the harness |
| D2 Learning/development frame | learner, history, weighting w, prior, correction signal | reduction in residual over time, sample efficiency | RLVR / ground feedback as the only metric (Fig 1 :6, :23) | has a real anchor when correction is external; loses it when the frame scores its own mechanism |
| D3 Habitat/contact frame | agent plus habitat actors, conditions, relations | contact and cascade, introduction not subtraction | the ground, which settles and does not decay (Fig 10 :24, Fig 11 :12-16) | legitimate anchor present, if enumerated rather than composed (Fig 11 :43-44) |
| D4 Verification frame | expert readers, second seat, wrong-ground maintainer | agreement and residual | the ground, never agreement (Fig 2 :29, :35) | in this simulated folder all seats share one evidence pool, so independence is zero |

**Partition or union.** D1 to D3 are a **union of overlapping conditions**, not a partition. A task score is read from a harness that is itself a habitat substitution; a learning curve is read from a process that may be detached from ground contact; a transfer test is a contact event that still runs through a harness. A single lever clears the region where only one condition holds and leaves the intersections standing (Fig 9 :54-58). D4 is not a condition of intelligence at all; it is the checking arrangement around the claim.

**Order by dependency (Fig 3).** Perception, vocabulary, objective, planning, learn and carry (Fig 3 :4-15). Before asking whether a system is good, ask which link it currently stops at and whether the answer was measured or assumed (Fig 3 :29-30). A measure of output sits at the end of the chain; if the break is at the objective link, an output score is a reading of nothing and effort spent on the score arrives at a step that cannot use it (Fig 3 :19-23).

## 3. Findings

### F1 — The question is the wrong question (decisive)

Fig 11 :34-36:

> Fig 11 :35 *"The wrong question, and this figure is the replacement for it."*
>
> Fig 11 :36 *"Where nothing maintains anything, intelligence is a label applied afterwards to a lineage that met its ground. Nothing was intelligent; selection ran. The word does no work there."*

The question asks which basis measures *intelligence*, treating intelligence as a property of the system under test. Fig 11 refuses that subject. Where nothing maintains anything, the word does no work; it is a label applied afterwards to a lineage that met its ground. The figure does not answer "which measure" because it declines the frame in which "intelligence" is a nameable property of an agent alone.

This is not a semantic dodge. It is the same move as Cayley separating forces from flapping: the substrate question dissolves and the contact question does not (Fig 11 :41). The replacement is stated at Fig 11 :39 and derived in F2.

### F2 — Capability is agent-and-habitat, never agent alone

Fig 11 :37-40:

> Fig 11 :37 *"Where a seat exists, something is doing work, and it is exactly what the seat does: enumerate the habitat, rank by cascade, introduce actors, build instruments, read the ground."*
>
> Fig 11 :38 *"That is not a metaphor for intelligence. It is a description of the practice."*
>
> Fig 11 :39 *"So the answerable question is not what intelligence is, but what makes a system capable. And capability is a property of agent-and-habitat, never of the agent alone."*
>
> Fig 11 :40 *"A system cannot be made more capable by improving the agent in isolation. An improvement that does not change contact changes nothing, however much it improves."*

All three candidate measures in the question are agent-internal readings. Output is what the agent emits; learning is what happens in the agent; flexible adaptation is a disposition of the agent. Fig 11 :39 says the object is a relation, agent-and-habitat, so no agent-internal reading is sufficient by itself. Fig 11 :40 gives the falsifiable form: an improvement that does not change contact changes nothing, however much it improves. That sentence is the load-bearing prediction of this whole solution.

- **Verdict D1 (assessment frame): `specified`, single-source on the figure side.** A score that rises without a change in contact has not measured capability. World corroboration from Goodhart's law is secondary (see `09`).
- **Verdict D2 (learning frame): `specified`, single-source.** Process improvement is necessary but not sufficient; it must be read against an external correction signal (Fig 1 :6, :23).
- **Verdict D3 (habitat frame): `specified`, triangulated.** Adaptability is the closest of the three, but only when read as agent-and-habitat (Fig 11 :39) and per slot (Fig 1 :19). Convergence across two independent figure routes, Fig 11 and Fig 1.

### F3 — "What a system can do" is disqualified as a sufficient measure

Three separate reasons, held apart:

1. **A harness is a substituted habitat.** Fig 11 :66-70, with the decisive sentence at :70:
   > Fig 11 :70 *"A test harness is a substituted habitat. Which is why a synthetic solve proves wiring and never capability."*

   A benchmark score is a solve inside a substituted habitat. It proves the wiring works in that habitat. It does not prove capability, because capability is agent-and-habitat (F2) and the harness is not the habitat the claim is about.
2. **A frame cannot score itself with what it produces.** Fig 1 :23-27:
   > Fig 1 :23 *"The ground enters as the only metric."*
   >
   > Fig 1 :27 *"They are frame-internal. A frame cannot score itself with a quantity it also produces."*

   Output is exactly a quantity the frame also produces. Counting it as the measure is the named disqualification.
3. **A reading does not determine its source.** The essay *The Hard Problem is About Source* (opened and quoted in `05` section 4): several paths can create one behaviour, so behaviour is a projection with lossy information. A behaviour reading cannot comment on the source. The meter that resolves metamerism is an instrument, not more behaviour.

None of these says output is useless. It says output alone is a metric without an anchor. When the output is an external, non-updating ground reading (Fig 2 :29), it becomes admissible evidence; the defect is treating the harness itself as that ground.

### F4 — "How it learns" is a mechanism reading, not a capability reading

Fig 1 :6-7 names the ground as *"ground: RLVR"* and *"the only metric"*. A learning process is legible only where a correction signal arrives from outside the frame. Fig 3 :14-15 names the last link: *"name what worked, verify it, and carry it to the next problem."* Learning is the loop that carries, and it is downstream of the objective link.

- Where the correction signal is external, the process reading is evidence about contact and is admissible.
- Where the correction comes from the frame's own output, the process reading is frame-internal (Fig 1 :27) and the channel is saturated (Fig 10 :25-26): *"stale percepts, dropped transients, saturated metrics, pooled readings."*
- A learning curve cannot tell which link the system stops at (Fig 3 :29-30), so it cannot by itself decide whether a system is capable.

**Refuter for F4:** show a learning process whose improvement is driven by its own generated reward with no external ground, and whose output changes contact anyway. That would break the claim that contact is the settling quantity (Fig 11 :40).

### F5 — "How flexibly it adapts" is the closest reading, but still insufficient as stated

Flexibility is a disposition to change under contact, so it is nearer to Fig 11 :39 than the other two. Two constraints stop it from being the answer as posed:

1. **It must be agent-and-habitat.** A flexibility score that lives only in the agent repeats F2. Adaptation is a cascade through a habitat (Fig 11 :43-44: enumerate everything in contact with the residual, then what contacts those, outward until the cascade stops mattering). Scoping to direct contact is how second-order effects are missed (Fig 11 :52-53).
2. **It must be per slot.** Fig 1 :19 and :22:
   > Fig 1 :19 *"The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated."*
   >
   > Fig 1 :22 *"Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average."*

   A single flexibility average hides the slot where the system fails. The world's comparative-cognition literature corroborates this: behavioural flexibility by itself cannot be used as evidence of complex cognition, and underlying mechanisms must be measured before a behaviour is labelled cognitively complex (Schnell et al. 2021, opened in `09`).

**Verdict D3 stays `specified`, triangulated, and is not upgraded to `grounded` by the world sources**, which share no anchor with the figures and are secondary.

### F6 — A universal single number aggregates away the signal

Fig 1 :13 and :19-22, Fig 3 :19-23, Fig 9 :4-5. A global score near zero with one live slot residual is a legal state (Fig 1 :13). Averaging across slots is how the signal that says where to look disappears (Fig 1 :22). Disagreements that do not shrink with effort, where each rule keeps working on a different subset, are not one hard question but several well-formed ones (Fig 9 :4-5), so they should be split rather than averaged into one intelligence quotient.

**Refuter:** an instrument that resolves a single global score into per-slot residuals and whose score still predicts ground contact at every slot. That would show aggregation is lossless here, which Fig 1 :22 says it is not.

### F7 — The three candidates are a union, not a partition

Fig 9 :54-58:

> Fig 9 :54 *"And when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again."*
>
> Fig 9 :57 *"What remains is smaller and it is also categorically different: a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either."*

Output, learning and adaptation overlap: a transfer task is an output measured after learning, in a substituted habitat. Weighting them into one figure is the error named at Fig 12 :125-127, where a price in bits and a price in actions are held apart because they do not add. A decision procedure must name which condition is being measured and keep the intersection as its own compound.

### F8 — No figure line returns a universal measurement formula (gap)

Fig 9 :32 *"you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself"* and Fig 13 :134 *"Unreachable is a verdict here, not a silence"*. The figures fix the shape of an admissible measure (agent-and-habitat, per slot, anchored, refusable) but do not select a numerical index, a test battery, or a weighting. That is a declared `gap` of the shape-not-a-name kind, not an invitation to invent one. "Unreachable is a verdict, not a silence."

## 4. Pre-registered refuter (written before the recommendation or conclusion)

The shape claim is refuted if any of the following holds. These were written before any verdict was assembled.

1. **Contact falsifier (governing).** An agent-only improvement is demonstrated that moves the ground, i.e. changes what settles the question, without changing habitat contact (breaks Fig 11 :40 and :72).
2. **Subject-term falsifier.** A reading is produced in which "intelligence" does work as an agent-only property, i.e. it predicts ground contact per slot without reference to a habitat (breaks Fig 11 :35-39).
3. **Harness falsifier.** A synthetic solve is shown to be capability, not merely wiring, under a source-discriminating instrument (breaks Fig 11 :70).
4. **Aggregation falsifier.** A single global score is shown to preserve every live slot residual against the ground (breaks Fig 1 :19, :22).
5. **Union falsifier.** The three candidates are shown to be a partition under one description with no shared intersection (breaks Fig 9 :54-58).
6. **Frame-internal falsifier.** A frame's own output metric is shown to be an admissible verdict about the world without an external non-updating anchor (breaks Fig 1 :27, Fig 2 :29).
7. **Gap falsifier.** A figure line is found that returns a universal measurement formula or weighting; the declared `gap` is then withdrawn.

If any fires, the corresponding claim is retracted in the log below and the answer is re-derived from the census. Until a ground reading exists, nothing here is labelled `grounded`.

## 5. Recommendation

1. **Refuse the either/or and restate the question** as "what makes a system capable" (Fig 11 :35, :39). Offer the three candidate readings as partial, separately assessed conditions, not as a weighted average.
2. **Make contact the settling quantity.** Ask of any proposed measure: does a rise in it change contact with a ground that does not update on the score? If not, it is frame-internal (Fig 1 :27, Fig 11 :40).
3. **Report per slot, never as one global number** (Fig 1 :19, :22). A benchmark suite is admissible only as a set of anchored readings, each with its own refuter.
4. **Distrust the harness as an anchor.** A test harness is a substituted habitat; a synthetic solve proves wiring (Fig 11 :70). Require an external, non-updating ground and keep the two silent substitution failures visible: what you failed to reproduce, and what you brought with you (Fig 11 :68-69).
5. **Name the `gap` honestly.** No figure returns a universal index. Report the shape and the falsifiers, and stop (Fig 9 :32, Fig 13 :134).
6. **Send the generator, not a conclusion.** Fig 4 :23: *"upward: a method that can be reapplied. Never the recording of a particular success."* Lead with the residual and refuter; offer links only after a verdict is recorded.
