# Expert verification packet - the Tether on an industrialized universal baseline

**What this is:** a claim in a form a cross-cultural methodologist can check. Mode, derivation and refuters are stated before the conclusion, so a reader knows what to look for and what would settle it.

**Mode: `general` on the shape, `specified` on each condition, not `grounded`.** No measurement of a human cognitive norm is claimed. This is a separator and a route, not a roster. Simulated derivation checked for internal consistency against the figures (`05_what_shape_means.md` §7).

## 1. The claim, in one paragraph

A baseline drawn from an industrialized population cannot serve as an anchor for a cross-population universality claim, because its answer changes when the population changes. The residual must stay indexed per population and per task slot. Search inside one population's closure adds no new population; the missing frame must be imported as a genuinely different closure with a provenance and a shadow test, and independence bought in proportion to the cost of being wrong. The instrument is improved from a worse one already returning something in each habitat, with those populations represented at every stage; a "culture-free" test specified from a description is not available. Capability is a property of the person and the habitat together, so no standing list of norms survives a change of question. Agreement on item equivalence is efficient for a convention and disqualifying as a verdict. Promotion to "universal" is permitted only with a non-updating ground in each habitat, an enumerated habitat, a measured round trip and a refuter written before the run. Which norms are universal is a declared `gap`, not a silence.

## 2. Three checkable sub-claims

### C1 - framework-internal plus metrology analogue; checkable by reading Figure 2 and the anchor test

- **Statement:** a single-population baseline fails the anchor test. A ground that updates on what a frame thinks has become another frame.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 2 :29-30 - "An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks. A measurement, an experiment, a counter, a market, a patient outcome. If it moves when you push on it, it is not an anchor." Also Fig 2 :33 - "The anchor must not update." And Fig 2 :47 - "A ground listed as one of the parties has already become a frame."
- **What to check:** take any proposed universal baseline and ask what would change its answer. If the answer depends on the population sampled, it is a frame in the anchor position. The metrology analogue is exact at the level of role: a working standard is traceable only until it decays; it is not the primary standard.
- **Refuter:** show a single-population sample whose answer does not move when the population changes, or a ground in this domain that updates on what a frame thinks. Also: show that Fig 2 :36 ("An anchor is legitimate when the question is constitutively about it") licences the promotion here, that is, that the question "what is human cognition" is constitutively about the industrialized sample.

### C2 - methodology-facing; checkable by a cross-cultural researcher against the instrument literature

- **Statement:** item identity and invariance statistics are conventions for shared meaning, not witnesses for universality. The instrument must be developed with all participating populations and improved from one already returning something.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 6 :32 - "An instrument is not built from a description. It is improved from a worse instrument already returning something." Fig 2 :41 - "efficient for a convention: agreement about a shared meaning is the product, not a corruption of it." Fig 2 :40 - "disqualifying for a verdict: agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool."
- **What to check:** Poortinga, van Herk and Fontaine 2025 write that "Any comparison between participants from two, or more, populations presumes a common quality (property, attribute) and requires a common measurement scale, a 'standard of comparison'", and list "representation of all participating populations at all stages of instrument development" plus content validity and partly overlapping item sets. Boehnke 2026 reports that "Semantic sameness is decoupled from covariance matrix invariance, only the latter counts." Do these procedures change the bond between item and ground, or only the ingredient list? Welzel et al. 2021/2022 argue that invariance criteria "favor constructs with low between-group variance"; does that objection change the convention reading?
- **Refuter:** show that within-population sampling effort removes the cross-population residual (breaks Fig 8 :32); or that a task's meaning is invariant across populations with no instrument work (breaks Fig 3 :25-27); or that agreement on items drawn from one literature carries verdict weight for a claim about the world (breaks Fig 2 :40).

### C3 - habitat and refusal; checkable by an anthropologist or cognitive scientist

- **Statement:** capability is agent-and-habitat, the habitat is enumerated rather than composed, and the promotion must be refusable by an operator that is not a bond.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 11 :39 - "capability is a property of agent-and-habitat, never of the agent alone." Fig 11 :51 - "No standing list of good actors survives a change of residual." Operators :58 - "a refutation is a negation, which is why a vocabulary without it can only accumulate." Fig 4 :30 - "So take the round trip and measure the gap. Without it you are navigating by dead reckoning: confident about a position nobody checked."
- **What to check:** pick a norm claimed to be universal. Enumerate what in the habitat is in contact with its expression, then what contacts those, outward until the cascade stops mattering (Fig 11 :43-48, :52-54). Name the second- and third-order effects. Then ask: what observation would make the promotion false, and was that written before the study? The low-overlap analogue is Uexküll's tick, whose world has three carriers of significance; organisms "can inhabit distinct perceptual realities" while sharing a physical environment.
- **Refuter:** show a cognitive performance invariant across habitats that differ in contact, using an instrument that discriminates sources; or show that the refusal can be written entirely as a subtraction (a held term removed) rather than a negation (a claim refused); or show that R_T is zero for a promotion across a changed habitat.

## 3. What is explicitly not claimed

- Not that no cognitive capacity is shared across societies. The claim is about the promotion rule.
- Not a roster. No named norm is asserted universal or non-universal. Fig 9 :32: "you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself."
- Not that larger or more diverse WEIRD samples fix the error, and not that translation alone fixes it. Those are budget and ingredient changes (Fig 9 :60: "Use filters for the budget and witnesses for the verdict. Never let a filter hand you a verdict.").
- Not that measurement invariance is worthless. It is efficient for a convention and disqualified as a verdict (Fig 2 :40-41).
- Not that the figures can name the missing roster. Fig 13 :134: "Unreachable is a verdict here, not a silence".
- Not `grounded`. Nothing here was settled by experiment.

## 5. What would settle it

A discriminating instrument in each habitat, not more argument. From *The Hard Problem is About Source*: an instrument that reads green "cannot comment on the source"; the spectrometer settled it. Here:

1. **Anchor Test:** what changes the answer of the proposed baseline? If sampling does, it is a frame.
2. **Independence Test:** do the agreeing studies draw on different closures and evidence pools?
3. **Instrument Test:** was the measure improved from one already returning something in each habitat, with those populations at every stage?
4. **Habitat Test:** does the contact cascade reach two or three steps out (Fig 11 :52-54)?
5. **Round Trip Test:** what is R_T when the claim is carried up and brought back down?
6. **Refutation Test:** what would make it false, and was that written before the run?

## 6. What an expert can check in twenty minutes

1. Read Fig 2 :29-33, Fig 6 :32, Fig 11 :39/:51 and Operators :58. Four short passages.
2. Apply the anchor test to one baseline you know. Does its answer move when the population changes?
3. Decide whether an invariance result in your field is being used as a convention or as a verdict. Name which one and why.
4. Name one instrument that would discriminate the sources of an equal score across two habitats, or say why none is possible.
5. Send the refutation. A failure is more useful than an endorsement.
