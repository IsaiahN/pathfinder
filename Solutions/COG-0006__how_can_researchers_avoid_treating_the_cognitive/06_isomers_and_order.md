# Isomers, functional groups, and order in the universal-baseline question

**Mode: `specified` on the structural claims, `general` on the shape. Simulated derivation checked against the figures, not a ground reading (see `05_what_shape_means.md` §7).**

## RESIDUAL

The question asks how to avoid an error, not for a list. Two structural questions are owed before the answer can be stated precisely:

1. Since the question looks like a choice between "universal" and "culture-specific", is it a **partition** or a **union** of overlapping conditions?
2. Are an industrialized-first promotion and a jointly developed promotion two versions of one thing, or different things whose ingredient list hides the difference? In figure terms: are they **isomers**, a **functional group**, or something else?

## DOMAIN CENSUS

Atoms held (from `01` §1): the residual indexed per population-and-task slot; a frame that predicts; a ground that does not update; a closure with an atom set; a habitat enumerated by contact; an instrument improved from a worse one; the seven bonds plus negation. No new primitive is available without import.

Bonds held: ⋛ comparison (a reference is selected), → sequence (promotion is ordered), − subtraction (a prior is removed), ¬ negation (a promotion is refused). The failure is that ordinary cross-cultural write-ups use only the **flat formula**: a list of ingredients (samples, items, scores, a p value) with no bond recorded. Fig 12 :5: "A set of ingredients is not one. The bond is the rest of the information, and it is the half nothing was recording."

## DERIVATION

### Q1 - Partition or union?

Fig 9 :4-5:

> **"First: is there a fact here? Disagreements that do not shrink with effort, where each rule keeps working well on a different subset, are not one hard question but several well-formed ones. Split it rather than search."**

The question is not one fact. In the literature, "universality" fails for different reasons in different subsets: the sampling frame (Henrich et al. 2010), the item set and its translation (Poortinga et al. 2025), the equivalence criterion (Boehnke 2026; Welzel et al. 2021/2022), and the causal source of an equal reading (*The Hard Problem is About Source*, metamerism). Different rules work well on different subsets. Split.

But the split is a **union**, not a partition. Fig 9 :54-55: **"And when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again. A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones."** The sampling frame and the item set overlap: a sample with a WEIRD prior tends to carry items with a WEIRD prior. Fig 9 :57: **"a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either. It stops being a mixture and becomes a specific compound."** So a single lever, such as "add more countries", clears the region where only sampling is wrong and leaves the compound standing.

**Falsifier, written before the run:** if the defects partitioned cleanly, one lever would be sufficient for each subset and there would be no compound case where two conditions bind at once. A demonstrated compound case (a translated item that is invariant in form but affords something different in the new habitat) kills the partition reading and confirms the union. This is observed in the methodology sources, so the partition reading is refused.

### Q2 - Are the two promotions isomers?

Fig 12 :117:

> **"isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."**

Fix the atom set: a population of participants, a task, an item, a score, a ground in each habitat, a claim of universality. Two arrangements over this same set:

- **Promotion A, industrialized first:** the item is written in P, translated to H, invariance is checked, non-equivalent items are dropped, and the surviving score is compared. Bond between item and ground: imported from P, debited against independence.
- **Promotion B, joint development:** developers in each culture independently create items for the same construct, collect data without translation, rank items by loading, amalgamate, and test invariance (Boehnke 2026). Bond between item and ground: local, with semantic sameness decoupled from covariance invariance.

Same atoms, different bonds, different resulting substances. By Fig 12 :117 they are **isomers**: the difference is invisible in the ingredient list (both report "N participants, k items, a measurement-invariance result") and decisive in behaviour (A tends to reproduce the P norm and record H as deviant; B can change which items survive). Fig 12 :94: "The arrangement is the substance." Fig 12 :46: "A bond is the typed relation between two operands. There are seven, they are not interchangeable, and which one holds is not recoverable from the operands."

This is why "we also used a cross-cultural sample" is not a fix. It changes an ingredient, not the bond.

**Falsifier:** if both procedures could be written with the same bond (for example, if the item-to-ground relation were identical in A and B), the isomer claim fails and they are one procedure with a different sample. The claim stands or falls on the bond, not on the atom list. This is a real test and has not been settled by a measurement in this folder; mode `specified`.

### Q3 - Functional group versus isomer

Fig 12 :123:

> **"functional group. A substructure whose presence fixes the outcome. The group says what kind; the qualifier says which member."**

If the atom set is **not** fixed, and A and B use different tasks or different item pools, then they are not isomers. They are two members of the same **functional group**: a `population → task → ground → promotion` skeleton with different fillers. The functional group is the shape the method must have; the qualifier (which items, which habitats) is the binding.

Under the figure's own limit, the functional group is what the framework can return. Fig 9 :32: "you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself." So:

- **Isomer reading (fixed atom set):** checkable, `specified`, with the bond as the variable.
- **Functional-group reading (variable atom set):** the honest shape for the open question, `general`.

Both are recorded; they are not averaged.

**Falsifier:** if a cross-cultural study can be written without the `task → ground → promotion` skeleton at all (no ground in the habitat, no promotion step), the functional-group claim fails. The study would be a raw score report, not a universality claim.

### Q4 - What order is the dependency chain?

Fig 12 :111-113:

> **"A vocabulary without bonds can name a state and cannot name a route to one."**
> **"A plan is a sequence. A goal is a comparison. Progress is a subtraction. A refutation is a negation, and that one is not a bond."**

Map the bonds to the method:

| bond | figure meaning | role here |
|---|---|---|
| ⋛ comparison | "a goal is a comparison, which is why a vocabulary without it cannot state one" (Operators :51) | selecting a baseline: a reference is being chosen, so the choice must be justified |
| → sequence | "A then B; B needs A to have happened" (Fig 12 :72) | describe residual before picking a frame (Fig 9 :27-28) |
| − subtraction | "A with B removed or absent" (Fig 12 :82) | remove the industrialization prior from the default; progress is a subtraction |
| ¬ negation | "this does not hold" (Operators :55); not a bond | refuse a promotion; a vocabulary with only + accumulates norms and cannot carry the correction |

Fig 3 :20: "The chain is ordered by dependency, not by importance." The order is:

```
perceive the slot (Fig 3 :4-5)
  → have terms for what the task affords here (Fig 3 :6-7)
  → say what would count as a promotion (Fig 3 :9-10)
  → choose the route: joint development or import with provenance (Fig 3 :12-13)
  → verify and carry to the next population (Fig 3 :14-15)
  → ¬ refuse when the ground turns against the promotion (Formula step 2)
```

The last step is the one a flat vocabulary cannot write. Operators :58: "a refutation is a negation, which is why a vocabulary without it can only accumulate." An equivalence-testing literature that can say "items are invariant" but not "this promotion is false" is a vocabulary that can only accumulate.

**Falsifier:** show that the refusal can be written entirely as a subtraction (a held term removed) rather than a negation (a claim refused). The figures distinguish them explicitly (Fig 12 :83: "is the second operand's absence the point? Yes"; Operators :73-74: "− is A with B removed or absent ... Negation is the refusal of a claim"). If the distinction collapses, the operator claim fails.

### Q5 - Catalysts and the cost of carrying the arrangement

Fig 12 :119:

> **"catalyst. A part that survives its own use. Spent and regenerating parts behave differently across uses, and nothing records which a term is."**

A jointly developed item bank is a catalyst if it survives its use in one habitat and can be reused in another. A translated item that is dropped after failing invariance in one study is a spent part. Nothing in a score table records which is which; the lineage does (Fig 13 :129-130: "A term carries the atoms it was built from and the bonds that joined them. That record is not documentation of the term. It is the term."). This is why `08_expert_verification_packet.md` asks for provenance, not just results.

Fig 12 :125-126 adds that the two currencies do not add: "Bits price the description. Actions price finding out whether it holds." An item bank is cheap to describe and expensive to settle in a new habitat; a single anomalous response is cheap to notice and expensive to interpret. Reporting only one number (a sample size, a CFI difference) counts one denominator and misses the other.

## CONCLUSION

The question is a **union compound**, not two opposite answers. Over a fixed atom set, an industrialized-first promotion and a jointly developed promotion are **isomers** under Fig 12 :117: same ingredients, different bond, different substance. Over a variable atom set they are members of one **functional group** (Fig 12 :123), which is the shape the framework can return. The dependency chain runs through the vocabulary and objective links (Fig 3 :25-27), and it ends in a negation: a promotion must be refusable, not merely accumulable (Operators :58). No figure line names which norm is universal; that remains the `gap` of `01` F10.

This framing rescues what is defensible in the question without inventing a roster: the thing to change is the **bond** between an item and its ground, and the thing to add is the **operator that can refuse**. Mode `specified` on the isomer claim (falsifier stated in Q2), `general` on the functional-group shape. `grounded` on none of it.
