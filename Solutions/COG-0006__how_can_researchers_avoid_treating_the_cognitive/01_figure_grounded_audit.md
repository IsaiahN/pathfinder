## 0. The residual, stated in effect terms

The question asks for a method. Stated as an effect: a researcher holds a baseline B measured in population P, typically Western, Educated, Industrialized, Rich and Democratic. They want to assert that a cognitive norm N is universal, meaning it would register the same way for any human anywhere. The residual R is the gap between N as it registers in P and N as it would register in some other habitat H, under a task whose meaning may itself change between P and H.

Three consequences follow before any figure is chosen.

1. **R is indexed, not global.** It lives at the intersection of a population slot and a task slot. Fig 1 :19: "The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated." Fig 1 :22: "Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average." A single "percent of variance explained by culture" is exactly the average that throws the signal away (Fig 1 :28: "The gap is measured per object, not per step. A system that averages across objects has thrown away the only signal that says where to look.").
2. **"Universal" is an ordered claim, not a symmetric finding.** A correlation between a WEIRD sample and a non-WEIRD sample does not choose the arrow from "measured here" to "holds everywhere". The Operators table defines the comparison bond ⋛ as "a goal is a comparison, which is why a vocabulary without it cannot state one" (Operators :51). A baseline is a threshold that picks a reference; the reference is the thing under examination.
3. **"Baseline" is a role, not a property of a population.** Fig 2 :29-30: "An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks. A measurement, an experiment, a counter, a market, a patient outcome. If it moves when you push on it, it is not an anchor." An industrialized sample changes its answer when you change the population sampled. By the figure's own anchor test, it is not an anchor. It is a frame that has been promoted into the anchor position.

**What an answer would look like (direction bound as an ordinary term, Formula step "Direction", Fig 5 :65).** An answer is not a list of universal norms. It is a separator: the set of conditions under which a local norm may be promoted to a universal claim, plus the conditions under which the promotion must be refused, plus an instrument that would settle the parts the figures leave open. Written down before the run, the sought-for shape is: `per-slot residual + differing closures + non-updating ground + measured round trip + a refuter that can fire`.

## 1. Domain census (before choosing any figure line)

**R by arity, symmetry and scale.**

- **Arity:** several conditions, not one. At minimum: (a) the sampling frame, (b) the instrument and its translation, (c) the ground in each habitat, (d) the promotion from local reading to universal claim. These overlap, so by Fig 9 :54-57 this is a **union compound**, not a partition: "A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones. A single lever clears the region where only one condition holds and leaves the intersections standing, because the second condition still binds." Fig 9 :57: "a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either. It stops being a mixture and becomes a specific compound." One lever, such as adding more WEIRD subjects, clears only the region where sampling is the sole defect.
- **Symmetry:** the promotion is ordered (local reading → all populations). The agreement between two samples is symmetric, so it cannot select the direction. The doc warns that a vocabulary with only symmetric bonds cannot say what would count as getting somewhere (Operators :58 on refutation; Fig 12 :112-113: "A vocabulary without bonds can name a state and cannot name a route to one.").
- **Scale:** individual cognition at one scale, the cultural habitat at another, with a membrane between (Fig 4 :21: "The membrane is the boundary between the sizes, and the rule about what may cross it. Not a wall: a filter with a direction."). Universal is a claim at the population-of-populations scale; a norm is a reading at the single-population scale. Any promotion is a round trip and owes a gap measurement (Fig 4 :30).

**One line per domain: actors, currency, anchor. No nameable anchor means it is not a domain yet.**

| domain | actors | currency | anchor | question type |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| D1, measurement and instrument frame | researchers, tasks, test items, translations, samples | readings per population-and-task slot | the ground in that habitat: what the task actually affords there, which does not update on the theory | where the dependency chain breaks (Fig 3) |
| D2, habitat and culture | practices, language, institutions, environments, daily activity | contact, conditions, relations | the world read off the habitat (Fig 11 :48), not a list composed in the lab | habitat enumeration, never composition |
| D3, verdict and generalization | populations as frames, the universal claim | agreement versus residual | a non-updating ground reached by triangulation, never agreement | union of conditions, per-frame verdicts |
| D4, refusal and method | the claim, its pre-registered refuter, the verifier | assertions and refusals | the ground, plus the recorded lineage (Fig 10 :62-66) | operator availability: can the frame say "this is false"? |

**Partition or union.** Union, not partition. D1 and D3 in particular overlap: a measurement invariance result is both an instrument check and an agreement claim. Treating them as separate tasks is the "several well-formed ones" error of Fig 9 :4-5 in reverse. They must be unbundled and then rejoined as a compound with its own loop.

**Order by dependency (Fig 3 :20: "The chain is ordered by dependency, not by importance.").**

1. Perception: can the researcher tell what the task affords in this population, and tell it apart from what it afforded before (Fig 3 :4-5)?
2. Vocabulary: are there terms for what the situation offers here (Fig 3 :6-7)?
3. Objective: can the researcher say what would count as progress, that is, as a legitimate promotion to universal, in its own terms (Fig 3 :9-10)?
4. Planning: a route to that objective under incomplete information (Fig 3 :12-13).
5. Learn and carry: name what worked, verify it, carry it to the next population (Fig 3 :14-15).

The break in cross-cultural work is usually at the vocabulary/objective link. Fig 3 :25-26: perception and planning "have obvious failures and obvious tests. The link between them, having terms for what a situation offers and being able to say what would count as progress, has neither, so it attracts the least attention and the fewest instruments." Fig 3 :27: "A link with no instrument is not a link that works. It is a link nobody has looked at." Measurement invariance tests are an instrument at link 3; Boehnke 2026 and Poortinga, van Herk and Fontaine 2025 are exactly about whether that instrument's output can be carried across habitats. Effort spent at links 1 and 2 cannot rescue a broken link 3 (Fig 3 :19).

## 3. Findings

### F1 - A single-population baseline fails the anchor test (decisive, triangulated)

Fig 2 :29-30:

> **"An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks. A measurement, an experiment, a counter, a market, a patient outcome. If it moves when you push on it, it is not an anchor."**

Fig 2 :33-34:

> **"The anchor must not update."**
> **"If it does, panel 3 turns into panel 1: the anchor becomes another frame, everything drifts together, and the drift is coherent, which is why nobody notices."**

A norm measured in an industrialized sample changes its answer when the population changes. If researchers range other populations against it as if it were the anchor, the anchor has been promoted from a frame. Fig 2 :47 names the failure: "A ground listed as one of the parties has already become a frame." The drift is coherent, so everyone involved can point at the "baseline" they were supposedly grounded on.

The metrology analogue is unusually clean. Wikipedia, *Standard (metrology)*, fetched 2026-11-22: "Standards are the fundamental reference for a system of weights and measures, against which all other measuring devices are compared." The same page records that "Working standards are expected to deteriorate, and are no longer considered traceable to a national standard after a time period or use count expires." A local norm is a working standard with an expiry, not a primary standard. The figure's phrasing is the same claim in general form: "the ground does not decay, the channel does" (Fig 10 :24).

**Triangulation:** Fig 2 (anchor test) plus Fig 10 (channel decay) plus the independent metrology frame (low overlap: engineering, not psychology). Verdict `triangulated`. The metrology source is a secondary analogue, not a witness for the human fact.

**Refuter:** show a single-population sample whose answer does not move when the population changes, or a ground in this domain that updates on what a frame thinks. Until then the baseline is a frame, not an anchor.

### F2 - More of the same population cannot add the missing atom (triangulated)

Fig 8 :32:

> **"Closure is idempotent. Composing inside a closed set never leaves it, so no search, however long, adds an atom. This one is definitional: it is what closure means."**

Fig 6 :21: **"MINT composes inside the closure and can never add an atom."** Fig 6 :23: **"No frame escapes its own closure. The bound moves only on contact with a frame whose closure differs."**

A larger WEIRD sample is more search inside the same closure. It reduces sampling error, which is a budget achievement, and it does not add the population whose absence is the residual. Fig 7 :12-13: "same closure, handed along; agreement compounds, evidence does not." Fig 7 :36: "A chain of mirrors is one long room. The next room must differ from the last, and match the residual it surfaced."

Fig 8 :53: **"Residual first, frame second."** The correct order is to describe the effect that failed to resolve, then find the frame whose closure predicts its shape. Adding subjects before describing the residual is picking the frame first.

**Triangulation:** Fig 6 (closure wall) plus Fig 7 (room chain) plus Fig 8 (idempotence), three low-overlap figures. Verdict `triangulated`.

**Refuter:** demonstrate that increasing N within one population removes the cross-population residual, or that composition inside a closure adds a primitive. Either breaks the figure's closure law.

### F3 - The instrument cannot be specified from a description; it is improved from a worse one already returning something (triangulated)

Fig 6 :32:

> **"An instrument is not built from a description. It is improved from a worse instrument already returning something."**

Fig 6 :34:

> **"Where nothing is, there is no edge to extend from, and the search for a proto-instrument is a search for one that is already there."**

The tempting answer to the question is "design a culture-free test." That specifies an instrument from a description, which the figure forbids. What exists is a worse instrument already returning something: the translated questionnaire, the item bank, the informant interview, the behavioural task that partially works in two populations. The work is to improve that instrument against the residual it produces, not to declare a neutral one into existence.

This is exactly the movement in Poortinga, van Herk and Fontaine 2025 (*Online Readings in Psychology and Culture*, fetched 2026-11-22): "Any comparison between participants from two, or more, populations presumes a common quality (property, attribute) and requires a common measurement scale, a 'standard of comparison'." They add three considerations: "(i) representation of all participating populations at all stages of instrument development, (ii) emphasis on content validity, and (iii) various tools such as item banks and item parameters from IRT models that can help to derive a comparison standard from only partly overlapping item sets." Item identity is relaxed; the standard of comparison is built jointly. That is an instrument improved from a worse one, not one specified from a description.

Boehnke 2026 (same venue, fetched 2026-11-22) goes further: "Culturally inclusive methodologies independently develop items across various cultures, translate them into a lingua franca, preserve translatable items, collect data, and corroborate invariance." His radical strategy has developers "independently create items, collect data without translation", then "rank the items based on their loadings on the primary factor", with "Semantic sameness is decoupled from covariance matrix invariance, only the latter counts." This is a deliberate decoupling of the shared meaning (a convention, Fig 2 :41) from the verdict about the world.

**Triangulation:** Fig 6 :32-34 plus Poortinga et al. 2025 plus Boehnke 2026. The two methodology sources share the ORPC venue and part of the cross-cultural evidence pool, so they count as one witness, not two. The figure line is primary. Verdict `triangulated` across figure and field, with world overlap declared.

**Refuter:** produce a genuinely culture-free instrument whose construction did not begin from a worse instrument already returning something, or show that an item set with no representation of the populations being compared is sufficient.

### F4 - A local norm is a recording of one occasion, and promoting it is the flat-formula error (triangulated)

Fig 4 :19-20:

> **"The kernel is the part that does not change with size: the same loop and the same rule for naming what worked, whether one agent is running it or a thousand. Only the actors, the currency and the anchor change."**

Fig 4 :25-26:

> **"A recording carried upward looks like knowledge and is a description of one occasion. Replayed downward, it produces a system that repeats a past success and cannot produce a new one."**

What varies across cultures, under this reading, is the actors, the currency and the anchor, not the kernel. The industrialized norm is a description of one occasion. Carrying it upward as "the human baseline" mistakes the recording for the method. Replaying it downward into another habitat produces a system that repeats a past success: administer the same item, obtain a different response, and record the population as deficient.

Fig 12 :5: "A set of ingredients is not one. The bond is the rest of the information, and it is the half nothing was recording." The questionnaire item's ingredients (the words, the scale, the response) are recorded; the bond to the local practice that gave them meaning is not. Fig 12 :94: "The arrangement is the substance." Poortinga et al. make the same move in methodological vocabulary by requiring content validity and joint development, not just item identity.

**Correction to my own first pass.** I initially wrote this as "a local norm can never be called universal." That overstates it. A local norm *can* be promoted if the ground settles it or if it arrives by a provenanced import with a shadow test (Fig 6 :23, Fig 8 :50-52). What is refused is the unexamined promotion, not every promotion. The correction is preserved here rather than silently replacing the first reading.

**Triangulation:** Fig 4 (membrane, recording versus method) plus Fig 12 (bond, arrangement) plus the methodology sources. Verdict `triangulated`.

**Refuter:** show that the recording of one occasion crosses upward as a re-applicable method without loss, or that the bond carries no information the operands do not.

### F5 - Agreement inside one evidence pool is one witness, not corroboration (decisive, triangulated)

Fig 2 :31-32:

> **"Independence, between two frames, is how little they draw on the same evidence. Two people reading the same report are not two frames; two instruments pointed at the same thing are."**

Fig 2 :40: **"disqualifying for a verdict: agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool"**

Fig 8 :48: **"How much two frames draw on the same evidence decides both whether their agreement means anything and whether their union has any surplus. Two frames that overlap almost completely are one frame wearing two names."**

Researchers who learned the same canon, read the same Henrich et al. review and use the same translated instrument are one frame wearing many names. The fact that 40 studies find the same WEIRD pattern is not 40 witnesses; it is one pool spelling. Fig 2 :42: "Buy independence in proportion to the cost of being wrong." The cost here is high, because the claim being checked is about the whole species.

The apparent counterexample in the literature is measurement invariance: Cheung and Rensvold 2002 and Steenkamp and Baumgartner 1998 (both cited in Wikipedia, *Measurement invariance*, fetched 2026-11-22) treat agreement of factor structure across groups as the criterion for comparability. Welzel, Brunkert, Kruse and Inglehart 2021 and Welzel, Kruse and Brunkert 2022 (same page) reply that "the invariance criteria favor constructs with low between-group variance, while constructs with high between-group variance fail these tests." This is the strain of §6 S4. It dissolves by re-census: an invariance test is a **convention** in Fig 2 :41's sense ("efficient for a convention: agreement about a shared meaning is the product, not a corruption of it"), not a witness for a verdict about the world. Requiring shared meaning before comparing scores is exactly right; treating the shared meaning as evidence that the construct is universal is the category error.

Henrich, Heine and Norenzayan 2010 (*Behavioral and Brain Sciences* 33(2-3):61-83, abstract via Europe PMC, fetched 2026-11-22) state the positive case: "there are no obvious a priori grounds for claiming that a particular behavioral phenomenon is universal based on sampling from a single subpopulation." They describe WEIRD subjects as "frequent outliers" across "visual perception, fairness, cooperation, spatial reasoning, categorization and inferential induction, moral reasoning, reasoning styles, self-concepts and related motivations, and the heritability of IQ." Note the scope: this is a reason to refuse the promotion, not a list of non-universals, and not an assertion that every domain varies.

**Triangulation:** Fig 2 plus Fig 7 plus Fig 8, with the world sources as secondary corroboration from a different field. Verdict `triangulated`.

**Refuter:** demonstrate that agreement among frames sharing an evidence pool carries verdict weight for a cross-population claim, or that two studies using the same instrument and the same literature are independent frames.

### F6 - Capability is agent-and-habitat; there is no standing list of universal norms (decisive, triangulated)

Fig 11 :39:

> **"So the answerable question is not what intelligence is, but what makes a system capable. And capability is a property of agent-and-habitat, never of the agent alone."**

Fig 11 :40: **"A system cannot be made more capable by improving the agent in isolation. An improvement that does not change contact changes nothing, however much it improves."**

Fig 11 :43-44: **"The habitat is enumerated, never composed. List everything in contact with the residual, then what is in contact with those, and outward until the cascade stops mattering."**

Fig 11 :48: **"You do not invent the list. You read it off the world, and what you cannot perceive or measure yet is the residual."**

Fig 11 :51: **"No standing list of good actors survives a change of residual."**

A cognitive norm is a property of the person-in-habitat. When the habitat changes, the same task can afford something different, so the same score need not register the same capacity. Fig 11 :49: "An actor's valence is relative to the residual, not a property it carries." A test item is not "culturally biased" or "culture-fair" in itself; its valence is relative to the residual being chased. And since no standing list of good actors survives a change of residual, no standing list of universal norms survives a change of question either.

The low-overlap corroborator here is biosemiotics and animal behaviour. Wikipedia, *Umwelt* (fetched 2026-11-22): "organisms, despite sharing the same physical environment, can inhabit distinct perceptual realities." The tick's umwelt is reduced to "three (biosemiotic) carriers of significance": butyric acid, 37 degrees Celsius, and the hairy topography of mammals. That is a non-human case of the same shape: the apparent world is habitat-relative, and no instrument reads the world "as it is" apart from a sensorium. The framework's own essay says it directly (*They're Scanning Books*, fetched 2026-11-22): "A minimum viable set of six terms is a human's careful guess at reality's compression axes; another sensorium's minimum set need not be those six." And: "Declaring the primitives does not make them neutral. It makes the choice visible."

**Triangulation:** Fig 11 plus Fig 4 :19-20 plus the animal-behaviour/semiotics analogue (genuinely low overlap). Verdict `triangulated`. The analogue supports the shape, not the human fact.

**Refuter:** show a cognitive performance invariant across habitats whose contact structures differ, or derive a standing list of norms whose valence does not change with the residual.

### F7 - Absence of variation found is not proof of universality (single-source on the figure side, corroborated by a different field)

Fig 8 :39:

> **"So a search that finds nothing is never a proof of absence."**

Fig 9 :44: **"Only a sealed room can be searched to the end, and no frame is a sealed room. So searching and finding nothing proves nothing."**

A study that finds no cross-cultural difference in some task has not found a universal. It has failed to find a difference with the instruments it held. Fig 6 :27: "Which is why some unreachability is depth and some is genuine absence, and from inside the frame the two look the same." The remedy is to ask SEARCHED first (is it in here and unfound?), then INWARD (is the representation adequate to hold it?), then OUTWARD (does another frame already hold it?), keeping the three questions distinct (Formula step 7; Fig 6 :28-31).

The framework's own *The Hard Problem is About Source* (fetched 2026-11-22) gives the instrument-level version: "If several paths create the same behavior, then behavior is a projection with lossy information." An instrument that reads green "cannot comment on the source." It was the spectrometer, not an argument, that resolved the case. Applied here: a score that reads the same across two populations does not say whether the same capacity produced it.

**Verdict:** `single-source` on the figure side (Fig 8 :39 is the governing line), with low-overlap corroboration from the metamerism argument and from Simpson's paradox in statistics (Wikipedia, *Simpson's paradox*, fetched 2026-11-22: "a trend appears in several groups of data but disappears or reverses when the groups are combined"). The statistics source corroborates the aggregation danger independently of the framework; it does not corroborate the figure's metalaw.

**Refuter:** show a sealed human frame whose exhaustive search would licence a universal claim, or a reading that determines its source without a discriminating instrument.

### F8 - The round trip is owed (single-source on the figure side)

Fig 4 :30:

> **"So take the round trip and measure the gap. Without it you are navigating by dead reckoning: confident about a position nobody checked."**

Fig 4 :31-32: "The gap is not an error to eliminate. It is the measurement of what the coarser description cannot hold, and it is the only honest report of how much was given up."

Take a norm from population P up to the claim "human cognitive norm", then back down to population H. The gap R_T between what was sent and what comes back is the thing to report. If it is assumed to be zero, the promotion is dead reckoning. `11_audit.md` records R_T qualitatively for this folder.

**Verdict:** `single-source` (Fig 4). The message-level analogue in *Decoding Tacit Knowledge* (fetched 2026-11-22) corroborates: "When contact isn't possible, the best you can send is a plain description of what you'd expect to see, because anyone can check that against the world." Same author as the figures, so no independent witness weight.

**Refuter:** show a promotion whose abstraction and concretisation are lossless, or a measurement of R_T equal to zero in a case where the habitat changed.

### F9 - The separator that survives (the answer)

The figures do not return a list of universal norms. They return the conditions any legitimate promotion must satisfy, and the refusal conditions. Mode `general` on the shape, `specified` on each condition.

**Conditions that must hold before a local norm is promoted:**

1. **Describe R in effect terms before picking a frame.** Fig 9 :27-28: "describe it before you go looking, or any frame you pick will seem to fit." Fig 8 :53: "Residual first, frame second."
2. **Index the residual per population and per task slot.** Fig 1 :19, :22, :28. Never average the live signal into a grand "culture effect".
3. **Name the industrialization prior as a binding, not a neutral primitive.** Fig 9 :32: "you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself." *They're Scanning Books*: "Declaring the primitives does not make them neutral. It makes the choice visible."
4. **Buy independence with genuinely different closures.** Fig 2 :31-32, :42; Fig 8 :48-49. Two instruments pointed at the same thing, not two reports of the same thing.
5. **Keep a non-updating ground in each habitat.** Fig 2 :29-30, :33; Fig 10 :24. The ground is what the task actually affords there, read off the world (Fig 11 :48), not agreement among researchers.
6. **Enumerate the habitat outward.** Fig 11 :43-48, plus contact cascades (Fig 11 :52-54: "first-order enumeration, second-order effect, and the residual that mattered was three steps out"). A one-population item set is a first-order scope.
7. **Measure the round trip.** Fig 4 :30-32.
8. **Bind the sought-for shape as an ordinary term and write a refuter before the run.** Fig 5 :65: "make the sought-for shape an ORDINARY TERM. Write down what an answer would look like, bind it, and let it accrue a residual like anything else." Seat map :287: "a falsifier written before the run is a design instrument, not only an honesty device."
9. **Keep the operator that can refuse.** Operators :58: "a refutation is a negation, which is why a vocabulary without it can only accumulate." A methodology with equivalence tests but no refusal operator can only accumulate norms.

**Conditions that require refusal:**

- The baseline moves when the population changes (F1).
- The only difference between frames is sample size or wording (F2, F5).
- The instrument was specified from a description rather than improved from a worse one (F3).
- The local norm is being replayed downward as a recording (F4).
- No variance found is being read as universality (F7).
- R_T is assumed zero (F8).

The business/management analogue corroborates the refusal side. Wikipedia, *Hofstede's cultural dimensions theory* (fetched 2026-11-22), records the sampling critique: "privileged males working as engineers or sales personnel in one of the elite organizations of the world, pioneering one of the first multinational projects in history, cannot be claimed to represent their nations" (citing Witte 2012). Even a widely used cross-cultural instrument fails the anchor test when its sampling frame is examined. That source is from organizational studies, a different pool from the cognitive literature, so it is a genuinely low-overlap corroborator of the shape.

### F10 - The literal answer is a declared gap (verdict, not silence)

No figure line names which cognitive norms are universal, which populations should be sampled, how many items make a valid standard of comparison, or what R_T equals for any particular norm. Fig 9 :32: "you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself." Fig 13 :134: "Unreachable is a verdict here, not a silence". Fig 13 :134-135 adds why naming is impossible from inside: "It cannot say which primitive is missing, because naming it would mean already holding it."

This is **not** a claim that the question is unanswerable in principle. Of the three reachability questions (Formula step 7), SEARCHED is first and cheapest; the answer is not in the figure library, so this is neither "unreachable at this budget" by search nor a proof of underivability. It is a **shape-not-a-name gap**: the figures return a separator, and an instrument is owed to fill the slot. Report it as unreached, not as silence and not as an invitation to invent a universal list.

World sources do not fill it either. Henrich et al. 2010 decline to provide a list; they propose "ways to structurally re-organize the behavioral sciences". Poortinga et al. 2025 and Boehnke 2026 provide procedures, not a roster of universals. A roster would need ground settlement per norm, which no source in this folder performs.

## 4. Pre-registered refuter (written before the conclusion)

Written here before §3 F9 was composed and before `07_the_tether_answer.md` existed. Per seat map :287, this is a design instrument. The separator is refuted if any of the following holds:

1. **R1, the list fires.** A figure line is found that returns a standing list of universal cognitive norms, or a figure derivation names one. That closes the declared gap and the separator must be rewritten around it.
2. **R2, the anchor fires.** A single-population sample is shown to serve as a non-updating anchor for a cross-population claim, that is, its answer does not move when the population changes. Breaks Fig 2 :29-30.
3. **R3, the pool fires.** Agreement among frames sharing an evidence pool is shown to carry verdict weight for a claim about the world. Breaks Fig 2 :40 and Fig 8 :48.
4. **R4, the habitat fires.** A cognitive performance is shown to be invariant across habitats that differ in contact, with a discriminating instrument. Breaks Fig 11 :39-40.
5. **R5, the middle link fires.** A task's meaning is shown to be invariant across populations without instrument work, that is, the vocabulary and objective links of Fig 3 need no measurement. Breaks Fig 3 :25-27.

### The derivation in seven steps (compact)

1. **Describe R first.** Per population-and-task slot, in effect terms (Fig 1 :19, Fig 9 :27-28). Binding the sought-for shape as an ordinary term makes the preference refusable (Fig 5 :65).
2. **Anchor test.** A baseline that moves with the sample is a frame (Fig 2 :29-30, Fig 10 :24).
3. **Closure test.** More of the same population adds no atom (Fig 6 :21, Fig 8 :32); independence must be bought (Fig 2 :42).
4. **Instrument test.** Improve a worse instrument already returning something (Fig 6 :32); joint development and content validity are the known instances (Poortinga et al. 2025).
5. **Habitat test.** Enumerate, never compose; capability is agent-and-habitat (Fig 11 :39, :43-48).
6. **Round trip.** Measure R_T rather than assuming zero (Fig 4 :30).
7. **Refusal.** Bind the shape, write the refuter, keep negation (Fig 5 :65, Operators :58, seat map :287). Where no line returns the list, record `gap` and an instrument owed (Fig 13 :134).

### Updated verdict

The separator survives; a universal roster does not. The figures decide that a single-population baseline fails the anchor test, that same-closure search cannot add the missing population, that the instrument must be improved from one already returning something, that capability is agent-and-habitat, that agreement inside one evidence pool is one witness, and that no standing list of norms survives a change of residual. What is owed is a procedure with a measured round trip and a fireable refuter. Over a fixed set of conditions, an industrialized-first promotion and a jointly developed promotion are isomers under Fig 12 :117 ("same atoms, different bond, different substance"), which is the supporting derivation in `06_isomers_and_order.md`. Mode `general` on the shape, `specified` on the conditions, `grounded` on none of them. Strains after re-census: none live.
