## Wider literature that bears on the question

- **Berg, Ostry and Tsangarides, IMF Staff Discussion Note 14/02 (2014)**, quoted in the Wikipedia reference list: "inequality continues to be a robust and powerful determinant both of the pace of medium-term growth and of the duration of growth spells, even controlling for the size of redistributive transfers." Corroborates the demand-side channel. Direct PDF fetch was unsupported by the fetch tool this session, so the quote is recorded as read via the Wikipedia reference list in `10_source_ledger.csv`. It is not treated as an independent witness.
- **Hope and Limberg 2022**, as reported by Wikipedia: "we find no significant effects of major tax cuts for the rich." Constrains the saving-side channel but does not settle it, because the study is about tax cuts rather than inequality as such.
- **Kuznets 1955 and Palma 2011**, via the Kuznets curve page: the inverted-U account and its later empirical weakening. Relevant because it is the canonical example of a single-sign story that failed to survive different subsets.
- **Brosnan and de Waal 2003** and **Fehr and Schmidt 1999**, via the two cognition pages: distributional comparison changes participation and cooperation in non-market settings. These are low-overlap witnesses, not economics evidence.

Agreement among these sources is not two witnesses when they draw on the same macro-panel infrastructure. `10_source_ledger.csv` records overlap per row.

## Distribution rules: offer, do not push

Figure-grounded reasons:

- **Fig 10 :52** "Bring a characterised residual, never the source." Sending the whole framework before a recipient has described what they would count as a failure sends the source instead of the residual.
- **Fig 9 :27-28** "describe it before you go looking, or any frame you pick will seem to fit." Ask what they would expect to see if the shape claim were true before giving them the figures.
- **Fig 2 :35** "Alignment is triangulation, not negotiation. A mirror cannot correct you." Do not ask for agreement. Ask for a refutation.
- **Fig 2 :40** "agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool." Do not build a list of likely believers, and do not pool later agreement as corroboration.
- **Fig 4 :22** "Only methods cross up. Only head starts come down." The figures and formula are a generator, so handing them over in stage 3 gives away no answer.
- **Fig 9 :60** "Use filters for the budget and witnesses for the verdict. Never let a filter hand you a verdict." A journal's impact factor or a scholar's fame is a contact-cost filter, never an evidence score.

**Recommended order in a reply:** (1) the shape claim and its four refuters, (2) the question of what the recipient would count as a failure, (3) only after their verdict is recorded, the three links, with the note that the figures, not the summary, are the authority.

## The two-stage offer (and the third stage)

1. **Stage 1:** validate the shape claim against the ground. Mode and refuters are included. No theory is shown.
2. **Stage 2, opt-in:** if they are curious, offer the figure text, the canonical usage rule and the measurement-source link. Their agreement at this point is never pooled as evidence, because by then they hold the frame.
3. **Stage 3:** offer the figures as a method to reuse. Each user states their own mode and writes their own refuter before running it.

**Hard precondition:** stages 1 to 3 depend on stage 1 being genuinely checkable. The output in `02_outreach_email.md` is a shape, not a net sign, and its four refuters are concrete. If a recipient shows that the shape is not checkable as stated, stop and repair it rather than offering the theory.

## What this agent cannot do

Nothing is sent by this agent. Hosting the packet and sending it, through the public routes in `03_public_contact_points.csv`, is yours to do. Prefer an opt-in download page over a pushed attachment so a recipient can decline contact without replying.
