# The Tether's answer on inequality and growth

**Mode: `general` on the structural shape, `specified` on the slot-indexed mechanism predictions. Not `grounded`.** This is a simulated derivation checked against the figures (`05_what_shape_means.md` section 7), not a ground reading. No economic measurement was taken.

## 1. The residual it answers

The question asks for the sign of the response of growth to a shift in income distribution. The reported quantity, a single aggregate growth rate, is not the residual: it is what hides the residual. Fig 1 :22 "Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average." The live signal is which slots carry spendable income mass when the distribution moves, and which residual each candidate mechanism is answering. Fig 1 :19 "The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated."

## 2. The shape

1. **Growth must be ranged per slot.** Fig 1 :13 "a global R near zero with one live slot residual is a legal state." A healthy aggregate can coexist with a failing distributional channel. There is no single sign until the slot is named.
2. **The two mechanisms are a union, not two answers.** Fig 9 :55 "A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones." The demand channel (H) and the saving channel (S) are both real, both can bind at once, and a lever that clears one leaves the other standing (Fig 9 :56 "A single lever clears the region where only one condition holds and leaves the intersections standing, because the second condition still binds."). Fig 9 :54 "And when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again."
3. **The sign of an actor is relative to the residual.** Fig 11 :50 "The same actor is a benefactor to one residual and a malfactor to another, so mutualism and parasitism are relations rather than kinds." A concentrated high-income household is a benefactor to investable capital and a malfactor to broad demand. Fig 11 :51 "No standing list of good actors survives a change of residual."
4. **The measurement frame is not the ground.** Fig 2 :30 "If it moves when you push on it, it is not an anchor." Reported inequality and growth statistics change with the frame (income versus consumption, survey versus tax records), so they are instruments, not the thing that settles.
5. **The concentrated difference is spendable, so the saving channel is not empty.** Fig 13 :22 "a difference that can be spent." The spendable difference at the top can become investment; the spendable difference at the bottom can become demand. This is a `specified` binding onto held atoms, not a derivation of economics from the six-term set.
6. **The net sign is a gap.** Fig 8 :39 "So a search that finds nothing is never a proof of absence." Fig 13 :134 "Unreachable is a verdict here, not a silence." No figure line governs an empirical net effect. That is a verdict of unreachability at this budget, not an omission and not a licence to pick a side.

## 3. What it explicitly does not say

- Not "inequality hinders growth" and not "inequality helps growth." Both are refused as written (F1 to F3 of `01`).
- Not a new empirical result, a reanalysis of panels, or a refutation of the existing literature.
- Not that the figures measure an economy. They constrain the shape of a checkable answer; the ground decides it.
- Not that the demand channel dominates the saving channel or the reverse. Verdicts are kept separate and never averaged.
- Not `grounded`. Nothing here is asserted as settled by measurement.
- Not a claim that having an aggregate is useless. Fig 13 :110 "And each composition becomes an atom for whatever composes over it." The aggregate is legitimate as a second-order composition over settled slot-level events. It just cannot carry the per-slot signal back to the mechanism.

## 4. What would settle it, the ground

A slot-indexed, frame-checked instrument, not a further argument. From *The Hard Problem is About Source*: an instrument that reads green "cannot comment on the source"; the spectrometer resolved it. Here the corresponding questions are:

- **Slot test:** at which income slots does an incremental unit of income change spending or investment, and by how much? An instrument that reports the marginal response per slot would name the mechanism.
- **Union test:** in which regions does H bind, in which S, and in which both? A design that estimates the two conditions separately rather than averaging them would decide the unbundling.
- **Frame test:** does the estimated response survive the income/consumption and survey/tax frame change? If it does not, the frame is still speaking for the ground.
- **Objective test:** what counts as broad-based, decided independently of the measure used to report growth? This is the broken link 3 of Fig 3.
- **Standing refuters:** an aggregate that carries the full slot signal; a single loop that settles both mechanisms at once; an actor whose sign is invariant across residuals; a frame-invariant statistic that settles the question at the ground. If any fires, the shape is withdrawn.

## 5. The honest stop

**Unreached, not unreachable in principle.** The shape stops at the limit of what the available instruments distinguish, and records the lineage so the stop is checkable. From *How Intelligence Actually Works*: "The recursion converges toward indiscernibility, and indiscernibility is not identity." A cross-country regression that fits the data is still a projection until a slot-level instrument discriminates the two mechanisms. The literature's own record supports the stop: the Kuznets curve page reports "The hypothesis remains highly contested among economists, with no consensus regarding its validity," and Palma's finding that "the statistical evidence for the 'upwards' side of the 'Inverted-U' between inequality and income per capita seems to have vanished."

## 6. One-paragraph version for outreach

> The Tether does not say that inequality helps growth or that it hinders growth. It says that growth cannot be ranged against a single aggregate when the two candidate mechanisms act at different slots: lower-income slots spend a larger share, so concentrating income away from them weakens demand; higher-income slots save and invest a larger share, so concentrating income toward them strengthens investable capital. The same concentration is a benefit to one residual and a cost to the other, and the sign of any actor depends on which residual you range. So the question is a union of two overlapping conditions, not one question with two possible answers, and it must be unbundled before it is searched. The reported growth and inequality statistics are frame products, not anchors; a slot-indexed, frame-checked instrument is what the question is owed. The framework returns that shape, the refuters written in advance, and the instrument that would close it. It does not return a net sign, and it stops there honestly.
