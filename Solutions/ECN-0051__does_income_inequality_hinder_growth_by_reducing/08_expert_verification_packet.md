# Expert verification packet: the Tether on inequality and growth

**What this is:** a claim in a form an inequality or growth researcher can check. Mode, derivation and refuters are stated before the conclusion, so a reader knows what to look for and what would settle it.

**Mode: `general` on the structural shape, `specified` on the slot-indexed mechanism predictions. Not `grounded`.** No empirical measurement is claimed. This is a simulated derivation checked for internal consistency against the figures, not a ground reading. It cannot settle the question it reports on.

## 1. The claim, in one paragraph

The response of growth to a shift in income distribution cannot be registered by a single aggregate rate, because the two candidate mechanisms act at different slots with different currencies. Lower-income slots spend a larger share of income, so moving income away from them reduces broad demand. Higher-income slots save and invest a larger share, so moving income toward them increases investable capital. These are two overlapping conditions, not two answers to one question: a lever that clears one leaves the other standing where both bind. The sign of any actor is relative to the residual, so "inequality helps" and "inequality hinders" are both under-specified until a slot and a residual are named. Reported growth and inequality statistics are frame products, not anchors. The framework returns this shape, refuters written in advance, and the instrument that would close it. It returns no net sign, and reports that absence as a `gap`.

## 2. Three checkable sub-claims

### C1 — framework-internal; checkable by reading Figures 1 and 9

- **Statement:** the residual is per slot, and the two mechanisms are a union of overlapping conditions that must be unbundled. No single net sign follows.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 1 :19 "The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated." Fig 1 :22 "Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average." Fig 9 :54 "And when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again." Fig 9 :55 "A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones."
- **Refuter:** show that an aggregate statistic carries the full slot-indexed signal, or exhibit a single closed loop with one ground that settles both mechanisms simultaneously with no overlapping-condition residue.

### C2 — social-science facing; checkable against the two mechanisms

- **Statement:** the demand channel and the saving channel operate at different slots with different currencies, and the same concentration benefits one residual while costing the other. Over a shared atom set they are a real isomer claim; as usually written they are a functional group.
- **What to check:** is it true at the margin that lower-income households have a higher propensity to consume and higher-income households a higher propensity to save and invest? Do the two conditions bind in different subsets (demand-constrained versus supply-constrained), so that a single lever leaves one of them standing? Is there any actor whose sign is invariant across residuals?
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 11 :49 "An actor's valence is relative to the residual, not a property it carries." Fig 11 :50 "The same actor is a benefactor to one residual and a malfactor to another, so mutualism and parasitism are relations rather than kinds." Fig 12 :117 "isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."
- **Refuter:** show an actor with invariant sign across residuals, or show that one mechanism requires an atom the other does not (which kills the isomer claim and leaves the functional-group claim standing).

### C3 — measurement facing; checkable against public data documentation

- **Statement:** the reported quantities are frame products, not anchors, and the ground owed is a slot-indexed frame-checked instrument.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 2 :29 "An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks." Fig 2 :30 "If it moves when you push on it, it is not an anchor." Fig 1 :27 "They are frame-internal. A frame cannot score itself with a quantity it also produces."
- **World check:** Our World in Data (2026) records that "income-based Gini coefficients are about 13% higher than consumption-based ones for the same country"; the WID FAQ states that its estimates "should however not be seen as perfect and definitive."
- **Refuter:** produce a growth or inequality statistic invariant across the income/consumption and survey/tax frame choices that settles the question at the ground.

## 3. What is explicitly not claimed

- Not "inequality hinders growth" and not "inequality helps growth."
- Not a net sign, not a new empirical result, not a reanalysis of panels.
- Not that the figures measure an economy or can decide an empirical causal effect.
- Not that the demand channel dominates the saving channel or the reverse. Verdicts are separate and are never averaged.
- Not `grounded`. No claim here is asserted as settled by experiment or data.
- Not that all existing inequality-and-growth work is wrong. Its non-shrinking disagreement is a symptom of the union (Fig 9 :4), not a refutation of the literature.

## 5. What would settle it

A slot-indexed, frame-checked instrument, not a further argument. From *The Hard Problem is About Source*: an instrument that reads green "cannot comment on the source"; the spectrometer resolved it. Here:

1. **Slot test:** the marginal response of spending and investment per income slot, measured.
2. **Union test:** which subset each condition binds in, estimated separately rather than averaged.
3. **Frame test:** whether the response survives the income/consumption and survey/tax frame change.
4. **Objective test:** what counts as broad-based, decided independently of the growth measure.

Any of the four refuters in section 2 firing would instead remove the shape claim.

## 6. What an expert can check in twenty minutes

1. Read Fig 1 :19, Fig 1 :22, Fig 9 :54-55, Fig 11 :49-50 and Fig 2 :29-30, eight short passages.
2. Check C2 against their own knowledge: do the two mechanisms operate at different slots, and does any actor keep the same sign across residuals?
3. Name one instrument that would report the marginal response per slot, or say why none is possible.
4. Send the refutation. A failure is more useful than an endorsement.
