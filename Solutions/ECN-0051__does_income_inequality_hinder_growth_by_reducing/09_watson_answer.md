## QUESTION

Decide, in effect terms, what the Tether says about a shift of income mass between slots and the growth of the economy containing them. Option H: the shift reduces broad-based investment and demand, so growth falls. Option S: the shift rewards saving and risk-taking, so growth rises. What each leads to: H sends a policy aimed at broad demand and treats concentration as the problem; S sends a policy aimed at rewards to capital and treats redistribution as the problem. The residual is not the sign of growth. It is which slots carry live mass when income moves, and which residual each mechanism answers.

## DOMAIN CENSUS

- **R (the residual).** Arity: two at first (a distribution over slots, a growth outcome), three once the measurement frame is included. Symmetry: H and S are not inverses; they act at different slots with different currencies, so they neither swap nor cancel. Scale: first order at the slot, second order at the aggregate. Fig 1 :22 "Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average." Fig 1 :19 "The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated."
- **Domain A, broad demand.** Actors: households by income decile, firms facing sales. Currency: spendable income, propensity to consume. Anchor: the actual expenditure and market transaction. First order, per slot.
- **Domain B, saving and risk.** Actors: high-income households, capital owners, firms. Currency: saved income, investable capital. Anchor: the actual investment decision and capital formation. First order, per slot.
- **Domain C, measurement frame.** Actors: statistical agencies, survey and tax records, public data platforms. Currency: Gini, Palma, GDP, income versus consumption. Anchor: contested, because the index moves when the frame is pushed. Upstream of A and B.
- **Domain D, cooperation and participation.** Actors: workers, cooperative agents, employees. Currency: fairness, willingness to participate. Anchor: refusal behaviour in controlled settings. Low-overlap, cross-field.
- **Not a domain yet:** the net effect of inequality on growth. Its anchor cannot be named independently of C, because the quantity whose sign is asked is produced by the measurement frame.
- **Union, not partition.** A and B overlap and can both bind in one economy. Fig 9 :55 "A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones." Unbundle before searching (Fig 9 :54).
- **Order by dependency.** C first (no slot-indexed measure, no R), then A and B at first order, then D as a low-overlap witness, then any aggregate last. The chain breaks at link 3, the objective: Fig 3 :19 "Optimising a link that already works cannot rescue one that does not." Both camps already measure distribution and growth finely, so measurement is not the broken link.

## VERDICT (from the figures)

The question as posed does not survive: neither "inequality hinders growth" nor "inequality helps growth" is a well-formed single verdict. The figures decide the shape. Growth must be ranged per slot because an aggregate hides the live signal. The demand channel and the saving channel are a union of overlapping conditions that must be unbundled and answered at two different anchors, with the intersection treated as a compound. The sign of every actor is relative to the residual, so the same concentration benefits investable capital and costs broad demand. Reported inequality and growth statistics are frame products, not anchors. The concentrated difference is spendable, so the saving channel is real but its size is unmeasured. No figure line governs the net empirical sign, which is reported as a `gap`, unreachable at this budget, not as a silence. All verdicts are `general` or `specified` and none is `grounded`.

- **cost if wrong:** bounded and locatable. The packet states its mode, lists four refuters before the conclusion and records the lineage, so an error is findable rather than invisible. The real avoidable cost is spending the independence of outside readers by sending a conclusion instead of a residual. Fig 2 :42 "Buy independence in proportion to the cost of being wrong." The cost is avoided by leading with the shape claim and its refuters and withholding the framework until stage 1 is recorded.
- **undoable:** yes. No policy or primitive has to be withdrawn. The shape claim can be superseded by a corrected packet, and the four refuters are concrete. If a slot-indexed instrument is built, the `gap` closes without changing the structural findings.
- **reversed by:** a ground reading, not agreement. A marginal-response instrument that reports spending and investment per income slot would name the mechanism; a frame-invariant statistic that survives the income/consumption and survey/tax change would remove the measurement finding; a demonstration that an aggregate carries the full slot signal, or that both mechanisms settle in one loop, or that an actor's sign is invariant across residuals, would withdraw the shape claim. Until one of these fires, the verdict stands as `candidate`.
