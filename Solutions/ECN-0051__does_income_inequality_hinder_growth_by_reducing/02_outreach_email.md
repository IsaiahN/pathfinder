**Use:** one-to-one, or as a cover note attached to a submission through the public venues in `03_public_contact_points.csv`. **Not** for bulk send.

**Hard precondition:** do not send until the recipient has been offered stage 1 alone. The residual and the refuter go first. The sources go only after a verdict on stage 1 is recorded. Fig 10 :52 "Bring a characterised residual, never the source."

**Subject:** One checkable claim about inequality and growth, before any theory

> Hello,
>
> I am writing about a derivation on whether income inequality helps or hinders economic growth. I am not asking you to agree with it, to read a framework first, or to trust anything. I have one checkable output and a refuter written in advance. If the output does not survive, there is nothing else to look at.
>
> **Mode: `general` on the structural claim, `specified` on the mechanism predictions. Nothing here is `grounded`.** This is a simulated derivation checked for internal consistency against a set of figures, not a ground reading. It cannot settle the empirical question it reports on.
>
> **The output.** Growth is normally reported as one aggregate rate. The derivation says that the response of growth to a shift in income distribution cannot be registered by that aggregate, because the two candidate mechanisms act at different slots with different currencies. Lower-income slots spend a larger share of income, so moving income away from them reduces broad demand. Higher-income slots save and invest a larger share, so moving income toward them increases investable capital. The same concentration is a benefit to one residual and a cost to the other.
>
> The claim is therefore not "inequality helps" and not "inequality hinders." It is that those are two overlapping conditions, not two answers to one question, and that a single net sign is not well defined until you name the slot and the residual you are ranging against.
>
> **What would refute it. I wrote these down before this message.**
> 1. Show that an aggregate growth statistic carries the full slot-level signal, so no slot-indexed residual is needed to decide between the demand mechanism and the saving mechanism.
> 2. Show a single closed loop with one ground that settles both mechanisms simultaneously, leaving no overlapping-condition residue.
> 3. Show an actor whose sign is invariant across residuals, so a distributional change always raises or always lowers growth whichever slot you track.
> 4. Produce a growth or inequality statistic that is invariant across the income/consumption and survey/tax measurement choices and settles the question at the ground.
>
> If any holds, the shape is wrong and I will retract it. A one-line "this fails because..." is more useful to me than agreement. If you would rather not engage, no reply is needed.
>
> **What I am not claiming:** a net sign for the effect of inequality on growth, a new empirical result, a refutation of the existing literature, or that either the demand side or the saving side is unimportant. I am also not claiming that the figures I used can measure an economy. They constrain the shape of a checkable answer; the ground decides it.
>
> **If it survives, and only then:** the sources are below. The figure text, not my summary of it, is the authority.
> - Verbatim figure text with line numbers (local extract): `FRAMEWORK/reference/03-FIGURES_TEXT.md`
> - Canonical usage rule (local): `FRAMEWORK/reference/FIGURES_README_canonical.md`
> - Verified public data sources on the measurement frame: https://ourworldindata.org/economic-inequality
>
> **If you want to use the machinery:** the figures are public. Run them on a problem you know well and can check against a ground. Write down what would refute your result *before* you run it, and state whether what you got is `general`, `specified`, or `grounded`. A reported failure is more useful to me than a reported success.
>
> Thank you for reading this far.

## Stage order

1. **Stage 1:** send the output and the four refuters. Ask what the recipient would count as a failure. Record the verdict before any theory is shown.
2. **Stage 2, opt-in only:** if they are curious, offer the three links, with the note that the figures are the authority and the summary is not.
3. **Stage 3:** offer the figures as a method to reuse, with the instruction that each user writes their own refuter and states their own mode.

Later agreement is never pooled as corroboration. Per Fig 2 :40, agreement drawn from one evidence pool is one witness, not two.
