## Two findings in the wider literature that bear directly on this question

1. **The candidate field is a pool, not a single witness.** Wikipedia lists axions, axion-like particles, fuzzy cold dark matter, sterile neutrinos, WIMPs, self-interacting dark matter, atomic dark matter, primordial black holes and MACHOs among the hypotheses. That is a union of overlapping conditions, exactly the structure Fig 9 :54 names: *"when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again."* The many candidates do not corroborate one another; they are alternative sources for one lossy gravitational reading.
2. **A null result is a filter, not the witness.** The xenon experiments have pushed cross-section limits down by orders of magnitude without a detection; ADMX has reached part of the plausible axion range; the LHC has not found supersymmetry. Fig 9 :60: *"Use filters for the budget and witnesses for the verdict. Never let a filter hand you a verdict."* The witness is a detection that discriminates among the candidate sources, which is why the Hard Problem is About Source's spectrometer point matters here: the gravitational instrument reads green, but cannot say whether the source is one light, a mixture, or a scheme not yet discovered.
