# Union, slots and order: why the dark matter options are not isomers

**Mode: `specified` on the structure, `general` on the rules. Simulated derivation checked against the figures, not a ground reading** (`05_what_shape_means.md` §6).

## RESIDUAL

The question offers four options (WIMP, axion, another particle, several components). Three sub-questions are owed before a verdict can be stated:

1. Do the options form a partition or a union?
2. Is the residual one global number or a set of per-slot readings?
3. In what order must the loops run, so that effort above a break is not wasted on a link below it?

## DOMAIN CENSUS

Atoms held in D2: WIMP, axion, sterile neutrino, primordial black hole, self-interacting dark matter, fuzzy cold dark matter, and other named candidates from the public reference page. Instruments in D3: xenon TPCs, resonant cavities, gamma-ray telescopes, colliders. Bonds held: the seven, plus negation. No new primitive is available without import, and none is needed to compose the held candidates.

## DERIVATION

### Q1 : Partition or union?

- Fig 9 :54: *"when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again."*
- Fig 9 :55-57: *"A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones. A single lever clears the region where only one condition holds and leaves the intersections standing, because the second condition still binds."* and *"a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either."*
- The Library of Babel essay, read this session: *"Perhaps your problem was a bundle or conflation of several problems masquerading as one? You will need to search all of those problems individually to find their answers and then combine them in a satisfactory way."*
- The world source confirms overlap: Wikipedia records that a 2024 review allows a component to *"coexist with other candidates in a mixed dark matter scenario"*, and that *"Mixtures of the above are also possible."*

**Conclusion:** union, not partition. The option "several components" is not a fourth candidate beside the other three; it changes the type of the question from a list to a compound. Each candidate must get its own loop and its own ground. **Falsifier, written before the run:** a result showing that the observations force exactly one occupant and that the conditions are disjoint (one lever closes every slot). If that holds, the union reading fails and the question is a partition after all. Triangulated (Fig 9 plus the essay plus the world source, low overlap).

### Q2 : Are the options "isomers" under Fig 12?

- Fig 12 :117: *"Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."*
- Fig 12 :124: *"functional group. A substructure whose presence fixes the outcome. The group says what kind; the qualifier says which member."*

The candidate options do **not** share an atom set in the figure sense. A WIMP and an axion are different particles with different masses, interactions and production mechanisms; they are different atoms, not the same atoms under a different bond. So they are not isomers by Fig 12's definition. They are closer to alternative occupants of the same **slot** in the observational frame, that is, members of a functional class defined by their effect (gravitating, non-baryonic, long-lived), with different identities.

The stronger isomer reading would require both candidates to be written over a shared atom set and to differ only in arrangement. No figure line licenses that, and the world source gives no evidence for it. **Falsifier:** a demonstration that two candidates are composed from the same primitives under different bonds and that the difference is decisive in behaviour. Until then, "isomer" is refused as a category error here. Mode: `specified`.

Why this matters: an isomer claim would let one lever address both candidates (same atoms, different arrangement). Refusing it keeps the unbundling requirement of Q1 intact. The two findings are consistent, not a strain.

### Q3 : One global residual or per-slot residuals?

- Fig 1 :19: *"The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated."*
- Fig 1 :22: *"Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average."*
- Fig 2 :31: *"Independence, between two frames, is how little they draw on the same evidence."*

The slots here are the distinct observations: rotation curves, cluster dispersions, lensing, CMB peaks, large-scale structure, nucleosynthesis. A candidate that fits the CMB but not small-scale structure leaves a positive residual in that slot; averaging the slots would hide it. The same rule applies inside a multi-component answer: each component contributes per slot, and the contributions do not add into a single global "dark matter" score.

**Conclusion:** per-slot, never averaged. **Falsifier:** a measurement showing that a single global mass number predicts every slot within error, so slot indexing carries no information. Triangulated (Fig 1 and Fig 2, different arguments).

### Q4 : What is the dependency order of the loops?

Fig 3 names the chain: perception, vocabulary, objective, planning, learn and carry (Fig 3 :19: *"Optimising a link that already works cannot rescue one that does not."*). Applied here:

| link | state in this question | consequence |
|---|---|---|
| 1 perception | the residual registers gravitationally in several slots | live; proceed |
| 2 vocabulary | WIMP, axion, PBH and others are already held in D2 | live; no new atom needed |
| 3 objective | what counts as progress: a discriminating detection or a declared mass-window exclusion | **this is the weak link**; "find dark matter" is not an operational objective |
| 4 planning | instruments are built and pointed at candidates | cannot be fixed until link 3 is stated |
| 5 learn and carry | exclusion limits feed back | works only if link 3 declared which window is being closed |

A link with no instrument is not a link that works; it is a link nobody has looked at (Fig 3 :27). Most effort in the literature lands at link 4 (bigger detectors), which is above the break. The figure-grounded move is to state link 3 in the frame's own terms *before* adding exposure.

**Conclusion:** the dependency order is perceive, state vocabulary, declare the objective, then plan instruments. **Falsifier:** a case where a detector built without a declared objective nevertheless closes a candidate unambiguously. Possible in practice, but it would not falsify the dependency relation; it would show the objective was implicit. Recorded as a limit, not a strain.

### Q5 : Which reachability question comes first?

- Fig 9 :6: *"the verdict: is φ* in here at all?"*
- Fig 8 :40-43: *"Underivable-in-principle and unreachable-at-this-budget are different facts, and they issue different instructions. Underivable: go outside. Unreachable-at-budget: the term is already in the closure and was not found, so the remedy is more search, not another atom."*
- Fig 6 :28: *"Which makes reachability two questions, not one. The instruments are opposed."*

The candidates are in Γ. So the first question is SEARCHED (is it in here and unfound?), then INWARD (extend the instrument from one already returning something), and only then OUTWARD (import a genuinely external kind with a source gate and a shadow test). Fig 8 :53: *"Residual first, frame second."* and :28: *"describe it before you go looking, or any frame you pick will seem to fit."* More searchers buy more search only if they are independent: Fig 6 :53: *"searchers drawing on the same evidence are one searcher wearing many names."* Two reviews of the same xenon data are one frame wearing two names.

**Conclusion:** SEARCHED, then INWARD, then OUTWARD. **Falsifier:** a candidate that is genuinely outside Γ, predicts a discriminating signal, and arrives with provenance and a shadow test; then an import is licensed and the order is satisfied, not broken.

## CONCLUSION

The four options form a union compound, not a partition and not a set of isomers. The residual is indexed per observation slot and must not be averaged. The loops must run in dependency order, with the objective (what would count as a discriminating result) stated before more instrument is built. Reachability is triaged as SEARCHED, then INWARD, then OUTWARD. Each conclusion carries its own falsifier above. None of these conclusions names a particle; the name remains a gap owned by the ground.
