## DOMAIN CENSUS

R by arity, symmetry and scale, then one line per domain with actors / currency / anchor, partition or union, and order by dependency.

- **R (the residual).** Observed gravitation minus the prediction from baryons plus general relativity, read in several slots (rotation curves, cluster dispersions, lensing, CMB peaks, large-scale structure, nucleosynthesis). Arity: one effect, variable number of occupants because "several components" is an option. Symmetry: candidates are not interchangeable under swap; they differ in mass, interaction and production, and the comparison is ordered by which instrument discriminates them. Scale: the residual registers at galactic and cosmological scale; candidate answers live at particle or astrophysical scale, so a lossy cross-scale transform is required (Fig 10 :68).
- **Domain 1, gravitational observation.** Actors: galaxies, clusters, CMB photons, lensing geometry. Currency: measured velocities, lensing masses, peak heights. Anchor: instruments and published data, which do not move when a theorist changes their mind. Question type: where R is registered; the ground end. **Verdict: `specified`, triangulated.**
- **Domain 2, particle theory.** Actors: WIMP, axion, sterile neutrino, primordial black hole, other beyond-Standard-Model terms. Currency: predicted cross sections, relic abundance, mass windows. Anchor: none of its own for identity; eventual detector confirmation. Question type: held terms in Γ forming a union candidate set. **Verdict: union structure `general`, triangulated; identity `gap`.**
- **Domain 3, detector and instrument.** Actors: xenon TPCs (XENONnT, LZ), resonant cavities (ADMX), gamma-ray (Fermi-LAT), collider (LHC). Currency: exclusion limits, exposure, null results, candidate events. Anchor: the ground, a detection or a closed declared window. Question type: the instrument that would settle which term pays. **Verdict: unreachable-at-this-budget, not underivable; `specified`.**
- **Domain 4, verification and outreach.** Actors: expert readers in dark matter candidates research. Currency: agreement and residual. Anchor: the ground, never agreement. In this simulated run all four domains draw on one common evidence pool, so this domain's independence is zero and its agreement is not corroboration. **Verdict: zero verdict weight for consensus; `general`.**
- **Order by dependency (Fig 3).** Perceive the gravitational residual first; hold the candidate vocabulary second; declare what counts as a discriminating objective third; build instruments fourth; carry exclusion limits back fifth. Fig 3 :19: "Optimising a link that already works cannot rescue one that does not." Describe the residual before choosing a frame (Fig 9 :27-28).

**Partition or union.** D1 is a partition of independent observations of one effect. D2 plus D3 is a union when several components are allowed, because candidates can each contribute to the same slot. The union must be unbundled into one loop per candidate before any single lever is applied.

**Strain test.** Mapping a particle onto a substrate term (axion onto Spectrum, WIMP onto Gradient) fits only by forcing the meaning of a directedness line, so the line governs another quantity. Re-census sends particle identity to D2, where it is a binding, not a figure verdict. No strain remains.

## VERDICT (from the figures)

The shape survives; no candidate name does. The missing mass registers gravitationally, so the question is hard rather than foreclosed. The four options form a union compound that must be unbundled into per-candidate loops with per-slot residuals, not a partition and not a set of isomers. Null results are budget filters, not proof of absence; the candidates are already held terms, so the correct triage is SEARCHED then INWARD then OUTWARD, with import only under provenance and a shadow test. The framework returns a shape, not a particle name, so the identity is a `gap` reported plainly rather than filled. The verdict belongs to a source-discriminating measurement; agreement among theorists has zero verdict weight. This is a `specified`, ungrounded result.

- **cost if wrong:** bounded and locatable. The packet is mode-labelled, states refuters before the conclusion and records the lineage, so an error is findable rather than invisible. The real cost is spending the independence of outside readers by sending a candidate conclusion instead of a generator, which Fig 2 :42 prices: "Buy independence in proportion to the cost of being wrong." That cost is avoidable by leading with the shape claim and its refuter. A second cost is premature import: Fig 6 :24 warns "A wrong atom costs more than starting from primitives."
- **undoable:** yes. The shape claim is separable from any candidate binding. A future detector result grounds a binding without requiring the shape to be withdrawn, and a sent packet can be superseded by a corrected one. No primitive has to be removed; under Fig 6 :42-43 a refutation adds a term that refuses and removes nothing from the record.
- **reversed by:** a ground reading, not agreement. A source-discriminating detection that measures a mass and an interaction strength would name the occupant and move that binding from candidate to grounded; per-slot measurements would settle how many components there are; a detector result reporting proof of absence for a candidate, or a demonstration that the four options are a partition with one occupant, would fire the standing refuters and break the shape claim; a figure line governing particle identity would falsify the gap. Until one of these fires, the verdict stands as `candidate`.
