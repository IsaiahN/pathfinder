**Subject:** Check one claim about state capacity before you decide whether the method behind it is worth reading

> Hi [name],
>
> I have one specific output I would like you to check against what you already know. I am not asking you to read the method first, and I am not asking you to agree with it. If the output does not survive, there is nothing else to look at.
>
> **The output (`general` on the structure, `specified` on the four named capacities, not `grounded`):** "Which form of state capacity matters most: tax collection, public services, enforcement, or information gathering?" is asked in a shape the framework cannot return. A minimum viable set has no rank and no dispensable member, and the live signal is indexed per slot, never aggregated. So the honest answer is not "tax collection first" or "information first." It is a shape with four constraints: the four capacities are jointly necessary and unordered; the binding one in any polity is the slot whose residual is positive, which is a measurement; they are ordered by dependency, not importance; and capability belongs to the state and the polity together, not to the state apparatus alone.
>
> **What I am not claiming:** that I can tell you which capacity is binding in your case. I cannot, without a ground reading. I am not claiming information gathering is always upstream in every polity, though the dependency reading predicts it usually is. I am not claiming the four capacities are literally any of the framework's six abstract substrate terms. They are a minimum viable set at the institutional stratum, with different actors, currency and anchor. No new primitive is added.
>
> **What would refute the shape claim:**
> 1. Show that one of the four can be removed and the same governance outcome still be derived against the same ground.
> 2. Show a stable, context-free rank that predicts which slot is live across cases without measuring each slot.
> 3. Show an agent-only improvement that leaves contact unchanged but still moves a governance outcome.
> 4. Show the four are a symmetric conjunction rather than an ordered dependency chain.
>
> I wrote these down before running this. If any one holds, I withdraw the claim. A one-line "this fails because..." is more useful to me than agreement. If you would rather not engage, no reply is needed.
>
> **If it survives, and only then:** the sources are below. The figures are the authority, not my summary of them.

## Two-stage offer

1. **Stage 1.** Send the shape claim, the four refuters, and the question "what would you count as a failure here?" Record the verdict **before** showing any framework interior.
2. **Stage 2, opt-in only.** If they are curious, offer the three public links and the two local framework files. The figures, not the summary, are the authority. Their stage-1 verdict is never re-scored by what they think of the framework afterwards.
3. **Stage 3.** Offer the formula as a generator to reuse on a problem they can check, with the instruction that each user writes a refuter first and states a mode.
