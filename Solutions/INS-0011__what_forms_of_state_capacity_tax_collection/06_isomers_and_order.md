# Are the four capacities a chain, a union, or isomers?

**Mode: `specified`.** This is a simulated derivation checked against the figures, not a ground reading (see `05_what_shape_means.md` section 7).

## RESIDUAL

The audit in `01` refuses a rank and returns a shape. Two questions remain: (1) what structure actually holds among tax collection, public services, enforcement and information gathering, and (2) if two polities hold the same four capacities in a different arrangement, are they different substances (Fig 12's isomers) or only different spellings of one? A third question is owed because the original question uses the word "most": what bond, if any, does a ranking apply?

## DERIVATION

### Q1 - Chain or symmetric set?

- Fig 3 :20: *"The chain is ordered by dependency, not by importance."* Fig 3 :5, :10, :13 give the link contents: perception, objective, planning.
- Mapping, declared as a binding from the institutional frame: information gathering is perception (link 1); public services state what counts as progress (link 3); enforcement is planning and action (links 4 and 5); tax collection is the currency that feeds every link (Fig 4 :20 *"Only the actors, the currency and the anchor change"*; Fig 13 :22 *"a difference that can be spent"*).
- Scott (2010, fetched 2026-11-23): *"To follow the process of state-making, then, is to follow the conquest of illegibility."* Information is upstream of assessment and compulsion. Tilly (via the Wikipedia page, fetched 2026-11-23) supplies the extraction and coercion pair: *"a surviving political organization inevitably formed from necessary tax collection and enforcement."*
- Fig 3 :19: *"Optimising a link that already works cannot rescue one that does not."* Effort follows the break, not a standing rank.
- **Result:** chain, `specified`. The four are not interchangeable. Suryanarayan's 2024 finding that *"the various dimensions of state capacity-extractive, coercive, legal-do not necessarily move together"* describes measured values of links, not their dependency order, so it does not break the chain reading (re-census in `01` F8).
- **Falsifier, written before the run:** if two arrangements of the same four capacities are shown to be order invariant, that is, swapping information gathering and enforcement leaves the governance outcome unchanged against the same ground, the chain reading fails and the symmetric-conjunction reading stands.

### Q2 - Are two regimes with the same four capacities isomers?

- Fig 12 :117: *"Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."*
- Fig 12 :104: *"A settled molecule becomes an atom for whatever composes over it. So irreducible is a fact about a level and never about the world."* Each capacity, settled in a case, becomes an atom for the regime that composes over it.
- Consider two polities with the same four capacities:
  - Regime A: information → tax → services → compliance. Revenue funds delivery; delivery sustains compliance.
  - Regime B: services to legitimacy to compliance to tax. Delivery comes first and builds the consent that makes extraction feasible.
- Same atoms (the four capacities, plus state and polity), different bond order, different behaviour under stress. Under Fig 12 :117 these are **isomers** at the regime level, not different amounts of the same thing.
- Weaker reading: if only the pattern matters, they are a **functional group**, Fig 12 :124 *"functional group. A substructure whose presence fixes the outcome. The group says what kind; the qualifier says which member."* The group is "perception → objective → action"; the qualifier is which capacity fills which slot.
- **Mode:** `specified`, ungrounded. No measurement of a regime's behaviour under stress was taken.
- **Falsifier, written before the run:** if two polities with the same four capacities in different orders converge to the same governance outcome against the same ground under the same stress, the isomer claim fails and the functional-group reading stands instead.

### Q3 - What is the order of the union?

- Fig 9 :54: *"And when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again."*
- Fig 9 :55: *"A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones."*
- Fig 9 :56: *"A single lever clears the region where only one condition holds and leaves the intersections standing, because the second condition still binds."*
- Fig 9 :59: *"not that the pile is smaller, but that the residue is finally describable."*
- The four capacities overlap: tax collection is a compound of information (who owns what), enforcement (compliance) and services (the return that sustains consent). A lever that improves only information clears the region where information alone was binding; the intersections where enforcement or consent still bind remain. The second-order value is that after the lever the residue becomes describable, hence measurable per slot (Fig 1 :19).
- **Falsifier:** if the four can be operationalised with zero interaction terms and no compound cases, the union reading fails and they are a partition after all.

### Q4 - What bond does "most important" apply?

- Fig 12 :87 defines comparison ⋛: *"a threshold relation between two quantities."* The Operators table test: *"is it about which is larger, rather than both being present? Yes."*
- A rank of capacities applies a comparison to operands that are **conditions in a union**, not quantities on one scale at one level. Fig 13 :74: *"Everything else is a composition across the two kinds, never within one."* The capacity terms are not all the same kind (information is perception-like, tax is currency-like, enforcement is action-like, services are objective-like).
- Alternatively a rank is a sequence → (*"A then B; B needs A to have happened"*, Fig 12 :71), but a rank asserts importance, not temporal or dependency precedence. Fig 3 :20 already assigns the precedence by dependency, so using → for importance would double-book the same bond with two meanings.
- **Result:** "most important" is a mistyped comparison applied across kinds. It is not a ninth bond and not a new operator. Fig 12 :90: *"A relation that fails all six is not a bond you are missing. It is a claim that has not been made yet."*
- **Strain, named not hidden:** whether settled capacities count as *"quantities"* at the regime level for a ⋛ comparison is not decidable from the text. It needs an instrument that can put two capacities on one scale at that level. Fig 12 :126: *"Bits price the description. Actions price finding out whether it holds. They are in different units, so a system holding one accounting cannot compare them."*

## CONCLUSION

The four capacities are related by dependency (Fig 3), compounded as a union (Fig 9), and at the regime level can form isomers over a shared atom set (Fig 12 :117) with a stated falsifier. They are not a ranked list, and "most important" is a mistyped comparison across kinds, not a licensed bond. This rescues what is defensible in the question without pretending it can be answered as posed: what survives is a chain, a union, and a falsifiable isomer claim. The strain about ⋛'s operand type at regime level remains open and is recorded, not smoothed.
