## QUESTION

Decide, in effect terms, what the Tether says about influence between knowledge systems. Option A: influence is agreement, convergence, or blending, and unequal relationships are an add-on to that diffusion. Option B: influence is import, the crossing of an atom from a frame whose closure differs, gated by provenance and a shadow test, and unequal relationships are collapses of the checking arrangement or an asymmetry of contact. What each leads to: A cannot explain why a system's reach changes and treats agreement as evidence; B yields checkable sub-claims, six refuters written in advance, and an honest stop at a shape rather than a named donor.

## DOMAIN CENSUS

R by arity, symmetry and scale, then one line per domain with actors / currency / anchor, partition or union, and order by dependency.

- **R (the residual).** Arity: two or more frames in contact, so a relation and not a property of one system. Symmetry: variable, since drift can run toward one party or both can range separately against a fixed ground. Scale: apprentice pair, community, institution, civilisation; the kernel does not change with size (Fig 4 :19-20). Fig 9 names this a union compound, not one hard question: mutual update, common cause, and mirror chain can all hold at once and must be unbundled before search.
- **Domain 1, frame-internal composition.** Actors: atoms and primitives of one knowledge system, the seven bonds, negation. Currency: composition inside closure(Γ) and settlement against the ground. Anchor: the ground, a measurement, a record, a practical consequence. Question type: partition (which compositions cross the two kinds and which do not compose at all).
- **Domain 2, cross-frame contact / influence.** Actors: two frames with differing closures, the atom that crosses. Currency: imports, debited against independence. Anchor: the ground plus the source gate and shadow test. Question type: union surplus, what two frames reach together that neither reaches alone.
- **Domain 3, verification and power.** Actors: frames, seats, audience, the more influential frame. Currency: agreement, residuals, credibility, provenance. Anchor: the ground, never agreement. Question type: collapse (three named failure shapes) and habitat asymmetry.
- **Order by dependency.** First, is there a live residual in the receiving frame, indexed per slot? Second, describe it in effect terms before choosing a donor frame (Fig 9 :27-28). Third, check the import gates. Fourth, settle against the receiving ground. Fifth, audit the checking arrangement. A step whose input never arrives cannot be diagnosed, only its predecessor (Fig 3 :22-23).

Strain test applied: "unequal" as a standing property of a system strains against Fig 11 :49. Re-census put it in Domain 3 and the habitat, not in a taxonomy of systems. The historical corpus as anchor remains a live strain and is reported in the verdict.

## VERDICT (from the figures)

The shape survives; a historical narrative does not follow from it. Influence between knowledge systems is import, gated by provenance and a shadow test, and a system cannot move its own wall by internal composition. What is called an unequal relationship is three collapse shapes plus a habitat asymmetry, not a taxonomy of dominant and subordinate systems: two frames update on each other and drift toward influence, or agree from one shared pool, or hand a question along a chain of mirrors, while an actor with a seat can introduce an atom and cannot subtract the habitat. Borrowing and convergence are distinguishable only by lineage, and a clean lineage gives legitimacy rather than truth. Valence is relative to the residual. The framework returns a shape, not a named donor, and no moral verdict. This is a `general` structural result with `specified` domain findings, ungrounded, candidate.

- **cost if wrong:** bounded and locatable, because the packet states mode, refuters, and lineage before the conclusion. The avoidable cost is spending an outside reader's independence by sending a finished historical narrative instead of the residual and its refuter. Fig 2 :42 prices it: "Buy independence in proportion to the cost of being wrong." Here the cost is high, since a false account of exchange can erase whose knowledge was borrowed.
- **undoable:** yes. The named-donor and moral-verdict claims are withdrawn, not repaired, and no primitive is committed. A sent shape claim can be superseded by a corrected one without withdrawing the mechanism. What cannot be undone cheaply is a provenance record that was never made, which is why the recommendation is to record lineage first.
- **reversed by:** ground, not agreement. A dated transmission chain plus a before-and-after check against the receiving system's own ground would name an atom; a movable anchor in a relationship would identify which collapse fired; a reach change with no contact, a name substitution that changes a reach, agreement that enlarges a reach with no immovable anchor, a standing list of dominant systems that survives a change of residual, or a content-level discriminator between borrowing and convergence would each fire one of the six refuters. A re-census that treats a historical corpus as a single immovable anchor would also dissolve the live strain, but only if the corpus actually meets the anchor condition. Until one of these fires, the verdict stands as candidate.
