## 1. A shape is a constraint, not a filler

The figures README states the limit: the framework returns *"what a missing thing would have to look like (a shape, not a name)."* Fig 9 :32: *"you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself."* Fig 13 :134-135: *"Unreachable is a verdict here, not a silence"* and *"It cannot say which primitive is missing, because naming it would mean already holding it."*

From *The Library of Babel*: *"You can cut your search short if the set of books returned is small and descriptive enough that it provides a general shape of the answer that satisfies your question."*

So a **shape** is the narrowed form of an answer you can state before you can name it. It constrains; it does not fill. For this question, the shape is: influence = import gated by provenance and a shadow test; unequal = a collapse of the checking arrangement or an asymmetry of introduction over exclusion. A named donor, a dated transfer, or a moral verdict about a specific episode is not a shape and is not delivered.

## 2. How the shape is obtained

- *Library of Babel*: use the vocabulary you already have *"closest to the thing"*, look for recurring new terms that overlap or correlate with it, recurse. *"With each new term gained, you narrow the space and deepen your vocabulary."*
- Fig 6 :30: *"The description travels. Fails by being sharpened into terms only we can read. Cast it in effect terms."* Fig 9 :30-31: *"your words are borrowed too, so describe the effect rather than the cause."* This is why the residual in `01` is stated as reach-change and anchor-loss rather than as "colonialism" or "diffusion": those words import a frame before the census.
- *Decoding Tacit Knowledge*: *"When contact isn't possible, the best you can send is a plain description of what you'd expect to see, because anyone can check that against the world."* The effect description is the one message that works across frames that do not share a history.
- *Library of Babel* on stopping: you are done when *"the finish line will also be the starting place"*, and the paradox is that by then you have *"composed the answer through the process of locating it."*

## 3. Why content cannot settle provenance

This is the load-bearing point for a history of knowledge exchange, and three separate sources give it.

*The Hard Problem is About Source* gives the instrument case: *"If several paths create the same behavior, then behavior is a projection with lossy information."* An instrument reads green and *"cannot comment on the source."* Several sources, one reading. What is owed is a discriminator, not an argument.

*How Intelligence Actually Works* gives the artifact case: *"The frame and the photograph are indistinguishable as artifacts, and they carry different warrants. One was taken; one was extracted. And nothing in the image records which."* And: *"very little about a composed term tells you, from looking at it alone, which route produced it."*

Fig 8 :51 gives the figure line: *"Convergent derivation and adopted import look identical in the contents. Only the record of where each came from separates them, and the surplus lives only where the closures differ."*

Consequence for this question: a claim that two knowledge systems independently invented the same practice, or that one borrowed it from the other, cannot be decided by comparing the practices. It is decided by the lineage: dated texts, translations, material traces, sequences of correction. Where the lineage was destroyed or never recorded, the honest verdict is `unreached`, not a guess dressed as a finding.

## 4. Why "unequal" is a shape about contact, not a taxonomy

*Decoding Tacit Knowledge* states the mechanism in plain language: two people who *"only check against each other drift"*, and the drift *"runs toward the listeners' own history."* It gives three safeguards: *"A ground that both are corrected by, separately"*, *"Separate experience"*, and *"Claims in effect terms."* And it names the paradox that makes this hard: *"Long, close contact pulls the frames together, so the process that makes communication easy slowly uses up that extra reach."*

Fig 2 :13-15 gives the same shape in the figures' notation: *"no anchor; each updates on the others"* and *"they converge, and what they converge on is whoever had the most influence."* Fig 11 :49 adds: *"An actor’s valence is relative to the residual, not a property it carries."*

So the shape of "unequal" is not "system A dominates system B". It is: the anchor that could have corrected both was replaced by influence, or one party could introduce into the shared habitat and the other could not subtract from it. That is checkable. A taxonomy of dominant and subordinate cultures is not.

## 5. What licenses a name

Only two things, both outside the shape itself:

1. **The ground settles it.** Formula step 5: *"the ground settles it. Verified only by what pays. A gate is not the ground."* For this question the ground is dated evidence a reader cannot move by persuasion: a manuscript, a measurement, a field record, an institution's own archive, an instrument outcome.
2. **A provenanced import from a frame whose closure differs** (Fig 6 :23, Fig 8 :50-51), with a source gate and a shadow test.

Until one of those, a named donor is the searcher's own contribution and must be recorded as such. *They are Scanning Books* sharpens the warning: *"Declaring the primitives does not make them neutral. It makes the choice visible, which is worth a great deal, because a visible choice can be revised and an emergent one cannot."* Applying a six-term or any other vocabulary to another knowledge system is a visible choice, not a neutral description of that system.

## 6. Prestige is not action

From *The Recursive Self-Improvement Trap*: *"Prestige must never be allowed to validate an action directly"* and *"Prestige can fund an action. It can give an action a budget to be tried. But it cannot settle an action. Only the ground can settle it. The Ground Maintainer must be blind to prestige."*

In this field the collapse is easy to make: the institution with the most citations, the largest archive, or the most authoritative journal is treated as the one whose account of the exchange must be right. That is prestige settling an action. The legitimate ask is the opposite: give the claim a budget to be tried (a public venue, an archive visit, a refuter), and let the ground - dated evidence neither side can move - settle it.

The same essay states the rule that governs this entire folder: *"the output is only as good as the validator. The agent, or the tool, or the frame, or the theory, cannot validate itself."* And: *"Metatheories cannot validate themselves."*

## 7. My own audit is a simulation, not a ground reading

From *The Recursive Self-Improvement Trap*: *"LLMs can quickly simulate an approximate answer/output with the formula found in the architecture post. It will often be better than the generic outputs that you will usually get otherwise too"*, but *"the truly testable output would need to be derived from the real architecture or working the formula yourself."* LLM output is *"simulacra."*

So `01_figure_grounded_audit.md`, `06_isomers_and_order.md`, and `07_the_tether_answer.md` are simulated derivations checked for internal consistency against the figures. They are **not** tests of the historical claim and must be labelled as such. They cannot settle anything they report on.

## 9. One correction carried forward

I initially wrote that a shape never permits naming anything. That is too broad. Fig 9 :32 governs a *missing* thing: a kind of thing, not the answer. Once an atom is held and an import is provenanced, naming it is a binding from a frame, not an invention of a primitive. What remains forbidden here is naming a donor from the contents of the artifact alone, because that is the metamerism error in section 3. Both claims are recorded so neither is quietly lost.
