## 0. The residual under audit

The question as asked: *"How have different knowledge systems influenced one another, including through unequal relationships?"*

Restated in effect terms. We observe contact between two or more bounded knowledge systems and we want to know two things:

1. **What crosses, and under what condition does contact change what a system can reach?** Option A: influence is agreement, convergence, or blending, so systems become more alike and that likeness is the influence. Option B: influence is import, the crossing of an atom from a frame whose closure differs, and no amount of agreement or blending changes a reach by itself.
2. **What makes a relationship unequal?** Option C: inequality is a standing property of one system (a dominant kind of knowledge). Option D: inequality is a relation to a residual and a property of the checking arrangement and habitat, so the same actor is a benefactor for one residual and a malfactor for another, and the drift has a direction because the anchor was replaced by influence.

What each leads to. A and C give a narrative of diffusion and of permanently dominant systems, and they treat unequal relations as an add-on to an otherwise symmetric process. B and D give a testable mechanism: a reach changes only when an atom crosses with a source gate and a shadow test, and an unequal relation is one of the named collapses in which the anchor stops being immovable or the channel to it dies. This audit tests B and D against the figures before it concludes anything.

**The sought-for shape, bound before the run.** Influence = import. Unequal = a collapse of the verification arrangement or an asymmetry of introduction over exclusion in the habitat. Written down here so it can be refused (Fig 5 :65-66: *"make the sought-for shape an ORDINARY TERM. Write down what an answer would look like, bind it, and let it accrue a residual like anything else."*).

## 1. Domain census (before choosing any figure line)

### R by arity, symmetry, scale

- **Arity.** The residual is a relation between at least two frames, not a property of one. Symbols table :111: *"A frame is any bounded system with a library: an agent, a person, a discipline, a model."* A knowledge system is a frame in this sense. It is not a metaphor: the rules that govern an agent govern a discipline in exactly the same way, with only the actors, currency, and anchor changed.
- **Symmetry.** Influence may be symmetric (two frames range separately against a shared ground) or asymmetric (one frame's terms come to govern what the other can say). Symmetry is therefore a variable to be measured, not an assumption.
- **Scale.** Individual knower, apprentice pair, community, institution, civilisation. Fig 4 :19-20: *"The kernel is the part that does not change with size: the same loop and the same rule for naming what worked, whether one agent is running it or a thousand. Only the actors, the currency and the anchor change."* The same census holds at every scale.

### One line per domain: actors, currency, anchor

| domain | actors | currency | anchor | question type |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **D1, frame-internal composition** (one knowledge system) | atoms and primitives of that system, the seven bonds, negation | composition inside `closure(Γ)`, minting terms, settling against the ground | the ground: a measurement, an experiment, a dataset, a practical or patient outcome, a public record | partition: what composes within, and what cannot be reached at all |
| **D2, cross-frame contact / influence** | two frames with differing closures, the atom that crosses, the source gate | imports, debited against independence | the ground, plus the source gate (provenance) and the shadow test | union surplus: what two frames reach together that neither reaches alone |
| **D3, verification and power (the unequal relation)** | frames, seats, audience, the more influential frame | agreement and residuals, credibility, provenance | the ground, never agreement | collapses: mutual update, common cause, mirror chain; and the habitat asymmetry |

No nameable anchor means it is not a domain yet. D1, D2, and D3 each have one: the ground, which does not change its answer because a frame thinks something (Fig 2 :29-30). Historical narrative by itself is not a fourth domain, because a narrative with no ground is a frame reporting its own agreement.

### Partition or union

D1 is a partition question (which compositions are legal across the two kinds). D2 is a union compound, not one hard question: two frames in contact with differing closures produce a surplus that is categorically different from either frame alone (Fig 8 :45). D3 is a union of overlapping failure conditions, not a list of separate vices: mutual update, common cause, and the mirror chain can all hold at once in one historical episode, so unbundling is required before searching (Fig 9 :54-58).

### Order by dependency (Fig 3)

1. Is there a live residual in the receiving frame, indexed by slot, that anything could answer? Fig 1 :19: *"The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated."* Fig 3 :19: *"Optimising a link that already works cannot rescue one that does not."*
2. Describe that residual in effect terms **before** choosing which donor frame to study. Fig 9 :27-28: *"describe it before you go looking, or any frame you pick will seem to fit."*
3. Only then check import: source gate, shadow test, debit against independence.
4. Only then settle the imported term against the ground.
5. Only then audit D3, because a verification collapse cannot be diagnosed until you know what the imported atom was supposed to explain. A reading taken below the break is a reading of nothing (Fig 3 :22-23).

### Strain test applied before concluding

The phrase "unequal relationships" initially tempted a reading in which one knowledge system is a dominant *kind*. That reading strains against Fig 11 :49-51, which makes valence relative to the residual rather than carried by an actor. Re-census: inequality belongs in D3 and in the habitat, not in D1. No strain survives the re-census; the re-census is recorded in section 8.

## 3. Findings F1 to F8

Verdicts are kept per domain and never averaged. `triangulated` means two figures with low overlap; `single-source` means one figure line governs and no second, independent line was found in the extract.

### F1 - Influence is import, and only import (D2, decisive)

Fig 6 :21-23:

> **"MINT composes inside the closure and can never add an atom."**
> **"NOVEL means novel relative to atoms, not to the world. IMPORT adds an atom, and the added atom is what expands the closure."**
> **"No frame escapes its own closure. The bound moves only on contact with a frame whose closure differs."**

Fig 6 :14 names the operator: *"the only operator that moves the wall"*. Fig 8 :32 gives the contrapositive: *"Closure is idempotent. Composing inside a closed set never leaves it, so no search, however long, adds an atom."* Fig 13 :115 closes the triangulation from a different starting figure: *"The set is closed. The arrangements are not. That gap is why import exists."*

Consequence. A knowledge system working only with its own concepts can generate infinitely many arrangements and still never change what it can reach. Contact with a different closure is a necessary condition for a new atom. This is not an analogy about cultures; it is the same rule that governs an agent, and the Symbols table defines a frame to include a discipline.

**Refuter for F1:** demonstrate a system whose reach grows (a new atom becomes composable and settles against the ground) with no contact with a frame whose closure differs. **Status: `triangulated`** (Fig 6, Fig 8, Fig 13, low overlap). **Mode: `general`** for the structure.

### F2 - What crosses is an atom, not a name or a recording (D2)

Fig 4 :22 and :25-26:

> **"Only methods cross up. Only head starts come down."**
> **"A recording carried upward looks like knowledge and is a description of one occasion. Replayed downward, it produces a system that repeats a past success and cannot produce a new one."**

Fig 6 :12-13 names the two gates on the crossing: *"source gate: provenance"* and *"shadow test: explains a gap"*. Fig 9 :32 limits what can be reported: *"you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself."*

Consequence. A translated text, a tabulated number, or a borrowed technique is a head start. It changes what a system can reach only if it becomes an atom that composes inside the receiving closure and pays for itself against the receiving system's ground. Substituting a local name for an imported term changes vocabulary, not reach. Fig 6 :25: *"An import adds no atom to the world."* The gain is access in the receiving frame, not a new fact conjured into existence.

**Refuter for F2:** show a case where a pure name substitution, with no composable atom and no settlement, enlarged the receiving frame's reach. **Status: `triangulated`** (Fig 4, Fig 6, Fig 9). **Mode: `specified`** on the claim about translations and techniques.

### F3 - Agreement is not evidence; the three collapses (D3, decisive for "unequal")

Fig 2 :13-15 shows the first collapse: *"no anchor; each updates on the others"* and *"they converge, and what they converge on is whoever had the most influence"*. Fig 2 :18-21 shows the second: *"one evidence pool"*, *"they never talk, and still agree"*, *"so the agreement is the pool talking, not two frames confirming each other"*. Fig 7 :12-13 shows the third: *"same closure, handed along; agreement compounds, evidence does not"*.

Fig 2 :35 and :40 state the rule:

> **"Alignment is triangulation, not negotiation. A mirror cannot correct you."**
> **"disqualifying for a verdict: agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool"**

Fig 7 :35: *"A room’s agreement is variation, not a verdict. Selection between rooms is where the ground votes."* Fig 8 :48-49: *"How much two frames draw on the same evidence decides both whether their agreement means anything and whether their union has any surplus. Two frames that overlap almost completely are one frame wearing two names."*

Consequence, kept strictly structural. An unequal relationship, in the figures' terms, is one of three things:

1. **Mutual update (coherent drift).** Two systems correct each other rather than ranging separately against an immovable ground. Convergence goes toward whoever had the most influence. It is invisible from inside because everyone can point at the "shared knowledge" they supposedly anchored on (Fig 2 :13-15, :34).
2. **Common cause.** The systems never need to talk. They agree because they draw on one evidence pool, one canonical text, one reviewer, one institution's standards. The agreement is the pool talking (Fig 2 :18-21).
3. **Mirror chain.** A question is handed along a chain of agreeable readers. Each handoff adds vocabulary and no evidence (Fig 7 :12-13, :36: *"A chain of mirrors is one long room. The next room must differ from the last, and match the residual it surfaced."*).

In all three, the correction that would have made the relation equal was to an anchor that cannot move. Fig 2 :45: *"Frames range against the ground. They do not negotiate with it."*

**Refuter for F3:** show two frames whose agreement about the world increased the reach of either without either ranging against a ground neither can move. **Status: `triangulated`** (Fig 2, Fig 7, Fig 8). **Mode: `general`** for the collapse shapes; `specified` on the claim that the drift has a direction toward influence. The attribution of any one collapse to any one historical episode is `unreached` and needs ground.

### F4 - Inequality is a relation to a residual and an asymmetry of contact (D3 and habitat)

Fig 11 :49-51:

> **"An actor’s valence is relative to the residual, not a property it carries."**
> **"The same actor is a benefactor to one residual and a malfactor to another, so mutualism and parasitism are relations rather than kinds."**
> **"No standing list of good actors survives a change of residual."**

Fig 11 :61: *"The seat’s power is asymmetric. Introduction is available; exclusion is not."* Fig 11 :66: *"Isolation is not removal of the habitat. It is substitution of one habitat for another."* Fig 11 :28-30 names the two silent failure modes of that substitution: *"what you failed to reproduce, and what you brought with you"*.

Consequence. There is no standing list of dominant and subordinate knowledge systems. The same institution can be a benefactor to one residual (it supplies an instrument that settles something) and a malfactor to another (it supplies the prestige that stops the question being asked). What *is* structural is the asymmetry: an actor with a seat can introduce an atom into a habitat, and cannot subtract the habitat, because contact is a fact about the world. When a knowledge system is transplanted into a new institutional habitat, two things are invisible until the goal fails: what did not survive the move, and what came along unbidden. And capability lives in agent-and-habitat: Fig 11 :40, *"An improvement that does not change contact changes nothing, however much it improves."*

**Refuter for F4:** exhibit a standing list of good or bad knowledge systems that survives a change of residual, or show an actor that can subtract a habitat. **Status: `triangulated`** (Fig 11 habitat lines + Seat Map :217, :225, which are the same law restated for the checking arrangement). **Mode: `general`** for the relation claim.

### F5 - Provenance is the whole discrimination between borrowing and convergence (D2 and D3)

Fig 8 :50-51:

> **"But shared atoms are not the signature. Two frames can hold the same atom by convergence, reaching it separately, which is corroboration at maximum independence."**
> **"Convergent derivation and adopted import look identical in the contents. Only the record of where each came from separates them, and the surplus lives only where the closures differ."**

Fig 10 :65: *"Provenance shows a ruling was reached legitimately. It never shows it was right: the ground does that, and a wrong ruling stays wrong however clean its record."*

Consequence. You cannot look at a body of knowledge and tell whether it was borrowed or independently derived. The contents are identical; only the lineage separates them. That is exactly why unequal relationships can erase their own traces: a borrowed term recorded without attribution becomes indistinguishable from a local discovery, and the surplus that came from contact is credited to one frame alone. Conversely, a clean provenance record does not make the borrowed claim true; the ground still has to pay. Fig 10 :60: *"Where a stake cannot be removed, declare it. An undeclared interest is modelled anyway and an unnamed model runs unchallenged."*

**Refuter for F5:** produce a content-level test that separates convergent derivation from adopted import with no access to the record of origin. **Status: `single-source`** for the content/record distinction (Fig 8), corroborated at low overlap by the seat map's access asymmetry and by *How Intelligence Actually Works*: *"very little about a composed term tells you, from looking at it alone, which route produced it."* **Mode: `general`**.

### F6 - A system cannot certify its own influence; credibility is frame-internal (D3)

Fig 1 :27: *"They are frame-internal. A frame cannot score itself with a quantity it also produces."* Fig 1 :24-26 disqualifies *"coverage, and compression achieved"* and *"predicates minted, and credibility accrued"*. Fig 2 :36: *"An anchor is legitimate when the question is constitutively about it."* Fig 10 :59: *"an interest in the outcome is unavoidable, an interest in the reading is disqualifying."*

Consequence. The prestige of the dominant system, the number of its adherents, and the volume of its publications are quantities it produces about itself. They cannot serve as evidence of the truth of what it transmitted. Where a stake cannot be removed (and in this field it usually cannot), the rule is not to pretend neutrality: declare it. Seat Map :171: *"Where a stake cannot be removed, declare it, an undeclared interest is modelled anyway and an unnamed model runs unchallenged."*

**Refuter for F6:** show a frame using a quantity it also produces as a valid ground for its own claim. **Status: `triangulated`** (Fig 1 + Fig 2 + Fig 10 / Seat Map). **Mode: `general`**.

### F7 - The answer stops at a shape, not a name (D1/D2 boundary)

Fig 9 :32 and :44:

> **"you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself"**
> **"Only a sealed room can be searched to the end, and no frame is a sealed room. So searching and finding nothing proves nothing."**

Consequence. This audit can give the shape of the mechanism and the shapes of the failure classes. It cannot name the specific system that borrowed the specific atom in a specific century; that is a ground question for documents, instruments, translations, field records, and oral histories. "Unreached is a verdict, not a silence" (Fig 13 :134: *"Unreachable is a verdict here, not a silence"*).

**Refuter for F7:** produce, from the figures alone, a dated attribution of a particular influence. **Status: `triangulated`** (Fig 9 + figures README). **Mode: `general`**.

## 4. Pre-registered refuter (written before the conclusion)

Required before any outreach (Seat Map :287: *"a falsifier written before the run is a design instrument, not only an honesty device."*). The shape claim is refuted if any of the following holds:

1. A knowledge system's reach grows (a new atom becomes composable and settles against the ground) with no contact with a frame whose closure differs. Breaks F1.
2. A pure name or vocabulary substitution, with no composable atom and no settlement, enlarges a receiving frame's reach. Breaks F2.
3. Two frames' agreement about the world increases the reach of either without either ranging against an anchor neither can move. Breaks F3.
4. "Unequal" is shown to be a standing property of an actor that survives a change of residual, or an actor is shown able to subtract a habitat. Breaks F4.
5. A content-level test separates convergent derivation from adopted import with no record of origin. Breaks F5.
6. A frame validly scores its own influence with a quantity it also produces. Breaks F6.

If any fires, the shape is withdrawn. Until one is run, no part of this may be labelled `grounded`. Falsifier 6 is the one most likely to fire in ordinary historical practice, because peer review often *is* a frame scoring itself; that is a finding about the practice, not about the figures.

## 5. Recommendation

1. Lead with F1 and F3: influence is import, and inequality is the loss of an immovable anchor. These two are figure-derived and triangulated.
2. Do not present the framework as a theory of history. It is a generator of the shape a historical account must have if the figures govern it. Fig 4 :30: *"So take the round trip and measure the gap. Without it you are navigating by dead reckoning: confident about a position nobody checked."*
3. For any concrete episode, start with the receiving system's residual, in effect terms, and only then pick donor frames (F7, Fig 9 :27-28). Otherwise every culture you look at will seem to have influenced every other.
4. Require a provenance chain before calling anything borrowing or independent discovery (F5). Record what a transplant failed to reproduce and what it unintentionally introduced (F4, Fig 11 :28-30).
5. Declare the stake. In this field the author's interest in a narrative is not removable; an undeclared one is modelled anyway and runs unchallenged (F6, Fig 10 :60).
6. Use Fig 4 :22: *"Only methods cross up. Only head starts come down."* Send the derivation and the refuter, not the finished narrative.

## 7. Strains and re-census

- **Fig 11 valence versus "unequal" as a moral category.** The figure says the same actor is benefactor and malfactor depending on the residual. Ordinary historical language treats "unequal" as a moral fact. Re-census puts the structural claim in D3 and declares the moral claim out of scope, needing its own ground (an actual harm, an actual distribution of credit). Not a figure conflict; a domain separation.
- **Fig 2's anchor versus a historical corpus.** The figures' anchor is a single immovable thing. A historical "ground" is a dispersed, revisable corpus of documents, instruments, and records. Re-census: the figures state the *condition* an anchor must meet; whether a given corpus meets it is a ground question. Recorded as `strained-for-now, needs ground`, not hidden and not used as an excuse to deliver a verdict.
- **Fig 8 :50 convergence versus Fig 2 :40 agreement disqualification.** These strain only if read as the same quantity. Re-census: Fig 8 :50 is about two frames reaching the same atom from independent evidence (maximum independence); Fig 2 :40 is about frames sharing one evidence pool. Different domains, so no strain after re-census.
- **After re-census: one live strain (historical corpus as anchor), named, none hidden.**

## 8. Verdict summary

**On the structural question: the shape survives.** Influence between knowledge systems is import, the only operator that moves a frame's wall, and it is gated by provenance and a shadow test. Unequal influence is not a kind of system; it is a collapse of the verification arrangement (mutual update toward the influential frame, common cause, mirror chain) or an asymmetry in which an actor can introduce into a habitat but cannot subtract it. Provenance is the only discriminator between borrowing and independent convergence. Valence is relative to the residual.

**On the historical question: unreached, not answered.** The figures cannot name a donor, date a transfer, or decide whether a particular relationship was unjust. That requires the ground: dated texts, instruments, field records, material traces, oral histories, and a checking arrangement whose anchor neither party can move. A search that finds nothing is never a proof of absence (Fig 8 :39).

**On every substantive claim in this file: mode stated.** `general` for the structural shape, `specified` for the domain findings. No `grounded` claim is made. This is a simulated derivation, internally consistent with the figures, not a ground reading.
