# Outreach draft - validate the output first, then decide about the theory

**Use:** one-to-one, or as a cover letter attached to a submission through the public venues in `03_public_contact_points.csv`. **Not** for bulk send. **No personal emails** are used or supplied.

**Hard precondition:** do not send until the shape claim and its refuter have been checked by the recipient in stage 1. If the output does not survive, there is nothing else to look at (F1-F7 in `01_figure_grounded_audit.md`).

---

**Subject:** Check one shape claim about knowledge exchange before you decide about the framework

> Hi [name],
>
> I am writing about one specific output, not about a theory. I am not asking you to read the
> framework first. I am not asking you to agree with it, and I am not asking you to trust it. If
> the output fails, there is nothing else to look at.
>
> **The output, `general` in shape and `specified` in the domain findings, not `grounded`:**
>
> Take any bounded knowledge system, a craft, a discipline, a community's ecological knowledge,
> a scientific institution. The framework treats each as a frame: a library of terms with a reach
> and a ground it can be checked against. From the figures it follows that a system cannot change
> what it can reach by working only with what it already holds. Composition inside a closed set
> never leaves it. The wall moves only on contact with a frame whose closure differs. So the
> mechanism of influence between knowledge systems is **import**, the crossing of an atom into
> the receiving system, gated by provenance and by whether it explains a gap that system already
> had. A translated text or a borrowed technique is a head start; it is not the atom itself.
>
> The second part is about unequal relationships. The same figures say the drift between two
> correcting systems runs toward whoever had the most influence when there is no anchor neither
> can move, and that agreement between systems drawing on one evidence pool is the pool talking,
> not two confirmations. So "unequal" is not a kind of knowledge system. It is a failure of the
> checking arrangement, or an asymmetry in which an actor can introduce something into a habitat
> and cannot subtract that habitat. Whether a term was borrowed or independently derived cannot be
> decided from its contents: only the record of origin separates those cases.
>
> **What I am not claiming:** that I can name which system borrowed which term in any particular
> century; that the framework delivers a moral verdict about a historical relationship; that the
> prestige or number of adherents of a system tells you anything about whether what it transmitted
> was true. A frame cannot score itself with a quantity it also produces.
>
> **What would refute the shape claim. I wrote these down before sending this:**
> 1. Show a knowledge system whose reach grows with no contact with a differing closure.
> 2. Show a pure name substitution, with no composable atom and no settlement, enlarging a reach.
> 3. Show two systems whose agreement increased the reach of either without either ranging
>    against an anchor neither can move.
> 4. Show a standing list of dominant and subordinate systems that survives a change of the
>    question, or an actor able to subtract a habitat.
> 5. Separate convergent derivation from adopted import by content alone, with no provenance.
>
> If any one holds, the shape is withdrawn. A one-line "this fails because..." is far more useful
> to me than agreement. If you would rather not engage, no reply is needed.
>
> **Stage 2, only if you are curious:** I will then send three links. The figures, not my summary
> of them, are the authority. I am also happy to hear about a historical case where this shape
> clearly fails; that is the most useful reply.
>
> **Stage 3, only on request:** the formula as a method to reuse. If you run it on a problem you
> know well and can check against a ground, write down what would refute your result *before* you
> run it, and state the mode of what you get. A reported failure is more useful to me than a
> reported success.
>
> With thanks for your time,
> [name / role]

## What is deliberately not asked

- Not "do you agree", and not "do you trust this".
- Not "please share with colleagues". A chain of agreeable readers is a mirror chain, Fig 7 :12-13: *"same closure, handed along; agreement compounds, evidence does not"*.
- Not a ranking of recipients by receptiveness. That manufactures the disqualifying correlation (Seat Map :163).
- Not a bulk send. Public, opt-in venues in `03_public_contact_points.csv` only, one-to-one where a person is involved.

## The two-stage rule, stated plainly

Stage 1 carries the residual and its refuter. Record the verdict. Stage 2 carries the links, and only if invited. Later agreement is never pooled as corroboration, because by then the reader holds the framework's interiors (Fig 2 :40: *"disqualifying for a verdict: agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool"*). A failure in stage 1 is the outcome we are looking for.
