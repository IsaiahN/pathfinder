# Expert verification packet - the Tether on knowledge exchange

**What this is:** a claim in a form a historian of science, anthropologist, or knowledge-exchange researcher can check. Mode, derivation, and refuters are stated before the conclusion, so a reader knows what to look for and what would settle it.

**Mode: `general` on the structural shape, `specified` on the domain findings. Not `grounded`.** This is a shape and a route, not a historical result. Simulated derivation checked for internal consistency against the figures, not a ground reading.

## 1. The claim, in one paragraph

Influence between knowledge systems is import: the crossing of an atom from a frame whose closure differs into a frame whose closure differs from it, gated by provenance and by whether the import explains a gap the receiving system already had. A system cannot move its own wall by internal composition alone. What we call an unequal relationship is not a kind of system but a relation in which the checking arrangement loses its immovable anchor (mutual update toward the influential frame, a shared evidence pool, or a mirror chain), or a habitat asymmetry in which an actor can introduce an atom and cannot subtract the habitat. Borrowing and independent convergence are indistinguishable by content; only the lineage separates them. The framework returns this shape and refuters, not a named donor or a moral verdict.

## 2. Three checkable sub-claims

### C1 - framework-internal; checkable by reading Figures 6, 8, and 13

- **Statement:** influence requires contact with a differing closure. Internal composition cannot add an atom, so a knowledge system working only with its own terms cannot change its reach.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 6 :23 - *"No frame escapes its own closure. The bound moves only on contact with a frame whose closure differs."* Fig 8 :32 - *"Closure is idempotent. Composing inside a closed set never leaves it, so no search, however long, adds an atom."* Fig 13 :115 - *"The set is closed. The arrangements are not. That gap is why import exists."*
- **Refuter:** exhibit a knowledge system whose reach grew (a new atom became composable and settled against its ground) with no contact with a differing closure. Or show that a pure name substitution, with no composable atom and no settlement, enlarged a reach.

### C2 - framework-internal; checkable by reading Figures 2, 7, and 11

- **Statement:** an unequal relation is a collapse of verification or an asymmetry of contact, not a standing kind of system. The drift runs toward whoever had the most influence when the anchor is movable; agreement from one evidence pool is not two confirmations; and an actor may introduce into a habitat but may not subtract it.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 2 :13-15 - *"no anchor; each updates on the others"* and *"they converge, and what they converge on is whoever had the most influence"*. Fig 7 :12-13 - *"same closure, handed along; agreement compounds, evidence does not"*. Fig 11 :61 - *"The seat’s power is asymmetric. Introduction is available; exclusion is not."* Fig 11 :49 - *"An actor’s valence is relative to the residual, not a property it carries."*
- **Refuter:** exhibit a standing list of dominant and subordinate knowledge systems that survives a change of residual, or an actor able to subtract a habitat, or two systems whose agreement enlarged a reach without an anchor neither could move.

### C3 - framework-internal derivation with a direct historical test

- **Statement:** borrowed terms and independently derived terms are identical in content; only the provenance record separates them. A clean record shows legitimacy, not truth.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 8 :51 - *"Convergent derivation and adopted import look identical in the contents. Only the record of where each came from separates them, and the surplus lives only where the closures differ."* Fig 10 :65 - *"Provenance shows a ruling was reached legitimately. It never shows it was right: the ground does that, and a wrong ruling stays wrong however clean its record."*
- **What to check in your own field:** take a case usually described as independent discovery or as borrowing. Is the claim decided by comparing contents, or by a dated chain of transmission? Are there cases where the chain was destroyed and the attribution is therefore `unreached` rather than `false`?
- **Refuter:** produce a content-level test that separates convergence from adoption with no access to the record of origin.

## 3. What is explicitly not claimed

- Not a theory of history, and no final narrative. The center stays a union compound with its own loop and its own ground.
- Not a moral verdict about any particular relationship. The figures describe structural asymmetry; deciding harm or justice requires a ground they do not supply.
- Not that all influence is domination. Convergent derivation at maximum independence is real (Fig 8 :50), as a special case.
- Not that a system can never change. It can rearrange forever; it cannot add a kind by rearranging (Fig 13 :115).
- Not that the prestige or citation count of a system bears on the truth of its claims (Fig 1 :27, *"They are frame-internal. A frame cannot score itself with a quantity it also produces."*).
- Not `grounded`. Nothing here was settled by an archive, instrument, or dataset.

## 5. What would settle it

Ground, not agreement. For this question the ground is dated evidence neither party can move:

1. A dated chain showing an atom crossing into a receiving closure, with source gate and shadow test (did it explain a gap already present?).
2. A before-and-after check against the receiving system's own ground: could it do something afterward it could not do before?
3. For a relationship: was the supposed anchor movable by either party? If so, name which collapse fired (mutual update, common cause, mirror chain).
4. For a transplant: enumerate what failed to reproduce and what was unintentionally introduced, and check the second-order cascade (Fig 11 :52-53).
5. The six refuters in `01_figure_grounded_audit.md` section 4. Any one firing withdraws the shape.

## 6. What an expert can check in twenty minutes

1. Read Fig 6 :23, Fig 8 :32, Fig 13 :115 (C1) and Fig 2 :13-15, Fig 7 :12-13, Fig 11 :49, :61 (C2). Eight short passages.
2. Test C3 against one case you know well: was the attribution decided by content or by provenance?
3. Name one piece of ground that would distinguish a genuine import from an independently derived term in your field. If none exists, say so; that is a finding.
4. Try to break C1 or C2 with a counterexample from the historical record.
5. Send the refutation. A failure is more useful than an endorsement, and no reply is needed if you would rather not engage.
