# The Tether's answer: how knowledge systems influence one another

**Mode: `general` on the structural shape, `specified` on the per-domain findings. Not `grounded`.** Simulated derivation checked for internal consistency against the figures (`05_what_shape_means.md` section 7), not a ground reading. No historical case was settled here.

## 1. The residual it answers

We observe contact between knowledge systems and want to know what crosses and what makes the relation unequal. The contact is a **union compound**, not one hard question (Fig 9 :54-55: *"A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones."*). It has its own loop and its own ground. Answering it requires separating what changed a reach (D2) from what corrupted the checking arrangement (D3).

## 2. The shape

1. **Influence is import, the only operator that moves a frame's wall.** Fig 6 :23: *"No frame escapes its own closure. The bound moves only on contact with a frame whose closure differs."* Fig 8 :32: *"Closure is idempotent. Composing inside a closed set never leaves it, so no search, however long, adds an atom."* A system working only with its own vocabulary can compose forever and reach nothing new. So the mechanism of influence is not diffusion or blending; it is the crossing of an atom from a differing closure. Fig 13 :115: *"The set is closed. The arrangements are not. That gap is why import exists."*
2. **What crosses is an atom, not a name or a recording.** Fig 4 :22: *"Only methods cross up. Only head starts come down."* Fig 6 :12-13 gives the two gates: *"source gate: provenance"* and *"shadow test: explains a gap"*. A translated text or a borrowed tool is a head start; it changes a reach only if it composes in the receiving system and settles against that system's ground. The gaining frame gets access, not a new world fact: Fig 6 :25, *"An import adds no atom to the world."*
3. **An unequal relationship is a collapse of verification, not a kind of system.** Fig 2 :13-15: *"no anchor; each updates on the others"* and *"they converge, and what they converge on is whoever had the most influence"*. Fig 2 :18-21: *"one evidence pool"*, *"so the agreement is the pool talking, not two frames confirming each other"*. Fig 7 :12-13: *"same closure, handed along; agreement compounds, evidence does not"*. In each case the correction that would have kept the relation equal was to an anchor neither party could move. Fig 2 :35: *"Alignment is triangulation, not negotiation. A mirror cannot correct you."*
4. **Valence is relational and the power asymmetry is structural.** Fig 11 :49-50: *"An actor’s valence is relative to the residual, not a property it carries."* and *"The same actor is a benefactor to one residual and a malfactor to another, so mutualism and parasitism are relations rather than kinds."* Fig 11 :61: *"The seat’s power is asymmetric. Introduction is available; exclusion is not."* A knowledge system can be introduced into a habitat; the habitat cannot be subtracted. What a transplant failed to reproduce and what it unintentionally brought are invisible until the goal fails (Fig 11 :28-30).
5. **Borrowing and independent discovery are indistinguishable by content.** Fig 8 :51: *"Convergent derivation and adopted import look identical in the contents. Only the record of where each came from separates them, and the surplus lives only where the closures differ."* So a claim of influence needs a lineage: dated texts, translations, material traces, sequences of correction. Where the lineage is gone, the honest verdict is `unreached`. Provenance establishes legitimacy, not truth: Fig 10 :65, *"Provenance shows a ruling was reached legitimately. It never shows it was right: the ground does that, and a wrong ruling stays wrong however clean its record."*
6. **A system cannot certify its own influence.** Fig 1 :27: *"They are frame-internal. A frame cannot score itself with a quantity it also produces."* Prestige, citation counts, and volume of output are quantities a system produces about itself; none can settle the truth of what it transmitted. Where a stake cannot be removed, declare it (Fig 10 :60: *"Where a stake cannot be removed, declare it. An undeclared interest is modelled anyway and an unnamed model runs unchallenged."*).
7. **Shared ingredients do not make two systems variants of one thing.** Fig 12 :117: *"isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."* Recording a knowledge system as an ingredient list erases the arrangement, which is where the substance lives (Fig 12 :94: *"The arrangement is the substance."*). See `06_isomers_and_order.md`.

## 3. What it explicitly does not say

- Not a historical narrative. No donor, date, or route is named. The figures return a shape, not a name (Fig 9 :32: *"you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself"*).
- Not a moral verdict. The framework describes structural asymmetry; it does not decide whether a particular relationship was unjust. That needs a ground it does not supply.
- Not a claim that all influence is domination. Convergent derivation at maximum independence is real (Fig 8 :50); it is a special case, not the general mechanism.
- Not a claim that a closed system can never change. It can change its arrangements without end; it just cannot add a kind. The distinction is exactly Fig 13 :115.
- Not `grounded`. No archive, instrument, or dataset was read to settle a historical case.

## 4. What would settle it - the ground

A source-discriminating record, not a further argument. The questions to put to the ground:

1. **Did an atom cross?** Is there a dated chain (translation, manuscript, instrument, field record, oral testimony) showing a term entering the receiving closure, with a provenance chain and a shadow test (does it explain a gap the receiver already had)?
2. **Did the reach change?** Could the receiving system do something after the crossing it could not do before, checked against its own ground, not against the donor's prestige?
3. **Was the anchor movable?** In the relationship, could either party's agreement move what was supposed to settle the question? If so, which of the three collapses (mutual update, common cause, mirror chain) actually fired?
4. **What was lost and what was introduced?** For a transplanted system, enumerate what failed to reproduce and what came unbidden, and check the cascade two or three steps out, not just at direct contact (Fig 11 :52-53).
5. **Standing falsifiers from the audit:** a reach change with no contact; a name substitution that changes a reach; agreement that increases a reach with no immovable anchor; a standing list of dominant systems that survives a change of residual; a content-level discriminator between borrowing and convergence.

## 5. The honest stop

**Unreached, not unreachable.** The ground for a historical question is a dispersed, revisable corpus, not a single immovable instrument. The figures state the condition an anchor must meet; whether a given corpus meets it is itself a ground question. That is the live strain, recorded in `01_figure_grounded_audit.md` section 7 and left visible. The answer stops at the limit of what the available instruments can distinguish and records the lineage so the stop is checkable. Indiscernibility is not identity: a well-told story that fits the shape is still a projection until the provenance pays.

## 6. One-paragraph version for outreach

> The Tether does not tell you which knowledge system borrowed which idea, and it does not deliver a moral verdict about a colonial encounter. It gives the shape any such account must have if the figures govern it. A knowledge system is a bounded frame with a reach, and a frame cannot move its own wall by thinking longer: influence is import, the crossing of an atom from a different closure, gated by provenance and by whether it explains a gap the receiving system already had. A translated text is a head start, not the atom. What we call an unequal relationship is not a kind of system but a failure of checking: two systems update on each other and converge on whoever had the most influence, or they agree because they share one evidence pool, or a chain of agreeable readers adds vocabulary and no evidence. The same actor can be a benefactor for one question and a malfactor for another, and the structural asymmetry is that you can introduce an actor into a habitat and cannot subtract the habitat. Borrowing and independent discovery look identical in the contents, so only the lineage separates them. What the framework offers is that shape, six falsifiers written in advance, and the kind of ground that would close it: dated evidence neither party can move. The specific history stays open.
