**Subject:** Check one output about Earth-system interactions before you decide whether the method is worth reading

> Hi [name],
>
> I have one specific output I would like you to check against what you already know. I am not asking you to read the framework first, and I am not asking you to agree with it. If the output does not survive, there is nothing else to look at.
>
> **The question as asked:** which interactions among atmosphere, oceans, ice, land and ecosystems most strongly shape Earth's long-term behavior?
>
> **The output (`specified`, not `grounded`):** a single ranked winner is not an available result. The framework requires that the residual be indexed per interaction slot and never aggregated, as it puts it: "The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated" (Fig 1 :19). It also says "Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average" (Fig 1 :22), and that an actor's valence is relative to the residual rather than a property it carries (Fig 11 :50). So the reachable answer is an enumerated union of per-slot flows, each embedded in a signed feedback cycle, with threshold relations kept as a compound where two or more slots bind at once. It is a shape of an answer, not a name of a winner.
>
> **What I am not claiming:** that ice-albedo feedback, ocean circulation, the carbon cycle, or any other named interaction is *the* strongest one at all scales. The framework returns "a kind of thing, never the answer itself" (Fig 9 :32). Naming a slot would be the searcher's own contribution, not a finding of the method. I also am not claiming that "tipping point" is a sixth realm; in this derivation it is a threshold relation over slots.
>
> **What would refute the output:**
> 1. one interaction carrying positive residual mass in every slot under one fixed residual, with an anchor returning the same order at local, regional and global scale;
> 2. the ten pairs among the five realms forming a partition, with each realm in exactly one edge, and the compound behaving as the sum of its single-condition cases;
> 3. long-term behavior fully accounted for by a linear chain with no output re-entering as input;
> 4. an interaction keeping the same sign and magnitude across two different residuals;
> 5. a figure line verbatim naming a single strongest interaction, or a ground measurement returning a stable ranking across all named scales and residuals.
>
> I wrote these down before running the derivation. If any one holds, the shape claim is withdrawn. A one-line "this fails because..." is more useful to me than agreement. If you would rather not engage, no reply is needed.
>
> **If it survives, and only then:** the sources are below. The figures are the authority, not my summary of them.
> - The verbatim figure text with line numbers (local extract): `FRAMEWORK/reference/03-FIGURES_TEXT.md`
> - Canonical usage rule (local copy): `FRAMEWORK/reference/FIGURES_README_canonical.md`
> - Public figures folder, branch `general-agent`: https://github.com/IsaiahN/tether/tree/general-agent/figures
>
