## Wider literature that bears on this question

1. **The four Wikipedia Earth-system pages share a citation pool.** They cite IPCC AR5 and AR6 assessment chapters repeatedly. By Fig 2 :40, "disqualifying for a verdict: agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool", their agreement is one witness, not four. They are used as low-overlap corroboration *relative to the framework* (different authors and institution), not as four independent confirmations of each other.
2. **Earth System Dynamics is the closest venue found.** Its own scope statement restates the question almost word for word, including life and human activity. It therefore serves both as a world corroboration of the union reading and as the most relevant public, opt-in submission venue.
3. **The aggregation warning is independent of the framework.** The Earth System Dynamics article "Spatial patterns in indirect effects of human activity on climate, land, and water" (Zoller et al., 2026) states on the fetched journal page that its results "highlight the risk of information loss inherent in commonly used aggregation approaches." That is a direct corroboration of Fig 1 :22 in a different field and by different authors.
4. **The tipping definition is contested in the field itself.** The Earth System Dynamics highlight "To tip or not to tip" (Börner and Dijkstra, 2026) states on the fetched page that "we identify three issues with existing tipping definitions that cause confusion about what 'tipping' means" and proposes "a persistent nonlinear transition". This corroborates the decision in `01_figure_grounded_audit.md` §1.2 to absorb X2 rather than treat it as a sixth realm.
