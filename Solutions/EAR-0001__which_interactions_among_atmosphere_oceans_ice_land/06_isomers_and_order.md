# What kind of composition an interaction is, and what order the union has

**Mode: `specified`.** Simulated derivation checked against the figures, not a ground reading (see `05_what_shape_means.md` §6).

## RESIDUAL

The question asks which interactions "most strongly shape" long-term behavior. Before ranking anything, two structural questions are owed: (1) what kind of term is an interaction, and (2) what is the order of the collection of interactions? A superlative over the wrong kind of object is a category error, not a measurement.

## DOMAIN CENSUS

Atoms held: the six substrate terms (space, time, energy states; spectrum, gradient, network structures), the seven bonds plus negation, and the four structural forms in Fig 12 (isomer, catalyst, cycle, functional group). The five realms are not atoms; they are a habitat enumerated from contact (Fig 11 :43). Bonds relevant here: → sequence, ⇒ production, ⋛ comparison, + conjunction.

## DERIVATION

### Q1 — Is an interaction a bond, a molecule, or a cycle?

- Fig 12 :46: "A bond is the typed relation between two operands. There are seven, they are not interchangeable, and which one holds is not recoverable from the operands."
- A single flux (for example heat from ocean to atmosphere) is a **molecule**: a gradient bonded to a state, producing a flow. Fig 13 :76: "Energy and a gradient compose a flow. A gradient over space and one over time are different terms, because which state it is bonded to is part of what it is."
- A **feedback** is a higher-order form: Fig 12 :121 "cycle. The output re-enters as input. The sign of the loop decides whether it closes, and a chain cannot close by construction." The flux is an operand of the cycle, not the cycle itself.
- ⇒ An interaction among realms is a **molecule embedded in a cycle**, and the ranking question asks about the cycle, not about the bare bond. A list of ten pairs is a flat formula: it records ingredients and not arrangement (Fig 12 :14: "the ingredients are recorded and the arrangement is not").

**Falsifier:** exhibit an interaction that changes the long-term outcome while its bond, operands and feedback sign are held fixed. That would show the bond does not carry the missing information.

### Q2 — Are the five realms isomers of one another?

- Fig 12 :117: "isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."
- The five realms do **not** share an atom set. Atmosphere, ocean, ice, land and ecosystems are distinct habitats, not rearrangements of one set of primitives. By Fig 12's definition they are **not isomers**.
- The **interaction patterns** can be isomers. Ice-albedo (state, structure, state with comparison feedback) and the biological carbon pump (state, structure, state with production feedback) can be written over the same substrate atoms with different bonds. That is the isomer claim worth testing.
- Fig 12 :123: "functional group. A substructure whose presence fixes the outcome. The group says what kind; the qualifier says which member." All per-slot cycles share the functional group `state → structure → state → comparison`; the qualifier is which state and which structure occupy the slots.

**Falsifier:** show two interaction patterns with identical atoms and identical bonds but different outcomes. That would break the bond-carries-the-difference claim and leave only a naming difference.

### Q3 — What order is the union?

- Fig 9 :55: "A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones." Fig 9 :57: "What remains is smaller and it is also categorically different: a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either."
- Fig 12 :104: "A settled molecule becomes an atom for whatever composes over it. So irreducible is a fact about a level and never about the world, and there is no bottom".
- Dependency order (Fig 3 :20): perception → vocabulary → objective → planning → learn and carry. Applied here: observations → flux vocabulary → definition of long-term behavior → Earth system models → validation. Effort spent above the break arrives at a step that cannot use it.
- The ten pairwise slots are therefore **not a sequence**. Writing them `D1 → D2 → ... → D10` would impose an ordering that Fig 13 :75 does not license: "Two states never bond directly. It is not space-time but space, a structure, time; and two structures join only through a state." The union is ordered by dependency, not by importance, and the dependency is a partial order over the loop, not a list.
- **Compound order:** D6 (thresholds and cascades) is second order in the sense of Fig 12 :104. Each settled first-order slot becomes an operand for the threshold relation ⋛ over two or more slots. ⋛ is "a threshold relation between two quantities" (Operators table :48). Whether a settled cycle counts as a quantity at that level is a type question that needs an instrument, not an argument. This is the one live strain in this derivation.

**Falsifier:** show that the order of the union is a total sequence and that reversing two slots leaves the long-term outcome unchanged; or show that ⋛'s operands must be bare quantities at every level.

### Q4 — What about the cost of the answer?

Fig 12 :126: "A composition costs in two currencies, and they do not add." Fig 12 :127: "Bits price the description. Actions price finding out whether it holds."

- **Bits:** naming ten slots and their bonds is cheap to state.
- **Actions:** settling even one slot against a ground requires instruments, long records and model intercomparison. These do not add, so a short description is not evidence of a cheap answer. Fig 12 :129: "Neither figure predicts the other, so both are read or the choice is made blind."

**Falsifier:** demonstrate a case where description length predicts settlement cost for these slots, against the figure's claim that the two readings come apart in both directions.

## CONCLUSION

An interaction is a molecule embedded in a cycle, not a bare bond. The five realms are not isomers; some of their interaction patterns are, with a shared functional group. The union is a dependency partial order with a second-order compound at the thresholds, not a ranked sequence. The ranking requested by the question is therefore a request to collapse a structured object into a scalar, which is the flat-formula failure Fig 12 names.
