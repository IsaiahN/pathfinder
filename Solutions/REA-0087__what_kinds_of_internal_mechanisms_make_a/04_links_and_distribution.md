## What the wider literature contributes, and what it does not

1. **The two pressures are already distinguished in the literature.** Szegedy et al. show a worst-case imperceptible perturbation; Hendrycks and Dietterich build a benchmark for common corruptions and explicitly contrast it with worst-case adversarial perturbations. That is the NOVEL versus REFUTED split in the figures, arrived at independently. It corroborates F6 but is a different evidence pool from the framework, so it is a real witness for the shape.
2. **A single lever warning.** Goodfellow et al. show adversarial training reduces error on one setting, while Hendrycks and Dietterich find a bypassed defence gives common-perturbation robustness. Neither result generalises across all conditions. That corroborates the union reading (Fig 9 :55-57) rather than a single-mechanism answer.
3. **Shortcut learning is the name the ML field gives to a wrong binding.** Geirhos et al. report rules that work on benchmarks and fail under transfer, and note the same pattern in comparative psychology, education and linguistics. That is a low-overlap witness for BROKEN rebinding and for the refusal requirement in F2, though it does not supply the negation operator.
4. **The resilience field independently warns about rigidity.** MathisonSlee et al. describe a rigidity trap in which a system becomes highly connected and inflexible and loses capacity for novelty. That corroborates F4 (more internal agreement is not more robustness) and F7 (the habitat matters), from a field that never cites the framework.
5. **Framework essays are not independent witnesses.** They share an author and an evidence pool with the figures, so their agreement is expected and carries little independent weight. They are used only for the canonical reading of "shape" and for the honesty rule that this derivation is a simulation.
6. **No source settles which architecture wins.** None of the papers names a mechanism that the figures would select. That is the declared gap F9, and these sources do not close it. They disagree with one another on what robustness generalises, which is itself corroboration for unbundling.

## Distribution rules - offer, do not push

Figure-grounded reasons:

- **Fig 4 :22-25** - *"Only methods cross up. Only head starts come down. A recording carried upward looks like knowledge and is a description of one occasion."* Sending the finished framework before anyone has described a residual sends a recording. Lead with the shape and its refuter; offer links after a verdict is recorded.
- **Fig 10 :42** - *"A verifier that can reconstruct the claim cannot verify it."* A recipient who has absorbed the architecture before being asked for a verdict has joined the frame being checked.
- **Fig 9 :27-28** - *"describe it before you go looking, or any frame you pick will seem to fit."* Ask what they would expect to see if the shape claim were true before showing any source.
- **Fig 2 :40** - *"agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool"* - a send selected for likely agreement produces a convention, not corroboration.
- **Fig 11 :49-51** - *"An actor's valence is relative to the residual"* and *"No standing list of good actors survives a change of residual."* Do not keep a permanent list of friendly reviewers; the residual selects the frame.
- Seat Map :171 - *"an interest in the outcome is unavoidable, an interest in the reading is disqualifying."* Declare the stake (this author wrote the framework) and do not ask anyone to adopt it.

**Recommended order in a reply:** (1) the shape claim and its four refuters; (2) ask what they would count as a failure, and record the answer; (3) only then offer the local links, with the note that the figure text, not this summary, is the authority. A Track B reply is a conversation, never corroboration, and carries zero verdict weight.

## The two-stage offer

1. **Stage 1:** send the residual in effect terms, the shape claim and the pre-registered refuter. Ask what failure they would expect to see. Record their verdict before any theory is shown.
2. **Stage 2, opt-in only:** if they are curious, offer the figure text and the canonical rule. Their later agreement is never pooled as corroboration.
3. **Stage 3:** offer the formula as a method to reuse, with the instruction that each user writes a refuter and states a mode before running it.

**Hard precondition:** all three stages work only if the stage-1 shape survives. The refuters in `08_expert_verification_packet.md` are written before the run for exactly this reason.
