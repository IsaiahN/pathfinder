# Isomers and order in the information question: structure, threshold, mechanism

**Mode: `specified` on the structure claim, `general` on the order claim. Not `grounded`.** This is a simulated derivation checked against the figures, not a ground reading (see `05_what_shape_means.md` §7).

## RESIDUAL

The question is posed as a binary: is the information lost, or is it encoded in the Hawking radiation? Two things are owed before that binary can be used:

1. Are 'lost' and 'encoded' **isomers** under Fig 12's definition, or are they related by a different operator?
2. What is the **order** of the derivation, and where does the union stop?

If the two options are isomers, then the answer is a rearrangement of one atom set and a mechanism might be derived inside the closure. If they are a negation pair, then no rearrangement inside the closure will produce one from the other, and the question has been mis-framed as a search.

## DERIVATION

### Q1. Are 'lost' and 'encoded' isomers? No. They are a negation pair

Fig 12 :117: "isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour." For two things to be isomers they must share an atom set and differ in the bond. 'Lost' and 'encoded' do not share a bond vocabulary: one is the assertion that a pure-to-mixed map is allowed, the other is the refusal of that assertion under the unitarity anchor.

Fig 12 :135: "The seven are bonds, and a bond is a relation between two things. Negation is not one: it takes a term and yields a term, and joins nothing." Operators table :73-74: "Subtraction is the nearest bond and is not it. − is A with B removed or absent, which is the absence of an operand. Negation is the refusal of a claim." Fig 5 :53: "refuted: a term we hold is wrong; a term is owed that says so."

So under the declared anchor the relation between the two options is ¬, not a bond. That is why searching harder inside the semiclassical closure cannot manufacture the encoded option: the wall is not a missing operand, it is a refused claim. Negation is not priced in the bond count (Operators table :77-78), which is why the refusal costs a different currency than a mint.

**Refuter:** exhibit a single atom set and a typed bond such that 'lost' and 'encoded' are two arrangements of it, with no negation in the description. Then the isomer reading holds and the negation reading fails.

### Q2. They do share a functional group

Fig 12 :123: "functional group. A substructure whose presence fixes the outcome. The group says what kind; the qualifier says which member."

Both options are answers to the same substructure: the map from an initial pure state to a final radiation state. The group fixes what kind of thing is being discussed, a map and its invertibility. The qualifier, ¬ or its refusal, says which member. This is the weaker, safer claim. It is the one that survives if the unitarity anchor is contested, because a reader who rejects the anchor can still agree that both positions are answers to one map question.

**Refuter:** show that the two literatures are not answering the same map question, for example that the information-loss programme is about a different observable than recoverability from the radiation. Then the functional-group claim fails too.

### Q3. First order inside each closure, second order at the union

- D1 is first order: a semiclassical prediction from a state through the horizon structure to a spectrum.
- D2 is first order: unitarity applied to the same state-to-state map, which fixes what counts as a possible final state.
- Their combination is second order, and needs no new primitive. Fig 12 :104: "A settled molecule becomes an atom for whatever composes over it. So irreducible is a fact about a level and never about the world". Each closure is settled in its own domain (the semiclassical calculation works for large holes; unitarity is experimentally supported in laboratory QM), so each can serve as an atom for the comparison.
- The Page curve is the second-order object: the fine-grained entropy of the radiation as a function of time, which compares what D1 predicts with what D2 requires. The **content** comes from Page 1993 and the 2019-2020 derivations. The figure supplies only the licence for the arrangement, not the entropy formula.

**Refuter:** show that the fine-grained entropy cannot be built from two settled first-order predictions without importing a third primitive that is not an atom of either closure. Then the union is not second order in this sense.

### Q4. The Page turnover has the shape of the comparison bond

Fig 12 :85-89 defines ⋛ as "comparison", "a threshold relation between two quantities". Operators table :48-51 repeats it: "a threshold relation between two quantities" with the test "is it about which is larger, rather than both being present? Yes."

The Page curve is a threshold between two quantities, the fine-grained entropy of the radiation and the entropy of the remaining black hole. The turnover occurs at about half the lifetime. Wikipedia's public reference entry states: "After the Page time, correlations appear and the radiation becomes increasingly information rich." That is a ⋛-shaped relation with no strain on the operand type: both operands are quantities.

World content: Page 1993 argued that a unitary evaporation requires the entropy to "initially increase and then decrease back to zero"; Penington 2019 reports "a phase transition in the location of the quantum Ryu-Takayanagi surface, at precisely the Page time"; Almheiri et al 2020 report "large corrections needed for the entropy to be consistent with unitary black hole evaporation."

**Refuter:** show that the correct fine-grained entropy never turns over, or that the turnover is not a relation between two quantities but a different bond under Fig 12's six tests. The latter would also fire Q3.

### Q5. The order of operations is part of the result

Dependency order, each step checked before the next:

1. **Describe before choosing.** Fig 9 :27-28: "describe it before you go looking, or any frame you pick will seem to fit." The residual is the union, not the binary.
2. **Refuse before mint.** `THE_FORMULA.md` step 2 sorts the gap first. Under the unitarity anchor, the pure-to-mixed map is a refuted kind of gap, not a missing item (Fig 5 :51: "The four kinds sort a gap by what is missing. None of them sorts a gap by what is wrong."). What is owed is a refusal (¬), not another explanation of where the information went.
3. **Settle each first-order molecule before comparing.** Fig 12 :104. The semiclassical result must be stated before its correction is sought, and unitarity must be held before the Page target is derived.
4. **Import after the wall is characterised.** Fig 6 :14: "the only operator that moves the wall". Fig 6 :23: "No frame escapes its own closure. The bound moves only on contact with a frame whose closure differs." Holography, AdS/CFT, quantum extremal surfaces and islands arrived this way.
5. **Ground last.** Fig 3 :27: "A link with no instrument is not a link that works. It is a link nobody has looked at." The chain stops at D4.

Order matters because the steps consume one another. Optimising a later link cannot rescue an earlier break (Fig 3 :19). A campaign that starts by naming a mechanism has already reversed steps 1 and 4.

**Refuter:** exhibit a derivation that arrives at a correct shape by starting with a mechanism name and searching inward, with no provenance for the import and no prior characterisation of R. Under the figure rules that is adoption, not derivation; a working example would still fire this refuter.

### Q6. Analogues that carry the structure, not the answer

| analogue | what it carries | limit |
|---|---|---|
| Chemistry, isomerism (Fig 12 :97-98, Liebig and Wöhler 1824-1826, Berzelius 1830) | same atoms, different bond, different substance: the arrangement is the substance | the black hole case is not an isomer case (Q1), so this analogue checks the bond rule, not the classification |
| Metamerism (*The Hard Problem is About Source*) | a reading cannot identify its source; depth and absence look the same from inside D1 | the spectrometer analogue has no existing instrument at D4 |
| Telescope/proto-instrument (Fig 6 :32-34) | an instrument is improved from a worse instrument already returning something | fails by refining forever on an axis that cannot carry the distinction |
| Gödel, Tarski, Chaitin (Fig 8 :34-44) | why a search inside a closure cannot certify absence | proves underivability, not that any particular atom is far away |

Fig 8 :40-43 states the scope explicitly: "Underivable-in-principle and unreachable-at-this-budget are different facts, and they issue different instructions." This matters here. Page's non-perturbative correlations are underivable at leading order, not unreachable at a larger budget of the same calculation. Importing against a budget limit would add an atom that was not needed.

## FALSIFIER, written before the conclusion

The structure claim is withdrawn if any of the following holds:

1. 'Lost' and 'encoded' are shown to be isomers over one atom set with a typed bond and no negation.
2. The fine-grained entropy is shown not to turn over, or the turnover is shown to be a different bond type under Fig 12's six tests.
3. The union is shown to require a third primitive not held in either closure, so that second-order composition fails.
4. A derivation that starts from a mechanism name and searches inward produces the shape without any import and without a prior residual description, with its provenance intact.
5. A discriminator is exhibited inside D1 that separates depth from absence on a case where the answer is independently known.

## CONCLUSION

The binary is not an isomer pair. Under the declared unitarity anchor it is a negation pair inside one functional group, which is why the question cannot be settled by composing the two options differently. The Page turnover has the ⋛ shape without strain. The union is second order over two settled first-order closures, and the order of operations is: describe, refuse, settle, import, ground. The framework supplies the shape and the order. Page 1993 and the 2019-2020 calculations supply the content, and Fig 8 :56 states that the triangulation claim "fits four cases already known and has not been tested forward". The holographic case here is therefore a retrodiction, not a prediction the framework generated.
