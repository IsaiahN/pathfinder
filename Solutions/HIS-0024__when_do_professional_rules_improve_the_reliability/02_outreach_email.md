# Outreach draft: check the partition test before you decide about the theory

**Use:** one-to-one, or as a cover letter attached to a submission through the public venues in `03_public_contact_points.csv`. **Not** for bulk send.

**Hard precondition:** do not send until the three checkable sub-claims in `08_expert_verification_packet.md` have been re-read against the verbatim figure text by the sender. The test must survive contact or the two-stage offer collapses on first reply.

---

**Subject:** One check on a claim about professional rules, before any theory

> Hi [name],
>
> I have one specific test I would like you to check against what you already know. I am not asking you to read a framework first, and I am not asking you to agree with anything. If the test does not survive, there is nothing else to look at.
>
> **The test (`general` on the shape, `specified` on any named case):** take any professional rule, one at a time, and ask which of two columns it falls in.
>
> - Column A, reliability-improving: the rule changes contact with the ground (it keeps the channel to experiment, data or outcome open), it buys independence in proportion to the cost of being wrong, it carries a record of where a ruling came from, and it stays a convention rather than deciding an outcome. Shared units, shared vocabulary and open protocols belong here. Agreement about a shared meaning is the product, not a corruption of it.
> - Column B, interest-protecting: the rule becomes the ground itself (the institution's ruling is final), it lets a convention decide an outcome, it protects standing earned on an earlier result, or it makes the people checking a claim draw on one evidence pool. "More of us agreed" is not an anchor.
>
> The two columns are not ends of one scale. A single institution can run both at once, so the answer has to be per rule, not per profession. That is the claim I want checked.
>
> **The governing lines, quoted exactly from the figures:**
> 1. Fig 2 :29: "An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks."
> 2. Fig 10 :47: "a convention that decides an outcome has stopped being one"
> 3. Fig 10 :59: "an interest in the outcome is unavoidable, an interest in the reading is disqualifying."
> 4. Fig 2 :40: "disqualifying for a verdict: agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool"
> 5. Fig 1 :19: "The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated."
>
> **What I am not claiming:** that any named profession has been shown, by a measurement in this work, to be good or bad. That would be `grounded` and it is not. That credentials are always a disguise, or that shared standards are always capture. They are not: a shared convention is efficient for coordination, and the figures say so. That a room of more peers upgrades the label. It does not.
>
> **What would refute this:**
> 1. A rule whose institution is the final arbiter, shown by an independent held-out measurement to track the world better than a low-correlation outside frame.
> 2. A rule whose main effect is to protect incumbents' standing, shown to raise the rate at which incumbent claims are refuted by an outside ground.
> 3. Two frames with nearly identical training and data, whose agreement on a world claim survives independent replication.
> 4. A rule that leaves ground contact unchanged yet improves ground-visible prediction per claim.
>
> I wrote these down before sending this. If any one holds, the partition fails and I will withdraw it. A one-line "this fails because..." is more useful to me than agreement. If you would rather not engage, no reply is needed.
>
> **If it survives, and only then:** the sources are below. The figures are the authority, not my summary of them.
> - Figure text with line numbers (local framework copy): `FRAMEWORK/reference/03-FIGURES_TEXT.md`
> - Canonical usage rule: `FRAMEWORK/reference/FIGURES_README_canonical.md`
> - The eight-step loop: `FRAMEWORK/figures/THE_FORMULA.md`
>
> **If you want to use the machinery:** the loop is in the formula link. Run it on a professional rule you know well and can check against a ground. Write down what would refute your result *before* you run it, and state the mode of what you get. A reported failure is more useful to me than a reported success.
>
> [your name]

## Two-stage offer, explicitly

1. **Stage 1.** Send the two columns, the five verbatim lines and the four refuters. Ask what the recipient would count as a failure. Record the verdict before showing any framework interior.
2. **Stage 2, opt-in only.** If they are curious, offer the three links, with the note that the figure text, not this email, is the authority.
3. **Stage 3, opt-in only.** Offer the formula as a method, with the instruction that each user writes their own refuter and states their own mode.

A later agreement with the framework is never pooled as corroboration (Fig 2 :40). A correction on shape, a named failure class, or the question "could this have been anything else?" is what a useful reply looks like (Seat Map, Seat 4).
