# Expert verification packet: the Tether on professional rules

**What this is:** a claim in a form a historian of science, sociologist of professions, metascientist or STS researcher can check. Mode, derivation and refuters are stated before the conclusion, so a reader knows what to look for and what would settle it.

**Mode: `general` on the shape, `specified` on the placement of any named rule. Not `grounded`.** No case was measured in this run. This is a simulated derivation checked for internal consistency against the figures, not a ground reading, and it cannot settle the claim it reports on.

## 1. The claim, in one paragraph

"Professional rules" is a union of two overlapping conditions, not one thing with a good and a bad end. A rule improves reliability when it changes contact with the ground, buys independence in proportion to the cost of being wrong, carries provenance, stays a convention rather than deciding an outcome, and resolves the residual per claim. A rule protects established interests when the institution becomes the ground, a convention decides an outcome, standing earned on an earlier result is shielded, or the checkers share one evidence pool. The two are isomers over the same institutional atoms: the difference is carried by the bond, invisible in the charter and decisive in behaviour. Both can run in one institution, so verdicts must be per rule and never netted. The framework returns this shape; placing a named case is `specified` and needs a ground reading.

## 2. Three checkable sub-claims

### C1: framework-internal; checkable by reading the figures

- **Statement:** the question is a union compound, so a single aggregated verdict on "professional rules" is illegal under the framework.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 9 :54: *"And when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again."* Also Fig 1 :19: *"The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated."* And Fig 1 :22: *"Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average."*
- **Refuter:** show that both effects are one residual on one slot, so a single lever moves them together in every case; or show that a field-level average predicts per-claim correction better than per-claim measurement.

### C2: framework-internal and history-facing; checkable against Fig 2 and Fig 10

- **Statement:** the separating condition is the ground relation. Column A ranges the rule against an unmoved anchor; column B makes the institution the anchor. The stake test is structural: an interest in the outcome is inevitable, an interest in which value the ground returns is disqualifying, and standing survives the outcome that earned it.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 2 :29: *"An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks."* Fig 2 :47: *"A ground listed as one of the parties has already become a frame."* Fig 10 :47: *"a convention that decides an outcome has stopped being one"* Fig 10 :59: *"an interest in the outcome is unavoidable, an interest in the reading is disqualifying."* Fig 10 :61: *"And standing is a stake that survives the outcome it was earned on."*
- **What to check:** is there a case where the institution's own ruling is final and yet a held-out, independent measurement shows it tracks the world better than a low-correlation outside frame? If yes, the separator fails.
- **Refuter:** such a case; or a rule whose principal function is to protect incumbents' standing shown to raise the rate at which incumbents' claims are refuted by an outside ground.

### C3: institutional design; checkable against Fig 2, Fig 7 and Seat Map

- **Statement:** adding peers from one evidence pool cannot move a rule from column B to column A. Agreement among correlated frames is the pool talking; independence must be bought in proportion to the cost of being wrong, and selection between rooms is where the ground votes.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 2 :40: *"disqualifying for a verdict: agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool"* Fig 2 :41: *"efficient for a convention: agreement about a shared meaning is the product, not a corruption of it"* Fig 7 :35: *"A room's agreement is variation, not a verdict. Selection between rooms is where the ground votes."* Fig 2 :42: *"Buy independence in proportion to the cost of being wrong."*
- **What to check:** the distinction between a shared convention (legitimate, column A) and correlated checking of a world claim (disqualifying, column B). A reviewer who conflates these has found a real defect in the claim.
- **Refuter:** two frames with rho near one, sharing training and data, whose agreement on a world claim survives an independent replication; or a demonstration that k correlated referees raise ground contact by the same factor as k independent frames.

## 3. What is explicitly not claimed

- Not that professional rules are generally good or bad, and not that any named profession has been measured here.
- Not that standards or credentials are always capture. A shared convention is *"efficient for a convention"* (Fig 2 :41, Seat Map :163); correlation is disqualifying only for a verdict about the world.
- Not that adding reviewers adds an anchor. Seat Map :283: *"A room does not upgrade the label."*
- Not that provenance proves correctness. Fig 10 :65: *"a perfectly provenanced wrong ruling is an unimpeachable record of nothing, and it is still to be corrected."*
- Not that the isomer bond assignment in `06` is `general`. The specific ¬ binding onto institutional refusal is `specified`, with its own falsifier recorded there.
- Not `grounded`. Nothing here was settled by an instrument in this run.

## 5. What would settle it

A case-level ground reading, with the refuters recorded first:

1. Per claim (Fig 1 :19), does the rate of ground-confirmed refutation of a community's claims rise after the rule, measured by a frame that did not share the evidence pool used to design it?
2. Is the institution its own final arbiter? If yes, column B is active regardless of the quality of the minutes.
3. Are the rule's beneficiaries the same people whose prior findings it shields? If yes, the standing stake is structural and must be declared (Fig 10 :60).
4. What is rho between checkers, and does their agreement survive an independent replication?

## 6. What an expert can check in twenty minutes

1. Read four short passages: Fig 9 :54, Fig 2 :47, Fig 10 :47, Fig 2 :40.
2. Check C2 against one professional rule you know well. Name the bond: is the record attached to a ground, or to the institution's own ruling?
3. Check the standing test: who gains from the rule's current reading, and is that interest declared?
4. Name one instrument or measurement that would distinguish column A from column B in that case, or say why none is possible.
5. Send the refutation. A failure is more useful than an endorsement.
