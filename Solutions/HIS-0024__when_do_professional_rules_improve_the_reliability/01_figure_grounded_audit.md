## 0. The claim under audit (residual stated as an effect)

> When do professional rules improve the reliability of knowledge, and when do they protect established interests?

Restated in effect terms, the residual R is this: a community installs a rule (a credential, a referee process, a reporting standard, a licence, an editorial policy). After the rule, two different things can happen. Claims can come to track the world more closely, or the rule can preserve the position of those it already favours. We want the condition that separates these, not an average over both.

Before choosing any figure line, the sought-for shape was bound as an ordinary term (formula step "Direction"). The expected answer shape is: a **per-rule partition test in two columns**, each column with its own verbatim figure line and its own refuter, with the two regimes kept separate and never averaged. Binding it first lets this run check its own direction (Fig 5 :62-66).

What an answer would look like:

- A rule is in column A (reliability-improving) if it changes contact with the ground and buys independence, and if its provenance is recorded.
- A rule is in column B (interest-protecting) if it becomes the ground itself, decides an outcome as a convention, protects a standing stake, or raises correlation among checking frames.
- The two are not points on one scale. A single institution can run both columns at once.

## 1. Domain census (done before any figure line was chosen)

### R by arity, symmetry and scale (Fig 9 :22, "arity · symmetry · scale")

- **Arity:** two or more conditions in the same institution (a rule that changes contact, a rule that changes standing), plus a ground they are both ranged against. Arity greater than one means this is not a single hard question.
- **Symmetry:** the relation of a rule to a claim is ordered, not symmetric. A rule can be a precondition for a claim (production, ⇒), a later filter (sequence, →), or a threshold comparison (⋛). Swapping rule and claim changes the thing being described.
- **Scale:** first order at the level of one claim and its checker; second order at the level of the institution that sets the rule (the maintainer level, Fig 10). The two scales have different actors and different currency, and a generator crosses between them while a recording does not (Fig 4 :22-25).

### One line per domain: actors / currency / anchor

| domain | actors | currency | anchor | nameable anchor? |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| D1, knowledge production (makers) | researchers, claims, instruments, data | residuals per claim; predictions paid or not | the ground: experiment, measurement, patient outcome | yes, experiment and outcome |
| D2, the professional institution (rule maker / maintainer) | editors, referees, boards, standard setters, licensers | conventions, rulings, routing, allocation, refresh | the ground, kept reachable through the channel | yes, the same ground, but the channel can die (Fig 10 :24) |
| D3, verification and outside frames | independent searchers, rival schools, newcomers, public | independence (1 minus rho), imports, disagreement | the ground, triangulated across low-overlap frames | yes, independent measurement |
| D4, the habitat of the field | everything in contact with the residual: funders, journals, instruments, gatekeepers, careers | introduction, not exclusion; contact cascades | the ground (the habitat is not the ground, Fig 11 :73) | yes, ground; habitat updates separately |

### Partition or union

**Union, not partition.** Fig 9 :54: *"And when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again."* Fig 9 :55-58: a single lever clears the region where only one condition holds and leaves the intersections standing, and what remains is *"categorically different."* A licence can both screen for competence and raise incumbents' rents (occupational licensing source, ledger row 6). Peer review can both certify quality and award prestige (JSTOR Daily source, ledger row 4). These are overlapping conditions in one institution, so the answer must be per rule, not per profession.

### Dependency order (Fig 3)

1. Can the community still perceive the ground (Fig 3 :4-5, perception)?
2. Does it have terms for what a claim affords, including a term for "this claim is false" (Fig 3 :6-7; Operators table :58, negation)?
3. Can it say what would count as progress here (Fig 3 :9-10, objective)?
4. Only then planning and carrying (Fig 3 :12-15).

Most professional rules operate at links 2 and 3. Fig 3 :19: *"Optimising a link that already works cannot rescue one that does not."* A rule that authors an outcome at link 3 cannot be repaired by better training at link 4.

## 3. Findings F1 to F12

### F1: The question is a union compound, so no single verdict is legal (decisive, structural)

Fig 9 :54: *"And when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again."* Fig 9 :55-58: a single lever clears the region where only one condition holds and leaves the intersections standing.

Trying to answer "are professional rules good?" requires aggregating two conditions into one number. Fig 1 :19: *"The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated."* Fig 1 :22: *"Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average."* The average of a reliability-improving rule and a rent-protecting rule is a number that describes neither.

**Verdict:** `general`, triangulated across Fig 9 and Fig 1 (low overlap: one governs question shape, the other governs measurement). The answer must be a per-rule partition test, not a judgement on professions.

**Refuter:** show that both effects are actually one residual on one slot, so that a single lever moves both together in every case. Then the union reading is wrong and the average would have been fine.

### F2: Column A: rules improve reliability when they keep the ground reachable (decisive)

Fig 2 :29: *"An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks."* Fig 10 :24: *"the ground does not decay, the channel does."*

A rule improves reliability when it changes **contact**, not merely vocabulary: pre-registration fixes the claim before the result is known; open data lets another frame re-run the reading; a recorded protocol carries provenance. Each of these keeps the anchor reachable or lengthens the channel. The rule does not itself become the answer.

**Verdict:** `general`, triangulated (Fig 2 anchor definition plus Fig 10 channel rule, different figures, low overlap). World corroboration: COS Registered Reports (ledger row 2) says peer review before data collection *"eliminates a variety of questionable research practices"*; Ioannidis (ledger row 1) says *"Adherence to common standards is likely to increase the proportion of true findings."* Both are secondary and share a metascience pool with each other.

**Refuter:** a rule that demonstrably leaves the channel to the ground unchanged (same instruments, same data access, same independent replication rate before and after) yet improves ground-visible prediction per claim. That would refute the contact requirement.

### F3: Column B: a rule becomes interest-protecting when it becomes the ground (decisive)

Fig 2 :47: *"A ground listed as one of the parties has already become a frame."* Fig 10 :47: *"a convention that decides an outcome has stopped being one."*

This is the cleanest separating line in the framework. A professional convention (shared meaning, shared method, shared vocabulary) is legitimate and useful. Seat Map :163: *"High correlation is disqualifying for a verdict and efficient for a convention."* It crosses into column B the moment it **decides an outcome**: whose claim is published, who is licensed, which result counts as established. At that point the institution is a ground listed as a party. It cannot range itself against itself, so there is no correction left.

**Verdict:** `general`, triangulated (Fig 2 :47 plus Fig 10 :47; one governs argument structure, the other governs the maintainer's office). World corroboration: the JSTOR Daily history (ledger row 4) reports that early refereeing at the Royal Society was *"more about institution-building (and fortification) than what we know as peer reviewing today,"* including protecting the Society's finances and awarding prestige.

**Refuter:** an institution whose own ruling is the final arbiter of truth, shown by an independent held-out measurement to track the ground better than a low-correlation outside frame. That would break the "ground as party" law.

### F4: The stake test: standing survives the outcome it was earned on (decisive)

Fig 10 :59: *"an interest in the outcome is unavoidable, an interest in the reading is disqualifying."* Fig 10 :61: *"And standing is a stake that survives the outcome it was earned on."* Seat Map :171 states the same law in the seat map.

A credential, a refereeing roster, a citation record and a prior finding are all standing. They are earned on an earlier outcome and then persist. When a rule is written so that protecting that standing is the rule's function (prior findings shielded from refutation, incumbents grandfathered in, review by a body invested in the established result), it is in column B by the framework's own test. The remedy is not to pretend the stake is absent but to declare it (Fig 10 :60: *"Where a stake cannot be removed, declare it."*).

**Verdict:** `general`, triangulated (Fig 10 :59 and Seat Map :171 are the same law in two documents, so this is one witness, not two; the second, independent route is Fig 10 :61 on standing, which governs the object that carries the stake). World corroboration: Merton's counter-norm of organized dogmatism (ledger row 3), *"Scientists invest their careers in promoting their own most important findings"*; occupational licensing (ledger row 6), incumbents grandfathered into new standards.

**Refuter:** show a rule whose main effect is to protect incumbents' earned standing and which nevertheless increases the rate at which incumbent claims are refuted by an outside ground. If refutation of incumbents rises, the standing stake was not doing the protecting.

### F5: Correlation: the same quantity is a virtue for a convention and a vice for a verdict

Fig 2 :40: *"disqualifying for a verdict: agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool."* Fig 2 :41: *"efficient for a convention: agreement about a shared meaning is the product, not a corruption of it."* Fig 7 :35: *"A room's agreement is variation, not a verdict. Selection between rooms is where the ground votes."*

A standard that makes everyone use the same units, the same vocabulary and the same reporting format is in column A by construction: agreement about a shared meaning is the product. The same homogeneity in **checkers of a claim about the world** is in column B: the agreement is the pool talking. This is not a contradiction in the figures. It is the union of F1 stated at the level of one quantity.

**Verdict:** `general`, single-source for the figure wording (Fig 2 :40-41 is one figure) but triangulated with Fig 7 :35 and Seat Map :163, which are separate statements with different subjects (room agreement, convention efficiency). World corroboration: peer review criticism on "role duality" (ledger row 5), *"people are in parallel in the role of being an evaluator and being evaluated."*

**Refuter:** two frames with rho near 1 (same training, same pool, same data) whose agreement on a world claim is shown to be independent by a held-out replication. Then agreement is not the pool talking.

### F6: A rule can only introduce, not exclude

Fig 11 :61: *"The seat's power is asymmetric. Introduction is available; exclusion is not."* Fig 11 :66: *"Isolation is not removal of the habitat. It is substitution of one habitat for another."*

A professional rule can add an actor (a new method, an outside reviewer, a registered protocol). It cannot subtract the habitat, because contact is a fact about the world. A closed shop does not eliminate outside pressure; it substitutes a different habitat with actors it did not choose, and both failure modes are silent (Fig 11 :68-69). Licensing that tries to exclude therefore spends its effort on the wrong lever: it cannot remove contact, only replace the habitat in which the claim is tested.

**Verdict:** `general`, triangulated (Fig 11 :61 and :66 are the same figure but different clauses about different moves; corroborated by Fig 6 :14, *"the only operator that moves the wall,"* which is a distinct figure). World corroboration: occupational licensing (ledger row 6) on restriction of entry and monopoly rents, and the midwifery case where licensing *"led to substantial reductions in maternal mortality,"* which shows introduction of a standard can improve an outcome even while exclusion serves incumbents.

**Refuter:** a rule that removes an actor from contact and is then shown, by a census of the habitat, to have reduced contact rather than substituted a habitat.

### F7: Provenance is the only thing that separates derivation from adoption

Fig 8 :51: *"Convergent derivation and adopted import look identical in the contents. Only the record of where each came from separates them."*

A professional rule that requires a time-stamped protocol, a data trail and a named method carries the record that lets a later reader tell whether a result was derived independently or adopted from a favoured frame. A rule that allows the provenance to be dropped produces a literature in which the two are indistinguishable. This is a column-A function. It is also a limit: provenance shows a ruling was reached legitimately, not that it was right (Fig 10 :65). The deepest version of the interest-protection regime is a perfectly documented wrong ruling, an *"unimpeachable record of nothing"* that is still to be corrected.

**Verdict:** `general`, triangulated (Fig 8 :51 plus Fig 10 :65, distinct figures). World corroboration: COS Registered Reports (ledger row 2) requires in-principle acceptance before results are known, an explicit record of where the decision came from.

**Refuter:** a corpus in which results with full provenance are systematically less reliable than results without it, on a held-out ground.

### F8: A room of peers does not upgrade the label

Seat Map :283: *"A room does not upgrade the label."* Fig 7 :35: *"A room's agreement is variation, not a verdict. Selection between rooms is where the ground votes."* Seat Map :163: *"High correlation is disqualifying for a verdict and efficient for a convention."*

Adding more referees from the same pool raises the vocabulary and does not add an anchor. This is the figure-grounded reason why the interest-protection regime is self-reinforcing: the institution's defence is always "more of us agreed," when agreement among correlated frames is exactly what cannot count. The legitimate response is to select between rooms against the residual the last one surfaced (Fig 7 :25, :36), and to buy independence in proportion to the cost of being wrong (Fig 2 :42).

**Verdict:** `general`, triangulated (Fig 7 and Seat Map are distinct documents with different subjects). World corroboration: Occupational licensing (ledger row 6) quotes Adam Smith on meetings of the same trade ending in *"a conspiracy against the public, or in some contrivance to raise prices."*

**Refuter:** a showing that adding k correlated referees to a decision raises its ground-contact by the same factor as adding k independent frames. The n_eff arithmetic in Seat Map :119 makes this unlikely, but a measured case would settle it.

### F9: Naming the frame before the residual manufactures a fit

Fig 9 :27-28: *"describe it before you go looking, or any frame you pick will seem to fit."* Fig 9 :30-31: *"your words are borrowed too, so describe the effect rather than the cause."* Fig 9 :60: *"Use filters for the budget and witnesses for the verdict. Never let a filter hand you a verdict."*

A rule that starts with "only credentialed experts may speak" has chosen the frame before describing the residual. Any frame so chosen will seem to fit, and the credential becomes a filter handing a verdict. Status, prestige and credentials are filters for the budget, never witnesses for the verdict. This is why the outreach artifacts in this folder ask for a refutation first and offer no ranking of people by receptiveness.

**Verdict:** `general`, triangulated (Fig 9 :27-28 plus Fig 9 :60, different clauses and different objects; corroborated by Seat Map :163). World corroboration: Merton's universalism (ledger row 3), *"scientific validity is independent of the sociopolitical status/personal attributes of its participants."*

**Refuter:** a case where the frame was named first and nevertheless produced an unexpected refutation of its own prior position at a higher rate than a frame chosen after the residual.

### F10: Outreach discipline follows the same partition

Fig 10 :52: *"Bring a characterised residual, never the source."* Fig 10 :42: *"A verifier that can reconstruct the claim cannot verify it."*

This is not a hygiene rule bolted onto the answer. It is F2 and F5 applied to the checking arrangement. If a recipient is given the full framework before giving a verdict, their agreement stops being evidence (they share the evidence pool), and their disagreement may be a disagreement with the framing rather than the claim. So the first contact is the per-rule partition test and its refuter, and the figures come only after a verdict is recorded. No request for trust, no request for agreement, no bulk list.

**Verdict:** `general`, triangulated (Fig 10 :42 and :52 are two distinct clauses, corroborated by Fig 2 :40). World corroboration: peer review source (ledger row 5) on *"lack of accountability"* and role duality.

**Refuter:** a two-stage trial in which recipients shown the theory first refute at a higher, independently verified rate than recipients shown only the residual. That would reverse this finding.

### F12: A named failure class for the question itself

Fig 3 :24-27: the link between having terms for what a situation affords and saying what counts as progress has *"neither"* obvious failures nor obvious tests, so it attracts the least attention and the fewest instruments. This question lives at that link. "Professional rules" is a vocabulary that names a situation, not a grammar that names a route. A vocabulary of ingredients cannot state a route (Fig 12 :111-113). The contribution of this folder is not a name for the good rule but a **grammar**: two columns, each with a verbatim line and a refuter, so a real case can be placed and checked.

## 4. Pre-registered refuter (written before the conclusion)

Required before any outreach, Seat Map :287: *"a falsifier written before the run is a design instrument, not only an honesty device."*

The two-column shape claim is refuted if any of the following holds:

1. A rule that has become the ground (the institution's own ruling is final) is shown, by an independent held-out measurement, to track the world better than a low-correlation outside frame (breaks F3).
2. A rule whose principal effect is to protect incumbents' standing increases the rate at which incumbent claims are refuted by an outside ground (breaks F4).
3. Frames with rho near 1, sharing an evidence pool, produce agreement on a world claim that survives an independent replication (breaks F5 and F8).
4. A rule that leaves ground contact measurably unchanged nevertheless improves ground-visible prediction per claim (breaks F2).
5. Both observed effects are one residual on one slot, so a single lever moves them together in every case (breaks F1).
6. A rule that removes an actor from contact is shown by a habitat census to have reduced contact rather than substituted a habitat (breaks F6).

If any fires, the partition is withdrawn and the question returns to census. Until one is run, no part of this may be called `grounded`.

## 5. Recommendation

1. Ask of each rule, separately: does it change contact, buy independence, carry provenance, and remain a convention? If yes, column A. If it becomes the ground, decides an outcome, protects standing or raises rho among checkers, column B.
2. Do not answer "are professional rules good?" That aggregates slots (F1). Unbundle first (Fig 9 :54).
3. Where both columns are present in one institution, report both. Do not net them.
4. For any named case, state `specified` and name the ground reading that would move it to `grounded`. If none exists, say `unreachable`, not silence (Fig 8 :39: *"So a search that finds nothing is never a proof of absence."*).
5. Use status, prestige and credentials as filters for the budget only (Fig 9 :60).
6. Send the partition test and the refuter, not the framework (Fig 10 :52).

### Strains, and what the re-census did with them

- **Fig 2 :40 versus Fig 2 :41.** Read as a contradiction at first ("correlation bad" versus "correlation good"). Re-census: the two clauses govern different quantities, a verdict about the world and a convention about shared meaning. Not strained. The union in F1 is the same split at the level of the question.
- **Fig 10 :45 versus Fig 10 :47.** Read as a contradiction at first (the seat authors conventions, but a convention that decides an outcome has stopped being one). Re-census: :45 is about conventions as shared meaning across a seam; :47 is the failure when that convention crosses into deciding an outcome. Different objects, no strain.
- **Fig 11 :61 versus licensing practice.** A rule appears to exclude actors, while the figure says exclusion is unavailable. Re-census: the rule substitutes a habitat (Fig 11 :66) rather than removing contact. The figure is not strained; the practice is misdescribed.

### Updated verdict

The shape survives: professional rules improve reliability when they keep the ground reachable, buy independence, carry provenance and stay conventions; they protect established interests when they become the ground, decide outcomes, protect standing that survived an earlier result, or raise correlation among checkers. The two columns are a union and must be reported separately. Strains after re-census: three apparent contradictions, all dissolved by distinguishing the quantity each line governs. No figure line was bent. Nothing here is `grounded`.
