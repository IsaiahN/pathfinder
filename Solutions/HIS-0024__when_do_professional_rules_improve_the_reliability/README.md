## What is being decided, in effect terms

A professional community adopts rules: credentialing, peer review, reporting standards, licensing, editorial policy. Two different effects are observed. Sometimes claims end up tracking the world more closely (errors get corrected, predictions improve, a failed claim is refused). Sometimes the rules freeze the position of incumbents (their prior findings, their standing, their right to speak). The question asks for the condition that separates those effects.

Option A: treat "professional rules" as one thing and ask whether it is good or bad on average. This aggregates the residual across slots and loses the only signal (Fig 1 :19, Fig 1 :22). Option B: treat the question as a union compound (Fig 9 :54), unbundle the rules that change ground contact from the rules that change standing, and give each its own loop and its own verdict. This folder takes B.

What an answer would look like was written down before the run and bound as an ordinary term (formula step "Direction"): a per-rule partition test with two columns, each column carrying a verbatim figure line and a refuter. That bound shape is what the WATSON and audit files check.

## What is deliberately not here

- No personal emails of named individuals and no purchased or scraped contact lists.
- No ranking of people by how likely they are to agree with the derivation.
- No bulk-send plan, and no request for trust or endorsement.
- No claim that a particular historical profession has been shown, by a ground reading in this run, to have improved or degraded reliability. Naming such a case is `specified`, not `grounded`.

The figure-grounded reasons are in `01` F8 to F10: a room does not add an anchor (Seat Map :283), correlation is disqualifying for a verdict (Seat Map :163, Fig 2 :40), and a verifier that can reconstruct the claim cannot verify it (Fig 10 :42). Selecting recipients for receptiveness manufactures the very correlation that would make their agreement worthless.
