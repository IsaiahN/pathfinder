# The Tether's answer: water management when the past stops predicting

**Mode: `specified` on the shape, `general` on the structural constraints. Not `grounded`.** This is a shape and a route, not a policy. Simulated derivation checked against the figures (`05_what_shape_means.md` section 6), not a ground reading.

## 1. The residual it answers

A manager acts on a belief built from a historical record. The ground returns actual supply. Under a shifted regime, the error signal `R = |Γ(b,a) − o′|` on some slots (basin, aquifer, season, user class) persists and does not trend down as more history is added. The record is not false as a record; it has become a stale channel to a ground that did not decay. Fig 10 :24: *"the ground does not decay, the channel does"*.

The question is a **union compound**, not one hard problem (Fig 9 :55: *"A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones"*). Climate, extraction, pollution, demand and infrastructure overlap. Single-lever optimisation clears the single-condition regions and leaves the intersections standing.

## 2. The shape

1. **Keep the ground reachable per slot, not the forecast.** The anchor is legitimate because the question is constitutively about future supply (Fig 2 :36), not about the hydrograph. Fig 10 :29: *"keep the anchor reachable"*. Report residuals per basin, aquifer and season. Fig 1 :22: *"Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average."*
2. **Refuse the stationarity term; do not refit it and do not mint a replacement.** Fig 5 :53: *"refuted: a term we hold is wrong; a term is owed that says so"*. The refusal is a negation. More search of the record cannot add an atom about the new regime: Fig 8 :32, *"no search, however long, adds an atom"*.
3. **Unbundle before optimising.** Split the union, then give the intersections their own loop (Fig 9 :5). A compound is not a harder single case.
4. **Hold objectives as comparisons and actions as sequences.** Fig 12 :112: *"A plan is a sequence. A goal is a comparison. Progress is a subtraction."* A fixed target derived from a past mean is a comparison that no longer ranges against the right ground.
5. **Book successive distinct rooms, not a final plan.** Fig 7 :36: *"The next room must differ from the last, and match the residual it surfaced."* Fig 7 :42: *"the next room you book is the direction you travel"*. Progress per handoff has two factors: bracket fidelity and ground pressure. Zero either and the chain runs in place.
6. **Change contact, not just the institution.** Fig 11 :40: *"An improvement that does not change contact changes nothing, however much it improves."* Fig 11 :63: *"a managed ecosystem is permanent work rather than a solved state"*. The habitat is enumerated from the world (actors, conditions, relations); measures can be introduced and cannot be subtracted (Fig 11 :61).
7. **Improve instruments from something already returning signal.** Fig 6 :32: *"An instrument is not built from a description. It is improved from a worse instrument already returning something."* Seek the indicator already registering the shift, not a perfect forecast that does not exist.
8. **Buy independent frames in proportion to the cost of being wrong.** Fig 2 :42: *"Buy independence in proportion to the cost of being wrong."* Agreement among models sharing one reanalysis is one witness. In this run all seats share one evidence pool, so agreement here is internal consistency only.
9. **Measure the round trip gap.** Fig 4 :30: *"So take the round trip and measure the gap. Without it you are navigating by dead reckoning: confident about a position nobody checked."* A historical rule replayed in a new regime is a recording, not a generator.

## 3. What it explicitly does not say

- Not a named policy. The framework returns a shape. "Robust adaptive management", "dynamic adaptive policy pathways", "real options" and "decision scaling" are names from world literatures, recorded as bindings in `04`, not figure outputs.
- Not "collect more data". That is the wrong lever when the closure of the record does not contain the new regime.
- Not that past data is useless. It is an instrument with a limited reach, not the anchor.
- Not a denial of settled hydrology. A gradient can still be spent; supply still composes from energy and gradient (Fig 13 :76). The claim is about the *predictive* term, not the physics.
- Not `grounded`. No basin was measured; no threshold was tested; no stakeholder was consulted.
- Not a last plan. No final room (Fig 7 :6).
- Not a verdict from expert agreement. Fig 2 :35: *"Alignment is triangulation, not negotiation. A mirror cannot correct you."*

## 4. What would settle it: the ground

A discriminator, not a further argument. *The Hard Problem is About Source* shows an instrument reading green *"cannot comment on the source"*; the spectrometer resolved it. Here:

- Which indicator is already returning a non-resolving signal about the regime shift, at what resolution, per basin? That names the instrument step (F10).
- Does the held stationarity term actually persist and stay flat in the residual ranking the selector already runs? If it trends down, no refusal is owed (F2).
- Does a threshold `⋛` hold between measured supply and the requirement at the decision date, or demonstrably not?
- Does one lever clear the intersections of the union compound, or only the single-condition regions (F4)?
- Does a paired-basin study show that bond arrangement changes behaviour (isomer claim in `06`)?

Standing falsifiers from `01`: a stationary basin at the decision resolution; a held term that predicts the shift without new atoms; an agent-only improvement that moves the ground; a rule that stays correct with no signposts and no revision; a vocabulary without comparison, sequence or subtraction that still names a route under change.

## 5. The honest stop

**Unreached, not unreachable.** The shape stops at the limit of the available instruments and records the lineage so the stop is checkable. Fig 8 :39: *"So a search that finds nothing is never a proof of absence."* Fig 9 :32: *"you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself"*. *Indiscernibility is not identity*: a management regime that looks correct against the historical record may still be a projection of it. The result is candidate until a ground pays.

## 6. One-paragraph version for outreach

> The Tether does not tell a water manager which portfolio to build. It gives the shape any answer under non-stationarity must have. The historical record is a channel to the ground, not the ground itself, and when the regime shifts that channel goes stale while the ground persists. So keep the ground reachable per basin, aquifer and season; refuse the term "future supply resembles past supply" rather than refitting it; unbundle the overlapping conditions before optimising; write objectives as thresholds and actions as revisable sequences; book successive distinct plans rather than a final one; and improve monitoring from whatever is already registering the shift. Prestige and agreement cannot settle any of this, only a measured threshold crossing can. The answer is a shape, a set of refuters written in advance, and a record of what would move it to grounded. The slot stays open until the ground pays.
