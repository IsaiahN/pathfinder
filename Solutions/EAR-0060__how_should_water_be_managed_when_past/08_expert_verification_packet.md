# Expert verification packet: the Tether on water management under non-stationarity

**What this is:** a claim in a form a water researcher or manager can check. Mode, derivation and refuters are stated before the conclusion, so a reader knows what to look for and what would settle it.

**Mode: `specified` on the shape, `general` on the structural constraints. Not `grounded`.** No basin measurement is claimed. This is a shape and a route, not a policy.

**Status:** simulated derivation checked for internal consistency against the verbatim figures, not a ground reading. It cannot settle the question it reports on.

## 1. The claim, in one paragraph

When past patterns stop predicting supply, the historical record is a stale channel to an unmoved ground, not a legitimate anchor. A management regime must therefore keep the ground reachable per slot, refuse the stationarity term instead of refitting it, unbundle the overlapping conditions before optimising, hold objectives as thresholds and actions as revisable sequences, and revise against surfaced residuals. Two regimes over the same measures are isomers if their bonds differ: an ingredient list cannot name a route. The framework does not name a policy. It offers the constraints any candidate answer must satisfy, refuters written in advance, and the instruments that would close it.

## 2. Three checkable sub-claims

### C1: framework-internal plus ground-facing; checkable by reading Fig 2 and Fig 10

- **Statement:** the historical record is an instrument, not the anchor, when the question is constitutively about future supply. The first obligation is to keep the ground reachable.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 2 :36 *"An anchor is legitimate when the question is constitutively about it."* Fig 10 :24 *"the ground does not decay, the channel does"*. Fig 10 :25 *"stale percepts, dropped transients,"*. Fig 10 :29 *"keep the anchor reachable"*.
- **What to check:** in your basin, is the decision about future supply, or about fit to the past record? If it is about future supply, the ground is the future hydrologic state, not the hydrograph.
- **Refuter:** show a basin where supply is stationary over the decision horizon at the resolution that matters, so the record is a legitimate anchor there; or show an instrument that moves when pushed and still settles the question. Either breaks C1.

### C2: framework-internal; checkable by reading Fig 5, Fig 8, Fig 9 and Fig 12

- **Statement:** the stationarity term must be refused, not refitted; the residual is a union compound and must be unbundled; a regime is a bonded arrangement, not an ingredient list.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 5 :53 *"refuted: a term we hold is wrong; a term is owed that says so"*. Fig 8 :32 *"no search, however long, adds an atom"*. Fig 9 :55 *"A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones."* Fig 12 :112 *"A plan is a sequence. A goal is a comparison. Progress is a subtraction."*
- **What to check:** does more historical data change the prediction of the shifted regime, or only the fit to the old one? Does one lever clear the intersections of climate, extraction, pollution and demand, or only single-condition regions? Does the regime statement contain a threshold, a sequence and a correction, or only a list of measures?
- **Refuter:** a held term that predicts the shifted regime without new atoms; or one lever that clears the whole union; or a vocabulary with no comparison, sequence or subtraction that still states a goal and a route under change. Any one breaks C2.

### C3: framework-internal derivation; checkable by reading Fig 7, Fig 11 and Fig 12

- **Statement:** no final plan. Successive rooms must differ from the last and match the residual it surfaced. Capability is agent-and-habitat, so contact must change, and a monitored regime is a cycle, not a chain.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 7 :36 *"The next room must differ from the last, and match the residual it surfaced."* Fig 7 :42 *"the next room you book is the direction you travel"*. Fig 11 :40 *"An improvement that does not change contact changes nothing, however much it improves."* Fig 12 :121 *"The output re-enters as input. The sign of the loop decides whether it closes"*.
- **What to check:** does the plan have signposts and a revision trigger, or is it declared final? Did the proposed change alter contact with gauges, thresholds and the habitat, or only the institution? Is there a monitored loop whose sign can close it?
- **Refuter:** a rule that remains correct across the full plausible range with no signposts and no revision; or an agent-only improvement that moves the ground (the falsifier the source figure itself states); or a demonstration that handoff fidelity and ground pressure do not affect adaptation outcomes. Any one breaks C3.

## 3. What is explicitly not claimed

- Not a named policy. RDM, DMDU, DAPP, adaptive management and real options are world literatures, not figure outputs.
- Not "collect more data". The record's closure may not contain the new regime.
- Not a denial of hydrology. Gradients, flows and networks still compose; the claim is about the predictive term.
- Not a verdict from agreement. Later agreement from an outside reader is a conversation, never corroboration (Fig 2 :40).
- Not `grounded`. Nothing here was settled by a measurement.
- Not a last room (Fig 7 :6: *"the limit: no last room"*).

## 5. What would settle it

A discriminator instrument, not another argument. From *The Hard Problem is About Source*: an instrument reading green *"cannot comment on the source"*; the spectrometer resolved it. Here:

1. Which indicator is already returning a non-resolving signal about the regime shift, at what resolution, per basin?
2. Does the held stationarity term persist and stay flat in the residual ranking, or does it trend down?
3. Does a threshold comparison between measured supply and requirement hold, or demonstrably not, at the decision date?
4. Do paired basins show that different bonds over the same measures produce different behaviour under a shift?
