## Restating the question as an effect

In effect terms, "past patterns no longer reliably predict future supply" says: the channel between a manager's belief (built from a historical record) and the ground (actual future water in a basin, aquifer, or season) has decayed. The question asks what a controller should do when its error signal `R = |Γ(b,a) − o′|` no longer trends down no matter how much history it accumulates. Option A: treat the historical record as the anchor and optimise a plan against it. Option B: treat the record as a now-stale instrument, keep the real hydrologic ground reachable, and hold the management regime as a revisable shape rather than a fixed answer. The two options lead to different investments, different monitoring, and different failure modes.

## What is deliberately not here

- No personal emails of named individuals.
- No ranking of people by how likely they are to agree with the shape claim.
- No bulk-send plan.

Three reasons, in the figures' own words:

1. **A room does not upgrade the label.** Fig 2 :35: *"Alignment is triangulation, not negotiation. A mirror cannot correct you."* Adding readers to a list raises the vocabulary and does not add an anchor.
2. **Selecting for receptiveness manufactures the disqualifying correlation.** Fig 2 :40: *"agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool."*
3. **Asking for trust before checking spends independence.** Fig 10 :42: *"A verifier that can reconstruct the claim cannot verify it."* The first contact carries the residual and its refuter, never the framework's interiors.

Legally: unsolicited bulk email to harvested addresses breaches GDPR and CAN-SPAM style rules in many jurisdictions. Public, opt-in institutional channels avoid that practical failure as well as the structural one.
