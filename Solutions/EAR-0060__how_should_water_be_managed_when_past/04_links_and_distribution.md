## What the world sources predict here

- **Sciences, hydrology.** Milly et al.'s title states the residual in the field's own words: *"Stationarity is dead: whither water management?"* Predicts that a record-based anchor cannot be assumed. Corroborates F1 and F2 from a different pool.
- **Engineering and public policy.** The RAND tool predicts forward-looking stress testing, robust strategies, signposts and deferred investments. Corroborates F6, F7 and F10. Its own words about *"variability or changes in supply and demand outside historical ranges"* are a near verbatim restatement of the question, which is useful but also a warning: this is a shared evidence pool with the field, not independent confirmation of the figures.
- **Decision science.** RDM predicts robustness rather than optimality and vulnerability-first rather than predict-then-act. Corroborates the shape's refusal step and its threshold objectives.
- **Ecology and natural resource management.** Adaptive management predicts iterative decisions with monitoring feedback and repeated decisions. Corroborates F6 and the cycle in F7.
- **Mathematics and decision theory.** Info-gap's critic predicts that local robustness around a bad estimate is worthless under severe uncertainty. Conflicts with a naive reading of "robustness"; after re-census it corroborates F10's demand for a real instrument. No figure was overturned.

No world source overrules a figure. The conflict encountered (info-gap critique) was sent back to the census and resolved there, exactly as the method requires.

## Distribution: offer, do not push

Figure-grounded rules:

- Fig 4 :22, *"Only methods cross up."* Sending a full framework before anyone has described a residual sends a recording, not a generator. Lead with the residual and refuter; offer links only after a verdict is recorded.
- Fig 10 :42, *"A verifier that can reconstruct the claim cannot verify it."* A recipient who has absorbed the framework before being asked for a verdict has joined the frame being checked.
- Fig 9 :27, *"describe it before you go looking, or"* *"any frame you pick will seem to fit"*. Ask what they would expect to see if the shape claim were true before giving them the article.
- Fig 2 :35, *"Alignment is triangulation, not negotiation. A mirror cannot correct you."* Do not select recipients for likely agreement.

**Recommended order in a reply:** (1) the residual, shape and five refuters; (2) the question "what would you count as a failure here?"; (3) only then the three links, with the note that the figures, not the summary, are the authority; (4) on request, the formula as a method with each user's own ground and refuter.
