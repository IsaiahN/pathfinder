# Outreach draft: check the output before deciding about the framework

**Use:** one-to-one, or as a cover note attached to a submission through a public venue in `03_public_contact_points.csv`. **Not** for bulk send.

**Hard precondition:** do not send until the shape claim and its refuter in `01_figure_grounded_audit.md` are stated cleanly. The offer collapses on contact if stage 1 does not survive checking.

---

**Subject:** One checkable claim about managing water when the historical record stops working

> Hi [name],
>
> I am working on a narrow question in water management under uncertainty and have one specific output I would like you to check against what you already know. I am not asking you to read a theory first, and I am not asking you to agree with anything. If the output does not survive, there is nothing else to look at.
>
> **The residual, in effect terms.** A water manager's belief is built from a historical record. It acts: sets allocations, operates reservoirs, issues permits. The ground returns actual supply. When past patterns stop predicting supply, the error signal on some slots (a basin, an aquifer, a season, a user class) persists and does not shrink as more history is added. The question is what the controller should do then.
>
> **The output (`specified`, not `grounded`).** A management regime has to: (1) keep the real hydrologic ground reachable per slot instead of treating the historical record as the anchor; (2) refuse the term "future supply resembles past supply" rather than refitting it; (3) hold objectives as revisable thresholds, not fixed targets; (4) sequence actions as pathways with signposts rather than declare one final plan; (5) be corrected by monitoring. This is a **shape**, a set of constraints any candidate answer must satisfy. It is not a named policy. I am not claiming that any particular portfolio, model, or institution is the answer.
>
> **What would refute it:** (1) a basin where supply is stationary over the decision horizon at the resolution that matters, so the record is a legitimate anchor; (2) a held term that predicts the shifted regime without new atoms, so no refusal is owed; (3) one lever that clears the whole residual across all overlapping conditions; (4) a rule that stays correct across the full plausible range with no signposts and no revision; (5) a vocabulary with no thresholds, no sequence and no subtraction that still states a goal and a route to it under change.
>
> I wrote these down before sending this. If any one holds, I withdraw the corresponding claim. A one-line "this fails because..." is more useful to me than agreement. If you would rather not engage, no reply is needed.
>
> **If it survives, and only then:** here are the sources. The figures, not my summary of them, are the authority.
> - RAND and US Bureau of Reclamation, Water Planning for the Uncertain Future: https://www.rand.org/pubs/tools/TL320/tool.html
> - PubMed record for Milly et al., "Stationarity is dead: whither water management?", Science 2008: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/18239110/
> - Decision-making under deep uncertainty, open encyclopedia entry: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Decision-making_under_deep_uncertainty
>
> **If you want to reuse the method:** the eight-step formula is in the figures material. Run it on a problem you know well and can check against a ground. Write down what would refute your result *before* you run it, and label the mode of what you get. A reported failure is more useful to me than a reported success.
>
> - [your name]

## Stage discipline

1. **Stage 1:** recipient checks the residual, the shape and the five refuters against their own ground. Their verdict is recorded before any theory is shown.
2. **Stage 2, opt-in only:** if curious, the three links above. Their later agreement is a conversation, never corroboration.
3. **Stage 3:** offer the formula as a method to reuse, with the instruction that each user writes a refuter and states a mode.

One recipient at a time. No list ranking by likely agreement. No bulk send. An opt-out line in every message.
