## DERIVATION

### Q1: Partition or union?

- Fig 9 :5: *"are not one hard question but several well-formed ones. Split it rather than search."*
- Fig 9 :55: *"A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones."*
- Fig 9 :56-57, continuing from that line: a single lever *"clears the region where only one condition holds and leaves the intersections standing"*, and the residue becomes *"a specific compound"*.
- The conditions here overlap: a drier climate raises extraction, which lowers groundwater, which raises pollution concentration, which constrains demand. No one of them is sufficient, and no lever clears all intersections.
- ⇒ It is a **union**, not a partition. Unbundle before searching. The intersections get their own loop.
- **Falsifier, written before the run:** exhibit one lever that clears the whole residual across all intersections, not just the single-condition regions. Then the union reading fails and a partition reading is enough. Triangulated at the figure level (Fig 9 :5 and :55); the world corroboration from RAND's multiple drivers is a different pool but one witness.

### Q2: What bonds make measures into a regime?

Fig 12 :111: *"A vocabulary without bonds can name a state and cannot name a route to one."* Fig 12 :112: *"A plan is a sequence. A goal is a comparison. Progress is a subtraction."* The bond table at Fig 12 :55-89 gives the typed tests. Applied to D3:

| bond | test from the figures | water-management reading |
|---|---|---|
| `+` conjunction, symmetric | *"both hold at once"* | a portfolio where two measures operate together; order of naming does not matter |
| `⋛` comparison, ordered | *"is it about which is larger, rather than both being present?"* | a threshold objective: supply against requirement, groundwater level against a trigger |
| `→` sequence, ordered | *"A then B; B needs A to have happened"* | an adaptation pathway: monitor, then trigger, then invest |
| `−` subtraction, ordered | *"is the second operand's absence the point? Yes"* | progress measured as residual reduction, not as infrastructure count |
| `⇒` production, ordered | *"A produces B; B did not exist before"* | a measure that creates new capability (recharge produces storage that did not exist) |
| `∥` disjunction, symmetric | *"remove one. Does it still work? Yes"* | redundancy: either source suffices |
| `≡` identity, symmetric | *"two names, one referent"* | two indicators that are the same measurement under different names |
| `¬` negation, unary, not a bond | *"a refutation is a negation"* | refusing the stationarity term; this changes the reach, not the vocabulary |

Fig 12 :121: *"cycle. The output re-enters as input. The sign of the loop decides whether it closes, and a chain cannot close by construction."* A monitoring-and-revision loop is a cycle, not a chain. A fixed plan is a chain and cannot close.

- ⇒ An ingredient list (dams, reuse, conservation) can name what a system contains and cannot name a goal, a route, or progress. The regime is the bonded arrangement.
- **Falsifier:** demonstrate a vocabulary containing only measures with no comparison, no sequence, no subtraction and no negation that still states a goal, a route and a correction under change. The figures say it cannot be done. Single-source for the figure rule (Fig 12), with the Operators table as a close corroborator, not an independent witness.

### Q3: Are two regimes isomers?

Fig 12 :117: *"isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."*

Take two basins with the same portfolio of measures: storage, conservation, reuse, groundwater banking. Regime A joins them with `+` under one fixed target. Regime B joins them with `→` under a threshold `⋛` and a monitoring cycle. Same atoms, different bonds, different behaviour under a shifted regime. They are **isomers** in the figure's sense: a formula listing only the measures cannot tell them apart, and the difference is decisive.

- **Falsifier:** show that bond arrangement makes no behavioural difference under a shifted supply regime, that only the ingredient set matters. Then the isomer reading fails. This is `specified`, unmeasured; it needs a paired basin study to settle.

### Q4: Order: what comes first?

Fig 3 :20: *"The chain is ordered by dependency, not by importance."* Fig 3 :19: *"Optimising a link that already works cannot rescue one that does not."* Formula step 2: a REFUTED residual *"no arrangement of the other four reaches it"*, so refusal comes before minting.

The dependency order for D3 is therefore:

1. **Perceive:** keep the ground reachable. Measure per slot. (Fig 10 :29)
2. **Refuse:** point the negation at the bound stationarity term before building on it. (Fig 5 :53)
3. **Vocabulary and objective:** name thresholds, what counts as progress. (Fig 12 :112)
4. **Plan:** choose a portfolio and a sequence of actions. (Fig 7 :36)
5. **Learn and carry:** monitor, revise, and carry up only methods. (Fig 4 :22)

A plan written at step 4 while step 2 is still unrefused is a reading below the break. Fig 3 :22: *"A step whose input never arrives cannot be diagnosed, only its predecessor can."*

- **Strain, named:** Fig 7 :36 says the next room must differ from the last and *"match the residual it surfaced"*, which sounds like a cycle rather than a linear order. Re-census: Q4's order is the dependency order inside one room; the cycle is the transition between rooms. Different objects, so no contradiction. Kept visible rather than smoothed.

## CONCLUSION

The residual is a union compound, so unbundling precedes optimisation. A management regime is a bonded arrangement, not an ingredient list: threshold comparisons, action sequences, subtraction as progress, a monitoring cycle, and a negation that refuses the stationarity term. Two regimes over the same measures are isomers if their bonds differ. The order is refuse, then objective, then plan, then monitor, with the room-to-room cycle sitting above it. The world literature converges on the shape but does not supply it, and much of it shares an evidence pool, so corroboration is weak. No new primitive was imported and no figure wall was moved.
