## 1. The claim, in one paragraph

The early-universe and nearby-universe expansion rates are two slots of one residual, not two estimates to be pooled. A disagreement that does not shrink with effort must be split, and this one splits into a union of two conditions: a channel failure in a measurement frame, and a missing primitive in the shared model's closure. From inside either frame the two conditions look the same, so the framework cannot name which is actual and returns a shape: keep the conditions separate, measure the lossy round trip from acoustic scale to present expansion rate, and obtain a reading from an arbiter frame with a genuinely different closure. The specific cause stays open until that reading exists.

## 3. What is explicitly not claimed

- Not a cause: not calibration error, not new physics, not early dark energy. Those are names; the framework returns a shape.
- Not a pooled expansion rate and not an average of the two methods.
- Not that significance level or team count decides the verdict; both are budget filters (Fig 9 :60).
- Not that the two routes are isomers. As practised they use different calibrating atoms and are members of one functional group (Fig 12 :124); see `06_isomers_and_order.md`.
- Not a theory of everything, not a final theory, and not the only account of the tension.
- Not `grounded`. No experiment settles any of this inside this packet.
- Not a claim that the arbiter already exists in full. TRGB is a partial instance: a different calibrator that sits between the two values, not yet a discriminator over both conditions.
