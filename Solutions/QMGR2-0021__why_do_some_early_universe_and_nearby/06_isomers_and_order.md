## DERIVATION

### Q1 - Are the two routes isomers?

- Fig 12 :117: **"isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."**
- Fig 12 :124: **"functional group. A substructure whose presence fixes the outcome. The group says what kind; the qualifier says which member."**

**First reading, recorded before re-census.** Both routes aim at one quantity H0 from one universe. Write the early route as `acoustic scale - model - H0` and the nearby route as `calibrated candle - redshift - H0`. Same target, different arrangement, different reported number. That looks like an isomer pair: same underlying atoms, different bond, different substance.

**Re-census and correction to my own first reading.** The definition requires *same atoms*. They are not the same. The early route needs the sound horizon and a model of pre-recombination physics; the nearby route needs none of those. The nearby route needs a geometric calibrator chain; the early route needs none of that. Even within the late frame the calibrators differ: Cepheids in SH0ES, TRGB in Freedman et al 2019, and Freedman's own text calls TRGB *"parallel to, but independent of the Cepheid distance scale."* Different atoms, not the same atoms under a different bond.

**Conclusion Q1.** As the methods are actually practised they are **not isomers**. They are two members of the same **functional group**, a calibrated standard mapped to H0 through redshift, with different qualifiers (sound horizon versus Candle). The isomer claim would be true only if both were written over one shared atom ledger, which is precisely what an arbiter frame would have to establish. I record the first reading and its refusal so the correction is visible rather than silent. The functional-group claim is `triangulated` (Fig 12 :124 plus the physics descriptions in the three papers); the isomer claim is **refused as written**.

**Falsifier, written before this conclusion:** if a single atom ledger can be written over which both routes are arrangements of the same primitives, differing only in bond, the isomer claim is restored. If not, the functional-group reading stands permanently for these methods.

### Q2 - What is the order of the checks?

- Fig 3 :19: **"Optimising a link that already works cannot rescue one that does not."**
- Fig 3 :20: **"The chain is ordered by dependency, not by importance."**
- Fig 3 :27: **"A link with no instrument is not a link that works. It is a link nobody has looked at."**

The chain here is: perception (calibration traceability) to vocabulary (what the model can express) to objective (which residual is live) to planning (which arbiter to build) to learn and carry. The break is most plausibly at the calibration-to-vocabulary seam, which is the middle link Fig 3 :24-27 says attracts the least attention and the fewest instruments. It is not acceptable to refine a model extension (above the break) while the geometric zero point (below it) is unassessed. Fig 3 :22: *"A step whose input never arrives cannot be diagnosed, only its predecessor can."*

Order for this question: settle the geometric calibrators first; only then ask whether the shared model closure needs a new atom; describe the residual before choosing either. This is also the order in the SH0ES paper's own practice (parallaxes, masers and eclipsing binaries calibrate the Cepheids before SNe reach the Hubble flow).

### Q3 - What do settled results become at the next level?

### Q4 - What do low-overlap fields contribute?

- **Metrology.** From the Calibration page: *"calibration is the comparison of measurement values delivered by a device under test with those of a calibration standard of known accuracy"*, and the standard is *"normally traceable to a national or international standard held by a metrology body."* This is an independent-field statement of Fig 10 :24: *"the ground does not decay, the channel does"*, and it makes the channel condition concrete. A drifted zero point is a loss of traceability, not a change in the universe.
- **Survey statistics.** From the Design effect page: *"the design effect is a number that shows how well a sample of people may represent a larger group of people"*, with effective sample size falling as observations correlate. This is the arithmetic behind Fig 2 :31 and :44: ten teams reading one evidence pool have fewer effective witnesses than ten teams reading different instruments. It corroborates the filter rule, not the cause verdict.

Both sources are from fields outside cosmology and share no author with the framework, so they are genuine low-overlap corroboration of the *mechanism shape*. Neither predicts which cause is actual.
