## What in the wider literature bears on the question

1. **The word "inferred" is doing real work.** Planck does not report a local measurement of H0; it reports a late-Universe parameter inferred under a model. The nearby ladder reads H0 through calibrated distances. That is the structural difference the split in `01` is built on, and it is visible in the Planck abstract itself.
2. **The literature's own uncertainty about the cause matches the figure verdict.** SH0ES says the source *"remains unknown"*; Kamionkowski and Riess say proposed systematics are not supported by the breadth of available data while conceding that untestable unknown errors cannot be excluded by definition. That is a report from one pool about two bins, not a ground ruling.
3. **TRGB is the arbiter already partially instantiated.** It is a different calibrator and it sits between the headline values. It weakens any claim that the late frame is monolithic, while also declining to pick a side. In figure terms it is a second closure within the same broad frame, not yet a full arbiter over both conditions.
4. **The blind analysis in DESI is a discipline the framework endorses.** Fig 8 :53 says *"Residual first, frame second."* A blind analysis is an institutional way of preventing the frame from being chosen to fit the hoped-for residual.
5. **The 2026 JCAP calibration paper refuses a naive single-cause answer.** A redshift-dependent SN Ia calibration effect is not supported within LCDM, but can reduce the tension when combined with an extension. That is the union condition appearing in the literature's own language, and it is corroboration only.

No world source conflicts with the figure verdict. The Kamionkowski and Riess preference for early-time solutions was tested against the verdict: it does not conflict, because the verdict refuses to name a winner. Had it claimed a ground settlement, the conflict rule would have sent the derivation back to the domain census.
