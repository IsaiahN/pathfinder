## QUESTION

Why do some early-universe and nearby-universe methods give different expansion-rate estimates?

Restated as an effect: why does the residual between a model-inferred expansion rate and a directly read expansion rate persist in two slots rather than trending to zero? R = |Gamma(b,a) - o'| is indexed per method, never averaged (Fig 1 :19). The anchor is the actual expansion rate, which does not change because a team re-analyses data (Fig 2 :29).

Option A: the difference is one cause, one lever. Leads to a single-culprit hunt that clears the region where one condition holds and leaves the intersection standing. Option B: the difference is a union of a channel condition and a closure condition, with the winner undecidable from inside either frame. Leads to two separate verdicts, a measured round-trip gap, an arbiter frame, and an open slot until the ground settles it. The verdict recorded below is Option B.

- **D3 Shared model closure.** Actors: six LCDM parameters and extensions. Currency: predicted expansion history. Anchor: fit to data, not an independent H0 ground. Admitted, flagged as possibly too small.
- **D4 Arbiter frame.** Actors: TRGB, masers, standard sirens, lensing, FRBs, BAO plus BBN. Currency: an H0 from a different closure. Anchor: the actual H0. Admitted, partly instantiated.
- **X1 Average the two values.** No nameable anchor and averaging is the named error (Fig 1 :22). Not a domain. Refused.
- **X2 One team blundered.** Absorbed into D2 channel maintenance (Fig 10 :24). Not a separate domain.
- **Union, not partition.** The residual lives in the overlap of C1 and C2 (Fig 9 :54, :57). Dependency order: calibration, then vocabulary, then objective, then arbiter, then carry (Fig 3 :19-20).
- **Overlap declared.** D1 and D2 draw on partly different evidence pools but both are reported through a shared model convention, so independence is partial, not full (Fig 2 :31). This constrains triangulation strength.

## VERDICT (from the figures)

The shape survives and the cause does not. The two expansion rates are two slots of one residual and must be reported separately; the disagreement is a union of a channel condition and a closure condition, not one hard question; from inside either frame the two are indiscernible, so an arbiter with a genuinely different closure is owed; and the round trip from acoustic scale to present expansion rate carries a gap that must be measured rather than assumed away. Which condition actually holds is a declared gap, and the framework returns a shape rather than a name. The routes are members of one functional group, not isomers as practised. Mode `specified`, ungrounded, candidate.

- **cost if wrong:** bounded and locatable. A wrong split would send an arbiter instrument at the wrong condition, but the packet states five falsifiers and a provenance trail, so the error is findable rather than invisible. The larger avoidable cost is spending an outside reader's independence by sending a preferred cause instead of a residual. Fig 2 :42 says "Buy independence in proportion to the cost of being wrong." That cost is avoidable by leading with the shape and its refuter.
- **undoable:** yes. No term is deleted if the claim fails. The figure rule is that a refutation adds a term and the reach falls because something was added, not because anything was struck out. The split, the falsifiers and the lineage are recorded so a later reader can locate the error. A superseding packet can replace this one without withdrawing any primitive.
- **reversed by:** a ground reading, not agreement. Any of the five pre-registered falsifiers: one correction closing both slots; a named extension confirmed by a different closure; a lossless acoustic-scale to H0 transform; zero independence between the frames; or a residual that trends to zero without a change of cause. Until one fires, the verdict stands as candidate.
