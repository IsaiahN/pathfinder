# Outreach draft: check the shape before you decide about the cause

**Use:** one to one, or as a cover letter attached to a submission through the public venues in `03_public_contact_points.csv`. **Not** for bulk send.

**Hard precondition:** do not send until the audit findings F1 to F10 in `01_figure_grounded_audit.md` are reflected in what you say. The stage-1 output must survive without the theory attached.

---

**Subject:** Check one claim about the Hubble tension before you decide what causes it

> Hi [name],
>
> I have one specific output about the Hubble tension I would like you to check against what you already know. I am not asking you to read a framework first, and I am not asking you to agree with a cause. If the output does not survive, there is nothing else to look at.
>
> **The output (`specified`, not `grounded`):** the reported expansion-rate values are two slots of one residual, not two estimates of one number to be averaged. The early slot infers H0 from the acoustic scale through a model; the nearby slot reads H0 from calibrated distances and redshifts. The split-and-sort rules I am using say two things. First, a disagreement that does not shrink with effort, where each rule keeps working well on its own subset, is several well-formed questions and must be split rather than searched as one. Second, the split here is a union of two conditions: a channel failure in a measurement frame, and a missing primitive in the shared model's closure. From inside either frame the two look the same, so no figure of the method can tell you which is actual. That part is a declared gap, not a hidden answer.
>
> **What I am not claiming:** that the tension is definitely a calibration error, or definitely new physics, or definitely early dark energy. The method returns a shape, not a name. I am also not claiming that the 5-sigma significance decides the cause. Significance and team count tell you how hard to look, not what you found. And I am not pooling agreement from teams using the same calibrator as independent corroboration.
>
> **What would refute the shape claim, written down before I sent this:**
> 1. One identified correction brings the early-inferred and nearby-read values into agreement within their combined uncertainties, with no categorically different residue left.
> 2. A specific extension, fitted to one frame, predicts the other frame's value and is confirmed by an instrument with a genuinely different closure.
> 3. The inference from acoustic scale to present expansion rate is shown to be lossless at the required precision, so there is no round-trip gap to explain.
> 4. The two frames are shown to draw on the same evidence pool in full, so there is only one frame here.
> 5. The residual trends down to zero under improved data without a change of cause.
>
> If any holds, I withdraw the shape. A one-line "this fails because..." is more useful to me than agreement. If you would rather not engage, no reply is needed.
>
> **What I would value from you:** not a verdict on the framework, but an answer to one question. What measurement would you count as the thing that distinguishes a drifted calibration from a changed early-universe sound horizon? If your answer differs from mine, that is the useful result.
>
> **If it survives, and only then:** the sources are below. The figures, not my summary of them, are the authority.
> - Planck 2018 cosmological parameters, arXiv:1807.06209 (the inferred 67.4 value)
> - SH0ES 2022 distance-ladder result, arXiv:2112.04510 (the directly read 73.04 value and its ~70 analysis variants)
> - TRGB independent calibration, arXiv:1907.05922 (a different calibrator, sitting between the two)
> - Kamionkowski and Riess, The Hubble Tension and Early Dark Energy, arXiv:2211.04492 (the current literature's preferred direction, offered as one pool only)
>
> **If you want to reuse the method:** run it on a problem you know well and can check against a ground. Write down what would refute your result *before* you run it, and state the mode of what you get. A reported failure is more useful to me than a reported success.
