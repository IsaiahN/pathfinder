# Outreach draft: check the output first, then decide about the theory

**Use:** one to one, or as a cover letter attached to a submission through the public venues in `03_public_contact_points.csv`. **Not** for bulk send.

**Hard precondition:** do not send until the shape claim in `01_figure_grounded_audit.md` §7 is stated in the form below and the refuter is attached. The output must survive validation or the two-stage offer collapses on contact.

---

**Subject:** Check one prediction about complex systems before you decide whether the framework is worth reading

> Hi [name],
>
> I have one specific output I would like you to check against what you already know. I am not asking you to read the theory first, and I am not asking you to agree with it. If the output does not survive, there is nothing else to look at.
>
> **The question in effect terms.** Which complex systems have stable ensemble-level statistics even though the single outcome cannot be predicted? My framework does not answer this with a list. It returns a shape, and it says a closed list is unavailable in principle.
>
> **The output (`general` on the shape, `specified` on the families).** Three conditions have to hold together:
> 1. the ensemble statistic is ranged against an external anchor, a counter or instrument that does not change its answer because of what any observer thinks;
> 2. there is a lossy cross-scale membrane between the unpredictable fine level and the stable coarse level, and the round trip between them has been taken with a bounded gap;
> 3. the regularity is a compressible description of the ensemble, not a restatement of every outcome.
>
> The framework's own line for why the question's two halves belong together: chaos and noise come from the same remainder, compounding at the fine scale and reappearing as noise above the crossing. Gas pressure from molecular chaos, the percentage from many bets, a radioactive half-life, climate bounds from chaotic weather, power-law statistics from critical avalanches: all share that motif. The framework does **not** name them as one substance. It calls the shared motif a functional group and leaves the specific bindings as claims from the science frame, with provenance, not as figure outputs.
>
> **The boundary claim, stated so it can fail.** The same shape says where the property breaks: distributions with no finite mean do not give a convergent average; a statistic that drifts as the system's structure changes is not an anchor; a forecast that changes the system it forecasts moves the ground; an average a model produces to score itself is not evidence.
>
> **What would refute this.**
> 1. A system with a stable, ground-anchored statistic and no fine/coarse membrane at all.
> 2. A system whose statistic stays settled while the instrument that returns it changes with the observer.
> 3. A heavy-tailed system with no finite mean that nevertheless has a convergent arithmetic mean.
> 4. A system that keeps a stable statistic after its forecast changes its own behaviour.
> 5. A finite, closed catalogue of such systems that admits new arrivals without remainder.
>
> I wrote these down before sending this. If any holds, the shape is wrong and I will retract it. A one-line "this fails because..." is more useful to me than agreement. If you would rather not engage, no reply is needed.
>
> **If it survives, and only then:** the sources are below. The figures are the authority, not my summary of them.
> - Figures text with line numbers, local extract: `FRAMEWORK/reference/03-FIGURES_TEXT.md` (in the package I can send)
> - Canonical figures README: https://github.com/IsaiahN/tether/blob/general-agent/figures/README.md
> - Public chaos entry, Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy: https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/chaos/
>
> **If you want to use the machinery:** the formula is in the figures link. Run it on a problem you know well and can check against a ground. Write down what would refute your result *before* you run it, and label the mode of what you get. A reported failure is more useful to me than a reported success.
>
> [your name]

## Distribution note

This is a conversation opener for one person at a time, addressed to a public submission channel or an opt-in contact. It is not a newsletter, not a campaign and not a request to forward. A reply that says "this fails because X" is a success. A reply that says "I agree" is recorded but carries no verdict weight, because by Fig 2 :40 agreement from a shared evidence pool is one pool talking.
