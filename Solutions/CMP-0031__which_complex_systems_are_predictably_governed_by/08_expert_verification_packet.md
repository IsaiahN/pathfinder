# Expert verification packet: the Tether on complex-systems prediction

**What this is:** a claim in a form a complexity researcher or nonlinear-dynamics specialist can check. Mode, derivation and refuters are stated before the conclusion.

**Mode: `general` on the shape, `specified` on the family bindings. Not `grounded`.** No measurement is claimed by this derivation. It is a shape and a route, not a settled classification.

## 1. The claim, in one paragraph

A complex system has a stable ensemble-level regularity while its individual outcomes are unpredictable only when three conditions hold together: the statistic is anchored to an external counter that does not move with the observer; a lossy cross-scale membrane connects the fine and coarse levels and the round trip between them has a bounded, measured gap; and the statistic is a compressible description that pays for itself. Chaos at the fine scale and noise at the coarse scale are one remainder seen from two sides of a crossing. Candidate families (many-body statistics, repeated trials, chaotic attractors, self-organized criticality) share a functional group, not an identity. A closed catalogue is unavailable in principle.

## 2. Three checkable sub-claims

### C1 - framework-internal, checkable by reading Figures 2 and 4

- **Statement:** a stable statistical regularity counts as a prediction only if it is anchored to something that does not update, and only if a cross-scale crossing with a measured round trip stands between the fine and coarse levels.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 2 :29 *"An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks. A measurement, an experiment, a counter, a market, a patient outcome."* Fig 2 :33 *"The anchor must not update."* Fig 4 :21 *"Not a wall: a filter with a direction."* Fig 4 :30 *"So take the round trip and measure the gap. Without it you are navigating by dead reckoning: confident about a position nobody checked."*
- **Refuter:** exhibit a system whose ensemble statistic remains a settled prediction while the instrument returning it changes with the observer, or while the discarded fine detail demonstrably re-enters and moves the coarse statistic.

### C2 - physics-facing, checkable against the chaos and criticality literature

- **Statement:** chaos and statistical noise are not independent phenomena; the fine-scale unpredictable remainder and the coarse-scale noise are two faces of one cross-scale loss. The four candidate families share a functional group (Fig 12 :124 *"the group says what kind; the qualifier says which member"*), and strange attractors realise the cycle reading (Fig 12 :121 *"The output re-enters as input. The sign of the loop decides whether it closes, and a chain cannot close by construction."*).
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 13 :32 *"a remainder compounding through a chain that amplifies is chaos, and the detail a crossing discards reappears above it as noise."*
- **What to check:** does the literature support finite predictability with stable ensemble statistics in chaotic systems? Does it support power-law stability with unpredictable individual events in SOC? The opened SEP entry states *"Hence, the idea that chaotic systems are unpredictable is a myth"* and gives ensemble forecasting; the opened Wikipedia chaos article gives the temperature-bounds example; the SOC article states *"there is no known set of general characteristics that guarantee a system will display SOC."*
- **Refuter:** exhibit a chaotic or critical system with no bounded invariant measure or no stable ensemble statistic, or a system with a stable statistic whose individual outcomes are also perfectly predictable (which falls outside the question's scope).

### C3 - framework-internal derivation about the answer's form

- **Statement:** a closed list is not the available answer form. The framework returns a shape, and a finite catalogue that admitted new systems without remainder is not obtainable.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 9 :32 *"you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself"*; Fig 8 :32 *"no search, however long, adds an atom"*; Fig 8 :39 *"a search that finds nothing is never a proof of absence."*; Fig 13 :115 *"The set is closed. The arrangements are not. That gap is why import exists."*
- **Refuter:** produce a finite, closed, non-defeasible catalogue of such systems with a rule that admits new arrivals without remainder.

## 3. What is explicitly not claimed

- Not a closed catalogue, and not a claim that any family list is complete.
- Not that the four families are one phenomenon. They share a functional group under Fig 12 :124.
- Not that chaos makes prediction impossible. Chaos bounds the horizon.
- Not that many trials guarantee a convergent average. Heavy tails with no finite mean fail.
- Not that SOC is guaranteed. The opened source says no general set of characteristics guarantees it.
- Not `grounded`. Nothing in this packet is asserted as settled by experiment.
- Not that the framework named the families. They are bindings from the science frame with provenance (Fig 8 :51-52).

## 5. What would settle it

1. An independent counter returning the statistic and not moving with the observer (anchor test).
2. A measured `R_T` with a bounded gap after carrying a coarse description up and back down (round-trip test).
3. A convergence check for the distribution: finite mean versus heavy tail.
4. A reflexivity test: does the statistic survive publication of the forecast?
5. The seven refuters in `01_figure_grounded_audit.md` §4, written before the conclusion.

## 6. What an expert can check in twenty minutes

1. Read Fig 2 :29 and :33, Fig 4 :21 and :30, Fig 13 :32, Fig 9 :32 and Fig 12 :124. Six short passages.
2. Check C2 against your own field: is the chaos or criticality example faithful? Do the bounds and power laws hold while single events stay unpredictable?
3. Name one instrument that would return the ensemble statistic independently of the observer, or say why none exists for your system.
4. Try the heavy-tail case: does the mean converge for the distribution you actually have?
5. Send the refutation. A failure is more useful than an endorsement.
