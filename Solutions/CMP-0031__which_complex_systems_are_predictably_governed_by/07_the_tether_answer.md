# The Tether's answer: which complex systems are predictably governed?

**Mode: `general` on the three-condition shape, `specified` on the candidate families and boundary failures. Not `grounded`.** Simulated derivation checked for internal consistency against the figures, not a ground reading (`05_what_shape_means.md` §7).

## 1. The residual it answers

The question appears to ask for a list. Stated in effect terms it asks: what must be true of a system and of our contact with it for an ensemble-level regularity to be predictable while the single outcome is not? A closed list is unavailable in principle (Fig 13 :115 *"The set is closed. The arrangements are not. That gap is why import exists."*; Fig 9 :32 *"you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself"*). What is deliverable is a shape, candidate families with provenance, boundary failures and written refuters.

## 2. The shape (`general`)

1. **An anchor that does not update.** The ensemble statistic must be ranged against an external counter, measurement or dataset. Fig 2 :29 *"An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks. A measurement, an experiment, a counter, a market, a patient outcome."* Fig 2 :33 *"The anchor must not update."* An average the frame produces to score itself is disqualified (Fig 1 :27 *"They are frame-internal. A frame cannot score itself with a quantity it also produces."*).
2. **A lossy cross-scale membrane with a measured, bounded round trip.** Fig 4 :21 *"Not a wall: a filter with a direction."* Fig 4 :30 *"So take the round trip and measure the gap. Without it you are navigating by dead reckoning: confident about a position nobody checked."* The fine detail discarded going up must not re-enter and move the coarse statistic.
3. **A large, compressible gap that pays for itself.** Fig 5 :18 *"seeks the gap that is large and compressible"*; Fig 5 :43 *"A new term is worth having when stating it, plus what remains unexplained after it, costs less than what was unexplained before."*

The reason the question's two halves belong together is Fig 13 :32: **"a remainder compounding through a chain that amplifies is chaos, and the detail a crossing discards reappears above it as noise."** Chaos at the fine scale and statistical regularity at the coarse scale are one remainder seen from two sides of a crossing. The property is therefore not monadic: it belongs to a system **and the scale at which it is ranged** (Fig 11 :39 *"capability is a property of agent-and-habitat, never of the agent alone"*).

The bundle is a **union**, not a single question (Fig 9 :54 *"And when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again."*). Each condition gets its own loop.

## 3. Candidate families (`specified`, ungrounded, provenance in `10_source_ledger.csv`)

These are bindings from the science frame onto the shape, not figure outputs (Fig 8 :51-52 *"Convergent derivation and adopted import look identical in the contents. Only the record of where each came from separates them"*).

| family | fine level, unpredictable | coarse level, stable | anchor |
|---|---|---|---|
| Many-body statistical systems (gas, diffusion) | molecular trajectories, single-molecule motion | pressure, temperature, Fick's-law diffusion | manometer, thermometer, concentration measurement |
| Repeated i.i.d. or exchangeable trials (coin, die, casino, radioactive decay) | single outcome, single decay time | relative frequency, expected value, half-life | ledger, counter, detector |
| Deterministic chaotic systems with an attractor (weather, Lorenz, double pendulum) | trajectory beyond the Lyapunov horizon | climate bounds, invariant measure, ensemble forecast probability | observations, fitted exponents, ensemble runs |

Under Fig 12 :124 these share a **functional group**, not an identity: *"the group says what kind; the qualifier says which member."* They are not claimed to be isomers, because their atoms differ. Where a bounded, folding loop re-enters its own input, Fig 12 :121 *"cycle. The output re-enters as input. The sign of the loop decides whether it closes"* licenses the attractor reading.

## 4. Boundary failures (`specified`)

The same shape excludes cases that a flat answer would wrongly include:

- **No finite mean.** For Cauchy distributions and Pareto distributions with exponent below 1, the opened law-of-large-numbers source reports that the sample average does not converge. Many trials are not sufficient.
- **Structural drift.** If the ensemble itself changes while being measured, the statistic moves with the system and is not an anchor (Fig 2 :33).
- **Reflexivity.** If a forecast changes the behaviour it forecasts, the ground moves. This is source-question 34 in the same section of complexity science.txt.
- **Self-scoring averages.** A regularity that only a model's own outputs produce is frame-internal (Fig 1 :27).
- **Unbounded round trip.** If the discarded detail re-enters and changes the coarse statistic, `R_T` is not bounded and the "stability" is an artefact (Fig 4 :30).

## 5. What it explicitly does not say

- Not a closed catalogue. No family list is complete or final.
- Not that all four families are the same phenomenon. They share a functional group.
- Not that chaos makes prediction impossible. SEP, *Chaos*: *"Hence, the idea that chaotic systems are unpredictable is a myth."* It bounds the horizon and changes what can be predicted.
- Not that many trials guarantee a stable mean. The heavy-tail boundary is real.
- Not that SOC is guaranteed. The opened source states there is *"no known set of general characteristics that guarantee a system will display SOC."*
- Not grounded. No measurement was taken in this derivation.

## 6. What would settle it (the ground)

A source-discriminating instrument and a measured round trip, not a further argument. Following *The Hard Problem is About Source*: an instrument that reads green *"cannot comment on the source"*; the spectrometer resolved it. Here:

1. **Anchor test:** an independent counter that returns the ensemble statistic and does not move when the observing frame changes.
2. **Round-trip test:** carry a coarse description up, bring it down, measure `R_T`. A bounded, stable gap supports membership; an unbounded one removes it.
3. **Convergence test:** does the average converge for this distribution? A heavy tail with no finite mean fails.
4. **Reflexivity test:** does the statistic survive an intervention in which the forecast is published and the system responds?
5. **Standing refuters from `01` §4:** a stable statistic with no membrane; a moving anchor; a self-scoring average; a convergent heavy-tailed mean; a closed catalogue.

## 8. One-paragraph version for outreach

> The Tether does not give a closed list of complex systems. It gives the shape any answer must have. A stable ensemble regularity is predictable only when three things hold: an external counter anchors it and does not move with the observer; a lossy crossing connects an unpredictable fine scale to a stable coarse scale and the round trip between them has a bounded, measured gap; and the regularity is a compressible description that pays for itself. Chaos at the fine scale and noise at the coarse scale are one remainder seen from two sides of the crossing. Gas pressure, casino percentages, radioactive half-life, climate bounds and critical power laws are candidate families, held as bindings from science with provenance, not as figure outputs. The boundary failures are just as important: no finite mean, a drifting structure, reflexivity, a self-scoring average. What is offered is a checkable shape, refuters written in advance, and the instruments that would settle any particular membership. The list stays open until the ground decides.
