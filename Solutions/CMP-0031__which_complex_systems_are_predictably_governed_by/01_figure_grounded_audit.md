## 0. The residual, stated in effect terms

The question asks for a selection: among complex systems, which ones have the property that a stable statistical regularity governs their behaviour even though the individual outcomes are not predictable.

State the two options and what each leads to.

- **Option A, treat "which" as a catalogue.** Enumerate systems and sort them into yes/no. This leads to an unbounded search inside a closed set: Fig 13 :115 *"The set is closed. The arrangements are not. That gap is why import exists."* and Fig 13 :117-118 *"a frame can search without end and still not arrive: infinite effort inside a finite reach."* A list is always defeasible by the next system, and the list's agreement with itself is frame-internal.
- **Option B, treat "which" as a shape.** Characterise the structural conditions under which the property holds, name candidate families as `specified` bindings with provenance, state the boundary failures, and write down what would settle any particular membership. This leads to a checkable claim with a measured round-trip gap and refuters.

Option B is chosen, and the choice is itself figure-grounded (F9). The residual is not "give me the list". It is: **what must be true of a system and of our contact with it for an ensemble-level regularity to be predictable while the single-outcome level is not?**

## 1. Domain census (before choosing any figure line)

### 1.1 R by arity, symmetry, scale

- **Arity:** two levels in contact, a fine level (individual outcomes, trajectories, molecules, trials) and a coarse level (ensemble statistic, invariant measure, distribution). Plus a membrane between them. The property is a relation, not a monadic property of one level.
- **Symmetry:** asymmetric and lossy. Going up discards detail; going down cannot restore it. Fig 4 :21 *"Not a wall: a filter with a direction."* Fig 4 :27 *"The transform between scales loses information, and that is the point."*
- **Scale:** fine scale is chaotic or stochastic; coarse scale is stable. The two are not the same object at two resolutions, because the coarse statistic is a compressed, ground-anchored quantity.

**Per-slot caution, not an aggregation licence.** Fig 1 :19 *"The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated."* Fig 1 :22 *"Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average."* The question's "stable statistical regularity" is an aggregate, so it is vulnerable to exactly this warning: an average a frame produces to score itself is not a ground. This becomes finding F3.

### 1.2 One line per domain: actors, currency, anchor

| domain | actors | currency | anchor | question type |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| D1 Fine and coarse physical/statistical frame | individual elements (molecules, atoms, agents, trials, trajectories) and the ensemble statistic | prediction versus observation, counts and averages | an external instrument, counter, dataset or experiment | union of two conditions, not one hard question |
| D2 Cross-scale membrane | the lossy transform up (`T_A`) and down (`T_E`), the round trip | what crosses up (a method or compressed regularity) and down (a head start) | the round-trip gap `R_T` | partition boundary (what may cross) |
| D3 Verification and outreach frame | expert readers, instruments, the residual | agreement and residual | the ground, never agreement | independence question |

A domain needs a nameable anchor. D1 has an instrument, D2 has `R_T`, D3 has the ground. No domain is admitted on actors alone.

### 1.3 Partition or union

The property in the question is a **union**, not a partition: it requires *both* (i) unpredictable individual outcomes and (ii) a stable ensemble regularity. Fig 9 :54 *"And when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again."* A case in two conditions is not a harder case of either. Each condition gets its own loop, and the compound gets its own ground. This is finding F2.

### 1.4 Order by dependency (Fig 3)

1. Anchor the ensemble statistic to a ground that does not move (Fig 2 :29, :33). Without this, there is nothing to predict.
2. Establish the membrane and measure the round trip `R_T` (Fig 4 :30). This decides whether the coarse statistic is a stable compressed regularity or a drifting description.
3. Describe the residual before choosing a frame (Fig 9 :27-28). Otherwise any field seems to fit.
4. Refuse the catalogue route before minting further terms (Formula step 2; Fig 13 :115-118).
5. Use filters for the budget and witnesses for the verdict (Fig 9 :60).

Effort spent at step 4 cannot rescue a missing step 1 (Fig 3 :19 *"Optimising a link that already works cannot rescue one that does not."*).

## 3. Findings

### F1 - The stable regularity must be anchored to something that does not update (decisive, `general`)

Fig 2 :29:

> **"An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks. A measurement, an experiment, a counter, a market, a patient outcome."**

Fig 2 :33: **"The anchor must not update."**

A "stable statistical regularity" is only a prediction if it ranges against an external counter: a gas pressure reading, a half-life measured by a detector, a Lyapunov exponent estimated from data, a Gutenberg-Richter slope fitted to a catalogue. If the statistic moves when the observing frame changes, it is a frame, not an anchor, and Fig 2 :35 applies: *"Alignment is triangulation, not negotiation. A mirror cannot correct you."*

This already divides the question's cases. A casino percentage (law of large numbers) is anchored to a ledger. A "regularity" that is only the average of a model's own outputs is not.

**Triangulation:** Fig 2 (anchor) plus Fig 1 :23 *"The ground enters as the only metric."* Two figures, low overlap (one is about frames and grounds, one is about what a loop may score). Verdict: `triangulated`.

**Refuter:** exhibit a system whose ensemble statistic is accepted as a settled prediction while no external instrument, counter or dataset can move it.

### F2 - The property is a union compound, not one question (`general`)

Fig 9 :54: **"And when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again."**

Two conditions are bundled in "even when individual outcomes are unpredictable":

1. the fine scale resists prediction (chaos or intrinsic stochasticity);
2. the coarse scale has a stable, ground-anchored statistic.

A single lever clears the region where only one holds. For example, self-averaging alone (condition 2) is enough for a casino ledger even when the individual outcome is merely unknown rather than chaotic; and chaos alone (condition 1) is not enough when no invariant measure with finite statistics exists (heavy-tailed cases, see WORLD). Fig 9 :57-58: *"a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either. It stops being a mixture and becomes a specific compound."*

**Triangulation:** Fig 9 (union/partition) plus the world source on the law of large numbers, which separates stable averages from the heavy-tail failure. Low overlap. Verdict: `triangulated` for the compound reading; `single-source` (Fig 9) for the framework's instruction to unbundle.

**Refuter:** show that one condition entail the other in all cases (then it is a partition and unbundling is unnecessary).

### F3 - The average is not automatically a ground (`general`)

Fig 1 :19 and :22 (quoted in §1.1). Fig 1 :27:

> **"They are frame-internal. A frame cannot score itself with a quantity it also produces."**

The question's phrase "stable statistical regularities" invites a self-scoring error. A frame that generates outcomes and then reports their average as its own validation is using a quantity it also produces. The regularity counts only when a separate instrument returns it. This is also the source-discrimination point in *The Hard Problem is About Source* (opened 2026-11-22): *"If several paths create the same behavior, then behavior is a projection with lossy information"*, and *"An instrument that can't distinguish these cases demonstrates its own inadequacy"*.

**Verdict:** `single-source` within the figures (Fig 1), corroborated by an essay with high author overlap. Do not count the essay as a second witness.

**Refuter:** show a regularity that settles a question with no instrument independent of the frame that produced the outcomes.

### F4 - The cross-scale membrane and the round-trip gap decide stability (`general`)

Fig 4 :22 **"Only methods cross up. Only head starts come down."** Fig 4 :30:

> **"So take the round trip and measure the gap. Without it you are navigating by dead reckoning: confident about a position nobody checked."**

The fine-level detail is discarded going up; that is what makes the coarse description compressible. But the coarse description must come back down and be compared. `R_T = gap(x, (T_E ∘ T_A)(x))` is nonzero always; the question is whether it is bounded and measured. Where the discarded detail feeds back and moves the coarse statistic (non-ergodicity, structural drift, reflexivity where the forecast changes the system), the "stable regularity" is an artefact of a stopped clock.

This is the single most useful structural test for "which systems": not whether a regularity was observed, but whether it survives the round trip with a bounded gap.

**Triangulation:** Fig 4 (round trip) plus Fig 2 :29 (anchor must not move). Low overlap. Verdict: `triangulated`.

**Refuter:** exhibit a system whose coarse statistic remains a valid prediction while `R_T` is unbounded or unmeasured and the fine detail demonstrably re-enters the coarse level.

### F5 - The regularity must be a large, compressible gap (`general`)

Fig 5 :18 **"seeks the gap that is large and compressible"** and Fig 5 :43:

> **"A new term is worth having when stating it, plus what remains unexplained after it, costs less than what was unexplained before."**

A stable statistical regularity is a compressed description of an ensemble: a mean, a distribution, a power law, an invariant measure. Under the MDL bargain it pays for itself if it explains the ensemble more cheaply than the outcomes it leaves unexplained. A "regularity" that merely restates every outcome fails the bargain (Fig 5 :44 *"a term that explains one occasion perfectly"* fails).

The world source on self-organized criticality supplies the boundary: *"there is no known set of general characteristics that guarantee a system will display SOC."* So power-law families are candidate regularities, not guaranteed ones.

**Verdict:** `single-source` for the figure's MDL rule (Fig 5); the SOC boundary is a world corroboration with low overlap (different field, different evidence pool).

**Refuter:** exhibit a stable ensemble regularity whose description costs more than the sequence of individual outcomes it purports to summarise.

### F6 - The property belongs to system-and-scale, not to the system alone (`general`)

Fig 13 :32 (the decisive generative line):

> **"a remainder compounding through a chain that amplifies is chaos, and the detail a crossing discards reappears above it as noise."**

Fig 11 :39: **"capability is a property of agent-and-habitat, never of the agent alone."**

Chaos and statistical noise are not two different phenomena needing two explanations. In the figures they are the two faces of one remainder: at the fine level it compounds through an amplifying chain (unpredictable individual outcomes); at the crossing it is discarded and reappears above as noise (the stable statistical description). So asking "which systems" is incomplete: the property is a relation between a system and the scale at which it is ranging. A gas is unpredictable at molecule scale and predictable at pressure scale; the same gas is not predictable at molecule scale.

Fig 13 :74 *"Everything else is a composition across the two kinds, never within one."* and :75 *"Two states never bond directly. It is not space-time but space, a structure, time; and two structures join only through a state."* govern what may sit in the slot between fine and coarse. They constrain the structure of a candidate theory of the property; they do not name which structure (Fig 9 :32 *"you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself"*).

**Triangulation:** Fig 13 (chaos/noise remainder) plus Fig 11 (capability is agent-and-habitat). Low overlap. Verdict: `triangulated`.

**Refuter:** exhibit a system that has the property at every scale, or at none, independent of a chosen crossing.

### F7 - Families share a functional group; they are not one substance (`specified`)

Fig 12 :124 **"functional group. A substructure whose presence fixes the outcome. The group says what kind; the qualifier says which member."** Fig 12 :117 **"isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."** Fig 12 :121 **"cycle. The output re-enters as input. The sign of the loop decides whether it closes, and a chain cannot close by construction."**

Gas pressure, casino percentages, radioactive half-life, Lorenz climate bounds and SOC power laws all share the motif *fine unpredictable → crossing → coarse predictable*. By Fig 12 :124 that is a **functional group**, not an identity: the atoms differ (molecules, bets, nuclei, trajectories, avalanches). Calling them all "the same phenomenon" is the flat-formula error (Fig 12 :14 *"the ingredients are recorded and the arrangement is not"* is about notation; here the error is to record only the motif).

Where two families share a full atom set and differ only in bond, the stronger **isomer** reading applies and is testable. Where a system's output re-enters as input with a closing sign (a strange attractor's folding), Fig 12 :121 licenses the cycle reading. Details in `06_isomers_and_order.md`.

**Verdict:** `single-source` (Fig 12) for the structural distinction; the family classifications are `specified` bindings from the science frame.

**Refuter:** show two families with the same fine/coarse atoms and the same bond yet different statistical behaviour, which collapses the isomer distinction.

### F8 - The two-stage offer follows from verifier non-access (`general`, outreach)

Fig 10 :42 **"A verifier that can reconstruct the claim cannot verify it."** Fig 10 :52 **"Bring a characterised residual, never the source."** Fig 2 :35 quoted in F1.

Send the shape claim and its refuter first. Ask what the recipient would count as a failure. Record that verdict before showing any theory. Their later agreement is never pooled as corroboration (Fig 2 :40). Fig 2 :42 **"Buy independence in proportion to the cost of being wrong."** The cost here is moderate: a misapplied classification can attribute stability to a drifting system. So independence is worth buying, but not at the price of a mass send.

**Verdict:** `triangulated` (Fig 10 plus Fig 2).

**Refuter:** show a case where sending the full framework first improves, rather than destroys, the check.

### F9 - The framework returns a shape, so a closed list is refused (`general`, decisive for the question's form)

Fig 9 :32: **"you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself"**. Fig 8 :32 **"no search, however long, adds an atom"**. Fig 8 :39 **"a search that finds nothing is never a proof of absence."** Fig 13 :115 and :117-118 quoted in §0.

A complete, closed catalogue of "which complex systems" would be a terminal answer with no outside left. The figures forbid that room: the set of kinds is closed and the arrangements are not, so search never arrives. What is deliverable is a kind of thing, three necessary conditions, named candidate families with provenance, and boundary failures. This is `unreachable as a list`, which is a verdict and not a silence.

**Triangulation:** Fig 9 (shape), Fig 8 (no search adds an atom), Fig 13 (closed set, unbounded arrangements). Three figures with low overlap. Verdict: `triangulated`.

**Refuter:** produce a finite, closed, non-defeasible catalogue with a rule that admits new systems without remainder.

## 4. Pre-registered refuter (written before the conclusion)

Required before the derivation is relied on. The shape claim is refuted if any of the following holds:

1. **No membrane needed.** A system has a stable, ground-anchored ensemble statistic with no fine/coarse lossy crossing, so the membrane condition (F4, Fig 4 :21, :27) is not necessary.
2. **The anchor can move.** A system's coarse statistic remains a settled prediction while the instrument that returns it changes its answer when frames change (breaks F1, Fig 2 :29, :33).
3. **The average self-scores.** A regularity settles a question with no instrument independent of the frame that produced the outcomes (breaks F3, Fig 1 :27).
4. **The heavy-tail boundary fails.** A distribution with no finite mean (Cauchy, Pareto with exponent below 1) is shown to have a convergent, stable arithmetic mean over arbitrary samples (breaks the WORLD boundary from the law of large numbers).
5. **Reflexive stability.** A system whose forecasts alter its own behaviour retains a stable statistic after the alteration (breaks F1 and F4 at the ground).
6. **Unbounded round trip.** `R_T` is measured as unbounded while the coarse statistic is still used as a stable prediction (breaks F4).
7. **A closed list exists.** A finite catalogue is produced that is closed under new arrivals (breaks F9, Fig 13 :115-118).

If any fires, the shape is wrong and the claim is withdrawn. Until one is run, no part of this may be labelled `grounded`.

Note on writing order: this refuter section was drafted before §5 and before `07_the_tether_answer.md`. That order is the design instrument the figures require.
