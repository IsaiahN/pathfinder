## VERDICT (from the figures)

The shape survives; the closed list does not. A system has the property in question only when an external anchor grounds the ensemble statistic, a lossy cross-scale membrane separates fine unpredictability from coarse stability with a bounded measured round trip, and the statistic is a compressible description that pays for itself. Chaos at the fine scale and noise at the coarse scale are one remainder at two scales. Gas, repeated trials, radioactive decay, chaotic attractors and critical systems are candidate families held as `specified` bindings with provenance, not as figure outputs; they form a functional group, not an identity. The boundary failures are heavy tails without a finite mean, structural drift, reflexivity and self-scoring averages. This is a `general`/`specified`, ungrounded result. The strongest single figure line is Fig 13 :32; the strongest limit is Fig 9 :32.

- **cost if wrong:** bounded and locatable. The refuters are explicit, so a misapplied classification is findable. The real cost is spending the independence of outside readers by sending a list instead of a shape, which Fig 2 :42 prices: "Buy independence in proportion to the cost of being wrong." That cost is avoidable by leading with the shape and its refuter.
- **undoable:** yes. The family bindings can be withdrawn or replaced without touching the three-condition shape. No primitive must be recalled, and a sent packet can be superseded by a corrected one.
- **reversed by:** a ground reading, not agreement. An independent counter that returns the statistic, a measured `R_T` that proves unbounded, a convergent mean where a heavy tail was claimed, a statistic that survives the publication of its own forecast, or a derivation of a stable regularity with no membrane would move the verdict. A finite, closed catalogue would break the shape itself. Until one of these fires, the verdict stands as `candidate`.
