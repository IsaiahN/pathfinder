## QUESTION

Decide, in effect terms, what a science of intelligence should use to settle what machines ought to do, given that it can already settle much of what they can learn to do. Option A: treat the "ought" as a harder capability problem and optimize the objective harder. Option B: treat the "ought" as a missing instrument at the objective link, build it by binding the sought-for shape as an ordinary term, name a non-updating anchor per domain, keep verdicts per slot, keep specifications as refusable conventions, and report the specific content as a stated gap. What each leads to: A spends budget above the break where the objective link stays assumed and a measure becomes a target; B produces a checkable shape, six written refuters and an open content question that a ground could close.

## DOMAIN CENSUS

R by arity, symmetry and scale, then one line per domain with actors, currency and anchor, partition or union, and order by dependency.

- **R (the residual).** The gap between a well-instrumented capability science and an uninstrumented objective link. Arity: at least three conditions plus a checking frame, so a union compound (Fig 9 :54 *"And when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again."*), not one question with opposite answers. Symmetry: a capability claim is a ranking ("more capable than") that does not choose an arrow, while an "ought" is ordered, using comparison for a goal, subtraction for progress and negation for refusal (Operators table :51, :46, :58). Scale: link 3 inside one agent, selection between rooms, and per-slot values across a population.
- **Domain 1, capability science.** Actors: learner, habitat, benchmark, instrument. Currency: ground-paid prediction accuracy; Fig 1 :23 *"The ground enters as the only metric."* Anchor: a measurement that does not move when the model reports (Fig 2 :29). Question type: partition boundary, can or cannot under a test. Order: link 5, downstream of the objective.
- **Domain 2, objective and direction.** Actors: the frame, the sought-for shape, the bound ordinary term. Currency: a residual the shape cannot accrue until it is bound (Fig 5 :62-66). Anchor: none until binding, then the ground. Question type: union with D1, a different quantity, not a harder capability. Order: link 3, upstream of planning and learning.
- **Domain 3, normative pluralism.** Actors: people, stakeholders, value frames, conventions. Currency: agreement as convention versus ground contact as verdict (Fig 2 :40-41). Anchor: human judgment for person-relative questions (Fig 2 :37), indexed per slot (Fig 1 :19). Question type: union of overlapping conditions, no standing list (Fig 11 :51). Order: alongside D2, supplying its anchor only where the question is about people.
- **Domain 4, verification and governance.** Actors: ground maintainer, outside frame, reward channel, convention. Currency: independence (Fig 2 :31-32). Anchor: the ground, never agreement (Fig 2 :45). Question type: a seat, not a content domain. Order: must exist before D2 can be settled.

Order by dependency: perception, vocabulary, objective, planning, learn and carry (Fig 3 :20). Refuse before minting (Formula step 2). Describe the residual before choosing a frame (Fig 8 :53).

Strain test applied: an early reading used Fig 11 :36 to treat values as retrospective labels, which strained against Fig 5 :65-66. Re-census: Fig 11 governs capability and contact; Fig 5 governs direction. Different objects, no strain after re-census. This is recorded, not hidden.

## VERDICT (from the figures)

The science should decide by building the missing objective instrument and applying a four-part procedure: bind the sought-for "ought" as an ordinary term before the run; name a non-updating anchor per domain; keep verdicts per slot; keep specifications as checkable, refusable conventions. "Can" and "ought" are isomers over a fixed vocabulary, and the objective sits upstream of learning at link 3. The specific content of an "ought" is a `gap`, reported as unreachable under present instruments, not silence and not an invented name. Every claim is `specified` or `general`, none `grounded`. A simulated derivation cannot settle what a science ought to decide; only an external ground can.

- **cost if wrong:** bounded and locatable. The packet states mode, sub-claims and refuters before the conclusion, so an error is findable. The larger cost is spending the independence of outside checkers by sending a fully packaged theory before their stage-1 verdict, which Fig 2 :42 prices: *"Buy independence in proportion to the cost of being wrong."* That cost is avoidable by leading with the shape and its refuters.
- **undoable:** yes. The procedure is separable from any particular value content. A defective specification can be withdrawn without touching the four parts, and no primitive has to be retracted. What cannot be undone cheaply is a channel whose ground has been moved by the system; that is why the anchor rule comes before optimisation.
- **reversed by:** a ground reading, not agreement. A source-discriminating instrument showing which candidate objective a behaviour actually serves would close the content gap; a scalar aggregation preserving every live per-slot residual (Fig 1 :19), a derivation of an objective inside a capability closure without import (Fig 6 :21), or an agent-only improvement that moves the ground would reverse parts of the shape. Prestige or majority agreement is not such an instrument.
