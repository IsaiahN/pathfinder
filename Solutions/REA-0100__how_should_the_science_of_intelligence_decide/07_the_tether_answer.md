# The Tether's answer: how the science of intelligence should decide an "ought"

**Mode: `specified` on the four-part procedure and on every per-domain verdict, `general` on the structural claim, `gap` on the specific content of an "ought". Not `grounded`.** This is a shape and a route, not a result. Simulated derivation checked against the figures (`05_what_shape_means.md` section 7), not a ground reading.

## 1. The residual it answers

The question contrasts two things a science can produce: a claim about what a machine can learn to do, and a decision about what it ought to do. These are not two answers to one question. They are different effects with different anchors, and the second sits at a link that currently has no instrument. Fig 3 :25-26: *"Perception has obvious failures and obvious tests. Planning has obvious failures and obvious tests. The link between them, having terms for what a situation offers and being able to say what would count as progress, has neither, so it attracts the least attention and the fewest instruments."* Fig 3 :27: *"A link with no instrument is not a link that works. It is a link nobody has looked at."*

The residual is therefore a missing instrument plus a missing direction discipline, not a shortage of capability or data.

## 2. The shape (four parts)

1. **Make the "ought" bindable before trying to use it.** Fig 5 :65-66: *"The remedy is a discipline rather than a branch: make the sought-for shape an ORDINARY TERM. Write down what an answer would look like, bind it, and let it accrue a residual like anything else. Then the preference is refusable by the same test as everything else. Committing a prediction before a run is this, already working."* A loop can rank its held terms but cannot check the direction of its own question, so the direction must be made a term first.
2. **Name a non-updating anchor per domain, not one anchor for everything.** Fig 2 :36: *"An anchor is legitimate when the question is constitutively about it."* Fig 2 :37: *"Human judgment is the right anchor for is this a good answer for a person, because the question is about people. It is only an instrument for is this code correct, and instruments can be wrong on axes they do not measure."* For person-relative questions, human judgment is the anchor. For correctness questions, the anchor is the measurement, and human judgment is a fallible instrument. Anything the machine can influence is not an anchor (Fig 2 :29, :33).
3. **Keep verdicts per slot and never aggregate them into one scalar.** Fig 1 :19: *"The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated."* Fig 11 :49: *"An actor's valence is relative to the residual, not a property it carries."* Fig 11 :51: *"No standing list of good actors survives a change of residual."* A universal utility function is a standing list in another notation.
4. **Keep specifications as checkable, refusable conventions.** Fig 10 :47: *"a convention that decides an outcome has stopped being one"*. Fig 10 :49: *"Declaring a meaning does not ground it."* A reward function or charter is a convention; when it is promoted to fix an outcome with no check above it, it becomes an authored constant carrying its author's authority. Declare the author, keep the seat outside the frame it grades (Fig 10 :42), and declare unavoidable stakes (Fig 10 :60).

Supporting rules that travel with the four parts:

- **"Can" and "ought" are isomers over a fixed vocabulary.** Same atoms, different bonds, different substances (Fig 12 :117). A capability vocabulary has sequence and production; an "ought" vocabulary additionally needs comparison for a goal, subtraction for progress, and negation for refusal (Operators table :51, :46, :58).
- **The objective is upstream of learning.** It is link 3, not link 5 (Fig 3 :20). More optimisation at link 5 cannot rescue a broken link 3 (Fig 3 :19).
- **Capability is agent-and-habitat.** Fig 11 :39: *"capability is a property of agent-and-habitat, never of the agent alone."* A harness proves wiring, not capability (Fig 11 :70). Enumerate the habitat rather than tuning the model in isolation.
- **Buy independence where being wrong is costly.** Fig 2 :42: *"Buy independence in proportion to the cost of being wrong."* Fig 7 :35: *"A room's agreement is variation, not a verdict. Selection between rooms is where the ground votes."* One feedback pool is one frame, however many labels it produces.
- **Imports need provenance and a shadow test.** Fig 8 :53-54: *"Residual first, frame second."* Describe the gap in effect terms, then find the frame whose closure predicts its shape.

## 3. What it explicitly does not say

- No utility function, moral theory, principle list, number, or standing set of "good" behaviours. The specific content is a declared `gap`, and "unreachable is a verdict, not a silence" (Fig 13 :134).
- Not that human preference is always the anchor. Fig 2 :37 makes it domain-relative.
- Not that reward functions are atoms or that more reward optimisation closes the objective. Closure is idempotent (Fig 8 :32) and *"MINT composes inside the closure and can never add an atom"* (Fig 6 :21).
- Not that a benchmark score is a verdict. Fig 1 :27: *"They are frame-internal. A frame cannot score itself with a quantity it also produces."*
- Not `grounded`. No measurement discriminates among candidate objectives at the required level.
- Not a theory of everything. There is no last room; each handoff closes part of the gap and no finite number arrives (Fig 7).

## 4. What would settle it, and what is owed

A source-discriminating instrument, not a further argument. A reading that can be produced by several sources does not identify its source. The questions that would close the gap:

1. **Instrument question:** what measurement would distinguish an objective that the system pursues from a specification it has learned to game or manipulate? A score cannot answer this; a spectrometer-style discriminator is owed.
2. **Convention question:** which seat can refuse a specification, and on what evidence, without being able to author it (Fig 10 :42-49)?
3. **Anchor question:** for each slot, does the ground move when the machine reports? If yes, it is a channel, not an anchor (Fig 2 :29-34).
4. **Standing falsifiers:** derive an objective from capability terms inside one closure without import; produce a scalar aggregation preserving every live per-slot residual; produce an agent-only improvement that changes contact; show a convention fixing outcomes while remaining checkable.

Until one of these fires, the four-part procedure is `candidate`, not accepted.

## 5. The honest stop

The four parts are a shape a decision procedure must have, not the decision itself. Fig 9 :32: *"you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself"*. Fig 13 :135: *"It cannot say which primitive is missing, because naming it would mean already holding it."* Stop at the limit of the available instruments and record the lineage, because indiscernibility is not identity. A candidate that matches the shape under present instruments may still be wrong. What remains open is stated, not filled.

## 6. One-paragraph version for outreach

> A science of intelligence cannot settle "what machines ought to do" by measuring harder what they can learn to do. The two are different effects: capability is agent-and-habitat contact against a ground, while an "ought" is a direction that the loop cannot see until it is made an ordinary term. So the decision procedure has four parts. Write down the sought-for shape before the run and let it be refusable. Name a non-updating anchor per domain, human judgment where the question is about people and an instrument where the question is correctness. Keep verdicts per slot, never aggregated into one scalar. Keep specifications as checkable conventions, declared and refusable, because a convention that decides an outcome has stopped being one. This is a `specified` shape, not a moral theory, not a grounded verdict, and not a universal "ought". The specific content stays a stated gap until a source-discriminating instrument settles it. Six refuters were written in advance, and a reported failure is more useful than an endorsement.
