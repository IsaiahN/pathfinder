## 1. The claim, in one paragraph

The science of intelligence should decide "what machines ought to do" by treating the objective as a missing instrument at link 3 of the dependency chain, not as more optimisation at link 5. Four parts survive: make the sought-for "ought" an ordinary bindable term so it can accrue a residual and be refused; name a non-updating anchor per domain, human judgment where the question is constitutively about people and a measurement where the question is correctness; keep verdicts per slot and never aggregate them into one scalar; keep specifications as checkable, refusable conventions because a convention that decides an outcome has stopped being one. Over a fixed vocabulary, "can" and "ought" are isomers, same atoms, different bonds, different substance. The specific content of an "ought" is a stated gap until a source-discriminating instrument settles it.

## 2. Three checkable sub-claims

### C1 — framework-internal; checkable by reading Figure 3 and Figure 5

- **Statement:** the objective is link 3, upstream of planning and learning, and it currently has no instrument; the loop cannot check the direction of its own question until that direction is bound as an ordinary term.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 3 :10 *"it can say what would count as progress here, in its own terms"*; Fig 3 :27 *"A link with no instrument is not a link that works. It is a link nobody has looked at."*; Fig 5 :65 *"The remedy is a discipline rather than a branch: make the sought-for shape an ORDINARY TERM. Write down what an answer would look like, bind it, and let it accrue a"* (line 66 continues with "residual like anything else..."). `THE_FORMULA.md` "Direction" states the same limit in compact form.
- **What to check:** is link 3 really upstream of links 4 and 5 in the figure's own ordering, and does Fig 5 actually say the direction is invisible to the mechanism? Both are short passages and can be checked against the local verbatim extract.
- **Refuter:** a figure line deriving a direction from capability terms inside one closure without import, or a working instrument that measures "what would count as progress" independently of the system's own output and the designer's declared reward.

### C2 — domain-facing; checkable by a philosopher of intelligence or AI-safety researcher

- **Statement:** human judgment is the right anchor only where the question is constitutively about people; for "is this code correct" it is an instrument and can be wrong on axes it does not measure. A preference the system can influence is not an anchor.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 2 :36 *"An anchor is legitimate when the question is constitutively about it."*; Fig 2 :37 *"Human judgment is the right anchor for is this a good answer for a person, because the question is about people. It is only an instrument for is this code correct, and instruments can be wrong on axes they do not measure."*; Fig 2 :29 *"An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks."*
- **What to check:** is the domain-relative reading faithful to the passage, and does the world literature support the mechanism? Open Krakovna et al. 2020 on reward tampering and the automation-bias review; both are secondary and neither decides the figure's reading.
- **Refuter:** a demonstrated case where human judgment settles a code-correctness question on an axis it does not measure, with a non-updating ground agreeing; or a specification the system can influence that still functions as an anchor.

### C3 — framework-internal derivation with a governance test

- **Statement:** a specification is a convention; when it decides an outcome with no check above it, it stops being a convention. The checker must be unable to author what it checks, and unavoidable stakes must be declared.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 10 :47 *"a convention that decides an outcome has stopped being one"*; Fig 10 :49 *"Declaring a meaning does not ground it."*; Fig 10 :42 *"A verifier that can reconstruct the claim cannot verify it."*; Fig 10 :60 *"Where a stake cannot be removed, declare it. An undeclared interest is modelled anyway and an unnamed model runs unchallenged."*
- **What to check:** does a real reward function or AI charter acquire authority by being declared, and who can refuse it? Goodhart's law and the reward-hacking literature are low-overlap corroboration for the failure mechanism, not for the figure's rule.
- **Refuter:** a specification that fixes outcomes, is uncheckable by any seat, and nevertheless remains a checkable convention. This is self-contradictory by the figure's definition; if produced, Fig 10 :47 needs re-census.

## 3. What is explicitly not claimed
