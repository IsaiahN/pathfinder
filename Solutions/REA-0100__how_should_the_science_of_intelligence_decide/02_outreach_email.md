# Outreach draft: check the output first, then decide about the frame

**Use:** one to one, or as a cover letter attached to a submission through the public venues in `03_public_contact_points.csv`. **Not** for bulk send. No request for trust, no request for agreement, no prestige framing.

**Hard precondition:** do not send until the stage-1 output below has been checked by the recipient and their verdict recorded. If it fails, there is nothing else to look at. This draft deliberately withholds the framework's interiors until after that verdict.

---

**Subject:** One checkable output on how a science of intelligence could decide an "ought"

> Hi [name],
>
> I have one specific output I would like you to check against what you already know. I am not asking you to read a theory first, and I am not asking you to agree with anything. If the output does not survive, there is nothing else to look at.
>
> **The residual, in effect terms.** A science of intelligence can say what a system can learn to do: loss, benchmark score, demonstrated capability. It has no instrument for the other half of the question, what the system ought to do. In the dependency chain, that is link 3, the objective link: the ability to say what would count as progress in the system's own terms. Perception and planning have obvious tests; the objective link has neither, so it is usually assumed rather than measured. Effort then lands on the links above the break, where the measurements already exist.
>
> **The output (`specified`, not `grounded`).** A four-part separator:
> 1. Make the sought-for "ought" an ordinary term. Write down what an answer would look like, bind it, and let it accrue a residual like anything else. A loop can enumerate its held terms but cannot check the direction of its own question, so the direction has to be made bindable before it can be refused.
> 2. Name a non-updating anchor per domain. For "is this a good answer for a person", human judgment is the right anchor because the question is about people. For "is this code correct", human judgment is an instrument, and instruments can be wrong on axes they do not measure. A preference the system can influence is not an anchor.
> 3. Keep verdicts per slot. Never aggregate them into one scalar "ought"; the valence of any actor or rule is relative to the residual, and no standing list of good actors survives a change of residual.
> 4. Keep specifications as conventions, checkable and refusable. A convention that decides an outcome has stopped being a convention. Declare who authored it, and keep it outside the system's ability to manipulate its representation.
>
> **What this is not claiming.** It does not name a utility function, a moral theory, or a number. It does not say human preference is always the anchor, that reward functions are atoms, or that the specific content of an "ought" can be derived from capability. The framework returns a shape, not a name. Where the specific content is concerned, the honest answer is a stated gap, not silence and not a plausible invention.
>
> **What would refute it (written before sending):**
> 1. a figure line deriving a direction from capability terms inside one closure without import;
> 2. a reward function or declared convention that fixes an outcome and still remains a checkable convention;
> 3. a non-updating ground, with provenance and a shadow test, settling a specific universal "ought";
> 4. an agent-only improvement that moves the ground and therefore changes contact;
> 5. an aggregation preserving every live per-slot residual and surviving a change of residual;
> 6. a single evidence pool adding evidence about the world by agreeing with itself.
>
> If any holds, the corresponding part is withdrawn. A one-line "this fails because..." is more useful to me than agreement. If you would rather not engage, no reply is needed.
>
> **If the output survives, and only then:** the sources are below. The figures are the authority, not my summary of them.
> - Verbatim figure text with line numbers (local extract): `FRAMEWORK/reference/03-FIGURES_TEXT.md` in this project.
> - Canonical usage rule (local copy): `FRAMEWORK/reference/FIGURES_README_canonical.md`.
> - Eight-step loop and mode rule (local): `FRAMEWORK/figures/THE_FORMULA.md`.
>
> **If you want to use the machinery:** the formula is in the figures link. Run it on a problem you know well and can check against a ground. Write down what would refute your result before you run it, and label the mode of what you get. The framework's own rule is that a loop cannot check the direction of its own question, and a reported failure is more useful to me than a reported success.
>
> Thanks,
> [name]

## Two-stage offer, restated

1. **Stage 1:** send the residual, the four-part separator and the six refuters. Ask what the recipient would count as a failure. Record their verdict before showing any framework material.
2. **Stage 2, opt-in only:** if they are curious, offer the three local framework links. Note that the figures, not the summary, are the authority. Their later agreement is never pooled as corroboration (Fig 2 :40).
3. **Stage 3, method only:** offer the formula as a generator to reuse on a problem with a real ground, with the instruction that each user writes their own refuter and states their own mode.

This folder does not send anything. You host and send. Prefer a channel that lets a recipient decline contact (an opt-in submission or public comment route) over an attachment pushed to a list.
