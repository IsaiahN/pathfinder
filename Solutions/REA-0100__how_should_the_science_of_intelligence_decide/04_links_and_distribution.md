## 5. Distribution: offer, do not push

- **Fig 4 :22** *"Only methods cross up. Only head starts come down."* Sending a full architecture before anyone has described a residual sends a recording, not a generator. Lead with the four-part separator and the six refuters; offer the links after.
- **Fig 10 :42** *"A verifier that can reconstruct the claim cannot verify it."* A recipient who has absorbed the framework before being asked for a verdict has joined the frame being checked.
- **Fig 9 :27-28** *"any frame you pick will seem to fit"* if you do not describe the residual first. Ask what they would expect to see if the shape claim were true before giving them the source links.
- **Fig 2 :40-41** agreement from one pool is disqualifying for a verdict and efficient for a convention. A list selected for likely agreement produces a convention.
- **Fig 2 :42** *"Buy independence in proportion to the cost of being wrong."* A decision about what machines ought to do is high cost, so spend effort on low-overlap checkers.

**Recommended order in a reply:** (1) the residual and the four-part separator; (2) ask what they would count as a failure and record the verdict; (3) only then the three local framework links, with the note that the figures, not the summary, are the authority; (4) if they ask, the formula as a method, with the instruction to write their own refuter and state their mode.

## 6. The two-stage offer

1. **Stage 1:** output first. The recipient checks the shape and its refuter without holding the framework's interiors.
2. **Stage 2, opt-in:** theory second, only if curious. Later agreement is never pooled as corroboration.
3. **Stage 3:** the formula as a generator to reuse on a problem with a real ground. Each user writes their own refuter.

**Hard precondition:** the stage-1 output must survive. If a recipient fires any of the six refuters in `02_outreach_email.md`, stop and withdraw that part before offering anything else.
