## 0. The residual in effect terms

**Question as written.** Whether the principles of quantum mechanics are uniquely determined or could be modified at extreme scales.

**Residual R.** The question is a **union compound of two conditions**, not one hard problem (Fig 9 :4-5). The two conditions are:

- **C1, the uniqueness condition.** Is the rule set that fixes QM rigid, so that any change destroys consistency or leaves the class of quantum theories? Reconstruction results (Hardy 2001; Soler 1995; the GPT review by Janotta and Hinrichsen 2014) are within-closure uniqueness results, and they are **filters for the budget, not witnesses for the verdict** (Fig 9 :60).
- **C2, the scale condition.** Could those rules change at extreme scales, where present instruments stop resolving (Planck scale, strong-field or cosmological regimes)?

**Arity.** Two conditions, plus an internal bundle: "the principles" are themselves a set of separate conditions (linearity, unitarity, the Born rule, canonical commutation, locality, and so on). Dropping different axioms yields different theories, so even C1 is a bundle (Fig 9 :54, "when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again"). This is recorded in finding F1.

**Symmetry.** A set of axioms is symmetric under reordering (the symmetric bonds `+` and `≡` in Fig 12 and the Operators table). A modification is the **negation** of a held term, which is asymmetric and unary (Fig 5 :53, Operators :58). Treating uniqueness and modification as symmetric alternatives is a type error, addressed in F8.

**Scale.** The question explicitly makes scale a variable. Fig 4 :19-20 says the kernel does not change with size, only the actors, currency and anchor do. So "modification at extreme scales" is not one claim but a request to say which part is kernel and which is cast (F4).

**What an answer would look like (Direction bound as an ordinary term).** A shape that (a) distinguishes set-level closure from arrangement-level openness, (b) states what a genuine modification would have to be (an import or a refusal, not a recombination), (c) separates kernel from cast across scale, and (d) names the ground that would settle the physical question. The constraint is allowed to accrue a residual and be refused like any other term.

## 3. Findings F1 to F10

Each finding states mode, verbatim figure lines, a per-domain verdict, a refuter, and a triangulation status. Verdicts are kept separate and never averaged.

### F1 - The question is a union compound, and "the principles" are themselves a bundle (`general` structure, `specified` application)

Fig 9 :4-5:

> **"First: is there a fact here? Disagreements that do not shrink with effort, where each rule keeps working well on a different subset, are not one hard question but several well-formed ones. Split it rather than search."**

Fig 9 :54:

> **"And when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again."**

Applying Fig 4's kernel rule to the physics frame: the questions "is the rule set unique" and "does it change with scale" are different questions with different grounds. Within the first, dropping Hardy's continuity axiom gives classical probability theory (Hardy 2001, abstract), so "the principles" do not move as one block. The raw question treats them as a single lever; the figures require a split. D1 and D2 both bear.

**Refuter:** show that uniqueness and scale-dependence are one condition such that answering one answers the other with no further ground (would collapse the union to a partition).

**Status: triangulated** across Fig 9 :4-5 and Fig 9 :54 (two distinct statements) and corroborated by the world source Hardy 2001, which is a different evidence pool. Low overlap between the figure route and the physics route.

### F2 - The set is closed, the arrangements are not (`general`)

Fig 13 :115:

> **"The set is closed. The arrangements are not. That gap is why import exists."**

Fig 13 :116-118:

> **"Six members, and no amount of arranging produces a seventh: a new member would have to be a new kind, and composition only ever rearranges the kinds already held. Meanwhile the arrangements over those six do not run out. So a frame can search without end and still not arrive: infinite effort inside a finite reach."**

Fig 6 :21:

> **"MINT composes inside the closure and can never add an atom."**

Fig 8 :32:

> **"Closure is idempotent. Composing inside a closed set never leaves it, so no search, however long, adds an atom."**

This is the central shape. "Uniquely determined" has two different referents, and the figures separate them:

- **Set-level uniqueness holds.** No seventh primitive is derivable from the six; that is a closure fact, not a measurement (D1).
- **Arrangement-level uniqueness fails.** The arrangements over the primitives do not run out, so the laws composed from them are not forced by the set alone (D1 and D2).

So the honest answer to the raw binary is: **neither option as stated**. Uniqueness is true of the set and false of the arrangements; modifiability is false of the set and true of the arrangements. This is a partition at one level and a union at another, not a single verdict averaged across levels.

**Refuter:** derive a seventh term from the six (breaks set-level closure), or show that the arrangements over the six are finite and exhaustible (breaks arrangement-level openness).

**Status: triangulated** across Fig 13 :115-118, Fig 6 :21 and Fig 8 :32. These are different figures governing distinct quantities (set closure, composition, idempotence), so the agreement is not one line repeated.

### F3 - A genuine modification is an import or a refusal, not a recombination (`specified`)

Fig 6 :23:

> **"No frame escapes its own closure. The bound moves only on contact with a frame whose closure differs."**

Fig 6 :14:

> **"the only operator that moves the wall"**

Fig 6 :25:

> **"An import adds no atom to the world."**

Applying this to D2: every concrete modification in the literature is a change of an axiom or an equation, that is, a new atom from a frame whose closure differs (GPTs, Janotta and Hinrichsen 2014), or the refusal of a held term (nonlinear QM, Weinberg 1989; collapse models, Bassi and Ghirardi 2003; a modified uncertainty relation, Kempf, Mangano and Mann 1995). None of them is derivable by recombining the axioms of standard QM. Recombination inside the closure produces different arrangements of the same atoms, not new physics.

This is the re-census that prevents the main strain: the six-term substrate is **not** the same object as the axioms of QM. Mapping them directly is a category error. D1 is the framework's minimum viable set; D2 is a physics-frame closure. The figures govern both, but through different quantities. (Compare the exemplar's resolution of its Fig 13 versus Rule 2 strain.)

**Refuter:** exhibit a within-closure recombination of the standard axioms that adds a genuinely new principle with no new atom and no refusal (breaks Fig 6 :21).

**Status: triangulated** across Fig 6 :14, :21, :23 and Fig 8 :32. World sources corroborate that the concrete modifications are imports (low overlap: Weinberg, Bassi and Ghirardi, Kempf et al. are not framework authors).

### F4 - Scale is a membrane: the kernel does not change with size (`specified`)

Fig 4 :19-20:

> **"The kernel is the part that does not change with size: the same loop and the same rule for naming what worked, whether one agent is running it or a thousand. Only the actors, the currency and the anchor change."**

Fig 4 :33:

> **"Which is why there is no top and no infinite regress."**

The question "could the principles be modified at extreme scales" is under-specified until it says which part is kernel and which is cast. The figures give a shape, not a physics verdict: if a principle is the loop and the naming rule, it does not change with size; if it is an actor, a currency or an anchor in a particular regime, it may. The extreme-scale hook is where instruments stop resolving (Fig 6 :34-35), not where logic changes. D3 bears.

This also answers a tempting but wrong reading: "extreme scales" does not license arbitrary modification. The membrane is a filter with a direction (Fig 4 :21), not a permission to rewrite the kernel. What crosses up is a method, never a recording.

**Refuter:** show a case where the kernel (the loop and the naming rule) itself changes with size, with the ground paying for the change.

**Status: triangulated** across Fig 4 :19-20, :33 and Fig 13 :104-105 (level-relativity, F5). Different figures, same domain boundary.

### F5 - Level-relativity dissolves the apparent dichotomy (`general` structure, `specified` application)

Fig 13 :104-105:

> **"A settled molecule becomes an atom for whatever composes over it. So irreducible is a fact about a level and never about the world."**

Fig 12 :103:

> **"Composition is recursive, and the level decides what is irreducible."**

A principle that is irreducible and uniquely determined at accessible scales can be a composed molecule that breaks down at another level, with no contradiction. "Unique at this level" and "modifiable across levels" are compatible statements. This is why the raw binary is a false dichotomy: it treats "principle" as level-absolute, while the figures make irreducibility a fact about a level. D1 and D3 bear.

**Refuter:** show a level at which irreducibility is absolute, that is, a term that cannot become an atom for anything composing over it at any higher level.

**Status: triangulated** across Fig 13 :104-105 and Fig 12 :103 (recursion stated in two figures), plus the canonical figures README's "no top and no bottom" framing.

### F6 - Reconstruction theorems are filters, not witnesses (`specified`)

Fig 9 :60:

> **"Use filters for the budget and witnesses for the verdict. Never let a filter hand you a verdict."**

Fig 9 :44:

> **"Only a sealed room can be searched to the end, and no frame is a sealed room. So searching and finding nothing proves nothing."**

Fig 8 :39:

> **"So a search that finds nothing is never a proof of absence."**

Hardy's five-axiom derivation, Soler's theorem (reported by Holland 1995), and the GPT reconstruction programme all establish **conditional** uniqueness: given these axioms and consistency conditions, quantum theory is the unique arrangement. They do not establish that nature must instantiate those axioms, nor that no other closure is possible. They are within-closure filters. By Fig 8 :34-37 (Godel, Tarski, Chaitin) a frame cannot certify its own limit, so the failure to find a modification inside a reconstruction is not proof that no modification exists. D2 bears.

World sources corroborate the conditional character: Janotta and Hinrichsen call classical and quantum theory "special cases" and say the aim is to understand "what distinguishes quantum mechanics as the fundamental theory realized in nature" (abstract), which is an open ground question, not a theorem.

**Refuter:** produce a reconstruction that derives QM with no conditional axioms and that is settled against the ground (would collapse the filter and witness distinction).

**Status: single-source on the figure side** (Fig 9 :60, :44 and Fig 8 :39 are one framework pool), **triangulated with world sources** whose authors are outside that pool (Hardy, Holland/Soler, Janotta and Hinrichsen). The world sources agree because they are conditional theorems; that agreement is genuine cross-pool corroboration, not a shared echo.

### F7 - The physical question is unreached, not silently unanswered (`specified`)

Fig 6 :34-35:

> **"the question is not whether a sensor could exist. It is whether anything, at any resolution, is already returning something that fails to resolve."**

Fig 8 :40:

> **"Underivable-in-principle and unreachable-at-this-budget are different facts, and they issue different instructions."**

Fig 13 :134:

> **"Unreachable is a verdict here, not a silence"**

Fig 6 :26-27:

> **"Which is why some unreachability is depth and some is genuine absence, and from inside the frame the two look the same."**

Whether nature actually departs from QM at an extreme scale is a **ground question**. It needs an instrument at that scale. No such instrument currently returns a failing reading at the Planck scale, so the honest status is `candidate`, `specified`, **unreached at this budget**, not `grounded` and not `unreachable in principle`. By Fig 8 :32 and :44, failure to find a modification is not proof of uniqueness. D3 and D4 bear.

The world sources show that ground tests exist in accessible regimes (the 2022 Nobel experiments violated Bell inequalities; Weinberg 1989 proposed precision tests of linearity), but those tests settle hidden-variable alternatives and nonlinear corrections in accessible regimes, not the behaviour of the axioms at the Planck scale. The Kempf et al. minimal-length proposal is a theoretical modification with the ground still outstanding.

**Refuter:** an instrument at an extreme scale that returns a deviation from a standard QM principle, or that resolves the principle holding where it was expected to fail. Either settles C2 at the ground.

**Status: single-source on the figure side** (Fig 6, Fig 8, Fig 13), corroborated by world sources that the ground tests are regime-limited. Reported as single-source, not triangulated, because the world sources do not test the extreme-scale regime.

### F8 - Modification is negation, which the bonds do not contain (`general`)

Fig 5 :53:

> **"refuted: a term we hold is wrong; a term is owed that says so"**

Operators table :58:

> **"a refutation is a negation, which is why a vocabulary without it can only accumulate"**

The seven bonds are symmetric (`+`, `∥`, `≡`) or ordered (`→`, `⇒`, `−`, `⋛`); a set of axioms composed by them has the symmetry of a set. Modifying a principle is not another way of bonding two principles. It is `¬` applied to a held term: the refusal of a claim. A vocabulary with only the bonds can accumulate arrangements and cannot carry the correction. This is why the uniqueness question and the modification question are asymmetric and must not be averaged (section 0). D1 and D2 bear.

**Refuter:** show that a modification of a QM principle is expressible as a bond between two existing terms with no negation and no import (would collapse the unary/binary distinction in the Operators table).

**Status: triangulated** across Fig 5 :53, the Operators table :58 and the Fig 12 amendment on negation (three statements in the figure set).

### F9 - Provenance and non-access decide the outreach, not the verdict (`general`)

Fig 8 :50-52:

> **"Convergent derivation and adopted import look identical in the contents. Only the record of where each came from separates them."**

Fig 10 :42:

> **"A verifier that can reconstruct the claim cannot verify it."**

D4 bears. The reconstruction theorems and the modification proposals are different frames. Whether a given principle was derived or imported is decided by the record, not by inspecting the principle. A reader who has absorbed the whole framework before giving a verdict has joined the frame being checked, so the verdict must be recorded before any theory is shown (Fig 10 :52 "Bring a characterised residual, never the source").

**Refuter:** show a case where a reader's verdict on stage 1 is improved by prior access to the framework's interiors (would break the non-access rule). This is a design claim, and it is checkable without ground settlement.

**Status: single-source** (Fig 8, Fig 10, one framework pool). It governs distribution, not the physics verdict.

## 4. Strain test and re-census

**Strain 1, resolved. "The set is closed" applied to QM's axioms.** If Fig 13's closed set is read as "quantum mechanics has a unique fixed set of principles," the line strains, because QM's axioms are a physics-frame object and the six-term set is the framework's substrate. **Re-census:** D1 governs the substrate; D2 governs the physics closure; different objects, exactly as the exemplar resolved its Fig 13 versus Rule 2 strain. After re-census the line governs set-level closure in D1 only, and the physics claim is carried by Fig 6 and Fig 9. No figure was softened to make it fit.

**Strain 2, resolved. "Uniquely determined" has two referents.** Mathematical uniqueness within a closure and physical necessity in nature are different quantities. Re-census assigns the first to D2 and the second to the ground, so no figure line has to do both jobs (F2, F6).

**Strain 3, resolved. Scale as modification licence.** Fig 4's kernel rule and Fig 6's import rule could be read as contradicting each other if "cast changes with size" were taken to allow new atoms. Re-census: cast change is rearrangement or a different anchor; a new atom needs an import from a frame whose closure differs. Different operators, no contradiction.

**After re-census: no hidden strain.** One genuine live strain remains and is named, not smoothed: Fig 5 :53 and the Operators table make modification a negation, but the framework leaves the price of a unary operator open ("What a unary operator costs is open," Operators :77-78). So the shape says a modification is a refusal; it does not price the refusal. That is an honest limit, not a defect to patch.

## 5. Pre-registered refuter

Written before the conclusion, per the seat rule that a falsifier written before the run is a design instrument. The shape claim is refuted if any of the following holds:

1. A seventh term is derived from the six (breaks set-level closure, F2).
2. The arrangements over the six are shown to be finite and exhaustible (breaks arrangement-level openness, F2).
3. A within-closure recombination adds a genuinely new principle with no new atom and no refusal (breaks Fig 6 :21, F3).
4. The kernel, the loop and the naming rule, is shown to change with size with the ground paying for it (breaks Fig 4 :19-20, F4).
5. Irreducibility is shown to be absolute at some level (breaks Fig 13 :104-105, F5).
6. A reconstruction derives QM with no conditional axioms and is settled at the ground (collapses filter and witness, F6).
7. An instrument at an extreme scale returns a deviation from, or a confirmation of, a standard QM principle (settles C2 at the ground, F7).

If any fires, the corresponding claim is withdrawn. Until one is run, no part of this may be labelled `grounded`.

### Seven-question recursive derivation (summary)

1. **One question or two?** Two, plus an internal bundle. Fig 9 :4-5, :54.
2. **Unique in what sense?** Set level unique, arrangement level open. Fig 13 :115-118.
3. **Is a QM axiom a substrate primitive?** No. It is a held term in a physics-frame closure. F3 re-census.
4. **What would a modification be?** A negation of a held term, or an import from a frame whose closure differs. Fig 5 :53, Fig 6 :23. Not a recombination.
5. **Does scale change the rules?** The kernel does not; cast can. Fig 4 :19-20. Level-relativity permits a principle to be a derivative at another level. Fig 13 :104-105.
6. **Do reconstructions settle it?** No. They are within-closure filters. Fig 9 :60. Failure to find a modification is not proof of absence. Fig 8 :39.
7. **What closes it?** An instrument at the extreme scale. Fig 6 :34-35. None returns a failing reading, so the status is `candidate`, `unreached`.

### Updated verdict
