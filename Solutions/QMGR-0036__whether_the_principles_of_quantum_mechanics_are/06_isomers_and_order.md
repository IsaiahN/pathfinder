# Set versus arrangement, kernel versus cast: the supporting derivation

**Mode: `specified`.** This is a simulated derivation checked against the figures, not a ground reading (see `05_what_shape_means.md` section 6).

## RESIDUAL

The raw question treats "the principles of quantum mechanics" as one object and "uniquely determined or modifiable" as one binary lever. Three supporting questions are owed: (1) do different axiomatisations stand as **isomers** under Fig 12, same atoms arranged differently, or as different atoms? (2) is the bundle a **partition** or a **union** under Fig 9 :54? (3) what is the dependency **order** among the conditions (Fig 3)? A fourth question, kernel versus cast, is the scale analogue of the bond distinction.

## DOMAIN CENSUS

Atoms held in D1: Space, Time, Energy (states) plus Spectrum, Gradient, Network (structures). Bonds held: the seven, including symmetric `+`, `∥`, `≡` and ordered `→`, `⇒`, `−`, `⋛`; negation `¬` is a unary operator, not a bond. In D2 the "atoms" are the held axioms of a given reconstruction (for Hardy 2001, the five axioms; for Soler via Holland 1995, orthomodularity plus the infinite orthonormal sequence; for GPT approaches, the operational assumptions). No new primitive is available without import.

## DERIVATION

### Q1 - Are different axiomatisations isomers?

Fig 12 :117: "isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour." Fig 12 :124: "functional group. A substructure whose presence fixes the outcome. The group says what kind; the qualifier says which member."

- **Strong form.** If two reconstructions are written over the same operational atoms (preparation, transformation, measurement) and differ only in which bond connects them, they are **isomers**: same atoms, different arrangement, different substance. Hardy's five axioms and a GPT reconstruction can be read this way. Fig 12 :117 governs.
- **Weak form.** If they hold different atoms (Hardy's continuity axiom versus Soler's orthomodularity), they are **not** isomers. They share only a functional group, the pattern "a small set of axioms yielding Hilbert space" (Fig 12 :124).
- **Why it matters.** The isomer claim is the checkable structural version of "the principles are uniquely determined." Same atoms arranged differently means uniqueness at the set level and non-uniqueness at the arrangement level, exactly the F2 split. The weak form means there is no single shared set to be unique about.
- **Falsifier, written before the run:** if one reconstruction requires an atom the other does not even at the operational level (for example, one requires a continuity assumption the other cannot express), the strong isomer claim fails and the functional-group reading stands. This is a check one can perform by reading the two axiom lists side by side.
- **Status: triangulated** for the figure definition (Fig 12 :117 and :124), single-source for the physics mapping.

### Q2 - Partition or union?

Fig 9 :54: "And when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again." Fig 9 :55-57: a bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions; a single lever clears the region where only one condition holds and leaves the intersections standing.

The raw question is a **union** of at least two conditions: uniqueness (C1) and scale dependence (C2). C1 is itself a union: Hardy (2001) shows that dropping one axiom yields classical probability theory, so the "principles" do not move as one block. A single answer to "unique or modifiable" clears only the regions where one condition holds; the intersection, "what is unique and yet changes with scale," stays standing and is categorically different (Fig 9 :57 "a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either. It stops being a mixture and becomes a specific compound"). The resolution is the level-relativity of F5: a principle can be unique at one level and a derivative that fails at another.

**Falsifier:** if C1 and C2 are shown to be one condition, such that settling uniqueness settles scale dependence with no further ground, the union reading fails and the compound dissolves.

### Q3 - What is the order?

Fig 3 :20-21: "The chain is ordered by dependency, not by importance. Each link consumes what the one above produces, so effort spent above the break arrives at a step that cannot use it." The dependency order established in `01` section 1 is:

1. Split the union (Fig 9 :4-5, :54).
2. State the residual in effect terms (Fig 9 :30-31).
3. Settle set-level closure before arrangement-level claims (Fig 13 :115-118).
4. Refuse before mint (Formula step 2): identify which held term would be negated before proposing a replacement.
5. Decide kernel versus cast before asking about scale (Fig 4 :19-20).
6. Name the ground that would close the residual (Fig 6 :34-35).

A link with no instrument is not a link that works (Fig 3 :27 "A link with no instrument is not a link that works. It is a link nobody has looked at"). The missing instrument here is the extreme-scale discriminator, so the chain stops at link 6 and the stop is reported as unreached, not assumed.

### Q4 - Kernel versus cast as a bond distinction

Fig 4 :19-20: "The kernel is the part that does not change with size: the same loop and the same rule for naming what worked, whether one agent is running it or a thousand. Only the actors, the currency and the anchor change." Fig 4 :21: "The membrane is the boundary between the sizes, and the rule about what may cross it. Not a wall: a filter with a direction."

- A principle that is the loop and the naming rule sits on the kernel side and is scale-invariant. It is bound with the symmetric, definitional bond `≡` in the sense that it is the same term at every size (Operators :23-27, identity: "two names, one referent").
- An actor, currency or anchor that changes with regime sits on the cast side. It is bound with the ordered bond `→` or the comparison `⋛` to a particular scale: the term changes when the scale changes.
- **The wrong move** is to treat the cast side as if it were the kernel and conclude that "the principles" change wholesale at extreme scales. The membrane is a filter, not a licence. What crosses up is a method, never a recording (Fig 4 :22-23).
- **Falsifier:** show a case where the kernel changes with size with the ground paying for it. Then the kernel/cast distinction collapses and the scale claim changes verdict.

**Status: triangulated** across Fig 4 :19-23 and Fig 13 :104-105 (level-relative irreducibility).

## CONCLUSION

The supporting derivation does three things. It turns "are the principles unique" into a checkable **isomer question** (same operational atoms, different arrangement) with a stated falsifier. It identifies the raw question as a **union** that must be unbundled before search, not one lever. It supplies the **dependency order** that puts set-level closure, refusal and kernel/cast ahead of any scale claim. The scale claim itself reduces to a kernel/cast distinction, which is a bond distinction, not a permission to rewrite everything.

What this framing rescues: the defensible content of the uniqueness intuition (set-level closure) and the defensible content of the modification intuition (arrangement-level openness plus level-relativity), without asserting either as a ground verdict. What it does not rescue: any single binary answer, and any claim that a particular axiom is the one nature changes.
