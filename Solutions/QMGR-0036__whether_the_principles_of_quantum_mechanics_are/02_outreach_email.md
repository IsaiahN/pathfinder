**Subject:** Check one structural claim about quantum axioms before we argue about modifications

> Hi [name],
>
> I have one specific output I would like you to check against what you already know. I am not asking you to read a theory first, and I am not asking you to agree with anything. If the output does not survive, there is nothing else to look at.
>
> **The output (`specified`, not `grounded`).** The question "are the principles of quantum mechanics uniquely determined or modifiable at extreme scales" is two questions with different grounds, not one. The figures' framework says the set of primitives is closed, no seventh member can be derived from the six, but the arrangements over those primitives do not run out. So uniqueness is true of the set and false of the arrangements. A genuine modification of a principle cannot be produced by recombining the existing ones: it has to be a refusal of a held term or an import from a different frame. And scale is a membrane: the kernel (the loop and the rule for naming what worked) does not change with size, only the actors, currency and anchor do.
>
> What this yields is a shape, not a physics verdict. It says *what any answer would have to look like*: split set-level closure from arrangement-level openness, say whether a proposed change is a new atom or a negation, and say which part is kernel and which is cast. It does not say that nature actually departs from quantum mechanics at the Planck scale. That is a ground question, and no instrument currently returns a failing reading there.
>
> **What I am not claiming:** that the six-term set is the axioms of quantum mechanics (it is not; that would be a category error); that Hardy's or Soler's reconstruction theorems prove quantum mechanics is forced (they are conditional, within-closure results); or that any particular modification, nonlinear quantum mechanics, collapse models, or a minimal length, is realized in nature. Those are bindings from other frames and carry zero evidentiary weight until provenance is shown.
>
> **What would refute the shape claim, written before sending this:**
> 1. deriving a seventh term from the six, or showing the arrangements over them are finite;
> 2. exhibiting a within-closure recombination that adds a genuinely new principle with no new atom and no refusal;
> 3. showing the kernel itself changes with size, with an instrument paying for it;
> 4. showing irreducibility is absolute at some level;
> 5. an experiment at an extreme scale that settles whether a principle holds or fails.
>
> If any one holds, I will retract the corresponding claim. A one-line "this fails because..." is more useful to me than agreement. If you would rather not engage, no reply is needed.
>
> **If it survives, and only then:** the sources are below. The figures are the authority, not my summary of them.

## Stage sequence

1. **Stage 1:** send the shape claim and the five refuters. Ask what they would count as a failure. Record their verdict **before** showing any theory.
2. **Stage 2, opt-in only:** if they are curious, offer the three framework links and note that the figures, not the summary, are the authority.
3. **Stage 3:** offer the formula as a method to reuse, with the instruction that each user writes their own refuter and states their own mode.

A stage-2 agreement is never pooled as corroboration (Fig 2 :40 "agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool"). Their stage-1 verdict is the only thing worth recording.
