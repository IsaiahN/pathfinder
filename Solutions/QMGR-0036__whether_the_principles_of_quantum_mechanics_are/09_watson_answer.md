## QUESTION

Option A: assert a single verdict, either "uniquely determined" or "modifiable at extreme scales." Option B: keep the figure-derived shape and refuse both names. ## VERDICT (from the figures)

The binary is refused. The set of primitives is closed, so no seventh member is derivable, but the arrangements over it are unbounded, so uniqueness is true of the set and false of the laws composed from it. A genuine modification of a principle is a negation of a held term or an import from a frame whose closure differs, never a recombination. Scale is a membrane whose kernel does not change with size; only cast changes, and irreducibility is a fact about a level, so unique here and modifiable there are compatible. Reconstruction theorems are conditional filters, not witnesses. Whether nature actually departs from QM at an extreme scale is unreached at this budget: no instrument currently returns a failing reading there. This is a `specified`, ungrounded, candidate result, not a physics verdict.

- **cost if wrong:** bounded and locatable. The shape is mode-labelled, states refuters before the conclusion and records the lineage, so an error is findable rather than invisible. The real cost is presenting a physics verdict the figures cannot ground, which Fig 9 :60 prices as letting a filter hand you a verdict, and spending the independence of outside readers by sending a recording instead of a generator. Both are avoidable by leading with the split and its refuters.
- **undoable:** yes. The bindings that fail (mapping the six-term set onto QM's axioms, treating reconstructions as proof, naming a modification as realized) are removable without touching the derivation. `07_the_tether_answer.md` and `08_expert_verification_packet.md` already remove them. A sent claim can be superseded by the corrected packet, and no primitive has to be withdrawn.
- **reversed by:** a ground reading, not agreement. An instrument at an extreme scale returning a deviation from, or a confirmation of, a standard QM principle would settle C2. A seventh term derived from the six, finite arrangements over them, a within-closure recombination adding a new principle, a demonstration that the kernel changes with size with the ground paying, or a proof that irreducibility is absolute would break the shape claims. Until one of these fires, the verdict stands as `candidate`.
