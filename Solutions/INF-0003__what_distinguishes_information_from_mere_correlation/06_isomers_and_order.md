# Symmetry, order and isomers of the information bond

**Mode: `specified`.** This is a simulated derivation checked against the figures, not a ground reading (see `05_what_shape_means.md` §7).

## RESIDUAL

`01` established that the answer is domain-indexed. Two supporting questions remain:

1. Is the relation "X is correlated with Y" symmetric or ordered, and what follows from each reading?
2. Are "correlation" and "information" isomers in the figure's sense, or are they different kinds of thing that merely share vocabulary?

A third question is structural: in what order must the four domains be worked, given Fig 3's dependency rule?

## DOMAIN CENSUS

Atoms held in the vocabulary for this question: a reading, a slot, a co-variation, a bond type, a source, an anchor, a code, an instrument. Bonds held: the seven, of which `+` (conjunction), `∥` (disjunction) and `≡` (identity) are symmetric, and `→` (sequence), `⇒` (production), `−` (subtraction) and `⋛` (comparison) are ordered; negation is the unary eighth operator and is not a bond. No new primitive is available without import.

## DERIVATION

### Q1 — Symmetry of the correlation relation

- Operators table :15 and :33 give the identifying tests: for conjunction, *"swap the operands. Does the meaning change? No."*; for sequence, *"swap them. Does it become a different thing? Yes."*
- Covariance and mutual information satisfy the first test: `I(X;Y) = I(Y;X)`. A correlation is therefore a **symmetric** bond under the figure's own classification. It records co-presence, which is the flat case. Fig 12 :5: *"A set of ingredients is not one. The bond is the rest of the information, and it is the half nothing was recording."*
- Production and sequence satisfy the second test. Any claim of the form "X carries information about Y" in the directional sense is using a bond the correlation does not contain.
- Operators table :34: *"the only bond a flat chain can express, so every term today is a sequence whether or not it should be"*. A flat correlation cannot say which ordered bond holds; calling it "information" silently promotes it to one.
- **Result:** the symmetry of correlation is not a defect in the measure. It is a statement about which bond it is. The defect is in the identification of a symmetric bond with an ordered one.

**Falsifier for Q1:** exhibit a correlation-like quantity whose swap test changes its meaning while still being the quantity the field calls correlation. That would break the classification. Status: `triangulated` (Operators table plus the SEP causal-modelling entry's treatment of latent common causes and interventions).

### Q2 — Are correlation and information isomers?

- Fig 12 :117: *"Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."*
- Read strictly, correlation and information are **not** isomers over the same atoms. "Information" names a family of relations (a typed bond, a source-discriminating reading, an anchor condition); "correlation" names one symmetric bond in that family. They share an ingredient set but not a bond set. By Fig 12's definition, the difference is not invisible in the formula, because one formula records a bond and the other does not.
- The genuine isomer case is internal to information: two systems with the same pairwise correlations can have different redundancy and synergy structure. Williams and Beer (2010, arXiv:1004.2515) report that interaction information *"confounds redundancy and synergy"* and can be negative, while their partial information atoms are *"never negative"*. Same correlations, different arrangement, different behaviour. That is the figure's isomer claim made precise in a different field.
- The perceptual twin is the metamerism case: one green reading from RGB, from CMYK, from yellow plus blue, or from a scheme not yet discovered. Same reading, different producing bond. *The Hard Problem is About Source*: *"If several paths create the same behavior, then behavior is a projection with lossy information."*

**Falsifier for Q2:** show that the partial information atoms are a mere reparametrisation of one scalar with no behavioural consequence, or that all producing schemes for a reading collapse to one under a ground-validated invariant. Status: `triangulated` for the structural claim, `specified` and unmeasured for the perceptual one.

### Q3 — Order of work (Fig 3's dependency rule)

Fig 3 :27: *"A link with no instrument is not a link that works. It is a link nobody has looked at."* The five links for this question:

1. **Perception.** Index the residual per slot. Fig 1 :19: *"The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated."* Without this, no co-variation can be located.
2. **Vocabulary.** Name the bond, the source and the anchor. This is the broken link: the field has a precise instrument for amount of co-variation and no standard instrument inside the definition for which typed bond or which source. Effort above this break cannot arrive.
3. **Objective.** State what counts as information here. Operators table: *"a goal is a comparison, which is why a vocabulary without it cannot state one."* The objective is the threshold `⋛` between co-variation with a bond and ground, and co-variation without one.
4. **Planning.** Design the intervention that separates the four arrangements (X produces Y, Y produces X, common cause, accident). This is the do-operator in causal modelling; in the figures it is an instrument improved from a worse one that already returns something (Fig 6 :32 to :34).
5. **Learn and carry.** Settle, then promote the method, never the recording (Fig 4 :22 to :25).

**Falsifier for Q3:** exhibit a working, standard instrument that returns bond type or source as part of the definition of information rather than as a separate causal or semantic step. If one exists, the break is mislocated and the diagnosis in `01` F7 falls. Status: `single-source` for the framework diagnosis; the SEP semantic entry's information-channel discussion corroborates the need to treat the channel as a relation rather than a quantity.

### Q4 — Valence is relative to the residual

Fig 8 and the essays agree that a relation's value is not carried by the relation. Fig 2 :39 to :41:

> **"Correlation has valence."**
> **"disqualifying for a verdict: agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool"**
> **"efficient for a convention: agreement about a shared meaning is the product, not a corruption of it"**

*How a Society of Agents Would Work* gives the social form: *"Ten copies watching the same thing are one opinion said ten times."* *Decoding Tacit Knowledge* gives the semantic form: *"Agreement is the whole point when you're settling what words mean. It's worthless for settling what's true when both sides learned from the same sources."* Valence is a function of `(relation, residual, anchor)`, not of the relation alone.

**Falsifier for Q4:** show a case where agreement from one evidence pool is a valid verdict about the world, or a shared meaning is corrupted by its own success when the question is about that meaning. Status: `triangulated` (Fig 2 plus two essays plus the SEP causal-modelling entry, with the overlap among the essays declared).

## CONCLUSION

Correlation is a symmetric bond, the flat conjunction of co-presence. Information, where the word does discriminating work, involves at least one ordered or unary operation and an anchor. The two are therefore not synonyms and not isomers over one bond set; the real isomer case is inside the multivariate structure, where the same pairwise correlations can carry different redundancy and synergy. The dependency chain for the question breaks at the vocabulary link, because the amount of co-variation has an instrument and the bond and source do not. Valence is relative to the residual and the anchor, which is why a single answer to "what distinguishes them" is not available.
