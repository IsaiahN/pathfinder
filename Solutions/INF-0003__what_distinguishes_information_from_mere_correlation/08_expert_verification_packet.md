## 1. The claim, in one paragraph

The question is a union of four conditions, not one definition dispute. In composition, correlation is the flat conjunction of co-presence while information is the typed bond between operands, which the operands do not carry. In verification, agreement among frames that share one evidence pool is disqualified as a verdict; information from agreement requires low-overlap frames ranged against an anchor that does not update. In perception, a single aggregate coefficient drops the live slot signal, and a reading does not identify its source until a discriminator exists. In semantic and convention matters, correlation is not a defect: where the question is constitutively about a shared meaning, agreement about that meaning is the product. The synthesis is that correlation is co-variation under a declared code, while information is co-variation plus a typed arrangement, a source-discriminating reading and an anchor, with the anchor condition changing in the convention domain.

## 2. Three checkable sub-claims

### C1 — framework-internal; checkable by reading Figures 12 and 2

- **Statement:** the bond carries information the operands do not, and correlation has two valences decided by the anchor.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 12 :5 — "A set of ingredients is not one. The bond is the rest of the information, and it is the half nothing was recording." Fig 2 :39 to :41 — "Correlation has valence." / "disqualifying for a verdict: agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool" / "efficient for a convention: agreement about a shared meaning is the product, not a corruption of it".
- **What to check:** do these lines say what the claim says they say, and is the union partition (four domains, different anchors) a fair reading rather than a strained one?
- **Refuter:** exhibit an invariant from operand sets and all pairwise correlations to a unique typed bond and source validated at the ground, or show that agreement from one evidence pool is a valid verdict about the world and that a shared-meaning convention is corrupt when the question is about that meaning. Either breaks C1.

### C2 — information-theory-facing; checkable against the literature

- **Statement:** one scalar does not hold the multivariate structure; the same pairwise correlations are compatible with different redundancy and synergy profiles, and position within a structure decides what a message means.
- **What to check:** is the reading of Williams and Beer (2010, arXiv:1004.2515) accurate? Their abstract says interaction information *"suffers from the problem that it is sometimes negative"* because it *"confounds redundancy and synergy"*, and that their partial information atoms are *"never negative"*. Is the SEP Information example correct that `0000011111` and `1001011100` carry the same Shannon information because they have the same counts? Is the SEP Causal Models definition correct that latent common causes make co-variation compatible with several causal arrangements?
- **Refuter:** show that the partial information atoms are a reparametrisation of one scalar with no behavioural consequence, or that the correlation value determines the producing arrangement without an instrument. This is the same falsifier as R1 in `01`, addressed to a different domain.

### C3 — verification-facing; checkable from the figures and standard survey arithmetic

- **Statement:** correlated observations are not independent witnesses. Independence is one quantity read twice: it controls both whether agreement means anything and whether two frames reach a surplus.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 8 :47 — "Independence is one quantity read twice." Fig 2 :40 — "agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool". Fig 2 :42 — "Buy independence in proportion to the cost of being wrong."
- **What to check:** the SEP Causal Models entry and the Kish effective-sample-size arithmetic (N correlated samples count as N / (1 + (N-1)ρ) independent ones) are the standard versions of this. Is the mapping from those to the figure's two collapses fair?
- **Refuter:** demonstrate two frames with fully shared evidence providing independent corroboration about the world. This breaks C3.

## 3. What is explicitly not claimed

- Not that mutual information is wrong, and not that Shannon's measure is useless. It is a precise quantity inside a declared code.
- Not that there is one true definition of information across all domains. The SEP semantic entry quotes Shannon's informational pluralism: *"It is hardly to be expected that a single concept of information would satisfactorily account for the numerous possible applications of the general field."*
- Not that a source is unknowable. It is unreached until a discriminator exists; the colour case was settled by the spectrometer, not by argument.
- Not that the framework names which world-definition wins. It returns a shape, and any name is a provenanced import.
- Not that this is `grounded`. Nothing here is asserted as settled by experiment.
- Not that agreement with this packet is evidence. A reply on stage 1 is recorded before any theory is shown, and later agreement is never pooled as corroboration.
