## DERIVATION

### Q1: Which bonds does the closing term need, and in what order?

- Fig 12 :46: "A bond is the typed relation between two operands. There are seven, they are not interchangeable, and which one holds is not recoverable from the operands."
- The one-shot defection result is a **chain**: defect → collect `T` → encounter ends. A chain is the degenerate case with one bond type (Fig 12 :48: "A chain is the degenerate case: one bond type, no branching. Nothing about a chain is a different kind of object."). Operators table :34: "the only bond a flat chain can express, so every term today is a sequence whether or not it should be".
- The closing term needs at least three additional bonds:
  1. **Sequence `→`** between rounds: Fig 12 :72, "A then B; B needs A to have happened". Today's cooperation must precede tomorrow's response.
  2. **Comparison `⋛`** between the present cost and the future benefit: Operators table :52, "a goal is a comparison, which is why a vocabulary without it cannot state one". This is where the `ω` threshold lives.
  3. **Conjunction `+`** or **production `⇒`** to compose multiple partners: the network is not one dyad. Fig 12 :57, "both hold at once"; Fig 12 :77, "A produces B; B did not exist before".
- And it needs a **cycle**, which is a structure rather than a bond: Fig 12 :121, "cycle. The output re-enters as input. The sign of the loop decides whether it closes, and a chain cannot close by construction." The sign matters: a negative loop (defection begets defection) also closes, and closes the wrong way. This is worth stating plainly, because the world source on pigeons suggests exactly a short-horizon negative loop (Wikipedia, Reciprocal altruism: "This may be due to favoring short-term thinking over long-term thinking").
- **Order of the bonds.** The dependency is comparison before sequence in the *model*, but sequence before comparison in the *world*: the actor must first have future rounds in which to respond (cycle), and only then can the threshold comparison be meaningful. Fig 3 :20: "The chain is ordered by dependency, not by importance." A frame that computes the threshold before establishing that output re-enters as input has put the later link first and will draw a wrong conclusion about `ω`.
- **Falsifier Q1:** exhibit a stable, costly-cooperation term that uses only the chain bond and no cycle, comparison, or multi-partner bond. Then the bond list is wrong. Scope: immediate-benefit mutualism is excluded, because there the chain closes at a stroke (Fig 11 :50 says mutualism is a relation, not a kind; the posed residual is the costly case).

### Q2: Are the candidate mechanisms isomers, a functional group, or catalysts?

- Fig 12 :117: "isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."
- Consider the mechanisms named in the literature (Wikipedia, The Evolution of Cooperation: "In a 2006 paper Nowak listed five mechanisms by which natural selection can lead to cooperation"): kin selection, direct reciprocity, indirect reciprocity, network reciprocity, group selection. Add punishment and conformist learning.
- **As the literature writes them, they are not one molecule.** They differ in atoms as well as bonds: kin selection uses relatedness `r`; direct reciprocity uses memory of a partner; indirect reciprocity uses reputation; network reciprocity uses topology; group selection uses between-group variance. Over the full shared set of all these atoms, some pairs are **isomers** (same atom inventory, different arrangement). Over the narrower set each theory actually uses, they are a **functional group**: Fig 12 :124, "functional group. A substructure whose presence fixes the outcome. The group says what kind; the qualifier says which member." The shared substructure is `state → structure → state → cycle`; the qualifier picks which structure mediates and which state pair it joins.
- **Catalyst reading.** Fig 12 :119: "catalyst. A part that survives its own use." A reputation mechanism or a sanctioning institution is a catalyst: it survives the interactions it stabilizes. A one-off payoff transfer is a spent part. The distinction matters because a model that bills a catalyst as a per-round cost misprices the arrangement.
- **Cycle sign.** Fig 12 :121 again: "The sign of the loop decides whether it closes." Kin selection and reciprocity are positive loops around costly acts; defection cascades and punishment escalation are negative loops. A chain cannot express either, which is why the one-shot frame cannot derive cooperation even though it holds the payoff atoms.
- **Falsifier Q2:** show that two named mechanisms have the same atoms, the same bonds, and the same behaviour in a discriminating test. Then they are one mechanism under two names and the isomer/functional-group distinction collapses for that pair. Alternatively, show that they differ in atoms only, which would make them different molecules rather than isomers.

### Q3: What order is the union of conditions?

- The four conditions in D1 to D4 (payoff environment, network habitat, selection dynamics, measurement of emergence) are a union, not a list (Fig 9 :54-57).
- Dependency order via Fig 3: perception of the per-slot residual → vocabulary for bonds and cycles → objective (what counts as emergence) → planning the model → carry and verify. The break typically sits between vocabulary and objective, because a model can name strategies and still lack an instrument for emergence (Fig 3 :27, "A link with no instrument is not a link that works. It is a link nobody has looked at.").
- The union has no single top link. Fig 3 :18: "The break is drawn between three and four as an illustration. Where a given chain stops is a reading, never a property of the chain." So the audit does not assert where *the* break is; it asserts that the four conditions overlap and must be unbundled (Fig 9 :54).
- **Cycle versus chain as a cross-level relation.** The individual decision is a chain (one round). The population pattern is a cycle (repeated rounds across generations). The membrane rule applies: Fig 4 :22, "Only methods cross up. Only head starts come down." What crosses from individual to population is the *method* of selecting on payoff, not the recording of one defection. A replay of one round carried upward would produce a model that repeats a past success and cannot produce a new one (Fig 4 :25).
- **Falsifier Q3:** show that the four conditions partition the cases, each cooperation observation in exactly one condition, with no shared fraction and no compound requiring two conditions at once. Then the union reading is wrong and a single lever generalizes.

### Q4: Where does the import condition bite?

- Fig 6 :21: "MINT composes inside the closure and can never add an atom." If the frame is the bare one-shot payoff model, the graph and the cycle are not in its closure, so importing them from graph theory or repeated-game theory is the only wall-moving operator (Fig 6 :14, "the only operator that moves the wall").
- If the frame is evolutionary game theory on graphs (Szabo and Fath 2007), the atoms are already inside the closure. The remedy is more search at the same reach, not another atom (Fig 6 :49: "more search at the same reach: is it in here and unfound?"). Importing against a budget limit adds an atom that was not needed (Fig 8 :40-43).
- **Falsifier Q4:** derive the graph and cycle atoms from the bare payoff matrix and a fitness update alone, with no topological or repeated-game input. Then import is never owed and the condition is void.

## CONCLUSION

The closing term is not a single bond. It is a priced arrangement: sequence and comparison over repeated rounds, conjunction over partners, and a positive cycle whose sign decides whether the arrangement closes on cooperation or on defection. The named mechanisms are a functional group over the shared bond substructure, with isomer pairs where the atom inventories match; they are not one mechanism with several labels and not a seventh primitive. The union of conditions has a dependency order but no single top link, and the chain-to-cycle step crosses a scale membrane that carries methods, not recordings. The import verdict is conditional on the frame's existing atoms.

This preserves the defensible part of the world literature (mechanism plurality with a common shape) without the part that fails (a single mechanism selected by the framework). The specific mechanism remains a `gap`.
