# Expert verification packet: the Tether on cooperation in networks

**What this is:** a claim in a form an evolutionary-dynamics or complex-systems researcher can check. Mode, derivation, and refuters are stated before the conclusion so the reader knows what to look for and what would settle it.

**Mode: `specified` on the per-domain shape, `general` on the structural limit, `gap` on the mechanism slot. Not `grounded`.** No experiment is claimed here.

**Status:** a simulated derivation checked for internal consistency against the figures, not a ground reading. It cannot settle the claim it reports on.

## 1. The claim, in one paragraph

The question "can evolutionary dynamics explain the emergence of cooperation when short-term incentives favor defection" is a union of overlapping conditions, not one question with a yes/no answer. A closing explanation must have four properties: cooperation and defection are relations whose sign is relative to the residual, not fixed actor kinds; the explanation lives in the agent and its network habitat, not the agent alone; the one-shot defection result is a chain that cannot close, while cooperation needs a cycle in which the output of one round re-enters as the input to the next; and the residual must be indexed per node or dyad, not averaged into a cooperation rate. Which mechanism occupies the slot is a declared gap. Whether a given frame owes an import depends on whether it already holds the graph and cycle atoms.

## 2. Three checkable sub-claims

### C1: Framework-internal; checkable by reading Figures 11 and 12

- **Statement:** the short-term-incentive result is a chain; a costly-cooperation equilibrium requires a cycle. A chain cannot close by construction, so no amount of one-shot iteration within a payoff-only closure produces cooperation.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 12 :121, "cycle. The output re-enters as input. The sign of the loop decides whether it closes, and a chain cannot close by construction." Fig 12 :112, "A plan is a sequence. A goal is a comparison. Progress is a subtraction. A refutation is a negation, and that one is not a bond." Operators table :34, "the only bond a flat chain can express, so every term today is a sequence whether or not it should be".
- **Refuter:** exhibit a stable, costly-cooperation equilibrium in a single interaction with no re-entry of output as input and no simultaneous mutual benefit. Immediate-benefit mutualism does not count; it is outside the posed residual.

### C2: World-facing; checkable by an evolutionary-dynamics or complex-systems researcher

- **Statement:** the network is part of the mechanism, not a container. An agent-only change that leaves contact variables fixed does not move stable cooperation at the ground, while a change to interaction structure can.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 11 :39, "And capability is a property of agent-and-habitat, never of the agent alone." Fig 11 :40, "A system cannot be made more capable by improving the agent in isolation. An improvement that does not change contact changes nothing, however much it improves." Fig 13 :24, "that entities relate, and influence travels".
- **What to check:** in a model or dataset you know well, hold the strategy rule fixed and vary only the interaction graph (degree, clustering, encounter rate, memory horizon). Does stable cooperation move? Then hold the graph fixed and vary only the strategy rule. Which lever actually changes the ground? The graph structure is known to modify outcomes: Szabo and Fath (2007, Physics Reports) write that "The major theme of the review is in what sense and how the graph structure of interactions can modify and enrich the picture of long term behavioral patterns emerging in evolutionary games."
- **Refuter:** an agent-only change, with all contact variables held fixed, that raises stable cooperation at the ground. A second refuter: a network-only change that never moves cooperation in a system where costly cooperation is observed.

### C3: Framework-internal derivation with a world-facing discriminator

- **Statement:** a cooperation reading does not identify its source. Several mechanisms produce the same observation, so the claim is `specified` until an instrument discriminates. Mechanism identity is a `gap`, not silence and not a figure-selected name.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 2 :40, "disqualifying for a verdict: agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool"; Fig 9 :32, "you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself"; Fig 13 :134, "Unreachable is a verdict here, not a silence". Fig 10 :42, "A verifier that can reconstruct the claim cannot verify it."
- **What to check:** can you name an observation that distinguishes reciprocity from kin selection, group selection, and reputation in a given network? One exists in the literature: Carter and Wilkinson (2013) found "Food received was the best predictor of food given across dyads, and 8.5 times more important than relatedness" under controlled mixed relatedness and equal familiarity.
- **Refuter:** a measurement whose cooperation reading is invariant under model change *and* that discriminates at least two candidate mechanisms, followed by a settlement using it. That would close the gap and move the relevant domain from `specified` to `grounded`.

## 3. What is explicitly not claimed

- Not that evolutionary dynamics is false, and not that it is the unique explanatory frame.
- Not that a specific mechanism (kin selection, direct or indirect reciprocity, network reciprocity, group selection, punishment, conformism) is selected by the framework. Mechanism identity is a binding from the world frame, provenanced as such.
- Not that any threshold value (`r > c/b`, `ω`, `q > c/b`) is derived by the framework. Those are filters from world models, never verdict witnesses (Fig 9 :60, "Use filters for the budget and witnesses for the verdict. Never let a filter hand you a verdict.").
- Not that a pooled cooperation rate is sufficient evidence (Fig 1 :19, "The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated.").
- Not that model agreement settles a source question (Fig 2 :35, "Alignment is triangulation, not negotiation. A mirror cannot correct you.").
- Not `grounded`. No measurement in this packet shows the residual falling.
- Not a claim that the short-term defection premise is false. It is the correct prediction of the one-shot frame inside that frame; the missing thing is the cycle and the habitat.

## 5. What would settle it

1. A source-discriminating instrument, as in Carter and Wilkinson 2013, applied to a specified network.
2. A cycle test: drive the probability of future interaction to zero while holding one-shot payoffs fixed, and check whether stable cooperation collapses.
3. A contact test: vary only the graph with the strategy rule fixed, then vary only the rule with the graph fixed, and compare which moves the ground.
4. A slot test: check whether an aggregate cooperation rate predicts which node or dyad class carries the live residual.
5. The standing refuters from `01` section 4, any one of which retracts a specific part of the shape.

## 6. What an expert can check in twenty minutes

1. Read Fig 11 :39-40 and :49-51, and Fig 12 :112 and :121. Four short passages, all in the local figure extract.
2. Check C2 against your own model or dataset: hold the strategy rule fixed and vary only the graph. Does cooperation move?

4. Check the import condition: does your frame already hold the graph and cycle atoms, or would it have to import them? (Fig 6 :21, :49.)
