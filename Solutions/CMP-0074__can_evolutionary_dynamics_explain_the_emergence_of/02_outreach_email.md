# Outreach draft: check the shape before you decide about the theory

**Use:** one-to-one, or as a cover note attached to a submission through the public venues in `03_public_contact_points.csv`. **Not** for bulk send.

**Hard precondition:** do not send until the union framing and the per-domain verdicts in `01_figure_grounded_audit.md` sections 3 and 6 are intact. The offer collapses on contact if the recipient is handed a theory before they have described the residual.

**Mode stated up front:** `specified` on the per-domain shape claims, `general` on the structural limit, `gap` on which mechanism occupies the slot. Not `grounded`. This is a simulated derivation checked for internal consistency against the figures, not a ground reading.

**Subject:** One shape claim about cooperation in networks, and the failure condition I wrote first

> Hi [name],
>
> I have one specific claim about the evolution of cooperation that you can check against what you already know. I am not asking you to read a theory first, and I am not asking you to agree with it. If the claim does not survive, there is nothing else to look at. I state its mode: `specified`, not `grounded`. This is a shape derived from a framework, not a measurement.
>
> **The residual, described before any frame is named.** In a one-shot interaction where cooperation is costly and the temptation payoff exceeds mutual cooperation, the payoff-maximizing move is defection. Yet cooperation persists and recurs in networked populations. At the cooperation slots the one-shot prediction misses the observation. I deliberately describe that before saying which explanatory frame might close it, because "describe it before you go looking, or any frame you pick will seem to fit" (Fig 9 :27-28).
>
> **The shape claim, in four parts.** Any explanation that closes this residual must:
> 1. treat cooperation and defection as relations whose sign depends on the residual, not as fixed kinds of actor; a benefactor to one residual is a malfactor to another (Fig 11 :49-51);
> 2. live in the agent-and-habitat, not the agent alone; an improvement that does not change contact changes nothing, however much it improves (Fig 11 :39-40);
> 3. be a cycle, in which the output of one round re-enters as the input to the next, because a chain cannot close by construction (Fig 12 :121); the one-shot defection result is exactly such a chain;
> 4. be indexed per slot, not averaged into a cooperation rate, because aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average (Fig 1 :19, :22).
>
> **The question is a union of at least four overlapping conditions**, the payoff environment, the network habitat, the selection dynamics, and the measurement of what counts as emergence. I keep verdicts separate for each and do not average them into a single "evolutionary dynamics explains cooperation" percentage (Fig 9 :54-58). Whether it is kin selection, direct or indirect reciprocity, network reciprocity, group selection, punishment, or something else that occupies the slot, the framework does not say. It returns a kind of thing, never the answer itself (Fig 9 :32). I report that as a gap, not as silence and not as a name.
>
> **What I am not claiming:** that this refutes evolutionary dynamics, that a single mechanism wins, that any particular parameter threshold (relatedness, discount factor, benefit-to-cost ratio) follows from the framework, or that the result is settled. Those are bindings from the world literature, provenanced as such, not derivations. Agreement between models that share a payoff and fitness evidence pool is one pool talking, not two frames confirming each other (Fig 2 :40).
>
> **What would refute the claim.** I wrote these down before sending this:
> 1. a context-independent actor classification that predicts its effect across changed residuals, so valence is carried rather than residual-relative;
> 2. an agent-only change, with all contact variables held fixed, that raises stable cooperation at the ground;
> 3. a stable, costly-cooperation equilibrium in a one-shot interaction with no re-entry of output as input;
> 4. a demonstration that the four conditions partition the cases and one lever generalizes across all of them;
> 5. an aggregate cooperation rate that preserves the slot information needed to locate the live residual.
>
> If any holds, the corresponding part of the shape is wrong and I will retract it. A one-line "this fails because..." is more useful to me than agreement. If you would rather not engage, no reply is needed. It would help to record your verdict on the shape before showing you any source, so that later agreement is not pooled as corroboration.
>
> **If it survives, and only then:** the sources are below. The figures, not my summary, are the authority.
> - Verbatim figure text with line numbers (local framework extract): `FRAMEWORK/reference/03-FIGURES_TEXT.md`. Check Fig 1 :19, :22, Fig 9 :27-28, :32, Fig 11 :39-40, :49-51, Fig 12 :121.
> - Canonical figures usage rule (local): `FRAMEWORK/reference/FIGURES_README_canonical.md`.
> - Evolutionary games on graphs, Szabo and Fath, Physics Reports 2007: https://arxiv.org/abs/cond-mat/0607344
> - Food sharing in vampire bats, Carter and Wilkinson, Proc. R. Soc. B 2013: https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC3574350/
>
> **If you want to reuse the machinery:** the formula is in the local formula file. Run it on a problem you know well and can check against a ground. Write down what would refute your result *before* you run it, and state the mode of what you get. A reported failure is more useful to me than a reported success.
>
> Thanks,
> [name]

## Two-stage offer, stated plainly

1. **Stage 1.** Send the residual, the shape claim, and the refuter. Ask what the recipient would count as a failure. Record that verdict *before* anything else is shown.
2. **Stage 2, opt-in only.** If the recipient is curious, offer the figure extract and the two world links. Tell them the figures are the authority and the world sources are secondary corroboration only.
3. **Stage 3, also opt-in.** Offer the formula as a reusable method, with the instruction that each user writes their own refuter and states their own mode.

The Academy of Management and several journals in adjacent fields restrict or disclose AI-assisted submissions. Check the current policy of any venue before submitting simulated work; the constraint is recorded in `04_links_and_distribution.md` and `08_expert_verification_packet.md`. Nothing in this folder sends anything.
