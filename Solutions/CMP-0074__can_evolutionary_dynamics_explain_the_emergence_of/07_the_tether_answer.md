# The Tether's answer: cooperation in networks when defection pays now

**Mode: `specified` on the per-domain shape, `general` on the structural limit, `gap` on which mechanism occupies the slot. Not `grounded`.** This is a shape and a route, not a result. It is a simulated derivation checked for internal consistency against the figures (`05_what_shape_means.md` §6), not a ground reading.

## 1. The residual it answers

A one-shot payoff model predicts defection at every cooperation slot, because temptation exceeds mutual cooperation. Observation returns cooperation present and stable in networked populations. `R = |Γ(b,a) − o′|` has positive live mass at those slots. The question asks whether "evolutionary dynamics" closes that residual.

Before answering, the frame is held. Fig 9 :27-28: "describe it before you go looking, or any frame you pick will seem to fit." Fig 8 :53: "Residual first, frame second." The answer below is therefore about the **shape** any closing explanation must have, not about whether one named research programme wins.

## 2. The shape

1. **Cooperation and defection are relations, not actor kinds.** Fig 11 :49: "An actor's valence is relative to the residual, not a property it carries." Fig 11 :51: "No standing list of good actors survives a change of residual." The same act can be defection relative to a one-shot payoff and cooperation relative to a repeated-interaction payoff. "Emergence" means a relation whose sign flips when the residual is taken at the network and generational scale.
2. **The explanation is agent-and-habitat, not agent alone.** Fig 11 :39-40: "And capability is a property of agent-and-habitat, never of the agent alone. A system cannot be made more capable by improving the agent in isolation. An improvement that does not change contact changes nothing, however much it improves." The network is part of the closing term. Retuning a strategy while holding contact fixed is an agent-only improvement and does not change the ground.
3. **The one-shot result is a chain; the closing term is a cycle.** Fig 12 :121: "cycle. The output re-enters as input. The sign of the loop decides whether it closes, and a chain cannot close by construction." Defect, collect `T`, encounter ends: no re-entry, so the chain cannot produce a cooperative equilibrium. Cooperation requires the output of one round to re-enter as the input to the next, with positive sign. It also needs the comparison bond for the threshold (Operators table :52, "a goal is a comparison, which is why a vocabulary without it cannot state one") and a sequence bond across rounds (Fig 12 :72, "A then B; B needs A to have happened").
4. **The residual is indexed per slot, not averaged.** Fig 1 :19: "The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated." Fig 1 :22: "Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average." A cooperation rate can hide which nodes or dyads carry the live residual. The relevant unit is the node class or dyad, not the population mean.
5. **The question is a union of four overlapping conditions, not one question with a yes/no answer.** Fig 9 :54: "And when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again." The payoff environment, the network habitat, the selection dynamics, and the measurement of emergence each have their own anchor. Verdicts are kept separate (section 3) and never averaged.
6. **Which mechanism occupies the slot is a gap.** Fig 9 :32: "you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself." The figures do not select kin selection, direct or indirect reciprocity, network reciprocity, group selection, punishment, or conformist learning, and do not return a threshold value. That is `gap`, not silence and not a name (Fig 13 :134, "Unreachable is a verdict here, not a silence").

So the direct answer to "can evolutionary dynamics explain it" is: **yes as a shape, conditionally as a mechanism, and not as a settled verdict.** A frame whose closure already holds the graph and cycle atoms can produce a closing term by searching its own arrangement space; a frame that lacks them owes an import with provenance and a shadow test (Fig 6 :23, "No frame escapes its own closure. The bound moves only on contact with a frame whose closure differs."). Neither case is settled until a discriminating ground pays (Fig 2 :29).

## 4. What it explicitly does not say

- Not that evolutionary dynamics is false, and not that it is the unique frame. The literature supplies several closures; the question's frame-first phrasing is an apophenia risk (F1).
- Not that one mechanism wins, and not that any threshold value (`r > c/b`, `ω`, `q > c/b`) follows from the framework. Those are bindings from the world frame, provenanced as such.
- Not that aggregating cooperation into a rate is enough (F7).
- Not that model agreement settles the claim. Fig 2 :40: "disqualifying for a verdict: agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool."
- Not `grounded`. No measurement in this folder shows the residual falling.

## 5. What would settle it: the ground

The needed instrument discriminates among sources that produce the same cooperation reading. *The Hard Problem is About Source* (2026): "If several paths create the same behavior, then behavior is a projection with lossy information." Concretely:

- **Source discriminator.** Holding association constant, does giving track past donations (reciprocity), relatedness (kin selection), group membership (group selection), or reputation (indirect reciprocity)? Carter and Wilkinson (2013) did one version: "Food received was the best predictor of food given across dyads, and 8.5 times more important than relatedness."
- **Cycle test.** Does the same interaction history with ω driven to zero abolish stable cooperation while leaving one-shot payoffs unchanged? That would fire the cycle claim at the ground.
- **Contact test.** Change only the network (edges, degree, clustering) while holding the strategy rule fixed. If stable cooperation moves, the agent-and-habitat claim pays; if it does not, F3 is in trouble.
- **Slot test.** Does a pooled cooperation rate predict which node class carries the live residual, or does it erase the signal (Fig 1 :22)?
- **Standing refuters** from `01` section 4: context-independent valence, agent-only sufficiency, one-shot chain closure, union collapsed to partition, aggregation suffices, model agreement used as settlement, residual-first frame uniqueness.

## 6. The honest stop

The shape stops where the instruments stop. Fig 13 :134: "Unreachable is a verdict here, not a silence." The six-term set is "a human's careful guess at reality's compression axes", not nature's own list; declaring it makes the choice visible, not neutral. Another sensorium's minimum set need not be these six. The mechanism slot stays open and the lineage is recorded so the stop is checkable. Indiscernibility is not identity; a cooperation reading that cannot distinguish kin selection from reciprocity has not distinguished them.

## 7. One-paragraph version for outreach

> The framework does not name a winning mechanism and does not claim the question has a single yes/no answer. It says that the residual is a union of four overlapping conditions, so verdicts must be kept separate; that cooperation and defection are relations whose sign depends on the residual, not fixed kinds of actor; that any explanation lives in the agent plus its network habitat, so changing a strategy without changing contact changes nothing; that the one-shot defection result is a chain, while cooperation requires a cycle in which one round re-enters the next; and that the residual must be read per node or dyad, not as a pooled rate. It refuses to name the mechanism, because it returns a shape, not a name. What it offers is the shape any closing explanation must have, five refuters written in advance, and the discriminating instrument that would close the slot. The center stays open until the ground settles it.
