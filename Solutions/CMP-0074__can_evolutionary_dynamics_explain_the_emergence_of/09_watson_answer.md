## DOMAIN CENSUS

R by arity, symmetry, and scale, then one line per domain with actors, currency, anchor, partition or union, and dependency order.

- **R (the residual).** A one-shot payoff model predicts defection at each cooperation slot (`T > R > P > S`), while observation returns stable cooperation in networked populations. Arity: at least two, and in networks a set of N slots with pairwise edges and higher-order relations. Symmetry: the payoff matrix is symmetric across players, but the desired outcome (mutual cooperation) is a symmetric state that is not the one-shot equilibrium; selection is ordered in generations and the threshold question is a comparison. Scale: individual one-shot up to population-and-generational. Fig 9 :54, "And when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again."
- **D1, the one-shot incentive frame.** Actors: one actor choosing C or D. Currency: immediate payoff. Anchor: the realized payoff counter, not the declared matrix, which is a convention. Its prediction of defection is correct inside this frame and owes no refusal.
- **D2, the network habitat.** Actors: nodes, edges, and second-order relations. Currency: contact and interaction opportunity. Anchor: measured interaction structure, which does not move when a model changes. This is where the closing term lives (Fig 11 :39).
- **D3, the evolutionary-dynamics frame.** Actors: strategies and replicators in a population. Currency: fitness. Anchor: observed cooperation frequency over generations. Whether it owes an import depends on whether it already holds the graph and cycle atoms (Fig 6 :23).
- **D4, the verification frame.** Actors: models, instruments, experiments. Currency: residual and independent agreement. Anchor: the empirical cooperation record, which does not update on any model. In this simulated run all four domains draw on the same evidence pool, so their agreement is one pool talking, not corroboration.
- **Union, not partition.** The payoff environment, habitat, selection dynamics, and measurement overlap. One lever leaves the intersections standing (Fig 9 :57-58). Verdicts are kept per domain and never averaged.
- **Order by dependency.** Perceive the per-slot residual, acquire vocabulary for bonds and cycles, state what counts as emergence, construct the model, verify and carry. The break typically sits between vocabulary and objective, where there is often no instrument for emergence (Fig 3 :27).

## VERDICT (from the figures)

`Yes as a shape, conditionally as a mechanism, not as a settled verdict.` The question is a union of four overlapping conditions, so per-domain verdicts are kept separate and never averaged. Cooperation and defection are relations with residual-relative valence; the closing term is agent-and-habitat, chain-to-cycle, and slot-indexed; a payoff-only frame owes an import of the graph and cycle atoms, while a graph-based evolutionary frame already holds them and owes only more search. Which mechanism occupies the slot is a `gap` that a discriminating instrument would close. Mode `specified` on the shape, `general` on the limit, not `grounded`.

- **cost if wrong:** bounded and locatable. The packet states its mode, writes five refuters before the conclusion, and keeps the lineage, so an error is findable. The real cost is spending an outside reader's independence by sending a mechanism name before they have described the residual, which Fig 2 :42 prices as "Buy independence in proportion to the cost of being wrong." That cost is avoidable by leading with the shape claim and its refuter.
- **undoable:** yes. The mechanism name is withheld, so nothing has to be retracted to revise the answer. If a discriminator settles a mechanism, that is an addition with provenance, not a withdrawal of the shape. A sent claim can be superseded by a revised packet, and no primitive has to be removed.
- **reversed by:** a ground reading, not agreement. A source-discriminating instrument identifying reciprocity versus kin selection in a specified network; a cycle test in which driving the probability of future interaction to zero abolishes cooperation while one-shot payoffs are unchanged; a contact test in which a network-only change moves cooperation; an agent-only change with contact fixed that nonetheless raises cooperation; or a demonstration that the four conditions partition the cases under one lever. Any of these fires a refuter in `01` section 4 and moves the relevant domain. Until one does, the verdict stands as `candidate`.
