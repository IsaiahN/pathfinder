## Residual in effect terms

A chain of inference steps consumes the output of the previous step and emits a claim plus an uncertainty. The residual R is the gap between what the chain predicts at the final slot and what the ground returns, given that every intermediate step carries a residual that re-enters the next. The question is not how to shrink each step's uncertainty (a budget question) but what makes the composition land on the ground rather than drift coherently (a verdict question). Symbols table :38: *"It is the aim, not only the error signal"*.

An answer would look like: a set of conditions, each separately checkable, that a chain must satisfy for its final claim to count as reliable, with a stated refuter for each. Those conditions are bound as ordinary terms below (formula step "Direction") so the preference for a particular answer shape is refusable rather than invisible.

## What is deliberately not here

- No personal emails of named individuals.
- No ranking of people by how likely they are to agree with the claim.
- No bulk-send plan.

Three reasons, in the figures' own words:

1. **Agreement is not an anchor.** Fig 2 :35: *"Alignment is triangulation, not negotiation. A mirror cannot correct you."* A list selected for agreement manufactures the correlation that disqualifies a verdict (Fig 2 :40).
2. **Independence is spent by teaching the interior.** Fig 10 :42: *"A verifier that can reconstruct the claim cannot verify it."* The outside frame must not hold the framework's interiors before giving a verdict.
3. **Prestige does not settle a chain.** A famous name on a list is a contact-cost estimate, not evidence. Fig 2 :42: *"Buy independence in proportion to the cost of being wrong."*

Legally: scraping personal emails for unsolicited bulk outreach breaches GDPR and CAN-SPAM style rules in many jurisdictions. Public opt-in venues avoid that failure as well as the structural one.
