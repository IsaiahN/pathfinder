## 1. A shape is a constraint, not a filler

The canonical figures README states the limit: the framework returns *"what a missing thing would have to look like (a shape, not a name)"*. Fig 9 :32: *"you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself"*. Fig 13 states it can say the answer is out of reach with the library as it stands, and that *"Unreachable is a verdict here, not a silence"*.

From *The Library of Babel* (fetched this session): *"This means that you can cut your search short if the set of books returned is small and descriptive enough that it provides a general shape of the answer that satisfies your question."* And on the method: *"If you don't know the right terms to search for, you will have to use the existing vocabulary you do know that is closest to the words that you wish to describe and grasp the shape of."*

So a **shape** is the narrowed form of an answer you can state before you can name it. It constrains; it does not fill. For this question, the shape is: *reliability is a union of per-link conditions under an unmoved ground*. The particular implementations (process reward models, self-consistency, proof checkers) are names from other frames and are not licensed by the figures.

## 2. How a shape is obtained

- *Library of Babel*: use the vocabulary you already have that is closest to the thing, look for recurring new terms that overlap or correlate with it, recurse. *"With each new term gained, you narrow the space and deepen your vocabulary."*
- Fig 6 :30: *"The description travels. Fails by being sharpened into terms only we can read. Cast it in effect terms."* Fig 9 :30-31: *"your words are borrowed too, so describe the effect rather than the cause."*
- *Decoding Tacit Knowledge*: *"When contact isn't possible, the best you can send is a plain description of what you'd expect to see, because anyone can check that against the world."*
- *Library of Babel* on the tether: *"When traversing an infinite library, you need something to tether and anchor you to reality, the same way a mountaineer needs a tether when rappelling or climbing."* The anchor here is the question itself and the ground it points to.
- *Library of Babel* on stopping: *"You will know that you are done because the finish line will also be the starting place"*, and the paradox is that by then you have *"composed the answer through the process of locating it."*

## 3. What licenses a name

Only two things, both outside the shape itself:

1. **The ground settles it.** Formula step 5: *"the ground settles it. Verified only by what pays."* The README: the framework *"stops at the limit of what the available instruments can distinguish, and records the lineage so the stop is honest."*
2. **A provenanced import from a frame whose closure differs** (Fig 6 :23, Fig 8 :50-52), with a source gate and a shadow test.

*They're Scanning Books* sharpens this: *"The real import is not a new term in the existing space. It is a new dimension of the space."* And: *"Declaring the primitives does not make them neutral. It makes the choice visible."*

For this question, naming "self-consistency" or "process supervision" as *the* answer to reliability is an import from the ML frame. It may be useful, but it adds no atom to the figure closure and must be recorded as a binding, not as a derivation.

## 4. Why the record's likely error is the source error

*The Hard Problem is About Source* (fetched this session) gives the mechanism. An instrument reads green and *"the instrument is speaking only about what it measured, it cannot comment on the source"*. Several sources can give the same reading. *"If several paths create the same behavior, then behavior is a projection with lossy information"*. The resolution came from an instrument: *"It was proven wrong not by argument, but by the arrival of a better instrument."*

Applied here: a final accuracy number, a confidence score or a majority-vote agreement is a reading of a chain's behaviour. It does not comment on the source, that is, on whether the chain reached the ground or merely drifted coherently. Several very different chains produce the same final answer. The corresponding mistake is naming "high agreement" or "high confidence" as reliability from the reading alone. What is owed is a source-discriminating instrument (per-slot residual, per-link status, an independent anchor), not a name.

*The Recursive Self-Improvement Trap* (fetched this session) gives the failure mode in almost literal chain language: *"You may all agree and be wrong. You may set your clock to someone else's, who was setting your clock to yours, and be off by many deviations over time. You need ground truth to define where you stand."* A multi-step inference in which each step trusts the previous step is exactly a chain of clocks setting one another. Fig 7 :36 names the same thing: *"A chain of mirrors is one long room."*

## 5. Q - what does "shape" license for this question?

**The answerable question is not "what is reliability" but "what makes a chain capable of registering its ground".** The figures give a shape: reliability is a property of chain-and-habitat, not of the chain alone (Fig 11 :39); it is carried per slot and per link (Fig 1 :19); it requires settlement before composition (Fig 12 :104); and it needs an unmoved anchor and an outside checker (Fig 2 :29, Fig 10 :42). The particular number, architecture or method is not given.

## 5b. Correction to my own §3 and §4

I initially overstated the "shape, not a name" rule as a ban on naming any implementation. The README's phrase is *"what a **missing** thing would have to look like (a shape, not a name)"*; the subject is a primitive not held in the library. Naming a method that is already in scope (a verifier, a per-step residual) is a **binding** onto terms the framework already holds, not the invention of a new primitive. What remains forbidden is claiming the binding moves the wall (Fig 6 :22: *"IMPORT adds an atom"* - it adds none). The §4 metamerism point is **not** retracted: a final score still cannot comment on its source. Both claims are kept so neither is quietly lost.

## 6. Two consequences for outreach

**Prestige is not action.** From *The Recursive Self-Improvement Trap*: *"Prestige must never be allowed to validate an action directly... Prestige can fund an action. It can give an action a budget to be tried. But it cannot settle an action. Only the ground can settle it."* Gathering famous names to agree that a chain is reliable is the named collapse, not corroboration. The legitimate ask gives the claim a budget to be tried and lets a ground settle it.

**This audit is a simulation, not a ground reading.** Same essay: *"LLMs can quickly simulate an approximate answer/output... However, the truly testable output would need to be derived from the real architecture or working the formula yourself."* So `01` is a simulated derivation checked for internal consistency against the figures. It is not a test of the claim and must be labelled as such. It cannot settle anything it reports on.
