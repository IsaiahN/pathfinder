# Outreach draft: check one discrimination claim before you decide whether the framework is worth reading

**Use:** one-to-one, or as a cover letter attached to a submission through the public venues in `03_public_contact_points.csv`. **Not** for bulk send. No recipient is selected for likely agreement (Seat Map :163: *"High correlation is disqualifying for a verdict and efficient for a convention."*).

**Hard precondition:** do not send until the shape claim and its refuter in `01_figure_grounded_audit.md` are stable. The output must survive validation or the two-stage offer collapses on contact.

---

**Subject:** Check one output before you decide whether the framework is worth reading

> Hi [name],
>
> I have one specific output I would like you to check against what you already know. I am not asking you to read the framework first, and I am not asking you to agree with it. If the output does not survive, there is nothing else to look at.
>
> **The output (`specified`, not `grounded`):** a chemical signature is a reading, not a source. The same reading can be produced by biology and by abiotic chemistry. Fig 12 :5 puts it as *"A set of ingredients is not one. The bond is the rest of the information, and it is the half nothing was recording."* So the discriminator cannot be an ingredient list. It has to be an arrangement, an isomer or an enantiomer, a catalyst that survives its own use, or a cycle that closes where a chain cannot. Even then, one reading is not a decision: Fig 2 :35 says *"Alignment is triangulation, not negotiation. A mirror cannot correct you."* The decision needs two frames whose evidence pools do not overlap, both ranged against a measurement that neither can move.
>
> **What we are not claiming:** that oxygen, methane, an isotope ratio or an enantiomeric excess is *the* biosignature. Fig 9 :32: *"you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself."* The framework returns a shape, not a name. A particular molecule would be a binding from the chemistry frame, provenanced as such, and it carries no evidentiary weight until that provenance is shown. We are also not claiming that a null detection proves absence: Fig 8 :39: *"So a search that finds nothing is never a proof of absence."*
>
> **What would refute the shape claim, written before this message:**
> 1. A reading that uniquely determines its source with no habitat context, no second frame and no provenance.
> 2. Two frames drawing on the same evidence pool settling source attribution as reliably as two independent frames, or one bearing plus context sufficing with no second bearing.
> 3. An abiotic process composing the exact bond-level arrangement claimed diagnostic, under a declared habitat and budget, with the same behaviour under the instrument.
> 4. A null reading under a complete instrument set entailing no biological source.
>
> I wrote these down before sending this. If any one holds, the shape is wrong and I will narrow or withdraw it. A one-line "this fails because..." is more useful to me than agreement. If you would rather not engage, no reply is needed.
>
> **The question I am asking:** what would you count as a failure of that shape claim? I will record your answer before showing you any theory.
>
> **If it survives, and only then:** the sources are below. The figures are the authority, not my summary of them.
> - Catling et al., "Exoplanet Biosignatures: A Framework for Their Assessment" (2018): https://arxiv.org/abs/1705.06381
> - Meadows et al., "Understanding Oxygen as a Biosignature in the Context of Its Environment" (2017): https://arxiv.org/abs/1705.07560
> - NASA, "What Would It Take to Say We Found Life?" (2025): https://www.nasa.gov/science-research/planetary-science/astrobiology/what-would-it-take-to-say-we-found-life-we-asked-a-nasa-expert-episode-63/

Write down what would refute your result *before* you run it, and state the mode of what you get. The framework's own rule is that a loop cannot check the direction of its own question, so a flattering pre-registration is a real failure mode. A reported failure is more useful to me than a reported success.
