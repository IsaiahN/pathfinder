# The Tether's answer: fixed topology versus link dynamics

**Mode: `specified`, not `grounded`.** This is a decision shape and a route, not a measured result. Simulated derivation checked against the figures (`05_what_shape_means.md` section 6), not a ground reading.

## 1. The residual it answers

A network model predicts a process outcome from a belief about adjacency and an intervention. It keeps being wrong. The error is indexed per node trajectory and per candidate link, never averaged into one global fit (Fig 1 :19, Fig 1 :28). The choice between fixed and dynamic is the question of which term is owed: more process detail on the same graph, or a refusal of the assumption that the graph is constant. It is a **union compound**, not one hard problem: the process can be wrong, the graph can be wrong, or both (Fig 9 :57). Each condition has its own loop and its own anchor.

## 2. The shape

1. **Index the residual per slot.** A global fit near zero does not decide anything. The live signal is the specific link whose observed interaction keeps contradicting the assumed adjacency. Fig 1 :22: *"Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average."*
2. **Treat fixed topology as a held term, not a truth.** "Adjacency is constant over the window" is a term in the library. It is correct when the link-slot residual is held (Fig 5 :11) and the node residual trends down. It is the term to refuse when the link residual persists and does not trend down (Fig 5 :59). The refusal is unary (¬), not a new process parameter: *"MINT composes inside the closure and can never add an atom."* (Fig 6 :21).
3. **Unbundle before choosing a lever.** More process detail clears the cases where only the process was wrong. It leaves standing the cases where the wiring also changed. One lever cannot clear a union (Fig 9 :54-57).
4. **The membrane is the decision surface.** Fixed and dynamic are two scales of one system; only the actors, currency and anchor change (Fig 4 :20). Adjacency and duration are distinct structures (Fig 13 :36: *"adjacency is not duration, an ordering is not a pathway, a pathway is not a direction."*). Run the round trip: predict with a fixed graph and with edge dynamics against the same held-out observations, and measure the gap. *"So take the round trip and measure the gap. Without it you are navigating by dead reckoning: confident about a position nobody checked."* (Fig 4 :30).
5. **The two regimes are structurally different, not one a refinement of the other.** With timescale separation the settled process acts as a catalyst for slow rewiring (Fig 12 :119). With comparable timescales the coupling is a cycle, state re-enters topology and back (Fig 12 :121), and a chain of process parameters cannot close it by construction.
6. **The anchor is an edge observation.** The question is constitutively about whether links existed and changed, so a time-resolved edge measurement settles it (Fig 2 :29, :36). Agreement between two reconstructions fed the same node time series is one evidence pool, not two witnesses (Fig 2 :40).
7. **The framework returns a shape, not a universal number.** No figure line gives a critical timescale ratio. The threshold is system-specific and must be measured. The universal version is a declared `gap`, not a hidden answer (Fig 9 :32, Fig 13 :134).

## 3. What it explicitly does not say

- Not that any named network has fixed or dynamic topology. That is a measurement, and none was taken here.
- Not that topology is "real" or "emergent" in a metaphysical sense. X in the census has no nameable anchor and is not a domain.
- Not that link prediction accuracy or a better static fit settles the choice. A projection that cannot distinguish sources demonstrates its own inadequacy (*The Hard Problem is About Source*, read this session).
- Not a universal timescale threshold, not a universal number of links, not a universal window length.
- Not grounded. Nothing here was settled by an instrument.

## 4. What would settle it, the ground

A source-discriminating instrument, not a further argument. From *The Hard Problem is About Source*: an instrument that reads green *"cannot comment on the source"*; the spectrometer resolved it. Here the corresponding questions are:

- **Edge observation:** does a time-resolved edge record exist at a resolution where the node residual is already small? If not, the honest verdict is `unreachable` at this budget for that system, not "dynamic topology required" (Fig 6 :34-35).
- **Round-trip test:** does admitting formation and removal close the gap on held-out observations? If the gap is near zero, the extra terms are not owed (Fig 5 :44 bargain).
- **Slotted refusal:** is the link residual positive and flat on specific pairs while the node residual is small? If it is zero or held, the refusal is pointed at the wrong term.
- **Standing falsifiers from section 4 of `01_figure_grounded_audit.md`:** fixed graph drives link residual to held while node error persists; edge dynamics do not reduce the gap; one intervention clears both residuals with no categorically different residue; no edge instrument at any resolution.

## 5. The honest stop

**Unreached, not unreachable.** The decision shape is stated; the threshold for any given system is not. The missing thing is a source-discriminating observation, characterisable in effect terms but not held here. The answer stops at the limit of the available instruments and records the lineage so the stop is checkable. *"Indiscernibility is not identity."* (canonical README). A search that finds no universal threshold is not a proof that none exists (Fig 8 :39).

## 6. One-paragraph version for outreach

> The Tether does not declare a network's topology fixed or changing in general, and it does not give a universal switching threshold. It gives a decision procedure. Index the prediction error per node and per link instead of averaging it; when a link keeps being missed while the node trajectories are already well predicted, the owed term is a refusal of the assumption that the adjacency is constant, not another process parameter on the same graph. Unbundle that case from the case where only the process was wrong, because one lever does not clear both. Then run the round trip: predict with a fixed graph and with edge dynamics against the same held-out observations and report the gap. The gap is what the fixed description cannot hold, and a time-resolved edge observation is what settles it. With timescale separation the fast process behaves as a catalyst for slow rewiring; with comparable timescales the coupling closes into a cycle. The framework stops at the instrument limit, states four refuters written in advance, and records the lineage. No measurement was taken, so the result is `specified`, not `grounded`.
