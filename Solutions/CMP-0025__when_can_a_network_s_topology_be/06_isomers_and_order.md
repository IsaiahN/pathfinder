# Supporting derivation: links as arrangements, timescale order, and isomers

## RESIDUAL

The decision shape in `01_figure_grounded_audit.md` says that a persistent link residual points a refusal at the held constant-adjacency assumption rather than at a new process parameter. Two questions are owed before that shape can be stated precisely: (1) is a formed or removed link a new atom in the library or a new arrangement of atoms already held, and (2) what order and what structural relation connect a fixed-graph description to an evolving-graph description?

## DOMAIN CENSUS

Atoms held: nodes and their states (states in the six-term sense), plus the structure "network" (adjacency and influence travel). Bonds held: the seven, including sequence (→), production (⇒), subtraction (−), comparison (⋛), and the unary refusal (¬). Timescales held: process time and topology time, treated as two scales with a lossy membrane (Fig 4). No new primitive is available without import.

## DERIVATION

### Q1 — Is a new link a new atom? NO. CORRECTION TO MY OWN FIRST PASS.

- Fig 6 :21: *"MINT composes inside the closure and can never add an atom."*
- Fig 13 :115: *"The set is closed. The arrangements are not. That gap is why import exists."*
- A link between two nodes already in the library is a re-bonding of held atoms, not a new member of the six. The closure over the held node set already contains that arrangement.
- Therefore: the owed term when a link residual persists is **not** a minted primitive and **not** an import that moves the wall. It is a ¬ refusal of the term "adjacency is constant over this window", which removes the standing of that term to be composed over. *"Nothing leaves the library. What falls is the reach."* (Fig 5 :55).
- **I overstated this in the first pass**, where link formation was treated as a MINT. Retracted here and in `01` section 7. What actually moves is the reach, not the atom set. Triangulated (Fig 6 and Fig 13, distinct roles).

### Q2 — Are a fixed graph and its rewired version isomers?

- Fig 12 :117: *"isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."*
- A fixed adjacency A and a rewired adjacency A' over the same node set share every atom and differ in which bonds hold. By the figure's definition they are **isomers**: same atoms, different arrangement, different behaviour. A process can behave one way on A and another way on A' even though the ingredient list is identical.
- The stronger claim: the static model and the dynamic model are the same atom set with different bond vocabularies (one has only + adjacency, the other adds →, ⇒, −). That is a difference in the bond *vocabulary*, priced by Fig 12 :100-101, not a new atom. It is still an isomer-style relation at the level of descriptions.
- **Falsifier, written before the run:** if either description requires a node type or a bond type the other cannot express, the isomer claim fails and the two are different substances in the literal sense, not rearrangements. A graph with node turnover that introduces new node identities would test this.

### Q3 — What order connects the two descriptions? Two regimes, two structures.

Fig 12 :119: *"catalyst. A part that survives its own use. Spent and regenerating parts behave differently across uses, and nothing records which a term is."*
Fig 12 :121: *"cycle. The output re-enters as input. The sign of the loop decides whether it closes, and a chain cannot close by construction."*

- **Timescale-separated regime (process fast, topology slow).** The process settles into its quasi-attractor while the graph barely changes; the settled process then informs the slow rewiring. The process behaves as a **catalyst**: it survives its own use and becomes available to the next topological step. This is the regime the world literature calls quenched or timescale-separated (Gross and Blasius, read this session).
- **Comparable-timescale regime.** The output of the process re-enters as input to the topology and back again: Fig 12's **cycle**. The sign of the loop decides whether it closes. A chain that only adds process detail cannot close this loop by construction. This is exactly why F2 says another process parameter cannot answer a link-slot residual: the needed bond is a cycle, not a longer chain.
- **Falsifier:** a system with demonstrably comparable timescales whose state/topology coupling is still fully decomposed into one-way catalyst steps with no re-entry. That would collapse the two regimes and break the order claim.

### Q4 — Which bonds are needed, and what does a vocabulary without them lack?

- Fig 12 :46: *"A bond is the typed relation between two operands. There are seven, they are not interchangeable, and which one holds is not recoverable from the operands."*
- Operators: ⇒ is *"A produces B; B did not exist before"*; − is *"A with B removed or absent"*; → is *"A then B; B needs A to have happened"*.
- A vocabulary that holds only the symmetric adjacency bond (+) can name **a graph** and cannot name **a route from one graph to another**. Fig 12 :111: *"A vocabulary without bonds can name a state and cannot name a route to one."* Formation needs ⇒, removal needs −, and the ordering of the two needs →. This is the structural reason "link dynamics" is not just "a graph with a index t": it adds ordered bonds with typed semantics.
- **Falsifier:** a formation or removal rule that is fully expressible with the symmetric adjacency bond alone, with no ordering and no production or subtraction. None was found in the sources read; the claim remains `specified`.

### Q5 — Two readings of the price, held apart

Fig 12 :125: *"A composition costs in two currencies, and they do not add."* A time-indexed adjacency costs more bits to state and fewer actions to refute if edges are directly observed. A fixed adjacency is the reverse. Fig 12 :126: *"Bits price the description. Actions price finding out whether it holds."* A model-selection rule that sums them is unsupported by the figures. The two remain separate columns; the budget decides how much of the space is reached.

## CONCLUSION

A formed or removed link is a new **arrangement**, not a new atom. The static and evolving descriptions are isomers over a shared node set where the bond vocabularies allow it, with a stated falsifier in Q2. Their order is not a linear refinement: a timescale-separated system is a catalyst chain (fast process settles, slow graph changes), while a comparable-timescale system is a cycle (state re-enters topology and back), and a chain cannot close a cycle by construction. This rescues what is defensible in "when fixed, when dynamic" without importing a new primitive or naming a universal threshold. The decision still has to be settled at the ground by an edge-revealing observation.
