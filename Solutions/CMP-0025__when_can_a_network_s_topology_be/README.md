## The question restated as an effect

What is being decided is not whether a graph "really" has fixed links, but which residual is owed a term. A model predicts a process outcome on a network from a belief about adjacency and an intervention; the observation returns a state trajectory; R = |Γ(b,a) - o'| is measured per slot, where the slots are node-state trajectories and candidate links, never as one global fit (Fig 1 :19, Fig 1 :22, Fig 1 :28).

- Option A, "topology is fixed over the window": leads to the prediction that the link-slot residual is held (Fig 5 :11, *"held: the model was right, nothing owed"*) and that persisting error is process detail still to be added on the same graph.
- Option B, "link formation and removal belong in the dynamics": leads to the prediction that the link-slot residual persists and does not trend down while the node-state residual is already small, and that the owed term refuses the assumption of constant adjacency rather than adding another process parameter on the same graph (Fig 5 :59, Fig 6 :21).
- What an answer would look like, written down and bound as an ordinary term before the run (Formula, "Direction"): a **decision shape** that names (i) which slot residual is live, (ii) what the round trip across the process/topology membrane costs, and (iii) which instrument would settle the choice. That shape is the sought-for term. Until it is bound it predicts nothing and no check in the loop can see it. Fig 9 :32: *"you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself."*

The verdict recorded in `09_watson_answer.md` keeps the two regimes separate rather than averaging them: fixed topology is the correct treatment when the link-slot residual is held and the process residual trends down; link dynamics must enter the model when the link-slot residual persists and does not trend down and the round-trip gap closes when edge dynamics are admitted. Both clauses are `specified`, ungrounded. There is no universal numerical threshold in the figures; that number is a declared `gap` owed from an instrument.

## What is deliberately not here

- No personal emails of named individuals.
- No ranking of people by how likely they are to agree.
- No bulk-send plan.
- No claim that this derivation is `grounded`, no claim that it settles the network-science question, and no claim that the framework is the only account of network dynamics.

Three figure-grounded reasons, plus one practical one:

1. A room is not an anchor. *"A room's agreement is variation, not a verdict. Selection between rooms is where the ground votes."* (Fig 7 :35) and *"Alignment is triangulation, not negotiation. A mirror cannot correct you."* (Fig 2 :35). Gathering agreeable readers manufactures the correlation that disqualifies a verdict.
2. Correlation has valence. *"agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool"* (Fig 2 :40). Two reconstruction methods fed the same node time series are one witness wearing two names.
3. A verifier must be outside the interior. *"A verifier that can reconstruct the claim cannot verify it."* (Fig 10 :42). The first contact therefore carries the residual and its refuter, not the framework.
4. Legally, unsolicited bulk email to scraped addresses breaches GDPR and CAN-SPAM style rules in many jurisdictions and gets the sending domain blacklisted. Public opt-in routes avoid that failure as well as the structural one.
