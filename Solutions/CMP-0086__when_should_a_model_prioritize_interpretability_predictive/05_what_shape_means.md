## 1. A shape is a constraint, not a filler

The figures README states the limit: the framework returns *"what a missing thing would have to look like (a shape, not a name)."* Fig 9 :32: *"you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself."* Fig 13: *"It cannot say which primitive is missing, because naming it would mean already holding it."*

From *The Library of Babel*: *"This means that you can cut your search short if the set of books returned is small and descriptive enough that it provides a general shape of the answer that satisfies your question."* And: *"The problem you have determines what makes a book relevant to you or not."*

So a **shape** is the narrowed form of an answer you can state before you can name its contents. It constrains; it does not fill. For this question the shape is not "accuracy wins" or "interpretability wins." It is the four-row assignment itself: which condition binds which slot, which anchor legitimately settles it, which currency prices it, and which instrument would show the row is wrong. That is a constraint that any acceptable answer must satisfy, and it stops short of naming a universal winner.

A ranking would be a name. A per-slot assignment with refuters is a shape.

## 2. How the shape is obtained

- *Library of Babel* on bootstrapping vocabulary: *"With each new term gained, you narrow the space and deepen your vocabulary."* Here the starting vocabulary is the four words in the question itself. Proximity work is to replace "prioritize" with "binding condition," "anchor," "currency," and "discriminating instrument."
- Fig 6 :30: *"The description travels. Fails by being sharpened into terms only we can read. Cast it in effect terms."* Fig 9 :30-31: *"your words are borrowed too, so describe the effect rather than the cause."* So the four objectives are described by what they do to a residual, not by their disciplinary names.
- *The Hard Problem is About Source*: *"If several paths create the same behavior, then behavior is a projection with lossy information."* A model's accuracy reading cannot comment on which generator produced it. That is why mechanistic realism gets a shape and a gap, not a name.
- *Library of Babel* on stopping: *"You will know that you are done because the finish line will also be the starting place, as there is nowhere else to search and nothing else that can describe the answer better."* The four-row shape is done when each row has an anchor and a firing falsifier and no fifth row can be derived.

## 3. What licenses a name

Only two things:

1. **The ground settles it.** Formula step 5: *"the ground settles it. Verified only by what pays."* For intervention usefulness, the name is licensed by the outcome in the habitat; for accuracy, by the held-out measurement.
2. **A provenanced import from a frame whose closure differs** (Fig 6 :23, Fig 8 :50-52), with source gate and shadow test. Rudin's rule lists are such an import from the interpretable-model frame into the deployment frame, and they carry provenance rather than being a derivation from the figures.

*They're Scanning Books When They Should* (listed, not quoted this session) sharpens the same point: the import is a new dimension, not a new name in the existing space. It is cited here as part of the canonical set only, not as an independent witness.

## 4. Why the four-objective question is a metamerism question

*The Hard Problem is About Source* gives the mechanism. An instrument reads green and *"cannot comment on the source"*: one green light, or yellow plus blue, or RGB, or CMYK, or a scheme not yet discovered. *"The answer is unreached until a better instrument exists."* It was the spectrometer, not an argument, that resolved it.

The model-evaluation analogue is direct. A benchmark score is a reading. It does not disclose whether the model succeeded by a real mechanism, by a shortcut, or by a dependence on the deployment habitat. Lipton (2016) records the pneumonia/asthma case: the association was real, the mechanism was treatment intensity, and deploying the model changed the treatment. A source-discriminating design, not a further argument, would have caught it.

This is why "prioritize accuracy" cannot be the whole answer. Accuracy is the reading. The source question is a different question with a different anchor.

## 5. Q: Does "shape, not a name" forbid ranking the four objectives? ANSWERED

**Nothing in the framework licenses a universal first objective.** The shape that the figures return is a per-slot, dependency-ordered assignment. A universal ranking would be a name, and in addition it would be an aggregate that loses the live slot (Fig 1 :22) and a single lever on a union (Fig 9 :56). So the ranking is refused on three independent figure grounds, not one.

**What licenses a local priority?** Only a measured binding condition in a described slot. "Here, contestability is binding, so interpretability is the priority for this decision" is a `specified` claim with an anchor and a refuter. "Interpretability is always first" is a `general`-mode verdict with no ground, and is refused.

## 5b. Correction to my own §1 and §5

I initially wrote that "shape, not a name" forbids naming any of the four objectives as a priority. That overstates the limit. The README's phrase governs a **missing** thing, a primitive or generator not held in Γ. Predictive accuracy and interpretability are not missing: they have instruments already. A local, measured priority among held objectives is a binding, not the invention of a new primitive, and is allowed. What remains forbidden is the universal ranking (no ground), and naming a mechanism where no source-discriminating instrument exists (F8). Both readings are kept so neither is quietly lost. Triangulated: README plus Fig 2 :36-38 plus Fig 9 :32.

## 6. Two consequences for outreach

**Prestige is not action.** From *The Recursive Self-Improvement Trap*: *"Prestige can fund an action. It can give an action a budget to be tried. But it cannot settle an action. Only the ground can settle it. The Ground Maintainer must be blind to prestige."* A list of evaluation experts selected because they are likely to agree with the shape manufactures exactly the correlation that disqualifies their agreement (Fig 2 :40). The legitimate ask is a budget to try the shape against a ground, not an endorsement.

**My own audit is a simulation, not a ground reading.** Same essay: *"LLMs can quickly simulate an approximate answer/output with the formula... However, the truly testable output would need to be derived from the real architecture or working the formula yourself."* And: *"the output is only as good as the validator. The agent, or the tool, or the frame, or the theory, cannot validate itself."* So `01` is a simulated derivation checked for internal consistency against the figures. It is not a test of the question and must be labelled as such. It cannot settle anything it reports on.

## 7. Summary for this question

A shape here means: a four-row constraint on any acceptable answer about model objectives. It says that priority is per slot, dependency-ordered, anchored by question type, priced in two non-additive currencies, and honest about the mechanism gap. It does not say which word wins. That missing name is owed to a ground, and until a ground pays, unreached is the verdict, not a silence.
