# WATSON answer, CMP-0086

**Mode: `specified`, not `grounded`.** Figures are primary and decide. World sources are secondary, corroborate but never overrule, and a conflict returns the derivation to the domain census. This is a simulated derivation checked for internal consistency against the figures, not a ground reading.

## QUESTION

When should a model prioritize interpretability, predictive accuracy, mechanistic realism, or usefulness for intervention?

Restated as an effect: a model Γ predicts Γ(b,a), the world returns o′, and R = |Γ(b,a) − o′| is indexed per case (Fig 1 :19). The four objectives are four anchors against which a case's residual can be ranged. Option A says one objective is globally first; it leads to a single lever applied to a union, which clears singly-bound cases and leaves the compound standing. Option B says priority is a per-case, dependency-ordered assignment of objectives to legitimate anchors, priced in two currencies, with a declared gap where no discriminating instrument exists; it leads to a smaller, categorically different residue that can be described and checked.

## DOMAIN CENSUS

R by arity, symmetry and scale, then one line per domain with actors, currency and anchor. Partition or union, and order by dependency.

- **R (the residual).** Arity: at least four-place (Γ, b, a, o′), indexed by case s. Symmetry: priority is an ordered comparison between conditions, not a symmetric join; "better" is relative to an anchor. Scale: single prediction, model library, deployment habitat, and the social decision that deploys the model, with lossy transforms between (Fig 4 :20).
- **D1 Validation ground.** Actors: model, held-out data, measuring instrument. Currency: residual mass. Anchor: the measurement. Admitted.
- **D2 Reader's understanding.** Actors: model terms and bonds, human reader. Currency: description bits and cognitive chunks. Anchor: human judgment, legitimate only for the question about people. Admitted with scope.
- **D3 World's generative structure.** Actors: parts and activities of the target system, atoms and bonds of the model. Currency: invariance under intervention, cross-kind composition. Anchor: a source-discriminating instrument. Admitted only where such an instrument exists; otherwise shape only.
- **D4 Intervention habitat.** Actors: model, decision maker, affected system and people. Currency: actions and outcomes. Anchor: the patient outcome, market or board. Admitted.
- **X1 Single scalar model quality.** No nameable anchor, currency already belongs to D1 to D4. Not a domain yet, declined.
- **X2 Standing universal priority.** Valence is relative to the residual (Fig 11 :51). Absorbed into D1 to D4 per case.

**Union, not partition.** D1 and D4 overlap when a prediction changes its environment; D2 and D3 overlap when an interpretable structure is also causal; D2 and D4 overlap when an explanation must be contestable. **Order by dependency:** representation and vocabulary precede the objective, the objective precedes planning, and D3 is what both D1 and D4 range against (Fig 3 :19-20).

Strain test applied: interpretability as anchor versus as instrument was resolved by re-census (different questions, Fig 2 :36-38). The world-source conflict between Rudin and Lipton on the tradeoff was scoped to different model classes and stages, not merged. No figure strain remains.

## TETHER (primary, verbatim, per domain)

Cited against `FRAMEWORK/reference/03-FIGURES_TEXT.md`. Quotes are verbatim from the SVGs.

**D1 Validation ground, status triangulated (Fig 1 and Fig 2 and Fig 9, distinct roles).**

- Fig 1 :19 "The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated." -> priority is per case, not global.
- Fig 1 :23 "The ground enters as the only metric." -> accuracy is the only metric for the prediction slot.
- Fig 1 :27 "They are frame-internal. A frame cannot score itself with a quantity it also produces." -> coverage, compression and credibility cannot score the frame.
- Fig 9 :60 "Use filters for the budget and witnesses for the verdict. Never let a filter hand you a verdict." -> accuracy is a filter for what gets tried, not the verdict for the other three.
- Reading: accuracy is necessary and not sufficient. Mode `general` for the metric rule, `specified` for the application. Refuter: a held-out accuracy figure that also settles whether a mechanism is real or an intervention is safe.

**D2 Reader's understanding, status triangulated (Fig 2 and Fig 12 and Fig 10, distinct roles).**

- Fig 2 :37 "Human judgment is the right anchor for is this a good answer for a person, because the question is about people." -> interpretability is a genuine anchor for the question about a person.
- Fig 2 :38 "It is only an instrument for is this code correct, and instruments can be wrong on axes they do not measure." -> the same judgment is only an instrument for code correctness.
- Fig 12 :111 "A vocabulary without bonds can name a state and cannot name a route to one." -> a readable feature list is not an intervention guide.
- Fig 10 :18 "a declared meaning is not a grounded one" -> interpretability must be installable and violable, not declared.
- Reading: interpretability is first when a person must understand and contest, and is a dependency prerequisite otherwise. Mode `specified`. Refuter: human judgment shown to settle code correctness without an instrument, or an interpretable model with no ordered structure guiding a novel intervention.

**D3 World's generative structure, status `gap` (Fig 9 and Fig 13 and Fig 6, shape only).**

- Fig 9 :32 "you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself" -> the framework returns a shape for a missing generator.
- Fig 13 :134 "Unreachable is a verdict here, not a silence," -> the gap is stated, not hidden.
- Fig 6 :34-35 "Where nothing is, there is no edge to extend from" -> no instrument means no edge to sharpen.
- Reading: mechanistic realism is a `specified` claim only where a discriminating instrument exists; otherwise `gap`. Refuter: a source-discriminating instrument settling a specific generator.

**D4 Intervention habitat, status triangulated (Fig 11 and Fig 2, habitat vs anchor).**

- Fig 11 :39 "capability is a property of agent-and-habitat, never of the agent alone" -> usefulness is not a property of the model alone.
- Fig 11 :40 "An improvement that does not change contact changes nothing, however much it improves." -> accuracy without changed contact does not improve capability.
- Fig 2 :29 "An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks. A measurement, an experiment, a counter, a market, a patient outcome." -> the outcome is the anchor for intervention.
- Fig 12 :126 "Bits price the description. Actions price finding out whether it holds." -> intervention is priced in actions, not bits.
- Reading: usefulness for intervention is first when acting on the world is the binding condition. Mode `specified`. Refuter: capability gained through held-out accuracy alone across a habitat substitution.

**Union and method, status triangulated (Fig 9 and Fig 3 and Fig 12).**

- Fig 9 :55 "A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones."
- Fig 9 :56 "A single lever clears the region where only one condition holds and leaves the intersections standing, because the second condition still binds."
- Fig 3 :19 "Optimising a link that already works cannot rescue one that does not."
- Fig 3 :20 "The chain is ordered by dependency, not by importance."
- Fig 12 :130 "Summing them into one figure is the error this names, not the fix for it."
- Fig 2 :42 "Buy independence in proportion to the cost of being wrong."
- Reading: unbundle the union, work the lowest unreached link, keep two ledgers, buy low-overlap checks in proportion to cost of error. Refuter: a single scalar preserving all four per-case residuals including intersections.

Quote: *"The claim of the research should match the type of the evaluation."* Also: the need for interpretability stems from *"an incompleteness in the problem formalization."* Prediction: interpretability is anchored by the evaluation type, not universally. **Corroborates** D2 and F10. - **Machine learning, critique.** Zachary C. Lipton, *The Mythos of Model Interpretability*, arXiv:1606.03490, 2016, revised 2017. Opened 2026-11-22. Quote: *"the demand for interpretability arises when there is a mismatch between the formal objectives of supervised learning (test set predictive performance) and the real world costs in a deployment setting."* Prediction: the pneumonia/asthma deployment invalidates the model's own prediction. **Corroborates** D1/D4 overlap and F7. Same pool as the previous source.
- **Machine learning, high-stakes decisions.** Cynthia Rudin, *Stop Explaining Black Box Machine Learning Models for High Stakes Decisions and Use Interpretable Models Instead*, Nature Machine Intelligence 1, 206-215, 2019, preprint arXiv:1811.10154. Quote: *"It is a myth that there is necessarily a trade-off between accuracy and interpretability."* Prediction: local interpretability priority is not a concession to accuracy; the smooth tradeoff curve is fictional. **Corroborates** F3 and scopes the Rudin/Lipton conflict. Quote: *"Correlational patterns are not mechanisms"*; Woodward's condition requires a generalization *"invariant under interventions."* Prediction: a mechanism claim needs an intervention test, not a fit. **Corroborates** F8 from a genuinely low-overlap field.
- **Animal behaviour and human cognition.** Wikipedia contributors, *Cognitive map*, accessed 2026-11-22. Opened 2026-11-22. Quote: Tolman's rats took the correct route from a new starting point rather than executing a fixed turn; Bennett's critique says simpler alternatives *"cannot be ruled out in tests of cognitive maps in non-human animals."* Prediction: a route-capable representation differs from a fixed-response model, and source claims need discriminating designs. **Corroborates** F5 and F8 at low overlap. ## VERDICT (from the figures)

Priority is a per-case, dependency-ordered assignment of four objectives to four legitimate anchors, priced in two currencies, with a declared gap where no discriminating instrument exists. Accuracy is the only metric for the prediction slot and is necessary, not sufficient. Interpretability is an anchor for the question constitutively about a person and only an instrument for code correctness. Mechanistic realism is a shape until a discriminating instrument exists. Usefulness for intervention belongs to model and habitat, not to the model alone. When conditions overlap, unbundle the union, work the lowest unreached link, and never sum bits and actions into one score. Mode `specified`, ungrounded, candidate. Kept per domain; never averaged.

- **cost if wrong:** a model is optimized on the wrong slot. A binding interpretability condition is answered with accuracy, or a binding habitat condition is answered with a mechanism name, and the compound case stays unresolved. The bounded cost is misdirected evaluation effort and, in high-stakes deployment, real harm; Fig 2 :42 prices the remedy as independence bought in proportion to that cost. The cost is reducible by leading with the four-row shape and its refuters.
- **undoable:** yes. No term is deleted if a row fails; a refutation is added and the reach falls because something was added rather than because anything was deleted (Fig 6 :42-43). The assignment and its eight falsifiers are recorded with provenance so a later reader can locate the error (Fig 10 :62-64).
- **reversed by:** a ground reading, not agreement. A held-out measurement that settles a mechanism or an intervention in the intended habitat; an application-grounded interpretability test showing a sparsity proxy was sufficient; a discriminating instrument naming a generator where the framework said only a shape was available; a model gaining intervention capability through accuracy alone across a habitat substitution; a single scalar preserving all four per-case residuals including their intersections; or an importance-ordered scheme rescuing a broken upstream link. Until one fires, the verdict stands as `candidate`.
