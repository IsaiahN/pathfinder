## The question restated as an effect

What is being decided is not which of the four words is best in the abstract. It is which condition is binding on a given slot at a given stage of the dependency chain. A model is a library Γ; it predicts Γ(b,a); the world returns o′; and the residual R = |Γ(b,a) − o′| is indexed per slot s, never averaged (Fig 1 :19, Fig 1 :22). The four objectives are four different anchors against which R can be ranged (Fig 2 :29), and they form a union of overlapping conditions, not a ranking (Fig 9 :54-57).

- Option A, "a model should have one priority, for example accuracy first": leads to the prediction that a single lever clears the problem. Under Fig 9 :56 this lever clears the region where only one condition holds and leaves the intersections standing, because the second condition still binds.
- Option B, "priority is assigned per slot and per dependency stage, with each objective ranged against its own legitimate anchor": leads to the prediction that unbundling the union produces a smaller, categorically different residue that can be described and checked (Fig 9 :57).
- What an answer would look like, written down and bound as an ordinary term before the run (Formula, "Direction"): a four-row assignment, one row per objective, each row naming (i) the slot whose residual is live, (ii) the anchor that legitimately settles that slot, (iii) the currency in which that objective is priced, and (iv) the instrument that would show the assignment failed. Until this shape is bound it predicts nothing and no check in the loop can see it.

The verdict recorded in `09_watson_answer.md` is Option B in `specified` mode, kept per domain and never averaged.

## What is deliberately not here

- No personal emails of named individuals.
- No ranking of people by how likely they are to agree.
- No bulk-send plan.
- No claim that this derivation is `grounded`, no claim that it settles the modeling question, and no claim that one of the four objectives is universally first.

Three figure-grounded reasons, plus one practical one:

1. A room is not an anchor. *"Alignment is triangulation, not negotiation. A mirror cannot correct you."* (Fig 2 :35). Gathering agreeable readers manufactures the correlation that disqualifies a verdict.
2. Correlation has valence. *"disqualifying for a verdict: agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool"* (Fig 2 :40). A list selected for receptiveness is one witness wearing many names.
3. A verifier must be outside the interior. *"A verifier that can reconstruct the claim cannot verify it."* (Fig 10 :42). The first contact therefore carries the residual and its refuter, not the framework.
4. Legally, unsolicited bulk email to scraped addresses breaches GDPR and CAN-SPAM style rules in many jurisdictions and gets the sending domain blacklisted. Public opt-in routes avoid that failure as well as the structural one.
