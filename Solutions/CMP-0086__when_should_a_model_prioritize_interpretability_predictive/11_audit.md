## Independent domain census

I redid the census before reading the primary's conclusions, using the WATSON order: R by arity, symmetry and scale; one line per domain with actors, currency and anchor; partition or union; order by dependency. I started from the residual line in the source file alone: *"When should a model prioritize interpretability, predictive accuracy, mechanistic realism, or usefulness for intervention?"*

- **R (arity / symmetry / scale).** A model Γ predicts Γ(b,a), the world returns o′, and the residual is indexed per case. Arity: four-place, indexed by slot, so a "priority" relation is a comparison between conditions. Symmetry: ordered comparison, not a symmetric join; every "better" is relative to an anchor. Scale: prediction, library, habitat, social decision, with lossy transforms. Not one number, so not one ranking.
- **D1 Validation ground.** Actors: model, held-out data, instrument. Currency: residual mass. Anchor: the measurement. Admitted.
- **D2 Reader's understanding.** Actors: model terms and bonds, human reader. Currency: description bits. Anchor: human judgment, legitimate only because the question about a person is about people. Admitted with scope.
- **D3 Generative structure.** Actors: parts and activities of the target system. Currency: invariance under intervention. Anchor: a source-discriminating instrument. Admitted only where such an instrument exists; otherwise shape only.
- **D4 Intervention habitat.** Actors: model, decision maker, affected system. Currency: actions and outcomes. Anchor: the outcome. Admitted.
- **X1 single scalar model quality.** No nameable anchor. Not a domain yet.
- **X2 standing universal priority.** Valence is relative to the residual. Absorbed.

My independent result is a **union** with D1/D4, D2/D3 and D2/D4 overlaps, and a **dependency order** (representation to objective to planning, with the generator ranged against both). ## Round trip gap R_T

The Symbols table defines R_T as the gap between what was sent up and what came back down, `gap(x, (T_E ∘ T_A)(x))`, measuring what the coarser description cannot hold. Fig 4 :30 requires it to be measured rather than assumed. I ran the round trip on the condensation the folder actually uses.

1. **Sent up (x):** the verbatim figure set, chiefly Fig 9 :55-57 (union), Fig 1 :19 and :22 (per-slot residual), Fig 2 :36-38 (anchor legitimacy), Fig 11 :39-40 (contact), Fig 12 :125-130 (two currencies).
2. **Abstraction (T_A):** the shorthand in `07`: four objectives, four anchors, two ledgers, lowest unreached link first, gap where no instrument exists.
3. **Concretisation (T_E):** a concrete deployment claim such as "for this model, interpretability is the priority because the decision is contestable, and accuracy is sufficient."

**R_T is nonzero.** Three things the shorthand holds do not survive the trip back down:

- the **scope of the human-judgment anchor**: the shorthand says "interpretability is an anchor"; the concrete claim must specify the question is about a person, or it becomes a `general`-mode verdict that Fig 2 :38 refuses;
- the **contact condition**: the shorthand says "usefulness belongs to model and habitat"; a concrete claim that names only the model loses the habitat and the claim becomes untestable under substitution;
- the **two-currency separation**: the shorthand says "keep bits and actions apart"; a concrete release criterion tends to recombine them into one score, which is the flat-formula error.

Conversely, one thing survives cleanly: a union of overlapping conditions, per-case residuals, and a dependency order. That is the residue worth sending. R_T was **not** measured numerically, because no ground reading exists; it is recorded qualitatively as the enumerated set of dropped constraints. Treating "the formula is the condensed version of the metalaws" as identity would set R_T to zero and would be the dead-reckoning error Fig 4 :30 names. The honest form is a lossy condensation with a recorded gap, which is what `09_watson_answer.md` states.
