## DERIVATION

### Q1 - Union or partition?

- Fig 9 :55: **"A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones."**
- Fig 9 :54: **"And when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again."**
- Fig 9 :57: **"What remains is smaller and it is also categorically different: a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either."**

The question names three changes: species, relationships, conditions. These overlap. Species loss changes relationships; a changed condition changes which relationships matter. A single lever (species richness) clears the region where only one condition holds and leaves the intersections standing. Therefore: **union**. Three loops plus a compound loop at the intersection, each with its own ground.

- **Falsifier, written before the run:** a single mechanism invariant across all three condition changes, demonstrated at the ground, collapses the union to a partition. Until then, a study that averages the three conditions is measuring a compound it has not described.

### Q2 - What is the order of the cascade?

- Fig 11 :44: **"List everything in contact with the residual, then what is in contact with those, and outward until the cascade stops mattering."**
- Fig 11 :52: **"Scoping the habitat to direct contact is how DDT reached the eagles: first-order enumeration, second-order effect, and the residual that mattered was three steps out."**
- Fig 11 :54: **"Carson, Silent Spring, 1962. The mechanism, biomagnification through the food chain to eggshell thinning, was established over the following decade."**

The order is not one of importance; it is one of contact. A first-order study does not produce a weak version of the third-order result. It produces a reading of a different object. Fig 3 :22-23: *"A step whose input never arrives cannot be diagnosed, only its predecessor can. Which is why a reading taken below the break is not a weak reading. It is a reading of nothing."*

This is corroborated at low overlap by the *Biomagnification* page: DDT accumulates in adipose tissue, biomagnifies up the food chain, and caused eggshell thinning and declines in bald eagles and peregrine falcons. The world source and the figure agree on the direction of travel (outward, up the chain), not merely on the fact of the effect.

- **Falsifier:** a first-order-only scope that predicts the same adaptation failures as a cascaded enumeration across several independent perturbations would remove the order requirement.

### Q3 - Are two ecosystems with the same species set isomers?

- Fig 12 :117: **"isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."**
- Fig 12 :95-96: **"Two entries with an identical ingredient set are not variants of one thing. They are different things whose notation cannot tell them apart, and the difference is carried entirely by the bond."**
- Fig 12 :124: **"functional group. A substructure whose presence fixes the outcome. The group says what kind; the qualifier says which member."**

Take two systems with the same actor set but different relation structure: a pollination network that is nested (specialists linked to generalists) versus one that is compartmentalized (relatively independent sub-networks). The *Ecological network* page documents both structures and notes that the level of nestedness "can be predicted by a dynamic adaptive model with species rewiring." Same atoms, different bond, different behaviour under perturbation. By Fig 12's definition they are **isomers**, not variants of one system. A species list cannot tell them apart; the relation structure carries the difference.

The same test separates two ideas often conflated in resilience work: functional redundancy and response diversity. If both are written over the same species set and the same function, they are isomers: the bond (which species responds to which change) is the difference, and it is not recoverable from the roster.

- **Falsifier, written before the run:** if two systems held to be isomers can be interconverted without changing any relation, or if their behaviour under perturbation is identical across several changes, the isomer claim fails and the functional-group reading stands instead: same kind, different member.

### Q4 - Cycle and catalyst in the ecological case

- Fig 12 :121: **"cycle. The output re-enters as input. The sign of the loop decides whether it closes, and a chain cannot close by construction."**
- Fig 12 :119: **"catalyst. A part that survives its own use. Spent and regenerating parts behave differently across uses, and nothing records which a term is."**

Positive feedbacks in a regime shift are cycles: phosphorus recycling keeps a lake turbid after nutrient inputs fall (the *Regime shift* page describes this as discontinuous change with hysteresis). A beaver dam is a catalyst-like part: the structure persists after the animal's action and changes the selection pressure (the *Ecosystem engineer* page: beavers alter channels, geomorphology and ecology). A catalyst and a spent part read the same in a single snapshot; only the record of uses separates them. This is why Fig 11 :63 says *"Which is why a managed ecosystem is permanent work rather than a solved state."*

- **Falsifier:** a feedback part that is consumed by its own use and still produces the same cross-use effect would break the catalyst reading; a loop whose output does not re-enter would break the cycle reading.

### Q5 - The cross-scale round trip R_T

- Fig 4 :30: **"So take the round trip and measure the gap. Without it you are navigating by dead reckoning: confident about a position nobody checked."**
- Fig 4 :31-32: **"The gap is not an error to eliminate. It is the measurement of what the coarser description cannot hold, and it is the only honest report of how much was given up."**
- Symbols table :75-78: R_T is *"The gap between what was sent up and what came back down"*, `gap(x, (T_E ∘ T_A)(x))`.

Sent up (`x`): the per-slot, perturbation-linked record of a lake's function. Abstraction (`T_A`): "the lake is resilient." Concretisation (`T_E`): a prediction about a specific bay under a specific phosphorus load. Things dropped in the trip: which slot carried the residual; which relation rewired; whether the turbid basin's feedback had already closed; which scale supplied the phosphorus. Walker et al. 2004 state the same dependence from the world side: "No SES can be understood by examining it at only one scale."

`R_T` is nonzero. It is not measured numerically here because no ground reading was taken in this simulation; it is recorded qualitatively as the enumerated set of dropped constraints. Treating "the lake is resilient" as a complete description would set `R_T` to zero and would be the dead-reckoning error Fig 4 names.

- **Falsifier:** a round trip in which the abstracted statement predicts every slot-level outcome after concretisation, with no dropped constraints, would set R_T to zero and remove this requirement.

## CONCLUSION

The question is a **union** of three overlapping conditions, not one problem. The cascade is **ordered by contact**, and a first-order scope is a study of a different object, not a weaker study of the same one. Two systems with the same species set but different relation structure are **isomers** under Fig 12, so a roster cannot answer the question; the bond carries the difference. Cycles and catalysts are present and their identity is a record question, not a snapshot question. The cross-scale round trip has a nonzero gap that must be reported, not assumed away.

Every claim here is `specified` and unmeasured. The falsifiers were written with the claims, so all five are refusable by the same test as any other term.
