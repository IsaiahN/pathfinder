# Expert verification packet: what evidence would show ecosystem resilience?

**What this is:** a claim in a form an ecologist can check. Mode, derivation and refuters are stated before the conclusion, so a reader knows what to look for and what would settle it.

**Mode: `specified`, not `grounded`.** No field measurement is claimed. This is a shape and a route, not a result.

**Status of this packet:** a simulated derivation checked for internal consistency against the figures, not a ground reading. It cannot settle the question it reports on.

## 1. The claim, in one paragraph

An ecosystem being intact at a time is not the same claim as its being able to adapt as species, relationships, and conditions change. The evidence that would discriminate them is a kind of record: per slot and per scale, enumerating actors, conditions and relations outward along the cascade, taken before and after a change, with recovery and warning-signal readings from instruments already in use, triangulated across frames with declared low overlap against an outcome none of them can move. A single aggregated intactness score, a fixed species roster, or a mesocosm result does not have that shape. The framework returns a shape, not a name, and refuses to set a threshold or a species list.

## 2. Three checkable sub-claims

### C1 - Framework-internal; checkable by reading Figures 1 and 11

- **Statement:** a global reading can be near zero while one slot carries the deciding residual, so aggregating across slots hides the signal the question asks for; and the habitat must be enumerated outward along the cascade, not scoped to direct contact.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 1 :13 *"a global R near zero with one live slot residual is a legal state"*; Fig 1 :22 **"Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average."** Fig 11 :43 **"The habitat is enumerated, never composed."** Fig 11 :52 **"Scoping the habitat to direct contact is how DDT reached the eagles: first-order enumeration, second-order effect, and the residual that mattered was three steps out."**
- **Refuter:** a single-time aggregated intactness index predicts post-perturbation adaptation as well as a per-slot, perturbation-linked record on independent low-overlap data; or a direct-contact-only scope predicts the same adaptation failures as a cascaded enumeration across several perturbations.

### C2 - Ecology-facing; checkable by an ecologist against their own system

- **Statement:** capability is a property of the system-and-habitat contact, not of the system read alone, and valence is relative to the function at issue, so no fixed species roster can carry the answer. Two systems with the same actor set but different relation structure are isomers and behave differently.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 11 :39 **"capability is a property of agent-and-habitat, never of the agent alone"**; Fig 11 :49 **"An actor's valence is relative to the residual, not a property it carries"**; Fig 11 :50 **"mutualism and parasitism are relations rather than kinds"**; Fig 12 :117 **"isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."**
- **What to check:** name a function in your system. Can the same actor be a benefactor under one change and a malfactor under another? Can you find two sites with the same species set but different interaction wiring whose post-disturbance trajectories differ? If yes, the roster reading fails and the relation reading stands. The *Ecological network* page documents nested versus compartmentalized structures and adaptive rewiring; it is corroboration, not authority.
- **Refuter:** a fixed roster predicts adaptation across changed residuals including a case where a former benefactor becomes a malfactor; or an agent-only improvement moves the post-perturbation outcome with habitat contact held fixed.

### C3 - Framework-internal derivation about what counts as evidence

- **Statement:** an anchor that moves with a frame is not an anchor, agreement among frames sharing an evidence pool is not corroboration, and no warning signal found is not proof of resilience.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 2 :29 **"An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks."** Fig 2 :40 **"disqualifying for a verdict: agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool"**; Fig 8 :39 **"So a search that finds nothing is never a proof of absence."** Fig 9 :60 **"Use filters for the budget and witnesses for the verdict. Never let a filter hand you a verdict."**
- **Refuter:** the proposed ground moves when a monitoring frame pushes on it; or a closed-system proof of a single basin of attraction for the function at issue makes "no warning" a proof of resilience.

## 3. What is explicitly not claimed

- Not a resilience score, a threshold, or a single index.
- Not that any species, trait, or functional group confers resilience.
- Not that a mesocosm, a model, or a restored site proves field capability. Fig 11 :70: **"A test harness is a substituted habitat. Which is why a synthetic solve proves wiring and never capability."**
- Not that absence of early-warning signals proves resilience.
- Not that species change, relationship change, and condition change are one problem.
- Not `grounded`. Nothing here was settled by a measurement.
- Not that the Tether's habitat is the ecological niche. The architecture article declines that mapping.

## 5. What would settle it

A ground reading, not agreement. In practice:

1. A per-slot before/after record across a real perturbation, with recovery rate and distributional signals read on each slot and scale.
2. An instrument that discriminates among the sources of a single function reading (for example redundancy versus response diversity, or a rewired relation versus a stable roster). The source essay: an instrument that reads green *"cannot comment on the source"*; "The answer is unreached until a better instrument exists."
3. Field observation, experiment, remote sensing, local knowledge and models ranged separately against the same outcome, with overlap recorded.
4. A substitution audit naming what was not reproduced and what was introduced for any model or mesocosm.
5. The four refuters in section 2 actually run and reported, including a negative result.

## 6. What an expert can check in twenty minutes

1. Read Fig 1 :13, :19, :22 and Fig 11 :43, :49, :50, :52, :70. Eight short passages.
2. Name one function in a system you know. Ask whether a fixed species roster would have predicted its behaviour across the last two changes it underwent. If not, C2 holds.
3. Ask whether the monitoring programme you know best averages across slots or scales. If it does, C1 describes a real blind spot.
4. Name one instrument that would distinguish two sources of the same function reading, or say why none is possible. That is what would settle C1 and C2 at the ground.
5. Send the refutation, not the endorsement. A failure is more useful than an endorsement.
