## 0. The residual in effect terms

The question asks for evidence that separates two states of one ecosystem:

- **State A, "intact today":** the current reading of the system is whole. Expected species are present, expected relations are visible, measured functions sit near a reference level. This is a snapshot. It is a reading of the system at a time.
- **State B, "resilient":** the system keeps its function and identity while species, relationships, and conditions change. This is a capacity that registers only at the contact between the system and a changing ground.

The residual **R** is the gap between the reading that says "intact" and the claim "able to adapt." Stated as an effect: **what observation would discriminate A from B, such that a system could read intact and still be found brittle, or read disturbed and still be found capable?**

The question is not asking for a resilience number. It is asking for a discriminator. The framework's own limit applies: *"you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself"* (Fig 9 :32). What an answer would look like, bound as an ordinary term before the run (formula step, "Direction"): **a per-slot, across-scale, perturbation-linked record of bounded residual in the enumerated habitat, ranged against an anchor that does not move, with the cascade and any habitat substitution written into the lineage.** That is the shape this audit tests. It is written down here before the figure census so it can be refused by the same test as everything else.

Two operations follow from the wording. "Remains" makes the claim longitudinal, not instant: Fig 11 :63 says *"Which is why a managed ecosystem is permanent work rather than a solved state."* "As species, relationships, and conditions change" makes the claim about contact under change, not about composition at rest: Fig 11 :40 says *"An improvement that does not change contact changes nothing, however much it improves."*

## 0b. Domain census (done before any figure line was chosen)

**R by arity, symmetry, scale.**

- **Arity:** many slots. Species, interactions, abiotic conditions, and functions are tracked separately. Fig 1 :19: *"The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated."* Symbols table :43-44: *"R is measured per slot, never as a single global number."* The arity of R is the number of live slots in the habitat, not one.
- **Symmetry:** asymmetric and directed. Influence travels along relations and cascades outward (Fig 11 :52). Valence is not carried by an actor: *"An actor's valence is relative to the residual, not a property it carries"* (Fig 11 :49); *"mutualism and parasitism are relations rather than kinds"* (Fig 11 :50). A symmetric sum over species destroys the question.
- **Scale:** multi-scale with a lossy transform between levels. Species, population, community, landscape, and region each close their own loop; a reading carried across a scale carries a round-trip gap (Fig 4 :30-32). Fig 13 :31 names the amplifying case: *"a remainder compounding through a chain that amplifies is chaos."*

**One line per domain: actors / currency / anchor.**

| domain | actors | currency | anchor (nameable?) | question type |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| D1, the ecological ground | species, conditions (light, water, temperature, season, terrain), relations (pollination, predation, competition, dependency) | contact: survival, reproduction, function performed, recovery from perturbation | yes: the observation or experiment that does not move when a frame changes its mind (Fig 2 :29) | union compound of three overlapping conditions |
| D2, the observation and indicator frame | field surveys, remote sensing, indices, models, Indigenous and local knowledge | readings, intactness scores, early-warning statistics | only when pinned to D1's ground; an index definition alone is a convention, and *"a declared meaning is not a grounded one"* (Fig 10 :18) | frame in danger of losing its anchor |
| D3, the perturbation and verification frame | disturbances, natural experiments, controlled manipulations, extreme events | the residual under change: recovery rate, variance, autocorrelation, flickering, persistence of function | yes: the ground's response after the perturbation, which does not consult any frame | the domain where "adapt as conditions change" registers |
| D4, the management seat (conditional) | managers, stakeholders, rules, introduced actors | conventions and decisions | yes, but only as outcomes in D1; agreement among managers is not an anchor | present only where a maintainer exists; otherwise pure contact |

D4 is a partition boundary: unmaintained habitat is pure contact and *"nothing selects, and what survives is what the ground kept"* (Fig 11 :56); maintained habitat has a seat that acts (Fig 11 :58). Both are domains for this question because the question does not say which; the answer must say which one it is in.

**Partition or union.** Union, not partition. The question bundles three overlapping conditions: species change, relationship change, condition change. Fig 9 :55: *"A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones."* Fig 9 :54: *"And when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again."* A single lever such as species richness clears the region where only one condition holds and leaves the intersections standing. So the three conditions get separate loops, and the compound gets its own loop at their intersection.

**Order by dependency (Fig 3).** (1) perception: can the rare and hidden interactions be seen at all; (2) vocabulary: are there terms for what species and relations afford (Fig 3 :7: *"it has terms for what things afford: what can be moved, opened, joined, blocked"*); (3) objective: can we say what counts as progress in our own terms (Fig 3 :10: *"it can say what would count as progress here, in its own terms"*); (4) planning: a perturbation design under incomplete information; (5) learn and carry: verify and carry to the next system. The chain usually breaks between 2 and 3: Fig 3 :27 says *"A link with no instrument is not a link that works. It is a link nobody has looked at."* "Resilience of what, to what" has no single operational definition, which is exactly a break at link 3.

## 2. Findings

### F1 - An intactness snapshot cannot answer the question (decisive for the contrast)

Fig 1 :13: **"a global R near zero with one live slot residual is a legal state"**. Fig 1 :22: **"Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average."** Fig 1 :23: **"The ground enters as the only metric."** Symbols table :38-40: *"It is the aim, not only the error signal."*

A system can read intact in every headline indicator while one slot carries a positive residual that will decide its fate under change. The question explicitly asks for the contrasting case, so the evidence cannot be a single aggregate score at one time. It must be indexed by the species, relation, condition, and function slots that the snapshot averages over.

**Refuter:** a single-time aggregated intactness index that predicts post-perturbation adaptation as well as a per-slot, perturbation-linked record, on data where the two frames share no evidence pool, collapses this finding.

### F2 - The habitat must be enumerated, and the cascade must be included

Fig 11 :43: **"The habitat is enumerated, never composed."** Fig 11 :44: **"List everything in contact with the residual, then what is in contact with those, and outward until the cascade stops mattering."** Fig 11 :46: **"conditions: light, water, temperature, season, terrain"**. Fig 11 :47: **"relations: pollination, predation, competition, dependency"**. Fig 11 :48: **"You do not invent the list. You read it off the world, and what you cannot perceive or measure yet is the residual."**

Evidence of resilience must name the actors, conditions, and relations in contact with the function of interest, then follow second- and third-order effects. A first-order-only scope is not a smaller version of the same study; it is a study of a different object.

**Refuter:** showing that a direct-contact scope predicts the same adaptation failures as a fully cascaded enumeration, across several independent perturbations, removes the cascade requirement.

### F3 - Resilience is capability at the contact, not a property read off the system alone

Fig 11 :39: **"capability is a property of agent-and-habitat, never of the agent alone"**. Fig 11 :40: **"An improvement that does not change contact changes nothing, however much it improves."** Fig 11 :63: **"Which is why a managed ecosystem is permanent work rather than a solved state."** Fig 11 :73: **"the habitat is not the ground. The habitat is where actors come from and it updates; the ground is what settles, and it does not."**

This is the figure-level answer to the wording "able to adapt as ... conditions change." Adaptation is a property of the system-plus-habitat contact observed across a change, not a trait measured while the habitat is held still. The verb "remains" is the same claim as permanent work.

**Refuter:** an agent-only improvement (a trait, a diversity count, a genetic measure) that moves the post-perturbation ground outcome, with habitat contact held fixed, refutes this capability claim. Fig 11 :71-72 itself names this as the standing refuter: *"an agent-only improvement that moved the ground would refute the capability claim."*

### F4 - Relations carry the answer; a standing species list does not

Fig 11 :49: **"An actor's valence is relative to the residual, not a property it carries."** Fig 11 :50: **"mutualism and parasitism are relations rather than kinds"**. Fig 11 :51: **"No standing list of good actors survives a change of residual."** Fig 13 :24: **"that entities relate, and influence travels"** (the figure's definition of the structure *network*).

So the evidence cannot be "species X is present" or "trait Y is abundant" as a fixed criterion. The same actor can be a benefactor for one function and a malfactor for another. What persists across changes is a relation structure that can rewire, not a roster.

**Refuter:** a fixed roster of species or traits that predicts adaptation across several changed residuals, including one where a former benefactor becomes a malfactor, removes the relation requirement.

### F5 - The anchor does not move, and agreement is not corroboration

Fig 2 :29: **"An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks."** Fig 2 :31: **"Independence, between two frames, is how little they draw on the same evidence."** Fig 2 :35: **"Alignment is triangulation, not negotiation. A mirror cannot correct you."** Fig 2 :40: **"disqualifying for a verdict: agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool"**. Fig 9 :60: **"Use filters for the budget and witnesses for the verdict. Never let a filter hand you a verdict."**

Field observation, experiment, remote sensing, Indigenous knowledge, and models count as independent witnesses only to the degree that they draw on different evidence. Several indices computed from one monitoring dataset are one frame wearing several names. The anchor is the post-perturbation ground outcome, not the index definition. Fig 10 :18: **"a declared meaning is not a grounded one"**; Fig 10 :19: **"→ install what can be violated."**

**Refuter:** showing that the proposed ground moves when a monitoring frame pushes on it (Fig 2 :30: *"If it moves when you push on it, it is not an anchor"*) disqualifies the anchor and the whole verdict built on it.

### F6 - The chain breaks at the objective link

Fig 3 :27: **"A link with no instrument is not a link that works. It is a link nobody has looked at."** Fig 3 :19: **"Optimising a link that already works cannot rescue one that does not."** Fig 3 :29: **"Before asking whether a system is good, ask which link it currently stops at, and whether the answer was measured or assumed."**

"Resilience" is not one operationally settled quantity. The world literature records 167 definitions and 70 different stability concepts in one inventory (Grimm and Wissel 1997, as reported in the *Ecological stability* page). The objective link, having terms for what a situation affords and being able to say what would count as progress, has neither the obvious failures nor the obvious tests of perception and planning (Fig 3 :24-26). So the first practical step in an evidence program is to bind "resilient to what, to which change, at which scale" as an ordinary term with a refuter, so it accrues a residual like anything else (formula, "Direction"). An evidence program that skips this step measures an assumed objective.

**Refuter:** a demonstrated operational definition of resilience with an instrument, agreed across two frames with disjoint evidence and confirmed at the ground, would close this break and make the step unnecessary.

### F7 - The conditions are a union and must be unbundled

Fig 9 :55: **"A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones."** Fig 9 :54: **"And when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again."** Fig 9 :57: **"What remains is smaller and it is also categorically different: a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either."**

Species turnover, relationship rewiring, and changing physical conditions are three overlapping conditions, not three instances of one problem. Evidence of adaptation to species loss does not answer adaptation to a new interaction, and neither answers adaptation to a changed climate. A study that averages them produces the compound it cannot describe.

**Refuter:** a single mechanism whose value is invariant across all three condition changes, demonstrated at the ground, would collapse the union to a partition and remove this requirement.

### F8 - No warning signal is not proof of resilience

Fig 8 :39: **"So a search that finds nothing is never a proof of absence."** Fig 9 :44: **"Only a sealed room can be searched to the end, and no frame is a sealed room. So searching and finding nothing proves nothing."** Fig 13 :134: **"Unreachable is a verdict here, not a silence"**. Fig 13 :135: **"It cannot say which primitive is missing, because naming it would mean already holding it."**

The world literature states the same arithmetic in its own terms: statistical rejection of multiple attractors does not establish a single attractor, and "evidence that data does not exhibit multiple regimes does not rule out the possibility a system could shift to an alternative regime in the future" (*Regime shift* page). Early-warning signals such as critical slowing down, rising autocorrelation, rising variance, and flickering are instruments already returning something; they are not a guarantee of coverage. Fig 6 :34-35: *"Where nothing is, there is no edge to extend from, and the search for a proto-instrument is a search for one that is already there."*

**Refuter:** a closed-system proof that a given ecosystem has a single basin of attraction for the relevant function and all reachable conditions would make "no warning" a proof of resilience. Fig 8 rules this out in principle, but the finding would have to be demonstrated, not asserted.

### F9 - Managed systems: permanent work, introduction without exclusion, substituted habitats

Fig 11 :61: **"The seat's power is asymmetric. Introduction is available; exclusion is not."** Fig 11 :63: **"Which is why a managed ecosystem is permanent work rather than a solved state."** Fig 11 :65: **"Introducing an actor for its cascade is ecosystem engineering, after Jones, Lawton and Shachak, 1994. Cane toads and kudzu are the same move with the cascade unenumerated."** Fig 11 :66: **"Isolation is not removal of the habitat. It is substitution of one habitat for another."** Fig 11 :68-69: **"what you failed to reproduce"** and **"what you brought with you"**. Fig 11 :70: **"A test harness is a substituted habitat. Which is why a synthetic solve proves wiring and never capability."**

For a managed ecosystem the evidence must include the lineage of introductions, the two silent failure modes of any substituted habitat (what the test omitted, what it added), and the fact that the work does not end. A mesocosm result proves that the wiring works in the substituted habitat; it does not prove capability in the habitat of interest. The world literature corroborates at low overlap: *Adaptive management* describes a "structured, iterative process of robust decision making in the face of uncertainty, with an aim to reducing uncertainty over time via system monitoring," and the *Ecosystem engineer* page records kudzu as an introduced species that changes the distribution and number of animal and bird species.

**Refuter:** a managed system that stays adapted after the seat stops acting, with no further introduction, would refute the permanent-work claim for that system.

### F10 - The cascade is not a metaphor: DDT and the eagles

Fig 11 :52: **"Scoping the habitat to direct contact is how DDT reached the eagles: first-order enumeration, second-order effect, and the residual that mattered was three steps out."** Fig 11 :54: **"Carson, Silent Spring, 1962. The mechanism, biomagnification through the food chain to eggshell thinning, was established over the following decade."**

This is the sharpest empirical example in the figure set for ecology. The *Biomagnification* page corroborates independently: "A well known example of the harmful effects of DDT biomagnification is the significant decline in North American populations of predatory birds such as bald eagles and peregrine falcons due to DDT causing eggshell thinning in the 1950s." A resilience assessment scoped to direct contact would have read the water and the insects and missed the residual that mattered.

**Refuter:** a case where first-order enumeration would have caught a third-order collapse at the same cost as a cascaded enumeration would weaken this finding; the DDT case itself would then be an exception, not a rule.

### F11 - The framework returns a shape, not a threshold number

Fig 9 :32: **"you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself"**. Fig 13 :134: **"Unreachable is a verdict here, not a silence"**. Fig 5 :62-63: *"A frame searching for an answer of a particular shape holds that shape as a PREFERENCE rather than as a term. It is not bound, it predicts nothing, and it accrues no residual, so nothing here can see it."*

The honest output is the form that admissible evidence must have, written before the run. It is not "resilience = 0.73" or "recovery within 14 months." A particular threshold is a binding from the ecology frame onto the shape, and it needs its own provenance and ground settlement. The canonical usage rule is explicit: the framework returns *"what a missing thing would have to look like (a shape, not a name)"* (`FIGURES_README_canonical.md`).

**Refuter:** a figure-derived threshold with its own verbatim line, rather than a binding from world ecology, would remove this limit. None was found in the figure text.

### F12 - Two currencies: a short index and a costly settlement

Fig 12 :125: **"A composition costs in two currencies, and they do not add."** Fig 12 :126-127: *"Bits price the description. Actions price finding out whether it holds. They are in different units, so a system holding one accounting cannot compare them and will not notice the shortfall."*

A resilience index can be cheap to state (a few bits) and require a whole program of perturbation and monitoring to settle (many actions). A proposal that counts only the bits will propose arrangements it cannot afford to test. Fig 1 :24-27 disqualifies frame-internal quantities as a score: *"coverage, and compression achieved"*, *"predicates minted, and credibility accrued"*, *"They are frame-internal. A frame cannot score itself with a quantity it also produces."*

**Refuter:** a demonstrated single currency in which description cost and settlement cost convert at a fixed rate, valid across these domains, would remove this warning.

## 3. Pre-registered refuter

Written before the recommendation, per the standing instruction that a falsifier written before the run is a design instrument. The evidence shape stated in this audit is refused if any of the following holds:

1. **Aggregation refuter.** A single-time aggregated intactness index predicts post-perturbation adaptation as well as the per-slot, across-scale, perturbation-linked record, on independent data with declared low overlap between the frames. (Breaks F1.)
2. **Roster refuter.** A fixed list of species or traits predicts adaptation across changed residuals, including a case where a former benefactor becomes a malfactor. (Breaks F3 and F4.)
3. **Direct-contact refuter.** A first-order-only habitat scope predicts the same adaptation failures as a cascaded enumeration across several independent perturbations. (Breaks F2 and F10.)
4. **Anchor refuter.** The proposed ground moves when a monitoring frame pushes on it, so it is not an anchor. (Breaks F5.)
5. **Capability refuter.** An agent-only improvement changes the post-perturbation ground outcome with habitat contact held fixed. (Breaks F3; this is Fig 11's own standing refuter.)
6. **Cost refuter.** Description cost and settlement cost are shown to convert in one currency at a fixed rate for these domains. (Breaks F12.)

If any fires, the shape is withdrawn or narrowed, and the correction is recorded in section 6 rather than erased. Until one is run, nothing here is labelled `grounded`.

## 4. Recommendation

The answer to the question, as a shape:

1. **Bind the objective first.** State "resilience of what function, to which change, at which scale, over which horizon" as an ordinary term with a refuter (Fig 3 :27; formula "Direction"). Without this, the evidence program measures an assumed objective.
2. **Enumerate the habitat and the cascade.** List actors, conditions, and relations in contact with the function, then what contacts those, outward until the cascade stops mattering (Fig 11 :43-48, :52). Include what cannot yet be measured as the residual, not as a zero.
3. **Measure per slot, across the perturbation.** Report recovery rate, variance, autocorrelation, flickering, and persistence of function per slot and per scale, before and after a change, not as one headline score (Fig 1 :19, :22; Fig 4 :30).
4. **Triangulate with declared independence.** Field observation, experiment, remote sensing, Indigenous and local knowledge, and models count separately only to the extent that they do not draw on the same evidence. Record the overlap (Fig 2 :31, :40; Fig 9 :60). Distribute the search and keep searchers independent (Fig 6 :49-50).
5. **Name the two silent failure modes for any substituted habitat.** For mesocosms, models, or restored sites, write down what was not reproduced and what was unintentionally introduced (Fig 11 :68-70). A synthetic solve proves wiring, not capability.
6. **Use early-warning instruments that are already returning something.** Critical slowing down, rising temporal autocorrelation, rising variance, and flickering are readings from instruments that exist; sharpen those, do not wait for a sensor of something that has never registered (Fig 6 :32-35; *Critical transition* page).
7. **Do not convert absence into a verdict.** No warning signal found is a budget report, not a proof of resilience (Fig 8 :39; Fig 9 :44; Fig 13 :134).
8. **Keep the lineage.** Dates, instruments, refuters, imports, substitutions, and the names of the frames that produced each reading. The section on the record and the reach in `THE_FORMULA.md` explains why a refusal is safe to compute only when the record grows. Fig 10 :62: *"A ruling carries provenance like an atom"*.

What this yields is a kind of evidence, not a certificate. The world literature converges on the same shape from an independent direction: the *Ecological resilience* page gives the capacity to "absorb disturbance and reorganize while undergoing change so as to still retain essentially the same function, structure, identity, and feedbacks," and Walker et al. (2004) add that the focus is "on the dynamics of the system when it is disturbed far from its modal state." Both are secondary corroboration; the figures decide.

## 5. Canonical usage check

Read after the first four sections, and they govern how the figures may be used here:

- `FRAMEWORK/reference/FIGURES_README_canonical.md`: *"They outrank everything else in the repo: the code, the rulings and the docs answer to them... the quotes are the authority, not this summary."* It states the re-census rule: *"a reading that makes one contradict another has misidentified its domain, not found a flaw."* It states the canonical limit: *"what a missing thing would have to look like (a shape, not a name)"* and *"Indiscernibility is not identity."* The public copy at `https://github.com/IsaiahN/tether/tree/general-agent/figures` was fetched on 2026-11-23 and contains the same rule.
- `FRAMEWORK/reference/60_FIGURES_AS_METALAWS.md`: the figures are the ground for design questions, and the seat must not ask for rulings the figures already decide. *"the ground does not decay, the channel does"* and the seat's job is to read the ground, not to ask the anchor's maintainer to decide the design.
- `FRAMEWORK/figures/THE_FORMULA.md`: step 7 separates SEARCHED, INWARD, and OUTWARD. SEARCHED comes first (cheapest); INWARD extends an instrument already returning something; OUTWARD imports a frame whose closure predicts R's shape. In this audit, SEARCHED corresponds to re-reading existing monitoring data per slot; INWARD corresponds to sharpening early-warning instruments; OUTWARD corresponds to bringing in perturbation ecology, which is where "resilience under change" already has a closure. INWARD and OUTWARD were not read against each other.

Consequence for F11: naming a threshold would be a binding from the ecology frame, not a figure derivation. It is allowed only with provenance and a ground settlement.
