# Outreach draft: validate the evidence shape first, then decide whether the framework is worth reading

**Use:** one-to-one, or as a cover note attached to a submission through a public venue in `03_public_contact_points.csv`. **Not** for bulk send.

**Hard precondition:** do not send until the four checks in `08_expert_verification_packet.md` section 6 have been run on the specific ecosystem at hand. A shape that has not been bound to a function and a change is not yet checkable.

---

**Subject:** Which observation would separate "intact today" from "able to adapt"?

> Hi [name],
>
> I have one specific claim about ecological evidence I would like you to check against what you already know. I am not asking you to read the theory first, and I am not asking you to agree with it. If the claim does not survive, there is nothing else to look at.
>
> **The claim (`specified`, not `grounded`).** A snapshot of an ecosystem as it stands cannot, by itself, show that the ecosystem is resilient. The evidence that would show resilience has a particular shape: it is indexed by the live slots (species, interactions, conditions, functions), it follows contact outward beyond direct interactions, it is taken before and after a change, and it is ranged against an outcome that does not move when a monitoring frame changes its mind. A headline intactness score can be near zero while one live slot carries the signal that will decide the outcome. This is a claim about the **kind of evidence that could discriminate**, not a proposed index or a threshold number.
>
> **What I am not claiming.** Not that any particular species or trait confers resilience. Not that a fixed roster of "good" actors survives a change in conditions. Not that a mesocosm or a model demonstrates capability in the field. Not that an absence of early-warning signals proves resilience. Not that the framework yields a resilience score. It returns a shape, and a particular threshold would be a binding from ecology onto that shape, needing its own provenance and its own ground settlement.
>
> **What would refute the shape claim, written before this message:**
> 1. A single-time aggregated intactness index predicts post-perturbation adaptation as well as a per-slot, perturbation-linked record, on independent data with low overlap between the two frames.
> 2. A fixed list of species or traits predicts adaptation across changed residuals, including a case where a former benefactor becomes a malfactor.
> 3. A direct-contact-only habitat scope predicts the same adaptation failures as a cascaded enumeration across several independent perturbations.
> 4. An agent-only improvement moves the post-perturbation outcome while habitat contact is held fixed.
>
> If any of these holds, I will narrow or withdraw the claim. A one-line "this fails because..." is more useful to me than agreement. If you would rather not engage, no reply is needed.
>
> **Before you answer, one question:** for the system you know best, what would you count as a failure of that evidence shape? I will record your answer before showing you any framework material.
>
> **If it survives, and only then:** here are the sources. The figures, not my summary of them, are the authority.
> - Figures and formula, public repository: https://github.com/IsaiahN/tether/tree/general-agent/figures
> - Walker, Holling, Carpenter and Kinzig 2004, *Resilience, adaptability and transformability in social-ecological systems*, Ecology and Society 9(2):5 (open access): https://www.ecologyandsociety.org/vol9/iss2/art5/inline.html
> - *Ecological resilience*, for the two competing definitions in the literature: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ecological_resilience
> - *Critical transition*, for early-warning instruments already in use: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Critical_transition
>
> **If you want to use the machinery:** the formula is in the figures link. Run it on a problem you know well and can check against a ground. Write down what would refute your result *before* you run it: the architecture's own write-up warns that outputs "must be seen as simulacra" and that "the truly testable output would need to be derived from the real architecture or working the formula yourself." A reported failure is more useful to me than a reported success.
>
> [name]
