## QUESTION

Decide, in effect terms, what evidence would distinguish an ecosystem that is merely intact today from one able to adapt as species, relationships, and conditions change. Option A: answer with a resilience index or threshold read from the current state. Option B: answer with the shape of a record that only a change can produce. What each leads to: A delivers a name where the framework returns a shape, hides a live slot inside an average, and scopes the habitat to direct contact; B delivers a per-slot, across-scale, perturbation-linked discriminator, written refuters, and an open slot until the ground settles it.

## DOMAIN CENSUS

R by arity, symmetry and scale, then one line per domain with actors / currency / anchor, partition or union, and order by dependency.

- **R (the residual).** The gap between an intactness reading and a capacity claim. Arity: many slots (species, interactions, conditions, functions), indexed per slot, never aggregated (Fig 1 :19; Symbols table :43-44). Symmetry: asymmetric and directed; influence travels along relations and cascades outward (Fig 11 :52), and valence is relative to R (Fig 11 :49-50). Scale: multi-scale with a lossy transform; the remainder compounding through the chain can become chaos (Fig 13 :31; Fig 4 :30). Fig 9 :55 names the three change conditions a union compound, not one hard question.
- **Domain 1, the ecological ground.** Actors: species, conditions (light, water, temperature, season, terrain), relations (pollination, predation, competition, dependency). Currency: contact, survival, reproduction, function performed, recovery. Anchor: the observation or experiment that does not move when a frame changes its answer (Fig 2 :29). Question type: union of three overlapping conditions.
- **Domain 2, the observation and indicator frame.** Actors: field surveys, remote sensing, indices, models, Indigenous and local knowledge. Currency: readings and scores. Anchor: present only when pinned to Domain 1; an index definition alone is a convention (Fig 10 :18). Question type: a frame at risk of losing its anchor and drifting with the others.
- **Domain 3, the perturbation and verification frame.** Actors: disturbances, natural experiments, manipulations, extreme events. Currency: the residual under change, recovery rate, variance, autocorrelation, flickering. Anchor: the ground's response after the change, which does not consult any frame. Question type: the domain where "adapt as conditions change" registers.
- **Domain 4, the management seat (conditional).** Actors: managers, stakeholders, introduced actors. Currency: conventions and decisions. Anchor: outcomes in Domain 1; agreement among managers is not an anchor. Present only where a seat exists; otherwise the habitat is pure contact (Fig 11 :56, :58).
- **Order by dependency.** Perception of hidden interactions; vocabulary for what actors afford; objective stated in our own terms; perturbation plan; verify and carry (Fig 3 :4-16). The chain usually breaks at the objective link because resilience has no single instrument (Fig 3 :27). Refuse before mint (formula step 2); describe the residual before choosing a frame (Fig 9 :27-28); settle a first-order molecule before a second-order comparison (Fig 12 :104).

Strain test applied: Fig 11's unmaintained mode (:56) and maintained mode (:58) are two modes of one habitat, not a contradiction. Fig 9's union reading and Fig 3's ordered chain govern different objects. No strain after re-census.

## VERDICT (from the figures)

The shape survives; a resilience score does not. The evidence that would show an ecosystem remains resilient is a per-slot, across-scale, perturbation-linked record of bounded residual in the enumerated habitat, with the cascade followed outward, the objective bound before measurement, the frames kept independent against an unmoving outcome, and any substituted habitat's drops written down. A headline intactness score, a fixed species roster, and a mesocosm success do not have that shape. Two systems with the same species set but different relations are isomers and need not behave alike. No warning signal found is a budget report, not proof. The result is `specified`, ungrounded, and stops at the current instruments.

- **cost if wrong:** bounded and locatable if the lineage is kept. The real cost is spending the independence of outside readers by sending a recording instead of a generator, which Fig 2 :42 prices: **"Buy independence in proportion to the cost of being wrong."** That cost is avoidable by leading with the shape claim and its refuters.
- **undoable:** yes. A threshold or roster binding is removable without touching the derivation; `07_the_tether_answer.md` and `08_expert_verification_packet.md` already remove them. A sent claim can be superseded by the corrected packet, and no primitive has to be withdrawn.
- **reversed by:** a ground reading, not agreement. A per-slot before/after dataset in which an aggregated intactness index predicts adaptation as well as the per-slot record; a fixed roster that works across changed residuals including a benefactor-to-malfactor flip; a direct-contact scope that matches a cascaded one; a ground that moves when frames push on it; an agent-only improvement that moves the ground; or a source-discriminating instrument that shows two "isomers" behave alike. Until one of these fires, the verdict stands as `candidate`.
