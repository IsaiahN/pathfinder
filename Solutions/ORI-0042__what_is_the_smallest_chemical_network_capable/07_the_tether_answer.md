# The Tether's answer: the smallest self-maintaining chemical network

**Mode: `specified` on the shape, `gap` on the number, no part `grounded`.** This is a shape and a route, not a measured result. Simulated derivation checked against the figures (`05_what_shape_means.md` section 6), not a ground reading.

## 1. The residual it answers

The question asks for the minimum reaction set that (a) harvests energy and (b) maintains itself. Both are effects, and they have different grounds. `R_s = |Γ(b,a) − o'|` per candidate network slot `s`: what the library predicts about persistence under a habitat, against what the habitat returns. The sought-for "smallest" is a preference, not a term, until bound as an ordinary term with a refuter (Fig 5 :62, :65).

## 2. The shape

1. **Split before search.** Two conditions make a union compound, not one hard question. Fig 9 :55: *"A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones."* Fig 9 :57: *"a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either."* Run two loops, then their compound.
2. **Harvesting energy is spending a gradient.** Energy is *"conserved, finite, directionless alone"* (Fig 13 :14); a gradient is *"a difference that can be spent"* (Fig 13 :22); *"Energy and a gradient compose a flow"* (Fig 13 :75-76). A network couples to an external disequilibrium; it does not create energy.
3. **A structure must mediate.** Two states never bond directly (Fig 13 :75). The energy state and the reacting metabolites need a structure between them. The figure requires *a* structure and does not select which one. The slot stays open.
4. **Self-maintenance needs a catalyst and a cycle.** Fig 12 :119: *"catalyst. A part that survives its own use."* Fig 12 :121: *"cycle. The output re-enters as input. The sign of the loop decides whether it closes, and a chain cannot close by construction."* The smallest network cannot be an acyclic chain, however short.
5. **Capability is network plus habitat.** Fig 11 :39: *"capability is a property of agent-and-habitat, never of the agent alone."* Fig 11 :70: *"A test harness is a substituted habitat. Which is why a synthetic solve proves wiring and never capability."* A lab pathway that runs proves the wiring, not the capability.
6. **The number is a `gap`.** Closure is idempotent (Fig 8 :32), and *"a search that finds nothing is never a proof of absence"* (Fig 8 :39). Report the answer as unreached at this reach, not as a silence and not as a proved absence.
7. **Two currencies, never added.** Fig 12 :125-126: *"A composition costs in two currencies, and they do not add. Bits price the description. Actions price finding out whether it holds."* Fewest reactions and fewest experiments are different orderings.

## 3. What it explicitly does not say

- Not a named pathway. The acetyl-CoA pathway, the reverse TCA cycle and the alpha-ketoacid analogue are bindings from the chemistry frame, not derivations and not answers to "smallest."
- Not that any published small network has been shown to maintain itself. The cited lab results are reaction sequences in substituted habitats.
- Not that "no bottom" proves there is no smallest network. That line governs levels of composition, not cardinality inside a level (Fig 12 :104 re-censused in `01` F9).
- Not a single number, not a ranking of networks by molecule count alone.
- Not `grounded`. No measurement settles the ordering of candidate networks at this reach.

## 4. What would settle it : the ground

A discriminating instrument, not a further argument. The questions are:

- **Cycle test:** does a candidate network persist across many turnovers with its catalyst regenerated, or does a product simply accumulate once? A time-course assay that distinguishes the two is the inward instrument.
- **Gradient test:** does persistence disappear when the external disequilibrium is removed, while the metabolites are held constant? This separates harvesting from maintenance.
- **Habitat test:** does the network persist in an unmaintained habitat, or only in the substituted habitat of the reactor? The first is capability; the second is wiring.
- **Ordering test:** are description length and experimental cost the same ordering for the candidate set? If not, "smallest" must state which currency it means.
- Standing falsifiers from `01` section 4: an acyclic self-maintaining chain; a self-maintaining network with no gradient; a single ordering of bits and actions; a derivation of one of the six from the other five.

## 5. The honest stop

**Unreached, not unreachable.** Formula step 7: ask SEARCHED first (is a smaller network in here and unfound? more independent experiments at the same reach), then INWARD (extend the instrument from one already returning something), then OUTWARD (import food-set and catalyst vocabulary with a source gate and a shadow test). Do not read INWARD against OUTWARD, and do not import against a budget limit: *"a wrong atom costs more than starting from primitives"* (Formula step 7). The framework returns *"a kind of thing, never the answer itself"* (Fig 9 :32); it records the lineage so the stop is checkable. *"Indiscernibility is not identity."*

## 6. One-paragraph version for outreach

> The Tether does not name a smallest chemical network, and it does not claim that any published small pathway has been shown to maintain itself. It says the question bundles two conditions that must be split: harvesting energy means spending a gradient, not producing energy, and maintaining oneself requires a catalyst that survives its own use and a cycle that closes, which an acyclic chain cannot do. A structure must sit between the energy state and the reacting metabolites, and capability belongs to the network plus its habitat, so a laboratory reactor proves wiring rather than capability. The smallest number is not settled at this reach, and reaction count and experimental cost are two currencies that do not add. What is offered is the shape any candidate answer must have, four falsifiers written in advance, and the instruments that would close it. The slot stays open until the ground settles it.
