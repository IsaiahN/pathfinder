## What the wider literature bears on the question

1. **Low-overlap corroboration of the catalyst and cycle shape.** Huson et al. (2024) formalise self-sustaining sets from a food source in mathematics and treat the catalyst as something not consumed. Fig 12 :119 defines the catalyst as *"A part that survives its own use."* The two statements were reached independently in different vocabularies. That is convergence, not an import of a new atom, and it is the strongest low-overlap witness in this packet.
2. **A small reaction set is possible; a self-maintaining small set is not demonstrated.** Stubbs et al. (2020) produce a sequence of Krebs-cycle analogues from two reactants under mild conditions. Preiner et al. (2020) produce metabolic precursors from H2, CO2 and minerals. Neither closes a cycle or shows persistence across uses. Both are substituted habitats in the Fig 11 sense: they prove wiring, not capability.
3. **"Minimal metabolism" is a heuristic, not a found object.** Lauber et al. (2021) explicitly frame the concept as *"a heuristic construct, halfway between chemistry and biology."* That is the world's own version of the figure rule that the framework returns a shape, not a name.
4. **The ordering of networks depends on what flows.** Milo et al. (2002) find different motif sets for food webs and for gene regulation networks, and connect the difference to energy flow versus information processing. Therefore "smallest" cannot be a single cross-domain number; it is relative to the currency. This corroborates Fig 12 :125-126 on two incommensurable costs.
5. **The field's own definition is larger than the question.** NASA's working definition adds Darwinian evolution. The question asks for energy harvesting and self-maintenance only, which is a necessary part of that definition, not the whole of it.

No world source conflicts with the figure verdict. Where a naive reading of Stubbs et al. or Preiner et al. would conflict with the cycle and habitat claims, the conflict is resolved by re-census: those papers report reaction sequences in substituted habitats, not self-maintaining networks. That is a different object, not a contradiction.

## Distribution: offer, do not push

- **Fig 10 :52** : *"Bring a characterised residual, never the source."* Send the shape and the refuter first. A recipient who has absorbed the whole framework before being asked for a verdict has joined the frame being checked (Fig 10 :42).
- **Fig 9 :27-28** : *"describe it before you go looking, or any frame you pick will seem to fit."* Ask what they would expect to see if the shape claim were true before giving them the article.
- **Fig 2 :40** : *"disqualifying for a verdict: agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool."* Do not select recipients for likely agreement, and do not pool later agreement as corroboration.
- **Fig 9 :60** : *"Use filters for the budget and witnesses for the verdict. Never let a filter hand you a verdict."* A venue's prestige is a contact-cost estimate, never evidence about the claim.
- **Fig 4 :22** : *"Only methods cross up. Only head starts come down."* Stage 3 sends a method, which is permitted; a recording of one success is not.

**Recommended order in a reply:** (1) the shape claim and its four refuters; (2) ask what they would count as a failure and record the answer; (3) only then the links, with the note that the figures, not the summary, are the authority; (4) offer the formula as a reusable method.
