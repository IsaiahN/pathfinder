# Outreach draft : check the shape before deciding whether to read the theory

**Use:** one-to-one, or as a cover letter attached to a submission through the public venues in `03_public_contact_points.csv`. **Not** for bulk send.

**Hard precondition:** do not send until the shape claim in `01_figure_grounded_audit.md` section 3 has survived the stated refuter. The output must survive validation or the two-stage offer collapses on contact.

---

**Subject:** Would this refute your account of a minimal self-maintaining network?

> Hi [name],
>
> I have one specific structural claim I would like you to check against what you already know. I am not asking you to read a theory first, and I am not asking you to agree. If the claim does not survive, there is nothing else to look at.
>
> **The claim (`specified`, not `grounded`):** the question "what is the smallest chemical network that harvests energy and maintains itself?" bundles two conditions that have different grounds. Split before searching. In effect terms:
>
> 1. "Harvest energy" does not mean produce energy. Energy is conserved, finite and directionless on its own; a gradient is a difference that can be spent, and the two compose a flow. So the network must couple to an external disequilibrium through a structure, not sit beside an energy source.
> 2. "Maintain itself" requires a catalyst that survives its own use and a cycle in which output re-enters as input. A chain cannot close by construction, however short it is.
> 3. The unit of capability is the network plus its habitat. A laboratory reactor is a substituted habitat, so a reaction that runs in one proves the wiring, not the capability.
>
> I am **not** claiming a number of reactions, a named pathway, or that any published small network has been shown to be the smallest. The framework returns a shape, not a name. "Smallest" in reaction count and "smallest" in experiments needed to settle a network are two currencies that do not add.
>
> **What would refute the claim, written before this message:**
> 1. a purely acyclic reaction chain that maintains itself indefinitely under a fixed food set;
> 2. a self-maintaining network with no external gradient or disequilibrium;
> 3. an experiment that establishes a unique smallest network and shows that description cost and experimental cost are the same ordering;
> 4. a derivation of any one of the six framework terms from the other five.
>
> If any one holds, I will retract the claim. A one-line "this fails because..." is more useful to me than agreement. If you would rather not engage, no reply is needed.
>
> **If it survives, and only then:** I can send the source material. The figures are the authority, not my summary of them. I am also happy to send the derivation and the refuter together, so you can check the route rather than only the conclusion.
>
> **If you want to reuse the method:** write down what an answer to your own question would look like before you run it, state what would refute it, and label the mode of what you get. A reported failure is more useful to me than a reported success.
>
> [name]

## The three stages

1. **Stage 1:** send the shape claim and the refuter. Ask what they would count as a failure. Record their verdict before any theory is shown.
2. **Stage 2, opt-in only:** if they are curious, send the sources. Their later agreement is never pooled as corroboration, because a reader who has absorbed the framework has joined the frame being checked (Fig 10 :42).
3. **Stage 3:** offer the formula as a method to reuse, with the instruction that each user writes their own refuter and states their own mode.
