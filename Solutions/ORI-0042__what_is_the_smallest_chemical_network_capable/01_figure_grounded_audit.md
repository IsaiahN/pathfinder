## 0. The residual, in effect terms

The question asks for an argmin: the smallest chemical network that (a) harvests energy and (b) maintains itself. Stated as an effect, not a name:

> For each candidate reaction set `s`, `R_s = |Γ(b,a) − o'|`: what the library predicts about the persistence of `s` under a plausible habitat, against what the habitat actually returns. Support is satisfied when `|R⁺_s| > 0` for some `s`, that is, when at least one candidate fails to persist as predicted. The sought-for answer would be the `s` of least description length with `R_s = 0`.

**The "smallest" is a preference, not a term.** Fig 5 :62: *"A frame searching for an answer of a particular shape holds that shape as a PREFERENCE rather than as a term."* Formula step "Direction": the loop *"cannot check the direction of its own question, because the shape a frame is searching for is not a term it holds."* So before the run I bind the sought-for shape as an ordinary term, as the discipline requires (Fig 5 :65: *"make the sought-for shape an ORDINARY TERM. Write down what an answer would look like, bind it, and let it accrue a residual like anything else"*):

> **Bound shape term (pre-registered):** a minimum self-maintaining network is a finite reaction set `C` with (i) an external food set and a spendable gradient; (ii) every catalyst of every reaction in `C` produced by `C` or present in the food set; (iii) a cycle, so output re-enters as input; and (iv) minimal `|C|` under a declared code, with description cost and experimental cost priced separately.

This term is refusable by the same test as everything else. Sections 3 and 4 give the refuter and the census that governs it.

## 3. Findings

### F1 : The question is a union compound and must be split before search (decisive for method)

Fig 9 :55: **"A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones."**
Fig 9 :57: **"a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either. It stops being a mixture and becomes a specific compound."**

"Harvesting energy" and "maintaining itself" have different grounds. The first is settled by a thermodynamic disequilibrium and a flow; the second by the persistence of catalysts across uses. Searching for one network that maximises both before splitting them is searching a compound with one lever. Verdict: two loops, then their compound. Mode: `general`, triangulated across Fig 9 and the dependency order in Fig 3.

### F2 : "Harvesting energy" is spending a gradient, not creating energy

Fig 13 :14: **"conserved, finite, directionless alone"** (energy).
Fig 13 :22: **"a difference that can be spent"** (gradient).
Fig 13 :75-76: **"Energy and a gradient compose a flow."**

Energy is directionless on its own. A network cannot harvest energy in the sense of producing it; it couples to a gradient, which is the directed, spendable structure, and the two compose a flow. So the question's first condition is already a cross-kind composition: a state (energy) with a structure (gradient) through a network (the relations among reactions, Fig 13 :24 **"that entities relate, and influence travels"**). Mode: `specified`, triangulated across three distinct figure lines. World corroboration: Preiner et al. 2020 show H2 and CO2 fixation over three hydrothermal minerals; NASA's life-detection page states that terran living systems *"exploit a thermodynamic disequilibrium."*

### F3 : Self-maintenance requires a catalyst and a cycle; a chain cannot close

Fig 12 :119: **"catalyst. A part that survives its own use. Spent and regenerating parts behave differently across uses, and nothing records which a term is."**
Fig 12 :121: **"cycle. The output re-enters as input. The sign of the loop decides whether it closes, and a chain cannot close by construction."**

A linear reaction sequence, however short, is *"the degenerate case: one bond type, no branching"* (Fig 12 :48) and cannot close by construction. So the smallest self-maintaining network cannot be an acyclic chain: it needs at least one cycle and at least one part that survives its own use. Mode: `specified`, triangulated across two distinct figure structures. World corroboration at low overlap: Huson, Xavier and Steel (2024) define a reflexively autocatalytic and food-generated set and treat a catalyst as *"any element that facilitates, speeds up or synchronizes a reaction without taking part in the reaction itself."* That is a convergence from biomathematics, not an import of a new atom.

### F4 : A structure must sit between the energy state and the reacting network

Fig 13 :74: **"Everything else is a composition across the two kinds, never within one."**
Fig 13 :75: **"Two states never bond directly. It is not space-time but space, a structure, time; and two structures join only through a state."**
Fig 13 :29: **"A state with no structure has no magnitude: three of what, ordered how, varying across what?"**

Applying Fig 13 :75 to the chemistry frame: the external energy source and the metabolites are states; they cannot bond directly. A structure (gradient and/or network) must mediate. The figure does not select which structure, only that one is required, so the slot stays open. Mode: `specified`, single-source for this domain (Fig 13 only); the world sources corroborate the need for a mineral or organic catalyst but do not derive the cross-kind rule.

### F5 : The unit of capability is the network plus its habitat, not the network alone

Fig 11 :39: **"capability is a property of agent-and-habitat, never of the agent alone."**
Fig 11 :40: **"An improvement that does not change contact changes nothing, however much it improves."**
Fig 11 :70: **"A test harness is a substituted habitat. Which is why a synthetic solve proves wiring and never capability."**

The two laboratory anchors in the literature are substituted habitats. Stubbs et al. (2020) produce an alpha-ketoacid analogue of the reverse Krebs cycle from pyruvate and glyoxylate under mild aqueous conditions, with no metals or enzymes; Preiner et al. (2020) produce formate, acetate and pyruvate from H2 and CO2 over three iron minerals at 100 °C. Both demonstrate that the reactions can run, which is wiring. Neither demonstrates a network that persists in an unmaintained habitat, which is capability. Mode: `specified`, triangulated across three figure lines plus two independent experiments.

### F6 : "Smallest" is invisible to the loop until it is bound as a term

Fig 5 :62: **"A frame searching for an answer of a particular shape holds that shape as a PREFERENCE rather than as a term."**
Fig 5 :65: **"make the sought-for shape an ORDINARY TERM. Write down what an answer would look like, bind it, and let it accrue a residual like anything else."**

This is why a literature that searches for "the smallest" can keep finding ever-smaller reaction sequences without ever settling the question: the criterion is a preference, not a measured quantity. The bound term in section 0 is the remedy, and it carries its own refuter. Mode: `general`, single-source (Fig 5) plus the formula's "Direction" paragraph.

### F7 : The specific number is unreached, not unreachable

Fig 8 :32: **"Closure is idempotent. Composing inside a closed set never leaves it, so no search, however long, adds an atom."**
Fig 8 :39: **"So a search that finds nothing is never a proof of absence."**
Fig 13 :134: **"Unreachable is a verdict here, not a silence"** (the sentence continues: the system cannot name the missing primitive).

Formula step 7 separates three questions, kept distinct: **SEARCHED** (is a smaller network in here and unfound? The closure does not change; more independent experiments at the same reach), **INWARD** (extend the instrument from one already returning something: a time-course assay that distinguishes transient production from persistence), **OUTWARD** (import the food-set and catalyst vocabulary from RAF mathematics with a source gate and a shadow test; that adds access to a frame, not an atom to the world). Answering SEARCHED first is cheapest and the other two assume its answer. Verdict on the number: `gap` at this reach. Mode: `general`, triangulated across Fig 8 and Fig 13.

### F8 : Bits and actions are two currencies and do not add

Fig 12 :125: **"A composition costs in two currencies, and they do not add."**
Fig 12 :126: **"Bits price the description. Actions price finding out whether it holds."**

A network with few reactions is cheap to describe but may take a whole experimental budget to settle; a network with many reactions may be easy to test and expensive to state. "Smallest" in the literature usually means fewest reactions or fewest molecules (bits). The ground asks a different question (actions). Huson et al. (2024) add a third, computational fact: finding a smallest irreducible RAF is NP-hard. That is a cost inside a given catalytic reaction system, not a framework closure limit. Named strain, not smoothed (section 5).

### F9 : A rejected strain: "no smallest level" does not answer "smallest network"

Fig 12 :104: **"A settled molecule becomes an atom for whatever composes over it."** (the line continues: *"and there is no bottom"*). Fig 4 :33: **"Which is why there is no top and no infinite regress."** The figures README says the theory has *"no smallest and no largest level."*

It is tempting to use these to answer the question directly: there is no smallest network because there is no bottom. **That is a strain and is refused.** Re-census: these lines govern the *level of composition* (irreducibility is level-relative; there is no privileged stratum), not the cardinality of a reaction set inside a level. Applying them to molecule count would govern another quantity. Recorded as a rejected strain, not a verdict. The same re-census rule is in the figures README: *"a reading that makes one contradict another has misidentified its domain."*

## 4. Pre-registered refuter

Written before the conclusion, per the standing instruction: a falsifier written before the run is a design instrument.

The shape claim is refuted if any of the following holds:

1. A purely acyclic reaction chain is demonstrated to maintain itself indefinitely under a fixed external food set (breaks Fig 12 :121, *"a chain cannot close by construction"*).
2. A self-maintaining network is demonstrated with no external gradient or thermodynamic disequilibrium, with energy harvested from a directionless, finite, conserved quantity alone (breaks Fig 13 :14, :22, :75-76).
3. A single experiment establishes a unique smallest network and the description-length ordering is shown to be the same in bits and in actions (breaks Fig 12 :125-126).
4. One of the six terms is derived from the other five (breaks Fig 13 indivisibility, :41 *"Each is indivisible: no arrangement of the other five yields it."*).

If any fires, the shape is withdrawn or narrowed accordingly. Until one is run, no part of this may be labelled `grounded`.

## 5. Recommendation

1. Split the two conditions before searching (F1). Do not look for one number that maximises both.
2. State the shape, not a reaction list (F2 to F5). Keep the mediating structure slot open.
3. Bind "smallest" as an ordinary term with a declared code and the refuter above (F6, F7).
4. Treat bits and actions as separate readings; report both or say which one is being used (F8).
5. Do not use "no bottom" as an answer to network size (F9).
6. Lead outreach with the shape and the refuter; offer the sources only after a verdict is recorded (Fig 10 :52, *"Bring a characterised residual, never the source."*).

### Seven-question recursive derivation (summary)

1. **Is the question one question?** No. Two conditions, a union compound (Fig 9 :55-57). Split.
2. **What does "harvest energy" mean in effect terms?** Spending a gradient, not producing energy (Fig 13 :14, :22, :75-76).
3. **What does "maintain itself" require?** A catalyst that survives its use and a cycle that closes (Fig 12 :119, :121). A chain is refused.
4. **Can energy bond directly to the reacting network?** No. Two states never bond directly; a structure mediates (Fig 13 :74-75). The slot is not filled by the six alone.
5. **Is a specific reaction list an import?** No atom is added; it is a binding onto held chemistry, so it needs provenance and a shadow test rather than a wall-moving claim (Fig 6 :22, :25-26).
6. **Is the minimum settled?** No ground pays for a unique minimum. SEARCHED, INWARD and OUTWARD are owed, in that order (Formula step 7).
7. **What is the honest stop?** A shape with a refuter; the number is recorded as `gap`. Prestige, agreement and a smaller reaction count are filters for the budget, never witnesses for the verdict (Fig 9 :60).

### Updated verdict

The shape survives: a union compound whose two loops are a spendable gradient coupled through a structure to a catalytic, cyclic network, with capability located in the network plus habitat and the smallest number left as a `gap`. What does not survive is any named smallest reaction set, any claim that a lab sequence is self-maintaining, any use of "no bottom" as an answer to size, and any single-currency ordering of "smallest." Strains after re-census: one computational cost (NP-hardness of a smallest iRAF) and one pricing duality (bits versus actions), both named, neither hidden.
