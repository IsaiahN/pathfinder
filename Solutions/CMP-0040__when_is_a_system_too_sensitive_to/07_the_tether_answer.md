# The Tether's answer: when is a system too sensitive for precise prediction, yet predictable at distributions or regimes?

**Mode: `specified` on the boundary rule, `general` on the structural shape, not `grounded`.** This is
a shape and a route, not a result. Simulated derivation checked against the figures
(`05_what_shape_means.md` §6), not a ground reading. No measurement was taken.

## 1. The residual it answers

The gap between a model's forecast and an observation, indexed by slot. Slot s1 is a precise state
forecast; slot s2 is a forecast of a distribution, attractor, or regime frequency. The question asks
when s1 fails while s2 survives. The two slots are not averaged: Fig 1 :13 makes "a global R near zero
with one live slot residual is a legal state," and Fig 1 :22 warns that "Aggregating across slots is
how a live signal disappears into an average." The question is a union compound with three domains
(chapter 1 of `01`), each with its own loop and its own ground.

## 2. The shape

1. **Precise prediction ends when the fine residual amplifies without trending down.** Fig 13 :31-32:
   "a remainder compounding through a chain that amplifies is chaos, and the detail a crossing
   discards reappears above it as noise." The figure does not give a number; it gives the mechanism.
   The fine forecast horizon is where amplification outruns the resolution held about the initial
   state. Fig 13 :88-89: "The residual of the turn just closed is what the next turn composes against,
   so no two passes begin from the same place."
2. **Distribution-level prediction survives because the crossing is lossy.** Fig 4 :27: "The transform
   between scales loses information, and that is the point." Fig 4 :28: "Going up throws away detail;
   that is what makes the result reapplicable." The coarse descriptor inherits only what survives
   the crossing, which is why a regime can be stable while trajectories inside it diverge.
3. **The amplified remainder must stay inside a bounded reach.** Fig 13 :115: "The set is closed. The
   arrangements are not." Fig 13 :118: "infinite effort inside a finite reach." If the remainder
   escapes the reach, there is no regime to predict; both slots fail.
4. **The loss must be measured, not assumed zero.** Fig 4 :30: "So take the round trip and measure the
   gap. Without it you are navigating by dead reckoning: confident about a position nobody checked."
   Fig 4 :31-32: "The gap is not an error to eliminate. It is the measurement of what the coarser
   description cannot hold, and it is the only honest report of how much was given up." The round trip
   gap R_T is the instrument-level content of "predictable at the distribution level."
5. **The verdict is per slot, against a ground.** Fig 2 :29: "An anchor is whatever settles the
   question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks." A stable-looking
   ensemble is not evidence until its distribution is ranged against observations. The world
   counterpart: reliability means the observation behaves as if drawn from the forecast distribution
   (*Ensemble forecasting*, opened 2026-11-22).

**One-line boundary rule:** the two levels come apart exactly when the fine residual amplifies beyond
the held resolution, the amplified remainder remains inside a bounded reach, and the lossy crossing
leaves a coarse descriptor whose round-trip gap has been measured against a ground instead of assumed.

## 3. What it explicitly does not say

- Not a number. No Lyapunov exponent, ensemble size, or predictability horizon is derived.
- Not a named attractor. "Attractor", "invariant measure" and "Lyapunov exponent" are bindings from
  the world frame, recorded as such (F6 of `01`).
- Not that every chaotic system is statistically predictable. Boundedness and a measured gap are
  conditions, not decorations.
- Not that better measurement restores the precise forecast. The fine and coarse instruments are two
  tracks, never one ladder (Fig 6 :31).
- Not that agreement among experts settles anything. Fig 2 :40 disqualifies a shared-evidence-pool
  verdict.
- Not `grounded`. Nothing here was settled by a measurement in this run.

## 4. What would settle it  -  the ground

A discriminating instrument, not a further argument. From *The Hard Problem is About Source*: an
instrument that reads green "cannot comment on the source"; the spectrometer resolved the case. Here:

- **Fine slot:** does the separation of two initially close states grow without trending down at
  every accessible resolution? A negative result at every resolution would fail F1.
- **Coarse slot:** is the forecast distribution a representative sample of the observed distribution?
  Slingo and Palmer (2011) record that early ensembles were "not sufficiently dispersive" and
  over-confident, and that model-specific biases can make the distribution of probable outcomes fail
  to reflect the observed distribution.
- **Round trip:** measure R_T between the fine and coarse descriptions. A gap assumed to be zero is
  dead reckoning (Fig 4 :30).
- **Boundedness:** does the amplified remainder stay inside a reach? A derivation showing it can
  leave breaks Fig 13 :115.

Standing falsifiers from `01` §3: a chaotic fine slot with no ground-settled coarse descriptor; a
settled coarse regime with no fine amplification; an under-dispersive or aggregate-only distribution
declared predictable; an amplifying remainder that leaves the closure; a numerical threshold derived
from the figures alone.

## 5. The honest stop

**Unreached, not unreachable.** Fig 13 :134: "Unreachable is a verdict here, not a silence." The
six-term substrate is "a human's careful guess at reality's compression axes" (*They're Scanning
Books*), so declaring it makes the choice visible, not neutral. The answer stops where the available
instruments stop discriminating, and records the lineage so the stop is checkable. Fig 8 :39: "So a
search that finds nothing is never a proof of absence." Fig 2 :42: "Buy independence in proportion to
the cost of being wrong." Three strains remain named, not hidden, in `01` §6 and `11_audit.md`.

## 6. One-paragraph version for outreach

> The Tether does not give a number for when a system becomes unpredictable. It gives the shape of the
> boundary. Precise prediction fails when the difference between two arbitrarily close initial states
> is amplified faster than the instrument can refresh its reading; that is chaos as the figure names
> it, a remainder compounding through an amplifying chain. Distribution or regime prediction can
> survive at the same time, and it survives for a structural reason: the move to the coarser
> description throws away exactly the detail that was being amplified, and what a crossing discards
> reappears above it as noise. So the regime is not luck and it is not a claim that the system is
> really predictable after all. It is a stable arrangement inside a bounded reach, bought by a loss
> that must be measured as a round-trip gap instead of being assumed away. The verdict is per slot,
> against a ground: a stable-looking ensemble is not evidence until its distribution matches what the
> world returns. What is offered is that shape, five refuters written in advance, and the instruments
> that would close it. The boundary stays open until the ground settles it.
