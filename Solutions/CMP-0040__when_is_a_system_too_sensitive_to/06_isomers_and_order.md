## DERIVATION

### Q1  -  Are the fine and coarse descriptions isomers?

- Fig 12 :117: "isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible
  in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."
- An isomer pair is two molecules at **one level**: same atoms, different bond. The fine trajectory
  and the coarse distribution are **not** two molecules at one level. They are the same system at two
  scales, related by a directional, lossy transform (Fig 4 :21: "The membrane is the boundary between
  the sizes, and the rule about what may cross it. Not a wall: a filter with a direction.").
- Fig 4 :22: "Only methods cross up. Only head starts come down." A trajectory recording does not
  cross up as a recording; a method for characterising it does.
- ⇒ The fine/coarse pair is **not an isomer pair**. Calling it one would misidentify a cross-scale
  transform as a same-level rearrangement. `general`, triangulated (Fig 4 membrane + Fig 12
  definition, low overlap: one governs scale, the other governs bond).

**Falsifier for Q1.** If a single formalism can write the trajectory and the distribution over the
same atom set with only a bond changed and no scale transform, the pair is an isomer pair after all
and Fig 4 is the wrong frame. Written before the run.

### Q2  -  What order governs the bond inside the fine description?

- Fig 12 :111-112: "A vocabulary without bonds can name a state and cannot name a route to one."
  "A plan is a sequence. A goal is a comparison. Progress is a subtraction."
- Amplification is forward in time. The residual of one turn is what the next turn composes against
  (Fig 13 :88-89: "The residual of the turn just closed is what the next turn composes against, so no
  two passes begin from the same place.").
- ⇒ The correct bond for the fine description is **→ sequence**, not **+ conjunction**. Under +
  the order of operands is free and the amplification would be invisible; under → the order is
  decisive. Operators table: → "swap them. Does it become a different thing? Yes."
- This is a structural reason the fine description cannot be collapsed into a single static state
  description. A static vocabulary can name the state at t; it cannot name the route by which a
  small difference became a large one.

**Falsifier for Q2.** A demonstration that the separation of nearby trajectories is symmetric under
time reversal at the level that matters for prediction would make + the correct bond and break this
finding. `specified`, unmeasured here.

### Q3  -  Are there isomers inside the fine description?

- Fig 13 :76: "A gradient over space and one over time are different terms, because which state it is
  bonded to is part of what it is."
- The same interaction can therefore be written as different first-order molecules according to
  which state the structure is bonded to. These **are** isomers in Fig 12's sense: same atoms,
  different bond target, different substance.
- This is the figure-grounded reason two systems with the same variables can have different
  predictability: the arrangement, not the inventory, decides. The world analogue is the logistic
  map bifurcation diagram (Attractor article): "one dynamic equation can have vastly different
  attractors depending on the choice of parameters."
- **Falsifier:** if two systems with the same atom inventory and the same bond target show different
  amplification rates, the isomer reading is incomplete and an axis outside the six is needed. This
  is the standing candidate strain, not hidden.

### Q4  -  What is the dependency order between the three domains?

Fig 3 orders a chain by dependency, not importance. Applied here:

1. **Perception.** Does any instrument return something about s1? If nothing registers at any

2. **Vocabulary.** Are there terms for what the state affords: separation, boundedness, spread? A

3. **Objective.** What counts as progress: a shrinking fine residual, or a settled coarse residual?

A reading of the coarse descriptor taken before the fine residual has been measured is a reading

Each analogue is a witness from a different field with low overlap with the framework. Agreement

shape but does not derive it.

## CONCLUSION

The fine/coarse pair is **not** an isomer pair. It is a cross-scale transform governed by Fig 4, and
the order of that transform is directional: methods cross up, head starts come down, and the gap R_T
must be measured. Inside the fine description there **are** real isomers: a gradient over space and a
gradient over time are different terms, which is the structural reason two systems with the same
inventory can differ in predictability. The governing bond for an amplifying trajectory is →
sequence, not + conjunction, because the residual re-enters and the ordering is decisive. Dependency
order runs from instrument, through amplification and closure, to the coarse descriptor; reversing it
produces a reading below the break.

This framing rescues what is checkable without importing a seventh primitive: a bounded coarse
descriptor, an amplifying fine residual, a directional transform, and a measured gap. The specific
numbers remain world-frame bindings, provenanced as such.
