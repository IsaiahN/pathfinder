## Three findings in the wider literature that bear directly on this question

1. **The world-frame counterpart of the figure shape is "locally unstable yet globally stable."** The *Attractor* article states it directly: nearby points diverge but never depart from the attractor. This is strong corroboration for the two-slot shape, not a derivation of it. It comes from a field with low overlap with the framework, so it is a genuine second witness for the structural claim, subject to the caveat that it is the standard view in that field.
2. **Predictability itself is flow dependent.** Slingo and Palmer (2011) report that some regimes are highly unpredictable while others contain substantial predictability, and that the climatic response can be predictable under weak forcing. This warns against treating "chaotic" as a single global verdict, which is the same warning Fig 1 :22 makes about aggregating across slots.
3. **A stable-looking distribution can be an artefact.** Slingo and Palmer document under-dispersion and model bias; the *Ensemble forecasting* article records that reliability must be checked against observations. This is the operational form of the Fig 4 :30 requirement to measure the round-trip gap instead of assuming it zero.

## The two-stage offer

**Stage 1.** Send the residual, the shape and the pre-registered refuter. No links, no framework, no request for trust. Verdict recorded before anything else.

**Stage 2, opt-in only.** If the reader is curious, offer the three public sources listed in the email. Their stage-1 verdict is never pooled as later corroboration.

**Stage 3.** Offer the formula as a method. Each user states a ground and a refuter before running it (formula, Direction). *The Recursive Self-Improvement Trap* states that "the truly testable output would need to be derived from the real architecture or working the formula yourself," so a simulated output is a candidate, not a settled result, and a failure report is more useful than a success report.
