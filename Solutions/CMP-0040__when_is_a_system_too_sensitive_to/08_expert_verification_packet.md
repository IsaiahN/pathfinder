# Expert verification packet  -  the Tether on fine versus distribution-level predictability

**What this is:** a claim in a form a complex-systems researcher, sensitivity analyst, or forecast
verifier can check. Mode, derivation and refuters are stated before the conclusion, so a reader knows
what to look for and what would settle it.

**Mode: `specified` on the boundary rule, `general` on the structural shape, not `grounded`.** No
measurement in this run settles the claim. This is a shape and a route, not a result.

## 1. The claim, in one paragraph

A system crosses from "precise prediction possible" to "precise prediction impossible but distribution
or regime prediction possible" under three conditions together: (i) the residual on the fine
trajectory slot amplifies without trending down faster than the instrument can refresh its reading of
the initial state; (ii) the amplified remainder remains inside a bounded reach, so a coarse descriptor
can settle; (iii) the move to the coarse description is lossy, and the loss is measured as a round-trip
gap rather than assumed away. The survival of the regime is bought by the loss at the crossing, not in
spite of it. The verdict is per slot against a ground, never from a global average. The framework
returns this shape, not a numerical threshold and not a named attractor.

## 2. Three checkable sub-claims

### C1  -  framework-internal plus observation-facing; checkable by reading two figures and one world criterion

- **Statement:** the fine slot's residual amplifies, and the amplification is a forward sequence, not
  a symmetric conjunction. A global average can hide a live fine slot.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 13 :31-32  -  "a remainder compounding through a chain that amplifies is
  chaos, and the detail a crossing discards reappears above it as noise." Also Fig 13 :88-89  -  "The
  residual of the turn just closed is what the next turn composes against, so no two passes begin from
  the same place." And Fig 1 :22  -  "Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an
  average."
- **What to check:** does the world criterion, positive maximal Lyapunov exponent plus boundedness
  (Chaos theory, opened 2026-11-22), pick out the same phenomenon the figure line describes? Is the
  separation of nearby trajectories ordered forward in time in the system you know?
- **Refuter:** a system whose fine residual demonstrably amplifies at every accessible resolution yet
  whose coarse descriptor fails to settle at any scale; or a system where separation is symmetric at
  the level that matters for prediction, making the conjunction bond correct.

### C2  -  forecast-facing; checkable by an ensemble modeller or sensitivity analyst

- **Statement:** the coarse descriptor is reapplicable because the cross-scale transform discards the
  amplified detail, and the discarded amount must be measured as R_T.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 4 :27  -  "The transform between scales loses information, and that is the
  point." Fig 4 :31-32  -  "The gap is not an error to eliminate. It is the measurement of what the
  coarser description cannot hold, and it is the only honest report of how much was given up." Fig 4
  :30  -  "So take the round trip and measure the gap. Without it you are navigating by dead reckoning:
  confident about a position nobody checked."
- **What to check:** is the forecast distribution a representative sample of the observed
  distribution? In the world literature, Leith 1974 required this (*Ensemble forecasting*) and Slingo
  and Palmer (2011) document under-dispersion and model bias as the ways it fails. Does anyone measure
  the loss between the fine and coarse descriptions, or is it assumed zero?
- **Refuter:** a distribution declared predictable that is under-dispersive, repeats one structural
  assumption, or was read off an aggregate rather than per slot.

### C3  -  framework-internal derivation; checkable by reading two figures

- **Statement:** regime prediction requires a bounded reach; the fine and coarse instruments are two
  opposed tracks and must not be read as one ladder.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 13 :115  -  "The set is closed. The arrangements are not." Fig 13 :118  - 
  "infinite effort inside a finite reach." Fig 6 :31  -  "Neither answer tells you much about the other.
  Run them as two tracks, and never read one against the other."
- **What to check:** can the amplified remainder leave the reach? The world counterpart is "locally
  unstable yet globally stable" (*Attractor*). Does improving initial-condition resolution change the
  distribution-level verdict, or does importing an invariant-measure frame change the trajectory-level
  verdict? If either, the two tracks collapse.
- **Refuter:** a derivation showing an amplifying remainder can leave the closure; or a demonstrated
  coupling that makes one instrument's improvement change the other slot's verdict.

## 3. What is explicitly not claimed

- Not a numerical Lyapunov exponent, ensemble size, or forecast horizon.
- Not a named attractor, and not the claim that the figure derives "attractor" or "invariant measure."
  These are bindings from the world frame, provenanced as such.
- Not that every chaotic system has a predictable distribution. Boundedness and a measured round-trip
  gap are conditions.
- Not that more measurement restores the precise forecast. The instruments are two tracks (Fig 6 :31).
- Not that expert agreement settles the claim. Fig 2 :40: "disqualifying for a verdict: agreement about
  the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool."
- Not `grounded`. Nothing here was settled by a measurement in this run.

## 5. What an expert can check in twenty minutes

1. Read Fig 13 :31-32, Fig 4 :27-32 and Fig 13 :115  -  three short passages.
2. Check C1 against a system you know: is the failure really amplification of a residual, and is the
   trajectory separation ordered forward in time?
3. Name one instrument that would measure the round-trip gap between the fine and coarse descriptions,
   or say why none is possible. This is the decisive question.
4. Check whether the ensemble you would trust is representative of the observed distribution, rather
   than merely narrow.
5. Send the refutation. A failure is more useful than an endorsement.
