# Outreach draft  -  check the boundary claim before you decide about the framework

**Use:** one-to-one, or as a cover letter attached to a submission through a public venue in
`03_public_contact_points.csv`. **Not** for bulk send.

**Hard precondition:** do not send until the refuter in `01_figure_grounded_audit.md` §3 is in the
first paragraph and the three links are in the last. The stage-1 claim must survive without the
theory attached.

---

**Subject:** One boundary claim about chaos and ensemble prediction, with the refuter written first

> Hi [name],
>
> I have one specific output I would like you to check against what you already know. I am not
> asking you to read a theory first, and I am not asking you to agree with anything. If the output
> does not survive, there is nothing else to look at.
>
> **The residual (`specified`, not `grounded`).** I am distinguishing two slots, and refusing to
> average them. Slot s1 is a precise state forecast. Slot s2 is a forecast of the distribution,
> the attractor or the regime frequency. The claim is that these come apart under one condition:
> when the difference between two arbitrarily close initial states is amplified by the dynamics
> faster than the instrument can refresh its reading of the initial state, s1 stops tracking, while
> s2 can remain stable *because the coarse description throws away exactly the detail that was
> being amplified*. The loss between the two levels is the thing that makes the regime reapplicable,
> and it has to be measured rather than assumed to be zero.
>
> This is a shape, not a number. It does not name a Lyapunov exponent or an attractor.
>
> **What I am not claiming.** Not that every chaotic system has a predictable distribution. Not
> that an ensemble which looks stable is therefore reliable. Not that better measurements will
> eventually restore the precise forecast. A global average can sit near zero while a single
> trajectory slot is live, so the claim has to be checked per slot against a ground.
>
> **What would refute it, written before sending this:**
> 1. a system whose fine residual amplifies at every accessible resolution, yet whose coarse
>    descriptor fails to settle against a ground at any scale;
> 2. a stable, ground-settled regime with no amplification of the fine residual at any scale;
> 3. a distribution declared predictable whose ensemble is under-dispersive, repeats one structural
>    assumption, or was read off an aggregate rather than per slot;
> 4. a demonstration that an amplifying remainder can leave the bounded reach;
> 5. a numerical threshold derived from the framework alone.
>
> If any holds, I will retract or narrow the claim. A one-line "this fails because..." is more
> useful to me than agreement. If you would rather not engage, no reply is needed.
>
> **Before you answer, what would you count as a failure?** I would like your verdict on this
> paragraph recorded before I show you any source.
>
> **If it survives, and only then:** the public sources are below. The figures, not my summary, are
> the authority.
> - Verbatim figure text with line numbers: the local framework extract cited as `Fig N :line` in the packet.
> - Chaos theory and attractor references: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chaos_theory and https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Attractor
> - Uncertainty in weather and climate prediction, Slingo and Palmer 2011: https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC3270390/
>
> **If you want to use the machinery:** the formula is public. Run it on a problem you know well
> and can check against a ground. Write down what would refute your result *before* you run it.
> A reported failure is more useful to me than a reported success.
>
>  -  [name]
