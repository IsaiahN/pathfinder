# Expert verification packet: the Tether on hydrothermal gradients

**What this is:** a claim in a form a hydrothermal geochemist, physical chemist, or origins researcher can check. Mode, derivation, and refuters are stated before the conclusion, so a reader knows what to look for and what would settle it.

**Mode: `specified` on the shape, `general` on the structural result, not `grounded`.** No Hadean measurement is claimed. This packet is a simulated derivation checked for internal consistency against the figures, not a ground reading.

## 1. The claim, in one paragraph

A gradient in the sense the figures use the term is "a difference that can be spent", and what makes it a gradient is directedness rather than staticness. Energy on its own is directionless; energy and a gradient compose a flow. Applied to early chemistry, the shape a sufficient setting must satisfy is: a spendable difference focused at the working site, maintained by a source that outlasts the chemistry it drives, and coupled through a flow into a network that closes as a cycle. "Strong" is a threshold comparison at the working site, not a bulk pH number. "Stable" is a maintained source-to-channel contact, not a static difference. A catalyst or a maintained H2 flux can occupy the driving role instead. The figures do not say whether any Hadean vent satisfied this; that is a ground question and the literature is in live conflict about it.

## 2. Three checkable sub-claims

### C1. Framework-internal; checkable by reading the figure text

- **Statement:** a gradient is a defined structural term: a spendable, directed difference. Energy alone is directionless; energy plus gradient compose a flow. A static distribution is not a gradient in this sense.
- **Source, verbatim:** Symbols table :97: "A difference that can be spent. Not merely a distribution: what makes it a gradient is directedness." Fig 13 :46: "What makes a gradient a gradient is that it can be spent, and neither an ordering nor a span of space has directedness." Fig 13 :14: "conserved, finite, directionless alone". Fig 13 :76: "Energy and a gradient compose a flow."
- **Refuter:** show a static distribution doing net compounding chemistry with no consumable directedness, or energy driving net work with no directed difference anywhere in the coupling. Either breaks the sub-claim at the ground.

### C2. Threshold and scale; checkable against the vent literature

- **Statement:** "strong" is a comparison between two quantities at a stated scale. The bulk ΔpH and the focused ΔpH available to a molecular machine are different operands, and both appear in the literature with very different values.
- **Source, verbatim:** Operators table :49: "a threshold relation between two quantities". Fig 9 :31: "your words are borrowed too, so describe the effect rather than the cause." Fig 13 :76: "A gradient over space and one over time are different terms, because which state it is bonded to is part of what it is."
- **What to check:** Schwander et al. 2023 report "a natural proton gradient of roughly three pH units" at the vent-ocean interface. Jackson 2016 calculates that in a thick inorganic membrane the gradient across the machine can be "only about 0.004 units". Are these two measurements of different quantities, as the shape claim requires, or is one of them the wrong operand?
- **Refuter:** show that the bulk number is the legitimate operand for "strong" in a real chemiosmotic coupling, or show one instrument that measures both numbers in the same units. Then the scale distinction fails.

### C3. Source and substitution; checkable against experiment

- **Statement:** persistence is a maintained source-to-channel contact, and the gradient is not the only structure that can occupy the "drives chemistry" role. A catalyst that survives its own use, and a maintained H2 flux, can do it instead.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 10 :24: "the ground does not decay, the channel does." Fig 11 :39: "capability is a property of agent-and-habitat, never of the agent alone." Fig 12 :119: "catalyst. A part that survives its own use." Fig 12 :121: "cycle. The output re-enters as input. The sign of the loop decides whether it closes, and a chain cannot close by construction."
- **What to check:** Schwander et al. 2023 report formate and acetate "in free solution with no gradients or compartments" using mineral catalysts; Brunk and Marshall 2021 write that "it does not seem that the proton gradient is needed as an energy source for the scenario to work". PNAS 2026 argues ancient systems had "shorter-lived and less focused venting".
- **Refuter:** show that all net synthesis in the relevant setting requires the gradient, with the gradient removed as the controlled variable; or show a spendable difference persisting with no maintaining source. The first breaks the substitution claim, the second breaks the persistence claim.

## 3. What is explicitly not claimed

- Not that any Hadean vent was strong and stable enough. That is a ground verdict, and no measurement is supplied here.
- Not that alkaline vents are the cradle of life, and not that pH gradients were necessary. Two of the cited sources say the gradient was not needed.
- Not a new primitive or a derivation of chemistry from the six-term set. Gradient and network are already held; a seventh term would be a wrong import (Fig 6).
- Not `grounded`, not a theory of everything, and not a uniqueness claim over frameworks.
- Not an inference from agreement. Most cited sources share the Russell and Martin evidence pool, so their agreement is one witness (Fig 2 :40).

## 5. What would settle it

1. **Operand test:** is the right "strong" quantity the bulk ΔpH or the focused ΔpH at the machine? Jackson 2016 and Schwander et al. 2023 supply the two competing numbers.
2. **Machine test:** a laboratory non-protein machine utilizing a ΔpH across an inorganic membrane. Jackson 2016 calls this the needed direct demonstration.
3. **Persistence test:** does the source maintain the focused difference longer than the chemistry it drives? Field lifetimes and PNAS 2026's critique are the two readings.
4. **Substitution test:** remove the gradient, keep catalysts and H2, and see whether carbon fixation is unchanged.
