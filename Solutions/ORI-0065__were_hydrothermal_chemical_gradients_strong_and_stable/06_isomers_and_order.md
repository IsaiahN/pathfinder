## DERIVATION

### Q1. Are "gradient over space" and "gradient over time" different terms?

Fig 13 :76: **"A gradient over space and one over time are different terms, because which state it is bonded to is part of what it is."**

So a pH difference across a membrane (gradient bonded to space) and a maintained flux over the lifetime of a vent (gradient bonded to time) are two terms. "Strong" governs the first; "stable" governs the second. They share the gradient atom but differ in the state they are bonded to, which is exactly Fig 12's point that the bond carries information the operands do not. Fig 12 :96: "Two entries can share every ingredient and be inverses of one another."

This is the figure-grounded reason the two conditions cannot be averaged into one "enoughness" score. A thesis that scores a vent high on spatial magnitude and low on temporal persistence has two different residuals, not one middling one.

**Falsifier:** show that a figure's gradient term is state-independent, so that bonding it to space or to time changes nothing. That breaks Fig 13 :76 and collapses the union reading of F6 in `01_figure_grounded_audit.md`.

### Q2. Is the gradient an isomer of a catalyst or a cycle?

Fig 12 :117: **"isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."**

Fig 12 :119: **"catalyst. A part that survives its own use. Spent and regenerating parts behave differently across uses, and nothing records which a term is."**

Fig 12 :121: **"cycle. The output re-enters as input. The sign of the loop decides whether it closes, and a chain cannot close by construction."**

Gradient, catalyst, and cycle are three *structures* described by Fig 12, but they are not isomers of one another in the strict sense: they are not the same atoms under a different bond. They are different structural roles. The geochemical sources make this concrete. Schwander et al. 2023 report CO2 reduction in free solution with no gradients or compartments, with awaruite, magnetite, and greigite as catalysts. That is a catalyst term doing work without a gradient term. Brunk and Marshall 2021 describe a maintained H2 source from serpentinization, which is a cycle term: the output of water-rock reaction re-enters as the input to carbon fixation.

So the defensible claim is **functional substitution**, not isomerism: more than one structure can occupy the "drives chemistry" role, and which one is occupied is a ground question. Fig 12 :123: **"functional group. A substructure whose presence fixes the outcome. The group says what kind; the qualifier says which member."** The functional group here is "a maintained directed difference"; the qualifier is which structure provides it.

**Falsifier:** show that a gradient and a catalyst are the same atoms under a different bond (a true isomer claim), or show that carbon fixation proceeds with neither. The first would require a derivation the figure text does not supply; the second would break the maintained-difference functional group and force a new term.

### Q3. What is the order of composition, and where does it close?

Fig 13 :78 to :85 gives one chain: **"a state / a structure / relations / fields / gradients / flows / networks / and on"** (opened in the verbatim extract). Applied to a vent:

1. **state**: energy, held in a hot reduced fluid and a cooler oxidized ocean.
2. **structure**: a gradient, the difference that can be spent (Fig 13 :22).
3. **relations / fields**: the mineral barrier that separates the two fluids and focuses the difference.
4. **gradients**: the spatial pH and redox differences; the temporal maintained flux.
5. **flows**: protons, electrons, H2, carbon species (Fig 13 :76, "Energy and a gradient compose a flow").
6. **networks**: carbon fixation and product synthesis, a cycle if the outputs feed back (Fig 12 :121).

Each step acts on one kind and yields the other (Fig 13 :30: "each step acts on one kind and yields the other"). The loop closes if the products regenerate the conditions, which is the sign question Fig 12 :121 raises. A chain cannot close by construction; a cycle can. So the difference between "a gradient drove one reaction" and "a gradient drove early chemistry" is the closure of the loop, not the size of the initial difference.

Fig 12 :104: **"A settled molecule becomes an atom for whatever composes over it. So irreducible is a fact about a level and never about the world."** Once a carbon-fixation cycle settles against the ground, it becomes an atom at the next level. Whether any Hadean cycle settled is a ground question.

**Falsifier:** show a one-pass reaction sequence with no feedback that the field would still call "early chemistry". That would break the cycle requirement and reduce the question to a single flow.

### Q4. What is the scale order of the instruments?

Three scales, three instruments (Fig 3 dependency order, not importance order):

| scale | quantity | instrument already returning something | source |
|---|---|---|---|
| molecular, nanometre barrier | focused ΔpH, redox potential at the machine | reactor and electrochemical measurements | Altair et al. 2021; Jackson 2016 says the demonstration is still owed |
| vent, micrometre to metre | microcompartment geometry, thermophoresis, concentration | chemical gardens, flow reactors, microscopy | Altair et al. 2021; Schwander et al. 2023 |
| field, decades to millennia | persistence and focusing of venting | field observation and dating | Schwander et al. 2023 (Lost City over 30,000 years); PNAS 2026 disputes the ancient case |

Fig 6 :32: **"An instrument is not built from a description. It is improved from a worse instrument already returning something."** All three rows return something, so these are INWARD moves, not searches for a proto-instrument. Fig 10 :80 on independence renewed by separate experience applies: the three rows must not be read as three votes from one pool.

**Falsifier:** show that one instrument legitimately measures the focused, vent, and field quantities in the same units. Then the three rows collapse and the scale ordering is wrong.

## CONCLUSION

"Strong" and "stable" are two different terms under the substrate: a gradient bonded to space and a gradient bonded to time (Fig 13 :76). They are not isomers of catalysts or cycles, but functional substitutes for one another in the "maintained directed difference" functional group, and which one does the work in a given setting is a ground question (Fig 12 :119, :121, :123). The composition runs state to structure to flow to network, and only a cycle that closes counts as chemistry that persists. Three scales require three instruments, already returning values at different levels of maturity. Nothing here fixes whether a Hadean vent satisfied any of it.
