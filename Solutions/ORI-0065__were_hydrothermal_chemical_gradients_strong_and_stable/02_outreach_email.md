**Subject:** One checkable claim about what a chemical gradient would have to be

> Hi [name],
>
> I have one specific output I would like you to check against what you already know. I am not asking you to read the framework first, and I am not asking you to agree. If the output does not survive, there is nothing else to look at.
>
> **The output (`specified`, not `grounded`):** a chemical gradient, in the sense the figures use the term, is "a difference that can be spent". It is defined by directedness, not by staticness. Energy on its own is "conserved, finite, directionless alone"; energy and a gradient compose a flow. Applied to early chemistry, that gives a shape rather than a verdict: a setting would have to hold a spendable difference, focused at the place where work is done, maintained by a source that outlasts the chemistry it drives, and coupled through a flow into a network of reactions.
>
> Two consequences of that shape are worth stating before any theory:
>
> 1. **"Strong" is a threshold at the working site, not a bulk number.** A bulk pH difference and the difference actually available to a molecular machine are different quantities. One review reports roughly three pH units at the vent-ocean interface; another calculates about 0.004 of a pH unit across a machine in a thick inorganic membrane. I would like to know which quantity you would count as the operand for "strong".
> 2. **"Stable" is a maintained channel, not a static difference.** A difference that simply stays put is a distribution, not a gradient. The question is whether the source keeps the difference spendable for long enough, not whether the difference is unchanging.
>
> **What would refute the shape claim (written down before this message):**
> 1. a static distribution doing net compounding chemistry with no consumable directedness;
> 2. energy driving net chemical work with no directed difference anywhere in the coupling;
> 3. a spendable difference persisting with no maintaining source;
> 4. "strong" and "stable" turning out to be one condition, or one alone being sufficient;
> 5. a demonstration that the bulk gradient, not the focused difference, is the right operand.
>
> If any one holds, I will withdraw or repair the shape claim in public. A one-line "this fails because..." is more useful to me than agreement. If you would rather not engage, no reply is needed.
>
> **What I am not claiming:** that any particular Hadean vent was strong and stable enough, that the alkaline vent hypothesis is right, or that pH gradients were the necessary energy source. Several experimental groups report CO2 reduction with no gradient at all, driven by catalysts and a maintained H2 supply. The figures return a shape, not a name and not a date. The empirical verdict belongs to measurement, and I do not have one.
>
> **If it survives, and only then:** the sources are below. The figures, not my summary of them, are the authority.
> - Figures and formula for direct download (SVG, branch `general-agent`): https://github.com/IsaiahN/tether/tree/general-agent/figures

The framework's own rule is that a loop cannot check the direction of its own question, and the essay record warns that outputs can be contrived to fit the formula.
