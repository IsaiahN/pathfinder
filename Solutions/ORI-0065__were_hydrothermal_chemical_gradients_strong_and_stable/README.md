# Solution ORI-0065

**Question:** Were hydrothermal chemical gradients strong and stable enough to drive early chemistry?

**Source:** origins of life.txt line 91

**Section:** 7. Energy, environment, and chemical cycles

**Mode:** `specified` on the shape claims, `general` on the structural result. The empirical question about actual Hadean gradients is a `gap` from the figures and is reserved to the ground. Nothing here is `grounded`.

**Status:** done

**Registry status:** pending

**What is being decided, in effect terms.** Does a spendable, directed difference in a hydrothermal setting have (a) enough magnitude at the site where work is done and (b) enough persistence to couple to energy and build chemical networks? Option A says yes and licenses the claim that vents drove early chemistry. Option B says the figures can fix the *shape* such a gradient must have but cannot return the empirical verdict, which belongs to measurement. The two options lead to different outreach: A sends a ground verdict we hold no instrument for; B sends a checkable shape, a pre-registered refuter, and a named set of measurements that would close it.

**Boundary.** Nothing here is a ground reading. This is a simulated derivation checked for internal consistency against the figures. It cannot settle the question it reports on. The Recursive Self-Improvement Trap, opened 2026-11-23: "the truly testable output would need to be derived from the real architecture or working the formula yourself." ## What is deliberately not here

- No personal emails of named individuals.
- No ranking of people by how likely they are to agree.
- No bulk-send plan.
- No claim that the figures settle an empirical question about the Hadean earth.

Three reasons, from the figure text:

1. Fig 2 :35: "Alignment is triangulation, not negotiation. A mirror cannot correct you." Agreement selected for receptiveness is a mirror, not a check.
2. Fig 2 :40: "agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool." Most origins-of-life sources cite the same small set of reviews, so their agreement is one witness.
3. Fig 10 :52: "Bring a characterised residual, never the source." The first contact carries the residual and its refuter, not the framework's interior.
