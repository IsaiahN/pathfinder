## DERIVATION

### Q1: Union, partition, or one term?

- Fig 9 :54: **"when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again."**
- *The Library of Babel*: *"Perhaps your problem was a bundle or conflation of several problems masquerading as one?"*
- The three conditions can hold at once in one observation: a forcing changes smoothly, a comparison fires, and an impact registers elsewhere later. That is overlap, so it is a **union**, not a partition.
- Fig 9 :55: *"A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones. A single lever clears the region where only one condition holds and leaves the intersections standing, because the second condition still binds."*
- ⇒ Each condition gets its own loop; the overlap (one event in which all three hold) gets a compound loop. **Falsifier:** a single figure line that governs all three conditions as one term. Triangulated (Fig 9 plus the essay), low overlap.

### Q2: What is the order of the loops?

- Fig 3 :20: **"The chain is ordered by dependency, not by importance."** Fig 3 :19: **"Optimising a link that already works cannot rescue one that does not."**
- Fig 9 :27-28: **"describe it before you go looking, or any frame you pick will seem to fit."**
- Order: (1) describe the residual as three conditions; (2) fix the comparison, because a delay is not assessable until there is something delayed; (3) locate the residual in slots, because a cascade cannot be read without an indexed residual; (4) enumerate the habitat outward, because the impact that matters may be three steps out; (5) take the round trip across scales, because a delay read at one scale only is dead reckoning (Fig 4 :30).
- **Falsifier:** a case in which the cascade can be assessed before the comparison is fixed, or the round trip before the slot is indexed. Single-source for the ordering (Fig 3 dependency plus Fig 4 round trip), but the two figures are independent within the framework.

### Q3: Are the two "threshold" senses isomers?

- Fig 12 :117: **"same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."**
- Fig 12 :124: **"functional group. A substructure whose presence fixes the outcome. The group says what kind; the qualifier says which member."**
- The framework uses the word threshold in two places with different atoms. Fig 5 :42 calls the MDL acceptance test *"a bargain, not a threshold."* Fig 12 :87 and the Operators table define ⋛ as *"a threshold relation between two quantities."* Those are not the same substance: one prices a mint, the other compares quantities.
- By Fig 12 they share no atom set, so they are **not isomers**. They are a **functional group**: both are comparisons (Fig 12 :112, *"A goal is a comparison"*), with different qualifiers, one a description-length price and one a physical relation.
- The stronger analogue: two Earth systems whose state variables approach critical values at the same rate but whose habitats differ (one with a three-step cascade, one with direct contact only) would have the same comparison atom and different impact substances. That **is** an isomer claim under Fig 12 :117, same atoms, different arrangement. **Falsifier:** show that the cascade arrangement adds an atom rather than rearranging held ones, or that two such systems have identical impact behaviour. Triangulated for the bond definition (Fig 12 plus Operators table); single-source for the Earth-system instance.

### Q4: The world analogues, and what they do and do not license

- **Mathematics:** Li, Ye, Qian and Huang 2019 separate a *breaking time* from a *point of no return*, with a quantified window between them. That is a world analogue of the comparison/cascade split, not a figure line. It corroborates Q1 and Q2 from a low-overlap field.
- **Climate modelling:** Ritchie et al. 2021 show a threshold can be temporarily exceeded without a state change when the overshoot is short relative to the system timescale. That corroborates the separation of comparison from impact.
- **Earth-system review:** Wang et al. 2023 reports the convention that reserves "tipping point" for cases with *"little temporal lag separating commitment and realization"*. That is the field's own version of the split.
- **Paleoclimate:** Brovkin et al. 2021 describes slow change passing a critical threshold and impacts cascading through coupled systems, an analogue of Fig 11 :52-53.
- None of these supplies a figure line and none can flip a figure verdict. They are secondary. Their agreement is partly one shared evidence pool (Fig 2 :40), so it counts as one witness with several names.

## CONCLUSION

The question's bundle is a **union**, not a partition and not one term. The loops run in dependency order: describe the three conditions, fix the comparison, index the residual, enumerate the cascade, then take the scale round trip. The two senses of "threshold" in the framework are a **functional group**, not isomers; the genuine isomer claim is two systems with the same atoms arranged into different cascade structures. R_T is nonzero and enumerated. This rescues what is defensible in the question without merging three different quantities or inventing a threshold value, a lag, or a seventh primitive.
