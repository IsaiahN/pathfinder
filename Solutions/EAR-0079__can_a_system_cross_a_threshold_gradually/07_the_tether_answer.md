# The Tether's answer: can a system cross a threshold gradually, with impacts appearing only much later?

**Mode: `specified` for the three shape claims, `general` for the structural rule. Not `grounded`.** Simulated derivation checked against the figures (`05_what_shape_means.md` §6), not a ground reading.

## 1. The residual it answers

The question bundles three conditions: a forcing that changes smoothly, a comparison that fires at a critical value, and an impact that registers at a different slot or scale much later (Fig 9 :4-5, :54). The figure-grounded move is to split before searching. A single answer that merges them would average the very signal that says where to look (Fig 1 :28: *"The gap is measured per object, not per step. A system that averages across objects has thrown away the only signal that says where to look."*).

## 2. The shape, by domain

**D1, threshold dynamics (mathematics).** Yes for the approach, no for the comparison. A threshold is *"a threshold relation between two quantities"* (Fig 12 :87), with the test *"is it about which is larger, rather than both being present? Yes."* (Fig 12 :89). A comparison is a relation with a truth value, not a duration. What can be gradual is the ordering being compared. Smoothness is a derived leftover, not a primitive: *"A continuum was the seventh and was removed for failing exactly this: it is a spectrum without gaps, or smoothness over space and time."* (Fig 13 :43-44). So a system can approach a critical value continuously and still have a comparison that fires at that value. **Verdict `triangulated`** (Fig 12 bond definition plus Fig 13 minimality argument).

**D2, Earth-system components.** The impact is not the crossing. The residual is indexed by slot: *"The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated."* (Fig 1 :19), and *"a global R near zero with one live slot residual is a legal state"* (Fig 1 :20). The impact that matters can be several steps away: *"Scoping the habitat to direct contact is how DDT reached the eagles: first-order enumeration, second-order effect, and the residual that mattered was three steps out."* (Fig 11 :52-53). If the impact is read at another scale, the bridge is lossy and the round trip must be measured: *"So take the round trip and measure the gap. Without it you are navigating by dead reckoning: confident about a position nobody checked."* (Fig 4 :30). **Verdict `triangulated`** (Fig 1 per-slot indexing plus Fig 11 contact cascade; the scale bridge is `single-source` within this run).

**D3, verification.** Agreement cannot settle any of this. *"An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks."* (Fig 2 :29). *"A verifier that can reconstruct the claim cannot verify it."* (Fig 10 :42). The anchor here is a measurement, a bifurcation experiment, a palaeorecord or an instrument, never consensus. **Verdict `single-source`**, since in this simulated run every seat shares one evidence pool.

## 3. What it explicitly does not say

- Not that any particular Earth system has been shown to do this. No ground reading is claimed.
- Not a threshold value, a lag in years, or the name of a tipping element. The framework returns *"a kind of thing, never the answer itself"* (Fig 9 :32).
- Not that the threshold itself moves as the system changes. That is a different question with its own loop.
- Not that a tipping cascade is guaranteed. The habitat is enumerated, not composed, and *"introduction is available; exclusion is not"* (Fig 11 :61).
- Not that the smooth approach identifies the mechanism. An instrument that reads a curve *"cannot comment on the source"* (*The Hard Problem is About Source*).
- Not `grounded`. Nothing here was settled by measurement.

## 4. What would settle it: the ground

A source-discriminating instrument, not a further argument. Concretely:

1. **Crossing test:** in a model or experiment, does the control quantity pass the critical value while the state remains in its basin, with a measured separation between the breaking time and the point of no return? (World analogue: Li et al. 2019; Ritchie et al. 2021.)
2. **Slot test:** is the residual live in a slot other than the one being watched, with a global reading near zero? (Fig 1 :19, :20.)
3. **Cascade test:** enumerate the habitat outward and measure the step count from crossing to impact. Is the residual that matters three steps out, or in direct contact? (Fig 11 :52-53.)
4. **Scale test:** take the round trip between the forcing scale and the impact scale and report the gap R_T, rather than assuming it is zero (Fig 4 :30).
5. **Standing falsifiers from `01`:** a figure line making ⋛ a duration; a derivation of smoothness from the other members; R shown to be a global scalar; a direct-contact late impact with no intermediate actor; a measured zero round-trip gap; or a single figure line collapsing the union.

## 5. The honest stop

**Unreached, not unreachable.** The six-term set is a visible guess, not a discovery of nature's own list; the framework stops where the instruments stop distinguishing and records the lineage so the stop is checkable. *"So a search that finds nothing is never a proof of absence."* (Fig 8 :39). *"Unreachable is a verdict here, not a silence"* (Fig 13 :134). The shape is available now; the numbers owe a ground reading.

## 6. One-paragraph version for outreach

> The question bundles three things, and the Tether keeps them apart. A threshold is an ordered comparison between two quantities, not a duration; the continuous part is the quantity being compared, and smoothness is a derived leftover rather than a primitive. The impact is not the crossing: the residual is indexed by slot, so a near-zero global reading with one live slot is a legal state, and the impact that matters can be three steps out in the habitat, arriving much later somewhere else. If it is read at a different scale, the bridge is lossy and the gap has to be measured. What the framework returns is that shape, not a threshold value, a lag, or a named tipping element. The test that would close it is a ground reading: a measured separation between crossing and state change, a live slot with a quiet global mean, an enumerated cascade, and a round-trip gap reported honestly. Until then the answer is `specified`, not `grounded`.
