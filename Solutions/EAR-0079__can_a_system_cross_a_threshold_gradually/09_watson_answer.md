## VERDICT (from the figures)

The qualified answer is **yes**: a system can approach a threshold continuously, because gradualness belongs to the ordering and the threshold is an ordered comparison on that ordering. The qualified answer is also **no**, if the question means one continuous process with one intrinsic delay: the comparison and the impact are separate terms. A threshold is a comparison; an impact is a residual registered in a slot and propagated through a habitat. A near-zero global reading with one live slot is a legal state, the residual that matters can be three steps out, and a delay read at another scale carries a round-trip gap that must be measured. The framework supplies the shape, not a threshold value, a lag or a named tipping element. This is `specified`, ungrounded, candidate. Per-domain verdicts stay separate: D1 `triangulated`, D2 `triangulated`, D3 `single-source`.

- **cost if wrong:** bounded and locatable. The packet is mode-labelled, states refuters before the conclusion and records the lineage, so an error is findable rather than invisible. The real cost is spending the independence of outside readers by sending a merged claim instead of a split shape, which Fig 2 :42 prices (*"Buy independence in proportion to the cost of being wrong."*). That cost is avoidable by leading with the three-condition shape and its refuter.
- **undoable:** yes. The union split is reversible by a re-census; no primitive is withdrawn and no import is spent. A sent claim can be superseded by a corrected packet, and each of the three loops can be refined separately.
- **reversed by:** a ground reading, not agreement. A measured separation between breaking time and point of no return, a live slot with a quiet global mean, an enumerated cascade with a measured step count, or a reported round-trip gap would move the claim toward `grounded`. A figure line making ⋛ a duration, a derivation of smoothness from the other members, or R shown to be a global scalar would break the shape. Until one fires, the verdict stands as `candidate`.
