## 0. The question under audit

> Can a system cross a threshold gradually, with impacts appearing only much later?

Stated in effect terms: an observer sees a forcing change smoothly, sees no qualitative change at the moment a critical value is passed, and then sees a large change much later. The question asks whether this is one process or several. The word "threshold" carries a comparison; the word "gradually" carries an ordering; the phrase "appearing only much later" carries a delay in registration at the ground. Those are three different quantities. The first move of the audit is to refuse to average them.

---

## 1. Residual, then domain census

### 1.1 The residual R, described before any frame is chosen

- **Arity:** three conditions, not one. (a) A monotonic, continuous change in a control quantity. (b) An ordered comparison between that quantity and a critical value. (c) A delayed registration of an impact at a slot or scale different from the one being watched. Fig 9 :4-5 warns that a disagreement which does not shrink with effort may be *"not one hard question but several well-formed ones. Split it rather than search."* *The Library of Babel* names the same failure: *"Perhaps your problem was a bundle or conflation of several problems masquerading as one?"*
- **Symmetry:** the comparison is ordered, not symmetric. Fig 12 :87 defines ⋛ as *"a threshold relation between two quantities"*, and the identifying test at Fig 12 :89 is *"is it about which is larger, rather than both being present? Yes."* An ordering is also asymmetric. A cascade is asymmetric in time. None of the three is a symmetric join.
- **Scale:** condition (a) is first order inside one subsystem; condition (c) is second order, since the residual moves between slots or between scales. Fig 13 :31-32 says *"a remainder compounding through a chain that amplifies is chaos, and the detail a crossing discards reappears above it as noise."*
- **Union or partition:** union. The three conditions can hold at once in the same event. Conditions (a) and (b) overlap at the control quantity but are not the same term. Fig 9 :54: *"when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again."* So each condition gets its own loop and the overlap gets a compound loop.
- **Order by dependency (Fig 3):** describe R first (Fig 9 :27-28). Then settle the comparison, since a delay is not assessable until there is something delayed. Then settle the registration at the ground, since a cascade cannot be read below a break (Fig 3 :19, :27). Fig 3 :20: *"The chain is ordered by dependency, not by importance."*

## 3. Findings

### F1: A threshold is a comparison, not a duration (governs condition b)

Fig 12 :87 and :89:

> **"a threshold relation between two quantities"**
> **"is it about which is larger, rather than both being present? Yes."**

The Operators table :49 gives the same definition. Fig 12 :112 adds *"A goal is a comparison. Progress is a subtraction."* A comparison is a relation with a truth value at a point; it is not a process with a duration. So the phrase "crossing a threshold gradually" cannot name the comparison itself. What can be gradual is one of the two quantities being compared.

**Sub-claim, `general`:** the comparison fires when an ordering crosses a value; the ordering can change continuously. **Refuter:** show that ⋛ in the operators table is a duration rather than an ordered relation, or derive a figure line that makes a comparison itself continuous. **Status: `triangulated`** across Fig 12 (bond table) and the Operators table (operator definition), two entries with low overlap within the framework.

### F2: Gradualness is a derived quantity, not a primitive (governs condition a)

Fig 13 :43-44:

> **"A continuum was the seventh and was removed for failing exactly this: it is a spectrum without gaps, or smoothness over space and time."**

Fig 13 :114: *"More compositions never add a member, and no chain reaches outside what the six and their bonds already span."* Smoothness and continuity are what a composition leaves behind, not members of the six (see also Fig 13 :124-125 on smoothness, symmetry, chaos and discreteness as leftovers).

So "gradual" is legal language for the *behaviour of an ordering* (spectrum, smoothness), but it cannot be a seventh primitive and it cannot be the threshold. Fig 13 :88-89:

> **"The residual of the turn just closed is what the next turn composes against, so no two passes begin from the same place."**

The continuous approach and the comparison are different turns. The first leaves a remainder that the second composes against. **Mode `specified`.** **Refuter:** derive a continuum from the other six without importing it, or show the six cannot produce an ordering that changes smoothly. **Status: `triangulated`** across Fig 13's minimality argument and Fig 12's bond definition of comparison.

### F3: The impact is not the crossing (governs condition c)

Fig 1 :19 and :20:

> **"The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated."**
> **"a global R near zero with one live slot residual is a legal state"**

Fig 1 :28: *"The gap is measured per object, not per step. A system that averages across objects has thrown away the only signal that says where to look."*

A system can therefore look unchanged at the level an observer is watching while a single slot carries a live residual. "No impact yet" is not "no crossing". It is a statement about the slot being observed. **Mode `specified`.** **Refuter:** show that R is a global scalar in the framework, or that a live slot must always be accompanied by a global signal above zero. **Status: `triangulated`** across Fig 1's per-slot indexing and the Symbols table's definition of R as always a slice.

### F4: Delayed impacts are the contact cascade

Fig 11 :52-53:

> **"Scoping the habitat to direct contact is how DDT reached the eagles: first-order enumeration, second-order effect, and the residual that mattered was three steps out."**

Fig 11 :43-44:

> **"The habitat is enumerated, never composed."**
> **"List everything in contact with the residual, then what is in contact with those, and outward until the cascade stops mattering."**

This is the figure-grounded content of "impacts appearing only much later". The residual exists in a slot that is not the one being watched, propagates through actors enumerated outward from it, and registers at the ground several steps later. The delay is a property of the habitat, not of the comparison. **Mode `specified`.** **Refuter:** exhibit a case where the residual that matters is in direct contact and still appears much later with no intermediate actor, or show that contact does not cascade. **Status: `triangulated`** across Fig 11's cascade rule and Fig 1's per-slot residual.

### F5: A delay across scales carries a measurable round-trip gap

Fig 4 :27 and :30:

> **"The transform between scales loses information, and that is the point."**
> **"So take the round trip and measure the gap. Without it you are navigating by dead reckoning: confident about a position nobody checked."**

If the impact is read at a different scale from the forcing, the two readings are related by a lossy transform. The delay cannot be read off one scale alone. This is why the framework offers *"a shape, not a name"* and why the stop must be recorded. **Mode `specified`.** **Refuter:** show that the transform between the forcing scale and the impact scale is lossless, or that the round-trip gap is zero for a threshold crossing. **Status: `single-source`** for the structure (Fig 4), corroborated only weakly by the general metalaws note.

### F6: The question is a union compound, so it must be unbundled before search

Fig 9 :54 and :4-5:

> **"when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again."**
> **"Disagreements that do not shrink with effort, where each rule keeps working well on a different subset, are not one hard question but several well-formed ones. Split it rather than search."**

Fig 9 :60: *"Use filters for the budget and witnesses for the verdict. Never let a filter hand you a verdict."* A single search for "the mechanism of gradual threshold crossing" will find a threshold dynamics paper, an ice-sheet paper and an early-warning paper and mistake three witnesses for one. **Mode `general`.** **Refuter:** produce a single figure line that governs all three conditions at once. **Status: `triangulated`** across Fig 9's union rule and *The Library of Babel*'s bundle warning.

### F7: The anchor for this question is the ground, not agreement

Fig 2 :29 and :35:

> **"An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks."**
> **"Alignment is triangulation, not negotiation. A mirror cannot correct you."**

The anchor here is a measurement: a state variable crossing an attractor boundary in a model or experiment, a palaeorecord, an observed cascade. Agreement among threshold-dynamics researchers is not an anchor. Fig 11 :36: *"Where nothing maintains anything, intelligence is a label applied afterwards to a lineage that met its ground. Nothing was intelligent; selection ran."* **Mode `general`.** **Refuter:** name an anchor for this question whose answer changes when a frame pushes on it. **Status: `triangulated`** across Fig 2 (anchor test) and Fig 10 (verifier non-access).

### F9: The answer, stated once before the refuter

Yes in a qualified sense. A system can approach a threshold continuously, because gradualness belongs to the ordering and the threshold is an ordered comparison on that ordering (F1, F2). The crossing and the impact are separate terms, not one event: the residual is per slot, a global near-zero reading is a legal state, and the impact that matters can be three steps out in the habitat (F3, F4). If the impact is read at another scale, the delay carries a round-trip gap that must be measured rather than assumed (F5). No: it is not one continuous process, and the framework supplies neither a numeric threshold nor a lag for any particular system. To ask for one is to ask for a name where the framework returns a shape (Fig 9 :32: *"you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself"*). Verdict per domain: D1 `triangulated`, D2 `triangulated`, D3 `single-source`. Verdicts are kept separate, never averaged.

## 4. Pre-registered refuter

Required before any outreach. Seat Map :287: *"a falsifier written before the run is a design instrument, not only an honesty device."*

The shape claim in F9 is refuted if any of the following holds:

1. A figure line is produced in which ⋛ is a duration or a continuous process rather than an ordered comparison between two quantities.
2. A continuum or smoothness is derived from the other five or six substrate members without import, contradicting Fig 13 :43-44.
3. R is shown to be a global scalar with no slot index, so that a live local residual with a near-zero global reading is impossible.
4. A case is demonstrated where the residual that matters is in direct contact and registers much later with no intermediate actor and no scale transform.
5. A ground reading shows that in a named system the comparison fires and the impact registers at the same instant at every scale, with a measured round-trip gap of zero.
6. A single figure line is found that governs the approach, the comparison and the cascade as one term, collapsing the union.

If any fires, the shape is withdrawn or re-censused. Until one is run, no part of this may be labelled `grounded`.

### Seven-question recursive derivation (summary)

1. **What is the residual?** Three conditions bundled. Fig 9 :4-5 and :54; *Library of Babel* bundle warning. Split.
2. **Is a threshold a duration?** No. Fig 12 :87, :89. It is an ordered comparison.
3. **Can the compared quantity be continuous?** Yes. Fig 13 :43-44 makes smoothness a derived leftover; a spectrum is one of the six.
4. **Does the comparison fire at a value or unfold?** At a value, as a relation. The unfolding belongs to the ordering, not the relation.
5. **Where is the impact?** In a slot. Fig 1 :19, :20. A near-zero global reading with one live slot is legal.
6. **Why later?** Contact cascade. Fig 11 :52-53. The residual that matters is three steps out.
7. **What closes it?** A ground reading (breaking time versus point of no return, a palaeorecord, an instrument), not agreement. Fig 2 :29, :35; Fig 8 :39: *"So a search that finds nothing is never a proof of absence."*
