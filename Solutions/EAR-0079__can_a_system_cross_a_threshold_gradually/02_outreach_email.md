**Subject:** Check one shape claim about gradual threshold crossing before you decide whether the framing is worth reading

> Hi [name],
>
> I am writing about one specific output, not about a theory. I am not asking you to read the framework first and I am not asking you to agree with anything. If the output does not survive, there is nothing else to look at.
>
> **The output (`specified`, not `grounded`).** The question "can a system cross a threshold gradually, with impacts appearing only much later?" bundles three different things. Unbundled, the framework says:
>
> 1. A threshold is an ordered comparison between two quantities. It is a relation with a truth value, not a duration. The gradual part can only be one of the quantities being compared, an ordering that changes continuously.
> 2. The residual is indexed by slot, not aggregated. A near-zero global reading with one live slot is a legal state. So "no impact yet" is a statement about which slot is being watched, not about whether the crossing happened.
> 3. The impact that matters can be three steps out in the habitat. Contact cascades, so a large change can register much later and somewhere other than the crossing. If it is read at another scale, the bridge is lossy and the gap has to be measured.
>
> This is a shape, not a result. It does not name a threshold value, a lag, or a tipping element. It says what any answer would have to have.
>
> **What I am not claiming:** that this settles whether any particular Earth system does this. That needs a ground reading: a bifurcation experiment, a palaeorecord, an instrument. It also does not claim that "the threshold itself moves" or that a tipping cascade is guaranteed. Those are separate questions with their own loops.
>
> **What would refute the shape claim:**
> 1. A figure line in which the threshold comparison is itself a continuous duration rather than an ordered relation.
> 2. A derivation of smoothness from the other substrate members, when the framework's own text says the continuum was removed for failing that test.
> 3. A case where the residual that matters is in direct contact and registers much later with no intermediate actor and no scale transform, with a measured round-trip gap of zero.
> 4. A single figure line that governs the approach, the comparison and the cascade as one term, collapsing the union.
>
> I wrote these down before sending this. If any holds, the shape goes back to census. A one-line "this fails because..." is more useful to me than agreement. If you would rather not engage, no reply is needed.
>
