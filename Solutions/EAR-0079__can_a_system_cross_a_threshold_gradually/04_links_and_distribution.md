## Wider literature that bears on the question

Three findings matter and none is used as a witness for the figure verdict:

1. **The threshold literature already separates crossing from impact.** Lenton et al. 2008 widened the definition precisely so that a transition can be slower than the forcing and can have *"no abruptness"* while still having a qualitative impact in the future. Wang et al. 2023 reports the Kopp et al. convention, which reserves "tipping point" for cases with *"little temporal lag separating commitment and realization"* and uses "tipping element" for committed shifts that may be rapid or gradual. That is the world's own version of the F3 unbundling.
2. **Mathematics separates the parameter crossing from the state transition.** Li et al. 2019 distinguishes the breaking time from the point of no return and quantifies a salvage window between them. Ritchie et al. 2021 applies the same idea to overshooting climate thresholds. This corroborates F1 and F3 without supplying a figure line. It is the lowest-overlap witness in the set (mathematics and biology rather than climate science).
3. **Impacts arrive late through coupling.** Brovkin et al. 2021 and Wang et al. 2023 both describe cascades in which one component's threshold pushes others across theirs over centuries. That is the world-facing counterpart of Fig 11 :52-53, but it is a separate evidence pool and is recorded as corroboration only.

Overlap warning: Lenton is a co-author on Brovkin 2021; Ritchie and Wang cite Lenton; Ritchie and Brovkin share the climate-tipping literature. Agreement among them is partly a shared pool, not three independent confirmations (Fig 2 :40: *"agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool"*). The one genuinely low-overlap source is Li et al. 2019.

**(1)** Validate the shape against the ground. **(2)** If the reader is curious, offer the sources. **(3)** Offer the formula as a method to reuse, with the instruction that each user writes their own refuter and states their own mode.
