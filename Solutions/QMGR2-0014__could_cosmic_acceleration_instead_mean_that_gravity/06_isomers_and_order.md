## DERIVATION

### Q1 - Are the two options isomers over the full shared atom set?

- Fig 12 :117: *"isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."*
- Fig 12 :124: *"functional group. A substructure whose presence fixes the outcome. The group says what kind; the qualifier says which member."*
- Written over the full shared set `{Space, Time, Energy, Gradient, Spectrum, Network}`, an added energy term and a changed gravitational law are the **same atoms under different bonds**. Both must say how a state relates to a structure and how that structure relates to a state; they differ in which atom is treated as the source of the change. Under Fig 12 :117 they are isomers with a decisive behavioural difference, which is exactly what the growth-versus-geometry programme tests.
- Written as the field usually writes them (Lambda as a term in the stress-energy tensor, modified law as a change to the geometric side), they do not share an identical ingredient list at the level of the written equations. That weaker reading is a **functional group**: both are `state - structure - state` composites, with different qualifiers. This is the honest `specified` position; the stronger isomer claim is a hypothesis about the right atom set, not a measurement.
- **Falsifier, written before the run:** if either option can be written without an atom the other requires (for example a modified law with no energy state, or an added term that does not bond a structure between two states), the isomer claim fails and the functional-group reading stands.

### Q2 - What is the order of the union?

- Fig 3 :20: *"The chain is ordered by dependency, not by importance. Each link consumes what the one above produces, so effort spent above the break arrives at a step that cannot use it."*
- Fig 3 :26-28: *"The link between them, having terms for what a situation offers and being able to say what would count as progress, has neither, so it attracts the least attention and the fewest instruments."* and *"A link with no instrument is not a link that works. It is a link nobody has looked at."*
- Dependency order for this residual:
  1. **Perception:** measure `a''` and `H(z)` (already done, SN Ia, CMB, BAO).
  2. **Vocabulary:** name the two candidate causes in effect terms (added energy term versus changed law). This is where the either/or framing currently fails.
  3. **Objective:** state what would count as progress, namely a reading that differs between the two candidates given the same background.
  4. **Planning:** choose the discriminating observable (growth index, slip, tensor speed).
  5. **Learn and carry:** record which candidate survived, with provenance, so the next question starts from a settled term.
- The background expansion is upstream of growth: growth is interpreted against `H(z)`. But upstream is not sufficient. Nesseris (2013) shows that a perfect background fit is compatible with both candidates. Fig 3 :19: *"Optimising a link that already works cannot rescue one that does not."* More distance precision lands above the break.

### Q3 - The low-overlap analogue, and its limit as an analogue

- The Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy entry, in its Duhem section, records that *"the physicist can never subject an isolated hypothesis to experimental test, but only a whole group of hypotheses"* and that a failed prediction *"does not designate which one should be changed."* Its Neptune example shows that protecting a theory by revising an auxiliary assumption can succeed; its Vulcan example shows that the same move can fail for a century.
- This is a genuine analogue from philosophy of science, drawn on a different evidence pool from the cosmology papers. It corroborates the *shape* of the figure verdict (choose which held term to refuse by an independent route, not by the failure itself), but it does not decide which term is wrong in cosmology. Fig 9 :30-31: *"your words are borrowed too, so describe the effect rather than the cause."*
- **Analogue limit:** Duhem's underdetermination is about confirmational holism; this question is about a degeneracy between two specific models. The analogue licenses caution about the either/or framing, not a verdict for either candidate. Recorded as analogue, not as a governing figure.

### Q4 - Screening as a catalyst-like substructure

- Fig 12 :119: *"catalyst. A part that survives its own use. Spent and regenerating parts behave differently across uses, and nothing records which a term is."*
- Modified-gravity models typically include a screening mechanism that suppresses the modification in high-potential regions (the Solar System) while leaving it active on large scales. Ferreira (2019): *"Gravitational screening can mask modifications to general relativity on small scales but may, itself, lead to distinctive signatures in the large-scale structure of the Universe."* Structurally, screening is a substructure that survives its own use across environments: the same term is spent (suppressed) in one habitat and regenerating (active) in another.
- This is a `specified` analogy, not a derivation of any screening model from the figures. The figures do not name chameleon, symmetron or Vainshtein mechanisms.
- **Falsifier:** if a screening term is consumed by its own action and cannot reappear in a different environment, the catalyst reading fails; if screening leaves no distinctive large-scale signature at all, F5's instrument route loses its target.

### Q5 - Negation is not subtraction

- Fig 12 :104: *"A settled molecule becomes an atom for whatever composes over it. So irreducible is a fact about a level and never about the world"*
- Operators table: *"a refutation is a negation, which is why a vocabulary without it can only accumulate."*
- Dropping the cosmological constant is subtraction: an operand is absent. Saying that GR-on-all-scales is false is negation: a claim is refused. A vocabulary with subtraction but not negation can remove a term and still cannot record that a held regularity failed. This is why the modified-gravity hypothesis owes a refusal (F6) before it owes a replacement law.

## CONCLUSION

Over the full shared atom set the two candidates are a real isomer claim with a stated falsifier; as the field writes them they are a functional group. The union is ordered by dependency: perception and vocabulary already exist, the objective (a discriminating observable) is the weak link, and planning follows only after that objective is stated. More background precision lands above the break and cannot rescue the missing link. Screening reads as a catalyst-like substructure by analogy, with its own falsifier. Duhem's underdetermination is a low-overlap analogue, not a governing figure. Nothing here is grounded.
