# Outreach draft: check the test before you decide about the theory

**Use:** one-to-one, or as a cover letter attached to a submission through the public venues in `03_public_contact_points.csv`. **Not** for bulk send.

**Hard precondition:** do not send until the four conditions in `01_figure_grounded_audit.md` section 5 are stated as tests rather than as a conclusion. If the test cannot survive a check, the two-stage offer collapses on contact.

---

**Subject:** Would you fail this four-part test for when "emergent" explains something?

> Hi [name],
>
> I have one small output I would like you to check against what you already know. I am not asking you to read a theory first, and I am not asking you to agree with it. If the output does not survive, there is nothing else to look at.
>
> **The output (`specified`, not `grounded`).** A macro term earns the word "explains" only if it passes four checks:
>
> 1. **Bargain.** Stating the macro term, plus whatever remains unexplained after it, costs less than the confusion it removes. If the word applies to every pattern, it says nothing.
> 2. **Contact.** The term changes a prediction or points to an instrument that could be built. A name applied afterwards to a pattern that already met its ground does no work.
> 3. **Bond.** The term records the arrangement the micro ingredients omitted. Naming the ingredients and the pattern is a flat formula; the bond is the rest of the information.
> 4. **Composition.** Once the term is settled against the ground, something can compose over it at the next level. Irreducibility is a fact about a level, not a declaration about the world.
>
> If a macro description passes all four, "emergent" is an abbreviation for a result. If it fails any one, the word is a rename: of a gap, of an alias, or of a route nobody has written. This is a test, not a claim that any particular system has been shown to be emergent.
>
> **What I am not claiming:** that emergence is always empty, that any field has been refuted, that a simulation settles a philosophical question, or that a community's agreement that a pattern is emergent makes it explanatory. A room's agreement is variation, not a verdict.
>
> **What would refute the test**, written down before I sent this:
>
> 1. a macro term that passes all four and is still only an alias with no added reach;
> 2. a retrospective label with no added composition that nevertheless shrinks the residual against a ground;
> 3. a complete explanation that writes no bond and names no route, only a state and a measurement;
> 4. a derivation that preserves all micro detail across a level crossing with no lossy transform;
> 5. a case the field accepts as explanatory that fails one of the four conditions.
>
> If any holds, I withdraw or narrow the test. A one-line "this fails because..." is more useful to me than agreement. If you would rather not engage, no reply is needed.
>
> **If it survives, and only then:** the three sources are below. The figures, not my summary of them, are the authority.
> - The figures with line numbers (local extract, primary source): `FRAMEWORK/reference/03-FIGURES_TEXT.md`
> - The canonical usage rule: https://github.com/IsaiahN/tether/blob/general-agent/figures/README.md
> - The eight-step loop with its mode rule: https://github.com/IsaiahN/tether/tree/general-agent/figures
>
> **If you want to use the machinery:** the formula is in the figures link. Run it on a problem you know well and can check against a ground. Write down what would refute your result *before* you run it, and state whether your result is general, specified or grounded. A reported failure is more useful to me than a reported success.

## The two stages

1. **Stage 1.** Send the shape and the five refuters. Ask what the reader would count as a failure. Record that verdict before any framework material is shown.
2. **Stage 2, opt-in only.** If they are curious, offer the three links. Their later agreement is never pooled as corroboration, because a reader who has absorbed the framework can no longer serve as an outside frame (Fig 10 :42).
