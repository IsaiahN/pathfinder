## 0. The question as a residual in effect terms

The word "emergent" is offered as a term φ at the contact between a micro description of a system and a macro regularity the micro description does not derive. The question asks whether φ does work. Stated as an effect:

> R = a macro regularity P is observed at stratum L, and the model at L-1, with its atoms, bonds and rules, does not derive P. Something is missing or wrong. The practice inserts the word "emergent" at that contact. What follows from inserting it?

Two options, with their consequences:

- **Option A, label.** Add a word to Γ. left(R, φ) is unchanged. No atom is added, no bond is written, nothing new composes over φ. The gap is now named, so the instrument that would close it is never built. Consequence: contact with the ground does not change. Fig 11 :40: "An improvement that does not change contact changes nothing, however much it improves."
- **Option B, derived macro term.** Treat the macro regularity as a candidate molecule. It must pay the bargain (Fig 5 :43), be settled against the ground (Fig 5 :69), record the arrangement the flat micro formula omitted (Fig 12 :5), and become an atom at the next level (Fig 12 :104). Consequence: the reach grows, and the prediction at L changes.

What an answer would look like, bound as an ordinary term before the run: a **decision test** with four conditions, each falsifiable, that separates A from B without asking whether anyone likes the word. This is the formula's "Direction" step applied to the question itself. The test, not a verdict about any particular system, is what this folder delivers.

## 1. Domain census, written before any figure line was chosen

### R by arity, symmetry and scale

- **Arity:** two conditions. (1) A micro model with a residual that does not trend down. (2) A macro regularity that is stable enough to be named. "Is this emergent?" is therefore a **union compound**, not one hard question. Fig 9 :54: "And when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again." A single lever that clears one condition leaves the intersection standing (Fig 9 :55-57).
- **Symmetry:** the macro term and the micro terms are not intersubstitutable. A symmetric bond would make them one term (Fig 12 :92: "Under a symmetric bond, k operands have k! spellings and one term"). "Emergent" is often used as if identity held, which is the `≡` test: "substitute one for the other. Is anything lost? No." (Fig 12 :67). If nothing is lost, the macro word is an alias, not an emergence claim. If something is lost, there is a bond to be written.
- **Scale:** first order inside a stratum, second order at the crossing. A settled macro molecule becomes an atom for the next level (Fig 12 :104). The membrane between sizes is a filter with a direction, not a wall (Fig 4 :21).

### One line per domain: actors / currency / anchor

| domain | actors | currency | anchor | type |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| D1, micro composition | atoms, bonds, Γ, the microdynamic | derivations inside closure(Γ); bits and actions (Fig 12 :125-127) | the figures as metalaws; the ground for settlement | partition question: what composes across the two kinds |
| D2, instrument and observation | instruments, measurements, observations o' | residual R, live mass R⁺_s | the ground, which does not move when pushed (Fig 2 :29-30) | depth versus genuine absence (Fig 8 :40-43) |
| D3, the naming practice | researchers, terms, peer frames | agreement and residuals | the ground, never agreement (Fig 2 :35, :40) | where the rename lives |
| D4, the level crossing | methods up, head starts down | compression and round-trip gap R_T (Fig 4 :30) | the round trip itself | methodological, not a fact about the world |

No domain lacks a nameable anchor, so all four are domains. D4 is not a fifth fact about the system; it is the rule for carrying a settled result between D1 and D3. It has an anchor (the measured round trip), so it earns its own loop.

**Partition or union:** union. D1, D2 and D3 overlap at the residual. A label introduced in D3 can mask a missing instrument in D2 and a missing bond in D1. The four verdicts below are kept separate and never averaged.

### Dependency order (Fig 3)

1. perception: is the macro regularity stable and distinguishable from what was there before?
2. vocabulary: do we have terms for what it affords, or only the word "emergent"?
3. objective: what would count as progress, and what would settle it?
4. planning: which derivation or instrument closes the residual?
5. learn and carry: settle the term, then carry the method up.

The middle link is where this question usually breaks. Fig 3 :25-27: a link with no instrument "has neither" obvious failures nor tests, and "A link with no instrument is not a link that works. It is a link nobody has looked at." The word "emergent" is most often applied at link 2 or 3, where it converts an uninstrumented link into a named one. Fig 3 :19: "Optimising a link that already works cannot rescue one that does not."

## 3. Findings

### F1 — The acceptance bargain is the line between explaining and renaming (D3)

**Mode: `specified`. Status: `triangulated`** (Fig 5 and Fig 12, two figures with different mechanisms: description length versus arrangement information).

Fig 5 :43: "A term that explains everything by saying nothing fails this, and so does a term that explains one occasion perfectly." Fig 5 :26: "does the new term cost less to state than the confusion it removes?" Fig 5 :42: "The acceptance test is a bargain, not a threshold."

A word that is applied to every macro regularity says nothing and fails. A word that names one occasion perfectly (this flock, this market, this ant colony) also fails, because it does not compress anything beyond the occasion. The operational question is never "is it emergent?" but "what is left(R, φ) after the term is stated, and is that smaller than before?"

**Refuter:** exhibit a macro term whose statement leaves the residual strictly smaller, measured against a ground both sides can range against, while adding no bond and no composability. If such a term exists, the bargain alone is a sufficient test and the other conditions below are too strict.

### F2 — A label applied afterwards is the named failure mode (D1 and D3)

**Mode: `specified`. Status: `triangulated`** (Fig 11 capability claim and Fig 5 bargain, low overlap).

Fig 11 :36: "Where nothing maintains anything, intelligence is a label applied afterwards to a lineage that met its ground. Nothing was intelligent; selection ran. The word does no work there." Fig 11 :40: "An improvement that does not change contact changes nothing, however much it improves."

This is the closest analogue in the figures to the question. Substitute "emergent" for "intelligent": where no derivation or instrument is doing work, the word is applied afterwards to a regularity that met its ground. The word does no work there. The test this yields is a **contact test**: if asserting the macro term changes no prediction and builds no instrument, it is in the F2 failure class.

**Refuter:** show a case where a purely retrospective label, with no added composition, nevertheless changes a prediction at the ground. That would refute the contact test.

### F3 — An alias is not an emergence claim (D1 and D3)

**Mode: `specified`. Status: `triangulated`** (Fig 8 union surplus and Fig 6 closure, different mechanisms).

Fig 8 :13-15: "same atoms, different names" / "their union adds nothing" / "two names for one frame". Fig 6 :21: "MINT composes inside the closure and can never add an atom." Operators :27: "substitute one for the other. Is anything lost? No."

If "the system is emergent" is just another spelling of "the micro rules produce this pattern, though we have not written the derivation," the two names share one frame. Their union has no surplus. Fig 12 :5: "The bond is the rest of the information, and it is the half nothing was recording." Naming the pattern without recording the bond is a flat formula, which Fig 12 :14-16 says records the ingredients and not the arrangement.

**Refuter:** produce two descriptions with identical atoms and bonds whose union nevertheless moves the reach. That would break the alias test.

### F4 — "Emergent" names a state; explanation needs a route (D3)

**Mode: `specified`. Status: `triangulated`** (Fig 12 notation claim and Fig 3 chain claim).

Fig 12 :111: "A vocabulary without bonds can name a state and cannot name a route to one." Fig 12 :112: "A plan is a sequence. A goal is a comparison. Progress is a subtraction." Fig 3 :27: "A link with no instrument is not a link that works. It is a link nobody has looked at."

The macro observation is a state. The word "emergent" is a state-name. What explains is the route: which bond, between which atoms, under what conditions, with what instrument to check it. Fig 9 :32: "you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself." A shape is the narrowed form of a route, not a substitute for one.

**Refuter:** exhibit a complete explanation that contains no route, only a state description and a ground measurement. If that counts as explanation in the field, Fig 12 :111 is not governing this domain and the finding must be re-censused.

### F5 — Level-relative irreducibility is the legitimate positive case (D4)

**Mode: `general`. Status: `triangulated`** (Fig 12 recursion and Fig 13 remainder).

Fig 12 :104: "A settled molecule becomes an atom for whatever composes over it. So irreducible is a fact about a level and never about the world". Fig 13 :124: "Smoothness, symmetry, chaos and discreteness are each what a particular composition leaves behind, and none is a seventh member." Fig 13 :31: "each turn leaves a remainder (the residual) that re-enters the next".

A macro property that is settled and can be composed over is a genuine new atom at the next level. Its irreducibility is a fact about that level, not a metaphysical declaration about the world, and there is no bottom (Fig 12 :104-105). This is the case where "emergent" does work, provided the four conditions of F1, F2, F3 and F4 are met. The property is a remainder of a composition, not a name for ignorance.

**Refuter:** derive one of the six substrate terms from the other five, or show a composition that cannot become an atom at any level. Either breaks the level-relativity anchor and with it the positive half of the verdict.

### F6 — "Underivable" and "unreachable" must not be conflated (D2)

**Mode: `specified`. Status: `triangulated`** (Fig 8 scope and Fig 13 honest stop).

Fig 8 :40-41: "Underivable-in-principle and unreachable-at-this-budget are different facts, and they issue different instructions." Fig 9 :44: "Only a sealed room can be searched to the end, and no frame is a sealed room." Fig 13 :134: "Unreachable is a verdict here, not a silence". Fig 8 :39: "So a search that finds nothing is never a proof of absence."

The rename typically happens at exactly this point. A macro regularity is not derived, so the speaker says "emergent," and the audience hears "underivable in principle." The figures require the two to be separated. If the term is in the closure and was not found, the remedy is more independent search (Fig 6 :49-50). If the closure lacks the atom, the remedy is an import with a source gate and a shadow test (Fig 6 :11-14; Fig 9 :43). If no instrument is returning anything, the honest report is `unreachable` at this budget, with a shape of what would close it.

**Refuter:** show that the two facts issue the same instruction, or that a frame can prove its own absence. That would collapse this distinction and the F6 finding with it.

### F7 — Agreement is not a verdict, and prestige is not a ground (D3)

**Mode: `general`. Status: `triangulated`** (Fig 2, Fig 7, Fig 9).

Fig 7 :35: "A room's agreement is variation, not a verdict." Fig 2 :35: "Alignment is triangulation, not negotiation. A mirror cannot correct you." Fig 9 :60: "Use filters for the budget and witnesses for the verdict. Never let a filter hand you a verdict." Fig 2 :42: "Buy independence in proportion to the cost of being wrong."

A community can agree that a pattern is emergent for a century, and that agreement is evidence about the community. Whether the word explains is settled by a ground none of them can move. The four-condition test in section 5 is offered to a reader as a thing to fail, not as a thing to endorse.

**Refuter:** produce a case where broad agreement among frames sharing one evidence pool raises the truth value of a naming claim. By Fig 2 :40 that would require the pool to be an anchor, which contradicts Fig 2 :29-30.

### F8 — The break is at the vocabulary/objective link, not at the data (D3)

**Mode: `specified`. Status: `triangulated`** (Fig 3 and Fig 6, different mechanisms).

Fig 3 :24-27: perception and planning have obvious failures and obvious tests; the link between them, "having terms for what a situation offers and being able to say what would count as progress, has neither". Fig 6 :34-35: "Where nothing is, there is no edge to extend from, and the search for a proto-instrument is a search for one that is already there."

This is why the rename is sticky. Once a macro pattern has a name, the vocabulary link appears to work, and the missing objective link (what would count as progress toward a derivation) is never inspected. Fig 3 :29: "Before asking whether a system is good, ask which link it currently stops at, and whether the answer was measured or assumed."

**Refuter:** show a case where adding a name to the vocabulary created a measurable objective and an instrument where none existed. That would be a genuine mint, and F8 would have to be narrowed to cases where it does not happen.

### F9 — What survives: the four-condition test

**Mode: `specified`.** The defensible residue, with its source and its own mode.

| condition | test | figure | mode |
|---|---|---|---|
| C1 bargain | stating the macro term plus what remains unexplained costs less than the unexplained residual | Fig 5 :43, :26 | `specified` |
| C2 contact | the term changes a prediction or builds an instrument; a retrospective label with no work fails | Fig 11 :36, :40 | `specified` |
| C3 bond | the arrangement the micro ingredients omitted is written, not just the ingredients named | Fig 12 :5, :111 | `specified` |
| C4 composition | the settled macro term becomes an atom for the next level, with a measured round trip | Fig 12 :104; Fig 4 :30 | `general` structural, `specified` per case |

A macro description passes all four, and "emergent" is an abbreviation for a hard-won result. It fails any one and the word is a rename, of the F2 kind if it fails C2, of the F3 kind if it fails C3. Failing C1 means the term is not worth keeping at all. Failing C4 leaves a settled but unusable term, which Fig 5 :72 allows: "a library that grows is not a reach that grows."

Verdicts per domain, kept separate:

- D1: a bond is owed whenever the micro formula records ingredients only. Verdict `triangulated`.
- D2: the residual is reported as underivable, unreachable-at-budget, or simply unfound, never as "emergent." Verdict `triangulated`.
- D3: the word has no verdict weight; the test has. Verdict `triangulated`.
- D4: the macro term earns composability only after settlement and a round trip. Verdict `triangulated`.

## 4. Pre-registered refuter

Written before the recommendation and the conclusion, as required. A falsifier written before the run is a design instrument.

The four-condition test is refuted if any of the following holds:

1. A macro term that passes C1 to C4 is nevertheless demonstrated to be a mere alias, with no added reach at any level.
2. A purely retrospective label with no added composition is shown to shrink the residual against a ground. That would make C2 false.
3. A complete explanation is exhibited that writes no bond and names no route, only a state and a measurement. That would make C3 false in this domain.
4. A derivation preserves all micro detail across a level crossing with no lossy transform, contradicting Fig 4 :27 and Fig 12 :104. That would make C4 false.
5. One of the six substrate terms is derived from the other five, or a seventh is found, breaking the level-relativity anchor.
6. A world case accepted by the field as a genuine explanation fails one of the four conditions. The candidate pool for this check is the Batterman universality case, the Anderson level case, and the Baker/Carroll simulation cases listed in `09_watson_answer.md`.
7. A frame is shown to prove its own absence from a closure. That would collapse F6.

If any fires, the test is withdrawn or narrowed. Until one is run, no system is classified by this folder, and nothing is `grounded`.

## 5. Recommendation

1. Replace "is it emergent?" with "which of C1 to C4 does this macro term pass, and what is the residual after it is stated?" (F1).
2. Ask for the instrument that would change the macro prediction. If none can be named, report `unreachable` and give the shape of what would close it, not the word (F2, F6).
3. Run the alias test: substitute the macro description for the micro description. If nothing is lost, the two are one frame and there is no surplus (F3).
4. Ask for the bond, not the ingredient list. A pattern name with no arrangement recorded is a flat formula (F4).
5. Keep the verdicts per domain and never average them into a single "degree of emergence" (Fig 1 :22: "Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average").
6. Send the test and its refuter, not the framework. Fig 4 :25: "A recording carried upward looks like knowledge and is a description of one occasion."

### Seven-question recursive derivation, summary

1. **Is the macro regularity a term or a residual?** Both, depending on the slot. It is a residual until settled, then a term. Fig 5 :69.
2. **Does the word pay?** Test C1. Fig 5 :43.
3. **Does it change contact?** Test C2. Fig 11 :36, :40.
4. **Is it an alias?** Substitute; run `≡`. Test C3, part 1. Operators :27, Fig 8 :13-15.
5. **Is the bond written?** Test C3, part 2. Fig 12 :5, :111.
6. **Does it compose at the next level?** Test C4. Fig 12 :104, Fig 4 :30.
7. **What is owed if a test fails?** SEARCHED first, then INWARD (a better instrument), then OUTWARD (an import with source gate and shadow test). Fig 6 :48-54, formula step 7. Do not mint a word to fill a missing atom.

## 7. Updated verdict

The shape survives: "emergent" explains exactly where the four conditions hold, and it renames where they do not. The structural claim is `general`; the four conditions are `specified` and falsifiable; nothing is `grounded`. All nine findings are triangulated across figures with low overlap except where marked; after re-census there is one live strain, which is recorded rather than smoothed.

**Live strain S1.** Fig 13's six-term substrate is a physical claim about states and structures. Applying the shape of F5 to a biological, social or computational system is a binding by analogy, not a derivation of those systems from the six terms. The figures themselves say the kernel does not change with size and only actors, currency and anchor change (Fig 4 :19-20), so the shape travels. But whether a given macro term in a given domain is genuinely a settled molecule is a question for that domain's ground, not for the substrate. Recorded as a strain at the domain boundary, not hidden and not used to flip a verdict.

A search that finds no four-condition case in a domain is `unreachable`, not a proof that no such case exists (Fig 8 :39, Fig 13 :134). Unreachable is a verdict, not a silence.
