# Expert verification packet - the Tether on children and conflicting norms

**What this is:** a claim in a form a developmental scientist can check. Mode, derivation and refuters are stated *before* the conclusion, so a reader knows what to look for and what would settle it.

**Mode: `specified` on the shape, `general` on the structural mapping, not `grounded`.** No measurement of a child is claimed. This is a shape and a route to a test, not a result. It is a simulated derivation checked for internal consistency against the figures, not a ground reading.

## 1. The claim, in one paragraph

When a child faces a conflict between a family norm, a peer norm and an institutional rule, what is learned is not a single rule and not an average of the three. The claim is that the child learns a four-part selector: it distinguishes which frame is speaking; it carries a weighting between inherited family prior and peer-derived private history that is itself updated from outcomes; for the specific conflict it selects the frame that best matches what actually happens; and it can refuse a rule in a context where the rule does not apply. Family and institutional rules are often one evidence pool, so the peer frame is usually the genuinely different one. Because the question is constitutively about social meaning, agreement is legitimately the product in the convention domain, but agreement among selected checkers is not evidence for the claim. The empirical mechanism, timing and cultural variation are an admitted `gap`.

## 2. Three checkable sub-claims

### C1 - framework-internal; checkable by reading Figures 1, 2 and 9

- **Statement:** the conflict is a union compound whose residual is indexed per slot; averaging across slots and mutual updating among frames are both named failures, not approximations.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 1 :19 *"The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated."* Fig 1 :22 *"Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average."* Fig 2 :15 *"is whoever had the most influence"* (the outcome of mutual update). Fig 9 :55 *"A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones."*
- **Refuter:** show that the three systems govern disjoint slots, that the residual can be averaged without losing the signal that selects a frame, or that mutual updating does not converge on influence.
- **World check:** the Stanford Encyclopedia entry reports that children distinguish moral from conventional transgressions and that their normative cognition is not undifferentiated. Compatible, not decisive.

### C2 - developmental-facing; checkable against norm-conflict data

- **Statement:** family and institutional norms often share a source, so their agreement is one pool; the peer frame carries the surplus; the developmental variable is a learnable weighting, not a wholesale frame swap.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 2 :20 *"so the agreement is the pool talking,"* Fig 2 :21 *"not two frames confirming each other."* Fig 8 :45 *"Two frames together reach what neither reaches alone."* Symbols table :24 to :26: *"w is learnable from the outcomes of past weightings. Two frames with identical architecture diverge because their histories and their w diverge."*
- **What to check:** in the domain studied, do family and institutional norms actually share an adult source, and are there profiles whose norms are incongruent across settings? Does conformity shift with context and age in a way consistent with a moving weighting?
- **Refuter:** demonstrate that family and institutional norms are independent frames in that domain, or that the weighting is fixed and not updated by outcomes.
- **World check:** Wang, Chen and Lee (2019) found congruent norms in only 16 percent of 11,086 adolescents and a majority with incongruent norms; Telzer et al. (2018) report that *"the relative weight of such definitions will determine whether an adolescent endorses the social norm."* Note Telzer is a co-author on the 2018 and 2020 papers, so those two are partly one pool.

### C3 - the operator claim; checkable by reading Figure 12 and the Operators table

- **Statement:** successful navigation requires refusing a claim's standing to apply, which is the unary operator ¬, not accumulation and not subtraction.
- **Source, verbatim:** Operators :57 *"a refutation is a negation, which is why a vocabulary without it can only accumulate"* and :73 *"A vocabulary with − and without ¬ can say what is missing from an arrangement and cannot say that an arrangement is wrong, so it can only ever accumulate."* Fig 12 :111 *"A vocabulary without bonds can name a state and cannot name a route to one."*
- **Refuter:** show that context-tagged accumulation fully reproduces refusal behaviour with no operator that negates a claim.
- **World check:** Do, McCormick and Telzer (2020) found adolescents *"sticking with their pre-existing attitudes 65 % of the time"* and resisting selectively; the SEP entry reports that children judge moral transgressions to be authority independent, which is a refusal of the frame's authority to change the rule.

## 3. What is explicitly not claimed

- Not the developmental mechanism, its timing, or its cross-cultural variation. Those are `gap` (Fig 9 :32 *"you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself."*).
- Not that the three systems are independent witnesses (Fig 2 :18 to :21).
- Not that averaging them is a good approximation (Fig 1 :22).
- Not that a child is an isolated frame: Fig 11 :39 *"capability is a property of agent-and-habitat, never of the agent alone."*
- Not that agreement among selected people corroborates the claim (Fig 2 :40). Agreement in the *convention* domain is the product (Fig 2 :41); that is a different clause about a different quantity.
- Not `grounded`. Nothing here is asserted as settled by experiment.

## 5. What would settle it

A study that fires the three refuters. Concretely:

1. Per-slot measurement, not a global conformity score: does behaviour track the kind of conflict and the outcome, or a single average?
2. A domain where family and institutional norms are tested for shared source, rather than assumed to be two witnesses.
3. A task that separates resistance from conformity by context, and a test of whether the weighting moves with feedback.
4. A measure of the child's terms for what each frame affords, since Fig 3 :26 names that link as the least instrumented: *"A link with no instrument is not a link that works. It is a link nobody has looked at."*

## 6. What an expert can check in twenty minutes

1. Read Fig 1 :19 and :22, Fig 2 :20 to :21 and :40 to :41, and Operators :57 and :73. Six short passages.
2. Say whether C1's union reading matches what a multi-setting norm study would actually see.
3. Name one instrument that would distinguish a moving weighting from fixed deference, or say why none is available.
4. Send the refutation. A failure is more useful than an endorsement.
