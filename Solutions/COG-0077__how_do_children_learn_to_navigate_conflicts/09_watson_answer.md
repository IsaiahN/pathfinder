## QUESTION

Decide, in effect terms, what the Tether says about a child facing conflicting prescriptions from family, peers and an institution. Option A: the learned thing is one rule, or an average of the three. Option B: the three are frames with different closures, and what is learned is a selector with four parts (distinguish the frame, carry a learnable weighting, select against the outcome, refuse where inapplicable). What each leads to: A sends a global conformity score that erases the per-slot signal and treats a shared adult source as two witnesses; B sends a checkable shape, three written refuters, and an honest `gap` for the empirical mechanism, timing and cultural variation. R by arity (three frames plus the child), symmetry (asymmetric in currency and anchor authority), scale (first order inside a frame, second order at contact, third across development).

## DOMAIN CENSUS

- **R (the residual).** Per-slot gap between a frame's prescription, the child's action and the outcome. Arity: a union compound of overlapping conditions (Fig 9 :55 *"A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones."*). Scale: first, second and developmental. Indexed per slot (Fig 1 :19).
- **Domain 1, the family frame.** Actors: caregivers, child. Currency: care, attachment, approval. Anchor: the caregiver's actual regulatory response and the child's welfare outcome, which is nameable and does not move with the frame's opinion (Fig 2 :36 *"An anchor is legitimate when the question is constitutively about it."*). Question type: a frame to be ranged against.
- **Domain 2, the peer frame.** Actors: peers, child. Currency: reciprocity, belonging, status. Anchor: actual peer acceptance or rejection in the situation. Question type: the low-overlap frame where the surplus lives.
- **Domain 3, the institutional frame.** Actors: teachers, rules, authorities. Currency: compliance, sanction. Anchor: rule, exam or counter, the most instrument-like of the three. Question type: a frame that often shares a source with D1.
- **Domain 4, the child's navigation mechanism.** Actors: belief b, weighting w, habitat. Currency: residual reduction. Anchor: the outcome that pays, which does not decay while the channel does (Fig 10 :24 *"the ground does not decay, the channel does"*). Question type: capability is agent-and-habitat.
- **Partition or union:** D1 to D3 are a union; D4 is the loop over the union. **Order by dependency** (Fig 3 :20 *"The chain is ordered by dependency, not by importance."*): perceive the frame, have terms for what it affords, state what counts as resolution, plan the selection, carry the method.

Strain test applied: Fig 2 :40 and :41 appear to contradict on agreement. Re-census resolves it: :40 governs verdicts about the world, :41 governs conventions about shared meaning, and this question is constitutively about the latter while still requiring an outcome the frames cannot move. No strain remains after re-census.

## VERDICT (from the figures)

The shape survives an internal re-census; the empirical "how" does not come from the figures. The three normative systems are frames in a union, not one rule set and not independent witnesses; family and institutional rules often share an adult source, while the peer frame carries the surplus. What is learned is a four-part selector: keep frames distinct, carry a weighting between inherited prior and private history that updates from outcomes, select a frame against the outcome for that slot, and refuse a rule where it does not apply. Capability is agent-and-habitat, and what crosses generations is a method, not a recording. Because the question is constitutively about people, agreement is the product in the convention domain, but that clause is kept separate from the rule that shared evidence disqualifies a verdict. The mechanism, its timing and its cultural variation are a `gap` (Fig 9 :32). Mode: `specified`, ungrounded, candidate.

- **cost if wrong:** bounded and locatable. The shape is mode-labelled, the three refuters precede the conclusion, and the gap is stated rather than filled, so an error is findable rather than invisible. The real cost is presenting a metatheory shape as though it settled a developmental question, which would spend the independence of outside readers. Fig 2 :42 *"Buy independence in proportion to the cost of being wrong."* That cost is avoidable by leading with the shape and its refuters and hosting sources behind an opt-in step.
- **undoable:** yes. The bindings to developmental content are removable without touching the derivation; `07_the_tether_answer.md` and `08_expert_verification_packet.md` already remove any claim to mechanism, timing or cultural variation. No primitive has to be withdrawn, and a sent claim can be superseded by a corrected packet.
- **reversed by:** a ground reading, not agreement. A per-slot study that fires any of the three refuters (averaging or single-frame dominance; family and institution independent in the domain; accumulation fully explaining refusal) would break the shape. A study of the child's affordance vocabulary would either locate the break predicted by Fig 3 :26 or show it is elsewhere. Agreement among selected readers, or a prestigious endorsement, is not such an instrument (*The Recursive Self-Improvement Trap*: *"Prestige can fund an action. It can give an action a budget to be tried. But it cannot settle an action. Only the ground can settle it."*). Until a ground fires, the verdict stands as `candidate`.
