**Subject:** Check one claim about children and conflicting norms before you decide whether the framework is worth reading

> Hi [name],
>
> I have one specific claim I would like you to check against what you already know. I am not asking you to read the framework first, and I am not asking you to agree with it. If the claim does not survive, there is nothing else to look at.
>
> **The claim (`specified`, not `grounded`):** when a child faces a conflict between a family norm, a peer norm, and an institutional rule, the thing that is learned is not a single rule and not an average of the three. The claim is that the child learns a selector with four parts: it tells which frame is speaking; it weights what was inherited from family against what was learned from peers (a weighting that is itself learnable from outcomes); it picks the frame that best matches what actually happens in that kind of situation; and it can refuse a rule in a context where the rule does not apply. The residual is tracked per kind of conflict, not averaged across conflicts.
>
> Two qualifications I want on the record before you check anything. First, family and institutional rules are often authored by the same adults, so their agreement is one evidence pool, not two independent witnesses; the genuinely different frame is usually the peer group. Second, this question is constitutively about people and social meaning, so agreement is legitimately the *product* in the convention domain. That is not the same as saying that agreement among people I select to check the claim would make it true. I am asking for a refutation, not a coalition.
>
> **What I am not claiming:** that I have measured a child, that this is the developmental mechanism, that it gives the age at which each capacity appears, or that it settles cultural variation. The framework returns a shape, not a name. The empirical "how", its timing, and its variation are a `gap` in what the figures can tell us. World research speaks to them, but that is secondary and I have labelled it as such.
>
> **What would refute the shape claim (written before this message):**
> 1. a controlled study showing children resolving all such conflicts by simple averaging or by one dominant frame, with no sensitivity to the kind of situation or the outcome;
> 2. evidence that family and institutional norms are fully independent frames in the relevant domain, with no shared adult source;
> 3. evidence that successful navigation is fully explained by accumulating rules, with nothing equivalent to refusing a claim's standing to apply.
>
> If any one holds, I will narrow or withdraw the claim. A one-line "this fails because..." is more useful to me than agreement. If you would rather not engage, no reply is needed.
>
> **If it survives, and only then:** sources are below. The figures are the authority, not my summary of them.
> - The figures and formula (local canonical extract, for download or inspection): `FRAMEWORK/reference/03-FIGURES_TEXT.md` and the public repository `https://github.com/IsaiahN/tether/tree/general-agent/figures`
> - Worked architecture essay: https://medium.com/@IsaiahNwukor/arrival-of-the-fittest-the-tether-architecture-1de7edf641ec
> - The developmental literature that bears on this claim is listed in `04_links_and_distribution.md`, which I will send only after you ask.
>
> **If you want to reuse the machinery:** the formula is in the figures link. Run it on a problem you know well and can check against an outcome. Write down what would refute your result *before* you run it, and state the mode of what you get. The framework's own warning is that a frame cannot check the direction of its own question, and that outputs can be contrived to fit the formula. A reported failure is more useful to me than a reported success.
>
> - [name]
