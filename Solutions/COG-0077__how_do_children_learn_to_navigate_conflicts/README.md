## What is being decided, in effect terms

A child in a concrete situation receives conflicting prescriptions from three normative frames: family, peers, and institution. The question asks what is learned that lets the child act in that conflict. Option A treats the answer as a single rule that accumulates (obey the loudest frame, or average them). Option B treats the three systems as frames with different closures and asks what the child learns in order to select and refuse among them. Each leads to a different object of study and a different instrument. The residual is indexed per conflict situation (slot), not averaged across situations, per Fig 1 :19 and :22.

An answer would look like: a mechanism that (i) tells which frame is speaking, (ii) weights inherited prior against private history, (iii) selects a frame against the actual outcome, and (iv) can refuse a rule in a context where it does not apply. That shape is bound as an ordinary term in `01` and `06`, so it can accrue a residual and be refusable.

## Boundary and honesty

This folder changes nothing outside itself. Figure quotes come only from the local `FRAMEWORK/reference/03-FIGURES_TEXT.md`, cited as `Fig N :line`. World sources are real pages opened on 2026-11-22 and recorded in `10_source_ledger.csv`. The world sources are secondary: they corroborate the shape but never overrule the figures. Because this is a simulated derivation, it cannot settle the developmental question it reports on.

A caution specific to this question: the framework returns a shape, not the developmental mechanism. The empirical "how", its timing, and its cultural variation are a `gap` at the figure level. Where world sources speak to them, that content is secondary and is labelled as such. See `09_watson_answer.md` VERDICT and `11_audit.md`.

## What is deliberately not here

- No personal emails of named individuals.
- No ranking of people by how likely they are to agree.
- No bulk-send plan.

Three reasons in the figures' own words:

1. **Agreement is not an anchor here.** Fig 2 :35: *"Alignment is triangulation, not negotiation. A mirror cannot correct you."*
2. **Selecting for receptiveness manufactures the disqualifying correlation.** Fig 2 :39: *"Correlation has valence."* Fig 2 :40: *"disqualifying for a verdict: agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool."*
3. **Sending the theory before the residual spends the reader's independence.** Fig 10 :52: *"Bring a characterised residual, never the source."* Fig 10 :42: *"A verifier that can reconstruct the claim cannot verify it."*

A qualification that matters for this question: because the question is constitutively about people and social meaning, agreement is legitimately *the product* in the convention domain (Fig 2 :36 and :41). That does not license selecting checkers for agreement. It means the social meaning itself is what is being studied, and it must still be ranged against an outcome the frames cannot move (Fig 2 :45).

Legally: unsolicited bulk email to scraped addresses breaches GDPR and CAN-SPAM style rules in many jurisdictions. Public institutional and opt-in channels avoid that failure as well as the structural one.
