## Independent domain census

I used the WATSON order: R by arity, symmetry and scale; one line per domain with actors / currency / anchor; partition or union; order by dependency.

- **R (arity / symmetry / scale).** The residual is the per-slot gap between a normative frame's prescription, the child's action and the outcome. Arity: three frames plus the child, with overlapping conditions, so a **union compound** (Fig 9 :55 *"A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones."*). Symmetry: the frames are asymmetric in currency and anchor authority, so no symmetric combination is licensed. Scale: first order inside a frame, second order at the contact, third across development.
- **Domain 1, family frame.** Actors: caregivers, child. Currency: care and approval. Anchor: actual regulatory response and welfare outcome, nameable. Not the ground; a frame to be ranged against.
- **Domain 2, peer frame.** Actors: peers, child. Currency: reciprocity and belonging. Anchor: actual peer acceptance or rejection. The low-overlap frame.
- **Domain 3, institutional frame.** Actors: teachers, rules, authorities. Currency: compliance and sanction. Anchor: rule, exam or counter. Often shares a source with D1.
- **Domain 4, navigation mechanism.** Actors: belief b, weighting w, habitat. Currency: residual reduction. Anchor: the outcome that pays. Capability is agent-and-habitat.
- **Union, not partition:** D1 to D3 overlap; D4 runs over the union. **Dependency order:** perceive the frame, affordance vocabulary, objective, plan the selection, carry the method.

My census matches the primary's four domains and its union reading. Where the primary derived D2 from Fig 8 and Fig 7, I derived it from Fig 2 :18 to :21 (common cause) plus Fig 9 :54 (unbundle before searching), and arrived at the same boundary: the peer frame is the different room. Different starting figures, same domain boundaries: low overlap between routes within one framework, so the agreement is weak but not circular at the level of figure choice. ## Round trip gap R_T

The Symbols table defines R_T as the gap between what was sent up and what came back down, `gap(x, (T_E ∘ T_A)(x))`, measuring what the coarser description cannot hold. Fig 4 :30 requires it to be measured rather than assumed. I ran the round trip on the condensation this folder actually uses.

3. **Concretisation (T_E):** a claim about a real child in a real family, peer and school setting.

**R_T is nonzero.** Three things the shorthand holds do not survive the trip back down:

- the **per-slot indexing** (Fig 1 :19 and :22), which a global conformity score silently drops;

Conversely, one thing survives cleanly: frames must be kept distinct, a selector must range them against an outcome, and refusal is a distinct operator. That is the residue worth sending. R_T was **not** measured numerically, because no ground reading exists; it is recorded qualitatively as the enumerated set of dropped constraints.
