# The Tether's answer: how children learn to navigate conflicts between family, peer, and institutional norms

**Mode: `specified` on the shape, `general` on the structural mapping, not `grounded`.** This is a shape and a route, not a developmental result. Simulated derivation checked against the figures (`05_what_shape_means.md` §6), not a ground reading. No child was observed.

## 1. The residual it answers

For one kind of conflict, R is the gap between what a single frame prescribes and what the child does and what the outcome returns. It is indexed per slot, not averaged (Fig 1 :19 *"The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated."*; Fig 1 :22 *"Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average."*). The three systems form a union compound with overlapping conditions (Fig 9 :55 *"A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones."*), so the unit of study is the compound, not three separate loyalties.

## 2. The shape, per domain, never averaged

**D1, the family frame.** It is a frame, not the ground. Fig 2 :45 *"Frames range against the ground. They do not negotiate with it."* The child must be able to range it against what actually happens. Verdict: **single-source** (Fig 2 anchor rule plus the Symbols weighting clause).

**D2, the peer frame.** It is usually the low-overlap frame. Fig 8 :45 *"Two frames together reach what neither reaches alone."* Fig 8 :48 *"How much two frames draw on the same evidence decides both whether their agreement means anything and whether their union has any surplus."* The surplus in a conflict is expected at this boundary, not at the family/institution boundary. Verdict: **triangulated** (Fig 8 plus Fig 7 room selection, low overlap).

**D3, the institutional frame.** It is a frame whose anchor is the most instrument-like (rule, exam, counter), but family and institutional rules often share an adult source. Fig 2 :20 *"so the agreement is the pool talking,"* Fig 2 :21 *"not two frames confirming each other."* Verdict: **single-source** (Fig 2 common-cause), with the empirical strain recorded in `01` F2.

**D4, the child's navigation mechanism.** Four parts follow from the figures:

1. **Keep frames distinct.** Fig 2 :13 *"no anchor; each updates on the others"* and :15 *"is whoever had the most influence"* name the failure: mutual update converges on influence, not learning.
2. **Carry a learnable weighting.** Symbols table :24 to :26: *"How far the belief leans on private history versus inherited prior. w_A + w_B = 1, and w is learnable from the outcomes of past weightings."* The developmental variable is the weighting and its update, not a wholesale swap of frames.
3. **Select against the outcome.** Fig 7 :36 *"A chain of mirrors is one long room. The next room must differ from the last, and match the residual it surfaced."* Fig 7 :35 *"A room's agreement is variation, not a verdict. Selection between rooms is where the ground votes."* The pick is a selector, not a compromise.
4. **Be able to refuse.** Operators :57 *"a refutation is a negation, which is why a vocabulary without it can only accumulate"*; :73 *"A vocabulary with − and without ¬ can say what is missing from an arrangement and cannot say that an arrangement is wrong, so it can only ever accumulate."* Saying "this rule does not apply here" is ¬, not a fifth rule.

Capability is not inside the child alone: Fig 11 :39 *"capability is a property of agent-and-habitat, never of the agent alone"* and :40 *"An improvement that does not change contact changes nothing, however much it improves."* What adults pass down is a head start, not a recording (Fig 4 :22). Verdict: **triangulated** (Symbols w, Fig 11 habitat, Fig 12/Operators negation, Fig 4 membrane; low overlap between them).

## 3. The qualification that changes how this answer reads

Because the question is constitutively about people and social meaning, agreement is legitimate in the convention domain. Fig 2 :36 *"An anchor is legitimate when the question is constitutively about it."* Fig 2 :37 *"Human judgment is the right anchor for is this a good answer for a person, because the question is about people."* Fig 2 :41 *"agreement about a shared meaning is the product, not a corruption of it."*

Two things must be kept apart:

- In the **convention** domain, agreement is the product. Learning a shared meaning is part of what the child is doing.
- In a **verdict** about what happens, Fig 2 :40 still holds: *"disqualifying for a verdict: agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool."*

So a study of navigation must not treat children's agreement with each other as evidence that the shape is correct, even though that agreement is itself a legitimate object of study. Re-census resolves the apparent contradiction; neither clause is dropped.

## 4. What it explicitly does not say

- Not the developmental mechanism, not its timing, not its cultural variation. Those are `gap` at the figure level (`01` F9). Fig 9 :32 *"you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself."*
- Not that family, peer and institutional norms are three independent witnesses. D1 and D3 share an evidence pool until shown otherwise.
- Not that averaging the three is a sensible approximation. Fig 1 :22 forbids it as a signal.
- Not that refusal is the same as subtraction or as context tagging. It is ¬.
- Not that a child is an isolated frame. Capability is agent-and-habitat.
- Not `grounded`. Nothing here is settled by measurement.

## 5. What would settle it - the ground

An instrument that measures the four parts separately and fires the three pre-registered refuters. Candidate instruments from the world literature, secondary here: the social-rules interview that probes authority independence and generalizability (Turiel tradition, via the SEP entry); the attitude-conformity task that separates conformity from resistance by context (Do et al. 2020); person-centered norm profiles across family, peer and school settings (Wang et al. 2019). The genuine ground is a study that can say which part fails. Fig 2 :42 *"Buy independence in proportion to the cost of being wrong."*

The sharpening that matters most, from Fig 3 :26: *"the link between them, having terms for what a situation offers and being able to say what would count as progress, has neither, so it attracts the least attention and the fewest instruments."* Measure the child's affordance vocabulary before declaring the chain healthy.

## 6. The honest stop

`specified`, ungrounded, **candidate**. The shape survives an internal re-census; the empirical "how" does not come from the figures. World sources corroborate pieces of the shape (distinct domains, incongruence as the norm, selective resistance, learnable weighting) but they are a secondary pool and cannot upgrade this to `grounded`. The stop is recorded so it is checkable. *"Indiscernibility is not identity."*

## 7. One-paragraph version for outreach

> The Tether does not give a developmental mechanism and does not claim to settle how children actually learn this. It gives a shape: in a conflict between family, peer and institutional rules, what is learned is not one rule and not an average, but a selector. The child keeps the frames distinct, weights what was inherited against what was learned from peers (a weighting that is itself learnable from outcomes), picks the frame that best matches what actually happens in that kind of situation, and can refuse a rule where it does not apply. Family and institutional rules often share an adult source, so their agreement is one evidence pool; the peer frame is usually the genuinely different one. Because the question is about social meaning, agreement is legitimately the product in the convention domain, but that is not the same as treating agreement among checkers as evidence. The framework returns a shape, not a mechanism, and states three refuters in advance. The empirical how, its timing and its cultural variation remain open until a study measures them.
