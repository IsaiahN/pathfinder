## 1. A shape is a constraint, not a filler

The local canonical README states the limit: the framework returns *"what a missing thing would have to look like (a shape, not a name)"* (it is line-wrapped in the README, as "a shape, not a" / "name)"). Fig 9 :32: **"you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself"**. Fig 13 :134-135 (as recorded in the extract) says it can report the answer is out of reach with the library as it stands but *"It cannot say which primitive is missing, because naming it would mean already holding it."*

From *The Library of Babel*, as recorded in the exemplar: *"You can cut your search short if the set of books returned is small and descriptive enough that it provides a general shape of the answer that satisfies your question."*

So a **shape** is the narrowed form of an answer you can state before you can name it. It constrains; it does not fill. For ECO-0073 the shape is:

> a relation between actor and habitat, evaluated per residual, with a union of two conditions and a cascade enumerated outward, settled at an ecosystem ground.

That is a shape. "Introduce species X to restore role Y" is a name, and it is not what the framework returns.

## 2. How the shape is obtained

- *Library of Babel*: use the vocabulary closest to the thing, look for recurring new terms that overlap it, and recurse; *"With each new term gained, you narrow the space and deepen your vocabulary."* Here the closest vocabulary is the habitat (Fig 11 :43-48), not the species.
- Fig 6 :30: **"The description travels. Fails by being sharpened into terms only we can read. Cast it in effect terms."** Fig 9 :30-31: **"your words are borrowed too, so describe the effect rather than the cause."** So describe the lost function and the new residual in effect terms, not as "the role of the species".
- *Decoding Tacit Knowledge*, as recorded: *"When contact isn't possible, the best you can send is a plain description of what you'd expect to see, because anyone can check that against the world."* This is why the outreach packet sends an expected observation, not a theory.
- Fig 11 :48: **"You do not invent the list. You read it off the world, and what you cannot perceive or measure yet is the residual."** The habitat list is the shape-getting instrument.

## 3. What licenses a name

Only two things, both outside the shape itself:

1. **The ground settles it.** Formula step 5: *"the ground settles it. Verified only by what pays."* The canonical README: the framework *"stops at the limit of what the available instruments can distinguish, and records the lineage so the stop is honest."* Here: a per-slot measurement showing the function returned and no third-order residual appeared.
2. **A provenanced import from a frame whose closure differs** (Fig 6 :23, Fig 8 :50-52), with a source gate and a shadow test. Here: invasion ecology, conservation translocation, ecosystem engineering.

*They're Scanning Books*, as recorded: *"The real import is not a new term in the existing space. It is a new dimension of the space."* And: *"Declaring the primitives does not make them neutral. It makes the choice visible."*

## 4. The source error that applies here

*The Hard Problem is About Source*, as recorded: metamerism means an instrument reading green *"cannot comment on the source"*; *"If several paths create the same behavior, then behavior is a projection with lossy information."* It was the spectrometer, not an argument, that resolved the case.

For ECO-0073 the analogue is direct and important. The world literature shows the same function can be achieved by different means (Andrews et al. 2022: *"ecosystems may be able to achieve similar function through different means"*). Measuring "pollination went up" cannot tell you which actor supplied it. Naming the introduced species as the restorer from that reading is the metamerism error: a source named from a projection. What is owed is a source-discriminating measurement per slot, not an endorsement of the species.

## 5. Correction to my own section 1 (kept visible)

I initially wrote the "shape, not a name" rule as if it forbade naming any species in any answer. That overstates it. The README's phrase governs *"what a missing thing would have to look like"*: a primitive not held in the library. In this question the missing thing is a **function**, not a primitive of the six-term set, and the introduced species is a binding from the world frame onto an actor in the habitat, not an invented new primitive. Fig 11 :65 itself names cane toads and kudzu. Naming a candidate species is therefore allowed when it is provenanced as a binding and checked at the ground; what is forbidden is presenting that binding as something the framework derived. Both claims are recorded so neither is quietly lost. Triangulated (README + Fig 11 :65).

## 6. Two consequences for outreach

**Prestige is not action.** From *The Recursive Self-Improvement Trap*, as recorded: *"Prestige must never be allowed to validate an action directly... Prestige can fund an action. It can give an action a budget to be tried. But it cannot settle an action. Only the ground can settle it. The Ground Maintainer must be blind to prestige."* A campaign that gathers famous names to agree that a species is benign is the named collapse, not corroboration. The legitimate ask gives the claim a budget to be tried (a monitoring design, a second frame) and lets the ecosystem measurement settle it.

**My own audit is a simulation, not a ground reading.** Same essay: *"LLMs can quickly simulate an approximate answer/output... However, the truly testable output would need to be derived from the real architecture or working the formula yourself."* LLM output is *"simulacra."* So `01_figure_grounded_audit.md` is a simulated derivation checked for internal consistency against the figures. It is not a test of the claim and must be labelled as such. It cannot settle anything it reports on.
