# The Tether's answer: when an introduced species fills a lost role

**Mode: `specified` - not `grounded`.** This is a shape and a route, not a result. Simulated derivation checked against the figures (`05_what_shape_means.md` section 6), not a ground reading. No ecosystem measurement was taken.

## 1. The residual it answers

An ecosystem has lost a function. Adding a species can restore the function and create new residuals at the same time. The question asks when each happens. It is a **union compound**, not one hard question: Fig 9 :57 - **"a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either."** Each condition gets its own verdict, and their compound gets a third. They are never averaged. *"The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated."* (Fig 1 :19).

## 2. The shape, by domain

**Domain A, the habitat.** The answer begins with the habitat, not the species. Fig 11 :43: **"The habitat is enumerated, never composed."** Fig 11 :44: **"List everything in contact with the residual, then what is in contact with those, and outward until the cascade stops mattering."** Disruption is a cascade: Fig 11 :52 - **"And contact cascades. Scoping the habitat to direct contact is how DDT reached the eagles: first-order enumeration, second-order effect, and the residual that mattered was three steps out."** So the "intensify disruption" half is answered by enumeration to second and third order, per slot. **Verdict: triangulated** (Fig 11 :43-54 with Fig 1 :19/:22 and Fig 13 :24). A first-order impact study that finds nothing is silent, not clean: *"So a search that finds nothing is never a proof of absence."* (Fig 8 :39).

**Domain B, the introduced actor, compounded with A.** There is no standalone actor verdict. Fig 11 :39: **"So the answerable question is not what intelligence is, but what makes a system capable. And capability is a property of agent-and-habitat, never of the agent alone."** Fig 11 :40: **"A system cannot be made more capable by improving the agent in isolation. An improvement that does not change contact changes nothing, however much it improves."** And the decisive reframe: Fig 11 :50 - **"The same actor is a benefactor to one residual and a malfactor to another, so mutualism and parasitism are relations rather than kinds."** So "when can it fill the role" is asked about a **relation in a habitat**, not about a species in the abstract. **Verdict: triangulated** for the valence claim (Fig 11 :49-50 plus Fig 9 :30-31); **single-source** for the capability claim (Fig 11 :39-40), with world corroboration from Chamberlain et al. 2014.

**Domain C, the management seat.** The seat can add and cannot subtract. Fig 11 :61: **"Introduction is available; exclusion is not."** Fig 11 :63: **"Which is why a managed ecosystem is permanent work rather than a solved state."** The two silent failure modes are stated by the figure: *"what you failed to reproduce is invisible until the goal fails"* (Fig 11 :68) and *"what you unintentionally introduced is invisible until it acts"* (Fig 11 :69). Fig 11 :65 makes the whole question turn on enumeration: **"Introducing an actor for its cascade is ecosystem engineering, after Jones, Lawton and Shachak, 1994. Cane toads and kudzu are the same move with the cascade unenumerated."** So "fill a role" and "intensify disruption" are not two kinds of act. They are one act with the cascade enumerated or not. **Verdict: single-source** (Fig 11 :61-69), corroborated by Jones et al. 1994 and Bergstrom et al. 2009.

**Domain D, the test and the ground.** A contained trial cannot settle capability. Fig 11 :66: **"Isolation is not removal of the habitat. It is substitution of one habitat for another."** Fig 11 :70: **"A test harness is a substituted habitat. Which is why a synthetic solve proves wiring and never capability."** The habitat is not the ground: Fig 11 :73 - **"The habitat is not the ground. The habitat is where actors come from and it updates; the ground is what settles, and it does not."** The anchor is an ecosystem measurement (Fig 2 :29), never agreement among frames (Fig 2 :35). **Verdict: triangulated** (Fig 11 :66-73 with Fig 2 :29/:35).

**The compound verdict.** The two conditions overlap. Fig 9 :55: **"A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones."** A single lever (introduce for the role) clears the region where only one condition holds and leaves the intersections standing. The same introduction can restore one function at one stage and fail another, as Andrews et al. 2022 measured. **Verdict: triangulated** (Fig 9 :54-57 plus the Andrews ground result).

## 3. The answer in one line

An introduced species fills a lost role when the destination habitat supplies the contacts the function requires and the cascade has been enumerated to the order where residuals actually appear; it intensifies disruption when that enumeration is skipped, when valence is assumed to be a property of the species rather than a relation to the measured residual, or when a substituted habitat is mistaken for the destination. Both can be true of the same introduction. Which one, in a given case, is owed at the ground.

## 4. What it explicitly does not say

- Not that any named species is good or bad. Valence is relative to the residual (Fig 11 :49-50).
- Not that a first-order impact study proves safety. Closure is idempotent and search cannot add the missing atom (Fig 8 :32, :39).
- Not that a fenced, captive, or modelled trial proves capability in the destination habitat (Fig 11 :70).
- Not that adding an actor is reversible by subtracting it. Introduction is available, exclusion is not (Fig 11 :61).
- Not a ground result. Which species fills which role in which habitat is not settled here.
- Not that the two conditions can be averaged into one "net impact" score. Fig 1 :19 forbids aggregating the per-slot residual.

## 5. What would settle it - the ground

A source-discriminating, per-slot measurement, not a further argument. From *The Hard Problem is About Source*, as recorded in `05`: an instrument that reads green *"cannot comment on the source"*; the spectrometer resolved it. Here the corresponding questions are:

1. **Function test:** does the lost function actually return, per slot, and which actor supplied it? Andrews et al. 2022 is the model: state the function as an objective, then measure it.
2. **Cascade test:** does any resident decline or relation change sign at second or third order? Enumerate outward until the cascade stops mattering (Fig 11 :44).
3. **Transfer test:** does capability measured in a substituted habitat transfer to the destination? If not, the harness proved wiring only (Fig 11 :70).
4. **Standing falsifiers from the audit:** an invariant valence list; isolation-only improvement that changes contact; a complete first-order enumeration with no third-order residual; a transferred capability from a substituted habitat; or a proof that the union is a partition.

## 7. One-paragraph version for outreach

> The Tether does not name a good or bad introduced species. It says that the question is a union of two conditions, not one: a species fills a lost role only when the destination habitat supplies the contacts that function needs, and it intensifies disruption when the habitat's cascade is left unenumerated and the damage shows up three steps out. Valence is a relation to the measured residual, not a property the species carries, so the same actor can be a benefactor to one residual and a malfactor to another. A fenced trial proves wiring, not capability, and adding an actor is not reversible by subtracting the habitat, so this is permanent work. What is offered is the shape any answer must have, the refuters written in advance, and the measurements that would close it. Which species, in which habitat, stays open until the ground settles it.
