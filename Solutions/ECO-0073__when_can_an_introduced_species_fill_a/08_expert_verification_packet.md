# Expert verification packet - the Tether on introduced species and lost roles

**What this is:** a claim in a form an invasion ecologist or conservation practitioner can check. Mode, derivation and refuters are stated before the conclusion, so a reader knows what to look for and what would settle it.

**Mode: `specified` - not `grounded`.** No field measurement is claimed. This is a shape and a route, not a result.

## 1. The claim, in one paragraph

"Can an introduced species fill a lost ecological role, and when does it intensify disruption" is a **union of two conditions**, not one question. A species fills a role only as a relation between actor and habitat: capability is a property of agent-and-habitat, and the actor's valence is relative to the residual, not carried by the species. Disruption has to be enumerated outward, because the residual that matters is often second or third order. Introduction is available and exclusion is not, so a managed ecosystem is permanent work, and a substituted habitat proves wiring rather than capability. The same act is ecosystem engineering when the cascade is enumerated and an unexamined introduction when it is not. Both outcomes can hold at once for one actor. Which holds in a given case is owed at the ground, per slot, and is not answered here.

## 2. Three checkable sub-claims

### C1 - union and valence (checkable against invasion ecology and the figure text)
- **Statement:** the two halves of the question are overlapping conditions, and an actor's sign is relative to the residual rather than a property of the species.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 9 :57 - *"a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either."* Fig 11 :50 - *"The same actor is a benefactor to one residual and a malfactor to another, so mutualism and parasitism are relations rather than kinds."* Fig 9 :55 - *"A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones."*
- **What to check:** can the same introduced species restore one function and harm a resident in the same system? Andrews et al. 2022 (hihi pollination) is a concrete case: seed germination improved at hihi sites, while other birds exceeded hihi's contribution to fruit set where hihi was absent.
- **Refuter:** a standing list of good or bad introduced species that survives every change of residual; or a proof that one condition is a strict subcase of the other in every case.

### C2 - habitat enumeration and cascades (checkable against network ecology)
- **Statement:** disruption must be read off the habitat outward to second and third order; a first-order impact study cannot prove absence.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 11 :43 - *"The habitat is enumerated, never composed."* Fig 11 :52 - *"And contact cascades. Scoping the habitat to direct contact is how DDT reached the eagles: first-order enumeration, second-order effect, and the residual that mattered was three steps out."* Fig 8 :39 - *"So a search that finds nothing is never a proof of absence."*
- **What to check:** do the documented cases show effects that first-order scoping would miss? Rogers et al. 2017 (brown treesnake, Guam) estimated a 61-92 percent decline in seedling recruitment via loss of frugivorous birds, an effect two steps from the introduction. Doizy et al. 2018 found indirect and counter-intuitive effects at network scale.
- **Refuter:** a complete first-order enumeration that predicts all second-order and third-order effects with no residual appearing, across several systems.

### C3 - capability, asymmetry, and the substituted habitat (checkable against translocation practice)
- **Statement:** capability belongs to agent-and-habitat, introduction is available while exclusion is not, and a contained trial does not demonstrate capability in the destination.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 11 :39 - *"capability is a property of agent-and-habitat, never of the agent alone."* Fig 11 :61 - *"Introduction is available; exclusion is not."* Fig 11 :70 - *"A test harness is a substituted habitat. Which is why a synthetic solve proves wiring and never capability."*
- **What to check:** does improving the organism without changing contact ever restore the function? Does the field distinguish engineering from an unexamined introduction by whether the cascade was enumerated (Fig 11 :65, citing Jones, Lawton and Shachak 1994)? Defra 2025 and the IUCN translocation guidelines both require an explicit objective and risk assessment before release.
- **Refuter:** an introduced species that restores a function with no change in contact; or a substituted-habitat trial whose capability transfers to the destination with a measurement.

## 3. What is explicitly not claimed

- Not that any named species is good or bad.
- Not that a first-order impact study proves safety.
- Not that a captive, fenced, or modelled trial proves capability.
- Not that an introduction can be undone by subtracting the habitat.
- Not a ground result. No species, habitat or function is bound here.
- Not that the two conditions can be averaged into one net-impact score (Fig 1 :19).

## 5. What would settle it

1. **Function test:** state the lost function as an objective, then measure it per slot and identify which actor supplied it (Andrews et al. 2022 as a design template).
2. **Cascade test:** enumerate contacts outward to second and third order and report any resident decline or sign change.
3. **Transfer test:** measure capability in the destination habitat, not only in a substituted one.
4. **Our standing falsifiers** are in section 2 and in `01_figure_grounded_audit.md` section 4, all written before this packet was compiled.

## 6. What an expert can check in twenty minutes

1. Read Fig 11 :39-40, :43-54, :61-70 and Fig 9 :54-57 (four short passages).
2. Check C1 against one system you know: did the same actor help one function and hurt another?
3. Name one third-order effect that a first-order impact study would have missed, or say why none is possible in that system.
4. Send the refutation. A failure is more useful than an endorsement.
