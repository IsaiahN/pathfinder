## Why these sources and not only one pool

Fields are deliberately mixed: community-ecology meta-analysis (Chamberlain 2014), conservation biology and functional restoration (Andrews 2022), theoretical network ecology (Doizy 2018), island ecology and mutualism disruption (Rogers 2017), conservation policy and national guidance (IUCN 2012/2013, Defra 2025), ecosystem engineering (Jones et al. 1994), and social-ecological rewilding (Collins 2026). They do not all draw on the same evidence. Where they overlap, the ledger says so. Agreement among them is secondary corroboration only; the verdict comes from the figures.

## Two things in the world literature that bear directly on the question

1. **Context-dependent outcomes are the rule, not the exception.** Chamberlain et al. 2014: *"Interaction outcomes are context-dependent when the sign and/or magnitude change as a function of the biotic or abiotic context."* Mutualism was the interaction most likely to change sign across contexts. This corroborates Fig 11 :49-50 (valence relative to the residual) from a different field and evidence pool.
2. **The same introduction can restore one function and not another.** Andrews et al. 2022 found that hihi improved seed germination at reintroduction sites but other birds exceeded its contribution to fruit set where it was absent. This is the union compound of Fig 9 :54-57 observed at the ground, and it explains why a single success metric hides the overlap.
