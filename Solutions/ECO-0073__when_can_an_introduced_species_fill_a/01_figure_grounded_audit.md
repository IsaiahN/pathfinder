## 0. The residual, in effect terms

An ecosystem has lost a function R (a role: pollination, seed dispersal, predation, nutrient cycling, habitat structure). A seat (a manager, agency, or landowner) can add an actor that was not there. The observed gap is not one residual but two conditions at once:

- R1: the lost function is still absent after the addition, or only partially restored;
- R2: new residuals appear after the addition (a native declines, a cascade fires, a relation changes sign).

The decision is: under which conditions does the addition lower R1 without raising R2, under which conditions does it raise R2, and can both conditions hold for the same introduction relative to different residuals. The answer is wanted per slot (per function, per actor, per interaction), not as a global average. *"The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated."* (Fig 1 :19). *"Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average."* (Fig 1 :22).

What an answer would look like (bound before the run): a set of conditions on habitat contact and actor valence that predicts, per slot, whether a function returns and whether new residuals appear. The sought shape is a relation plus a set of conditions, not the name of a species.

## 2. Domain census

### R by arity, symmetry, scale

- **Arity:** two conditions (fill the role, intensify disruption). A **union compound** (Fig 9 :54-57), not one hard question.
- **Symmetry:** the actor's valence is not symmetric under swapping residuals. The same actor is benefactor to one residual and malfactor to another (Fig 11 :50). So there is no single signed quantity to optimise.
- **Scale:** first order (direct contact: predation, pollination), second order (a cascade through one intermediate), third order (the residual that matters is three steps out, Fig 11 :52).

### One line per domain (actors / currency / anchor)

- **Domain A, the habitat / field of contact.** Actors: residents, conditions (light, water, temperature, season, terrain), relations (pollination, predation, competition, dependency). Currency: contact. Anchor: the ground, an ecosystem measurement that does not move because a frame thinks otherwise. Nameable anchor exists, so this is a domain. Fig 11 :43-48.
- **Domain B, the introduced actor.** Actors: the organism and what it brings with it. Currency: effects on R, valence relative to the residual. Anchor: the same ground. **Not a standalone domain.** Fig 11 :39 says *"capability is a property of agent-and-habitat, never of the agent alone."* An actor with no habitat of its own has no independent anchor, so it is a **compound with Domain A**, not a separate loop. This is a re-census result, recorded rather than hidden.
- **Domain C, the management seat.** Actors: agency, landowner, reviewer. Currency: interventions (introduction, monitoring, control). Anchor: the ground. Nameable anchor exists. Power is asymmetric: *"Introduction is available; exclusion is not."* (Fig 11 :61).
- **Domain D, the verification / outreach frame** (used in `09_watson_answer.md`). Actors: expert readers. Currency: agreement and residual. Anchor: the ground, never agreement. In this simulated run all seats draw on one evidence pool, so its independence is zero.

### Partition or union

**Union, not partition.** Fig 9 :54: *"And when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again."* A single lever (introduce the actor for the lost role) clears the region where only one condition holds and leaves the intersections standing (Fig 9 :55). A case in two conditions is categorically different from a harder case of either (Fig 9 :57). Therefore the two halves of the question get separate verdicts and a third verdict for their compound. They are never averaged.

### Order by dependency (Fig 3)

Perceive the lost function -> have terms for what the habitat and actor afford -> say what counts as filling the role -> plan the introduction -> learn and carry (monitor and publish). The chain is ordered by dependency, not by importance: *"Each link consumes what the one above produces."* (Fig 3 :20). The middle link is where chains usually break (Fig 3 :24), and the world literature confirms it: few translocation programs state or monitor function as an objective (Andrews et al. 2022, `10_source_ledger.csv`).

## 3. Findings F1..F10

### F1 - The question is a union compound (decisive, structural)

Fig 9 :54: **"And when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again."**
Fig 9 :55: **"A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones."**
Fig 9 :57: **"a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either."**

"When can it fill the role" and "when does it intensify disruption" are not two phrasings of one question. They are two conditions that can hold at once for the same introduction relative to different residuals. The correct move is to unbundle, answer each, then reconvene the compound. **Mode `general`.** Triangulated: Fig 9 union rule plus Fig 11 :50 (valence relative to two residuals), low overlap between the leave/arrive figure and the habitat figure. **Refuter:** show that one condition is a strict subcase of the other for every introduction, so no intersection exists. The Andrews 2022 result (a reintroduced pollinator restored one function at one stage and not another) is a concrete intersection and counts against this refuter.

### F2 - Valence is relative to the residual, not carried by the species

Fig 11 :49: **"An actor's valence is relative to the residual, not a property it carries."**
Fig 11 :50: **"The same actor is a benefactor to one residual and a malfactor to another, so mutualism and parasitism are relations rather than kinds."**

The words "fills a role" and "intensifies disruption" name relations, not kinds of organism. A standing list of good or bad introduced species does not survive a change of residual. This is the figure's own answer to the question's hidden assumption. **Mode `general`.** Triangulated: Fig 11 :49-50 plus Fig 9 :30-31 (*"describe the effect rather than the cause"*), different figures. World corroboration: Chamberlain et al. 2014 (Ecology Letters) found interaction outcomes change sign with context, most often for mutualism. **Refuter:** exhibit an actor whose sign is invariant across all residuals and habitats, with a published measurement behind it. The mutualism-parasitism continuum literature runs the other way.

### F3 - Capability is agent-and-habitat, never agent alone

Fig 11 :39: **"So the answerable question is not what intelligence is, but what makes a system capable. And capability is a property of agent-and-habitat, never of the agent alone."**
Fig 11 :40: **"A system cannot be made more capable by improving the agent in isolation. An improvement that does not change contact changes nothing, however much it improves."**

Whether a species can fill a role depends on the contacts the destination habitat supplies (the residents, conditions and relations of Domain A), not on a trait list carried by the species. This is why "well adapted to the role" is not an answer: it prices the agent and ignores contact. **Mode `specified`.** Single-source within the figures (Fig 11), triangulated with the anchor rule (Fig 2) only in the weaker sense that the ground settles it. The two figure readings have low overlap, so we mark the *capability* claim single-source and the *must be settled at ground* claim triangulated. **Refuter:** an introduced species that restores a function in a habitat with no change in contact, or a controlled trial where improving the organism alone (without changing where it contacts) restores the function.

### F4 - Enumerate the habitat outward; disruption is a cascade

Fig 11 :43: **"The habitat is enumerated, never composed."**
Fig 11 :44: **"List everything in contact with the residual, then what is in contact with those, and outward until the cascade stops mattering."**
Fig 11 :52: **"And contact cascades. Scoping the habitat to direct contact is how DDT reached the eagles: first-order enumeration, second-order effect, and the residual that mattered was three steps out."**

Disruption is not a property visible at first contact. The DDT example is the figure's own case of a residual three steps out. So "when does it intensify disruption" is answered by enumeration to second and third order, not by checking direct effects. Fig 1 :19 requires the enumeration be kept per slot. **Mode `general`.** Triangulated: Fig 11 :43-54 plus Fig 1 :19/:22 (per-slot residual) plus Fig 13 :24 (network, influence travels), three different figures with low overlap. World corroboration: Doizy et al. 2018 (indirect and counter-intuitive cascades), Rogers et al. 2017 (mutualism disruption three steps out), Bergstrom et al. 2009 (indirect effects of removal). **Refuter:** a complete first-order enumeration that predicts all second-order effects with no missed residual, across several systems. The cited cascade literature counts against this.

### F5 - Asymmetry and the two silent failure modes

Fig 11 :61: **"Introduction is available; exclusion is not."**
Fig 11 :63: **"Which is why a managed ecosystem is permanent work rather than a solved state."**
Fig 11 :68: **"what you failed to reproduce is invisible until the goal fails"**
Fig 11 :69: **"what you unintentionally introduced is invisible until it acts"**
Fig 11 :65: **"Introducing an actor for its cascade is ecosystem engineering, after Jones, Lawton and Shachak, 1994. Cane toads and kudzu are the same move with the cascade unenumerated."**

The seat can add an actor and cannot subtract the habitat, because *"contact is a fact about the world and does not consult you"* (Fig 11 :61). Fig 11 :65 is decisive for the question: the same act is called ecosystem engineering when the cascade is enumerated and cane toads or kudzu when it is not. The difference is not the species; it is whether the habitat was enumerated (F4). So "fill a role" and "intensify disruption" are not two kinds of act. They are one act with the cascade enumerated or not. **Mode `specified`.** Single-source (Fig 11), with the Jones et al. 1994 source named by the figure itself and independently read this session. **Refuter:** a demonstrated technology for subtracting a habitat (not an actor) after introduction, which would remove the asymmetry.

### F6 - Isolation is substitution, not removal

Fig 11 :66: **"Isolation is not removal of the habitat. It is substitution of one habitat for another."**
Fig 11 :67: **"Move the residual and you must reproduce everything its goal required, and you inherit the new habitat's actors, which you did not choose."**
Fig 11 :70: **"A test harness is a substituted habitat. Which is why a synthetic solve proves wiring and never capability."**

A captive or fenced trial of the introduction cannot settle the question by itself. It reproduces some conditions, inherits actors the manager did not choose, and proves wiring, not capability in the destination habitat. **Mode `general`.** Single-source (Fig 11), triangulated with F3 (capability is agent-and-habitat) in the weak sense that both deny the isolated-agent test. **Refuter:** a substituted habitat that reproduces every condition the role requires and inherits no unchosen actors, with a measurement showing capability transfers.

### F7 - The ground settles it; the habitat is not the ground

Fig 11 :73: **"The habitat is not the ground. The habitat is where actors come from and it updates; the ground is what settles, and it does not."**
Fig 2 :29: **"An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks."**
Fig 2 :35: **"Alignment is triangulation, not negotiation. A mirror cannot correct you."**

The anchor here is a measurement (does the function return, does a resident decline, does a cascade fire), not a vote among ecologists and not the actor's reputation. The habitat updates when the actor is added; the ground does not. **Mode `general`.** Triangulated: Fig 11 :73 plus Fig 2 :29/:35, two figures with different subject matter. **Refuter:** a settlement procedure whose answer changes when the frames are changed while the ecosystem is held fixed.

### F8 - Reachability: absence of observed impact proves nothing

Fig 8 :32: **"Closure is idempotent. Composing inside a closed set never leaves it, so no search, however long, adds an atom."**
Fig 8 :39: **"So a search that finds nothing is never a proof of absence."**
Fig 6 :14: **"the only operator that moves the wall"** (import)

More monitoring inside the same frame does not add the missing concept; the missing thing arrives from a frame whose closure differs (the actual species, the field data, a second discipline). A search that records no impact at first order is silent about third order. **Mode `general`.** Triangulated: Fig 8 :32/:39 plus Fig 6 :14/:23, plus F4's cascade sources. **Refuter:** a closed-world proof that no unobserved interaction exists, which Fig 8 :39 rules out by construction.

### F9 - Verification discipline for the packet

This is an outreach finding, not an ecological one, but it governs how the ecological claim may be checked. **Mode `general`.** Triangulated: Fig 10 and Fig 2. **Refuter:** a demonstration that a checker given the full derivation is no more likely to confirm it than one given only the residual. If that were shown, the stage ordering would be unnecessary.

## 4. Pre-registered refuter (written before the conclusion)

Per the discipline quoted in the exemplar: *"a falsifier written before the run is a design instrument, not only an honesty device."* The shape claim is refuted if any of the following holds:

1. A standing, nameable list of good or bad introduced species survives every change of residual (breaks Fig 11 :49-51).
2. A species can be shown to restore a function without changing its contact with the destination habitat, for example by improving the organism in isolation (breaks Fig 11 :40).
3. A complete first-order enumeration of the habitat predicts every relevant second-order and third-order effect, with no residual appearing three steps out (breaks Fig 11 :52 and F4).
4. A substituted habitat can demonstrate capability in the destination habitat, with a measurement that transfers (breaks Fig 11 :70).
5. One condition (fill role / intensify disruption) is a strict subcase of the other in every case, so the union is a partition (breaks Fig 9 :54-57).

If any fires, the affected finding is withdrawn, not softened. Until one is run, no part of this may be labelled `grounded`.

## 5. Recommendation

1. Unbundle the question (F1) and give the two conditions and their compound separate verdicts.
2. Enumerate the habitat per slot to second and third order before introducing anything (F4), and record the enumeration as the lineage, not as an afterthought.
3. State what would count as filling the role before choosing the species. This is the middle link of Fig 3 and the documented failure point in the world literature (Andrews et al. 2022; Defra 2025).
4. Treat the introduction as an import into the manager's model and as an added actor in a habitat the manager did not author (F5, F6). Price both the atoms and the bonds, not just the species.
5. Settle at the ground with measurements, per slot, and never aggregate role restoration and disruption into one score (F7, Fig 1 :19/:22).
6. Send `08_expert_verification_packet.md`, which withholds the interiors until stage 1 is recorded (F9).

## 7. Step 7 discipline when no term closes the residual

For the open binding (which species, which habitat):

- **SEARCHED** (cheapest, asked first): has the field already measured this function in this habitat? Several sources did (Andrews et al. 2022; Collins et al. 2026), and they report contested, context-dependent outcomes. Not proof of absence.
- **INWARD**: sharpen the instrument in our own terms: per-slot function measurements, second-order network mapping (Doizy et al. 2018). This extends access.
- **OUTWARD**: import the frame whose closure predicts the shape: invasion ecology, conservation translocation practice, and ecosystem engineering (Jones et al. 1994, named by the figure itself). Source gate and shadow test are supplied in `10_source_ledger.csv`.

INWARD and OUTWARD are kept separate. Underivable-in-principle is not the same as unreachable-at-this-budget; here the binding is reachable with a ground measurement, so importing another framework into the verdict would add an atom that was not needed.
