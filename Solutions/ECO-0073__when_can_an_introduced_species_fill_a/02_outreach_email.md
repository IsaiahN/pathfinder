# Outreach draft - check the output first, then decide about the framework

**Use:** one-to-one, or as a cover letter attached to a submission through the public venues in `03_public_contact_points.csv`. **Not** for bulk send.

**Hard precondition:** do not send until the stage-1 output below survives the recipient's check. The offer collapses on contact if the output cannot be examined without the theory.

---

**Subject:** One checkable claim about introduced species and lost ecological roles

> Hi [name],
>
> I have one specific output I would like you to check against what you already know about invasion ecology and functional restoration. I am not asking you to read a theory first, and I am not asking you to agree with me. If the output does not survive, there is nothing else to look at.
>
> **The residual.** When a species is lost, an ecological function can go with it. Adding a species to fill that function can do two things at once: restore the function, and create new residuals (a decline in a resident, a cascade three steps out). The question "when does it fill the role and when does it intensify disruption" treats those as one question. The output says it is a **union of two conditions**, and that a single lever (introduce for the role) leaves the overlap standing. That is why a reintroduced pollinator can improve one function at one life stage and not another.
>
> **The output (`specified`, not `grounded`):**
> 1. Whether an introduced species fills a role is a relation between the actor and the habitat, not a property carried by the species. The same actor can be a benefactor to one residual and a malfactor to another. Mutualism and parasitism are relations, not kinds.
> 2. Capability is a property of agent-and-habitat. Improving the organism in isolation, without changing what it contacts, changes nothing.
> 3. Disruption has to be enumerated outward. Direct contact is first order; the residual that matters is often third order (the figure's own example is DDT reaching eagles). A first-order impact study that finds nothing is not evidence of no impact.
> 4. Introduction is available and exclusion is not. A managed ecosystem is permanent work, and a fenced or captive trial proves wiring, never capability in the destination habitat.
>
> **What I am not claiming:** that a particular named species is good or bad; that a substituted-habitat trial settles the case; that first-order monitoring proves safety; or that this is a measured result. It is a shape and a route. Which species fills which role in which habitat is owed at the ground.
>
> **What would refute it (written before this message):**
> 1. a standing list of good or bad introduced species that survives every change of residual;
> 2. a case where improving the organism in isolation, with contact unchanged, restores the function;
> 3. a complete first-order enumeration that predicts all second-order and third-order effects with nothing missed;
> 4. a substituted habitat that demonstrates capability in the destination habitat with a measurement that transfers;
> 5. a demonstration that one of the two conditions is a strict subcase of the other in every case.
>
> If any one holds, I will retract the corresponding claim. A one-line "this fails because..." is more useful to me than agreement. If you would rather not engage, no reply is needed.
>
> **If it survives, and only then:** the sources are below. The figures are the authority, not my summary of them.
> - Local figure text with line numbers and the canonical usage rule are in this package (`01_figure_grounded_audit.md`, `09_watson_answer.md`).
> - Andrews et al. 2022, "Evaluating the success of functional restoration after reintroduction of a lost avian pollinator", Conservation Biology: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9545379
> - Jones, Lawton and Shachak 1994, "Organisms as ecosystem engineers", Oikos: https://www.caryinstitute.org/sites/default/files/public/reprints/Jones_et_al_1994_Organisms_as_Oikos_69_373-386.pdf
> - Doizy et al. 2018, "Impact of cyber-invasive species on a large ecological network", Scientific Reports: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6125364
>
> **If you want to use the machinery:** the formula is in the package. Run it on a problem you know well and can check against a ground. Write down what would refute your result *before* you run it, and state the mode of what you get. A reported failure is more useful to me than a reported success.
