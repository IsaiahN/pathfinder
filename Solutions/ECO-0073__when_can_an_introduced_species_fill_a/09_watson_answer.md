## QUESTION

Decide, in effect terms, what the Tether says about introducing a species to fill a role lost by an extinction or decline. Option A: treat it as one question with a single answer, a good or bad species, and a first-order impact check. Option B: treat it as a union of two conditions with per-slot verdicts, a habitat enumerated outward, and a ground measurement. What each leads to: A names a species before describing the residual, hides third-order effects, and spends the independence of the people asked to check it; B gives a checkable shape, written refuters, and an open binding until the ground settles it.

## VERDICT (from the figures)

The shape survives and the binding stays open. The question is a union of two conditions; valence is relative to the measured residual, not carried by the species; capability belongs to agent-and-habitat; disruption must be enumerated outward to second and third order; introduction is available and exclusion is not, so this is permanent work; and a substituted habitat proves wiring, never capability. The same act is ecosystem engineering when the cascade is enumerated and an unexamined introduction when it is not. Both outcomes can hold for one actor. This is a `specified`, ungrounded result, and the figures govern it directly, so it is not a `gap`. No species is named as good or bad, and no first-order impact check is treated as proof of safety.

- **cost if wrong:** bounded and locatable. If the union verdict is wrong, the two conditions collapse into one and a single lever would settle both; the cost is an unnecessarily complex monitoring design, not a false clearance. The larger cost is using a first-order impact study to approve a release, because Fig 8 :39 says a search that finds nothing is not proof of absence and Fig 11 :52 puts the residual three steps out. Fig 2 :42 prices it: "Buy independence in proportion to the cost of being wrong." That cost is avoidable by enumerating the habitat before release and recording the enumeration.
- **undoable:** yes, conditionally. The shape is structurable and reversible: the habitat enumeration can be redone at a finer scale, the objective can be restated, and the per-slot residuals can be remeasured. The introduction itself is not undoable in the same sense. Fig 11 :61 says exclusion is not available, so an added actor cannot simply be subtracted and the claim must be corrected rather than recalled. What can be withdrawn cleanly is our reading, not the release.
- **reversed by:** a ground reading, not agreement. A per-slot, source-discriminating measurement showing the function returned and no third-order residual appeared would bind a species to a role. An invariant valence list, an isolation-only improvement that changes contact, a complete first-order enumeration with no third-order residual, or a substituted-habitat capability that transfers would each break a named finding. A figure re-census that merges Domain B back into a standalone actor with its own anchor would also change the verdict. Until one of these fires, the verdict stands as `candidate`.
