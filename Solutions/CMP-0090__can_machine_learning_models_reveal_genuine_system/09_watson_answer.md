## QUESTION

Decide, in effect terms, what the Tether says about the choice offered by complexity science.txt line 109: do fitted models reveal genuine system mechanisms, or do they mainly capture correlations that fail under changing conditions? Option A: credit a model with a mechanism on the strength of strong in-distribution prediction and interpretation. Option B: withhold mechanism-credit until a regularity survives a push on the ground from a frame that shares no evidence pool with training, with the bond rather than the ingredient set doing the work. What each leads to: A scores the frame with a quantity the frame also produces and spends the independence of the people checking it; B delivers a checkable shape, falsifiers written in advance and an open instance until the ground pays. Bound shapes before the run (README): M-shape (mechanism credited after independent intervention) and C-shape (correlation only, high in-distribution score, failure under substitution).

## DOMAIN CENSUS

R by arity, symmetry and scale, then one line per domain with actors / currency / anchor, partition or union, order by dependency.

- **R (the residual).** The gap between a model's prediction and the observation under a changed habitat. Arity: at least two frames (model and probe) across at least two habitats (training and changed). Symmetry: directional, the ground is the anchor and does not update on the model (Fig 2 :25 *"pinned: it does not update on A or B"*). Scale: one level for the fitted model, a crossing at intervention where model-and-habitat becomes the object (Fig 11 :39 *"capability is a property of agent-and-habitat, never of the agent alone"*). Under i.i.d. test R is near zero for both mechanism and shortcut models, so the training pool cannot discriminate sources.
- **Domain 1, the fitted model as frame.** Actors: weights, features, training distribution, objective. Currency: in-distribution loss and accuracy. Anchor: none internal, since its data moves with the frame and fails the Fig 2 anchor test. Status: a frame folded into Domain 3 as an object under test, not a grounded domain.
- **Domain 2, the composition inside the model.** Actors: variables as atoms, structural or causal relations as bonds. Currency: bits for the description and actions for testing whether it holds (Fig 12 :125-126 *"Bits price the description. Actions price finding out whether it holds."*). Anchor: intervention behaviour. Question type: partition of arrangements over a shared atom set.
- **Domain 3, the evaluation ground and verification frame.** Actors: held-out environments, interventions, deployment conditions, researchers, reviewers, benchmarks. Currency: per-slot residual under change, and agreement. Anchor: the measurement that does not move when the model is pushed, never agreement. Question type: union compound needing unbundling.
- **Partition or union.** Union, not partition: a model can hold a mechanism in one slot and a Clever Hans shortcut in another. Fig 9 :55-56 *"A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones."* Split into Q_a reachability/settlement, Q_b bond, Q_c habitat, each with its own loop and ground.
- **Order by dependency.** Perception of a failed prediction under shift, then vocabulary (bond versus ingredient), then objective (residual stays down under an independent push), then intervention planning, then learn and carry with provenance (Fig 3). Refuse before mint (Formula step 2); describe the residual before choosing a frame (Fig 9 :27-28).

Strain test: the independence rule (Fig 2) and the bond rule (Fig 12) both fit but govern different domains, so no strain. Fig 11's substituted habitat and Fig 6's outward import govern a substitution and a translation respectively. After re-census no live strain remains (01 §6).

## WORLD (secondary sources read)

World sources corroborate only; figures decide. Sources are ordered by overlap with the framework, framework-author sources declared high overlap.

- **ML / causal representation.** Arjovsky, Bottou, Gulrajani and Lopez-Paz, "Invariant Risk Minimization", arXiv:1907.02893, 2019 (v3 2020): "we show how the invariances learned by IRM relate to the causal structures governing the data and enable out-of-distribution generalization." Predicts that what survives distribution change is tied to causal structure, not in-sample fit. Corroborates C2 and F6. Overlap: low (different authors, venue and evidence pool).
- **ML / reliability.** D'Amour et al., "Underspecification Presents Challenges for Credibility in Modern Machine Learning", arXiv:2011.03395, 2020 (JMLR 2022): "An ML pipeline is underspecified when it can return many predictors with equivalently strong held-out performance in the training domain ... such predictors can behave very differently in deployment domains." Predicts that equal held-out scores do not identify the deployment-relevant regularity. Corroborates C1 and C3. Overlap: low, though it shares the OOD literature with Geirhos and Ilyas.
- **ML / comparative psychology.** Geirhos et al., "Shortcut learning in deep neural networks", Nature Machine Intelligence 2, 665 to 673, 2020: "Shortcuts are decision rules that perform well on standard benchmarks but fail to transfer to more challenging testing conditions, such as real-world scenarios. Related issues are known in comparative psychology, education and linguistics, suggesting that shortcut learning may be a common characteristic of learning systems, biological and artificial alike." Predicts benchmark success plus deployment failure across biological and artificial learners. Corroborates C3 at low overlap (animal behaviour and education fields).
- **ML / robustness.** Ilyas et al., "Adversarial Examples Are Not Bugs, They Are Features", arXiv:1905.02175, 2019: "adversarial examples can be directly attributed to the presence of non-robust features: features derived from patterns in the data distribution that are highly predictive, yet brittle and incomprehensible to humans." Predicts that highly predictive and brittle coexist in one reading. Corroborates C1 and C2. Overlap: low author overlap, shares robustness literature.
- **AI / cognition.** Chollet, "On the Measure of Intelligence", arXiv:1911.01547, 2019: "solely measuring skill at any given task falls short of measuring intelligence, because skill is heavily modulated by prior knowledge and experience: unlimited priors or unlimited training data allow experimenters to 'buy' arbitrary levels of skills for a system, in a way that masks the system's own generalization power." Predicts that score can be bought without capability. Corroborates Fig 11 :40 and C1. Overlap: low.
- **ML / interpretability.** Lapuschkin et al., "Unmasking Clever Hans predictors and assessing what machines really learn", Nature Communications 10, 1096, 2019: "we need to go beyond predictive performance measures, such as the test set error, towards understanding the AI system" and "whether the learned strategy is valid and generalizable or whether the model has based its decision on a spurious correlation in the training data". The paper's tag-cutting intervention is the source-discriminating test in miniature. Corroborates C3 and F9. Overlap: low.
- **Framework author / source discrimination.** Isaiah N, "The Hard Problem is About Source", adventuresinml, 2026: "If several paths create the same behavior, then behavior is a projection with lossy information (it cannot define consciousness in full)." Predicts that accuracy cannot comment on its source. Corroborates the metamerism reading of C1. Overlap: high (framework author).
- **Framework author / shape and split.** Isaiah N, "The Library of Babel: Locating The Book of Books", adventuresinml, 2026: "you can cut your search short if the set of books returned is small and descriptive enough that it provides a general shape of the answer that satisfies your question." The agent, or the tool, or the frame, or the theory, cannot validate itself." Predicts that this derivation cannot settle its own claim. Corroborates the simulated status. Overlap: high.

## VERDICT (from the figures)

The shape survives; the instance stays open. In-distribution accuracy is frame-internal and cannot witness a mechanism; it is a budget filter. The discriminating measurement is the per-slot residual under a changed habitat, taken by a probe that shares no evidence pool with training. Correlation versus mechanism is ingredients versus bond, a Fig 12 isomer difference that accuracy cannot see and intervention can. A benchmark is a substituted habitat whose solve proves wiring, not capability. The question's either/or phrasing is a union of three conditions and must be split, with separate verdicts never averaged. The figures do not say that models cannot reveal mechanisms, and they do not certify any model class. This is `general` on the shape, `specified` on each domain, `grounded` on none. Cost if wrong: if we credit a mechanism when it is a shortcut, we deploy a model that fails under shift; the DDT contact cascade in Fig 11 :52-54 shows a second-order effect three steps out. If we refuse a genuine mechanism, we discard a working tool. Fig 9 :40-41 "a wrong cut removes the answer and speeds up so keep every cut ranked and reversible". Undoable: yes. No primitive is withdrawn; the criterion, the split and the refuters are all detachable from the derivation, and `07_the_tether_answer.md` already states the corrected form. Reversed by: a ground reading, not agreement. An intervention with near-zero per-slot residual from a disjoint probe refutes the "mainly correlations" side for that model; same ingredients with identical intervention behaviour refutes the mechanism side; a measured zero round trip gap clears the benchmark objection; a derivation of a new atom that changes which arrangement pays moves the frame's closure (Fig 6 :23). A figure re-census that makes accuracy sufficient for mechanism would also reverse the verdict, but no such line exists. Until one of these fires, the verdict stands as candidate.
