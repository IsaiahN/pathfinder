## Distribution: offer, do not push

- **Fig 4 :22-25**: *"Only methods cross up. Only head starts come down. A recording carried upward looks like knowledge and is a description of one occasion."* Lead with the criterion and the refuter; offer the links only after.
- **Fig 10 :42**: *"A verifier that can reconstruct the claim cannot verify it."* A recipient who has absorbed the full framework before giving a verdict has joined the frame being checked.
- **Fig 9 :27-28**: *"describe it before you go looking, or any frame you pick will seem to fit"*. Ask what they would expect to see if the criterion were true before showing the essay.
- **Fig 2 :35**: *"Alignment is triangulation, not negotiation. A mirror cannot correct you."* Never ask for agreement or trust.
- **Seat Map :163**: *"High correlation is disqualifying for a verdict and efficient for a convention."* Do not select recipients for likely assent.

**Recommended order in a reply:** (1) the shape claim and its refuter; (2) ask what they would count as a failure; (3) record their verdict before showing any theory; (4) only then the links, with the note that the figures, not the summary, are the authority.

## The two-stage offer (and the optional third)

1. **Stage 1.** Validate the criterion against a ground the recipient knows. No framework shown.
2. **Stage 2, opt-in.** If they are curious, offer the three source links. Their stage-1 verdict was already recorded, so later agreement is never pooled as corroboration (Fig 2 :40).
3. **Stage 3, opt-in.** Offer the formula as a reusable generator. Each user writes their own refuter and states their own mode, because a loop *"cannot check the direction of its own question"* (`THE_FORMULA.md`, Direction).

**Hard precondition:** stages 1 to 3 work only if the stage-1 criterion survives. As written in `01_figure_grounded_audit.md`, the structural shape does survive re-census, but the two halves of the question were split (F8) and the blanket readings were retracted (§6). Send the split, mode-labelled version, not the original either/or phrasing.
