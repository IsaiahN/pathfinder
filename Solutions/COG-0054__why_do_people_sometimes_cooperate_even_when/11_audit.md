## Independent domain census

I redid the census from the residual alone, in WATSON order.

- **R (arity / symmetry / scale).** The residual is the excess of cooperation over what a direct-reward frame predicts. Arity: at least two, because cooperation is a relation; the slot cannot be one actor. Symmetry: symmetric payoff form, directed and residual-relative valence. Scale: the act is first order; anything that can carry a delayed return is second order or beyond. So R is a union compound across at least two conditions, not one hard question (Fig 9 :54-58).
- **Domain 1, the failing frame.** Actors: one isolated actor, immediate payoff. Currency: immediate material reward or sanction. Anchor: the immediate payoff. This is a frame whose closure fails on R.
- **Domain 2, habitat and relations.** Actors: the actor plus everything in contact (kin, partners, community, institutions, conditions, relations). Currency: contact and cascade, valence relative to R. Anchor: the ground. Question type: partition boundary between isolated actor and embedded actor.
- **Domain 3, iterated bond.** Actors: the same actors across encounters, plus reputation observers. Currency: memory, trust, the part that survives use. Anchor: loop closure. Question type: cycle.
- **Domain 4, selection between rooms.** Actors: groups. Currency: variation inside, selection between. Anchor: ground votes between rooms. Question type: union with Domain 3.
- **Domain 5, transmission.** Actors: agent and population across the membrane. Currency: methods up, head starts down. Anchor: ground. Question type: union with Domain 2.

Order by dependency: enumerate habitat, then assign valence, then close the bond, then price selection between rooms, then transmit methods. Refuse before mint (Formula step 2); describe the residual before naming a frame (Fig 9 :27-28).
