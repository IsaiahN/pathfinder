## QUESTION

Decide, in effect terms, what the Tether says about cooperation that occurs with no expected direct reward or punishment. Option A: the direct-reward frame is complete, so the cooperation must be a hidden immediate payoff or a mismeasured sanction. Option B: the direct-reward frame scopes the actor away from its habitat, and the return arrives in a currency or at a scale the frame cannot carry. What each leads to: A adds an unobserved payoff to save the frame and makes the question unfalsifiable; B gives a checkable shape, six refuters written in advance, and a discriminating instrument to build.

## DOMAIN CENSUS

R by arity, symmetry and scale, then one line per domain with actors, currency, anchor; partition or union; order by dependency.

- **R (the residual).** Observed cooperation in the no-incentive slot minus the direct frame's predicted cooperation. Arity: at least dyadic, so the slot is a relation, not an actor. Symmetry: the payoff form is symmetric between roles, but the valence is directed and flips with the residual. Scale: the act is first order; the mechanisms that can carry the return are second and third order. Fig 1 :19 "The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated."
- **Domain 1, direct-incentive decision frame (the failing frame).** Actors: one isolated actor and a payoff matrix. Currency: immediate material reward or punishment. Anchor: the immediate payoff. This is a frame whose closure fails on R, not a domain that governs the answer.
- **Domain 2, habitat and relations.** Actors: the actor plus kin, partners, community, institutions, conditions and relations. Currency: contact and cascade, with valence relative to R. Anchor: the ground (what survives). Question type: partition boundary between actor-in-isolation and actor-in-habitat.
- **Domain 3, iterated bond and memory.** Actors: the same actors across repeated encounters, plus observers of reputation. Currency: memory, trust, the surviving catalyst. Anchor: whether the loop closes. Question type: cycle rather than one-shot payoff.
- **Domain 4, selection between rooms.** Actors: groups of actors. Currency: variation inside, selection between. Anchor: the ground votes via selection between rooms. Question type: union with Domain 3, since both can hold at once.
- **Domain 5, social learning and norm transmission.** Actors: one agent and the population across the membrane. Currency: methods carried up, head starts sent down. Anchor: the ground. Question type: union with Domain 2, since norms are relations transmitted.
- **Order by dependency (Fig 3).** Enumerate the habitat before assigning valence; close the bond before pricing selection between rooms; transmit methods only after settlement, never recordings. Describe the residual before choosing a frame (Fig 9 :27-28).
- **Union, not partition.** The act is usually in two or three conditions at once, so the compound has its own loop and the verdicts stay separate (Fig 9 :54-58).

Strain test applied: Fig 5 :46 governs term minting, not action selection; it was removed from the derivation by re-census rather than smoothed over (`01` §5). Fig 11 :36 governs the unmaintained mode, not the proximate motive; de Waal (2007) confirms the same distinction for primates.

## VERDICT (from the figures)

The shape survives; the direct-reward frame does not. A no-direct-reward cooperative act contacts something beyond the immediate payoff, through relations in a habitat, an iterated bond whose output re-enters, selection between rooms, and norms transmitted as methods. Valence is relative to the residual, so cooperation is a relation rather than a kind of person. The return is a contact cascade whose effect is second or third order. The figures do not name which mechanism fills a given case; mechanism names are provenanced imports from the world literature. Five domain verdicts are kept separate and never averaged. This is a `specified`, ungrounded, candidate result.

- **cost if wrong:** bounded and locatable. The packet states its mode, refuters and provenance before the conclusion, so an error is findable rather than invisible. The real cost is spending the independence of outside readers by sending a finished mechanism list instead of a shape with a refuter, which Fig 2 :42 prices: "Buy independence in proportion to the cost of being wrong." That cost is avoidable by leading with the shape and the refuter.
- **undoable:** yes. The mechanism names are detachable from the derivation; nothing in the six-term substrate has to be withdrawn. A sent claim can be superseded by a corrected packet. The five domains can be re-censused independently.
- **reversed by:** a ground reading, not agreement. A source-discriminating instrument that identifies the currency of the return for a specific cooperative act would fill the slot; the isolation test (cooperation with all habitat contact removed), the hidden-payoff test (a complete immediate payoff in every case), the cascade test (strictly first-order consequence) or the loop test (no re-entry of output) would break the shape claim. A figure re-census that admits a single mechanism name derived from the six-term set would also change it. Until one of these fires, the verdict stands as `candidate`.
