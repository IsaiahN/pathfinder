## Wider literature that bears on the question

- Nowak (2006) is the single best map of the mechanism space. It is the source for the names that the figures refuse to supply. Its group-selection section states that "selection on the lower level (within groups) favors defectors, while selection on the higher level (between groups) favors cooperators", which is the world-language counterpart of Fig 7 :35.
- Ostrom (2009) is the institutional case: cooperation sustained by communication, a well-defined "we", rules and monitoring, not by an external sword. This is D1 and D4.
- Fehr, Fischbacher and Gächter (2002) and Tomasello (2009) are the two strongest cases against the "hidden immediate reward" reading of R: no material return is needed, and material rewards can undermine helping. This is what forced the retraction in `01` section 8.
- de Waal (2007) separates the ultimate reason from the current decision, which is the same re-census that dissolved the Fig 11 :36 "selection versus proximate motive" strain.
- Axelrod (2000) and the Dutra (2021) entry carry the "shadow of the future" into the iterated-bond domain, the counterpart of Fig 12 :121.

**Overlap warning.** These sources are not all independent witnesses to one claim. Nowak cites Axelrod and Fehr; de Waal cites Trivers and Tomasello; Ostrom cites game theory. Agreement among them is partially a shared evidence pool. The ledger records overlap per row. The source that carries the most independent weight for "no immediate reward needed" is the convergence of Fehr (experimental economics), de Waal (animal behaviour) and Tomasello (developmental psychology), three different methods and species.
