**Subject:** Check one claim about cooperation before you decide whether the framework is worth reading

> Hi [name],
>
> I'm working on a figure-grounded derivation of why people cooperate when no direct reward or punishment is expected. I have one specific output I'd like you to check against what you already know. I am not asking you to read the framework first, and I am not asking you to agree with it. If the output does not survive, there is nothing else to look at.
>
> **The output (`specified`, not `grounded`):** the puzzle is usually framed as one actor deciding whether to cooperate with a higher immediate payoff available for defecting. The derivation says that frame is the problem, not the actor. It strips the actor of its habitat. Under the framework's substrate rule, capability is a property of the agent and the habitat together, never of the agent alone. So "no direct reward" does not mean "no return". It means the frame measured only first-order material contact and cannot carry the currencies in which the return actually arrives: repeated encounters, reputation, group-level differences, and norms learned as methods.
>
> The figures also say that an actor's valence is relative to the residual, not a property it carries. The same act is beneficial to one residual and harmful to another. So cooperation is a relation, not a kind of person. The derivation keeps five domains separate and does not average them into one story.
>
> **What I am not claiming:** that any single mechanism (kin selection, reciprocity, reputation, group selection, strong reciprocity, internalized norms) is *derived* from the figures. The figures return a shape, not a name. Those mechanism names are imports from your field, provenanced as such, and carry no weight until the source and the shadow test are shown. I am also not claiming this is a measurement, or that it settles the empirical question.
>
> **What would refute the shape claim:**
> 1. a cooperative act where the actor is genuinely isolated from all habitat contact, no kin, no repetition, no reputation channel, no group, no norm, and still cooperates systematically;
> 2. cooperation shown to be a fixed trait of the actor that does not flip with the residual or the relation;
> 3. a complete immediate material payoff or sanction found in every case, so the direct-reward frame predicts the cooperation and the residual goes to zero;
> 4. the selective consequence shown to be strictly first order, with no second or third order effect.
>
> I wrote these down before running the derivation. If any holds, I will narrow or withdraw the claim. A one-line "this fails because..." is more useful to me than agreement. If you'd rather not engage, no reply is needed.
>
> **If it survives, and only then:** the figures and the loop are below. The figures, not my summary, are the authority.
