# Expert verification packet: cooperation without direct reward (COG-0054)

**What this is:** a claim in a form a cooperation researcher can check. Mode, derivation, and refuters are stated before the conclusion, so a reader knows what to look for and what would settle it.

**Mode: `specified` on the mechanism-shape and on each domain verdict, `general` on the structural invariant. Not `grounded`.** No measurement is claimed to settle the "why" in this derivation.

**Status:** simulated derivation checked for internal consistency against the figures, not a ground reading. It cannot settle the claim it reports on.

## 1. The claim, in one paragraph

The no-direct-reward cooperative act is not explained by a hidden immediate payoff. It is explained by the actor being embedded in a habitat, where the return arrives through a relation in a currency the direct-reward frame cannot carry. Five domains carry it, kept separate: relations in a habitat with valence relative to the residual; an iterated bond whose output re-enters as input; selection between groups; norms transmitted as methods rather than recordings; and the contact cascade that makes first-order accounting miss the effect. The figures return that shape, not the name of a mechanism. Kin selection, reciprocity, reputation, group selection, strong reciprocity and internalized norms are provenanced imports from the literature, not figure derivations. Six refuters were written before the run.

## 2. Three checkable sub-claims

### C1 — framework-internal; checkable by reading Figures 11 and 12

- **Statement:** an isolated actor with one immediate payoff is not an adequate frame for a cooperative act. Capability belongs to the agent and habitat together, and an act's valence is relative to the residual.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 11 :39 "capability is a property of agent-and-habitat, never of the agent alone." Fig 11 :49 "An actor's valence is relative to the residual, not a property it carries." Fig 11 :50 "The same actor is a benefactor to one residual and a malfactor to another, so mutualism and parasitism are relations rather than kinds."
- **Refuter:** find a systematically cooperative act with the actor isolated from all habitat contact (no kin, no repetition, no reputation channel, no group, no norm, no internalized rule), and still cooperating. Or show cooperation is a fixed actor-trait that does not flip with the residual.

### C2 — framework-internal; checkable by reading Figures 12, 7 and 4

- **Statement:** the return need not be immediate because a repeated encounter closes a cycle and a reputation or trust is a catalyst that survives its use. Group-level selection can carry cooperation that within-group selection removes. Norms generalize only as methods, not recordings.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 12 :119 "catalyst. A part that survives its own use." Fig 12 :121 "cycle. The output re-enters as input." Fig 7 :35 "A room's agreement is variation, not a verdict. Selection between rooms is where the ground votes." Fig 4 :22 "Only methods cross up. Only head starts come down."
- **What to check:** are those four figure lines being applied to the right quantities (memory for the cycle, surviving reputation for the catalyst, group differential for the room, reapplicable norm for the method)? A binding to the wrong quantity is a strain, and the claim should be re-censused, not defended.
- **Refuter:** show a repeated setting where the output never re-enters and cooperation is unchanged; or show the group-level differential absent; or show norm transmission is verbatim replay that generalizes to no new situation yet cooperation holds.

### C3 — world-facing; checkable against the empirical literature

- **Statement:** the empirical literature documents cooperation with no immediate material reward across at least three independent methods, and the mechanisms are multiple rather than one. The figures cannot discriminate among them.
- **What to check:** Nowak (2006, Science) lists five mechanisms and states that "natural selection implies competition and therefore opposes cooperation unless a specific mechanism is at work." Fehr, Fischbacher and Gächter (2002, Human Nature) define strong reciprocity as acting "even if this is costly and provides neither present nor future material rewards for the reciprocator." de Waal (2007, PLoS Biology) writes "Clearly, return-benefits matter only insofar as they are knowable to the actors." Tomasello (2009, MIT Press) reports that material rewards "seemed to have actually undermined their natural, intrinsic motivation" in young children.
- **Refuter:** show that a single hidden immediate material payoff accounts for all four bodies of evidence, so the residual is zero; or show that the sources are one evidence pool rather than three methods (experimental economics, animal behaviour, developmental psychology). If they are one pool, their agreement is one witness, not four.

## 3. What is explicitly not claimed

- Not a mechanism name derived from the figures. The canonical rule is a shape, not a name. The mechanism names are imports with provenance.
- Not a hidden immediate material payoff. The empirical sources above speak against that reading of R.
- Not that selection explains the proximate motive. Empathy and internalized norms are a different domain from the ultimate account.
- Not `grounded`. Nothing here is asserted as settled by experiment.
- Not that agreement among cooperators, or among researchers, is evidence. Fig 2 :35 "Alignment is triangulation, not negotiation. A mirror cannot correct you."
- Not an isomer claim. The named mechanisms do not share an atom set; they are a functional group. See `06_isomers_and_order.md`.

## 5. What would settle it

A source-discriminating instrument, not a further argument. From *The Hard Problem is About Source*: an instrument that reads green "cannot comment on the source", and "If several paths create the same behavior, then behavior is a projection with lossy information."

1. An instrument that discriminates the currency of the return for a specific no-incentive act.
2. The isolation test: cooperation persists with all habitat contact removed.
3. The hidden-payoff test: a complete immediate payoff found in every case.
4. The cascade test: the consequence shown strictly first order.
5. The loop test: no re-entry of output, cooperation unchanged.
