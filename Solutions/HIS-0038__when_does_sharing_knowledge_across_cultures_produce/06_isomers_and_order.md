# Are exchange and extraction isomers? Bonds, order, and analogues

**Mode: `specified`.** This is a simulated derivation checked against the figures, not a ground reading (see `05_what_shape_means.md` §7).

## RESIDUAL

Two transfers over the same atoms receive different verdicts. Are they different substances in Fig 12's sense, or the same substance described twice? And in what order must the checks run?

## DOMAIN CENSUS

Atoms held: the two frames, the item moved, the method or recording that carries it, the lineage record, the receiving ground, and the habitat. Bonds held: the seven (`+ ∥ ≡ → ⇒ − ⋛`) plus negation. No new primitive is available without import.

## DERIVATION

### Q1 - Are E and X isomers?

Fig 12 :117 *"Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."* Fig 12 :94 *"The arrangement is the substance."*

Take the shared atom set `{frame A, frame B, item, lineage, receiving ground, habitat}`. The two verdicts bond them differently:

| verdict | bonding | figure |
|---|---|---|
| **E, exchange** | the source frame `⇒` a new term in B that did not exist before; `⇒` is production, *"A produces B; B did not exist before"* (Fig 12 :77) | Fig 12 :77; Fig 6 :14 |
| **X, extraction** | the item arrives by `→` sequence and is replayed; *"A then B; B needs A to have happened"* (Fig 12 :72), or the two vocabularies are merely conjoined by `+`, which is the blend collapse *"vocabulary, never an atom"* (Fig 8 :5) | Fig 12 :72; Fig 8 :5 |
| **false convergence** | two frames are declared `≡`, *"two names, one referent"* (Fig 12 :67), hiding the fact that one adopted and the other derived | Fig 12 :67; Fig 8 :51 |
| **withdrawal** | the item is removed by `−`, *"A with B removed or absent"* (Fig 12 :82), which the figures say is substitution of habitat, not removal (Fig 11 :66) | Fig 12 :82; Fig 11 :66 |
| **the check** | `⋛` compares the item against the receiving ground, *"a threshold relation between two quantities"* (Fig 12 :87) | Fig 12 :87 |

Over this full shared set the two verdicts are **isomers**: same atoms, different bond, different substance. As they are usually reported, however, they share only "an item crossed between cultures", so the weaker claim is that they are members of the same **functional group** (Fig 12 :124 *"the group says what kind; the qualifier says which member"*), namely cross-frame transfer. Both readings are kept; the stronger one is the checkable structural claim.

**Falsifier, written before the run:** if both verdicts can be written with the same bond over the same atoms and still differ at the ground, the isomer claim fails and the two verdicts are the same substance with a different label. If `≡` can be used to hide a one-way import without loss of information, Fig 8 :51 is wrong.

### Q2 - What is the order of the checks?

Fig 3 :20 *"The chain is ordered by dependency, not by importance."* The order is not a matter of preference; a step whose input never arrives cannot be diagnosed (Fig 3 :22 to :23).

1. **Describe the residual in effect terms** before choosing a frame. Fig 9 :27 to :28 *"describe it before you go looking, or any frame you pick will seem to fit."*
2. **Enumerate the habitat** (Fig 11 :43 *"The habitat is enumerated, never composed."*). Contact cascades; first-order enumeration misses third-order effects (Fig 11 :52 to :53).
3. **Refuse before mint** (`THE_FORMULA.md` step 2). If the receiving frame holds a term that persists and does not trend down, a refusal is owed before any new term is imported.
4. **Check the record** (Fig 8 :51). Convergent derivation and adopted import look identical in the contents; only the record separates them.
5. **Settle at the ground** (`THE_FORMULA.md` step 5 *"the ground settles it. Verified only by what pays. A gate is not the ground."*). A signed agreement is a gate, not a ground.
6. **Measure the round trip** (Fig 4 :30 *"So take the round trip and measure the gap. Without it you are navigating by dead reckoning: confident about a position nobody checked."*). A method carried from A to B and back will not match what was sent; the gap is the measurement of what the coarser description could not hold.

Reversing steps 4 and 5 is the common failure: declaring a transfer legitimate because the process was clean, then looking for a ground to confirm it. Fig 10 :65 forbids that order: *"Provenance shows a ruling was reached legitimately. It never shows it was right."*

**Falsifier:** show a case where settlement at the ground is established before the lineage is described and the verdict is unchanged across a population of such cases. Then the dependency order is a preference, not a constraint.

### Q3 - What is the analogue, and how far does it carry?

Three low-overlap analogues, each with its own failure:

1. **Frame versus photograph** (*How Intelligence Actually Works*, 2026-09-16): *"One was taken; one was extracted. And nothing in the image records which."* This is the exact shape of F1. Limitation: a photograph is a single artifact; a cultural practice is a lineage with many artifacts, so traces may exist where the photograph has none. The essay itself gives the diamond case where isotope ratios recover provenance.
2. **War of the Ghosts** (Bartlett, reported in *Decoding Tacit Knowledge*): a story passed person to person loses what does not fit and becomes more familiar, until each report mirrors its own culture. This is Fig 7 :13 *"agreement compounds, evidence does not"* in human memory. Limitation: it is within-culture drift, not a cross-frame import, so it corroborates the mirror-chain half and is silent on the import half.
3. **Diamonds and green light** (*How Intelligence Actually Works*): some provenance is recoverable with a better instrument, some is not. Limitation: it governs discrimination of source, not the normative question of benefit. It cannot decide fairness; only whether a reading can be sourced.

None of the three settles a real case. They corroborate the shape that E and X are indistinguishable in content and separable only by lineage plus an instrument.

### Q4 - What does the two-currency pricing change?

Fig 12 :125 *"A composition costs in two currencies, and they do not add."* Fig 12 :126 *"Bits price the description. Actions price finding out whether it holds."*

A transfer that has full consent documentation, benefit-sharing language and an ethics certificate has paid in bits. A transfer whose receiving frame has actually tested the imported term against its own ground has paid in actions. The first does not predict the second. The field sources reflect this split: the Biopiracy and Traditional knowledge articles are largely about authorization and benefit (bits), while the Traditional ecological knowledge article records concerns about use without consent and the Ecological Society literature on coproduction is closer to the settlement question (actions).

**Falsifier:** produce a transfer with high bits and zero actions whose term nonetheless settles at the receiving ground across repeated trials. Then the action currency is not necessary and F8 falls.

## CONCLUSION

Exchange and extraction are isomers over a shared atom set: the atoms are the same, the bonds differ, and the difference is decisive in behaviour while invisible in a description that names only the item that crossed. The checks must be ordered (describe, enumerate, refuse, check the record, settle, measure the round trip), because Fig 3 makes dependency rather than importance the ordering principle. The analogues corroborate the provenance half strongly and the settlement half weakly; the action currency remains unmeasured.
