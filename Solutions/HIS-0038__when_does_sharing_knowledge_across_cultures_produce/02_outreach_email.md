**Subject:** Check one discriminator about knowledge sharing before you decide whether the framework is worth reading

> Hi [name],
>
> I have one specific output I would like you to check against what you already know. I am not asking you to read the theory first, and I am not asking you to agree with it. If the output does not survive, there is nothing else to look at.
>
> **The output (`specified`, not `grounded`).** A transfer of knowledge between two cultural frames can end in two states that look identical in their contents. In one, the receiving frame's closure expands, a new term settles against its ground, and the source frame's reach is undiminished. In the other, agreement is produced and an item crosses, but the receiving wall is unmoved, the lineage of the item is unrecorded, or the item is installed as something that cannot be refused there. The figures say convergent derivation and adopted import *"look identical in the contents. Only the record of where each came from separates them."* So the discriminator is the record plus the ground, not the content and not the stated intentions of the partners.
>
> **One consequence that surprised me.** This is not a property of people or institutions. The same actor is a benefactor to one residual and a malfactor to another, so mutualism and parasitism are relations, not kinds. A verdict of "the whole exchange was extraction" is therefore the wrong shape: the residual is indexed per object slot, not aggregated. What I am offering is a test you could apply to one practice in one partnership.
>
> **What I am not claiming:** that any historical case has been decided by this, that benefit-sharing language or consent documents settle anything, or that withdrawal repairs an extraction. Benefit sharing is a filter for the budget, never a witness for the verdict. Withdrawal substitutes one habitat for another; it does not restore the old one. I am also not claiming that prestige or institutional reputation tells us anything about whether the transferred item settled.
>
> **What would refute the discriminator:**
> 1. a transfer where the receiving closure expands and the item settles at the receiving ground, with no recorded lineage and with both frames drawing on one evidence pool;
> 2. a recording, replayed without its history, that generates a new term settling at the receiving ground;
> 3. an actor that is extractive relative to every residual in every habitat;
> 4. a single measure that predicts both documentation quality and settlement, with both axes measured.
>
> I wrote these down before sending this. If any one holds, I will narrow or withdraw the claim. A one-line "this fails because..." is more useful to me than agreement. If you would rather not engage, no reply is needed.
