## 1. A shape is a constraint, not a filler

The canonical README states the limit: the framework returns *"what a missing thing would have to look like (a shape, not a name)."* Fig 9 :32 *"you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself."* Fig 13 :134 to :135: *"Unreachable is a verdict here, not a silence, It cannot say which primitive is missing, because naming it would mean already holding it."*

From *The Library of Babel*: *"you can cut your search short if the set of books returned is small and descriptive enough that it provides a general shape of the answer that satisfies your question."*

So a **shape** is the narrowed form of an answer you can state before you can name the particular case. It constrains; it does not fill. For this question the shape is a discriminator: a relation among methods, lineages, ground and habitat that returns E or X for one slot of one transfer. It is not a list of historical cultures that were "really" extractive.

## 2. How the shape is obtained

- *Library of Babel*: use the vocabulary you already have that is *closest* to the thing, look for new terms that overlap or correlate with it, recurse. *"With each new term gained, you narrow the space by definition and deepen your vocabulary."*
- Fig 6 :30 *"The description travels. Fails by being sharpened into terms only we can read. Cast it in effect terms."* Fig 9 :30 to :31 *"your words are borrowed too, so describe the effect"* / *"rather than the cause."*
- *Decoding Tacit Knowledge*: *"When contact isn't possible, the best you can send is a plain description of what you'd expect to see, because anyone can check that against the world."*
- *Library of Babel* on stopping: *"You will know that you are done because the finish line will also be the starting place, as there is nowhere else to search and nothing else that can describe the answer better."*

Applied here: instead of naming cultures or institutions, describe the effect (closure expands and settles, or it does not; lineage recorded, or not; ground reachable, or repointed). Those effects can be checked by anyone.

## 3. Provenance loss is exactly the word "extraction" in this framework

This is the single most directly relevant passage found, and it uses the question's own word. *How Intelligence Actually Works*, the video-frame versus photograph case:

> *"The frame and the photograph are indistinguishable as artifacts, and they carry different warrants. One was taken; one was extracted. And nothing in the image records which."*

And the general statement:

> *"that's provenance loss, and it's the thing the framework is built around: often, very little about a composed term tells you, from looking at it alone, which route produced it."*

The same essay gives the two cases and refuses to collapse them: lab-grown and mined diamonds are distinguishable once isotope ratios become readable (a trace existed and an instrument found it), whereas the colour green carries no trace of whether it was RGB or CMYK (no depth helps). So "extraction" in this framework is a **provenance failure**, and the remedy is either the recorded chain or a discriminator instrument, never an argument from the artifact. This is the essay-level corroboration of F1 in `01_figure_grounded_audit.md`.

The same essay also gives the limit: *"The recursion converges toward indiscernibility, and indiscernibility is not identity."* A transfer that looks like exchange at every available resolution is not thereby exchange.

## 4. What licenses a name

Only two things, both outside the shape itself:

1. **The ground settles it.** `THE_FORMULA.md` step 5: *"the ground settles it. Verified only by what pays. A gate is not the ground."* Fig 10 :65 *"Provenance shows a ruling was reached legitimately. It never shows it was right: the ground does that"*.
2. **A provenanced import from a frame whose closure differs** (Fig 6 :23, Fig 8 :51), with a source gate and a shadow test.

*The Hard Problem is About Source* gives the instrument requirement: *"If several paths create the same behavior, then behavior is a projection with lossy information."* The colour case was resolved *"not by argument, but by the arrival of a better instrument."* Applied here: a signature of extraction (readmission of a practice, shifting benefit, loss of a local classification) is a reading. Several histories could produce it. What is owed is an instrument that discriminates source, not a verdict from the reading.

*They're Scanning Books* sharpens the cross-cultural point: *"Change the sensors, and not just the data changes, the shape of the compression changes: the categories, the primitives, what counts as similar to what."* And: *"A minimum viable set of six terms is a human's careful guess at reality's compression axes; another sensorium's minimum set need not be those six."* So the possibility that another culture holds a different primitive cannot be dismissed as a difference of vocabulary. And: *"Declaring the primitives does not make them neutral. It makes the choice visible."*

## 5. Two-way contact, and the cost of too much of it

*Decoding Tacit Knowledge* supplies the mechanism and the warning in one passage:

> *"Long, close contact pulls the frames together, so the process that makes communication easy slowly uses up that extra reach."*

This is Fig 8 :47 and Fig 8 :48 in plain language. A long, comfortable partnership can lose the very difference that made the exchange valuable. The same essay says the loop only closes if the learner acts, and that a false context, a false pretext or a false subtext is how deception works by substitution. That is the F7 mechanism.

*How a Society of Agents Would Work* sets the rule and its cost plainly:

> *"A group is only worth having while its members stay different."*

> *"The group shares methods, never recordings."*

And the honest price: *"Sharing is what makes a collective work, and it is also its biggest cost."* It also refuses the easy fix of more participants: ten copies watching the same thing are one opinion said ten times. That is F3 at the group scale.

## 6. "Shape, not a name" applied correctly here (correction to my own §1)

I initially read the README rule as forbidding the naming of the discriminator itself. That was an overreach, and I record the correction rather than erasing it.

- The README's phrase is *"what a **missing** thing would have to look like (a shape, not a name)."* The subject is a primitive not held in Γ.
- The discriminator named in this folder is built entirely from atoms the framework already holds: methods, recordings, imports, closure, ground, habitat, provenance. None is a seventh primitive. So naming it is a **binding** from this question onto held atoms, not the invention of a new member.
- What remains forbidden is naming a particular historical case as *the* answer, or claiming the discriminator settles real cases. That would be a name where this derivation returns a shape.

Both statements are kept: the prohibition on naming missing primitives, and the permission to bind held atoms into a checkable relation. The exemplar records the same correction in its own `05` §5b.

## 7. Prestige and simulation, stated on every substantive claim

*The Recursive Self-Improvement Trap*: *"Prestige can fund an action. It can give an action a budget to be tried. But it cannot settle an action. Only the ground can settle it."* In this question, institutional reputation, publication venue, the size of an ethics statement and the standing of a partner are budget inputs, never verdict witnesses.

Same essay, on this folder's status: LLMs *"can quickly simulate an approximate answer/output with the formula"*, but *"the truly testable output would need to be derived from the real architecture or working the formula yourself."* *Arrival of the Fittest* is blunter: simulated outputs *"could never be verified if the solutions presented were not contrived to fit the formula."*

Therefore the audit in `01` is a simulated derivation checked for internal consistency against the figures. It is not a test of any real exchange and cannot settle the question it reports on. That limitation is carried into `07`, `08`, `09` and `11`.

## 8. Answer to the question "what shape does an answer have here?"

An answer has the shape of a **relation with three checks and one stop**:

1. a lineage check (was the route recorded, or is the artifact indistinguishable from one arrived at independently),
2. a reach check (did the receiving closure expand and settle against its own ground),
3. a contact check (did the frames remain different, and did the habitat change in ways that can be enumerated),
4. a stop at the limit of the instruments, with the record kept so a later reader can locate the error.

It does not have the shape of a rule about which cultures share well, nor a rule about which institutions are extractive. Both would be names where the framework returns a shape, and Fig 11 :51 says no standing list of good actors survives a change of residual.
