# Expert verification packet: exchange versus extraction in cross-cultural knowledge sharing

**What this is:** a claim in a form a researcher in cross-cultural knowledge exchange can check. Mode, derivation and refuters are stated before the conclusion, so a reader knows what to look for and what would settle it.

**Mode: `specified`, not `grounded`.** No real case is claimed to be decided. This is a discriminator and a route, not a result. Simulated derivation checked for internal consistency against the figures (`05_what_shape_means.md` §7).

## 1. The claim, in one paragraph

Two transfers of knowledge between cultural frames can look identical in their contents and still differ in verdict. The difference is carried by the lineage and by the receiving frame's ground, not by the item transferred. Exchange carries a method that can be reapplied, occurs between frames whose closures differ, and ends with a new term settled in the receiving frame while the source frame's reach is undiminished. Extraction carries a recording of one occasion without its lineage, leans on agreement among frames drawing on one evidence pool, or installs the imported item where the receiving ground used to be so that it cannot be refused. Mutualism and parasitism are relations to a residual rather than kinds of actor, so the verdict attaches to one practice in one partnership, never to a whole culture. Consent documents and benefit sharing are filters for the budget, never witnesses for the verdict. Withdrawal substitutes a habitat rather than restoring the old one.

## 2. Three checkable sub-claims

### C1 - framework-internal; checkable by reading Figures 4, 6 and 8
- **Statement:** an import moves the receiving wall only on contact with a frame whose closure differs, and content alone cannot distinguish a convergent derivation from an adopted import.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 6 :14 *"the only operator that moves the wall"*. Fig 6 :23 *"No frame escapes its own closure. The bound moves only on contact with a frame whose closure differs."* Fig 8 :51 *"Convergent derivation and adopted import look identical in the contents. Only the record of where each came from separates them"*.
- **What to check:** are these lines in the figure text as quoted, and do they govern a transfer event rather than only an internal search?
- **Refuter:** a transfer in which a new term settles at the receiving ground with no recorded lineage and with both frames drawing on one evidence pool. If that can be shown, content is sufficient and the record is not the discriminator.

### C2 - field-facing; checkable against the researcher's own cases
- **Statement:** the same transfer can be described as sharing or as taking depending on lineage, independence and the receiving ground, and the two descriptions are not resolved by the artifact.
- **What to check:** take one case the reader knows well. (a) Can the route by which the practice entered the receiving institution be reconstructed from a record, or is the result indiscernible from one developed locally? (b) Did the receiving institution's own reach expand, measured by something it could not previously do or predict and checked against its own ground, or did documentation and agreement increase while reach did not? (c) Did the two sides draw on separate evidence, or on one report, one review and one donor framework? (d) Did withdrawal, if any, leave the habitat unchanged and the goal reachable?
- **Field corroboration, secondary only:** Wikipedia, Biopiracy, *"the unauthorized appropriation of knowledge and genetic resources of farming and indigenous communities by individuals or institutions seeking exclusive monopoly control through patents or intellectual property."* Wikipedia, Traditional ecological knowledge: TEK *"does not function as a universal set of principles, but as a family of culturally situated rules and practices rooted in specific places and ecological worldviews."* Wikipedia, Cultural learning, citing van Schaik and Burkart: *"Opportunities for social learning allow an individual to acquire many learned skills during development that it could not acquire on its own."* These three are different fields and do not use the framework vocabulary; they corroborate the surface forms, not the verdict.
- **Refuter:** a case in which the receiving institution's reach demonstrably expanded and the term settled at its own ground, with no lineage, one shared evidence pool, and no source-frame loss. The E/X distinction then fails on a real case.

### C3 - framework-internal derivation
- **Statement:** valence is relational, withdrawal is substitution, and the verdict must be per slot; these follow from the habitat figure and the residual figure, not from a moral premise.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 11 :50 *"The same actor is a benefactor to one residual and a malfactor to another, so mutualism and parasitism are relations rather than kinds."* Fig 11 :66 *"Isolation is not removal of the habitat. It is substitution of one habitat for another."* Fig 1 :19 *"The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated."*
- **Refuter:** show an actor extractive relative to every residual in every habitat, or a removal that leaves the receiving habitat unchanged and the ground reachable with no inherited actors. Either breaks C3.

## 3. What is explicitly not claimed

- Not a verdict on any real culture, community, institution, state or historical episode.
- Not that a person or organisation is extractive by kind. Valence is relative to the residual (Fig 11 :50).
- Not that benefit sharing, consent, documentation or an ethics certificate decides anything. They are filters (Fig 9 :60). Provenance is legitimate-but-not-correct (Fig 10 :65).
- Not that agreement, duration of partnership or absence of complaint is evidence (Fig 2 :40, Fig 7 :13).
- Not that withdrawal repairs an extraction (Fig 11 :66).
- Not that the framework names a missing primitive or a real case (Fig 9 :32).
- Not `grounded`. No measurement was taken.

## 5. What would settle it

A source-discriminating instrument, not a further argument. Four tests: a lineage test (can the route be reconstructed, and does a trace exist?), a reach test (did the receiving closure expand and settle against its own ground?), an independence test (separate evidence, checked against a third ground?), and a habitat test (after transfer and withdrawal, is the goal reachable and which actors were inherited?). Full list in `01_figure_grounded_audit.md` §4.

## 6. What an expert can check in twenty minutes

1. Read Fig 6 :14, :23 and Fig 8 :51. Three short passages. Confirm the record is the discriminator, not the content.
2. Read Fig 11 :50 and :66. Confirm that valence is relational and that isolation is substitution.
3. Take one case you know. Apply the four questions in C2. Write down which of E or X it returns, and what would change your answer.
4. Try to break C1 or C3 with a counterexample from your own work. A failure is more useful than an endorsement. Send the refutation.
