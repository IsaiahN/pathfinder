# Structure, order and analogues: constants as parameters, not atoms

**Mode: `specified`.** Simulated derivation checked against the figures (`05_what_shape_means.md` §5), not a ground reading.

## RESIDUAL

Three structural questions are owed before either half of QMGR-0084 can be answered: (Q1) are the constants atoms of the six-term set, or parameters of the models that compose those atoms; (Q2) what is the symmetry that "fixed" is a claim about, and can a changing number be a meaningful observation; (Q3) what is the dependency order of value, invariance and convention, and do the two halves stand as isomers under Fig 12's definition?

## DOMAIN CENSUS

Atoms held: Space, Time, Energy (states), Spectrum, Gradient, Network (structures). Bonds held: the seven, plus unary negation. Fig 13 :115: "The set is closed. The arrangements are not. That gap is why import exists." No new primitive is available without import. Parameters such as alpha, G and Lambda are not in atoms(Γ). ## DERIVATION

### Q1. Atoms or parameters?

- Fig 13 :41: "Each is indivisible: no arrangement of the other five yields it." Fig 13 :116-118: "So a frame can search without end and still not arrive: infinite effort inside a finite reach."
- A parameter is not a member of the generative set. Changing the value of alpha would not add a seventh term to the six; it would change a coefficient in the composing models. That is why a search over arrangements cannot yield the value (F1 in `01`).
- Fig 6 :21: "MINT composes inside the closure and can never add an atom." A varying constant promoted to a dynamical field **would** be an import: a new atom from a physics frame with a different closure. Uzan's later review says exactly this: it would mean replacing the constant with a field whose dynamics introduce new parameters.
- **Falsifier, written before the run:** produce a derivation of any numerical constant from the six terms and seven bonds under a declared code, or derive one of the six from the other five. Both would break the structural distinction. Triangulated (Fig 13 + Fig 6 + Fig 8), single-source for the physics reading (Uzan).

### Q2. Symmetry, and the dimensionlessness constraint

- "Fixed" is not a substance claim; it is an invariance claim under displacement. Fig 13 :62 names "no spatial symmetry for Noether, and no region to make isolated mean anything" and Fig 13 :64 names "no time-translation symmetry, and no finite operations to leave a remainder" as what would be lost without space and time. **Re-census note:** those lines are minimality arguments about deriving the terms, not assertions about the symmetries of our laws (see `01` R2). The physics symmetry link is sourced to Uzan 2011 s2.2.1, where constancy is tied to local position invariance and the universality of free fall.
- Uzan 2011 s2.1.2 gives the crucial constraint: only the variation of dimensionless constants can be measured, and if such a variation is detected it is impossible to determine which dimensional constant is varying. That is not a verbal quibble. It means a statement like "c changed" is underdetermined until a dimensionless ratio is specified.
- Fig 12 :126: "Bits price the description. Actions price finding out whether it holds." A change of units prices bits (a convention); a change in a dimensionless ratio prices an action (a measurement). Summing the two into one "did the constant change" figure is the error this bond names. Fig 10 :49: "Declaring a meaning does not ground it."
- **Falsifier:** exhibit a measurement of a dimensional constant varying while all dimensionless ratios remain fixed and the unit system is held fixed. Under the standard metrological argument this is not possible; if it were demonstrated, the metrology layer of the answer would be wrong.

### Q3. Order and the union

- Fig 9 :54: "And when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again." The two conditions in the question are a union. Fig 9 :57: "a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either".
- Dependency order by Fig 3: (1) fix the convention and name the dimensionless ratio (D4, the objective link); (2) settle the instrument and its ground (D1, perception and vocabulary); (3) test independence between frames (D3, planning under incomplete information); (4) attempt the value derivation last, with the recognition that it needs an import (D2). Work spent on the value question before the ratio and the anchor are named lands above a break and cannot be used below it (Fig 3 :19-21).
- Are the two halves isomers? Fig 12 :117: "isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour." As framed here they share no atoms beyond the generic six; they are not isomers. They are two members of a **functional group**, Fig 12 :123: "functional group. A substructure whose presence fixes the outcome. The group says what kind; the qualifier says which member." The shared substructure is "a dimensionless ratio ranged against a ground by an instrument". The value half and the invariance half differ in which bond closes the structure: the value half needs an import bond across the closure wall (Fig 6, Fig 8); the invariance half needs a comparison bond ⋛ against a threshold set by the instrument's resolution. Fig 12 :126-127 warns that the two currencies (bits and actions) are held apart.
- **Falsifier:** show that the value and invariance questions can be closed by one and the same operator with no import and no instrument. Then the union reading fails and the question is one problem, not two.

### Q4. Analogues worth using, and one to refuse

- The Ives transformation in color constancy (Morimoto 2024) is a **production** analogue: a transformation applied to cone responses produces an illuminant-corrected signal. It is not the same as a constant being produced by a field; it is an operation on readings, and it fails for extreme spectra. Use it as an analogue of instrument compensation, not as evidence for new physics.
- The use of memory colors and of "anchors" in vision is a **comparison** analogue: identity across illuminations is established by cues that work like references. That matches Fig 2's anchor rule at low overlap.
- Refuse the ⋛ analogy with unification. The Operators table defines ⋛ as "a threshold relation between two quantities". Saying "a constant varies" is not a ⋛ between two quantities; it is a claim that a parameter has become a field. Importing the glyph here would add no explanatory atom and would cost clarity.

## CONCLUSION

Constants are parameters, not atoms; the value half is a figure-level gap, the invariance half is an instrument and anchor question, and the SI convention half is a declared meaning. The two substantive halves are not isomers but members of one functional group, and their dependency order runs from convention and instrument to independence and only then to derivation. This framing keeps the ground-visible residue without inventing a seventh term or a new field. The specific claim that a varying alpha has been detected remains unproven and is reported here as a tentative dipole with systematics comparable to the signal (Milakovic 2023; Wilczynska et al. 2015; Bambi 2022).
