# Outreach draft: check the output before you decide about the claim

**Use:** one-to-one, or as a cover letter attached to a submission through the public venues in `03_public_contact_points.csv`. **Not** for bulk send. No trust or agreement is requested at any stage.

**Hard precondition:** the first stage stands on its own. If the shape claim or the refuter does not survive the reader's own check, there is nothing else to send.

---

**Subject:** Would this refuter change how you read a varying-constants claim?

> Hello,
>
> I am writing about one narrow question, not asking you to read a theory. The question is whether the values of the fundamental constants are fixed, and what it would take to show that any of them varies across space or time. I have written the claim and its refuter below. I am not asking you to agree with either.
>
> **The shape claim, stated as a prediction rather than a measurement.** Two questions are bundled in the usual phrasing and should be separated. The value question (why this number for alpha or G) is not answered by anything I hold; the framework I use has a closed generative set and returns a shape, not a name, so I report the value question as a gap. The invariance question is answerable in principle but only against an anchor: a measurement that does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks. Only dimensionless ratios can be tested for variation. A claim of variation based on readings that share an instrument model or a reduction pipeline is closer to one witness with two names than to two independent witnesses. That is the whole output.
>
> **What would refute it, written before this run:**
> 1. A derivation of one of the six generative terms from the other five, or a figure-governed derivation of a numerical value for a parameter. That would close the value gap.
> 2. Two genuinely low-overlap instruments, with separate calibration and separate reduction, returning a residual in a dimensionless ratio that trends with position or epoch and survives a common-cause test.
> 3. A source-discriminating measurement showing that a quantity called constant is a dynamical field with its own evolution law.
> 4. A demonstration that the apparent variation is purely a unit-convention artefact with no dimensionless residual, which would dissolve the question rather than answer it.
>
> If any of those holds, I withdraw the claim as written. A one-line "this fails because..." is more useful to me than agreement. If you would rather not engage, no reply is needed.
>
> **If it survives, and only then:** I can send the source links. The figures, not my summary of them, are the authority, and the published measurements are the ground for the physics half.
>
> **If you want to use the method:** the formula is in the figures link. Run it on a problem you know well and can check against a ground. Write down what would refute your result before you run it, and state the mode of what you get. A reported failure is more useful to me than a reported success.
