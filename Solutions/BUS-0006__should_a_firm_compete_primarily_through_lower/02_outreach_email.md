**Use:** one-to-one, or as a cover note attached to a submission through a public venue in `03_public_contact_points.csv`. **Not** for bulk send.

**Hard precondition:** do not send until the union reading in `01_figure_grounded_audit.md` F1 and F8 is understood by the sender. If the claim is presented as a single winner for all firms, the two-stage offer collapses on contact.

**Subject:** One checkable claim about "cost vs differentiation vs changing the game"

> Hi [name],
>
> I have one specific output I would like you to check against what you already know about competitive strategy. I am not asking you to read the framework first, and I am not asking you to agree with it. If the output does not survive, there is nothing else to look at.
>
> **The residual (`general` shape, `specified` for any one firm, not `grounded`):** a firm is not getting a durable advantage. Three levers are offered, lower cost, differentiation, and changing the rules of competition. The question asks which should be "primary." I think that question bundles two different kinds of move. Cost and differentiation compose inside the accepted game. Changing the rules changes the game itself. Aggregating them into one ranked choice throws away the per-customer signal. The claim is therefore about the **shape** of a correct answer, not about a winner.
>
> **The shape claim, stated so you can refuse it:**
> 1. The three levers are a union, not a partition. Decide per customer slot; treat "primary" as a budget decision made afterwards, not as a verdict.
> 2. Within a game, cost and differentiation can never add a new basis of competition. They can only re-arrange what the game already contains.
> 3. Changing the rules is the only move that expands what a firm can do, and it is real only if an anchor the firm cannot move, the market, pays for it. A rule that decides the outcome has stopped being a rule and has become a verdict the firm authored.
>
> **What I am not claiming:** that one of the three always wins; that the framework names a winner for your firm or any firm; that this is measurement. The framework returns a shape, not a name. Which lever pays, for which customer, in which market, is owed to the ground, not to this derivation.
>
> **What would refute it:**
> 1. A single global ranking of the three levers that predicts customer choice across all slots with no live residual in any slot.
> 2. A firm escaping a saturated game by cost or differentiation alone, with no new basis of competition, and the market paying.
> 3. A lever choice settled by internal agreement alone, with no market reading, and the market paying anyway.
>
> I wrote these down before sending this. A one-line "this fails because..." is more useful to me than agreement. If you would rather not engage, no reply is needed.
>
> **If it survives, and only then:** sources and the longer derivation are below. The figures, not my summary of them, are the authority.
> - Figure text with line numbers, so you can check every quote: https://github.com/IsaiahN/tether/tree/general-agent/figures

>
