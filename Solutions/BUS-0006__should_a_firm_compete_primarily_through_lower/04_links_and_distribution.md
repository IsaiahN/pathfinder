## What the wider literature bears on the claim

1. **Business, competing within a game.** Porter's generic strategies and the "stuck in the middle" warning support the need to commit rather than average, but the Tuck report of Adner and colleagues shows the either-or rule holds only under stated conditions (scalability of investment, variability of preferences). That is a **conflict** with any universal primacy rule, and it corroborates the figure-derived split in F1 rather than flipping the verdict. Fig 9 :4-5: *"Disagreements that do not shrink with effort, where each rule keeps working well on a different subset, are not one hard question but several well-formed ones."*
2. **Business, changing the game.** Blue Ocean Strategy is a direct, low-overlap corroboration of F3: the rules of the red-ocean game are given, the rules of a blue ocean are waiting to be set. Its claim that blue ocean strategy is *"an 'and-and' not an 'either-or' strategy"* also corroborates the union reading.
3. **Marketing, the failure of a narrow frame.** Marketing Myopia corroborates F3 and F4 from inside the named field: the threat is a failure of management's definition of the business, not market saturation. This maps onto a firm trapped in a closure rather than a firm in a genuinely exhausted market.
4. **Economics, structural change.** Schumpeter corroborates that new markets, methods, and forms of organization, not price competition alone, are the fundamental impulse. This is a game-change analogue, not a proof of the figure line.
5. **Evolutionary biology and complexity, low overlap.** The Red Queen result that coevolution is a zero-sum game while the game keeps changing corroborates the distinction between climbing inside a fixed landscape and entering a shifting one. The fitness-seascape account says the same in different terms. These two share an evidence pool with each other, so they count as one corroborating frame, not two independent witnesses.
6. **Framework essays.** The two essays read this session support the "shape, not a name" reading and the warning that a bundled problem must be split. They are framework material, not outside corroboration.

Overlaps are recorded in the ledger. Agreement between sources drawn from the same pool is one witness, not two (Fig 2 :40; Fig 2 :31-32: *"Independence, between two frames, is how little they draw on the same evidence."*).

## Distribution: offer, do not push

- **Fig 10 :52:** *"Bring a characterised residual, never the source."* Lead with the shape claim and its refuter; offer links only after a verdict on stage 1 is recorded.
- **Fig 2 :35:** *"Alignment is triangulation, not negotiation. A mirror cannot correct you."* Do not ask for agreement or trust.
- **Fig 9 :27-28:** *"describe it before you go looking, or any frame you pick will seem to fit."* Ask what the recipient would expect to see if the shape claim were true before showing them the architecture.
- **Seat Map :163:** *"High correlation is disqualifying for a verdict and efficient for a convention."* No receptiveness ranking, no bulk send, no personal addresses.
- **Fig 4 :22:** *"Only methods cross up. Only head starts come down."* The formula may be given away as a generator; the conclusion should not be pushed as a recording.

**Order in a reply:** (1) the shape claim and its pre-registered refuter; (2) ask what they would count as a failure; (3) record their verdict; (4) only then the three links, with the note that the figures, not the summary, are the authority.
