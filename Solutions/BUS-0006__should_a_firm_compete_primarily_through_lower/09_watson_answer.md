# WATSON answer: BUS-0006

## QUESTION

Should a firm compete primarily through lower costs, differentiated offerings, or a strategy that changes the rules of competition? Stated as an effect: a firm lacks a durable advantage and must decide what kind of contact with the market to change. The word "primarily" asks for one lever ranked above the others across all customers and situations.

## DOMAIN CENSUS

R: arity four or more (firm, rivals, customers, the institutional setup); symmetry broken (cost and differentiation are interchangeable positions on one axis, rule change is not, it changes the axis); scale mixed (transaction, cycle, level).

Domains, each with actors / currency / anchor:
- D1 The market game: firm, rivals, customers / price, cost, margin, feature value / the market (Fig 2 :29 names "a market" as an anchor).
- D2 The rule-setting seam: firm, industry, standards and regulators / rules, conventions, the basis of competition / the same market contacted from a different closure, tested by Fig 10 :47 "a convention that decides an outcome has stopped being one".
- D3 Selection: firms as lineages / entry, exit, survival / market selection, Fig 7 :35 "Selection between rooms is where the ground votes".
- D4 Dependency chain (method, not a verdict domain): the firm's loop / perception, vocabulary, objective, plan / a measurable objective at link 3.

Union, not partition: cost and differentiation lie in one closure, rule change in another, and they overlap in the compound "change the game, then compete inside it". Order by dependency (Fig 3): perceive per slot, state a measurable objective, test the closure, change the game if the gate fires, choose a within-game position, let the market settle.

Mode: general for the shape, specified for any one firm, not grounded. This is a simulated derivation checked for internal consistency against the figures, not a ground reading.

## VERDICT

From the figures: the question as worded is a bundled union and is refused as a single primacy choice. Unbundled, the verdicts are kept separate and never averaged. Within the current game, lower cost and differentiation are compositions that cannot add a new basis of competition; the market decides between them per customer slot. Changing the rules is the only operator that moves the game's boundary, and it counts only when it introduces a new basis of competition with provenance and is paid by an anchor the firm cannot move. "Primary" is a budget decision made after those per-slot verdicts, never a witness for what is true. A global winner for an arbitrary firm is a gap, reported as a shape rather than a silence.

cost if wrong: the firm misallocates its strategy effort, treats a renamed old game as a new one, or lets an internal plan substitute for a market reading. The cost rises with reversibility, so buy independence in proportion to it (Fig 2 :42).
undoable: mostly yes. A within-game position can be changed; a budget can be re-allocated. A badly authored rule that has become the scoreboard is harder to undo, because stakeholders have adapted to it, so that step deserves the most checking before it is taken.
reversed by: a global ranking of the three levers that predicts all slots with no live residual; a firm escaping a saturated closure by composition alone with the market paying; a lever settled by internal agreement alone with the market paying; or a market reading showing the chosen levers do not change contact with customer choice.
