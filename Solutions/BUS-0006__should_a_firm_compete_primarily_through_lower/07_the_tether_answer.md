# The Tether's answer on cost, differentiation, and rule change

**Mode: `general` for the shape, `specified` for any one firm. Not `grounded`.** Simulated derivation checked for internal consistency against the figures (`05_what_shape_means.md` §6), not a ground reading. No market measurement was used.

## 1. The residual it answers

A firm wants a durable advantage and does not have one. Three levers are offered, lower cost, differentiation, and changing the rules of competition. The question asks which should be "primary." The residual is per customer and per market, not a single global score. Fig 1 :19: *"The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated."*

## 2. The shape

1. **The three levers are not one ranked list.** Cost and differentiation live inside the accepted game. Rule change changes the game. Fig 9 :54: *"And when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again."* The compound, change the game and then compete inside it, has its own loop. A single "primary" choice across all slots is refused as posed.
2. **Cost and differentiation are within-game moves.** Fig 6 :21: *"MINT composes inside the closure and can never add an atom."* Fig 8 :32: *"Closure is idempotent. Composing inside a closed set never leaves it, so no search, however long, adds an atom."* They can re-arrange what the game already contains; they cannot add a new basis of competition. Over the full shared atom set they have a real Fig 12 :117 isomer claim: *"isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."*
3. **Rule change is the import, the only move that moves the wall.** Fig 6 :14 names *"the only operator that moves the wall"*; Fig 6 :23: *"No frame escapes its own closure. The bound moves only on contact with a frame whose closure differs."* It is real only with provenance and a new basis of competition. Fig 8 :51: *"Convergent derivation and adopted import look identical in the contents. Only the record of where each came from separates them, and the surplus lives only where the closures differ."*
4. **The market, not the plan, settles it.** Fig 2 :29: *"An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks. A measurement, an experiment, a counter, a market, a patient outcome."* Fig 2 :35: *"Alignment is triangulation, not negotiation. A mirror cannot correct you."* A board room agreement is a frame; it has zero verdict weight.
5. **"Primary" is a budget decision, not a witness.** Fig 9 :60: *"Use filters for the budget and witnesses for the verdict. Never let a filter hand you a verdict."* Decide per slot first, then allocate effort. An aggregate primary score discards the signal (Fig 1 :22: *"Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average."*).
6. **Rule change is conditioned on an external anchor.** Fig 10 :47: *"a convention that decides an outcome has stopped being one."* A firm that sets the rules and then wins because of its own rule has not been paid by the market; it has changed the scoreboard.

## 3. What it explicitly does not say

- Not that one of the three always wins. No figure line ranks them globally. That remaining choice is an honest `gap`, not a silence (Fig 8 :39: *"a search that finds nothing is never a proof of absence."*).
- Not which lever pays for a particular firm. That is `specified`, owed to a market reading.
- Not that "rule change" is free or automatically superior. It is the only wall-moving operator, but it carries a source gate, a shadow test, and an external anchor.
- Not that Porter is wrong or that Blue Ocean is right. World sources corroborate the figure-derived split; they never decide it.
- Not `grounded`. No measurement settles `left(R,φ)` here.

## 4. What would settle it — the ground

A market reading, not a further argument. The questions a discriminator must answer:

- Per customer slot, does the chosen lever change contact with customer choice? Fig 11 :40: *"A system cannot be made more capable by improving the agent in isolation. An improvement that does not change contact changes nothing, however much it improves."*
- Is the claimed "new game" a real import or a renamed old game? Require provenance: what atom is new, where it came from, and which gap it explains (Fig 6 :12-13 source gate and shadow test).
- Does the new rule get paid by an anchor the firm cannot move, or does it merely decide the outcome itself?

**Standing falsifiers from `01` §5:** a global ranking that predicts all slots with no live residual; a firm escaping a saturated closure by composition alone with the market paying; a lever settled by internal agreement alone with the market paying; a controlled reading showing no change in contact. If any fires, the shape is withdrawn.

## 5. The honest stop

**Shape reached, firm-level answer unreached.** Fig 9 :32: *"you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself."* The framework says what a correct strategy must look like: per slot, market-anchored, with game change kept categorically distinct from within-game play and with provenance on any claimed new game. It cannot say which lever this firm should adopt on Tuesday. That is not a failure of the derivation; it is the limit of what the available instruments can distinguish here. The lineage is recorded so the stop is checkable. *"Indiscernibility is not identity."*

## 6. One-paragraph version for outreach

> The Tether does not pick a winner among lower cost, differentiation, and changing the rules of competition, and it does not claim to. It says the three are not one ranked list: cost and differentiation re-arrange the game you are already in, while changing the rules is the only kind of move that can move the game's boundary. It says "primary" is a budget decision, not a verdict, and that the lever must be chosen per customer slot rather than averaged into a global score. It says the market, not the strategy room, settles which move paid, and that a firm which sets the rules and then wins by its own rule has authored a verdict rather than found a ground. What it offers is the shape any answer must have, a provenance test for any claimed new game, and falsifiers written in advance. The firm-specific answer stays open until a market reading closes it.
