## 1. The residual, stated as an effect

A firm's advantage is not arriving or is not durable. Three candidate changes are on the table: reduce cost, add differentiation, or change the rules of competition. The residual is the gap between the firm's current contact with the market and a durable advantage. It is indexed by customer and by market, not by the firm's overall intention. The question asks which single lever should be "primary." Before choosing, we must establish whether that question names one domain or several. If it bundles domains, answering it as posed manufactures a verdict where none is available.

The sought-for shape, written down as an ordinary term before the run (Formula step "Direction"): *an ordering of actions that a firm can take, per customer slot, such that each action is settled by the market rather than by the firm's preference, and such that the kind of action (within-game move or game change) matches the state of the game's closure.* Binding this shape lets it accrue a residual and be refused.

## 2. DOMAIN CENSUS (before any figure line is chosen)

### a. R by arity, symmetry, scale (Fig 9 :22, :27-28)

- **Arity.** The residual relates at least four things: the firm, its rivals, its customers, and the institutional setup of the game. More than two parties with a shared field means there is a seam, not a single two-reader measurement.
- **Symmetry.** Cost and differentiation are commonly treated as symmetric alternatives on one axis: give up price to gain uniqueness, or give up uniqueness to gain price. Rule change is **not** symmetric with either. It does not move along the axis, it replaces the axis. A list that puts all three in one row hides this asymmetry.
- **Scale.** Competing happens in transactions (steps), in product and market cycles (cycles), and in the structure of the game itself (level). Cost and differentiation operate at the step and cycle scales. Rule change operates at the level scale. Mixing scales in one ranked list is a category error unless it is declared a compound.

### b. One line per domain: actors / currency / anchor

A domain needs a nameable anchor that does not move when a frame pushes on it (Fig 2 :29-30). No nameable anchor means it is not a domain yet.

| domain | actors | currency | anchor (nameable?) |
|---|---|---|---|
| **D1. The market game** (within-game competition) | the firm, rivals, customers | price, cost, margin, feature value, share | **yes: the market**. Fig 2 :29 names *"a market"* as an anchor. Customer choice and profit do not update because the firm believes its plan is good. |
| **D2. The rule-setting seam** (game change / import) | the firm, industry players, standards and regulatory bodies | rules, conventions, standards, the basis of competition | **yes: the same market, contacted from a different closure**. A new rule is settled only if the market pays for it. Fig 10 :47: *"a convention that decides an outcome has stopped being one"* is the failure mode when the rule, not the market, decides. |
| **D3. Selection / lineage** (which strategies survive) | firms as lineages, entrants, incumbents | entry, exit, survival, imitation | **yes: the market as selector**. Fig 7 :35: *"Selection between rooms is where the ground votes."* Fig 11 :36: *"Where nothing maintains anything, intelligence is a label applied afterwards to a lineage that met its ground. Nothing was intelligent; selection ran."* |
| **D4. The dependency chain** (forming the per-slot move) | the firm's own loop | perception, vocabulary, objective, plan | **yes, conditionally: an objective that can be measured at link 3**. Fig 3 :19: *"Optimising a link that already works cannot rescue one that does not."* If the objective is unmeasurable, the chain breaks and no lever fixes it. |

D4 is a method domain (it orders the work); D1, D2, and D3 are the verdict domains. Keeping that distinction prevents the auditor from averaging a method into a verdict.

### c. Partition or union?

**Union, not partition.** Cost and differentiation live in D1, the same closure. Rule change lives in D2, a different closure. They overlap in the compound *change the game, then compete inside the new game*. Fig 9 :54: *"And when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again."* Fig 9 :57-58: *"a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either. It stops being a mixture and becomes a specific compound."* The compound has its own loop and its own ground, so it gets its own verdict. Treating the three as a list of separate options is the bundled error Fig 9 names.

### d. Order by dependency (Fig 3)

1. Perceive the residual by slot (D4 links 1 and 2). Fig 1 :19: *"The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated."*
2. State a measurable objective per slot (D4 link 3). Without it, the rest is unassessable.
3. Decide whether the current closure can still pay (D2 question). If not, the game change is upstream of any within-game move, because it sets what moves are available. Fig 13 :115: *"The set is closed. The arrangements are not. That gap is why import exists."*
4. Then choose a within-game position, cost or differentiation, per slot (D1).
5. The market settles all of it (D3). Fig 2 :45: *"Frames range against the ground. They do not negotiate with it."*

## 4. Findings (verbatim quote, derivation, per-domain verdict, refuter)

### F1 — The question is a union masquerading as a partition (decisive, `general`)

Fig 9 :4-5: *"First: is there a fact here? Disagreements that do not shrink with effort, where each rule keeps working well on a different subset, are not one hard question but several well-formed ones. Split it rather than search."*

Fig 9 :54: *"And when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again."*

Cost and differentiation are two positions in one closure. Rule change is a move on the closure itself. Asking which is "primary" puts a level-scale move and two step-scale positions in one ranked list. The world corroborates the split rather than settling it: the Porter tradition says commit to one generic strategy, while Adner, Ruiz Aliseda, and Zemsky report firms that dominate from the middle under conditions of scalable investment and heterogeneous preferences (Tuck, 2016). Each rule works on a different subset, which is Fig 9's exact signature of several questions, not one.

- **Verdict, D1:** answerable per slot, not globally.
- **Verdict, D2:** answerable by asking whether the current closure still pays.
- **Verdict, the combined list:** refused as posed. `triangulated` (Fig 9 + Fig 1).
- **Refuter:** show that the three levers are positions in one closure and that a single global ranking predicts customer choice across slots without residual.

### F2 — Cost and differentiation are mints inside the current game (`general`)

Fig 6 :21: *"MINT composes inside the closure and can never add an atom."*

Fig 8 :32: *"Closure is idempotent. Composing inside a closed set never leaves it, so no search, however long, adds an atom."*

Lower cost re-arranges the value chain within the accepted rules. Differentiation re-arranges the offering within the accepted rules. Neither adds a new basis of competition. If the game's closure cannot yield a durable advantage, more of the same, however long, cannot manufacture one. Fig 11 :40: *"A system cannot be made more capable by improving the agent in isolation. An improvement that does not change contact changes nothing, however much it improves."* A cost programme that never changes what the customer sees does not change contact, so it does not change capability.

- **Verdict, D1:** both are legitimate within-game moves; the figures do not rank them globally. `triangulated` (Fig 6 + Fig 8).
- **Refuter:** exhibit a cost or differentiation move that adds a new atom to the game's closure without changing the rules, and show the market paying for the new atom.

### F3 — Rule change is the import, the only operator that moves the wall (`general`)

Fig 6 :14: *"the only operator that moves the wall"*; Fig 6 :23: *"No frame escapes its own closure. The bound moves only on contact with a frame whose closure differs."*

Fig 13 :115: *"The set is closed. The arrangements are not. That gap is why import exists."*

Changing the rules of competition means introducing a new basis on which customers choose. That is an import: it adds an atom and expands what the firm can compose. This is why it cannot be deduced from inside the current game, and why it is structurally different from cost and differentiation. The world corroborates: Schumpeter's *"new consumers' goods, the new methods of production or transportation, the new markets, the new forms of industrial organization"* (Econlib, quoting *Capitalism, Socialism and Democracy*, 1942) and Kim and Mauborgne's *"In blue oceans, competition is irrelevant because the rules of the game are waiting to be set"* both describe a change of game, not a position inside it.

- **Verdict, D2:** when the residual is "the current closure no longer pays," game change is the only route that expands the reach. `triangulated` (Fig 6 + Fig 13).
- **Condition:** an import needs a source gate and a shadow test (Fig 6 :12-13, Fig 8). A claimed "new game" that is only the old game with new names is the blend collapse, not a wall move. Provenance is the discriminator: Fig 8 :51 *"Convergent derivation and adopted import look identical in the contents. Only the record of where each came from separates them, and the surplus lives only where the closures differ."*
- **Refuter:** show a firm escaping a saturated closure by composition alone, with no new basis of competition and no provenanced import, and with the market paying.

### F4 — The market, not the internal plan, settles the lever (`general`)

Fig 2 :29: *"An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks. A measurement, an experiment, a counter, a market, a patient outcome."*

Fig 2 :35: *"Alignment is triangulation, not negotiation. A mirror cannot correct you."*

Fig 7 :35: *"A room's agreement is variation, not a verdict. Selection between rooms is where the ground votes."*

A board room, a strategy consulting exercise, or a satisfied management team is a frame. It cannot anchor the choice. The market must pay, and it must pay separately for each slot. This is why the derivation cannot name a winning lever for an arbitrary firm. It can only say what a winning answer must look like and what would settle it.

- **Verdict, D3:** the market selects; internal agreement has zero verdict weight. `triangulated` (Fig 2 + Fig 7).
- **Refuter:** produce a lever choice settled by internal agreement alone and show that the market paid without having been consulted by the choice.

### F5 — "Primarily" discards the per-slot signal (`general`)

Fig 1 :19: *"The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated."*; Fig 1 :22 *"Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average."*

A firm sells to many customer slots. Cost may win one, differentiation another, and a new game a third. Reducing the decision to one primary lever averages across slots. Fig 9 :60: *"Use filters for the budget and witnesses for the verdict. Never let a filter hand you a verdict."* "Primarily" is a budget allocation, useful for deciding where to spend, and it is not a witness for which lever is true.

- **Verdict, D1 and D2:** the lever is chosen per slot; "primary" is a resource decision made after the per-slot verdicts. `single-source` for the slot rule (Fig 1), corroborated by world evidence on condition-dependence.
- **Refuter:** show that a single global lever predicts outcomes in every slot without leaving a live residual in any slot.

### F6 — The three words name positions; a strategy is a route (`general`)

Fig 12 :111: *"A vocabulary without bonds can name a state and cannot name a route to one."*

Fig 12 :112: *"A plan is a sequence. A goal is a comparison. Progress is a subtraction."*

Cost leadership and differentiation are names of positions. What a firm needs is a route: a sequence of moves, a comparison against the market, and a subtraction from the current shortfall. Rule change is a change of the composition itself. A list of three nouns cannot say what counts as getting somewhere. This is the middle-link failure of Fig 3 :19: *"Optimising a link that already works cannot rescue one that does not."*

- **Verdict, D4:** bind the sought shape as a route with a measurable objective before ranking levers. `single-source` (Fig 12, with Fig 3 corroborating the ordering).
- **Refuter:** derive a complete route, with sequence, comparison, and progress, from the three nouns alone and no additional bond vocabulary.

### F7 — Rule change is not a free override (`general`)

Fig 10 :47: *"a convention that decides an outcome has stopped being one"*

When a firm sets the rules and then wins because of the rule it set, the rule has stopped being a neutral convention and has become an authored verdict. Then the market has not paid for the move; the firm has simply changed the scoreboard. Fig 2 :33-34 warns that an anchor that updates becomes another frame. Fig 2 :42: *"Buy independence in proportion to the cost of being wrong."* The higher the cost of a wrong strategy, the more the firm needs an outside anchor it cannot move.

- **Verdict, D2:** game change is licensed only if the new rule is tested by an anchor the firm cannot move. `triangulated` (Fig 10 + Fig 2).
- **Refuter:** show a rule change where the firm both authors the rule and legitimately settles the outcome, with no external anchor, and show that customers pay anyway.

### F8 — The global primacy question is a `gap`, not a silence (`general`)

After re-census, no figure line picks cost over differentiation globally, and none picks rule change over both out of context. Fig 8 :39: *"a search that finds nothing is never a proof of absence."* Fig 13 :134: *"Unreachable is a verdict here, not a silence,"* and the framework can report *"a kind of thing, never the answer itself"* (Fig 9 :32).

The honest report is: the figures return a **shape** (per-slot, market-anchored, import when the closure fails, with provenance), not a single winner. This is a `gap` for the question as worded and a verdict for the unbundled question. It is not an evasion: the missing piece is characterized in effect terms, which is what an import needs in order to be found (Fig 9 :30-31: *"your words are borrowed too, so describe the effect rather than the cause"*).

- **Verdict, combined question:** `gap`. `triangulated` (Fig 8 + Fig 9 + Fig 13).
- **Refuter:** quote a figure line that ranks the three levers for an arbitrary firm without a slot or a ground reading.

## 5. Pre-registered refuter (written before the conclusion)

Required before any outreach: Seat Map :287, *"a falsifier written before the run is a design instrument, not only an honesty device."*

The shape claim is refuted if any of the following holds:

1. **Union refuter.** A single global ranking of the three levers is shown to predict customer choice across all slots with no live residual in any slot. That breaks F1 and F5.
2. **Closure refuter.** A firm is shown escaping a saturated game's closure by cost or differentiation composition alone, with no new basis of competition and no provenanced import, and the market pays. That breaks F2 and F3.
3. **Anchor refuter.** A lever choice is settled by internal agreement alone, with no market reading, and the market pays anyway. That breaks F4 and F7.
4. **Ground refuter.** A controlled market reading shows the firm's per-slot lever choices do not change contact with customer choice. That breaks Fig 11 :40 as applied here.

If any fires, the unbundled shape is wrong and is withdrawn. Until one is run, no part of this may be labelled `grounded`. No claim of market measurement is made anywhere in this folder.

## 6. Recommendation

1. Lead with F1: refuse the forced single choice, then unbundle into the market game, the rule-setting seam, and selection (Section 2).
2. Treat cost and differentiation as within-game mints and rule change as an import, with the provenance test attached (F2, F3).
3. Decide per customer slot, and treat "primary" as a budget allocation made after the per-slot verdicts, never as a witness (F5, Fig 9 :60).
4. Put the market anchor in the answer: ask what would show the lever is not working, before choosing it (F4, F7).
5. Fig 4 :22: *"Only methods cross up. Only head starts come down."* Send the derivation and the refuter, not the conclusion.
