# Solution BUS-0006 — figure-grounded package

**Question:** Should a firm compete primarily through lower costs, differentiated offerings, or a strategy that changes the rules of competition?

**Source:** `Questions/business.txt` line 10 (item 6).

**Section:** 1. Competitive strategy and firm performance.

**Field for public contact points:** competitive strategy and marketing.

**Mode:** general for the shape, specified for any firm-level application, never grounded without market measurement.

**Status:** done.

**Registry status:** pending.

## What is being decided, in effect terms

A firm is not getting the durable advantage it wants. Three levers are offered: lower cost, differentiation, and changing the rules of the game. Each changes a different kind of contact with the market. The word "primarily" asks for one lever to be ranked above the others across all customers and all situations. The derivation asks whether that question picks out one real thing, or whether it bundles several domains that must be answered separately.

## Boundary and sources

Nothing outside this solution folder was modified. Authority order followed the project README and master plan: the verbatim figure text at `FRAMEWORK/reference/03-FIGURES_TEXT.md` (cited as `Fig N :line`), `THE_FORMULA.md`, the canonical figures README, and the seat map field guide. World sources were opened on 2026-11-22 and are recorded in `10_source_ledger.csv`. Every figure quote in this folder was checked against the local verbatim extract, not against a summary.

This package is a **simulated derivation checked for internal consistency against the figures**, not a ground reading. It cannot settle the question it reports on. No market measurement was taken and no experiment was run.

## What is deliberately not here

- No personal emails of named individuals.
- No ranking of people by how likely they are to agree.
- No bulk-send plan.

Figure-grounded reasons: Fig 2 :35, *"Alignment is triangulation, not negotiation. A mirror cannot correct you."*; Fig 2 :40, agreement about the world is worthless when the frames share an evidence pool; Seat Map :163, *"High correlation is disqualifying for a verdict and efficient for a convention."* Selecting recipients for receptiveness manufactures the correlation that would make their agreement worthless. Fig 10 :52: *"Bring a characterised residual, never the source."*
