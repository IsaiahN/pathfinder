**Subject:** Check one output before you decide whether grand unification is worth reading

> Hi [name],
>
> I have one specific output I would like you to check against what you already know. I am not asking you to read the theory first, and I am not asking you to agree with it. If the output does not survive, there is nothing else to look at.
>
> **The output (`specified`, not `grounded`):** the framework I am using has a rule that irreducibility is a fact about a level, not about the world. Read across to energy scales as a declared binding, it says that "how many fundamental forces there are" is a scale-relative question. Three couplings at low energy and one coupling above a breaking scale are not a contradiction; they are one referent seen at different levels. That is a shape, not a measurement, and I am not claiming the framework derives a gauge group.
>
> **What the shape commits to:** one simple gauge group with one coupling above a scale M_X, broken to the Standard Model below it, with the three observed couplings as the running images of that one coupling, and a low-energy trace such as proton decay. The bond structure matters here: at M_X the three couplings stand in identity, three names for one referent, and the extrapolation test is a threshold comparison among three quantities. Those are two different relations and I keep them separate.
>
> **What I am not claiming:** that the MSSM coupling plot is evidence that nature is unified. It is a filter for which models to try, not a witness. I am not claiming the framework names SU(5), SO(10) or E6; a larger gauge group is an import from another frame and needs provenance before it carries any weight. I am not claiming a grand unified theory is a theory of everything; the framework's rule is that there is no last room.
>
> **What would refute the shape claim:**
> 1. a single coupling derived entirely inside the Standard Model closure with no imported larger group;
> 2. a demonstration that the three high-energy couplings require two or more referents, not one;
> 3. a ground reading, such as a proton-decay result incompatible with every simple-group embedding, that settles convergence against the shape.
>
> I wrote these down before sending this. If any one holds, I will retract the relevant claim. A one-line "this fails because..." is more useful to me than agreement. If you would rather not engage, no reply is needed.
>
