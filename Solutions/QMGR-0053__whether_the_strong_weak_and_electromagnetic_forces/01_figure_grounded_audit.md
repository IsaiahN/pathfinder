## 0. The claim under audit, stated as an effect

The three gauge interactions of the Standard Model, strong SU(3), weak SU(2) and hypercharge U(1), have three coupling constants g3, g2, g1 measured at low energy. They run with energy under the renormalization group. The question asks whether, at a high scale, they become one coupling under one larger simple gauge group.

**Residual R, in effect terms.** Three distinct couplings are observed at accessible energy; one coupling is predicted at high energy. The residual is the gap between those two descriptions plus the absence of any direct reading at the high scale. It is not a single number. It is indexed by three slots: g1, g2, g3, and by the question of what referent the three names have.

**Bind the sought-for shape as an ordinary term (Formula "Direction").** Before deriving, write down what an answer would look like so it can accrue a residual and be refused:

> **SHAPE TERM (bound before the run, `specified`):** a single simple gauge group G with one gauge coupling g_G above a breaking scale M_X; G broken to SU(3) x SU(2) x U(1); the three low-energy couplings are the running images of one coupling; a falsifiable low-energy trace, such as proton decay at a predicted rate. The three names g1, g2, g3 refer to one coupling above M_X and to three below it.

This term is a preference until the ground pays for it. Binding it is what lets the figures refuse it rather than silently steering the derivation toward it (Fig 5 :62 to :66).

**What an answer would lead to.** If the shape holds, the three forces are not three things that happen to meet; they are one thing seen at different scales, and the number of fundamental forces is scale-relative. If it fails, the three couplings are genuinely distinct and their apparent convergence is a projection of our extrapolation, not a referent.

## 1a. Domain census (before any figure line is chosen)

**R by arity, symmetry, scale.**

- **Arity:** three slots (g1, g2, g3) plus a threshold scale. Not a scalar. Fig 1 :19: *"The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated."*
- **Symmetry:** the high-energy claim is an **identity**, three names one referent. The test of convergence is a **comparison**, a threshold relation between three quantities. These are two different bonds and must not be conflated (Fig 12 :67, :87).
- **Scale:** the putative meeting is around 10^16 GeV, roughly twelve orders of magnitude above current collider energies. This makes the question partly a reachability question, not only a consistency question (Fig 6 :27).

**One line per domain (actors / currency / anchor). No nameable anchor means it is not a domain yet.**

- **Domain 1, framework-internal composition.** Actors: the six terms, the seven bonds, negation. Currency: composition inside closure(Γ), then settlement. Anchor: the figures as metalaws; the ground for any measurement. This is a real domain: the anchor is nameable (the figure set and the ground).
- **Domain 2, the particle-physics / GUT frame.** Actors: gauge groups, coupling constants, beta functions, the unification scale, proton decay modes, superpartners. Currency: measured couplings and predicted lifetimes. Anchor: experiment (precision coupling measurements, Super-Kamiokande, Hyper-Kamiokande). Nameable anchor, so a real domain.
- **Domain 3, the verification and outreach frame.** Actors: expert readers. Currency: residuals and agreement. Anchor: the ground, never agreement. In this simulated run all three domains draw on one evidence pool, so the independence of this domain is zero (Fig 2 :40).

**Partition or union.** Union. The question bundles at least four conditions: model consistency, empirical convergence, experimental reach, and model uniqueness. A single lever that makes the couplings look beautiful cannot clear the intersections (Fig 9 :55).

**Order by dependency (Fig 3).** Measure the couplings (perception), have terms for what a gauge group affords (vocabulary), state what would count as progress, namely convergence within errors (objective), extrapolate under the renormalization group (planning), then name what worked and carry the refuter (learn and carry). The middle link, having terms and an objective, is where this chain is most likely to break: Fig 3 :26 says it *"has neither, so it attracts the least attention and the fewest instruments."*

## 2. Findings

### F1 - The question is a union compound, not one hard question

Fig 9 :5: *"are not one hard question but several well-formed ones. Split it rather than search."* Fig 9 :55: *"A single lever clears the region where only one condition holds and leaves the intersections standing, because the second condition still binds."*

The Library of Babel essay names the same failure: *"Perhaps your problem was a bundle or conflation of several problems masquerading as one?"*

The question bundles at least four conditions:

1. **Consistency:** can one simple group with one coupling reproduce the observed low-energy couplings and fermion representations?
2. **Empirical convergence:** do the three measured couplings actually meet, within errors, at one scale?
3. **Reach:** is that scale testable by any feasible instrument?
4. **Uniqueness and falsification:** which group, and what observation would refute it?

The often-quoted fact that the couplings "look beautiful" when extrapolated in the MSSM clears only condition 2. It leaves 1, 3 and 4 standing. Splitting before searching is the figure's instruction. **Mode: `general` (structural). Triangulated:** Fig 9 and the Library of Babel essay are two low-overlap frames (formal figure vs narrative essay). **Refuter:** show the four conditions are a partition (one condition) rather than overlapping conditions.

### F2 - Number of fundamental forces is level-relative, not an absolute fact about the world

Fig 12 :103: *"Composition is recursive, and the level decides what is irreducible."* Fig 12 :104: *"So irreducible is a fact about a level and never about the world."* Fig 4 :33: *"there is no top and no infinite regress."*

In the framework, what counts as irreducible is a fact about a level. Applied to this question as a **binding**, the three forces being distinct at low energy and one at high energy is not a contradiction and not the replacement of a false description by a true one. It is a change of the level at which the description is irreducible. This is the deepest figure-grounded point available here.

**Strain test and correction.** "Level" in Fig 12 means a composition stratum, not an energy scale. Mapping it to energy is a structural analogy, not a figure-given identity. I first wrote this as a direct derivation and retract that: it is a binding whose validity must be checked. **Mode: `specified`, with the level-to-energy mapping declared as a binding. Triangulated** across Fig 12 and Fig 4 (low overlap: composition recursion vs scale membrane), but the mapping itself is single-source. **Refuter:** show that irreducibility in the framework cannot be read across to effective-theory energy scales, in which case F2 falls to a pure analogy and must be labelled as such.

### F3 - Unification is an identity claim tested by a comparison, not a merge into a new substance

Fig 12 :67: *"two names, one referent"*, with the test Fig 12 :69: *"substitute one for the other. Is anything lost? No."* The comparison bond is Fig 12 :87: *"a threshold relation between two quantities"*, with the test *"is it about which is larger, rather than both being present?"*

At M_X the claim g1 = g2 = g3 is an identity: three names, one referent (≡). The running-and-meeting is a comparison (⋛): a threshold relation among three quantities. These are different bonds. Treating the unified interaction as a new substance blended from three others is the flat-formula error Fig 12 names: *"the parts are not the thing"* (Fig 12 :17).

**Mode: `specified`. Triangulated** across Fig 12 and the Operators table (two presentations of the same bond set, so low independence; declared). **Refuter:** show that the unification claim requires two referents rather than one, or that the convergence test is not a threshold comparison; either breaks the bond assignment.

### F4 - The larger gauge group is an import, not derivable inside the Standard Model closure

Fig 6 :21: *"MINT composes inside the closure and can never add an atom."* Fig 6 :23: *"No frame escapes its own closure. The bound moves only on contact with a frame whose closure differs."* Fig 6 :14 names IMPORT as *"the only operator that moves the wall"*. Fig 8 :32: *"Closure is idempotent. Composing inside a closed set never leaves it, so no search, however long, adds an atom."*

SU(5), SO(10) and E6 are not obtainable by rearranging the Standard Model's own primitives. They are imported primitives from frames whose closure differs. Imports carry a source gate and a shadow test (Fig 6 :12 to :13: *"source gate: provenance"*, *"shadow test: explains a gap"*). Fig 6 :25: *"An import adds no atom to the world."* It adds access to the frame, so the unified group is not created by the act of proposing it.

Fig 8 :50: *"Convergent derivation and adopted import look identical in the contents. Only the record of where each came from separates them."* Hence a claim that coupling convergence "predicts" a specific GUT is an adopted import unless the provenance is shown.

**Mode: `general` (structural). Triangulated** across Fig 6 and Fig 8 (different figures, overlapping authorship, so weak independence; declared). **Refuter:** derive a single coupling entirely by composition inside the Standard Model closure with no imported group; that would break F4.

### F5 - The ground is experiment, and much of it lies beyond reach

Fig 2 :29: *"An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks. A measurement, an experiment, a counter, a market, a patient outcome."* `THE_FORMULA.md` step 5: *"SETTLE - the ground settles it. Verified only by what pays. A gate is not the ground."*

For this question the anchor is precision coupling data and proton-decay searches, not the aesthetic appeal of a plot. Fig 6 :27: *"some unreachability is depth and some is genuine absence, and from inside the frame the two look the same."* The GUT scale being beyond collider reach is a budget fact, not a proof of absence. Fig 8 :39: *"So a search that finds nothing is never a proof of absence."* Fig 9 :44: *"Only a sealed room can be searched to the end, and no frame is a sealed room. So searching and finding nothing proves nothing."*

So Super-Kamiokande's non-observation of proton decay rules out specific models (the simplest SU(5)) but does not settle the general question. Conversely, the MSSM coupling match is not a settlement; it is a fit. Fig 5 :26 states the bargain: *"does the new term cost less to state than the confusion it removes?"*

**Mode: `specified`. Triangulated** across Fig 2, Fig 5, Fig 8 and Fig 9 (four figures, different functions, shared source pool). **Refuter:** exhibit a ground reading that settles convergence directly at an accessible energy; that would move the status from candidate to grounded in that direction.

### F6 - Unity is a filter, not a witness

Fig 9 :60: *"Use filters for the budget and witnesses for the verdict. Never let a filter hand you a verdict."* Fig 2 :40 already cited: *"agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool."*

The Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy entry *The Unity of Science* opens by asking *"Is unity a feature of reality or of our modes of cognition?"* and later states *"Unity has been considered an epistemic virtue and goal."* That is a low-overlap, human-cognition source corroborating the figure's separation: our preference for unification is a property of the searcher, not a witness for the world.

The MSSM convergence plot is a legitimate filter for which models to try first. It is not evidence that nature is unified. **Mode: `general`. Triangulated** across Fig 9 and SEP (physics metatheory vs philosophy of science, genuinely low overlap). **Refuter:** show that the aesthetic convergence carries independent ground contact beyond the data used to produce it.

### F7 - The framework returns a shape, not a name

Fig 9 :32: *"you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself."* The figures README states the framework returns *"what a missing thing would have to look like (a shape, not a name)."* Fig 13 :134: *"Unreachable is a verdict here, not a silence,"* and :135: *"It cannot say which primitive is missing, because naming it would mean already holding it."*

The shape is: one simple gauge group, one coupling above a breaking scale, breaking to the Standard Model, with an observable trace. The framework cannot name SU(5), SO(10) or E6. Those are bindings from the physics frame, provenanced as such.

**Correction recorded.** I initially wrote that "shape, not a name" forbids naming any specific group. The README's phrase governs a **missing** primitive. SU(5) and SO(10) are not held primitives of the six-term set, so naming them is a binding from an external frame, not the invention of a seventh substrate term. The correct statement is that the framework does not derive the name; a provenanced import may carry one. Both readings are kept visible here. **Mode: `general`. Triangulated** across Fig 9, Fig 13 and the canonical README. **Refuter:** produce a figure-given derivation of a specific gauge group, which would break the shape limit.

### F8 - A confirmed GUT would still not be a final room

Fig 7 :6: *"the limit: no last room."* Fig 7 :30 to :31: *"every room closes some of the gap; no finite number of them ever arrives."* The figures README has a section titled *"This is not a Theory of Everything - and that is the point."*

A grand unified theory omits gravity, and even a theory that included it would be a room with an outside. This matters because the question is routinely conflated with a theory of everything. **Mode: `general`. Triangulated** across Fig 7 and the canonical README. **Refuter:** exhibit a terminal frame with no outside, which the figures rule out by construction.

### F9 - Keep the residuals per coupling, not averaged

Fig 1 :19: *"The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated."* Fig 1 :22: *"Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average."*

A single "average running coupling" would hide which of g1, g2, g3 fails to meet and by how much. The checkable practice is to track three curves and their pairwise crossings separately. This is the most direct technical transfer from the figures to this question. **Mode: `specified`. Single-source** (Fig 1), corroborated by standard RG practice in the physics frame. **Refuter:** show that a single aggregated statistic preserves the per-coupling failure signal.

## 3. Pre-registered refuter

Required before any outreach, per the seat map rule: *"a falsifier written before the run is a design instrument, not only an honesty device."*

The shape claim is refuted or moved if any of the following holds:

1. A single coupling is derived by composition entirely inside the Standard Model closure with no imported larger group (breaks F4).
2. It is shown that the three couplings at high energy require two or more referents rather than one (breaks F3).
3. A ground reading, such as a direct measurement at accessible energy or a proton-decay result incompatible with every simple-group embedding, settles convergence against the shape (moves F5 from candidate).
4. The four conditions in F1 are shown to be a partition rather than a union (breaks F1).
5. A figure re-census shows irreducibility cannot be read across to energy scales at all (breaks F2, downgrading it to an analogy).

If any fires, the relevant claim is withdrawn and the retraction is recorded below. Until one is run, no part of this may be labelled `grounded`.
