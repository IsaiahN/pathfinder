## DERIVATION

### Q1 - Identity or comparison

- Fig 12 :67 defines identity: *"two names, one referent."* The test, Fig 12 :69: *"substitute one for the other. Is anything lost? No."*
- Fig 12 :87 defines comparison: *"a threshold relation between two quantities."* The test: *"is it about which is larger, rather than both being present?"*
- At M_X the claim g1 = g2 = g3 is **identity**: the three names have one referent. Substituting one coupling for another loses nothing by hypothesis.
- The extrapolation test is **comparison**: it asks whether three running quantities cross a common threshold at one scale. That is a ⋛ relation.
- These are different bonds. Calling unification a "blend" or a new substance commits the flat-formula error Fig 12 :17 names: *"the parts are not the thing."*
- **Falsifier:** show the high-energy claim needs two referents, or that convergence is not a threshold relation. Either breaks the bond assignment. **Mode: `specified`. Triangulated** across Fig 12 and the Operators table (low independence, declared).

### Q2 - Are the three forces isomers

- Fig 12 :117: **"isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."**
- Fig 12 :123: **"functional group. A substructure whose presence fixes the outcome. The group says what kind; the qualifier says which member."**
- As written in the Standard Model, the three forces share no single atom set: SU(3), SU(2) and U(1) are different group factors with different couplings. They are **not** isomers by the figure's definition. They are members of a **functional group**, gauge interactions of the same cross-kind form, with different group factors as qualifiers.
- The stronger, checkable version: write all three over the full atom set of a candidate unified group, differing only in which breaking channel produces which force. Then they are isomers, same atoms, different arrangement, different low-energy substance. This is exactly the claim a GUT makes and it is testable.
- **Falsifier:** if any one force can be written without an atom the others require, in the candidate unified set, the isomer claim fails and the functional-group reading stands. **Mode: `specified`. Single-source** for the figure definition; the physics-facing check is unmeasured and is not supplied by this derivation.

### Q3 - Order and irreducibility

- Fig 12 :103: **"Composition is recursive, and the level decides what is irreducible."** Fig 12 :104: **"A settled molecule becomes an atom for whatever composes over it. So irreducible is a fact about a level and never about the world."**
- Each of the three forces, settled by decades of experiment, is an atom at the low-energy level. The unified coupling would be an atom at a higher level. Nothing in the framework requires one level to be the last: Fig 13 :33 says *"there is no top and no infinite regress."*
- **Correction recorded.** "Level" in Fig 12 is a composition stratum, not an energy scale. Reading it across to renormalization-group energy is a binding that must be defended, not a figure-given identity. This correction was made before the conclusion and is kept visible in `01` section 6.
- **Falsifier:** derive one of the six substrate terms from the other five, or show that irreducibility cannot be read across to energy scales at all. **Mode: `specified`, with the mapping declared a binding.**

### Q4 - Union, not partition

- Fig 9 :55: **"A single lever clears the region where only one condition holds and leaves the intersections standing, because the second condition still binds."**
- The four conditions (model consistency, empirical convergence, experimental reach, uniqueness and falsification) overlap. A precision measurement of convergence can clear condition 2 while leaving 1, 3 and 4 standing.
- Fig 9 :5: **"are not one hard question but several well-formed ones. Split it rather than search."** The Library of Babel essay says the same in narrative form: *"Perhaps your problem was a bundle or conflation of several problems masquerading as one?"*
- **Falsifier:** show the four conditions reduce to one, with no overlap, so that a single lever clears all of them. **Mode: `general` (structural). Triangulated** across Fig 9 and the essay (two low-overlap frames).

## CONCLUSION

The high-energy claim is an identity tested by a comparison, not a blend. At low energy the three forces are a functional group, not isomers; over a full shared atom set they become a genuine isomer claim with a stated falsifier. Irreducibility attaches to a level, so three forces at one scale and one at another are not contradictory, though the level-to-energy mapping remains a declared binding. The question is a union of four overlapping conditions and must be split before it is searched. No primitive was added and no new atom was minted in this derivation.
