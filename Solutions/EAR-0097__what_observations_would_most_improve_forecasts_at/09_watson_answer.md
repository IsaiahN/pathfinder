## QUESTION

Decide, in effect terms, what the Tether says about the request for the observations that would most improve forecasts at the scales where infrastructure and land-use decisions are made. Option A: return a ranked list of observations as the answer. Option B: return the figure-derived shape an observation must satisfy, keep verdicts separate per decision-slot class, and declare the ranked list a `gap` with a stated instrument to close it. What each leads to: A delivers a name where the figures return a shape, averages away the live per-slot residual, and presents a filter as a witness; B delivers checkable conditions, five written refuters, and an honest stop at the limit of the available instruments.

## DOMAIN CENSUS

R by arity, symmetry and scale, then one line per domain with actors, currency and anchor, partition or union, and dependency order.

- **R, the residual.** R = |Γ(b,a) − o′| per decision slot s. Arity: three operands (prediction, action, outcome), indexed by slot, plus a second arity for comparing candidate observations, which is a threshold relation (Fig 12 :87). Symmetry: ordered, not symmetric; observation precedes decision precedes outcome. Scale: cross-scale by construction, because the observation often enters a global model and the decision is local. Fig 1 :19: **"The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated."**
- **D1, the forecasting frame.** Actors: models, data assimilation, downscaling chains. Currency: forecast error per slot and ensemble spread. Anchor: the observed local outcome that settles the forecast (Fig 2 :29).
- **D2, the decision frame.** Actors: planners, engineers, regulators, communities. Currency: avoided loss, robust choice, lead time. Anchor: the realized consequence at the decision site.
- **D3, the scale membrane.** Actors: regional models, downscaling, the T_A / T_E round trip. Currency: information lost across the transform, R_T. Anchor: local observations against which a downscaled projection can be ranged.
- **D4, the instrument and channel frame.** Actors: gauges, satellites, reanalysis, surveys, data streams. Currency: what can be represented at all, and contact. Anchor: the ground that persists while the channel decays (Fig 10 :24).
- **D5, the habitat and cascade frame.** Actors, conditions and relations in contact with the decision residual. Currency: contact cascades out to second and third order. Anchor: the world read off, not the model's variable list (Fig 11 :48).
- **Excluded candidates.** "More data everywhere" has no anchor independent of D1; it is a candidate answer with no residual described. "A perfect forecast" is a terminal room, absorbed into D1 as a limit.
- **Union, not partition.** The four conditions overlap; a single lever clears only the region where one condition holds and leaves the intersections standing (Fig 9 :54). Verdicts are kept separate per domain and never averaged.
- **Dependency order.** Perception, vocabulary, objective, planning, learn and carry (Fig 3 :20). The local perception link is the one that usually has no instrument (Fig 3 :27).

Strain test applied: the proto-instrument rule (Fig 6 :32) and the import rule (Fig 8 :9) were initially read as one instruction. Re-census assigns them to opposed instruments, INWARD and OUTWARD (Fig 6 :31), so no strain remains. The two currencies of observation value (Fig 12 :125) remain a live type question, named rather than smoothed.

## VERDICT (from the figures)

The question as posed asks for a ranked list, and the figures return a shape. The defensible residue is four conditions plus an enumeration rule, applied per decision-slot class and never averaged: reduce a live per-slot residual; range it against a local anchor that does not update on the model; measure the global-to-local round trip rather than assuming it away; extend a proto-instrument already returning something that fails to resolve, or import a new modality with a source gate and a shadow test. Enumerate the candidate set from the world's contact network and follow the cascade outward because the driver can be several steps from the decision variable. Channel maintenance is part of the answer, not overhead. The specific winning observation is a declared `gap` of the unreached-at-this-budget kind, with a discriminator and five refuters written in advance. Mode `specified` on the conditions, `general` on the structure, not `grounded`.

- **cost if wrong:** bounded and locatable. The packet states mode, refuters and provenance before the conclusion, so an error is findable. The real cost is spending the independence of outside readers by sending a recording of one observing campaign instead of the generator, which Fig 2 :42 prices: "Buy independence in proportion to the cost of being wrong." That cost is avoidable by leading with the shape and its refuter. A second cost is misallocating observation budget by trusting a global average that hides a live failing slot, which is exactly the aggregation failure in Fig 1 :22.
- **undoable:** yes. No specific observing system is asserted, so nothing concrete has to be withdrawn if the shape fails. The refuters are written so each condition can be removed independently and the census re-run. A sent packet can be superseded by a corrected one. The `gap` is recorded rather than hidden, so no name has to be unclaimed later.
- **reversed by:** a ground reading, not agreement. A slot-resolved before/after measurement that removes the live residual; a verification that stays fixed when the model's output changes; a measured zero round-trip gap at the relevant slots; a sensor of a never-registered quantity built from a description alone that later reduces the slot residual; a decision residual whose habitat demonstrably closes at first order; or a figure re-census that yields a line naming the winning observation without strain. Until one of these fires, the verdict stands as `candidate`.
