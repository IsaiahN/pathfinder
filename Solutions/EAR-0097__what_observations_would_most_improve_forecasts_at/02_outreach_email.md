# Outreach draft: validate the output first, then decide about the theory

**Use:** one-to-one, or as a cover letter attached to a submission through the public venues in `03_public_contact_points.csv`. **Not** for bulk send.

**Hard precondition:** do not send until the four-condition separator in `01_figure_grounded_audit.md` sections 3 to 5 is intact and the pre-registered refuter in section 4 is unchanged. The output must survive validation or the two-stage offer collapses on contact.

---

**Subject:** One checkable claim about decision-scale forecasts, before any theory

> Hi [name],
>
> I have one specific output I would like you to check against what you already know from earth system observation. I am not asking you to read the framework first, and I am not asking you to agree with anything. If the output does not survive, there is nothing else to look at.
>
> **The output (`specified`, not `grounded`).** Forecast skill is usually reported as a global or regional average. That average can improve while one catchment, coastal segment, or land-use parcel is still wrong in the way that matters for an actual decision. The figures I am working with index the residual per slot, not in aggregate, and say that aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average. So the claim I want checked is this:
>
> An observation counts as decision-scale-improving only if it satisfies four conditions at once. (1) It reduces forecast error on at least one live decision slot, not only in a global mean. (2) It is read against an anchor that does not update on the model's own output, so an independent local reading can range the forecast. (3) Its value survives the coarse-to-local round trip, meaning the information lost between a global projection and the decision site is measured rather than assumed away. (4) It extends an instrument that is already returning something that fails to resolve, or, if it is a genuinely new modality, it arrives as a provenanced import with a stated source and a stated gap it explains.
>
> A fifth point is an enumeration rule rather than a condition: the set of candidate observations is read off the world's contact network and followed outward until adding another causal step stops changing the decision residual. The observation that matters can be two or three steps from the variable the model actually carries. There is no standing list of valuable observations that survives a change of the decision residual.
>
> **What I am not claiming.** I am not naming a winning sensor, network, or variable. I am not claiming more data everywhere helps, or that larger models settle this. I am not claiming that global skill averages are useless, only that they are the wrong instrument for a per-slot verdict. I am not claiming any measurement has settled the four conditions. This is a shape and a route, marked `specified` and ungrounded.
>
> **What would refute it, written down before this message:**
> 1. One observation is shown to reduce forecast error on every decision slot class with equal force and to keep working when the residual changes.
> 2. A global skill metric is shown to preserve the live per-slot residual with no information loss.
> 3. An observation improves forecasts while drawing on the same evidence pool as the model, with no independent local reading, and the improvement is real at the slot.
> 4. The round-trip gap between the coarse projection and the local outcome is measured to be zero at the relevant slots.
> 5. An observation of something that has never registered at any resolution is built from a description alone and later shown to reduce the slot residual.
>
> If any of these holds, I will retract the relevant condition and re-run the census. A one-line "this fails because..." is more useful to me than agreement. If you would rather not engage, no reply is needed.
>
> **If it survives, and only then:** here are the three public sources. The figures, not my summary of them, are the authority.
> - Figures and formula for direct download (SVG, branch `general-agent`): https://github.com/IsaiahN/tether/tree/general-agent/figures
> - Worked architecture and the per-slot residual rule: https://medium.com/@IsaiahNwukor/arrival-of-the-fittest-the-tether-architecture-1de7edf641ec
> - Four essays, including the source-discriminator argument: https://adventuresinml.substack.com/p/the-hard-problem-is-about-source
>
> **If you want to reuse the method:** the formula is in the figures link. Run it on a problem you know well and can check against a ground, and write down what would refute your result *before* you run it. A reported failure is more useful to me than a reported success.
>
> Thanks for reading,
> [name / role]

## Two-stage discipline

1. **Stage 1.** Send the residual in effect terms, the four-condition shape, and the five refuters. Ask what the recipient would count as a failure. Record their verdict **before** showing any theory.
2. **Stage 2, opt-in only.** If they are curious, offer the three links. Their later agreement is never pooled as corroboration, because by then they hold the frame (Fig 2 :40: *"agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool"*).
3. **Stage 3.** Offer the formula as a method to reuse, with the instruction that each user names a ground and writes their own refuter.

No trust request, no agreement request, no bulk send, no receptiveness ranking. All contact routes are institutional and opt-in, from `03_public_contact_points.csv`.
