# Supporting derivation: union, order, and isomers across schools of thought

**Mode:** `specified` on the structural claims, `general` on the order rule, not `grounded`. Simulated derivation checked against the figures (`05_what_shape_means.md` section 6), not a ground reading.

## RESIDUAL

The main audit (F1, F2) refuses the yes/no framing and substitutes a union of overlapping conditions. Two further questions are owed:

1. **Order.** In what order must the four-part separator be applied, and what breaks if the order is reversed?
2. **Structure.** Are the six schools isomers over a fixed atom set (Fig 12), or do they differ in atoms as well as bonds? What survives if the strong isomer claim fails?

## DOMAIN CENSUS

Atoms available inside a frame: the primitives a school treats as unbuilt (class, effective demand, fundamental uncertainty, dispersed knowledge, habit, gendered power, emergence, and the mainstream's optimizing agent, equilibrium, representative agent). Bonds available: the seven typed relations, including `+` conjunction (symmetric), `→` sequence (ordered), `⋛` comparison (threshold), `⇒` production, and the unary negation `¬`. No new primitive is available without import (Fig 6 :21).

Frames: seven, one mainstream and six heterodox. Currency: in-closure composition plus settlement. Anchor: an empirical discriminator per slot, never the frames' own agreement. Dependency order: describe residual, test closure difference, install anchor, import with provenance.

## DERIVATION

### Q1 What order is the separator, and what breaks if reversed?

- Fig 9 :53: "Residual first, frame second." Fig 9 :27-28: "describe it before you go looking, or any frame you pick will seem to fit." The sought-after frame cannot be chosen before the residual is described, because with an undescribed residual any frame fits. Applied here: "which school explains crisis" will fit any school; "which school predicts that a demand contraction persists after nominal wages are flexible" narrows the field.
- Formula step 2 routes a residual before minting: the first four bins sort what is *missing*, the fifth (REFUTED) sorts what is *wrong*, and "no arrangement of the other four reaches it." A school that claims a mainstream term is false owes a refusal (`¬`), not just another composition. The refusal is a unary operator and not a bond (Operators table :70-74), so a vocabulary with only `+` can accumulate schools but cannot carry a correction.
- Fig 3 :20: "The chain is ordered by dependency, not by importance." Fig 3 :19: "Optimising a link that already works cannot rescue one that does not." Most methodology debates spend effort at the method link, where instruments exist, while the broken link is the objective link: whether the question's own standard can be stated in terms that a ground can answer (Fig 3 :25-27).
- Fig 6 :24 adds a constraint on the last step: "Import from a live peer is debited against that frame's independence. A wrong atom costs more than starting from primitives."

**Order, fixed before the run:** (1) describe residual per slot; (2) refuse any held term that is wrong before composing a new one; (3) test closure difference at evidence level; (4) install a non-updating anchor; (5) import with source gate and shadow test; (6) keep verdicts per slot, never average. **Falsifier:** if a school's surplus can be demonstrated with the order reversed (frame first, residual described afterwards, and the same anchor), the order rule is decorative and should be dropped. The 2023 interview study's "methodological blindness" wording is the kind of frame-first claim that would trigger this test.

### Q2 Are the six schools isomers over a fixed atom set?

- Fig 12 :117: "isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour." The chemistry precedent is Fig 12 :97-98: silver fulminate and silver cyanate, "same composition and different properties," Liebig and Wöhler 1824 to 1826, named isomerism by Berzelius 1830.
- Fig 12 :123: "functional group. A substructure whose presence fixes the outcome. The group says what kind; the qualifier says which member."
- As the sources describe them, the schools do **not** hold the same atoms. Lee 2008 (reproduced by the AHE) says Marxists bring theories of class and the economic surplus, feminists a holistic account of gender, class and ethnicity, post-Keynesians effective demand, uncertainty and a monetary theory of production in real time, institutionalists a pragmatic approach to change. Those are different atom sets. The Economics Network handbook adds that Austrian economics is weak on power, which one of the six does not carry. By Fig 12's definition they are therefore **not** isomers as described.
- What they share is a **functional group**: an ordered composition of the form `historical process → social structure → agency`, or Fig 12 :123's "the group says what kind; the qualifier says which member." Lee 2008 names the shared kind: economics as "the science of the social provisioning process." That is a functional group, not an atom list.
- The **stronger, testable form**: if each school is rewritten over the full shared atom set {class, demand, uncertainty, knowledge, habit, gendered power, emergence, time}, differing only in which bond mediates which pair, then the schools **are** isomers: same atoms, different arrangement, different substance. That is the claim worth testing, because it would let a mainstream frame import one atom at a time instead of adopting a school wholesale (Fig 6 :14, "the only operator that moves the wall").
- **Falsifier, written before the run:** if any school requires an atom none of the others hold in a way that changes its prediction (not merely its vocabulary), the strong isomer claim fails and the functional-group reading stands. If, at the opposite extreme, all six reduce to one atom under one bond with no residual left, the union claim in F1 fails as well. Both outcomes are live; neither is assumed away.

### Q3 What is the analogue for "distinct but not surplus"?

Fig 8 :7 names the collapse "interdisciplinarity, no mint": an undirected union of two frames with different vocabularies but the same atoms. Fig 8 :48-49 says "Two frames that overlap almost completely are one frame wearing two names." In bond terms, the two schools are related by `≡` identity (Operators table :23-28: "two names, one referent" with the test "substitute one for the other. Is anything lost? No."), not by `⇒` production ("A produces B; B did not exist before") or `⋛` comparison. The methodological literature makes the same distinction with "complementary" versus "substitutive" plurality (Gräbner and Strunk, following Mäki 1997). A complement can be added without moving the wall; a substitute may move it, but only with provenance and a shadow test.

**Falsifier:** exhibit two schools whose different names track a genuinely different prediction that neither can derive from the other, with no shared dataset. The `≡` reading is then wrong for that pair and the surplus claim survives for it.

### Q4 What is the analogue for a school being "right" about a slot?

Fig 2 :45: "Frames range against the ground. They do not negotiate with it." Fig 8 :50-52: "two frames can hold the same atom by convergence, reaching it separately, which is corroboration at maximum independence." Fig 8 :51 says "Convergent derivation and adopted import look identical in the contents. Only the record of where each came from separates them."

So a school is not "right" because it is heterodox, nor because its account is richer in vocabulary. It is right at a slot when its prediction survives a non-updating ground and its derivation is independent of the frames it is being compared with. The record of provenance is what distinguishes convergence from borrowing. Lee 2008's observation that heterodox journals "cited each other so extensively that no single journal or subset of journals was isolated" is exactly why the six schools' mutual agreement cannot be counted as six witnesses.

**Falsifier:** show two schools that reach the same prediction from demonstrably non-overlapping evidence with separate derivations recorded before contact. That is maximum-independence corroboration and it should raise the verdict for both, not lower it for being correlated.

## CONCLUSION

The six schools are a union of overlapping conditions with a shared functional group (the social provisioning process in historical time), not a partition and not, as currently written, strict isomers. The strong isomer claim (all schools over one shared atom set, different bonds) is checkable and has a stated falsifier. The order of the separator is fixed by dependency: describe the residual, refuse wrong held terms, test closure difference, install an anchor, import with provenance, keep verdicts per slot. The bond types matter: `≡` names the rename failure, `+` the undirected union, `⋛` the threshold claim hidden in "miss," and `¬` the refusal that a merely additive pluralism cannot carry.
