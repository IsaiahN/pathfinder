## Restating the question as an effect

The question asks for a comparison: are the six named approaches genuinely different from mainstream models in what they can explain, and do they reach things mainstream models leave unregistered? Stated as an effect, the residual R is the set of phenomenon slots where a mainstream model returns "no term for this" while a heterodox frame returns a prediction. Three outcomes are possible, and the figures decide among them differently:

1. **Genuine surplus.** The closures differ at the evidence level, a specific residual is described, and a non-updating ground would settle it. Two frames together reach what neither reaches alone (Fig 8 :45).
2. **Mute union.** The closures differ but no residual is described, so "nothing was aimed; the union is mute" (Fig 8 :21). Adding schools adds vocabulary, not surplus.
3. **One frame wearing many names.** The schools draw on the same evidence pool, so "Two frames that overlap almost completely are one frame wearing two names" (Fig 8 :49). Their agreement or disagreement carries no information (Fig 13 :36-37).

What an answer would look like: not a single "yes" or "no", but a per-slot, per-school verdict stating which closure difference is real, which residual is described, which anchor would settle it, and which apparent distinctness is rename or mute union. Bind that as an ordinary term before the run (Formula, "Direction"), so the sought-for shape is refusable like anything else.

## What is deliberately not here

- No personal emails of named economists.
- No ranking of people by how likely they are to agree that heterodox economics has surplus.
- No bulk-send plan.
- No claim that a citation count, a consensus, or a school's own standard settles the surplus.

The figures' reasons:

1. **Agreement is not an anchor.** Fig 2 :35: "Alignment is triangulation, not negotiation. A mirror cannot correct you." Fig 2 :40: "disqualifying for a verdict: agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool." A list selected for receptiveness manufactures the correlation that would make its agreement worthless.
2. **A verifier must not hold the interiors.** Fig 10 :42: "A verifier that can reconstruct the claim cannot verify it." Sending the full framework before asking for a verdict converts possible outside frames into members of the frame being checked.
3. **A question cannot be settled by a frame scoring itself.** Fig 1 :26-27: "They are frame-internal. A frame cannot score itself with a quantity it also produces." The 2023 interview study reports that heterodox economists value realism and pluralism, which are their own standards, so their agreement about their own surplus is one pool talking.

Legally: scraping personal emails for unsolicited bulk outreach breaches GDPR and CAN-SPAM style rules in many jurisdictions. The venues in `03` are public, opt-in, and institutional, which avoids that practical failure as well as the structural one.
