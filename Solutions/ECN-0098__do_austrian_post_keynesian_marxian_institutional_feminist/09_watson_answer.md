## QUESTION

Decide, in effect terms, whether the six named approaches contain explanatory content that the mainstream frame cannot reach. Option A: answer yes, they offer distinct explanations mainstream models miss. Option B: answer no, the appearances are vocabulary differences already inside the mainstream reach. What each leads to: A without the figure gates sends a symmetric verdict dressed as an ordered one, counts six frames that share one evidence pool as six witnesses, and accepts a consensus anchor that updates on the profession's own influence. B without the gates ignores genuine closure unreachability documented in the field's own methodology literature. The figure-grounded third option is the separator: split the question into slots, describe each residual, test closure difference, install a non-updating anchor, keep frames independent, import with provenance, and keep verdicts per slot.

## DOMAIN CENSUS

R by arity, symmetry and scale, then one line per domain with actors, currency and anchor; partition or union; order by dependency (Fig 3).

- **R (the residual).** The set of phenomenon slots where a mainstream model returns no term and a heterodox frame returns a prediction. Arity: at least seven frames (six schools plus the mainstream reference), so not a two-body comparison. Symmetry: "distinct" is symmetric, "miss" is ordered, so the residual mixes two bonds and must be split (Fig 9 :4-5). Scale: first order inside a closure, second order across frames at the import boundary. Question type: union of overlapping conditions, not a partition (Fig 9 :54-57).
- **Domain 1, the mainstream research frame.** Actors: optimizing agents, representative agents, equilibrium, utility and profit maximization. Currency: in-closure prediction and formal consistency. Anchor: empirical fit claimed as the market test, but shown in F6 to be a pool that updates on the profession's own citations. Question type: one frame with a closure.
- **Domain 2, the six heterodox frames.** Actors: class, surplus, effective demand, fundamental uncertainty, dispersed knowledge, habit and routine, gendered power, care, emergence, nonlinearity. Currency: explanation of the historical process of social provisioning. Anchor: an open-system ground, the actual economy in historical time. Question type: union of overlapping conditions, not a partition.
- **Domain 3, methodology and verification.** Actors: methodologists, editors, citation networks, curricula, conference committees. Currency: agreement, residuals, citation counts. Anchor: a non-updating empirical discriminator, never consensus. Question type: union of conditions (quality control, communication, independence).
- **Domain 4, provenance and history of thought.** Actors: historical schools, founding problems, institutional homes. Currency: lineage rather than result. Anchor: the published record of who derived what first. Question type: a habitat to be enumerated, not composed.
- **Order by dependency.** Describe the residual per slot before choosing a frame (Fig 9 :27-28); refuse a wrong held term before minting (Formula step 2); test closure difference at the evidence level (Fig 8 :48-49); install an anchor that does not update (Fig 2 :29); import with source gate and shadow test, debited against independence (Fig 8 :12-13, Fig 6 :24).

## VERDICT (from the figures)

The question as written is refused; the separator stands. The six approaches are a union of overlapping conditions, not six rival answers, so verdicts are taken per phenomenon slot and never averaged. Distinctness is a necessary gate but not surplus: a rename is "one frame wearing two names" and an un-aimed union is mute. "Miss" has a literal reading as closure unreachability, so a genuinely new atom (class, gendered power, fundamental uncertainty, habit, emergence) requires an import from a differing closure, which is available but priced, provenanced and debited against independence. The market test of ideas fails the anchor test because it updates on the profession's own influence structure; the required anchor is a prediction that can fail in historical time. No figure line ranks the schools, so the literal yes/no is a `gap`, a verdict and not a silence. Mode `specified` per school and slot, `general` on the structure, ungrounded, candidate. The world sources corroborate the shape but share one methodology evidence pool, so their agreement is one witness, not twelve.

- **cost if wrong:** bounded and locatable. The packet states its mode, refuters and provenance before the conclusion, so an error is findable. The real cost is spending the independence of outside readers by sending a recording of a conclusion instead of a generator. Fig 2 :42 prices this: "Buy independence in proportion to the cost of being wrong." There is also a concrete cost if the separator is wrongly rejected: the method and communication warnings in Gräbner and Strunk would be overlooked, and a pluralism that is only topical would be mistaken for a plurality of methodologies.
- **undoable:** yes. The literal verdict can be revised to a per-slot ranking once a non-updating ground is brought; no primitive, school or claim has to be withdrawn for that. If one of the seven pre-registered falsifiers fires, the affected finding is retracted with its reason visible in `01` section 6 and the separator is re-censused. The functional-group-as-isomer temptation was already retracted and preserved rather than silently erased.
- **reversed by:** a ground reading, not agreement. A per-slot discriminator showing that a contested atom is unnecessary would reverse the unreachability verdict for that atom; a mainstream derivation of the atom before any heterodox contact would reverse it structurally; a demonstration that all six schools draw on one evidence pool with zero independence would reverse the surplus verdict; a single anchor that settles all seven frames with one reading would make the per-slot split unnecessary; a figure line ranking schools would close the gap. Until one of these fires, the verdict stands as `candidate`. Prestige, consensus and citation share are not such instruments.
