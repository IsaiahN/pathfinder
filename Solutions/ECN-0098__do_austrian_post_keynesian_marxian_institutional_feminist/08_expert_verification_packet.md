# Expert verification packet: economic methodology and schools of thought

**What this is:** a claim in a form an economic methodologist or historian of economic thought can check. Mode, derivation and refuters are stated before the conclusion, so a reader knows what to look for and what would settle it.

**Mode: `specified` on the per-school and per-slot claims, `general` on the structure, not `grounded`.** No economic measurement is claimed. This is a separator and a route, not a result. It is a simulated derivation checked for internal consistency against the verbatim figures, not a ground reading.

## 1. The claim, in one paragraph

The question "Do Austrian, Post-Keynesian, Marxian, institutional, feminist and complexity approaches offer distinct explanations that mainstream models miss?" cannot be answered with a single yes or no. It is a union of overlapping conditions, so verdicts must be taken per phenomenon slot and never averaged. Distinctness is necessary but not sufficient: different names for the same atom are one frame wearing two names, and a union with no described residual is mute. "Miss" has a literal structural reading: composition inside a closure never adds a primitive, so an atom such as class, gendered power, fundamental uncertainty or an institutional habit is unreachable from the mainstream closure without an import from a frame whose closure differs. That import is possible but priced; it needs provenance and a shadow test, and it is debited against independence. The profession's own citation-weighted consensus cannot serve as the anchor because it updates on what the frames think. The framework returns the shape any answer must have, the per-school verdicts, and falsifiers written in advance. No figure line ranks the schools, so the literal question is a declared gap.

## 2. Three checkable sub-claims

### C1 Framework-internal: the yes/no framing is a union error, and distinctness is not surplus

- **Statement:** the question bundles several well-formed questions; verdicts must be indexed per slot, and a difference in vocabulary or professional association does not establish a surplus.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 9 :4-5: "Disagreements that do not shrink with effort, where each rule keeps working well on a different subset, are not one hard question but several well-formed ones. Split it rather than search." Fig 9 :57: "a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either." Fig 1 :19: "The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated." Fig 8 :21: "nothing was aimed; the union is mute." Fig 8 :49: "Two frames that overlap almost completely are one frame wearing two names."
- **What to check:** are the six approaches better described as overlapping conditions than as six rival answers to one question? Lee 2008 (New Palgrave, reproduced by the AHE) says the multiple heterodox critiques of a single mainstream proposition "overlap in argumentation" and that school boundaries are sometimes "not there at all." ICAPE lists neoclassical economics as one tradition among many and says pluralism is not "anything goes."
- **Refuter:** exhibit one mechanism that generates all six schools' claims as special cases with no residual left, or exhibit a global surplus scalar that does not lose which slot is live. Either breaks the union or per-slot claim.

### C2 Economics-facing: "miss" is closure unreachability, and the market test is not an anchor

- **Statement:** a mainstream closure cannot compose certain heterodox atoms from inside itself; the common "market test of ideas" defence fails because it updates on the profession's own influence structure.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 6 :21: "MINT composes inside the closure and can never add an atom." Fig 8 :32: "no search, however long, adds an atom." Fig 6 :23: "No frame escapes its own closure. The bound moves only on contact with a frame whose closure differs." Fig 2 :29: "An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks." Fig 2 :33: "The anchor must not update." Fig 2 :40: "disqualifying for a verdict: agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool."
- **What to check:** Mearman, Berger and Guizzo 2023 (Journal of Economic Issues 57(4)) report a "methodological blindness": mainstream economics "does not study things that are very relevant in economic reality because it does not have the method to do it." Gräbner and Strunk (Journal of Economic Methodology) find that under 3 percent of mainstream citations go to heterodox work and that the level playing field assumed by the market-test argument does not exist because of path dependence and asymmetric citation. Do those descriptions match your own reading of the literature?
- **Refuter:** derive one of the contested atoms from inside the mainstream closure before any heterodox contact, or show a case where adoption was demonstrably independent of the profession's prior influence structure. Either weakens the claim for that atom or that case.

### C3 Derivation and import: the record separates convergence from borrowing, and the schools are a functional group rather than isomers as written

- **Statement:** a school is credited at a slot only when its prediction survives a non-updating ground and its derivation is independent of the frames it is compared with; imports must carry provenance; the six schools share a functional group, not an atom list.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 8 :50-52: "two frames can hold the same atom by convergence, reaching it separately, which is corroboration at maximum independence" and "Only the record of where each came from separates them, and the surplus lives only where the closures differ." Fig 6 :24: "Import from a live peer is debited against that frame's independence. A wrong atom costs more than starting from primitives." Fig 12 :123: "functional group. A substructure whose presence fixes the outcome. The group says what kind; the qualifier says which member." Fig 12 :117: "Same atoms, different bond, different substance."
- **What to check:** Lee 2008 records dense cross-citation among the eight principal heterodox journals, so the six schools are not independent of one another; he also names the shared functional group, economics as "the science of the social provisioning process." O'Hara 2002, quoted there, assigns different contributions to Marxists (class, surplus), feminists (gender, class, ethnicity), post-Keynesians (effective demand, uncertainty, real time) and institutionalists (change, policy). Is that an atom difference or only a vocabulary difference in your view?
- **Refuter:** show two schools reaching the same prediction from demonstrably non-overlapping evidence with separate derivations recorded before contact, which is maximum-independence corroboration, or show that all six reduce to one atom under one bond with no residual left, which breaks the union.

## 3. What is explicitly not claimed

- Not that any school wins, or that all six have surplus.
- Not that mainstream economists are careless or hostile. Unreachability is a property of a closure.
- Not that citation counts, curriculum share or consensus are evidence. They are "efficient for a convention" (Fig 2 :41), not a verdict.
- Not that the schools are strict isomers as currently written. They share a functional group; the strong isomer claim needs a fixed atom set and has a falsifier in `06` Q2.
- Not that this folder is a ground reading. It is a simulated derivation checked for internal consistency only.
- Not that the framework can certify its own completeness. Fig 8 :34-37 gives the metaframe limits; Fig 9 :44: "Only a sealed room can be searched to the end, and no frame is a sealed room."
- Not `grounded`. Nothing here has been settled by an economic measurement.

## 5. What would settle it

A per-slot discriminator, not a further argument. From *The Hard Problem is About Source*: an instrument that reads green "cannot comment on the source"; the spectrometer resolved it. Here:

1. A prediction that fails if a contested heterodox atom is unnecessary, tested on a dataset neither camp used to build its model.
2. A threshold test: does a `⋛` comparison hold between two frames' predictions at a specified magnitude, rather than both merely being present?
3. A convergence test with derivations recorded before contact (Fig 8 :50-52).
4. An import test: is the provenance and shadow test recorded when a mainstream model absorbs a heterodox atom (Fig 8 :12-13)?
5. Seven standing falsifiers are written before the conclusion in `01_figure_grounded_audit.md` section 3.

## 6. What an expert can check in twenty minutes

1. Read Fig 9 :4-5 and :57, Fig 1 :19, and Fig 8 :21 and :49. Decide whether the union and per-slot reading is faithful to those lines.
2. Read Fig 6 :21 and :23, Fig 8 :32, and Fig 2 :29 and :33. Decide whether "miss as unreachability" and "anchor must not update" are figure-derived or merely asserted.
3. Check C2 against your own knowledge of the methodology literature: does the Mearman/Berger/Guizzo and Gräbner/Strunk evidence support the claim that the market test is a mirror rather than an anchor?
4. Name one instrument that would discriminate, at a specific phenomenon slot, whether a heterodox atom is needed, or say why none is possible.
5. Send the refutation. A failure is more useful than an endorsement.
