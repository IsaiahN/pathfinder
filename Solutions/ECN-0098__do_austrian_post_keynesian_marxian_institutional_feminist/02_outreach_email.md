**Subject:** Check one separator before deciding whether heterodox economics has explanatory surplus

> Hi [name],
>
> I am writing about a question in economic methodology: do Austrian, Post-Keynesian, Marxian, institutional, feminist and complexity approaches explain things that mainstream models miss? I have one specific output I would like you to check against what you already know. I am not asking you to read a theory first, and I am not asking you to agree with a position on pluralism. If the output does not survive, there is nothing else to look at.
>
> **The output (`specified` on the per-school claims, `general` on the structure, not `grounded`):** the question as posed is a union of overlapping conditions, not one question with a yes/no answer. Each named approach is a frame with its own primitives and its own closure. An apparent "miss" is structural: composition inside a closure never adds a new primitive, so a genuinely new explanatory atom (class, gendered power, fundamental uncertainty, an institutional habit, emergence) is unreachable from inside the mainstream closure unless something is imported from a frame whose closure differs. That is a claim about what a closure can compose, not a claim that mainstream economists are careless.
>
> But being different is not enough. A union whose closures differ but with no described residual is mute. Adding a school can add vocabulary without adding an atom. So the checkable output is a four-part separator, applied per phenomenon slot:
> 1. describe the residual in effect terms before choosing any school;
> 2. show the school's closure differs at the evidence level, not only in wording or professional association;
> 3. name a non-updating ground (a prediction that can fail in historical time) that would settle the slot;
> 4. keep the schools independent, because agreement from one shared evidence pool is one witness, not several.
>
> **What I am not claiming:** that all six schools have surplus, that any one wins, that a citation count or a curriculum share settles anything, or that this note settles the question. No figure line in the source framework ranks schools, so the literal yes/no is recorded as a gap. I also cannot claim this is a ground reading: it is a simulated derivation checked for internal consistency, so it cannot settle what it reports on. What it offers is the shape any answer must have and the refuters written in advance.
>
> **What would refute the separator:**
> 1. a school whose "distinct explanation" is a pure renaming of a mainstream atom with the same evidence source;
> 2. a mainstream model that derives one of the contested atoms from inside its own closure, before any contact with the heterodox frame;
> 3. a demonstration that the apparent surplus comes from one shared evidence pool, so independence is zero;
> 4. a non-updating ground that settles all seven frames with one reading, so no per-slot verdict is needed.
>
> I wrote these down before sending this. If any holds, the affected part is withdrawn. A one-line "this fails because..." is more useful to me than agreement. If you would rather not engage, no reply is needed.
>
> **If it survives, and only then:** the sources are below. The figures, not my summary, are the authority.
> - The canonical figures text with line numbers (local extract): `FRAMEWORK/reference/03-FIGURES_TEXT.md` in the project folder.
> - Lee 2008, "Heterodox Economics," New Palgrave entry, reproduced publicly by the Association for Heterodox Economics: https://hetecon.net/about-the-ahe/heterodox-economics/
> - Gräbner and Strunk, "Pluralism in economics: its critiques and their lessons," Journal of Economic Methodology: https://www.exploring-economics.org/en/discover/pluralism-in-economics-its-critiques-and-lessons
> - Mearman, Berger and Guizzo 2023, "What is Heterodox Economics? Insights from Interviews with Leading Thinkers," Journal of Economic Issues 57(4): https://ideas.repec.org/a/mes/jeciss/v57y2023i4p1119-1141.html
>
> **If you want to use the machinery:** the eight-step formula is in the framework files. Run it on a problem you know well and can check against a ground, write down what would refute your result *before* you run it, and state the mode of what you get (general, specified, grounded). A reported failure is more useful to me than a reported success.
