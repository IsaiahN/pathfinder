## 4. Round trip gap R_T

The Symbols table defines R_T as the gap between what was sent up and what came back down, `gap(x, (T_E ∘ T_A)(x))`, measuring what the coarser description cannot hold. Fig 4 :30 requires it to be measured rather than assumed. I ran the round trip on the condensation the folder actually uses.

2. **Abstraction (T_A):** the shorthand carried in `07` and `09`: "union not partition; distinct is not surplus; miss is unreachability; anchor must not update; keep frames independent; import with provenance; verdict per slot."
3. **Concretisation (T_E):** the concrete question the folder would have to answer, "yes, heterodox approaches explain what mainstream models miss," or "no, they do not."

**R_T is nonzero.** Five things the shorthand holds do not survive the trip back down:

- the **bond-type difference** between symmetric "distinct" and ordered "miss," which a yes/no answer erases (F1, `06` Q1);
- the **per-slot indexing**, which an averaged verdict destroys (Fig 1 :19-22);
- the **provenance and shadow test** on imports, which a simple "integration of heterodox ideas" omits (Fig 8 :50-52);
- the **anchor requirement**, which a citation-weighted consensus violates while looking like an anchor (Fig 2 :29, :33-34);
- the **gap itself**, which a forced yes/no would convert into a fabricated ranking (Fig 9 :32, Fig 13 :134).

Conversely, one thing survives cleanly: a genuine surplus requires differing closures at the evidence level, a described residual, an unmoved anchor, and independent frames. That is the residue worth sending. R_T was **not** measured numerically, because no economic ground reading exists in this simulated run; it is recorded qualitatively as the enumerated set of dropped constraints. Treating "the formula is the condensed version of the figures" as identity would set R_T to zero and would be the dead-reckoning error Fig 4 :30 names.
