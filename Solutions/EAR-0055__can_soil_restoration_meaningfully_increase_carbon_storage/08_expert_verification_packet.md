# Expert verification packet - EAR-0055

**What this is:** a claim in a form a soil scientist, agronomist or hydrologist can check. Mode, derivation and refuters are stated before the conclusion.

**Mode: `specified` on the empirical synthesis, `general` on the structural shape, not `grounded`.** No paired field measurement was run for this packet. This is a simulated derivation checked for internal consistency against the figures and the sources opened this session.

**Status:** a simulated derivation, not a ground reading. It cannot settle the question it reports on.

## 1. The claim, in one paragraph

"Can soil restoration meaningfully increase carbon storage while also improving food production and water quality?" is a union compound of three slots, not one question. Each slot needs its own anchor: paired soil coring and laboratory analysis for carbon, harvest measurement for yield, nutrient and sediment loads at an outlet for water quality. The three signs can diverge, so no composite score is admissible. The effect must be enumerated to third order, from plot carbon to off-site nitrate to the receiving water body. A restoration bundle is a molecule rather than a set, so the order and arrangement of practices change the outcome. The figures deliver that shape and a pre-registered refuter. The empirical signs reported from the literature are conditional and stay `specified` until a paired multi-site measurement settles them.

## 2. Three checkable sub-claims

### C1 - framework-internal; checkable by reading Figures 1, 2 and 9

- **Statement:** three slots, three anchors, no aggregate score; unbundle before searching.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 1 :19 - *"The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated."* Fig 1 :22 - *"Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average."* Fig 2 :29-30 - *"An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks. A measurement, an experiment, a counter, a market, a patient outcome."* Fig 9 :54 - *"And when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again."* Fig 9 :57-58 - *"a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either. It stops being a mixture and becomes a specific compound."*
- **Refuter:** a validated composite index whose components move independently and whose aggregate still preserves every live slot signal; or a demonstration that one instrument clears all three conditions across contrasting soils and climates.

### C2 - soil-science facing; checkable against the paired-observation literature

- **Statement:** carbon gains are real in many settings but finite and context-dependent; no reliable carbon-yield win-win has been demonstrated in the temperate paired data; water-quality effects depend on the specific lever, not on "restoration" as a class.
- **What to check:** Jordon et al. 2022 (Environmental Research Letters, DOI 10.1088/1748-9326/ac8609) report significant SOC increases for reduced tillage and ley-arable rotations but no effect of cover crops, no yield reduction during cropping years, and no evidence of a win-win. Patil et al. 2025 (Scientific Reports, DOI 10.1038/s41598-025-12149-6) report positive SOC effects across 147 Indian studies, strongest beyond five years. Thapa et al. 2018 (Journal of Environmental Quality, DOI 10.2134/jeq2018.03.0107) report about 56 percent reduction in nitrate leaching with nonleguminous cover crops. Liang et al. 2025 (EGU abstract, DOI 10.5194/egusphere-egu25-12605) report higher topsoil SOC under no-till alongside higher nitrate leaching. Are those characterisations faithful to the papers?
- **Figure line bearing on the check:** Fig 11 :50 - *"The same actor is a benefactor to one residual and a malfactor to another, so mutualism and parasitism are relations rather than kinds."*
- **Refuter:** a pre-registered multi-site trial showing mean SOC change indistinguishable from zero after baseline, depth and equivalent-mass correction; or a measured case where any carbon gain requires a significant yield or water-quality loss with no bundle clearing the intersection.

### C3 - framework-internal derivation with a physical consequence

- **Statement:** a restoration bundle is a molecule, not a set; the operative bonds are ordered, so timing and arrangement change the outcome; the effect propagates from plot to outlet.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 12 :5 - *"A set of ingredients is not one. The bond is the rest of the information, and it is the half nothing was recording."* Fig 12 :74 - *"swap them. Does it become a different thing? Yes."* Fig 11 :52-53 - *"Scoping the habitat to direct contact is how DDT reached the eagles: first-order enumeration, second-order effect, and the residual that mattered was three steps out."*
- **Refuter:** a factorial trial showing that swapping practice order has no detectable effect on any slot, and that two bundles with the same practices in different arrangements give the same three-slot outcome within measurement error. In that case the bond is symmetric and this sub-claim is withdrawn.

## 5. What would settle it

1. A pre-registered, multi-site factorial trial with paired carbon, yield and outlet-water measurement, order of practices controlled, sampling below 0.3 m and equivalent soil mass used.
2. A measurement that discriminates whether the yield response at low SOC is caused by carbon itself or by associated structure, water and nutrient changes.
3. A third-order measurement at the receiving water body.
4. An audit of the evidence pools behind the cited reviews, to see whether there is more than one independent room.

## 6. What an expert can check in twenty minutes

1. Read Fig 1 :19 and :22, Fig 2 :29-30, Fig 9 :54 and :57-58, Fig 11 :50 and :52-53, Fig 12 :5 and :74. Nine short passages.
2. Check C2 against the four DOI records listed there. Are the reported signs and effect sizes stated correctly?
3. Name one instrument that would measure carbon, yield and outlet-water load in the same trial, or say why none exists.
4. Send the refutation. A failure is more useful than an endorsement.
