## Residual in effect terms

The question asks whether one intervention class (soil restoration) can produce three outcomes at once: a measurable increase in soil organic carbon stock, a non-decrease or increase in food production, and an improvement in water quality. Option A says yes, meaningfully and generally. Option B says yes only conditionally, and that the three outcomes must be measured separately because they can move in different directions. What an answer would look like is bound as an ordinary term here: a per-slot table of sign and magnitude for carbon, yield and water, each with its own anchor, plus a statement of which conditions make the three signs coincide. That binding follows the formula's Direction rule, which says to make the sought-for shape an ordinary term so it can accrue a residual like anything else (local: `FRAMEWORK/figures/THE_FORMULA.md`).

## What an answer would look like, and what it does not

The figure-derived shape is: this is a **union compound**, not one hard question, so it must be unbundled before it is searched; each slot needs its own instrument; agreement among reviews is not a witness unless they draw on different evidence. The empirical signs come from world sources, not from the figures. The figures decide the shape of a checkable answer. They do not decide the sign of a field measurement.

## What is deliberately not here

- No personal emails of named individuals.
- No ranking of people by how likely they are to agree with a conclusion.
- No bulk-send plan.
- No claim that this folder settled the empirical question. Fig 2 :29-30: *"An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks. A measurement, an experiment, a counter, a market, a patient outcome."* The anchor here is a paired field measurement, which this folder did not run.
