# Bundles, order and analogues

**Mode: `specified` on the structure, `general` on the shape.** This is a simulated derivation checked against the figures, not a ground reading (see `05_what_shape_means.md` section 6).

## RESIDUAL

"Soil restoration" is used as if it were one thing. It is a bundle of practices. Does the bundle behave like a set, where order and arrangement do not matter, or like a molecule, where the bond carries information the ingredients do not? And what is the right analogue for the three outcomes: parts of one substance, or different substances from the same atoms?

## DOMAIN CENSUS

Atoms held in this question: reduced tillage, cover crops, organic amendments, residue retention, ley-arable phases, agroforestry. Bonds available: the seven named in Fig 12, including symmetric conjunction and ordered sequence. Currency: a measured outcome in one of three slots. Anchor: the instrument for that slot. The habitat (soil type, climate, slope, season) is enumerated, not composed (Fig 11 :43).

## DERIVATION

### Q1 - Is a bundle a set or a molecule?

Fig 12 :5: **"A set of ingredients is not one. The bond is the rest of the information, and it is the half nothing was recording."**

Fig 12 :46: **"A bond is the typed relation between two operands. There are seven, they are not interchangeable, and which one holds is not recoverable from the operands."**

The literature read this session supports the molecule reading. Patil et al. (2025) found that synergistic combinations, such as farmyard manure with cover crops or conservation tillage, were more effective than standalone practices. Minasny et al. (2017) frame the question as what bundle of best management practices can reach a rate, not which single ingredient does. Jordon et al. (2022) found no effect of cover crops on SOC in their dataset but positive effects of reduced tillage and ley-arable rotations. Same broad goal, different atoms and bonds, different substance. **A bundle is a molecule, not a shopping list.**

### Q2 - Which bonds are in play, and does order matter?

Fig 12 :59 (conjunction, symmetric): **"swap the operands. Does the meaning change? No."**
Fig 12 :74 (sequence, ordered): **"swap them. Does it become a different thing? Yes."**
Fig 12 :78 (production, ordered): **"is there a B before A fires? No."**

A cover crop must establish before the cash crop is planted; the nitrogen it takes up must be present during the non-growing season. Liang et al. (2025) found that cover crops reduce nitrate leaching by taking up soil inorganic nitrogen in the non-growing season, and that planting date and shoot biomass were drivers of effectiveness. Thapa et al. (2018) likewise identified planting date as a driver. That is sequence (`→`) and production (`⇒`), not conjunction (`+`). Swapping the order changes the substance. If the practices were combined under a symmetric bond, order would not matter and these drivers would not exist.

### Q3 - Are restoration systems isomers or a functional group?

Fig 12 :117: **"isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."**
Fig 12 :124: **"functional group. A substructure whose presence fixes the outcome. The group says what kind; the qualifier says which member."**

Two bundles over the same practice set, for example reduced tillage plus organic amendments versus cover crops plus organic amendments, share atoms but differ in the bond and in the resulting three-slot outcome. Under Fig 12 that is an **isomer** claim: same ingredients, different behaviour, and the difference is not visible if you record only the ingredient list.

Across studies that use different ingredients but the same kind of structure (soil cover plus organic input plus reduced disturbance), the right term is the weaker **functional group**: the group fixes the kind of outcome, the qualifier picks the member. This matters because it tells us what to compare. A comparison of two different bundles is an isomer test; a comparison of a bundle against bare conventional practice is a functional-group test. Conflating them is a type error.

### Q4 - Order of cascade, not just order of practices

Fig 11 :52-53: **"Scoping the habitat to direct contact is how DDT reached the eagles: first-order enumeration, second-order effect, and the residual that mattered was three steps out."**

The three slots have a physical order of propagation:

1. first order, plot: SOC stock and soil structure change;
2. second order, field and edge: nitrate and sediment move off-site;
3. third order, basin and receptor: loading, water treatment and aquatic condition.

A practice can be positive at order one and negative at order two or three. Liang et al. (2025) is exactly this: higher topsoil SOC alongside higher nitrate leaching, which is why they modelled a 492,000 km2 basin rather than stopping at the plot. Fig 12 :112: **"A plan is a sequence. A goal is a comparison. Progress is a subtraction."** Progress in this question is the subtraction of a residual per slot, not a single additive total.

### Q5 - What the experiments must look like

If bundles are molecules, then the minimum experiment is a factorial one: practices varied and combined, order controlled, and all three slots measured at plot and outlet. Jordon et al. (2022) did this for SOC and yield (paired observations) but not for water. Liang et al. (2025) added water and yield through modelling rather than a paired field design across all three. The missing instrument is the third slot measured paired with the first two in the same trial.

## FALSIFIER, WRITTEN BEFORE THE CONCLUSION

The isomer and ordered-bond reading fails if a pre-registered factorial trial shows that (a) swapping the order of practices has no detectable effect on any of the three slots, and (b) two bundles with the same practices but different arrangement produce the same three-slot outcome within measurement error. In that case the bond is symmetric, "soil restoration" really is a set, and the bundle analysis in this file is withdrawn. A second, independent falsifier: an off-site measurement showing no third-order effect, which would collapse the cascade order in Q4.

## CONCLUSION

A restoration bundle is a molecule, not a set. The operative bonds are ordered (sequence and production), so the arrangement and timing of practices change the outcome. Bundles built from the same practices in different arrangements are isomers under Fig 12; bundles sharing only a structural motif are a functional group. The effect propagates in a three-order cascade, so a plot-level carbon gain does not settle the water slot. The figure deliverable is a design constraint on the experiment, not a prediction of which bundle wins.
