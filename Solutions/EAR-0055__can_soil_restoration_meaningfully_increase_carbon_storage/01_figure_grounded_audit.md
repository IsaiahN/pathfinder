## 0. The claim under audit

> Can soil restoration meaningfully increase carbon storage while also improving food production and water quality?
>
> Option A: yes, generally, and one practice class delivers the three outcomes together.
> Option B: yes only conditionally; the three outcomes are separate slots with separate anchors, they can move in different directions, and the joint case is a compound that needs its own loop and its own ground.

What an answer would look like is written down before the search, per the formula's Direction rule: a three-row sign table (carbon stock, yield, water quality), each row with a measured magnitude, a named instrument and a named condition set, plus an explicit statement of the overlap cases where one lever clears one slot and leaves the intersections standing. This is the bound ordinary term. It is refusable by the same test as everything else.

---

## 1. Residual in effect terms

R is the gap between what is claimed and what is measured when a restoration practice is applied.

- **Arity: three conditions, not one.** Carbon stock, food production and water quality are three quantities with three different instruments. They are not three readings of one quantity: a tonne of carbon, a tonne of yield and a nitrate concentration in mg/L answer different questions (Fig 13 :35-36, applied by analogy to the outcome set: *"Space, time and energy are not three readings of one quantity."* Used as a shape, not as a physical claim).
- **Symmetry: asymmetric.** The three quantities do not commute under intervention. A no-till change can raise topsoil carbon while increasing nitrate leaching (Liang, Zhang and Zhao 2025, read via Crossref record). Cover crops can cut nitrate leaching by about 56 percent (Thapa, Mirsky and Tully 2018) but can also raise evapotranspiration and worsen water availability in a dry year (Liang et al. 2025). The order in which practices are bundled changes the outcome, so the bond between them is ordered, not symmetric.
- **Scale: three scales at once.** Carbon stock changes over 5 to 20 years and saturates; yield is read annually; water quality responds to single storm events. Plot scale, field scale and watershed scale do not give the same answer, because third-order effects cross the plot boundary (Fig 11 :52-53).

## 2. Domain census before choosing any figure line

One line per domain: actors, currency, anchor. A domain with no nameable anchor is not a domain yet.

| domain | actors | currency | anchor (what settles it) |
|---|---|---|---|
| D1 Soil carbon stock | soil microbes, roots, mycorrhizal networks, clay minerals, organic amendments, tillage implements | change in SOC stock per hectare at fixed depth or equivalent soil mass | repeated paired soil coring plus laboratory carbon analysis, before and after, over years |
| D2 Food production | crops, farmers, pollinators, pests, fertiliser and seed inputs | yield per hectare and yield stability across seasons | harvest measurement over multiple seasons, nutrition where relevant |
| D3 Water quality | rainfall, runoff, nitrate, phosphorus, sediment, pesticides, receiving streams | concentration and load of nutrients and sediment | paired water sampling at a defined outlet, event and baseflow |
| D4 Habitat and conditions (carrier, not a verdict domain) | climate, soil texture, slope, season, topography, land history, market access | contact conditions, not an outcome currency | weather station, soil survey, land-use record |

**Partition or union.** This is a **union**, not a partition. The three outcome domains share a habitat and their conditions overlap:

- D1 and D2 share the carbon-yield coupling (yield tends to rise with SOC at low concentrations, see Jordon et al. 2022 introduction).
- D1 and D3 share erosion and dissolved organic carbon loss.
- D2 and D3 share nutrient management and runoff.
- The triple intersection is the whole-farm or whole-watershed system.

Fig 9 :57-58: *"a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either. It stops being a mixture and becomes a specific compound."* With three conditions the same rule holds with more force. Each overlap is a compound with its own loop and its own ground; it is not a harder case of any single outcome.

**Order by dependency (Fig 3).** The chain is ordered by what consumes what, not by importance.

1. Perception and instrument: can each slot be measured at all? Fig 3 :27: *"A link with no instrument is not a link that works. It is a link nobody has looked at."*
2. Vocabulary: terms for what a practice affords (sequestration, yield stability, nitrate load).
3. Objective: what counts as progress in each currency, stated separately.
4. Planning: which bundle of practices to try under incomplete information.
5. Learn and carry: name what worked, verify it against the ground, carry the method.

Fig 3 :19: *"Optimising a link that already works cannot rescue one that does not."* A better carbon model cannot rescue a missing water-quality instrument.

## 4. Findings

### F1 - It is a union compound, so unbundle before searching (decisive on shape)

Fig 9 :55-56:

> **"A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones. A single lever clears the region where only one condition holds and leaves the intersections standing, because the second condition still binds."**

Fig 9 :54:

> **"And when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again."**

Fig 9 :57-58:

> **"a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either. It stops being a mixture and becomes a specific compound."**

"Soil restoration" bundles reduced tillage, cover crops, organic amendments, residue retention, ley-arable phases and agroforestry. These are not one lever. Jordon et al. (2022) found significant SOC increases for reduced tillage and ley-arable rotations but **no effect of cover crops** on SOC in their temperate dataset, and Liang et al. (2025) found that no-till raised topsoil SOC while nitrate leaching also rose. A lever that clears carbon can leave water standing. Status: **triangulated** across Fig 9 and two low-overlap world results (a paired-observation meta-analysis and a watershed modelling abstract). **Refuter:** one practice, with one instrument, shown to clear all three conditions across contrasting soil and climate contexts in a pre-registered multi-site trial.

### F2 - Keep the residual per slot; never average it into one score

Fig 1 :19: **"The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated."**
Fig 1 :22: **"Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average."**

A composite "soil health index" would let a carbon gain hide a nitrate loss. The honest output is three rows with three signs. Jordon et al. (2022) explicitly required **paired** SOC and yield observations, because syntheses of one outcome or the other cannot identify synergy or trade-off. Status: **single-source** for the figure line (Fig 1), corroborated by the paired-observation design in Jordon et al. **Refuter:** a validated composite index whose components demonstrably move independently and whose aggregate still preserves every live slot signal.

### F3 - The anchor is measurement, not review agreement

Fig 2 :29-30:

> **"An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks. A measurement, an experiment, a counter, a market, a patient outcome."**

Fig 2 :35: **"Alignment is triangulation, not negotiation. A mirror cannot correct you."**

The FAO protocol for measurement, monitoring, reporting and verification of soil organic carbon in agricultural landscapes (FAO, 2020, DOI 10.4060/cb0509en, title and year verified via Crossref) is the institutional form of this anchor: an agreed measurement procedure, not an agreed opinion. A review that cites the same underlying trials is one pool talking. Status: **triangulated** (Fig 2 plus the MRV protocol). **Refuter:** a soil-carbon metric whose reported value changes when a reviewer pushes on it, or a demonstration that the MRV procedure does not settle the stock question.

### F4 - Enumerate contact to third order; the off-site effect is the residual

Fig 11 :52-53:

> **"Scoping the habitat to direct contact is how DDT reached the eagles: first-order enumeration, second-order effect, and the residual that mattered was three steps out."**

Fig 11 :40: **"An improvement that does not change contact changes nothing, however much it improves."**

A plot-scale carbon gain is first-order contact. Nitrate moving to a stream is second order. A change in downstream drinking-water treatment cost or aquatic ecology is third order. Thapa et al. (2018) measured nitrate leaching into freshwater, not just soil nitrogen; Liang et al. (2025) modelled a 492,000 km2 basin because plot results did not settle watershed loading. Status: **triangulated** (Fig 11 plus two hydrology-facing sources). **Refuter:** a complete habitat enumeration showing no off-site or third-order effect for a given practice in a given watershed, with a measurement at the outlet.

### F5 - No standing list of good practices; valence is relative to the residual

Fig 11 :49: **"An actor's valence is relative to the residual, not a property it carries."**
Fig 11 :50: **"The same actor is a benefactor to one residual and a malfactor to another, so mutualism and parasitism are relations rather than kinds."**

Cover crops are a benefactor to the nitrate-leaching residual (Thapa et al. 2018, about 56 percent reduction) and can be a malfactor to the water-availability residual in a dry year (Liang et al. 2025). Manure-based amendments gave the largest SOC gains in Patil et al. (2025), but biochar's high effect size came largely from short-term studies with high variability. There is no practice that is good for all three slots in all habitats. Status: **triangulated** (Fig 11 plus conflicting world results across regions). **Refuter:** a practice with positive signs in all three slots across contrasting agro-ecological zones in a pre-registered design.

### F6 - Two currencies: a cheap description is not an expensive measurement

Fig 12 :125: **"A composition costs in two currencies, and they do not add."**
Fig 12 :126: **"Bits price the description. Actions price finding out whether it holds."**

It is cheap to state "soil restoration increases carbon" and expensive to settle it: paired coring, equivalent soil mass, a decade of monitoring. Minasny et al. (2017) note that most SOC studies sample only topsoil (up to 0.3 m), while the 4 per mille headline number was computed over the whole global profile. The description and the measurement price different things. Status: **single-source** (Fig 12), corroborated by the depth mismatch documented in Minasny et al. **Refuter:** a cheap, fast, validated measurement that settles stock change at scale without a corresponding action cost.

### F7 - Finite capacity, and the strain it creates

Fig 13 :14: **"conserved, finite, directionless alone"** (the state "energy").

This line is strained when applied to a carbon stock. Soil organic carbon is not the conserved quantity of thermodynamics. I record the strain rather than hide it: the empirical saturation result comes from world sources, not from Fig 13. Minasny et al. (2017): *"areas which have reached equilibrium will not be able to further increase their sequestration."* Patil et al. (2025) report rapid early gains followed by plateau, and Hatano and Iwasaki (2026) report a negative sequestration rate once the SOC to clay ratio passes about 1/13. The figure line offers a shape (finiteness) but does not govern the empirical magnitude. Status: **single-source and strained for the figure; triangulated in the world literature**. **Refuter:** a site showing unbounded SOC accumulation with no plateau under a fixed practice.

### F8 - SEARCHED first, then INWARD and OUTWARD, kept distinct

Formula step 7 (local `THE_FORMULA.md`) asks SEARCHED, INWARD, OUTWARD in that order. Fig 6 :29:

> **"inward: is our representation adequate to hold it? The instrument extends access. Fails by refining forever on an axis that cannot carry it. Sharpen in our own terms."**

Fig 6 :30:

> **"outward: does another frame already hold it? The description travels. Fails by being sharpened into terms only we can read. Cast it in effect terms."**

- **SEARCHED:** yes. There is a substantial literature; meta-analyses exist (Jordon 2022, Patil 2025, Thapa 2018). Searching more of the same pool will not fix the disagreement about co-benefits.
- **INWARD:** change the instrument. Sample below 0.3 m, use equivalent soil mass, pair water sampling with carbon measurement, lengthen the time series.
- **OUTWARD:** import from hydrology (nitrate loading, evapotranspiration), agronomy (practice bundles), and accounting or finance (the cost and verification of claims, Medeckytė-Žydelė 2026). INWARD and OUTWARD are opposed and must not be read against each other (Fig 6 :31).

Status: **general**, derived from the formula and Fig 6. **Refuter:** a single closed frame that settles all three slots without either a sharpened instrument or an imported frame.

### F9 - Cost and expertise are filters, never witnesses

Fig 9 :60: **"Use filters for the budget and witnesses for the verdict. Never let a filter hand you a verdict."**

A cost ranking of practices (biochar expensive, manure cheaper) decides what gets tried first. It decides nothing about whether carbon rose. Medeckytė-Žydelė (2026) notes that an SOC-linked financial reporting framework faces *"implementation challenges due to cost intensity, technical expertise requirements and the need for region-specific adaptations."* That is a budget statement, not an evidence statement. Status: **single-source**. **Refuter:** a cost or expertise score used as the sole basis for a verdict about stock change.

### F10 - A verifier must not hold the interiors before giving a verdict

Fig 10 :42: **"A verifier that can reconstruct the claim cannot verify it."**
Fig 10 :52: **"Bring a characterised residual, never the source."**

The first contact with an expert should carry the three-slot residual and its refuter, not this folder. If the recipient absorbs the framework first, their later agreement is not a witness. Status: **triangulated** with Fig 2 :40. **Refuter:** a verifier who reconstructs the claim from our frame and still produces an independent refutation.

### F11 - Agreement across reviews is one pool unless independence is bought

Fig 2 :40: **"agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool"**
Fig 7 :12-13: **"same closure, handed along; agreement compounds, evidence does not"**
Fig 7 :35: **"A room's agreement is variation, not a verdict. Selection between rooms is where the ground votes."**

Jordon et al. (2022), Patil et al. (2025) and Thapa et al. (2018) are different rooms: temperate paired observations, Indian agro-ecological zones, and a global nitrate-leaching meta-analysis. Their partial agreement is weak corroboration because all are agronomy-facing and some share underlying trials. The finance source (Medeckytė-Žydelė 2026) and the engineering/monitoring source (FAO MRV protocol) are lower overlap. Status: **triangulated** across Fig 2 and Fig 7. **Refuter:** proof that all cited reviews draw on a single shared dataset and that no independent room exists.

## 5. SEARCHED / INWARD / OUTWARD, kept separate

The question "can it do all three" is underivable from the figures alone. The figures give the shape; the sign comes from a ground. Formula step 7 says what to do when no candidate in the closure closes R:

1. **SEARCHED (asked first, cheapest).** The literature already contains the three slots and their trade-offs. Done. Do not mistake more of the same search for progress.
2. **INWARD (sharpen our own instrument).** Paired SOC and yield observations, deeper coring, equivalent soil mass, outlet water sampling, longer time series. This is a measurement upgrade.
3. **OUTWARD (import a frame whose closure differs).** Hydrology for water loading, agronomy for bundles, finance and accounting for verification cost. Source gate and shadow test apply: each import must be provenanced and must explain a gap in the three-slot residual.

INWARD and OUTWARD answer different questions. Running one against the other is the error Fig 6 :31 forbids.

## 6. Pre-registered refuter (written before the conclusion)

Per the seat rule that a falsifier written before the run is a design instrument, this was written before the verdict table in section 8:

1. A pre-registered, multi-site trial with paired measurements showing that the mean SOC stock change under restoration is indistinguishable from zero after correcting for baseline, sampling depth and equivalent soil mass.
2. A measured case where any increase in SOC is achieved only by a significant loss of yield or a significant degradation of water quality, and where no bundle clears the resulting intersection.
3. A demonstration that all apparently independent supporting reviews draw on one evidence pool, so the apparent joint result is one witness wearing several names, with no independent room left.

If any fires, the conditional "yes" is withdrawn. Until one is run, no part of the empirical verdict is `grounded`.

## 7. Recommendation

1. Unbundle the question into three slots before searching further (F1, F2).
2. Attach an instrument and an anchor to each slot (F3).
3. Enumerate contact to third order, including the receiving water body (F4).
4. Refuse any claim of a universally good practice (F5).
5. State what is cheap to describe and what is expensive to settle (F6).
6. Treat saturation as an empirical limit, not a figure derivation (F7, strain recorded).
7. Run INWARD and OUTWARD as two tracks; use SEARCHED first (F8).
8. Use cost and expertise only as budget filters (F9).
9. Send the residual and its refuter first (F10).
