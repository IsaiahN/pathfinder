# The Tether's answer on soil restoration and three outcomes

**Mode: `specified` on the empirical synthesis, `general` on the structural shape, not `grounded`.** Simulated derivation checked against the figures and the sources opened this session, not a ground reading (`05_what_shape_means.md` section 6).

## 1. The residual it answers

The question asks whether one intervention class can produce three outcomes at once. Arity: three. Symmetry: asymmetric, the slots can move in opposite directions. Scale: plot, field and basin over years to decades. Fig 9 :57-58: **"a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either. It stops being a mixture and becomes a specific compound."** With three conditions, the joint case is a specific compound with its own loop and its own ground.

## 2. The shape

1. **Three slots, three anchors, no average.** Fig 1 :19: **"The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated."** Carbon stock is settled by paired coring and laboratory analysis; food by harvest measurement; water quality by nutrient and sediment loads at an outlet. A composite score can hide a nitrate loss behind a carbon gain.
2. **The anchor is a measurement, not review agreement.** Fig 2 :29-30: **"An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks. A measurement, an experiment, a counter, a market, a patient outcome."** Fig 2 :35: **"Alignment is triangulation, not negotiation. A mirror cannot correct you."**
3. **Contact must be enumerated to third order.** Fig 11 :52-53: **"Scoping the habitat to direct contact is how DDT reached the eagles: first-order enumeration, second-order effect, and the residual that mattered was three steps out."** Plot carbon is first order; off-site nitrate is second; the receiving water body is third.
4. **No standing good practice.** Fig 11 :50: **"The same actor is a benefactor to one residual and a malfactor to another, so mutualism and parasitism are relations rather than kinds."** Cover crops reduce nitrate leaching and can increase evapotranspiration in a dry year.
5. **A bundle is a molecule, not a set.** Fig 12 :5: **"A set of ingredients is not one. The bond is the rest of the information, and it is the half nothing was recording."** Order and arrangement change the outcome (`06_isomers_and_order.md`).
6. **The figure delivers a shape, not a sign.** Local figures README: *"what a missing thing would have to look like (a shape, not a name)"*; Fig 9 :32: **"you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself."** The empirical signs come from world sources and stay `specified`.

## 3. The empirical synthesis, kept per slot and never averaged

- **Carbon stock.** Restoration practices can raise SOC in many settings. Jordon et al. (2022) found significant SOC increases for reduced tillage and ley-arable rotations over an average 15 years, but no effect of cover crops. Patil et al. (2025) found positive effects across 147 Indian studies, strongest with organic amendments and over longer durations. Minasny et al. (2017) and Hatano and Iwasaki (2026) report saturation or even negative rates at high SOC or high SOC-to-clay ratios. Sign: positive but finite and context-dependent.
- **Food production.** Jordon et al. (2022): none of the studied practices reduced yield during cropping years, but there was no evidence of a carbon-yield win-win. Encarnation et al. (2025): 16 percent of 5,709 observations achieved both gains, 7.5 percent showed losses. Sign: usually not negative, jointly positive only in a minority of conditions.
- **Water quality.** Thapa et al. (2018): nonleguminous cover crops reduced nitrate leaching by about 56 percent. Liang et al. (2025): no-till raised topsoil SOC by 14.2 percent relative to high-intensity tillage while nitrate leaching was 4.9 percent higher, and cover crops could aggravate water availability under future climate. Sign: strongly lever-dependent, not a property of "restoration" as a class.
- **The union.** Joint gains exist but are a minority and are governed by practice combination, nitrogen rate and crop type (Encarnation et al. 2025) and by region and duration (Patil et al. 2025). This is `specified`, not a settled global yes.

## 4. What it explicitly does not say

- Not a single global yes with one number. The three signs are separate.
- Not that any named practice is universally good.
- Not that the figures predicted the measured magnitudes. They constrain the form of the check.
- Not `grounded`. No paired field measurement was run for this folder.
- Not that agreement among reviews is corroboration. Fig 2 :40: **"agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool"** - the three meta-analyses are agronomy-facing and may share trials.
- Not that cost ranks truth. Fig 9 :60: **"Use filters for the budget and witnesses for the verdict. Never let a filter hand you a verdict."**

## 5. What would settle it - the ground

1. A pre-registered, multi-site factorial trial with paired carbon, yield and outlet-water measurements, order of practices controlled, sampling below 0.3 m and equivalent soil mass used.
2. A source-discriminating measurement of the carbon-yield coupling: does the yield response at low SOC come from the carbon itself or from associated changes in structure, water and nutrients?
3. A third-order measurement at the receiving water body, not only a plot or field estimate.

**Standing refuters from `01`:** a null SOC result after depth and baseline correction; a case where any carbon gain requires a yield or water loss with no bundle clearing the intersection; proof that all supporting reviews draw on one evidence pool.

## 6. The honest stop

**Reachable, not settled.** The joint answer is reachable with instruments that already exist; the obstruction is that this folder did not run them. Fig 8 :39: **"So a search that finds nothing is never a proof of absence."** The stop is recorded so the route is checkable. Indiscernibility is not identity: a composite score that cannot distinguish a carbon gain from a nitrate loss is not evidence that the two are the same thing.

## 7. One-paragraph version for outreach

> The Tether does not answer yes or no to whether soil restoration can do all three things. It says the question is three questions in one: carbon stock, food production and water quality are separate slots with separate instruments, and a gain in one can hide a loss in another. Each slot needs its own anchor (paired soil coring, harvest measurement, water sampling at the outlet), and the measurement has to follow the effect out to the receiving water body, because a no-till practice can raise topsoil carbon while raising nitrate leaching. Restoration practices are not a shopping list; their order and arrangement change the result, and bundles built from the same practices are different substances in the figures' sense. What the literature actually reports is conditional: carbon gains are real but finite and saturating, yields are usually not reduced but reliable carbon-yield win-wins are uncommon, and water-quality effects depend on the specific lever. The framework returns that shape and a refuter written in advance. It does not return a global verdict, and it did not run the measurement that would settle one.
