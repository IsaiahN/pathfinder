## Wider literature that bears on the question

The sources cluster into four low-overlap pools, so their agreement is weak but not circular (Fig 2 :31, Fig 8 :50-52):

1. **Philosophy of science.** Collins's experimenter's regress shows the circle between a good instrument and a correct result. The SEP records Franklin's counter that calibration and reasoned judgment can break it, and Radder's example of different thermometers giving the same boiling point. This bears directly on C1 and C2.
2. **History of science.** N-rays are the standing artifact case: many observers, many papers, a respected discoverer, no world source. Wood's removal of the prism is a blind control before the term existed. The photographic emulsion explanation is the instrument-generated artifact in Fig 10 :26 terms.
3. **Color science and human cognition.** Metamerism is the clean demonstration that the reading does not determine the source. The spectrometer is the imported instrument that discriminates.
4. **Framework essays.** All by the framework author, so high overlap with the figures and weak independent weight. They supply the source language and the simulation limit, not corroboration.

No world source was allowed to flip a figure verdict. The one apparent conflict, Collins's claim that controversies are closed by social negotiation rather than epistemological criteria, was returned to the census and re-mapped to the convention domain (see `01`, section 6, R1).

## Distribution: offer, do not push

Figure-grounded reasons:

- **Fig 10 :52** — *"Bring a characterised residual, never the source."* Sending the full framework before anyone has described a residual sends a recording, not a generator. Lead with the claim and its refuter.
- **Fig 10 :42** — *"A verifier that can reconstruct the claim cannot verify it."* A recipient who has absorbed the framework before giving a verdict has joined the frame being checked.
- **Fig 9 :27-28** — *"describe it before you go looking, or any frame you pick will seem to fit."* Ask what they would expect to see if the shape claim were false before giving them the essays.
- **Fig 2 :31-32** — *"Two people reading the same report are not two frames; two instruments pointed at the same thing are."* Do not send a list of people selected for likely agreement. That manufactures a convention, not a witness.
- **Fig 9 :60** — *"Use filters for the budget and witnesses for the verdict."* Contact cost decides how to approach someone, never whether their answer counts.

**Recommended order in a reply:** (1) the shape claim and its refuter; (2) ask what they would count as a failure; (3) record that verdict; (4) only then the links, with the note that the figure text, not the summary, is the authority.

## The two-stage offer

1. **Stage 1:** validate the shape claim against the ground, without the framework.
2. **Stage 2, opt-in only:** if curious, offer the source links. The figures are the authority. A later agreement is never pooled as corroboration (Fig 2 :40).
3. **Stage 3:** offer the formula as a method to reuse, with the instruction that each user writes their own refuter and states their own mode.

**Hard precondition:** stages 1 to 3 work only if the stage-1 claim survives. `08_expert_verification_packet.md` is the artifact to send; `01_figure_grounded_audit.md` is the working record, not the pitch.

## What is not sent

No bulk send, no purchased list, no personal emails, no request for trust, no ranking by likely agreement. The venues in `03` are public institutional or opt-in channels. An unsolicited personal email campaign would breach GDPR and CAN-SPAM style rules in many jurisdictions, and would fail the structural rule in Fig 2 :40 as well.
