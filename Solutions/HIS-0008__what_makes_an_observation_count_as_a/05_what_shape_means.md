## 1. A shape is a constraint, not a filler

The figures README states the limit: the framework returns *"what a missing thing would have to look like (a shape, not a name)."* Fig 9 :32: *"you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself."* Fig 13 :135: *"It cannot say which primitive is missing, because naming it would mean already holding it."*

From *The Library of Babel*: *"you can cut your search short if the set of books returned is small and descriptive enough that it provides a general shape of the answer that satisfies your question."*

So a **shape** is the narrowed form of an answer you can state before you can name it. For HIS-0008 it is the set of conditions an observation must meet to be a fact: a non-updating anchor, an instrument with a differing closure, a maintained channel with traceable calibration, and active discrimination of artifact hypotheses. A **name** would be "the spectrometer", "the blind control", or "the peer review process" asserted as *the* answer for every case. The shape constrains; it does not fill.

## 2. How a shape is obtained

- *The Library of Babel*: use the vocabulary already held that is closest to the thing, then look for recurring new terms that overlap or correlate with it. *"With each new term gained, you are able to narrow the space by definition and deepen your vocabulary."*
- Fig 6 :30: *"The description travels. Fails by being sharpened into terms only we can read. Cast it in effect terms."* Fig 9 :30-31: *"your words are borrowed too, so describe the effect rather than the cause."*
- *Decoding Tacit Knowledge*: *"When contact isn't possible, the best you can send is a plain description of what you'd expect to see, because anyone can check that against the world."*
- *The Library of Babel* on stopping: *"the finish line will also be the starting place"*, and the paradox that by then you have *"composed the answer through the process of locating it."*

For this question, the effect terms are fixed first: reading, source, anchor, independence, channel, artifact. Only then were figure lines chosen. Had the framework been named first, as *The Library of Babel* warns, any venue in the field would have seemed to fit.

## 3. What licenses a name

Only two things, both outside the shape itself:

1. **The ground settles it.** Formula step 5: *"the ground settles it. Verified only by what pays."* The README: the framework *"stops at the limit of what the available instruments can distinguish, and records the lineage so the stop is honest."*
2. **A provenanced import from a frame whose closure differs** (Fig 6 :23, Fig 8 :50-52), with a source gate and a shadow test.

*The Hard Problem is About Source* gives the mechanism for this question. Metamerism: an instrument reads green and *"the instrument is speaking only about what it measured, it cannot comment on the source."* The reading is compatible with one green light, or yellow plus blue, or RGB, or CMYK, or a scheme not yet discovered. *"If several paths create the same behavior, then behavior is a projection with lossy information."* What resolved it was not an argument: *"It was proven wrong not by argument, but by the arrival of a better instrument."*

In this solution, naming the spectrometer as the general answer is therefore refused. It is the closing instrument for one case, imported from color science, and it must be recorded as such (Fig 8 :50-52).

## 4. Why the record must stay a shape

Fig 2 :35: *"Alignment is triangulation, not negotiation. A mirror cannot correct you."* A historical claim about which observations became facts is settled by comparing readings against named anchors, not by counting how many historians agree. Fig 7 :13: *"agreement compounds, evidence does not."*

The N-ray case is the standing warning. Hundreds of papers agreed. The agreement was the shared method talking. Wood's prism removal was a source discriminator, and the later emulsion analysis named the artifact source (MIT Libraries, 2011). An answer that named "consensus" or "replication" as the fact-maker would have certified N-rays as facts. That is the error the shape avoids.

## 5. Q2 — what licenses a particular discriminator in a particular case?

**Answered: nothing in the framework.** The framework returns the conditions a discriminator must satisfy. A particular instrument is licensed only by the ground settling it in that case, or by a provenanced import with a shadow test. Until one of those, the name is the searcher's own contribution and must be recorded as such.

This is not a ban on naming held instruments. The phrase *"a shape, not a name"* governs a primitive or discriminator not held in the frame. Naming an instrument already in the library is a binding from another frame onto what is held, and the binding is an import that adds no atom (Fig 6 :25, *"An import adds no atom to the world."*). What is forbidden is claiming the binding moves the wall or that the framework derived it.

## 6. Prestige is not action

From *The Recursive Self-Improvement Trap*: *"Prestige must never be allowed to validate an action directly."* And: *"Prestige can fund an action. It can give an action a budget to be tried. But it cannot settle an action. Only the ground can settle it."*

Blondlot was a respected physicist and a member of the French Academy. The prestige funded the action. It did not settle whether the rays existed. A check campaign that gathers respected names to endorse this derivation would repeat the N-ray failure rather than diagnose it.

## 7. My own audit is a simulation, not a ground reading

Same essay: *"LLMs can quickly simulate an approximate answer/output with the formula found in the architecture post."* and *"the truly testable output would need to be derived from the real architecture or working the formula yourself."* Also: *"the output is only as good as the validator. The agent, or the tool, or the frame, or the theory, cannot validate itself."*

So `01_figure_grounded_audit.md`, `06_isomers_and_order.md`, `07_the_tether_answer.md`, `08_expert_verification_packet.md`, `09_watson_answer.md`, and `11_audit.md` are simulated derivations checked for internal consistency against the figures. They are **not** tests of the historical question and must be labelled as such. None can settle what it reports on.

## 8. What the four conditions look like as a shape

| condition | shape form | name withheld |
|---|---|---|
| C1 source discrimination | a reading whose source can be separated from the reading itself | which instrument in a given case |
| C2 anchoring | a reference that does not move when frames change | which measurement for a given observation |
| Independence | a second frame whose evidence does not overlap the first | which second observer or method |
| Channel and control | maintained contact, traceable calibration, blind and known-positive fixture | which protocol for a given discipline |

The table is the deliverable of this section: a checkable shape with four slots and no filled names, so a historian of science can refute it without first accepting the framework.
