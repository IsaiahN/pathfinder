# Isomers of one reading, and the order of the discriminator

**Mode: `general` for the structure, `specified` for the ordering claim. Simulated derivation checked against the figures, not a ground reading (see `05_what_shape_means.md` §7).**

## RESIDUAL

Two readings are indistinguishable at the instrument, yet one is a fact and one is an artifact. What is the structure that distinguishes them, and in what order must the checks run? The difference is not in the reading's ingredients, so it must be in the bond between the reading and its source (Fig 12 :5, *"A set of ingredients is not one."*).

## DOMAIN CENSUS

Atoms held: the reading `o′`, the frame that produced it, the world at slot `s`, the anchor, a second differing frame, a blind and a control. Bonds held: the seven, including sequence (`→`), comparison (`⋛`), identity (`≡`), and subtraction (`−`), plus negation (`¬`), which is not a bond. No new primitive is available without an import.

## DERIVATION

### Q1 — What is the molecule of a fact claim?

- Fig 12 :46: **"A bond is the typed relation between two operands. There are seven, they are not interchangeable, and which one holds is not recoverable from the operands."**
- A bare reading is `o′` alone: an atom with no bond to a source. It has the ingredients of a fact claim and not the structure.
- Reading plus anchor uses `⋛`, *"a threshold relation between two quantities"* (Fig 12 :87), because the question is whether the reading matches within tolerance, not whether it is identical.
- Reading plus world source uses `≡` only when the instrument can actually discriminate sources. Metamerism is the case where that bond is not licensed: the reading is a projection with lossy information.
- Reading plus method uses `⇒`, *"A produces B; B did not exist before"* (Operators table :36-38), when the artifact hypothesis is true. This is the bond that must be refused, and refusing it requires negation, not subtraction. Fig 12 :112: **"A refutation is a negation, and that one is not a bond."**

### Q2 — Are two readings of the same value isomers?

- Fig 12 :117: **"isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."**
- The color case is the exact isomer: same tristimulus reading, different spectral source. The formula (the reading) is identical; the bond to the source differs, and the behavior differs under a changed illuminant. Wikipedia's *Metamerism (color)* records that samples *"match when viewed under one light source but not another"*, which is the behavioral difference that makes them different substances.
- The N-ray case is the inverse: same reading across many observers, same bond to expectation rather than world. The many reports are not many isomers; they are one term with many spellings under a symmetric bond. Fig 12 :23: **"this bond is symmetric, so the four are one term"**.
- **Falsifier, written before the run:** if two readings with the same recorded value and the same recorded source bond behave identically under every change of illuminant, method, and observer, the isomer claim fails and the two are one term, not two.

### Q3 — What order do the checks run in?

- Fig 12 :111: **"A vocabulary without bonds can name a state and cannot name a route to one."** A list of checks is not a discriminator; the order is part of the substance.
- Fig 3 :20: **"The chain is ordered by dependency, not by importance."**
- The dependency order is:
  1. **Perceive.** Does anything register at all? If no reading exists, there is no edge to extend from (Fig 6 :34-35).
  2. **Vocabulary.** Does the frame have a term for "this reading was produced by the method"? Without it, the artifact is invisible (Fig 3 :27).
  3. **Objective.** Bind C1 and C2 as ordinary terms before the run (Formula, "Direction"). Otherwise the sought-for shape is a preference that no check can see (Fig 5 :62-64).
  4. **Plan.** Install the blind and the control. A known-positive fixture proves sensitivity, never specificity (Seat Map :275).
  5. **Range.** Bring in the second frame with a differing closure (Fig 2 :31-32). Two readings of the same report are not this step.
  6. **Carry.** Record the provenance of the instrument and the calibration (Fig 10 :62-66). The ground decides; the record only shows the ruling was reached legitimately.
- Sequence (`→`) is the bond inside steps 1 to 6; comparison (`⋛`) is the bond at step 6 when the reading is ranged against the anchor. Conflating them is the error of treating a procedure as a verdict. Fig 12 :112: **"A plan is a sequence. A goal is a comparison."**

### Q4 — What is the analogue at the community scale?

- Fig 4 :19-20: **"The kernel is the part that does not change with size: the same loop and the same rule for naming what worked, whether one agent is running"** it or a thousand. *"Only the actors, the currency and the anchor change."*
- At the single-reading scale, the actors are observer, instrument, and source; the currency is residual; the anchor is the world at `s`.
- At the community scale, the actors are instruments, laboratories, and conventions; the currency is independence; the anchor is still the world at `s`, never the community itself (Fig 11 :73, *"The habitat is where actors come from and it updates; the ground is what settles, and it does not."*).
- The transform between scales is lossy, so the round trip must be measured: Fig 4 :30, **"So take the round trip and measure the gap. Without it you are navigating by dead reckoning: confident about a position nobody checked."**

## CONCLUSION

The difference between a fact and an artifact is carried by the bond between reading and source, not by the reading's ingredients. Two readings with the same value can be isomers when their source bonds differ, and that difference is decisive under a changed instrument. The discriminator is ordered: perceive, name the artifact, bind the shape, blind and control, range a differing frame, then let the ground decide and record the lineage. A vocabulary of checks without this order can name a state and cannot name a route to one.
