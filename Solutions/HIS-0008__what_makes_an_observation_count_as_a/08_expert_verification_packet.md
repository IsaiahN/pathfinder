# Expert verification packet — HIS-0008, fact versus artifact

**What this is:** a claim in a form a historian or philosopher of science can check. Mode, derivation, and refuters are stated before the conclusion, so the reader knows what to look for and what would settle it.

**Mode: `general` shape, `specified` operational consequences, not `grounded`.** No measurement at the historical case is claimed here. This packet is a simulated derivation checked for internal consistency against the figures, not a ground reading.

## 1. The claim, in one paragraph

A recorded value does not say where it came from, so a reading alone cannot decide whether an observation is a fact or an artifact of the method that produced it. An observation counts as a fact when four conditions hold together: its reading can be ranged against a world anchor that does not change because of what any frame thinks; a second instrument whose evidence does not overlap the first corroborates it; the channel to the anchor is maintained and its calibration is traceable; and the artifact explanations are actively discriminated by a blind, a control, and a known-positive fixture. It counts as an artifact when the reading is generated frame-internally, when agreement comes from a shared evidence pool, when the instrument carries the defect it was built to catch, or when a community convention decides the outcome. The framework returns this shape and not the name of a closing instrument for any particular case.

## 2. Three checkable sub-claims

### C1 — A reading does not authenticate its own source

- **Statement:** in a case where several distinct sources give the same reading, no feature of the reading alone identifies which source produced it. Depth and absence are indistinguishable from inside the frame.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 6 :27, *"Which is why some unreachability is depth and some is genuine absence, and from inside the frame the two look the same."* Fig 6 :26, *"The world does not gain primitives; a frame gains access."* Fig 9 :30-31, *"your words are borrowed too, so describe the effect rather than the cause."*
- **What to check:** is there a case in the recipient's own field where two sources demonstrably gave the same reading, and did the reading alone settle which? Color metamerism is the clean instance, documented independently in Wikipedia's *Metamerism (color)* (2026), and by Isaiah N's *The Hard Problem is About Source* (2026), which is framework-internal and carries high overlap.
- **Refuter:** an instrument shown to discriminate its own source without any change of instrument, control, or frame. That breaks metamerism and Fig 6 :27.

### C2 — Fact-hood needs a non-updating anchor; agreement cannot supply one

- **Statement:** the reference against which an observation is called a fact must not change when frames change, and the question must be constitutively about that reference.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 2 :29, *"An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks."* Fig 2 :33, *"The anchor must not update."* Fig 2 :35, *"Alignment is triangulation, not negotiation. A mirror cannot correct you."* Fig 2 :31-32, *"Two people reading the same report are not two frames; two instruments pointed at the same thing are."*
- **What to check:** in the N-ray affair, hundreds of papers agreed and the effect was an artifact. APS News (2007) records that after Wood removed the prism, *"Blondlot continued to insist he saw the very same pattern he had claimed to see when the prism was in place."* Did the agreement range against a fixed world reference, or against the shared method?
- **Refuter:** an observation accepted as a fact solely because every frame agrees, with no reference any frame can fail to match; or two readers of the same report counting as corroboration. That breaks Fig 2 :29, :31-32, :33, :35.

### C3 — The instrument can carry the defect it was built to catch

- **Statement:** a new instrument's first output is a claim about the instrument; a known-positive fixture proves sensitivity, never specificity. Both are required. The channel to the ground decays even though the ground does not.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 10 :24, *"the ground does not decay, the channel does"*; Fig 10 :26, *"saturated metrics, pooled readings"*; Fig 10 :28, *"you have lost touch"*. Seat Map :275: *"A new instrument's first output is a claim about the instrument, not about the system, and a known-positive fixture proves sensitivity, never specificity. Both are required."*
- **What to check:** MIT Libraries (2011) records that in the N-ray photographs, *"differences among the photographs were caused by inconsistencies in photographic emulsions, exposure time, and development of the film."* The IOPSpark entry says the observations were *"caused by psycho-physiological effects"* and the case is *"often cited as a demonstration of the dangers of experimenter bias."* Was the artifact source in the instrument or in the world?
- **Refuter:** a known-positive fixture shown to establish specificity as well as sensitivity for the instrument that produced the reading, with no change of observer, control, or frame. That breaks Seat Map :275.

## 3. What is explicitly not claimed

- Not that consensus never matters: agreement about a shared meaning is a convention and the product (Fig 2 :41).
- Not that replication is useless: under one closure it is a budget instrument, not a witness (Fig 7 :13, Fig 9 :60).
- Not that all instruments are unreliable: Fig 6 :34-35 separates a foreclosed question from a hard one.
- Not that the framework can name the instrument that closes a given case (Fig 9 :32): that is a `gap`.
- Not `grounded`: no measurement here settles the historical question.
- Not that prestigious observers are irrelevant: they can fund a check, *"But it cannot settle an action. Only the ground can settle it."* (Isaiah N, *The Recursive Self-Improvement Trap*, 2026).

## 5. What would settle it

A source-discriminating instrument and a blind, not a further argument. From *The Hard Problem is About Source*: an instrument that reads green *"cannot comment on the source"*; the question was closed *"not by argument, but by the arrival of a better instrument."* For this question:

1. **Source test:** can two distinct world sources be shown to give the same reading on the instrument? If not, C1 is satisfied as held.
2. **Anchor test:** is there a reference the reading can fail to match, and does that reference stay fixed when the observer's belief changes?
3. **Independence test:** do the two instruments draw on different evidence, or is one a reading of the other's report?
4. **Observer test:** does a known-positive fixture establish specificity, and was the observer blind to the expected result?

## 6. What an expert can check in twenty minutes

1. Read Fig 6 :26-27, Fig 2 :29-35, and Fig 10 :24-28. Three short passages.
2. Check C2 against the N-ray record: was there a fixed world reference, or only a shared method? (APS News, 2007.)
3. Name one instrument that would have distinguished N-rays from expectation in 1904, or say why none was possible.
4. Send the refutation. A failure is more useful than an endorsement.
