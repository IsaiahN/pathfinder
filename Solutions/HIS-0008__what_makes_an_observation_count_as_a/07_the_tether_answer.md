# The Tether's answer: what makes an observation a fact

**Mode: `general` for the shape, `specified` for its operational consequences, not `grounded`.** This is a shape and a route, not a historical verdict. Simulated derivation checked against the figures (`05_what_shape_means.md` §7), not a ground reading.

## 1. The residual it answers

An observer's method predicts a reading, and the reading is entered into the record. The question is not how large the gap between prediction and reading is, but where the reading came from. Several world sources and several method states can produce the same value. A recorded value does not say which. In color science the same perceived color can come from a single wavelength or from mixtures; in the N-ray case the same reported brightness came from expectation rather than radiation. The residual is a **union compound** of two conditions: source discrimination (C1) and anchoring (C2). A case failing one is not a harder case of the other (Fig 9 :54-57).

## 2. The shape

1. **A reading cannot authenticate its own source.** Fig 6 :27: *"Which is why some unreachability is depth and some is genuine absence, and from inside the frame the two look the same."* What is owed is a discriminator, not another argument.
2. **Fact-hood requires a non-updating anchor, legitimately about the world at the slot.** Fig 2 :29: *"An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks."* Fig 2 :33: *"The anchor must not update."* Consensus, reputation, and the instrument's self-report move when pushed.
3. **Corroboration requires low overlap, not many observers.** Fig 2 :31-32: *"Two people reading the same report are not two frames; two instruments pointed at the same thing are."* Fig 2 :40: *"agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool."*
4. **The channel to the ground must be maintained.** Fig 10 :24: *"the ground does not decay, the channel does"*; Fig 10 :26: *"saturated metrics, pooled readings"*; Fig 10 :28: *"you have lost touch"*. Calibration by a surrogate alone is not independent (SEP, Collins).
5. **Artifact hypotheses must be actively discriminated.** Seat Map :275: *"A new instrument's first output is a claim about the instrument, not about the system, and a known-positive fixture proves sensitivity, never specificity. Both are required."* The blind and the control are the historical answer.
6. **Agreement that decides an outcome is no longer a convention.** Fig 10 :47: *"a convention that decides an outcome has stopped being one."* N-rays had hundreds of agreeing papers and still were an artifact.
7. **The framework does not name the closing instrument.** Fig 9 :32: *"you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself."* The spectrometer, the blind, the control, and peer review are bindings from other frames, imported with provenance, never derived from the six-term set.

## 3. What it explicitly does not say

- Not that consensus never matters. Agreement about a shared meaning is a convention and the product of the arrangement (Fig 2 :41).
- Not that replication is useless. Replication under one closure is a budget instrument, not a witness (Fig 7 :13; Fig 9 :60).
- Not that I can name which instrument closes a given historical case. That is a `gap`, filled only by a ground reading.
- Not that all instruments are unreliable. Fig 6 :34-35 separates a foreclosed question from a hard one: the rule is about instruments that never registered anything, not about instruments that can be sharpened.
- Not `grounded`. No observation in this folder was measured by an anchor.

## 4. What would settle it — the ground

A source-discriminating instrument and a blind, not a further argument. The corresponding questions are:

- **Source test:** can several distinct world sources be shown to give the same reading on this instrument? If not, metamerism does not bite in this case and C1 is satisfied by the instrument as held.
- **Anchor test:** is there a reference the reading can fail to match, and does that reference stay fixed when the observer's belief changes? If the reference moves when pushed, it is not an anchor (Fig 2 :30).
- **Independence test:** do the two instruments draw on different evidence? Two readings of the same report are one witness (Fig 2 :31-32).
- **Observer test:** does a known-positive fixture establish specificity, and did the observer know the expected result? If not, the instrument may carry the defect it was meant to catch (Seat Map :275).
- **Standing falsifiers:** an observation accepted as a fact with no independent anchor; two same-report readers counting as corroboration; an instrument authenticating its own source; a convention deciding a world outcome; a known-positive fixture establishing specificity. Any one fires against the shape claim.

## 5. The honest stop

**Unreached, not unreachable.** The four conditions are a shape that any defensible answer must have. Which instrument, which blind, which anchor in a particular historical case is not given. Fig 13 :134: *"Unreachable is a verdict here, not a silence"* and Fig 8 :39: *"So a search that finds nothing is never a proof of absence."* The answer stops where the available instruments can no longer distinguish and records the lineage so the stop is checkable. Indiscernibility is not identity.

## 6. One-paragraph version for outreach

> An observation does not become a fact by being recorded, repeated, or believed. A reading does not say where it came from: several sources can produce the same value, and from inside the instrument a case where the instrument is too coarse looks the same as a case where there is nothing there. The Tether's answer is a shape with four conditions. The reading must be rangeable against a world anchor that does not move when frames change. A second instrument with a different evidence base must corroborate it. The channel to the anchor must be maintained and its calibration traceable, because the ground does not decay and the channel does. And the artifact hypotheses must be actively ruled out by a blind, a control, and a known-positive fixture, because an instrument's first output is often a claim about the instrument itself. Agreement that decides an outcome has stopped being a convention and is not a verdict. The framework does not name the closing instrument for any particular case; it returns the shape any closing instrument must have, the refuters written in advance, and the ground that would settle it. N-rays are the standing case of a fact that was only an artifact, and the prism in Wood's hand was the discriminator.
