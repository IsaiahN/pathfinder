**Subject:** One checkable claim about why a cell keeps DNA as an archive

> Hi [name],
>
> I have one specific output I would like you to check against what you already know. I am not asking you to read a framework first, and I am not asking you to agree with it. If the output does not survive, there is nothing else to look at. No reply is needed if you would rather not engage.
>
> **The output (`specified` on the shape, `grounded` on the chemistry I cite):**
>
> A cell needs two different things from a carrier of sequence information. For a long-term archive it needs the arrangement to persist, so that each generation performs a lookup rather than re-deriving the sequence. For working roles it needs a carrier whose deriving is not carried forward, so a message, adapter or catalyst can be made, used and replaced. Those are two different currencies, and one polymer chemistry does not price both the same way.
>
> The figure I am using puts it this way. Fig 12 :108-109: *"Where it is not, the result carries forward and the deriving does not, so every lineage pays again."* The complementary half is where the arrangement is recorded, so re-deriving it is a lookup and the actions were spent once. Fig 12 :126: *"Bits price the description. Actions price finding out whether it holds."*
>
> **What I am claiming:** that this is a shape forced by the chemistry, not just a description of what happens to be true here. The ribose 2'-OH makes RNA more prone to hydrolysis, so deoxyribose is the better low-reactivity archive; uracil is a normal base in RNA but a damage flag in DNA, where uracil-DNA glycosylase removes deaminated cytosine products. RNA retains the reactive group that makes catalysis and folding possible, which is why the working roles are RNA's. I am claiming the shape, and I am recording the sugar and base names as a binding from the chemistry frame, not as something the framework derived.
>
> **What I am not claiming:** that RNA never stores genetic information (RNA viruses exist), that DNA cannot catalyze (deoxyribozymes have been selected in the lab), that the assignment is universal across all life, or that selection was avoided. RNA came first biosynthetically, and the historical order is a second condition that I keep separate from the chemical one. I am also not claiming a ground reading by me. This is a simulated derivation checked for internal consistency against the figures and against chemistry pages I opened.
>
> **What would refute it, written down before this run:**
> 1. A measurement under cellular conditions showing that the 2'-OH does not materially reduce strand persistence, or that uracil-directed repair does not reduce mutational load.
> 2. A cell that maintains a long-lived heritable archive with no substrate in which the arrangement is recorded, paying the derivation cost every generation at no loss.
> 3. A lineage whose heritable polymer is not downstream of RNA in its own biosynthesis, or direct evidence that DNA-like polymers came first.
> 4. A re-census showing that Fig 12 :107-108 governs cross-boundary settlement only and not molecular persistence.
>
> A one-line "this fails because..." is more useful to me than agreement. What would you count as a failure here?
>
