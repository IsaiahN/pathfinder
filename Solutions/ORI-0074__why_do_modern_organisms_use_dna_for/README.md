## Restating the question as an effect

What must be explained is a division of labour between two closely related polymers that is present in every modern cell: one molecule, DNA, is retained as a durable archive; another, RNA, performs transient working roles (message, adapter, ribosome structure, catalysis, regulation). The residual is the fact of the division itself, not a preference for one molecule.

Two candidate answers lead to different places:

- **Option A, necessity.** The division is a shape forced by chemistry. One polymer is fit to be a record, the other is fit to intervene. This predicts that any lineage with long-term heredity will converge on a stable archive and a reactive working set, whatever the molecules are called.
- **Option B, contingency.** The division is a frozen historical accident. RNA came first, DNA was bolted on, and the present arrangement is one lineage's record rather than a forced shape. This predicts that alternative divisions are live possibilities and that the specific assignment carries no necessity.

What an answer would look like, bound as an ordinary term before the run: a claim about **which property is conserved when a molecule is used as a record, and which property is spent when a molecule is used as a working intermediate**. That term is stated in `01_figure_grounded_audit.md` §0 and is allowed to accrue a residual like anything else.
