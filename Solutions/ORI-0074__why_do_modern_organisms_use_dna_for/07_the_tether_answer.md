# The Tether's answer on DNA storage and RNA work roles

**Mode: `grounded` on the reported nucleic-acid chemistry, `specified` on the figure-derived shape and on the historical reading.** Nothing here is a ground reading taken by this author. Simulated derivation checked for internal consistency against the figures (`05_what_shape_means.md` §6).

## 1. The residual it answers

The cell keeps one polymer as a durable archive and uses a closely related polymer for transient work. That asymmetry is the residual, not the identity of the molecules. It is a **union** of two conditions: a chemical condition (which polymer can be a record) and a historical condition (which polymer arrived first). They are answered separately and never averaged (Fig 9 :54).

## 2. The shape

1. **A long-term archive is a substrate in which the arrangement is recorded.** Fig 12 :107-108 gives the rule: the cost of establishing the arrangement was paid once, so each inheriting cell performs a lookup. The complementary half, Fig 12 :108-109: *"Where it is not, the result carries forward and the deriving does not, so every lineage pays again."* That is what an intermediate role is: the working copy is made, used and replaced, and the derivation is paid again each time.
2. **The two roles are priced in different currencies.** Fig 12 :126: *"Bits price the description. Actions price finding out whether it holds."* Archive quality is bits (density, fidelity, persistence). Work quality is actions (speed, catalytic turnover, cheap replacement). A single scalar cannot price both.
3. **The chemical ground supplies the names, as a binding.** The ribose 2'-OH attacks the phosphodiester backbone, so RNA is more prone to hydrolysis; deoxyribose lacks it. Uracil is a normal base in RNA but a damage flag in DNA, where uracil-DNA glycosylase excises deamination products. The same 2'-OH that disqualifies RNA as an archive is the nucleophile in self-cleaving ribozymes, which is why RNA keeps the catalytic roles. These are `grounded` reports from opened sources, not figure lines and not derivations from the six-term set.
4. **The two polymers are not interchangeable.** They are not isomers under Fig 12 :117 (different atom sets); they are members of one functional group (Fig 12 :123). Their biosynthesis is ordered, not symmetric: RNA is made before DNA, so `RNA → DNA` (Fig 12 :72).
5. **History is a second condition, not a footnote.** RNA precedes DNA biosynthetically and probably historically. Selection, not design, fixed the assignment. Fig 11 :36: *"Where nothing maintains anything, intelligence is a label applied afterwards to a lineage that met its ground."* Adding a stable record changed contact with time, which is what Fig 11 :40 requires for a real improvement.
6. **The framework returns a shape, not the polymer names.** Fig 9 :32: *"you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself"*. Deoxyribose and thymine are a provenanced binding from the chemistry frame. Naming them is permitted because they are already held atoms; claiming the framework derived them is not.

## 3. What it explicitly does not say

- Not that RNA never stores information. RNA viruses exist.
- Not that DNA cannot catalyze. Deoxyribozymes have been selected in the laboratory.
- Not that the division is universal across all life. It is a predominant feature of modern cellular life, with live exceptions.
- Not that chemistry alone settles the question. Chemistry and arrival history are two conditions.
- Not that selection was suspended or that a designer chose the assignment.
- Not that this is a ground reading by the author. The chemistry is reported from opened pages; the derivation is simulated.
- Not that the first genetic polymer is known. That is unreached with present instruments.

## 4. What would settle it, and what would break it

The ground here is measurement, not agreement (Fig 2 :29-30).

- **Break the chemistry:** show under cellular conditions that the 2'-OH does not materially reduce persistence, or that uracil-directed repair does not reduce mutational load.
- **Break the structure:** find a cell that maintains a long-lived heritable archive without a substrate in which the arrangement is recorded, paying the derivation cost every generation at no loss.
- **Break the history:** find a lineage whose heritable polymer is not downstream of RNA in its own biosynthesis, or direct evidence of DNA-first polymers.
- **Move toward ground:** compare archive persistence and mutational load across RNA-genome and DNA-genome lineages under matched conditions; or synthesize a backbone that is both stable and catalytic and test whether a lineage can use it. The NASA Astrobiology FAQ identifies exactly this stability challenge for synthetic genetic polymers.
- **Re-census trigger:** if Fig 12 :107-108 is shown to govern cross-boundary settlement only and not molecular persistence, F1 is withdrawn and a new figure line is owed.

## 5. The honest stop

The prebiotic first-polymer question is **unreached, not unreachable in principle**. Formula step 7 distinguishes SEARCHED (modern biochemistry, well swept), INWARD (sharper comparative instruments on extant lineages) and OUTWARD (prebiotic chemistry and synthetic XNA work). More searching of modern biochemistry will not settle the first polymer, because all extant life already has DNA. Fig 8 :39: *"So a search that finds nothing is never a proof of absence."* The answer stops where the instruments stop and records the lineage so the stop is checkable.

## 6. One-paragraph version for outreach

> The cell's division between DNA and RNA is a shape forced by chemistry and delivered by history, not a free choice and not an accident with no reason. A long-term archive needs a substrate in which the arrangement is recorded, so each generation performs a lookup instead of re-deriving the sequence; working roles need a carrier whose deriving is not carried forward, so it can be made, used and replaced. Those are two different currencies, bits for the record and actions for the work. The ribose 2'-OH that makes RNA reactive enough to catalyze also makes it prone to hydrolysis, while deoxyribose and thymine give DNA a stable record and a damage flag that repair enzymes can read. RNA came first biosynthetically and probably historically, so selection joined the two roles rather than designing them. The framework returns that shape; the molecule names are a provenanced binding from chemistry, not a derivation. What would break it is a measurement showing the 2'-OH does not matter for persistence, a cell with no recorded archive, or a DNA-first lineage. Until one of those fires, the claim stays `specified` where the framework speaks and `grounded` where the chemistry reports.
