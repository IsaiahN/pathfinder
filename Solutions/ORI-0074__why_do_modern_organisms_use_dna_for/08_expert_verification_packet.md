## 1. The claim, in one paragraph

A long-term archive requires a substrate in which the arrangement is recorded, so inheritance is a lookup rather than a fresh derivation; working roles require a carrier whose deriving is not carried forward, so the copy can be made, used and replaced. Those are two different currencies, priced in bits and actions respectively. The chemistry frame binds the archive to deoxyribose and thymine (low reactivity, damage flag) and the working roles to RNA (2'-OH reactivity, catalytic range); that binding is provenance-recorded, not derived from the six-term set. RNA precedes DNA biosynthetically and probably historically, so the union of chemistry and arrival history explains the division, with neither condition averaged into the other. The figure set returns the shape; the molecule names come from measurement.

## 2. Three checkable sub-claims

### C1: framework-internal, checkable by reading Figure 12

- **Statement:** the record-versus-work split follows from the substrate rule for recorded arrangements. DNA is where the arrangement is recorded; RNA is where the result carries forward and the deriving does not.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 12 :107: *"Settling compresses the description and not what it cost to establish."* Fig 12 :108-109: *"Where it is not, the result carries forward and the deriving does not, so every lineage pays again."* Fig 4 :22 triangulates from a different figure with low overlap: *"Only methods cross up. Only head starts come down."*
- **Mode:** `specified`, not `grounded`.
- **Refuter:** find a cell that maintains a long-lived heritable archive with no substrate in which the arrangement is recorded, paying the derivation cost each generation at no loss; or show that Fig 12 :107-109 governs cross-boundary settlement only and not molecular persistence (a re-census trigger, not a patch).

### C2: chemistry-facing, checkable by a nucleic-acid chemist

- **Statement:** deoxyribose and thymine are the archive-grade binding because the 2'-OH is absent and uracil is a damage flag; RNA's 2'-OH is the source of both its catalytic reach and its shorter persistence. The same chemical feature produces opposite consequences in the two roles.
- **Sources, reported not derived:** Wikipedia, *RNA world*: *"RNA is less stable, being more prone to hydrolysis due to the presence of a hydroxyl group at the ribose 2' position."* And: *"This group makes the molecule less stable because, when not constrained in a double helix, the 2' hydroxyl can chemically attack the adjacent phosphodiester bond to cleave the phosphodiester backbone."* Wikipedia, *Uracil-DNA glycosylase*: *"Its most important function is to prevent mutagenesis by eliminating uracil from DNA molecules by cleaving the N-glycosidic bond and initiating the base-excision repair (BER) pathway."* Wikipedia, *Ribozyme*: *"are RNA molecules that have the ability to catalyze specific biochemical reactions, including RNA splicing in gene expression, similar to the action of protein enzymes."*
- **Mode:** `grounded` as a report of what those pages say; not independently measured here.
- **Refuter:** a measurement under cellular conditions showing that the 2'-OH does not materially reduce strand persistence, or that uracil-directed repair does not reduce mutational load; or a working catalyst whose catalytic power comes from no group that also shortens its persistence.

### C3: structure and history, checkable by an origin-of-life researcher

- **Statement:** DNA and RNA are not isomers under the figure definition, they are members of one functional group ordered biosynthetically as RNA then DNA, and the first-polymer question is unreached with present instruments.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 12 :117: *"isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."* Fig 12 :123: *"functional group. A substructure whose presence fixes the outcome. The group says what kind; the qualifier says which member."* Fig 12 :72: *"A then B; B needs A to have happened"*.
- **Chemistry check:** ribose and deoxyribose differ by an oxygen; uracil and thymine differ by a methyl group, so the atom sets are not identical. *RNA world*: *"a cell must have the ability to make RNA before it can make DNA."*
- **Mode:** `specified` on the figure reading; `grounded` on the reported atom counts and biosynthetic order.
- **Refuter:** a pair of polymers with identical atom sets but different bonds filling the two roles; or a lineage whose deoxyribonucleotides are not made from ribonucleotides; or direct evidence of DNA-first polymers.
