# Are DNA and RNA isomers? Structure, order and two currencies

**Mode: `specified` on the figure reading; `grounded` on the chemical facts reported in the opened sources.** This is a simulated derivation checked against the figures, not a ground reading (see `05_what_shape_means.md` §6).

## RESIDUAL

The question compares two molecules and two roles. Two supporting questions are owed: (1) are DNA and RNA **isomers** under Fig 12's definition, and (2) is the relationship between them **ordered** or symmetric? A wrong answer to either would corrupt the main derivation by making the two polymers interchangeable, which they are not.

Atoms held in the chemistry frame: phosphate backbone, a five-carbon sugar (ribose or deoxyribose), nitrogenous bases (adenine, guanine, cytosine, plus uracil or thymine). ## DERIVATION

### Q1: Are DNA and RNA isomers? No (falsifiable)

- Fig 12 :117: *"isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."*
- RNA and DNA do **not** have the same atoms. Ribose carries a hydroxyl at the 2' position that deoxyribose lacks, and uracil differs from thymine by a methyl group. These are different atom counts, not a different bond between the same atoms.
- ⇒ Under Fig 12's own definition they are **not isomers**. Calling them isomers would be a use of the word that the figure does not license.
- What they are: members of one **functional group**, Fig 12 :123: *"functional group. A substructure whose presence fixes the outcome. The group says what kind; the qualifier says which member."* The shared phosphate-sugar-base architecture fixes "kind: nucleic acid"; the sugar and base qualifiers fix "which member: archive-grade or work-grade".
- **Falsifier, written before the run:** if a pair of polymers with identical atom sets but different bonds were found filling the archive and work roles in one cell, the isomer claim would become live and this conclusion would be withdrawn. If DNA and RNA were shown to have identical atom sets, the functional-group reading would be retracted in favour of the isomer reading.

### Q2: Is the relationship ordered? Yes (falsifiable)

- Fig 12 :72 (and Operators table :31): *"A then B; B needs A to have happened"*. This is the → sequence bond; swapping the operands makes a different thing.
- The opened source states the order: *"a cell must have the ability to make RNA before it can make DNA."* The reason given is that deoxyribonucleotides are made from ribonucleotides by removing the 2'-hydroxyl group. The relation is therefore `RNA → DNA` in biosynthesis, not `DNA + RNA` as a symmetric pair.
- Fig 12 :92: *"Symmetry is what decides whether an arrangement is a new thing."* Under the ordered bond, the two orders are different terms. The historical claim in `01_figure_grounded_audit.md` F6 depends on this: reversing the order would change the claim, not restate it.
- **Falsifier:** a lineage found whose deoxyribonucleotides are not made from ribonucleotides, or in which a DNA-like polymer precedes RNA in its own biosynthesis, breaks the order claim. The salvage would not be to symmetrise the pair but to record a different bond.

### Q3: The two currencies do not reduce to one (figural)

- Fig 12 :125: *"A composition costs in two currencies, and they do not add."* Fig 12 :126: *"Bits price the description. Actions price finding out whether it holds."*
- Archive quality is priced in bits: how much sequence can be held, how faithfully it is copied, how long the arrangement persists. Work quality is priced in actions: how fast a message is transcribed, how many catalytic turns a ribozyme completes, how cheaply a damaged copy is replaced.
- The chemical fact that one sugar has a reactive group and the other does not is the physical reason the two prices come apart. *RNA world* reports that the 2'-OH can attack the adjacent phosphodiester bond; *Ribozyme* reports that the same group is the nucleophile in self-cleaving ribozymes. One atom, two ledgers.
- This is why a single question of the form "which molecule is better?" is malformed. It is a union of two questions with different currencies (Fig 9 :54 on unbundling).

### Q4: Catalyst and record are different structures (figural)

- Fig 12 :119: *"catalyst. A part that survives its own use. Spent and regenerating parts behave differently across uses, and nothing records which a term is."*
- A ribozyme survives its own catalytic turn, so it is a catalyst in this exact sense. It is **not** the record: the arrangement it uses is not thereby stored across generations.
- DNA is the record: the arrangement persists and the cost of establishing it was paid once. It is not usually the catalyst in the cell.
- Conflating these two "survives" senses was an earlier error of this author and is recorded as retracted in `01_figure_grounded_audit.md` §6.

## CONCLUSION

DNA and RNA are **not isomers** under Fig 12 :117. They are two members of a nucleic-acid **functional group** (Fig 12 :123), ordered by biosynthesis as `RNA → DNA` (Fig 12 :72), and priced in two currencies that do not add (Fig 12 :125-126). The catalyst line (Fig 12 :119) governs RNA's working roles, not DNA's archive role. This keeps the main derivation honest: the two polymers are not interchangeable spellings of one term, and the direction of the arrow is load-bearing.
