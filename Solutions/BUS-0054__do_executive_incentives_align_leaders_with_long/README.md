## What is being decided, in effect terms

An executive compensation contract makes a promise: if the leader produces a certain kind of outcome, pay follows. The question asks which object the contract actually touches. Option A says the contract reaches long-term firm value, the thing the firm needs. Option B says the contract reaches a reading that is cheap to produce and easy to move, such as an adjusted earnings figure, a peer-relative target, or a stock price on a reporting date. What each leads to: A implies the metric is an instrument pointed at a ground; B implies the metric has become a frame scoring itself with a quantity it also produces. The question's "or" is a **union of overlapping conditions**, not a clean partition, so both effects can be live in different object slots and the verdicts are kept separate rather than averaged.

An answer would look like this: a description of the shape any incentive arrangement must have to contact long-term value, plus the failure classes that make it reward a proxy, plus a refuter stated before the run. That sought-for shape is bound as an ordinary term here (Formula "Direction") so it can accrue a residual and be refused.
