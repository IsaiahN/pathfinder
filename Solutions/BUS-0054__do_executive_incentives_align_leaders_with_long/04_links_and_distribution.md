## 2. Wider literature that bears on the question

The sources split into three low-overlap groups. This matters because agreement from a shared evidence pool is one witness, not two (Fig 2 :40).

1. **Pay sensitivity and horizon.** Hall and Liebman find large pay-to-performance sensitivity once stock and options are counted, while noting that options reward market-wide movements and do not establish firm-relative performance. This is the strongest world evidence for the "alignment" side, and it is also the source of its own caveat. It does not conflict with the figure verdict; it forces the re-census distinction between *sensitivity to a metric* and *contact with long-term value*.
2. **Metric corruption and selection.** Goodhart (1975, as reported), Campbell (1979, as reported), Hall and Murphy (2004), and Chen, Hajda and Kalmenovitz (2025) supply the "easy to measure and manipulate" side from social science, personnel economics, and financial regulation. The ECGI paper is particularly sharp: performance pay raised measurable rulemaking effort by 58 to 63 percent while raising voluntary exits by 40 to 54 percent, with leavers earning 38 percent higher private-sector starting salaries. The measurable target moved; the long-term capability of the organisation did not necessarily follow.
3. **Measurement infrastructure and attention.** Frankenreiter (2026) and White (2026) show that what can be studied and what a board attends to are themselves scarce, shaped resources. This corroborates Fig 3's middle-link finding: the missing instrument is not a missing opinion.

**Apparent conflict handled as a re-census, not a verdict flip.** Hall and Liebman appear at first to support Option A (incentives align). Their own abstract and the NBER Digest supply the qualification: the sensitivity comes from equity revaluation, and equity can move on market-wide sentiment. Re-census of R separates the slot "sensitivity to reported performance" from the slot "contact with realized long-term outcomes." The two are different quantities, so there is no conflict at the level the figures govern. The world source is recorded as corroborating the per-slot finding, not as overruling the figures.

## 3. Distribution rules

- **Fig 4 :22**  -  *"Only methods cross up. Only head starts come down."* Sending a recording (a finished conclusion) before a residual has been described delivers an occasion, not a generator. Lead with the refuter and the shape claim.
- **Fig 10 :42**  -  *"A verifier that can reconstruct the claim cannot verify it."* A recipient who has absorbed the framework before giving a verdict has joined the frame being checked. Record the stage-1 verdict first.
- **Fig 9 :27-28**  -  *"describe it before you go looking, or any frame you pick will seem to fit."* Ask what they would expect to see if the shape claim were true before giving them the packet.
- **Fig 7 :13**  -  *"agreement compounds, evidence does not"*, and seat map :163: *"High correlation is disqualifying for a verdict and efficient for a convention."* Do not rank or select recipients by likely agreement. Do not pool later agreement as corroboration.
- **Fig 10 :52**  -  *"Bring a characterised residual, never the source."* The first contact is the residual, the shape claim, and the refuter.
- **Legal.** Use institutional submission portals and public comment dockets. No scraped personal emails, no bulk send, no automation, and an opt-out line in every direct message. The venues in `03_public_contact_points.csv` are public and opt-in by construction.

## 4. The two-stage offer

**Stage 1.** Send the residual, the shape claim, and the five pre-registered refuters. Ask one question: what would you count as a failure here? Record the answer before any theory is shown. A reply is a conversation, not corroboration.

**Stage 2, opt-in only.** If they are curious, send the three public links: the figure text and formula, the canonical usage note, and `08_expert_verification_packet.md`. State that the figures, not this summary, are the authority.

**Stage 3, opt-in only.** Offer the formula as a method to reuse. Each user writes their own refuter, states their own mode, and names a ground that can refuse their result. `THE_FORMULA.md` "Direction" says a frame cannot check the direction of its own question; the architecture article warns outputs *"could never be verified if the solutions presented were not contrived to fit the formula."*

**Hard precondition.** Stages 1 to 3 only work if the stage-1 claim survives. The refuter in `01_figure_grounded_audit.md` §3 is live; no part of this may be presented as `grounded` until one of its terms is tested against a real contract and a real long-term outcome.
