**Subject:** One checkable claim about incentive contracts, before any theory

> Hi [name],
>
> I am working on a figure-grounded derivation in executive compensation and corporate governance. I have one specific output I would like you to check against what you already know from the literature. I am not asking you to read the framework first, and I am not asking you to agree with anything. If the output does not survive, there is nothing else to look at.
>
> **The residual, in effect terms:** an incentive contract is supposed to turn leadership effort into pay through performance measures. The residual is the gap between what the contract rewards (a reading, such as adjusted EPS, revenue, TSR or peer rank) and what the firm actually needs (realized long-term outcomes). "Easy to measure and manipulate" is the case where the reading moves and the ground does not. This is a union of at least four conditions, not a single question: horizon mismatch, metric manipulability, a verifier that shares management's evidence pool, and a contract whose bonds make the measurable target the objective.
>
> **Stage-1 claim (`specified`, not `grounded`):** an incentive arrangement can contact long-term value only if (1) its anchor is an outcome no party to the contract can move by opinion, (2) residuals are read per metric and per horizon and never averaged into one alignment number, (3) the arrangement (vesting, hurdles, comparison basis, discretion, clawback) carries the information, not the ingredient list, and (4) the verifier lacks access to the maker's interior and shares no evidence pool with it. This is the shape of an answer, not a named metric. The framework returns a shape, not a name.
>
> **What I am not claiming:** that a particular metric is the correct one for your firm or for firms in general; that higher pay-performance sensitivity implies alignment; that independent directors are automatically independent merely by being outside directors; that any empirical regularity in the pay literature settles a specific contract. Those are filters for where to look, not witnesses for the verdict.
>
> **The refuter I wrote down before running this:**
> 1. a contract whose anchor is a movable metric still tracks long-term outcomes out of sample;
> 2. one aggregated pay-performance correlation identifies the failing metric and horizon without losing slot information;
> 3. the metric and long-term value are the same quantity at the relevant horizon, so metric-only movement is impossible in principle;
> 4. two contracts with identical ingredients but different bonds behave identically on realized long-term outcomes;
> 5. a verifier with full access to the maker's interior and evidence pool corrects metric-only movement as reliably as one with independent access.
>
> If any of those holds, I withdraw or narrow the claim. A one-line "this fails because ..." is more useful to me than agreement. If you would rather not engage, no reply is needed.
>
> **If it survives, and only then:** I can send three public sources: the figure text and formula, the canonical usage note, and the verification packet with the verbatim lines and provenance. The figures, not my summary, are the authority.
>
> **If you want to reuse the method:** the formula is in the public figures link. Run it on a compensation problem you can check against a ground. Write down what would refute your result *before* you run it, and state the mode of what you get. A reported failure is more useful to me than a reported success.
>
> Thanks,
> [your name]
