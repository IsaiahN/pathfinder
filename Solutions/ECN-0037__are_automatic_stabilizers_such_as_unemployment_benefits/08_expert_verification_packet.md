# Expert verification packet: the Tether on automatic stabilizers

**What this is:** a claim in a form a fiscal-policy researcher can check. Mode,
derivation and refuters are stated before the conclusion, so a reader knows what to look
for and what would settle it.

**Mode: `specified` on the per-domain reliability shape, `general` on the union framing,
`gap` on magnitude. Not `grounded`.** No aggregate estimate is claimed. Simulated
derivation checked for internal consistency against the figures, not a ground reading.

## 1. The claim, in one paragraph

The comparison "automatic stabilizers versus discretionary stimulus" is a union of two
conditions with different anchors, so no single number ranks them. On the channel-and-
bond properties the figures do return a shape. A standing rule such as unemployment
benefits or progressive taxes is a closed loop bonded to a counter that does not move
when a frame changes its mind: it fires per case without a new authorisation, scales with
the shock by the same kernel, and reverses sign with the gap. A new discretionary package
is a dependency chain whose perception, objective and planning links can break, whose
objective is a frame with a stake in the reading, and whose lags rotate the loop toward
oscillation. The two differ in bond type (cycle versus sequence), not in amount. No figure
line returns a multiplier or a universal winner, so magnitude is recorded as a `gap`.
What is offered is a testable shape, refuters written in advance, and the instruments
that would close the gap.

## 2. Three checkable sub-claims

### C1 - framework-internal, checkable by reading Fig 12 and Fig 2

- **Statement:** a standing rule has the shape of a closed feedback cycle bonded to a
  reference that does not update on the policy frame's reports. A sequence cannot close
  by construction, so a discretionary chain is a different bond type, not a smaller or
  larger instance of the same one.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 12 :121 - *"cycle. The output re-enters as input. The sign of
  the loop decides whether it closes, and a chain cannot close by construction."* Fig 2
  :29 - *"An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer
  because of what any frame thinks. A measurement, an experiment, a counter, a market, a
  patient outcome."* Fig 10 :24 - *"the ground does not decay, the channel does"*
- **What to check:** is the bond to realised income and employment, or to an estimate
  that updates on the policy's own reports? Is the response sign countercyclical in both
  directions?
- **Refuter:** show the automatic counter to be a frame that updates, or measure an
  automatic response that amplifies the gap. Either fails the claim.

### C2 - fiscal-policy facing, checkable by a researcher against observed episodes

- **Statement:** the discretionary route is a chain with separate perception, objective,
  planning and action links; the middle link (terms for what the situation affords plus
  a statement of what counts as progress) is the one most likely to have no instrument;
  the objective carries an interest in the reading; lags rotate the loop.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 3 :19 - *"Optimising a link that already works cannot rescue
  one that does not."* Fig 3 :27 - *"A link with no instrument is not a link that works.
  It is a link nobody has looked at."* Fig 10 :59 - *"an interest in the outcome is
  unavoidable, an interest in the reading is disqualifying."*
- **What to check:** in an episode you know, did the objective change while the shock was
  live? Which link actually stopped the chain? Was the channel read continuously or
  through pooled and stale estimates? A source-discriminating design matters here:
  reading the same episode that produced the policy cannot separate the two correctors.
- **Refuter:** exhibit a discretionary process with a pre-committed objective, a counter
  bond, no new authorisation and per-slot application. It is then automatic in the
  figure's sense and the domain boundary is wrong.

### C3 - framework-internal derivation about the limit

- **Statement:** the question is a union compound and no figure line returns an
  aggregate magnitude or a universal ranking. Size and net effect are a `gap`, which is a
  verdict and not a silence; the disagreement in the empirical literature belongs to
  that different quantity.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 9 :55 - *"A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping
  conditions, not a list of separate ones."* Fig 9 :32 - *"you get a kind of thing, never
  the answer itself."* Fig 8 :39 - *"So a search that finds nothing is never a proof of
  absence."*
- **What to check:** can you name a figure line that ranks two policy instruments by
  multiplier or stabilisation share? If you cannot, the `gap` is honest. If the empirical
  literature is contested, confirm that the contest is about magnitude rather than
  about the channel shape.
- **Refuter:** supply a figure line that returns a magnitude ranking, or show that the
  empirical disagreement is actually about the channel and anchor properties. Then the
  `gap` closes or the shape verdict narrows.

## 3. What is explicitly not claimed

- Not that automatic stabilizers are larger, better funded, or sufficient for a large
  shock. That is magnitude and is a `gap`.
- Not that discretionary stimulus is useless or always late. A counter-bonded,
  pre-committed discretionary process would satisfy the shape.
- Not that any instrument carries a fixed valence. Fig 11 :51: *"No standing list of
  good actors survives a change of residual."*
- Not that the economics literature has settled the question. The 2011 AEA follow-up
  found the earlier consensus *"had dissolved and was by then roughly evenly disputed."*
- Not `grounded`, and not a claim that the framework settles the question it reports on.
  This is a simulated derivation with an internal-consistency check only.

## 5. What would settle it

A source-discriminating instrument, not a further argument. From *The Hard Problem is
About Source*: a reading *"cannot comment on the source,"* and *"An instrument that can't
distinguish these cases demonstrates its own inadequacy."* Here:

1. Does the response track realised income and employment, or an estimate that moves
   with the frame? An instrument separating those readings names the source.
2. Is the reference reachable continuously, or through stale and pooled readings?
3. Does the measured response dampen the gap in both directions, or does the lag rotate
   the loop into oscillation?
4. What is the aggregate contribution of each corrector under exogenous variation? This
   closes the magnitude `gap`; a same-episode reading cannot.
5. Do any of the five pre-registered refuters in `07_the_tether_answer.md` §5 fire?

## 6. What an expert can check in twenty minutes

1. Read Fig 12 :121, Fig 2 :29, Fig 10 :24, Fig 3 :19 and :27, Fig 10 :59, Fig 9 :55
   and :32. Eight short passages, verbatim in the local figure text.
2. Check C2 against one episode you know: did the objective move while the shock was
   live, and which link actually stopped the chain?
3. Name one instrument that would separate a counter-driven response from an
   estimate-driven response in the same episode, or say why none is available.
4. Send the refutation. A failure is more useful than an endorsement, and no reply is
   needed if you would rather not engage.
