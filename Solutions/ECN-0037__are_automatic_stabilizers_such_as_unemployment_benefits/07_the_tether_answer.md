# The Tether's answer on automatic stabilizers versus discretionary stimulus

**Mode: `specified` on the per-domain reliability shape, `general` on the union framing,
`gap` on the empirical magnitude ranking. Not `grounded`.** Simulated derivation checked
for internal consistency against the figures (`05_what_shape_means.md` §7), not a ground
reading.

## 1. The residual it answers

A shock opens a gap between actual and potential employment and output. The residual is
that gap, indexed per household and firm, not aggregated (Fig 1 :19: *"The residual is
indexed by object slot, not aggregated."*; :22: *"Aggregating across slots is how a live
signal disappears into an average."*). Two correctors are offered: a standing rule
(unemployment benefits, progressive taxes) and a new decision (discretionary stimulus).
"More reliable" is a comparison of two conditions with different anchors, so it is a
**union compound**, not one dial (Fig 9 :55: *"A bundled problem is usually a union of
overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones."*).

## 2. The answer, layer by layer

1. **The single-dial verdict is refused.** "A is more reliable than B" flattens a union
   into a partition. Fig 9 :57: *"What remains is smaller and it is also categorically
   different: a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either."* Verdicts are
   kept per domain and never averaged. This is a `general` structural refusal.
2. **The standing rule has the reliability shape.** It is a closed cycle bonded to a
   counter that does not move when a frame thinks (Fig 12 :121: *"cycle. The output
   re-enters as input. The sign of the loop decides whether it closes, and a chain cannot
   close by construction."*; Fig 2 :29: *"An anchor is whatever settles the question and
   does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks."*). Income falls,
   receipts fall, transfers rise, disposable income is partly restored, and the output
   re-enters. It fires without a new authorisation, acts per eligible case, scales with
   the size of the shock by the same kernel (Fig 4 :19-20), and its sign reverses with
   the sign of the gap (Fig 11 :49). This is `specified`, not `grounded`.
3. **The discretionary route has to rebuild the channel and the objective each time.**
   It is a dependency chain with separate perception, objective, planning and action
   links, and the middle link is the one that most often has no instrument (Fig 3 :19,
   :20, :27). Its nominal anchor is a model estimate and a political objective, which are
   frames and can update on the policy's own reports. Because political stakes attach to
   the reading, the seat is disqualified under Fig 10 :59: *"an interest in the outcome
   is unavoidable, an interest in the reading is disqualifying."* Delays are not only
   delays: they rotate the phase of the loop and can turn a damper into an oscillator.
   This is `specified` on shape, corroborated from low-overlap control theory and game
   theory but not derived from them.
4. **The two differ in bond type, not amount.** Automatic is a cycle and a catalyst;
   discretionary is a sequence with a comparison goal and a spent part. Over a fixed atom
   set they are a genuine isomer claim (Fig 12 :117); as usually described they are
   members of one functional group (Fig 12 :123). See `06_isomers_and_order.md`.
5. **Magnitude is a `gap`.** No figure line returns a multiplier, a stabilisation share
   of GDP, or a universal winner. Fig 9 :32: *"you get a kind of thing, never the answer
   itself."* Fig 13 :134: *"It can say the answer is out of reach with the library as it
   stands, so something has to be imported before it is asked again."* The empirical
   literature is contested: a 2000 AEA survey showed 71 percent agreeing that activist
   fiscal policy should be avoided, and a 2011 follow-up found that consensus *"had
   dissolved and was by then roughly evenly disputed."* That is evidence about magnitude,
   a different quantity, and it is kept separate rather than used to flip the shape
   verdict.

## 3. What it explicitly does not say

- Not that automatic stabilizers are bigger, better funded, or always sufficient. A
  standing rule can be too small for a large shock, and that is a magnitude question the
  figures do not answer.
- Not that discretionary stimulus is useless. A discretionary process with a
  pre-committed objective and a counter bond would be automatic in the sense used here;
  that is written as a refuter in §5 and in `08_expert_verification_packet.md`.
- Not that an instrument carries a fixed valence. Fig 11 :51: *"No standing list of good
  actors survives a change of residual."* A progressive schedule stabilises a shortfall
  and withdraws demand in a boom.
- Not that the empirical literature is settled. It is not, and the disagreement is
  recorded rather than smoothed over.
- Not `grounded`. No measurement at the level of the question was taken in this folder.
- Not a claim that the framework settles the question it reports on. It is a simulation,
  checked for internal consistency only.

## 4. What would settle it

A discriminator, not a further argument. *The Hard Problem is About Source* (opened this
session): an instrument that reads a value *"cannot comment on the source"*; several
paths can create the same reading, and the right response is a better instrument. Here
the corresponding questions are:

1. **Anchor test.** Does the corrective response track realised income and employment,
   or an estimate that moves when the policy frame reports change? An instrument that
   separates those two readings would name the source.
2. **Channel test.** Is the reference reachable continuously, or through stale and
   pooled readings arriving after the gap has moved? Fig 10 :24-26 gives the failure
   signature.
3. **Phase test.** Does the measured response dampen the gap in both directions, or does
   the lag rotate the loop into oscillation? This is a control-theory measurement, not
   a debate about intentions.
4. **Magnitude test.** On the dimension actually scored, what is the aggregate
   contribution of each corrector, identified with exogenous variation rather than
   read off the same episode that produced the policy? This is what would close the
   `gap`. It is a budget-office and empirical-instrument task (INWARD), not a new figure
   import (OUTWARD).
5. **Standing falsifiers.** A measured amplification of the gap by the automatic
   response; a discretionary process that is counter-bonded, pre-committed, per-slot and
   lag-free; a fresh decision required when the shock changes scale; a figure line that
   directly ranks the two instruments. Any of these redraws the answer.

## 5. Pre-registered refuter

Written before the conclusion (see `01_figure_grounded_audit.md` §4). The shape claim is
refuted if:

1. the automatic counter is shown to be a frame that updates on its own reports;
2. the automatic response is measured to amplify the gap;
3. a discretionary process is exhibited with a pre-committed objective, a counter bond,
   no new authorisation and per-slot application;
4. the standing rule is shown to require a fresh decision when the shock moves;
5. a ground measurement on the scored dimension flips the magnitude comparison, which is
   accepted as a separate-domain finding and never averaged into the shape verdict.

## 6. The honest stop

**Unreached for magnitude, not unreachable in principle.** The instruments exist and are
in use: budget estimates, identified empirical studies, household-level transfer data.
They are simply not the figures, and their results are contested. The framework stops at
the limit of what its instruments can distinguish and records the lineage so the stop is
checkable. Fig 13: *"Unreachable is a verdict here, not a silence."* The record of the
route matters more than a forced answer (Fig 13 :127-129). What survives is the shape and
the refuter, not a winner.

## 7. One-paragraph version for outreach

> The Tether does not say that automatic stabilizers beat discretionary stimulus, and it
> does not return a multiplier. It says the comparison is a union of two conditions with
> different anchors, so no single number ranks them. On the channel-and-bond properties
> it does give a verdict: a standing rule such as unemployment benefits or progressive
> taxes is a closed loop bonded to a counter that does not move when a committee changes
> its mind, firing per case, scaling with the shock and reversing sign with the gap. A new
> discretionary package is a chain whose perception, objective and planning links can
> break, whose objective is a frame with a stake in the reading, and whose lags rotate the
> loop toward oscillation. That is a shape and a set of six tests, not a measurement.
> The magnitude question is declared a gap because the figures return no ranking and the
> empirical literature is contested. What is offered is the shape any defensible answer
> must have, the refuters written in advance, and the instruments that would close the
> gap. The score stays open until the ground settles it.
