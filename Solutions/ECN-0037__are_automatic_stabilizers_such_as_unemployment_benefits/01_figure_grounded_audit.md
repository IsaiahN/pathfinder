## 0. The residual, stated in effect terms

The question compares two correctors of the same deviation. A shock opens a gap
between actual and potential output and employment. The residual `R` is that gap,
indexed per slot: which household lost income, which plant idled, which region's tax
base shrank. Fig 1 :19: *"The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated."*
Fig 1 :22: *"Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average."*

"Reliable" is not a primitive in the figures. It must be stated as an effect before
any figure line is chosen. A corrector is reliable here when all of the following
hold:

| property | effect test | figure that governs it |
|---|---|---|
| anchor | it ranges against a reference that does not move when a frame changes its mind | Fig 2 :29, Fig 10 :24 |
| channel | the reference stays reachable while the residual is live; no stale percepts | Fig 10 :25-26 |
| decision | no new authorisation is required for the response to fire | Fig 4 :19-20, Fig 7 :36 |
| index | it acts per slot, not on an aggregate average | Fig 1 :19, :22 |
| sign | its valence flips with the sign of the gap | Fig 11 :49-51 |
| scale | the same kernel works at one household and at a million | Fig 4 :19-20 |

The question asks for a comparison of two conditions on those six properties. It is
therefore a **union compound**, not one dial with two settings. Fig 9 :55: *"A bundled
problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones."*
Fig 9 :57: *"What remains is smaller and it is also categorically different: a case in
two conditions is not a harder case of either."*

**What an answer would look like, bound as an ordinary term (Direction).** The sought
shape is: *a standing rule bonded to a non-updating counter, firing per slot with a
valence that flips, versus a seat-authored sequence that must re-establish the
channel and the objective each time.* Binding it now makes the preference refusable
by the same test as everything else (Formula "Direction"; Fig 5 :65-66). The refuter
in §3 is written before any conclusion is drawn.

## 1. Domain census (before choosing any figure line)

R by arity, symmetry and scale, then one line per domain with actors, currency and
anchor. A line with no nameable anchor is not yet a domain.

- **R (the residual).** Arity: two corrector conditions plus one shock, so a union,
  not a partition. Symmetry: asymmetric. The automatic response is a closed cycle
  whose sign reverses with the gap; the discretionary response is an ordered sequence
  with an authorising seat at its head. Scale: individual household and firm slots up
  to the national aggregate, with per-slot residuals hidden by aggregation.
- **Domain 1, the standing-rule domain (automatic stabilizers).** Actors: households,
  firms, the tax schedule, the benefit eligibility rules, the payment system. Currency:
  disposable income and aggregate demand. Anchor: realised income and employment, a
  counter and a market outcome that does not update on what any frame thinks (Fig 2
  :29). Question type: a **cycle** (Fig 12 :121), not a preference or a vote.
- **Domain 2, the seat-authored domain (discretionary stimulus).** Actors: legislature,
  executive, agencies, committees. Currency: authorised outlays and votes. Anchor: a
  model estimate of the output gap and a political objective. That anchor is itself a
  frame reading and can move when the frame's own reports change, so it fails the
  anchor test until re-grounded. Question type: a **dependency chain** (Fig 3) with
  separate perception, objective, planning and action links, and a new room booked
  for each shock (Fig 7 :36).
- **Domain 3, the verification domain.** Actors: budget offices, forecasters,
  econometricians. Currency: estimates, multipliers, counterfactual residuals. Anchor:
  the realised outcome, which is only readable after the event. This domain has low
  independence: most estimates draw on the same national accounts and model families
  (Fig 2 :31). Question type: measurement, not decision.

**Partition or union.** Union. Both correctors can be live at once, and one lever
(a larger stimulus bill) clears only the region where the other condition is absent
(Fig 9 :54, :56). Verdicts must be kept per domain and never averaged into a single
"more reliable" score.

**Order by dependency (Fig 3 :20).** The chain is ordered by dependency, not by
importance: *"The chain is ordered by dependency, not by importance."* So the order is
(1) is the channel to a non-updating anchor reachable at all, (2) is there a term for
what the situation affords and what would count as progress (Fig 3 :27, *"A link with
no instrument is not a link that works. It is a link nobody has looked at."*), (3) can
a route be planned and authorised, (4) does the response fire per slot. Effort spent
above a break arrives at a step that cannot use it (Fig 3 :19: *"Optimising a link
that already works cannot rescue one that does not."*).

**Strain test applied before any verdict.** My first pass tried to map "automatic"
onto one of the six substrate terms in Fig 13, treating it as a kind of Network or
Gradient. That strains: Fig 13 :41 says *"Each is indivisible: no arrangement of the
other five yields it,"* and the six are physical states and structures, not policy
properties. Re-census: "automatic" is a property of the **bond to the ground and the
absence of a new decision**, not a member of the six. The apparent conflict dissolves,
and the strain is recorded in §7 rather than smoothed over.

## 3. Findings F1 to F10

### F1 - The comparison is a union, not a partition (decisive for the shape of the answer)

Fig 9 :54: *"And when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before
you search again."* Fig 9 :55: *"A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping
conditions, not a list of separate ones."* Fig 9 :56: *"A single lever clears the region
where only one condition holds and leaves the intersections standing, because the second
condition still binds."*

Automatic stabilizers and discretionary stimulus coexist in every modern fiscal system.
Asking which is "more reliable" as though one must win treats a union as a partition.
The correct move is to unbundle into the six properties in §0 and give a verdict per
domain. A single answer of the form "A is more reliable than B" is a filter for a
headline, not a witness for the world (Fig 9 :60: *"Use filters for the budget and
witnesses for the verdict. Never let a filter hand you a verdict."*).

- **Mode:** `general` structural, `specified` per domain.
- **Status:** triangulated across Fig 9 :54-57 and the dependency order of Fig 3 :20
  (low overlap: union logic versus chain order).
- **Refuter:** show that the two conditions are mutually exclusive by construction, so
  that a partition is the correct form. No source read says this; the world sources
  describe both operating together.

### F2 - The standing rule has the shape of a closed cycle bonded to a non-updating counter

Fig 12 :121: *"cycle. The output re-enters as input. The sign of the loop decides whether
it closes, and a chain cannot close by construction."*

Fig 2 :29: *"An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer
because of what any frame thinks. A measurement, an experiment, a counter, a market, a
patient outcome."*

Fig 10 :24: *"the ground does not decay, the channel does"*

A progressive tax schedule and an unemployment benefit rule read realised income and
employment directly. Income falls, receipts fall and transfers rise, disposable income
is partly restored, demand is supported, and output re-enters as the next input. The
reference is a counter and a market outcome, not a committee's estimate. The loop is
closed without a new authorisation because the rule is the regulator, and the signal it
reduces is exactly the gap between the measured variable and its set point. This is why
the automatic domain satisfies the anchor, channel, index, sign and scale properties in
§0. Critically, the loop does **not** require the rule to be wise. It requires only that
the bond be to the counter and that the sign be countercyclical. Reliability here is a
property of the contact, not of the intelligence of the designer, which is Fig 11 :40:
*"An improvement that does not change contact changes nothing, however much it improves."*

- **Mode:** `specified`. The shape is claimed; no magnitude is claimed.
- **Status:** triangulated across Fig 12 (bond structure), Fig 2 (anchor definition) and
  Fig 10 (channel decay). Three different figures, low conceptual overlap.
- **Refuter:** show that the transfer and tax readings are driven by a frame that updates
  on its own reports, so the anchor moves and panel 3 of Fig 2 collapses into panel 1.
  Or show that the response amplifies rather than dampens the gap (sign of the loop
  wrong). Either fires → the automatic-domain verdict is withdrawn.

### F3 - Discretionary stimulus inserts a seat and a chain with breakable links

Fig 3 :19: *"Optimising a link that already works cannot rescue one that does not."*
Fig 3 :20: *"The chain is ordered by dependency, not by importance."*
Fig 3 :27: *"A link with no instrument is not a link that works. It is a link nobody has
looked at."*

Fig 7 :36: *"A chain of mirrors is one long room. The next room must differ from the last,
and match the residual it surfaced."*

A discretionary package requires perception (is there a gap?), a vocabulary and an
objective (what counts as progress, and for whom?), planning (which instrument, which
district, which timeline?), and only then action. Each is a separate link that can stop
the chain. Fig 3 :25-26 names the middle link as the one that usually breaks because it
has neither obvious failures nor obvious tests: having terms for what a situation
affords and being able to say what would count as progress. That is precisely the
political disagreement about what the stimulus is for. A reading taken below such a
break is a reading of nothing (Fig 3 :22-23). And because each package is a new room,
the room must differ from the last and match the residual it surfaced; otherwise the
chain is one long room producing motion without progress (Fig 7 :32: *"one long room:
motion, no progress"*).

- **Mode:** `specified`.
- **Status:** triangulated across Fig 3, Fig 7 and Fig 10 :25-26, all framework-internal,
  so this is **one evidence pool** internally. The low-overlap corroboration comes from
  the world sources in §6 (automaticity in cognition; lags in control theory).
- **Refuter:** exhibit a discretionary process whose objective is fixed before the shock,
  whose response is bonded to a non-updating counter, and which fires per slot without a
  new decision. That process is automatic in the figure's sense and the domain boundary
  collapses. I would then withdraw the discretionary-domain verdict.

### F4 - Scale invariance: the same kernel versus a fresh occasion

Fig 4 :19-20: *"The kernel is the part that does not change with size: the same loop and
the same rule for naming what worked, whether one agent is running it or a thousand. Only
the actors, the currency and the anchor change."*

An automatic rule is the kernel. It runs for one claimant and for ten million with the
same eligibility condition and the same payment logic. Only the actors and the amounts
change. A discretionary package is a fresh occasion: it must be authored, authorised
and targeted anew, and each authoring is a new room (Fig 7 :36). This is not a claim that
one is bigger than the other. It is a claim about what has to be rebuilt when the shock
changes size or location. Fig 4 :25: *"A recording carried upward looks like knowledge and
is a description of one occasion."* A stimulus bill is a recording of one occasion
presented as a general rule; the standing schedule is the generator.

- **Mode:** `specified`.
- **Status:** triangulated (Fig 4 :19-20 plus Fig 7 :36, low overlap between scale
  invariance and room selection).
- **Refuter:** show a discretionary mechanism with a standing delegated rule that fires
  identically at all scales without new authorisation. Then the distinction is one of
  label, not of bond, and this finding reduces to F3's refuter.

### F5 - Interest in the reading disqualifies the seat

Fig 10 :59: *"an interest in the outcome is unavoidable, an interest in the reading is
disqualifying."* Fig 10 :47: *"a convention that decides an outcome has stopped being one"*

The automatic rule does not consult a preference about the outcome. Its reading is the
counter. A discretionary objective, by contrast, is authored by a seat with a stake in
the reading: an administration that wants re-election, a legislature that wants credit,
a committee that wants to protect its previous programme. Fig 10 :60: *"Where a stake
cannot be removed, declare it. An undeclared interest is modelled anyway and an unnamed
model runs unchallenged."* When a declared target (say, a numerical employment goal) is
used to settle the fiscal response, a convention has become a verdict and stops being a
convention. The world source on dynamic inconsistency gives this an independent frame:
the Kydland and Prescott result and the central-bank-discretion passage in the Wikipedia
entry show that discretion under political pressure delivers a time-inconsistency bias,
which is the same structural failure described in game-theory terms rather than seat
terms (low overlap with the figures).

- **Mode:** `specified` on the structural claim; world corroboration is secondary.
- **Status:** triangulated across Fig 10 and the game-theory source. The two are
  genuinely low-overlap frames, so their agreement is weak corroboration, not a pooled
  tautology.
- **Refuter:** exhibit an incentive-compatible discretionary process whose objective
  cannot be moved by its own stake and whose record shows no drift. That refutes the
  seat-capture claim for that process, not for the domain in general.

### F6 - Per-slot versus aggregate action

Fig 1 :19: *"The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated."* Fig 1 :22:
*"Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average."*

Unemployment benefits and withholding schedules act on each eligible household's
income as a direct function of that household's state. A discretionary package is
typically allocated by aggregate criteria, region or sector, and arrives as a lump. It
can miss the live slot: the household whose residual is still positive while the
national average has recovered. This is not an argument that discretion is always
worse targeted. It is an argument that aggregation is the failure mode to test for,
and that per-slot indexing is a property the automatic domain has by construction.
Fig 1 :27 disqualifies the self-scoring response: *"They are frame-internal. A frame
cannot score itself with a quantity it also produces."* A package that reports its own
success using the employment it created is scoring itself with a quantity it also
produces.

- **Mode:** `specified`.
- **Status:** single-source on the figure side (Fig 1 only), so not upgraded to
  triangulated. Corroborated in mechanism shape by the per-household transfer evidence
  in the Federal Reserve FEDS paper (a different field, but only a shape analogue).
- **Refuter:** show a discretionary instrument that is per-slot by construction (for
  example, an automatic trigger within a discretionary act) and the indexing distinction
  between the two domains dissolves for that instrument.

### F7 - Channel decay, lags and the phase of the loop

Fig 10 :25-26: *"stale percepts, dropped transients, saturated metrics, pooled readings"*
and *"the output is not a verdict, it is you have lost touch"* (Fig 10 :27-28).

The recognition, legislative and implementation lags of discretionary policy are not
mere delays. In a closed loop, a delay rotates the phase of the feedback signal. The
control-theory source states the engineering result directly: Maxwell's analysis of the
centrifugal governor found *"self-oscillation, in which lags in the system may lead to
overcompensation and unstable behavior,"* and the negative-feedback entry notes that
*"negative feedback systems can still be subject to oscillations. This is caused by a
phase shift around any loop."* The automatic loop has the shorter channel because the
counter is read continuously; the discretionary loop's channel is the one that decays
(Fig 10 :24). This is the strongest genuinely low-overlap corroboration in the folder.

- **Mode:** `specified` on the figure claim, `general` on the control-theory analogue.
- **Status:** triangulated across Fig 10 :24-28 (channel decay) and the engineering
  sources (lag-induced instability), with low overlap.
- **Refuter:** show a discretionary process whose lag is short enough and phase margin
  large enough that it does not overcompensate in a measured episode. That is a
  ground measurement and would narrow, not flip, the structural verdict.

### F8 - What the figures cannot decide: magnitude, multiplier, universal winner

Fig 9 :32: *"you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself."*
Fig 13 :134: *"It can say the answer is out of reach with the library as it stands, so
something has to be imported before it is asked again."*
Fig 8 :39: *"So a search that finds nothing is never a proof of absence."*

No figure line returns a multiplier, a stableisation share of GDP, or a ranking of two
instruments by aggregate effect. That quantity needs a ground: budget-office estimates,
identified empirical studies, or a natural experiment. The world literature on the
magnitude is genuinely contested. The Wikipedia fiscal-policy entry records that a 2000
survey of 298 AEA members found 84 percent agreeing that fiscal policy has a significant
stimulative impact, while 71 percent also agreed that activist fiscal policy should be
avoided, and that *"In 2011, a follow-up survey of 568 AEA members found that the previous
consensus about the latter proposition had dissolved and was by then roughly evenly
disputed."* A conflict in the world literature sends us back to the census, not to a
flipped verdict. Re-census: the disagreement is about **magnitude and net effect**, a
different quantity from the **channel and bond shape** the figures decide. Both are kept
separate. The magnitude is declared `gap`, and the gap is a verdict, not a silence
(Fig 13: *"Unreachable is a verdict here, not a silence"*).

- **Mode:** `gap` on magnitude, `specified` on shape.
- **Status:** gap after genuine re-census. Not `unreachable-in-principle`; the instruments
  exist (budget estimates, empirical identification), but they are outside the figures
  and are contested.
- **Refuter:** a figure line that directly ranks two policy instruments by aggregate
  magnitude. None exists in `03-FIGURES_TEXT.md`. If one were supplied, this gap would
  close.

### F9 - Agreement in the economics literature is one pool, not several witnesses

Fig 2 :31: *"Independence, between two frames, is how little they draw on the same
evidence."* Fig 2 :40: *"disqualifying for a verdict: agreement about the world is
worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool"* Fig 8 :50: *"Two frames can
hold the same atom by convergence, reaching it separately, which is corroboration at
maximum independence."*

The institutional sources read for this folder (budget office, fiscal-policy survey,
automatic-stabilizer reference) largely draw on the same national-accounts and
model-based evidence pool. Their agreement about the short-run functioning of
stabilizers is therefore one witness, not three. The genuinely lower-overlap witnesses
are the control-theory sources, the game-theory time-inconsistency result, and the
cognitive-science account of automaticity. Those corroborate the **shape**, not the
magnitude. Their overlap is declared in `10_source_ledger.csv`.

- **Mode:** `general` on verdict weight.
- **Status:** triangulated only where the low-overlap sources are used (F2, F5, F7).
- **Refuter:** show that the control-theory and economics sources share a single
  originating evidence pool. They do not; they are different fields with different
  instruments, though the mapping is an analogue and not a derivation.

### F10 - The sign of an automatic response is relative to the residual

Fig 11 :49: *"An actor's valence is relative to the residual, not a property it carries."*
Fig 11 :51: *"No standing list of good actors survives a change of residual."*

It is tempting to say that automatic stabilizers are "good" and discretionary stimulus
is "slow." Both are standing lists of actor properties, and Fig 11 refuses them. A
progressive tax schedule is a stabiliser when the gap is a demand shortfall; the same
schedule withdraws demand when the gap is an inflationary boom, and can then be the
wrong instrument. The sign flips with the residual (Fig 12 :121: *"The sign of the loop
decides whether it closes"*). Reliability is therefore not a property carried by an
instrument. It is a relation between that instrument's bond, the sign of the current
gap, and a ground that does not move with the frame. This is recorded as a standing
constraint on every verdict in `07_the_tether_answer.md`.

- **Mode:** `specified`.
- **Status:** triangulated (Fig 11 :49-51 and Fig 12 :121, different figures, low overlap).
- **Refuter:** exhibit a fixed instrument whose valence is invariant to the residual
  across both boom and bust. That would refute the valence-relative claim, not the whole
  derivation.

## 4. Pre-registered refuter

Required before any conclusion, per the seat-map discipline: a falsifier written before
the run is a design instrument. The shape claim is refuted if any of the following
holds:

1. **Anchor collapse:** a discretionary corrector is shown whose objective updates on
   its own reports and whose readings are pooled with the thing it measures, so that
   its agreement is the pool talking (Fig 2 :13-15, :35, :40). This actually *strengthens*
   the discretionary-failure verdict, so the operative falsifier is the converse: the
   automatic counter is shown to be a frame that updates, collapsing Fig 2 panel 3 into
   panel 1.
2. **No closed loop:** the automatic response is shown to amplify the gap rather than
   reduce it in a measured episode (wrong sign, Fig 12 :121).
3. **Automaticity without automaticity:** a discretionary process is exhibited with a
   pre-committed objective, a counter-bonded response, no new authorisation and per-slot
   application. It is then automatic in the figure's sense and the domain boundary
   drawn in §1 is wrong.
4. **Magnitude inversion on the measured dimension:** a ground measurement shows the
   discretionary aggregate dominating the automatic response on the dimension actually
   being scored. This is accepted as a separate-domain finding, not as a refutation of
   the channel-shape claim; the two are never averaged.
5. **Scale failure:** the standing rule is shown to require a new decision when the
   shock's size or location changes (breaks Fig 4 :19-20).

If any of 1, 2, 3 or 5 fires, the shape claim is withdrawn or redrawn. Until a ground
reading exists, no part of this folder may be labelled `grounded`.

## 5. Recommendation

1. Lead with the union framing and the six property tests, not with a verdict about
   which instrument wins (F1).
2. State the mode on every claim: `specified` for shape, `general` for the union, `gap`
   for magnitude (F8).
3. Present the automatic domain as a cycle bonded to a counter, and the discretionary
   domain as a chain with breakable links and a potentially moving objective (F2, F3).
4. Corroborate with the low-overlap frames only: control theory, game theory, cognitive
   automaticity. Do not pool the economics sources as independent witnesses (F9).
5. Ask for a refutation of the six property tests, not for agreement with the verdict.
   A failure is more useful than an endorsement.
6. Keep the shape claim and the refuter in front of the links. Fig 10 :52: *"Bring a
   characterised residual, never the source."*

## 7. Strains, re-census and honest stop

- **S1, substrate strain (resolved):** "automatic" is not one of the six terms. Re-census
  moves it to the bond and the decision rule. Kept visible above rather than erased.
- **S2, anchor of the discretionary domain (live but narrowed):** the objective is a
  frame, not an anchor. That is a testable defect, not a reason the domain cannot be
  censused, because a counter can be installed. F3's refuter carries it.
- **S3, world/figure apparent conflict (dissolved):** contested multiplier estimates do
  not conflict with a claim about channel shape. Different quantities.
- **S4, control-theory analogue (live, named):** a control loop's set point and a
  fiscal objective are not the same kind of thing. The analogue supports the lag and
  sign claims only. It is not used as a derivation of fiscal magnitudes.
- **After re-census: two live narrowed strains, two dissolved, none hidden.**

**Honest stop.** The figure-derived residue is a shape and a route: a standing rule
bonded to a non-updating counter has the reliability properties; a seat-authored
sequence has to rebuild the channel, the objective and the room each time. No figure
line settles which delivers more aggregate stabilisation in a given recession. The
folder stops there and records the lineage (Fig 13 :126-129: the record is what makes
the composition checkable). Unreachable at this budget for magnitude, not silence.
