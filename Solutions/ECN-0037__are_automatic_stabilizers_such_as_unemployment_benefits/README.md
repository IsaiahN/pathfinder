# Solution ECN-0037

**Question:** Are automatic stabilizers, such as unemployment benefits and progressive
taxes, more reliable than new discretionary stimulus?

**Source:** economics questions.txt line 50

**Section:** 4. Government spending, deficits, and public debt

**Question restated as an effect:** A shock opens a gap between actual and potential
employment and output. Something must close that gap. Two candidate correctors are
offered: a standing rule that fires without a new decision (automatic stabilizers),
and a newly authored decision (discretionary stimulus). The word to resolve is
"reliable." In effect terms, a corrector is reliable when its channel to a
non-updating reference stays reachable while the residual is live, when it indexes
the affected slots rather than an average, when it scales with the shock without a
new decision, and when its direction flips with the sign of the gap. An answer would
look like a per-domain verdict on those properties, plus a plain statement of what
the figures cannot decide.

**Mode:** `specified` on the per-domain reliability shape, `general` on the union
framing, `gap` on the empirical magnitude ranking. Nothing here is `grounded`.
Every substantive file is labelled a simulated derivation checked for internal
consistency against the figures, NOT a ground reading, so it cannot settle the
question it reports on.

## What is deliberately not here

- No personal emails of named individuals.
- No ranking of people by how likely they are to agree with the verdict.
- No bulk-send plan.

Three reasons, in the figures' own words:

1. A room does not upgrade a label. Agreement among frames is a weighted average of
   influence, and adding people raises the vocabulary without adding an anchor
   (Fig 2 :35, *"Alignment is triangulation, not negotiation. A mirror cannot correct you."*).
2. Selecting for receptiveness manufactures the correlation that disqualifies a
   verdict (Fig 2 :40, *"agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing
   frames share an evidence pool"*).
3. Asking for trust before a check spends the checker's independence (Fig 10 :42,
   *"A verifier that can reconstruct the claim cannot verify it."*).

Legally: unsolicited bulk email to scraped addresses breaches GDPR and CAN-SPAM style
rules in many jurisdictions and gets the sending domain blacklisted. That is a
practical failure on top of the structural one.

picked for likely agreement. Nothing is sent by this agent.
