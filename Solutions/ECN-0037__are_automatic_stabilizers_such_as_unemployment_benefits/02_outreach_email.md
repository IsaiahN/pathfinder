# Outreach draft: check the shape claim before you decide about the framework

**Use:** one to one, or as a cover note attached to a submission through the public
venues in `03_public_contact_points.csv`. **Not** for bulk send.

**Hard precondition:** do not send until a reader can answer the six property tests in
§1 without seeing the framework. If the stage-1 output cannot be checked on its own,
the two-stage offer collapses on contact.

---

**Subject:** Six tests for a fiscal-stabilizer claim, before any theory

> Hi [name],
>
> I am working on a figure-grounded derivation of a narrow question in fiscal policy. I
> have one specific output I would like you to check against what you already know. I am
> not asking you to read the framework first, and I am not asking you to agree with the
> conclusion. If the output does not survive, there is nothing else to look at.
>
> **The question in effect terms.** A shock opens a gap between actual and potential
> employment and output. Two correctors are offered: a standing rule that fires without
> a new decision (unemployment benefits, progressive taxes), and a newly authored
> decision (discretionary stimulus). "Reliable" is the word I need to resolve. In effect
> terms, a corrector is reliable when its channel to a non-updating reference stays
> reachable while the gap is live, when it acts per case rather than on an average, when
> it scales without a fresh authorisation, and when its sign flips with the sign of the
> gap.
>
> **The output (`specified` on shape, `general` on the union framing, `gap` on
> magnitude).** I get two domain verdicts, not one winner. The standing rule has the
> shape of a closed loop bonded directly to a counter: income falls, receipts fall and
> transfers rise, disposable income is partly restored, and output re-enters as the next
> input. The reference is realised income and employment, which do not move because a
> committee changes its mind. The discretionary route is a chain with separate links,
> perception, objective, planning and action, and a seat with a stake in the reading.
> Its reference is a model estimate and a political objective, which are frames and can
> move. The lags are not just delays: in a closed loop they rotate the phase of the
> feedback signal and can turn a damper into an oscillator.
>
> **What I am explicitly not claiming.** Not that automatic stabilizers are always
> larger than discretionary stimulus. The figures return no multiplier and no aggregate
> ranking; the empirical literature on magnitude is genuinely contested, so I record a
> `gap` rather than borrow a verdict. Not that discretion is always bad: a discretionary
> process with a pre-committed objective and a counter bond would be automatic in the
> sense used here, and I have written that as a refuter. Not that an instrument carries
> a fixed valence. A progressive schedule stabilises a demand shortfall and withdraws
> demand in a boom; the sign is relative to the residual.
>
> **The six tests I would like you to run:**
> 1. **Anchor.** Does the response range against realised income and employment, or
>    against an estimate that updates on the policy frame's own reports?
> 2. **Channel.** Is the counter reachable continuously, or through stale and pooled
>    readings that arrive after the residual has moved?
> 3. **Decision.** Does the response fire without a new authorisation when the shock
>    changes size or location?
> 4. **Index.** Does it act per household and firm, or on an aggregate average that
>    hides the live slots?
> 5. **Sign.** Does the response dampen the gap in both directions, or flip the wrong
>    way in a boom?
> 6. **Magnitude.** On the dimension you actually score, which dominates in the data you
>    trust? I accept this as a separate-domain finding; I will not average it into the
>    shape verdict.
>
> **What would refute the shape claim, written before this note:** (a) the automatic
> counter is shown to be a frame that updates on its own reports; (b) the automatic
> response is measured to amplify the gap; (c) a discretionary process is exhibited with
> a pre-committed objective, a counter bond, no new authorisation and per-slot
> application; (d) the standing rule is shown to need a fresh decision when the shock
> moves. If any holds, I redraw the derivation. A one-line "this fails because..."
> is more useful to me than agreement. If you would rather not engage, no reply is
> needed.
>
> **Please record your verdict on tests 1 to 6 before reading further.** That matters:
> once you have the framework's interior, your agreement stops being an outside check
> and becomes a conversation inside the frame.
>
> **If it survives, and only then:** the sources are below. The figures are the
> authority, not my summary of them.
> - Verbatim figure text with line numbers: `FRAMEWORK/reference/03-FIGURES_TEXT.md`
>   (local to this project; branch and canonical rule in the links file).
> - Automatic stabilizers, reference definition: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Automatic_stabilizer
> - Control theory, open loop versus closed loop and lag-induced oscillation:
>   https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Control_theory
> - Dynamic inconsistency, rules versus discretion:
>   https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dynamic_inconsistency
>
> **If you want to use the machinery:** the formula is in the figures. Run it on a
> problem you know well and can check against a ground. Write down what would refute
> your result *before* you run it, and state the mode of what you get. A reported
> failure is more useful to me than a reported success.
>
> Thanks for reading.
> [name]

## Why this version is ordered this way

| the move | figure-grounded reason |
|---|---|
| Output and tests first, theory second | Fig 10 :52 - *"Bring a characterised residual, never the source."* Frames range against the ground separately. |
| Do not ask for agreement | Fig 2 :35 - *"Alignment is triangulation, not negotiation. A mirror cannot correct you."* |
| Pre-registered refuter | The seat-map discipline: a falsifier written before the run is a design instrument, not only an honesty device. |
| Mode stated on every claim | `THE_FORMULA.md` - *"Mode. general · specified · grounded. State which."* |
| Verdict before links | Fig 10 :42 - *"A verifier that can reconstruct the claim cannot verify it."* |
| Magnitude held as `gap` | Fig 9 :32 - *"you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself."* No figure line returns a multiplier. |
| No ranking of recipients by receptiveness | Fig 2 :40 - agreement inside a shared pool is the pool talking. |
| No request for trust or prestige | *The Recursive Self-Improvement Trap*: prestige can fund an action but cannot settle it; only the ground can. |
| Ask users to state their own ground and refuter | Formula "Direction": a frame cannot check the direction of its own question. |
| One to one, no bulk send | Public opt-in channels only; no personal emails, no purchased lists, no automation. |
