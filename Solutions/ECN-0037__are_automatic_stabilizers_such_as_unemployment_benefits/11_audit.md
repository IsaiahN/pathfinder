## Independent domain census

I redid the census from the residual line alone, before relying on the primary's
conclusions, using the WATSON order: R by arity, symmetry and scale; one line per domain
with actors, currency and anchor; partition or union; order by dependency.

- **R (arity / symmetry / scale).** The residual is the unclosed gap between actual and
  potential employment and output after a shock. Arity: two corrector conditions plus the
  shock, so a **union compound**, not a partition. Fig 9 :55: *"A bundled problem is
  usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones."* Symmetry:
  asymmetric. The standing response is a sign-reversing cycle; the new decision is an
  ordered chain with an authorising seat. Scale: per household and firm slot up to the
  national aggregate, with aggregation hiding the live slots.
- **Domain 1, standing rule.** Actors: households, firms, schedule, benefit rules,
  payment system. Currency: disposable income and demand. Anchor: realised income and
  employment, a counter and a market outcome. Question type: cycle.
- **Domain 2, seat-authored decision.** Actors: legislature, executive, agencies.
  Currency: authorised outlays and votes. Anchor: a model estimate and a political
  objective, frames that can update on the policy's own reports. Question type: chain.
- **Domain 3, verification.** Actors: budget offices, forecasters. Currency: estimates.
  Anchor: realised outcome after the event; low independence because of shared accounts
  and model families.

Order by dependency: counter before objective before plan before outlay; refuse the
single-dial framing before minting a winner; describe the residual before choosing a
frame. Effort above a break cannot rescue it (Fig 3 :19).

**Comparison with the primary census.** My census matches the three domains and the

where the primary started from Fig 1's per-slot indexing and Fig 2's anchor definition.
Different starting figures, same domain boundaries, so the agreement has low internal

## Round trip gap R_T

The Symbols table defines R_T as the gap between what was sent up and what came back
down, `gap(x, (T_E ∘ T_A)(x))`, measuring what the coarser description cannot hold. Fig 4
:30 requires it to be measured rather than assumed. I ran the round trip on the
condensation this folder actually uses.

1. **Sent up (x):** the verbatim figure set, chiefly Fig 12 :121 (cycle versus chain),
   Fig 2 :29 and Fig 10 :24 (anchor and channel), Fig 9 :55 and :60 (union and filters).
2. **Abstraction (T_A):** the shorthand carried in `01` and `07`: a standing rule is a
   counter-bonded cycle, a new decision is a seat-authored chain, magnitude is a gap.
3. **Concretisation (T_E):** the concrete fiscal claim about unemployment benefits,
   progressive taxes and a new stimulus package.

**R_T is nonzero.** Four things the shorthand holds do not survive the trip back down:

- the **per-slot indexing** of Fig 1 :19/:22, which the phrase "stabilizes demand"
  flattens into an aggregate;
- the **interest-in-the-reading condition** of Fig 10 :59, which the phrase "political
  lag" reduces to a timing problem instead of a disqualifying stake;
- the **union structure** of Fig 9 :54-57, which the headline "more reliable than"
  restores to a partition;
- the **gap itself**, which a concrete policy comparison is tempted to replace with a
  borrowed magnitude from the empirical literature.

Conversely, one thing survives cleanly: a rule bonded to a non-updating counter has the
reliability shape, and a seat-authored chain has to rebuild the channel and the objective.
That is the residue worth sending. R_T was **not** measured numerically, because no
ground reading exists; it is recorded qualitatively as the enumerated set of dropped
constraints. Treating the formula as identical to the law set would set R_T to zero and
would be the dead-reckoning error Fig 4 :30 names. The honest form is a lossy condensation
with a measured gap, which `09_watson_answer.md` states.
