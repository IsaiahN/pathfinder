# The Tether's answer: causal explanation versus predictive correlation

**Mode: `specified` on the verdict, `general` on the operator shape. Not `grounded`.** This is a shape and a route, not a measurement. Simulated derivation checked for internal consistency against the figures (`05_what_shape_means.md` §6), not a ground reading.

## 1. The residual it answers

A correlation fits the observations; a causal explanation claims what would happen under intervention. When the action stays passive, both can score well. Change the action to `do(X)`, move to a new environment, or introduce an instrument that discriminates hidden sources, and the correlation's prediction can fail while the causal claim need not. That failure, indexed per case and per intervention, is the residual. A global fit hides it: Fig 1 :19 "The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated." and Fig 1 :22 "Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average."

## 2. The shape

1. **Correlation records co-occurrence; causal explanation carries the typed bond.** A correlation gives the operands and their joint strength, which is a magnitude, not a relation type. Fig 12 :46-47 "A bond is the typed relation between two operands. There are seven, they are not interchangeable, and which one holds is not recoverable from the operands. A recipe that names only its ingredients has recorded half of itself." The flat case is named at Fig 12 :14-16: "the ingredients are recorded and the arrangement is not", "nothing downstream can tell them apart, and nothing signals that anything was lost." The causal side adds sequence or production: Fig 12 :72 "A then B; B needs A to have happened" and Fig 12 :77 "A produces B; B did not exist before". The test that separates them is symmetry: Fig 12 :59 "swap the operands. Does the meaning change? No." against Fig 12 :74 "swap them. Does it become a different thing? Yes." Correlation passes the symmetric test; causation fails it by design. That is the smallest answer to "more informative".
2. **The extra information is the arrangement, so the alternatives are isomers.** Fig 12 :117 "isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour." Confounding, reverse causation and direct production can share the same correlation and disagree under intervention. Their difference is not more data; it is a different bond over the same observations.
3. **A causal explanation is a generator; a correlation is a recording.** Fig 4 :25 "A recording carried upward looks like knowledge and is a description of one occasion." Fig 11 :40 "An improvement that does not change contact changes nothing, however much it improves." The generator travels to a new context and supports an intervention; the recording is bound to the occasion it was fitted to. This is why the extra information shows up in transfer, not in fit.
4. **It ranges against an anchor, not against agreement.** Fig 2 :29 "An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks. A measurement, an experiment, a counter, a market, a patient outcome." For a causal claim that anchor is the intervention outcome or a source-discriminating instrument. By contrast, fit is frame-internal: Fig 1 :27 "They are frame-internal. A frame cannot score itself with a quantity it also produces."
5. **The claim is domain-scoped, not a global victory.** Fig 2 :39-41 gives agreement its valence: "Correlation has valence.", "disqualifying for a verdict: agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool", "efficient for a convention: agreement about a shared meaning is the product, not a corruption of it." For within-context forecasting and for coordinating a shared expectation, a correlation can be the better instrument. More informative holds for verdicts, interventions and transfer.
6. **There is no single informativeness score.** Fig 12 :125-126 "A composition costs in two currencies, and they do not add.", "Bits price the description. Actions price finding out whether it holds." The ordered bond costs more bits and buys intervention reach. A scalar that sums them is the named error.
7. **An unpaid causal label is not extra information.** Multiple hidden sources can produce the same reading, and the reading cannot say which (see `05_what_shape_means.md` §4). Without a discriminating instrument or a recorded prior ground reading, "causal" is a name where a shape is owed. In that case the honest correlation is the stronger claim.

## 3. What it explicitly does not say

- Not that causation always beats correlation. Correlation is efficient for convention (Fig 2 :41).
- Not that "more informative" means more mutual information, higher R-squared, or lower description length alone (Fig 1 :27; Fig 12 :125-126).
- Not that any story labelled causal is informative. The label must be settled at the ground (§2.7).
- Not which causal model is correct for any particular X and Y. Fig 9 :32 "you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself."
- Not that a panel of experts settles it. Fig 2 :35 "Alignment is triangulation, not negotiation. A mirror cannot correct you."
- Not grounded. No intervention was run for this derivation.

## 4. What would settle it - the ground

A discriminating action, not a further argument.

1. **Intervention test.** Does `P(Y | do(X))` differ from the observational conditional in the way the causal claim predicts, with the intervention and its outcome recorded? This is the anchor in Fig 2 :29.
2. **Transfer test.** Move the system to a new environment. Does the causal model still support a successful action where the correlation-only model fails? If both transfer equally, the contact difference is absent.
3. **Source test.** Find an instrument that discriminates among the hidden structures producing the same correlation (the spectrometer move from *The Hard Problem is About Source*). If no instrument can, the causal label stays below the union horizon.
4. **Currency test.** Exhibit a single unit in which bond cost and intervention cost add without loss, and show it reproduces the ranking. If none exists, the scalar reading is retired.
5. **Standing refuters from the audit:** a symmetric relation determining all post-intervention distributions; two same-correlation readings agreeing on every runnable intervention; a zero-cost bond under a declared code.

## 5. The honest stop

**Unreached, not unreachable.** The framework returns a shape: carry the typed ordered bond, make the hidden third term explicit, keep the residual per case, and range against an intervention anchor. It does not return a verdict about any real X and Y. Instruments already returning something can be sharpened (formula step 7, INWARD), and a frame whose closure already predicts the intervention shape can be found (OUTWARD, with a source gate and a shadow test). Where nothing registers, the honest report is a gap, not a silence. Fig 8 :39 "a search that finds nothing is never a proof of absence." And indiscernibility is not identity: the correlation that survives every available test is still not shown to be the causal structure.

## 6. One-paragraph version for outreach

> A correlation tells you that X and Y move together; it does not tell you what joins them. In the figures' terms it records the ingredients and their co-occurrence, while a causal explanation adds the typed, ordered bond, sequence or production, which is precisely the information that cannot be recovered from the operands. That is why competing causal stories over the same data are isomers: identical in the flat formula, decisive under intervention. The causal claim is also a generator rather than a recording, so it travels to a new setting and supports an action, and it ranges against an anchor (an intervention outcome or a discriminating instrument) instead of against agreement with the same evidence pool. None of this makes causation universally better: inside a fixed context correlation is efficient for prediction and convention, and description bits and intervention actions are two currencies that do not add. Finally, a causal label with no discriminating instrument behind it is an unpaid bond, not extra information. What the framework offers is that shape plus refuters written in advance, not a verdict about any particular causal claim.

## 7. Refuter (written before the conclusion, repeated here for the reader)

Withdraw or narrow if: (1) observed joint distributions determine `P(Y | do(X))` for all interventions at issue with no causal assumptions; (2) a correlation-only model transfers and acts as well as the causal one; (3) agreement within one evidence pool settles a causal verdict; (4) bond cost and intervention cost add in one scalar currency that reproduces the ranking; (5) a causal label increases held-out fit while its bond stays ungrounded and that is the whole of what is meant by "informative".
