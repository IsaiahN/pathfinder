## 1. A shape is a constraint you can state before you can name it

The canonical rule: the framework returns "what a missing thing would have to look like (a shape, not a name)". Fig 9 :32 "you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself". Fig 13 :134 "Unreachable is a verdict here, not a silence", and what it can say is a shape rather than a name.

From *The Library of Babel* (2026-10-02): "you can cut your search short if the set of books returned is small and descriptive enough that it provides a general shape of the answer that satisfies your question."

So for INF-0067 a shape is not "causality is better". It is the narrowed form of an answer: *extra information consists in a typed ordered bond, a generator's reach across contexts, and an anchor that does not move with a frame, while correlation keeps its legitimate convention role*. That constrains; it does not pick a particular causal model.

## 2. How the shape is obtained

- *Library of Babel*: use the vocabulary closest to the thing, look for recurring new terms that overlap with it, recurse. "With each new term gained, you are able to narrow the space by definition and deepen your vocabulary." Stop when "the finish line will also be the starting place".
- Fig 6 :30 "The description travels. Fails by being sharpened into terms only we can read. Cast it in effect terms." Fig 9 :30-31 "your words are borrowed too, so describe the effect rather than the cause." This is why the residual is stated as a failure under intervention, not as a definition of causation.
- *Decoding Tacit Knowledge* (2026-11-10): "When contact isn't possible, the best you can send is a plain description of what you'd expect to see, because anyone can check that against the world." A shape is the sendable part.
- *How Intelligence Actually Works* (2026-09-16): "The recursion converges toward indiscernibility, and indiscernibility is not identity." A shape can become indiscernible from an answer without being that answer, so the lineage must be kept.

## 3. What licenses a name, and what this question must not name

Names are licensed only by the ground settling a term or by a provenanced import with a shadow test (formula step 5; Fig 6 :23). For this question the framework can name the *operator type* (ordered versus symmetric) because that is in the figure set, but it cannot name *which causal model* is correct for a given X and Y. That needs an intervention with a recorded outcome.

*They're Scanning Books* (2026-09-06) sharpens the limit: "The real import is not a new term in the existing space. It is a new dimension of the space." Also: "A minimum viable set of six terms is a human's careful guess at reality's compression axes; another sensorium's minimum set need not be those six." And: "Declaring the primitives does not make them neutral. It makes the choice visible." So the answer's operator vocabulary is a visible choice, not nature's own list; that must be declared, not hidden.

## 4. Why "causal" can be a metamerism

*The Hard Problem is About Source* (2026-08-28) gives the mechanism, and it is the closest essay to this question. Metamerism: an instrument reads green and "it cannot comment on the source". Several paths (one green light, yellow plus blue, RGB, CMYK, an undiscovered scheme) produce the same reading. "If several paths create the same behavior, then behavior is a projection with lossy information." The spectrometer, not an argument, resolved it: "It was proven wrong not by argument, but by the arrival of a better instrument." And: "An instrument that can't distinguish these cases demonstrates its own inadequacy".

Applied here: a correlation is the reading; confounding, reverse causation and direct production are the possible sources. Calling one of them the cause without a discriminating instrument is exactly the error the essay names. The causal explanation is more informative only when a source-discriminating instrument or intervention has actually narrowed the set of sources. This is the honest stop in `07_the_tether_answer.md`. The same point appears in *How Intelligence Actually Works* through the video-frame/photograph thought experiment: the two artifacts "carry different warrants. One was taken; one was extracted. And nothing in the image records which." That is provenance loss, and it is why the causal label cannot be read off the correlation.

## 5. Prestige, rooms and the shape of verification

*The Recursive Self-Improvement Trap* (2026-11-19): "Prestige must never be allowed to validate an action directly... Prestige can fund an action. It can give an action a budget to be tried. But it cannot settle an action. Only the ground can settle it." A consensus of respected scholars that causal explanations are somehow deeper is the named collapse, not corroboration.

*How a Society of Agents Would Work* (2026-10-22): "their separation as frames make them viable vantage points to range against an unmovable verifying ground that they tether to." And: "the group's agreement is never the verdict." This is Fig 2 :35 applied to a room: "Alignment is triangulation, not negotiation. A mirror cannot correct you."

*Arrival of the Fittest: The Tether Architecture* (2026-08-19): under "Three mappings offered and declined", Bayesian updating was declined because "there is no prior, no likelihood and no posterior anywhere in the mechanism." The same discipline applies here: do not dress the operator distinction in information-theoretic clothing it does not have (for example, a claim that mutual information itself ranks explanation over correlation). The article also records that the triangulation claim "fits four cases already known and has not been tested forward", so agreement across fields is a retrodiction until a forward test exists.

It cannot settle whether causal explanations are in fact more informative in any particular field. It can only state the shape such an answer must have and the refuter that would break it.
