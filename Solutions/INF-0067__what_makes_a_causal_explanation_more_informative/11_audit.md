## Independent domain census

I redid the census from the residual line alone before relying on the primary's conclusions, using the WATSON order: R by arity, symmetry and scale; one line per domain with actors / currency / anchor; partition or union; order by dependency. I then compared.

- **R (arity / symmetry / scale).** The residual is the failure of a co-occurrence prediction when the action changes to an intervention, a new environment or a discriminating instrument. Arity: at least three. A correlation is a binary reading of X and Y; every explanation of its failure invokes a third term, whether confounder, mechanism or context. Symmetry: the correlation is symmetric under swap, the causal relations are not. This is the cleanest discriminator I found and I did not need the primary's finding to see it. Scale: first order inside one dataset, second order across interventions or frames.
- **Domain 1, predictive / statistical frame.** Actors: variables, observations, loss, parameters. Currency: fit, frame-internal. Anchor: none that settles the world; held-out data share the pool. Question type: union with D2, not a partition.
- **Domain 2, causal / interventional frame.** Actors: variables, mechanisms, interventions, confounders, instruments. Currency: counterfactual reach priced in actions plus bits for the bond. Anchor: intervention outcome or discriminating instrument. Question type: adds a bond and a new anchor to D1.
- **Domain 3, verification / outreach frame.** Actors: readers, instruments, experiments. Currency: agreement versus residual. Anchor: ground, never agreement. Independence zero in this simulated run.

Order by dependency: describe the residual before naming the frame (Fig 9 :27-28); refuse fit-as-verdict before minting (formula step 2); settle each bond before composing over it (step 5).
