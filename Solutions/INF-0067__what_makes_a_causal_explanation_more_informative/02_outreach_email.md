**Subject:** Check one claim about causal explanation before you decide whether the theory is worth reading

> Hi [name],
>
> I have one specific output I would like you to check against what you already know. I am not asking you to read the framework first, and I am not asking you to agree with it. If the output does not survive, there is nothing else to look at.
>
> **The residual, in effect terms.** A correlation between X and Y predicts well while the action is passive observation. Change the action to an intervention on X, move the system to a new environment, or bring in an instrument that discriminates among hidden sources, and the same correlation can fail. The gap between the co-occurrence prediction and what the intervention actually returns is the residual. A global fit hides it; the per-case residual shows where it is (Fig 1 :19, :22).
>
> **The output (`specified`, not `grounded`).** A causal explanation is more informative than a predictive correlation in three defined ways, and not in a fourth:
>
> 1. It carries a typed, ordered bond. A correlation records that two things hold at once; a causal explanation records production or sequence. In the framework's own operator set, that is the difference between the symmetric conjunction `+` and the ordered bonds `→` (sequence) and `⇒` (production). The arrangement is the extra information, not more data.
> 2. It is a generator, not a recording. It says what would happen under an intervention and therefore travels to a new context. A correlation describes the occasion it was fitted to; improving its fit without changing contact changes nothing.
> 3. It ranges against an anchor that does not move when a frame pushes on it. For a causal claim the anchor is an intervention outcome or a source-discriminating instrument. Agreement among readers who share the same evidence is not that anchor.
>
> **What I am not claiming.** I am not saying causation always beats correlation. Inside a fixed context, and for coordinating a shared expectation, correlation is efficient and can be the better instrument. The figures give agreement a valence: disqualifying for a verdict, efficient for a convention. Nor am I claiming a single scalar "informativeness" score: the cost of describing a bond and the cost of finding out whether it holds are in different units. And I am not claiming that any story labelled causal is informative. If no instrument discriminates among the sources that produce the same reading, the causal label is an unpaid bond and the honest correlation is the stronger claim.
>
> **What would refute the output.** I wrote these down before sending this:
> 1. `P(Y | do(X))` is determined by the observed joint distribution for all interventions of interest, with no causal assumptions, so symmetric co-occurrence already carries the interventional content.
> 2. A correlation-only model transfers to a new environment and supports a successful intervention as well as the causal model.
> 3. Two readers drawing on the same evidence pool can settle a causal verdict by agreeing, with no intervention and no discriminating instrument.
> 4. Bond cost and intervention cost add in one currency, and that scalar reproduces the ranking.
>
> If any one holds, I retract the claim or narrow it. A one-line "this fails because..." is more useful to me than agreement. If you would rather not engage, no reply is needed.
>
> **If it survives, and only then:** the sources are below. The figures are the authority, not my summary of them.
> - Figures text with line numbers (local extract): `FRAMEWORK/reference/03-FIGURES_TEXT.md`. The relevant passages are Fig 12 :5, :46-47, :59, :72-77, :94, :117, :125-126; Fig 1 :19, :22, :27; Fig 2 :29, :35, :39-41; Fig 4 :22, :25; Fig 11 :39-40.
