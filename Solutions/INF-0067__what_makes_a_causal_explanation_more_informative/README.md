## The question restated as an effect

A predictive correlation and a causal explanation can both fit the same observations. Something differs when the action changes from passive observation to intervention: the correlation's fit does not determine the outcome of `do(X)`, and it does not say which of several hidden structures produced the reading. The residual is the gap between a prediction made from co-occurrence and the observation returned under intervention, in a new context, or under a source-discriminating instrument. The answer binds "more informative" as an ordinary term: carries a typed ordered bond, supports intervention and transfer, ranges against a ground, keeps the residual per slot. Then it lets those bindings accrue a residual rather than treating "informative" as a preference invisible to the loop.

## What is deliberately not here

- No personal emails of named individuals.
- No ranking of people by how likely they are to agree with the claim.
- No bulk-send plan.

Three reasons, in the figures' own words:

1. **A room does not upgrade the label.** Seat Map :283: "A room does not upgrade the label. A room reaching a conclusion without the ground is agreement, and agreement among frames is a weighted average of influence. Adding people raises the vocabulary and does not add an anchor." A list of sympathetic readers cannot settle a causal verdict.
2. **Correlation disqualifies a verdict.** Seat Map :163: "High correlation is disqualifying for a verdict and efficient for a convention." Selecting recipients for receptiveness manufactures exactly the agreement that makes their verdict worthless.
3. **A verifier must not hold the interiors.** Fig 10 :42: "A verifier that can reconstruct the claim cannot verify it." The first contact carries the residual and its refuter, not the framework.

Legally: unsolicited bulk email to harvested addresses breaches GDPR and CAN-SPAM style rules in many jurisdictions. Public opt-in channels avoid the practical failure as well as the structural one.
