## What in the wider literature bears directly on this question

1. **Aggregation is not harmless.** De Domenico and colleagues put the central finding in their own words: throwing away structural information *"can generate misleading results."* This is the world-facing twin of Fig 1 :22 and Fig 9 :55-56. Do not present it as a figure result; present it as a corroboration from a separate pool.
2. **Overlap decides the transition.** Radicchi (2015) splits the system into the intersection and the remainders; smooth when the intersection dominates, abrupt when the remainders dominate. Radicchi and Arenas (2013) recommend avoiding repeated edges. Both are the same shape as Fig 8 :48-49 (high overlap is one layer wearing two names; surplus lives where closures differ). Different communities, low overlap with the framework.
3. **The currencies pull in different directions.** Awareness inhibits disease (Granell), coupled risk cascades can be catastrophic and reverse the single-network robustness rule (Buldyrev), cooperation can be enhanced or destroyed by the same coupling depending on coordination (Wang), and information needs wide bridges while disease needs only one contact (Centola and Macy). This is why this solution refuses an averaged verdict.
4. **Multi-hop transfer is real outside network physics.** Ward and colleagues show information transfer and division of labour in fish shoals. A low-overlap check on Fig 11 :52-53's contact cascade.
5. **One caution for the outreach.** All the framework essays are by the framework's author. Their agreement with the figures is expected and carries little independent weight (Fig 2 :40). The physics, sociology and animal-behaviour papers above are the independent part of the pool.

## Distribution: offer, do not push

- **Fig 4 :22**: *"Only methods cross up. Only head starts come down."* And Fig 4 :25: *"A recording carried upward looks like knowledge and is a description of one occasion."* Send the shape and its refuter first; offer the links after a verdict is recorded.
- **Fig 10 :42**: *"A verifier that can reconstruct the claim cannot verify it."* A recipient who has absorbed the framework before being asked for a verdict has joined the frame being checked.
- **Fig 9 :27-28**: *"describe it before you go looking, or any frame you pick will seem to fit."* Ask what they would expect to see if the shape were true before giving them the article.
- **Fig 2 :40**: agreement from a shared evidence pool is *"worthless"* as a verdict. Do not pool later agreement as corroboration.

**Order in a reply:** (1) the shape claim and the five refuters; (2) ask what they would count as a failure; (3) only then the figure links, with the note that the figures, not the summary, are the authority.

## The two-stage offer

- **Stage 1 before stage 2** is Fig 10 :52 and Fig 2 :45. The recipient checks the output without holding the framework's interior.
- **Stage 2 is opt-in by construction.** Once a recipient has absorbed the theory, their agreement stops being evidence, so the stage-1 verdict must be recorded first.
- **Stage 3 sends a method, which is permitted.** Fig 4 :22: *"Only methods cross up."* The formula is a generator; giving it away does not give away an answer.
- **Stage 3 still needs a ground per problem.** `THE_FORMULA.md` "Direction": a frame *"cannot check the direction of its own question, because the shape a frame is searching for is not a term it holds."* Each user writes their own refuter and states their mode.

**Hard precondition:** none of this works if the stage-1 output fails. The five refuters in `01_figure_grounded_audit.md` §4 are the test.
