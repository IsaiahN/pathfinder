**Use:** one-to-one, or as a cover letter attached to a submission through the public venues in `03_public_contact_points.csv`. **Not** for bulk send.

**Hard precondition:** do not send until the shape claim and its five refuters in `01_figure_grounded_audit.md` §4 are written into the recipient's own terms. The output must survive validation or the two-stage offer collapses on contact.

**Subject:** Check one claim about overlapping networks before deciding whether the framework is worth reading

> Hi [name],
>
> I have one specific output about multiplex spread that I would like you to check against what you already know. I am not asking you to read the framework first, and I am not asking you to agree with it. If the output does not survive, there is nothing else to look at.
>
> **The output (`specified`, not `grounded`):** the effect of overlapping, multiplex layers on spread is governed by the independence of the layers, not by their number. Two layers whose edges are almost the same are one network wearing two names: their union adds no path, no reach, and no threshold change. Two layers whose edges are independent produce a surplus, a set of reachable node pairs that neither layer alone contains. The compound is categorically different from either layer, so single-layer results cannot be averaged into it. This predicts a shape, not a number: it does not give you a reproduction number or a threshold value, and it does not say which layer wins.
>
> I am keeping four verdicts separate, because information, disease, risk and cooperation have different currencies and different anchors. The same structure can enhance awareness, inhibit disease, cause abrupt failure cascades, or collapse cooperation if the coordination between layers is disturbed. A single averaged claim would erase the signal.
>
> **What I am not claiming:** that I have run a simulation or measured a real network here. This is a simulated derivation checked for internal consistency against the figures. It cannot settle the question it reports on. I am not claiming that overlap alone determines everything, that the framework names a mechanism, or that agreement with me counts as evidence.
>
> **What would refute the shape claim (written before this message):**
> 1. two near-identical layers measurably change spread beyond the best single-layer projection;
> 2. two independent layers produce no new reach beyond each layer alone;
> 3. the overlap rule reverses sign or fails in at least two of the four currencies after their anchors are controlled;
> 4. a one-source and a multi-source contagion respond identically to overlap;
> 5. changing a shared edge's provenance from convergence to copy, with edges fixed, leaves the surplus unchanged.
>
> If any holds, the shape is withdrawn. A one-line "this fails because..." is more useful to me than agreement. If you would rather not engage, no reply is needed.
>
> **If it survives, and only then:** the figure text is below. The figures, not my summary, are the authority.
> - Verbatim figure text with line numbers (local extract): `FRAMEWORK/reference/03-FIGURES_TEXT.md`
> - Canonical usage rule (local copy): `FRAMEWORK/reference/FIGURES_README_canonical.md`
> - The eight-step loop: `FRAMEWORK/figures/THE_FORMULA.md`
>
> **If you want to use the machinery:** write down what would refute your own result *before* you run it and state your mode (`general` / `specified` / `grounded`). A reported failure is more useful to me than a reported success.
>
> Thanks,
> [name]
