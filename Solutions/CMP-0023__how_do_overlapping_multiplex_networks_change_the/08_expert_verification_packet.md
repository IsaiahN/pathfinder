# Expert verification packet: multiplex networks and spread

**What this is:** a claim in a form a network scientist, epidemiologist or complex-systems researcher can check. Mode, derivation and refuters are stated before the conclusion, so a reader knows what to look for and what would settle it.

**Mode: `specified` on the substantive claims, `general` on the shape, not `grounded`.** No simulation or measurement of a real multiplex network is claimed here. This is a shape and a route, not a result.

## 1. The claim, in one paragraph

A multiplex spread compound is governed by the independence of its layers, not by their number. Layers whose edges are almost the same are one network wearing two names and add no reach; independent layers produce a union surplus of node pairs that neither layer alone reaches. The compound is categorically different from either layer, so single-layer results must not be averaged into it, and shared edges need a provenance record because convergent derivation and copying look identical in the adjacency matrix. Spread is a multi-hop flow over a network structure, so the measurement is compound reachability per node slot. Information, disease, risk and cooperation keep separate verdicts because their anchors differ. The framework returns this shape, not a numeric threshold.

## 2. Three checkable sub-claims

### C1 — framework-internal; checkable by reading Fig 8 and Fig 9

- **Statement:** the compound is a union with its own loop, and overlap (independence) decides the surplus. High overlap adds nothing; low overlap adds reach.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 8 :48-49 *"Two frames that overlap almost completely are one frame wearing two names."* Fig 8 :45 *"Two frames together reach what neither reaches alone."* Fig 9 :57-58 *"a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either. It stops being a mixture and becomes a specific compound."* Fig 2 :31-32 *"Independence, between two frames, is how little they draw on the same evidence."*
- **Refuter:** near-identical layers that change spread beyond the best single-layer projection, or independent layers that add no new reach.

### C2 — dynamics-facing; checkable by a network scientist against simulation or data

- **Statement:** the union surplus is measurable as new reachable node pairs at the cascade horizon, and it is not recoverable by averaging the single-layer results. Provenance of a shared edge changes the surplus classification.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 11 :52-53 *"And contact cascades. Scoping the habitat to direct contact is how DDT reached the eagles: first-order enumeration, second-order effect, and the residual that mattered was three steps out."* Fig 8 :51-52 *"Convergent derivation and adopted import look identical in the contents. Only the record of where each came from separates them, and the surplus lives only where the closures differ."* Fig 1 :22 *"Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average."*
- **What to check:** hold the transmission rule and node set fixed; vary edge overlap from near-identical to disjoint; measure reachable set, threshold and final size per slot; then repeat with identical edge sets but different edge provenance. Does the predicted dependence appear?
- **Refuter:** aggregation reproduces the compound outcomes exactly, or provenance at fixed edges leaves surplus unchanged.

### C3 — cross-domain; checkable by comparing the four currencies

- **Statement:** the four currencies keep separate verdicts; the same structure can enhance information, inhibit disease, cause abrupt risk cascades, and either support or destroy cooperation.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 4 :20 *"Only the actors, the currency and the anchor change."* Fig 11 :50 *"The same actor is a benefactor to one residual and a malfactor to another, so mutualism and parasitism are relations rather than kinds."* Fig 12 :121 *"cycle. The output re-enters as input. The sign of the loop decides whether it closes, and a chain cannot close by construction."*
- **What to check:** from the literature, awareness inhibits epidemics (Granell 2013), interdependent failures can be catastrophic and reverse the single-network robustness rule (Buldyrev 2010), cooperation can exceed isolated-network limits only if coordination holds (Wang, Szolnoki, Perc 2013), and information needs wide bridges while disease needs one contact (Centola and Macy 2007). Do any two of these share a sign and an anchor? If they do, the separation is weakened.
- **Refuter:** one scalar parameter predicting all four outcomes with the same sign and the same anchor.

## 3. What is explicitly not claimed

- Not a numeric threshold, reproduction number, or winning layer. Fig 9 :32: *"you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself"*.
- Not that adding a layer always increases spread. High overlap adds nothing; inhibitory coupling can reduce disease.
- Not one averaged verdict across the four currencies.
- Not that a single overlap coefficient is sufficient; edge provenance is part of the quantity.
- Not `grounded`. This packet is a simulated derivation checked for internal consistency against the figures, not a ground reading. It cannot settle the claim.

## 5. What would settle it

The ground is a per-slot, per-layer measurement with edge provenance, not further argument. From *The Hard Problem is About Source*: an instrument that reads green *"cannot comment on the source"*; the spectrometer resolved it. Here:

1. Does adding a near-identical layer change the compound threshold beyond the best single layer? A yes breaks C1.
2. Do disjoint layers add reachable node pairs that neither layer has? A no breaks C1.
3. At fixed edges, does changing provenance from convergence to copy change the surplus? A no breaks C2.
4. Do the four currencies share a sign and an anchor? A yes breaks C3.
5. What instrument discriminates convergence from copying at fixed edge sets, the analogue of the spectrometer here?
