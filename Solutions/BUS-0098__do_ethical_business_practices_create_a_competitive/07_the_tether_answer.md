# The Tether's answer on ethics and competitive advantage

**Mode: `specified` on the per-domain claims, `general` on the structural shape - not `grounded`.** This is a shape and a route, not a result. Simulated derivation checked against the figures (`05_what_shape_means.md` section 7), not a ground reading. No market measurement pays for any claim here.

## 1. The residual it answers

An answer would explain when an ethical practice shows up as a small R = |Γ(b,a) - o′| against a market reading, and when the market reading is not the thing that settles whether the practice should be kept. R is indexed by firm, never averaged (Fig 1 :19, :22). The question is about the **anchors and the conditions of contact**, not about the level of a firm's virtue or the size of its return.

The either/or must be unbundled before it is searched (Fig 9 :54). It is two conditions, not one hard question with opposite answers:

- **C1, the pay condition:** the firm's channel to a ground that still pays for the practice stays reachable, and its reach is renewed from a closure that differs from its own.
- **C2, the obligation condition:** an anchor legitimate for a question about harm to persons governs, and a seat able to refuse the market's verdict stays available.

## 2. The shape

1. **The two options have different anchors.** Fig 2 :29 names the market as an anchor for "does it pay". Fig 2 :36: *"An anchor is legitimate when the question is constitutively about it."* Fig 2 :37: *"Human judgment is the right anchor for is this a good answer for a person, because the question is about people."* So the market does not settle whether a practice is right. The either/or is a union, not a partition.
2. **Ethics as advantage is a relation, not a stock.** Fig 11 :39: *"capability is a property of agent-and-habitat, never of the agent alone."* Fig 11 :40: *"An improvement that does not change contact changes nothing, however much it improves."* A list of ethical practices is Γ, a candidate library whose reach must be tested against the ground, not the answer.
3. **The value of a practice can flip sign.** Fig 11 :49: *"An actor’s valence is relative to the residual, not a property it carries."* Fig 11 :51: *"No standing list of good actors survives a change of residual."* Fig 12 :119: a catalyst is a part that survives its own use, and *"nothing records which a term is"*. The strength and the rigidity can be the same code.
4. **Renewal needs an import, not more optimisation.** Fig 6 :23: *"No frame escapes its own closure. The bound moves only on contact with a frame whose closure differs."* Fig 8 :32: closure is idempotent, so no amount of internal search adds an atom. A practice that competitors can copy adds nothing (Reinhardt, in the opened CSR page). The low-overlap biological source adds the condition: reciprocal altruism needs *"A mechanism for detecting 'cheaters' must exist."* and repeated opportunities. Advantage from ethics is a repeated game with detection, not a one-off declaration.
5. **"Regardless of return" selects an anchor, not a forecast.** It refuses the market's standing to decide a question about persons. It does not assert that ethics never pays. But a declared commitment is only a convention until it can be violated: Fig 10 :18 *"a declared meaning is not a grounded one"*, Fig 10 :19 *"→ install what can be violated"*, Fig 10 :47 *"a convention that decides an outcome has stopped being one"*. An ethics code that costs nothing on breach is a slogan carrying the seat's authority.
6. **Reputation and ratings cannot settle either branch.** Fig 1 :26-27: *"predicates minted, and credibility accrued"* and *"They are frame-internal. A frame cannot score itself with a quantity it also produces."* The essay's rule is the same: prestige can fund an action but cannot settle it. The signalling source from evolutionary biology agrees: *"If a signal is to remain reliable... the signal has to be evolutionarily costly."*
7. **Two firms with the same code can still differ.** Under Fig 12 :117 they are isomers if the bonds differ: *"same atoms, different bond, different substance."* A published code records ingredients and not the arrangement, which is *"the half nothing was recording"* (Fig 12 :5).
8. **The break is usually in the middle of the chain.** Fig 3 :24 and :27: the vocabulary/objective link has no instrument, and a link with no instrument is a link nobody has looked at. Firms can list principles and still not say what progress on the harm axis would look like.
9. **A profit reading cannot name its source.** The source essay's metamerism argument: a reading of green *"cannot comment on the source"*, and *"The answer is unreached until a better instrument exists."* The CSR page's own specification dispute shows the sign of "CSR pays" moving with the model. Which practice pays is a `gap`, not a hidden answer.

## 3. What it explicitly does not say

- Not `grounded`. No prospective market measurement or harm measurement has paid for this shape.
- Not a name. It does not say which practice, which code, which stakeholder group or which industry produces a durable advantage. Fig 9 :32: *"you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself."*
- Not that ethics never pays, and not that ethics always pays. Both are standing-property claims, and Fig 11 :49-51 refuses the standing list.
- Not an average. It makes no claim about the percentage of returns attributable to ethics, stakeholders or any other factor. Such numbers are filters for budget, not witnesses (Fig 9 :60).
- Not that duties, leaders or stakeholders are irrelevant. They are Γ and D2. The claim is that Γ alone does not settle persistence and that the market alone does not settle obligation.
- Not corroboration by agreement. Donaldson and Preston, Freeman, Goodpaster, the CSR and Friedman pages and Barney-derived work share one evidence pool (Fig 2 :31, :40). The low-overlap sources from animal behaviour, behavioural economics and evolutionary biology corroborate the mechanism, not the firm-level fact.
- Not a theory of everything about business. It stops where the instrument stops.

## 4. What would settle it - the ground

A source-discriminating instrument, not a further argument. The readings are profit, share, survival and harm; the candidate sources are an ethical practice, a protected resource, a favourable habitat, a temporary asymmetry, or luck. The falsifiers, pre-registered in `01` and repeated in `08`:

1. a case where the market return alone legitimately settles whether a practice is right, with the question not about harm to any person;
2. a practice's measured contribution to return invariant across a habitat substitution that removes the market's payment;
3. an ethics commitment with no detection or breach cost still changing what the market pays;
4. a costless ethics signal reliably changing what the market pays with cheap imitators present;
5. the same closure renewed for years with no import, no repeated interaction and no cheater detection, yet still passing the ground test across a habitat change;
6. one intervention restoring both conditions with nothing categorically different left over;
7. a source-discriminating instrument that names the source of a profit reading (this closes the gap, it does not refute the shape).

A prospective study that measures C1 and C2 separately per firm, records the bonds as well as the atoms, names the anchor used for each verdict, and reports which condition failed before the decline would turn this from `specified` toward `grounded`.

## 5. The honest stop

**Unreached, not unreachable.** Fig 13 :134: *"Unreachable is a verdict here, not a silence,"* and Fig 8 :39: *"So a search that finds nothing is never a proof of absence."* The shape stops at the limit of the available instruments and records the lineage so the stop is checkable. The missing thing is a discriminator of sources, and it is owed. Naming a winner from a profit reading would be the metamerism error the source essay forbids. The nine points in section 2 are a shape; the tenth thing, which practice actually pays next, is not held and is not pretended.

## 6. One-paragraph version for outreach

> The Tether does not rank firms or name the ethical practice that wins, and it does not choose between "ethics pays" and "ethics is only a duty" as if they were opposite answers to one question. It says they are two conditions with two different anchors. Whether a practice pays is a relation between firm and habitat: the firm has to keep its channel to a market that still rewards the practice reachable, keep taking in something new from ways of working that differ from its own, and keep a way to detect free riders, because a code that competitors can copy adds nothing. The value of the practice can flip sign when the market changes, so the same code can be the source of trust and later a rigid ritual. Whether the practice is right is a different question. Where it is constitutively about harm to people, the market return is an instrument and not the verdict; "regardless of return" selects that anchor, and it does not mean ethics never pays. An ethics commitment that costs nothing on breach is a convention, not an obligation. Reputation and ratings are the frame scoring itself. What is offered is that shape, seven falsifiers written in advance, and the instrument that would close it. Which particular practice pays in a given market stays open until a source-discriminating instrument exists, and the stop is recorded rather than filled.
