## The question restated as an effect

The loop defines R = |Γ(b,a) - o′| per slot s (Formula step 1; Symbols table, `R`). Here:

- **b** is the firm's belief about what the market and the affected parties will return or require.
- **a** includes the firm's practices, among them its ethical conduct and its declared obligations.
- **Γ(b,a)** is what the firm's library of routines, assets and stated principles predicts will happen.
- **o′** is what actually happens: the market return, the survival of the firm, and the harm or benefit to the people the practice touches.
- **s** is the firm, indexed by time. R is per firm, never an industry average (Fig 1 :19, Fig 1 :22).

The question as posed is an either/or. Both options are candidate answers to one residual, and the derivation tests whether they are in fact one question.

- Option A, "ethics creates competitive advantage": leads to the prediction that ethical practice shows up as a smaller R against a market anchor, and that the practice should be adopted where it pays.
- Option B, "ethics is an obligation regardless of financial return": leads to the prediction that the practice must be adopted whether or not the market pays, and that the market return is not the thing that settles the question.

What an answer would look like, written down and bound as an ordinary term before the run (Formula, "Direction"): a description of the anchors that settle each option, the conditions under which each holds, and an instrument that would discriminate a failure of one from a failure of the other. Until that shape is bound it predicts nothing and no check in the loop can see it. It is now bound, so the pre-registered refuter in `01` section 5 can fire on it.

The verdict recorded in `09_watson_answer.md` is that the either/or is a **union of two conditions with different anchors**, not one question with opposite answers. Option A is a `specified` claim about firm-and-habitat contact. Option B is an anchor-selection claim, not an empirical claim that ethics never pays. Verdicts are kept separate per domain and never averaged. Which specific practice pays in a given market is a declared `gap`.

## What is deliberately not here

- No personal emails of named individuals.
- No ranking of people by how likely they are to agree.
- No bulk-send plan.
- No claim that this derivation is `grounded`, no claim that it settles the business-ethics question, and no claim that the framework is the only true account of business ethics.

Three figure-grounded reasons, plus one practical one:

1. A room is not an anchor. Fig 7 :35 *"A room’s agreement is variation, not a verdict. Selection between rooms is where the ground votes."* and Fig 2 :35 *"Alignment is triangulation, not negotiation. A mirror cannot correct you."* Gathering agreeable readers manufactures the correlation that disqualifies a verdict.
2. Correlation has valence. Fig 2 :40 *"agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool"*. A list selected for receptiveness is one witness wearing many names.
3. A verifier must be outside the interior. Fig 10 :42 *"A verifier that can reconstruct the claim cannot verify it."* The first contact therefore carries the residual and its refuter, not the framework.
4. Legally, unsolicited bulk email to scraped addresses breaches GDPR and CAN-SPAM style rules in many jurisdictions and gets the sending domain blacklisted. Public opt-in routes avoid that failure as well as the structural one.
