## QUESTION

Do ethical business practices create a competitive advantage, or are they primarily obligations that firms should meet regardless of financial return?

Restated as an effect: when does R = |Γ(b,a) - o′| stay small when the practice a is an ethical one, and when is the market reading o′ not the thing that legitimately settles whether a should be kept? R is indexed per firm and per time, never aggregated (Fig 1 :19). Two anchors are in play: the market, named in Fig 2 :29, and the affected persons for questions constitutively about them, named in Fig 2 :36-37. Option A leads to a pay prediction; Option B leads to an anchor-selection rule. The derivation tests whether these are rival answers to one question or two conditions of a union.

## DOMAIN CENSUS

R by arity, symmetry and scale, then one line per domain giving actors / currency / anchor, partition or union, and order by dependency.

- **R (arity / symmetry / scale).** A three-place relation among firm Γ, practice a and return o′, indexed by slot and ordered by time. "Superior" is a threshold comparison, not an absolute quantity (Fig 12 :87). The two options are not symmetric alternatives: one measures a return, the other refuses the return standing to decide. Scale spans the single firm loop, the population of firms and affected parties, and the time channel across repeated interactions. The membrane between scales is lossy (Fig 4).
- **D1 Competitive-advantage loop.** Actors: firm, routines, customers, competitors. Currency: residual reduction, profit, share, survival. Anchor: realized market return (Fig 2 :29). Admitted.
- **D2 Normative domain.** Actors: affected persons, the firm as actor with a seat able to refuse. Currency: violation, benefit, standing to be composed over. Anchor: a non-updating ground for questions constitutively about persons (Fig 2 :36-37), not the market. Admitted.
- **D3 Practice library.** Actors: atoms (practices, codes, routines) and bonds. Currency: reach, |φ| cost. Anchor: settlement against the ground (Fig 5 :32). Admitted.
- **D4 Time and reputation channel.** Actors: instruments, ratings, percepts. Currency: contact. Anchor: the ground that does not decay (Fig 10 :24); prestige is not an anchor (Fig 1 :26-27). Admitted.
- **X1 Ethics as a free-floating virtue that always pays.** No anchor independent of the market verdict and actual harm. Not a domain yet.
- **X2 Ethics as pure declared duty with no world contact.** Only a declared meaning; *"a declared meaning is not a grounded one"* (Fig 10 :18). Not admitted as a verdict-producing domain.

**Partition or union:** union. The two named conditions are C1 (pay condition) and C2 (obligation condition), with D1/D3 and D2/D4 forming two compounds and the central case in both (Fig 9 :54-57).

**Dependency order:** perception → vocabulary → objective → planning → learn and carry (Fig 3). The middle link, terms for what the situation affords and what counts as progress, has no instrument.

## VERDICT (from the figures)

**The figure-derived verdict:** the either/or is a union of two conditions with two different anchors, not one question with opposite answers. C1, the pay condition, is firm-and-habitat: a practice keeps paying while the channel to a ground that still rewards it stays reachable, while reach is renewed by contact with a differing closure, and while free riders can be detected; the practice's valence can flip with the residual. C2, the obligation condition, is anchor selection: where the question is constitutively about harm to persons, the market return is an instrument and not the verdict, so "regardless of return" refuses that standing without asserting that ethics never pays; a commitment with no violation cost remains a convention. Reputation and ratings are frame-internal and settle nothing. Correlations are filters, not witnesses. Which specific practice pays is a declared `gap` at the instrument boundary. Verdicts are kept separate per domain and never averaged. Mode `specified` on firm claims, `general` on the structural shape, not `grounded`. The one apparent world conflict, Friedman's market-anchor position versus the harm anchor, was returned to the census and resolved by Fig 2 :36-37; it did not flip the verdict.

- **Cost if wrong:** misdirected diagnosis and a spent check. A firm failing the pay condition would be sold an obligation statement, or a firm whose obligation has become a slogan would be told to import more practices, and a single lever would be applied to a union that needs two. In outreach terms, an expert's twenty minutes would be spent refuting a shape that was never the right shape. The cost is real but bounded and largely reversible. Fig 2 :42 says *"Buy independence in proportion to the cost of being wrong."*
- **Undoable:** yes. No term is deleted if the claim fails; a refutation is added, and the reach falls because *"something was added rather than because anything was deleted"* (Fig 6 :42-43). The shape and its eight falsifiers are recorded in a provenance trail so a later reader can locate the error (Fig 10 :62-64).
- **Reversed by:** any of the eight pre-registered falsifiers, in particular (1) the market return alone settling a question not about persons, (2) practice contribution invariant across habitat substitution, (3) an unbreachable commitment changing R, (4) a costless signal reliably paying with imitators present, (5) internal-only renewal across a habitat change, or (6) one intervention restoring both conditions with no categorically different residue. A source-discriminating instrument naming the cause of a profit reading would close the `gap` and move the answer toward `grounded` rather than reverse it. Agreement from the same business evidence pool is not such an instrument.
