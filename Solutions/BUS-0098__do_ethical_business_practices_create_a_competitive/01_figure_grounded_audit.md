## 0. The residual in effect terms

The loop defines R = |Γ(b,a) - o′| per slot s (Formula step 1; Symbols table, `R`). For this question:

- **b** is the firm's belief about what the market will pay for and what the affected parties will require.
- **a** is what the firm does. Its practices include its ethical conduct, its code, and its declared obligations.
- **Γ(b,a)** is what the firm's library predicts will happen when it acts with those practices.
- **o′** is what the market and the parties actually return: profit, share, survival, trust, harm, benefit.
- **s** is the firm, indexed by time. R is per firm and per time, never an industry average.

> **"The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated."** (Fig 1 :19)
> **"Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average."** (Fig 1 :22)

The question as posed asks for a choice between two options. Stated in effect terms, the residual is: *under what conditions does an ethical practice show up as a small R against a market reading, and under what conditions is the market reading not the thing that settles whether the practice should be kept?*

This description deliberately names no theory, no "stakeholder", no "duty", no "virtue", no "reputation". Those are candidate names and are withheld until the census.

**Anchor test.** Fig 2 :29: *"An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks."* The same line names the market directly: *"A measurement, an experiment, a counter, a market, a patient outcome."* Fig 2 :30: *"you push on it, it is not an anchor."* So the market is a legitimate anchor for the question "does this practice pay". But Fig 2 :36 says *"An anchor is legitimate when the question is constitutively about it."* and Fig 2 :37 says *"Human judgment is the right anchor for is this a good answer for a person, because the question is about people."* The question "is this practice right" is constitutively about the people it touches, not about the market return. Two different anchors govern the two options. This is the first structural result and it is developed in F1 and F3.

**Bind the sought-for shape as an ordinary term** (Formula, "Direction"), before the run: the answer would be (i) the conditions under which an ethical practice changes contact with a paying ground, (ii) the anchor that legitimately governs a question about harm to persons, and (iii) an instrument that can discriminate a practice that pays because of its ethics from a practice that pays because of a protected habitat or luck. Until bound, that shape predicts nothing and no loop check can see it. It is now bound, so the refuter in section 5 can fire on it.

## 1. Domain census (before choosing any figure line)

### 1.1 Arity, symmetry, scale of R

- **Arity.** R is a three-place relation: firm Γ, practice a, and the return o′, indexed by slot s and ordered by time t. It carries an **ordered** bond (`→`, sequence: *"A then B; B needs A to have happened"*, Fig 12 :72) because a habit formed now changes what pays later. "Superior" is a **comparison** bond (`⋛`, *"a threshold relation between two quantities"*, Fig 12 :87), relative to competitors, not an absolute quantity. The relation is not symmetric: firm and market are not interchangeable.
- **Symmetry.** The two options are not symmetric alternatives. Option A measures a return; Option B refuses to let a return be the verdict. That difference of kind, not of degree, is the signal the census must preserve.
- **Scale.** Three scales are in view: the single firm's step loop (Fig 4 one-agent panel), the population of firms and stakeholders with *"something that ranks what they produce"* (Fig 4 :5), and the time channel across repeated interactions. The membrane between scales is *"Not a wall: a filter with a direction"* (Fig 4 :21), and *"Only methods cross up. Only head starts come down."* (Fig 4 :22). The transform is lossy, so the round-trip gap R_T must be measured (Fig 4 :30). The world supplies the same structural point from a low-overlap field: reciprocal altruism requires *"A large (indefinite) number of opportunities to exchange aid must exist"* (Reciprocal altruism, Trivers/Stephens conditions), so the time scale is part of the question, not a background.

### 1.3 Partition or union

D1 and D3 overlap: the library Γ is inside the firm loop, so a firm's ethical code is a Γ operand whose reach must be tested. D2 and D4 overlap: the question of harm is asked through a channel that can decay into ratings and pooled readings. D1 and D2 overlap at the central case, where a practice both pays and touches people. The whole question is a **union**, not a partition.

> **"And when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again."** (Fig 9 :54)
> **"A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones."** (Fig 9 :55)
> **"What remains is smaller and it is also categorically different: a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either."** (Fig 9 :57)

The unbundled conditions are:

- **C1:** a maintained channel to a ground that still pays for the practice (the competitive-advantage condition, D1 plus D4).
- **C2:** an anchor that is legitimate for a question about harm to persons and a seat able to refuse a verdict that the market would hand down (the obligation condition, D2).

A firm failing only C1 and a firm failing only C2 are not different degrees of one failure. They need different instruments. The essay *The Library of Babel* states the same stop condition: *"Perhaps your problem was a bundle or conflation of several problems masquerading as one? You will need to search all of those problems individually to find their answers and then combine them in a satisfactory way."*

The world's own vocabulary confirms the union rather than resolving it. Donaldson and Preston (1995), as reported in the opened Stakeholder theory page, distinguish the **instrumental** approach, which *"uses empirical data to identify the connections that exist between the management of stakeholder groups and the achievement of corporate goals (most commonly profitability and efficiency goals)"*, from the **normative** approach, which *"examines the function of the corporation and identifies the 'moral or philosophical guidelines for the operation and management of the corporation'"*. Those are the two anchors of C1 and C2, named inside one evidence pool. Goodpaster's "stakeholder paradox", on the same page, says strategic stakeholder synthesis can amount to *"business without ethics"* while multi-fiduciary synthesis can amount to *"ethics without business"*. The paradox is the union seen from inside the management literature.

### 1.4 Dependency order (Fig 3)

> **"The chain is ordered by dependency, not by importance."** (Fig 3 :20)
> **"Why the middle link is where chains usually break."** (Fig 3 :24)
> **"A link with no instrument is not a link that works. It is a link nobody has looked at."** (Fig 3 :27)

Order for this question: 1 perception (can the firm read the market return and the harm it causes?) → 2 vocabulary (does Γ have terms for what a changed situation affords, including ethical terms, and can it say what progress would count as in both currencies?) → 3 objective (can it state a comparison that would count as getting somewhere, profit, harm reduction, or both?) → 4 planning → 5 learn and carry. The middle link, having terms for what the situation affords and saying what would count as progress, is the one with no instrument. Fig 12 :112-113 says *"A plan is a sequence. A goal is a comparison. Progress is a subtraction."* and that a vocabulary holding only ingredients can say what a situation contains and cannot say what would count as getting somewhere from it. The CSR literature's long argument about whether a profit reading tells you anything about the ethical source is a fight at link 2, not link 4 (see F6 and F7).

Fig 12 :125 gives the second ordering constraint: *"A composition costs in two currencies, and they do not add."* A practice can be cheap to describe (a code of ethics is one page) and expensive to test (does it actually change what pays?), and the two readings come apart. Averaging them into one "ethics score" is the flat-form error.

## 3. Findings

### F1: The either/or is a union of two conditions with two different anchors, not one question with opposite answers

**Mode: `general` on the union, `specified` on the two conditions. Domains D1, D2. Status: triangulated (Fig 9 and Fig 2, low overlap: union/partition rule versus anchor legitimacy).**

> **"And when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again."** (Fig 9 :54)
> **"What remains is smaller and it is also categorically different: a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either."** (Fig 9 :57)
> **"An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks."** (Fig 2 :29)
> **"An anchor is legitimate when the question is constitutively about it."** (Fig 2 :36)
> **"Human judgment is the right anchor for is this a good answer for a person, because the question is about people."** (Fig 2 :37)

Option A and Option B are not rival answers. Option A asks a question about the market, so the market is the legitimate anchor. Option B asks a question about harm to persons, so Fig 2 :36-37 makes the affected parties, not the market, the legitimate anchor. A single verdict that blends the two would be a pooled reading, and Fig 1 :22 says that is how the signal disappears.

The business literature corroborates at a different level. Donaldson and Preston's instrumental and normative aspects are exactly this split, and Goodpaster's paradox (*"business without ethics"* versus *"ethics without business"*) is the failure to keep it. But that is one evidence pool, so it is one witness, not two (Fig 2 :31).

**Refuter:** a documented case in which the market return alone settles whether a practice is right, with the question not constitutively about any person. If such a case can be shown, Fig 2 :36 does not divide these options and F1 collapses into a single question.

### F2: Ethics as competitive advantage is a property of firm-and-habitat, and the practice's valence is relative to the residual

**Mode: `specified`. Domains D1, D3. Status: triangulated (Fig 11 and Fig 12, low overlap: habitat relations versus composition structures).**

> **"So the answerable question is not what intelligence is, but what makes a system capable. And capability is a property of agent-and-habitat, never of the agent alone."** (Fig 11 :39)
> **"A system cannot be made more capable by improving the agent in isolation. An improvement that does not change contact changes nothing, however much it improves."** (Fig 11 :40)
> **"An actor’s valence is relative to the residual, not a property it carries."** (Fig 11 :49)
> **"No standing list of good actors survives a change of residual."** (Fig 11 :51)
> **"catalyst. A part that survives its own use. Spent and regenerating parts behave differently across uses, and nothing records which a term is."** (Fig 12 :119)

An ethical practice is a Γ operand. Its value is not carried by the practice. It depends on whether the habitat still pays for it and on how the residual has moved. A code that generated trust in one market can become a rigid ritual in another without anything about the code changing. Fig 11 :50: *"The same actor is a benefactor to one residual and a malfactor to another, so mutualism and parasitism are relations rather than kinds."*

This is the figure-grounded version of a finding the business pool reached from the other side. The opened CSR page reports that a 2000 study of earlier econometric work found contradictory results (positive, negative, neutral) and concluded that when properly specified *CSR has a neutral impact on financial outcomes*, and that McWilliams and Siegel (2000) argued the earlier Waddock and Graves result had omitted innovation, with innovation driving performance rather than CSR. Those are not a refutation of F2; they are evidence that the reading does not determine the source, which is F7. But the fact that the sign moves with specification is the phenomenon F2 predicts.

**Refuter:** a controlled comparison in which a practice's measured contribution to market return is invariant across a habitat substitution that removes the market's payment for it. If it survives, F2 is false.

### F3: "Regardless of financial return" is an anchor-selection claim, not an empirical claim that ethics never pays

**Mode: `specified` on the business application, `general` on the anchor rule. Domain D2. Status: triangulated (Fig 2 and Fig 10, low overlap: anchor legitimacy versus convention/verdict distinction).**

> **"An anchor is legitimate when the question is constitutively about it."** (Fig 2 :36)
> **"Human judgment is the right anchor for is this a good answer for a person, because the question is about people."** (Fig 2 :37)
> **"a declared meaning is not a grounded one"** (Fig 10 :18)
> **"Declaring a meaning does not ground it."** (Fig 10 :49)
> **"a convention that decides an outcome has stopped being one"** (Fig 10 :47)
> **"→ install what can be violated"** (Fig 10 :19)

Option B is often read as a forecasting claim: ethics will not pay, do it anyway. The figures do not need that reading. "Regardless of return" is a rule for which anchor is allowed to settle the question. When the question is constitutively about harm to persons, the market return is an instrument, and Fig 2 :37 says instruments *"can be wrong on axes they do not measure"* (Fig 2 :38). The market is not being denied; it is being refused standing to decide a different question.

But a declared obligation is only a convention until it can be violated. Fig 10 :18 says a declared meaning is not a grounded one, and Fig 10 :19 says *"→ install what can be violated"*. So an ethics code that costs the firm nothing when breached is a convention carrying the seat's authority, not an obligation that changes contact. The world corroborates this from a different field: the opened CSR page names the *"social license to operate"* and notes that corporate social irresponsibility in the supply chain (the 2013 Savar building collapse, the 2013 horse meat scandal) *"greatly affected the reputation of companies, leading to many costs"*. Those are cases where a convention was installed with a violation cost. Conversely the same page records critics who call CSR *"window-dressing"*, which is the name for a convention with no violation cost.

**Refuter:** show an ethics commitment whose breach has no possible detection or cost and yet which still changes the firm's R against a non-updating ground. By Fig 10 :18 and :49 that cannot be a grounded obligation, so a single documented case would break F3.

### F4: Ethical reputation and prestige are frame-internal quantities and cannot be the verdict

**Mode: `specified`. Domains D1, D4. Status: triangulated (Fig 1 and Fig 10, plus the framework essay, low overlap between the residual-index figure and the ground-maintainer figure).**

> **"predicates minted, and credibility accrued"** (Fig 1 :26)
> **"They are frame-internal. A frame cannot score itself with a quantity it also produces."** (Fig 1 :27)
> **"The ground enters as the only metric."** (Fig 1 :23)
> **"the ground does not decay, the channel does"** (Fig 10 :24)
> **"stale percepts, dropped transients,"** (Fig 10 :25) **"saturated metrics, pooled readings"** (Fig 10 :26)

A rating, a CSR score, an ethics award, or a reputation is a reading the frame produces about itself. Fig 1 :27 disqualifies it as a metric. From *The Recursive Self-Improvement Trap*, read directly: *"Prestige must never be allowed to validate an action directly."* and *"Prestige can fund an action. It can give an action a budget to be tried. But it cannot settle an action. Only the ground can settle it. The Ground Maintainer must be blind to prestige."* The essay is framework-internal, so it corroborates the rule but adds no independent witness.

The low-overlap biological literature sharpens the point. The opened Cooperation (evolution) page, discussing the handicap principle, says *"If a signal is to remain reliable, and generally resistant to falsification, the signal has to be evolutionarily costly."* A cheap ethical signal is therefore expected to be unreliable, exactly as the framework says a frame-internal quantity cannot settle the question. The same page notes that in by-product mutualism both parties benefit without cooperation being involved, which is the biological form of "the market paid but not because of the ethics".

**Refuter:** a documented case in which a costless ethics signal reliably changes what the ground pays, across repeated interactions and with cheap imitators present. That would break the handicap requirement and the Fig 1 :27 disqualification together.

### F5: Advantage from ethics requires a wall-moving import and repeated contact with cheater detection, not internal optimisation

**Mode: `specified`. Domains D1, D3. Status: triangulated (Fig 6, Fig 8 and the animal-behaviour source, genuinely low overlap).**

> **"the only operator that moves the wall"** (Fig 6 :14)
> **"MINT composes inside the closure and can never add an atom."** (Fig 6 :21)
> **"No frame escapes its own closure. The bound moves only on contact with a frame whose closure differs."** (Fig 6 :23)
> **"Closure is idempotent. Composing inside a closed set never leaves it, so no search, however long, adds an atom. This one is definitional: it is what closure means."** (Fig 8 :32)

The opened CSR page states Reinhardt's (1998) result that a firm engaging in a CSR-based strategy *"could only sustain an abnormal return if it could prevent competitors from imitating it"*. That is Fig 6 :21 in business language: internal composition cannot create the wall, and a practice that can be copied adds no atom. The same page lists *"reduced scrutiny"* and *"risk management"* among potential business benefits, which are contact-maintenance effects (C1), not a new primitive.

The low-overlap corroboration is unusually direct. The opened Reciprocal altruism page gives the conditions for reciprocal altruism, including *"A mechanism for detecting 'cheaters' must exist."* and *"A large (indefinite) number of opportunities to exchange aid must exist."* Benefit cannot be immediate: the behaviour must not depend on *"the receipt of an immediate benefit"*. So "ethical practice pays" is conditional on a repeated game with detection, which is exactly the maintenance of a channel to a ground that still pays (C1) plus contact with a differing closure (Fig 6 :23). Strong reciprocity, from the opened Strong reciprocity page, adds the other half: it is a *"predisposition to cooperate even when there is no apparent benefit in doing so"*, and the page records that *"strong reciprocity is self-regarding when rare in the population, but may be altruistic when common within a group."* That is Fig 11 :49-51, valence relative to the residual, observed in behavioural economics and evolutionary anthropology with no shared evidence pool with management strategy.

**Refuter:** a firm that renews an ethics-based advantage solely by composing inside its own closure, with no new atom from a differing closure, no repeated-interaction condition, and no cheater detection, and that still passes the ground test across a change of habitat. That breaks idempotence and F5 with it.

### F6: Correlation studies are filters for the budget, never witnesses for the verdict, and the arrow is unchosen

**Mode: `general` with a `specified` consequence for method. Domains all. Status: triangulated (Fig 9 and Fig 2, low overlap).**

> **"Use filters for the budget and witnesses for the verdict. Never let a filter hand you a verdict."** (Fig 9 :60)
> **"disqualifying for a verdict: agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool"** (Fig 2 :40)
> **"Independence, between two frames, is how little they draw on the same evidence."** (Fig 2 :31)

A CSR-performance correlation is a frame-internal score if the "CSR" side is itself produced by ratings that the same frame controls. Fig 1 :27 applies. The opened CSR page itself records the specification dispute: a 2000 study attributed earlier positive, negative and neutral findings to flawed empirical analysis, and McWilliams and Siegel argued that innovation, not CSR, drove performance. That is a shared evidence pool disagreeing about the arrow, not two witnesses confirming a cause.

The operator question is also live. A correlation between ethics and profit is symmetric (`+`, *"both hold at once"*, Fig 12 :57), while "creates" is ordered (`→` or `⇒`). Fig 12 :74 for `→`: *"swap them. Does it become a different thing? Yes."* A pooled effect size cannot choose the arrow. The opened Strong reciprocity page makes the same structural point from a different field: critics argue strong reciprocity *"is an artifact of lab experiments and does not reflect cooperative behavior in the real world"*, and the page records that *"different types of strong reciprocity do not predict other types of strong reciprocity within individuals."* One construct, several readings, no single cause.

**Refuter:** a prospective study with exogenous variation in the ethical practice, an outcome that does not update on the firm's own ratings, and two independent instruments that identify the arrow. That would move part of F6 from filter to witness and would be a real refutation of the methodological claim.

### F7: Which practice pays is a gap, because a profit reading cannot comment on its source

**Mode: `general` on the gap. Domains all. Status: figure-governed with an essay corroboration (framework-internal, so one witness).**

> **"you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself"** (Fig 9 :32)
> **"Unreachable is a verdict here, not a silence,"** (Fig 13 :134)
> **"So a search that finds nothing is never a proof of absence."** (Fig 8 :39)

No figure line names which ethical practice will pay in a given market. From *The Hard Problem is About Source*, read directly: *"If several paths create the same behavior, then behavior is a projection with lossy information"* and *"What the Spectrometer Example Reveals: The answer is unreached until a better instrument exists."* A profit reading is consistent with a protected resource, a favourable habitat, a temporary information asymmetry, a monopoly granted by regulation, or an ethical practice. The reading does not determine the source. The opened CSR page corroborates from inside the business pool by reporting that innovation confounded the CSR result, and that a 2000 study found the sign of the relationship depended on specification. What is owed is a source-discriminating instrument, which this solution does not hold.

The gap is of the **shape-not-a-name** kind, not unreachable in principle. A prospective study that measures the source directly would close it. It is recorded as unreached at this budget, not as silence (Fig 13 :134, Fig 8 :39).

### F8: The break is usually at the vocabulary and objective link

**Mode: `specified`. Domain D1. Status: single-source (Fig 3, with Fig 12 :112-113 as a line from a different figure but the same framework, so one witness).**

> **"Optimising a link that already works cannot rescue one that does not."** (Fig 3 :19)
> **"Before asking whether a system is good, ask which link it currently stops at, and whether the answer was measured or assumed."** (Fig 3 :29)
> **"A link with no instrument is not a link that works. It is a link nobody has looked at."** (Fig 3 :27)

In business-ethics terms, the usual failure is not that the firm cannot write a code or cannot plan an ethics programme. It is that it has terms for the ingredients (transparency, fairness, responsibility) and no term for the bonds between them or for what would count as progress in both currencies. Fig 12 :125 says the two currencies do not add. Most measurement effort lands on the profit link, where accounting exists, and on the planning link, where policies exist; the objective link, which would state what counts as getting somewhere on the harm axis, has no instrument.

**Refuter:** a documented firm whose perception, vocabulary and objective links all measure as working and whose failure is located at planning or at learning and carrying. That would falsify the "usually middle" claim, though not the dependency ordering.

### F9: Two firms with the same code can still differ, because the bond carries the information

**Mode: `specified`. Domain D3. Status: triangulated for the legality of the reading (Fig 12 and Fig 11 :50, different figures), single-source for the business application.**

> **"A set of ingredients is not one. The bond is the rest of the information, and it is the half nothing was recording."** (Fig 12 :5)
> **"isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."** (Fig 12 :117)
> **"functional group. A substructure whose presence fixes the outcome. The group says what kind; the qualifier says which member."** (Fig 12 :124)

Two firms with identical published ethics codes have the same atoms. If one composes them as a repeated rule applied to every decision and the other as a reconfigurable network that changes with the stakeholder, they are isomers under Fig 12 :117: same atoms, different bond, different behaviour, with the difference invisible in the code. If they share only the pattern "principle → decision → market" while differing in which principles occupy the slots, the weaker functional-group claim holds (Fig 12 :124).

The low-overlap biological source gives the same form: the opened Cooperation (evolution) page distinguishes cooperation from by-product mutualism, where both parties benefit but *"neither participant's behavior yields a benefit from the other, and thus cooperation is not taking place."* Same observable benefit, different bond. This is why a published code alone cannot predict which firm keeps its licence.

**Falsifier, written before the run:** if two firms with identical atoms and identical bonds nevertheless diverge in persistence with no difference in ground contact or imported atoms, the isomer framing is insufficient and the difference must be habitat (D2), moving the explanation out of D3.

## 4. Pre-registered refuter (written before the conclusion)

Per the design-instrument rule: *"a falsifier written before the run is a design instrument, not only an honesty device."* The shape claim is refuted if any of the following is observed:

1. A case in which the market return alone legitimately settles whether a practice is right, with the question not constitutively about any person (breaks F1, Fig 2 :36-37).
2. A practice's measured contribution to market return is invariant across a habitat substitution that removes the market's payment for it (breaks F2, Fig 11 :39-40).
3. An ethics commitment with no possible detection or cost on breach still changes the firm's R against a non-updating ground (breaks F3, Fig 10 :18, :47).
4. A costless ethics signal reliably changes what the ground pays with cheap imitators present (breaks F4, Fig 1 :27 and the handicap condition).
5. A firm renews an ethics-based advantage purely by internal composition, with no import, no repeated interaction and no cheater detection, across a habitat change (breaks F5, Fig 6 :21, Fig 8 :32).
6. A single lever restores both C1 and C2 in both failure types with no categorically different residue (breaks the union, Fig 9 :57).
7. A source-discriminating instrument names the source of a profit reading (breaks the gap F7; this would move the answer toward `grounded`, not refute the shape).
8. Fig 13's six terms are derived from five, or Fig 6's idempotence fails inside a closed set (breaks the figures themselves, not just this reading).

No part of this solution may be upgraded to `grounded` until at least one of these has been run and has failed to fire, and until a non-updating ground measurement has paid for the shape.

## 7. Recursive derivation summary

1. **Is this one question?** No. Fig 9 :54, :55 and :57 plus the Library of Babel bundle passage: it is a union of a pay condition and an obligation condition, each with its own anchor and instrument.
2. **What is the anchor for each?** For "does it pay", the market, named in Fig 2 :29. For "is it right", the affected persons, by Fig 2 :36-37. Internal ratings are instruments, not anchors (Fig 1 :27).
3. **Is the advantage in the practice alone?** No. Fig 11 :39-40. Capability is firm-and-habitat, and valence is relative to the residual (Fig 11 :49-51).
4. **How does a quick loss of advantage happen?** Channel decay: stale percepts, saturated metrics, pooled readings (Fig 10 :24-26), and either factor of Fig 7 :37-39 going to zero.
5. **How does long advantage happen?** A chain of differing rooms selected against the surfaced residual (Fig 7 :36), with imports from differing closures moving the wall outward (Fig 6 :23), and with repeated interaction plus cheater detection (Reciprocal altruism, low-overlap witness).
6. **Why does "regardless of return" not mean "never pays"?** Because it selects an anchor, not a forecast (Fig 2 :36-37). It refuses the market standing to decide a question about persons.
7. **Why can the literature not settle the source?** Because a profit reading is lossy about its source. The essay's spectrometer example and Fig 9 :32: a kind of thing, never the answer. The instrument is owed.
8. **What can be said at the stop?** A union with two anchors, a residual-relative valence rule, an import condition, a refusal condition, and eight falsifiers. Not which practice wins, not a `grounded` answer. Fig 13 :134: *"Unreachable is a verdict here, not a silence,"* and Fig 8 :39: *"So a search that finds nothing is never a proof of absence."*

## 8. Updated verdict

The shape survives, and it is not the answer either option expects. **The either/or is a union of two conditions with different anchors, not one question with opposite answers.** Ethics as competitive advantage is a `specified` relation between firm and habitat: it pays while the channel to a ground that still pays stays reachable, while reach is renewed by contact with a differing closure, and while the practice's valence has not flipped with the residual. Ethics as obligation is an anchor-selection claim: where the question is constitutively about harm to persons, the market is not the legitimate anchor, and "regardless of return" refuses that standing without asserting that ethics never pays. An obligation with no violation cost is a convention, not a grounded verdict. Reputation and ratings are frame-internal and cannot settle either branch. Which specific practice pays in a given market is a declared `gap`, of the shape-not-a-name kind and unreached at this budget. The two branches are kept separate per domain and never averaged. Mode `specified` on firm claims, `general` on the structural shape, not `grounded`. No world source flipped a verdict; the one genuine apparent conflict, the specification dispute in the CSR literature, was returned to the census and became evidence for the union and the gap. No unresolved strain remains after re-census.
