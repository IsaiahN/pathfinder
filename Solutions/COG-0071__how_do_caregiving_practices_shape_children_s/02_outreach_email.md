**Use:** one-to-one, or as a cover letter attached to a submission through the public venues in `03_public_contact_points.csv`. **Not** for bulk send.

**Hard precondition:** do not send until the live strain in `06_isomers_and_order.md` Q1 is stated in the recipient's own terms and the refuter below is written in front of them. If the output does not survive stage 1, there is nothing else to look at.

**Subject:** One checkable claim about caregiving and child outcomes, before any theory

> Hi [name],
>
> I have one specific output I would like you to check against what you already know. I am not asking you to read the theory first, and I am not asking you to agree with it. If the output does not survive, there is nothing else to look at.
>
> **The claim (`specified`, not `grounded`):** under this framework, a caregiving practice changes a child by changing what the child is in contact with, not by improving the child in isolation. The governing line is: *"A system cannot be made more capable by improving the agent in isolation. An improvement that does not change contact changes nothing, however much it improves."* (Fig 11 :40). Applied to your field, that predicts three per-slot effects, not one pooled parenting effect:
>
> 1. **Attention** follows which actors and conditions are in the child's habitat and where the child's and caregiver's probes point. It is the perception and vocabulary link (Fig 3 :27: *"A link with no instrument is not a link that works. It is a link nobody has looked at."*), not a standing attention-span trait.
> 2. **Emotional regulation** is a maintained channel to a ground that does not move when the child's reading moves (Fig 10 :24: *"the ground does not decay, the channel does"*). What crosses from caregiver to child is a reapplicable method or a head start, not a recording of one calm occasion (Fig 4 :22).
> 3. **Expectations of other people** are a belief with a learnable weighting between private history and inherited prior. They are correctable only against other people's actual behaviour, which must not update on what the child expects (Fig 2 :33: *"The anchor must not update."*).
>
> These three have different currencies and different anchors, so they form a union of overlapping conditions and a single lever will leave the intersections standing (Fig 9 :55). I am **not** claiming a named practice fills any slot. The framework returns *"a kind of thing, never the answer itself"* (Fig 9 :32). Which practice works for which child is a declared `gap` that needs a source-discriminating instrument, and I am not filling it with a favourite technique.
>
> **What would refute the claim, written before this message:**
> 1. A child slot improves while habitat contact is measured invariant: no new actor, condition, relation, or instrument, and no change in ground feedback.
> 2. One lever moves all three outcomes by the same amount under the same anchor, with no categorically different residue.
> 3. A self-regulation gain persists across a substituted habitat with no reproduced contact, or a replayed instruction produces a novel regulated response at first contact with a new stressor.
> 4. Expectations remain unchanged after repeated independent contradiction, or move only with caregiver influence and not with ground feedback.
>
> If any one holds, the affected finding is withdrawn. A one-line "this fails because..." is more useful to me than agreement. If you would rather not engage, no reply is needed.
>
> **Before you answer:** what would you expect to see if this shape claim were true, and what would you count as a failure? Please record that verdict before reading any theory.
>
> **If it survives, and only then:** the figures are the authority, not my summary of them.
> - Verbatim figure text with line numbers (local framework file): `FRAMEWORK/reference/03-FIGURES_TEXT.md`
> - Canonical usage rule (local framework file): `FRAMEWORK/reference/FIGURES_README_canonical.md`
> - The eight essays, including the apprenticeship and contact argument: https://adventuresinml.substack.com/p/decoding-tacit-knowledge-why-context
>
> **If you want to use the machinery:** the formula is in the figures link. Run it on a problem you know well and can check against a ground. Write down what would refute your result *before* you run it, and label the mode of what you get. A reported failure is more useful to me than a reported success.
