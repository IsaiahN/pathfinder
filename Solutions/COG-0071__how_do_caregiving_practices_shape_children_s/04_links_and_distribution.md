## Wider literature read for this question

All were opened on 2026-11-22 through the PubMed eutils abstract endpoint (`eutils.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/entrez/eutils/efetch.fcgi`), which returns the publisher's abstract text; the direct PubMed HTML page requires cookies and was not used as a source.

1. **Animal behaviour, low overlap.** Liu, Diorio, Tannenbaum, Caldji, Francis, Freedman, Sharma, Pearson, Plotsky and Meaney, *Maternal care, hippocampal glucocorticoid receptors, and hypothalamic-pituitary-adrenal responses to stress*, Science 277(5332), 1997. PMID 9287218. Quote: *"These findings suggest that maternal behavior serves to 'program' hypothalamic-pituitary-adrenal responses to stress in the offspring."* Also *"Each measure was significantly correlated with the frequency of maternal licking and grooming (all r's > -0.6)."* Corroborates F4 with a ground measure, and warns that a correlation is a filter, not a witness (Fig 9 :60).
2. **Animal behaviour, low overlap.** Liu, Diorio, Day, Francis and Meaney, *Maternal care, hippocampal synaptogenesis and cognitive development in rats*, Nature Neuroscience 3(8), 2000. PMID 10903573. Quote: *"although not all neonates were equally sensitive to variations in maternal care."* This is the per-slot, valence-relative point (Fig 11 :49), not a uniform effect.
3. **Animal behaviour, low overlap.** Weaver, Champagne, Brown, Dymov, Sharma, Meaney and Szyf, *Reversal of maternal programming of stress responses in adult offspring through methyl supplementation*, Journal of Neuroscience 25(47), 2005. PMID 16306417. Quote: *"they are potentially reversible in the adult brain."* Corroborates the maintained-method reading rather than a permanent-trait reading.
4. **Developmental psychopathology, low overlap with the animal pool.** Fish, Shahrokh, Bagot, Caldji, Bredy, Szyf and Meaney, *Epigenetic programming of stress responses through variations in maternal care*, Annals of the New York Academy of Sciences 1036, 2004. PMID 15817737. Quote: *"Early life experiences shape an individual's physical and mental health across the lifespan."* Also: *"A central theme that arises from studies in human and nonhuman species is that the effects of adversity are mediated by the interactions between a mother and her young."*
5. **Developmental psychology intervention meta-analysis.** Prime, Andrews, Markwell, Gonzalez, Janus, Tricco, Bennett and Atkinson, *Positive Parenting and Early Childhood Cognition: A Systematic Review and Meta-Analysis of Randomized Controlled Trials*, Clinical Child and Family Psychology Review 26(2), 2023. PMID 36729307. Quote: *"Effect sizes were smaller and nonsignificant for executive functioning (g = 0.07, N = 3628; k = 14) and pre-academics (g = 0.16, N = 2365; k = 7)."* The decisive per-slot dissociation for F2.
6. **Social development / mind-mindedness.** Colonnesi, Zeegers, Majdandzic, van Steensel and Bogels, *Fathers' and Mothers' Early Mind-Mindedness Predicts Social Competence and Behavior Problems in Childhood*, Journal of Abnormal Child Psychology 47(9), 2019. PMID 30929182. Quote: *"nonattuned mind-mindedness (i.e., misinterpretations of the child's mental states)"* and *"mothers' frequent use of nonattuned comments at 12 and 30 months and fathers' nonattuned comments at 30 months predicted children's externalizing behavior."*
7. **Social development / mind-mindedness and biology.** Baron-Cohen, Fearon, Meins, Feldman, Hardiman, Rosan and Fonagy, *Maternal mind-mindedness and infant oxytocin are interrelated and negatively associated with postnatal depression*, Development and Psychopathology 37(4), 2025. PMID 39363731. Quote: *"Previous studies show that maternal mind-mindedness positively impacts children's social development."* Also the negative result: nasal oxytocin *"did not significantly influence levels of mind-mindedness"* in a small double-blind study, recorded so the positive association is not over-read.
8. **Attachment and social information processing.** Xu, Wu, Xu, Ding, Zhou and Long, *The Role of Parent-Child Attachment, Hostile Attribution Bias in Aggression: A Meta-Analytic Review*, Trauma, Violence, and Abuse 25(3), 2024. PMID 37970850. Quote: *"In Eastern culture, the association between HAB and aggression was stronger than in Western culture."* Corroborates Fig 11 :51 (no standing list survives a change of residual/habitat).
9. **Attachment and early adversity.** Noonan and Pilkington, *Intimate partner violence and child attachment: A systematic review and meta-analysis*, Child Abuse and Neglect 109, 2020. PMID 33039816. Quote: *"IPV was significantly associated with less secure child attachment"* with pooled effects *"small (r = -.22...; r = -.10...)"*. Small effects, and larger when measured observationally in infancy, are a warning against a single pooled lever.
10. **Human cognition and development.** Gagne, Lemelin and Tarabulsy, *Non-verbal and verbal parental mentalization as predictors of infant attachment security*, Infant Behavior and Development 65, 2021. PMID 34418793. Quote: *"maternal sensitivity to be a factor that partly explains the influence of PEM on attachment security in children."* A mediator, not a universal cause: one witness in one evidence pool.

**Overlap declaration.** Sources 5 to 10 are all in the developmental-psychology and caregiving pool; pooled effect sizes across them are one witness, not six. Sources 1 to 4 share the Meaney laboratory and the rat maternal-care protocol, so they are one additional witness, not four. The essays share the framework author's evidence pool completely and carry no independent witness weight. The two low-overlap witnesses are the animal-care protocol (mechanism shape) and the human parenting RCT meta-analysis (per-slot dissociation). No world source conflicts with the figure verdict; where Prime's null EF result first looked like a conflict, it was returned to the census and re-mapped to a different quantity (F2 in `01`).

## Distribution: offer, do not push

- **Fig 4 :22** - *"Only methods cross up. Only head starts come down."* Sending the full architecture before anyone has described a residual sends a recording, not a generator. Lead with the shape and the refuter.
- **Fig 10 :42** - *"A verifier that can reconstruct the claim cannot verify it."* A recipient who has absorbed the framework before being asked for a verdict has joined the frame being checked.
- **Fig 9 :27-28** - *"describe it before you go looking, or any frame you pick will seem to fit"* Ask what they would expect to see if the shape were true before giving them the essays.
- **Fig 2 :42** - *"Buy independence in proportion to the cost of being wrong."* One careful outside reader is worth a list of sympathetic readers.
- **Seat Map :163** - *"High correlation is disqualifying for a verdict and efficient for a convention."* Selecting recipients for likely agreement produces a convention, not corroboration.

**Recommended order in a reply:** (1) the shape claim and its six refuters; (2) ask what they would count as a failure and what they would expect to see; (3) record their verdict; (4) only then the links, with the note that the figures, not the summary, are the authority.

## The two-stage offer

1. **Stage 1, output only.** The three per-slot shape claims and the refuter. No theory shown. Fig 10 :52: *"Bring a characterised residual, never the source."*
2. **Stage 2, opt-in.** If they are curious after recording a verdict, offer the three framework links. Their later agreement is never pooled as corroboration (Fig 2 :40).
3. **Stage 3, method reuse.** Offer the formula as a generator, with the instruction that each user names their own ground and writes their own refuter before running it (`THE_FORMULA.md` "Direction").

**Hard precondition:** stages 1 to 3 only work if the stage-1 output survives. The live strain in `06` Q1 (the ordered "shapes" arrow versus two-way contact) must be stated, not hidden, before sending.
