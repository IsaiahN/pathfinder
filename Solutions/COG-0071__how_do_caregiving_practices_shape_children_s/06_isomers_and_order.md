# Isomers, bond type and order in the caregiving question

**Mode: `specified` on the structure, `general` on the bond-type claim, `gap` on which specific practice fills which slot. Not `grounded`.** Simulated derivation checked against the figures, not a ground reading (see `05_what_shape_means.md` section 7).

## RESIDUAL

The question writes "caregiving practices shape children". Two derivations are owed: (1) what kind of bond the word "shape" can be under the operators table, and (2) whether two different caregiving regimes over the same atoms are isomers, a functional group, or something else, and in what order their effects compose. If the bond is misidentified, every downstream claim inherits the error.

## DOMAIN CENSUS (kept separate from `01`, then compared)

Atoms held: the caregiver, the child Γ with belief b and weighting w, the conditions and relations of the habitat, and the ground that settles. Bonds held: the seven, of which →, ⇒, and the network reading of Fig 13 :24 are ordered/relational, while + is symmetric. Negation ¬ is available for refutations. No new primitive is available without import.

- R arity: at least three-place (care act, child state, ground reading), indexed per child and time (Fig 1 :19).
- Symmetry: the ordinary-language arrow is ordered, but the observed object is two-way contact, so the first pass is strained.
- Scale: within-child loop, caregiver-child membrane, developmental room chain.
- Union, not partition (Fig 9 :55-57): the three outcomes share atoms and their overlap is a compound.

## DERIVATION

### Q1 - Is "shapes" an ordered arrow or a two-way network bond? CORRECTION TO MY FIRST PASS

- Operators table :30-33: *"sequence"* / *"A then B; B needs A to have happened"* / test: *"swap them. Does it become a different thing? Yes."*
- Operators table :36-40: *"production"* / *"A produces B; B did not exist before"* / test: *"is there a B before A fires? No."*
- Fig 13 :24: *"that entities relate, and influence travels"*
- Fig 11 :45: *"actors: what acts on it, and what it acts on"*
- Fig 12 :46: *"A bond is the typed relation between two operands. There are seven, they are not interchangeable, and which one holds is not recoverable from the operands."*

A one-way reading of "shapes" as ⇒ (production) would require the child outcome not to exist before the caregiving act fires. That is false for a child who arrives with a Γ, a prior and a history. A → (sequence) reading requires order but not production, and is defensible for a specific act before a specific outcome. But the caregiver also reads and is changed by the child: *"Between two people the loop runs both ways at once. Each person speaks and listens in every turn"* (*Decoding Tacit Knowledge*, opened directly). So the governing object is a two-way contact relation, a network bond as Fig 13 :24 describes, with → reserved for a single measured act-outcome pair.

**I overstated the arrow in my first pass.** "Shapes" was written as one-way production. Retracted as applied to the caregiver-child relation. The correct form is two-way contact with typed direction per slot. Triangulated (Operators table + Fig 13 + the tacit-knowledge essay). The correction is visible in `01` section 7 and here; the original is not erased.

**Falsifier, written before the run:** if a caregiving act can be shown to produce a child capacity that did not exist in any form before the act (a genuine ⇒ case, not a change of weighting or contact), the pure production reading is restored and the network reading is narrowed. Until such a case is measured, → and network are held apart, not merged.

### Q2 - Are caregiving regimes isomers over a shared atom set?

- Fig 12 :117: *"isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."*
- Fig 12 :123: *"functional group. A substructure whose presence fixes the outcome. The group says what kind; the qualifier says which member."*
- Fig 12 :94: *"The arrangement is the substance."*

Two regimes, R1 (high sensitivity plus high mind-mindedness plus low harsh discipline) and R2 (the same three ingredients with different timing, different speaker, or a different bond to the child), over a fixed atom set {caregiver, child Γ, b, w, conditions, relations, ground}, are:

- **not isomers as ordinarily reported**, because the literature lists different ingredients for each regime (one has mind-mindedness, the other does not). Under Fig 12's definition they are then members of a **functional group**: the shared substructure is "a seat acts on a habitat by introduction" and the qualifiers pick members.
- **isomers under the stronger form**, if both regimes are written over the full shared atom set and differ only in which bond attaches which actor to which state. Then they have the same atoms, a different arrangement, and a different substance. This is the checkable structural claim. The world instance is Colonnesi et al. 2019: appropriate mind-related comments and nonattuned comments use the *same* act (talking about the child's mental state) with a different bond (correct reading versus misinterpretation), and the outcomes differ (social competence versus externalizing problems).

**Falsifier, written before the run:** if either regime can be written only by adding an atom the other does not require (for example a practice that genuinely imports a new condition rather than re-bonding a held one), the isomer claim fails and the functional-group reading stands. Prime et al. 2023 already give a partial instance: the same positive-parenting intervention moves mental abilities and language but not executive functioning (g = 0.07, nonsignificant), so the "one regime, one substance" reading is too coarse.

### Q3 - What order do the effects compose in?

- Fig 3 :20: *"The chain is ordered by dependency, not by importance. Each link consumes what the one above produces, so effort spent above the break arrives at a step that cannot use it."*
- Fig 3 :27: *"A link with no instrument is not a link that works. It is a link nobody has looked at."*
- Fig 12 :104: *"A settled molecule becomes an atom for whatever composes over it. So irreducible is a fact about a level and never about the world, and there is no bottom"*
- Fig 4 :22: *"Only methods cross up. Only head starts come down."*

Dependency order for a child slot: perception → vocabulary → objective → planning → learn and carry. A caregiver aiming at an objective cannot rescue a missing vocabulary link (Fig 3 :19: *"Optimising a link that already works cannot rescue one that does not."*). This predicts a specific failure shape: an intervention that coaches a child's goal-setting will not fix an attention slot in which the relevant actor was never in the habitat.

Across scales, a settled first-order child capacity becomes an atom for the next level (Fig 12 :104). What crosses the caregiver-child membrane is a method or a head start; a recording of one occasion does not (Fig 4 :25: *"A recording carried upward looks like knowledge and is a description of one occasion."*). The world analogue is Weaver et al. 2005: an early care-induced marking could be reversed in adulthood, so the arrangement is not a permanent one-way inscription.

**Falsifier:** if a goal-level intervention repairs a missing perception or vocabulary link while those links are measured absent and unchanged, the dependency order is wrong and Fig 3 has been misapplied to this domain.

### Q4 - Union or partition, and why it decides the lever count

- Fig 9 :57: *"What remains is smaller and it is also categorically different: a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either."*
- Fig 9 :60: *"Use filters for the budget and witnesses for the verdict. Never let a filter hand you a verdict."*

If the three outcomes were a partition, one lever per outcome would clear each region. They are a union: a child in two conditions (for example insecure expectations and a channel that has decayed) is not a harder case of either, and a single lever leaves the intersection standing. Effect sizes are filters for which lever to try first; they are not witnesses for the verdict. Prime et al.'s g values are used exactly this way.

### Q5 - Analogues, with their limits

- **Apprenticeship** (*Decoding Tacit Knowledge*, opened directly): the closest human analogue. Two-way contact with correction; the learner must rebuild the pretext through doing. Limit: a trade has a stable criterion, a child has three moving slots.
- **Cross-fostering in rats** (Liu et al. 2000; Szyf et al. 2005 abstracts opened): a seat substitutes a habitat and reads the ground again. Limit: the rat habitat is experimentally enumerable in a way a human family habitat is not (Fig 11 :43-48).
- **Ecosystem engineering** (Fig 11 :65: *"Introducing an actor for its cascade is ecosystem engineering"*): a caregiver adds an actor for its downstream cascade. Limit: introduction is available, exclusion is not (Fig 11 :61), so the analogy must not be read as a clean control experiment.
- **Habitat substitution** (Fig 11 :66-69): a change of caregiver is a new habitat with unchosen actors, not the removal of one. Limit: none; this is a direct constraint, not an analogy.

## CONCLUSION

"Shapes" is not a symmetric correlation and not a simple production arrow. It is two-way contact with a typed direction per slot, plus → for a specific measured act-outcome pair. Caregiving regimes are a functional group as usually reported and isomers under the stronger shared-atom form, with a stated falsifier for each. The effects compose in dependency order, a settled child capacity becomes an atom at the next level, and only a method or head start crosses the caregiver-child membrane. The three outcomes are a union, so one lever cannot clear the intersections. The specific practice-to-slot binding remains a `gap`.
