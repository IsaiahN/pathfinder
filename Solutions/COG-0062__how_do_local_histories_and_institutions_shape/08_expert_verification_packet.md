# Expert verification packet - the Tether on local histories and moral judgment

**What this is:** a claim in a form an institutional or cultural historian, or a cross-cultural moral psychologist, can check. Mode, derivation, and refuters are stated before the conclusion, so a reader knows what to look for and what would settle it.

**Mode: `specified` - not `grounded`.** No local judgment distribution was measured in this derivation. This is a shape and a route, not a result.

## 1. The claim, in one paragraph

Fairness, harm, loyalty, and authority are four separate object slots whose residual must be indexed per slot and never averaged into one moral score. For a question constitutively about people, the ground is observed human judgment, measured per case and per slot, not theory, institutional self-description, or prestige. A locality's judgment pattern is a property of person-and-habitat: the way to explain a local difference is to enumerate the habitat (actors, conditions, relations, sanctions) from records, not to compose a general coefficient. An institution's valence changes when the local situation changes; a tradition is a chain of handoffs and imports rather than a stored recording; and a declared code is a convention until practice settles it. The framework does not name which institution caused which shift. That attribution stays open until a source-discriminating instrument exists. What it offers is the shape any answer must have, falsifiers written in advance, and the description of the instrument that would close it.

## 2. Three checkable sub-claims

### C1 - framework-internal plus method-facing; checkable by reading Figure 11 and Figure 2

- **Statement:** judgments are a property of person-and-habitat, and the habitat must be enumerated from the world. The anchor for this question is human judgment.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 11 :39 - *"So the answerable question is not what intelligence is, but what makes a system capable. And capability is a property of agent-and-habitat, never of the agent alone."* Fig 11 :43 - *"The habitat is enumerated, never composed."* Fig 2 :37 - *"Human judgment is the right anchor for is this a good answer for a person, because the question is about people."*
- **What to check:** does an enumeration of actors, conditions, and relations from a locality you know actually change the prediction of its per-slot judgments, where a list of individual traits does not? Is the observed judgment distribution the thing that settles a disagreement here, or are we settling it by theoretical preference?
- **Refuter:** judgment pattern invariant across a documented habitat substitution in which actors, conditions, relations, and sanctions are independently measured as changed and contact is maintained. Or: the anchor moves when a frame pushes on it.

### C2 - field-facing; checkable by a historian or cross-cultural researcher against cases

- **Statement:** institutions have no standing valence, and history transmits as handoffs and imports. Over the shared atom set of a locality, two arrangements differing in bond are isomers: the difference is invisible in an institution list and decisive in behavior.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 11 :49 - *"An actor's valence is relative to the residual, not a property it carries."* Fig 11 :50 - *"The same actor is a benefactor to one residual and a malfactor to another, so mutualism and parasitism are relations rather than kinds."* Fig 7 :13 - *"agreement compounds, evidence does not"*; Fig 12 :117 - *"isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."*
- **What to check:** find one institution whose role in fairness and authority judgments changed sign across a documented change in local conditions. Then ask whether a list of institutions would have predicted that change. If two localities have the same institutions but different judgment patterns, can the difference be stated as a different arrangement rather than a different ingredient?
- **Refuter:** an institution whose measured valence is invariant across a documented change of residual on all four slots; or two localities with identical atoms and bond arrangement returning different judgments under the same ground pressure.

### C3 - framework-internal derivation with a method consequence

- **Statement:** the four slots are a union of overlapping conditions to be unbundled, a written code is a convention until practice settles it, and the specific cause is a declared `gap`.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 9 :55 - *"A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones."* Fig 10 :47 - *"a convention that decides an outcome has stopped being one."* Fig 9 :32 - *"you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself."*
- **What to check:** does a case you know engage two slots at once (nepotism, an order to injure) such that treating it as one slot loses information? Is there a local code that people cite as if it settled the verdict, where the actual practice differs? Does anyone reading the record have an instrument that discriminates among the several sources of the same observed shift?
- **Refuter:** a code alone that prospectively predicts the judgments with no reading of practice; or a source-discriminating instrument that successfully names the cause in a documented case, which would close the `gap` rather than refute the method.

## 3. What is explicitly not claimed

- Not a universal moral psychology that erases cultural difference, and not a replacement for institutional history or cross-cultural moral psychology.
- Not a claim that the moral-foundation kinds are the framework's six-term substrate. That was a strain, dissolved by re-census.
- Not a claim that any named council, court, temple, or guild caused a particular shift. That is the declared `gap`.
- Not a single scalar for "moral culture". Averaging across the four slots destroys the signal.
- Not `grounded`. Nothing here is asserted as settled by a measurement taken in this derivation.

## 5. What would settle it

1. A per-slot measurement in a locality that does not pool fairness, harm, loyalty, and authority. Does the four-slot structure survive?
2. A habitat substitution in which actors, conditions, relations, and sanctions are independently measured. Does the judgment pattern move with them?
3. A source-discriminating reading of local records that names which of several candidate causes produced a shift, with provenance. That would close the stated `gap`.

## 6. What an expert can check in twenty minutes
