# WATSON answer, COG-0062

**Mode: `specified`, not `grounded`.** Figures are primary and decide. World sources are secondary, corroborate but never overrule, and a conflict returns the derivation to the domain census. This is a simulated derivation checked for internal consistency against the figures, not a ground reading.

## QUESTION

How do local histories and institutions shape judgments about fairness, harm, loyalty, and authority?

Restated as an effect: why does R = |Γ(b,a) - o'| vary systematically by locality, by institution, by historical moment, and by which of four object slots is in play? R is indexed per slot, never averaged (Fig 1 :19). Anchor: observed human judgment, because the question is constitutively about people (Fig 2 :37).

## DOMAIN CENSUS

Arity: at least four-place (person, act, local habitat, slot), ordered by historical time; carries an ordered bond and a comparison bond, not symmetric ones. Symmetry: fair and loyal are threshold judgments relative to a local baseline (Fig 12 :87). Scale: the single person's loop, the local habitat as a population with a ranking, and the intergenerational channel; the membrane between scales is lossy (Fig 4).

## TETHER (primary, verbatim, per domain)

**D1 Judging person, status triangulated (Fig 2 and Fig 11, anchor rule vs habitat valence).**

> *"An anchor is legitimate when the question is constitutively about it."* (Fig 2 :36)
> *"Human judgment is the right anchor for is this a good answer for a person, because the question is about people."* (Fig 2 :37)
> *"So the answerable question is not what intelligence is, but what makes a system capable. And capability is a property of agent-and-habitat, never of the agent alone."* (Fig 11 :39)

Reading: the ground is observed per-slot judgment; capability is not locatable in the person alone. Mode `specified`. Refuter: invariance across a measured habitat substitution, or an anchor that moves when pushed.

**D2 Local habitat, status triangulated (Fig 11 and Fig 4, habitat enumeration vs membrane).**

> *"The habitat is enumerated, never composed."* (Fig 11 :43)
> *"You do not invent the list. You read it off the world, and what you cannot perceive or measure yet is the residual."* (Fig 11 :48)
> *"Only methods cross up. Only head starts come down."* (Fig 4 :22)

Reading: the deliverable for a case is an enumerated list of actors, conditions, and relations, not a universal coefficient. Mode `general` for the method, `specified` for cases. Refuter: one scalar that predicts per-slot across independently enumerated habitats.

**D3 Institutional memory, status triangulated (Fig 6, Fig 8, Fig 13, distinct roles).**

> *"No frame escapes its own closure. The bound moves only on contact with a frame whose closure differs."* (Fig 6 :23)
> *"Convergent derivation and adopted import look identical in the contents. Only the record of where each came from separates them"* (Fig 8 :51)
> *"The set is closed. The arrangements are not. That gap is why import exists."* (Fig 13 :115)

Reading: new judgmental reach arrives through contact with a differing closure, and provenance separates local derivation from adoption. Mode `specified`. Refuter: new judgments with no such contact.

**D4 Intergenerational channel, status triangulated (Fig 7 and Fig 2, room chain vs coherent drift).**

> *"agreement compounds, evidence does not"* (Fig 7 :13)
> *"A chain of mirrors is one long room. The next room must differ from the last, and match the residual it surfaced."* (Fig 7 :36)
> *"everything drifts together, and the drift is coherent, which is why nobody notices"* (Fig 2 :34)

Reading: history transmits as handoffs; repeated closure produces coherent drift. Mode `specified`. Refuter: same closure for generations plus new ground-verified discriminations with no import.

**Union, valence, and method, status triangulated (Fig 9, Fig 1, Fig 10, Fig 12).**

> *"A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones."* (Fig 9 :55)
> *"The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated."* (Fig 1 :19)
> *"An actor's valence is relative to the residual, not a property it carries."* (Fig 11 :49)
> *"a convention that decides an outcome has stopped being one"* (Fig 10 :47)
> *"isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."* (Fig 12 :117)

Reading: four slots, union compounds, no standing institutional valence, codes as conventions, isomer reading for same atoms arranged differently. Mode `general` and `specified`.

Fig 9 :32: *"you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself."* Fig 13 :134: *"Unreachable is a verdict here, not a silence"* and :135 *"It cannot say which primitive is missing, because naming it would mean already holding it."* Fig 8 :39: *"So a search that finds nothing is never a proof of absence."* The missing thing is a source-discriminating instrument, characterized in effect terms but not held.

## WORLD (secondary, each source read; field, author/venue/year, quote, prediction, corroborates|conflicts)

**Evidence pool A, cross-cultural moral psychology (one witness, not three).**

- **Cross-cultural moral psychology.** Jonathan Haidt, Silvia Helena Koller and Maria G. Dias, *Affect, culture, and morality, or is it wrong to eat your dog?*, Journal of Personality and Social Psychology 65(4), 1993. Read via indexed full text and DOI record 2026-11-22. Quote: *"cultural norms and culturally shaped emotions have a substantial impact on the domain of morality and the process of moral judgment."* Prediction: local norms and culturally shaped emotion change which acts are moralized. **Corroborates** D1 and D2 at a different stratum. The paper also reports larger social-class differences within countries than between them, so "culture as one national variable" is disconfirmed.
- **Moral psychology / cultural psychology.** Jonathan Haidt and Jesse Graham, *When morality opposes justice*, Social Justice Research 20, 2007. Read via indexed full text 2026-11-22. Quote: *"from an anthropological perspective, the moral domain is usually much broader, encompassing many more aspects of social life and valuing institutions as much or more than individuals."* Prediction: institutions themselves are moral objects in many cultures. **Corroborates** the institutional-valence claim's premise. Same evidence pool as the 1993 paper.
- **Moral psychology.** Jesse Graham et al., *Moral Foundations Theory: The Pragmatic Validity of Moral Pluralism*, Advances in Experimental Social Psychology 47, 2013. ## VERDICT (from the figures)

The figure-derived verdict: judgments are a property of person-and-habitat; the habitat is enumerated from records; institutions change valence with the residual; history transmits as handoffs and imports, so repeated closure drifts and new reach requires a differing closure; the four slots stay separate as union compounds; the anchor is observed human judgment; declared codes are conventions until practice pays; and the specific historical cause is a declared `gap`. Verdicts are kept per domain, never averaged. Mode `specified`, ungrounded. Pool A corroborates the habitat shape, pool B corroborates persistence and institutions at low overlap. The North conflict was returned to census and re-mapped to the convention rule, not used to flip the verdict. Which institution caused which shift stays `gap` until a source-discriminating instrument exists.

- **cost if wrong:** misdirected case work. A locality failing the handoff condition would be sold import instruments, or a locality failing the contact condition would be told to reform its codes, and one lever would be applied to a union that needs two. In outreach terms, an expert's twenty minutes would be spent refuting a shape that was never the right shape. The cost is real but bounded and largely reversible.
- **undoable:** yes. No term is deleted if the claim fails. A refutation is added and the reach falls because *"something was added rather than because anything was deleted"* (Fig 6 :42). The shape and its six falsifiers are recorded with provenance so a later reader can locate the error (Fig 10 :62).
- **reversed by:** any of the six pre-registered falsifiers in `01` section 5, in particular (1) invariance of the four-slot pattern across a measured habitat substitution, (2) one scalar predicting per-slot across independently enumerated habitats, (3) invariant institutional valence across a change of residual, (4) same closure for generations plus new ground-verified discriminations with no import, (5) a code alone predicting judgments without practice, or (6) an anchor that moves when pushed. A source-discriminating instrument naming a cause would close the `gap` for that case rather than reverse the shape.
