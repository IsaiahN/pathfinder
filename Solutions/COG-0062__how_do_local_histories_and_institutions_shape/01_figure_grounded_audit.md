## 0. The residual in effect terms

The loop defines R = |Γ(b,a) - o'| per slot s (Formula step 1; Symbols table, `R`). Here:

- **b** is the person's belief about what a local situation affords morally. It is formed from private history and inherited priors, mixed by `w`: *"How far the belief leans on private history versus inherited prior. w_A + w_B = 1, and w is learnable from the outcomes of past weightings. Two frames with identical architecture diverge because their histories and their w diverge."* (Symbols table).
- **a** is the act of judgment: condemn, excuse, obey, disobey, distribute, punish.
- **Γ(b,a)** is what the person's library predicts they will judge, given what their locality taught them to see.
- **o'** is what the local ground returns: actual practice, sanctions, rulings, and the observed distribution of judgments in that community.
- **s** is the case slot. The question names four: **fairness, harm, loyalty, authority**.

> **"The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated."** (Fig 1 :19)
> **"The gap is measured per object, not per step. A system that averages across objects has thrown away the only signal that says where to look."** (Fig 1 :28)

The phenomenon to explain is not the *content* of any moral code but the **systematic variation of R across localities and across the four slots**. The same person moved between neighborhoods, institutions, or historical periods returns different o' for the same act description; and fairness judgments move differently from loyalty judgments. A frame that predicts all four slots move together, or that a person's pattern is invariant across a habitat substitution, has a residual here.

State the residual as: *why does the gap between what a person's local moral library predicts and what the local community actually returns differ systematically by place, by institution, by historical moment, and by which of four judgment slots is in play?* This is an effect description. It names no theory, no "culture", no "moral foundation", no "socialization". Those are candidate names and are withheld until the census.

**Bind the sought-for shape as an ordinary term** (Formula, "Direction"), before the run: the answer would be (i) an enumerated habitat, actors, conditions, relations, that makes R small prospectively and per slot; (ii) an instrument that discriminates a channel failure from a reach failure; and (iii) an unbundling of the four slots into union compounds. Until bound, that shape predicts nothing and no loop check can see it. It is now bound, so the refuter in section 5 can fire on it.

**Anchor test.** Fig 2 :29: *"An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks."* The same line names candidates: *"A measurement, an experiment, a counter, a market, a patient outcome."* Fig 2 :30: *"If it moves when you push on it, it is not an anchor."* For this question there is a decisive line:

> **"An anchor is legitimate when the question is constitutively about it."** (Fig 2 :36)
> **"Human judgment is the right anchor for is this a good answer for a person, because the question is about people."** (Fig 2 :37)

So the ground is **observed local judgment**, per case and per slot. It is not moral-philosophy consensus, not the researcher's preferred theory, not an institution's declared code, and not the prestige of the source. Those are instruments at best, and *"instruments can be wrong on axes they do not measure"* (Fig 2 :37). Viable anchor: the community's actually returned judgments and sanctions, measured per slot, which do not change because a theorist pushes on them.

## 1. Domain census (before choosing any figure line)

### 1.1 Arity, symmetry, scale of R

- **Arity.** R is at least four-place: person b, act a, local habitat H, and slot s (fairness / harm / loyalty / authority), ordered by historical time t. It is a sequence rather than a set, so it carries an **ordered** bond (`→`, sequence: *"A then B; B needs A to have happened"*, Fig 12 :70-73) and a **comparison** bond for "fair", "loyal", "excessive" (`⋛`, *"a threshold relation between two quantities"*, Fig 12 :85-89). It is not a symmetric relation: history shapes the judgment, not the reverse at the same stratum.
- **Symmetry.** "Fair" and "loyal" are thresholds relative to a local baseline, not absolute quantities (Fig 12 :85-89; Operators table :46-51). History is asymmetric in time: a lost institution is not recovered by reversing the act that removed it.
- **Scale.** Three scales are in view: the single person's judgment loop (Fig 4 one-agent panel); the local habitat, a population with *"a shared library, and something that ranks what they produce"* (Fig 4 :5); and the intergenerational time channel. The membrane between scales is *"Not a wall: a filter with a direction"* (Fig 4 :21), and *"Only methods cross up. Only head starts come down."* (Fig 4 :22). The transform is lossy, so the round-trip gap R_T must be measured (Fig 4 :30). This is the first figure-grounded hit on "local history": what crosses down is a **head start**, not a recording. *"A recording carried upward looks like knowledge and is a description of one occasion."* (Fig 4 :25).

### 1.2 One line per domain: actors, currency, anchor

A domain is admitted only if an anchor can be named. Where none can be named, it is not a domain yet.

| domain | actors | currency | anchor, nameable? | status |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **D1 The judging person** (one agent, one scale) | the individual, their Γ, b, w | acts and residual reduction | yes: observed judgment on a case; human judgment is the legitimate anchor for a question about people (Fig 2 :37) | admitted |
| **D2 The local habitat** (community and history) | kin, peers, courts, councils, guilds, temples, schools; conditions such as scarcity, risk, terrain, season; relations such as dependency, exchange, descent, authority | sanction, survival, legitimacy, trust | yes: the community's actual practice and sanctions; the habitat is read off the world (Fig 11 :48) | admitted |
| **D3 The institutional memory** (composition inside the culture) | atoms: stories, precedents, norms, roles; bonds between them | reach, that is what can be justified; cost |φ| of a justification | yes: settlement, *"then check it against the ground, then carry it up"* (Fig 5 :32) | admitted |
| **D4 The intergenerational channel** (across centuries) | transmitters: parents, teachers, officials, rituals, records | contact with the ground | yes: the ground, *"the ground does not decay, the channel does"* (Fig 10 :24) | admitted |
| **X1 A free-floating moral faculty alone** | the innate foundations considered in isolation | purity of intuition | **no** anchor independent of the habitat's verdict; an agent-only explanation that may not change contact (Fig 11 :40) | not a domain on its own; absorbed into D1 as operand |
| **X2 "Culture" as one monolithic cause** | the culture | coherence | already D2 + D3 + D4; a bundled name, not a separate domain | union, unbundle before search |
| **X3 Universal foundations as the sole cause** | the candidate universal kind-set | universality | measurable, and the kind-set is closed, but the arrangements are not (Fig 13 :115-118), so the kinds alone cannot close R | admitted only as the closed kind-set inside D1, not as a verdict domain |

X1 fails the anchor test as a *sufficient* explanation and is excluded before any figure line is applied to it. This is a structural exclusion, not a claim that innate predispositions never matter. X3 is kept as the closed kinds inside D1, which is exactly the role Fig 13 gives to a minimum set: *"The set is closed. The arrangements are not. That gap is why import exists."* (Fig 13 :115).

### 1.3 Partition or union

D1 and D3 overlap: the person's Γ is a local instance of the institutional memory. D2 and D4 overlap: the channel is exercised inside the habitat. The four judgment slots overlap: a case can be a fairness violation and a loyalty violation at once (nepotism), or an authority command and a harm at once (an order to injure). The whole question is a **union**, not a partition.

> **"And when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again."** (Fig 9 :54)
> **"A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones."** (Fig 9 :55)
> **"What remains is smaller and it is also categorically different: a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either."** (Fig 9 :57)

The unbundled conditions are: (C1) a maintained channel to a local ground that still pays; (C2) renewal of reach through contact with a closure that differs. A community failing only C1 and one failing only C2 are not different degrees of one failure; they are different compounds and need different instruments. *The Library of Babel* states the same stop condition: *"Perhaps your problem was a bundle or conflation of several problems masquerading as one?"*

### 1.4 Dependency order (Fig 3)

> **"The chain is ordered by dependency, not by importance."** (Fig 3 :20)
> **"Why the middle link is where chains usually break."** (Fig 3 :24)
> **"A link with no instrument is not a link that works. It is a link nobody has looked at."** (Fig 3 :27)

Order for this question: 1 perception (can the person read the local response to a judgment?) -> 2 vocabulary (does their Γ have terms for what the local situation affords: "honour", "face", "convention", "duty", "rights"?) -> 3 objective (can they say what would count as a fair or loyal judgment here, in local terms?) -> 4 planning -> 5 learn and carry. The middle link, having terms for what the local situation affords and saying what would count as progress, is the one with no instrument. This is the dependency answer to "how": the local history supplies the vocabulary and the objective, not just new content. *Decoding Tacit Knowledge* names the missing term as **pretext**, the history the listener brings before the words arrive: *"Writing carries the context well and the subtext partly, but it almost never carries the pretext, the 'you had to be there' part."* Most diagnosis effort lands above the break because that is where the measurements already exist (Fig 3 :29-30).

## 3. Findings

Mode tag on each: `general` if structural, `specified` if a falsifiable prediction about localities, `grounded` only if settled by a measurement. None is `grounded`.

### F1: Judgments are a property of person-and-habitat, not of the person alone

**Mode: `specified`. Domains D1, D2. Status: triangulated (Fig 11 and Fig 4, low overlap: habitat vs membrane).**

> **"So the answerable question is not what intelligence is, but what makes a system capable. And capability is a property of agent-and-habitat, never of the agent alone."** (Fig 11 :39)
> **"A system cannot be made more capable by improving the agent in isolation. An improvement that does not change contact changes nothing, however much it improves."** (Fig 11 :40)
> **"it or a thousand. Only the actors, the currency and the anchor change."** (Fig 4 :20)

If the question is "why does this locality return this judgment", the answer cannot be a property of the person considered alone. The habit of answering with a list of individual traits (empathy, moral reasoning stage, political orientation) is an agent-only improvement that may not change contact. What changes contact is the habitat: who sanctions, what is scarce, which relations bind. The kernel, the person's loop, is the same at one and a thousand; only actors, currency, and anchor change (Fig 4 :19-20). Fig 11 :72 gives the standing refuter in the figure's own words: an agent-only improvement that moved the ground would refute the capability claim.

**Refuter:** a documented case in which a community's pattern across all four slots changes while the enumerated habitat (actors, conditions, relations, sanctions) is independently measured as held constant and only individual beliefs are changed. If it survives, F1 is false.

**World bearing (secondary only):** Haidt, Koller and Dias 1993 report that *"cultural norms and culturally shaped emotions have a substantial impact on the domain of morality and the process of moral judgment"* (abstract). That is the same shape at the cross-cultural stratum. It is one witness, not a proof, and its samples are WEIRD-weighted. North 1993 supplies a low-overlap witness from institutional history: *"Institutions are the humanly devised constraints that structure human interaction"* (Nobel lecture). Conflict check: North also says formal rules are often created to serve those with bargaining power, which strains a neutral "convention" reading. That conflict is returned to the census and re-mapped to F8, where Fig 10 :47 disqualifies a convention that decides an outcome. The verdict is not flipped by the conflict.

### F2: The habitat must be enumerated, never composed; local history is pretext, not a coefficient

**Mode: `general` with a `specified` consequence for case work. Domains all. Status: triangulated (Fig 11 and Fig 9, low overlap: habitat enumeration vs effect-term description).**

> **"The habitat is enumerated, never composed."** (Fig 11 :43)
> **"List everything in contact with the residual, then what is in contact with those, and outward until the cascade stops mattering."** (Fig 11 :44)
> **"You do not invent the list. You read it off the world, and what you cannot perceive or measure yet is the residual."** (Fig 11 :48)
> **"your words are borrowed too, so describe the effect rather than the cause"** (Fig 9 :30-31)

The correct output of this question is not a universal coefficient for "culture" or "institutions" but an enumerated habitat for the specific locality: actors (who judges, who sanctions, who is trusted), conditions (scarcity, risk, terrain), relations (dependency, exchange, authority, descent), then what contacts those, outward until the cascade stops mattering. This is why institutional and cultural history research is the right contact field: it reads the list off records rather than composing a general theory. The essay *Decoding Tacit Knowledge* names the same object as pretext: the history a listener brings before the words arrive, which text almost never carries.

**Refuter:** a single general formula over a fixed small set of variables that prospectively predicts, per case and per slot, which locally enumerated habitat will return which judgment, across habitats enumerated independently of the formula. If one exists, enumeration is not necessary. A variance decomposition does not qualify: *"Use filters for the budget and witnesses for the verdict. Never let a filter hand you a verdict."* (Fig 9 :60).

### F3: An institution's valence is relative to the residual, not a property it carries

**Mode: `general` for the relation, `specified` for named institutions. Domains D2, D3. Status: triangulated (Fig 11 and Fig 12, low overlap: habitat relations vs composition structures).**

> **"An actor's valence is relative to the residual, not a property it carries."** (Fig 11 :49)
> **"The same actor is a benefactor to one residual and a malfactor to another, so mutualism and parasitism are relations rather than kinds."** (Fig 11 :50)
> **"No standing list of good actors survives a change of residual."** (Fig 11 :51)
> **"catalyst. A part that survives its own use. Spent and regenerating parts behave differently across uses, and nothing records which a term is."** (Fig 12 :119)

A council, chief, court, temple, or family is not intrinsically fairness-producing or authority-corrupting. Its sign changes when the residual changes, and nothing in its description records which it currently is. This is the figure-grounded reason the question cannot be answered by a standing list of "benevolent" versus "oppressive" institutions. A catalyst that regenerates across uses and a spent part that does not behave differently, and the label on the institution does not tell you which. It bears directly on loyalty and authority: the same chief is protector under one residual and predator under another.

**Refuter:** any institution whose measured valence is invariant across a documented change of the local residual on all four slots. If no such change of valence can be found anywhere, F3 weakens to `general`.

### F4: A tradition is a room handed along; agreement inside it compounds while evidence does not

**Mode: `specified`. Domains D2, D4. Status: triangulated (Fig 7 and Fig 2, low overlap: selection between rooms vs anchor triangulation).**

> **"same closure, handed along;"** (Fig 7 :12) **"agreement compounds, evidence does not"** (Fig 7 :13)
> **"A room's agreement is variation, not a verdict. Selection between rooms is where the ground votes."** (Fig 7 :35)
> **"A chain of mirrors is one long room. The next room must differ from the last, and match the residual it surfaced."** (Fig 7 :36)
> **"everything drifts together, and the drift is coherent, which is why nobody notices"** (Fig 2 :34)

Local history is not just stored content; it is a sequence of handoffs. If the next generation or room only repeats the same closure (the same stories, the same authorities, the same verdicts), then agreement compounds while evidence does not, and the community drifts together without noticing, because every correction lands easily and every frame confirms the others. That is the mechanism by which history shapes judgment without anyone intending it. The two-factor test applies: *"Progress per handoff has two factors. Zero either and the chain runs in place:"* (Fig 7 :37), namely *"the bracket's fidelity across the handoff"* (Fig 7 :38) and *"the ground's pressure on the room"* (Fig 7 :39).

**Refuter:** a community that keeps the same closure across multiple generations and still produces new ground-verified discriminations on all four slots without any import. Under Fig 7 :36 that should be impossible; if observed, F4 is wrong.

**World bearing:** North 1993 gives a low-overlap institutional-history instance: *"It is culture that provides the key to path dependence"* and *"The current learning of any generation takes place within the context of the perceptions derived from collective learning."* Predicts that a locality's past rules persist after the conditions that produced them change. Corroborates F4 and the maintenance half of F1. Different evidence pool from the moral-psychology papers, so it counts as a second witness for the mechanism shape, not for the specific cognitive claim.

### F5: Only contact with a differing closure adds judgmental reach

**Mode: `specified`. Domain D3, D2. Status: triangulated (Fig 6, Fig 8, Fig 13, three figures with distinct roles).**

> **"the only operator that moves the wall"** (Fig 6 :14)
> **"MINT composes inside the closure and can never add an atom."** (Fig 6 :21)
> **"No frame escapes its own closure. The bound moves only on contact with a frame whose closure differs."** (Fig 6 :23)
> **"Closure is idempotent. Composing inside a closed set never leaves it, so no search, however long, adds an atom. This one is definitional: it is what closure means."** (Fig 8 :32)
> **"The set is closed. The arrangements are not. That gap is why import exists."** (Fig 13 :115)

When a locality meets traders, migrants, conquerors, reformers, or another moral code, new judgments become reachable (a new notion of fairness, a new scope for loyalty) through that contact, not by rethinking inside the old closure. So "local history" is partly a history of imports, and the historian's source gate is part of the answer: *"Convergent derivation and adopted import look identical in the contents. Only the record of where each came from separates them"* (Fig 8 :51). Fig 8 :53 puts the order: **"Residual first, frame second."** Describe the change in o' before naming the foreign frame that caused it.

**Refuter:** a community that expands its repertoire of moral judgments on all four slots purely by internal reflection, with no contact with a differing closure, across a documented period. That breaks idempotence and F5 with it.

### F6: The four slots are a union compound; never average them into one moral score

**Mode: `general` for indexing, `specified` for the four-slot claim. Domains all. Status: triangulated (Fig 1 and Fig 9, low overlap: per-slot residual vs union unbundling).**

> **"The ground enters as the only metric."** (Fig 1 :23)
> **"They are frame-internal. A frame cannot score itself with a quantity it also produces."** (Fig 1 :27)
> **"Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average."** (Fig 1 :22)
> **"a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either"** (Fig 9 :57)

Fairness, harm, loyalty, and authority are four object slots. A case engaging two of them (nepotism, an order to injure) is a specific compound with its own loop, not a harder case of either parent slot. A single "moral orientation" score averages away the only signal that says where to look. A survey scale or a cultural index that reports one number per society is a budget filter, not a witness (Fig 9 :60). The four named slots map onto the moral-foundation kinds reported by Graham et al. 2013, but mapping the kinds is not deriving the arrangements, which is why F6 is about indexing rather than about which foundations exist.

**Refuter:** a single scalar over a fixed variable set that predicts prospectively and per slot which judgment a local community will return, with no loss of per-slot signal. If one exists, F6's methodological consequence is false.

### F7: The ground for this question is human judgment, because the question is constitutively about people

**Mode: `general`. Domain D1, all. Status: triangulated (Fig 2 and the Seat Map anchor rule, distinct statements).**

> **"An anchor is legitimate when the question is constitutively about it."** (Fig 2 :36)
> **"Human judgment is the right anchor for is this a good answer for a person, because the question is about people."** (Fig 2 :37)
> **"If it moves when you push on it, it is not an anchor."** (Fig 2 :30)
> **"Alignment is triangulation, not negotiation. A mirror cannot correct you."** (Fig 2 :35)

The ground is observed local judgment, per case and per slot. It is not agreement among moral philosophers, not an institution's self-description, not a prestige ranking, and not the researcher's theory. Any of those that moves when a frame pushes on it is an instrument or another frame, not an anchor. This is the one place the question's subject matter settles the anchoring question directly.

**Refuter:** a case where the proposed local ground changes because a theorist, court, or funder pushed on it. Then it moved when pushed and was not an anchor.

### F8: A declared institutional norm is a convention until the ground pays; a convention that decides an outcome has stopped being one

**Mode: `general` plus `specified` for the historian's method. Domains D3, D2. Status: triangulated (Fig 10 and Fig 2, low overlap: convention/verdict seam vs anchor guard).**

> **"a declared meaning is not a grounded one"** (Fig 10 :18)
> **"→ install what can be violated"** (Fig 10 :19)
> **"a convention that decides an outcome has stopped being one"** (Fig 10 :47)
> **"Guard, from Figure 10: a convention that decides an outcome has stopped being one."** (Fig 2 :43)

Local institutions publish codes about fairness, harm, loyalty, and authority. Those codes are conventions: shared meanings that make coordination possible and are useful precisely because they make no claim about the world. The failure occurs when a convention is used to settle the verdict about whether a judgment was fair. At that moment it has become a ground that someone benefits from, which the arrangement disqualifies. The historian's job is to range the code against practice separately, not to read the code as the answer. This is the re-census destination for North's conflict in F1.

**Refuter:** evidence that a community's written code alone, with no independent reading of practice, prospectively predicts its judgments per slot. Then the convention is behaving as a ground, contradicting Fig 10 :47.

### F9: Which specific local history caused which judgment is a declared `gap`

**Mode: `general`. Status: `gap` after a genuine re-census.**

> **"you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself"** (Fig 9 :32)
> **"Unreachable is a verdict here, not a silence"** (Fig 13 :134)
> **"It cannot say which primitive is missing, because naming it would mean already holding it."** (Fig 13 :135)
> **"So a search that finds nothing is never a proof of absence."** (Fig 8 :39)

The framework returns the shape: enumerate the habitat, keep the four slots separate, use human judgment as the anchor, expect imports from differing closures, and keep provenance. It does not return the name of the specific institution that changed a specific judgment in a specific century. Naming one is a source attribution that requires an instrument capable of discriminating among sources of the same reading. From *The Hard Problem is About Source*: *"the instrument is speaking only about what it measured, it cannot comment on the source."* What is owed is that instrument, not a confident name.

**Refuter:** a source-discriminating instrument that identifies, with provenance, which imported or local actor produced a change in a named slot in a documented case. Then the gap closes for that case. Until then, the honest report is unreached, not unreachable.

### F10: Prestige and institutional eminence are not verdicts

**Mode: `general` with a `specified` consequence for outreach. Domains all. Status: triangulated (Fig 2 and the essays, but the essay shares the author with the figures, so the independent witness count is one).**

> **"disqualifying for a verdict: agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool"** (Fig 2 :40)
> **"A verifier that can reconstruct the claim cannot verify it."** (Fig 10 :42)

From *The Recursive Self-Improvement Trap*: *"Prestige can fund an action. It can give an action a budget to be tried. But it cannot settle an action. Only the ground can settle it."* A renowned court, a canonical philosopher, or a famous department can get the claim read; none can settle whether a locality's judgments changed. The Seat Map adds: *"an interest in the outcome is unavoidable, an interest in the reading is disqualifying."* So the checking venues must be public and opt-in, and the first contact carries the residual and its refuter, never this folder's interior.

**Refuter:** a documented case where the prestige of an institution, with no measured change in practice or contact, prospectively changes the local judgment pattern on all four slots. Then the rule fails in this domain.

## 5. Pre-registered refuter (written before the conclusion)

Required before any outreach, per the Seat Map :287: *"And a falsifier written before the run is a design instrument, not only an honesty device."*

The shape claim is refuted if any of the following holds:

1. A person's judgment pattern on all four slots is invariant across a documented habitat substitution in which actors, conditions, relations, and sanctions are independently measured as changed and contact is maintained (breaks F1, Fig 11 :40).
2. A single general scalar over a fixed small variable set prospectively predicts per-slot judgments across habitats enumerated independently of the formula, with no loss of per-slot signal (breaks F2 and F6).
3. An institution's valence is invariant across a documented change of the local residual on all four slots (breaks F3, Fig 11 :49-51).
4. A community keeps the same closure across multiple generations and still produces new ground-verified discriminations on all four slots with no import (breaks F4 and F5, Fig 7 :36, Fig 8 :32).
5. A community's written code alone, with no independent reading of practice, prospectively predicts its judgments per slot (breaks F8, Fig 10 :47).
6. The proposed anchor moves when a theorist, court, or funder pushes on it (breaks F7, Fig 2 :30).
