### Q2 - Are fairness, harm, loyalty, and authority atoms or bonds?

- Fig 12 :94: **"The arrangement is the substance."**
- Fig 12 :111: **"A vocabulary without bonds can name a state and cannot name a route to one."**
- A list of institutions names what a locality contains. It cannot name what would count as getting somewhere in a moral dispute. The missing half is the bond.
- Reading of the four slots as candidate functional groups (Fig 12 :124: *"functional group. A substructure whose presence fixes the outcome. The group says what kind; the qualifier says which member."*):
  - **harm** is centred on a subtraction (`−`, *"A with B removed or absent"*, Fig 12 :80-83): the victim without the good;
  - **fairness** is centred on a comparison (`⋛`, *"a threshold relation between two quantities"*, Fig 12 :85-89): more or less, owed or not owed;
  - **loyalty** is centred on a sequence (`→`, *"A then B; B needs A to have happened"*, Fig 12 :70-73): an allegiance that had to be formed before betrayal is possible;
  - **authority** is centred on a production (`⇒`, *"A produces B; B did not exist before"*, Fig 12 :74-79): a command that brings an obligation into being.
- These are **candidate** groupings, `specified` and unmeasured. They are a structural hypothesis about which typed relation dominates each slot, not a claim that the words mean only that. Mode `specified`. Single-source for the figure mapping; the cross-cultural literature corroborates the existence of the four concerns, not the bond assignment.

### Q3 - Are two localities isomers?

- Fig 12 :117: **"isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."**
- If two localities hold the same institutions (same atoms) but arrange them under different dominant bonds, for example one treats the council as producing obligation and the other as comparing claims, then they are isomers under Fig 12: an institution list cannot tell them apart, and their judgments differ decisively.
- The weaker reading: localities with different institutions but the same dominant bond share a **functional group** (Fig 12 :124). That is why a universal "authority" variable can travel between societies and still mislead: the group says what kind, the qualifier says which member.
- **Falsifier, written before the run:** if two localities can be shown to share both the atom set and the bond arrangement and still return different per-slot judgments with the same ground pressure, the isomer/functional-group claim fails. Equally, if no locality's judgment pattern can be related to a bond arrangement at all, the bond hypothesis fails and only the enumeration half of F2 stands.

### Q4 - What order is the union?

- Fig 9 :54: **"And when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again."** Fig 9 :57: **"a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either."**
- The four slots overlap. Nepotism is fairness plus loyalty; an order to injure is authority plus harm. Each is a specific compound with its own loop, not a harder case of one parent slot.
- Fig 3 :20: **"The chain is ordered by dependency, not by importance."** Order: 1 perception (can the person read the local response?) -> 2 vocabulary (are there terms for honour, face, duty, rights?) -> 3 objective (what would count as progress here?) -> 4 planning -> 5 learn and carry.

- Formula step 2: refuse before mint. The claim that a universal faculty alone explains the pattern is a REFUTED bin candidate, not a novel term to mint, because Fig 11 :40 already refuses agent-only explanations that do not change contact.

## CONCLUSION

The four slots are best treated as **candidate functional groups** over a locally enumerated atom set, with a dominant typed bond each, `specified` and unmeasured. Two localities with the same institutions arranged differently are **isomers** in Fig 12's sense: the difference is invisible in an institution list and decisive in behavior. The union must be unbundled before a lever is chosen, and the dependency order puts vocabulary and objective (links 2 and 3) before planning and learning, which is where local history actually enters. Naming an institution as present is required enumeration; naming it as causal is a `gap` until a source-discriminating instrument exists.

This framing keeps what is defensible without pretending to a derivation of specific causes. No new primitive is needed, the agent-only explanation is refused in the correct bin before minting, and the bond assignment is a checkable hypothesis with a stated falsifier.
