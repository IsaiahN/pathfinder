**Use:** one-to-one, or as a cover letter attached to a submission through the public venues in `03_public_contact_points.csv`. **Not** for bulk send.

**Hard precondition:** do not send until the four-slot indexing and the anchor statement in `01_figure_grounded_audit.md` sections 0 and 3 are understood as `specified`, not `grounded`. The output must survive validation or the two-stage offer collapses on contact.

**Subject:** Check one output about local moral judgment before you decide whether the account is worth reading

> Hi [name],
>
> I have one specific output I would like you to check against what you already know. I am not asking you to read the theory first, and I am not asking you to agree with it. If the output does not survive, there is nothing else to look at.
>
> **The output (`specified`, not `grounded`):** the question is how a locality's history and institutions change judgments about fairness, harm, loyalty, and authority. The framework I am using says those are four separate object slots, not four faces of one moral score. Its residual is indexed per slot, so a single "moral culture" number throws away the signal that says where to look. It also says the proper ground here is observed human judgment, because the question is constitutively about people, and that a declared institutional code is a convention until practice settles it.
>
> The shape it returns is this: a person's judgments are a property of person-and-habitat, not of the person alone; the habitat has to be enumerated from records (actors, conditions, relations), not composed from a general formula; an institution's valence changes when the local situation changes; and a tradition is a chain of handoffs and imports rather than a stored recording. It does not name which institution caused which judgment. That part stays open until an instrument can discriminate sources.
>
> **What I am not claiming:** that this is a universal moral psychology, that it replaces cross-cultural research, that any named institution is the cause, that the four foundations are the framework's six-term substrate, or that the account is settled. It returns the shape an answer must have, not the answer.
>
> **What would refute the shape:**
> 1. a person's judgment pattern on all four slots stays the same when the local habitat (actors, conditions, relations, sanctions) is independently measured as changed;
> 2. one general scalar predicts per-slot judgments across independently enumerated habitats with no loss of per-slot signal;
> 3. a community keeps the same closure for generations yet keeps producing new ground-verified discriminations with no import;
> 4. a written code alone, with no reading of practice, predicts the judgments.
>
> I wrote these down before sending this. If any one holds, I will withdraw or narrow the claim. A one-line "this fails because..." is more useful to me than agreement. If you would rather not engage, no reply is needed.
>
> **If it survives, and only then:** the sources are below. The figures are the authority, not my summary of them.
> - Figures and formula for direct download (SVG, branch `general-agent`): https://github.com/IsaiahN/tether/tree/general-agent/figures
> - Worked architecture essay: https://adventuresinml.substack.com/p/arrival-of-the-fittest-the-tether
> - On source discrimination: https://adventuresinml.substack.com/p/the-hard-problem-is-about-source
>
> **If you want to use the machinery:** the formula is in the figures link. Run it on a locality you know well and can check against a ground. Write down what would refute your result *before* you run it, and state the mode of what you get. A reported failure is more useful to me than a reported success.
>
> - Isaiah
