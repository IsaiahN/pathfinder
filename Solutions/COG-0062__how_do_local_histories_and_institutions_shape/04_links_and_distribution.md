## What the wider literature bears on the question

1. **Cross-cultural moral psychology is the primary field witness.** Haidt, Koller and Dias 1993 found that *"cultural norms and culturally shaped emotions have a substantial impact on the domain of morality and the process of moral judgment"*, and that social-class differences within a country could exceed national differences. That corroborates the habitat shape (F1) at a different stratum and warns against treating "culture" as one national variable. Haidt and Graham 2007 note that *"from an anthropological perspective, the moral domain is usually much broader, encompassing many more aspects of social life and valuing institutions as much or more than individuals"*, which is a direct corroboration of F3's institution-valued claim. Graham et al. 2013 add that *"Cultures select areas of human potential that fit with their social structure, economic system, and cultural traditions."* All three share an evidence pool and an author lineage, so they are **one witness**, not three. Their samples are also Westernized/Internet-access segments, which the source itself caveats.

2. **Institutional history is the low-overlap witness.** North 1993 supplies the non-psychological frame: institutions as constraints, organizations as players, and culture as the carrier of path dependence. It corroborates F1, F4, and the persistence half of F5. It also supplies the one honest conflict in this folder: North describes formal rules as serving those with bargaining power, which strains a neutral convention reading. The conflict was sent back to the census and re-mapped to F8, where Fig 10 :47 disqualifies a convention that decides an outcome. The verdict did not flip.

3. **Comparative survey work is a weak witness.** The WVS site reports long-run Latin American work linking unfairness evaluations with institutional trust. It corroborates the need to keep fairness separate from authority (F6) at the survey level, but a programme blurb is not a finding and it is silent on mechanism. It is recorded, not leaned on.

4. **Framework essays are canonical reading, not world witnesses.** All eight are by the framework's author. Their agreement with the figures is expected and carries no independent weight.

## Distribution: offer, do not push

Figure-grounded reasons:

- **Fig 4 :22-25** - *"Only methods cross up. Only head starts come down. A recording carried upward looks like knowledge and is a description of one occasion."* Sending the full architecture before anyone has described a residual sends a recording, not a generator. Lead with the four-slot claim and its refuter; offer the links after.
- **Fig 10 :42** - *"A verifier that can reconstruct the claim cannot verify it."* Seat 4 holds a different vocabulary and nothing else. A recipient who has absorbed the whole framework before being asked for a verdict has joined the frame being checked.
- **Fig 9 :27-28** - *"describe it before you go looking, or any frame you pick will seem to fit."* Ask what they would expect to see in a locality whose history had shaped the four slots differently, before giving them the architecture.
- **Fig 2 :40** - *"disqualifying for a verdict: agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool"* A mass send selected for people who already agree produces a convention, not corroboration.

**Recommended order in a reply:** (1) the four-slot shape, the anchor, and the refuter; (2) ask what they would count as a failure in their own field; (3) record the verdict; (4) only then the links, with the note that the figures, not this summary, are the authority.

## The two-stage offer

The proposition is: **(1)** validate the output against the ground, **(2)** only if curious, read the theory that produced it, **(3)** use the formula recursively on other problems. This order follows from the figures:

- **Stage 1 before stage 2** is Fig 10 :52 - *"Bring a characterised residual, never the source"* - and Fig 2 :45 - *"Frames range against the ground. They do not negotiate with it."* The recipient checks the output without holding the framework's interior.
- **Stage 2 is opt-in by construction.** Seat 4 must not hold the interiors. Once a recipient has absorbed the theory, their agreement stops being evidence. So their stage-1 verdict is recorded *before* any theory is shown, and later agreement is never pooled as corroboration.
- **Stage 3 sends a method, which is permitted.** Fig 4 :22 - *"Only methods cross up. Only head starts come down."* The formula is a generator, so giving it away does not give away an answer.
- **Stage 3 still needs a ground per problem.** `THE_FORMULA.md` "Direction": a frame *"cannot check the direction of its own question, because the shape a frame is searching for is not a term it holds."* Each user must write what would refute their result before running it, and label the mode of what they get.

**Hard precondition:** stages 1-3 only work if the stage-1 output survives. That is why the four falsifiers are in the first contact and the theory is not.
