# The Tether's answer: should inflation have produced a detectable primordial gravitational wave background?

**Mode: `specified` on the shape and the union reading, `general` on the structural rule that detection is a comparison. Not `grounded`.** The published bound `r < 0.036` is reported from the literature as a ground reading; nothing in this answer is a measurement made by this simulation.

## 1. The residual it answers

An instrument has a sensitivity threshold. A sky may or may not carry a tensor perturbation background above it. CMB polarisation analyses return an upper limit rather than a detection. The question merges three conditions that have different anchors: did inflation generate tensors, is the amplitude above the threshold, and can the instrument resolve it against foregrounds, lensing and systematics. ## 2. The shape

1. **Inflation licenses a tensor background, but does not name its amplitude.** Fig 13 :20: "ordering, so more and less can be said" (spectrum). Fig 13 :14: "conserved, finite, directionless alone" (energy). Energy scale sets the amplitude, the spectrum makes it scale dependent. BK18 states that inflation "makes an additional prediction which has not yet been observed: a background of tensor perturbations". This is a `specified` prediction, not a detection and not a verdict that inflation occurred.
2. **Detectability is a threshold comparison, not an existence claim.** Fig 12 :87: "a threshold relation between two quantities". Bind the quantities: `r` (tensor amplitude at a pivot scale) and `sigma(r)` (instrument sensitivity at that scale and sky fraction). Detection is `r ⋛ sigma(r)` with null tests and multi-frequency foreground separation. Fig 12 :89 gives the identifying test: "is it about which is larger, rather than both being present?"
3. **A null result is not an absence.** Fig 8 :39: "So a search that finds nothing is never a proof of absence." Fig 9 :44: "Only a sealed room can be searched to the end, and no frame is a sealed room. So searching and finding nothing proves nothing." Fig 6 :27: "Which is why some unreachability is depth and some is genuine absence, and from inside the frame the two look the same." The honest report is "unreached at this budget", with the reach stated.
4. **Budget and principle are different facts.** Fig 8 :40-43: "Underivable: go outside. Unreachable-at-budget: the term is already in the closure and was not found, so the remedy is more search, not another atom." The tensor spectrum is already representable. A new inflation model invented to fit the bound adds an atom that was not needed.
5. **The channel, not the ground, is what decays.** Fig 10 :24: "the ground does not decay, the channel does." Dust, synchrotron, E-to-B leakage, lensing sample variance and systematics are channel failures. BK18 reports lensing sample variance dominating `sigma(r)`; LiteBIRD 2023 says delensing is critical. Maintaining the channel is the legitimate INWARD work.
6. **Per slot, never averaged.** Fig 1 :19: "The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated." BICEP3 95 GHz, Keck 150 and 220 GHz, Planck low-ell, direct detectors and pulsar timing arrays have distinct reaches. One pooled "no detection" throws away the signal that says where to look.
7. **Primordial and lensing B modes are isomers.** Fig 12 :117: "isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour." They share the BB observable; they differ in source and in delensing response. A spectrometer-equivalent operation, not a new theory, is what separates them.

## 3. What it explicitly does not say

- Not that inflation is ruled out. The bound excludes some large-field models at a stated pivot scale, not inflation as such.
- Not that no primordial gravitational waves exist. Non-detection is a bound (F2, F3 of `01_figure_grounded_audit.md`).
- Not that a background has been detected. No B-mode spectrum with `r > 0` survives the foreground and null tests in the sources read.
- Not a value of `r`, not a forecast promoted to evidence. Fig 9 :60: "Use filters for the budget and witnesses for the verdict. Never let a filter hand you a verdict." CMB-S4's forecast remains a budget benchmark after its 2025 defunding, not evidence about the sky.
- Not `grounded`. The figures do not settle this question, and this derivation is a simulation, not a ground reading.

## 4. What would settle it: the ground

A source-discriminating measurement, not a further argument. From *The Hard Problem is About Source*: a green reading "cannot comment on the source"; the spectrometer resolved it.

- **Threshold Test:** a B-mode spectrum with `r > 0` at a stated pivot, surviving multi-frequency foreground separation and null tests and not attributable to lensing. That settles `r ⋛ sigma(r)` in favour of detection and measures the energy scale of inflation.
- **Channel Test:** show that `sigma(r)` is limited by a maintained channel rather than by a systematic that moves with analysis choices. BK18's internal consistency tests and variable-analysis checks are the current instance.
- **Isomer Test:** demonstrate by frequency scaling and delensing that primordial and lensing B modes are different substances under the same BB formula, or, conversely, that no instrument at any frequency can separate them (which would fire the falsifier in `06_isomers_and_order.md` Q3).
- **Absence Test:** exhibit an instrument whose closure covers every predicted tensor amplitude at the relevant scale with a demonstrably maintained channel, and which still returns no signal. Only that converts "unreached" into "absent".
- **Standing figure refuters:** derive one of the six terms from the other five; show detection is a symmetric bond rather than `⋛`; show the published bound is not an anchor because it moves when pushed (Fig 2 :29-30, "If it moves when you push on it, it is not an anchor").

## 6. One-paragraph version for outreach

> The Tether does not say inflation is ruled out, and it does not say a primordial gravitational wave background has been found. It says the question is three conditions, not one: whether inflation generated tensor modes, whether their amplitude clears an instrument's threshold, and whether the instrument can resolve them against dust, lensing and systematics. Primordial and lensing B modes are isomers under the same BB observable, so a source-discriminating instrument, not another argument, is what would settle the reading. What the framework offers is that shape, three falsifiers written in advance, and the honest note that this is a simulated derivation, not a ground reading.
