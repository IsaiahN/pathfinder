# Outreach draft: check one shape before deciding about the cosmology

**Use:** one-to-one, or as a cover letter attached to a submission through the public venues in `03_public_contact_points.csv`. **Not** for bulk send.

**Hard precondition:** do not send until the binding in `01_figure_grounded_audit.md` F5 is stated with its pivot scale and the union split in F1 is intact. The stage-1 object must survive a twenty-minute check or the two-stage offer collapses on contact.

**Subject:** A threshold shape for primordial gravitational waves, and what would break it

> Hi [name],
>
> I have one checkable shape, not a cosmology result. I am not asking you to agree with it, and I am not asking you to read any theory material first. If the shape does not survive, there is nothing else to look at.
>
> **The residual, in effect terms.** An instrument has a sensitivity threshold. A sky may or may not carry a tensor perturbation background above it. The current CMB polarisation analyses return an upper limit, `r(0.05 Mpc^-1) < 0.036` at 95% confidence (BICEP/Keck BK18), rather than a detection. That is a bound, not an absence. I am separating three conditions that are usually merged: did inflation generate tensors at all, is the amplitude above the threshold, and can the instrument resolve it against dust, synchrotron, lensing and systematics. These have different anchors and must be checked separately.
>
> **The shape (`specified`, not `grounded`).** Any answer here must be a threshold comparison: a tensor spectrum amplitude `r` compared with an instrument sensitivity `sigma(r)` at a stated pivot scale and sky fraction. Detection is the case where the amplitude clears the sensitivity and the reading survives null tests and multi-frequency foreground separation. The figure vocabulary calls that a comparison bond, a threshold relation between two quantities, not a symmetric joining of two things and not a new field. The framework returns a shape, not a name: it does not tell you the value of `r`, and it does not tell you that inflation happened.
>
> **What the shape does not say.** Not that inflation is ruled out by the bound. Not that a background exists because the theory licenses one. Not that a forecast for a future experiment is evidence about the sky. Current direct detectors at high frequency and CMB polarisation at low frequency are different instruments with different reaches; a limit from one does not transfer to the other.
>
> **What would refute it, written before this message:**
> 1. A B-mode spectrum measured with `r > 0` at a stated pivot, surviving foreground separation and null tests and not attributable to lensing. That settles the threshold in favour of detection and ends the "only upper limits" claim.
> 2. A result showing inflation cannot generate tensor perturbations in any allowed model class.
> 3. Evidence that the published upper limit moves with analysis choices or is dominated by an unmodelled systematic, so it is not an anchor for the instrument claim.
> 4. A demonstration that detection is not a comparison between amplitude and sensitivity, or that the operands are not those two quantities.
>
> If any holds, I retract the corresponding claim. A one-line "this fails because..." is more useful to me than agreement. If you would rather not engage, no reply is needed.
>
> **If it survives, and only then:** here are the sources. The figures are the authority, not my summary of them.
> - BICEP/Keck XIII, BK18 bound and data products: https://arxiv.org/html/2110.00483 and http://bicepkeck.org/bk18_2021_release.html
> - CMB-S4 forecasting paper, the threshold design statement: https://arxiv.org/html/2008.12619
> - LiteBIRD multitracer delensing forecast: https://arxiv.org/html/2312.05194
> - Figure text with line numbers (local extract): `FRAMEWORK/reference/03-FIGURES_TEXT.md`
>
> **If you want to reuse the method:** the eight-step loop is in the figures link. Run it on a problem you can check against a ground, write down what would refute your result *before* you run it, and state whether your claim is general, specified or grounded. A reported failure is more useful to me than a reported success.
>
> Thanks,
> [name]
