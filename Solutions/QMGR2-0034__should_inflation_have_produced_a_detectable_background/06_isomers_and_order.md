### Q1 — Partition or union?

- Fig 9 :54: "when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again."

There are three conditions: generation by inflation, amplitude above threshold, instrument resolution. They overlap. ### Q3 — Are primordial and lensing B modes isomers?

- Fig 12 :117: **"isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."**
- Fig 12 :124: **"functional group. A substructure whose presence fixes the outcome. The group says what kind; the qualifier says which member."**

Both primordial tensor modes and gravitational lensing produce curl-like (B-mode) polarisation on the sky. They share the observed atoms: a BB power spectrum with bandpowers, a multipole range, a sky fraction. They differ in the bond that produced them: tensor perturbations generated at recombination and reionisation versus weak lensing remapping of E modes after last scattering. In a single-frequency BB formula the two are invisible apart; their frequency scaling, angular scale and response to delensing are decisive in behaviour. BK18 fixes the lensing spectrum using Planck 2018 parameters and searches for an excess; LiteBIRD 2023 builds a lensing B-mode template to subtract. Both are exactly the isomer-discriminating operation Fig 12 names.

So: **primordial and lensing B modes are isomers** under the observed BB atoms. As the two theories are sometimes written in the literature (a tensor spectrum versus a lensing convergence spectrum) they share only the BB observable, which makes the weaker **functional-group** claim: both are members of the class "curl polarisation generated by a structure acting on a state", with different atoms in the source slots.

- **Falsifier, written before the run:** if a source-discriminating instrument finds that primordial and lensing B modes have identical frequency dependence, spatial spectral index and delensing response at every accessible scale, so that no operation separates them, the isomer claim fails and the flat-form reading stands. Note the asymmetry from *How Intelligence Actually Works*: "Being 'below the union horizon' is never a settled verdict, only a report that nobody has reached it yet." Failure to discriminate so far does not fire the falsifier; only a demonstrated identity does.

### Q4 — What order is the detection route?

- Fig 12 :71: `→` is **sequence**: "A then B; B needs A to have happened." The ordered bonds are `→`, `⇒`, `−`, `⋛`. Detection has an order: first an upper limit, then a channel that can be maintained, then a comparison, then a reading.
- Fig 12 :104: **"A settled molecule becomes an atom for whatever composes over it. So irreducible is a fact about a level and never about the world."** A published, null-tested upper limit is settled at its level and becomes an atom for the next analysis. BK18's likelihood is reused by Tristram et al. 2022 and by the LiteBIRD forecast, so the settled term is genuinely composable rather than merely recorded.
- Fig 3 :18: "Where a given chain stops is a reading, never a property of the chain." So the break found in Q2 is a reading of the current instruments, not a law about inflation.

The route is: `bandpowers → foreground model → lensing template → r ⋛ sigma(r) → detection or bound`. Each step acts on the residual of the previous one. Fig 13 :75-76: "A gradient over space and one over time are different terms, because which state it is bonded to is part of what it is." A limit from a ground-based 95 GHz receiver and a limit from a space mission at 140 GHz are different terms even if both are quoted in `r`, because the state each is bonded to differs.

- **Falsifier:** show that a bound produced by one channel can be composed with a detection from another without carrying the channel's foregound and sky-fraction record. If the transform loses nothing, level relativity is being claimed where it does not hold.

- **Falsifier:** exhibit a model whose tensor prediction is not representable by the existing spectrum vocabulary at any scale, so that a genuinely new primitive is owed. None of the sources read supplies one.

## CONCLUSION

The question is a **union compound** of three conditions, not one hard problem and not a partition. Its dependency chain is intact through vocabulary and breaks at the objective: the field has `r` and `sigma(r)` but not a settled detection criterion across channels. Primordial and lensing B modes are **isomers** under the observed BB atoms, same formula, different source bond, decisive in frequency and delensing behaviour. The detection route is ordered, and each settled limit can become an atom for the next analysis, which is why independently produced limits are composable rather than merely accumulated. A new inflation model is not the remedy for a budget limit. This framing rescues what is defensible (the union split, the threshold comparison, the isomer test) without asserting a detection, an absence, or a value of `r`.
