# Isomers, functional groups and order in the memory-source question

**Mode: `specified`.** This is a simulated derivation checked against the figures, not a ground reading. See `05_what_shape_means.md` section 6.

## RESIDUAL

The question lists three candidate sources for a cultural memory difference. Two structures are possible and they are not the same:

1. Same memory atoms, different arrangement (isomer).
2. Same arrangement kind, different member (functional group).

The figures decide which structure is licensed. If the cross-cultural data show the same memory components bound differently, the invariant is the atom set and the variation is the bond. If they show the same bond kind with different fillers, the invariant is the functional group and the variation is the member. Both readings are figure-derived; which applies in a given domain is `gap` here and is settled by the empirical instrument.

## DERIVATION

### Q1: What is the structure of the invariant and the variation?

- Fig 12 :117: *"isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."*
- Fig 12 :123: *"functional group. A substructure whose presence fixes the outcome. The group says what kind; the qualifier says which member."*
- Fig 12 :5: *"A set of ingredients is not one. The bond is the rest of the information, and it is the half nothing was recording."*

**Result.** The recurring cross-cultural pattern is the functional-group form. The machinery of encoding, consolidation and retrieval is present in every group; what varies is which member fills the slot, for example self-referencing in one sample and other-referencing in another. Dujmic et al. 2026 report that the same two encoding strategies had different effects in Taiwanese and American older adults, which is a change of member and of bond under a shared functional group. Where two groups use the same memory atoms with different goal-strategy bonds, the finer description is isomer: same atoms, different bond, different behavioural substance.

**Level note.** Fig 12 :104: *"A settled molecule becomes an atom for whatever composes over it. So irreducible is a fact about a level and never about the world."* What is a whole memory strategy at one level can be an atom at the next. Nothing in this derivation makes a particular culture's folk account of memory into a primitive.

### Q2: What bond does each candidate use?

- Operators table: `+` is conjunction, symmetric; `→` is sequence and ordered: *"A then B; B needs A to have happened"*; `⇒` is production, where the second operand did not exist before; `⋛` is comparison, *"a threshold relation between two quantities"*, and the table notes that *"a goal is a comparison, which is why a vocabulary without it cannot state one."*
- Fig 12 :46: *"A bond is the typed relation between two operands."*

**Result.** A strategy is best read as a sequence `→` of encoding operations. A social goal is best read as a comparison `⋛` between a current state and a remembered or anticipated one. A form of experience is not a bond at all but the habitat in which bonds are available and paid for (Fig 11 :39). The three candidates are therefore not even the same grammatical kind: two are typed relations and the third is a condition of contact. That is a further reason the question's either/or framing is a union with a category error inside it, not a three-way partition.

**Falsifier for this sub-derivation:** if an empirical case shows a strategy behaving as a symmetric conjunction rather than an ordered sequence, or a goal behaving as a production that creates new material rather than a comparison, the bond assignments must be re-censused.

### Q3: Is the sort rule ordered or symmetric?

- Operators table: *"Symmetry decides whether an arrangement is a new thing."*
- Fig 12 :46: *"A bond is the typed relation between two operands."*

The memory-phenomenon by cultural-group residual is non-symmetric, so the sort rule is ordered. Under a symmetric bond, swapping operands leaves one term; under an ordered bond it produces a different term. A claim that "culture affects memory" without saying which group and which phenomenon has silently treated an ordered relation as symmetric. That is the averaging error of Fig 1 :22: *"Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average."*

**Result.** The sort rule is ordered. Verification: swap the group labels and re-run the sort; if the assignment changes, the relation was ordered all along.

### Q4: What order governs the search?

Fig 3 :20: *"The chain is ordered by dependency, not by importance."* The order is perception, vocabulary, objective, planning, learn and carry. Here D4 (is the difference real) is the uninstrumented break: the vocabulary and objective for cross-cultural comparability are weaker than the data-collection methods. Fig 3 :27: *"A link with no instrument is not a link that works. It is a link nobody has looked at."*

Fig 9 :43: *"The verdict tells you to leave. The lookup tells you where to go. Elimination is what you do after you arrive."* Applied here: the verdict is whether the difference is in the comparative closure at all; the lookup is which low-overlap frame holds a discriminating instrument (encoding manipulation, motive manipulation, exposure record); elimination is the per-slot check after arrival.

**Result.** Do not rank candidate sources by how plausible they look. Rank the *work* by dependency: fix comparability first, then the discriminating instrument, then the attribution.

### Q6: A worked union

Worked case: D1 and D2 on specific versus general memory, `specified`.

- Invariant functional group: incidentally encoding material and later recognising it.
- Variable member: self-referencing in one cultural sample, other-referencing in another (Dujmic et al. 2026).
- Variable bond: the relation between the social content of the material and the reference instruction.
- Union, not partition: the same phenomenon shows a strategy effect, a goal effect and an experience effect at different levels. Fig 9 :57: *"a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either. It stops being a mixture and becomes a specific compound."*
- What would settle the disputed part: an instrument that returns each source separately. *The Hard Problem is About Source*: the green-reading instrument *"cannot comment on the source"*; the spectrometer resolved the case.

## FALSIFIER, WRITTEN BEFORE CONCLUSION

The isomer and functional-group reading is refuted if:

1. A domain shows variation that requires a new kind of cognitive primitive rather than a new member or bond of an existing group (Operators :68: *"A relation that fails all [tests] is not an operator you are missing; it is a claim that has not been made yet."*).
2. The memory-phenomenon by cultural-group residual turns out to be symmetric, so swapping group labels changes nothing and the ordered-bond reading is wrong.
3. The union collapses to a partition in every domain, with no phenomenon showing both invariant machinery and variable member or bond.
4. A candidate source is correctly modelled as a symmetric conjunction where this derivation assigns an ordered sequence or a comparison.

Any of these sends the derivation back to the domain census; it does not produce a softer reading of the same figure line.

## CONCLUSION

The three candidates are not the same grammatical kind, and their relation to a memory difference is a union rather than a partition. The recurring structure is functional group with variable member or bond, with isomer as the finer-resolution case when the atom set is held fixed. The sort rule is ordered, so no pooled shortcut is available. Search order is dependency order, with comparability the uninstrumented break. Low-overlap analogues from animal behaviour corroborate the shape without naming a source for any human case.
