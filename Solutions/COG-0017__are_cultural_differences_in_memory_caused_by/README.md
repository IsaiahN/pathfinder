# Solution COG-0017

**Question:** Are cultural differences in memory caused by different strategies, different social goals, or different forms of experience?

**Source:** `cognitive science.txt` line 22

**Section:** 2. Perception, attention, and learning

**Mode:** `general` on the structural shape, `specified` per domain, `gap` on which cause produced a given observed difference. No claim in this folder is `grounded`.

**Status:** done

**Registry status:** pending

## The question as an effect

An answer would take an observed cross-cultural difference in memory, for example one group recalling more perceptual detail than another, and assign it to one of three candidate sources: a different strategy used at encoding or retrieval, a different social goal that makes some material worth remembering, or a different history of experience and practice. The three predictions are not exclusive. A social goal can select a strategy, and a history of experience can supply both the goal and the strategy. The split is therefore a union of overlapping conditions, not a partition (Fig 9 :54).

Restated in effect terms: given a memory reading and two cultural groups, what instrument would tell us which of the three sources produced the reading? The figures' answer is a shape plus a separator, not a name. An instrument that reads the effect cannot comment on its source, so the source of a particular difference is `gap` until a discriminating instrument pays for it (Fig 9 :32; Fig 13 :134).

## What is deliberately not here

- No personal emails of named individuals.
- No ranking of people by how likely they are to agree with the shape claim.
- No bulk-send plan.
- No claim that the figures name which of the three causes produced a given difference.

The reasons are figure-grounded. Selecting for likely agreement manufactures the correlation that disqualifies a verdict (Fig 2 :40). A verifier handed the full interior cannot verify the claim (Fig 10 :42). A room cannot add an anchor (Fig 7 :35). The framework refuses to name a missing primitive, and here the missing thing is a source-discriminating instrument, not a fourth cause (Fig 13 :135).
