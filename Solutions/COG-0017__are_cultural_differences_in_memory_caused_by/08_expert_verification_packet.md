# Expert verification packet: the Tether on cultural differences in memory

**What this is:** a claim in a form a memory or culture researcher can check. Mode, derivation and refuters are stated before the conclusion, so a reader knows what to look for and what would settle it.

**Mode: `general` on the shape, `specified` per domain, `gap` on which cause produced a given difference. Not `grounded`.**

## 1. The claim, in one paragraph

An observed cross-cultural difference in memory is a reading, and a reading cannot identify which of three candidate sources produced it: a different encoding strategy, a different social goal, or a different history of experience. Those three are not alternatives; they form a union of overlapping conditions, so a single lever clears only the cases where one condition binds alone. The shape any source attribution must have is: the difference indexed per memory phenomenon and group; a check that the test is a non-updating anchor; independent readings that do not share one evidence pool; and a discriminating instrument that returns the three sources separately. Across groups the memory machinery is a shared functional group whose members and bonds vary, and experience is the habitat, not a fourth primitive. The framework returns this shape and its refuters, not a source name for any particular case.

## 2. Three checkable sub-claims

### C1: framework-internal; checkable by reading Figures 1, 9 and 13
- **Statement:** the three candidates form a union, not a partition, and the specific source of a given difference is a declared `gap`, not an omitted answer.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 9 :54: *"And when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again."* Also Fig 9 :55: *"A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones."* And Fig 13 :134: *"Unreachable is a verdict here, not a silence"*.
- **Refuter:** a clean partition in which every observed difference is attributable to exactly one candidate with the other two absent, across independent slots and groups; or a verbatim figure line that assigns a specific difference to one source.

### C2: framework-internal; checkable by reading Figures 2, 6 and 8
- **Statement:** a source attribution requires a non-updating anchor, independent readings and a discriminating instrument. A pooled or single-dataset attribution is one witness wearing three names.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 2 :33: *"The anchor must not update."* Fig 2 :31: *"Two people reading the same report are not two frames; two instruments pointed at the same thing are."* Fig 6 :32: *"An instrument is not built from a description. It is improved from a worse instrument already returning something."*
- **What to check:** in a study you know, could the same recall score have arisen from more than one of the three sources? Was the instrument shown not to move across groups? Were the strategy, goal and experience readings independent?
- **Refuter:** a single reading that uniquely identifies one source across independent frames with no intervention; or an alleged anchor that moves when the researcher changes the group.

### C3: framework-internal derivation with an empirical check
- **Statement:** the invariant item is a memory functional group and variation is a change of member or bond; experience is the habitat rather than a fourth primitive.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 12 :123: *"functional group. A substructure whose presence fixes the outcome. The group says what kind; the qualifier says which member."* Fig 12 :117: *"isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."* Fig 11 :39: *"capability is a property of agent-and-habitat, never of the agent alone."*
- **What to check:** do Dujmic et al. 2026 and Millar et al. 2013 show a shared memory construct with a changed member or bond, rather than a new kind of memory? Do Pravosudov et al. 2015 and Payne et al. 2021 show shared machinery with ecology-dependent members?
- **Refuter:** a variation that needs a new cognitive primitive rather than a new member or bond; or a strategy effect that persists unchanged when the habitat is fully reproduced.

## 3. What is explicitly not claimed

- Not that strategies, goals or experience is the correct cause in general, and not that they partition the cases.
- Not that a particular cultural group uses a particular strategy or goal. Those are bindings from the empirical frame, provenance-stamped, not derivations.
- Not that "culture" is a fourth cognitive primitive. Experience is the habitat.
- Not that a correlation between strategy and recall fixes the arrow between them.
- Not `grounded`. Nothing here is asserted as settled by experiment.
- Not that this packet settles the empirical question. It is a simulated derivation checked for internal consistency against the figures.

## 5. What would settle it

A discriminating instrument, not a further argument. From *The Hard Problem is About Source*: the green-reading instrument *"cannot comment on the source"*; the spectrometer resolved the case. Here:

1. **Anchor test.** Does the difference survive translation, familiarity and sampling checks with the instrument shown not to move across groups?
2. **Discriminator test.** Is there an intervention that returns strategy, goal and experience effects separately, with independent readings that share no participants or tests?
3. **Bond test.** Is an observed strategy-recall association `→`, `⇒`, or `+`? Only a manipulation decides.

## 6. What an expert can check in twenty minutes

1. Read Fig 9 :54-55, Fig 2 :31 and :33, and Fig 12 :123: four short passages.
2. Check C2 against a study you know: could one recall score have had more than one source, and were the readings independent?
3. Name one instrument that would return the three sources separately, or say why none is possible.
4. Send the refutation. A failure is more useful than an endorsement.
