## World sources that bear on this question

1. **Timing is multi-factor, not one dial.** Wellman, Cross and Watson 2001, "Meta-Analysis of Theory-of-Mind Development: The Truth about False Belief", Child Development 72(3), 655-684. Opened via the ERIC record at https://eric.ed.gov?id=EJ639719. Quote: "a combined model including age, country of origin, and four task factors accounted for 55 percent of the variance in false-belief performance." Use it as a budget filter for where to look, never as a verdict: task factors are a channel quantity (Fig 1 :27, Fig 9 :60).
2. **Paradigm and publication bias are channel effects.** Barone, Corradi and Gomila 2020, "Do young children have a theory of mind?", Ciencia Cognitiva 14:1, 19-22. Opened at https://www.cienciacognitiva.org?p=1937. Quotes: "results varied strongly from study to study, much more than it would be expected by chance, suggesting that other unknown factors modulate the effect"; "a possible publication bias in this body of work"; "The more recent the study was, the less likely it was of finding that infants pass the task." It also gives the ordered form claim: "from an intuitive sensitivity to others' intentions ... to the ability to develop complex explanations of the others' behaviors".
3. **Culture changes the sequence, not just the rate.** Taumoepeau 2025, "Pragmatics and Theory of Mind across cultures", Philosophical Transactions of the Royal Society B 380(1932):20230500. Opened at https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12351313. Quotes: "social understanding development moves from a purely individual cognitive endeavour to a socio-cognitive endeavour, centred in social interactions"; "language and communication form a conduit of cultural knowledge and a critical source of insight for how children learn about the mind"; "consideration of cross-cultural variation in pragmatic intentions in parent-child interactions may help elucidate the timing differences in theory-of-mind milestones."
4. **Bond, not atom, is the variable in responsibility.** Piaget 1932, *The Moral Judgment of the Child*. Direct PDF fetch from https://www.burmalibrary.org/docs20/Piaget-moral_judgment_of_the_child-en-ocr-tu.pdf returned "Unsupported fetched file content type: application/pdf"; the primary text was read via the public search index of that same file. Quotes: "These two attitudes may co-exist at the same age and even in the same child"; "Objective responsibility diminishes on the average". Recorded with reduced weight in the ledger. A secondary summary at https://www.simplypsychology.org/piaget-moral.html states "Heteronomous children judge acts by their consequences; autonomous children judge by the intentions behind them", used only as a label.
5. **Contact with differing frames supplies new terms.** Tomasello, Kruger and Ratner 1993, "Cultural learning", Behavioral and Brain Sciences 16, 495-552. Abstract read via https://philpapers.org/rec/TOMCL and the Cambridge index. Quote: "Cultural learning manifests itself in three forms during human ontogeny: imitative learning, instructed learning, and collaborative learning – in that order."
6. **Social understanding is constructed in interaction.** Carpendale and Lewis 2004, "Constructing an understanding of mind: the development of children's social understanding within social interaction", Behavioral and Brain Sciences 27(1), 79-96. Target-article text read via the public search index of the ORCA and GitHub PDF copies (direct PDF unsupported by the fetcher); record at https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/15481944. Quote from the commentary text: "children construct social understanding through interactions with others and that social knowledge is fundamentally based on action within these relationships."
7. **A task change can change the answer without changing the animal.** Hare, Call and Tomasello 2001, "Do chimpanzees know what conspecifics know?", Animal Behaviour 61(1), 139-151. Full text read via https://evolutionaryanthropology.duke.edu/sites/evolutionaryanthropology.duke.edu/files/site-images/Hare%20et%20al_%202001_%20Do%20chimpanzees%20know%20what%20conspecifics%20know.pdf. Quotes: "their failure in this experiment was likely to be due to the changed nature of the competition under these circumstances and not to a failure of social-cognitive skills"; "We attribute the positive results in our study to an experimental situation that was more naturalistic than previous ones." This is the genuinely low-overlap witness for the ground-versus-channel distinction.
8. **Timing in lives.** Elder 1998, "The Life Course as Developmental Theory", Child Development 69(1), 1-12. Full text read via http://www.rinaldipsych.synthasite.com/resources/Elder.pdf and the five-principle page at https://elder.web.unc.edu/research-projects/. Quotes: "the developmental impact of a succession of life transitions or events is contingent on when they occur in a person's life"; "lives are lived interdependently, and social and historical influences are expressed through this network of shared relationships."
9. **A plain-language field anchor.** Encyclopedia on Early Childhood Development, "Social cognition: theory of the mind in early childhood", https://www.child-encyclopedia.com/social-cognition/according-experts/development-theory-mind-early-childhood. Quote: "environmental factors do influence its development. It can be enhanced by opportunities". Used as a field-facing statement, not as a primary result.

**Overlap declaration.** Sources 1, 2, 3, 5, 6 and 9 are all in the developmental-psychology pool and count as one witness for the verdict even where they agree. Source 4 (Piaget) is historical developmental psychology, still close to that pool. Source 7 (animal behaviour) and source 8 (life-course sociology) are the two genuinely low-overlap witnesses. All framework essays share the framework author's evidence pool completely and carry no independent witness weight. No world source conflicted with a figure verdict; the one apparent conflict (Hare et al.'s failed condition) was returned to the census and re-mapped to the channel domain (F3).

## Distribution: offer, do not push

- **Fig 4 :22** "Only methods cross up. Only head starts come down." Sending the full framework before anyone has described a residual sends a recording, not a generator. Lead with the union claim and the refuter; offer the links after.
- **Fig 10 :42** "A verifier that can reconstruct the claim cannot verify it." A recipient who has absorbed the framework before giving a verdict has joined the frame being checked. Record their stage-1 verdict before any theory is shown.
- **Fig 9 :27-28** "describe it before you go looking, or any frame you pick will seem to fit". Ask what they would expect to see if the shape claim were true before giving them the article.
- **Fig 2 :35** "Alignment is triangulation, not negotiation. A mirror cannot correct you." A list selected for people likely to agree produces a convention, not corroboration.
- **Fig 2 :42** "Buy independence in proportion to the cost of being wrong." A wrong shape here misdirects a decade of intervention, so the check is worth real independence.

**Recommended order in a reply:** (1) the shape claim and its four refuters; (2) ask what they would count as a failure; (3) record that verdict; (4) only then the three framework links, with the note that the figures, not the summary, are the authority; (5) offer the formula as a method to reuse, with the instruction that each user writes their own refuter and states their own mode.

## The two-stage offer (and the optional third stage)

- **Stage 1 before stage 2** is Fig 10 :52, "Bring a characterised residual, never the source", and Fig 2 :45, "Frames range against the ground. They do not negotiate with it." The recipient checks the shape without holding the framework's interior.
- **Stage 2 is opt-in by construction.** Once a recipient has absorbed the theory, their agreement stops being evidence. Their stage-1 verdict is never pooled as corroboration (Fig 2 :40, "disqualifying for a verdict: agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool").
- **Stage 3 sends a method, which is permitted** (Fig 4 :22). The formula is a generator, so giving it away does not give away an answer.
- **Stage 3 still needs a ground per problem.** `THE_FORMULA.md` "Direction": a frame "cannot check the direction of its own question, because the shape a frame is searching for is not a term it holds." Each user writes down what would refute their result before running it.

## Venue constraints

SRCD's Child Development page links a **Generative Artificial Intelligence Policy** and **Registered Reports author guidelines**. The policy governs whether simulated work of this kind may be submitted at all; the Registered Reports format is the natural home for the pre-registered refuter if a submission is attempted. Both are linked in `03_public_contact_points.csv` and recorded as constraints, not as submission venues. The ISSBD site warns of fake emails sent in its name, which is a further reason to use only public site routes. Nothing is sent by this agent; hosting and sending are yours to do. Prefer an opt-in download page that lets a recipient decline contact over an attachment pushed to a list.
