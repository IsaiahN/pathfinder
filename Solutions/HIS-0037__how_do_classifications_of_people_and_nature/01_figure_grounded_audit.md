## 0. The residual, in effect terms

The ordinary question asks whether classifications "reflect the interests" of creators. Restated as an effect:

> A classification is a cut of a habitat (people and nature) into named bins. The question asks what the cut encodes about the cutter. The effect to be explained is this: when a bin decides an outcome, the criteria that placed someone in it turn out to be traceable to what the classifier was positioned to want, to see, and to reward, and the trace is usually invisible in the published list of categories.

Two things follow before any figure is chosen.

1. **The effect is per slot.** It happens to one person, one specimen, one diagnosis, one census row. It must not be averaged into "all knowledge is biased". Fig 1 :19: "The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated." Fig 1 :22: "Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average."
2. **The effect is a union, not a partition.** Interest in the reading, absence of a non-updating anchor, aggregation across slots, and a convention that decides an outcome overlap in the same case. One lever clears only the region where a single condition holds. Fig 9 :54: "And when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again." Fig 9 :57: "A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones."

What an answer would look like, bound as an ordinary term before the run (Formula "Direction"): a *checkable separator* that says when a cut is a verdict about the world and when it is an authored convention, with a failure condition written in advance. This is a shape, not a list of bad classifications.

## 3. Findings F1 to F11

### F1 The interest is in the reading, and it is disqualifying, not merely a bias (decisive, `general`)

Fig 10 :59: "an interest in the outcome is unavoidable, an interest in the reading is disqualifying."
Fig 10 :60: "Where a stake cannot be removed, declare it. An undeclared interest is modelled anyway and an unnamed model runs unchallenged."

The cut is the reading. So a classification that "reflects interests" is not an accidental contamination added to a neutral operation. It is the structural condition of any cut made from inside a frame. The figures do not demand impossible disinterest; they demand declaration. An undeclared interest is modelled anyway by whoever uses the classification, so hiding it does not remove it, it only removes the challenge to it.

**Strain check.** Read literally, "an interest in the reading is disqualifying" would make all classification impossible, since every cut is someone's. Re-census with Fig 10 :58 to :60: the object is a *seat* that grades a claim. What is disqualifying is an interest in which value the ground returns, not the presence of a perspective. A maker can classify; a verifier cannot do so with a stake in the verdict. The question asks about creators, so this line governs them as makers whose interest must be declared when their output is used as a verdict. Narrowed, not discarded. **Verdict: `general`, triangulated** (Fig 10 stake rule and Fig 2 anchor rule are low-overlap figures).

### F2 A classifier with no non-updating anchor is a mirror (`specified`)

Fig 2 :35: "Alignment is triangulation, not negotiation. A mirror cannot correct you."
Fig 2 :29: "An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks."
Fig 7 :36: "A chain of mirrors is one long room. The next room must differ from the last, and match the residual it surfaced."

When classifiers validate their categories only against other classifiers in the same discipline, they converge. Fig 2 describes the convergence in the mutual-update panel: "they converge, and what they converge on is whoever had the most influence". The "interest of the creator" is then simply the name we give to the direction of that convergence. Linnaeus's four human varieties matched the four known continents and the four humours, not a discriminating reading of human variation (Linnean Society 2020; Kenyon-Flatt 2021, both opened). The mirror returned the culture that built it.

**Verdict: `specified`, triangulated** (Fig 2 mirror, Fig 7 mirror chain, plus two world sources from one historical pool counted as one witness).

### F3 The cut is chosen before the residual is described (`specified`)

Fig 9 :27-28: "describe it before you go looking, or any frame you pick will seem to fit."
Fig 9 :30-31: "your words are borrowed too, so describe the effect rather than the cause."

If you start with four bins, the world will supply four things to put in them. The Linnean Society page records that "the four corners of the world" recursion appeared in Linnaeus's plants and commodities as well as people, and that the ten humoral and moral attributes were added in the 1758 tenth edition, after the four geographical varieties were already fixed. The category preceded the discriminating observation. Bhopal 2007 records the parallel for Blumenbach: he "emphasises that no clear cut subdivisions of human species exist", yet still named five varieties whose labels stuck.

**Verdict: `specified`, triangulated** (Fig 9 plus history-of-science sources; world sources corroborate the shape, they do not decide it).

### F4 Categories are frame-internal quantities; a classifier cannot score itself with them (`general`)

Fig 1 :26: "predicates minted, and credibility accrued"
Fig 1 :27: "They are frame-internal. A frame cannot score itself with a quantity it also produces."
Fig 5 :44: "A term that explains everything by saying nothing fails this, and so does a term that explains one occasion perfectly."

Bowker and Star 1999 define an ideal classification as complete and mutually exclusive, and immediately note that no real system meets those requirements. Treating completeness or category count as success is scoring the frame with a quantity the frame produces. A total theory of "everything is an expression of interest" also fails the Fig 5 bargain: it explains everything by saying nothing. This is why the answer here is a separator with a refuter, not a universal slogan.

**Verdict: `general`, triangulated** (Fig 1 and Fig 5, with Bowker and Star as world corroboration).

### F5 Aggregating people into bins hides the live per-slot signal (`specified`)

Fig 1 :19: "The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated."
Fig 1 :22: "Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average."

Gould 1981 names the exact operation: "the abstraction of intelligence as a single entity, its location within the brain, its quantification as one number for each individual, and the use of these numbers to rank people in a single series of worthiness". A single number per person, averaged over groups, is the aggregation the figure forbids. Blumenbach's observation that varieties "run into one another by insensible degrees" is the per-slot continuum the discrete cut discards.

**Strain check.** This does not say bins are forbidden; it says the residual is not an average. Re-census resolves the apparent conflict with F4: bins are the library, per-slot residuals are the error signal. **Verdict: `specified`, triangulated** (Fig 1 plus two low-overlap world sources, palaeontology/history of science and public-health history).

### F6 A convention that decides an outcome has stopped being a convention (`specified`, strongest world-facing line)

Fig 2 :43: "Guard, from Figure 10: a convention that decides an outcome has stopped being one."
Fig 10 :47: "a convention that decides an outcome has stopped being one"
Fig 10 :49: "Declaring a meaning does not ground it."

Bowker and Star's apartheid race classification, and the use of IQ categories to rank immigrants and to justify the 1924 United States Immigration Restriction Act (Gould 1981; review literature opened), are cases where a naming decided who got what. At that point the naming is a verdict about people, and no declaration of neutrality grounds it. This is the mechanism by which an interest becomes consequential, and it is checkable in records rather than inferred from motives.

**Verdict: `specified`, triangulated** (Fig 2 and Fig 10, plus Bowker/Star and Gould).

### F7 The habitat is enumerated, not composed, and the list is read off the world (`specified`)

Fig 11 :43: "The habitat is enumerated, never composed."
Fig 11 :44: "List everything in contact with the residual, then what is in contact with those, and outward until the cascade stops mattering."
Fig 11 :48: "You do not invent the list. You read it off the world, and what you cannot perceive or measure yet is the residual."

A classification that invents its list from the classifier's vocabulary carries the interest into the object. A classification that enumerates the habitat, including second-order effects, keeps the interest outside the cut where it can be declared. The SAPIENS account records that information about Africans and Indigenous Americans came to European classifiers "not from direct experience ... but rather primarily from those involved in the slave trade, missionaries", which is a habitat enumerated through an interested channel. The defect is in the channel to the ground, not in the existence of a perspective (Fig 10 :24: "the ground does not decay, the channel does").

**Verdict: `specified`, single-source on the figure side (Fig 11), corroborated in world.** Not upgraded to triangulated, because no second figure governs enumeration of a habitat.

### F8 Valence is relative to the residual, so no standing category list survives a changed question (`specified`)

Fig 11 :49: "An actor's valence is relative to the residual, not a property it carries."
Fig 11 :51: "No standing list of good actors survives a change of residual."

The Linnean Society page records that the order of Linnaeus's human varieties flipped between editions, Europaeus first in 1735, Asiaticus in the lecture notes, Americanus in the tenth edition under the influence of the noble savage idea, while Africanus "consistently remained at the bottom" and was described "the longest, most detailed and physical, and also the most negative". The ranking moved with the classifier's residual, not with a new measurement. Categories that look stable are often a standing list whose valence was fixed on an old question.

**Verdict: `specified`, triangulated** (Fig 11 plus two history sources counted as one pool).

### F9 The arrangement, not the ingredient list, carries the interest (`specified`)

Fig 12 :5: "A set of ingredients is not one. The bond is the rest of the information, and it is the half nothing was recording."
Fig 12 :94: "The arrangement is the substance."
Fig 8 :51: "Convergent derivation and adopted import look identical in the contents. Only the record of where each came from separates them, and the surplus lives only where the closures differ."

Two classifications can publish the same categories and be different things, because the criteria and the bond between criterion and category are not recoverable from the list. Bowker and Star's "infrastructural inversion" is the same move: make the invisible work of classification visible by reading the design record. Without that record, a convergent derivation and an adopted prejudice look identical in the output.

**Verdict: `specified`, triangulated** (Fig 12 and Fig 8 on provenance, plus Bowker and Star).

### F10 Keep every cut ranked and reversible (`general`, recommendation)

Fig 9 :40: "a wrong cut removes the answer and speeds up"
Fig 9 :41: "so keep every cut ranked and reversible"
Fig 9 :42: "narrowing gets you close, the ground decides"

The cost of a bad classification is not just that it is wrong; it removes the case that would have shown it wrong and accelerates the narrowing. Fig 9 :60: "Use filters for the budget and witnesses for the verdict. Never let a filter hand you a verdict." A category count, a coverage statistic, or a prestige-weighted list of classifiers is a filter for where to look, never a witness for what the categories mean.

**Verdict: `general`, single-source (Fig 9), carried into the outreach files as a rule.**

### F11 What survives: the shape, not the name of a correct taxonomy (`gap`, `general`)

Fig 9 :32: "you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself"
Fig 13 :134: "Unreachable is a verdict here, not a silence"
Fig 13 :135: "It cannot say which primitive is missing, because naming it would mean already holding it."

No figure line selects Linnaeus's four varieties over another partition, ICD over another disease scheme, or one census race category over another. The figures return the *shape any accountable classification must have*: per-slot indexing, a declared interest in the reading, a habitat enumerated rather than composed, a non-updating anchor, a cut kept ranked and reversible, and an outside frame able to refuse. They do not name which classification is correct. That is a `gap` of the shape-not-a-name kind, reported as a verdict and not as silence (Fig 8 :39: "So a search that finds nothing is never a proof of absence.").

**Verdict: `gap` on the name, `general` on the shape, triangulated** (Fig 9 and Fig 13, low overlap).

## 4. Pre-registered refuter (written before the conclusion)

Per Seat Map :287: "And a falsifier written before the run is a design instrument, not only an honesty device." The shape claim is refuted if any of the following holds:

1. A classification is exhibited whose cut is set by a ground that does not update on any frame's opinion, whose residuals remain indexed per slot, whose maker's interest is declared, and whose cut is reversible, and the interests of its makers still determine the cut. That would make interest in the reading non-structural.
2. A classification with a declared interest and a reversible cut is shown to be *worse* at the ground than one with an undeclared interest. That reverses Fig 10 :60.
3. A naming that decides an outcome is exhibited and still functions as a convention rather than a verdict. That breaks Fig 2 :43.
4. A classification is derived from the habitat by composition alone, with no cut at all. That breaks the enumeration requirement of Fig 11 :43.
5. Aggregating across slots is shown to preserve the live signal that per-slot reading would lose. That breaks Fig 1 :22.

If any fires, the shape is withdrawn or narrowed, and the correction is recorded here rather than silently applied. Until one is run, nothing in this folder is labelled `grounded`.

## 5. Recommendation

1. Do not answer "yes, all classifications are ideology". That is a `general`-mode verdict with no measurement, and it fails the Fig 5 bargain (F4). Answer with the separator: a cut is a verdict only when it ranges against a non-updating anchor with a declared interest and a reversible criterion.
2. State the mode on every substantive claim (F1 to F11 are labelled).
3. Lead with the decisive pair from Fig 10 :59-60: interest in the reading is disqualifying, and an undeclared interest runs unchallenged. This is the figure-grounded answer to "reflect the interests".
4. Treat a classification that decides outcomes as a verdict and demand independent frames (F6), not a consensus of agreeable readers (Fig 2 :35, Fig 7 :36).
5. Keep the lineage, not just the category list. Fig 4 :22: "Only methods cross up. Only head starts come down." Send the criterion and the refuter, not a finished taxonomy.
6. Record the `gap` honestly: no figure names the correct classification (F11). "Unreachable is a verdict here, not a silence."
