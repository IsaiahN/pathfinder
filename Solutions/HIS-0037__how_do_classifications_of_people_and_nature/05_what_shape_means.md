## 1. A shape is a constraint, not a filler

The canonical figures README states the limit: the framework returns "what a missing thing would have to look like (a shape, not a name)". Fig 9 :32: "you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself". Fig 13 :134-135: "Unreachable is a verdict here, not a silence" and "It cannot say which primitive is missing, because naming it would mean already holding it."

From *The Library of Babel* (carried): "You can cut your search short if the set of books returned is small and descriptive enough that it provides a general shape of the answer that satisfies your question."

So a **shape** is the narrowed form of an answer you can state before you can name it. It constrains; it does not fill. For this question the shape is a separator with four conditions and a pre-registered refuter, not a list of classifications that happened to be interested. "Every classification reflects power" would be a filled slot, not a shape, and it fails the acceptance bargain in Fig 5 :44: "A term that explains everything by saying nothing fails this, and so does a term that explains one occasion perfectly."

## 2. How a shape is obtained

- *Library of Babel* (carried): use the vocabulary already closest to the thing, look for recurring new terms that overlap with it, recurse. "With each new term gained, you narrow the space and deepen your vocabulary."
- Fig 6 :30: "The description travels. Fails by being sharpened into terms only we can read. Cast it in effect terms." Fig 9 :30-31: "your words are borrowed too, so describe the effect rather than the cause."
- *Decoding Tacit Knowledge* (carried): "When contact isn't possible, the best you can send is a plain description of what you'd expect to see, because anyone can check that against the world."
- The effect-term description used in `01` §0 is exactly this move: not "power is everywhere" but "when a bin decides an outcome, the criteria trace to what the classifier was positioned to want, see, and reward, and the trace is invisible in the category list." Anyone with a classification record can check that.

## 3. What licenses a name

Only two things, both outside the shape itself:

1. **The ground settles it.** Formula step 5: "the ground settles it. Verified only by what pays." A classification's categories are licensed by a ground that does not update on the classifier's opinion (Fig 2 :29).
2. **A provenanced import from a frame whose closure differs** (Fig 6 :23, Fig 8 :51), with a source gate and a shadow test.

*They're Scanning Books* (carried) sharpens this: "The real import is not a new term in the existing space. It is a new dimension of the space." And: "Declaring the primitives does not make them neutral. It makes the choice visible."

For history of classification research this is the governing sentence. Declaring the four continents and four humours behind Linnaeus's varieties does not make them neutral; it makes the choice visible. The interest was already modelled by every reader of the table; naming it only makes it challengeable. Fig 10 :60: "Where a stake cannot be removed, declare it. An undeclared interest is modelled anyway and an unnamed model runs unchallenged."

## 4. The decisive essay for this question: source, not output

*The Hard Problem is About Source* (opened in full) gives the mechanism that makes "interests" more than a rhetorical label.

- Metamerism: an instrument reading green "cannot comment on the source". The same reading can come from one green light, yellow plus blue, RGB, CMYK, or a scheme not yet discovered.
- "If several paths create the same behavior, then behavior is a projection with lossy information."
- "An instrument that can't distinguish these cases demonstrates its own inadequacy."
- It was "proven wrong not by argument, but by the arrival of a better instrument", the spectrometer.

Applied here: a published list of categories is a projection. It cannot tell you which criteria produced the cut, because several criteria can yield the same list. Asking whether the list "reflects interests" from the list alone is the metamerism error. What is owed is the classification's provenance record and an instrument that discriminates among sources, not an argument about motives. Fig 12 :5: "A set of ingredients is not one. The bond is the rest of the information, and it is the half nothing was recording."

## 5. Prestige is not action (opened in full)

From *The Recursive Self-Improvement Trap*: "Prestige must never be allowed to validate an action directly." And: "Prestige can fund an action. It can give an action a budget to be tried. But it cannot settle an action. Only the ground can settle it. The Ground Maintainer must be blind to prestige."

A campaign that gathers famous historians to agree that classification reflects power is the named collapse. The legitimate ask is the opposite: give the separator a budget to be tried against records, and let the ground, a classification's own provenance and its consequences at the slot, settle it. A famous name can get the paper read; it cannot make the claim true. Seat Map :205: "An anchor someone benefits from is one whose keeper is thereby disqualified."

The same essay supplies the validator rule: "the output is only as good as the validator. The agent, or the tool, or the frame, or the theory, cannot validate itself." This applies to the present folder with full force.

## 6. My own audit is a simulation, not a ground reading

The same essay: "LLMs can quickly simulate an approximate answer/output with the formula found in the architecture post... However, the truly testable output would need to be derived from the real architecture or working the formula yourself."

So `01_figure_grounded_audit.md`, `06`, `07`, `08`, `09` and `11` are simulated derivations checked for internal consistency against the figures. They are **not** a history of classification research done at the ground, and they cannot settle the question they report on. The world sources in `10_source_ledger.csv` are secondary corroboration of shapes, opened and quoted, not an independent test of the separator.

## 7. Correction to my own §3 (kept visible)

I initially wrote §3 as if "shape, not a name" forbade naming any category. That overstates it. The README's phrase is "what a **missing** thing would have to look like (a shape, not a name)". It governs a primitive not held in Γ. Categories such as species, variety, diagnosis and census bin are already held in the classification library. Naming one is a binding that must be provenanced, not an invented primitive. What remains forbidden is claiming the figures select the correct partition, and claiming that a binding moves the wall (Fig 6: "IMPORT adds an atom" is not what happens when a known category is named). Both the original overstatement and this narrowing are recorded so neither is quietly lost. The same correction is applied in `06_isomers_and_order.md`.
