# Expert verification packet: the Tether on classification and interest

**What this is:** a claim in a form a historian of classification, an STS scholar, or an information scientist can check. Mode, derivation and refuters are stated before the conclusion, so a reader knows what to look for and what would settle it.

**Mode: `general` on the structural shape, `specified` on the per-domain findings, not `grounded`.** No history was measured at the ground here. This is a shape and a separator, not a result. Simulated derivation checked for internal consistency against the figures, not a ground reading.

## 1. The claim, in one paragraph

A classification is a cut of a habitat into named bins. An interest in the outcome of that cut is unavoidable for its maker, but an interest in the reading is disqualifying when the cut is used as a verdict, so the interest must be declared or it runs unchallenged. The cut is checkable only when the habitat was enumerated rather than composed from the classifier's vocabulary, when residuals stay indexed per person or specimen rather than being averaged into a group number, when the criteria and bonds are preserved in the record rather than only the category names, and when the cut can be ranged against a ground that does not update on what the classifier thinks. Where a naming decides an outcome, it has stopped being a convention and must be checked as a verdict. The framework returns this separator with five failure conditions written in advance. It does not name the correct taxonomy.

## 2. Three checkable sub-claims

### C1 — framework-internal, checkable by reading Figure 10
- **Statement:** interest in the reading is disqualifying; where the stake cannot be removed it must be declared, because an undeclared interest is modelled anyway and runs unchallenged.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 10 :59 "an interest in the outcome is unavoidable, an interest in the reading is disqualifying". Also Fig 10 :60 "Where a stake cannot be removed, declare it. An undeclared interest is modelled anyway and an unnamed model runs unchallenged."
- **What to check:** take any published classification in your field. Does its record declare the criterion that decided the cut and the interest that criterion served? If the answer is no, did readers nevertheless model the interest from context? The claim is that the second is always true and the first is what makes it challengeable.
- **Refuter:** exhibit a classification using a non-updating ground and a declared interest whose cut is still determined by the maker's interests; or show that a declared, reversible cut is worse at the ground than an undeclared one.

### C2 — history-facing, checkable against the published record
- **Statement:** a naming that decides an outcome has stopped being a convention and must be checked as a verdict, with frames that do not share one evidence pool.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 2 :43 "Guard, from Figure 10: a convention that decides an outcome has stopped being one." Also Fig 10 :49 "Declaring a meaning does not ground it." And Fig 2 :35 "Alignment is triangulation, not negotiation. A mirror cannot correct you."
- **What to check:** in Bowker and Star's apartheid race classification, or in the use of IQ categories behind the 1924 United States Immigration Restriction Act (Gould 1981), did the category decide an outcome? If so, was it subsequently treated as a neutral convention? The claim predicts yes, and predicts that an interested convention cannot be corrected by agreement among the classifiers who authored it.
- **Refuter:** exhibit a naming that decides an outcome and still functions as a convention; or show that a discipline's own internal agreement, without an external anchor, corrected the outcome-fixing use.

### C3 — framework-internal plus record-facing, checkable against a classification's scheme
- **Statement:** the interest lives in the criteria and bonds, and aggregation destroys the signal that would expose it.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 12 :5 "A set of ingredients is not one. The bond is the rest of the information, and it is the half nothing was recording." Fig 1 :19 "The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated." Fig 1 :22 "Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average." Fig 8 :51 "Convergent derivation and adopted import look identical in the contents. Only the record of where each came from separates them."
- **What to check:** compare two systems with the same category names but different criteria (for example Linnaean human varieties keyed to geography and humours, and modern census categories keyed to self-identification). Does the published category list alone tell you which is which? If not, is there a provenance record that does? Are the underlying residuals per person, or pooled into group averages?
- **Refuter:** exhibit a classification derived from the habitat by composition alone with no cut; or show that averaging across slots preserves the live signal that per-slot reading would lose; or show the category list does determine the criteria without a record.

## 3. The five standing refuters (pre-registered before the conclusion)

1. A cut made against a non-updating ground, with per-slot residuals, a declared interest and a reversible criterion, whose maker's interests still determine the cut.
2. A declared, reversible cut shown to be worse at the ground than an undeclared one.
3. A naming that decides an outcome and still functions as a convention.
4. A classification derived from the habitat by composition alone, with no cut.
5. Aggregation across slots shown to preserve the live per-slot signal.

If one fires, the shape is withdrawn or narrowed. A reported failure is more useful than an endorsement.

## 4. What is explicitly not claimed

- Not that all classifications are ideology. Fig 5 :44: "A term that explains everything by saying nothing fails this, and so does a term that explains one occasion perfectly."
- Not that no classification can track a ground. The figures require an anchor; they do not deny anchors.
- Not that the figures name the correct taxonomy. Fig 9 :32: "you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself."
- Not a psychological claim about a named classifier's motives. Linnaeus used "varieties" rather than "race" and held skin colour to be largely climatic; Blumenbach warned that varieties "run into one another by insensible degrees" and wrote against African inferiority. Both are part of the record.
- Not `grounded`. Nothing here is asserted as settled by experiment or by a source-discriminating instrument.

## 6. What would settle it

A provenance record and a discriminating instrument, not a further argument. From *The Hard Problem is About Source*: an instrument reading green "cannot comment on the source", and "If several paths create the same behavior, then behavior is a projection with lossy information". Here: (1) does the classification record the criteria and bonds, not just the bins? (2) are residuals per slot? (3) is there a ground that does not update? (4) has an outcome-fixing naming been refused the label of convention?

## 7. What an expert can check in twenty minutes

1. Read Fig 10 :59-60, Fig 2 :43, and Fig 1 :19 and :22. Four short passages.
2. Open one classification scheme you know well. Find its declared criterion and its maker's interest in the reading. If either is missing, note whether readers modelled it anyway.
3. Check C2 against one historical case where a category decided an outcome. Did the scheme get treated as neutral afterwards?
4. Name one instrument or record that would discriminate among the sources that could have produced the same category list, or say why none is possible.
5. Send the refutation. A failure is more useful than an endorsement.
