# Outreach draft: check the separator before you decide about the theory

**Use:** one to one, or as a cover note attached to a submission through a public venue in `03_public_contact_points.csv`. **Not** for bulk send.

**Hard precondition:** do not send until the stage-1 shape survives the refuter in `01_figure_grounded_audit.md` §4. The offer collapses on contact if the thing being checked cannot fail.

**Two-stage order:** stage 1 sends the residual, the shape claim, and the refuter, and asks what the recipient would count as a failure. Their verdict is recorded before any figure text is shown. Stage 2 is opt-in: only if they are curious, offer the sources. Stage 3 offers the formula as a method to reuse, with the instruction that each user writes their own refuter and states their own mode.

---

**Subject:** Check one separator before you decide whether the framework is worth reading

> Hi [name],
>
> I am working on a question in the history of classification: how do classifications of people and nature reflect the interests of those who create them? I have one specific output I would like you to check against what you already know. I am not asking you to read the theory first, and I am not asking you to agree with it. If the output does not survive, there is nothing else to look at.
>
> **The output (`general` on the structure, `specified` per domain, not `grounded`):** the figures make a distinction that matters here. An interest in the outcome is unavoidable for anyone who makes a classification. An interest in *the reading*, in which value the ground returns, is disqualifying for anyone who uses that classification as a verdict. Where the stake cannot be removed, the rule is to declare it, because an undeclared interest is modelled anyway and an unnamed model runs unchallenged.
>
> From that and three other figure rules a separator follows. A cut of a habitat is a verdict about the world only when (1) the habitat was enumerated, not composed from the classifier's vocabulary, (2) residuals stay indexed per person or specimen and are not averaged into a group number, (3) the maker's interest in the reading is declared and the criterion is kept ranked and reversible, and (4) the cut can be ranged against a ground that does not update on what the classifier thinks. Where a naming decides an outcome, it has stopped being a convention and must be checked as a verdict, with frames that do not share the same evidence pool.
>
> **What I am not claiming:** that every classification is merely ideology, that no classification can track a real ground, or that the figures name the correct taxonomy. They return a shape, not a name. A total claim that "everything is power" explains everything by saying nothing and fails the figures' own acceptance bargain. Particular bindings, for example Linnaeus's four varieties of man, are provenanced historical claims, not outputs of the framework.
>
> **What would refute the separator:**
> 1. exhibit a cut made against a non-updating ground, with per-slot residuals, a declared interest, and a reversible criterion, and show that the maker's interests still determine the cut;
> 2. show that a declared, reversible cut is worse at the ground than an undeclared one;
> 3. exhibit a naming that decides an outcome and still functions as a convention;
> 4. derive a classification from the habitat by composition alone, with no cut;
> 5. show that averaging across slots preserves the live signal that per-slot reading would lose.
>
> I wrote these down before sending this. If any holds, I will withdraw or narrow the claim. A one-line "this fails because..." is more useful to me than agreement. If you would rather not engage, no reply is needed.
>
> **If it survives, and only then:** the sources are below. The figures are the authority, not my summary of them.
> - Canonical figures text with line numbers (local extract): `FRAMEWORK/reference/03-FIGURES_TEXT.md`
> - Canonical usage rule: https://github.com/IsaiahN/tether/blob/general-agent/figures/README.md
> - Canonical essay on stakes and prestige: https://adventuresinml.substack.com/p/the-recursive-self-improvement-trap
>
> **If you want to use the machinery:** the formula is in the figures link. Run it on a problem you know well and can check against a ground. Write down what would refute your result *before* you run it, and label the mode of what you get. A reported failure is more useful to me than a reported success.
