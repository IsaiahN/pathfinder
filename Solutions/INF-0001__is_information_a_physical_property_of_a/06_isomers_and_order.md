# Union, two currencies, and bond order

**Mode: `specified` on the structural claims, `gap` on the single definition.** This is a simulated derivation checked against the figures, not a ground reading (see `05_what_shape_means.md` section 6).

## RESIDUAL

The ordinary question offers three options: property, relation, or both. Three supporting questions are owed:

1. Is the "both" option a partition (two exclusive kinds) or a union (overlapping conditions)?
2. If the physical and relational readings are both real, are they one quantity measured twice or two currencies that cannot be added?
3. Does the order of operands in the relation matter, and can the physical and relational readings be isomers of one another over a fixed atom set?

### Q1 — Is "both" a partition or a union?

- Fig 9 :54: **"And when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again."**

### Q2 — Are the two readings one quantity or two currencies?

- Fig 12 :125: **"A composition costs in two currencies, and they do not add."**
- Fig 12 :126: **"Bits price the description. Actions price finding out whether it holds."**
- Fig 12 :127: **"They are in different units, so a system holding one accounting cannot compare them and will not notice the shortfall."**
- Fig 12 :130: **"A price in bits and a price in actions are two readings, held apart."**

The bit-price of a state description (Shannon, Landauer, Bérut in the world) and the action-price of settling a typed bond are in different units. Summing them is the named error, not the fix. This is the structural reason the question cannot be answered by a single measure of "amount of information."

**Result:** two currencies held apart. Triangulated (Fig 12 pricing rule plus Fig 4 :27 lossy transform; world corroboration from physics at reduced weight because that pool is one witness).

### Q3 — Does operand order matter in the relation?

- Operators table :59-62: under a symmetric bond, *"Under a symmetric bond, k operands have k! spellings and one term, so a code that charges for the sequence overcharges by log₂(k!) while the bond term undercharges. Both wrong, in opposite directions, and only one is usually noticed. Under an ordered bond, different orders are different terms, and charging for the sequence is correct."*
- Fig 12 :53: **"Symmetry is what decides whether an arrangement is a new thing."**

The question's "relation between a system and an observer" is directional: a constraint from a source type to a receiver type is not the reverse constraint. Fig 13 :36: **"adjacency is not duration, an ordering is not a pathway, a pathway is not a direction."** So the relevant bond is ordered, and a symmetric treatment would undercharge the relation. This is why a bare correlation (symmetric) is not yet information-on-a-channel (ordered).

**Result:** ordered. Single-source on the figure side (Operators table and Fig 12), corroborated by the world's information-channel tradition (SEP semantic entry) at low overlap.

### Q4 — Are the physical and relational readings isomers over a fixed atom set?

- Fig 12 :117: **"isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."**
- Fig 12 :123: **"functional group. A substructure whose presence fixes the outcome. The group says what kind; the qualifier says which member."**

Over the full shared atom set `{Space, Time, Energy, Spectrum, Gradient, Network}`, the carrier reading and the bond reading would be isomers if they used the same atoms under different typed bonds. But they do not use the same atoms: the carrier reading is a unary price on a state description, while the bond reading is a typed relation between operands. They are not isomers; they are **two different compositions with different arities that share a functional group**, namely the cross-kind `state -> structure -> state` arrangement (Fig 13 :75: *"Two states never bond directly. It is not space-time but space, a structure, time"*).

The stronger isomer claim is available only if both readings are first written over the same atom set. That is a discipline, not a discovery: bind both as ordinary terms and check. As normally stated, the claim fails the figure's definition.

**Result:** not isomers as stated; functional-group relation with a fixed-atom condition on the stronger claim. **Falsifier, written before the run:** if both readings can be written over the same atom set with the same atoms and different typed bonds, and their behaviour differs, the isomer claim is established; if either requires an atom the other does not, the functional-group reading stands and the isomer claim is withdrawn.

### Q5 — What is the order of the chain?

Dependency order (Fig 3 :20: **"The chain is ordered by dependency, not by importance."**):

1. Read the state of a slot (D1). Without this, no description cost can be charged.
2. Type the bond between relata (D2). Without this, no channel can be distinguished from a shared pool.
3. Range the frame's reading against a ground (D3 and D4). Without this, meaning drifts coherently (Fig 2 :34).
4. Settle (step 5). A gate is not the ground.

Fig 3 :27: **"A link with no instrument is not a link that works. It is a link nobody has looked at."** The missing instrument in this question is at link 2 to 3: nothing yet discriminates "the same bit-pattern carried by a physical channel" from "the same bit-pattern produced by a shared evidence pool."

**Result:** D1 before D2 before D3 before D4. Strain: none after re-census; S3 in `01` records the temptation to use Fig 11 :40 as the whole answer and narrows it to an analogue.

## CONCLUSION

"Both" is a union of overlapping conditions with two currencies that do not add, not a partition and not an average. The relation condition is ordered, so correlation alone is insufficient. The physical and relational readings are not isomers as normally stated; they share a functional group and can be tested as isomers only under an explicit fixed-atom binding. The chain runs state reading, then bond typing, then ground ranging, then settlement. The single definition remains a `gap`, and the missing instrument is at the bond-typing to ground-ranging link.

## FALSIFIER (pre-registered)

The union claim fails if the two conditions form a clean partition with no overlapping case. The two-currency claim fails if bits and actions can be added in one accounting. The order claim fails if a bond can be typed before a state is read, or if a frame can be ranged against a ground without a channel. The isomer qualification fails under the fixed-atom test stated in Q4. Any of these fires a retraction in `01_figure_grounded_audit.md` section 6.
