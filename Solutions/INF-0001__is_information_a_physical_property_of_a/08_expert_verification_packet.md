# Expert verification packet — the Tether on property, relation, or both

**What this is:** a claim in a form a philosopher of information, information theorist, or philosopher of physics can check. Mode, derivation and refuters are stated before the conclusion, so the reader knows what to look for and what would settle it.

**Mode: `general` on the structural shape, `specified` on the per-domain verdicts, `gap` on the single definition. Not `grounded`.** No measurement is claimed. Simulated derivation checked for internal consistency against the figures, not a ground reading.

## 1. The claim, in one paragraph

The question "property, relation, or both?" is a union of two conditions with different currencies, not a partition and not a single averaged property. In the figure vocabulary: (i) the typed bond carries information the operands do not, so a complete inventory of a system is half a record; (ii) bits price the description and actions price settling it, and these two currencies do not add; (iii) the observer is a frame with a library and a residual whose reading must be ranged against a ground that does not update, but low-overlap frames can converge on the same term, so the relation is not whim. The question's "both" is correct only when unbundled and checked per slot. No figure line returns a single univocal definition of information across carrier, bond and frame; that is a declared `gap`, and an instrument that would close it is characterised as a shape.

## 2. Three checkable sub-claims

### C1 — framework-internal; checkable by reading Figures 12 and 13

- **Statement:** in the framework's own vocabulary, "information" is the bond, not the ingredients; a unary property reading alone is therefore incomplete. The observer-side term is a *frame*, not a *seat*.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 12 :5 — *"A set of ingredients is not one. The bond is the rest of the information, and it is the half nothing was recording."* Fig 12 :46 — *"A bond is the typed relation between two operands. There are seven, they are not interchangeable, and which one holds is not recoverable from the operands."* Fig 13 :24 — *"that entities relate, and influence travels."* Symbols :111 — *"A frame is any bounded system with a library: an agent, a person, a discipline, a model."*
- **Refuter:** a figure line in which the bond is recoverable from the operands alone, or in which a single state in isolation carries the full information of a composed term; or a demonstration that the observer in the question must be a checking seat rather than a reading frame (Fig 10 :43-44, a seat *"transmits verdicts and issues none"*).

### C2 — framework-internal and physics-facing; checkable against Fig 12 and the thermodynamic literature

- **Statement:** there are two currencies, and they do not add. The physical bits-reading is real but cannot be summed with the relational bond-cost.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 12 :125 — *"A composition costs in two currencies, and they do not add."* Fig 12 :126 — *"Bits price the description. Actions price finding out whether it holds."* Fig 12 :130 — *"Summing them into one figure is the error this names, not the fix for it."*
- **What to check against the world:** Shannon 1948 restricts the engineering measure to selection from a set and sets semantics aside; Landauer 1961 and Bérut et al. 2012 give the bit-price a physical ground. Does any of these supply an additive measure that also prices the typed relation? If so, C2 fails.
- **Refuter:** a figure line putting bits and actions in one accounting, or a measured quantity that preserves both readings under one currency.

### C3 — framework-internal derivation; the union and the gap

- **Statement:** "both" is a union of overlapping conditions (Fig 9 :55 — *"A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones"*), so verdicts stay per slot and are never averaged (Fig 1 :19 — *"The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated."*). The single definition is a `gap`: Fig 9 :32 — *"you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself"* and Fig 13 :134 — *"Unreachable is a verdict here, not a silence."*
- **What to check:** is there a case where the carrier condition and the bond condition are disjoint, which would make it a partition? Is there a figure line returning one definition across all three domains? If either, C3 fails.
- **Refuter:** a clean partition with no overlapping case, or a univocal figure definition.

## 3. What is explicitly not claimed

- Not a definition of information. The framework returns a shape, not a name.
- Not "information is physical" as an exclusive verdict, and not "information is purely relational" either. Both one-sided readings were retracted in `01` section 6.
- Not that information is just correlation. The relevant bond is ordered; a symmetric correlation undercharges it (Operators table :59-62).
- Not that meaning is whatever an observer says. The frame must range against an anchor that does not update (Fig 2 :29, :34).
- Not that the two currencies can be summed (Fig 12 :130).
- Not that the observer is a checking seat. That was an error and is recorded as such.
- Not `grounded`. No measurement was used; this packet cannot settle the claim it reports on.

## 5. What would settle it

A source-discriminating instrument, not a further argument. Specifically:

1. A discriminator between "same bit-pattern carried by a physical channel" and "same bit-pattern produced by a shared evidence pool."
2. An experiment that varies the bit-price of a description independently of the cost of settling the typed bond.
3. A channel test that distinguishes the ordered bond from a symmetric correlation: remove the source and ask whether the receiver's state changes.

## 6. What an expert can check in twenty minutes

1. Read Fig 12 :5, :46, :125-126, :130 and Fig 9 :54-57, :32. Six short passages.
2. Check C1's wording of "information" against the figure text character by character. No summary is authoritative.
3. Check C2 against the thermodynamic literature: is the bit-price additive with anything that prices a typed relation?
4. Name one instrument that would discriminate a channel from a shared evidence pool, or say why none is possible.
5. Send the refutation. A failure is more useful than an endorsement.
