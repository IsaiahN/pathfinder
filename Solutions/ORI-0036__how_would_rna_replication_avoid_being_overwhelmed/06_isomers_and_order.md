# Structure, order and analogues: catalyst, cycle, functional group and isomer

**Mode: `specified` on the structures, `gap` on the historical arrangement.** This is a simulated derivation checked against the figures, not a ground reading (see `05_what_shape_means.md` §6).

## RESIDUAL

The primary audit establishes that a defective sequence is a structural absence (Fig 12 `−`), not a refusal (¬), and that any working solution must be a closed catalytic loop with between-room selection. Two questions are owed: (1) what exactly is the structure that carries a replicator and the structure that carries a parasite, and (2) are they isomers, or members of a functional group, or neither? A third question is the order question: what must be settled before what can compose?

## DERIVATION

### Q1. Is a defective sequence a negation? CORRECTION TO FIRST PASS

- Operators table: *"a refutation is a negation, which is why a vocabulary without it can only accumulate"*, and *"Negation is the refusal of a claim."*
- Operators table on the nearest bond: *"A with B removed or absent, which is the absence of an operand."*
- A copied-but-inert molecule does not assert that the catalytic claim is false. It simply does not supply the operand (the catalytic contribution) the loop requires. So it is `−` (absence), not `¬` (refusal).
- **Falsifier, written before the run:** if a defective sequence actively suppresses the catalyst (an interference mechanism, not mere absence), then `¬` becomes the right operator and this correction is itself retracted. The DI-RNA literature records both cases: Pathak and Nagy 2009 describe molecules that *"attenuate the symptoms caused by the helper virus"* (active interference) and also D-RNAs that do not interfere. So the two operators may be needed at different strata. Triangulated (Operators table plus the world source), not smoothed.

### Q2. What is the structure of a working replicator?

- Fig 12 :119 *"catalyst. A part that survives its own use. Spent and regenerating parts behave differently across uses, and nothing records which a term is."*
- Fig 12 :121 *"cycle. The output re-enters as input. The sign of the loop decides whether it closes, and a chain cannot close by construction."*
- Fig 12 :123 *"functional group. A substructure whose presence fixes the outcome. The group says what kind; the qualifier says which member."*
- A working unit is therefore a **catalyst in a cycle**: the catalytic part survives its own use, the output re-enters as the input, and a substructure (the catalytic motif, World: the functional group) fixes the outcome. A linear chain cannot close and so cannot be the unit, even if every step is individually fast.
- **Falsifier:** a purely linear catalytic cascade that maintains function under copying errors as well as a closed loop. If demonstrated, the cycle finding fails and the functional-group claim stands alone.

### Q3. Are a replicator and its parasite isomers?

- Fig 12 :117 *"isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."*
- As **molecules**, a catalytic RNA and a parasitic deletion variant are often close to isomeric in the loose chemical sense: similar nucleotide composition, different arrangement, different behaviour. Pathak and Nagy 2009 report DI-RNAs built from *"noncontiguous portions of their helper virus' genomes"*, which is a rearrangement of the same atom set, and the World calls them *"maximally competitive under that particular environment/selection pressure"*.
- As **terms in the framework**, the answer is weaker. A replicator and a parasite share the atoms {template, copy operation, resource} but differ in the bond that returns the catalytic contribution: the replicator closes the loop (`⇒` back into the cycle), the parasite has the absence (`−`) of that return. Same atoms, different bond, different system-level substance. Under Fig 12 :117 that is a genuine isomer relation **if both are written over the same atom set**.
- If instead they are written as different molecules with different sequences, they share only the functional group (Fig 12 :123): *"the group says what kind; the qualifier says which member."* Both readings are recorded; the stronger isomer claim is the one worth testing.
- **Falsifier, written before the run:** if a parasite can be written without an atom the replicator requires (for example, no shared motif recognised by the same replicase), it is not an isomer over the shared atom set and the functional-group reading stands instead. Colizzi and Hogeweg 2016 model parasites as a separate class with a template advantage, which is a warning that the isomer reading may be too strong in the general case; it is marked `specified`, ungrounded.

### Q4. What is the order? What must be settled before what can compose?

- Fig 12 :104 *"A settled molecule becomes an atom for whatever composes over it. So irreducible is a fact about a level and never about the world, and there is no bottom"*
- The catalytic loop must be **settled against the ground** before it can serve as an atom for a compartment-level or population-level composition. A loop that only replicates its own description, without a function assay paying for it, is not settled and cannot be composed over (Formula step 5: *"the ground settles it. Verified only by what pays. A gate is not the ground."*).
- Dependency order for this question: (1) perception of per-slot function (D4 instrument); (2) vocabulary of catalyst, template, absence, boundary; (3) objective, defined as maintained catalytic function rather than copy count; (4) arrangement, the closed loop plus between-room selection; (5) carry: settled loops become atoms for a population-level loop. This is the Fig 3 chain applied to the molecular case.
- **Falsifier:** show that a population-level arrangement (compartments) can be composed over an unsettled catalytic loop and still pay at the ground. That would break the order and make the chain's break location wrong.

### Q5. Analogues: what transfers and what does not

- **Hardin 1968 (ecology and policy).** The shared template pool is a commons. *"Freedom in a commons brings ruin to all."* What transfers is the structural point that individual rationality at a shared resource destroys the resource; what does not transfer is Hardin's specific remedy (private property or mutual coercion), because molecules do not legislate. Used as a low-overlap analogue, not as evidence for a molecular mechanism.
- **Colizzi and Hogeweg 2016 (spatial theoretical biology).** Travelling waves and empty space make parasitism functional: *"parasites may not be considered a problem for evolution in a prebiotic system, but a degree of freedom..."* This is a direct instance of Fig 7's between-room selection, arrived at independently. It corroborates the shape; it does not name the historical mechanism.
- **Pathak and Nagy 2009 (virology).** Interference can attenuate symptoms. This is a warning that a parasite's valence can invert at the system level, exactly as Fig 11 :50 predicts. It is a different stratum (an infected host, not an origin-of-life protocell) and is not used as a ground reading for ORI-0036.

## CONCLUSION

The working unit is a catalyst in a closed cycle, with the catalytic motif acting as a functional group. A defective sequence is the absence (`−`) of the returning catalytic operand, not a negation (`¬`), unless it actively interferes, in which case both operators may be needed at different strata. Replicator and parasite are isomers over the shared atom set in the strong reading and members of a functional group in the weak reading; the strong reading has a stated falsifier and is ungrounded. Order requires the loop to settle at the ground before it can be composed over. The historical analogue never becomes a name: the mechanism slot stays `gap`.
