## What is being decided, in effect terms

An RNA replicase copies templates *in trans*. The effect that makes this system fragile is that the property which gets a sequence copied (its attractiveness as a template) comes apart from the property which keeps the system going (its contribution as a catalyst). Defective or parasitic sequences are the name we give to that separation. The question asks what would keep the functional signal from being swamped. Option A: improve the replicator in isolation, for example by evolving higher fidelity, hoping errors disappear. Option B: change the habitat so that the valence of a copied sequence is reattached to its catalytic contribution, accepting that error generation cannot be subtracted. The figures decide for B as a shape, and refuse to name which molecular arrangement historically did it.

## What is deliberately not here

- No personal emails of named individuals. The ISSOL contact page publishes personal addresses; this folder does not reproduce them.
- No ranking of people by how likely they are to believe the claim.
- No bulk-send plan.

Three reasons, in the figures' own words:

1. **A room does not upgrade the label.** Fig 2 :35: *"Alignment is triangulation, not negotiation. A mirror cannot correct you."* Agreement among selected believers is the collapse the framework names, not corroboration.
2. **Correlation disqualifies a verdict.** Seat Map :163: *"High correlation is disqualifying for a verdict and efficient for a convention."* Selecting recipients for receptiveness manufactures the correlation that makes their agreement worthless.
3. **Asking for trust before checking spends independence.** Fig 10 :42: *"A verifier that can reconstruct the claim cannot verify it."* Fig 10 :52: *"Bring a characterised residual, never the source."*

Legally: unsolicited bulk outreach to personal addresses breaches GDPR and CAN-SPAM style rules in many jurisdictions. Public opt-in channels avoid that failure and the structural one.
