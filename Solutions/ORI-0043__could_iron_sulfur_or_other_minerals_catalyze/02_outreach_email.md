# Outreach draft: check the shape before you decide about the theory

**Use:** one to one, or as a cover note attached to a submission through a public venue in `03_public_contact_points.csv`. **Not** for bulk send.

**Hard precondition:** send only the stage-1 output below. Do not attach the framework, the eight essays, or this solution folder until a verdict on stage 1 is recorded.

---

**Subject:** One checkable shape claim about mineral catalysis in early metabolism

> Hello,
>
> I am writing about a narrow question in metabolism-first origins research: could iron, sulfur, or other minerals have catalyzed early metabolic reactions? I have one specific output I would like you to check against what you already know. I am not asking you to read a theory first, and I am not asking you to agree with anything. If the output does not survive, there is nothing else to look at.
>
> **The output (`specified` shape, not a measurement):** a positive answer needs three properties at the same time. (1) The mineral survives its own use, so it is a catalyst and not a consumed reagent. (2) The accelerated reactions close a cycle, with products re-entering as inputs. (3) The network is coupled to a spendable difference, such as a pH or redox gradient or H2 generated by FeS precipitation. If any one of these is missing, the claim fails in a nameable way. This is a shape the answer must have, not a new fact about chemistry and not a claim that any particular mineral is the one.
>
> **Verdict recorded before any theory was shown:** the published experiments I have read report that FeS and NiS catalyze carbon fixation steps, that FeS precipitation generates H2 which supports hydrogenotrophic growth, and that an FeS membrane can act as a catalytic boundary at a pH and redox front. On that evidence the empirical answer is yes, with the caveat that a complete pyrites-pulled reverse Krebs cycle remains disputed. I am asking you to check the shape and that caveat, not to endorse a framework.
>
> **What would refute it:**
> 1. a run in which the mineral is consumed stoichiometrically and not regenerated, while the reaction still accelerates;
> 2. a reaction set whose products cannot re-enter as inputs, so no cycle closes;
> 3. a network that requires a continuously supplied activated reagent, with no measurable gradient coupled across the mineral boundary;
> 4. controlled experiments under plausible early Earth conditions that supersede the reports above.
>
> I wrote these down before sending this. A one-line "this fails because..." is more useful than agreement. If you would rather not engage, no reply is needed.
>
> **If it survives, and only then:** I can send the source list and the figures the shape was derived from. The figures, not my summary of them, would be the authority.
>
> **If you want to reuse the method:** I can point to the eight-step loop and ask you to write your own refuter before you run it. A reported failure is more useful to me than a reported success.

## Stage order

1. **Stage 1:** shape claim plus refuter. Ask what they would count as a failure. Record their verdict before any theory is shown.
2. **Stage 2, opt in only:** send the three to five source links and the figure extract. Keep the record of where each claim came from, because convergent derivation and adopted import look identical in their contents.
3. **Stage 3:** offer the formula as a method to reuse, with the instruction that each person writes their own refuter and states their own mode.
