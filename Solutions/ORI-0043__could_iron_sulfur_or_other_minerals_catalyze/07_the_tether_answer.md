# The Tether's answer on mineral catalysis in early metabolism

**Mode: `specified` on the shape the figures govern. The empirical yes is `grounded` by published experiments that were read, not run, in this simulated derivation.** No part of this file is a new ground reading.

## 1. The residual it answers

The question asks whether a mineral could have catalyzed early metabolic reactions. In effect terms: can an environmental structure persist through the reactions it accelerates, so that a set of such reactions closes into a network driven by geochemical energy, before encoded enzymes exist? Fig 1 :19 "The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated." So there is no single answer for "minerals"; there are answers per mineral and per setting.

## 2. The shape

1. **A catalyst is a part that survives its own use.** Fig 12 :119 "catalyst. A part that survives its own use." A mineral that is consumed stoichiometrically is a reagent, not a catalyst, under this figure. First test, and the first link in the dependency chain.
2. **Metabolism is a cycle, not a chain.** Fig 12 :121 "cycle. The output re-enters as input. The sign of the loop decides whether it closes, and a chain cannot close by construction." A linear string of mineral-catalyzed conversions is not yet a metabolism.
3. **The network needs a spendable difference.** Fig 13 :22 "a difference that can be spent"; Fig 13 :46 "What makes a gradient a gradient is that it can be spent". A stock of minerals is not an energy source; a pH or redox front or H2 from FeS precipitation is.
4. **The anchor is the experiment, not agreement.** Fig 2 :29 "An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks. A measurement, an experiment, a counter, a market, a patient outcome." The figures decide the shape. The experiments decide the yes.
5. **Settlement makes a step composable.** Fig 12 :104 "A settled molecule becomes an atom for whatever composes over it. So irreducible is a fact about a level and never about the world." A mineral-catalyzed step that pays against the ground becomes an atom for the network at the next level. No new primitive is owed; Fig 6 :21 "MINT composes inside the closure and can never add an atom."
6. **The mineral name comes from the world, not from the framework.** The framework returns the shape, not the name. FeS, NiS, mackinawite and greigite are bindings from the geochemistry frame, licensed by the experiments in `10_source_ledger.csv`.

## 3. What it explicitly does not say

- Not a figure verdict that "iron catalyzes metabolism". No figure line asserts a chemical fact about iron or sulfur. That fact is ground-settled.
- Not a claim that all minerals behave alike. Fig 1 :19 forbids the aggregate. Slots stay separate.
- Not a claim that a complete reverse Krebs cycle is pyrites-pulled. Camprubi et al. (2017) explicitly question that. The acetyl-CoA route and gradient coupling are the supported parts.
- Not a claim that FeS versus NiS are isomers. They differ in composition; the correct figure object is the functional group (Fig 12 :123). See `06_isomers_and_order.md`.
- Not `grounded` by this derivation. The simulation checks internal consistency only.
- Not an endorsement of any venue, journal or society. Fig 9 :60 "Use filters for the budget and witnesses for the verdict. Never let a filter hand you a verdict."

## 4. What would settle it, and what has settled it so far

The ground needed here is an instrument or experiment on a named mineral slot, not a further argument.

- **Survival test:** recover and quantify the mineral after turnover. Does it remain the same actor (Fig 12 :119)?
- **Cycle test:** do products re-enter as inputs (Fig 12 :121)?
- **Gradient test:** is a spendable difference measurable across the mineral boundary (Fig 13 :22)?
- **Superseding experiment:** controlled early-Earth-condition runs that fail to reproduce the reported FeS or NiS effects would refute the empirical yes.

Published work read in this run reports: (Fe,Ni)S converting CO and CH3SH into an activated thioester that hydrolyzes to acetic acid (Huber and Wächtershäuser, 1997); an FeS membrane at a redox and pH front acting as a semipermeable catalytic boundary (Russell and Hall, 1997); mackinawite and greigite producing abiotic H2 that supports hydrogenotrophic growth and induces acetyl-CoA gene expression (Helmbrecht et al., 2025); a wide range of synthesized iron sulfides producing methanol by reverse water-gas shift in simulated hot springs (Tran, 2024). These are the ground reports. What remains open is the scope caveat, not the existence of the effect.

## 5. The honest stop

**Reached at the world level for several slots, unreached for the general case.** The specific experiments settle specific minerals under specific conditions. They do not settle "all minerals" or the complete reverse Krebs cycle. Indiscernibility is not identity: where the sources do not discriminate among mineral slots, this solution does not merge them. The record stops at the limit of the instruments actually read and records the lineage so the stop is checkable.

## 6. One-paragraph version for outreach

> The Tether does not hand you a list of magic minerals. It gives the shape any positive answer must have: the mineral survives its own use, the reactions close a cycle, and the whole thing rides a spendable geochemical difference such as a pH front or the H2 released when iron sulfide precipitates. That is a constraint, not a chemistry result. The chemistry result comes from the ground, and the published experiments read here report it: FeS and NiS catalyze carbon fixation steps, iron sulfides generate H2 that supports acetyl-CoA based growth, and an FeS membrane can sit at a redox and pH front as a catalytic boundary. The remaining dispute is about one particular complete cycle, not about whether mineral catalysis is possible. What this package offers is the shape, the four refuters written in advance, and the provenance of each claim. The general case stays open until further experiments narrow it.
