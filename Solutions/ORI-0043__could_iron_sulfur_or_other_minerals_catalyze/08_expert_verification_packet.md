# Expert verification packet: mineral catalysis in early metabolism

**What this is:** a claim in a form a chemist or geobiologist can check. Mode, derivation and refuters are stated before the conclusion, so the reader knows what to look for and what would settle it.

**Mode: `specified` on the shape. The empirical yes is `grounded` by published experiments that were read, not run, in this simulated derivation.**

## 1. The claim, in one paragraph

A positive answer to "could iron, sulfur, or other minerals catalyze early metabolic reactions?" requires three properties at once: the mineral survives its own use (so it is a catalyst, not a consumed reagent); the accelerated reactions close a cycle, with products re-entering as inputs; and the network is coupled to a spendable difference such as a pH or redox gradient or H2 from FeS precipitation. The figures govern that shape and no chemical fact. The published experiments read here report that shape for FeS, NiS, mackinawite and greigite in several settings, with one caveat about a complete pyrites-pulled reverse Krebs cycle. This packet asks the recipient to check the shape and the caveat, not to endorse a framework.

## 2. Three checkable sub-claims

### C1 - framework-internal, checkable by reading Figure 12

- **Statement:** a catalyst is defined by survival of its own use; a consumed reagent is not a catalyst under this definition.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 12 :119 "catalyst. A part that survives its own use." The same passage notes that spent and regenerating parts behave differently across uses.
- **What to check:** in the reported FeS or NiS experiments, is the mineral recovered or regenerated, or does it appear in the products? Ask for the mass balance or the spectroscopic before-and-after.
- **Refuter:** stoichiometric consumption without regeneration, while the accelerated rate persists, refutes the catalysis claim for that mineral slot.

### C2 - framework-internal, checkable by reading Figure 12

- **Statement:** metabolism requires a cycle; a chain cannot close. Related mineral clusters are better read as a functional group than as isomers.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 12 :121 "cycle. The output re-enters as input. The sign of the loop decides whether it closes, and a chain cannot close by construction." Also Fig 12 :123 "functional group. A substructure whose presence fixes the outcome. The group says what kind; the qualifier says which member."
- **What to check:** do the accelerated steps form a loop, or a one-off conversion? Is the Fe-S core the shared substructure between mineral catalysis and enzymatic Fe-S clusters (Fig 12 :117 defines isomer as "Same atoms, different bond, different substance", which FeS and NiS do not satisfy)?
- **Refuter:** a reaction set whose products cannot re-enter, requiring a fresh activated reagent each round, refutes the cycle claim. Activity that tracks the whole lattice rather than the Fe-S core refutes the functional-group reading.

### C3 - framework-internal, checkable by reading Figure 13 and Figure 2

- **Statement:** the network needs a spendable difference, and the empirical question is settled by the ground, not by agreement.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 13 :22 "a difference that can be spent"; Fig 2 :29 "An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks. A measurement, an experiment, a counter, a market, a patient outcome."
- **What to check:** is a pH or redox gradient or abiotic H2 production demonstrably coupled to the accelerated steps? Is the reported result an experiment, not a model preference or a consensus?
- **Refuter:** a network that runs only on a continuously supplied activated reagent, with no measurable coupled gradient, refutes the coupling condition.

## 3. What is explicitly not claimed

- Not a figure assertion that iron or sulfur catalyzes anything. No figure line states a chemical fact; the fact comes from the experiments.
- Not a pooled verdict over all minerals. Fig 1 :19 "The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated."
- Not the pyrites-pulled complete reverse Krebs cycle. Camprubi et al. (2017): "the 'iron-sulphur world' hypothesis has had a mixed reception, with questions raised especially about the feasibility of a pyrites-pulled reverse Krebs cycle."
- Not that FeS and NiS are isomers. They differ in composition.
- Not `grounded` by this derivation. It is a simulation checked for internal consistency against the figures.
- Not a request for agreement or endorsement. A failure is more useful than an endorsement.

## 5. What would settle it

1. A survival-of-use measurement on a named mineral slot (C1).
2. A closure demonstration that the products re-enter (C2).
3. A gradient-coupling measurement across the mineral boundary (C3).
4. A controlled early-Earth-condition run that supersedes the published positive reports.

## 6. What an expert can check in twenty minutes

1. Read Fig 12 :119, :121, :123 and Fig 13 :22, four short passages.
2. Check C1 and C2 against one paper you know well. Does the mineral survive, and does the loop close?
3. Name the instrument that would distinguish a catalyst from a consumed reagent in your system, or say why none exists.
4. Send the refutation. A one-line failure is enough.
