## 0. The residual, stated in effect terms

The question asks about a **capability**, not a list of minerals. In effect terms:

> Can an environmental, non-encoded structure persist through a reaction it accelerates, so that a set of such reactions closes into a network that keeps running on geochemical energy before any encoded enzyme exists?

What each option leads to:

- **Option A, yes:** a mineral is a part that survives its own use; reactions re-enter as input; an environmental gradient pays for the network. The mineral is a candidate primitive for a metabolism-first account.
- **Option B, no:** the mineral is consumed stoichiometrically, or the reactions form a chain that cannot close, or no spendable difference drives them. Then mineral catalysis is not a primitive for metabolism and the residual moves to another carrier.

What an answer would look like, bound as an ordinary term before the search (Formula "Direction"): a positive answer must exhibit three properties at once, persistence through use, cycle closure, and coupling to a spendable difference. That binding is what gets tested below. Note that the answer is not being treated as a single slot: Fig 1 :19 "The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated."

## 1. Domain census

R by arity, symmetry and scale, before choosing any figure line.

- **Arity.** The question is nominally one question but is a **union compound** of at least three conjunctive conditions: catalyst persistence, cycle closure, and energy coupling, plus a fourth condition of persistence across changing conditions. Fig 9 names this shape: a bundled problem is "usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones." All four conditions must hold; failing one fails the compound in a specific, nameable way.
- **Symmetry.** The catalyst relation is **not symmetric in use**. A catalyst is the same actor before and after; a reactant is not. Fig 12 :41 distinguishes "same two atoms, three substances" by the bond, and Fig 12 :119 says spent and regenerating parts "behave differently across uses." Direction through the loop matters, so this is a sequence or cycle object, not a conjunction of interchangeable parts.
- **Scale.** Molecular and surface scale for the catalytic event; network scale for metabolism; geological scale for the vent or hot spring gradient. The transform between scales is lossy and must be bracketed (Fig 4 :30).

### One line per domain (actors / currency / anchor)

A domain is admitted only if it has a nameable anchor. Where no nameable anchor exists, no domain is claimed.

| domain | actors | currency | anchor | partition or union |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| D1 mineral surface chemistry | FeS, NiS, iron oxides, sulfur species, mineral surfaces, metal clusters | reaction rate, activation barrier, surface regeneration | a laboratory measurement that the mineral persists and the rate rises (Fig 2 :29) | union member, conjunctive |
| D2 reaction network / metabolism | intermediates, acetyl-CoA or reverse water-gas-shift steps, compartments | flux, cycle closure, regeneration of intermediates | a demonstrated self-sustaining network (Fig 12 :121) | union member, conjunctive |
| D3 energy coupling | pH gradient, redox gradient, thermal gradient, H2 from FeS precipitation | a difference that can be spent (Fig 13 :22) | a measured gradient and demonstrated coupling to the network | union member, conjunctive |
| D4 verification and outreach | experimental geochemists, reviewers, readers | residuals and agreement | the ground, never agreement (Fig 2 :35) | separate frame, not a domain of the chemistry |

**No domain is claimed for "vital spark", "life force", or an unnameable organizer.** No anchor can be named for any of them, so by the census rule they are not domains yet. This is an honest exclusion, not a silence: "Unreachable is a verdict here, not a silence" (Fig 13 :134).

### Dependency order (Fig 3)

Fig 3 :20 "The chain is ordered by dependency, not by importance."

1. Link 1, perception: is there a measurable rate change on a named mineral surface?
2. Link 2, vocabulary: do we have terms for what the surface affords, binding, activation, regeneration?
3. Link 3, objective: what counts as progress here, a cycle that closes and persists?
4. Link 4, planning: which experiment tests persistence, closure and coupling separately?
5. Link 5, learn and carry: name what paid, verify it, carry it to the next mineral.

A reading taken below a broken link is a reading of nothing (Fig 3 :22-23). So the verdicts are ordered: settle D1 before D2, D2 before D3, and only then speak of a primitive metabolism. No averaging across links is permitted.

## 3. Findings

### F1 - The figure names catalysis explicitly, and defines it by survival (decisive for D1)

Fig 12 :119:

> **"catalyst. A part that survives its own use."**

The same passage adds that "Spent and regenerating parts behave differently across uses, and nothing records which a term is." Fig 12 :116 introduces this as one of "Four structures a compositional system exhibits."

This governs the residual directly. "A mineral catalyzed the reaction" is not yet a catalysis claim under the figure; the mineral must be shown to be the same actor before and after, or regenerated. A stoichiometric reagent that happens to accelerate a reaction once is not a catalyst in this sense. This is a `general` structural constraint from the figure. The world test is measurable: weigh or spectroscopically track the mineral before and after turnover.

**Refuter:** a controlled run in which the putative mineral is consumed stoichiometrically and not regenerated, while the reaction still proceeds at the accelerated rate, refutes the claim for that mineral slot.

### F2 - Metabolism is a cycle, and a chain cannot close (decisive for D2)

Fig 12 :121:

> **"cycle. The output re-enters as input. The sign of the loop decides whether it closes, and a chain cannot close by construction."**

A linear sequence of mineral-catalyzed conversions is not a metabolism under this figure. The products must re-enter as inputs, and the sign of the loop determines whether it closes. The relevant world structures are the acetyl-CoA (Wood-Ljungdahl) pathway and the reverse water-gas shift, both of which are described in the world sources as cyclic or pathway-level, not one-off reactions.

**Refuter:** a demonstrated mineral-catalyzed reaction set whose products cannot re-enter as inputs, requiring a fresh external supply of an activated intermediate at every round, refutes the cycle condition for that set.

### F3 - The energy source is a spendable difference, not an inventory (decisive for D3)

Fig 13 :22:

> **"a difference that can be spent"**

Fig 13 :46:

> **"What makes a gradient a gradient is that it can be spent"**

Fig 13 :76 adds that "Energy and a gradient compose a flow." The world sources report a pH and redox front across an FeS membrane (Russell and Hall, 1997) and abiotic H2 generated by FeS and greigite precipitation (Helmbrecht et al., 2025). Those are spendable differences, not just stocks of material. This is why a list of minerals is insufficient: the claim is about a mineral in a gradient.

**Refuter:** a network that runs only when an external activated reagent is continuously supplied, with no coupled gradient measurable across the mineral boundary, refutes the coupling condition.

### F4 - The question is ground-settled, not figure-settled (mode discipline)

Fig 2 :29:

> **"An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks. A measurement, an experiment, a counter, a market, a patient outcome."**

The figures decide the shape (F1 to F3). They do not contain the fact of whether FeS catalyzes carbon fixation. That fact is settled by an experiment. This is the central honesty point of this solution: the Tether output is a shape plus a test list, and the empirical yes reported in section 7 belongs to the experiments, not to this derivation. Mode on the figure-governed claims is `specified`; mode on the world-reported empirical yes is `grounded` as reported, and the simulation itself is neither.

**Refuter:** if a figure line were produced that directly asserted a chemical fact about iron or sulfur, this finding would be wrong. No such line exists in the extract. A search of the full extract this session returned no mineral or catalysis substance claim outside the generic structure list in Fig 12 :116-124.

### F5 - Verdicts are per slot, never averaged

Fig 1 :19 "The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated." Fig 1 :22 "Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average."

There is no single verdict for "minerals." Iron, sulfur, FeS, NiS, iron oxides and mixed metal sulfides are separate slots with separate ground readings. A pooled statement such as "minerals probably helped" would throw away the only signal that says which one pays. This audit therefore keeps D1 verdicts per mineral wherever the sources permit it.

**Refuter:** a result showing identical catalytic behavior and identical regeneration across all tested mineral classes would collapse the slots and would refute the need for per-slot verdicts here. The sources read so far do not show that; they report different mechanisms on land and at depth.

### F6 - No new primitive is owed for the mineral; settlement makes it composable

Fig 6 :21 "MINT composes inside the closure and can never add an atom." Fig 12 :104:

> **"A settled molecule becomes an atom for whatever composes over it. So irreducible is a fact about a level and never about the world."**

Iron and sulfur are not missing primitives that the framework must invent. They are already present in the chemistry frame. What the figures price is the **arrangement**: a mineral held in a gradient, in a cycle, with a persists-through-use record. Once a mineral-catalyzed step is settled against the ground, it becomes an atom for the next level, the network. This is the recursion Fig 12 names, not an import that moves a wall.

**Refuter:** a demonstration that a mineral-catalyzed step cannot be composed into a network without importing a genuinely new primitive would break this finding.

### F7 - The habitat is unmaintained and enumerated, not composed

Fig 11 :36:

> **"Where nothing maintains anything, intelligence is a label applied afterwards to a lineage that met its ground. Nothing was intelligent; selection ran."**

Fig 11 :43 "The habitat is enumerated, never composed." Fig 11 :39 "capability is a property of agent-and-habitat, never of the agent alone."

Prebiotic chemistry is an unmaintained habitat: no seat, no maintainer, and what survives is what the ground kept. So the right move is to enumerate actors (FeS, NiS, H2, CO2, pH), conditions (temperature, acidity, UV) and relations (precipitation, hydrogenation, carboxylation), then read what the ground kept. It is not to compose a story about a "first metabolism" from ingredients.

**Refuter:** evidence of an maintaining seat acting on the prebiotic network before the first encoded catalyst would move this case from the unmaintained panel to the maintained panel and break this finding.

### F8 - The world caveat is a scope note, not a conflict

Camprubi et al. (2017) write that "the 'iron-sulphur world' hypothesis has had a mixed reception, with questions raised especially about the feasibility of a pyrites-pulled reverse Krebs cycle." Under the conflict rule, this sends the analysis back to the census, never flips the verdict. Re-census: the disputed item is the **pyrites-pulled complete reverse Krebs cycle** in D2, not the acetyl-CoA pathway in D1 or the gradient coupling in D3, both of which the same paper supports. So the caveat narrows which loop is claimed; it does not overturn the catalyst finding. Marked as a strain inside D2, not hidden and not smoothed.

**Refuter:** a result showing that no Fe(Ni)S-centered pathway, acetyl-CoA included, can be driven under plausible prebiotic conditions would move the caveat from scope to conflict and would require a new census.

## 4. Pre-registered refuter

Written before the conclusion, per the standing instruction that a falsifier written before the run is a design instrument.

The shape claim is refuted if any of the following holds:

1. A mineral accelerates a reaction but is consumed stoichiometrically and not regenerated, so it fails "catalyst. A part that survives its own use." (Fig 12 :119)
2. The accelerated reactions cannot close a cycle; products do not re-enter as inputs (Fig 12 :121).
3. No spendable difference (Fig 13 :22) is measurably coupled to the network; it runs only on a supplied activated reagent.
4. Ground-level refutation: controlled experiments under plausible early Earth conditions fail to reproduce FeS or NiS catalysis of carbon fixation or H2 generation, superseding the experiments listed in section 7.

If any fires, the claim is withdrawn for that slot or narrowed as indicated. Until a refuter is run against a slot, no part of this may be labelled `grounded` for that slot on the figure side. The world sources are reported as published evidence, not as our own ground reading.

## 5. Recommendation

1. Lead with the shape, not with a list of minerals: persists through use, closes a cycle, couples to a spendable gradient.
2. Keep the four domains' verdicts separate. Do not average iron with sulfur or hot springs with vents.
3. State the mode on every substantive claim. The empirical yes is `grounded` by published experiments; the figure shape is `specified`; this derivation is a simulation.
4. Put the refuter in front of the links, as `02_outreach_email.md` does.
5. Do not let a venue's prestige stand in for the ground. Fig 9 :60: "Use filters for the budget and witnesses for the verdict. Never let a filter hand you a verdict."
