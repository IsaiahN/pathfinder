# Isomers, order and union structure

**Mode: `specified` on the domain structure, `general` on the bond rules.** Simulated derivation checked against the figures, not a ground reading (`05_what_shape_means.md` section 6).

## RESIDUAL

The main audit shows two domains that are a union, and a design frame that must produce a route rather than a list of principles. Three questions are owed: (1) is the union a partition or a compound, (2) are the world mechanisms isomers of one another or members of a functional group, and (3) what order and what bonds must the answer contain?

## DOMAIN CENSUS

Atoms available without import: affected actors (present enfranchised, present powerless, not-yet-born cohorts), a decision, a threshold of who counts, a channel, a record, an anchor, a seat. Bonds available: the seven plus negation. No new primitive is available. The six physical substrate terms are re-censused out of this derivation (strain S1 in `01`), so no claim here depends on calling a constitution a gradient.

## DERIVATION

### Q1: partition or union?

- Fig 9 :54: *"And when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again."*
- Fig 9 :55: *"A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones."*
- Fig 9 :57: *"a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either"*
- A not-yet-born person is temporally absent AND voiceless. A disenfranchised child is contemporaneously powerless AND a future adult. The intersection is a **specific compound**, not a harder case of either condition.
- ⇒ Two loops, not one. An ombudsman loop clears the present-powerless region; a long-horizon loop clears the future region; the intersection needs both in a defined order.
- **Falsifier, written before the run:** if a single measured parameter (for example an effective discount rate) predicts both the present-powerless residual and the future residual with no interaction term, the union reading is false and the two domains collapse into one dial.

### Q2: are the world mechanisms isomers?

- Fig 12 :5: *"A set of ingredients is not one. The bond is the rest of the information, and it is the half nothing was recording."*
- Fig 12 :117: *"isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."*
- Fig 12 :123: *"functional group. A substructure whose presence fixes the outcome."*
- Consider three world bindings: an ombudsman for present powerless people, a future-generations commissioner, and a lotteried citizens' assembly. As described by their public sources they do **not** share a fixed atom set (the ombudsman atom set is complaints and recommendations, the commissioner's is long-horizon impact and reporting, the assembly's is a sampled present public). By Fig 12's definition they are therefore **not isomers**. They are members of a **functional group**: each is a seat ranged against something it cannot move, which is the substructure that fixes the outcome.
- The stronger isomer claim would require the three to be written over a shared atom set `{affected actor, anchor, channel, seat, ruling}` and to differ only in which bond connects them. Under that fixed-atom condition they would be genuine isomers: same atoms, different arrangement, different substance. The fixed-atom condition is **live and unmeasured**; it is a strain, not a result.
- **Falsifier:** if any mechanism can be written without an atom the others require (an assembly with no anchor that does not update, or an ombudsman with no provenance record), the isomer claim fails and only the functional-group claim stands.

### Q3: what order?

- Fig 3 :20: *"The chain is ordered by dependency, not by importance."*
- Fig 12 :104: *"A settled molecule becomes an atom for whatever composes over it. So irreducible is a fact about a level and never about the world"*.
- Order for INS-0074: **perceive the absent actor as a slot** (Fig 1 :19) → **characterise the residual in effect terms** (Fig 9 :30-31) → **find the frame whose closure holds the needed atom** (Fig 6 :23, Fig 8 :53) → **install the channel and check the seat's independence** (Fig 10 :42, :47) → **record the ruling with provenance and a refuter** (Fig 10 :62).
- A settled ruling at one level becomes an atom for the next loop (Fig 12 :104), so the institution is a **cycle** (Fig 12 :121: *"cycle. The output re-enters as input."*), not a one-off report. The sign of the loop decides whether it closes; a chain cannot close by construction.
- The independent seat is a **catalyst** (Fig 12 :119: *"catalyst. A part that survives its own use."*) if its mandate is renewed and its independence is refreshed; a permanent, unrotated seat degrades by the shelf life in the Seat Map.
- **Falsifier:** if the ruling does not re-enter as an input to the next assessment (no recorded residual, no date, no revision), the cycle claim fails and the institution is a chain that emits reports.

### Q4: which bonds must the answer contain?

- Fig 12 :111: *"A vocabulary without bonds can name a state and cannot name a route to one."*
- Fig 12 :112: *"A plan is a sequence. A goal is a comparison. Progress is a subtraction."*
- **Comparison (⋛)**: "who counts" is a threshold relation between two quantities. Operators table: *"a threshold relation between two quantities"*. A franchise is a comparison, which is why a vocabulary without it cannot state who is inside.
- **Sequence (→)**: assessment before ruling, refuse before mint (formula step 2). The order is not decoration; swapping the operands produces a different thing.
- **Subtraction (−)**: progress is a difference between the absent actor's baseline and its measured state. Without it the institution can name a state and cannot say how far along it is.
- **Negation (¬)**: Operators table :58, *"a refutation is a negation, which is why a vocabulary without it can only accumulate."* An institution that cannot write "this claim about the absent actor's interest is false" can only add reports. This is the one operator that is not a bond, and whether an institution can carry it at group scale is an open item.
- **Conjunction (+)** of two conditions is the union: both channels must hold at once; removing either breaks the answer (Operators table :20: *"remove one. Does it still work? Yes."* tests disjunction, not conjunction).
- **Falsifier:** if a working institution can be described entirely with nouns and no typed relation, the bond claim is refuted.

## CONCLUSION

The two halves of the question are a union with a compound intersection (Fig 9 :54-57), not one dial. The world mechanisms are a functional group until the fixed-atom condition is met; under that condition they become a real isomer claim with a stated falsifier. The order is perceive → characterise → import → channel → seat → ruling, and the ruling re-enters as input, making a cycle whose independent seat is a catalyst. The answer must be written with bonds, not a list of principles: a threshold of who counts, a sequence, a subtraction, a negation, and the conjunction of both channels. One live strain (fixed atoms for the isomer claim), two additional falsifiers (single-parameter collapse; no ruling re-entry), none hidden.
