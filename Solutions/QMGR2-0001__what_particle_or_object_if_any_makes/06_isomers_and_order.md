## DERIVATION

### Q1 - Are the candidates isomers?

Fig 12 :117: "isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."

- As the candidates are **commonly written**, a WIMP is a massive, weakly interacting particle; an axion is a light field oscillating in a magnetic field; a PBH is a gravitationally collapsed compact object. These do not share the same atom set. They share, at most, the property "gravitating, non-baryonic mass." Under Fig 12's definition they are **not isomers** as commonly written.
- Fig 12 :124: "functional group. A substructure whose presence fixes the outcome. The group says what kind; the qualifier says which member." The shared substructure is the cross-kind composition `state - structure - state` that gravitates. That is the functional group. "WIMP", "axion" and "PBH" are qualifiers selecting members of it.
- The **stronger form**: if each candidate is written over the full shared atom set `{Space, Time, Energy, Spectrum, Gradient, Network}`, differing only in which structure mediates which state pair, then they satisfy Fig 12 :117 and **are** isomers, same atoms arranged differently. This is the checkable structural claim worth testing, not the loose everyday one.

**Falsifier, written before the run:** if any candidate requires an atom that none of the others hold, and that atom is not one of the six, the isomer claim fails and only the functional-group claim stands. For example, a candidate whose defining feature is not expressible as a cross-kind composition over the six would break it.

### Q2 - What bond joins the candidates, and what bond is "if any"?

- The Operators table defines `+` (conjunction): "both hold at once", symmetric, test "swap the operands. Does the meaning change? No." It defines `∥` (disjunction): "either suffices; not both required", symmetric. Dark matter may be **multi-component**, so the candidate set is a `+` over possible components, not an exclusive `∥`. Writing it as exclusive disjunction silently rules out mixed models, which the source literature explicitly leaves open.
- The "if any" branch (no new substance; gravity is modified) is a **negation** `¬` of the mass-substance claim, not a subtraction `−` of one candidate from another. The Operators table amendment (lines 70-78) is explicit: "Seven of the eight are bonds: they join two operands... Negation joins nothing" and "Subtraction is the nearest bond and is not it. − is A with B removed or absent... Negation is the refusal of a claim." Modified gravity is ¬(new substance); it is not "(dark matter) with the particle removed." Treating it as `−` is the flat-form error Fig 12 names.
- Fig 9 :55: "A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones." The union here is `+` over overlapping candidate components with a `¬` branch, so `01` F5 (unbundle before searching) follows from the bond structure, not just from the prose.

### Q3 - What is the order of the loops?

`→` is sequence ("A then B; B needs A to have happened", Operators table). Candidate existence is **not** a sequence: WIMP does not need axion to have happened. What *is* ordered is the investigation, by dependency (Fig 3):

1. settle the existence of the per-slot mass residual (domain 1, settled);
2. run the identity loop for each candidate against its own ground (domains 2 and 3, open);
3. run the `¬` branch (modified gravity) separately, since it answers a different question (domain 4, open).

Ordering the *candidates* as if they were stages would be adding an ordering the figures do not license, the same error the exemplar found in the QM/GR chain.

### Q4 - Level and irreducibility

Fig 12 :104: "A settled molecule becomes an atom for whatever composes over it. So irreducible is a fact about a level and never about the world, and there is no bottom." If a candidate is ever settled against the ground, it becomes an atom for cosmology composition (structure formation, CMB fitting). Until then it is a candidate term, not a settled operand. This is why the identity verdict cannot be used as a premise for "what made galaxies": that would compose over something never admitted as a part (Fig 12 :106, "Nothing composes over what was never admitted as a part").

## CONCLUSION

The candidates are not isomers as commonly written; they are members of one **functional group** (cross-kind composition that gravitates, with a qualifier naming the member). Over the full shared atom set the stronger **isomer** claim becomes real and checkable, with a falsifier stated above. The candidate union is a **conjunction** `+` over possible components, not an exclusive `∥`; the "if any" branch is a **negation** `¬`, not a subtraction `−`. The investigation is ordered by dependency (existence, then each identity loop, then the negation branch), not by candidate sequence. Irreducibility is level-relative: no candidate may be composed over until the ground settles it.

This derivation needs no new primitive, no import that moves the wall, and no naming of a seventh term. It leaves the identity question open, as it should.

## FALSIFIER (summary)

If any candidate requires an atom outside the six, the isomer claim fails. If mixed models are ruled out by a source gate, the `+` reading fails. If "modified gravity" is shown to be a subtraction of a component rather than a refusal of the substance claim, the `¬` reading fails. Any of these sends the census back to `01` section 1.
