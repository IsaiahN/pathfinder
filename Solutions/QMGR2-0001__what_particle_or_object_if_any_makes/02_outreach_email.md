# Outreach draft: check the shape claim before deciding about the framework

**Use:** one-to-one, or as a cover letter attached to a submission through the public venues in `03_public_contact_points.csv`. **Not** for bulk send.

**Hard precondition:** do not send until a ground-visible reader has checked the expert packet in `08_expert_verification_packet.md` and the verdict on stage 1 has been recorded before any theory is shown.

**Subject:** One checkable claim about the dark-matter residual

> Hi [name],
>
> I have one specific, checkable claim about a residual in your field. I am not asking you to read a framework first, and I am not asking you to agree with anything. If the claim does not survive, there is nothing else to look at.
>
> **The residual (`specified`, not `grounded`):** known physics plus measured baryons misses the observed gravitational mass in several distinct slots: galaxy rotation curves, cluster dynamics, lensing (the Bullet Cluster), the CMB and large-scale structure. The readings are many-to-one: WIMPs, axions, primordial black holes, other compact objects, several components, or modified gravity can produce the same gravitational projection. The reading does not comment on its source. So the open question is a source-discrimination problem, not a missing-number problem.
>
> **The shape claim (`general`):** a framework I am testing returns the shape any answer must have, not a name. That shape is: a constituent that gravitates at the measured strength, is non-baryonic and cold enough to match the CMB and structure, is stable over a Hubble time, and leaves a discriminating signature in at least one instrument that the alternatives do not. The framework explicitly cannot select WIMP versus axion versus PBH. It stops where the instruments stop and records the route. It reports the specific identity as a `gap`, not a silence.
>
> **What would refute the shape claim**, written down before this message:
> 1. deriving the identity of the constituent from the framework's six-term set and figures alone, with no imported particle physics;
> 2. showing the gravitational data alone force a unique source, so no source-discriminating instrument is owed;
> 3. showing the candidate conditions are a partition (mutually exclusive and exhaustive), so the problem need not be unbundled;
> 4. showing that a popularity or prestige ranking has correctly settled a particle identity without a discriminating measurement.
>
> If any holds, I withdraw the claim. A one-line "this fails because..." is more useful to me than agreement. If you would rather not engage, no reply is needed.
>
> **If it survives, and only then:** the sources are below. The figures, not my summary of them, are the authority.
> - Figures and formula, verbatim text with line numbers (local framework extract): `FRAMEWORK/reference/03-FIGURES_TEXT.md`
> - Canonical usage rule (local): `FRAMEWORK/reference/FIGURES_README_canonical.md`
> - Public overview of the architecture: https://medium.com/@IsaiahNwukor/arrival-of-the-fittest-the-tether-architecture-1de7edf641ec
>
> **If you want to use the machinery:** the formula is in the figures link. Run it on a problem you know well and can check against a ground. Write down what would refute your result *before* you run it, and state the mode of what you get. A reported failure is more useful to me than a reported success.

## Stage order

1. **Stage 1:** send the residual, the shape claim and the four refuters. Ask what the reader would count as a failure. Record their verdict before any theory is shown.
2. **Stage 2, opt-in only:** if curious, offer the three links, with the note that the figures are the authority.
3. **Stage 3:** offer the formula as a method to reuse, with the instruction that each user writes their own refuter and states their own mode.

This order follows Fig 10 :52 and Fig 2 :45 ("Frames range against the ground. They do not negotiate with it."). A reader who has absorbed the framework before giving a verdict has joined the frame being checked, so their later agreement is never pooled as corroboration (Fig 2 :40).
