## 1. The residual, stated in effect terms

The decision: can a system form trust verdicts about sources that track the world rather than the sources' bias?

- **Option A**, learn trust by weighting/aggregating sources by their track record or by consensus: leads to the common-cause collapse. When sources draw on one evidence pool, their agreement is the pool talking (Fig 2 :20-21), so the "trust" learned is the bias of the dominant pool, and the drift is coherent and invisible (Fig 2 :34).
- **Option B**, range each source separately against a ground that does not update, buy independence, record provenance, and treat bias as a refusable term: leads to a checkable discipline, with one honest limit, that the system cannot certify from inside that its weights carry zero inherited bias.

**What an answer would look like (bound as an ordinary term, Formula step "Direction"):** a trust assignment that (i) updates only on residuals against a non-updating ground, (ii) measures independence so shared-pool agreement cannot pose as corroboration, (iii) carries provenance so convergence and adopted import are distinguishable, and (iv) can refuse a frame's standing to be composed over (negation, Fig 12 :140). Binding this shape as a term makes the sought-for preference refusable by the same test as everything else, instead of an invisible direction.

**R by arity, symmetry, scale.**
- *Arity:* at least three conditions (trust verdict, bias non-inheritance, self-certification). Union compound, not one hard question (Fig 9 :55).
- *Symmetry:* trust is not a symmetric join. Two sources agreeing do not thereby give two independent pieces of evidence; "Correlation has valence" (Fig 2 :39). Bias inheritance is directional: the belief is "Formed from its own history and from what it inherited, mixed by w" (Symbols table, b).
- *Scale:* per source (per slot), not a global average. Fig 1 :19: *"The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated."* And Fig 1 :28: *"The gap is measured per object, not per step. A system that averages across objects has thrown away the only signal that says where to look."* A single global "trust score" throws away the only signal that says which source to fix.

## 4. Findings F1..F7

### F1 â€” Trust verdicts must be ranged against a ground that does not update (decisive; domain A)

Fig 2 :29: *"An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks."*
Fig 2 :45: *"Frames range against the ground. They do not negotiate with it."*
Fig 2 :35: *"Alignment is triangulation, not negotiation. A mirror cannot correct you."*

A source's trustworthiness cannot be settled by its agreement with other sources. When a source is treated as another frame whose answer moves with what the system believes, it becomes a mirror. The ground for a trust verdict is the outcome that pays: did the source's claim survive contact with a measurement, a counter, a market, a patient outcome? Fig 2 :36: *"An anchor is legitimate when the question is constitutively about it."* Here the question is constitutively about contact with a world, so the ground is legitimate.

**Mode: `general` (structural). Triangulated:** Fig 2 (anchor) and Fig 7 :35 (*"A room's agreement is variation, not a verdict. Selection between rooms is where the ground votes."*) have low overlap: one is a geometry of frames around an anchor, the other a dynamics of handoffs. They converge on the same bar on agreement-as-verdict.

**Refuter:** show a trust verdict that is settled by mutual agreement among frames while every candidate ground is shown to move when the frames push on it (breaks Fig 2 :29-30, :33-34).

### F2 â€” Bias inheritance is the w term plus the shared-pool collapse (domain B)

Symbols table (w): *"How far the belief leans on private history versus inherited prior. w_A + w_B = 1, and w is learnable from the outcomes of past weightings. Two frames with identical architecture diverge because their histories and their w diverge."*
Symbols table (b): *"What the system takes to be the case before it acts. Formed from its own history and from what it inherited, mixed by w. Not the observation."*
Fig 2 :31: *"Independence, between two frames, is how little they draw on the same evidence. Two people reading the same report are not two frames; two instruments pointed at the same thing are."*
Fig 2 :20-21: *"so the agreement is the pool talking, not two frames confirming each other"*
Fig 6 :53: *"searchers drawing on the same evidence are one searcher wearing many names. Independence decides whether more of them buy more search or only more spelling"*

This is the load-bearing distinction. The system **can** learn weights: the Symbols table says w is learnable from outcomes of past weightings. So "learn which sources deserve trust" is possible in principle as weight learning. But **the weight is not the bias removed**. The bias lives in what the frames do not register: if they share an evidence pool, raising the weight of the agreeing set only amplifies the shared pool. Fig 7 :13: *"agreement compounds, evidence does not."* The escape is not more agreeing sources; it is independence bought in proportion to the cost of being wrong (Fig 2 :42: *"Buy independence in proportion to the cost of being wrong."*), plus a record that separates a frame reached independently from one adopted (Fig 8 :51).

Fig 11 :49: *"An actor's valence is relative to the residual, not a property it carries."* and :51: *"No standing list of good actors survives a change of residual."* So "source S is trustworthy" is not a permanent property of S; it is a relation to the current residual. A fixed whitelist is the error the figures forbid.

**Mode: `specified` (the discipline is real but unmeasured here). Triangulated:** Symbols table (w learnable) and Fig 2 :31/Fig 6 :53 (independence) are separate mechanisms: the first is a within-frame mixer, the second a between-frame geometry. Low overlap, same conclusion that reweighting alone cannot buy independence.

**Refuter:** show that increasing the weight of a set of sources whose evidence overlap is demonstrably high increases ground-contact evidence (breaks Fig 2 :40, Fig 7 :13), or show w is not learnable from outcomes (breaks the Symbols table).

### F3 â€” Self-certification of zero inherited bias is underivable from inside (domain C; honest limit)

Fig 8 :34: *"A frame cannot reach its own metaframe."*
Fig 8 :37: *"A frame cannot certify its own limit. Chaitin: beyond a fixed constant, no system proves that any object exceeds its own complexity."*
Fig 8 :39: *"So a search that finds nothing is never a proof of absence."*
Fig 10 :42: *"A verifier that can reconstruct the claim cannot verify it."*

A system cannot certify from inside its own frame that its trust weights carry zero inherited bias. That would be a frame certifying its own metaframe. Searching harder inside cannot settle it, because closure is idempotent: Fig 8 :32, *"no search, however long, adds an atom."* The only legitimate route is an outside frame or a ground (the spectrometer of the analogue), and that route gives a correction on shape, not a self-certificate.

This is the part of the question that cannot be done as stated, and it must be said plainly. *"Unreachable is a verdict, not a silence."* (figures README, canonical limit). Mode: `gap` for self-certification from inside; the discipline of A and B remains.

**Refuter:** exhibit a system that certifies, from inside its own frame, that its weights contain no inherited prior (breaks Fig 8 :34/37). Note: an external audit is not a refuter of this finding; it is the prescribed import.

### F4 â€” The question is a union compound; unbundle before searching

Fig 9 :55: *"A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones."*
Fig 9 :54: *"And when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again."*
Fig 9 :60: *"Use filters for the budget and witnesses for the verdict. Never let a filter hand you a verdict."*

Three questions are bundled: can weights be learned (yes, in principle); can bias be kept from being inherited (only by independence and provenance, never by consensus); can the system certify it succeeded (no, from inside). A single lever (e.g. "train on more diverse sources") clears the region where only one condition holds and leaves the intersections standing, because the second condition still binds (Fig 9 :55-58). Keep the verdicts separate.

**Mode: `general`. Triangulated:** Fig 9 (union/partition) and Fig 1 :19/:28 (per-slot residual) are independent: one is about problem structure, the other about measurement granularity. Both forbid the global averaged answer.

**Refuter:** show the three conditions are one condition such that clearing any one clears all (breaks Fig 9 :55-58).

### F5 â€” Bias must be a refusable term, not merely reweighted

Fig 12 :140: *"A vocabulary that cannot refuse can only accumulate."*
Fig 5 :56: *"Requires an operator for negation, which the seven bonds do not contain."* (amendment on the fifth gap)
Operators table :58: *"a refutation is a negation, which is why a vocabulary without it can only accumulate"*
Fig 6 amendment :42: *"A refutation adds a term that refuses; it removes nothing, and the reach falls because something was added rather than because anything was deleted."*

A system holding only weights can accumulate trust and cannot refuse a source's standing to be composed over. To stop inheriting a frame's bias, the frame must become a refusable term: the system must be able to add a term that says this source's claim does not hold, and thereby lose the reach that depended on it. This is why "downweight the biased source" is insufficient; downweighting keeps the frame composable. Refusal is structural, and the record holds both a term and its refusal so the fall is computable rather than stipulated.

**Mode: `general`. Single-source for the mechanism** (Fig 12 amendment + Operators table + Fig 6 amendment are one framework family), but the negation requirement is explicit and checkable in the verbatim text.

**Refuter:** show a vocabulary with weights but no negation refusing a source's standing to be composed over, or show a refusal deletes a term from the record (breaks Fig 6 :42-46, Fig 12 :140).

### F6 â€” Source reputation is a stake-inducing channel, not a trust signal

Fig 9 :51: *"what the verifier may see: evidence bearing on the residual is admissible; evidence bearing only on its maker is a stake-inducing channel, and the verifier is denied it"*
Fig 10 :93: *"A ground with a reputation to protect is a ground someone benefits from, and its keeper is thereby disqualified."*
Fig 10 :59: *"an interest in the outcome is unavoidable, an interest in the reading is disqualifying."*
Fig 10 :60: *"Where a stake cannot be removed, declare it."*

Trust in a source because of its reputation, authority, or prestige is evidence about the maker, not about the residual. It is exactly the channel the verifier is denied. And a source that has a reputation to protect cannot serve as the ground that settles the question, because its keeper benefits from the answer. The honest move is to declare the stake and exclude it from the verdict, not to average it in.

**Mode: `general`. Triangulated:** Fig 9 :51 (admissible evidence) and Fig 10 :93 (ground with reputation) are separate moves, one about input channels, one about who may occupy the anchor. The RSI trap's *"Prestige can fund an action. It can give an action a budget to be tried. But it cannot settle an action. Only the ground can settle it."* corroborates from the essays (high overlap, author).

**Refuter:** show that a source's reputation, with no bearing on the residual, settles a trust verdict against a non-updating ground (breaks Fig 9 :51, Fig 10 :93).

## 5. Pre-registered refuter (written before the conclusion)

Required by seat reasoning: a falsifier written before the run is a design instrument. The shape claim (partly yes, as a discipline, with a stated limit) is refuted if any of the following holds:

1. A system certifies from inside its own frame that its trust weights carry zero inherited prior (breaks the metaframe limit, Fig 8 :34/37).
2. Agreement among sources shown to share one evidence pool is demonstrated to add ground-contact evidence (breaks Fig 2 :40, Fig 7 :13).
3. Evidence bearing only on a source's maker or reputation is shown to settle a trust verdict against a non-updating ground (breaks Fig 9 :51, Fig 10 :93).
4. A vocabulary with weights but no negation is shown to refuse a source's standing to be composed over (breaks Fig 12 :140, Operators :58).

If any fires, the corresponding finding is withdrawn. Until one is run, no part of this may be labelled `grounded`.

## 6. Recommendation

1. Answer the question as a **union**, not a yes/no. "Learn trust" and "certify no bias" are different questions with different answers (F4).
2. Learn weights from ground-contact outcomes; this is possible in principle (F2, Symbols w). Do not pretend the weight is the bias removed.
3. Range each source separately against a non-updating ground; buy independence in proportion to the cost of being wrong; record provenance (F1, F2).
4. Exclude source reputation and prestige from the verdict channel; declare unavoidable stakes (F6).
5. Give the system a negation operator so bias can be refused rather than only downweighted (F5).
6. State the limit honestly: self-certification of zero bias is unreachable from inside. Offer an outside frame or instrument instead (F3). "Unreachable is a verdict, not a silence."
