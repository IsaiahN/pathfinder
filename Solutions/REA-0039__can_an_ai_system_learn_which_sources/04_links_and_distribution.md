## Three findings in the wider literature that bear directly

1. **Bias inheritance is a measured engineering failure, not a metaphor.** Shumailov et al. show that recursively generated training data degrades a model and erases distribution tails. This is the independent, low-overlap witness for the claim that inheriting a source's output inherits its compression and loses what the source could not represent. It corroborates Fig 7 :13 (*"agreement compounds, evidence does not"*) and the Symbols table's w being a mixer of inherited prior. It does **not** corroborate the framework's specific six-term vocabulary.
2. **Human source trust is a calibrated vigilance, not a fixed list.** The epistemic-vigilance literature shows trust is adjusted by perceived competence and benevolence, and is fooled by authority and consensus cues (ad verecundiam, ad populum). This corroborates Fig 11 :51 (*"No standing list of good actors survives a change of residual"*) and Fig 9 :51 (maker evidence is a stake-inducing channel). It is a separate evidence pool from the framework essays.
3. **Source discrimination needs an instrument.** The Hard Problem is About Source argues that a reading which cannot distinguish sources demonstrates its own inadequacy, and that the spectrometer resolved the case. This is the analogue for the F3 limit: the way past "we cannot certify zero bias" is a discriminating instrument or outside frame, not more internal search.
