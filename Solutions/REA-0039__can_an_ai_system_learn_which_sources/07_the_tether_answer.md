# The Tether's answer: can an AI learn source trust without inheriting bias?

**Mode: `general` on the structural shape, `specified` on the tractable discipline, `gap` on self-certification of zero inherited bias. Not `grounded`.** Simulated derivation checked against the figures (`05_what_shape_means.md` Â§7), not a ground reading.

## 1. The residual it answers

The question bundles three conditions, not one. Can a system learn weights for sources from outcomes (A)? Can it keep a source's bias from being inherited (B)? Can it certify that it succeeded (C)? Fig 9 :55: *"A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones."* A single lever (more data, more sources, more consensus) clears the region where only one condition holds and leaves the intersections standing. Verdicts stay separate and are never averaged (Fig 9 :60).

## 2. The shape

1. **Weights are learnable.** The Symbols table defines w as *"How far the belief leans on private history versus inherited prior. w_A + w_B = 1, and w is learnable from the outcomes of past weightings."* So "learn which sources deserve trust" has a real, finite sense: learn the weight from outcomes. This is `specified`, structural.
2. **The weight is not the bias removed.** Bias inheritance is the shared-evidence-pool collapse. Fig 2 :40: *"disqualifying for a verdict: agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool."* Fig 6 :53: *"searchers drawing on the same evidence are one searcher wearing many names."* Raising the weight of an agreeing set amplifies the pool; it does not add ground contact. Fig 7 :13: *"agreement compounds, evidence does not."*
3. **Trust must be ranged per source against a ground that does not update.** Fig 2 :29: *"An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks."* Fig 2 :45: *"Frames range against the ground. They do not negotiate with it."* A single global trust score throws away the only signal that says which source to fix (Fig 1 :19, :28).
4. **Independence must be bought and provenance recorded.** Fig 2 :42: *"Buy independence in proportion to the cost of being wrong."* Fig 8 :51: *"Convergent derivation and adopted import look identical in the contents. Only the record of where each came from separates them."* Without the record, a source reached independently and one adopted from a pool look the same.
5. **Bias must be refusable, not only downweighted.** Fig 12 :140: *"A vocabulary that cannot refuse can only accumulate."* A system holding only weights keeps the frame composable; it needs negation to refuse a source's standing to be composed over.
6. **There is no permanent trusted-source list.** Fig 11 :49: *"An actor's valence is relative to the residual, not a property it carries."* and :51: *"No standing list of good actors survives a change of residual."* A source's trust is a relation to the current question, not a property of the source.
7. **Self-certification of zero bias is unreachable from inside.** Fig 8 :37: *"A frame cannot certify its own limit."* A system cannot certify from its own frame that its weights carry no inherited prior. That is the `gap` and it must be stated, not hidden.

## 3. What it explicitly does not say

- Not "yes by consensus." Shared-pool agreement is disqualifying for a verdict (Fig 2 :40), not a weak form of evidence.
- Not "no, impossible." Weight learning is real (Symbols w), and the independence/provenance discipline is tractable; only self-certification fails.
- Not a numeric trust score or a whitelist. Those are names where the framework returns a shape.
- Not that reputation is evidence. Fig 9 :51: *"evidence bearing only on its maker is a stake-inducing channel, and the verifier is denied it."*
- Not that the framework can validate its own trust calibration. Fig 10 :42: *"A verifier that can reconstruct the claim cannot verify it."*
- Not `grounded`. No measurement here settles a trust verdict.

## 6. One-paragraph version for outreach

> The Tether does not answer "yes" or "no" to whether an AI can learn source trust without inheriting bias, because that question bundles three questions with different answers. Weights can be learned from outcomes, and that part is real. But the weight is not the bias removed: when sources share an evidence pool, their agreement is the pool talking, so more agreement compounds the bias instead of adding evidence. To keep bias from being inherited, a system must range each source separately against a ground that does not change when the system believes something, buy independence in proportion to the cost of being wrong, record provenance so a source reached independently can be told from one adopted from a pool, and carry a negation operator so a biased frame can be refused rather than merely downweighted. There is also no permanent trusted-source list, because a source's trust is relative to the question. What the system cannot do is certify from inside its own frame that it has zero inherited bias; that is a limit, stated as a limit, and the way past it is a source-discriminating instrument or an outside frame, not more internal search. What the framework offers is that shape, the refuters written in advance, and the honest stop.
