**Subject:** Check one output about source trust before you decide whether the framework is worth reading

> Hi [name],
>
> I have one specific output I'd like you to check against what you already know. I am not asking you to read the framework first, and I am not asking you to agree with it. If the output does not survive, there is nothing else to look at.
>
> **The question, in effect terms:** can a system assign trust to sources that tracks the world rather than the sources' own bias? There are three different questions bundled here, and they do not have one answer.
>
> **The output (`general` on the shape, `specified` on the discipline, `gap` on the last part):**
> 1. A trust verdict has to be ranged against something that does not change its answer because of what the system believes. Agreement among sources cannot be that something. Fig 2 :29 defines the anchor: *"An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks."* When sources share an evidence pool, their agreement is the pool talking, not two independent confirmations.
> 2. The system can learn weights from outcomes, but the weight is not the bias removed. Independence has to be bought, and provenance recorded, or a reweighting just amplifies the shared pool. *"agreement compounds, evidence does not"* (Fig 7 :13).
> 3. The system cannot certify from inside its own frame that it carries zero inherited bias. That would be a frame reaching its own metaframe (Fig 8 :34). I state this as a limit, not a failure to try harder.
>
> **What I am not claiming:** that consensus among many sources produces trust; that a source's reputation is evidence about its reliability (it is evidence about its maker, a channel the verifier is denied, Fig 9 :51); that there is a permanent list of trustworthy sources (valence is relative to the residual, Fig 11 :49, :51); or that this framework is the only true one.
>
> **What would refute the shape claim:**
> 1. a system certifying from inside that its weights carry no inherited prior;
> 2. agreement among sources with demonstrably shared evidence shown to add ground-contact evidence;
> 3. source reputation alone shown to settle a trust verdict against a non-updating ground;
> 4. a system with weights but no negation shown to refuse a source's standing to be composed over.
>
> I wrote these down before sending this. If any holds, I retract the claim. A one-line "this fails because..." is more useful to me than agreement. If you'd rather not engage, no reply is needed.

## Two-stage offer, adapted

- **Stage 1:** send the union split, the three findings, and the four refuters. Ask what the recipient would count as a failure. Record their verdict **before** showing any framework.
- **Stage 2, opt-in only:** if curious, offer the three links. The figures, not the summary, are the authority. Their later agreement is never pooled as corroboration (Fig 2 :40).
- **Stage 3:** offer the formula as a method to reuse, with the instruction that each user writes their own refuter and states their own mode.
