## What this question is, in effect terms

The question asks whether a system can form trust verdicts about sources that track the world rather than the sources' own bias. Option A (learn trust by weighting and aggregating sources by track record or consensus) leads to the common-cause collapse: agreement from a shared evidence pool is the pool talking, and the bias is inherited through the weighting. Option B (range each source separately against a ground that does not update, keep frames independent, record provenance, and treat bias as a refusable term) leads to a checkable discipline with one honest limit: the system cannot certify from inside that it carries zero inherited bias. An answer would look like a trust function that updates only on ground-contact residuals, distinguishes convergence from import by provenance, and can refuse a source's standing to be composed over. That shape is bound as an ordinary term in `01_figure_grounded_audit.md` (Formula step "Direction").

## What is deliberately not here

- No personal emails of named individuals.
- No ranking of people by how likely they are to believe the claim.
- No bulk-send plan.

Three reasons, in the figures' own words:

1. **Agreement is not a ground.** *"Alignment is triangulation, not negotiation. A mirror cannot correct you."* (Fig 2 :35). Selecting recipients for receptiveness manufactures the exact correlation that disqualifies their agreement as a verdict.
2. **A shared evidence pool is one witness.** *"agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool"* (Fig 2 :40).
3. **Asking for trust before checking spends independence.** *"A verifier that can reconstruct the claim cannot verify it."* (Fig 10 :42).
