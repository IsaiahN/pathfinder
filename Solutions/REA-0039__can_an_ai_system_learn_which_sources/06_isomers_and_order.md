### Q1 â€” Is the question one question or a union?

- Fig 9 :55: **"A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones."**
- Fig 9 :56 (continuation): a single lever clears the region where only one condition holds and leaves the intersections standing, because the second condition still binds.

- Apply: the three conditions are (A) can weights be learned from outcomes, (B) can bias be kept from being inherited, (C) can the system certify it succeeded. ### Q2 â€” What is the dependency order?

### Q3 â€” Are two systems with the same architecture but different sources isomers?

- Fig 12 :117: **"Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."**
- Symbols table (w): **"Two frames with identical architecture diverge because their histories and their w diverge."** And w is **"learnable from the outcomes of past weightings."**
- A system's architecture is the atom set. Its history and its weighting w play the role of the bond: same atoms, different arrangement of evidence and inherited prior, different trust behavior. The difference is invisible in the architecture description and decisive in which sources the system trusts.
- So "same model, different sources, different bias" is a real **isomer** claim in the figure's sense: same atoms, different bond, different substance. This is why two systems with identical code can disagree about trust without either being defective; their w and histories diverged (Fig 2 :31: two people reading the same report are not two frames).
- Fig 12 :119: **"A part that survives its own use. Spent and regenerating parts behave differently across uses, and nothing records which a term is."** A source that is reconsulted and regenerates its evidence behaves differently across uses from one that is spent; a trust system that does not record which is which cannot tell earned credibility from replayed prestige.
- Verdict: the isomer reading is **licensed** (`general`), with the physics-side check unmeasured. Triangulated for the structure (Fig 12 :117 plus Symbols w); single-source for the claim that history and w are the binding.

### Q4 â€” What analogue settles the source question?

- *The Hard Problem is About Source* (Isaiah N, Substack, 2026-08-28): *"The instrument is speaking only about what it measured, it cannot comment on the source."* And: *"It was proven wrong not by argument, but by the arrival of a better instrument."*
- The analogue: a trust reading that cannot discriminate ground contact from shared-pool agreement demonstrates its own inadequacy. The way past F3 is not a better argument inside the frame but a discriminating instrument or outside frame.
- Independent corroboration: Shumailov et al. (Nature, 2024) show that recursive training on generated data erases distribution tails, i.e. the signal that would have discriminated ground from pool is lost by generational recompression. This is a different evidence pool from the essays.
- Verdict: the analogue is a **source-discriminating instrument**, not a consensus mechanism. `specified` on what it must do; no such instrument is supplied here.

## Falsifier, written before the run

The union/order/isomer structure is refuted if:

1. The three conditions are shown to be one, such that clearing any one clears all (breaks Fig 9 :55-58).
2. Self-certification is shown to be achievable by effort in A or B alone without an import (breaks Fig 3 :20, :27 and Fig 8 :37).
3. Two systems with identical architecture, history, and w are shown to diverge in trust purely by chance with no difference in bonded evidence (breaks the Symbols table's w and Fig 12 :117).
4. A trust reading that cannot discriminate ground contact from shared-pool agreement is shown to settle a source verdict anyway (breaks the source analogue and Fig 9 :51).

If any fires, the corresponding claim is withdrawn. None was run in this simulation.
