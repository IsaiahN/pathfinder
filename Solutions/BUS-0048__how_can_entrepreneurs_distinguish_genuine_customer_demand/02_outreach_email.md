**Use:** one-to-one, or as a cover letter attached to a submission through the public venues in `03_public_contact_points.csv`. **Not** for bulk send.

**Hard precondition:** do not send until the stage-1 output below survives a refuter check by someone whose ground contact is real. The offer collapses on contact if the output does not survive.

**Subject:** Check one output before you decide whether the framework is worth reading

> Hi [name],
>
> I have one specific output I would like you to check against what you already know about customer discovery. I am not asking you to read the theory first, and I am not asking you to agree with it. If the output does not survive, there is nothing else to look at.
>
> **The output (`specified`, not `grounded`):** the question is not how much interest a product attracts. It is why the gap between what an interest signal predicts and what a customer actually pays stays large in some customer-slots and closes in others. The signal is measured per customer, never as a pooled total. From the figure that governs the anchor, the thing that settles "will this customer pay" is a counter or a market: money changing hands at a stated price. Enthusiasm, signups and survey answers are instruments, not the anchor, because they move when the founder pushes on them and they can be wrong on the axis they do not measure. There is also a world result behind this: in large field studies, asking people about purchase intent increased the measured intent-to-purchase correlation by 58 percent among the people who were asked, compared with otherwise identical people who were not asked. The measurement changed the reading.
>
> The discriminator we derive is a shape, not a product. It has two conditions that must both hold and have different failure modes: a channel to the paying ground that stays unpooled and is not moved by the asking, and a signal that costs the customer something, so it can be ranged against that ground. Where neither can be run, the answer stops as a declared gap rather than a guess.
>
> **What I am not claiming:** that any particular survey question, waitlist, pre-order page, price or deposit is *the* answer. Those are bindings from the startup-practice frame. The framework returns a kind of thing, never the answer itself. I am also not claiming a theory of everything about markets, and I am not claiming the framework is the only true account of customer demand.
>
> **What would refute the shape claim:**
> 1. an interest signal with no payment and no cost to the customer that prospectively predicts which named customers will pay at a stated price through a change of habitat;
> 2. a controlled survey versus no-survey comparison showing zero measurement reactivity in the target category;
> 3. a pooled aggregate interest count that predicts the next named purchaser better than a per-customer reading;
> 4. a zero-cost signal that discriminates purchasers from non-purchasers as well as a payment across matched slots;
> 5. a single lever that restores both conditions with no categorically different residue left over.
>
> I wrote these down before sending this. If any one holds, the shape is wrong and I will retract it. A one-line "this fails because..." is more useful to me than agreement. If you would rather not engage, no reply is needed.
>
> **If it survives, and only then:** the sources are below. The figures are the authority, not my summary of them.
> - The figure set and formula for direct download: https://github.com/IsaiahN/tether/tree/general-agent/figures
> - Full architecture (19 min): https://medium.com/@IsaiahNwukor/arrival-of-the-fittest-the-tether-architecture-1de7edf641ec
> - Worked narrative and the six-term derivation: https://adventuresinml.substack.com/p/how-intelligence-actually-works
>
> **If you want to use the machinery:** the formula is in the figures link. Run it on a discovery problem you know well and can check against a ground. Write down what would refute your result *before* you run it, and state the mode of what you get. The framework's own rule is that a loop cannot check the direction of its own question, so a reported failure is more useful to me than a reported success.

## Two-stage offer, recorded explicitly

**Stage 1.** Send the residual, the shape claim and the five refuters. Ask what the recipient would count as a failure. Record that verdict **before** any framework material is shown. A Track B conversation reply is a conversation, not corroboration.

**Stage 2, opt-in only.** If the recipient is curious, offer the three links, with the note that the figures, not the summary, are the authority. Their later agreement is never pooled as corroboration (Fig 2 :40, *"agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool"*).

**Stage 3.** Offer the formula as a method, with the instruction that each user writes their own refuter and states their own mode.
