## Q1 - Are interested non-buyers and paying customers isomers?

> **"isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."** (Fig 12 :117)
> **"functional group. A substructure whose presence fixes the outcome. The group says what kind; the qualifier says which member."** (Fig 12 :124)
> **"Symmetry is what decides whether an arrangement is a new thing."** (Fig 12 :92)
> **"A set of ingredients is not one. The bond is the rest of the information, and it is the half nothing was recording."** (Fig 12 :5)

Take two people who both visited the site, joined the waitlist, clicked the email and told a friend they liked the idea. The ingredient lists are the same. If one of them paid a deposit and the other did not, the bond between those atoms differs: for one, attention is composed with money at a price; for the other, attention is composed only with free expressions. Under Fig 12 :117 they are **isomers**: same atoms, different bond, different behaviour, and the difference is invisible in the resource or engagement list. This is the figure-grounded version of "it is not what they clicked, it is what they gave up", but stronger, because the arrangement is itself a substance and carries information the ingredients do not.

If the two share only a pattern, `customer → signal → response`, while differing in which atoms occupy the slots, the weaker claim holds: they are members of the same **functional group** (Fig 12 :124). The presence of the pattern fixes the kind, not the outcome.

The distinction is testable. An engagement-list comparison that finds no difference between two customers cannot distinguish isomer from functional-group; the bond has to be recorded. Fig 12 :117 says the difference is *"invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour"*, so a paid conversion is exactly the behaviour in which it becomes visible.

**Falsifier, written before the run:** if two customers with identical atoms and identical bonds (both free, or both paid) nevertheless diverge in purchase with no difference in ground contact or in the price they faced, the isomer framing is insufficient and the difference must be habitat (D2), which moves the explanation out of D4.

**Status:** `specified`, triangulated for the legality of the reading (Fig 12 and Fig 11 :49, different figures), single-source for the business application. World corroboration is the conversion specificity in Morwitz, Steckel and Gupta 2007 (trial rates and brand-level questions beat totals and category-level questions), but that is the same broad marketing pool as the rest of the business witnesses.

### Q1b - The flat score is the named notation failure

> **"the ingredients are recorded and the arrangement is not"** (Fig 12 :14)
> **"nothing signals that anything was lost"** (Fig 12 :16)
> **"→ the parts are not the thing"** (Fig 12 :17)

A single "engagement score" that adds signups, clicks, page views and survey enthusiasm is a flat formula. It records the ingredients and not the bond, so nothing downstream can tell a free click from a payment. Fig 12 :14-17 is the direct figure warrant for refusing it as a demand measure. It is not that the score is useless as a budget filter; it is that it cannot be a witness (Fig 9 :60, *"Use filters for the budget and witnesses for the verdict. Never let a filter hand you a verdict."*).

## Q2 - What order do the conditions bind in?

> **"The chain is ordered by dependency, not by importance. Each link consumes what the one above produces, so effort spent above the break arrives at a step that cannot use it."** (Fig 3 :20-21)
> **"Optimising a link that already works cannot rescue one that does not."** (Fig 3 :19)

Order for a founder facing a possible demand gap:

1. **Perception.** Can the founder read a customer response at all? Failure here is visible and has obvious tests.
2. **Vocabulary.** Does Γ have terms for what the customer affords: trying, paying, returning, referring? This is a middle link.
3. **Objective.** Can the founder state a comparison that would count as getting somewhere? Fig 12 :112: *"A plan is a sequence. A goal is a comparison. Progress is a subtraction."* An interest-only vocabulary can say "they liked it" and cannot say "they paid above our price".
4. **Planning.** Which probe to run, under incomplete information.
5. **Learn and carry.** Name what worked, verify it, carry it to the next segment.

The two conditions from the primary audit map onto this chain. C1 (unpooled contact) is mostly a failure of links 1 and 2, and of the channel's upkeep. C2 (costly commitment) is mostly a failure of link 3, because the objective was set to "interest" rather than to a comparison against a price. That mapping is why a single lever cannot clear both (Fig 9 :55).

The order also decides which instrument to buy first. Fig 3 :27: *"A link with no instrument is not a link that works. It is a link nobody has looked at."* Link 1 has analytics and CRM data; link 3 has almost none. Budget therefore piles up at link 1 and link 4 while the break sits at 2 and 3.

The probe sequence itself is an ordered bond, not a conjunction. *"A then B; B needs A to have happened"* (Fig 12 :72) describes interest → commitment → payment → repeat. Writing them with `+` (conjunction) would assert that all four hold at once, which is the flat-score error above.

**Falsifier:** a founder whose chain demonstrably works at 1, 2 and 3 and fails only at 4. That would falsify the "usually middle" claim of F6 in the primary audit.

## Q3 - Competing explanations, and what each can and cannot reach

| candidate explanation | what it names | figure-line bearing | what it cannot reach |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Validated learning / Lean Startup** (Ries 2009) | the build-measure-learn loop, actionable metrics | close to C1 and Fig 3 link 5; *"turn ideas into products, measure how customers respond, and then learn whether to pivot or persevere"* | It is a business-pool binding, not a derivation. It does not say what would refute it, and it does not separate C1 from C2. Its own "validated learning" can be gamed by the flat score |
| **Purchase-intention research** (Morwitz, Steckel, Gupta 2007; Hulland and Houston 2021) | the intention-behavior gap and its moderators | matches F1 and F3: specificity beats volume; intention alone is not a verdict | It measures stated intent, which self-generated validity shows can be changed by the asking. It cannot be the anchor |
| **Self-generated validity** (Chandon, Morwitz, Reinartz 2005) | measurement reactivity | matches F2 and Fig 2 :33-34, *"The anchor must not update"* | It explains why asking moves the reading; it does not supply a substitute instrument |
| **Hypothetical bias / stated WTP** (Kanya et al. 2019; List and Gallet 2001) | systematic overstatement of hypothetical willingness | matches F4's weaker form: stated valuation is not revealed valuation | Mostly a health and environmental economics pool; no founder-level measurement here. Two reviews partly share a pool, so one witness plus a related one |
| **Costly signalling / handicap principle** (Penn and Számadó 2020) | cost as the guarantor of honest signals | **conflicts** with a naive F4: cost is *"neither sufficient nor necessary"* for honesty | Returned to census; F4 narrowed to "a free signal is not a reading of WTP". It cannot license a claim that payment proves quality |
| **Market size / TAM** | addressable spend | part of D2 habitat | Fig 9 :60: a budget filter, not a witness. It says nothing about which named slot pays |
| **Founder intuition / "gut feel"** | a private reading inside the founder's Γ | frame-internal, Fig 1 :27 | *"A frame cannot score itself with a quantity it also produces."* Excluded as an anchor |
| **Luck / a lucky early yes** | an unearned early reading that persists while unfuted | Fig 8 :40-41: *"Underivable-in-principle and unreachable-at-this-budget are different facts"* | Cannot be excluded from inside a frame; Fig 8 :39: *"So a search that finds nothing is never a proof of absence."* Honest `gap`, not a rival verdict |

No candidate is discarded by prestige or familiarity. Each is ranged against the same residual, in the order Fig 8 :53 requires. The point is not to pick one; it is to notice that the first four are all in one broad business and marketing pool (Fig 2 :31), that the fifth is a genuine conflict from a low-overlap field, and that the two economics reviews share part of their evidence pool.

## Q4 - Does the shape generate the literature's answers?

Partly. The shape can generate:

- the intention-research emphasis on specific, near-term, brand-level questions, as the per-slot reading under a non-reactive instrument;
- the practitioner emphasis on *"the thing customers want and will pay for"*, as C2 and Fig 5 :32;
- the self-generated-validity warning, as Fig 2 :33-34;
- the stated-versus-revealed-willingness gap, as F4's weaker form;
- the habitat emphasis of market sizing, as D2.

It cannot generate the *name* of the specific probe or price that will work. That is the `gap` recorded in F9 of `01`. It is a gap at the instrument boundary, not a silence: Fig 13 :134 (*"Unreachable is a verdict here, not a silence,"*) and Fig 8 :39 apply. The essay's source rule states why: an instrument that reads a result *"cannot comment on the source"*, and *"The answer is unreached until a better instrument exists."*

## CONCLUSION

An interested non-buyer and a paying customer with the same atoms are isomers under Fig 12 :117 if the bond differs, and members of one functional group under Fig 12 :124 if they share only the pattern. A flat engagement score is the notation failure named in Fig 12 :14-17, so it is a filter and never a witness. The conditions bind in dependency order, with the vocabulary and objective links the usual breaks, which is why C1 and C2 need different instruments. The standard explanations are mostly bindings of one evidence pool and are generated by the shape only up to the point where a name would be required. The one low-overlap witness that conflicts (Penn and Számadó) was returned to the census and narrowed the claim rather than flipping the verdict. The isomer claim has a written falsifier; the order claim has one; the naming gap is declared rather than filled.
