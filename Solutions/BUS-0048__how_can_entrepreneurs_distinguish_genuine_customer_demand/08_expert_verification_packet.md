# Expert verification packet - the Tether on genuine customer demand

**What this is:** a claim in a form an entrepreneurship researcher or customer-discovery practitioner can check. Mode, derivation and refuters are stated before the conclusion, so a reader knows what to look for and what would settle it.

**Mode: `specified` - not `grounded`.** No per-customer payment measurement is claimed in this folder. This is a shape and a route, not a result.

## 1. The claim, in one paragraph

Genuine demand is not a stronger kind of interest. It is a reading taken at a costly, non-reactive, per-slot probe where the customer's judgment ranges against a paying ground. Expressed interest is a frame-internal instrument: a pooled engagement total hides the only signal that says where to look, and asking the question can move the reading. Two conditions must both hold and fail differently, a channel to the paying ground that stays unpooled and a signal that costs the customer something. The framework does not name which probe, price or deposit will work. It offers the shape any candidate answer must have, five falsifiers written in advance, and an instrument gap declared rather than filled.

## 2. Three checkable sub-claims

### C1 - framework-internal and world-measured; checkable by reading Fig 2 and the Chandon study

- **Statement:** the purchase with real money is the anchor; interest expressed in a survey is an instrument whose reading changes when the question is asked.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 2 :29, *"An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks. A measurement, an experiment, a counter, a market, a patient outcome."* Fig 2 :33, *"The anchor must not update."* Fig 2 :34, *"If it does, panel 3 turns into panel 1: the anchor becomes another frame, everything drifts together, and the drift is coherent, which is why nobody notices."*
- **World bearing:** Chandon, Morwitz and Reinartz 2005, *Journal of Marketing* 69(2), read via the author-reprinted summary 2026-11-22: *"the correlation between latent intentions and purchase behavior is 58% greater among surveyed consumers than it is among similar nonsurveyed consumers."*
- **Refuter:** a pre-registered survey versus no-survey field comparison in the target category showing statistically indistinguishable intention-to-purchase correlations between the two groups.

### C2 - framework-internal; checkable by reading Fig 1 and Fig 10

- **Statement:** the reading must be kept per customer and never pooled into an engagement total. A pooled reading destroys the signal that says where to look.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 1 :19, *"The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated."* Fig 1 :22, *"Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average."* Fig 10 :26, *"stale percepts, dropped transients, saturated metrics, pooled readings"*.
- **What to check:** does a per-customer paid-conversion reading predict the next named conversion better than the aggregate engagement count, prospectively and across a habitat change?
- **Refuter:** a pooled aggregate interest count that prospectively predicts the next named purchaser better than the per-slot reading.

### C3 - framework-internal with a recorded world conflict; checkable by reading the Operators table and Fig 5

- **Statement:** a signal that costs the customer nothing is not a reading of willingness to pay. A commitment that costs something can be ranged against the paying ground.
- **Source, verbatim:** Operators table :66, *"A bond that costs nothing is a bond that carries nothing, which is the flat case."* Fig 5 :43, *"A new term is worth having when stating it, plus what remains unexplained after it, costs less than what was unexplained before."* Fig 5 :32, *"then check it against the ground, then carry it up"*.
- **Conflict, declared:** Penn and Számadó 2020, *Biological Reviews*, report that *"signalling costs paid at the equilibrium are neither sufficient nor necessary to maintain signal honesty"*. The claim is therefore **narrowed** to the form above and does **not** assert that cost guarantees honesty. The figure line prices a relation in a description; applying it to a deposit is a binding from the business frame, recorded as such.
- **Refuter:** a zero-cost signal that discriminates purchasers from non-purchasers as well as a payment across matched slots and through a habitat change.

## 3. What is explicitly not claimed

- Not a theory of everything about markets; no final room.
- Not "interest is useless" or "do not talk to customers". Interviews build vocabulary; they are disqualified as the settlement.
- Not "cost proves honesty". The biology witness conflicts and the claim is narrowed.
- Not "TAM proves demand". Market size is a filter for the budget, not a witness for the verdict (Fig 9 :60).
- Not "Lean Startup validated learning is the answer". That is a business-pool binding with zero verdict weight (Fig 8 :50-52).
- Not a derivation of one entrepreneurship method from another, and no new primitive.
- Not grounded. Nothing here is asserted as settled by a payment measurement.

## 5. What would settle it

1. Reactivity: is the intention-to-purchase correlation unchanged by asking, in the target category, under pre-registration?
2. Cost: do matched free and priced signals produce equally good per-slot predictions of payment at a stated price, through a habitat change?
3. Per-slot: does a named customer's paid conversion beat a pooled engagement count at predicting the next named conversion?
4. Union: does one intervention restore both conditions with no categorically different residue?
5. Instrument: does any instrument, at any resolution, return a per-slot revealed-preference reading without a payment or switching cost? If not, the gap stands as a verdict.

## 6. What an expert can check in twenty minutes

1. Read Fig 2 :29, :33-34, Fig 1 :19, :22 and Operators table :66, five short passages.
2. Check C1 against the Chandon result: does the 58 percent reactivity effect transfer to the category you know? Name a reason it would not.
3. Name one instrument that would distinguish a free interest signal from a willingness-to-pay reading per customer, or say why none is possible.
4. Send the refutation. A failure is more useful than an endorsement.
