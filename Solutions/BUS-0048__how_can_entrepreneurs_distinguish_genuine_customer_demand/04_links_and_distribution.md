## What the wider literature bears on the question

1. **Intention and behavior are not the same measurement.** Hulland and Houston 2021 (*Journal of the Academy of Marketing Science*, editorial) say intention-only studies *"often undermine the potential contribution of the work, resulting in early rejection"*, quote Sheeran 2002 that *"intentions explain only 28% of the variance in behavior"*, and conclude that *"intentions get translated into action approximately one-half of the time"*. The editorial also records the Nielsen BASES assumption that *"consumers do not usually do what they claim to do, and often the difference between claims and behaviors is very significant"*. This corroborates F1 from a marketing-practice pool.
2. **Asking changes the answer.** Chandon, Morwitz and Reinartz 2005 report self-generated validity and a 58 percent inflation of the intention-to-purchase correlation among surveyed consumers. This is the world-side measurement of the figure rule that an anchor must not update (Fig 2 :33-34). It corroborates F2.
3. **Stated willingness exceeds actual willingness.** Kanya et al. 2019 (*Social Science and Medicine*) found *"Hypothetical WTP was on average 3.2 times greater than actual WTP"*; List and Gallet 2001 (*Environmental and Resource Economics*) found an overstatement *"by a factor of about 3 in hypothetical settings"*. These are health and environmental economics, low overlap with startup practice, so they corroborate the mechanism shape rather than the founder-level fact. The two partly share the contingent-valuation pool, so they are one witness plus a related one, not two independent confirmations.
4. **Specificity beats volume.** Morwitz, Steckel and Gupta 2007 found intentions correlate better with *"trial rates"* than with *"total market sales"*, and better for *"specific brands or models"* than at the *"product category level"*. This is the closest world statement of Fig 1 :19, *"The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated."*
5. **The practitioner frame names the same shape, but is not the framework's answer.** Eric Ries 2009 writes that *"The only metrics that entrepreneurs should invest energy in collecting are those that help them make decisions"* and calls aggregate hits and downloads *"Vanity Metrics"*. The Lean Startup methodology page says the unit of progress is *"validated learning"* and describes the goal as *"the thing customers want and will pay for"*. These bindings are recorded with zero verdict weight (Fig 8 :50-52: convergent derivation and adopted import look identical in the contents; only the record separates them).
6. **A genuine conflict, not a corroboration.** Penn and Számadó 2020 (*Biological Reviews*) report that *"signalling costs paid at the equilibrium are neither sufficient nor necessary to maintain signal honesty"* and argue the Handicap Principle should be retired. This conflicts with a naive "cost proves honesty" reading. The derivation was returned to the census and F4 was narrowed to the weaker claim that a free signal is not a reading of willingness to pay. The conflict did not flip the verdict.

## Distribution: offer, do not push

Figure-grounded reasons:

- **Fig 4 :22-25**: *"Only methods cross up. Only head starts come down. A recording carried upward looks like knowledge and is a description of one occasion."* Sending the full architecture before anyone has described a residual sends a recording, not a generator. Lead with the refuter and the shape claim; offer the links after.
- **Fig 10 :42**: *"A verifier that can reconstruct the claim cannot verify it."* A recipient who has absorbed the entire framework before being asked for a verdict has joined the frame being checked.
- **Fig 9 :27-28**: *"describe it before you go looking, or any frame you pick will seem to fit"*. Ask what the recipient would expect to see if the shape claim were true before giving them the article.
- **Fig 2 :40**: *"disqualifying for a verdict: agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool"*. A send selected for people who already agree produces a convention, not corroboration.

**Recommended order in a reply:** (1) the shape claim and its five refuters; (2) ask what they would count as a failure and record the answer; (3) only then the links, with the note that the figures, not the summary, are the authority.

## The two-stage offer

**Stage 1 before stage 2** is Fig 10 :52, *"Bring a characterised residual, never the source"*, and Fig 2 :45, *"Frames range against the ground. They do not negotiate with it."* The recipient checks the output without holding the framework's interior.

**Stage 2 is opt-in by construction.** Once a recipient has absorbed the theory, their agreement stops being evidence. Their verdict on stage 1 must be recorded *before* any theory is shown, and their later agreement is never pooled as corroboration.

**Stage 3 sends a method, which is permitted.** Fig 4 :22: *"Only methods cross up. Only head starts come down."* The formula is a generator, so giving it away does not give away an answer. Each user must write down what would refute their result before running it, and label the mode of what they get.

## Practical and legal constraint

This is LLM-assisted work. The Academy of Management publication policy quoted in the BUS-0001 pilot ledger states: *"Artificial Intelligence: Work submitted to AOM must be created by the authors and not the product of artificial intelligence tools unless appropriate to the research question and properly cited."* Nothing in this folder is written for direct submission. A human researcher owns and writes anything submitted. Public opt-in channels avoid the additional failure of unsolicited bulk email, which breaches GDPR and CAN-SPAM style rules in many jurisdictions and gets the sending domain blacklisted.
