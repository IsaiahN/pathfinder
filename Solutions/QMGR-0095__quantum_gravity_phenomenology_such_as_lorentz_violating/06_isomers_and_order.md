# Isomers, sequence and threshold: the supporting derivation

## RESIDUAL

The main audit left three structural questions open. (1) Are the linear (`n = 1`) and quadratic (`n = 2`) LIV models two different things or two spellings of one thing? (2) In what order do the pieces of a detection compose? (3) Which operator is the detection itself? No new primitive is available without import.

## DERIVATION

### Q1 — Are linear and quadratic LIV isomers?

- Fig 12 :117: *"isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."*
- Fig 12 :94: *"The arrangement is the substance."*
- Fig 12 :92: *"Symmetry is what decides whether an arrangement is a new thing."*

Both models are written over the same ingredient set: a photon energy `E`, a momentum `p`, a scale `E_QG`, and a modification of the dispersion relation. Wei and Wu (2021) write the modification as `E^2 ≃ p^2 c^2 (1 ± sum a_n)` with `a_n = (E/E_QG,n)^n`, and note that with current detector sensitivity only `n = 1` and `n = 2` matter. The difference is the exponent, which is a choice in how the atoms are arranged, not a new atom.

- The two share every ingredient. They differ in the bond: first power versus second power of the same ratio.
- Their phenomenology is decisively different: the 2009 Fermi limit `E_QG,1` above about 1.2 Planck disfavours linear models, while the 2021 review states quadratic limits remain much weaker (of order 10^11 GeV in the tabulated Fermi limits). A difference invisible in the ingredient list is decisive in behaviour. That is Fig 12 :117 exactly.
- ⇒ **They are isomers over a shared atom set.** This is not a metaphor: the source paper's own series expansion makes the two an arrangement choice over one vocabulary.

**Falsifier, written before the run:** if either order can be written without an atom the other requires, for example a quadratic term that needs a new structure outside the six, the isomer claim fails and the two are simply different imports. Note the fixed-atom condition is load-bearing and is named as a live strain in `01` §7.

### Q2 — What is the order of the detection chain?

- Fig 12 :70-74 defines `→` sequence: *"A then B; B needs A to have happened."* The test is *"swap them. Does it become a different thing? Yes."*
- Fig 3 :20: *"The chain is ordered by dependency, not by importance."*

The chain cannot be reordered. A photon must be emitted before it propagates; it must propagate before it is detected; it must be detected before its arrival time can be compared with a prediction; the comparison must precede any claim of registration.

```
emission (state) -> modified dispersion (structure over energy)
  -> propagation over distance (state) -> accumulated delay (structure)
  -> detected arrival time (state) -> comparison with prediction (threshold)
```

- The accumulation is a `→` sequence, and the operand order matters: a delay applied after detection is not the same thing as a delay during propagation.
- Fig 13 :75 governs the composition: *"Two states never bond directly. It is not space-time but space, a structure, time; and two structures join only through a state."* The chain alternates state / structure / state. No direct state-state bond appears.
- Fig 3 :19: *"Optimising a link that already works cannot rescue one that does not."* More photons at higher timing resolution cannot rescue a broken objective link, where "detection" has not been defined as a bindable ordinary term. That is why the ordinary term was written down in `01` §0 before the run.

**Falsifier:** exhibit a representation in which detection and propagation commute, so that swapping them yields the same observable. If they commute, the sequence bond is wrong and the chain is a flat conjunction.

### Q3 — Which operator is the detection?

- Operators table :51: *"a goal is a comparison, which is why a vocabulary without it cannot state one"*; Fig 12 :87: *"is it about which is larger, rather than both being present? Yes."*
- Fig 12 :79-84 defines subtraction: *"A with B removed or absent"*, test *"is the second operand's absence the point? Yes."*
- Operators table :58: *"a refutation is a negation, which is why a vocabulary without it can only accumulate"*; Fig 12 :135-136: negation takes one operand and joins nothing.

Three distinct operations are bundled in the phrase "a LIV signal":

1. The **observed minus predicted delay** is a subtraction `−`, with the prediction as the removed operand. It says how far along the comparison the data sit.
2. The **threshold test** on that difference is a comparison `⋛`: is the measured delay larger than what sources can produce? This is where a `goal is a comparison` applies.
3. A **failure of exact Lorentz invariance**, if registered, is a negation `¬` of a held term, not a subtraction and not a new field. Operators table :73-74: *"− is A with B removed or absent, which is the absence of an operand. Negation is the refusal of a claim."*

Collapsing (2) and (3) is what the first pass of `01` did and the correction is visible in `01` §6. Keeping them apart is the point of this derivation.

**Falsifier:** write the detection as a single bond and show that the swap test and the absence test give the same answer. If they do, the three-operator distinction is redundant.

### Q4 — Two currencies again

- Fig 12 :125: *"A composition costs in two currencies, and they do not add."* :126: *"Bits price the description. Actions price finding out whether it holds."*
- Fig 9 :60: *"Use filters for the budget and witnesses for the verdict. Never let a filter hand you a verdict."*

The linear model over one 31 GeV photon is cheap in bits and expensive in actions; a 49-burst population study with an intrinsic lag-luminosity relation is expensive in bits and cheaper per burst in actions. The two readings come apart in both directions (Fig 12 :128-129). A limit on `E_QG` is a filter for which models get observing time next, not a witness for whether quantumgravity phenomenology is correct.

**Falsifier:** a measured fixed conversion rate between description bits and observation actions for this question, with the conversion derived rather than declared.

## CONCLUSION

Linear and quadratic LIV are **isomers over the shared dispersion atom set**: same ingredients, different exponent bond, decisively different phenomenology, subject to the fixed-atom condition. The detection chain is a **sequence** whose operand order is load-bearing, and it alternates states and structures as Fig 13 :75 requires. The detection itself is a **comparison** at a threshold, the delay residue is a **subtraction**, and a failure of exact Lorentz invariance is a **negation**, three operators that must not be merged. Description cost and action cost are two readings held apart.

This rescues what is structural in the question without naming a model. `E_QG` bounds remain measurements about reach (F10 of `01`), and no seventh primitive is introduced.
