The question in effect terms. GR and QM are each settled in their own domain. At their contact, near the Planck scale of about 1.22 times 10^19 GeV, no accelerator reaches the ground. The residual is a **ground-access gap**: the contact between the two theories fails to register against any accessible instrument. Quantum gravity phenomenology answers it by looking for a tiny per-unit-length effect of a modified dispersion relation that accumulates over cosmological baselines, and gamma-ray bursts supply the distance, the short timescale and the high-energy photons. Option A: read an energy-dependent arrival lag as a detection of Lorentz invariance violation (LIV) and hence as evidence about quantum gravity. Option B: read it as a union of conditions, most of which can produce the same lag without any QG effect, and withhold the verdict until the conditions are separated. What each leads to is developed below.

The ordinary term that must be bound before the run (Formula, "Direction"): **"a detection"** = an energy-dependent arrival-time delay that (i) increases with energy and redshift as the model predicts, (ii) is independent of the source's emission state, and (iii) is registered by at least two low-overlap instruments. Written down before the conclusion so it can accrue a residual and be refused.

## 1. Domain census, written before any figure line is chosen

### 1a. R by arity, symmetry and scale

- **Arity.** The residual relates two settled frames (GR, QM) through a third channel (astrophysical propagation). The measured object is a three-term relation: arrival-time difference `Delta t`, energy difference `Delta E`, and distance or redshift `z`. It is a seam residual, not a property of one theory.
- **Symmetry.** A pooled `Delta t` reading is symmetric in its possible causes: source-intrinsic emission lag and propagation-induced lag produce the same observable. Two readings of one event are one report. Under the framework this is a seam, not two independent frames (Fig 10 :13-14).
- **Scale.** A Planck-scale modification is first-order in the micro description; its observable is a cross-scale accumulation over a macro baseline. The composition is between a structure over energy and the states time and distance. It is second-order in the sense of Fig 12 :104 only if the propagation law has first been settled and can serve as an operand.

### 1b. One line per domain (actors / currency / anchor)

| domain | actors | currency | anchor (nameable?) |
|---|---|---|---|
| D1 theory frame (LIV model) | modified dispersion relation, LIV coefficient, scale `E_QG`, order `n = 1` or `2` | predicted `Delta t(E, z)` | internal consistency only. No ground inside this frame; a model cannot settle itself (Fig 1 :23). |
| D2 propagation channel | photons of different energy, vacuum, cosmological path, redshift | accumulated delay, lossy transform from micro to macro | the distance baseline and the observed sharpness of the light curve; a registerable quantity |
| D3 observation frame | Fermi LAT/GBM, MAGIC, HESS, LHAASO, polarization instruments; GRBs at many redshifts | `Delta t`, spectra, redshift, polarization | the arrival-time and spectral measurement. Yes: a counter that does not update on what the models think (Fig 2 :29). |
| D4 verification / selection frame | independent analyses, independent instruments, independent GRB populations | agreement versus residual; false-alarm probability | the same ground, never agreement. In this simulated run D1 to D4 share one literature pool, so its independence is low, not zero. |

No nameable anchor means it is not a domain yet. D1 alone fails that test: a dispersion relation with no prediction that meets a counter is not a domain, it is a library entry. D2 and D3 pass because the arrival time is a measurement. D4 passes only where instruments differ at the evidence level.

### 1c. Partition or union?

This is a **union**, not a partition. The conditions that produce an energy-dependent lag overlap: source-intrinsic spectral lag, instrument energy-dependent response, selection of the emission episode (`t_start`), cosmological propagation, and a genuine modification of dispersion. A case in two conditions is not a harder case of either (Fig 9 :57). One lever, such as better timing, leaves the intersections standing because the emission-model and source-population conditions still bind.

### 1d. Order by dependency (Fig 3)

1. Perception: can the instrument resolve a sharp feature at all? (Fig 3 :4-5)
2. Vocabulary: does the frame have a term for what a modified dispersion affords, namely an energy-ordered delay? (Fig 3 :6-7)
3. Objective: what counts as progress, written as a prediction before the run? This is the link that usually breaks: the link between having terms and saying what would count has neither obvious failure nor an obvious instrument (Fig 3 :25-27).
4. Planning: choose redshifts, energy bands and instruments that separate the conditions.
5. Learn and carry: name what survived, with provenance, to the next GRB.

Refuse before mint (Formula step 2): if the held term is "Lorentz invariance is exact", the residual that persists and does not trend down owes a refusal of that term, not a new field (Operators table :58: "a refutation is a negation, which is why a vocabulary without it can only accumulate"). Order matters; the first pass of this audit got it backwards and the correction is kept in §6.

## 3. Findings, each with a verbatim quote and a refuter

### F1 — The residual is a ground-access problem, and the search has two opposed instruments (triangulated)

Fig 6 :34 says: "The question is not whether a sensor could exist. It is whether anything, at any resolution, is already returning something that fails to resolve." Fig 6 :32 says: "An instrument is not built from a description. It is improved from a worse instrument already returning something." Fig 6 :33 adds: "Nobody specified a telescope from an account of Mars. A wandering point in the sky was already a reading, and every sharper instrument was built on the residual the last one produced."

GRB timing did not begin from a finished theory of quantum spacetime. It began from a reading: bursts vary on subsecond timescales, and sharp features were already resolved by BATSE, then RHESSI, HETE-2, INTEGRAL, Swift and finally Fermi LAT and GBM. Each sharper instrument was built on the residual the previous one produced. Formula step 7 keeps the two instruments distinct and opposed:

- **INWARD:** refine the instrument already returning something (better timing, more energy bands, more redshift coverage). Fig 6 :29: sharpen in our own terms.
- **OUTWARD:** characterise the residual in effect terms and find the frame whose closure predicts its shape (a modified dispersion relation). Fig 6 :30: cast it in effect terms.

Never read one against the other. The theory preceding its instrument is allowed: Fig 6 :37 says "theory routinely precedes its instrument, and is welcome to." Fig 6 :38: "Neptune was calculated before it was seen, and the prediction is what told anyone where to point instruments". So a prediction of an energy-dependent delay is legitimate even before a dedicated QG sensor exists; what is not legitimate is claiming the prediction is settled.

**Refuter:** show that no existing instrument at any resolution returns a sharp feature that fails to resolve, that is, there is no edge to extend from. Then the proto-instrument search is empty and this domain is `unreachable`, not hard.

**Status:** triangulated across Fig 6 and Formula step 7, low overlap between the instrument rule and the loop routing rule.

### F2 — LIV in GRBs is a cross-scale, cross-kind composition (general structural shape)

Fig 4 :20: "Only the actors, the currency and the anchor change." Fig 4 :22: "Only methods cross up. Only head starts come down." Fig 13 :74: "Everything else is a composition across the two kinds, never within one." Fig 13 :75: "Two states never bond directly. It is not space-time but space, a structure, time; and two structures join only through a state."

The LIV signature is not a single-scale object. It is a structure over energy, the modified dispersion relation, bonded to the states time and space/distance: `energy structure, over space and time, gives a delay`. Wei and Wu (2021, Handbook of X-ray and Gamma-ray Astrophysics, arXiv:2111.02029) put the accumulation in plain terms: deviations "can increase with energy and accumulate to detectable levels over large distances." Vasileiou et al. (2010, Fermi LAT and GBM collaborations) say "even a tiny variation in the speed of light, when accumulated over cosmological light-travel times, may be revealed by high temporal-resolution measurements of sharp features in Gamma-Ray Burst (GRB) lightcurves."

This is the framework's kernel at two sizes: the same loop, different actors, currency and anchor. The gradient is the thin case named in Fig 13 :45-47: what makes a directedness spendable is that it can be spent, and a directedness over distance is what turns a per-unit effect into a cumulative delay. No seventh primitive is introduced.

Fig 13 :88: "The residual of the turn just closed is what the next turn composes against, so no two passes begin from the same place." Each burst begins from the remainder the last instrument left, which is why the population cannot be treated as interchangeable copies.

**Refuter:** exhibit an energy-dependent lag that does not grow with distance, or a per-unit modification that cannot be composed with the propagation baseline. That breaks the cross-scale composition claim.

**Status:** `general` on the structural shape, triangulated across Fig 4, Fig 13 and the Wei and Wu / Vasileiou descriptions of accumulation. The specific numeric scale `E_QG` is world content, not a figure name.

### F3 — The anchor is the arrival-time measurement, and it must not update (triangulated)

Fig 2 :29: "An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks. A measurement, an experiment, a counter, a market, a patient outcome." Fig 2 :33: "The anchor must not update." Fig 2 :35: "Alignment is triangulation, not negotiation. A mirror cannot correct you."

The arrival time of the 31 GeV photon in GRB 090510, the redshift z = 0.903, the sharpness of the sub-MeV pulses: these are counters. They do not change because a LIV model predicts them or because an analysis prefers them. Abdo et al. (2009, Nature) report that the burst's short duration and cosmological distance make GRBs well suited to constraining LIV, and they set a limit of about 1.2 times the Planck energy for a linear energy dependence under stated emission assumptions. The measurement is the ground for the *model's reach*, even though it is not a ground for the *theory of quantum gravity*, a distinction F7 develops.

Fig 2 :36-38 adds a limit on choosing the anchor: it is legitimate when the question is constitutively about it. Here the question is constitutively about photon arrival times, so the anchor is legitimate. A questionnaire about which model researchers find compelling would not be.

**Refuter:** show that the measured arrival times move when the model or the analysis is changed, that is, the alleged anchor is a frame. Then this is panel 1 drift (Fig 2 :34), not triangulation.

**Status:** triangulated across Fig 2 and the measurement reports; the world sources share the Fermi event pool, so they are one witness for the anchor's existence, not several.

### F4 — Independence across instruments and redshifts is what makes agreement mean something (triangulated)

Fig 2 :31: "Independence, between two frames, is how little they draw on the same evidence." Fig 2 :42: "Buy independence in proportion to the cost of being wrong." Fig 7 :36: "A chain of mirrors is one long room. The next room must differ from the last, and match the residual it surfaced." Fig 8 :53: "Residual first, frame second."

The field already does this, though not always by name. Fermi LAT and GBM are two instruments on one spacecraft; MAGIC and H.E.S.S. and LHAASO are ground-based Cherenkov arrays with different energy thresholds; Swift covers a different energy range and redshift set; polarization instruments test a second signature (vacuum birefringence) rather than the same arrival-time projection. The LIGO/Virgo/Fermi/INTEGRAL GW170817 campaign is a public example of the same move: the LIGO Scientific Collaboration notes that localization used "triangulation from the time delay between Fermi and INTEGRAL", and that several optical teams "independently detected the same transient over the next minutes and hours."

But independence is a parameter, not a label. Ten papers reanalysing the same GRB 090510 photon list are one frame wearing ten names (Fig 8 :48-49). Two instruments pointed at the same burst are two frames. Agreement from the first pool cannot corroborate; agreement from the second can.

**Refuter:** demonstrate zero independence between the two alleged frames, that is, identical data, identical emission assumptions and identical analysis pipeline. Then their agreement is the pool talking (Fig 2 :40) and the triangulation verdict is withdrawn.

**Status:** triangulated across Fig 2, Fig 7 and Fig 8, plus the multi-messenger source. Low overlap between the figure rule and the instrument record.

### F5 — Source-versus-propagation degeneracy is a seam and a metamerism failure (single-source on the figure side)

Fig 10 :13-14 names the seam: "two frames, one field, no shared reading / a disagreement nothing can state is not yet a defect". Fig 10 :26 lists the channel failures: "stale percepts, dropped transients, saturated metrics, pooled readings". Fig 9 :30-31 instructs: "your words are borrowed too, so describe the effect rather than the cause."

A measured energy-dependent lag is a reading with at least two possible sources: emission physics at the burst and modified propagation. Wei and Wu write that "it is difficult to exclude the possibility that the energy-dependent delays are of pure astrophysical origins" and that to prove LIV "one needs to systematically collect numerous delay events and demonstrate that they all match the prediction of LIV." The 2023 PRD study *Revisiting tests of Lorentz invariance with gamma-ray bursts: Effects of intrinsic lags* reports that "source contributions can strongly impact resulting LIV tests, modifying their limits by up to a factor of a few." The 2024 Physics Letters B paper on energy-dependent intrinsic delay explicitly builds a model "to distinguish and evaluate their respective contributions using the observed GRB data."

The *Hard Problem is About Source* essay (opened 2026-11-23) gives the discriminator in plain terms: "the instrument is speaking only about what it measured, it cannot comment on the source." And: "It was proven wrong not by argument, but by the arrival of a better instrument." Kalderon 2008 (Mind) states the epistemic form: "In an environment known to be populated by metameric pairs, a perceiver does not know which colour he is perceiving." What is owed is a source-discriminating instrument, not a cleverer fit to one light curve.

**Refuter:** exhibit an instrument or observable that does discriminate source-intrinsic from propagation delay for a single burst. That would dissolve the seam and upgrade this finding.

**Status:** `single-source` on the figure side (Fig 10 seam), with world corroboration from the review and the perception literature. The perception corroborator is genuinely low overlap.

### F6 — The question is a union compound, not a partition (triangulated)

Fig 9 :4-5: "Disagreements that do not shrink with effort, where each rule keeps working well on a different subset, are not one hard question but several well-formed ones. Split it rather than search." Fig 9 :54: "And when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again." Fig 9 :57: "What remains is smaller and it is also categorically different: a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either."

The lag question bundles at least five overlapping conditions: emission lag, instrument response, choice of `t_start`, propagation model, and a possible dispersion modification. A single lever, such as a higher-energy photon, clears the region where only the emission condition holds and leaves the intersections standing because the propagation and selection conditions still bind (the 2009 Nature analysis compares seven choices of `t_start`, with limits ranging from about 1.19 to greater than 100 times the Planck scale). Fig 9 :59 names the second-order value: what remains is finally describable.

**Refuter:** demonstrate that one condition accounts for all measured lags across populations and instruments, so the bundle is really a partition. Then the union reading is withdrawn.

**Status:** triangulated across Fig 9 and the documented multiplicity of emission assumptions and instruments.

### F7 — Null results are bounds on models, not proof of absence (triangulated)

Fig 8 :39: "So a search that finds nothing is never a proof of absence." Fig 6 :27: "Which is why some unreachability is depth and some is genuine absence, and from inside the frame the two look the same." Fig 9 :44: "Only a sealed room can be searched to the end, and no frame is a sealed room."

The Fermi result is stated honestly in the literature as "We find no evidence for the violation of Lorentz invariance, and place a lower limit of 1.2EPlanck on the scale of a linear energy dependence". That is a statement about the reach of one class of models, not a settlement of quantum gravity. Vasileiou et al. say the linear scale "must be well above the Planck scale, something that renders any affected QG models highly implausible." The 2021 review tabulates limits for both subluminal and superluminal cases at first and second order, and notes that quadratic limits remain much weaker. A bound is a filter for the budget (Fig 9 :60), not a witness for the verdict.

The statistics literature corroborates from a low-overlap field. The BMJ editorial *Absence of evidence is not evidence of absence* (PMC351831) says: "The correct answer is 'never,' because some uncertainty will always exist." A 2025 Biology Letters paper says "conventional p-value analysis can only argue against the null hypothesis, never in favour of it." A non-detection is a constraint with a confidence interval, not a verdict of non-existence.

**Refuter:** produce a sealed room, that is, a closed model space with a complete search and an instrument whose non-registration entails absence. Then Fig 8 :39 no longer applies and the null result is a proof of absence. No such room has been shown here.

**Status:** triangulated across Fig 8, Fig 6, Fig 9 and two low-overlap statistics sources.

### F8 — A LIV term is a candidate, not a settled term (triangulated)

Formula step 3 gives a product gate: SUPPORT `|R^+_s| > 0` for some slot; REACHABILITY `phi` in `closure(Gamma)`; NOVELTY `phi` not in `atoms(Gamma)`. Fig 5 :41: "Any one of them at zero stops the mint entirely." Step 5: "the ground settles it. Verified only by what pays. A gate is not the ground."

For a LIV model:
- **SUPPORT:** a residual that persists and does not trend down exists in the arrival-time data. Positive and present.
- **REACHABILITY:** a modified dispersion relation composes from the held spectrum and gradient structures plus energy and time. Present.
- **NOVELTY:** the parameters `E_QG` and `n` are already in the physics library; the model adds no new atom to the six-term set (Fig 13 :115). Novelty depends on the frame, so this factor is weak rather than zero.
- **SETTLEMENT:** no instrument has registered a deviation from Lorentz invariance. Zero.

A zero factor forces inertness. The model is a candidate, not `grounded`. Fig 5 :43-44 gives the bargain: "A new term is worth having when stating it, plus what remains unexplained after it, costs less than what was unexplained before." A LIV term that explains one burst perfectly and leaves the source-intrinsic lag unexplained does not pay the bargain.

**Refuter:** register a deviation that survives the source-discriminating test, so the ground pays. Then settlement is achieved and this finding changes.

**Status:** triangulated across Formula steps 3 and 5, Fig 5 and Fig 13.

## 4. Pre-registered refuter (written before the conclusion)

Per Fig 2 :42 ("Buy independence in proportion to the cost of being wrong") and the standing rule that a falsifier written before the run is a design instrument, the shape claim is refuted if any of the following holds:

1. **Accumulation fails.** A robust energy-dependent lag is measured that does not grow with redshift, or a modified per-unit effect is shown not to compose with the propagation baseline (breaks F2).
2. **The anchor moves.** Arrival-time or redshift values change with the model or the analysis pipeline, so the alleged counter is a frame (breaks F3).
3. **Independence is zero.** Two alleged corroborating analyses draw on identical data, identical emission assumptions and identical pipelines (breaks F4).
4. **The seam is dissolved the other way.** A discriminator shows the lag is wholly source-intrinsic for the bursts in question (breaks F5, and removes the residual).
5. **A sealed room is produced.** A closed model space plus a complete search is exhibited so that a null result entails absence (breaks F7).
6. **The ground pays against the shape.** A registered deviation that is source-independent, distance-growing and triangulated is found; this does not break the shape but upgrades the candidate to `grounded` and requires the verdict to be rewritten.

Until at least one is run, no part of this may be labelled `grounded`. A failure is more useful than an endorsement.

## 5. Recommendation

1. Lead with the shape claim and its refuter, not with a preferred QG model (F10, Fig 10 :52).
2. Treat each null result as a bound on a model class, with its emission assumptions stated, and never as the absence of quantum gravity (F7, Fig 8 :39).
3. Buy independence where being wrong is costly: different instruments, different energy bands, different redshift populations, and at least one source-discriminating observable (F4, F5, Fig 2 :42).
4. Unbundle the union before searching again: emission lag, instrument response, `t_start` choice and propagation are separate loops (F6, Fig 9 :54).
5. Keep the two currencies apart: the bit-cost of a model and the action-cost of a burst sample do not add (F9, Fig 12 :125).
6. Send the generator, not a recording. Fig 4 :25: "A recording carried upward looks like knowledge and is a description of one occasion." The generator here is the residual, the shape and the refuter.
