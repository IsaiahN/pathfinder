**Subject:** One checkable separator for Lorentz-violation claims in gamma-ray bursts

> Hi [name],
>
> I have one specific output I would like you to check against what you already know about GRB timing and Lorentz-invariance tests. I am not asking you to read a theory first, and I am not asking you to agree with anything. If the output does not survive, there is nothing else to look at.
>
> **The output (`specified`, not `grounded`):** the residual in this field is a ground-access problem. General relativity and quantum mechanics each work in their own domain; at their contact near the Planck scale no accelerator reaches a measurement. What is measurable is a cross-scale accumulation: a modification of the photon dispersion relation, tiny per unit length, applied over a cosmological baseline, shows up as an energy-ordered arrival-time delay in a sharp GRB light curve. The shape any detection must have is threefold: the delay grows with energy and redshift, it is independent of the source's emission state, and it is registered by at least two instruments that do not share the same photons and the same emission assumptions.
>
> **What I am not claiming:** that a specific quantum-gravity model is right; that any particular `E_QG` value is the true scale; that a null result proves Lorentz invariance exact. The framework returns a shape, not a name. A published bound is a statement about the reach of a model class under stated assumptions about the emission start time, not a settlement of quantum gravity. Convergent derivation and adopted binding look identical in the contents and only the record separates them.
>
> **What would refute the shape claim, written down before this message:**
> 1. an energy-dependent lag is measured that does not grow with redshift;
> 2. the arrival-time or redshift values move when the model or the pipeline changes, so the alleged anchor is a frame;
> 3. two alleged corroborating analyses draw on identical data, identical emission assumptions and identical pipelines;
> 4. a discriminator shows the lag is wholly source-intrinsic for the bursts in question;
> 5. a closed model space plus a complete search is exhibited so that a null result entails absence.
>
> If any one holds, I withdraw the claim. A one-line "this fails because..." is more useful to me than agreement. If you would rather not engage, no reply is needed.
