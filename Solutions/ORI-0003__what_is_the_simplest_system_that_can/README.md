# Solution ORI-0003: What is the simplest system that can undergo Darwinian evolution?

**Mode:** `general` on the structural shape (the necessary conditions any Darwinian system must meet), `specified` on the mapping of those conditions onto the six-term substrate and the habitat/selection loop. Nothing here is `grounded`: no experiment was run and no measurement settles the binding between the figure vocabulary and a particular chemical instantiation.

**Status: done.**

**Registry status:** pending (set by the orchestrator, not by this author).

**Source:** `Questions/origins of life.txt` line 7.
**Section:** 1. What counts as "life," and what counts as its origin?
**Field for public contact points:** minimal evolving systems research.

**Question restated as an effect.** We are asked for a floor: the least arrangement such that selection can act and cumulative change can occur. "Simplest" is not one quantity. It resolves into (a) the smallest substrate any composing system needs, which the figures govern, and (b) the smallest unit that can evolve, which the figures do not name. This solution keeps both and does not average them. An answer that gave only a molecule (an RNA replicator, a hypercycle, a vesicle) would answer (b) by importing a name where the framework explicitly returns a shape.

**What an answer would look like, bound as an ordinary term (Formula step "Direction").** A checkable answer states: the actors (replicator slots and a habitat), the currency (per-slot residual, bracket fidelity, ground pressure), the bond structure (a cycle, not a chain), the anchor (a ground that does not update), and a refuter (what would falsify the shape). That description is bound here and accrues a residual like any other term, so the preference for a "smallest entity" answer is refusable rather than invisible.

## Boundary and standing

This folder contains a **simulated derivation checked for internal consistency against the figures**, not a ground reading. The author of the framework's essays writes that LLMs *"can quickly simulate an approximate answer/output with the formula"* but that *"the truly testable output would need to be derived from the real architecture or working the formula yourself"* (Recursive Self-Improvement Trap, 2026-11-19, read 2026-11-23). This package cannot settle the question it reports on. It offers a shape, a refuter, and a route to a ground that could settle it.

All figure citations are to `FRAMEWORK/reference/03-FIGURES_TEXT.md` as `Fig N :line`. All world sources in `10_source_ledger.csv` were fetched and read on 2026-11-23. Framework files were read locally; they are marked `local:` in the ledger. No file outside this solution folder was modified.

## What is deliberately not here

- No personal emails of named individuals (the ISSOL contact page prints two; they are not used here).
- No ranking of people by how likely they are to agree.
- No bulk-send plan.

Three reasons, in the figures' own words: Fig 2 :35 *"Alignment is triangulation, not negotiation. A mirror cannot correct you."*; Fig 2 :40 *"agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool"*; Fig 10 :42 *"A verifier that can reconstruct the claim cannot verify it."* Selecting for receptiveness manufactures the disqualifying correlation, and sending the full framework before asking for a verdict spends the reader's independence.
