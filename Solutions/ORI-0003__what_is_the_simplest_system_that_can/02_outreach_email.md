# Outreach draft: check one shape before you decide about a smallest system

**Use:** one-to-one, or as a cover note attached to a submission through a public venue in `03_public_contact_points.csv`. **Not** for bulk send.

**Hard precondition:** do not send until a checker can see the residual and the refuter without the framework. If stage 1 does not survive, there is nothing to offer at stage 2.

---

**Subject:** What would refute this shape of a minimal evolving system?

> Hello,
>
> I have one specific claim I would like you to check against what you already know. I am not asking you to read a theory first, and I am not asking you to agree with me. If the claim does not survive, there is nothing else to look at.
>
> **The question I am working on:** what is the simplest system that can undergo Darwinian evolution?
>
> **The residual (`general` on the shape, `specified` on the mapping):** "simplest" splits into two questions that should not be averaged. The first is the substrate floor: any composing system needs six terms in two kinds, states (space, time, energy) and structures (spectrum, gradient, network), with composition running across kinds and never within one. The second is the unit floor: how small can the evolving unit be, and which physical thing is it. The framework answers the first and explicitly refuses to name the second. It returns a shape, not a name.
>
> **The shape claim:** Darwinian evolution needs an agent-and-habitat pair, not an agent alone. Selection is between variants, with the residual indexed per variant rather than averaged over a population. Reproduction is a cycle (the output re-enters as input), not a chain, and a template that survives its own copying is a catalyst in the framework's sense. Two quantities both have to be nonzero: the fidelity with which a difference is carried across a handoff, and the pressure of the ground that selects between variants. Zero either and the chain runs in place. Genuinely new atoms have to come from contact with a closure that differs; ordinary variation comes from the unbounded arrangements over a closed set of kinds. In the unmaintained case, nothing selects on behalf of a maintainer, and what survives is what the ground kept. "Nothing was intelligent; selection ran."
>
> **What I am not claiming:** that the answer is a named molecule. I am not claiming an RNA replicator, a hypercycle, a lipid vesicle, or a clay crystal is *the* simplest evolving system. Those are bindings from the chemistry frame onto an open slot, not figure-derived names. I am also not claiming this derivation is a measurement. It is a simulated derivation checked for internal consistency, not a ground reading.
>
> **What would refute the shape:**
> 1. cumulative Darwinian evolution with zero inheritance of the selected difference;
> 2. cumulative Darwinian evolution with zero selection pressure, or with selection suspended and directional change continuing;
> 3. cumulative evolution fully predicted by a single population average with no per-unit residual;
> 4. a seventh substrate term that is indivisible, or one of the six derived from the other five;
> 5. a governing rule that names a specific chemical unit as the simplest evolving system.
>
> I wrote these down before sending this. If any one holds, I will retract the shape claim. A one-line "this fails because..." is more useful to me than agreement. If you would rather not engage, no reply is needed.
>
> **If it survives, and only then:** the sources are below. The figures, not my summary of them, are the authority.
>
> **If you want to use the method:** run the eight-step loop on a problem you know well and can check against a ground. Write down what would refute your result *before* you run it. Report failures; they are more useful than successes.
