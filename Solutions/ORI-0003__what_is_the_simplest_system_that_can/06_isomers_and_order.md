## DERIVATION

### Q1 - Is reproduction a cycle or a chain?

- Fig 12 :121: **"cycle. The output re-enters as input. The sign of the loop decides whether it closes, and a chain cannot close by construction."**
- Fig 12 :119: **"catalyst. A part that survives its own use."**
- A chain `A → B → C` has an end; once C is produced, nothing re-enters as the input to A. A cycle `A → B → C → A` has no end and can be iterated. Heredity is precisely the property that the output of one generation is the input to the next.
- ⇒ Reproduction is a **cycle bond**, and the persistent template is Fig 12's catalyst. A chain cannot be a replicator lineage by construction.
- World check (not a figure line): *"a hypercycle is an abstract model of organization of self-replicating molecules connected in a cyclic, autocatalytic manner"* and *"each of them catalyses the creation of its successor, with the last molecule catalysing the first one"* (Hypercycle (chemistry), Wikipedia, read 2026-11-23). The chemistry field reaches the same bond shape independently. **Triangulated** for the shape; `specified` and unmeasured for the claim that the first evolving system was a hypercycle.
- **Falsifier:** an indefinitely evolving linear chain whose output does not re-enter as input, or a template that is consumed by its own copying and still inherits the difference.

### Q2 - What is the order of the two conditions?

- Fig 9 :55: **"A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones."**
- Fig 9 :57: **"What remains is smaller and it is also categorically different: a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either."**
- The two conditions are C1 (a closed, inheritable cycle with variation) and C2 (a ground that selects between variants). Their overlap is where evolution happens. A lever that only increases replication rate does not clear C2, and a lever that only increases selection pressure does not clear C1.
- Fig 3 :19: **"Optimising a link that already works cannot rescue one that does not."** Fig 3 :27: **"A link with no instrument is not a link that works. It is a link nobody has looked at."**
- Dependency: a variant must exist before it can be selected; a difference must be carried before selection can accumulate it. So C1 is upstream of C2 in the loop, but both must be present for the compound to run. This is a union, not a sequence to be completed once.
- Fig 7 :37-39 gives the product form: **"Progress per handoff has two factors. Zero either and the chain runs in place:"**, **"the bracket's fidelity across the handoff"**, **"the ground's pressure on the room"**. The society-of-agents essay identifies this as *"borrowed from breeding"*, which gives h² its ordinary meaning as heritability. That is a world provenance note for the figure's notation, not a figure line.
- **Falsifier:** cumulative evolution observed with either factor measured at zero, or evidence that selection can accumulate a difference with no heritable carrier.

### Q3 - Are the candidate chemical systems isomers under Fig 12?

- Fig 12 :117: **"isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."**
- Fig 12 :124: **"functional group. A substructure whose presence fixes the outcome. The group says what kind; the qualifier says which member."**
- Consider three candidates as they are usually described: an RNA replicator (template plus catalysis), an autocatalytic set (mutual catalysis with no single template), and a compartment-selecting protocell (a boundary plus internal templates). They do **not** share an atom set as described: the first needs a specific polymer, the second needs a network, the third needs a boundary. By Fig 12's definition they are not isomers; they are different **functional groups** that share a broad motif (a persistent arrangement that survives its own use and can be selected).
- The stronger isomer claim would require all three to be written over one shared atom set and to differ only in the bond arrangement. The six-term substrate permits that writing in principle (Fig 13 :115: **"The set is closed. The arrangements are not. That gap is why import exists."**), but no measurement shows that the three candidates actually use the same atoms. So the isomer claim is **withheld**, and the functional-group claim is kept. This mirrors the exemplar's discipline: do not promote a structural resemblance into an identity.
- **Falsifier:** a demonstration that two candidate systems can be written over the identical atom set with different bonds and still differ in evolvability. That would promote the claim from functional group to isomer.

## CONCLUSION

Reproduction is a cycle bond and the persistent template is a catalyst; a chain cannot close by construction. The two conditions form a union whose progress is the product of bracket fidelity and ground pressure, with C1 upstream of C2 but both jointly necessary. The candidate chemical systems are a **functional group**, not demonstrated isomers, over the shared substrate. The order claim and the cycle claim need no new primitive and no import that moves the wall. Which physical thing first realized the cycle is the `gap` recorded in F8 of `01`.
