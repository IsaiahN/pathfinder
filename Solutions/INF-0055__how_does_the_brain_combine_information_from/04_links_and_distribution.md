## Three things in the wider literature that bear directly on the question

1. **The single-cause default fails at disparity.** Körding et al. 2007 show forced fusion explains R² = 0.56 and is ruled out by the bias data, while the causal-inference model explains the full response distributions. This is the empirical counterpart of the figure verdict that the problem is a union of two conditions, not one hard average.
2. **Weights are precision-dependent, not prestige-dependent.** Ernst and Banks 2002 show that degrading vision lowers the visual weight, and the cat superior colliculus work (Meredith and Stein 1986; Stein and Stanford 2008) shows the largest enhancement for the weakest individual cues. This is the inverse-effectiveness result and it is why a fixed hierarchy (vision always wins) is the wrong shape.
3. **Dominance is domain-specific.** Shams et al. 2000 and Hirst et al. 2020 show audition altering visual event count in the temporal domain, while Ernst and Banks show visual dominance in spatial size. So "which sense wins" is a specified, task-dependent fact, not a general law. Mark it `specified` accordingly.

## Distribution: offer, do not push

Figure-grounded reasons:

- **Fig 4 :22** *"Only methods cross up. Only head starts come down."* Sending the whole architecture before anyone has described a residual sends a recording, not a generator. Lead with the shape claim and its refuter; offer links after.
- **Fig 10 :42** *"A verifier that can reconstruct the claim cannot verify it."* A recipient who has absorbed the framework before being asked for a verdict has joined the frame being checked.
- **Fig 9 :27 to :28** *"describe it before you go looking, or"* / *"any frame you pick will seem to fit."* Ask what they would expect to see if the shape claim were true before giving them the sources.
- **Fig 2 :40** agreement from a shared evidence pool is disqualifying for a verdict. A send selected for people who already agree produces a convention, not corroboration.

**Recommended order in a reply:** (1) the shape claim and its four refuters; (2) ask what they would count as a failure and record the answer; (3) only then the two core links, with the note that the figures, not the summary, are the authority.

## The two-stage offer

1. **Stage 1.** Residual and refuter only. The recipient checks the shape without holding the framework's interiors (Fig 10 :52 *"Bring a characterised residual, never the source."*; Fig 2 :45 *"Frames range against the ground. They do not negotiate with it."*).
2. **Stage 2, opt-in.** If curious, they get the links. Their stage-1 verdict is already recorded and later agreement is never pooled as corroboration (Fig 2 :40).
3. **Stage 3, method reuse.** The formula is a generator, so giving it away does not give away an answer (Fig 4 :22). Each user must write their own refuter and state their own mode, because a frame cannot check the direction of its own question.

**Hard precondition:** stages 1 to 3 only work if the stage-1 output survives. The four refuters in `02_outreach_email.md` are live; if one fires, the shape is re-censused before any send.
