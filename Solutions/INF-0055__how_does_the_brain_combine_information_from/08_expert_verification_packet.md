# Expert verification packet — the Tether on conflicting senses

**What this is:** a claim in a form a sensory or multisensory researcher can check. Mode, derivation and refuters are stated before the conclusion, so a reader knows what to look for and what would settle it.

**Mode: `specified` on the shape, `general` on the structural form, not `grounded`.** No new measurement is claimed. This is a shape and a route, not a result.

## 1. The claim, in one paragraph

When two senses conflict, the percept reports an estimate but does not determine the source. The brain's answer has the shape: decide whether the cues share one cause or two, then fuse with reliability-based weights in the one-cause case or keep the cues separate in the two-cause case. A blind average of all cues is refused because it destroys the per-sense disagreement signal and mistakes agreement from one source for two independent witnesses. The figures license that shape; the algebra and the numbers (precision weights, fitted priors, modality-dominance asymmetries) are imported from the published psychophysics and neural literature and are recorded as such.

## 2. Three checkable sub-claims

### C1 — framework-internal; checkable by reading Figures 1, 2 and 9

- **Statement:** per-slot residuals must be kept live, and the one-cause and two-cause cases must be unbundled before any fusion rule is applied.
- **Verbatim source:** Fig 1 :19 *"The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated."* Fig 1 :22 *"Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average."* Fig 9 :54 *"And when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again."* Fig 9 :57 *"a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either"*
- **Refuter:** a dual-report experiment with large disparity and explicit evidence of two distinct sources in which observers still fuse on essentially every trial and show no separate-source signal. Also: a single fixed rule that fits both conditions with no decision variable.

### C2 — the common-cause reading of Fig 2; checkable against causal-inference data

- **Statement:** when cues come from one hidden source, their agreement is one pool talking, not two independent confirmations; the organism must detect this and only then decide to fuse.
- **Verbatim source:** Fig 2 :18 *"one evidence pool"*, :19 *"they never talk, and still agree"*, :20 *"so the agreement is the pool talking,"*, :21 *"not two frames confirming each other"*. Anchor rule: Fig 2 :29 *"An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks."*
- **What to check:** in a dual-report auditory-visual task, does the fitted common-cause model predict both the unity judgments and the bias, and does forced fusion fail at large disparity? Körding et al. 2007 report R² = 0.97 for the causal-inference fit and R² = 0.56 for forced fusion.
- **Refuter:** observers treating cues from one hidden source as independent witnesses, or cues from two sources as one, beyond the model's predicted bounds; or a forced-fusion model that survives the large-disparity dual-report data.

### C3 — world-facing; checkable against the psychophysics and physiology

- **Statement:** inside the fused regime, weights track measured cue reliability, and the multisensory gain is largest for the weakest individual cues; dominance is task-dependent, not a fixed hierarchy.
- **Figure line bearing on the structure:** Fig 12 :5 *"A set of ingredients is not one. The bond is the rest of the information, and it is the half nothing was recording."* and Fig 8 :45 *"Two frames together reach what neither reaches alone."*
- **What to check:** Ernst and Banks 2002 (degrading vision lowers visual weight); Meredith and Stein 1986 and Stein and Stanford 2008 (inverse effectiveness); Shams et al. 2000 and Hirst et al. 2020 (audition alters visual event count in the temporal domain).
- **Refuter:** measured weights that do not track measured cue variances; a large bimodal benefit when both modalities carry identical redundant information; or a single modality matching the bimodal condition when both carry independent information.

## 3. What is explicitly not claimed

- Not that the brain literally performs Bayesian inference, and not that any fitted prior is a law.
- Not universal optimality. Körding et al. note there is "no reason why the nervous system should be optimal under all circumstances".
- Not a universal modality hierarchy. Dominance depends on task and reliability.
- Not a complete neural implementation; that is named as an open step in the literature.
- Not `grounded`, and not a claim that the six-term physical substrate governs perceptual frames (audit F9 withdraws that line for this domain).
- Not that the algebra was figure-derived. It is an import with provenance.

1. Read Fig 1 :19 and :22, Fig 2 :18 to :21 and :29, Fig 9 :54 and :57, Fig 8 :45, Fig 12 :5. Eight short passages.
2. Check C2 against the causal-inference paradigm: do the large-disparity data actually force segregation, or is fusion still adequate?
