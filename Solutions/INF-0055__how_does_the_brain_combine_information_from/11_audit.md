## Independent domain census

I redid the census before relying on the primary's conclusions, using the WATSON order: R by arity, symmetry and scale; one line per domain with actors / currency / anchor; partition or union; order by dependency.

- **R (arity / symmetry / scale).** A per-slot conflict between two measurement distributions with different variances. Arity: k >= 2 channels in contact with one world state. Symmetry: the channels are not interchangeable, so a symmetric bond is the wrong type. Scale: first order within a sense, second order at the source decision, third at readout and development. My first pass called this "a fusion problem that needs a better weight". I corrected myself: the existence of a two-source regime means no single weight can be the answer, so this is a union of two conditions (Fig 9 :54, :57), not a harder estimation problem.
- **Domain 1, modality frames.** Actors: receptors and afferents. Currency: per-modality noisy estimates with variance sigma_i. Anchor: the distal stimulus. Union, because the closures differ.
- **Domain 2, convergence.** Actors: multisensory neurons. Currency: response change relative to the best unisensory response. Anchor: the event, one cause or two. Union until the decision splits it.
- **Domain 3, causal decision.** Actors: the inference about C. Currency: p(C=1 given the cues). Anchor: the real causal structure. Partition: one cause versus two.
- **Domain 4, readout and habitat.** Actors: effectors, attention, development. Currency: action and task success. Anchor: task and survival ground. Union of agent and habitat.

Order by dependency: resolve each modality and estimate its variance, then have terms for common cause and reliability, then state the objective, then decide fuse or segregate and set weights, then calibrate from experience. Refuse forced fusion before minting the decision (THE_FORMULA step 2).
