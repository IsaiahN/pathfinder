# Union, partition, bond and order in multisensory conflict

**Mode: `specified`.** This is a simulated derivation checked against the figures, not a ground reading (see `05_what_shape_means.md` section 7).

## RESIDUAL

The percept of a conflicting pair of cues is a projection: it reports an estimate, not the source. Two questions are owed: (1) is the conflicting pair a union or a bundle of two conditions, and (2) what relation (bond) joins the cues, and in what order is it applied?

## DOMAIN CENSUS

Held frames: visual, auditory, haptic, vestibular, each with its own closure and currency. Held relation vocabulary: symmetric `+`, ordered `→`, comparison `⋛`, subtraction `−`, negation `¬` (Operators table). No new primitive is available without an import with a source gate.

## DERIVATION

### Q1 — Union or partition?

- Fig 9 :54: *"And when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again."*
- Fig 9 :57: *"a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either"*
- The conflicting pair lives in two conditions: C=1 (one source) and C=2 (two sources). A single integration lever (blind fusion) clears the C=1 region and leaves C=2 standing. Therefore the problem is a **union of two conditions**, and it must be unbundled into a decision plus two per-condition rules.
- Result: the structure is `decide(C) → [fuse(C=1) OR segregate(C=2)]`, not `fuse(all cues)`.
- **Falsifier, written before the run:** if a single fixed rule fits both conditions with no decision variable, the union reading fails. Körding et al. 2007 already refute the fixed rule for large disparities (forced fusion R² = 0.56), but a new dataset could reverse that.

### Q2 — What bond joins the cues?

- Fig 12 :5: *"A set of ingredients is not one. The bond is the rest of the information, and it is the half nothing was recording."*
- The ingredients are the two estimates x_V and x_A. The bond is the causal relation. A model that only records the ingredients has recorded half of itself.
- Fig 12 :23: *"this bond is symmetric, so the four are one term"* warns that under `+` the senses would be interchangeable. They are not: weights track precision (Ernst and Banks 2002). So the relation is **not** the symmetric conjunction bond. It is a priced, ordered arrangement: first a comparison (`⋛`-like in effect, deciding one cause versus two), then either a weighted composition or a kept-apart pair.
- Operators table type caveat: `⋛` is *"a threshold relation between two quantities"*. At the perceptual level the operands are cue distributions, whose precisions are quantities, so the type is legal. I record this as legal with the caveat, not as a derived fact. Single-source on the figure definition, corroborated by the psychophysics.

### Q3 — What is the order of operations?

- Fig 3 :20: *"Each link consumes what the one above produces, so effort spent above the break arrives at a step that cannot use it."*
- Required order: resolve each modality's own estimate and σ_i (perception) → have terms for common cause and reliability (vocabulary) → state what counts as progress (objective) → choose fuse or segregate and set weights (planning) → calibrate from experience (learn and carry).
- A forced-fusion model breaks at link 2: it has no term for C. Sharpening each single sense (link 1) cannot rescue it.
- Fig 5 :62 describes the sought-for shape as a preference held outside the term set. Binding C as an ordinary term (from world sources, provenanced) makes it refusable, which is what Q1's falsifier does.

### Q4 — Analogues that carry the structure

- **Triangulation versus mirror (Fig 2 :35):** *"Alignment is triangulation, not negotiation. A mirror cannot correct you."* Two senses from two sources are two instruments pointed at the world; two senses from one source are one reading wearing two names. The organism must tell these apart. That is the causal decision.
- **The blend (Fig 7 :14):** *"blending: novelty swamped to the mean"* is the failure of blind averaging. The ventriloquist effect is the useful blend when C=1; it becomes an error when C=2 and the sources are actually separate.
- **Metamerism (Hard Problem essay):** the percept reads green and cannot comment on the source. The dual-report experiment is the spectrometer. Fig 6 :32: *"An instrument is not built from a description. It is improved from a worse instrument already returning something."*
- **Inverse effectiveness (Stein and Stanford 2008 glossary):** the weakest individual cues give the largest multisensory gain. Structurally this is a union-surplus claim (Fig 8 :45 *"Two frames together reach what neither reaches alone."*), not an extra primitive.

### Q5 — Two currencies, not one

- Fig 12 :125: *"A composition costs in two currencies, and they do not add."*
- Fig 12 :126: *"Bits price the description. Actions price finding out whether it holds."*
- A weighted-fusion rule is cheap to state (a few bits) and may be expensive to settle (a psychophysical experiment with manipulated causal structure). A segregation rule is cheap to state and expensive to verify neurally. Summing description cost and action cost into one score would hide which one is short. Keep them apart.

## CONCLUSION

The conflicting-senses case is a **union of two conditions** that must be unbundled before it is searched. Its structure is a decision followed by one of two rules; its bond is a priced causal relation and not the symmetric sum of two cues; its order puts the causal decision before fusion; and its cost has two currencies. This rescues what is defensible in the "Bayesian integration" literature (fusion with precision weights, segregation at disparity) without pretending the figures derived the algebra. The label "causal inference" remains an import with provenance (Körding et al. 2007; Shams et al. 2005), not a figure-derived name.
