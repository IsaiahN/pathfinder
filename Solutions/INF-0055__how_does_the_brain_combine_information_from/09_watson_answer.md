## QUESTION

Decide, in effect terms, how the brain combines information from different senses when they conflict. Option A: all cues are fused by one blind weighted average. Option B: the system decides whether the cues share a source, then fuses with precision weights (one cause) or segregates (two causes). What each leads to: A loses the per-sense disagreement signal and mistakes agreement from one source for two witnesses; B keeps a live per-slot residual, treats the common source as one pool, and splits the two cases into different loops.

## DOMAIN CENSUS

- **R (the residual).** The per-slot conflict between two measurement distributions with different variances. Arity: k >= 2 sensory channels in contact with one residual. Symmetry: the frames are not interchangeable (different closures, different precisions), so their composition is not the symmetric conjunction bond. Scale: first order within a modality, second order at the causal decision, third at readout and development. Fig 9 calls this a union of two conditions, not one hard question.
- **Domain 1, peripheral modality frames.** Actors: receptors and afferents. Currency: per-modality distributions with variance sigma_i. Anchor: the distal stimulus for that modality. Union, because the closures differ.
- **Domain 2, multisensory convergence.** Actors: multisensory neurons in superior colliculus, STS, parietal cortex. Currency: response change relative to the best unisensory response, spatial and temporal register. Anchor: the physical event, one cause or two. Union until the causal decision splits it.
- **Domain 3, causal decision.** Actors: the organism's inference about C. Currency: posterior p(C=1 | x_V, x_A). Anchor: the real causal structure of the world, which does not update on what any frame thinks. Partition: one cause versus two causes.
- **Domain 4, readout and habitat.** Actors: muscles, attention, development. Currency: action and task success. Anchor: survival and task ground. Union of agent and habitat.
- **Order by dependency.** Resolve each modality and estimate sigma_i, then have terms for common cause and reliability, then state the objective, then choose fuse or segregate and set weights, then calibrate from experience (Fig 3). Refuse forced fusion before minting the decision (THE_FORMULA step 2).

## VERDICT (from the figures)

The shape decides; the algebra is imported. Conflict between senses is a union of two conditions that must be unbundled before any fusion rule is applied. Keep the per-sense residual live, range each modality against the same world, treat agreement from a shared source as one witness, then fuse with precision weights in the one-cause case or segregate in the two-cause case. Blind averaging is refused. Modality dominance is task-dependent and is a world finding, not a figure law. The six-term physical substrate does not govern this domain (F9). This is a `specified`, ungrounded, candidate result.

- **cost if wrong:** bounded and locatable. The packet states its mode and four refuters before the conclusion, so an error is findable. The real cost is spending an outside reader's independence by sending a recording (a finished computational story) instead of a generator (the shape and its refuter). Fig 2 :42 "Buy independence in proportion to the cost of being wrong." That cost is avoidable by leading with the shape and the refuter.
- **undoable:** yes. The structure (decide union or partition, then act) can be restated without withdrawing any primitive. A sent claim can be superseded by a corrected packet. The precision-weighting numbers are imports and can be replaced without touching the figure-derived shape.
- **reversed by:** a ground reading, not agreement. A dual-report experiment that varies causal structure independently of reliability and still forces fusion would fire refuter 1; weights that ignore measured reliability fire refuter 2; systematic common-cause misclassification fires refuter 3; a single modality matching the bimodal condition with independent information fires refuter 4.
