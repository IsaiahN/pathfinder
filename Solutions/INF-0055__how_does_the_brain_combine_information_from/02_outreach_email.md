# Outreach draft — check the output before you decide about the shape

**Use:** one to one, or as a cover letter attached to a submission through the public venues in `03_public_contact_points.csv`. **Not** for bulk send.

**Hard precondition:** do not send until you can state the shape claim and its refuter in your own words. The output must survive validation or the two-stage offer collapses on contact.

---

**Subject:** One claim about conflicting senses that you could refute in a paragraph

> Hi [name],
>
> I have one specific output I would like you to check against what you already know. I am not asking you to agree with it, and I am not asking you to read any theory first. If the output does not survive, there is nothing else to look at.
>
> **The output (`specified`, not `grounded`):** when two senses conflict, the thing to explain is not the size of the compromise. It is that the brain sometimes fuses the cues into one percept and sometimes keeps them as two separate events. The shape claim is that a decision about source structure comes first: if the cues share one cause, they are fused with weights set by each cue's measured precision; if they have two causes, they are kept separate. A blind average of all cues would lose the per-sense disagreement signal that tells the system which case it is in.
>
> This is a shape and a route, not a measurement and not a claim that the brain literally runs Bayes. Species, task and development all change the numbers.
>
> **What I am not claiming:** that vision always wins, that there is one universal modality hierarchy, that the framework derived the inverse-variance weighting rule, or that any new measurement settles this. Those are mappings from other work onto the vocabulary. Where a reading comes from is recorded, because convergent derivation and adopted import look identical in the contents.
>
> **What would refute the shape claim (written before this run):**
> 1. a dual-report experiment with large disparity and explicit evidence of two sources in which observers still fuse on essentially every trial and show no separate-source reports;
> 2. cue weights that do not track measured cue reliability;
> 3. observers treating cues from one hidden source as independent witnesses, or two sources as one, beyond the model's predicted bounds;
> 4. a single modality matching the bimodal condition when both carry independent information.
>
> If any one holds, I will retract or re-census the claim. A one-line "this fails because..." is more useful to me than agreement. If you would rather not engage, no reply is needed.
>
> **If it survives, and only then:** here are the sources. The figures, not my summary, are the authority.
> - Ernst and Banks 2002, Nature: https://www.nature.com/articles/415429a
> - Körding et al. 2007, PLoS ONE: https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371/journal.pone.0000943
> - Local figure text extract and the rest of the packet are available on request through the public venues in my folder.
>
> **If you want to use the method:** write down what would refute your result before you run it, state the mode of what you get, and keep the per-slot residuals instead of averaging them into one number. A reported failure is more useful to me than a reported success.
>
> — [name]
