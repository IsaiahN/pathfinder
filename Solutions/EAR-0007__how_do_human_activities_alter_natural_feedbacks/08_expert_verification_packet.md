# Expert verification packet: the Tether on anthropogenic change of natural feedbacks

**What this is:** a claim in a form an Earth-system scientist or ecologist can check. Mode, derivation and refuters are stated before the conclusion, so the reader knows what to look for and what would settle it.

**Mode: `specified` on structural claims, `general` on the shape, not `grounded`.** No measurement in this packet is claimed to settle which feedback changed.

**Status:** simulated derivation checked for internal consistency against the figures, not a ground reading. It cannot settle the claim it reports on.

## 1. The claim, in one paragraph

Adding an external pressure and altering a natural feedback are different kinds of change. Adding a pressure introduces an actor into the cast; altering a feedback re-bonds the same actors so that the sign or closure of a cycle changes. Same atoms, different arrangement, different substance. A feedback is a cycle, so the size of the forcing cannot tell you how the feedback changed. Actor valence is relative to the residual, so no standing list of good and bad activities answers the question. The altered loop typically appears one or two steps beyond the direct contact, and a claim that it changed needs a response measurement under comparable forcing that does not move when the model is pushed. The framework names none of the particular feedbacks; it supplies the shape, the refuters written in advance, and the instrument that would close it.

## 2. Three checkable sub-claims

### C1: framework-internal; checkable by reading Figures 6 and 12

- **Statement:** actor addition and feedback alteration are different operations. Adding a pressure changes the atom set; altering a feedback re-bonds held atoms, which is isomerism.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 6 :22 "IMPORT adds an atom, and the added atom is what expands the closure." Fig 12 :117 "isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour." Fig 12 :94 "The arrangement is the substance."
- **Refuter:** a forcing that changes system response while the actor set and bond structure are both held fixed; or every feedback alteration fully described as a new actor (then C1 collapses).

### C2: structure-facing; checkable by an ecologist or Earth-system scientist

- **Statement:** a feedback is a signed cycle, so the question "how was the feedback altered?" is a question about the loop's sign and closure, not about forcing magnitude. The change often appears at second or third order.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 12 :122 "the sign of the loop decides whether it closes, and a chain cannot close by construction." Fig 11 :52-53 "Scoping the habitat to direct contact is how DDT reached the eagles: first-order enumeration, second-order effect, and the residual that mattered was three steps out."
- **What to check:** is there a case where the loop sign changed but the forcing did not? Is there a case where first-order scoping found the pressure and missed the loop change? Regime-shift and ecosystem-engineering literature supplies examples; none is evidence for the framework, but all are checkable against the claim.
- **Refuter:** loop sign changed by scaling the forcing alone; or first-order enumeration captured every altered-feedback residual in a stated test set.

### C3: verification-facing; checkable by anyone who works with models and observations

- **Statement:** a claim that a feedback changed needs a non-updating anchor. A model tuned to the observed outcome is not an anchor, and agreement among frames sharing an evidence pool is one witness, not two.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 2 :29-30 "An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks." Fig 2 :35 "Alignment is triangulation, not negotiation. A mirror cannot correct you." Fig 2 :40 "agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool".
- **Refuter:** a frame's own prediction accepted as the settlement of whether its feedback changed; or a feedback verdict based only on agreement among models that share observations and structure.

## 3. What is explicitly not claimed

- Not which feedback changed, where, or by how much. That is a declared `gap`.
- Not a list of human activities with fixed signs. Valence is relative to the residual (Fig 11 :49-51).
- Not a global scalar for feedback change. That would aggregate away the live signal (Fig 1 :22).
- Not that management can subtract a habitat or restore a loop permanently (Fig 11 :61-63).
- Not `grounded`. Nothing here is asserted as settled by experiment.
- Not that any world source corroborates the atom-versus-bond distinction independently. Control theory corroborates the cycle-sign claim from a low-overlap field; the isomer claim is single-source in the figures.

## 5. What would settle it

A measurement that discriminates a re-bonding from a larger push:

1. Under the same forcing, a response that a fixed-cast, fixed-bond model cannot reproduce.
2. A measured change of loop sign, from damping to amplifying, with the forcing held comparable.
3. Persistence after the driver is reduced, the criterion used in the regime-shift literature.
4. A source-discriminating instrument that separates one cause of an observed response from another, as a spectrometer separates sources of green.

## 6. What an expert can check in twenty minutes

1. Read Fig 6 :22, Fig 12 :94, Fig 12 :117, Fig 12 :122 and Fig 2 :29-30. Five short passages.
2. Check C2 against a case you know: did the loop sign change, or only the forcing?
3. Name one measurement that would discriminate a re-bonding from a bigger push, or say why none exists.
4. Send the refutation. A failure is more useful than an endorsement.
