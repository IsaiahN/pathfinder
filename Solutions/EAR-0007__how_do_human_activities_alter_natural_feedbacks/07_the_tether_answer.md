# The Tether's answer: how human activities alter natural feedbacks

**Mode: `specified` on the structural findings, `general` on the overall shape, not `grounded`.** This is a shape and a separation of channels, not a result about any named ecosystem. It is a simulated derivation checked for internal consistency against the figures, not a ground reading (`05_what_shape_means.md` section 4).

## 1. The residual it answers

The same external forcing can produce a different Earth-system response after a human activity. What failed to resolve is not the size of the push but the mechanism by which the system's own loops changed. The question's either/or is a **union compound** (Fig 9 :57 "a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either. It stops being a mixture and becomes a specific compound"): most interventions both add an actor and re-wire a coupling. The shared fraction is recorded, not averaged.

## 2. The shape

1. **Adding pressure changes the cast.** A new emitter, species, or land cover is introduced into the habitat. Fig 6 :22 "IMPORT adds an atom, and the added atom is what expands the closure." Fig 11 :61 "Introduction is available; exclusion is not." That is a first-order contact change. It does not, by itself, assert that the loop structure changed.
2. **Altering a feedback changes the arrangement.** Same atmosphere, ocean, ice, land, biota, coupled differently. Fig 12 :117 "isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour." Fig 12 :94 "The arrangement is the substance." This is why an inventory of actors cannot see the change: the bond is the half the inventory does not record (Fig 12 :5).
3. **A feedback is a signed cycle.** Fig 12 :122 "the sign of the loop decides whether it closes, and a chain cannot close by construction." Whether the returned signal damps or amplifies is a property of the loop, not of the push's magnitude. That is why "how much pressure" cannot answer "how was the feedback altered."
4. **Valence is relative to the residual.** Fig 11 :49 "An actor's valence is relative to the residual, not a property it carries." The same activity is a pressure for one residual and a loop-restorer for another. A standing list of good and bad activities is not an answer.
5. **The change appears downstream.** Fig 11 :52-53 "Scoping the habitat to direct contact is how DDT reached the eagles: first-order enumeration, second-order effect, and the residual that mattered was three steps out." Enumerating only the contact captures the pressure and misses the changed loop. Dependency order, not importance (Fig 3 :20).
6. **Management is asymmetric and permanent.** Fig 11 :62 "You can add an actor and you cannot subtract the habitat, because contact is a fact about the world and does not consult you." Fig 11 :63 "Which is why a managed ecosystem is permanent work rather than a solved state." Capability is agent-and-habitat, so an improvement that does not change contact changes nothing (Fig 11 :40).
7. **A claim needs a non-updating anchor.** Fig 2 :29-30 "An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks." For a feedback claim the anchor is a response measurement under comparable forcing, not a model tuned to the outcome. Correlated model agreement is one pool talking (Fig 2 :40).
8. **The particular feedback is not named by the framework.** Fig 9 :32 "you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself." Naming ice-albedo or moisture recycling is a binding from Earth science, recorded as provenance, not a figure derivation.

## 3. What it explicitly does not say

- Not a claim that any specific feedback changed, or by how much. That is the declared `gap`.
- Not a list of human activities with fixed good or bad signs.
- Not that altering a feedback requires a new actor or a new law of nature. It is a re-bonding of held atoms.
- Not that a global index of feedback strength preserves the live signal. Fig 1 :22 "Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average."
- Not `grounded`. No measurement in this derivation settles anything.
- Not a theory of everything. The union compound keeps its own loop and its own ground.

## 4. What would settle it: the ground

A source-discriminating instrument, not a further argument. *The Hard Problem is About Source*: a reading of green "cannot comment on the source"; the spectrometer resolved it. Here the corresponding questions are:

- Under the same forcing, does the response function change in a way that a fixed-cast, fixed-bond model cannot reproduce? That would discriminate a re-bonding from a bigger push.
- Does a loop that previously damped now amplify? A measurement of the loop sign, not of the forcing size, is the instrument owed.
- Does the effect persist after the driver is reduced? Regime-shift research uses exactly this criterion (Biggs et al. 2009, read on the fetched page).
- Does the residual that matters appear at second or third order? A first-order-only study cannot answer this; its silence is not evidence of absence (Fig 8 :39 "So a search that finds nothing is never a proof of absence").

Standing falsifiers from `01` section 4 are unchanged. Any one firing withdraws the corresponding claim.

## 6. One-paragraph version for outreach

> The Tether does not say which feedback changed, and it does not claim a final answer about Earth. It says that adding a pressure and altering a feedback are different kinds of change: the first adds an actor to the cast, the second re-bonds the same actors, so the loop's sign and closure change. Same actors, different arrangement, different substance. Because a feedback is a cycle, "how much forcing" cannot tell you "how the feedback changed"; because actor valence is relative to the residual, no standing list of good and bad activities can answer it either. The change usually appears one or two steps beyond the direct contact, and a claim that it happened needs a measurement that does not move when the model is pushed. What the framework offers is that shape, the refuters written in advance, and the instrument that would close it. The particular feedback stays open until the ground settles it.
