## 0. The residual under audit

**Question as written:** "How do human activities alter natural feedbacks, rather than simply add external pressure?"

**Residual stated in effect terms.** Given the same external forcing, the Earth system can respond differently after a human activity than before it. What failed to resolve is not the magnitude of the push but the mechanism by which the system's own loops changed. Two channels are named by the question:

- **Pressure channel:** a human activity enters as an extra actor, forcing, or input applied to an existing loop.
- **Feedback channel:** a human activity changes the loop itself, its bonds, its sign, or its closure.

The question asks how the second differs from the first. It is not asking for a list of activities. It is asking for the shape of the difference.

**Direction bound as an ordinary term.** The sought-for answer would: (1) assign each channel to the quantity it governs; (2) keep verdicts per slot and per domain separate, never averaged; (3) name the anchor that would settle a claim that a feedback actually changed; (4) state plainly what cannot be named at this budget. Binding this shape now lets it be refused by the same test as everything else (Formula, "Direction"; Fig 5 :62-63).

## 1. Domain census (before choosing any figure line)

### R by arity, symmetry, scale

- **Arity:** at least two conditions. A single human activity is simultaneously an added actor and a potential re-wirer of loops. Fig 9 :55-57: "a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either. It stops being a mixture and becomes a specific compound." So R is a **union compound**, not one hard question.
- **Symmetry:** a feedback is a cycle whose sign decides closure. Fig 12 :122: "the sign of the loop decides whether it closes, and a chain cannot close by construction." The relation is ordered and signed, not a symmetric join. Adding an actor is a `+` addition; re-wiring a loop is a change of bond. They are not the same operation.
- **Scale:** contact cascades. Fig 11 :52-53: "Scoping the habitat to direct contact is how DDT reached the eagles: first-order enumeration, second-order effect, and the residual that mattered was three steps out." Cross-scale composition is lossy, so a statement about a local loop and one about a planetary boundary are not the same statement (Fig 4 :27-30).

### One line per domain: actors / currency / anchor

A domain needs a nameable anchor. Where none can be named, it is not yet a domain.

| domain | actors | currency | anchor | nameable anchor? |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| D1 Unmaintained habitat | climate, water, ice, land, organisms; conditions and relations (Fig 11 :46-47) | contact and survival; what the ground kept | the ground, which does not update (Fig 11 :56) | yes |
| D2 Maintained habitat (human seat) | institutions, technologies, land use, introduced actors, instruments | introduction, not subtraction; capability is agent-and-habitat | the ground still decides (Fig 11 :22 in the maintained panel) | yes |

D1 and D2 are not two views of one habitat. They are the habitat before and under a maintainer, and the maintainer's power is asymmetric across them (F8). D3 is the object the question actually asks about. D4 is the frame that would settle D3. All four have anchors, so all four are domains.

### Partition or union

Union (Fig 9 :54: "when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again"). The pressure channel and the feedback channel overlap in every real intervention: adding a species changes actors and bonds; adding greenhouse gas changes forcing and also weakens carbon-sink feedbacks. The shared fraction cannot be assigned from the figure set, so it is recorded, not averaged away. Verdicts per domain are kept separate.

### Order by dependency (Fig 3)

1. Enumerate the habitat before identifying loops (Fig 11 :43-44). A loop cannot be named before its actors are on the list.
2. Describe the residual before choosing a frame (Fig 9 :27-28). Otherwise any frame will seem to fit.
3. Refuse before mint (Formula step 2): refuse "add pressure alone explains it" before minting a feedback term.
4. Settle first-order contact before second-order cascade (Fig 11 :52-53); a reading below the break is a reading of nothing (Fig 3 :22-23).
5. Range each frame against the anchor separately (Fig 2 :45-46).

## 3. Findings

### F1: The question's either/or is a union, not a partition

Fig 9 :54: "when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again." Fig 9 :55-56: "A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones. A single lever clears the region where only one condition holds and leaves the intersections standing, because the second condition still binds." Fig 9 :57: "a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either. It stops being a mixture and becomes a specific compound."

A human activity usually both adds a pressure and changes a loop. Reducing the question to "which one" loses the compound. The correct form is: for each feedback slot, which channel governs the observed residual, and what fraction is shared?

**Mode:** `specified`. **Status:** triangulated across Fig 9 and the domain census (low overlap: one is a metalaw, the other an enumeration of this residual). **Refuter:** if the two channels can be shown to be mutually exclusive in every observed case, so that no activity does both, the union reading fails and the question becomes a partition with two clean answers.

### F2: The residual must stay indexed per feedback slot

Fig 1 :19: "The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated." Fig 1 :22: "Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average." Fig 1 :23: "The ground enters as the only metric."

An "overall amount of feedback change" is the forbidden aggregate. Ice albedo, carbon-sink strength, moisture recycling, and nutrient retention are different slots with different anchors. A single global index would let one amplifying loop hide inside a near-zero mean (Fig 1 :13).

**Mode:** `general` on the rule, `specified` on this question. **Status:** triangulated (Fig 1 plus the Symbols table definition of R as a slice). **Refuter:** if a single scalar is shown to preserve the sign and location of every live feedback residual, the per-slot requirement is refuted.

### F3: "Add external pressure" is an actor addition, an import at first order

Fig 6 :21: "MINT composes inside the closure and can never add an atom." Fig 6 :22: "IMPORT adds an atom, and the added atom is what expands the closure." Fig 6 :23: "No frame escapes its own closure. The bound moves only on contact with a frame whose closure differs." Fig 11 :61: "Introduction is available; exclusion is not."

Under the habitat reading, adding a pressure introduces an actor or a forcing into the existing set: a new emitted gas, a new species, a new land cover. That is the introduction operation. It changes what is in contact, and therefore the first-order response, without yet asserting that the loop structure changed. Fig 6 :25-26: "The world does not gain primitives; a frame gains access." What the manager adds is a contact, not a new law of nature.

**Mode:** `specified`. **Status:** triangulated (Fig 6 import rule plus Fig 11 introduction rule, different objects: frame closure versus habitat). **Refuter:** if a forcing changes system response while the actor set and the bond structure are both held fixed, then pressure is not an actor addition and this identification fails.

### F4: "Alter a feedback" is a change of arrangement, an isomeric change

Fig 12 :5: "A set of ingredients is not one. The bond is the rest of the information, and it is the half nothing was recording." Fig 12 :94: "The arrangement is the substance." Fig 12 :117: "isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."

This is the figure-grounded core of the question. If the actor set is unchanged but the bonds between actors change, the system is a different substance under Fig 12. That is what "altering a feedback" means structurally: same atmosphere, ocean, ice, land, biota, different couplings, different outcome. It is not an import and it adds no atom. It is a re-bonding, and the difference is invisible in a list of ingredients. The pressure channel (F3) adds an atom; the feedback channel rearranges atoms. Same actors, different bond, different substance.

**Mode:** `specified`. **Status:** triangulated (Fig 12 :5 and :117 for the bond/isomer claim, Fig 4 :19-20 for "only the actors, the currency and the anchor change" across a scale). **Refuter:** if every observed feedback alteration can be fully described as the addition of a new actor, the isomer reading collapses into F3 and the question loses its distinction.

### F5: A feedback is a cycle whose sign decides closure

Fig 12 :121: "cycle. The output re-enters as input." Fig 12 :122: "the sign of the loop decides whether it closes, and a chain cannot close by construction." Fig 12 :119: "catalyst. A part that survives its own use."

A pressure is a push along a chain. A feedback is output returning as input, and whether it damps or amplifies is a property of the loop's sign, not of the push's size. This is why "how much pressure" cannot answer "how was the feedback altered": the question is about a sign and a closure, which are bond properties. A catalyst (Fig 12 :119) matters because a part that survives its own use changes behaviour across uses, and nothing in the ingredient list records which part that is.

**Mode:** `specified`. **Status:** single-source for the cycle definition (Fig 12), corroborated in a low-overlap field by control theory (Negative feedback, see `09_watson_answer.md` WORLD). **Refuter:** if loop sign can be changed by scaling the forcing alone, with no change of bond or closure, the cycle reading fails.

### F6: Actor valence is relative to the residual, not intrinsic

Fig 11 :49: "An actor's valence is relative to the residual, not a property it carries." Fig 11 :50: "The same actor is a benefactor to one residual and a malfactor to another, so mutualism and parasitism are relations rather than kinds." Fig 11 :51: "No standing list of good actors survives a change of residual."

A technology or land use is not "a pressure" or "a feedback restorer" by kind. Its role depends on which residual is tracked. Nitrogen fertilizer raises yield for a food residual and alters a nutrient-cycle feedback for a biogeochemical residual. The answer therefore cannot be a standing list of human activities with fixed signs. The residual selects the valence.

**Mode:** `specified`. **Status:** triangulated (Fig 11 valence rule plus the D1 to D4 census showing the same actor across different anchors). **Refuter:** if a single activity keeps the same sign for every residual, so that a standing classification suffices, this finding fails.

### F7: The residual that matters can be several steps out

Fig 11 :43: "The habitat is enumerated, never composed." Fig 11 :44: "List everything in contact with the residual, then what is in contact with those, and outward until the cascade stops mattering." Fig 11 :52-53: "Scoping the habitat to direct contact is how DDT reached the eagles: first-order enumeration, second-order effect, and the residual that mattered was three steps out." Fig 3 :27: "A link with no instrument is not a link that works. It is a link nobody has looked at."

Feedback alteration typically appears downstream of the direct contact: nutrient runoff to eutrophication to a new phosphorus-recycling loop; deforestation to weakened moisture recycling to a forest-to-savanna regime. First-order scoping records the pressure and misses the changed loop. A missing instrument at that link is not evidence that the loop works (Fig 3).

**Mode:** `specified`. **Status:** triangulated (Fig 11 cascade plus Fig 3 dependency order, different objects: habitat extent versus chain readability). **Refuter:** if first-order enumeration captures every altered feedback residual in a set of test cases, the cascade claim is refuted for those cases.

### F8: The maintainer's power is asymmetric; management is permanent work

Fig 11 :61: "Introduction is available; exclusion is not." Fig 11 :62: "You can add an actor and you cannot subtract the habitat, because contact is a fact about the world and does not consult you." Fig 11 :63: "Which is why a managed ecosystem is permanent work rather than a solved state." Fig 11 :39: "A system cannot be made more capable by improving the agent in isolation. An improvement that does not change contact changes nothing, however much it improves."

Human activity can introduce actors and re-bond loops, but cannot remove the habitat or switch off contact. That is why "restoring a natural feedback" is not a one-off repair: the manager keeps acting on a habitat they did not author. Capability is agent-and-habitat (Fig 11 :39), so an intervention that does not change contact changes nothing, however well funded.

**Mode:** `specified`. **Status:** triangulated (Fig 11 asymmetric power plus Fig 11 :39 capability claim; these share a figure but govern different quantities: seat power versus contact change, so the overlap is declared). **Refuter:** if an intervention can restore an original loop by subtraction and remain restored with no further maintenance, the asymmetry claim fails.

### F9: A claim that a feedback changed needs a non-updating anchor

Fig 2 :29-30: "An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks." Fig 2 :35: "Alignment is triangulation, not negotiation. A mirror cannot correct you." Fig 2 :42: "Buy independence in proportion to the cost of being wrong." Fig 10 :24: "the ground does not decay, the channel does".

For D3, the anchor is a measurement of system response under comparable forcing: an experiment, a natural experiment, a palaeo-record, a long time series. A model tuned to the observed outcome is not an anchor, because it moves when the claim is pushed (Fig 2 :30). Agreement among models that share an evidence pool is one pool talking (Fig 2 :40), not corroboration.

**Mode:** `specified`. **Status:** triangulated (Fig 2 anchor rule plus Fig 10 channel decay, different objects). **Refuter:** if a frame's own prediction is accepted as the settlement of whether its feedback changed, the anchor requirement is violated and any verdict from it is withdrawn.

### F10: Substrate reading: what "alter" changes

Fig 13 :22: "a difference that can be spent." Fig 13 :24: "that entities relate, and influence travels." Fig 13 :76: "Energy and a gradient compose a flow. A gradient over space and one over time are different terms, because which state it is bonded to is part of what it is." Fig 13 :32: "a remainder compounding through a chain that amplifies is chaos".

In substrate terms, human activity alters feedbacks by changing what can be spent (fossil energy subsidy, nutrient flux), what relates to what (habitat fragmentation, species introductions), and which state a gradient is bonded to. Because each turn leaves a remainder that re-enters the next, a small contact change can compound through an amplifying chain into a large response. This is a binding from the Earth-science frame onto the six held atoms; it is not a derivation of a particular feedback from the substrate.

**Mode:** `specified`, ungrounded. **Status:** single-source for the substrate mapping (Fig 13), with the world corroboration in `09` carrying the same Earth-system evidence pool as the question itself. **Refuter:** if a specific feedback's sign and magnitude can be derived from the six-term set alone, with no binding from Earth science, then the shape-not-a-name limit in F11 is breached and this mapping is promoted or the limit is wrong.

### F11: The `gap`: which feedback, where, with what sign and magnitude

Fig 9 :32: "you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself." Fig 13 :134: "Unreachable is a verdict here, not a silence". Fig 8 :39: "So a search that finds nothing is never a proof of absence." Fig 9 :27-28: "describe it before you go looking, or any frame you pick will seem to fit".

The figure set fixes the shape of the distinction (atom addition versus bond rearrangement; cycle sign; valence relative to residual; contact cascade; anchor) but it does not name which Earth-system feedback is altered by which activity, or by how much. Naming "the ice-albedo feedback" or "the Amazon moisture-recycling feedback" is a binding from the Earth-science frame, provenance to be recorded, not a figure derivation. This is `unreachable` at this budget, not underivable in principle; a source-discriminating instrument or a clean natural experiment could close it.

**Mode:** `general` on the limit. **Status:** triangulated (Fig 9 :32 plus Fig 13 :134 plus the canonical README's "shape, not a name", three low-overlap statements of the same limit). **Refuter:** if a figure line is produced that selects a specific feedback and its sign for a given activity, this gap is closed and the claim is withdrawn.

## 4. Pre-registered refuter (written before the conclusion)

Per Fig 5 :65-66, the sought-for shape is bound so that it can be refused. The following are written in advance. Any one firing withdraws the corresponding claim; none is hidden in the final section.

1. **Union (F1):** a demonstration that the pressure and feedback channels never overlap in any observed case.
2. **Per-slot (F2):** a single scalar that preserves sign and location of every live feedback residual.
3. **Pressure = actor addition (F3):** a forcing that changes response while actor set and bond structure are both fixed.
4. **Feedback = re-bonding (F4):** every feedback alteration fully described as a new actor, collapsing F4 into F3.
5. **Cycle (F5):** loop sign changed by scaling the forcing alone.
6. **Valence (F6):** one activity with the same sign for every residual.
7. **Cascade (F7):** first-order enumeration capturing every altered feedback residual in a test set.
8. **Asymmetry (F8):** a restored loop that stays restored with no maintenance after subtraction.
9. **Anchor (F9):** a frame's own prediction accepted as settlement of its own feedback change.
10. **Gap (F11):** a figure line selecting a specific feedback and sign for a given activity.

If 4 or 5 fires, the central distinction of the question dissolves and the answer must be rewritten as a single-channel account. Until one is run, nothing here is `grounded`.

## 5. Recommendation

1. Lead with F3 to F5: the question's distinction is the atom-versus-bond distinction, not a magnitude distinction.
2. Keep verdicts per slot (F2). Report shared fractions as union residues, not as averages (F1).
3. Ask what the anchor would be before naming a feedback (F9). A claim without an anchor is a `specified` hypothesis, not a verdict.
4. Enumerate two steps out, not one (F7). The changed loop is usually downstream of the direct pressure.
5. Record the maintenance requirement (F8). "Restored" is a standing contact, not a solved state.
6. State the gap plainly (F11). Naming a feedback is a binding from Earth science, recorded as provenance.
7. Send the residual and refuter first (Fig 10 :52). Offer the figures only after a verdict on stage 1 is recorded.
