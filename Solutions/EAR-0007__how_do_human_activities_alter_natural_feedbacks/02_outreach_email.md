**Subject:** One checkable distinction about feedbacks, before any theory

> Hi [name],
>
> I have a specific distinction I would like you to check against what you already know. I am not asking you to read a framework first, and I am not asking you to agree with anything. If the distinction does not survive, there is nothing else to look at.
>
> **The residual (`specified`, not `grounded`).** The same external forcing can produce a different Earth-system response after a human activity, because the system's own loops changed. Two ways this is described in the literature are an external forcing or pressure, and a change in internal feedbacks. My claim is that these are not the same kind of thing, and that conflating them hides the mechanism.
>
> **The shape claim.** An added pressure changes what is in contact: a new emitted gas, a new species, a new land cover. That is an addition to the cast. An altered feedback changes the arrangement: the same atmosphere, ocean, ice, land and biota, coupled differently, so the loop's sign or closure changes. Same actors, different bond, different substance. A feedback is a cycle whose sign decides whether it damps or amplifies, so "how much pressure" cannot tell you "how the feedback changed." That is a separate question with a separate anchor: a measurement of response under comparable forcing, not a model tuned to the outcome.
>
> **What this does and does not say.** It does not name which feedback changed, where, or by how much. It does not say a feedback is a new actor. It does not rank human activities as good or bad: the same activity has different signs for different residuals. It does not claim a single global measure of feedback change; that would average away the loops that matter. It does not claim any of this is settled by measurement. I am offering a shape and a separation, not a verdict about a particular ecosystem.
>
> **What would refute it (written down before this message):**
> 1. a forcing changes system response while the actor set and bond structure are both held fixed;
> 2. every observed feedback alteration is fully described as the addition of a new actor, so the arrangement claim collapses;
> 3. loop sign can be changed by scaling the forcing alone, with no change of bond or closure;
> 4. first-order enumeration captures every altered feedback residual in a set of test cases, so the cascade claim fails;
> 5. a frame's own prediction is accepted as the settlement of whether its feedback changed, which would break the anchor requirement.
>
> If any one holds, I will withdraw the corresponding part. A one-line "this fails because..." is more useful to me than agreement. If you would rather not engage, no reply is needed.
>
> **What I would value from you:** what would you count as evidence that an internal feedback, rather than the forcing, actually changed? I am asking for your criterion, not your endorsement.
>
> **If it survives, and only then:** the source links are below. The figures, not my summary, are the authority.
> - Local verbatim figure text with line numbers: `FRAMEWORK/reference/03-FIGURES_TEXT.md`
> - Verified public planetary-boundaries overview: https://www.stockholmresilience.org/research/planetary-boundaries.html

>

Write down what would refute your result *before* you run it, and state the mode of what you get. A reported failure is more useful to me than a reported success.

## Two-stage offer, recorded

1. **Stage 1.** Send the residual, the shape claim and the five refuters. Ask what the recipient would count as a failure. Record their verdict before any theory is shown.
2. **Stage 2, opt-in only.** If they are curious, send the four links with the note that the figures are the authority. Their later agreement is never pooled as corroboration, because stage 2 recipients have seen the interiors (Fig 2 :40 "agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool").
