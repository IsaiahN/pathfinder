# Isomers and order: what "alter" changes and when it shows up

**Mode: `specified`.** This is a simulated derivation checked against the figures, not a ground reading (see `05_what_shape_means.md` section 4).

## RESIDUAL

The question asks how altering a feedback differs from adding pressure. Two supporting derivations are owed: (1) what kind of change each is, in atom-and-bond terms; (2) in what order the change appears, since the residual that matters is often far from the first contact. A third quantity, the cost of checking, is kept separate because the figures price it in different units.

## DOMAIN CENSUS

Atoms held in the Earth-system frame: atmosphere, ocean, ice, land, biota, and the gradients and networks that couple them (a binding onto the six, not a derivation). Bonds held: addition of an actor, re-bonding of couplings, sign of a cycle, and the flow a gradient makes when bonded to a state. No new primitive is available without an import. Anchors: a measurement of response under comparable forcing for the loop claim; a non-updating frame for the claim about change. Verdicts are kept per slot.

## DERIVATION

### Q1: Are "add pressure" and "alter feedback" isomers?

- Fig 12 :117 "isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."
- Fig 12 :5 "A set of ingredients is not one. The bond is the rest of the information, and it is the half nothing was recording."
- Fig 12 :94 "The arrangement is the substance."

**Case A, pressure added.** A new emitter, species or land cover enters the cast. This is an actor addition. Fig 6 :22 "IMPORT adds an atom, and the added atom is what expands the closure." The atom set changes; the loop arrangement may stay the same. This is not isomerism, because the atoms are not the same.

**Case B, feedback altered.** The cast is unchanged but the couplings change: a sink becomes a source, a damping loop becomes amplifying, moisture recycling weakens. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. This **is** Fig 12 isomerism. It is invisible in an inventory of actors and decisive in behaviour, which is exactly why an "amount of pressure" metric misses it.

**Case C, two altered states compared.** Two post-intervention states over the same atom set, differing only in which structure mediates which state pair, are isomers of each other. Fig 13 :76 "Energy and a gradient compose a flow. A gradient over space and one over time are different terms, because which state it is bonded to is part of what it is." Which state a gradient is bonded to is part of the term, so re-bonding makes a different term.

- **Falsifier, written before the run:** if every observed feedback alteration involves a new actor, then Case B never occurs, the isomer claim fails, and the question collapses to Case A. Recorded in `01` refuter 4.

### Q2: Sign and closure of the loop

- Fig 12 :121 "cycle. The output re-enters as input."
- Fig 12 :122 "the sign of the loop decides whether it closes, and a chain cannot close by construction."

A pressure travels along a chain; a feedback is a cycle. Whether the returned signal damps or amplifies is a property of the loop, not of the push's size. In substrate terms a feedback couples a state to a structure and back: Fig 13 :74 "Everything else is a composition across the two kinds, never within one." A direct state to state coupling is not the composition the figure permits; a structure must sit between. That is why altering a feedback is a change of bond type or sign, not a change of magnitude.

Low-overlap corroboration (world, secondary): the control-theory page fetched this session states that negative feedback promotes stability, positive feedback tends to instability, and both require a loop. It corroborates the sign claim, not the atom-versus-bond claim. **Mode:** `specified`, single-source for the figure mapping with a low-overlap corroborator for the sign.

### Q3: Order of appearance: first, second, third

- Fig 11 :52-53 "Scoping the habitat to direct contact is how DDT reached the eagles: first-order enumeration, second-order effect, and the residual that mattered was three steps out."
- Fig 3 :20 "The chain is ordered by dependency, not by importance."
- Fig 11 :43 "The habitat is enumerated, never composed."

The intervention touches an actor (first order). That actor changes a coupling (second order). The changed coupling changes the loop the original residual was ranging against (third order). Enumerating only direct contact captures the pressure and misses the altered feedback. The dependency order is not the same as a rank of importance: effort spent on the first link cannot rescue a broken second link (Fig 3). A link with no instrument is not a working link (Fig 3 :27).

Cross-scale, the map from a local loop to a planetary reading is lossy: Fig 4 :30 "So take the round trip and measure the gap. Without it you are navigating by dead reckoning: confident about a position nobody checked." The round-trip gap `R_T` here is the set of constraints a global index cannot hold: which slot, which sign, which coupling, which history. Recorded qualitatively; not measured numerically.

- **Falsifier:** if first-order enumeration captures every altered-feedback residual in a set of test cases, the cascade order claim fails. Recorded in `01` refuter 7.

### Q4: Two currencies, kept apart

- Fig 12 :125 "A composition costs in two currencies, and they do not add."
- Fig 12 :126 "Bits price the description. Actions price finding out whether it holds."
- Fig 9 :60 "Use filters for the budget and witnesses for the verdict. Never let a filter hand you a verdict."

Describing an altered loop is cheap in bits. Measuring whether it actually changed is expensive in actions: a field campaign, a natural experiment, a long time series. A system that holds only one accounting will not notice the shortfall. A magnitude estimate (parts per million, watts per square metre) is a **filter for the budget**; it is not a witness for the claim that a feedback changed. This is why the packet in `08` asks for a criterion and a measurement, not a number.

## CONCLUSION

Adding pressure changes the atom set; altering a feedback re-bonds held atoms and changes a cycle's sign. Two altered states over the same cast are isomers: same actors, different arrangement, different substance. The change appears at second or third order, so first-order scoping misses it, and a cross-scale reading carries a round-trip gap that must be recorded. Description cost and measurement cost are different currencies, so a cheap magnitude estimate cannot stand in for the measurement that would settle the claim. All of this is `specified`; none of it names a particular Earth-system feedback.
