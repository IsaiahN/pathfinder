## QUESTION

Decide, in effect terms, what the Tether says about the contrast in the question: human activity as added external pressure versus human activity as alteration of a natural feedback. Option A: treat the two as one question about how much forcing is applied. Option B: treat them as two overlapping channels with different objects, different currency and different anchors. What each leads to: A produces a magnitude metric that can hide a changed loop behind a near-zero average and cannot say what would settle a claim; B produces a shape, separate per-domain verdicts, an anchor requirement, refuters written in advance, and a declared gap where the framework cannot name a feedback.

## DOMAIN CENSUS

R by arity, symmetry and scale, then one line per domain with actors, currency and anchor; partition or union; order by dependency.

- **R (the residual).** Same forcing, different response after human activity. Arity: two overlapping conditions, so a **union compound** (Fig 9 :57 "a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either. It stops being a mixture and becomes a specific compound"), not one hard question. Symmetry: a feedback is a cycle whose sign decides closure (Fig 12 :122), so the relation is signed and ordered, not a symmetric join. Scale: pressure at first contact, re-bonding at second order, changed residual at third (Fig 11 :52-53).
- **Domain 1, unmaintained habitat.** Actors: atmosphere, ocean, ice, land, organisms, conditions and relations. Currency: contact and survival. Anchor: the ground, which does not update (Fig 11 :56). Nameable, so a domain.
- **Domain 2, maintained habitat.** Actors: institutions, technologies, land use, introduced actors, instruments. Currency: introduction, not subtraction. Anchor: the ground still decides (Fig 11 :22). Nameable, so a domain.
- **Domain 3, loop structure.** Actors: cycles, catalysts, gradients, networks, valence relative to R. Currency: bonds, sign, and two incommensurable costs (Fig 12 :125). Anchor: a response measurement under comparable forcing. Nameable, so a domain.
- **Domain 4, verification frame.** Actors: researchers, instruments, models with differing closures. Currency: agreement and residual. Anchor: the ground, never agreement (Fig 2 :35). In this simulated run all seats share one evidence pool, so independence is low.
- **Partition or union:** union. The two channels overlap in nearly every real intervention. The shared fraction is assigned by measurement, not by the figure set.
- **Order by dependency:** enumerate habitat before loops (Fig 11 :43-44); describe the residual before naming a frame (Fig 9 :27-28); refuse the pressure-alone reading before minting (Formula step 2); settle first-order contact before second-order cascade; range each frame against the anchor separately (Fig 2 :45-46).

## VERDICT (from the figures)

The question's either/or is a union of overlapping channels, not one question with opposite answers. Adding pressure introduces an actor into the cast (import, first-order contact); altering a feedback re-bonds held atoms and changes a cycle's sign, which is isomerism, not atom addition. Valence is relative to the residual, so no standing activity list answers the question. The altered loop appears downstream of the direct contact, and a claim that it changed needs a non-updating response measurement. The framework names no particular feedback; that remains a `gap`, `unreachable` at this budget rather than underivable in principle. Mode `specified` on the structural claims, `general` on the shape, not `grounded`. All verdicts are per domain and per slot, never averaged.

- **cost if wrong:** bounded and locatable. The packet states mode, refuters and provenance before the conclusion, so an error is findable. The real cost is spending an outside reader's independence by sending a recording instead of a residual and a refuter (Fig 2 :42 "Buy independence in proportion to the cost of being wrong."). It is avoidable by leading with the shape claim. A second cost is scientific: treating a re-bonding as a bigger forcing can misdirect measurement toward magnitude and away from the loop that actually changed.
- **undoable:** yes. The gap is a limit on naming, not a withdrawal of the shape. A source-discriminating instrument can close it without changing any primitive. A wrong binding from Earth science is provenance-labelled and can be superseded. The pressure channel is not refused; it is assigned to its own domain.
- **reversed by:** a ground reading, not agreement. A response measurement under comparable forcing that a fixed-cast, fixed-bond model cannot reproduce would support the re-bonding claim; a case where the actor set and bond structure are held fixed and the response still changes would refute C1; a measured loop sign flip with forcing held comparable would support C2; a frame prediction accepted as its own settlement would break C3; a figure line selecting a specific feedback and sign would close the gap. Until one of these fires, the verdict stands as `candidate`.
