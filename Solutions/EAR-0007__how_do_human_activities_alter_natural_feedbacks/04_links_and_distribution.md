## Wider literature that bears on this question, read this session

1. **Climate feedbacks.** The IPCC AR6 framing quoted on the fetched page distinguishes the forcing (emissions) from the feedbacks that control sensitivity to it. This is the world-language version of the question's own contrast. It corroborates F3 to F5 but is not evidence for the figure set.
2. **Regime shifts.** The Biggs et al. 2009 definition, quoted on the fetched page, says a regime shift changes internal dynamics and feedbacks so that the system does not return even when the driver is reduced. That is the strongest single world corroboration of "alter a feedback, not just add pressure." It is an Earth-system source, so it shares the question's evidence pool.
3. **Ecosystem engineering.** Jones, Lawton and Shachak 1994 is the paper the figures themselves cite (Fig 11 :65). The fetched page confirms the introduction framing and the "humans as ecosystem engineers" section. This is corroboration of the figure line, not an independent witness to it.
4. **Control theory.** Negative feedback promotes stability, positive feedback tends to instability, and both require a loop. This is the lowest-overlap field in the set (engineering, not Earth science) and it corroborates the cycle-sign claim in F5. It does not corroborate the atom-versus-bond distinction, which remains single-source in the figures plus an isomer analogy.
5. **Planetary boundaries.** The mutual dependence of the nine processes corroborates the union reading (F1) and the cascade claim (F7). It shares the Earth-system evidence pool.

## Conflict handling

The one apparent conflict, that regime shifts can be triggered by an external shock as well as by weakening internal feedbacks, is not a conflict: the two are the two channels of the union in F1, and the world source confirms rather than denies the overlap.

## Distribution: offer, do not push

Figure-grounded rules:

- **Fig 4 :22 "Only methods cross up. Only head starts come down."** Sending the full architecture before anyone has described a residual sends a recording, not a generator. Lead with the shape claim and its refuter; offer links after.
- **Fig 10 :42 "A verifier that can reconstruct the claim cannot verify it."** A recipient who has absorbed the framework before giving a verdict has joined the frame being checked.
- **Fig 9 :27-28 "describe it before you go looking, or any frame you pick will seem to fit."** Ask what they would count as a failure before giving them the article.
- **Fig 2 :40 "agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool".** A send selected for people likely to agree produces a convention, not corroboration.
- **Fig 2 :42 "Buy independence in proportion to the cost of being wrong."** A wrong claim about feedbacks has a real cost, so spend effort on a few genuinely different frames, not a large agreeing list.

**Recommended order in a reply:** (1) the residual and the shape claim; (2) the five refuters, with the ask "what would you count as a failure?"; (3) record their verdict before showing any theory; (4) only then, if they opt in, the four public links, with the note that the figures are the authority.

## The two-stage offer and its limit

Stage 1 is `Bring a characterised residual, never the source` (Fig 10 :52). Stage 2 is opt-in by construction. Stage 3 sends a method, which is permitted because a generator can be given away without giving away an answer (Fig 4 :22). Each user must write their own refuter and state their own mode, because a frame cannot check the direction of its own question (Formula, "Direction") and outputs "could never be verified if the solutions presented were not contrived to fit the formula" (Arrival of the Fittest essay).

**Hard precondition:** the stage-1 output must survive re-checking against the verbatim figure text. This folder is a simulated derivation, not a ground reading, so it must be labelled as such in every message that carries it.
