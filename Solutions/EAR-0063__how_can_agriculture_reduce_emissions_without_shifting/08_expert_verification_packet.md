# Expert verification packet: the Tether on agricultural emissions and shifted harm

**What this is:** a claim in a form a researcher in agricultural emissions can check. Mode, derivation and refuters are stated before the conclusion, so a reader knows what to look for and what would settle it.

**Mode: `specified` on the structural claims, `general` on the shape. Not `grounded`.** No measurement of any real supply chain is claimed.

**Status:** a simulated derivation checked for internal consistency against the figures, not a ground reading. It cannot settle the question it reports on.

## 1. The claim, in one paragraph

A claimed reduction in agricultural emissions is not admissible as "without shifting harm" unless the habitat in contact with the residual is enumerated out to the point where the cascade stops mattering; the residual is reported per region slot and per harm currency rather than as one aggregate; the objective is written as a comparison of total net burden before and after against an anchor that does not move when the adopting frame pushes on it; at least two frames with low evidence overlap agree on that comparison; and what crosses between regions is the checking method, not a recipe from one occasion. The reason is structural: moving a residual substitutes a habitat rather than removing it, so the new habitat's actors are inherited, what the goal failed to reproduce is invisible until the goal fails, and what was unintentionally introduced is invisible until it acts. The framework does not name a practice. It offers the shape, refuters written in advance, and the instruments that would close it.

## 2. Three checkable sub-claims

### C1 - framework-internal; checkable by reading Figures 1 and 11

- **Statement:** the unit of accounting is the region slot, and moving the residual substitutes a habitat.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 1 :19 "The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated." Fig 1 :22 "Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average." Fig 11 :66 "Isolation is not removal of the habitat. It is substitution of one habitat for another." Fig 11 :67 "Move the residual and you must reproduce everything its goal required, and you inherit the new habitat's actors, which you did not choose." Also Fig 11 :68 "what you failed to reproduce is invisible until the goal fails" and Fig 11 :69 "what you unintentionally introduced is invisible until it acts".
- **Refuter:** a per-region, per-medium accounting across the full cascade showing no positive residual in any slot after the change, measured against an anchor independent of the adopting frame; or a documented case where a practice's full habitat is reproduced elsewhere with no unchosen actors and no invisible failure.

### C2 - world-facing; checkable by an agricultural emissions researcher against published evidence

- **Statement:** the two conditions in the question (emissions down here, harm not moved there) have been repeatedly separated in practice, so a single boundary does not close them. The structural name for this is substitution; published cases include indirect land-use change and telecoupled sending, receiving and spillover systems.
- **What to check:** Searchinger et al. 2008 (*Science*, DOI 10.1126/science.1151861, abstract read via Crossref 2026-11-22) report that earlier analyses *"have failed to count the carbon emissions that occur as farmers worldwide respond to higher prices and convert forest and grassland to new cropland to replace the grain (or cropland) diverted to biofuels."* Liu et al. 2013 (*Ecology and Society* 18(2):26) describe *"sending, receiving, and spillover systems"* and note that forest transitions *"often occur at the cost of forests in other countries through trade of forest and agricultural products."* Ritchie 2019 (*Our World in Data*) explains that consumption-based accounting must *"include all CO2 emissions that were emitted in the production of that good"* when a good is imported. Are these fair descriptions of the literature? Do they show a change in the conditions rather than a harder instance of one condition?
- **Refuter:** published evidence that in a defined set of supply sheds the two conditions covary so tightly that one boundary closes both, with per-slot measurements rather than a model aggregate. That would make the union reading wrong for that set.

### C3 - framework-internal derivation of the checking procedure

- **Statement:** the anchor must be outside the frame that reports the saving, and two low-overlap frames are needed; the objective is a comparison with a refusal condition.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 1 :27 "They are frame-internal. A frame cannot score itself with a quantity it also produces." Fig 2 :29 "An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks." Fig 8 :45 "Two frames together reach what neither reaches alone." Fig 8 :49 "Two frames that overlap almost completely are one frame wearing two names." Fig 12 :112 "A plan is a sequence. A goal is a comparison. Progress is a subtraction. A refutation is a negation, and that one is not a bond."
- **Refuter:** show that a territorial inventory is an anchor for the no-shift condition (that it does not move when the region pushes on it), or that two frames drawn from the same activity data and emission factors have surplus, or that the objective can be stated without a threshold comparison and still close the question.

## 3. What is explicitly not claimed

- Not a named practice, cultivar, inhibitor, certification, diet, or technology.
- Not a claim about any real farm, region, or supply chain; no measurement was taken.
- Not that local emissions reductions are worthless; they are a legitimate first-order result in their own frame.
- Not that all harm is carbon. Nitrogen, water, land cover and biodiversity are separate currencies and do not add to CO2 in one figure.
- Not that a certification or border adjustment subtracts a habitat. Fig 11 :61 gives introduction, not exclusion.
- Not `grounded`. Nothing here is asserted as settled by an instrument.

## 5. What would settle it

A source-discriminating instrument, not a further argument. From *The Hard Problem is About Source* (opened 2026-11-22): an instrument that reads green *"cannot comment on the source"*; the spectrometer resolved the cases. Here:

1. **Source discrimination:** does the emissions fall come from abatement, imported feed, displaced processing, or land conversion elsewhere? Pair a territorial inventory with a consumption-based reconstruction and an independent land-cover series, each with provenance.
2. **Threshold test:** does total net burden across the enumerated habitat fall from before to after, per region slot and per harm currency, against an anchor that does not move when any frame pushes on it?
3. **Substitution and cascade tests:** are the goal's required conditions reproduced at the new location with declared actors, and does the residual stay zero at second and third order after the programme has run long enough for those effects to act?

## 6. What an expert can check in twenty minutes

1. Read Fig 1 :19, :22, :27 and Fig 11 :66-69, :43-44. Six short passages.
2. Check C2 against the published record: is the separation of the two conditions real, and are the three quoted sources described fairly?
3. Name one instrument that would discriminate genuine abatement from displaced production in a supply shed you know, or say why none is possible.
4. Try to fire one of the four refuters in `01` §4. A failure is more useful than an endorsement.
