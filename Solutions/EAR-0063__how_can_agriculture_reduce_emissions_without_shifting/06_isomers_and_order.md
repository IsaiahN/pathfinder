# Isomers and order in agricultural mitigation

**Mode: `specified`.** Simulated derivation checked against the figures, not a ground reading (see `05_what_shape_means.md` §6).

## RESIDUAL

The main audit establishes that emissions down and harm not moved form a union compound and that the right unit is the region slot, not the aggregate. Two supporting questions remain:

1. Are two agricultural systems with the same inputs but different arrangements the same thing or different things?
2. In what order must the checking loop run, and where does it usually break?

## DOMAIN CENSUS (supporting)

Atoms available in the agricultural frame: land, water, fertilizer, feed, livestock, energy, labour, capital, and the emission species CO2, CH4, N2O. Bonds available: the seven typed relations plus negation. Fig 12 :46: "A bond is the typed relation between two operands. There are seven, they are not interchangeable, and which one holds is not recoverable from the operands." No new atom is introduced here. Scale: first order on a farm, second order across a supply shed, third order along the cascade. Valence of each actor is relative to the residual, not carried by it (Fig 11 :49).

## DERIVATION

### Q1 - Are two systems with the same inputs different substances?

Fig 12 :117: "isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."

Fig 12 :96: "Two entries with an identical ingredient set are not variants of one thing. They are different things whose notation cannot tell them apart, and the difference is carried entirely by the bond."

Consider two systems with the same atom multiset: one hectare, one unit of nitrogen, one unit of feed, one livestock unit, one unit of energy.

- **System A (sequence bond, `→`):** fertilizer is applied, yield rises, surplus feed is exported, land elsewhere is converted. Atoms: the same. Arrangement: a chain whose last link is outside the reporting boundary.
- **System B (cycle bond, closed):** manure returns to land, nitrogen is captured in a rotation, the output re-enters as input. Atoms: the same. Arrangement: a loop whose sign decides whether it closes.

Under Fig 12 these are **isomers**, not variants of one practice with a different efficiency label. A list that records only the ingredients, "one hectare, one unit of nitrogen, one livestock unit," records half of itself. The difference is decisive in behaviour: A can satisfy E1 and fail E2; B can satisfy both or fail both depending on whether the loop actually closes.

**Falsifier, written before the conclusion:** if two systems with identical atom multisets and different bond structures show identical total net burden across the full habitat, measured per slot and per medium, the isomer claim fails and the flat ingredient account is adequate. I expect it to fire rarely; if it fires, `01` F8 is weakened.

### Q2 - Which structures matter beyond isomerism?

Fig 12 :119: "catalyst. A part that survives its own use. Spent and regenerating parts behave differently across uses, and nothing records which a term is."

Fig 12 :121: "cycle. The output re-enters as input. The sign of the loop decides whether it closes, and a chain cannot close by construction."

Fig 12 :124: "functional group. A substructure whose presence fixes the outcome. The group says what kind; the qualifier says which member."

- **Catalyst:** soil organic matter, a monitoring institution, or a skilled practitioner survives its own use. A practice that consumes its catalyst (depleting soil while raising yield) behaves differently across uses from one that regenerates it. An ingredient list does not record which kind a term is, so a one-year measurement cannot distinguish them.
- **Cycle:** a nutrient loop closes only if its sign is right. A chain cannot close by construction, so a linear "input to yield to waste" arrangement cannot satisfy a no-shift condition without an import from outside the chain. That import is the shifted harm.
- **Functional group:** `state → structure → state` is the shared substructure of many practices. "Regenerative" says what kind; the qualifier says which member. A label fixes the kind, not the member, so it cannot certify the outcome.

### Q3 - What order does the loop run in, and where does it break?

Fig 3 :19: "Optimising a link that already works cannot rescue one that does not."

Fig 3 :20: "The chain is ordered by dependency, not by importance."

The dependency chain for this question is:

1. **Perceive.** Measure emissions and non-carbon harm per region slot. This link usually works; instruments exist.
2. **Vocabulary.** Have terms for what the farm and the supply shed afford: imports, embodied emissions, displaced land, nutrient cascades, and the harm currencies. This link is patchy and has few instruments.
3. **Objective.** State what counts as progress: total net burden before versus after against a pinned anchor, with a refusal condition. This is the link that usually breaks, because it has neither obvious failures nor obvious tests.
4. **Planning.** Choose a practice under incomplete information. This link has many instruments and attracts most effort.
5. **Learn and carry.** Name what worked, verify it, carry the method (not the recording) to the next habitat.

Fig 3 :25-27 names the middle as the usual break: perception and planning have obvious failures and tests, while having terms for what a situation affords and being able to say what would count as progress has neither. A link with no instrument is not a link that works; it is a link nobody has looked at. So effort spent on better emission factors at link 1 or better practice portfolios at link 4 arrives at a step that cannot use it if link 3 is broken.

**Falsifier:** show that a real programme's chain did not break at link 3, by producing a pinned before/after comparison and a refusal condition that were actually measured before the practice was chosen. If that is common, the dependency reading is wrong for this domain.

### Q4 - What crosses between levels?

Fig 12 :104: "A settled molecule becomes an atom for whatever composes over it."

A verified practice in one region is a settled molecule at that level. It can become an atom in the next level's comparison, but only if it was settled against the ground and only as an operand, not as a universal recipe. Fig 4 :22: "Only methods cross up. Only head starts come down." The new habitat remakes the atom, so the cross is lossy and R_T must be recorded (Fig 4 :30).

**Falsifier:** a practice that crosses into a new habitat and retains its measured effect without re-enumeration or re-measurement. That would make the transform lossless and remove the need for R_T.

## CONCLUSION

The supporting derivation gives two results. First, agricultural systems with the same inputs under different bond structures are isomers, not variants of one practice; the flat ingredient list is the failure mode, not a neutral description. Second, the checking loop is ordered by dependency and usually breaks at the objective link, so better emissions measurement cannot rescue a missing definition of "without shifting harm." Catalyst, cycle and functional group give the three structures a flat metric cannot write. All four claims are `specified` and unmeasured.
