**Subject:** One checkable claim about local recruitment vs structural shifts in movements

> Hi [name],
>
> I have one specific output I would like you to check against what you already know about social movements and collective dynamics. I am not asking you to read any theory first, and I am not asking you to agree with anything. If the output does not survive, stop there.
>
> **The residual (`specified`, not `grounded`):** for a named movement, observed growth is not predicted by a single mechanism. Two candidate descriptions compete. One says growth is composition inside the network and habitat the movement already has, so new participants are reachable from existing ones over existing ties. The other says growth requires an atom the local closure does not hold: a new participant class, a new resource, a new institution, or a change in repression or opportunity.
>
> **The shape claim I derived:** these are not two strengths on one dial. Local recruitment composes inside a closure and adds no atom. A structural shift is the operator that can add an atom or change what contact means, so it moves the wall. Which one binds for a given movement is decided by whether the atoms growth requires are already in its closure and reachable through its tie arrangement. Tie count is not enough; the arrangement of ties is the thing that carries recruitment.
>
> **What I am not claiming:** that any particular movement grew by one route; that there is a universal tipping point; that "structural shift" means "big local cascade"; or that a stalled recruitment drive proves local recruitment failed. A stalled drive can mean the needed route is in the closure and unfound, or that the closure lacks it. From inside the movement's own frame those two look the same.
>
> **What would refute the claim (written down before this email):**
> 1. One measured variable predicts both local-only and shift-dependent growth with no interaction term and no change of anchor.
> 2. A movement adds an atom to its closure through local composition alone, with no import or habitat change.
> 3. A condition change that adds no atom and changes no contact reliably produces the spurt in an independent data set.
> 4. Across networks with the same tie count but different clustering or bridging, recruitment trajectories are statistically indistinguishable.
>
> If any holds, I retract the claim at that point. A one-line "this fails because..." is more useful to me than agreement. If you would rather not engage, no reply is needed.
>
> **What I would actually like from you:** what would you expect to see in your data if the shape claim were true, and what would you count as a failure? Please answer that before looking at any of the material below, so your verdict is recorded while you still hold only the residual.
>
> **If it survives, and only then:** the sources are below. The figures are the authority, not my summary of them.
> - Figures and formula, branch `general-agent` (SVG and text): https://github.com/IsaiahN/tether/tree/general-agent/figures
> - Full architecture and the thirteen figures: https://adventuresinml.substack.com/p/arrival-of-the-fittest-the-tether
> - Worked narrative: https://adventuresinml.substack.com/p/how-intelligence-actually-works
>
> **If you want to use the machinery:** the formula is in the figures link. Run it on a movement you know well and can check against a ground. Write down what would refute your result *before* you run it. A reported failure is more useful to me than a reported success.
>
> - [name]

## The three stages, made explicit

1. **Stage 1 (this email's first half):** residual, shape claim and refuter. Ask what the reader would expect to see and what they would count as a failure. Record the answer.
2. **Stage 2, opt-in only:** if they are curious, offer the three links. The figures, not the summary, are the authority.
3. **Stage 3:** offer the formula as a method to reuse, with the instruction that each user writes their own refuter and states their own mode.

Stage 1 before stage 2 is Fig 10 :52 and Fig 2 :45 (*"Frames range against the ground. They do not negotiate with it."*). Stage 3 sends a method, which Fig 4 :22 permits. Stage 3 still needs a ground per problem: from *Arrival of the Fittest*, outputs *"could never be verified if the solutions presented were not contrived to fit the formula."*
