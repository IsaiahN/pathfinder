## 1. World sources actually fetched (secondary corroboration only)

| source | URL | what was actually there |
|---|---|---|
| Complex contagion (Wikipedia, citing Centola and Macy 2007; Easley and Kleinberg 2010) | https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Complex_contagion | *"Complex contagion is the phenomenon in social networks in which multiple sources of exposure to an innovation are required before an individual adopts the change of behavior."* Four mechanisms listed: coordination, credibility, legitimacy, emotional contagion. States that weak ties and small-world structure can slow complex contagions. Directly about high-risk social movements. |
| Threshold model (Wikipedia, covering Sakoda, Schelling, Granovetter) | https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Threshold_model | Schelling: *"there is no simple correspondence of individual incentive to collective results"* and a general theory of tipping. Granovetter: threshold is *"the number or proportion of others who must make one decision before a given actor does so"*, and *"threshold is situation-specific. The distribution of the thresholds determines the outcome of the aggregate behavior."* |
| Global cascades model (Wikipedia, citing Watts 2002 PNAS) | https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Global_cascades_model | *"large and rare cascades that are triggered by exogenous perturbations which are relatively small compared with the size of the system."* Gives an explicit cascade condition on the average vulnerable cluster size; sparse networks give power-law cascade sizes, denser ones bimodal. Network heterogeneity plays a significant role. |
| Critical mass (sociodynamics) (Wikipedia, citing Schelling 1978, Granovetter 1978, Oliver et al. 1985) | https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Critical_mass_(sociodynamics) | *"critical mass is a sufficient number of adopters of a new idea, technology or innovation in a social system so that the rate of adoption becomes self-sustaining and creates further growth."* Notes that qualitative properties (commitments, capabilities, goals, decisions) also matter, not just a count. |
| Diffusion of innovations (Wikipedia, Rogers 1962 and later) | https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Diffusion_of_innovations | *"diffusion is the process by which an innovation is communicated through certain channels over time among the participants in a social system."* The social system combines external influences (mass media, institutional or governmental mandates) and internal ones (strong and weak relationships). *"diffusion requires a certain degree of heterophily to introduce new ideas into a relationship; if two individuals are identical, no diffusion occurs because there is no new information to exchange."* |
| Political opportunity / political process theory (Wikipedia, citing Tarrow 1998, McAdam, Eisinger 1973, Meyer 2004) | https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Political_opportunity | *"success or failure of social movements is affected primarily by political opportunities."* Three components: insurgent consciousness, organisational strength, political opportunities. *"When there are no political opportunities, simply having grievances (organizational consciousness) and resources is not enough."* Criticism: the theory *"place[s] too great an emphasis on the role of social networks and often almost entirely ignore[s] the cultural underpinnings."* |
| Resource mobilization (Wikipedia, citing McCarthy and Zald 1977, Tilly, McAdam) | https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Resource_mobilization | *"the success of social movements depends on resources (time, money, skills, etc.) and the ability to use them."* *"grievances are not sufficient to explain creation of social movements. Instead, access to and control over resources is the crucial factor."* Five resource types including moral, cultural, social-organisational, material, human. |

**Overlap warning.** These seven pages share a pool: several are Wikipedia entries that cite the same canonical sociology (Granovetter, Schelling, Rogers, Tilly, McAdam, Tarrow, Watts). Two pages quoting the same review are one witness, not two. They corroborate the figure verdict; they do not overturn it. The political-opportunity page's own criticism is recorded above because it is a live conflict, not a footnote.

## 4. Two things in the world sources that bear directly on this question

1. **The threshold literature is not a single mechanism.** The threshold page distinguishes individual thresholds from the *distribution* of thresholds and says thresholds are situation-specific. The global-cascades page adds an explicit network condition: cascade size depends on the vulnerable-cluster structure, and the initiators matter. This corroborates F5 (arrangement, not count) and is a check on anyone who reads "local recruitment" as "one universal tipping point".
2. **The structural literature and the network literature are in tension.** Political-process theory says opportunities, not just networks and resources, decide emergence; its critics say it ignores the cultural underpinnings that let networks form. Resource mobilization says access to resources, not grievances, is the crucial factor. That tension is exactly the union compound of F1: neither literature alone closes the residual. Do not present one as refuting the other; present the overlap as the thing to measure.

## 5. Distribution: offer, do not push

- **Fig 4 :22**: *"Only methods cross up. Only head starts come down."* Sending the whole architecture before anyone has described a residual sends a recording, not a generator. Lead with the shape claim and refuter.
- **Fig 10 :42**: *"A verifier that can reconstruct the claim cannot verify it."* A recipient who has absorbed the theory before being asked for a verdict has joined the frame being checked.
- **Fig 9 :27-28**: *"describe it before you go looking, or any frame you pick will seem to fit."* Ask what they would expect to see if the claim were true before giving them the links.
- **Fig 2 :40**: *"agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool"*; Fig 2 :42: *"Buy independence in proportion to the cost of being wrong."* Never select recipients for likely agreement; never pool later agreement as corroboration.
- **Fig 10 :59**: *"an interest in the outcome is unavoidable, an interest in the reading is disqualifying."* Declare the author's stake in the framing; do not assert it as neutral.

**Recommended order in a reply:** (1) the shape claim and its four refuters; (2) ask what they would count as a failure, and record the answer; (3) only then the three links, with the note that the figures, not the summary, are the authority; (4) only if asked, the formula as a method with a request for their own refuter and mode.
