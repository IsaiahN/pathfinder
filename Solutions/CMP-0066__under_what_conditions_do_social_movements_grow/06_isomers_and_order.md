# Union structure, bond type and order in the growth question

**Mode: `specified`.** A simulated derivation checked against the figures, not a ground reading (see `05_what_shape_means.md` section 6).

## RESIDUAL

The primary audit leaves four things to derive properly: (1) whether the two growth conditions form a union or a partition, (2) what "same network, different outcome" means under Fig 12, (3) what order the checking steps run in, and (4) whether a catalyst or cycle is present in recruitment. Each has its own falsifier.

## DERIVATION

### Q1 - Union or partition?

`Fig 9 :55`: *"A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones."* `Fig 9 :57`: *"a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either."*

The two conditions overlap empirically: an opportunity shift can create the tie that then carries local recruitment, and local success can change the structure on which the next shift acts. Under a union, a single lever clears only the region where one condition holds and leaves the intersection standing (`Fig 9 :56`: *"A single lever clears the region where only one condition holds and leaves the intersections standing, because the second condition still binds."*).

**Order consequence:** unbundle before searching (Fig 9 :54), then run one loop per condition, then examine the intersection as a compound with its own ground. **Falsifier:** a single variable with no interaction term predicting both branches and the intersection.

### Q2 - Are two movements with the same members "isomers"?

`Fig 12 :117`: *"isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."*

- **Weak form:** two movements sharing the same member set but with different tie arrangements (clustered vs bridging) are isomers under Fig 12. Same atoms, different bond, different trajectory. This is the claim worth testing.
- **Functional-group form:** two movements sharing only a substructure (for example a core organiser plus a reinforcing tie) but not the full member set are a **functional group**: `Fig 12 :123`, *"functional group. A substructure whose presence fixes the outcome. The group says what kind; the qualifier says which member."*
- As normally reported (different members, different ties, different context), two movements are neither isomers nor one functional group. They are two different substances. Claiming isomorphism from a matching headcount is the flat-formula error.

**Falsifier:** if across the measured range the same member set under clustering and under bridging yields statistically indistinguishable recruitment trajectories, the isomer claim fails and tie arrangement is idle for this quantity.

### Q3 - Is there a catalyst and a cycle?

`Fig 12 :119`: *"catalyst. A part that survives its own use."* Organisers and institutions are candidates: they are spent in one recruitment event and regenerating across events. `Fig 12 :121`: *"cycle. The output re-enters as input. The sign of the loop decides whether it closes"*.

Recruitment is a cycle. A new member becomes a recruiter, so output re-enters as input. The sign matters: reinforcement closes the loop; suppression or burnout opens it. A vocabulary that records only recruits, not who recruits whom, cannot write this. `Fig 12 :111`: *"A vocabulary without bonds can name a state and cannot name a route to one."*

**Falsifier:** a movement in which new members never recruit further members across the whole period, with no organiser surviving its own use. Then the catalyst and cycle reading is wrong for that case.

### Q4 - What order do the checks run in?

Dependency order from `Fig 3 :20`: *"The chain is ordered by dependency, not by importance."*

1. Perceive: name the growth residual for a defined network and period (Fig 1 :19, residual per slot).
2. Vocabulary: acquire terms for closure, atom, contact, bond, habitat.
3. Objective: state what would count as growth in that slot, measured independently of the movement's own reports.
4. Plan: choose the lever only after 1 to 3.
5. Learn and carry: keep the lineage so the next movement pays less (Fig 4 :22).

When no term closes the residual, the exit order is fixed: SEARCHED first, then INWARD, then OUTWARD (`THE_FORMULA.md` step 7). `Fig 6 :29`: *"inward: is our representation adequate to hold it?"* `Fig 6 :30`: *"outward: does another frame already hold it?"* These are opposed and must not be read against each other.

Round trip: when the full derivation is condensed to "local or structural", measure the gap. `Fig 4 :30`: *"So take the round trip and measure the gap. Without it you are navigating by dead reckoning: confident about a position nobody checked."* `11_audit.md` runs this.

### Q5 - Seed versus habitat, restated as an order claim

`Fig 4 :34-35`: *"A seed carries no small tree: it carries what will grow one, and the conditions it lands in decide what it becomes."* The seed is a head start (downward); the conditions are not authored by the seed. `Fig 11 :61`: *"Introduction is available; exclusion is not."* So an organiser can be introduced, but the habitat cannot be subtracted by fiat. Any intervention plan that assumes removal of a condition is available has mis-stated the operation.

**Falsifier:** a documented intervention that removed a condition (not merely added another that masked it) and whose effect was measured on the ground. That would break the asymmetry claim.

## CONCLUSION

The two growth conditions are a union (Q1). Movements with the same members under different tie arrangements are a real isomer claim; movements sharing only a core substructure are a functional-group claim; movements differing in both are neither (Q2). Recruitment carries a catalyst and a signed cycle that a headcount notation cannot write (Q3). The checking order is describe, name the objective, then plan, and SEARCHED before INWARD before OUTWARD (Q4). Seeds are head starts whose fate is decided by conditions the seed did not author (Q5). Every sub-claim has a falsifier above; none is `grounded`.
