## 0. The residual, in effect terms

R is the gap between an observed growth trajectory of a movement and what a single mechanism predicts. The question offers two candidate descriptions of the same effect:

- **Local recruitment:** growth is composition inside the network and habitat the movement already has. New members are reachable from existing members through existing ties, using existing resources and framings.
- **Structural shift:** growth requires something the local closure does not hold. A new class of participant, a new resource, a new institution, a change in repression or opportunity, or a different frame.

What each option leads to. If local recruitment suffices, the lever is seeds, tie structure, and the arrangement of ties. If a structural shift is required, seeding the same network will plateau, and the lever is a change in conditions that adds an atom the movement did not have. Binding the answer as an ordinary term, as the formula's Direction step requires: **the condition is a reachability relation between the movement's current closure and the atoms growth requires.** That is the shape to be refuted, not a numeric tipping point.

`Fig 9 :22` gives the description triad: *"arity · symmetry · scale"*. `Fig 1 :19`: *"The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated."* So the verdict is kept per movement, per slot. Averaging movements into one global growth law is the named failure (`Fig 1 :22`: *"Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average."*).

## 1. Domain census (before any figure line is chosen)

**R by arity, symmetry, scale.** Arity: two conditions plus their intersection, so a union rather than a partition (`Fig 9 :55`: *"A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones."*). Symmetry: asymmetric. Local recruitment is monotone composition within a fixed closure, it adds no atom. Structural shift adds an atom or changes contact, so it moves the wall. These are not two strengths on one dial. Scale: local is first order inside one frame and habitat, structural is cross-scale, it changes the frame or the habitat the movement ranges against.

**Per-domain actors, currency, anchor.** A domain needs a nameable anchor or it is not a domain yet.

| domain | actors | currency | anchor | question type |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| D1 Local recruitment | individuals, ties, network structure, local seeds, organisers | adoptions and recruits transmitted over bonds | observed recruitment events in a defined network and period | partition boundary inside a closure (which compositions are reachable) |
| D2 Structural shifts | institutions, policy, repression, resources, economic conditions, external frames | changes in contact and in available atoms | observed growth before and after the shift, measured independently of the movement's own reports | import or habitat substitution (union with D1) |
| D3 Verification and research | researchers, datasets, models, reviewers | residuals and evidence | the ground (data), never agreement | checking arrangement; in this simulated run independence is zero |

**Partition or union.** Union. The two conditions overlap: a structural change can create the tie that then carries local recruitment, and local success can alter the structure it later depends on. `Fig 9 :57`: *"a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either."* Treating the question as a lever on one variable is the collapse `Fig 9 :54` names: *"when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again"*.

**Order by dependency (`Fig 3 :20`: *"The chain is ordered by dependency, not by importance."*).** Name the residual per movement (perception), acquire terms for bond, closure, import and habitat (vocabulary), state what counts as growth (objective), then plan interventions, then carry methods. Describe R before choosing a frame: `Fig 9 :27-28`: *"describe it before you go looking, or any frame you pick will seem to fit"*. Keep the three exit questions distinct and in order: SEARCHED first, then INWARD, then OUTWARD (`THE_FORMULA.md` step 7).

## 3. Findings

Every finding carries a mode. `general` = structural claim from the figures. `specified` = claim about this question's domains, unmeasured. No finding is `grounded`.

### F1 - The question is a union compound, not one lever (`general`, decisive for method)

`Fig 9 :54`: *"when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again"*. `Fig 9 :55`: *"A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones."* `Fig 9 :57`: *"a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either."*

An answer that gives one threshold ("growth is local until X, then structural") rebundles what the figure says to unbundle. The correct output is two loops with an overlap, each with its own ground, plus a rule for which loop is currently binding. `Fig 9 :32`: *"you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself"*.

**Refuter:** show that for a defined class of movements a single measured variable (for example a single critical-mass statistic) predicts both local-only and shift-dependent trajectories with no interaction term and no change of anchor. If that holds, the union reading fails and a partition is owed.

### F2 - Local recruitment composes inside a closure and adds no atom (`general`, triangulated)

`Fig 6 :21`: *"MINT composes inside the closure and can never add an atom."* `Fig 8 :32`: *"no search, however long, adds an atom"*. `Fig 13 :115`: *"The set is closed. The arrangements are not."*

Local recruitment can fill every arrangement the movement can already reach, but it cannot manufacture a participant class, resource or institution that is not in the closure. Fig 6 (reachability), Fig 8 (idempotent closure) and Fig 13 (closed set, open arrangements) are three low-overlap routes to the same constraint. `triangulated`.

**Refuter:** a documented movement growth in which every required atom (new participant class, new resource, new institution, new framing) was demonstrably absent from its closure at the start and no import or habitat change occurred. That would show local composition adding an atom, which these three figures forbid.

### F3 - Structural shifts are imports or habitat substitutions (`general`, triangulated)

`Fig 6 :14`: *"the only operator that moves the wall"*. `Fig 6 :23`: *"No frame escapes its own closure. The bound moves only on contact with a frame whose closure differs."* `Fig 6 :22`: *"NOVEL means novel relative to atoms, not to the world. IMPORT adds an atom, and the added atom is what expands the closure."*

`Fig 11 :39`: *"capability is a property of agent-and-habitat, never of the agent alone."* `Fig 11 :40`: *"An improvement that does not change contact changes nothing, however much it improves."* `Fig 11 :61`: *"Introduction is available; exclusion is not."* `Fig 11 :66`: *"Isolation is not removal of the habitat. It is substitution of one habitat for another."*

A structural shift is not a bigger local recruitment. It is either an import from a frame whose closure differs, or a change of habitat that re-decides what contact means. `Fig 8 :53`: *"Residual first, frame second."* Triangulated across Fig 6 (wall) and Fig 11 (habitat), which share no anchor.

**Refuter:** a change in conditions that added no atom and changed no contact, yet reliably preceded the growth spurt in an independent data set. That would reduce D2 to a correlation and remove the import reading.

### F4 - From inside the frame, depth and absence look the same (`general`, the honest limit)

`Fig 6 :27`: *"from inside the frame the two look the same"*. `Fig 8 :39`: *"a search that finds nothing is never a proof of absence."* `Fig 9 :44`: *"Only a sealed room can be searched to the end, and no frame is a sealed room."* `Fig 6 :29`: *"inward: is our representation adequate to hold it?"* `Fig 6 :30`: *"outward: does another frame already hold it?"*

So a stalled local recruitment drive is underivable-from-inside until a different frame is consulted. `Fig 8 :41-42`: *"Underivable: go outside. Unreachable-at-budget: the term is already in the closure and was not found, so the remedy is more search, not another atom."* This is why the answer is a shape and a route, not a single conditional number. `single-source` for the distinction itself (Fig 6 and Fig 8 are one framework), but the world literature independently reaches the same operational split (see `09_watson_answer.md`, threshold and opportunity-structure sources).

**Refuter:** an instrument that, for a named movement, discriminates "the needed atoms are in the closure and unfound" from "the needed atoms are not in the closure" without consulting a second frame. Until one exists, the two readings are not distinguishable from inside.

### F5 - Arrangement, not tie count, conditions local recruitment (`general`, triangulated)

Fig 13 :24 names the network as a structure: *"that entities relate, and influence travels"*. Symbols table :101: *"That entities relate at all, and that influence travels between them."* `Fig 12 :111`: *"A vocabulary without bonds can name a state and cannot name a route to one."* `Fig 12 :117`: *"isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."* `Fig 12 :123`: *"functional group. A substructure whose presence fixes the outcome. The group says what kind; the qualifier says which member."*

Two networks with the same members and the same tie count are not one case written twice. Under a symmetric bond the spellings collapse (`Fig 12 :92`), under an ordered bond they do not. So "network density" is a flat formula; clustering, bridging and sequence are bonds. `Fig 12 :121`: *"cycle. The output re-enters as input. The sign of the loop decides whether it closes"*; recruitment is a cycle whose sign is not recorded by counting recruits. Triangulated across Fig 13 (network as structure) and Fig 12 (bond, isomer, cycle).

**Refuter:** two networks with identical tie counts but different clustering or bridging that produce statistically indistinguishable recruitment trajectories across the full measured range. The arrangement claim would then be idle for this quantity.

### F6 - Seeds are head starts, not recordings (`general`, single-source for the framework claim)

`Fig 4 :22`: *"Only methods cross up. Only head starts come down."* `Fig 4 :34-35`: *"A seed carries no small tree: it carries what will grow one, and the conditions it lands in decide what it becomes."* `Fig 7 :33`: *"variation inside, selection between"*. `Fig 7 :35`: *"A room's agreement is variation, not a verdict. Selection between rooms is where the ground votes."*

Planting organisers or a initial cohort is a downward head start. Its fate is decided by the habitat it lands in. Copying a past winning tactic is a recording; replayed in a new habitat it produces the old success or nothing. `Fig 11 :39` supplies the condition. `single-source` (Fig 4 and Fig 7 are one framework); the world sources on seeds, thresholds and political opportunity corroborate separately.

**Refuter:** the same seed set reproducing the same growth curve across habitats whose conditions differ on every enumerated axis. That would make the habitat clause false.

### F7 - Minting can be legitimately inert (`general`, single-source)

`Fig 5 :41`: *"Any one of them at zero stops the mint entirely. Which is why a system that is minting nothing may have nothing wrong with its minting."* `Fig 5 :26`: *"does the new term cost less to state than the confusion it removes?"*

A movement with no new tactics is not therefore a movement with broken recruitment. The guards are a product: support, reachability, novelty, then the ground. Absence of new terms is not evidence of failure of the mechanism. This blocks the common inference "no visible innovation means weak local recruitment."

**Refuter:** show a movement where the three guards are all positive and the term is still not minted, with no settlement gate intervening. That would falsify the product reading.

### F8 - Verification discipline: ground, per-slot residuals, no filter as witness (`general`, triangulated with the outreach rule)

`Fig 2 :35`: *"Alignment is triangulation, not negotiation. A mirror cannot correct you."* `Fig 2 :40`: *"agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool"*. `Fig 2 :42`: *"Buy independence in proportion to the cost of being wrong."* `Fig 9 :60`: *"Use filters for the budget and witnesses for the verdict. Never let a filter hand you a verdict."* `Fig 10 :42`: *"A verifier that can reconstruct the claim cannot verify it."* `Fig 10 :59`: *"an interest in the outcome is unavoidable, an interest in the reading is disqualifying."*

Growth studies that share datasets, the same few canonical cases and the same reviewers are one witness. Selecting experts for likely agreement is the disqualifying correlation. This governs `02`, `03`, `04` and the second seat in `11`, not the substantive answer.

**Refuter:** demonstrate that an outside frame with zero shared evidence, having been shown only the residual, returns a verdict that an anchor later confirms, while the primary frame is shown to be wrong. That would show verifier non-access is not required for soundness.

### F9 - Condensing the answer to "local or structural" loses three constraints (`general`, round trip)

`Fig 4 :30`: *"So take the round trip and measure the gap. Without it you are navigating by dead reckoning: confident about a position nobody checked."* `Fig 4 :31`: *"The gap is not an error to eliminate. It is the measurement of what the coarser description cannot hold"*

The shorthand drops (1) the union structure from F1, (2) the bond and arrangement from F5, and (3) the depth/absence limit from F4. R_T is therefore nonzero and qualitative here, not measured numerically, because no ground reading was taken. `11_audit.md` runs the round trip explicitly.

## 4. Pre-registered refuter (written before the conclusion)

Required before any outreach. The shape claim is refuted if any of the following holds:

1. A single measured variable predicts both local-only and shift-dependent growth without interaction or change of anchor (breaks F1).
2. Local composition demonstrably adds an atom to a movement's closure with no import or habitat change (breaks F2).
3. A condition change that adds no atom and changes no contact reliably produces the spurt in independent data (breaks F3).
4. An instrument discriminates depth from absence for the same movement without a second frame (dissolves F4, which would be a welcome refutation).
5. Tie count alone, with no arrangement term, predicts recruitment across networks that differ in clustering or bridging (breaks F5).

If any fires, this derivation is retracted at that finding and re-censused. Until one is run, nothing here is `grounded`.

## 7. Strains, re-census, updated verdict

- **Fig 6 versus Fig 8 on the wall.** No strain after re-census. Fig 6 names import as the operator; Fig 8 explains why nothing inside the closure can do it.
- **Fig 12 isomer versus functional group for local networks.** Not strained. Same atoms plus different bond is an isomer claim (F5); same substructure with different members is a functional group claim. Both are available and they are different tests.
- **D2 anchor.** The ground for a structural shift is "growth before and after, measured independently of the movement's own reports." This is an instrument specification, not a claim that such an instrument exists for every movement. Where it does not exist, the verdict is `unreached`, not `unreachable` (`Fig 8 :39`).
- **After re-census: no hidden strains.** One honest limit remains visible: depth versus absence cannot be settled from inside a single frame (F4).

**Updated verdict.** The answer shape survives: local recruitment is composition inside a closure and cannot add an atom; structural shifts are imports or habitat substitutions that can; tie arrangement, not tie count, decides which local compositions are reachable; and the researcher cannot tell an unfound local route from a genuinely absent one without a second frame. Verdicts are kept separate per domain, never averaged. `general` on the shape, `specified` on the domain conditions, nothing `grounded`.
