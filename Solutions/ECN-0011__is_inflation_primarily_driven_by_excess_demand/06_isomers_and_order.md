## DERIVATION

### Q1: Peers or functional group?

Fig 12 :117: "isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance."
Fig 12 :124: "functional group. A substructure whose presence fixes the outcome. The group says what kind; the qualifier says which member."

Write each candidate as a `state → structure → state` molecule over the price index:

- demand: `income/employment (state) → spending pressure (structure/gradient) → prices (state)`
- supply: `capacity (state) → constraint (structure/gradient) → prices (state)`
- money and credit: `monetary base (state) → credit availability (structure/network) → prices (state)`
- expectations: `belief (state) → wage and price setting (structure/network) → prices (state)`

They do not share one atom set, so under Fig 12 :117 they are **not isomers**. They share a **functional group**: the same cross-kind shape `state → structure → state`, with different atoms in the state and structure slots. Fig 12 :124 says the group fixes the kind and the qualifier picks the member.

This matters for the question. If the candidates were isomers, a single comparison would rank them. As members of a functional group, they are different substances with the same grammatical form, and a comparison that ignores the qualifiers compares nothing.

Triangulated for the structural reading (Fig 12 :117 and :124 are two definitions in one figure, tested by different questions); single-source for the figure vocabulary itself. Mode `general`.

### Q2: Where does expectations sit, and what does its sign do?

Fig 12 :121: "cycle. The output re-enters as input. The sign of the loop decides whether it closes, and a chain cannot close by construction."

The other three candidates are chains: an impulse arrives and prices respond. Expectations are the output of the price process fed back as an input to wage and price setting. A chain cannot close by construction, so treating expectations as a fourth chain member is a notation error. The sign decides the behaviour: positive feedback amplifies a shock and can make it persistent; negative feedback closes the loop and damps it.

Bernanke (2013) supplies the world-side reading: "the effects of monetary policy on the economy today depend importantly not only on current policy actions, but also on the public's expectations of how policy will evolve." The Fed's 2 percent FAQ says stable expectations let households and businesses "make sound decisions regarding saving, borrowing, and investment." Neither source measures the sign; both confirm the loop exists.

Single-source for the figure definition, world corroboration from an institutional economics frame (low overlap with the metatheory). Mode `specified`. The sign is left unmeasured.

### Q3: Which order does the union have?

Fig 3 :20: "The chain is ordered by dependency, not by importance. Each link consumes what the one above produces, so effort spent above the break arrives at a step that cannot use it."
Fig 9 :54: "And when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again."

Dependency order:

1. Fix the index, the window and the subcategory (D1). Without this, `R` is undefined.
2. Enumerate the habitat outward (Fig 11 :43-44), including second and third order effects.
3. Unbundle the union into overlapping conditions and identify which are live in each slot.

5. Attach the expectations cycle and estimate its sign.
6. Range each frame separately against the anchor (Fig 2 :45).

A step whose input never arrives cannot be diagnosed. Asking step 4 before step 1 is a reading of nothing (Fig 3 :22-23).

Single-source for Fig 3, with the Fed's published measurement procedure as institutional corroboration. Mode `general`.

### Q4: Two currencies, and the round trip

Fig 12 :126: "Bits price the description. Actions price finding out whether it holds."
Fig 12 :126: "They are in different units, so a system holding one accounting cannot compare them"

"Some combination" is a short description and an expensive settlement. A single-cause story is equally short and may cost a whole research programme to refute. A ranking that sums descriptive simplicity and empirical cost is summing different units.

Now the round trip required by Fig 4 :30: "So take the round trip and measure the gap. Without it you are navigating by dead reckoning: confident about a position nobody checked."

- **Sent up (x):** a verbatim figure set, chiefly Fig 1 :19/:22 (per-slot residual), Fig 9 :54-57 (union), Fig 2 :29/:45 (anchor), Fig 12 :121 (cycle).
- **Abstraction (T_A):** the shorthand carried in the outreach material: "the question is misaggregated, unbundle it, let the ground decide."
- **Concretisation (T_E):** a targeted empirical claim: "in sub-index s, episode e, the live condition is supply constraint, and the expectations feedback sign is positive."

**`R_T` is nonzero.** The shorthand holds three things that do not survive the trip back down:

1. Which sub-index and which episode are at issue. The shorthand does not carry them.
2. Which of the overlapping conditions is live in that slot. Union membership is not slot membership.
3. The sign of the expectations feedback. The figure says the sign decides; it does not say what it is here.

One thing survives cleanly: a single headline driver cannot be read where the residual is per slot. That is the residue worth sending. `R_T` was not measured numerically because no ground reading was taken; it is recorded qualitatively as the enumerated set of dropped constraints. Treating "the formula is the condensed version of the metalaws" as identity would set `R_T` to zero and repeat the dead-reckoning error.

Single-source for the round trip rule (Fig 4), triangulated for the residue (Fig 1 plus Fig 9). Mode `general`.

## FALSIFIER, written before the run

3. If causal attribution is invariant to index, window and subcategory, Q3's dependency order is refuted.
