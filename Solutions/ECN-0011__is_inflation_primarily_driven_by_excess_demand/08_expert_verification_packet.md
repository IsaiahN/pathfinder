# Expert verification packet: the Tether on the drivers of inflation

**What this is:** three claims in a form an inflation researcher can check. Mode, derivation and refuters are stated before the conclusion, so a reader knows what to look for and what would settle it.

**Mode: `general` on the shape, `specified` on the expectations sub-claim, `grounded` nowhere.** No inflation series was measured here.

**Status:** a simulated derivation checked for internal consistency against the figures, not a ground reading. It cannot settle the question it reports on. Verification here means a qualified person could check the packet in twenty minutes, not that anyone did.

## 1. The claim, in one paragraph

The question "is inflation primarily driven by demand, supply, money and credit, expectations, or a combination" is posed in the wrong unit. The residual is indexed by price sub-index, country and episode, so a headline average hides the live signal. The four candidates are overlapping conditions in a union, sharing a functional group `state → structure → state` with different atoms in the slots, not exclusive rivals; "some combination" restates the union rather than answering it. Expectations are a feedback cycle whose sign decides amplification, not a fourth linear driver. No causal frame may score itself with the quantity it produces, and agreement among schools is one evidence pool. From the figures alone, which driver dominates in any episode is unreachable, and that gap is reported rather than filled with a preferred school.

## 2. Three checkable sub-claims

### C1: The headline aggregation loses the live signal (framework-internal, checkable by reading Figure 1 and a price index)

- **Statement:** the residual must be read per sub-index and per episode. A single aggregate driver cannot be ranked from a headline rate.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 1 :19 "The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated." Also Fig 1 :22 "Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average." Also Fig 1 :23 "The ground enters as the only metric."
- **World bridge to check:** the Federal Reserve FAQ states that "various indexes can send diverse signals about inflation" and that "policymakers routinely examine the subcategories that make up a broad price index".
- **Refuter:** show that one aggregate driver predicts every sub-index residual as well as a per-slot decomposition, judged against an anchor that does not move when a school of thought changes its mind. If so, C1 fails.

### C2: The candidates form a union of overlapping conditions (framework-internal plus a checkable reading of an inflation episode)

- **Statement:** demand, supply, money and expectations overlap in the same episode. The question is a union, not a partition, and unbundling precedes any lever.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 9 :55 "A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones." Also Fig 9 :54 "And when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again." Also Fig 9 :57 "a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either. It stops being a mixture and becomes a specific"
- **World bridge to check:** the public account of the 2021 to 2023 surge lists supply chains, fiscal and monetary stimulus, price gouging, housing shortage, climate and deficits together, and records "how much of a role profits played is the subject of controversy".
- **Refuter:** demonstrate that the four conditions are mutually exclusive across the slots at issue, so a single lever clears every intersection. If so, C2 fails and a single-cause answer becomes viable.

### C3: Expectations are a cycle, and the figures do not hold a dominant driver (one structural, one limit)

- **Statement:** expectations re-enter as input to the process whose output they are; the sign decides amplification. And no figure line names a dominant macro driver, so the empirical name is a `gap`, not a hidden answer.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 12 :121 "cycle. The output re-enters as input. The sign of the loop decides whether it closes, and a chain cannot close by construction." For the limit: Fig 8 :39 "So a search that finds nothing is never a proof of absence." and Fig 13 :134 "Unreachable is a verdict here, not a silence,"
- **World bridge to check:** Bernanke (2013) states that "the effects of monetary policy on the economy today depend importantly not only on current policy actions, but also on the public's expectations of how policy will evolve"; the quantity theory page records Friedman's caveat that "The statistical connection itself, however, tells nothing about direction of influence".
- **Refuter:** show that the expectations feedback has negative sign at the measured scale so the loop closes; or derive a unique dominant driver from the figure lines without importing a data series. Either breaks C3.

## 3. What is explicitly not claimed

- Not that demand, money, supply or expectations is unimportant. Valence is relative to the residual (Fig 11 :49).
- Not that inflation is always monetary, or always supply driven.
- Not that "some combination" is a result. It restates the union (Fig 9 :55).
- Not that a survey, a citation count or a school's reputation is evidence. Fig 9 :60: "Use filters for the budget and witnesses for the verdict. Never let a filter hand you a verdict."
- Not grounded. No data was measured, no sign estimated, no episode resolved.
- Not a claim that the figure set is a macroeconomic model. It is a domain-agnostic metatheory; that is why the name is a gap.

## 5. What would settle it

1. **For the shape:** any of the four refuters in section 2, or the full set in `01_figure_grounded_audit.md` section 4.
2. **For the name:** a source-discriminating instrument. *The Hard Problem is About Source*: one reading can come from several sources and "cannot comment on the source"; "The answer is unreached until a better instrument exists." Concretely, a per-sub-index demand/supply decomposition with a stated identification strategy, plus a natural experiment isolating one condition, plus replication.
3. **For the expectations sign:** an estimate of the feedback from expected prices into wage and price setting at a named scale.

## 6. What an expert can check in twenty minutes

1. Read Fig 1 :19, :22, :23, Fig 9 :54-55, Fig 12 :121, Fig 8 :39, Fig 13 :134. Seven short passages.
2. Check C1 against a price index you know: do the subcategories tell a different story from the headline?
3. Check C2 against one episode you know: were demand and supply conditions both present?
4. Say whether you know of an instrument that discriminates among the sources of a price reading. Name it, or say none exists.
5. Send the refutation. A failure is more useful than an endorsement.

**Do not ask the reviewer for agreement, for trust, or for a school preference.** Stage 1 ends with their verdict on these claims. Sources are offered only if they are curious, and later agreement is never pooled as corroboration.
