**Use:** one-to-one, or as a cover note attached to a submission through a public venue in `03_public_contact_points.csv`. **Not** for bulk send. No request for trust, no request for agreement, no receptiveness ranking.

**Hard precondition:** do not send until the shape claim and the five refuters below are exactly as stated in `01_figure_grounded_audit.md` section 3. The output must survive validation or the two-stage offer collapses on contact.

**Subject:** One checkable claim about black hole microstate counts

> Hi [name],
>
> I have one specific output I would like you to check against what you already know. I am not asking you to read a theory first, and I am not asking you to agree with me. If the output does not survive, there is nothing else to look at.
>
> **The output (`specified`, not `grounded`):** the question "does counting black hole microstates reproduce the Bekenstein-Hawking entropy" is two conditions in one sentence, not one yes/no question. Condition one: is there a count that returns `A/4`? Condition two: are the counted states the degrees of freedom of a general black hole? Condition one is met, with a published derivation record, for extremal and near-extremal supersymmetric black holes. Condition two is not characterized for a non-supersymmetric hole such as Schwarzschild. Those verdicts are different and I do not average them.
>
> The shape claim is narrower than the usual phrasing. A successful count must (1) be indexed per black-hole class, (2) enumerate distinguishable states with the symmetric-relabeling equivalences quotiented out before taking `log Ω`, and (3) have a derivation record that shows the count was not normalized by demanding `A/4`. Where the count was derived independently in a closure that differs from the semiclassical one, the agreement is convergent derivation and carries corroboration. Where it was fitted, the contents look identical and only the record separates the two. This is a shape and a provenance condition, not a name of the microstates.
>
> **What I am not claiming:** that I have identified the microscopic degrees of freedom for a general black hole; that the string and loop counts are the same states; that a match of numbers settles the identification question; that this simulated derivation is a measurement. The framework returns a kind of thing, not the answer itself. My own analysis is a simulation checked for internal consistency against the figures, not a ground reading, and it cannot settle the claim it reports on.
>
> **What would refute the shape claim (written before I ran this):**
> 1. A count that returns `A/4` only after a parameter is chosen by demanding `A/4`, with no independent derivation record.
> 2. A count that cannot be extended beyond its protecting symmetry, offered as a general answer.
> 3. A demonstration that the independent-count frames draw on the same evidence pool, so their agreement is one witness.
> 4. A forward prediction from the counted states that fails at a discriminator.
> 5. A derivation of one of the six substrate terms from the other five, or a direct state-state composition the framework cannot refuse.
>
> If any one holds, I withdraw the affected claim. A one-line "this fails because..." is more useful to me than agreement. If you would rather not engage, no reply is needed.
>
> **If it survives, and only then:** the sources are below. The figure text, not my summary of it, is the authority.
> - Figures with line numbers (local extract): `FRAMEWORK/reference/03-FIGURES_TEXT.md` in this project.
> - Canonical figures README: https://github.com/IsaiahN/tether/blob/general-agent/figures/README.md
> - Worked architecture essay: https://medium.com/@IsaiahNwukor/arrival-of-the-fittest-the-tether-architecture-1de7edf641ec
>
> **If you want to use the machinery:** run it on a problem you can check against a ground. Write down what would refute your result *before* you run it, and state the mode of what you get. An unmeasured quantity is a specification of what to measure, not a verdict. A reported failure is more useful to me than a reported success.
