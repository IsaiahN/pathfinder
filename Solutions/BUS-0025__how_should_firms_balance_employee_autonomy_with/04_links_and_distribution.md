## 2. What the wider literature bears on the question

**One evidence pool, several witnesses.** The SDT meta-analysis, the job-characteristics study, the workplace-surveillance study and the EPM/reactance study are all survey-based work and organizational research. They partly share samples, measures and citation chains. Per Fig 2 :31, *"Independence, between two frames, is how little they draw on the same evidence."* Their agreement is one witness, not four.

- **Autonomy support and motivation.** Hagger and Star (2026) find that perceived need support relates to adaptive outcomes through need satisfaction and autonomous motivation, with controlled motivation on the maladaptive side. This corroborates the person-ground condition C4 and the independence condition C3, but only at the individual stratum. Fig 4 :20 requires the anchor to be re-named at the firm scale.
- **Job design.** Steyn and Vawda (2014) find autonomy and feedback contribute uniquely to job satisfaction (standardised beta 0.287 and 0.184) but job characteristics explain only about 7% of stress and depression. This corroborates the refusal to promote a job-design convention into a general verdict (Fig 10 :47), and it also warns against using satisfaction as if it were the paid outcome.
- **Monitoring.** Glavin et al. (2024) find the negative association of surveillance with distress runs through pressure, reduced autonomy and privacy violations, while a positive direct association with satisfaction is cancelled by those indirect paths. Brinson et al. (2024) find trust predicts a positive attitude toward monitoring more strongly than reactance predicts a negative one. Together they support the valence-flip finding F8 and the two-anchor split F2. They do not show that monitoring raises or lowers paid output, which is why no verdict about productivity is taken from them.
- **A directly named collision.** Jensen and Raver (2012) title their paper *"When self-management and surveillance collide: Consequences for employees’ trust, autonomy, and discretionary behaviors"*. Only the bibliographic record was opened, so the title is used as a label for the union, not as evidence.

**Low-overlap witnesses, each from a different field and each read directly:**

1. **Cybernetics and control engineering.** The Variety page quotes Ashby's *"only variety can destroy variety"* and the result that a perfect regulator must have at least as many distinguishable states as the system it regulates. Beer's example is the important one here: no amount of variety recording a patient's average temperature would detect a fever signal, so monitoring must be on individuals. This corroborates Fig 1 :22 (per-slot residual, not pooled) and the channel condition C1 from a field with no shared evidence pool with management research. It does not amend Fig 6; it governs a different quantity (strain S4).
2. **Animal behaviour.** van Zeeland et al. (2023) show Grey parrots working for food when identical food is freely available, an *"intrinsic motivation to engage in foraging-related activities"* that *"contradicts optimal foraging theory"*. This is a low-overlap instance of the shape, not a human result: contact with the work itself can carry value that the delivered outcome does not explain. Fig 4 :20 requires the anchor to be re-named at the human organizational scale, so it corroborates the mechanism shape only.

**Framework-internal essays** are used for the canonical reading of "shape" in `05_what_shape_means.md` only. Their author wrote the figures, so they are not independent world witnesses. That overlap is recorded in the ledger.

## 3. Distribution rules

Figure-grounded reasons for how material is offered:

- **Fig 4 :22:** *"Only methods cross up. Only head starts come down."* Send the generator, the residual and the refuter, not the conclusion. A recording carried upward *"looks like knowledge and is a description of one occasion"* (Fig 4 :25).
- **Fig 10 :42:** *"A verifier that can reconstruct the claim cannot verify it."* A recipient who has absorbed the framework before being asked for a verdict has joined the frame being checked.
- **Fig 9 :28:** *"any frame you pick will seem to fit"* if the residual is not described first. Ask what they would expect to see if the shape claim were true before showing the source.
- **Fig 2 :40:** agreement from a shared pool is worthless as a verdict. Later agreement about the framework is never pooled as corroboration.
- **Fig 10 :59:** *"an interest in the outcome is unavoidable, an interest in the reading is disqualifying."* Declare the stake; do not ask for belief.

**A hard practical constraint.** The AOM Publishing page states: *"Artificial Intelligence: Work submitted to AOM must be created by the authors and not the product of artificial intelligence tools unless appropriate to the research question and properly cited."* This whole solution is a simulated derivation produced with an LLM and is labelled as such everywhere. It must **not** be submitted as an authored empirical or theoretical article. The legitimate use is as a private checking instrument: post the residual and the refuter, invite a human to run the check, and let that human author whatever they conclude. Any submission must be rewritten and owned by a human author who can defend the derivation, with the AI contribution disclosed under the applicable policy.

**Order in any reply:** (1) the shape claim and the pre-registered refuter; (2) the question "what would you count as a failure here?"; (3) only after a verdict is recorded, the three source links with the note that the figures, not the summary, are the authority; (4) the formula as a reusable method, with the instruction that each user writes their own refuter and states their own mode.

## 4. The two-stage offer, compressed

**Stage 1.** Send `08_expert_verification_packet.md` sections 1 to 3 only: the one-paragraph claim, the three checkable sub-claims with their verbatim figure lines and refuters, and the non-claims. No provenance bundle, no essay links.

**Stage 2, opt-in only.** If the recipient is curious and has already recorded a verdict on stage 1, offer the three public links: the figure text, the formula and usage rule, and the essays. Their later agreement is a conversation, not corroboration (Fig 2 :40).

**Stage 3.** Offer the formula as a method, never as an answer (Fig 4 :22). Each user writes a refuter before running it and states the mode of what they get.

**Stop rule.** If stage 1 fails, the theory is not offered. A failure is more useful than an endorsement.

**Hosting.** Nothing here is hosted or sent by this agent. The human operator hosts the packet on an opt-in page and sends one to one. Prefer a download page that lets a recipient decline contact over an attachment pushed to a list.
