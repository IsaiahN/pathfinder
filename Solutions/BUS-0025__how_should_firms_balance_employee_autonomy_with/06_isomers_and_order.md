## Q1 - Are two teams with the same ingredients isomers?

> **"isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."** (Fig 12 :117)
> **"functional group. A substructure whose presence fixes the outcome. The group says what kind; the qualifier says which member."** (Fig 12 :123)
> **"Symmetry is what decides whether an arrangement is a new thing."** (Fig 12 :92)
> **"A set of ingredients is not one. The bond is the rest of the information, and it is the half nothing was recording."** (Fig 12 :5)

Take a fixed atom set for two teams: `{employee, task, metric, standard, manager, customer outcome}`. If both teams hold exactly these atoms but one composes them as `employee → customer outcome` (direct contact, metric as a read-only instrument) and the other as `employee → metric → manager → customer outcome` (the metric and the manager sit in the route), the two have the same atoms under different bonds. Under Fig 12 :117 they are **isomers**: same ingredients, different arrangement, different behaviour, with the difference invisible in an inventory of practices. This is the figure-grounded version of "it is not what you have, it is how it is arranged", stronger than the slogan because it says the arrangement is itself a substance and carries information the ingredient list does not.

If the teams share only a pattern (both run `employee → standard → outcome`) while differing in which atoms occupy the slots, the weaker claim holds: they are members of one **functional group** (Fig 12 :123), the qualifier saying which member. The presence of the pattern fixes the kind, not the outcome.

The distinction is testable in principle. A comparison that lists only metrics, standards and autonomy levels cannot distinguish isomer from functional group; the bond has to be recorded, that is, whether the metric is an instrument beside the route or an operand inside it. The cybernetics witness makes the same point at a different stratum: Beer's average temperature reading is the wrong bond between patient and signal, and *"Monitoring is required on individuals thus amplifying variety"* (Wikipedia, Variety page).

**Falsifier, written before the run:** if two teams with identical atoms and identical recorded bonds nevertheless diverge in paid outcome with no difference in ground contact or imported atoms, the isomer framing is insufficient and the difference must be habitat (D4), which moves the explanation out of D3.

**Status:** `specified`, triangulated for the legality of the reading (Fig 12 and Fig 7 :36, different figures), single-source for the business application. The opened literature corroborates only at the person scale: SDT and job-characteristics work show that arrangement matters, but neither records the bond between metric and manager. **Live strain:** the fixed-atom condition is an idealisation, because real teams add or drop roles when they change; it is named, not hidden, and the claim is conditional on holding the atom set fixed.

## Q2 - What order do the conditions bind in?

> **"The chain is ordered by dependency, not by importance. Each link consumes what the one above produces, so effort spent above the break arrives at a step that cannot use it."** (Fig 3 :20-21)
> **"Optimising a link that already works cannot rescue one that does not."** (Fig 3 :19)
> **"The link between them, having terms for what a situation offers and being able to say what would count as progress, has neither, so it attracts the least attention and the fewest instruments."** (Fig 3 :25-26)

Order for a work slot facing changed work:

1. **Perception.** Can the firm read the slot's return at all? Failure here is visible and has obvious tests: cycle time, defects, complaints, safety events.
2. **Vocabulary.** Does its Γ have terms for what the changed situation affords, and can it say what would count as progress there? This is the middle link. It is the one with no instrument.
3. **Objective.** Can it state a comparison that would count as getting somewhere? Fig 12 :112: *"A plan is a sequence. A goal is a comparison. Progress is a subtraction."* A vocabulary with only ingredients cannot state a goal.
4. **Planning.** A route to that objective under incomplete information.
5. **Learn and carry.** Name what worked, verify it, carry it to the next slot.

The four conditions map onto this chain. C1 (channel) is mostly a failure of links 1 and 2: the readings exist but are stale, saturated or pooled. C2 (method) is mostly a failure of link 5: the team names what worked but replays the recording instead of carrying a method. C3 (independence) is a failure of link 2 at the population scale: every room holds the same terms. C4 (person ground) is a separate anchor, not a link in this chain, which is why it must not be averaged into the others.

The order also decides which instrument to buy first. Fig 3 :27: *"A link with no instrument is not a link that works. It is a link nobody has looked at."* Links 1 and 4 have dashboards and plans; link 2 and link 5 have almost none. Budget therefore piles up where measurement already exists while the break sits elsewhere.

**Falsifier:** a work slot whose perception, vocabulary and objective links all measure as working and whose failure is located at planning. That would falsify the "usually middle" claim of F9 in the primary audit.

## Q3 - Competing explanations, and what each can and cannot reach

| candidate explanation | what it names | figure-line bearing | what it cannot reach |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Self-determination theory** (Hagger and Star 2026: need support, need satisfaction, autonomous versus controlled motivation) | the person-ground mechanism for why autonomy support relates to adaptive outcomes | C3 and C4; Fig 2 :36-37 makes human judgment an anchor for the person question | It is a meta-analysis of mostly self-report correlations at the individual scale. It cannot say whether the work paid, and its *"substantive between-study variability"* is a warning against a universal setting |
| **Job characteristics model** (Hackman and Oldham, via Steyn and Vawda 2014: skill variety, identity, significance, autonomy, feedback) | the attribute list of a designed job | Γ, the library operand; Steyn and Vawda find autonomy and feedback predict satisfaction | It explains only about 26% of satisfaction and about 7% of stress/depression in that study. | **Electronic performance monitoring research** (Glavin et al. 2024; Brinson et al. 2024) | the channel and the valence split: pressure, reduced autonomy, privacy, trust, reactance | C1 and F8, Fig 10 :24-29 and Fig 11 :49-51 | Glavin et al. find no settled productivity verdict; Brinson et al. find trust dominates reactance in attitude. Neither pays for a productivity claim. Survey evidence, one broad pool |
| **Managerial control as authority** | the seat | D4 and Fig 11 :37; the seat enumerates, introduces, builds instruments, reads ground | Fig 11 :40: authority that does not change contact changes nothing. No anchor independent of what pays (X1) |
| **Cybernetics / requisite variety** (Ashby, Conant, Beer via the Variety page) | how much variety a regulator must hold, and that monitoring must be on individuals | C1 and Fig 1 :22, Fig 7 :36 | It governs controller capacity, not how a frame gains an atom. | **Contrafreeloading** (van Zeeland et al. 2023) | intrinsic motivation to work for a resource when it is free | A low-overlap instance of point 3: contact with the activity is not reducible to the delivered outcome | Animal behaviour, not an organizational measurement. Fig 4 :20 requires the anchor to be re-named at the human scale |
| **"Trust" or "culture" as the answer** | a named atmosphere | A binding from the management frame onto a held atom, not a seventh primitive (correction in `05` section 6) | Correlational and source-ambiguous. No candidate is discarded by prestige or familiarity. Each is ranged against the same residual, in the order Fig 8 :53 requires. The point is not to pick one; it is to notice that the first four are largely one survey-based evidence pool (Fig 2 :31) and that only two low-overlap fields corroborate the mechanism.

## Q4 - Does the shape generate the literature's answers?

Partly. The shape can generate:

- the SDT emphasis on autonomy support, as C3 plus the person anchor C4;
- the job-characteristics emphasis on autonomy and feedback, as held atoms with provenance owed;
- the monitoring literature's stress and reactance findings, as the valence flip of F8 under a changed residual;
- the cybernetic emphasis on monitoring individuals, as C1 and Fig 1 :22;
- the manager-as-seat literature, as D4 and Fig 11 :37.

It cannot generate the *name* of the metric that will stay unpooled in a particular job, the *number* of standards that makes a head start rather than a replay, or the *identity* of the differing closure that will move a given team's reach. Those are `gap` rows or instrument-bound bindings, not hidden answers (Fig 9 :32, Fig 13 :134). The essay's source rule states why: an instrument that reads a result *"cannot comment on the source"*, and *"the answer is unreached until a better instrument exists"*.

## CONCLUSION

Two teams with identical atoms but different bonds are isomers under Fig 12 :117; two teams sharing only a `person → standard → outcome` pattern are members of one functional group under Fig 12 :123. The conditions bind in dependency order, with the vocabulary and objective link and the carry link the usual breaks, which is why C1 and C2 need different instruments and why C4 sits outside the chain. The standard work-design explanations are mostly bindings of one evidence pool and are generated by the shape only up to the point where a name or a number would be required. The isomer claim has a written falsifier, the order claim has one, and the naming gap is declared rather than filled.
