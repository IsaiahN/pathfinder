# Expert verification packet - the Tether on autonomy versus monitoring and control

**What this is:** a claim in a form a job-design researcher, organizational scholar or empirically minded manager can check. Mode, derivation and refuters are stated before the conclusion, so a reader knows what to look for and what would settle it.

**Mode: `specified` - not `grounded`.** No per-slot measurement is claimed. This is a shape and a route, not a result. It is a simulated derivation checked for internal consistency against the figures, not a ground reading (`05_what_shape_means.md` section 8).

**Boundary of the check.** The first three sections are the whole stage-1 ask. Provenance, links and the method offer come later and only if a verdict on stage 1 is recorded first.

## 1. The claim, in one paragraph

"Balancing employee autonomy with monitoring, standardization and managerial control" is not one dial with a right setting. It is four conditions with different anchors and different failure modes. First, the slot's channel to what the work pays has to stay reachable: monitoring is legitimate as that channel and fails when readings go stale, saturate (everyone green) or get pooled into a team average. Second, what crosses to the employee has to be a re-applicable method or a head start that makes the next search cheaper, not a recording of one past success or a convention that fixes an outcome. Third, the checking arrangement has to hold frames that differ at the evidence level, because otherwise internal agreement compounds while evidence does not; autonomy is the structural supply of those differing frames. Fourth, whether the arrangement is good for the worker is a separate question anchored in human judgment, not in the output reading. A practice that helped can change sign when the work changes, so there is no standing list of good practices. The claim does not name a percentage, a metric or a task to standardize; that is a declared gap at the instrument boundary.

## 2. Three checkable sub-claims

### C1 - Framework-internal; checkable by reading Figures 9, 2 and 1

- **Statement:** the question is a union of four conditions, not a dial; a blend adds vocabulary, not an atom; there are two anchors, the paying outcome for production and human judgment for the person question; the residual is per slot, never pooled.
- **Source, verbatim:**
  - Fig 9 :55 - *"A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones."*; Fig 9 :57 - *"a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either."*
  - Fig 8 :5 - *"vocabulary, never an atom"*
  - Fig 2 :29 - *"An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks. A measurement, an experiment, a counter, a market, a patient outcome."*
  - Fig 2 :37 - *"Human judgment is the right anchor for is this a good answer for a person, because the question is about people."*
  - Fig 1 :19 - *"The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated."*
- **Refuter:** one intervention that restores all four conditions in all failure types and leaves no categorically different residue; or a demonstration that an output reading fully settles whether an arrangement is good for the person. Either fires against C1.

### C2 - Framework-internal derivation plus one business-facing check

- **Statement:** monitoring is a channel condition and fails as channel decay; standardization is a head start and fails as a replay or an outcome-fixing convention; autonomy supplies differing frames, and one shared closure is a mirror chain.
- **Source, verbatim:**
  - Fig 10 :24 - *"the ground does not decay, the channel does"*; Fig 10 :26 - *"saturated metrics, pooled readings"*; Fig 10 :29 - *"keep the anchor reachable"*
  - Fig 4 :22 - *"Only methods cross up. Only head starts come down."*; Fig 4 :24 - *"downward: something that makes the next search cheaper. Never a replay of what worked once."*
  - Fig 10 :47 - *"a convention that decides an outcome has stopped being one"*
  - Fig 7 :36 - *"A chain of mirrors is one long room. The next room must differ from the last, and match the residual it surfaced."*
  - Fig 2 :31 - *"Independence, between two frames, is how little they draw on the same evidence."*
- **What a researcher can check against their own knowledge:** Glavin, Bierman and Schieman (2024) report that surveillance's associations with distress run through *"job pressures, reduced autonomy, and privacy violations"*, alongside a positive direct association with satisfaction that those indirect effects cancel. Brinson et al. (2024) report that *"employer trust was strongly correlated with employees’ positive attitude toward monitoring, which exceeded the level of negative response associated with their reactance"*. Do these not show the same practice carrying different signs on different paths, which is the valence claim? And does either study show that monitoring raised paid output? A "yes" to the second question would be a real refutation of the channel reading as stated.
- **Refuter:** a slot whose output declines while its readings are current, unsaturated and unpooled and the ground keeps paying comparable slots; a replay with no re-application that keeps producing ground-verified readings across habitat changes; or a team whose members all share one closure and one evidence base that nevertheless keeps producing new ground-verified readings. Any one breaks C2.

### C3 - Structure, order and competing explanations

- **Statement:** two teams with identical atoms but different bonds are isomers, so an inventory of metrics and standards cannot predict which arrangement pays; the chain usually breaks at vocabulary and objective; a formerly good practice changes valence when the residual changes; and no figure returns the winning allocation.
- **Source, verbatim:**
  - Fig 12 :117 - *"isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."*
  - Fig 3 :27 - *"A link with no instrument is not a link that works. It is a link nobody has looked at."*
  - Fig 11 :51 - *"No standing list of good actors survives a change of residual."*
  - Fig 12 :119 - *"catalyst. A part that survives its own use. Spent and regenerating parts behave differently across uses, and nothing records which a term is."*
  - Fig 9 :32 - *"you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself"*
- **Refuter:** two teams with identical atoms and identical recorded bonds whose paid outcomes diverge with no difference in ground contact or imported atoms, which would move the explanation to habitat; or a practice whose measured valence is invariant across a documented change of residual. Either breaks C3. The isomer sub-claim is conditional on holding the atom set fixed, and that condition is declared.

## 3. What is explicitly not claimed

- Not `grounded`. No measurement has paid for any part of this.
- Not a theory of everything about management, and no final or unique theory claim.
- Not a percentage, ratio, span or practice list. That is a `gap` at the instrument boundary, not a hidden answer.

- Not that managers, standards, trust or culture are irrelevant. They are Γ and the seat; the claim is that Γ alone does not settle the outcome.

- Not that the work-design literature independently corroborates this. The SDT, job-characteristics and EPM streams partly share one survey evidence pool. The cybernetics and animal-behaviour sources corroborate the mechanism shape, not the organizational fact.

## 5. What would settle it

A prospective, per-slot study that measures C1 to C4 separately, records bonds as well as atoms, uses two anchors, and reports which condition failed before the decline. Concretely:

1. **Channel test:** does the slot's reading of what pays degrade while the ground keeps paying comparable slots? Indicators: stale percepts, saturated metrics, pooled readings (Fig 10 :25-26).
2. **Fidelity and pressure test:** are bracket fidelity and ground pressure both nonzero at each handoff? Fig 7 :37-39 says zero either stops the chain.
3. **Method versus replay test:** did what crossed to the employee make the next search cheaper, or was it a recording of one occasion (Fig 4 :23-25)?
4. **Independence test:** did the checking arrangement hold frames that differed at the evidence level, or one shared closure (Fig 7 :36, Fig 2 :31)?
5. **Person-ground test:** was "good for the worker" ranged against human judgment rather than the output reading (Fig 2 :36-38)?
6. **Source-discriminating instrument:** can the study distinguish lost ground contact from channel decay from closure exhaustion from habitat change from a valence flip when all produce the same falling return? The source essay says a reading alone *"cannot comment on the source"*.

## 6. What an expert can check in twenty minutes

1. Read Fig 9 :55 and :57, Fig 2 :29 and :37, Fig 1 :19, Fig 10 :24 and :47, Fig 4 :22-24, Fig 7 :36, Fig 12 :117 and :119, Fig 11 :51, and Fig 9 :32. Fourteen short passages, about eight minutes.
2. Check C2 against your own knowledge of work design: is there a documented case of a replay, with no re-application, that kept producing ground-verified readings across habitat changes? About four minutes.
3. Name one instrument that would separate lost ground contact from saturated metrics from an exhausted closure using observable work data, or say why none exists. About five minutes.
4. Send the refutation. A failure is more useful than an endorsement.
