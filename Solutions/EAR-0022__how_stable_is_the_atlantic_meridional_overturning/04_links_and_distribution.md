## What the wider literature bears on the question

1. **A model run is not a projection.** Rahmstorf's clarification of van Westen et al. is the single most important reading note in this folder: the hosing experiment traces an equilibrium stability curve, and the "on tipping course" claim comes from reanalysis observations. Reading the model run as a forecast would be a category error and would manufacture a date the paper does not give. Cite this when anyone asks for a collapse year.
2. **The two research groups are probably one pool.** van Westen et al. (Utrecht) and Portmann et al. (Bordeaux) use overlapping CMIP6 ensembles and the same RAPID record. Their agreement about a stability bias is retrodiction from a shared evidence pool, not two independent witnesses (Fig 2 :40, Fig 8 :50-52). The Bordeaux result adds a different method, ridge-regularized observational constraint, which is a genuine sub-method difference, but it is not a new ground.
3. **The proxy record is contested.** Reconstructions disagree about whether the AMOC has weakened since 1900, by how much (5% to 25%), and whether the post-2004 RAPID record fits longer reconstructions. The Matters Arising exchange around Caesar et al. 2021 is the clearest example. This is evidence for the union verdict, not a reason to average the estimates (Fig 9 :4-5).
4. **Different slots, different readings.** The Florida Current is reported stable over four decades after a magnetic-field correction, while RAPID shows a downturn after 2010-11 and the subpolar gyre can behave independently. That is F6 in the world.

## Distribution: offer, do not push

Figure-grounded reasons:

- **Fig 4 :22**: *"Only methods cross up. Only head starts come down."* Sending the full framework before anyone has described a residual sends a recording, not a generator. Lead with the shape claim and its refuter; offer the links after a stage-1 verdict is recorded.
- **Fig 10 :42**: *"A verifier that can reconstruct the claim cannot verify it."* A recipient who has absorbed the framework before being asked for a verdict has joined the frame being checked.
- **Fig 9 :30-31**: *"your words are borrowed too, so describe the effect rather than the cause."* Ask what they would expect to see if the shape claim were true before giving them the article.
- **Fig 2 :40**: *"disqualifying for a verdict: agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool."* A mass send selected for people who already agree produces a convention, not corroboration.

**Recommended order in a reply:** (1) the three-condition shape claim and its refuter; (2) ask what they would count as a failure and which observation they would treat as the anchor; (3) record that verdict; (4) only then the links, with the note that the figures, not the summary, are the authority.

## The two-stage offer

**Stage 1.** Validate the shape against the ground. The recipient checks the claim without holding the framework's interior. Fig 10 :52: *"Bring a characterised residual, never the source."*

**Stage 2, opt-in only.** If they are curious, offer the verbatim figure text, the canonical usage rule and the two open-access papers. Their later agreement is never pooled as corroboration (Fig 2 :40). Their stage-1 verdict is recorded first.

**Stage 3, opt-in only.** Offer the formula as a method to reuse. Fig 4 :22 permits sending a generator. Each user writes their own refuter and states their own mode, because a frame cannot check the direction of its own question.

**Hard precondition:** stages 1 to 3 work only if the stage-1 shape survives. The live strain S4 in `01_figure_grounded_audit.md` must be stated in the covering note, not hidden.
