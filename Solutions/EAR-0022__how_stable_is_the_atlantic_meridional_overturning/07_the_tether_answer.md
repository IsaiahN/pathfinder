# The Tether's answer on AMOC stability and its observation channels

**Mode: `specified` on the per-domain claims, `general` on the structural shape, `gap` on the numerical tipping date and the winning single observing system. Not `grounded`.** This is a simulated derivation checked against the figures (`05_what_shape_means.md` section 7), not a ground reading. No measurement was taken.

## 1. The residual it answers

"How stable is the AMOC, and what observations best track its long-term changes?" is a union, not one question. The stability half splits into a trend condition, a variability condition and a threshold condition, each with a different instrument; the observation half splits into channels that are anchors and channels that are instruments. Fig 9 :4-5: *"First: is there a fact here? Disagreements that do not shrink with effort, where each rule keeps working well on a different subset, are not one hard question but several well-formed ones. Split it rather than search."* Verdicts are kept per domain and never averaged.

## 2. The shape

1. **R is indexed per observing slot, not into one stability scalar.** Fig 1 :19: *"The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated."* The 26°N array, the subpolar arrays, the 34°S section, the Florida Current cable and the SST fingerprints disagree among themselves. That disagreement is the signal that says where to look, not noise to be averaged away.
2. **Direct transport arrays are the anchors; fingerprints and model confidence labels are instruments.** Fig 2 :30: *"you push on it, it is not an anchor."* A mooring at 26°N and a freshwater-transport section at 34°S return values that do not move when a model changes its projection. SST cold blobs, carbon-sink anomalies, gyre indices and IPCC confidence language do. An instrument that reads green cannot comment on its source; a discriminator is owed, not a name (essay, Hard Problem is About Source).
3. **The three stability conditions are isomers over a shared atom set.** Fig 12 :117: *"isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."* Trend is a comparison, variability is a sequence, threshold is a comparison over a cycle whose sign matters. A single index is the flat-form collapse (Fig 12 :5).
4. **Models are too stable in their own closure, and only observation-constrained import moves the wall.** Fig 6 :23: *"No frame escapes its own closure. The bound moves only on contact with a frame whose closure differs."* The CMIP freshwater-transport sign bias is the wall; the RAPID and SST/SSS constraints are the import with a source gate and a shadow test. The Utrecht hosing result and the Bordeaux constrained estimate are one evidence pool until provenance separates them (Fig 8 :50-52).
5. **Threshold distance is reachable as a shape, not as a number at this budget.** The physics-based diagnostic is the minimum of AMOC-induced freshwater transport at 34°S, which led the observed crossing by about 25 model years in one CESM run. Current reanalyses are too short to estimate the distance to that minimum. Fig 9 :32: *"you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself"*; Fig 13 :134: *"Unreachable is a verdict here, not a silence,"*
6. **The channel decays, the ground does not.** Fig 10 :24: *"the ground does not decay, the channel does"*; Fig 10 :26 names *"stale percepts, dropped transients, saturated metrics, pooled readings."* A short array record is a decaying channel, not an unstable ocean.
7. **The observation habitat is enumerated, never composed.** Fig 11 :44: *"List everything in contact with the residual, then what is in contact with those, and outward until the cascade stops mattering."* The cascade runs from freshwater forcing through salinity, density and convection to transport, heat, SST fingerprints and regional climate three steps out. Valence is relative to the residual (Fig 11 :51).

## 3. What it explicitly does not say

- Not a collapse date, not a probability of crossing before 2100, and not a claim that the IPCC assessment is wrong. The figures return no date; the world literature is contested.
- Not that any single observing system is best. The question is a union, so there is no global winner (Fig 9 :54-57).
- Not that SST fingerprints, gyre indices or carbon-sink anomalies are useless. They are instruments whose readings must be discriminated against transport; they carry zero verdict weight until provenance is shown.
- Not that models are worthless. They are the closure that the import enters; the claim is about where the information comes from.
- Not `grounded`. No mooring data were analysed here and no reanalysis was recomputed.

## 4. What would settle it: the ground

1. **Anchor-discrimination test:** does an SST or carbon-sink index preserve its ranking of AMOC states under a change of source with no transport measurement used in validation? If yes, it is an anchor; if no, it stays an instrument.
2. **Threshold instrument:** a measurement that returns distance to the tipping point directly, with stated uncertainty, validated on an observed crossing. This would close the F7 gap and fire refuter 4 in `01` section 5.
3. **FovS sign test:** a model that reproduces the observed freshwater-transport sign at 34°S without observational constraint and still projects only small weakening. That would break the import claim.
4. **Sustained 34°S and 26°N records:** the diagnostic lives at 34°S and the trend baseline at 26°N. The records are the ground that pays, and they have to be long enough that internal variability does not dominate.
5. **Standing falsifiers from `01` section 5:** union collapse to one scalar; source-independent index; direct distance measurement; unconstrained model passing the sign test; all channels returning one state vector; disjoint-frame convergence.

## 5. The honest stop

**Reached, but not settled.** The shape is available and checkable: three conditions, an anchor/instrument discriminator, a physics-based threshold diagnostic, and a recorded import path. The number is not. The six-term substrate is a visible guess, and the figures stop where instruments can no longer distinguish (README, canonical limit). *"Indiscernibility is not identity."* Any specific collapse year is a binding from a frame, recorded as such, not a figure result. One strain remains live: the operand type of the comparison ⋛ at the threshold level (section 6 of `01`).

## 6. One-paragraph version for outreach

> The Tether does not give a collapse date and does not rank the AMOC's observing systems. It says that "stability" is three questions wearing one word: a trend, a variability budget, and a threshold distance, each with its own instrument. Direct transport arrays at 26°N and 34°S are the anchors because they do not change their answer when a model changes its projection; SST fingerprints, gyre indices and confidence labels are instruments that need a discriminator before they can settle anything. The three conditions are isomers over a shared set of ocean quantities, so a single stability score throws away the bond that carries the information. Climate models are too stable in their own closure, and only observation-constrained import with a source gate moves that wall. The distance to a tipping point is visible as a shape, a freshwater-transport minimum at 34°S, but too short a record exists to read the number. What the framework offers is the shape, the falsifiers written in advance, and the instruments that would close it. The stop is recorded, not hidden.
