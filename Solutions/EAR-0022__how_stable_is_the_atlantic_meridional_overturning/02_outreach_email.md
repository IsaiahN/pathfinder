**Subject:** Check one shape claim about AMOC stability before you decide whether the method is worth reading

> Hi [name],
>
> I have one specific claim I would like you to check against what you already know. I am not asking you to read any theory first, and I am not asking you to agree with it. If the claim does not survive, there is nothing else to look at.
>
> **The claim (`specified` on the parts, `general` on the shape, not `grounded`):** "How stable is the AMOC?" is not one question with one answer. It is a union of three conditions with different instruments. (1) Trend: is the overturning drifting from a historical baseline, and is the record long enough to tell? (2) Variability: how much of what we see is internal variability on days-to-decade scales? (3) Threshold: is there a control value past which the circulation changes qualitatively, and can any observation tell us how far away it is? The first two are partly settled by direct transport arrays such as RAPID at 26°N and SAMBA at 34°S. The third is not settled: the physics-based freshwater-transport diagnostic at 34°S is observable, but current records are too short to estimate the distance to its minimum.
>
> **What I am not claiming:** I am not claiming an AMOC collapse date, or that a collapse before 2100 is more likely than the IPCC assessment says. I am not claiming that a cold-blob SST index, a carbon-sink trend, a subpolar-gyre index or a CMIP6 confidence label is an anchor. Those are instruments that must be ranged against transport. An instrument that reads green cannot comment on its source.
>
> **What would refute the shape claim:** I wrote these down before sending this.
> 1. One observation channel resolves trend, variability and threshold distance with no information loss, so the three verdicts reduce to one scalar.
> 2. An SST, carbon-sink or gyre index keeps its ranking of AMOC states under a change of source with no transport measurement used in validation.
> 3. A measurement returns distance to the tipping point directly, with validated uncertainty, without a model stability curve.
> 4. A model reproduces the observed freshwater-transport sign at 34°S without observational constraint and still projects only small weakening.
> 5. Two frames with genuinely disjoint evidence converge on the same stability verdict by separate derivation. That would be real corroboration, and it would show my "one pool" claim is wrong.
>
> If any of these holds, the shape is wrong and I will retract it. A one-line "this fails because..." is more useful to me than agreement. If you would rather not engage, no reply is needed.
>
> **If it survives, and only then:** the figures and their verbatim text are below. The figures, not my summary, are the authority.

Write down what would refute your result *before* you run it, and state the mode of what you get (general, specified, grounded). A reported failure is more useful to me than a reported success.
