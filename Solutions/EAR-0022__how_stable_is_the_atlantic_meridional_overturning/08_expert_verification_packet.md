# Expert verification packet: the Tether on AMOC stability

**What this is:** a claim in a form an oceanographer or climate modeller can check. Mode, derivation and refuters are stated before the conclusion, so a reader knows what to look for and what would settle it.

**Mode: `specified` on the three sub-claims, `general` on the shape, `gap` on the numerical tipping date. Not `grounded`.** No measurement is claimed here.

## 1. The claim, in one paragraph

"How stable is the AMOC?" is a union, not one scalar. It splits into a trend condition, a variability condition and a threshold condition, each with its own instrument and refuter. The observation question is a separate union of channels: direct transport arrays are anchors because they do not update on a model's opinion, while SST fingerprints, gyre indices, carbon-sink anomalies and confidence labels are instruments that must be ranged against transport. The three conditions are isomers over a shared atom set, so a single stability index is the flat-form collapse that loses the bond. Models are too stable in their own closure, and observation-constrained import with a source gate is what moves the wall. The distance to a tipping point is reachable as a shape, the freshwater-transport minimum at 34°S, but not as a number with the records currently held.

## 2. Three checkable sub-claims

### C1: framework-internal; checkable by reading Figures 1, 2 and 9

- **Statement:** R is indexed per slot; the anchor is whatever does not update on a frame's opinion; the question is a union, so verdicts stay separate.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 1 :19: *"The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated."* Fig 2 :29-30: *"An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks. A measurement, an experiment, a counter, a market, a patient outcome. If it moves when you push on it, it is not an anchor."* Fig 9 :54: *"And when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again."*
- **Refuter:** show one channel that resolves trend, variability and threshold distance with no information loss, or show an "observation" that changes its answer when a model changes its projection and is still a valid anchor.

### C2: oceanography-facing; checkable against the observing systems

- **Statement:** direct transport is the anchor; fingerprints are instruments needing a shadow test. Trend baseline lives at 26°N, the physics-based threshold diagnostic at 34°S. The channels disagree, so no single winner exists.
- **What to check:** does the RAPID record at 26°N and the SAMBA section at 34°S return values independent of model projections? Does a cold-blob SST index preserve its ranking under a change of source with no transport validation? Do the Florida Current cable, the subpolar gyre and the 26°N array really report different things?
- **World bearing (secondary):** RAPID reports *"sustaining the observations for more than 20 years"* and *"meridional coherence of the MOC appears much more complicated than previously expected"* (rapid.ac.uk). van Westen et al. 2024 identify the 34°S freshwater-transport minimum as an observable diagnostic and note that reanalyses are *"too short (maximum of ∼100 years)"* to estimate the distance to it.
- **Refuter:** a source-independent index that passes validation without transport, or a demonstration that all Atlantic channels return the same state vector at all latitudes and resolutions.

### C3: modelling-facing; checkable against CMIP6 and the constraint studies

- **Statement:** the model closure is too stable; only provenanced import moves the wall. The Utrecht hosing study and the Bordeaux observational constraint are one pool until their origins are separated.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 6 :21: *"MINT composes inside the closure and can never add an atom."* Fig 6 :23: *"No frame escapes its own closure. The bound moves only on contact with a frame whose closure differs."* Fig 8 :50-52: *"Convergent derivation and adopted import look identical in the contents. Only the record of where each came from separates them, and the surplus lives only where the closures differ."*
- **What to check:** does the CMIP6 freshwater-transport sign bias persist; does the ridge-regularized constraint actually change the estimate (32 ± 37% to 51 ± 8% reported); do the two groups share CMIP6 and RAPID inputs?
- **Refuter:** an unconstrained model that reproduces the observed FovS sign and still projects only small weakening, or two frames with genuinely disjoint evidence that converge on the same verdict by separate derivation.

## 3. What is explicitly not claimed

- Not a collapse date, not a crossing probability before 2100, and not that the IPCC assessment is wrong.
- Not that any one observing system wins overall, and not that SST or carbon-sink indices are useless.
- Not that models are worthless, and not that the two research groups are dishonest; the claim is about evidence-pool overlap, which is a structural property they would be first to declare.
- Not `grounded`. Nothing here was settled by a measurement taken in this work.
- Not a claim that the structure in the slot is a seventh primitive. FovS is a held instrument from the oceanography frame, a binding recorded as provenance (see `05` section 5).

## 5. What would settle it

1. A source-discriminating observation that names what an SST reading cannot: transport at 26°N and freshwater transport at 34°S, with records long enough to resolve the distance to the FovS minimum.
2. The FovS sign test on an unconstrained CMIP6 model.
3. A direct measurement of distance to threshold with validated uncertainty.
4. Disjoint-frame convergent derivation, which would be real corroboration rather than a shared pool.

## 6. What an expert can check in twenty minutes

1. Read Fig 1 :19, Fig 2 :29-30, Fig 6 :23 and Fig 9 :54: four short passages.
2. Check C2 against their own knowledge: which of the Atlantic channels actually updates on a model projection, and which does not?
3. Name one observation that would distinguish a cold-blob source from a transport change, or say why none exists.
4. Send the refutation. A failure is more useful than an endorsement.
