# Isomers, cycles and order in the AMOC stability question

**Mode: `specified` on the structural comparison, `general` on the bond typology. This is a simulated derivation checked against the figures, not a ground reading (see `05_what_shape_means.md` section 7).**

## RESIDUAL

The question as posed invites a ranking: which observation is best, and is the circulation stable or unstable. Two derivations are owed: (1) are the three stability conditions (trend, variability, threshold) different substances over a shared atom set, or just different spellings of one thing? (2) in what dependency order do the observation channels and the physical variables sit, and where does the chain break?

## DERIVATION

### Q1: Are trend, variability and threshold isomers, or spellings of one thing?

- Fig 12 :117: **"isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."**
- Fig 12 :94: **"The arrangement is the substance."**
- Fig 12 :46: **"A bond is the typed relation between two operands. There are seven, they are not interchangeable, and which one holds is not recoverable from the operands."**

Over the shared atom set {M, H, F, S, T, ρ, φ, τ, t}, the three conditions use different bonds:

| condition | primitive bond | figure |
|---|---|---|
| Trend | comparison ⋛ of transport at t1 against a baseline at t0 | Fig 12 :87, Operators table :49 |
| Variability | sequence → over t, with a threshold between signal and noise decided by scale | Fig 12 :72, Operators table :30 |
| Threshold | cycle, the salt-advection feedback whose sign decides closure, plus comparison of FovS against its minimum | Fig 12 :121, :87 |
| Fingerprint reading | conjunction + of several correlated indicators (SST, carbon sink, sea level) | Fig 12 :57 |

They share atoms and differ in bond, so under Fig 12 :117 they are **isomers**, not spellings of one substance. A single "AMOC stability index" would be the flat-form collapse Fig 12 :5 names: *"A set of ingredients is not one. The bond is the rest of the information, and it is the half nothing was recording."*

**Falsifier, written before the run:** if one channel can be shown to carry the same information about all three conditions with no bond change, so that the four rows above reduce to one, the isomer claim fails and the union collapses to a partition.

### Q2: What is the order of the physical chain?

Fig 3 :20: **"The chain is ordered by dependency, not by importance."** Fig 13 :75: **"Two states never bond directly. It is not space-time but space, a structure, time; and two structures join only through a state."** The physical chain, read off the world and not composed, is:

1. freshwater forcing φ (state-like input) acts on salinity S (structure);
2. S and temperature T compose density ρ;
3. ρ governs deep convection in the Labrador, Irminger and Nordic Seas;
4. convection and wind together set volume transport M;
5. M carries heat H and freshwater F;
6. F at 34°S feeds back on S through the salt-advection cycle;
7. H sets the SST pattern that the fingerprints read;
8. the SST pattern affects regional rainfall, ice and ecosystems three steps out.

This is a chain of alternating state and structure, so it is legal under Fig 13 :74: **"Everything else is a composition across the two kinds, never within one."** The feedback at step 6 is Fig 12 :121's cycle: **"The output re-enters as input. The sign of the loop decides whether it closes, and a chain cannot close by construction."** Whether the sign is stabilizing or destabilizing is exactly what the FovS diagnostic measures (van Westen et al. 2024).

**Falsifier:** if convection and transport can be shown to vary independently under all observed forcings, step 3 to step 4 of the dependency chain is broken, and the "read below the break is a reading of nothing" rule applies (Fig 3 :22-23).

### Q3: Are the observing systems a functional group?

- Fig 12 :123: **"functional group. A substructure whose presence fixes the outcome. The group says what kind; the qualifier says which member."**
- RAPID at 26°N, OSNAP in the subpolar North Atlantic and SAMBA at 34°S are moored transport arrays: same functional group (a section that returns transport), different atoms (latitude, measured variable, length of record).
- Fig 12 :119: **"catalyst. A part that survives its own use. Spent and regenerating parts behave differently across uses, and nothing records which a term is."** A sustained array is a catalyst; a one-off campaign is a spent part. Both can produce a correct number once, and only one keeps the channel to the ground reachable.
- The Florida Current cable is another member of the group, but its reported stability over four decades (Wikipedia, Observations) applies to a different slot. Under Fig 11 :51, **"No standing list of good actors survives a change of residual,"** no array is permanently "the best." Its valence is relative to which condition is being decided.

**Falsifier:** if a one-off campaign and a sustained array return indistinguishable verdicts for all three stability conditions across a decade, the catalyst/spent-part distinction has no purchase here and should be withdrawn.

### Q4: Two currencies: bits and actions

Fig 12 :125: **"A composition costs in two currencies, and they do not add."** :126: **"Bits price the description. Actions price finding out whether it holds."**

- A global stability index is cheap in bits (one number) and very expensive in actions: it requires deciding which of several competing reconstructions is right.
- A per-slot report with three conditions is expensive in bits (more numbers, more qualifications) and cheap in actions: each condition has a named instrument and a refuter that a working oceanographer can check in twenty minutes.
- Summing the two into one "optimal monitoring design" is the error Fig 12 :130 names: *"Summing them into one figure is the error this names, not the fix for it."*

**Falsifier:** a design that is simultaneously minimal in description length and minimal in observing cost for all three conditions would collapse the two currencies. If demonstrated, this Q4 is false.

### Q5: Order of the outreach chain (Fig 3)

1. perception: does the recipient accept that transport, not SST, is the anchor for state?
2. vocabulary: do they hold a term for the threshold diagnostic (FovS minimum), not only for AMOC strength?
3. objective: would they count "no trend", "no crossing" or "recovery after perturbation" as the same thing? This is the middle link with no instrument (Fig 3 :24-27).
4. planning: which array, which reanalysis, which model ensemble?
5. learn and carry: record the verdict before showing the theory, then offer the method.

Most effort in public discussion lands at step 4 or above: more models, more proxies, more confident language. Fig 3 :19: **"Optimising a link that already works cannot rescue one that does not."**

## CONCLUSION

The three stability conditions are **isomers over a shared atom set**, not spellings of one quantity (Q1). The physical chain alternates state and structure and closes through a feedback whose sign is what the threshold diagnostic measures (Q2). The observing systems form a **functional group** whose members change valence with the residual, and a sustained array is a **catalyst** while a one-off campaign is a spent part (Q3). Description cost and settlement cost do not add (Q4). The chain breaks at the objective link, where "stable" has no instrument (Q5). The isomer claim is checkable and has a stated falsifier; nothing here needs a seventh primitive or a wall-moving import.
