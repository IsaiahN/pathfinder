## 1. A shape is a constraint, not a filler

The figures README states the limit: the framework returns *"what a missing thing would have to look like (a shape, not a name)."* Fig 9 :32: *"you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself."* Fig 13 :134: *"Unreachable is a verdict here, not a silence,"* and :135 *"It cannot say which primitive is missing, because naming it would mean already holding it."*

From *The Library of Babel*, opened this session: *"you can cut your search short if the set of books returned is small and descriptive enough that it provides a general shape of the answer that satisfies your question."*

So a **shape** is the narrowed form of an answer you can state before you can name it. It constrains; it does not fill. For EAR-0022 the shape is: three separable stability conditions (trend, variability, threshold), indexed per observing slot, with a classified set of anchor channels and instrument channels, and a physics-based threshold diagnostic that is observable but not yet resolvable as a number. A claim of *"the AMOC will collapse in 2065"* is a name where the framework returns a shape.

## 2. How a shape is obtained

- *Library of Babel*, opened: use the vocabulary you already have that is closest to the thing, look for recurring new terms that overlap or correlate with it, recurse. *"With each new term gained, you are able to narrow the space by definition and deepen your vocabulary."* For this question the bootstrap vocabulary is transport, salinity, density, convection, freshwater forcing, and FovS; the search narrows from "stability" to a per-slot comparison.
- The same essay names the stop condition: *"Perhaps your problem was a bundle or conflation of several problems masquerading as one?"* That is Fig 9 :54-57 applied to the AMOC question. The bundle is trend plus variability plus threshold plus observation choice.
- Fig 6 :30: *"outward: does another frame already hold it? The description travels. Fails by being sharpened into terms only we can read. Cast it in effect terms."* Fig 9 :30-31: *"your words are borrowed too, so describe the effect"*, *"rather than the cause."*
- *Decoding Tacit Knowledge*, opened: *"When contact isn't possible, the best you can send is a plain description of what you'd expect to see, because anyone can check that against the world."* This is why the outreach packet leads with refuters, not with the framework.
- *Library of Babel* on stopping: the finish line is where *"the finish line will also be the starting place"* and the paradox is that you have *"composed the answer through the process of locating it."*

## 3. What licenses a name

Only two things, both outside the shape itself:

1. **The ground settles it.** Formula step 5: *"the ground settles it. Verified only by what pays."* The README: the framework *"stops at the limit of what the available instruments can distinguish, and records the lineage so the stop is honest."* A sustained transport measurement that returns distance to threshold would name the slot.
2. **A provenanced import from a frame whose closure differs** (Fig 6 :23, Fig 8 :50-52), with a source gate and a shadow test. The observational constraint on CMIP6 in Portmann et al. 2026 is such an import: the source is the RAPID and SST/SSS record, and the shadow test is that the bias-corrected estimate explains the observed salinity pattern.

*They're Scanning Books When They Should* `[carried]`: *"The real import is not a new term in the existing space. It is a new dimension of the space."* Also: *"A minimum viable set of six terms is a human's careful guess at reality's compression axes; another sensorium's minimum set need not be those six."* And: *"Declaring the primitives does not make them neutral. It makes the choice visible."*

## 4. Why the source essay governs the observation half

*The Hard Problem is About Source*, opened this session, gives the mechanism for the anchor-versus-instrument distinction. An instrument that detects green *"cannot comment on the source."* The same reading can come from one green light, yellow plus blue, RGB, CMYK, or *"a completely novel color scheme that humans have not yet discovered."* The full set of sources is not closed. *"If several paths create the same behavior, then behavior is a projection with lossy information."* The resolution came *"not by argument, but by the arrival of a better instrument."*

Applied here: a subpolar SST cold blob, a reduced North Atlantic carbon sink, a gyre slowdown and a changing phytoplankton record can all read as "AMOC weakening." They are projections with lossy information. A transport array at 26°N or a freshwater-transport section at 34°S is the discriminator that comments on the source. The essay's rule: *"An instrument that can't distinguish these cases demonstrates its own inadequacy, it cannot claim anything substantial about what is being measured or what causes it."*

This is **not** retracted or narrowed by the correction in section 5. The correction concerns which primitives the "shape, not a name" rule forbids naming. It does not touch the metamerism point: the reason we need transport is the missing discriminator, not a ban on naming held atoms.

## 5. Q: does "shape, not a name" forbid naming FovS or the RAPID array?

**Answer: no, and the earlier overstatement is corrected here, visibly.**

- The README's phrase is *"what a **missing** thing would have to look like (a shape, not a name)."* The subject is a primitive not held in Γ.
- FovS, salinity, temperature, freshwater transport and mooring arrays are already held instruments in the oceanography frame. Naming one is a **binding** from that frame onto a held quantity, not the invention of a new primitive.
- What remains forbidden is claiming that the binding is derived from the figures, or that it moves the wall by itself. Fig 6 :21: *"MINT composes inside the closure and can never add an atom."* Fig 6 :22: *"NOVEL means novel relative to atoms, not to the world."*
- **Correction kept visible:** an earlier draft of this section wrote "the framework returns a shape, not a name, so we cannot say which observation to use." That was wrong. The framework can say which observation is an anchor and which is an instrument, because that classification is a relation to a non-updating ground, not the naming of a missing primitive. What it cannot do is return a tipping date. The narrowed claim stands: shape for the date, binding for the channels.

The same narrowing appears in `06_isomers_and_order.md` Q1 and in the retraction table of `01_figure_grounded_audit.md` section 6, so nothing is quietly lost.

## 6. Prestige is not action

From *The Recursive Self-Improvement Trap*, opened this session: *"Prestige is not equal to actions."* and *"Prestige must never be allowed to validate an action directly."* Then: *"Prestige can fund an action. It can give an action a budget to be tried. But it cannot settle an action. Only the ground can settle it. The Ground Maintainer must be blind to prestige."*

For this question: the authority of the IPCC, the size of a model ensemble, and the number of scientists who sign an open letter can fund a monitoring programme. They cannot decide whether the AMOC is approaching a threshold. The ground is the transport and salinity record. This is why `03_public_contact_points.csv` contains institutional opt-in venues and no ranked list of famous experts, and why the first contact asks for a refutation.

## 7. My own audit is a simulation, not a ground reading

Same essay, opened: *"LLMs can quickly simulate an approximate answer/output with the formula found in the architecture post. It will often be better than the generic outputs that you will usually get otherwise too. However, the truly testable output would need to be derived from the real architecture or working the formula yourself."*

So `01_figure_grounded_audit.md` is a simulated derivation, checked for internal consistency against the figures. It is **not** a test of the AMOC question and must be labelled as such. It cannot settle anything it reports on. The world papers in `09_watson_answer.md` are secondary corroboration only, and most of them share the CMIP6 and RAPID evidence pool.
