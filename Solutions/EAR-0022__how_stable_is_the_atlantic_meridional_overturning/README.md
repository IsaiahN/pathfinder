## The question restated as an effect

The question looks like one stability question plus one method question. In effect terms it is a union of at least three conditions with different anchors, and the observation half is a separate union of channels (Fig 9 :4-5, :54, :55, :57).

- **b** is what a frame (a climate model, a proxy reconstruction, an index) believes about the overturning before it measures.
- **a** is what the frame does: run a hosing experiment, deploy a mooring array, fit a fingerprint to sea surface temperature, assemble an observational constraint.
- **Γ(b,a)** is what the frame predicts: an overturning strength in sverdrups, a percentage weakening by 2100, a distance to a threshold.
- **o′** is what the ocean returns: measured transport, salinity, heat content, convection, regional temperature.
- **s** is the slot. R is indexed per observing system, per latitude, per variable, and per timescale. It is never averaged into one "AMOC stability score" (Fig 1 :19, :22).

Two options are compared:

- **Option A, "stability is one scalar property, and the best observation is the one that correlates most with it":** leads to a global stability number, a ranked list of indices, and a verdict that can be manufactured by selecting agreeable indices. A pooled index is a frame-internal quantity that cannot score itself (Fig 1 :27).
- **Option B, "stability is a union of trend, variability and threshold conditions indexed per slot, and observations are anchors or instruments depending on their relation to a ground that does not update":** leads to separate verdicts, an explicit discriminator between a channel that settles and a channel that merely correlates, and an honest stop where no instrument can resolve the distance to the threshold.

What an answer would look like, written down and bound as an ordinary term before the run (Formula, "Direction"): a shape with three separable conditions, each with its own instrument and refuter; a classification of observation channels into anchors and instruments; and a stated limit where the available instruments cannot distinguish. Until bound, that shape predicts nothing and no loop check can see it. It is now bound, so the refuter in `01_figure_grounded_audit.md` section 5 can fire on it.

The verdict recorded in `09_watson_answer.md` is Option B in `specified` mode on the structural claims, `general` on the shape, and a declared `gap` for the numerical tipping date.

## What is deliberately not here

- No personal emails of named individuals.
- No ranking of people by how likely they are to agree.
- No bulk-send plan.
- No claim that this derivation is `grounded`, no claim that it settles whether the AMOC will tip, and no claim that a figure line returns a tipping date.

Three figure-grounded reasons, plus one practical one:

1. A room is not an anchor. *"A room’s agreement is variation, not a verdict. Selection between rooms is where the ground votes."* (Fig 7 :35) and *"Alignment is triangulation, not negotiation. A mirror cannot correct you."* (Fig 2 :35). A list of agreeable assessors manufactures the correlation that disqualifies a verdict.
2. Correlation has valence. *"disqualifying for a verdict: agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool"* (Fig 2 :40). Two model groups that both use CMIP6 and RAPID are one pool wearing two names until provenance separates them.
3. A verifier must be outside the interior. *"A verifier that can reconstruct the claim cannot verify it."* (Fig 10 :42). The first contact therefore carries the residual and its refuter, not the framework.
4. Legally, unsolicited bulk email to scraped addresses breaches GDPR and CAN-SPAM style rules in many jurisdictions and gets the sending domain blacklisted. Public opt-in routes avoid that failure as well as the structural one.
