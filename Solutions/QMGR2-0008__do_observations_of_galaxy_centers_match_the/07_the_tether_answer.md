# The Tether's answer: do observations of galaxy centers match simulated dense dark-matter profiles?

**Mode: `specified` — not `grounded`.** This is a shape, a per-domain verdict set and a route, not a result. Simulated derivation checked for internal consistency against the figures (`05_what_shape_means.md` §7), not a ground reading. No measurement was taken.

## 1. The residual it answers

The prediction (`Γ`) is a collisionless cold-dark-matter halo: a dense, cusp-like inner profile, approximately universal across halo mass (NFW 1997). The observation (`o′`) for a given galaxy is a rotation or dispersion profile from which an enclosed mass and inner slope are **inferred**. The residual `R_s = |Γ_s − o′_s|` is indexed by object slot. Fig 1 :19: *"The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated."*

So the question has no single yes/no. In many low-mass dwarfs and low-surface-brightness disks the inferred central density is approximately constant and the inner enclosed mass is below the prediction (de Blok; Oman et al.). In more massive dwarfs and at least one dwarf-sized ultra-diffuse galaxy the inferred inner profile is cuspy (Cooke et al., plus a case listed on the Wikipedia reference page and not opened here). Fig 1 :22: *"Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average."*

## 2. Per-domain verdicts, kept separate and never averaged

**D1, dark-matter-only simulation frame.** The cusp prediction is confirmed *as a frame prediction*. Fig 6 :21: *"MINT composes inside the closure and can never add an atom."* No amount of further N-body search inside the collisionless closure produces a core. Fig 6 :23: *"No frame escapes its own closure. The bound moves only on contact with a frame whose closure differs."* Verdict: `general` on the closure law, `specified` on this halo class, **triangulated** (NFW plus the de Blok quote plus Fig 6). This is not a ground verdict.

**D2, observation and inference frame.** Mixed by slot. Many low-mass systems show cores or inner mass deficits; massive dwarfs and some UDGs show cusps. Oman et al. reframes the mismatch as an *"inner mass deficit"* problem rather than a pure slope problem. The anchor is the instrument, but the quantity is inferred through a channel with a documented 50 percent+ failure mode (Sands et al. 2024). Fig 10 :24: *"the ground does not decay, the channel does"*. Verdict: `specified`, **triangulated** across de Blok, Oman, Cooke and the reference overview for the per-slot distribution; **single-source** for the channel strain (Sands et al., a 2024 simulation study with its own closure). The channel strain is live, not smoothed.

**D3, baryonic physics frame.** A candidate import exists: repeated, energetic feedback creates potential fluctuations that irreversibly transfer energy to collisionless particles, flattening the cusp (Pontzen and Governato 2012). It has a source gate (a published, simulated, cited mechanism) and a shadow test (it explains the inner mass deficit). But Oman et al. report deficits *"well in excess of that reported in recent simulations"*, so it does not close every slot. Verdict: `specified`, **single-source** for the specific atom, with a corroborating qualitative statement in the reference overview; ungrounded for any particular galaxy.

**D4, verification frame.** Agreement among simulation groups and observers who share ΛCDM priors and compilations is one evidence pool. Fig 2 :40: *"disqualifying for a verdict: agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool"*. Verdict: agreement here has **zero corroboration weight** until a genuinely low-overlap frame returns the same per-slot shape. The simulated second seat in `11_audit.md` shares the primary's pool and is not a witness.

**Union, not average.** These four verdicts are not combined into a single score. Fig 9 :54-55 says to unbundle a union before searching again. The compound's intersections are the real target.

## 3. The shape, and what it does not name

The shared shape across the closing domains is: *an inner mass profile that is less dense than the collisionless cusp prediction in the low-mass slots, with the direction and magnitude of the change stated at fixed maximum rotation speed.* Fig 9 :32: *"you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself."*

The framework does not name which mechanism realizes that shape. Calling it supernova feedback, warm dark matter, self-interacting dark matter or modified gravity is a binding from a particular frame, provenanced as such. Fig 8 :50: convergent derivation and adopted import look identical in the contents; only the record separates them. Cusp and core themselves are Fig 12 :117 isomers — *"Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."* — not two different dark matters.

## 4. What it explicitly does not say

- Not "the simulations are wrong." The cusp prediction is correct inside its closure.
- Not "the observations are wrong." The readings are real; the inference is the contested link.
- Not "CDM is falsified." A residual in one regime, with an open channel and an untested import, is not a model verdict.
- Not "baryonic feedback solves it." It is a candidate import with a source gate, and it does not close every slot.
- Not "all dwarfs are cored." Massive dwarfs and some UDGs show cusps.
- Not `grounded`. No measurement settles `left(R,φ)` for this derivation.

## 5. What would settle it — the ground

A source-discriminating instrument, not a further argument. From *The Hard Problem is About Source* (fresh 2026-11-23): a colorimeter *"cannot comment on the source"*; it was the spectrometer that resolved the case, *"not by argument, but by the arrival of a better instrument."* Here:

1. **Independent mass probe:** stellar kinematics, lensing or proper motions that return the inner enclosed mass in the same slots where H I/CO rotation curves infer a core, with no shared reconstruction systematics. This is the refuter of the channel strain (F4).
2. **Import test:** a hydrodynamical simulation that reproduces the observed inner mass deficits at fixed V_max using observed star-formation histories, with convergence and resolution tests reported. This is the refuter of the "new mechanism owed" reading (F3).
3. **Slot test:** the same independent probe showing low-mass dwarfs at fixed V_max have NFW-consistent inner slopes. This refutes the cored-slot reading (F1).
4. **Standing falsifiers from the audit:** derive the cusp prediction away within the dark-matter-only closure; show the residual is a partition rather than a union; show cusp and core require different dark-matter species (breaks the isomer reading).

If 2 and 1 both close every slot, the import stands and no refusal is owed. If they fail to close the residual, the fifth kind applies and the correct output is a negation of the held binding "collisionless CDM halo profiles match observed centers in the low-mass regime," not another explanatory story.

## 6. The honest stop

**Unreached, not unreachable.** Fig 8 :39: *"So a search that finds nothing is never a proof of absence."* Fig 13 :134: *"Unreachable is a verdict here, not a silence"*. The derivation stops where the instruments stop: the literature itself says the 10-100 pc scales on which core formation acts are unresolved in many large simulations. The lineage is recorded so the stop is checkable. Indiscernibility is not identity. No last room: this is not a theory of everything of galaxy formation, and it does not claim to be.

## 7. One-paragraph version for outreach

> The figures do not return a single yes or no to this question. They say the residual is indexed by object, not averaged: many low-mass galaxy centers show an inner mass deficit relative to the dense cusps that collisionless simulations predict, while more massive dwarfs and some ultra-diffuse galaxies match the cusp. The simulation prediction is correct inside its own closure, and no amount of searching inside that closure produces a core. A candidate mechanism exists in a different closure, supernova-driven potential fluctuations that transfer energy to collisionless particles, but it is an import with provenance, not a derivation, and it does not close every reported deficit. The measurement channel is the live problem: dark-matter profiles are inferred from rotation curves that can err by 50 percent or more. What the derivation offers is the shape any answer must have, four refuters written in advance, and the instruments that would close it. It does not say CDM is wrong, and it is not grounded.
