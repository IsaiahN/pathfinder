# Outreach draft — check the per-slot claim before you decide about the mechanism

**Use:** one-to-one, or as a cover note attached to a submission through the public venues in `03_public_contact_points.csv`. **Not** for bulk send.

**Hard precondition:** send only after the inference check (F4) has been stated honestly. If you present an inferred inner slope as a directly measured dark-matter profile, the two-stage offer collapses on contact.

---

**Subject:** One checkable claim about dwarf-galaxy centers, before any theory

> Hi [name],
>
> I have one specific output I would like you to check against your own knowledge of galaxy centers. I am not asking you to read a framework first, and I am not asking you to agree with anything. If the output does not survive, there is nothing else to look at.
>
> **The output (`specified`, not `grounded`):** the residual between prediction and observation in this question is indexed by object, not averaged. In many low-mass, dark-matter-dominated galaxies, the central density inferred from gas kinematics is approximately constant, and the enclosed mass inside the inner few hundred parsecs is lower than a collisionless cold-dark-matter halo predicts. In some massive dwarfs and at least one dwarf-sized ultra-diffuse galaxy, the inferred inner profile is cuspy and consistent with the predicted profile. So the honest answer to "do observations match the dense profiles" is: match in some slots, mismatch in many low-mass slots. A single yes or no is the wrong shape of answer.
>
> **What I am not claiming:** that this settles whether cold dark matter is correct. That baryonic feedback is the answer. That the inferred profile is a direct measurement of dark matter; it is a reading of a potential, and simulations of rotation-curve reconstruction show errors of 50 percent or more from non-circular motions and non-thermal stresses. That all dwarfs are cored. Those claims are withheld.
>
> **What would refute the shape claim, written before I sent this:**
> 1. An independent mass measurement (stellar kinematics, lensing, or proper motions, not the same gas rotation curve) showing that low-mass dwarfs at fixed maximum rotation speed have inner slopes consistent with the predicted cusp.
> 2. A hydrodynamical simulation that reproduces the observed inner mass deficits at fixed maximum rotation speed using observed star-formation histories, with convergence and resolution tests reported.
> 3. A demonstration that the rotation-curve inferred profiles agree with independent mass probes in the same objects, which would close the measurement-channel worry.
> 4. A demonstration that this is one hard question rather than a compound of dark-matter physics, baryonic response and measurement inference.
>
> If any of these holds, I withdraw the corresponding claim. A one-line "this fails because..." is more useful to me than agreement. If you would rather not engage, no reply is needed.
>
> **If it survives, and only then:** here are the sources I actually read. The papers and the figures, not my summary, are the authority.
> - de Blok, "The Core-Cusp Problem" (arXiv:0910.3538): https://arxiv.org/abs/0910.3538
> - Navarro, Frenk and White, "A Universal Density Profile from Hierarchical Clustering" (arXiv:astro-ph/9611107): https://arxiv.org/abs/astro-ph/9611107
> - Oman et al., "The unexpected diversity of dwarf galaxy rotation curves" (arXiv:1504.01437): https://arxiv.org/abs/1504.01437
> - Pontzen and Governato, "How supernova feedback turns dark matter cusps into cores" (arXiv:1106.0499): https://arxiv.org/abs/1106.0499
> - Sands et al. summary via the FIRE project, on rotation-curve limits: https://fire.northwestern.edu/2024/06/18/confronting-the-diversity-problem-the-limits-of-galaxy-rotation-curves-as-a-tool-to-understand-dark-matter-profiles
>

Write down what would refute your result *before* you run it. A reported failure is more useful to me than a reported success.

## Stage 2 and stage 3 (opt-in only)

- **Stage 2:** only after the recipient's verdict on stage 1 is recorded, offer the figure set and the loop. If they have already absorbed the framework, their agreement stops being evidence (Fig 2 :40). Never pool it as corroboration.
- **Stage 3:** offer the loop as a reusable method, with the instruction that each user writes their own refuter and states their own mode. The loop cannot check the direction of its own question (Formula, "Direction").

**Record to keep per recipient:** the residual as they restated it, their refuter, their verdict on stage 1, the date, and whether they opted in to stage 2. That is provenance for a correction, not a tally of agreement (Fig 10 :62-65).
