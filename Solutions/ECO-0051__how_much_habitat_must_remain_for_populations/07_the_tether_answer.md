# The Tether's answer on habitat requirements

**Mode: `specified` - not `grounded`.** This is a shape and a route, not a result. Simulated derivation checked against the figures (`05_what_shape_means.md` §8), not a ground reading.

## 1. The residual it answers

The question asks for a single standing amount of habitat that will keep populations and ecological interactions persisting over the long term. The gap is between a proposed amount and what actually happens at the ground: a population persists or disappears, an interaction persists or disappears. It is not one gap. It is indexed by population, by interaction, by configuration and by regime, so it is a **union compound** with its own loop, not one hard question (Fig 9 :54-55).

## 2. The shape

1. **Habitat is enumerated, never composed.** Fig 11 :43: *"The habitat is enumerated, never composed."* Fig 11 :44: *"List everything in contact with the residual, then what is in contact with those, and outward until the cascade stops mattering."* The first thing an answer must give is a contact list, not a percentage.

2. **Population and interaction verdicts are separate slots.** Fig 11 :47 names *"relations: pollination, predation, competition, dependency"* as part of the habitat. Fig 1 :19: *"The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated."* A single habitat percentage averages the slots and loses the signal that says where to look.

3. **Valence is relative to the residual.** Fig 11 :49: *"An actor's valence is relative to the residual, not a property it carries."* The same hectare can help a population and fail an interaction. No standing list of good habitat survives a change of residual, so no universal percentage does either.

4. **Contact cascades beyond direct neighbours.** Fig 11 :52: *"And contact cascades. Scoping the habitat to direct contact is how DDT reached the eagles"* A first-order area enumeration misses the residual that mattered, which was three steps out. The answer must name the depth counted.

5. **Equal area is not equal habitat.** Fig 12 :117: *"isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."* Two landscapes of the same area and different arrangement are different things, not variants of one thing (see `06_isomers_and_order.md`).

6. **Two regimes; "over the long term" splits.** Unmaintained: Fig 11 :56 *"Unmaintained. No maintainer and no live frame in the seat. The habitat is pure contact, nothing selects, and what survives is what the ground kept."* Maintained: Fig 11 :61 *"The seat's power is asymmetric. Introduction is available; exclusion is not."* and Fig 11 :63 *"Which is why a managed ecosystem is permanent work rather than a solved state."* Selection and permanent work are different answers to the same phrase.

7. **Isolation substitutes a habitat, it does not remove one.** Fig 11 :66: *"Isolation is not removal of the habitat. It is substitution of one habitat for another."* Offsets, translocations and captive fragments inherit actors nobody chose and must reproduce contacts that were not named.

8. **The framework returns no number.** Fig 9 :32: *"you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself."* A numeric threshold is ground-settled per species and per landscape (Hanski 2011), and is an import with provenance, not a figure derivative. The literal numeric part of the question is `gap` at the figure level.

## 3. What it explicitly does not say

- Not a universal habitat percentage. No figure line returns one, and Fig 1 :19 forbids aggregating the slots.
- Not that the framework's habitat is the ecological niche. The canonical article declines that mapping explicitly; the definitions do not align.
- Not that interactions are reducible to species counts or to area. They are structure-governed (network) and are lost before the species (Lurgi 2019).
- Not that a habitat offset removes habitat. It substitutes one.
- Not that this is a measurement. Every substantive claim is `specified` or `general`; nothing is `grounded` here.
- Not a final answer. There is no last room; the ground keeps deciding as habitats and residuals change.

## 4. What would settle it - the ground

A better discriminator, not a larger review. Fig 2 :29: *"An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks."* The discriminating questions are:

- **Per slot:** does a given persistence criterion predict the observed fate of the focal population and of the focal interaction separately? A criterion that works for one and not the other is a failed criterion, not a rounded number.
- **Cascade depth:** does the effect appear at second or third order where a first-order area measure predicts none? The DDT case supplies the pattern; occupancy and interaction data supply the test.
- **Arrangement:** do two landscapes of equal area and equal species lists differ in persistence as the isomer claim predicts? A result that removes the difference would fire the falsifier in `06_isomers_and_order.md`.
- **Threshold:** for a named species in a named landscape, does a ground-settled extinction threshold exist and hold across years? Hanski 2011 supplies the method and the caveat that no universal answer exists.
- Standing falsifiers from `01_figure_grounded_audit.md` §4: a universal percentage that works across taxa and interactions; intrinsic valence; no cascade; managed persistence as a solved state; isolation as removal.

## 5. The honest stop

**Unreached, not unreachable.** The framework gives the form of the answer and stops where the instruments stop. Fig 8 :39 says *"So a search that finds nothing is never a proof of absence."* So the absence of a universal number in the figures is not proof that no such number could exist; it is a report that no figure line returns one and that the world literature itself reports per-case thresholds and no universal factor ranking. The lineage is recorded so the stop is checkable. Indiscernibility is not identity.

## 6. One-paragraph version for outreach

> The Tether does not tell you what percentage of habitat to protect, and it does not claim there is one. It says habitat is enumerated, never composed: for a named population or a named interaction, you list what is in contact with it and then what is in contact with those, outward until the cascade stops mattering. The same hectare can help one residual and harm another, so population and interaction answers must be kept separate and never averaged into a single number. Equal area is not equal habitat, because arrangement carries the difference the formula omits. And "over the long term" means two different things: unmaintained habitat is selection, while maintained habitat is permanent work. What the framework offers is the shape any habitat answer must have, falsifiers written in advance, and the kinds of ground reading that would close it. A universal number is not refused as impossible; it is simply not returned, and the literature's own thresholds are per species and per landscape.
