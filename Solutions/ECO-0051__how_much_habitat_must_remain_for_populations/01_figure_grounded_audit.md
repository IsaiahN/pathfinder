## 0. The question under audit, restated as an effect

> "How much habitat must remain for populations and ecological interactions to persist over the long term?"

Stated in effect terms: we are asked to name a standing quantity H such that, at some later time, (a) a focal population is still viable and (b) a focal ecological interaction still occurs. Each option leads somewhere different. A single number H offered in advance treats habitat as a scalar stock. An enumerated contact field treats habitat as a per-residual description that the ground settles. The framework's own instruction for a searched-for shape is to bind it as an ordinary term before the run: *"make the sought-for shape an ORDINARY TERM - write down what an answer would look like, bind it, and let it accrue a residual like anything else."*

**The shape bound before the run:** *an answer to this question would name, for a specified residual, the contacts and conditions that must persist, the depth of the cascade that must be counted, and the mode (unmaintained or maintained) under which persistence is claimed.* A bare percentage does not have this shape. It is a number, not a description, and it accrues no residual against the question's two residuals.

**R (the residual).** The gap between a claimed amount of habitat and what actually happens at the ground: persistence or loss of a population, persistence or loss of an interaction. R is not one scalar gap. It is indexed by which population and which interaction, and by which mode.

## 1. Domain census before choosing any figure line

Describe R by arity, symmetry and scale first; then one line per domain with actors, currency and anchor; then partition or union; then dependency order.

- **Arity.** At least two conditions: population persistence and interaction persistence. A third distinction runs through both: unmaintained versus maintained. This is a **union**, not one hard question.
- **Symmetry.** Habitat amount is treated as symmetric and scalar in the question (more is better; one number serves all). Under Fig 11 the relation is asymmetric and per-residual: the same actor can help one residual and harm another. The question's symmetry assumption is the first thing to test, not a premise.
- **Scale.** Local patch, landscape, region, and time. Contact cascades across scale, and the response of species to loss is often delayed, so "over the long term" is itself a scale choice.

| domain | actors | currency | anchor | nameable? |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| D1 Population persistence, unmaintained | individuals, populations, habitat patches, dispersers, matrix; conditions of light, water, temperature, season, terrain | occupancy, viable population size, extinction threshold | censused presence or absence; the ground keeps what survives (Fig 11 :56) | yes |
| D2 Interaction persistence | species pairs and networks: pollination, predation, competition, dependency | occurrence and strength of the interaction | observed interaction, often lost before the species (Fig 11 :47) | yes |
| D3 Spatial configuration and connectivity | patches, corridors, matrix, barriers, dispersers | connectivity, metapopulation capacity, arrangement | colonisation and extinction data; threshold depends on configuration | yes |
| D4 Maintained habitat (the garden) | a maintainer seat, introduced actors, restoration actions | introductions (actions), not area alone | the ground still decides; permanent work (Fig 11 :61-63) | yes |
| D5 Isolation and substitution | a moved residual, a new habitat, inherited actors | reproduction of the contacts the goal required | whether the goal still fails (Fig 11 :66-70) | yes |

All five have nameable anchors, so all five are domains. None is admitted on a metaphor alone.

**Partition or union.** D1 and D2 overlap: a population in a landscape participates in interactions, and the same hectare is a different habitat for each residual. D3 cuts across both, and D4 and D5 are regimes rather than separate places. Fig 9 :54: *"And when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again."* Fig 9 :55: *"A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones."* So this is a **union with its own loop**; the verdicts are kept separate and never averaged.

**Dependency order (Fig 3).** First enumerate habitat (perception, link 1). Second, have terms for what a landscape affords: area, quality, connectivity, cascade depth (vocabulary, link 2). Third, say what counts as persistence for this residual (objective, link 3). Fourth, plan. Fifth, learn and carry. The question "how much" states link 3 before links 1 and 2 exist. Fig 3 :19: *"Optimising a link that already works cannot rescue one that does not."* Fig 3 :27: *"A link with no instrument is not a link that works. It is a link nobody has looked at."*

## 3. Findings

### F1 - The question is a union dressed as one number (decisive for the question's form)

Fig 11 :34-35: *"What is intelligence? The wrong question, and this figure is the replacement for it."* The same move applies here. "How much habitat?" is the wrong question form; the replacement is "which contacts must persist, for which residual, and how deep does the cascade go?"

Fig 9 :54: *"And when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again."*
Fig 9 :55: *"A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones."*
Fig 1 :19: *"The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated."*
Fig 1 :22: *"Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average."*

A single percentage aggregates the population slot and the interaction slot. That is exactly how the live signal that says where to look is lost. **Verdict:** the literal demand (one number) is not answerable in this form. The answerable form is per-residual. Marked `triangulated` (Fig 11 plus Fig 1 plus Fig 9, low overlap between the habitat figure, the per-slot rule and the union rule).

### F2 - Habitat is enumerated, never composed; the currency is contact, not area

Fig 11 :43: *"The habitat is enumerated, never composed."*
Fig 11 :44: *"List everything in contact with the residual, then what is in contact with those, and outward until the cascade stops mattering."*
Fig 11 :46: *"conditions: light, water, temperature, season, terrain"*
Fig 11 :47: *"relations: pollination, predation, competition, dependency"*
Fig 11 :48: *"You do not invent the list. You read it off the world, and what you cannot perceive or measure yet is the residual."*
Fig 11 :73: *"The habitat is not the ground. The habitat is where actors come from and it updates; the ground is what settles, and it does not."*

So habitat is not a stock that can be read off a map. It is the enumerated field of contact with R. Area is one predicate among many; the list is read off the world, not composed from theory. The distinction between habitat and ground matters: the habitat updates, the anchor does not. **Verdict:** `triangulated` (Fig 11 plus Fig 1 :23 "The ground enters as the only metric"; the habitat/ground distinction is load-bearing in both).

### F3 - Valence is relative to the residual, so no standing number survives a change of residual

Fig 11 :49: *"An actor's valence is relative to the residual, not a property it carries."*
Fig 11 :50: *"The same actor is a benefactor to one residual and a malfactor to another, so mutualism and parasitism are relations rather than kinds."*
Fig 11 :51: *"No standing list of good actors survives a change of residual."*

Apply the strain test. A proposed habitat target can be fitted to a population (say, a forest specialist) and fail for an interaction (say, a pollination link that needs the matrix as well as the patch). If the line only fits by assuming one residual, it governs that slot, not "habitat" generally. Re-census confirms: it governs D1 and D2 separately. There is no single "good habitat" list that covers both. **Verdict:** `triangulated` (Fig 11 valence rule plus Fig 1 per-slot rule).

### F4 - Contact cascades; first-order enumeration misses the residual that matters

Fig 11 :52: *"And contact cascades. Scoping the habitat to direct contact is how DDT reached the eagles"*
Fig 11 :54: *"Carson, Silent Spring, 1962. The mechanism, biomagnification through the food chain to eggshell thinning, was established over the following decade."*

A habitat measurement scoped to direct contact (hectares of patch) is a first-order enumeration. The effect that decides persistence can be third order. This is not a side example; the figure uses it to define how far enumeration must go. World corroboration from Lurgi 2019: interactions change *"well before extinctions are detected"*, which is the same causal shape in a different field. **Verdict:** `triangulated` (Fig 11 cascade plus the ecology source named in §4).

### F5 - Two modes; "over the long term" means different things in each

Fig 11 :56: *"Unmaintained. No maintainer and no live frame in the seat. The habitat is pure contact, nothing selects, and what survives is what the ground kept."*
Fig 11 :61: *"The seat's power is asymmetric. Introduction is available; exclusion is not."*
Fig 11 :63: *"Which is why a managed ecosystem is permanent work rather than a solved state."*
Fig 11 :65: *"Introducing an actor for its cascade is ecosystem engineering, after Jones, Lawton and Shachak, 1994."*

In D1 (unmaintained), long-term persistence is selection; there is no maintainer and the ground keeps what survives. In D4 (maintained), long-term persistence is continuous work; the seat can add actors but cannot subtract the habitat, because contact does not consult it. A single habitat target cannot cover both modes, because one is a one-off stock question and the other is a permanent practice. Fig 11 :40: *"An improvement that does not change contact changes nothing, however much it improves."* **Verdict:** `single-source` for the regime distinction (Fig 11 only; no second figure governs it).

### F6 - Isolation substitutes a habitat; it does not remove one

Fig 11 :66: *"Isolation is not removal of the habitat. It is substitution of one habitat for another."*
Fig 11 :67: *"Move the residual and you must reproduce everything its goal required, and you inherit the new habitat's actors, which you did not choose."*
Fig 11 :70: *"A test harness is a substituted habitat. Which is why a synthetic solve proves wiring and never capability."*

This governs habitat banking, offsets, translocation and captive breeding fragments of the question. The failure has two invisible halves: what you failed to reproduce shows up only when the goal fails, and what you unintentionally introduced shows up only when it acts. **Verdict:** `single-source` (Fig 11 only).

### F7 - The number ecology reports is a ground-settled, per-residual threshold, not a universal constant

Fig 2 :29: *"An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks."*
Fig 9 :32: *"you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself"*
Fig 9 :60: *"Use filters for the budget and witnesses for the verdict. Never let a filter hand you a verdict."*

World sources do report numbers: Hanski 2011 defines an extinction threshold as *"the critical minimum amount of habitat that is necessary for long-term persistence of metapopulations"*, and reports species-specific cases (roughly 20% forest cover for specialist small mammals in one landscape, a metapopulation-capacity threshold for a butterfly in another) while stating that *"there cannot be a universal answer to the question"* of which factor matters most. These are ground readings of a **different quantity**: a per-species, per-landscape threshold settled by data. They are witnesses for that quantity, not for a Tether-derived constant. Under Fig 9 :60, the fact that a threshold exists is a filter on where to look, not a verdict handed to this question. The figure level for "one universal number" is `gap`: Fig 8 :39 says *"So a search that finds nothing is never a proof of absence."* **Verdict:** `triangulated` for "ground-settled per slot"; `gap` for a universal number.

### F8 - The chain breaks at the objective, not at the area measurement

Fig 3 :25-26: *"having terms for what a situation offers and being able to say what would count as progress"*
Fig 3 :27: *"A link with no instrument is not a link that works. It is a link nobody has looked at."*

Habitat area has obvious instruments. What is missing is link 3: a stated account of what counts as persistence for this residual, in its own terms, before the number is chosen. Most effort lands above the break, where the measurements already exist. This is why the question can be debated for decades as a percentage debate. **Verdict:** `triangulated` (Fig 3 plus Fig 9 :27-28 *"describe it before you go looking, or any frame you pick will seem to fit"*).

### F10 - Equal area is not equal habitat

Fig 12 :5: *"A set of ingredients is not one. The bond is the rest of the information, and it is the half nothing was recording."*
Fig 12 :94: *"The arrangement is the substance."*
Fig 12 :117: *"isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."*

Two landscapes with the same area can be different substances if the arrangement differs. The "how much" formula records the ingredients and not the bond, so nothing downstream can tell them apart and nothing signals that anything was lost. Developed in `06_isomers_and_order.md`. **Verdict:** `triangulated` (Fig 12 plus Fig 11 cascade; the percolation threshold source is a secondary, model-level corroboration).

## 4. Pre-registered refuter

Written before the conclusion, per the standing instruction that a falsifier written before the run is a design instrument. The shape claim is refuted if any of the following holds:

1. A single habitat percentage, stated without naming a residual, predicts persistence across independent taxa and independent interaction types at the ground (breaks F1 and F3).
2. An actor's valence is demonstrated to be intrinsic to the actor, so the same actor is a benefactor regardless of which residual is in question (breaks Fig 11 :49-51).
3. A habitat effect is shown to be strictly first-order in a case where the figure predicts a cascade, with no additional effect at second or third order (breaks Fig 11 :52-54).
4. Managed persistence is demonstrated as a solved state needing no further work (breaks Fig 11 :63).
5. An isolation or offset is shown to remove rather than substitute a habitat, so nothing new is inherited and nothing needs reproduction (breaks Fig 11 :66-67).

If any fires, the claim is withdrawn. Until one is run, no part of this may be labelled `grounded`.

## 5. Recommendation

1. Lead with F1 and F2: the question is a union and habitat is enumerated. Do not open with a percentage.
2. Split before searching: population slot, interaction slot, configuration, regime. Keep their verdicts separate and never average them (F1, F3).
3. State what counts as persistence for each slot before choosing any number (F8).
4. Follow contact out past direct neighbours and name the depth counted (F4).
5. Treat published thresholds as ground-settled imports with provenance, not as figure derivatives (F7, Fig 8 :50-52).
6. Send the shape claim and its refuter, not the framework: Fig 10 :52 *"Bring a characterised residual, never the source."*
