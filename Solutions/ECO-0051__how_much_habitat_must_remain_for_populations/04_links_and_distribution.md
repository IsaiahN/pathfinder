## What the wider literature bears on the claim

1. **A number exists, but it is per species and per landscape.** Hanski 2011 gives extinction thresholds, the 30 percent / 10 percent findings from Andrén's review, and a roughly 20 percent forest-cover threshold for specialist small mammals, while insisting there is no universal answer to which factor matters most. This corroborates the per-slot reading and conflicts with any single constant. It does not hand over a verdict, because it is a ground reading of a different quantity (Fig 9 :60).
2. **Interactions are a separate residual.** Lurgi 2019 reports that interactions are lost independently and more concealed than species, and change well before extinctions are detected. This corroborates F3 and F4 in `01_figure_grounded_audit.md`.
3. **A threshold is model-relative.** The percolation threshold exists only relative to an occupation rule and a lattice. This is a low-overlap, model-level corroboration that "how much" has an answer only inside a specified frame. It is secondary and does not decide the ecological question.

All three sources are corroboration only. They were opened and quoted, and each is recorded in `10_source_ledger.csv` with author, venue, year, accessed date, quote, field and overlap.
