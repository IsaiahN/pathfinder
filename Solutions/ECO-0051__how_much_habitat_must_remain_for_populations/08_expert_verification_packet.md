# Expert verification packet - the Tether on habitat requirements

**What this is:** a claim in a form a habitat researcher or conservation biologist can check. Mode, derivation and refuters are stated before the conclusion, so a reader knows what to look for and what would settle it.

**Mode: `specified` - not `grounded`.** No habitat threshold is claimed as measured by this derivation. This is a shape and a route, not a number.

**Status:** a simulated derivation checked for internal consistency against the figures, not a ground reading. It cannot settle the question it reports on.

## 1. The claim, in one paragraph

The question "how much habitat must remain for populations and ecological interactions to persist?" bundles at least two residuals and two regimes. The figure set returns a shape rather than a number: habitat is enumerated for a named residual (actors, conditions, relations, then what contacts those, outward until the cascade stops mattering); population and interaction verdicts are separate and never averaged because valence is relative to the residual; equal area is not equal habitat because arrangement carries the difference; "over the long term" splits into unmaintained selection and maintained permanent work; and isolation substitutes a habitat rather than removing one. Published thresholds are ground-settled per species and per landscape and enter only as provenanced imports. The framework gives no universal percentage, and this packet does not either.

## 2. Three checkable sub-claims

### C1 - framework-internal and ecology-facing; checkable by reading Fig 11 and comparing with your own practice

- **Statement:** habitat must be enumerated per named residual; a scalar area figure is not the answer shape.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 11 :43 - *"The habitat is enumerated, never composed."* Fig 11 :44 - *"List everything in contact with the residual, then what is in contact with those, and outward until the cascade stops mattering."* Fig 1 :19 - *"The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated."*
- **What to check:** when you specify habitat requirements for a species or interaction, do you in fact enumerate conditions, actors and relations, and does an area-only summary lose information that changes the conclusion? If it does not, the claim is weak for your case.
- **Refuter:** a single habitat percentage, stated without naming a residual, predicts persistence across independent taxa and independent interaction types at the ground.

### C2 - framework-internal; checkable by reading Fig 11 against the two residuals in the question

- **Statement:** population persistence and interaction persistence are separate slots. The same actor can help one and harm the other, so there is no standing "good habitat" list and no single number covering both.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 11 :47 - *"relations: pollination, predation, competition, dependency"*; Fig 11 :49 - *"An actor's valence is relative to the residual, not a property it carries."*; Fig 11 :50 - *"The same actor is a benefactor to one residual and a malfactor to another, so mutualism and parasitism are relations rather than kinds."*; Fig 11 :51 - *"No standing list of good actors survives a change of residual."*
- **What to check:** can you name a case where the amount of habitat that holds a population is not the amount that holds one of its interactions? Lurgi 2019 reports that interactions are lost independently and well before extinctions are detected. If your data show both always move together, the separation claim fails for that system.
- **Refuter:** an actor's valence is intrinsic to the actor, demonstrated by the same actor benefiting every residual regardless of the question; or population and interaction persistence always share one threshold across independent cases.

### C3 - framework-internal and management-facing; checkable by reading Fig 11

- **Statement:** contact must be followed beyond direct neighbours; unmaintained and maintained persistence are different answers; isolation substitutes a habitat.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 11 :52 - *"And contact cascades. Scoping the habitat to direct contact is how DDT reached the eagles"*; Fig 11 :56 - *"Unmaintained. No maintainer and no live frame in the seat. The habitat is pure contact, nothing selects, and what survives is what the ground kept."*; Fig 11 :61 - *"The seat's power is asymmetric. Introduction is available; exclusion is not."*; Fig 11 :63 - *"Which is why a managed ecosystem is permanent work rather than a solved state."*; Fig 11 :66 - *"Isolation is not removal of the habitat. It is substitution of one habitat for another."*
- **What to check:** does your case show a second- or third-order effect that a direct-contact area measure missed? Is the persistence you are counting the outcome of selection or of ongoing management? Does an offset or translocation inherit actors and contacts nobody chose?
- **Refuter:** a habitat effect demonstrated as strictly first-order where the claim predicts a cascade; managed persistence demonstrated as a solved state needing no further work; an isolation shown to remove rather than substitute a habitat.

## 3. What is explicitly not claimed

- Not a universal habitat percentage, and not a Tether-derived threshold of any size.
- Not that the framework's habitat is the ecological niche. The canonical article declines that mapping: the definitions do not align.
- Not that interactions reduce to species counts, or that area is irrelevant. Area is one predicate in the enumeration.
- Not that habitat offsets cannot work. The claim is that they substitute a habitat and inherit unchosen actors, with two failure modes that stay invisible until the goal fails or the actor acts.
- Not `grounded`. Nothing in this packet was settled by measurement in this derivation.

## 5. What would settle it

A ground reading, not agreement. The discriminators are:

1. Do per-slot persistence criteria predict the observed fate of the population and the interaction **separately**, across independent cases?
2. Does an effect appear at second or third order where a first-order area measure predicts none?
3. Do two landscapes of equal area and equal species and interaction lists differ in persistence as the isomer claim predicts?
4. For a named species in a named landscape, does a threshold hold across years, and is it reported with its species and landscape attached?

A failure on any standing refuter in `01_figure_grounded_audit.md` §4 outweighs any agreement with this packet.

## 6. What an expert can check in twenty minutes

1. Read Fig 11 :43-44, :49-52, :56, :61-66 and Fig 1 :19. Nine short passages.
2. Say whether your own habitat-requirements work enumerates contacts or states an area, and which one actually predicts your outcomes.
3. Name one case from your data where population persistence and interaction persistence came apart, or say you know of none.
4. Name one instrument that would distinguish arrangement from area in your system, or say why none is available.
5. Send the refutation. A failure is more useful than an endorsement.
