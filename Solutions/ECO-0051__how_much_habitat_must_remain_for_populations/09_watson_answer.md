## QUESTION

Decide, in effect terms, what the Tether says about the question: how much habitat must remain for populations and ecological interactions to persist over the long term? Option A: answer with a single universal habitat percentage, taking the ecology literature's headline thresholds as the result. Option B: keep the figure-derived shape and refuse the scalar. What each leads to: A aggregates two residuals across two regimes into a number no figure line returns, and it promotes ground-settled per-species thresholds into a constant; B sends a checkable per-residual shape, written refuters, and an honest `gap` where the number would be.

## DOMAIN CENSUS

R by arity, symmetry and scale, then one line per domain with actors / currency / anchor, partition or union, and order by dependency.

- **R (the residual).** The gap between a claimed habitat amount and what happens at the ground: a population persists or disappears; an interaction persists or disappears. Arity: at least two conditions plus two regimes, so a **union compound** (Fig 9 :55: *"A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones."*). Symmetry: the question assumes area is scalar and symmetric, while the figures make valence relative to the residual. Scale: patch, landscape, region and delayed time, with contact cascading across scale.
- **D1, population persistence, unmaintained.** Actors: individuals, populations, patches, dispersers, matrix, conditions of light, water, temperature, season, terrain. Currency: occupancy, viable population size, extinction threshold. Anchor: censused presence or absence; what survives is what the ground kept. Union member with D2.
- **D2, interaction persistence.** Actors: species pairs and networks: pollination, predation, competition, dependency. Currency: occurrence and strength of the interaction. Anchor: observed interaction, often lost before the species. Union member with D1.
- **D3, spatial configuration.** Actors: patches, corridors, barriers, dispersers. Currency: connectivity, arrangement, metapopulation capacity. Anchor: colonisation and extinction data; thresholds depend on configuration. Cross-cuts D1 and D2.
- **D4, maintained habitat.** Actors: a maintainer seat, introduced actors, restoration actions. Currency: introductions (actions), not area alone. Anchor: the ground still decides; permanent work. Regime, not a place.
- **D5, isolation and substitution.** Actors: a moved residual, a new habitat, inherited actors. Currency: reproduction of the contacts the goal required. Anchor: whether the goal still fails. Regime, not a place.
- **Order by dependency.** Enumerate habitat for each residual, acquire terms for what the landscape affords, state what counts as persistence for that residual, then plan, then carry (Fig 3 :20). The question states the objective as a number before the vocabulary exists.

Strain test applied before choosing figure lines: the ecology literature supplies numbers while Fig 11 supplies no number. Re-census shows these govern different quantities, a ground-settled per-species threshold and the shape of the answer, so there is no strain (see VERDICT and `01_figure_grounded_audit.md` §6).

## VERDICT (from the figures)

The shape survives; the scalar does not. Habitat is enumerated per residual, population and interaction verdicts stay separate, contact is followed past direct neighbours, equal area is not equal habitat, unmaintained and maintained regimes are different answers, and isolation substitutes rather than removes. The framework returns no universal habitat percentage, and the ecology literature it was tempting to quote reports ground-settled per-species and per-landscape thresholds while stating there is no universal answer to which factor matters most. The ecological niche mapping is declined by the canonical article. Every substantive claim is `specified`; the numeric part is `gap` at the figure level. This is a candidate shape, not a settled result.

- **cost if wrong:** bounded and locatable. The packet states its mode, writes four refuters before the conclusion and records the lineage, so an error is findable rather than invisible. The real cost is spending the independence of outside readers by sending a number as if the framework produced it. Fig 2 :42 says *"Buy independence in proportion to the cost of being wrong."* That cost is avoidable by leading with the shape claim and its refuter. If the shape is wrong, the falsifiers in `01_figure_grounded_audit.md` §4 are specific enough to fire.
- **undoable:** yes. The scalar reading is removable without touching the figure derivation; `07_the_tether_answer.md` and `08_expert_verification_packet.md` already remove it. A sent claim can be superseded by the corrected packet, and no primitive has to be withdrawn. An offset error is harder to undo in the world, which is why the substitution rule is stated before the number.
- **reversed by:** a ground reading, not agreement. A single percentage, stated without a residual, that predicts persistence across independent taxa and interaction types would break the shape; intrinsic valence, a strictly first-order effect where a cascade is predicted, or managed persistence as a solved state would each break a specific finding. A per-species threshold that holds across years and landscapes for a named residual would settle the numeric part for that residual and only that one. Until one of these fires, the verdict stands as `candidate`.
