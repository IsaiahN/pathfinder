# Outreach draft: check the shape before you decide about the number

**Use:** one-to-one, or as a cover letter attached to a submission through the public venues in `03_public_contact_points.csv`. **Not** for bulk send.

**Hard precondition:** do not send until the four checks in `08_expert_verification_packet.md` §6 are ones you can answer from your own ground. The offer collapses on contact if the shape claim cannot survive a twenty-minute check.

---

**Subject:** Check one shape claim about habitat requirements before we argue about a percentage

> Hi [name],
>
> I have one specific output I would like you to check against what you already know. I am not asking you to read the framework first, and I am not asking you to agree with it. If the output does not survive, there is nothing else to look at.
>
> **The output (`specified`, not `grounded`):** the question "how much habitat must remain for populations and ecological interactions to persist?" bundles at least two residuals and two regimes. The framework's habitat figure says habitat is enumerated, never composed: you list everything in contact with the residual, then what contacts those, outward until the cascade stops mattering. Valence is relative to the residual, so the same actor can help one population and harm one interaction. There is no standing list of good actors that survives a change of residual. The figure returns that shape, not a number.
>
> So the claim I am offering is not "30 percent" or any other constant. It is: a habitat answer has to be per residual, has to name the contacts and the cascade depth counted, and has to say whether the regime is unmaintained (selection, what survives is what the ground kept) or maintained (permanent work; introduction is available, exclusion is not). Published thresholds are ground-settled per species and per landscape; they are evidence for that species and that landscape, not a universal constant delivered by the framework.
>
> **What I am not claiming:** that the framework returns a habitat percentage, that its "habitat" is the ecological niche, that isolation removes habitat rather than substituting one, or that this derivation is a measurement. It is a simulated derivation checked for internal consistency against the figures. The numeric part of the question is `gap` at the figure level.
>
> **What would refute the shape claim:**
> 1. A single habitat percentage, stated without naming a residual, predicts persistence across independent taxa and independent interaction types at the ground.
> 2. An actor's valence turns out to be intrinsic to the actor, so the same actor is a benefactor regardless of the residual.
> 3. A habitat effect is strictly first-order where the figure predicts a cascade.
> 4. Managed persistence is a solved state needing no further work.
>
> I wrote these down before sending this. If any holds, I retract the claim. A one-line "this fails because..." is more useful to me than agreement. If you would rather not engage, no reply is needed.
>
> **If it survives, and only then:** the sources are below. The figures are the authority, not my summary of them.
> - The figure text extract with line numbers (local framework file): `FRAMEWORK/reference/03-FIGURES_TEXT.md`, especially Fig 11 (The Habitat), Fig 1 (per-slot residual), Fig 9 (union before search) and Fig 12 (arrangement).
> - Canonical usage rule (local framework file): `FRAMEWORK/reference/FIGURES_README_canonical.md`.
> - Habitat Loss, the Dynamics of Biodiversity, and a Perspective on Conservation, Ilkka Hanski, Ambio 2011: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3357798
> - Habitat loss doesn't just affect species, it impacts networks of ecological relationships, Miguel Lurgi, The Conversation 2019: https://theconversation.com/habitat-loss-doesnt-just-affect-species-it-impacts-networks-of-ecological-relationships-117687
>
> **If you want to use the machinery:** the formula is in the figure folder. Run it on a problem you know well and can check against a ground. Write down what would refute your result *before* you run it, and state the mode of what you get. The framework cannot check the direction of its own question, and its own author warns that outputs can be contrived to fit the formula. A reported failure is more useful to me than a reported success.
>
> - [your name]
