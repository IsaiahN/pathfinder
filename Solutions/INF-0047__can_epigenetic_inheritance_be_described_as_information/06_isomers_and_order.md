### Q2 — Are "transmission" and "inherited state" isomers?

- Fig 12 :117: **"isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."**
- Fig 12 :94: **"The arrangement is the substance."**
- Over the shared atom set {mark, boundary, receiver, habitat}:
  - **Transmission** = `mark → receiver`, an ordered sequence bond. Operators :30: *"A then B; B needs A to have happened"*; symmetric column `no`.
  - **Persisting state** = `mark + receiver`, conjunction/co-presence, symmetric. Operators :12: *"both hold at once"*; Operators :15: *"swap the operands. Does the meaning change? No."*
  - **Head start** = `mark ∥ habitat` disjunction of sufficiency, *"either suffices; not both required"* (Operators :18), if either the mark or the habitat can set the offspring's starting conditions.
  - **Bias** = a weighting or threshold over outcomes, closer to `⋛` comparison (Operators :47-48: *"a threshold relation between two quantities"*) than to any copy bond.
- Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The two are **isomers** under Fig 12's definition. This is the strongest structural result available: it explains why a list of ingredients cannot settle the question and why the behaviour differs despite the identical formula.
- **Falsifier, written before the run:** if transmission can be written without an atom that state persistence requires (or vice versa), the isomer claim fails and the two are simply different molecules, not arrangements of one set.

### Q3 — Which order does each bond require?

- Fig 12 :111: **"A vocabulary without bonds can name a state and cannot name a route to one."**
- Fig 12 :112: **"A plan is a sequence. A goal is a comparison. Progress is a subtraction. A refutation is a negation, and that one is not a bond."**
- Transmission is `→`: ordered, the receiver needs the mark to have happened. Swapping the operands gives a different thing (Operators :33: *"swap them. Does it become a different thing? Yes."*).
- State is `+`: symmetric. Swapping gives the same term.
- A symmetric statistical association (SEP: *"First, correlational information is symmetrical: if A carries information about B, then B carries information about A."*) cannot generate the `→` order. Operators :59-61: *"Symmetry decides whether an arrangement is a new thing."* So the question's word "transmission" is an **ordering claim** smuggled into a symmetric observation, which is the structural form of the error in F3.
- **Bias is not a bond at all in the copy sense.** It is a prior weight (Symbols :23-24: *"How far the belief leans on private history versus inherited prior"*) or a comparison `⋛`. A system holding only copy bonds can say what was carried and cannot say that the receiving system was already disposed. That is the Fig 12 :111 bound at the middle of the question.
- **Falsifier:** an ordered intervention shown to be unnecessary, that is, a symmetric statistic alone settling transmission versus state, kills this order claim.

### Q4 — The analogues: catalyst, head start, functional group

- Fig 12 :119: **"catalyst. A part that survives its own use. Spent and regenerating parts behave differently across uses, and nothing records which a term is."** Structural templating in prions and ciliates (Wikipedia TEI; Wikipedia Epigenetics) is a catalyst: the template survives its own use.
- Fig 12 :121: **"cycle. The output re-enters as input. The sign of the loop decides whether it closes, and a chain cannot close by construction."** A self-sustaining methylation loop is a cycle. It is inherited without being sent.
- Fig 4 :22: **"Only methods cross up. Only head starts come down."** Fig 4 :24: **"downward: something that makes the next search cheaper. Never a replay of what worked once."** Maternally supplied mRNA or yolk hormone is a head start if it cheapens the offspring's search, independently of whether it is decoded.
- Fig 12 :124: **"functional group. A substructure whose presence fixes the outcome. The group says what kind; the qualifier says which member."** All four conditions share a functional group: a cross-boundary effect on offspring phenotype. The qualifier, the bond, decides whether that effect is transmission, state, head start or bias.
- Fig 12 :92: **"Symmetry is what decides whether an arrangement is a new thing."** Under `+` the four conditions would collapse to one term; under `→`, `⋛` and `∥` they are distinct. The question's difficulty is a notation problem as much as a biological one.
- **Falsifier:** if the biological cases cannot be sorted by bond type (every case behaves the same under mark-erase, habitat-substitution and receiver-variation), the functional-group reading fails and the four conditions are one phenomenon with four names.

## CONCLUSION

Transmission and inherited state are **isomers over the shared atom set**: same ingredients, different bond, different substance. Transmission requires the ordered `→`; state persistence takes the symmetric `+`; head start takes `∥`; bias takes a threshold/prior rather than a copy bond. A symmetric correlation cannot generate the order, so the word "information" in the question must be split before it can be used. The negative cases are a functional group with four qualifiers, not one negative case. Naming a molecular carrier remains a provenanced binding, not a forbidden invention and not a wall-moving import (Q1). All three structural claims carry falsifiers written before the run.
