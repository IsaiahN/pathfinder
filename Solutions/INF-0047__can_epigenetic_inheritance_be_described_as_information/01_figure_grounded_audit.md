## 0. The claim under audit

> Epigenetic inheritance (heritable change in gene expression or phenotype not due to DNA sequence change) can be described as information transmission from parent to offspring. What distinguishes it from inherited state or bias is that a message is carried and read, rather than a state merely persisting or a receiving system merely being predisposed.

Two readings are in play and must be kept separate:

- **Reading A (the question's own framing):** "information transmission" names a real difference. Something is encoded, sent across a generational boundary and decoded by a receiver. Inherited state and bias are the negative cases where no such message exists.
- **Reading B (the reduction offered by correlational accounts):** every parent-offspring resemblance carries mutual information in the Shannon sense, so "transmission" is just a name for the correlation. Inherited state and bias carry information too, so the distinction collapses.

The residual is not "which reading is popular." It is: **what instrument would discriminate a carried-and-read message from a persisting state or a biased receiver, given that all three produce the same observed reading?** That is a source-discrimination residual, and its answer must be a separator, not a definition.

## 1. Domain census (before any figure line is chosen)

### 1.1 R by arity, symmetry and scale

- **Arity.** Three conditions are named: transmission, state persistence, bias. The observed resemblance can occupy more than one at once. This is therefore a **union compound**, not a partition (Fig 9 :55: *"A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones."* Fig 9 :57: *"a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either."*). One lever clears the region where only one condition holds and leaves the intersections standing.
- **Symmetry.** "Transmission" is ordered: parent then offspring, sender then receiver. "State" is co-presence; "bias" is a weighting of prior outcomes. The Operators table separates these by symmetry: `→` is *"A then B; B needs A to have happened"* (Operators :30-31, symmetric column `no`), while `+` is *"both hold at once"* (Operators :12-13, symmetric `yes`). A symmetric correlation cannot choose the arrow. This is the first substantive finding, not a stylistic point.
- **Scale.** First order: a mark is copied into the germline or zygote (within-lineage, across a cell-generation boundary). Second order: a receiver in the offspring responds to the mark (across an organism-generation boundary). A third scale is the population/evolutionary one, where the resemblance changes the distribution of heritable variation (Uller et al. 2018; developmental bias). The three scales have different anchors and must not be averaged.

### 1.2 One line per domain: actors, currency, anchor

| domain | actors | currency | anchor (nameable?) | question type |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **D1 mechanism / inheritance** | methylation marks, histone states, small RNAs, prions, structural templates, DNMT1 and UHRF1, germline, zygote | molecular copies, mark persistence | **yes**: cross-fosting, embryo transfer, germline-specific erase, mark-rescue experiments | partition at first order (is a mark carried across the boundary?) |
| **D2 information / semantics** | sender, signal, channel, receiver, interpreter, code | mutual information (bits), semantic content, normativity | **yes**: an evolved, discriminating receiver response; an intervention that varies the mark and reads the receiver | ordered bond versus flat co-presence |
| **D3 development / bias / habitat** | developmental system, gene regulatory network, maternal effect, G and M matrices, habitat conditions | heritable phenotypic variation, propensity to vary | **yes**: mutation accumulation lines, experimental evolution, habitat substitution | union with D1 and D2 (bias can produce the same reading) |

All three domains have a nameable anchor, so all three are genuine domains. A domain with no anchor would be declared `gap` here rather than smuggled in.

### 1.3 Partition or union

Union. The three conditions overlap: a mark can be carried (D1), read by a receiver (D2) and land in a biased developmental system (D3) simultaneously. Reading the mark alone does not tell you which condition is doing the work. Fig 9 :30-31: *"your words are borrowed too, so describe the effect rather than the cause."*

## 3. Findings

### F1 — The mark is an operand; "transmission" is a bond claim (decisive, triangulated)

Fig 12 :5:

> **"A set of ingredients is not one. The bond is the rest of the information, and it is the half nothing was recording."**

Fig 12 :94:

> **"The arrangement is the substance."**

Fig 12 :46:

> **"A bond is the typed relation between two operands. There are seven, they are not interchangeable, and which one holds is not recoverable from the operands."**

A methyl group, a histone modification, a small RNA or a prion conformation is an operand. Saying that it was "transmitted" adds a typed relation between an upstream carrier and a downstream reader. That relation is exactly the half the ingredient list does not record. Fig 12 :111: *"A vocabulary without bonds can name a state and cannot name a route to one."*

Consequence: the question is not answered by listing inherited molecules. A list establishes a state; it cannot establish a route. The word "transmission" is a bond claim and must be priced and typed as one. Triangulated across Fig 12 (bond, arrangement, route) and the Operators table (`→` versus `+`), which have low overlap (one is composition law, one is operator table).

### F2 — The same reading has several possible sources: a metamerism problem (decisive, triangulated)

Fig 9 :30-31:

> **"your words are borrowed too, so describe the effect rather than the cause"**

Fig 12 :46 again: which bond holds is not recoverable from the operands. Figure 13 :129:

> **"Nothing about a composed term tells you, from looking at it, which route produced it or whether that route was available."**

The observed offspring phenotype does not say whether the route was a sent-and-read message, a persisting state, or a biased receiving system. These are three sources of one reading. The world source makes the same point in instrument terms: an instrument that reads green *"cannot comment on the source"* (*The Hard Problem is About Source*, carried canonical quote); *"If several paths create the same behavior, then behavior is a projection with lossy information."* The corresponding discriminator here is an experiment that breaks the mark while holding habitat fixed, and breaks habitat while holding the mark fixed. Until one runs, the source is unassigned. This is the central reason the answer is a separator and not a definition.

Triangulated across Fig 9 (describe effect not cause), Fig 12 (bond not recoverable from operands), Fig 13 (route not recoverable from artefact) and the SEP entry on biological information (low-overlap world witness).

### F3 — "Information" in the correlational sense cannot do the distinguishing work (triangulated)

The Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy entry on biological information (2024) states the baseline characterization: information is *"asymmetric, normative, and non-ubiquitous"*, and then notes that Shannon mutual information has none of these features: *"First, correlational information is symmetrical: if A carries information about B, then B carries information about A."* It is also *"ubiquitous"*, so almost any physical system carries a large amount of it.

The figures say the same thing in their own notation. Operators :12-15 (`+`, symmetric, *"swap the operands. Does the meaning change? No."`) versus Operators :30-33 (`→`, asymmetric, *"swap them. Does it become a different thing? Yes."*). A parent-offspring correlation is a `+` fact; a transmission claim is a `→` claim. You cannot derive an ordered bond from a symmetric co-occurrence, and Fig 12 :111 says a state is nameable where a route is not.

The SEP entry also reports the two positions that matter here: Jablonka's account, where *"information is conferred by the interpreter"* and an entity carries information when a receiver has an evolved response to it; and Shea's, where *"only inheritance systems can carry semantic information as part of their function."* Both make the receiver, not the mark, the seat of the distinction. That is a low-overlap corroboration of F1 and F2, not a figure line.

### F4 — Inherited state and bias are two different figure structures, not one negative case

The question lumps "state or bias" together. The figures separate them.

- **Inherited state** is a persisting part or a closed loop. Fig 12 :119: *"catalyst. A part that survives its own use."* Fig 12 :121: *"cycle. The output re-enters as input. The sign of the loop decides whether it closes, and a chain cannot close by construction."* A prion template or a self-sustaining methylation loop is a catalyst/cycle: it survives its own use and re-enters. It is inherited without being a message.
- **A head start** crosses a boundary without being a replay. Fig 4 :22: *"Only methods cross up. Only head starts come down."* Fig 4 :24: *"downward: something that makes the next search cheaper. Never a replay of what worked once."* A maternally supplied mRNA or hormone that changes the offspring's starting conditions is a head start under this rule if it cheapens the offspring's search, whether or not it is decoded as a message.
- **Bias** is a weighting of the receiver before the mark arrives. Symbols :20-21: the belief is *"Formed from its own history and from what it inherited"*; Symbols :23-24: the weighting *"How far the belief leans on private history versus inherited prior."* Developmentally, the counterpart is the G and M matrices: variation is nonuniform by construction. Uller et al. 2018 (Genetics): *"Phenotypic variation is generated by the processes of development, with some variants arising more readily than others"* (quote stops before an em dash in the source). The same outcome can appear with no carried mark at all.
- **Habitat** is the fourth thing the question omits. Fig 11 :39: *"capability is a property of agent-and-habitat, never of the agent alone."* Fig 11 :66: *"Isolation is not removal of the habitat. It is substitution of one habitat for another."* A cross-fostering experiment substitutes a habitat; the resemblance that disappears may have been habitat contact, not a lost message.

Verdict in D1 and D3 remain separate. "State or bias" is not one condition; it is at least three.

### F5 — The separator the figures return (the shape)

The separator is not "was a molecule carried" and not "is there a correlation." Both are true of all three conditions. The figures license this shape:

> **A case is information transmission only if (i) an ordered bond connects a carrier to a reader, (ii) the bond is typed and priced, so which relation holds is not recoverable from the operands alone, (iii) a receiver exists whose response changes with variation in the mark, and (iv) an anchor that does not update on the classification settles whether the response tracks the mark or the habitat. If (iii) or (iv) fails, the case is inherited state, bias or habitat contact, and the word "transmission" is refused.**

Each clause is figure-derived or explicitly imported with provenance:

1. ordering: Operators :30-31 (`→`); Fig 12 :111 (a route is not a state).
2. typed, priced bond: Fig 12 :46; Fig 12 :42 (*"(k+1)·log₂(|atoms|+1)  +  (k−1)·log₂(|bonds|)"*).
3. discriminating receiver: Jablonka's interpreter (SEP, world corroboration), and Fig 2 :29 (anchor) for the test.
4. non-updating anchor: Fig 2 :29 *"An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks."* Fig 2 :35: *"Alignment is triangulation, not negotiation. A mirror cannot correct you."*

This is a **shape**, not a name. Fig 9 :32: *"you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself."* No figure line says which molecule in a given case is the transmitter. Naming DNA methylation or a small RNA as *the* transmitter is an import from the biology frame and needs a source gate and a shadow test (Fig 6 :14, *"the only operator that moves the wall"*; Fig 8 :12-13).

### F6 — The question is a union, so verdicts are kept per domain

Fig 9 :55 and :57 (quoted in §1.1) govern. A single lever does not clear the case. Cross-fostering removes habitat contact but leaves the mark; germline erase removes the mark but leaves the habitat and the bias; receiver-ablation removes the decoding step but leaves both. Each lever clears only the region where one condition holds. The intersections (mark plus bias, mark plus habitat, mark plus receiver) stay standing and become a specific compound, not a harder version of one condition.

## 4. Pre-registered refuter

Written before the conclusion, per Formula step "Direction" and the standing rule that a falsifier written before the run is a design instrument. The shape claim in F5 is refuted if any of the following holds:

1. **Bond recoverable from operands.** A measurement procedure is demonstrated that reads sender, receiver and bond type directly from the mark, with no separate receiver assay or intervention. This breaks Fig 12 :46 as applied here.
2. **Partition, not union.** A controlled series shows the three conditions are mutually exclusive: every case with a mark and a receiver has no bias contribution, and every bias case has no mark. That collapses Fig 9 :55-57.
3. **Symmetry suffices.** A symmetric correlational statistic alone is shown to settle the transmission/state distinction without an ordered intervention. This breaks Operators :30-33 versus :12-15.
4. **Anchor moves.** The proposed receiver test is shown to update on the observer's classification, so Fig 2 :29/:33 fails and the test becomes a mirror chain.
5. **A figure line names the transmitter.** A verbatim figure line is found that returns a specific molecule or a universal discriminating threshold, contradicting Fig 9 :32 and the shape-not-a-name limit.
6. **Settlement without a discriminator.** A ground reading settles a specific case as transmission while all source-discriminating experiments remain unrun. This breaks Formula step 5.

If any fires, the separator is withdrawn or narrowed. Until one is run, no part of this may be labelled `grounded`.

## 5. Strains and re-census

**S1, Shannon symmetry versus directed transmission.** World source (SEP) says correlational information is symmetric and ubiquitous; the question says transmission is directed. Re-census: these are two senses of one word. Fig 12 :111 and Operators :30-33 govern the ordered bond; the SEP governs the statistical quantity. Different objects, no strain after re-census. Recorded as a finding, F3.

**S2, Fig 4 "never a replay" versus molecular templating.** Epigenetic maintenance looks like a copy of one occasion, which Fig 4 :24 appears to forbid coming down. Re-census: Fig 4 governs movement between *learning* scales (method versus recording) in a system with a search budget. Molecular templating within a lineage is a cycle/catalyst under Fig 12 :119/:121, not a cross-scale replay in Fig 4's sense. Different objects. Dissolved, kept visible.

**S3, inherited prior versus message.** Symbols :20-24 makes the inherited prior part of the belief, not an observation. There is no strain with F1: the prior is the bias condition, the message is the ordered bond condition. The question's phrase "state or bias" conflates them; the audit separates them.

**S4, live: who is the receiver?** Fig 11 :44 requires the habitat to be enumerated, not composed, and contact cascades (*"first-order enumeration, second-order effect, and the residual that mattered was three steps out"*). In a multicellular organism the responding system may be several steps from the mark. The receiver in F5 clause (iii) is therefore an instrument to be built, not a term already held. Recorded as an owed instrument, not a verdict and not a strain to be smoothed.

**After re-census: one live instrument gap (S4), no hidden contradictions.**

### Seven-question recursive derivation (summary)

1. **Is a mark a message?** No. It is an operand; a message adds a typed bond (Fig 12 :5, :46).
2. **Does correlation decide?** No. It is symmetric and ubiquitous (Operators :12-15; SEP).
3. **Can the source be read off the phenotype?** No. Three sources give one reading (Fig 9 :30-31; Fig 13 :129).
4. **What distinguishes?** An ordered, typed, priced bond to a discriminating receiver, with an anchor that does not update (Operators :30-33; Fig 2 :29; SEP Jablonka).
5. **What are the negative cases?** Catalyst/cycle, head start, prior weighting, habitat substitution (Fig 12 :119/:121; Fig 4 :22/:24; Symbols :20-24; Fig 11 :39/:66).
6. **Import?** Mechanism names cross from biology and need provenance plus a shadow test (Fig 6 :14; Fig 8 :12-13).
7. **Status?** `specified` per domain, `general` on the shape, `gap` on the specific molecule, ungrounded. The separator is a shape, and the ground has not paid for any instance.

### Updated verdict

The shape survives: the observed resemblance is a union of at least four conditions, the mark alone cannot discriminate them, and "information transmission" is licensed only for an ordered typed bond to a receiver with a discriminating response, tested against an anchor that does not update. What does not survive is the question's implied two-way partition and any claim that a molecular list answers it. Strains after re-census: one live instrument gap (who is the receiver), named, not hidden.
