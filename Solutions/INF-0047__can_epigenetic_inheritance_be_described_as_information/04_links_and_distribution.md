## What in the wider literature bears on this question

1. **The information side already contains the distinction the question asks for.** The SEP entry states that correlational information is symmetric and ubiquitous, while the notion at work in biological practice is asymmetric, normative and non-ubiquitous. Jablonka's account makes the interpreter the seat of information; Shea's restricts semantic information to inheritance systems. Both agree with F1 to F3: the mark is not the message, and the receiver is the missing term. This is a genuinely low-overlap corroborator (philosophy of biology), not another epigenetics review.

2. **The mechanism side calls the copied item a template, not a decoded message.** The Epigenetics article introduces "epigenetic templating" specifically to mark the difference from canonical Watson-Crick transmission of genetic information, and reports the view that histone state cannot be read linearly as a digital information carrier. That is the world-language version of Fig 12 :5: the ingredient list is not the arrangement.

3. **The confound side refuses a single-cause reading.** The transgenerational inheritance page reports the limitation on human observational studies: shared psychosocial, environmental and cultural confounders cannot be isolated from biological transmission. The maternal-effect page makes the same point in different terms: the offspring phenotype is determined by the mother's environment and genotype as well as its own. These are two records of one evidence pool, counted as one witness.

4. **The developmental-bias side supplies the third positive condition.** Uller et al. 2018 show that variation is nonuniform by construction and that bias can evolve and impose direction on adaptation. So a parent-offspring resemblance may arise with no carried mark and no decoding step at all. This is the lowest-overlap substantive corroborator (evolutionary developmental biology) and it is the one most likely to refute a transmission-only reading.
