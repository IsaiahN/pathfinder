**Question:** Can epigenetic inheritance be described as information transmission, and what distinguishes it from inherited state or bias?

**Mode:** `general` on the structural shape, `specified` on the per-domain separators, not `grounded`. Every substantive claim in this folder is a simulated derivation checked for internal consistency against the figures, not a ground reading. It cannot settle the question it reports on.

**Status:** done

**Registry status:** pending

## The question restated as an effect

The observed effect is that offspring resemble ancestors in a way not accounted for by DNA sequence. Two descriptions are offered for that same effect. The first says a message was sent and read: information transmission. The second says no message was sent: a state persisted, or the receiving system was already biased toward that outcome. What each leads to is different. If transmission is the right description, a sender, a typed bond, a receiver and a source-discriminating ground are owed. If state or bias is the right description, the owed object is a persisting part, a cycle, or a propensities-and-habitat account. What an answer would look like is a **separator**: a criterion that assigns a given observed resemblance to one condition, or states honestly that the conditions overlap and cannot be assigned from the mark alone. That separator is bound here as an ordinary term so it can accrue a residual like anything else.

## What is deliberately not here

- No personal emails of named individuals.
- No ranking of people by how likely they are to agree.
- No bulk-send plan.

Three reasons in the figures' own words:

1. A room does not upgrade the label. Adding agreeable recipients raises the vocabulary and does not add an anchor (Fig 2 :29).
2. Selecting for likely agreement manufactures the correlation that disqualifies a verdict (Fig 2 :40: *"agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool"*).
3. Asking a reader to trust before checking spends their independence (Fig 10 :42: *"A verifier that can reconstruct the claim cannot verify it."*).
