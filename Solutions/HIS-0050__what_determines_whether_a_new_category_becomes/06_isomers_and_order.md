# Isomers, order and analogues for category uptake

**Mode: `specified` on the structural claims, `general` on the bond vocabulary. Simulated derivation checked against the figures, not a ground reading (`05_what_shape_means.md` §6).**

## RESIDUAL

The shape in `01` says that uptake needs a paid term, changed contact, selection between rooms and a recorded route. Two further questions are owed: (1) what is the **order** of those constraints, and (2) are two widely used categories **isomers** of one another, a functional group, or something else? The answer matters because the same atoms arranged differently are different substances (Fig 12 :117), so history's repeated categories may differ only in arrangement.

## DOMAIN CENSUS (supporting derivation)

Atoms held: a candidate term φ, a community, an instrument, a habitat, a room chain, a record, and the six substrate terms (Fig 13 :74). Bonds held: `→` sequence, `⋛` comparison, `+` conjunction, `⇒` production, `−` subtraction, `¬` negation. No new primitive is available without an import with a source gate (Fig 6 :12-14).

## DERIVATION

### Q1 - What order do the four constraints run in?

- Fig 3 :20: "The chain is ordered by dependency, not by importance. Each link consumes what the one above produces".
- Fig 5 :41: "Any one of them at zero stops the mint entirely." The guards are a product; support, reach and novelty are checked before, during and after the search.
- Formula step 3 mints before step 5 settles; formula step 2 refuses before it mints.
- Fig 9 :27-28: "describe it before you go looking, or any frame you pick will seem to fit".
- Fig 7 :35: "A room's agreement is variation, not a verdict. Selection between rooms is where the ground votes."

⇒ The dependency order is:

1. **Describe the residual** in effect terms (spread differential, arity 2, threshold scale).
2. **Refuse first** if the held category is false. Negation is unary and is not a bond (Operators table :70-78); a vocabulary without ¬ can only accumulate, so a wrong category cannot be removed, only refused.
3. **Mint / pay the bargain** (Fig 5 :42-43). Stating the candidate plus the leftover must cost less than the confusion before it.
4. **Introduce into a habitat** and change contact (Fig 11 :32, :40). This is `⇒` production in the sense that a new discrimination appears that was not there before.
5. **Selection between rooms** (Fig 7 :35). Each next room must differ and match the surfaced residual; otherwise it is one long room.
6. **Settle at the ground** (formula step 5). A gate is not the ground.
7. **Record the route** (Fig 8 :50-52). Without provenance the derived and the imported look identical.

This is a chain, not a checklist. A link whose input never arrives cannot be diagnosed (Fig 3 :22-23); a category that never paid its bargain cannot be rescued by better marketing above the break.

**Falsifier for Q1:** show a case where selection between rooms happened before a term was worth having, and still produced a durable discrimination. Then the order is wrong.

### Q2 - Are widely used categories isomers?

Fig 12 :117: "isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour." Fig 12 :124: "functional group. A substructure whose presence fixes the outcome. The group says what kind; the qualifier says which member."

Two cases:

- **Weak case (functional group).** Two categories that both read `community → instrument → community` share a substructure but may differ in every concrete atom. They are members of a functional group, not isomers. The group says what kind (a category that changes counts); the qualifier says which member.
- **Strong case (isomer).** Two categories over the same full atom set, differing only in which structure mediates which pair, are isomers: same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the list of atoms and decisive in behaviour. A historical case in the Hacking source read this run is the pair "fugue" and the later diagnostic categories it fed into: same clinical territory, different bond to the classified person, different consequences.

**Falsifier for Q2, written before the run:** if either category can be written without an atom the other requires (for example, a category in common use with no instrument or no record), the isomer claim fails and only the functional-group claim stands.

**Status: single-source** for the figure definition (Fig 12 :117, :124); the physics-free historical application is unmeasured and stays `specified`.

### Q3 - What kind of bond is uptake?

- `+` is symmetric (Fig 12 :55-59): "both hold at once". Uptake is not symmetric; a proposer and a community do not swap roles.
- `→` is ordered (Fig 12 :70-74): "A then B; B needs A to have happened". Adoption has this shape, but sequence alone does not give the threshold.
- `⋛` is a comparison (Operators table :47-51): "a threshold relation between two quantities", and "a goal is a comparison, which is why a vocabulary without it cannot state one". Uptake is threshold-like: a category is adopted or not once enough users cross a line.

⇒ The honest bond reading is `→` for the route and `⋛` for the threshold, with the caveat that the two operands of ⋛ are quantities at the level where the comparison is made. If a community's uptake count and the term's cost are not commensurable, the comparison cannot be written in this vocabulary without an import. **Strain named, not hidden.**

**Falsifier for Q3:** show that uptake is symmetric under exchange of proposer and community, or that no threshold can be identified in a documented adoption. Then both bond readings fail.

### Q4 - Which structures from Fig 12 show up in the historical record?

- **Catalyst:** "A part that survives its own use. Spent and regenerating parts behave differently across uses" (Fig 12 :119). A category reproduced by textbooks is a catalyst: it survives its use and regenerates each time a student is trained. Fleck's SEP entry describes exactly this: "This occurs when textbooks are created", turning a subjective judgment into a proven fact.
- **Cycle:** "The output re-enters as input. The sign of the loop decides whether it closes" (Fig 12 :121). A looping kind is a cycle: classifying changes the classified, which changes the classification. The sign is positive in Hacking's cases, negative when the category is refused and stops being reproduced.
- **Functional group:** covered in Q2.
- **Isomer:** covered in Q2.

**Falsifier for Q4:** show a category in wide use that is neither regenerated, nor re-enters as input, nor shares a substructure with other categories in its field. Then the Fig 12 analogue set does not cover category uptake and a new analogue is owed.

## CONCLUSION

The order is: describe, refuse, mint, introduce, select, settle, record. A skipped link is not a weaker version of the chain; it is a break (Fig 3 :22-23). Widely used categories stand to each other as a functional group in the weak case and as isomers in the strong case, with a stated falsifier. Uptake binds as `→` along the route and `⋛` at the threshold, with an open type question about the operands. The catalyst, cycle, functional group and isomer structures in Fig 12 all have counterparts in the historical record, corroborated at low overlap by the SEP entries on Fleck and Kuhn and by the STS source on boundary objects.
