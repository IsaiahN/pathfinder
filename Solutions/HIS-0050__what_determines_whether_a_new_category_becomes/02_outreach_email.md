# Outreach draft: check the shape first, then decide whether the theory is worth reading

**Use:** one-to-one, or as a cover letter attached to a submission through the public venues in `03_public_contact_points.csv`. **Not** for bulk send.

**Hard precondition:** do not send until the shape claim and the five refuters below survive a reader's check. If the shape does not survive, there is nothing else to offer.

---

**Subject:** One checkable claim about why scientific categories catch on

> Hi [name],
>
> I have one specific output I would like you to check against what you already know about classificatory practice. I am not asking you to read the framework first, and I am not asking you to agree with it. If the output does not survive your check, there is nothing else to look at.
>
> **The output (`specified` on the shape, `general` on the split, `gap` on naming a winner):** "whether a new category becomes widely accepted and used" bundles two questions that need separate answers. Wide uptake is a **convention**, and for a convention agreement about a shared meaning is the product, not a corruption. Whether the category is true is a **verdict**, and there agreement is disqualified when the agreeing people share one evidence pool. The shape any accepted category must have has four parts: (1) it pays its description-length bargain, that is, stating it plus what it leaves unexplained costs less than the confusion it removes; (2) it changes contact in a habitat of instruments, counts, predictions and teaching; (3) it is selected between different rooms rather than handed along a chain of identical ones; (4) its route of arrival is recorded, because a derived term and an imported term look identical in the contents. The framework does not name which category will win; that is a `gap`, not a silence.
>
> **What I am not claiming:** that wide use makes a category true; that a popular category is settled; that any particular historical case is decided by this shape; or that a category that decides outcomes can keep being a mere convention. Those are separate claims and I am not making them.
>
> **The boundary failure I am most interested in:** a convention that decides an outcome has stopped being one. Once a category determines a diagnosis, an allocation or a legal status, its popularity is no longer a check. I would value a historical counterexample to this more than an endorsement.
>
> **What would refute the shape, written before this message was sent:**
> 1. a category stays widely used while costing more to state and apply than the confusion it removed, with no drop in residual;
> 2. a category's universal use changes no instrument, count, prediction, record or training, yet yields a durable new discrimination;
> 3. wide agreement settles a factual question with no ground, or a category that decides outcomes remains checkable by popularity alone;
> 4. the figures, after a fresh census, yield a line that names which category will be accepted;
> 5. a category's route of arrival is readable from its final form without a provenance record.
>
> If any one holds, I retract the corresponding part. A one-line "this fails because..." is more useful to me than agreement. If you would rather not engage, no reply is needed.
>
> **If it survives, and only then:** here are the sources. The figures are the authority, not my summary of them.
> - Figures text extract with line numbers (local framework file): `FRAMEWORK/reference/03-FIGURES_TEXT.md`
> - Figures README, canonical usage rule: https://github.com/IsaiahN/tether/blob/general-agent/figures/README.md
> - The formula, eight-step loop and mode rule: https://github.com/IsaiahN/tether/tree/general-agent/figures
>
> **If you want to use the machinery:** the formula is in the figures link. Run it on a classificatory problem you know well and can check against a ground. Write down what would refute your result *before* you run it, and state the mode of what you get. The framework's own limit is that a loop cannot check the direction of its own question, so a reported failure is more useful to me than a reported success.

## Why this version is ordered this way

| the move | figure-grounded reason |
|---|---|
| Output first, theory second | Fig 10 :52 "Bring a characterised residual, never the source." |
| Do not ask for agreement | Fig 2 :35 "Alignment is triangulation, not negotiation. A mirror cannot correct you." |
| Separate convention from verdict before answering | Fig 2 :41 "efficient for a convention: agreement about a shared meaning is the product, not a corruption of it" and Fig 2 :40 "disqualifying for a verdict: agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool" |
| Pre-registered refuter | A falsifier written before the run is a design instrument, not an honesty device (exemplar `01` §3). |

| No claim that wide use grounds a category | Fig 10 :18 "a declared meaning is not a grounded one" |
| No name for the winning category | Fig 9 :32 "you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself" |
| Offer the formula as a method, not an answer | Fig 4 :22 "Only methods cross up. Only head starts come down." |
| No prestige ask | The Recursive Self-Improvement Trap: "Prestige can fund an action. It can give an action a budget to be tried. But it cannot settle an action." The ask gives the claim a budget to be tried; it does not ask anyone to endorse it. |
