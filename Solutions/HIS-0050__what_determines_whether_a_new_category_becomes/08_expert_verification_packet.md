# Expert verification packet: the Tether on category uptake

**What this is:** a claim in a form a historian or philosopher of science can check. Mode, derivation and refuters are stated before the conclusion, so a reader knows what to look for and what would settle it.

**Mode: `specified` on the shape, `general` on the verdict/convention split, `gap` on naming a winner. Not `grounded`.** No historical measurement is claimed. This is a shape and a route, not a result.

## 1. The claim, in one paragraph

"Whether a new category becomes widely accepted and used" bundles two questions. Wide uptake is a convention, and for a convention agreement about shared meaning is the product, not a corruption; whether the category is true is a verdict, and there agreement is disqualified when the frames share an evidence pool. Any category that stays accepted must satisfy four constraints: it pays a description-length bargain, it changes contact in a habitat of instruments and teaching, it is selected between different rooms rather than handed down a mirror chain, and its route of arrival is recorded. A category that decides an outcome has stopped being a convention and owes an anchor. The framework does not name which category will win; that is a `gap`. What it offers is the shape, refuters written in advance, and the instruments that would close it.

## 2. Three checkable sub-claims

### C1 - Convention and verdict are different quantities; agreement has opposite valence in each.

- **Statement:** wide uptake is a convention, so agreement is the product there; the same agreement cannot certify truth.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 2 :41 "efficient for a convention: agreement about a shared meaning is the product, not a corruption of it". Fig 2 :40 "disqualifying for a verdict: agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool". Fig 2 :43 "Guard, from Figure 10: a convention that decides an outcome has stopped being one."
- **What to check:** does this distinction survive a real historical case in your field, for example a classification that became universal without ever being decided by a discriminating observation?
- **Refuter:** show a case where wide agreement alone settled a factual question with no ground, or a category that decides outcomes remained checkable by popularity alone.

### C2 - The paid-bargain and contact constraints are necessary for durable uptake.

- **Statement:** a category that stays used must cost less to state and apply than the confusion it removes, and must change contact in its habitat.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 5 :43 "A new term is worth having when stating it, plus what remains unexplained after it, costs less than what was unexplained before". Fig 5 :41 "Any one of them at zero stops the mint entirely." Fig 11 :40 "An improvement that does not change contact changes nothing, however much it improves."
- **What to check:** for a category you know well, name what instrument, count, prediction or training record changed when it was introduced, and whether stating it reduced the descriptive burden. Independent corroboration is available in Kuhn's five theory-choice criteria (simplicity as organising confused phenomena) and in Rosch's basic-level result that winning categories maximise within-category and minimise between-category attributes.
- **Refuter:** a category that remained widely used while costing more than the confusion it removed, or whose use changed no contact yet produced a durable new discrimination.

### C3 - Spread is selection between rooms, not a mirror chain; provenance is the whole record.

- **Statement:** uptake is meaningful only when adopting communities differ and each selects against a surfaced residual. Wide use alone cannot distinguish a derived category from an imported one.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 7 :35 "A room's agreement is variation, not a verdict. Selection between rooms is where the ground votes." Fig 7 :13 "same closure, handed along; agreement compounds, evidence does not". Fig 8 :50-52 "Convergent derivation and adopted import look identical in the contents. Only the record of where each came from separates them".
- **What to check:** reconstruct the adoption chain of a classification. Did each adopting group differ from the last, and is there a dated record of who introduced it from where?
- **Refuter:** a category that became universal purely by repetition through rooms of identical closure and still increased discrimination, or a category whose route of arrival is fully readable from its final form with no provenance record.

## 3. What is explicitly not claimed

- Not that wide use makes a category true. Fig 10 :18 "a declared meaning is not a grounded one".
- Not that this decides any historical case. No archive, instrument or measurement was used in this run.
- Not that prestige or authority is a determinant of truth. Prestige can fund a trial, not settle it.
- Not a theory of everything. "every room closes some of the gap; no finite number of them ever arrives" (Fig 7 :30-31).
- Not grounded. Every sub-claim is `specified` or `general`.
- Not a name for the winning category. Fig 9 :32 "you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself".

## 5. What would settle it

A source-discriminating instrument or a checkable historical measure, not further agreement. From *The Hard Problem is About Source*: an instrument that reads green "cannot comment on the source"; the spectrometer resolved it. Here:

1. **Bargain test:** measure description length or residual before and after introduction of a category against a documented baseline.
2. **Contact test:** date the instrument, count, prediction or training record that changed on introduction.
3. **Room test:** show that successive adopting communities differed in closure and selected against a surfaced residual, rather than mirroring.
4. **Boundary test:** identify the anchor and check installed when a category began deciding outcomes.
