## 1. A shape is a constraint, not a filler

The canonical usage rule (local: `FRAMEWORK/reference/FIGURES_README_canonical.md`) states the limit: the framework returns "a shape, not a name". Fig 9 :32: "you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself". Fig 13 :133: "what it returns is a shape rather than a name."

From *The Library of Babel*, re-read this run: "you can cut your search short if the set of books returned is small and descriptive enough that it provides a general shape of the answer that satisfies your question." The same essay: "With each new term gained, you narrow the space by definition and deepen your vocabulary."

So for HIS-0050 a **shape** is the narrowed form of the determinants of uptake that we can state before we can name a winning category. It constrains: any accepted category must pay its bargain, change contact, survive selection between rooms, and arrive with a recorded lineage. It does not fill: it does not say "oxygen", "gene", "plate tectonics" or "dwarf planet" was or will be accepted.

## 2. How the shape is obtained here

- Fig 9 :27-28: "describe it before you go looking, or any frame you pick will seem to fit". We described the residual in effect terms (a spread differential) before choosing history of science, STS, philosophy of convention and cognitive science as frames. Had we picked the fields first, any of them would have seemed to fit.
- Fig 6 :30: "The description travels. Fails by being sharpened into terms only we can read. Cast it in effect terms." The four constraints are written in effect terms: cost paid, contact changed, rooms selected, route recorded. None names a framework primitive that a historian must accept.
- *Decoding Tacit Knowledge* (from the exemplar's recorded reading, not re-fetched): "When contact isn't possible, the best you can send is a plain description of what you'd expect to see, because anyone can check that against the world." Hence the five refuters in `01` and `02` are stated as things a reader could look for.
- *Library of Babel* on stopping: "you will know that you are done because the finish line will also be the starting place". Here the stop is at the point where the shape is stated and the name is refused.

## 3. What would license a name

Two things only, both outside the shape:

1. **The ground settles it.** Formula step 5: "the ground settles it. Verified only by what pays." For a scientific category, that means a discriminating instrument or observation that the category predicts and its rivals do not. *The Hard Problem is About Source*, re-read this run: "It was proven wrong not by argument, but by the arrival of a better instrument." Also from that essay: "If several paths create the same behavior, then behavior is a projection with lossy information". Wide usage is such a projection: several routes (a derived term, an imported term, a prestige-backed term) yield the same reading (everybody uses it). The reading cannot name the source.
2. **A provenanced import from a frame whose closure differs** (Fig 6 :23, Fig 8 :50-52), with a source gate and a shadow test. The SEP Fleck entry gives the historical version: thought styles are often incommensurable, and "what is a fact to the members of a thought collective A sometimes does not exist to the members of a thought collective B". A category crossing that boundary needs a record.

Until one of these, a name is the searcher's own contribution and must be recorded as such.

## 4. Why wide use is not a ground

Fig 10 :18: "a declared meaning is not a grounded one". Fig 10 :49: "Declaring a meaning does not ground it." Fig 2 :43: "Guard, from Figure 10: a convention that decides an outcome has stopped being one."

*The Recursive Self-Improvement Trap*, re-read this run: "You may all agree and be wrong." And: "The prestige loop confirms itself. The high-prestige person is believed because they are high-prestige." A category accepted because its proponents are prestigious has a budget to be tried, but its acceptance is not a verdict. From the same essay: "Prestige can fund an action. It can give an action a budget to be tried. But it cannot settle an action." This is why `03_public_contact_points.csv` contains no personal addresses and no receptiveness rank: selecting recipients for likely agreement manufactures the correlation that would make their agreement worthless.

## 5. The specific correction this question needs

An earlier reading of mine treated "the framework returns a shape, not a name" as a blanket ban on naming categories. That was overbroad. The canonical phrase governs a **missing** thing, a term not held in Γ; it does not forbid naming an atom already held. For HIS-0050 the missing thing is the discriminator that would tell which of several competing categories will be accepted. That discriminator is not in the frame, so the answer is a `gap` at the naming level. It is not a ban on discussing well-known categories as illustrations. Both readings are recorded so neither is quietly lost.
