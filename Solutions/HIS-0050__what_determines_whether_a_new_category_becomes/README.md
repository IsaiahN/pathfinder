## The residual in effect terms

A proposed category C is put into a community. In some cases C becomes a working coin: it appears in papers, instruments, textbooks, and administrative codes, and a next generation reproduces it. In other cases it is used once and disappears. The residual is the difference in that outcome that our present terms cannot predict. The question asks which determinants close that difference.

The figures immediately split it. "Widely accepted and used" is a **convention** question, not a **verdict** question. Fig 2 :41 states that for a convention "agreement about a shared meaning is the product, not a corruption of it". Fig 2 :40 states that for a verdict "agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool". One answer cannot serve both. What an answer would look like, bound as an ordinary term before the run: a four-part shape (a term that pays its description-length bargain, a habitat whose contact it changes, selection between rooms rather than mirror handoff, and a record that preserves provenance) plus the refusal to name which category will fill it.

## What is deliberately not here

- No personal emails of named individuals.
- No ranking of people by how likely they are to believe the claim.
- No bulk-send plan.
- No claim that this simulated derivation settles the historical question.

The first three follow the exemplar's rule: a room selected for agreement produces a convention, not corroboration (Fig 2 :40, Fig 7 :35). The fourth follows the framework's own limit: LLM output is a simulation, not a ground reading, and "the truly testable output would need to be derived from the real architecture or working the formula yourself" (local: the Recursive Self-Improvement Trap essay, cited in `05_what_shape_means.md`).
