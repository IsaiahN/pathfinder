## 0. The residual, stated in effect terms

A new category C is proposed inside a community. The observed effect is a spread differential: C either becomes a working coin (papers, instruments, textbooks, codes, a next generation of users) or fails to. The residual is the part of that differential our terms cannot predict.

- **Arity.** At least two conditions are bound: a term worth having, and a community that takes it up. Fig 9 names this a compound: "a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either" (Fig 9 :55-57). It is a **union**, not a single hard problem, and each condition needs its own loop.
- **Symmetry.** Adoption is not a symmetric `+` bond. It has a direction (proposer to community) and a threshold (enough users to be reproduced). That is closer to `→` sequence and `⋛` comparison than to a symmetric join. Operators table :47-51 defines ⋛ as "a threshold relation between two quantities".
- **Scale.** First order inside one room/community; second order when the category travels across rooms into a habitat (Fig 7 :35, Fig 11 :39-40).

What an answer would look like, written down and bound before the run (Formula "Direction"): **a shape with four constraints and one refusal**. The four constraints are: the term pays its bargain, it changes contact in a habitat, it is selected between rooms rather than mirrored, and its provenance is recorded. The refusal is: the framework does not name which term will fill the slot. Binding the shape as an ordinary term lets it accrue a residual and be refuted like anything else.

Selection between rooms comes after a room has produced a residual worth selecting on (Fig 7 :35). Refusal precedes mint where a term held is false (formula step 2); a category that misdescribes its subject owes a refusal, not another category.

## 3. Findings F1 to F8, each with a verbatim quote and its own refuter

### F1 - The question bundles a convention and a verdict. Keep the verdicts separate.

Fig 2 :41: "efficient for a convention: agreement about a shared meaning is the product, not a corruption of it". Fig 2 :40: "disqualifying for a verdict: agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool".

A category is a shared meaning, so its wide uptake is a **convention** and agreement is the product, not a corruption. The same agreement is worthless as evidence that the category is true, because the agreeing community is one evidence pool (Fig 2 :20 "so the agreement is the pool talking," and Fig 2 :21 "not two frames confirming each other"). Asking one question ("what determines acceptance?") and expecting one answer is the error. Two verdicts are owed: is the term worth having (domain 1), and does it become a working convention (domain 2). They are never averaged.

**Refuter:** show a case where a community's wide agreement on a category settled a factual question without any ground reading, so that Fig 2 :41 and Fig 2 :40 govern the same quantity. Then the split fails.

**Status: triangulated** across Fig 2 :40 and Fig 2 :41 (different clauses of the same figure, low internal overlap) plus the independent Convention entry (world, philosophy) which distinguishes social conventions from facts about the world.

### F2 - A category must pay the description-length bargain.

Fig 5 :42: "The acceptance test is a bargain, not a threshold." Fig 5 :43: "A new term is worth having when stating it, plus what remains unexplained after it, costs less than what was unexplained before." Fig 5 :41: "Any one of them at zero stops the mint entirely."

A category is a term φ. It is worth having only if stating it plus what it leaves unexplained costs less than the confusion it replaces, and only if support, reachability and novelty all pass. This is minimum description length (Fig 5 :45: "This is minimum description length, Rissanen 1978"). Wide use without this bargain is a fashion, not a licensed term; it will not survive the next generation's budget.

**Refuter:** produce a documented case where a category became and stayed widely used while costing more to state and apply than the confusion it removed, with no reduction in the community's residual. Then F2 is refuted.

**Status: triangulated.** Fig 5 alone is single-source, but it is corroborated at low overlap by two independent fields: Kuhn's five shared criteria (accuracy, consistency, scope, simplicity, fruitfulness, with simplicity glossed as "organizing otherwise confused and isolated phenomena") and Rosch's basic-level result that basic categories are "a decomposition of the world into maximally informative categories" that "maximize the number of attributes shared by members of the category, and minimize the number of attributes shared with other categories". Different evidence pools, same bargain.

### F3 - Acceptance is not settlement. A declared meaning is not grounded.

Fig 10 :18: "a declared meaning is not a grounded one". Fig 10 :49: "Declaring a meaning does not ground it." Fig 2 :29: "An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks."

A category becomes widely used by being declared, taught and reproduced. None of that makes it true. Only a ground that does not move when the community pushes on it settles whether the category carves nature at a joint. This is the difference between a category being *accepted* and being *grounded*. The world source on Ian Hacking records his "historicised form of nominalism that traces the mutual interactions over time between the phenomena of the human world and our conceptions and classifications of them": the classification and its object interact. That is exactly the case where agreement must not be mistaken for settlement.

**Refuter:** show a category whose truth was settled solely by its wide acceptance, with no measurement, instrument, prediction or discriminating observation. Then Fig 10 :18 does not govern this case.

**Status: triangulated** across Fig 10 :18, Fig 10 :49 and Fig 2 :29, with independent corroboration from the philosophy-of-science source on Hacking (low overlap).

### F4 - Spread is selection between rooms, not a mirror chain.

Fig 7 :13: "same closure, handed along; agreement compounds, evidence does not". Fig 7 :35: "A room's agreement is variation, not a verdict. Selection between rooms is where the ground votes." Fig 7 :36: "A chain of mirrors is one long room. The next room must differ from the last, and match the residual it surfaced."

A category that spreads by handoff inside one closure is one long room: motion without progress. Wide uptake is meaningful only when successive rooms differ in closure and each is selected against the residual the previous one surfaced. The independent Fleck entry supplies the same shape from history of science: "The victory of a thought style depends on whether it will be possible to have a next generation of its followers, and to prevent influences of other styles." The Kuhn entry adds: "the factors determining the outcome of a scientific dispute, particularly in modern science, are almost always to be found within science, specifically in connexion with the puzzle-solving power of the competing ideas." Puzzle-solving power is the ground's vote, not prestige.

**Refuter:** document a category that became universal purely by repetition through rooms with identical closure and no selection against any residual, and that still increased the community's ability to discriminate. Then Fig 7 :35 is wrong for this domain.

**Status: triangulated** across Fig 7 :13, :35, :36, with two independent world sources (Fleck, Kuhn) that share neither the framework nor each other's evidence pool.

### F5 - The category must change contact in a habitat.

Fig 11 :39: "capability is a property of agent-and-habitat, never of the agent alone". Fig 11 :40: "An improvement that does not change contact changes nothing, however much it improves." Fig 11 :32: "introduce, never subtract".

A category does not act alone. It is introduced into a habitat of instruments, measurement practices, training, textbooks and adjacent categories, and it is the habitat plus the category that is capable of new discrimination. If the category changes no contact (no new observation, no new count, no new prediction, no new teaching), its wide use changes nothing. Introduction is available; exclusion is not, so the habitat already there still binds. The independent STS source on boundary objects gives the same mechanics in different terms: such objects are "plastic enough to adapt to local needs and the constraints of the several parties employing them, yet robust enough to maintain a common identity across sites" and "allow coordination without consensus".

**Refuter:** show a category whose universal use changed no instrument, count, prediction, record or training, yet produced a durable new discrimination. Then Fig 11 :40 does not govern this case.

**Status: triangulated** across Fig 11 :32, :39, :40, with low-overlap corroboration from STS (boundary objects) and history of science (Fleck's active and passive elements).

### F6 - A category that decides an outcome has stopped being a convention.

Fig 2 :43: "Guard, from Figure 10: a convention that decides an outcome has stopped being one." Fig 10 :47: "a convention that decides an outcome has stopped being one". Fig 10 :48: "a convention nothing can check is a constant the seat authored, carrying the seat's authority."

This is the boundary failure. When a widely used category begins to decide outcomes, a diagnosis, a resource allocation, a legal status, it stops being a shared meaning and becomes ground-bearing. It now needs an anchor and a check, and its mere popularity cannot supply either. This is the danger the Hacking source names: classifications of human kinds interact with what they classify, so a convention that assigns the kind changes the thing assigned. The category "fugue" in Hacking's Mad Travelers (1998, recorded in the source read) is the standing case.

**Refuter:** show a category that routinely decides outcomes while remaining purely a convention with no ground contact and no checkable consequence, and that does no harm to the things classified. Then the guard does not fire.

**Status: triangulated** across Fig 2 :43, Fig 10 :47-48, with independent corroboration from the philosophy-of-science source on Hacking.

### F7 - Provenance is the whole issue for the historian.

Fig 8 :50-52: "Convergent derivation and adopted import look identical in the contents. Only the record of where each came from separates them, and the surplus lives only where the closures differ." Fig 9 :27-28: "describe it before you go looking, or any frame you pick will seem to fit".

From the artefact alone (a category in a textbook), a historian cannot tell whether it was derived independently in that community or imported from another frame. Wide use does not distinguish them. The record of the route does. This is why the answer we can give is a shape plus a lineage, not a verdict on any historical case.

**Refuter:** show a category whose route of arrival is recoverable from its final form alone, with no provenance record. Then Fig 8 :50-52 does not govern this case.

**Status: single-source** for the figure claim (Fig 8), with the method corroborated independently by Fleck's observation that participants do not record the moments when new ideas arise from misunderstandings, so the route is lost from the artefact.

### F8 - The framework returns a shape, not a name. This is a genuine gap at the naming level.

Fig 9 :32: "you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself". Fig 13 :133: "what it returns is a shape rather than a name." Fig 13 :134-135: "Unreachable is a verdict here, not a silence".

After the census there is no figure line that selects *which* category will be accepted. That is not a silence; it is a `gap` verdict. The framework can say the shape any accepted category must have, and can say that naming the winner would mean already holding the discriminator. The Library of Babel essay gives the method in effect terms: "you can cut your search short if the set of books returned is small and descriptive enough that it provides a general shape of the answer that satisfies your question." The Hard Problem essay names what would close it: "It was proven wrong not by argument, but by the arrival of a better instrument."

**Refuter:** exhibit a figure line that predicts a specific category's uptake, or a historical case where the shape alone predicted the winner without a ground reading. Then the `gap` is closed.

**Status: triangulated** across Fig 9 :32 and Fig 13 :133, with the canonical figures README (local) and the Library of Babel essay. High overlap for the essay (framework author); the figure lines are primary.

## 4. Pre-registered refuter (written before the conclusion)

Per the exemplar's rule, a falsifier written before the run is a design instrument. The shape claim is refuted if any of the following holds:

1. **Bargain failure (F2).** A category stays widely used while costing more to state and apply than the confusion it removed and with no drop in residual.
2. **Contact failure (F5).** A category's universal use changes no instrument, count, prediction, record or training, yet yields a durable new discrimination.
3. **Boundary failure (F1, F3, F6).** Wide agreement on a category settles a factual question with no ground, or a category that decides outcomes remains checkable purely by its popularity.
4. **Name success (F8).** The figures, after a fresh census, yield a line that names which category will be accepted.
5. **Provenance failure (F7).** A category's route of arrival is fully readable from its final form without a record.

If any fires, the shape is withdrawn or narrowed, and the retraction is written here rather than erased. Until one is run, no part of this may be labelled `grounded`.

## 5. Strain test and canonical usage check

**Strain 1, Fig 2 :41 versus Fig 2 :40.** Read together they look contradictory: agreement is both the product and worthless. Re-census resolves it: Fig 2 :41 governs a **convention** (shared meaning), Fig 2 :40 governs a **verdict** (a claim about the world). Different objects, different quantities. Not strained after re-census, exactly the rule in the figures README: a reading that makes two figures contradict one another has misidentified its domain.

**Strain 2, Fig 11 :40 versus Kuhn's consensus account.** Kuhn says a breakthrough "draws away adherents from the other schools, and a widespread consensus is formed around the new puzzle-solutions", which can look like agreement alone doing the work. Re-census: Kuhn's consensus is *about a puzzle-solution that solved anomalies*, so it is selection against a ground, not mirror handoff. Fig 7 :35 and the Kuhn source agree after re-census. No strain left.

**Strain 3, "convention that decides an outcome" (Fig 2 :43) versus a category's legitimate use.** A category can be used to allocate without being corrupt. The guard does not forbid use; it demands an anchor once an outcome depends on it. Named, not hidden.

**Canonical usage check.** The figures README (local copy read this session) says the figures "outrank everything else in the repo" and that "the quotes are the authority, not this summary". It also says the framework stops at "a shape, not a name" and that "Indiscernibility is not identity". `THE_FORMULA.md` supplies the mode rule and the three-question limit: SEARCHED, INWARD, OUTWARD are distinct. Here the missing discriminator is not found by more search inside a frame (Fig 8 :32: "no search, however long, adds an atom"); it needs an instrument or an import with a source gate and a shadow test.

## 6. Recommendation

1. Ask the convention question and the verdict question separately; never average their answers (F1).
2. Test a proposed category against the MDL bargain and the three guards before asking why it spread (F2).
3. Trace its habitat: what instrument, count, prediction or training changed when it was introduced (F5).
4. Reconstruct the room chain: did each adopting room differ from the last and select against a surfaced residual, or was it a mirror (F4)?
5. Check whether the category now decides outcomes; if so, demand an anchor and a check (F6).
6. Record the route, and report the shape and its refuters rather than a name (F7, F8).
7. Do not send this claim as a verdict. Lead with the shape and the pre-registered refuter, then offer links only after a reader has recorded their own verdict.
