# Outreach draft: check one residual before you decide about the explanation

**Use:** one to one, or as a cover note attached to a submission through the public venues in `03_public_contact_points.csv`. **Not** for bulk send.

**Hard precondition:** send only the stage-1 shape claim and its refuter. Do not attach the framework, the figures or the eight essays until the recipient has recorded a verdict on stage 1. See `04_links_and_distribution.md`.

---

**Subject:** One checkable claim about bright early galaxies, and the observation that would kill it

> Hello [name],
>
> I have one small, checkable claim about the bright high-redshift galaxy excess, and I would like your verdict on it before I show you anything else. I am not asking you to agree with a theory, and I am not asking you to trust a framework. If the claim does not survive, there is nothing further to look at.
>
> **The claim (`specified` on the per-slot verdicts, `general` on the shape, not `grounded`):** a reported "too bright for its age" galaxy is a positive residual on one object slot, the gap between a model prediction and an instrument reading. Three different things can produce it, and they have different anchors:
>
> 1. a channel defect in the reading (photometric redshift, contamination, dust or AGN light);
> 2. a term the model already holds but has mis-set (star-formation efficiency, burstiness, IMF, dust geometry, feedback);
> 3. an atom the model closure does not hold (for example a different early-dark-energy cosmology).
>
> These are not competing answers to one question. They are overlapping conditions, so the same object can carry two of them at once. My claim is only about the **route**: settle the reading with a spectrum before re-fitting the model, and re-fit a held term before importing a new one. I do not claim to know which condition dominates your favourite object.
>
> **What I am not claiming:** that ΛCDM is broken, that all the bright candidates are real, that high star-formation efficiency explains the whole excess, or that any one mechanism is the answer. The framework I am using returns a shape and a route, not a name. A specific mechanism would need a ground reading to be stated as settled.
>
> **What would refute the claim, written before I wrote the conclusion:**
> 1. a spectroscopically confirmed z > 10 galaxy whose stellar mass exceeds the baryon bound for the expected halos, with contamination, lensing, AGN light and calibration excluded; this would force an outward import rather than a re-fit;
> 2. a sample in which every bright candidate loses its excess under a clean spectrum, so the whole residual was a channel defect;
> 3. two supposedly independent readings shown to share calibration, priors or SED libraries, so their agreement is one evidence pool and carries no weight;
> 4. a single lever that closes every slot including the overlaps, which would make this a partition rather than a union.
>
> I wrote these before sending this. A one-line "this fails because..." is more useful to me than agreement. If you would rather not engage, no reply is needed.
>
> **If it survives, and only then:** I can send the three public source links. The figures, not my summary of them, are the authority. Stage 2 is opt in, and I will not count any later agreement as independent corroboration.
>
> Thanks for reading,
> [name / affiliation]
