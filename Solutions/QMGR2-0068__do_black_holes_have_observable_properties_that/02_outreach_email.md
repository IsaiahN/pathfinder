**Subject:** Check one output on black hole observables before you decide whether the theory is worth reading

> Hi [name],
>
> I have one specific output I would like you to check against what you already know. I am not asking you to read the theory first, and I am not asking you to agree with it. If the output does not survive, there is nothing else to look at.
>
> **The output (`specified`, not `grounded`):** any observable that distinguishes a black hole from another compact object must satisfy four conditions. It must be a ground-side measurement that does not move when a frame changes its mind; it must be indexed per object, never averaged into a population score; it must already be returned by an instrument that fails to resolve the classes; and it must constrain the hidden source, not only the projection. These are four conditions on a reading, not a claim about which reading wins. Fig 1 says the residual is indexed per object slot. Fig 6 asks whether anything at any resolution is already returning something that fails to resolve, rather than whether a sensor could exist.
>
> What follows from that: readings of this shape exist. Waveform phasing gives mass and spin, ringdown gives quasinormal modes, a VLBI array gives a shadow diameter, and inspiral phase gives tidal deformability. The finding is not a new signature. It is that the question is well posed at the ground and that the specific observables are bindings from the physics frame, with provenance, not names the framework derived.
>
> **What I am not claiming:** that "consistent with Kerr" proves an object is a black hole. A reading does not name its source. A Kerr-consistent ringdown and a Kerr-consistent shadow are projections, and several proposed compact objects are built to mimic them. The single reading that would separate black holes from every exotic compact object at every mass and spin is not in hand. I report that as a gap and describe the missing instrument in effect terms, rather than filling it. Nor am I claiming that signal-to-noise or a false-alarm rate settles the class: those price the observation, they do not constitute a verdict.
>
> **What would refute the shape claim:**
> 1. a class-separating reading whose value is fixed by convention or prior rather than by a measurement;
> 2. a proof that every candidate reading is many-to-one onto all compact-object classes at every resolution, so the classes are indiscernible rather than merely unresolved at this budget;
> 3. a demonstration that two supposedly independent confirmations draw on the same strain data or the same evidence pool;
> 4. a source name assigned from a single projection, with no discriminating instrument, that survives challenge.
>
> I wrote these down before sending this. If any one holds, the claim is withdrawn. A one-line "this fails because..." is more useful to me than agreement. If you would rather not engage, no reply is needed.
>
> **If it survives, and only then:** the sources are below. The figures are the authority, not my summary of them.
> - Figures and formula for direct download (SVG, branch `general-agent`): https://github.com/IsaiahN/tether/tree/general-agent/figures

> - How Intelligence Actually Works (Substack): https://adventuresinml.substack.com/p/how-intelligence-actually-works
>
> **If you want to use the machinery:** the formula is in the figures link. Run it on a problem you know well and can check against a ground. Write down what would refute your result *before* you run it, and state the mode of what you get. A reported failure is more useful to me than a reported success.
