## The question restated as an effect

What is being decided is not whether an object "is really" a black hole in some frame's vocabulary. It is whether a reading exists that separates two classes of compact object on the ground side. In effect terms: an observer points an instrument at an object-slot s and gets a reading o'. A model predicts Gamma(b,a). The residual R = |Gamma(b,a) - o'| is indexed per object-slot, never aggregated (Fig 1 :19, Fig 1 :22). The question asks for an observable O whose value on the black-hole class and on the other-compact-object classes does not overlap at some resolution, so that the reading discriminates the source.

- Option A, "black holes have a unique observational signature": leads to the prediction that some instrument reading is class-separating and that the classes are distinguishable at the ground.
- Option B, "no such signature exists": leads to the prediction that all readings overlap across the classes at every resolution, so the classes are indiscernible, not merely unresolved at this budget (Fig 6 :27, Fig 8 :39).
- What an answer would look like, written down and bound as an ordinary term before the run (Formula, "Direction"): a *shape* giving the four conditions any distinguishing observable must satisfy, namely ground-side anchor (Fig 2 :29), per-slot readable (Fig 1 :19), already failing to resolve on an instrument that returns something (Fig 6 :32-35), and source-discriminating (the reading must constrain the hidden source, not only the projection). Until that shape is bound it predicts nothing and no check in the loop can see it.

The verdict recorded in `09_watson_answer.md` is Option A in the `specified` sense of a shape: distinguishing observables exist and are already returning readings, but the framework names none of them, and the specific list is a provenanced binding from the physics frame. The single categorical discriminator across all compact-object classes is declared a `gap`. Verdicts are kept per domain and never averaged.

## What is deliberately not here

- No personal emails of named individuals.
- No ranking of people by how likely they are to agree.
- No bulk-send plan.
- No claim that this derivation is `grounded`, no claim that it settles the physics question, and no claim that the framework is the only true account of compact objects.

Three figure-grounded reasons, plus one practical one:

1. A room is not an anchor. *"Alignment is triangulation, not negotiation. A mirror cannot correct you."* (Fig 2 :35) and *"agreement compounds, evidence does not"* (Fig 7 :13). Gathering agreeable readers manufactures the correlation that disqualifies a verdict.
2. Correlation has valence. *"agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool"* (Fig 2 :40). A list selected for receptiveness is one witness wearing many names.
3. A verifier must be outside the interior. *"A verifier that can reconstruct the claim cannot verify it."* (Fig 10 :42). The first contact therefore carries the residual and its refuter, not the framework.
4. Legally, unsolicited bulk email to scraped addresses breaches GDPR and CAN-SPAM style rules in many jurisdictions and gets the sending domain blacklisted. Public opt-in routes avoid that failure as well as the structural one.
