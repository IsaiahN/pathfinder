## What the wider literature bears on the question

1. **There are at least two independent instrument families.** LIGO/Virgo/KAGRA strain analysis and EHT 1.3 mm VLBI imaging draw on different evidence pools. Under Fig 2 :40 (*"agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool"*) their joint agreement is weak but genuine corroboration. By contrast, every ringdown analysis of GW250114 uses the same strain and is one pool.
2. **The reported discriminations are threshold statements, not source identifications.** GW250114 reports percent-level agreement with the Kerr prediction; EHT reports an image consistent with a Kerr shadow. Both are projections. The source essay (*The Hard Problem is About Source*): the instrument *"cannot comment on the source."* This is why the packet asks a referee to decide whether any reading actually discriminates an exotic compact object.
3. **Tidal deformability is the clearest class lever, but it does not clear the whole union.** GW170817 and De et al. show that matter-bearing neutron stars have a measurable tidal response. Fig 9 :55 (*"A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones."*) explains why that lever, applied to the neutron-star condition, leaves the exotic-compact-object intersection standing. The Shen et al. review is the world-side statement of that remaining condition.
4. **No source read supplies the categorical discriminator.** The Berti review is forward looking; the echoes are unconfirmed. This is reported as `gap` in `01_figure_grounded_audit.md` F8, with Fig 13 :134 (*"Unreachable is a verdict here, not a silence"*).
