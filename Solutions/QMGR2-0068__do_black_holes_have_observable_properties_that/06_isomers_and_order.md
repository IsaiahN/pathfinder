## RESIDUAL

The residual is a discrimination, not an essence: what arrangement of readings would make one object-slot fall on the black-hole side of a threshold and another fall on the other-compact-object side? Three questions are owed: (1) what is the bond type of a distinction, (2) are the candidate observables isomers or a functional group under Fig 12, and (3) what is the dependency order of the inference chain.

## DERIVATION

### Q1 - What is the bond type of "distinguish"?

- Fig 12 :92: *"Symmetry is what decides whether an arrangement is a new thing."* The symmetric bonds are +, ∥ and ≡. A distinction is not any of these: swapping the two classes changes the claim, so the bond is ordered.
- Fig 12 :87: *"is it about which is larger, rather than both being present? Yes."* This is the comparison test. A distinguishing observable is a claim about which class gives the larger (or smaller) value of a quantity, so ⋛ is the correct bond.
- Fig 12 :94: *"The arrangement is the substance."* The discrimination lives in the arrangement of readings, not in any single reading. A mass measurement alone does not distinguish; a mass plus a tidal-deformability-plus-ringdown pattern does.
- **Falsifier, written before the run:** if a single reading at a single value is proven class-separating with no threshold and no arrangement, the comparison form is wrong and should be replaced by identity. Not observed in the sources read.

Status: `specified`, framework-internal and physics-facing. Refuter: a class identity with no residual.

### Q2 - Are the candidate observables isomers or a functional group?

- Fig 12 :117: *"Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."*
- Fig 12 :124: *"functional group. A substructure whose presence fixes the outcome. The group says what kind; the qualifier says which member."*
- As the physics frame holds them, ringdown {mass, spin, mode frequencies}, shadow {mass, diameter, ring asymmetry} and tidal response {mass, deformability, radius} share the atom "mass" and differ in the rest. By Fig 12's definition they are **not isomers** of one another; they are members of the same **functional group** (a mass plus a class-sensitive structural reading), with different qualifiers.
- The stronger version: if all three are written over the full shared atom set {mass, spin, deformability, mode spectrum, shadow diameter}, differing only in which structure mediates the mass-to-class comparison, then they **are** isomers: same atoms, different arrangement, different discriminating behaviour. That is the testable structural claim, and it needs a ground reading to decide.
- **Falsifier:** if any one of the three observables can be written without an atom the others require (for example a shadow observable with no mass, or a ringdown with no spin), the isomer claim fails and the functional-group reading stands instead.

Status: `specified`. Triangulated for the definitions (Fig 12 plus Operators); single-source for the physics mapping, still unmeasured.

### Q3 - What is the dependency order of the inference chain?

- Fig 3 :20: *"The chain is ordered by dependency, not by importance."* The five links are perception, vocabulary, objective, planning, learn and carry (Fig 3 :4-15).
- Applied: 1 **perception**, does any instrument return a reading that fails to resolve the classes (LIGO strain, EHT ring) : 2 **vocabulary**, do we have class-sensitive terms (mass, spin, tidal deformability, QNM frequency, shadow diameter) : 3 **objective**, what would count as a separation (non-overlapping distributions at stated confidence) : 4 **planning**, which instrument and which probe (next-generation detectors, source-discriminating observables) : 5 **learn and carry**, the public data release and the catalog.
- Fig 3 :27: *"A link with no instrument is not a link that works. It is a link nobody has looked at."* The third link, the objective, is the one most likely to be assumed rather than measured: it is tempting to assert a class without declaring what reading would have counted as a separation.
- Fig 4 :30: *"So take the round trip and measure the gap. Without it you are navigating by dead reckoning: confident about a position nobody checked."* Going from a single event to a population catalog is a lossy transform; the round-trip gap R_T between an event-level class assignment and a population-level claim must be reported, not assumed zero.
- **Falsifier:** if a classification is produced while links 1 or 3 are demonstrably absent, the chain is broken and the result is a reading of nothing (Fig 3 :22-23).

### Q4 - Union or partition across compact-object classes?

- Fig 9 :55: *"A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones."*
- Fig 9 :57: *"What remains is smaller and it is also categorically different: a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either."*
- "Other compact objects" is a union of neutron stars, white dwarfs and proposed exotic compact objects. A lever that separates black holes from neutron stars (tidal deformability) leaves the exotic-compact-object intersection standing, because a second condition still binds. Unbundle before searching again.
- The echoes review (Shen et al. 2026) is the world-side statement of the same union: attempts to mimic a horizon leave different high-overtone behaviour, so the exotic-compact-object class is a separate condition, not a harder neutron star.
- **Falsifier:** a single lever that clears every class intersection at once, with no categorically different residue, would collapse the union to a partition.

## CONCLUSION

A distinction is an ordered comparison at a threshold, not a symmetric join and not an identity proof (Fig 12 :87, :94). The candidate observables form a functional group as currently written and become a genuine isomer claim only over the full shared atom set, which needs a ground reading (Fig 12 :117, :124). The inference chain is ordered by dependency, with the objective link the one most likely to be assumed without an instrument (Fig 3 :20, :27), and the event-to-catalog transform carries a nonzero round-trip gap (Fig 4 :30). The classes form a union, so a discriminator against one class does not clear the others (Fig 9 :55, :57).

This framing keeps what is checkable (the bond type, the order, the union) and refuses what is not (a named categorical discriminator). No new primitive, no import that moves the wall, and no claim that the figures name an observable.
