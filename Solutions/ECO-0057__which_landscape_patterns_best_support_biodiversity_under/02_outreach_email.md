# Outreach draft - check the output before you decide whether the frame is worth reading

**Use:** one-to-one, or as a cover letter attached to a submission through the public venues in `03_public_contact_points.csv`. **Not** for bulk send. No automation. An opt-out line is included.

**Hard precondition:** do not send until the audit findings in `01_figure_grounded_audit.md` have been read by the sender. The stage-1 claim must survive validation or the two-stage offer collapses on contact.

---

**Subject:** One checkable claim about landscape patterns and climate, before any theory

> Hello [name],
>
> I have one specific output I would like you to check against what you already know. I am not
> asking you to read a framework first, and I am not asking you to agree with it. If the output
> does not survive, there is nothing else to look at.
>
> **The output (`specified`, not `grounded`).** The question "which landscape patterns best
> support biodiversity under changing climate?" asks for a ranking that holds while the climate
> changes. The derivation returns a shape instead:
>
> 1. The residual is per species and per time, not an aggregate biodiversity score. A single
>    landscape index averages away the population that says where to look.
> 2. A habitat is enumerated from contact outward, not composed from a recipe: actors,
>    conditions (temperature, water, season, terrain), and relations (pollination, predation,
>    competition, dependency), including effects two and three steps out.
> 3. The valence of any element is relative to the residual. A corridor, a large core, or a
>    refugium helps one species and harms another, and a change of climate is a change of the
>    residual. So no standing "best" list survives the change.
> 4. The problem is a union of at least three overlapping conditions: contact maintained now,
>    contact maintained under a shifted ground, and contact carried across scales. One lever
>    (more corridors, or bigger cores) clears the region where only one condition holds and
>    leaves the intersection standing, in a form that is categorically different.
> 5. The ground is the measured persistence outcome, not the climate projection and not the
>    number of reviews recommending a pattern. The specific ranking is left open: the framework
>    returns a shape, not a name.
>
> **What I am not claiming:** that connectivity is always good, that large cores beat small ones
> in general, or that heterogeneity is intrinsically beneficial. Those are bindings from the
> ecology frame onto a held vocabulary, and they change sign with the residual. I am also not
> claiming a new theory of landscape ecology, or a result settled by measurement.
>
> **What would refute the shape claim:**
> 1. A fixed, once-chosen pattern that keeps per-species persistence low and non-trending across
>    two or more documented climate states, measured at the ground, with no re-ranking.
> 2. A pattern that improves persistence while leaving contact with the ground unchanged.
> 3. A single intervention that clears all three conditions and leaves no categorically
>    different residue.
> 4. Agreement among reviews sharing one evidence pool accepted as settling the ranking without
>    a ground measurement.
>
> I wrote these down before sending this. If any one holds, I will narrow or withdraw the claim.
> A one-line "this fails because..." is more useful to me than agreement. If you would rather
> not engage, no reply is needed and I will not follow up.
>
> **If it survives, and only then:** the sources are below. The figures, not my summary of them,
> are the authority.
> - Verbatim figure text with line numbers (local extract):
>   `FRAMEWORK/reference/03-FIGURES_TEXT.md`
> - Canonical usage rule (local copy):
>   `FRAMEWORK/reference/FIGURES_README_canonical.md`
> - The eight-step loop and the mode rule (local):
>   `FRAMEWORK/figures/THE_FORMULA.md`
>
> **If you want to use the machinery:** the formula is in the figures link. Run it on a landscape
> problem you know well and can check against a ground. Write down what would refute your result
> before you run it. A reported failure is more useful to me than a reported success.
>
> If you do not want further messages from me, reply "stop" and I will remove you from any list
> I keep.
>

## Why this version is ordered this way

| the move | figure-grounded reason |
|---|---|
| Output first, theory second | Fig 10 :52 - *"Bring a characterised residual, never the source."* The recipient checks the claim without holding the framework's interior. |
| Do not ask for agreement | Fig 2 :35 - *"Alignment is triangulation, not negotiation. A mirror cannot correct you."* |
| Pre-registered refuter | Fig 10 :59 - *"an interest in the outcome is unavoidable, an interest in the reading is disqualifying."* Writing the refuter first declares the stake. |
| Mode on every claim | `THE_FORMULA.md` - *"Mode. general · specified · grounded. State which."* |
| No pattern name asserted | Fig 9 :32 - *"you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself."* |
| Sources only after the verdict | Fig 10 :42 - *"A verifier that can reconstruct the claim cannot verify it."* |
| No request for trust or prestige | Prestige can fund a trial and cannot settle it. Only the ground settles an action. |
| One-to-one, opt-out, no bulk | Fig 2 :40 - agreement from a shared pool is disqualifying for a verdict; a purchased list is a shared pool with a subject line. |
| Offer the formula as a method | Fig 4 :22 - *"Only methods cross up. Only head starts come down."* |
| Ask each user for their own ground and refuter | `THE_FORMULA.md` "Direction": a frame cannot check the direction of its own question. |

## Note on who should receive this

The first contact is not chosen for receptiveness. The residual selects the members: public, checkable work that touches per-species persistence, habitat enumeration, cascade effects, or the union of area and connectivity under climate change. Contact cost is recorded in a separate column from evidence and never used as a verdict weight. A Track B reply is a conversation, never corroboration.
