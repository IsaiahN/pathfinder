# Expert verification packet - the Tether on landscape patterns and biodiversity under climate change

**What this is:** a claim in a form a landscape ecologist or conservation biologist can check. Mode, derivation and refuters are stated before the conclusion, so a reader knows what to look for and what would settle it.

**Mode: `specified` on the ecological claims, `general` on the structural shape. Not `grounded`.** No field measurement is claimed. This is a shape and a route, not a result. Simulated derivation checked for internal consistency against the figures, not a ground reading.

## 1. The claim, in one paragraph

The question "which landscape patterns best support biodiversity under changing climate?" asks for a standing ranking. The derivation returns a shape and refuses the ranking. The shape: score per species and per climate state, never as one index; enumerate the habitat from contact outward, including second- and third-order effects; treat the value of corridors, cores, stepping stones and refugia as relative to the species residual, so a change of climate changes the ranking; treat the problem as a union of contact-now, contact-under-shift and cross-scale contact, which one lever cannot clear; treat implementation as permanent work rather than a solved design; and let the ground be measured persistence, not a suitability map or a count of reviews. No figure line names a winning pattern, so the ranking is declared a `gap`. Five refuters were written before the run.

## 2. Three checkable sub-claims

### C1 - framework-internal; checkable by reading Figure 11

- **Statement:** there is no standing list of good landscape patterns across a change of residual. Element valence is relative to the residual.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 11 :49 - *"An actor’s valence is relative to the residual, not a property it carries."* Fig 11 :50 - *"The same actor is a benefactor to one residual and a malfactor to another, so mutualism and parasitism are relations rather than kinds."* Fig 11 :51 - *"No standing list of good actors survives a change of residual."*
- **What to check:** is a change of climate a change of residual for the species you know? If a corridor or a core keeps its sign across a documented shift, this is the place to say so.
- **Refuter:** exhibit a fixed, once-chosen pattern whose per-species persistence residual stays low and non-trending across two or more documented climate states, measured at the ground, with no re-ranking.

### C2 - ecology-facing; checkable by a landscape ecologist against the field's own results

- **Statement:** the problem is a union of overlapping conditions, not a partition, so a single lever leaves a categorically different residue. Per-slot indexing is required.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 9 :55 - *"A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones."* Fig 9 :57 - *"What remains is smaller and it is also categorically different: a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either."* Fig 1 :19 - *"The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated."*
- **What to check:** Ranius et al. 2022 found the review recommendations *"are not always consistent with each other"* and state that which strategy is best depends on disturbance extent, dispersal biology and habitat requirements. Keeley et al. 2018 attach a disadvantage to every connectivity strategy. Macdonald et al. 2025 found corridors effective only at high dispersal thresholds. Do those results support a union reading, or can one lever clear all the conditions?
- **Refuter:** a single intervention that clears contact-now, contact-under-shift and cross-scale contact and leaves no categorically different residue, measured at the ground; or a demonstration that a single aggregated biodiversity index predicts every species slot's response.

### C3 - framework-internal and field-facing; checkable against substitution cases

- **Statement:** a landscape intervention substitutes a habitat rather than adding to a neutral background. It inherits unchosen actors and can fail to reproduce what the goal required, both invisible until late. Contact cascades beyond direct adjacency.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 11 :66 - *"Isolation is not removal of the habitat. It is substitution of one habitat for another."* Fig 11 :68 - *"what you failed to reproduce is invisible until the goal fails."* Fig 11 :69 - *"what you unintentionally introduced is invisible until it acts."* Fig 11 :52 - *"And contact cascades. Scoping the habitat to direct contact is how DDT reached the eagles: first-order enumeration, second-order effect, and the residual that mattered was three steps out."*
- **What to check:** in a corridor or reserve case you know, were the introduced actors enumerated before they acted? Was everything the target species needed reproduced? Was the measured effect first-order or three steps out?
- **Refuter:** a substituted habitat that preserves persistence with no unchosen actor ever acting, nothing from the goal missing at failure, and no measurable second- or third-order contribution.

## 3. What is explicitly not claimed

- Not that connectivity is always good, that large cores beat small ones, or that heterogeneity is intrinsically beneficial. These are bindings from the ecology frame whose sign depends on the residual.
- Not that no pattern is good. The `gap` is about a standing ranking, not about value.
- Not that Fig 11's habitat is the ecological niche. The canonical article declines that mapping: *"A niche is defined by an organism's relation to its environment. A habitat here is defined by contact with a residual, and the residual moves so the same actors constitute a different habitat when the question changes."*
- Not a new metric, not a seventh habitat primitive, not a derivation of ecology from the six-term substrate.
- Not `grounded`. Nothing here is asserted as settled by a field measurement.

## 5. What would settle it

A per-species, per-climate instrument, not a further argument. From *The Hard Problem is About Source*: an instrument that reads green *"cannot comment on the source"*; the spectrometer resolved it. Here:

1. **Per-slot persistence:** for each named species and climate state, is `R_s` low and non-trending, measured rather than projected?
2. **Contact test:** does the pattern change contact with what the species needs, or only the species in isolation? Fig 11 :40 - *"An improvement that does not change contact changes nothing, however much it improves."*
3. **Union test:** after one lever, is the residue categorically different? Fig 9 :57.
4. **Round trip:** what is `R_T` for patch to region and back? Fig 4 :30 - *"So take the round trip and measure the gap. Without it you are navigating by dead reckoning: confident about a position nobody checked."*

The five pre-registered refuters in `01_figure_grounded_audit.md` §3 remain live. Any one firing narrows or withdraws the shape.

## 6. What an expert can check in twenty minutes

1. Read Fig 11 :49–51, :66–69 and Fig 9 :55–57. Six short passages.
2. Check C2 against one case you know: did a single lever clear all three conditions, or was the residue categorically different?
3. Name one instrument that would measure per-species persistence under a named climate shift, or say why none exists. If you can name one, the `gap` can start closing.
4. Send the refutation. A failure is more useful than an endorsement.
