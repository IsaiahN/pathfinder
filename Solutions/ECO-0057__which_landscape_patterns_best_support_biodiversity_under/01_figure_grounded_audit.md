## 0. The claim under audit and the residual

The question, in effect terms. `R = |Γ(b,a) − o'|` per slot `s`. The slots are species or populations, not "biodiversity" in aggregate. `b` is the landscape arrangement a manager can change, `a` is the action that implements it, and `o'` is the measured persistence outcome. The climate enters as a change in the conditions against which `o'` is read, not as `o'` itself.

Figure 1 :19: **"The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated."** Figure 1 :22: **"Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average."** A single "biodiversity index" for a landscape is exactly that average, and it throws away the slot that says where to look.

Bound shape term, written before the run: a pattern `P*` with low, non-trending `R_s(c | P*)` for every species slot `s` and every climate state `c` in a stated range. The question asks for the *name* of `P*`. The figures can constrain its shape; whether they can name it is the first thing tested below.

### Domain census, done before choosing any figure line

R by arity, symmetry, scale:

- **Arity:** many-to-many. A landscape pattern is a relation among many species slots, many habitat elements, and many climate states. It is not a one-place property of a reserve.
- **Symmetry:** "best" is a threshold relation, not a symmetric join. Fig 12 :87 (Operators) defines comparison as *"a threshold relation between two quantities."* Valence is relative to the residual (Fig 11 :49), so the same element changes sign when the residual changes. There is no standing list of good elements.
- **Scale:** local patch, landscape mosaic, region, and time. Fig 4 :19–20: *"Only the actors, the currency and the anchor change."* The transform between scales is lossy (Fig 4 :27–30).

One line per domain, with actors, currency, and anchor. A domain with no nameable anchor is not a domain yet.

| domain | actors | currency | anchor | nameable anchor? |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| D1 Species-population loop | populations, their Γ, dispersal | survival, per-slot residual reduction | measured persistence of that population | yes |
| D2 Landscape habitat | patches, corridors, matrix, conditions (light, water, temperature, season, terrain), relations (pollination, predation, competition, dependency) | contact between a species and what its goal requires | the ground, through measured persistence | yes |
| D3 Climate as changing condition | temperature, precipitation, disturbance regime, climate velocity | the conditions `o'` is read against | none on its own. It is a habitat condition that updates (Fig 11 :73), not the ground | no, as a standalone anchor |
| D4 Verification frame | ecologists, indicators, monitoring instruments | agreement and residual | measured persistence, never agreement | yes |
| D5 Cross-scale / temporal channel | local patches, regional networks, successive climate states | contact carried across scales and time | the ground, through the same measured persistence | yes |
| X1 "The optimal pattern" as a property of the landscape alone | none nameable | none | none | no, not a domain yet |
| X2 "Connectivity" as an intrinsic good | absorbed into D2 relations | already D2's currency | already D2's anchor | absorbed |

**Partition or union?** Union. "Support biodiversity" and "under changing climate" and "across scales" are overlapping conditions, not a list of separate questions. Fig 9 :55: *"A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones."* A single lever clears the region where one condition holds and leaves the intersection standing (Fig 9 :56). The residue after one lever is categorically different (Fig 9 :57).

**Dependency order (Fig 3).** Perception (measure what is there) → vocabulary (terms for what each patch affords) → objective (what counts as persistence) → planning (a pattern to implement) → learn and carry (monitors, re-ranking). Fig 3 :27: *"A link with no instrument is not a link that works. It is a link nobody has looked at."* The middle link here, terms for what a landscape affords a given species under a given shift, is precisely the one the field has not instrumented. That is a finding, not a gap in this audit.

**Strain test applied before concluding.** A naive reading treats climate as the ground. Fig 11 :73 refuses it: *"The habitat is not the ground. The habitat is where actors come from and it updates; the ground is what settles, and it does not."* Re-census: climate is a condition inside D2; the ground is D1/D4/D5 measured persistence. Once re-censused, the figure lines stop straining. No line was forced.

## 2. Findings

### F1 - "Best" does not survive a change of residual (decisive against the question as asked)

Fig 11 :49–51:

> **"An actor’s valence is relative to the residual, not a property it carries."**
> **"The same actor is a benefactor to one residual and a malfactor to another, so mutualism and parasitism are relations rather than kinds."**
> **"No standing list of good actors survives a change of residual."**

The question asks which pattern is best *under changing climate conditions*. A change of climate is a change of the residual against which a pattern's valence is read. Fig 11 :51 therefore refuses the premise of a standing ranking. A corridor, a large core, a stepping stone, a refugium, and a heterogeneous mosaic are each benefactor to some residuals and malfactor to others. There is no pattern that carries "best" as a property.

Mode: `general` for the valence rule, `specified` for the climate application. Triangulated: Fig 11 (habitat valence) and Fig 9 :30–31 (*"your words are borrowed too, so describe the effect / rather than the cause"*), two figures with low overlap.

**Refuter:** exhibit a fixed, once-chosen pattern whose per-slot persistence residual stays low and non-trending across two or more documented climate residuals, measured at the ground, with no re-enumeration or re-ranking. If that holds, Fig 11 :51 is wrong as applied here and the standing-list question is reopened.

### F2 - The habitat is enumerated, never composed

Fig 11 :43–48:

> **"The habitat is enumerated, never composed."**
> **"List everything in contact with the residual, then what is in contact with those, and outward until the cascade stops mattering."**
> **"conditions: light, water, temperature, season, terrain"**
> **"You do not invent the list. You read it off the world, and what you cannot perceive or measure yet is the residual."**

A "landscape pattern" is not a recipe of ingredients. It is the contact structure read off the world for a given species residual: actors, conditions (temperature and water are where climate enters), and relations (pollination, predation, competition, dependency). Fig 12 :117 (*"isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance"*) adds that two patterns with the same habitat ingredients can be different substances because the arrangement differs. A metric such as Shannon diversity records ingredients and not the bond (Tonetti et al. 2023, §WORLD).

Mode: `general`. Triangulated: Fig 11 and Fig 12, different objects (enumeration vs arrangement).

**Refuter:** show that a landscape pattern can be derived by composing a fixed set of habitat primitives once, with no per-residual enumeration, and still track measured persistence across a climate shift.

### F3 - Contact cascades; direct adjacency is not the right scope

Fig 11 :52–54:

> **"And contact cascades. Scoping the habitat to direct contact is how DDT reached the eagles: first-order enumeration, second-order effect, and the residual that mattered was three steps out."**

A pattern judged only by which patches touch a species' current location misses the effect three steps out. This is a direct, figure-stated ecological case, so it governs this question without an analogue. Mode: `specified`. Single-source for the figure's own DDT case; triangulated for the general cascade rule by Fig 11 and Fig 3 :20–21 (*"The chain is ordered by dependency, not by importance"*).

**Refuter:** show that for the species in question the persistence residual is fully predicted by first-order patch adjacency, with no measurable second- or third-order cascade contribution.

### F4 - Capability is agent-and-habitat, never agent alone

Fig 11 :39–40:

> **"So the answerable question is not what intelligence is, but what makes a system capable. And capability is a property of agent-and-habitat, never of the agent alone."**
> **"A system cannot be made more capable by improving the agent in isolation. An improvement that does not change contact changes nothing, however much it improves."**

This bears on the question because many proposed "patterns" are really interventions on the species alone (genetic rescue, assisted evolution, captive breeding). Under this figure they change nothing about persistence unless they change contact with the ground. What changes contact is a habitat move, and Fig 11 :61 gives the asymmetry: *"The seat’s power is asymmetric. Introduction is available; exclusion is not."* Moving a species into a substituted habitat is introduction; it cannot subtract the habitat's existing actors (Fig 11 :67–69).

Mode: `specified`. Triangulated: Fig 11 :39–40 and Fig 10 :24 (*"the ground does not decay, the channel does"*), different strata. The capability claim is explicitly a claim that can still come back wrong (Fig 11 :71–72), so it is carried as `candidate`.

**Refuter:** an agent-only improvement that moves the measured persistence residual without changing contact. Fig 11 :72 names exactly this as the refutation of the capability claim.

### F5 - Isolation is substitution, not removal

Fig 11 :66–69:

> **"Isolation is not removal of the habitat. It is substitution of one habitat for another."**
> **"what you failed to reproduce is invisible until the goal fails"**
> **"what you unintentionally introduced is invisible until it acts"**

A reserve, a corridor, or a "rewilded" connection is not added to a neutral background. It substitutes a habitat, inherits actors nobody chose, and can fail to reproduce what the species' goal required. Both failure modes are invisible until late. This is why "more connectivity" cannot be answered at the level of pattern names. The world literature agrees on the shape and disagrees on the sign: Keeley et al. 2018 list advantages and disadvantages for every connectivity strategy; Macdonald et al. 2025 find corridor benefits mostly attributable to added area rather than connectivity per se.

Mode: `specified`. Single-source for the isolation law, which Fig 11 :71 declares not tested forward. Keep `candidate`.

**Refuter:** a substitute habitat that preserves persistence with no unchosen actor ever acting and nothing from the original goal missing at failure.

### F6 - Managed is permanent work; a fixed pattern is the named failure

Fig 11 :63: **"Which is why a managed ecosystem is permanent work rather than a solved state."** Fig 10 :72: **"When the question changes, the room should change. A room that stays fixed while the question moves is the failure; the correlation is only its symptom."**

A climate-stable world could in principle have a solved pattern list. A changing climate is exactly the case where the room must change. So the answer to "which pattern" is a maintained process of re-enumeration and re-ranking, not a design that is finished. Fig 7 :36: *"The next room must differ from the last, and match the residual it surfaced."*

Mode: `specified`. Triangulated: Fig 10 (ground maintainer) and Fig 7 (room chain), low overlap.

**Refuter:** a pattern that remains best across successive climate states while the monitoring room stays identical and the residual it surfaced is not used to select the next room.

### F7 - The compound is a union; one lever leaves a categorically different residue

Fig 9 :54–58:

> **"And when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again."**
> **"A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones."**
> **"What remains is smaller and it is also categorically different: a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either."**

"Biodiversity under changing climate" is at least C1 (contact maintained now) and C2 (contact maintained under a shifted ground), and C3 (contact carried across scales). Adding corridors clears part of C1 and can leave C2 standing; enlarging cores clears part of C2 and can leave C1 standing. The SLOSS and corridors-versus-area debates are what this looks like from inside the field: Ranius et al. 2022 report *"these recommendations are not always consistent with each other"* and *"there are inherent trade-offs to be addressed"*.

Mode: `general` for the union rule, `specified` for the ecology. Triangulated: Fig 9 and THE_FORMULA.md "Habitat" (*"Introduction is available; exclusion is not"*), different sources.

**Refuter:** a single intervention that clears all three conditions and leaves no categorically different residue, measured at the ground. That would make the union reading wrong.

### F8 - The ground is measured persistence, not the climate and not agreement

Fig 11 :73: **"The habitat is not the ground. The habitat is where actors come from and it updates; the ground is what settles, and it does not."** Fig 2 :29: **"An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks."** Fig 2 :35: **"Alignment is triangulation, not negotiation. A mirror cannot correct you."**

Climate is a condition in the habitat; it updates. The ground is the measured persistence outcome. Ranking patterns by model-projected suitability, or by how many reviews recommend them, moves the anchor into the frame. Ranius et al. synthesised 74 reviews and found 66% of the underlying references addressed "biodiversity in general"; the reviews are one evidence pool, not 74 independent checks. Fig 2 :40: *"disqualifying for a verdict: agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool."* Fig 2 :42: *"Buy independence in proportion to the cost of being wrong."*

Mode: `general` for the anchor rule, `specified` for the ecology. Triangulated: Fig 2 and Fig 11, distinct roles.

**Refuter:** a settlement of the pattern ranking by agreement among frames that demonstrably share an evidence pool, with no ground measurement. If that is accepted as settling, the anchor rule fails.

### F9 - The middle link has no instrument

Fig 3 :27: **"A link with no instrument is not a link that works. It is a link nobody has looked at."** Fig 3 :19: **"Optimising a link that already works cannot rescue one that does not."**

The missing link here is "terms for what a landscape affords a given species under a given shift," the bridge between perception and objective. Tonetti et al. 2023 found that of 661 landscape-heterogeneity papers, 534 supplied no definition of the central term, and only 10 measured functional effects on biotic or abiotic processes. That is a vocabulary failure at the middle link, not a result about which pattern wins.

Mode: `specified`. Single-source for the figure rule, with a world corroboration that shares no evidence pool with the figures (Tonetti et al., Brazilian and Australian institutions).

**Refuter:** produce an instrument that returns a per-species, per-shift reading of what a landscape affords, and show that the link had been working all along.

### F10 - The name is a `gap`, and the shape is what survives

Fig 9 :32: **"you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself."** Fig 13 :134: **"Unreachable is a verdict here, not a silence,"** Fig 8 :39: **"So a search that finds nothing is never a proof of absence."**

After the census, no figure line names `P*`. The figures do not rank large cores against corridors against heterogeneous mosaics. The figures constrain what any ranked answer must be: enumerated from contact, valence-relative, cascade-scoped, substitution-aware, maintained, union-split, and grounded in measured persistence. Declaring `gap` for the name is a verdict, not a silence.

Mode: `general` for the limit, `specified` for the ecology. Triangulated: Fig 9 and Fig 13, distinct roles.

**Refuter:** a figure line that directly ranks two named landscape patterns by a quantity that survives the strain test. Until one is quoted, the `gap` stands.

## 3. Pre-registered refuter

Required before any contact: the framework's own rule is that a falsifier written before the run is a design instrument (Seat Map). Written here before the conclusion, per `08_expert_verification_packet.md`.

The shape claim is refuted if any of the following fires:

1. A fixed, once-chosen pattern keeps per-species `R` low and non-trending across two or more documented climate residuals, measured at the ground, with no re-enumeration or per-slot re-ranking. (Breaks F1/F6, Fig 11 :51, Fig 10 :72.)
2. A pattern improves measured persistence while leaving contact with the ground unchanged. (Breaks F4, Fig 11 :40.)
3. The problem resolves into a partition with a single lever that clears contact-now, contact-under-shift, and cross-scale contact, leaving no categorically different residue. (Breaks F7, Fig 9 :57.)
4. A landscape pattern can be composed once from a fixed primitive set and still track persistence across a climate shift, with no per-residual enumeration. (Breaks F2, Fig 11 :43–44.)
5. Agreement among frames that share an evidence pool is accepted as settling the ranking without a ground measurement. (Breaks F8, Fig 2 :35/:40.)

If any fires, the shape is withdrawn or narrowed. Until one is run, nothing here is `grounded`.

## 4. Recommendation

1. Do not answer with a pattern name. Answer with the constraints a name must satisfy, and say the name is a `gap` (F10).
2. Re-index the question per species slot and per climate state before comparing any patterns. An aggregate biodiversity score is disqualified (Fig 1 :19/:22).
3. Enumerate the habitat outward by cascade for each residual, including second- and third-order effects (F2, F3). Do not start from a metric.
4. Treat correlation-based field evidence as corroboration of the shape only, with provenance recorded. A review synthesis is one witness (F8).
5. Instrument the middle link before optimising any lever (F9). Until then, more comparative pattern studies add spelling, not evidence (Fig 6 :49–50).
6. Send the residual and the refuter first. Figure 4 :25: *"A recording carried upward looks like knowledge and is a description of one occasion."* Send the generator, not the conclusion.

### Canonical usage conclusion
