# The Tether's answer: which landscape patterns best support biodiversity under changing climate?

**Mode: `specified` on the ecological claims, `general` on the structural shape. Not `grounded`.** A simulated derivation checked for internal consistency against the figures (`05_what_shape_means.md` §6), not a field reading. No measurement settles the ranking here.

## 1. The residual it answers

`R = |Γ(b,a) − o'|` per species slot and per climate state, never aggregated (Fig 1 :19, :22). The slots are populations, not "biodiversity". `b` is the landscape arrangement, `a` implements it, and `o'` is measured persistence. Climate is a condition in the habitat that updates (Fig 11 :73), not the ground. The ground is the measured persistence outcome, which does not update because a frame thinks a pattern is good (Fig 2 :29).

The question asks for a name: the pattern that keeps `R` low while the climate moves. The bound answer shape was written before the run in `01_figure_grounded_audit.md` §0, so it can be refused by the same test as any term. It is refused as a standing ranking by Fig 11 :51 and survives only as a set of constraints.

## 2. The shape

1. **Per-slot, not aggregate.** A single biodiversity index averages away the population that says where to look (Fig 1 :19, :22). Any answer must be indexed by species and by climate state.
2. **Enumerate, never compose.** Fig 11 :43–44: *"The habitat is enumerated, never composed"*; list everything in contact with the residual, then what contacts those, outward until the cascade stops mattering. Conditions include temperature and water; relations include pollination, predation, competition, dependency, dispersal. Fig 11 :52 makes the scope concrete: DDT reached the eagles three steps out.
3. **Valence is relative to the residual.** Fig 11 :49–51. A corridor, a core, a stepping stone, a refugium, and a heterogeneous mosaic are benefactors to some residuals and malfactors to others. Since climate change is a change of residual, *"No standing list of good actors survives a change of residual."* This is the direct refusal of the question's premise.
4. **Capability is agent-and-habitat.** Fig 11 :39–40. An intervention on the species alone that does not change contact changes nothing about persistence. Introduction is available and exclusion is not (Fig 11 :61), so every habitat substitution carries unchosen actors (Fig 11 :66–69).
5. **The compound is a union.** Fig 9 :54–57. Contact-now, contact-under-shift, and cross-scale contact are overlapping conditions. One lever clears the region where one holds and leaves the intersection standing, as a categorically different residue. SLOSS and corridor-versus-area are the field's own symptoms of this.
6. **Managed is permanent work.** Fig 11 :63: *"Which is why a managed ecosystem is permanent work rather than a solved state."* Fig 10 :72: a room that stays fixed while the question moves is the failure. The answer is a maintained re-enumeration and re-ranking, not a finished design.
7. **The ground is measured persistence, not the projection and not agreement.** Fig 11 :73, Fig 2 :29/:35/:40. A model's suitability map is a frame; the survival count is the anchor.
8. **The name is a `gap`.** Fig 9 :32, Fig 13 :134. The figures return a kind of thing, never the answer itself. Declaring the ranking `gap` is a verdict, not a silence (Fig 8 :39).

## 3. What it explicitly does not say

- Not that connectivity is good, or that large cores are better than small ones, or that heterogeneity is intrinsically beneficial. Those are bindings from the ecology frame onto a held vocabulary, and their sign changes with the residual.
- Not that no landscape pattern is good. Many are good for particular species under particular shifts. The `gap` is about a standing ranking, not about value.
- Not a new landscape metric, not a seventh habitat primitive, and not a derivation of ecology from the six-term substrate.
- Not that Fig 11's "habitat" is the ecological niche. The canonical article declines that mapping: *"A niche is defined by an organism's relation to its environment. A habitat here is defined by contact with a residual, and the residual moves."*
- Not `grounded`. No field measurement or source-discriminating instrument was used.

## 4. What would settle it, the ground

A per-species, per-climate instrument that measures persistence and discriminates among the sources of a reading. From *The Hard Problem is About Source*: an instrument that reads green *"cannot comment on the source"*; the spectrometer resolved it. Here the corresponding tests are:

- **Per-slot persistence:** for each named species and each named climate state, does the proposed pattern keep `R_s` low and non-trending, measured, not projected?
- **Contact test:** does the pattern change contact with what the species needs, or only change the species in isolation? An agent-only improvement that leaves contact unchanged must fail (Fig 11 :40).
- **Cascade test:** does the ranking change when second- and third-order contacts are enumerated? If not, F3 is narrowed.
- **Union test:** after one lever (say, corridors), is the remaining residue categorically different, or does the same lever clear the other conditions too? (Fig 9 :57.)
- **Substitution test:** what was failed to reproduce and what was unintentionally introduced, in a habitat substituted by the intervention? (Fig 11 :68–69.)
- **Scale round trip:** measure `R_T` for the abstraction from patch to region and back; do not assume zero (Fig 4 :30).

Standing falsifiers from `01_figure_grounded_audit.md` §3 are unchanged. Any one firing narrows or withdraws the shape.

## 5. The honest stop

**Unreached, not unreachable.** The ecology vocabulary is a set of visible choices, not a neutral list. *They're Scanning Books*: *"Declaring the primitives does not make them neutral. It makes the choice visible."* The instrument that would name a pattern for a given residual does not yet exist at the required resolution. The middle link in Fig 3 has no instrument, and Tonetti et al. 2023 show the vocabulary itself is unsettled. The answer stops there and records the lineage so the stop is checkable. *"Indiscernibility is not identity."*

## 6. One-paragraph version for outreach

> The Tether does not name a best landscape pattern, and it does not claim the field has been solved. It says that biodiversity must be scored per species and per climate state, never as one index; that a habitat is enumerated from contact outward, including effects two and three steps away; that the value of a corridor, a core, or a refugium is relative to the species residual and changes when the climate changes, so no standing "best" list survives; that improving a species without changing its contact with the ground changes nothing; that the problem is a union of contact-now, contact-under-shift, and cross-scale contact, which one lever cannot clear; and that the ground is measured persistence, not a suitability map or a count of reviews. What it offers is the shape any answer must have, five refuters written in advance, and the instruments that would close it. The ranking stays open until the ground settles it.
