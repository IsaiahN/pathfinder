# Supporting derivation: union, isomer, and dependency order

**Mode: `general` for the structural claims, `specified` for the falsifier.** This is a simulated derivation checked against the figures, not a ground reading (see `05_what_shape_means.md` §6).

## RESIDUAL

The primary audit separates the classes by objective link and contact. Two supporting questions remain: (1) is the tool/agent split a partition or a union, and (2) can the two be written over the same vocabulary with a different bond, so that the difference is structural rather than a difference of ingredients? A third question follows: in what order must the checks be run?

## DERIVATION

### Q1 — Partition or union?

- Fig 9 :54: "when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again."
- Fig 9 :57: "a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either."
- A system can follow an instruction and also hold an aim in its own vocabulary. The same system can be tool-like on one slot and agent-like on another. The classes therefore overlap; they are a **union compound**, not a partition.
- **Consequence:** a single lever (for example, "does it have a goal term?") clears the region where only one condition holds and leaves the intersections standing, because the other condition still binds. The unbundled conditions are: objective held in own vocabulary; channel maintained to ground; live slot retained after cut. Each gets its own loop.
- **Falsifier, written before the run:** exhibit a case where the two conditions never co-occur in any system, at any scale, so that the split is genuinely a partition. Then the union derivation is unnecessary.

### Q2 — Are tool and agent isomers?

- Fig 12 :117: "Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."
- Fig 12 :112: "A plan is a sequence. A goal is a comparison. Progress is a subtraction."
- Operators table :52: "a goal is a comparison, which is why a vocabulary without it cannot state one"; Operators table :46: "progress is a subtraction, which is why a vocabulary without it cannot say how far along it is".
- Write the two systems over the same atom set: percepts, library Γ, actions, ground, R per slot. The difference is the bond that holds the aim.
  - Tool: the ⋛ comparison and the − subtraction that define progress are bound in the instructor's vocabulary; the system's own Γ carries → (sequence) from percept to action, and the aim is an adopted import with no provenance check inside the system.
  - Agent: the ⋛ and − are bound in the system's own Γ; the aim is derivable inside closure(Γ) and survives when the external instruction stream is removed.
- Same atoms, different bond placement, different behaviour at the channel cut. By Fig 12 :117 that is an **isomer** claim. The difference is "invisible in the formula" if one records only the ingredients (percepts, actions, library) and not the bonds that hold the aim.
- **Caveat, not hidden:** the framework's isomer definition is about the bond between operands. Here the difference is partly about *where a term is bound* (inside Γ versus in the instructor), which is provenance rather than bond type. The claim is therefore an isomer reading `specified` and unmeasured, not a settled derivation. If the checker shows the two systems also differ in atoms, the isomer claim fails and the weaker reading is a functional group: both are loops with an aim, with different operand sets.
- **Falsifier:** if tool and agent can be written only with different atoms (for example one has no comparison operator at all), they are not isomers and Fig 12 :117 does not apply. Note this does not hurt the primary audit: F1 already predicts that the tool's Γ need not contain the comparison.

### Q3 — What is the order of the checks?

- Fig 3 :20: "The chain is ordered by dependency, not by importance."
- Fig 3 :10: "it can say what would count as progress here, in its own terms" is link 3, the objective. It consumes perception and vocabulary, and planning consumes it.
- Formula step 2 places REFUTED before MINT: a term held is false, and what is owed is a refusal. Applied here, the label "agent" must be refused as a classifier before any new definition is minted.
- Fig 9 :43: "The verdict tells you to leave. The lookup tells you where to go. Elimination is what you do after you arrive."
- Therefore the order is: (1) refuse the name as classifier (D3); (2) check the objective link in the system's own vocabulary (D1); (3) cut the channel and check per-slot residual (D2 operational test); (4) only then discuss what to call the system. Running the tests in any other order lets the label smuggle in the verdict.
- Fig 1 :22: "Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average." The per-slot check must not be replaced by a global success score.

### Q4 — Does the channel cut change the habitat?

- Fig 11 :66: "Isolation is not removal of the habitat. It is substitution of one habitat for another."
- Fig 11 :70: "A test harness is a substituted habitat. Which is why a synthetic solve proves wiring and never capability."
- Cutting the instruction stream is not the same as removing all contact. The ground, the conditions and the other actors remain; only one actor (the instructor) is withdrawn. To treat the cut as isolation would be to substitute a new habitat and then claim the original classification, which the figure forbids.
- **Operational requirement:** record the habitat before and after the cut, and name what was introduced or withdrawn. A synthetic channel-cut test proves wiring, not capability, unless the ground is the same ground.

## CONCLUSION

The split is a union compound and must be unbundled into three conditions, each with its own loop. Tool and agent can be read as isomers over a shared atom set, with the difference in where the comparison and subtraction are bound; this is `specified`, unmeasured, and fails if the atoms differ. The checks must run in dependency order: refuse the label, check the objective link, then cut the channel per slot. The channel cut is a withdrawal of one actor, not an isolation, and the habitat must be recorded on both sides. No new primitive is used in any of these steps.
