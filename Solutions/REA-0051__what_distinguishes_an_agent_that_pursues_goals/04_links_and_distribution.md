## What in the wider literature bears on this question

1. **The name is field-relative.** In engineering a thermostat is an intelligent agent; in principal-agent economics an agent is one who acts for another. These are not two claims about the same class, they are two uses of one word. This is why the derivation refuses to classify by name and asks for a shape in effect terms (F3). The two sources are low-overlap frames (AI engineering and institutional economics), so their disagreement is informative, but they are both secondary: the verdict comes from Fig 11.
2. **The intentional stance is observer-relative.** Dennett's quote that "the critical feature is that the observer ascribes them to the agent" is a direct corroboration of F5: agreement about agency is an ascription, and ascriptions are not anchors. His thermostat case corroborates the dissolution: if the ascription gives no traction, the design stance is the right one. Corroboration only; Fig 2 :35 decides.
3. **Subjective agency is not an external test.** The sense-of-agency literature measures ownership and agency separately and notes the difficulty of measurement. This supports the choice of an external, channel-cut test rather than a self-report criterion, without importing the framework's vocabulary into the lab.
4. **Self-maintenance versus producing something other than itself.** Autopoiesis gives a low-overlap biological analogue of F2 (contact and organisation, not interior). Its allopoietic contrast, a factory producing a car rather than itself, is an instance of the tool/agent shape. The Haraway criticism is recorded honestly: the analogue is a parallel, not a derivation, and the figure verdict does not depend on it.
5. **Prestige and agreement are not validators.** The Recursive Self-Improvement Trap gives the strongest world-side support for the two-stage order: "You may all agree and be wrong." Same author as the figures, so overlap is high and the corroboration is weak.

No world source overrules a figure line. The engineering and economics sources appear to conflict with each other, but the conflict was sent back to the census and resolved there (see `01_figure_grounded_audit.md` §6): it supports the dissolution rather than flipping the verdict. No source conflicts with the figure verdict itself.
