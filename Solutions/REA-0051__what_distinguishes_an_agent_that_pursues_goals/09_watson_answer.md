## QUESTION

Decide, in effect terms, what distinguishes a system that pursues goals from one that follows instructions. Option A: an intrinsic difference, a goal or intention inside the agent and absent from the tool. Option B: a provenance difference, the aim comes from outside in the tool. Option C, the derived shape: the difference is a contact fact about system and habitat, namely whether the comparison that defines progress is bound in the system's own vocabulary and whether its channel to the ground survives the instructor's removal. What each leads to: A gives a name no outside checker can measure; B collapses whenever an instructor supplies an aim the system also holds; C gives a shape, a channel-cut test, written refuters, and an honest stop. Question ID REA-0051, source `reasoning.txt` line 64, section 6.

## VERDICT (from the figures)

The shape survives, the names do not. The objective link is where the classes separate: a goal is a comparison and progress is a subtraction, so the discriminator is whether those bonds are held in the system's own vocabulary. Capability is a property of agent-and-habitat, so the channel to the ground, not an interior essence, is the contact point. The question "what distinguishes an agent from a tool" is a substrate question in the "what is intelligence" family and is dissolved in favour of "what makes a system capable". The operational test is to cut the instruction stream, keep the ground fixed, and read one slot: a persisting aim-like residual means the aim was held; a collapsed aim means it was borrowed. Over a shared atom set the two can be read as isomers with the difference in bond placement, which is `specified` and unmeasured. No new primitive is required. This is a `general` shape with one `specified` test, a simulated derivation checked for internal consistency against the figures, not a ground reading. A label, a vote, and a definition without a ground are all inadmissible as classifiers.

- **cost if wrong:** bounded and locatable. The packet states mode, refuters and provenance before the conclusion, so an error is findable rather than invisible. The real cost is spending a checker's independence by showing the framework before their stage-1 verdict, which Fig 2 :42 prices as buying independence in proportion to the cost of being wrong. That cost is avoidable by leading with the shape and its refuter. A second cost is misclassifying a system as a tool because an average hid a live slot, which Fig 1 :22 names directly.
- **undoable:** yes. The labels are withdrawn without touching the derivation; `07_the_tether_answer.md` and `08_expert_verification_packet.md` already do this. A sent claim can be superseded by the corrected packet, and no primitive has to be retracted. The channel-cut test can be re-run with a different slot or a cleaner habitat audit.
- **reversed by:** a ground reading, not agreement. A per-slot channel-cut observation with a documented habitat audit would settle the operational test in either direction. An externally aimed system that pursues a goal with no internal comparison breaks the objective-link verdict. An interior-only change that moves the ground breaks the capability verdict. A pure tool that retains an aim-like live slot after the cut breaks the test. A stable cross-field definition of "agent" with a ground that pays for it would make the dissolution unnecessary. Until one of these fires, the verdict stands as `candidate`.
