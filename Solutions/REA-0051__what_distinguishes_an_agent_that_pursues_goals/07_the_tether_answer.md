# The Tether's answer: What distinguishes an agent that pursues goals from a tool that follows instructions?

**Mode: `general` on the shape, `specified` on the channel-cut test. Not `grounded`.** This is a simulated derivation checked against the figures (`05_what_shape_means.md` §6), not a ground reading.

## 1. The residual it answers

The same outward behaviour supports two descriptions: a system pursuing a goal and a system following instructions. The ambiguity is not about how the system behaves while the instruction stream is present; it is about what happens to the aim when that stream is removed. Two unions are bundled here (Fig 9 :54, :57): where the comparison defining progress is bound, and whether contact with the ground is maintained. They need separate loops.

## 2. The shape

1. **Refuse the name as the classifier.** Fig 11 :34-35: "What is intelligence? The wrong question, and this figure is the replacement for it." Fig 11 :41 asks "what generates lift rather than whether a thing is a bird". The question "what distinguishes an agent from a tool" is in the same family. The answerable question is what makes a system capable. "Agent" and "tool" are field-relative labels: engineering counts a thermostat as an agent, while principal-agent economics defines the agent as a delegate. No name decides the class.

2. **The objective link is the internal discriminator.** Fig 3 :10: "it can say what would count as progress here, in its own terms". Fig 12 :112: "A plan is a sequence. A goal is a comparison. Progress is a subtraction." A tool can be driven by a comparison that lives in the instructor's vocabulary. An agent holds the comparison and the subtraction in its own Γ, so the aim is in its own closure and remains available when the instruction stops. This is the middle link of the dependency chain, the one that "has neither" obvious failures nor obvious tests (Fig 3 :25-27), so it is the one most often assumed rather than measured.

3. **Capability is a property of agent-and-habitat.** Fig 11 :39-40: "capability is a property of agent-and-habitat, never of the agent alone" and "An improvement that does not change contact changes nothing, however much it improves." Fig 10 :24: "the ground does not decay, the channel does." The difference is not an interior possession. A tool's contact is supplied by the instructor and ends with the channel; an agent maintains a channel to the ground itself. Fig 11 :36: "intelligence is a label applied afterwards to a lineage that met its ground".

4. **The operational test is a channel cut, read per slot.** Fig 1 :19: "The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated." Fig 1 :22: "Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average." Withdraw the instruction stream, keep the ground and the rest of the habitat fixed, and watch whether a live slot residual persists and is treated as the aim of the next bet. If the aim collapses with the channel, the system was a tool on that slot. If a live slot remains and steers, the aim was held in the system's own vocabulary. Symbols table :39: R is "the aim, not only the error signal". This is `specified` and single-source; it is a test to run, not a result.

5. **No new primitive is required.** Fig 13 :115: "The set is closed. The arrangements are not. That gap is why import exists." The discriminator uses ⋛, −, →, R and the habitat, all already held. No seventh term is minted. Over the shared atom set, tool and agent can be read as Fig 12 isomers ("Same atoms, different bond, different substance") with the difference in where the comparison is bound; that reading is `specified` and has a stated falsifier in `06`.

## 3. What it explicitly does not say

- Not that the system has desires, intentions, consciousness, or human-like goals. The shape is about vocabulary and contact, not inner life.
- Not that "agent" names a natural kind. The binary is a substrate question and is dissolved, not answered.
- Not that a system is one or the other globally. The split is a union compound; a system can be agent-like on one slot and tool-like on another.
- Not that expert agreement can classify. Fig 2 :35: "Alignment is triangulation, not negotiation. A mirror cannot correct you." Fig 7 :35: "A room's agreement is variation, not a verdict. Selection between rooms is where the ground votes."
- Not that the channel-cut test has been run on a real system here. No measurement was taken.
- Not a new definition of agency offered as a verdict. A definition with no ground behind it is a convention, and Fig 10 warns that "a convention that decides an outcome has stopped being one".

## 4. What would settle it — the ground

A channel-cut observation with a per-slot residual record and a fixed habitat, not a further argument. Concretely:

1. **Definition test (cheapest):** before cutting, can the system state what would count as progress on the tracked slot without restating the instruction? Its own vocabulary, not the instructor's, must carry the comparison.
2. **Channel-cut test:** remove the instruction stream, keep the ground and habitat fixed, and record whether the live slot residual persists and steers the next bet. A global success score is inadmissible (Fig 1 :22).
3. **Habitat audit:** record what was withdrawn and what was introduced. Fig 11 :70: "A test harness is a substituted habitat. Which is why a synthetic solve proves wiring and never capability."
4. **Standing falsifiers from `01` §4:** an externally aimed system that pursues a goal with no internal comparison; an interior-only change that moves the ground; a stable cross-field label that classifies by itself; a pure tool retaining an aim after the cut; a vote that settles the case with no ground reading.

Anything short of these is an ascription, and Dennett's point applies: "the critical feature is that the observer ascribes them to the agent". Ascriptions are useful, but they are not the ground.

## 5. The honest stop

**Unreached, not unreachable.** The framework says it returns "a shape, not a name" for a missing primitive. Nothing is missing here; the vocabulary is sufficient. What is missing is an instrument and a recorded channel-cut observation. Fig 9 :44: "Only a sealed room can be searched to the end, and no frame is a sealed room." Fig 13 :134: "Unreachable is a verdict here, not a silence". The derivation stops at the limit of what the available instruments distinguish and records the lineage so the stop is checkable. If the channel-cut observation pays, the `specified` test becomes a candidate for settlement. Until then it stays candidate. Prestige or agreement is not such an observation: "Prestige can fund an action. It can give an action a budget to be tried. But it cannot settle an action."

## 6. One-paragraph version for outreach

> The Tether does not give a new definition of "agent", because that is the wrong-shaped question: Figure 11 asks what makes a system capable, not what a thing essentially is, and capability belongs to the system and its habitat together. The discriminator is a shape with two parts. First, can the system say what would count as progress in its own terms? A goal is a comparison and progress is a subtraction, so this asks whether those bonds are held in the system's vocabulary or supplied by an instructor. Second, does the channel to the ground survive removal of the instruction stream? Cut that stream, keep the ground fixed, and watch one object slot rather than an average: a live residual that still steers the next bet means the aim was held; an aim that collapses with the channel means it was borrowed. The differences are structural, not about inner life, and the whole claim is a `general` shape with one `specified` test and five written refuters. It is a simulation checked against the figures, not a measurement, and a failure is more useful to me than agreement.
