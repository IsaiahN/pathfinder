# Expert verification packet: the agent and the tool

**What this is:** a claim in a form a specialist can check. Mode, derivation and refuters are stated before the conclusion, so a reader knows what to look for and what would settle it.

**Mode: `general` on the shape, `specified` on the operational test. Not `grounded`.** No measurement at a ground is claimed. This is a shape and a proposed check, not a result. It is a simulated derivation checked for internal consistency against the figures.

## 1. The claim, in one paragraph

The agent/tool distinction is not an interior possession and not a name. It is a contact fact about system and habitat. Two conditions separate them: the comparison that defines progress is bound in the system's own vocabulary rather than the instructor's, and the channel to the ground survives removal of the instruction stream. The operational test is to cut that stream, hold the habitat fixed, and read one object slot: a live residual that still steers the next bet means the aim was held; an aim that collapses with the channel means it was borrowed. Over the shared atom set the two can be read as isomers, with the difference in where the comparison is bound. The binary itself is the wrong question; the answerable question is what makes a system capable.

## 2. Three checkable sub-claims

### C1 — framework-internal; checkable by reading Figures 3, 12 and the Operators table

- **Statement:** the objective link is where the classes separate. A system whose own vocabulary cannot state what counts as progress is a tool on that slot, however well it follows instructions.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 3 :10 — "it can say what would count as progress here, in its own terms". Fig 12 :112 — "A plan is a sequence. A goal is a comparison. Progress is a subtraction." Operators table :52 — "a goal is a comparison, which is why a vocabulary without it cannot state one". Supporting: Fig 3 :26-27, the middle link "has neither" obvious failures nor obvious tests.
- **Refuter:** exhibit a system that genuinely pursues a goal while holding no comparison term in its own vocabulary, with the comparison demonstrably in the external instruction stream. If that exists, C1 fails.

### C2 — framework-internal plus contact; checkable by reading Figures 11 and 10

- **Statement:** capability belongs to agent-and-habitat, so the discriminator is the channel, not the interior. A tool's contact is mediated by the instructor and ends with it; an agent maintains the channel.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 11 :39 — "capability is a property of agent-and-habitat, never of the agent alone". Fig 11 :40 — "A system cannot be made more capable by improving the agent in isolation. An improvement that does not change contact changes nothing, however much it improves." Fig 10 :24 — "the ground does not decay, the channel does". Fig 11 :36 — "intelligence is a label applied afterwards to a lineage that met its ground".
- **Refuter:** an isolated interior improvement that moves the ground with contact unchanged. The figure names this itself at Fig 11 :72: "an agent-only improvement that moved the ground would refute the capability claim." Also: exhibit a stable cross-field definition of "agent" that classifies by itself, with a ground that pays for it.

### C3 — operational and physics-facing; checkable by designing the channel-cut observation

- **Statement:** withdraw the instruction stream, keep the ground and other habitat actors fixed, and watch one object slot. A live residual that persists and steers the next bet means the aim was held. A global success score is inadmissible.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 1 :19 — "The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated." Fig 1 :22 — "Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average." Fig 1 :23 — "The ground enters as the only metric." Fig 11 :70 — "A test harness is a substituted habitat. Which is why a synthetic solve proves wiring and never capability."
- **What to check:** is the tracked slot specified before the cut? Is the ground the same ground on both sides? Is the habitat recorded so that withdrawal of the instructor is not confused with substitution of a whole habitat? Symbols table :39 says R is "the aim, not only the error signal"; confirm it is the aim that persists, not merely an unzeroed error.
- **Refuter:** a system classified as a pure tool on independent grounds that retains an aim-like live slot after the instruction stream is cut, with the habitat audit clean. That breaks the test.

## 3. What is explicitly not claimed

- Not that the system has desires, intentions, consciousness or human-like goals.
- Not that "agent" names a natural kind. The binary is dissolved in favour of a contact question (Fig 11 :34-35, :41).
- Not that a system is globally one or the other. The split is a union compound (Fig 9 :54, :57).
- Not that experts can settle the case by agreement. Fig 2 :35: "Alignment is triangulation, not negotiation. A mirror cannot correct you." Fig 7 :35: "A room's agreement is variation, not a verdict. Selection between rooms is where the ground votes."
- Not that this is `grounded`. No channel-cut observation was run for this packet.
- Not the isomer claim as settled. It is `specified`, with a falsifier in `06_isomers_and_order.md` Q2.

## 5. What would settle it

1. A channel-cut observation with a per-slot residual record and a documented habitat audit, run on a system whose own vocabulary can be inspected for the comparison term.
2. A counter-case for C1: a genuinely goal-pursuing system with no internal comparison.
3. A counter-case for C2: an interior-only change that moves the ground.
4. A counter-case for C3: an independently classified pure tool that keeps an aim-like live slot after the cut.
5. A ground that pays for a single cross-field definition of "agent". None is claimed to exist here.

As Dennett's entry puts it, "the critical feature is that the observer ascribes them to the agent". What is owed is an observation that does not depend on the ascription.

## 6. What an expert can check in twenty minutes

1. Read Fig 3 :10, Fig 12 :112 and Operators :52 (three short passages). Decide whether the objective link is a plausible discriminator in your field.
2. Read Fig 11 :39-40 and Fig 10 :24 (two short passages). Decide whether capability as a system-and-habitat property is a better frame than an interior essence.
3. Design, in one paragraph, the channel-cut observation for a system you know. Name the slot, the ground, and what you would record before and after.
4. Send the failure, if there is one. A failure is more useful than an endorsement. Do not send agreement; it is not evidence here.
