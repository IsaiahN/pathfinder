## 1. A shape is a constraint, not a filler

The figures README states the limit: the framework returns *"what a missing thing would have to look like (a shape, not a name)."* Fig 9 :32: "you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself". Fig 13 :134: "Unreachable is a verdict here, not a silence".

From *The Library of Babel*: "you can cut your search short if the set of books returned is small and descriptive enough that it provides a general shape of the answer that satisfies your question."

So a **shape** is the narrowed form of an answer you can state before you can name it. It constrains; it does not fill. For this question, "the difference is a contact relation between a held aim and a maintained channel" is a shape. "The system has an inner goal" is a name, and so is "the system is an agent".

The same essay names the failure mode this question is prone to: "perhaps your problem was a bundle or conflation of several problems masquerading as one?" That is Fig 9 :54's union, and it is why the census in `01` unbundles before searching.

## 2. How a shape is obtained

- *Library of Babel*: use the vocabulary you already have that is closest to the thing, look for recurring new terms that overlap or correlate with it, recurse. "With each new term gained, you are able to narrow the space by definition and deepen your vocabulary."
- Fig 9 :27-28: "describe it before you go looking, or any frame you pick will seem to fit." Fig 9 :30-31: "your words are borrowed too, so describe the effect rather than the cause."
- Fig 11 :43: "The habitat is enumerated, never composed." The discriminator was obtained by listing what is in contact with the residual: the instruction stream, the ground, the vocabulary that holds the comparison, the slot that carries the live residual. Nothing was invented for the list.
- *Library of Babel* on stopping: the answer is reached when "the finish line will also be the starting place", and the paradox is that by then "you have already composed the answer through the process of locating it."

## 3. What licenses a name

Only two things, both outside the shape itself:

1. **The ground settles it.** Formula step 5: "the ground settles it. Verified only by what pays. A gate is not the ground." A channel-cut observation that pays is an instrument; an argument is not.
2. **A provenanced import from a frame whose closure differs** (Fig 6 :23, Fig 8 :50-52), with a source gate and a shadow test. The world sources used here are imports of *location*, not of machinery: they tell us the word "agent" is field-relative and that self-maintenance has a literature. They add no primitive.

For this question, no name is licensed. "Agent" and "tool" are both borrowed labels from fields whose usages conflict. What is licensed is the shape and the test.

## 4. The ground and the source problem

*The Recursive Self-Improvement Trap* gives the validation rule directly: "the output is only as good as the validator. The agent, or the tool, or the frame, or the theory, cannot validate itself." And: "You may all agree and be wrong." This is Fig 2 :35 in prose: "Alignment is triangulation, not negotiation. A mirror cannot correct you."

The same essay contains the sentence that most nearly answers the user's question in the framework's own terms: "The LLM was not the intelligence. The LLM was a tool. The intelligence, if it was going to exist, had to come from the architecture I was building." Note what the author does here: he does not locate the difference in an inner glow. He locates it in the architecture and its coupling to a ground ("The ARC games have a score. You either solve the level or you don't. That is the ground."). That is the same contact move as Fig 11 :39-40.

## 5. Answer to the shape question

**The shape any answer must have:** a checkable relation between (a) a comparison held in the system's own vocabulary, (b) a channel to the ground that does not depend on the instructor, and (c) a per-slot residual that remains the aim when the instruction stream is removed. It is a shape, not a name, because it says what any discriminator must constrain without saying what the system "really is" inside.

Two boundaries are explicit:

- The binary itself is the wrong question (Fig 11 :34-35). The answerable question is what makes a system capable, and capability is a property of agent-and-habitat.
- The framework returns *"a shape, not a name"* for a **missing** primitive. Here nothing is missing from the vocabulary (⋛, −, →, R and the habitat are all held), so the shape is not a placeholder for a seventh term. This corrects the easy over-reading of "shape, not a name" as if it forbade all talk of goals. It forbids naming a missing primitive and naming an essence; it does not forbid describing a comparison that is already in the vocabulary.

## 6. Consequences for contact

**My own derivation is a simulation, not a ground reading.** *The Recursive Self-Improvement Trap*: "LLMs can quickly simulate an approximate answer/output with the formula ... However, the truly testable output would need to be derived from the real architecture or working the formula yourself." So `01`, `06`, `07` and `09` are simulated derivations checked for internal consistency against the figures. They cannot settle the question they report on and must be labelled as such.

**Prestige is not action.** Same essay: "Prestige must never be allowed to validate an action directly"; "Prestige can fund an action. It can give an action a budget to be tried. But it cannot settle an action. Only the ground can settle it." A list of famous people who agree that a system "has goals" is the named collapse, not a discriminator.
