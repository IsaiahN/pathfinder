**Use:** one-to-one, or as a cover letter attached to a submission through the public venues in `03_public_contact_points.csv`. **Not** for bulk send. Replace the bracketed name and signature before use.

**Hard precondition:** do not send until the figure lines in `01_figure_grounded_audit.md` F1, F2 and F4 have been checked against the verbatim extract. The output must survive validation or the two-stage offer collapses on contact.

**Subject:** Check one output before you decide whether the primordial black hole claim is worth reading

> Hi [name],
>
> I have one specific output I would like you to check against what you already know. I am not asking you to read a theory first, and I am not asking you to agree with me. If the output does not survive, there is nothing else to look at.
>
> **The output (`specified`, not `grounded`):** dark matter is not one measurement, it is a set of gravitational readings indexed by slot (rotation curves, lensing, the CMB, structure, the merger population). Two claims hide inside "some or all": "some" needs one surviving mass window, while "all" must clear every slot at once. My derivation keeps those two verdicts separate and refuses to average them. It says that a primordial black hole population is a reachable candidate inside current physics, that the readings so far do not name the source, and that the honest present status is open, not excluded and not confirmed.
>
> **What would refute the output.** I wrote these down before writing this message:
> 1. an instrument that maps the gravitational reading uniquely to one source, so that source discrimination needs no new discriminator;
> 2. a complete, assumption-free exclusion of primordial black holes across every mass window;
> 3. a demonstration that one global residual suffices, so the slot split is spurious.
>
> If any one holds, I will retract the shape. A one-line "this fails because..." is more useful to me than agreement. If you would rather not engage, no reply is needed.
>
> **What I am not claiming:** that all dark matter is primordial black holes; that primordial black holes are excluded; that the figure framework derives the candidate. The framework returns a shape, not a name. Calling the source "primordial black hole" is a binding from the physics literature, and it carries provenance, not a derivation. Where an exclusion depends on an accretion model, a clustering assumption or a mass function, I want that assumption on the record and reversible.
>
> **What I would count as a settlement:** an instrument that returns a reading one candidate predicts and the others do not, not another fit to the same data. A reported failure of my output is more useful than an endorsement.
>
> **If it survives, and only then:** the sources are below. The verbatim figures, not my summary, are the authority.
> - Constraints on Primordial Black Holes (Carr, Kohri, Sendouda, Yokoyama): https://arxiv.org/abs/2002.12778
> - Primordial Black Holes as a dark matter candidate (Green and Kavanagh): https://arxiv.org/abs/2007.10722
> - Primordial black holes: constraints, potential evidence and prospects (Carr et al. 2026): https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.06024
>
> **If you want to use the machinery:** the formula is in the public figures repository. Run it on a problem you know well and can check against a ground. Write down what would refute your result *before* you run it, and state the mode of what you get. A failure reported honestly is more useful to me than a success.
>
>  -  [Your name]
