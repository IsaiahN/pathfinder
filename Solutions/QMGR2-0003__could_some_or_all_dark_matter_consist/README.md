## What is being decided, in effect terms

Dark matter is a residual: gravitational readings (rotation curves, lensing, the CMB, large-scale structure) whose source is not identified. The question asks whether that source is, in whole or in part, a population of primordial black holes (PBHs) formed from density perturbations in the early universe. Option A: it is. Option B: the readings come from another source or from a modification of gravity. A would require a formation mechanism, a mass function and an abundance that survive every instrument. B requires a different source and an instrument that separates it from PBHs. The derived answer below keeps the **shape** of that requirement and refuses to name the source, because the figures return a shape, not a name.

## What is deliberately not here

- No personal emails of named individuals.
- No ranking of people by how likely they are to agree that PBHs are dark matter.
- No bulk-send plan.

Three reasons, in the figures' own words:

1. **A room does not upgrade the label.** Fig 7 :35: *"A room's agreement is variation, not a verdict. Selection between rooms is where the ground votes."* A list of people selected for agreeing that PBHs are the answer produces a convention, not corroboration.
2. **Correlation disqualifies a verdict.** Fig 2 :40: *"disqualifying for a verdict: agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool"*. Most PBH reviews draw on the same body of microlensing, gamma-ray and gravitational-wave measurements. They are one witness with many names.
3. **Asking for trust before checking spends independence.** Fig 10 :42: *"A verifier that can reconstruct the claim cannot verify it."* The first contact carries the residual and the refuter, never the framework's interiors.

Legally: scraping personal emails for unsolicited bulk outreach breaches GDPR and CAN-SPAM style rules in many jurisdictions. That is a practical failure on top of the structural one.
