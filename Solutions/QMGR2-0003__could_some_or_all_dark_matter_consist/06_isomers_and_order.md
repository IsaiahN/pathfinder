# Isomers and order in the primordial black hole question

**Mode: `specified`.** This is a simulated derivation checked against the figures, not a ground reading (see `05_what_shape_means.md` §6).

## RESIDUAL

The question reads "some or all dark matter consists of primordial black holes". That phrasing bundles three structurally different cases. Two questions are owed: (1) is "some" a union or a partition problem, and (2) do two formation channels that yield the same mass function count as one candidate or two? The order in which the cases are searched is determined by dependency, not by convenience (Fig 3).

## Q1  -  Is "some or all" a partition or a union?

- Fig 9 :54: *"And when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again."*
- Fig 9 :55: *"A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones."*
- Fig 9 :57: *"What remains is smaller and it is also categorically different: a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either"* (spans figure lines 57 to 58).

The three cases are:

| case | logical form | what would settle it |
|---|---|---|
| C_all | PBH is the source in every slot | a constraint or detection in every slot; the conjunction |
| C_some | PBH is a subcomponent in at least one slot | one surviving window with a positive source-specific reading |
| C_none | PBH contributes negligibly | a robust, assumption-free exclusion across windows |

These are not points on a single "how much PBH" line. C_all fails if one slot excludes PBH. C_some survives if one window stays open. C_none requires all windows closed. Under Fig 9 they are a **union of overlapping conditions**, so they must be unbundled before searching. Reporting a single probability that "dark matter is PBH" aggregates across slots and is the error named in Fig 1 :22: *"Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average."*

**Result:** union, not partition. Each case gets its own verdict. Triangulated (Fig 9 union rule + Fig 1 slot rule).

## Q2  -  What is the dependency order?

- Fig 3 :20: *"The chain is ordered by dependency, not by importance. Each link consumes what the one above produces, so effort spent above the break arrives at a step"* (spans figure lines 20 to 21).
- Fig 3 :19: **"Optimising a link that already works cannot rescue one that does not."**
- Fig 3 :23: *"It is a reading of nothing."*

The chain here is:

```
describe the residual in effect terms (Fig 9 :27-28)
  -> formation mechanism fixes the mass function f(M)
    -> instrument constraints cut the (mass, abundance) plane
      -> per-slot verdict (some / all / none)
        -> settlement or honest stop
```

A constraint quoted without its mass function and accretion assumptions is a reading taken below the break. It is not a weak reading of the constraint; it is a reading of nothing, because its input never arrived. This is why `01` F5 requires every cut to be recorded with its assumption attached and reversible (Fig 9 :41: *"so keep every cut ranked and reversible"*).

**Result:** formation is upstream of constraints; the verdict is downstream of both. Triangulated (Fig 3 + Fig 9).

## Q3  -  Are formation channels isomers?

- Fig 12 :5: **"A set of ingredients is not one. The bond is the rest of the information, and it is the half nothing was recording."**
- Fig 12 :117: **"isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."**
- Fig 12 :124: *"functional group. A substructure whose presence fixes the outcome. The group says what kind; the qualifier says which member."*
- Fig 8 :50: **"But shared atoms are not the signature. Two frames can hold the same atom by convergence, reaching it separately, which is corroboration at maximum independence."**

A PBH formed from a high-sigma inflationary perturbation and one formed during a first-order phase transition can present the same end object and a similar mass function. Under Fig 12 they are **isomers at the formation level**: same ingredients, different bond (the formation history), and the difference is invisible in the mass function alone. The record of which route produced them is part of what the candidate is, not documentation appended to it (Fig 13 :130: *"A term carries the atoms it was built from and the bonds that joined them. That record is not documentation of the term. It is the term."*).

The weaker reading is that the mass windows are a **functional group**: each window plays the same role (a region of parameter space not yet excluded) while differing in which member it is. Fig 12 :124 governs that. The 2026 review by Carr et al. reports constraints *"across the full mass range"* and ties them to formation scenarios and mass functions, which is exactly the structure this reading predicts.

**Result:** formation channels are isomers; mass windows are a functional group. Single-source for the figure definition (Fig 12), with the physics-facing check still unmeasured.

## Q4  -  Order is not sequence

- Fig 12 :30: *"→ sequence"* means *"A then B; B needs A to have happened"*.
- Fig 13 :75: *"Two states never bond directly. It is not space-time but space, a structure, time; and two structures join only through a state."* (recorded here only to show why the substrate derivation was re-censused in `01` §2, not to derive PBHs.)

The arrow chain in Q2 is a **dependency order**, not a claim that formation temporally precedes constraint in the instrument. Using `→` here would add an ordering this derivation does not license. The figure text for dependency is a chain of links that consume what the link above produces (Fig 3 :20), which is a different relation from sequence. Marking this matters because the exemplar's first error was exactly an unlicensed `→`. Here it is avoided before the conclusion.

**Result:** dependency order, not sequence. Triangulated (Fig 3 + Operators table).

## CONCLUSION

"Some or all" is a union of three overlapping cases with separate grounds, not one question with one answer (Q1). Formation is upstream of constraints and the verdict is downstream of both, so a constraint quoted without its assumptions is a reading of nothing (Q2). Formation channels yielding the same mass function are Fig 12 isomers, and mass windows are a functional group (Q3). The chain is dependency, not temporal sequence (Q4). This framing rescues the checkable structure in the question without asserting either answer: "PBH is all dark matter" and "PBH is excluded" are both refused as verdicts.
