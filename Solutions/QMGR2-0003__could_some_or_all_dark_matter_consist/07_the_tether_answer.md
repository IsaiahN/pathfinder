# The Tether's answer: could dark matter be primordial black holes?

**Mode: `specified`  -  not `grounded`.** This is a shape and a route, not a result. Simulated derivation checked against the figures (`05_what_shape_means.md` §6), not a ground reading. No instrument settles the identity of dark matter here.

## 1. The residual it answers

Dark matter is a set of gravitational readings with an unidentified source. The question asks whether a population of black holes formed from density perturbations in the early universe is that source, in whole or in part. The residual is indexed by slot (Fig 1 :19: *"The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated."*), so "some" and "all" are different claims with different grounds. This is a **union compound**, not one hard question (Fig 9 :55: *"A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones."*).

## 2. The shape

1. **The readings do not name the source.** Fig 2 :29: **"An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks."** Fig 9 :32: **"you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself"**. Rotation curves, lensing and the CMB are projections with lossy information about the source. The Bullet Cluster (Clowe et al. 2006) removed modified gravity in that system, not PBH versus particles.
2. **PBH is a reachable candidate, not a new primitive.** Fig 6 :21: **"MINT composes inside the closure and can never add an atom."** Inflationary perturbations plus general relativity already supply the candidate inside current physics. Its status is `candidate`, not `grounded`.
3. **"Some" and "all" get separate verdicts.** "All" is the conjunction over slots; "some" needs one surviving window. Fig 1 :22: *"Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average."* The 2026 review states the correct combined form: PBHs *"may have formed in the early Universe and may account for all or part of the dark matter."* That is a question with two answers, not one.
4. **Existing instruments already return partial readings.** Fig 6 :34: **"The question is not whether a sensor could exist. It is whether anything, at any resolution, is already returning something that fails to resolve."** Microlensing, the gamma-ray background, CMB distortions and the merger population are proto-instruments that fail to resolve the source. The stop is **unreached, not unreachable**.
5. **Every exclusion is a reversible cut.** Fig 9 :41: **"so keep every cut ranked and reversible"**. Limits depend on mass functions, accretion histories, clustering and halo phase-space structure. Carr et al. (2021) stress that *"many of the constraints are associated with observational or theoretical uncertainties."* A null in one window is not a proof of absence (Fig 8 :39: **"So a search that finds nothing is never a proof of absence."**).
6. **Formation channels are isomers.** Fig 12 :117: **"isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."** Channels giving the same mass function are not one candidate; the record of the route is part of the term.

## 3. What it explicitly does not say

- Not "all dark matter is primordial black holes". That is a `specified` claim at best and is refused as a verdict.
- Not "primordial black holes are excluded as dark matter". Fig 8 :39 forbids that conclusion from null results.
- Not that the figure framework derives PBHs. The candidate is a binding from the physics frame, provenanced as such. The framework returns a shape, not a name.
- Not that the six-term substrate composes PBHs. That reading strained and was re-censused in `01` §2.
- Not a derivation of one candidate from another, and no new primitive.
- Not `grounded`. No measurement at any mass window settles `left(R, phi)` here.

## 4. What would settle it  -  the ground

A source-discriminating instrument, not a further argument. From *The Hard Problem is About Source*: an instrument that reads green *"cannot comment on the source"*; the spectrometer resolved it. Here the corresponding questions are:

- **Mass-window test:** does a microlensing survey, a gamma-ray background measurement or a CMB distortion return a source-specific signal in an open window rather than another upper limit?
- **Merger-population test:** does the mass spectrum, ellipticity distribution and stochastic background of black-hole mergers discriminate a primordial population from an astrophysical one? Bird et al. (2016) note that PBH mergers *"have no optical nor neutrino counterparts"* and may be distinguished by *"the observed mass spectrum, their high ellipticities, or their stochastic gravitational wave background."*
- **Assumption test:** which exclusion cuts survive when the mass function is extended rather than monochromatic, and when accretion and clustering assumptions are varied? A cut that collapses under its own assumption was never ground.
- **Standing falsifiers from `01` §5:** an instrument mapping readings uniquely to a source; an assumption-free exclusion over all windows; one global residual replacing the slot split; an instrument distinguishing formation histories with identical mass functions.

## 5. The honest stop

**Unreached, not unreachable.** The reviews agree that the field has open questions (Green and Kavanagh 2021) and that many constraints carry uncertainties (Carr et al. 2021). The answer stops at the limit of what the available instruments distinguish and records the lineage so the stop is checkable. Fig 13 :134: **"Unreachable is a verdict here, not a silence,"** and Fig 8 :39: **"So a search that finds nothing is never a proof of absence."** Agreement from the shared measurement pool is one witness, not corroboration (Fig 2 :40: *"disqualifying for a verdict: agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool"*).

## 6. One-paragraph version for outreach

> The Tether does not say dark matter is made of primordial black holes, and it does not say they are excluded. It says that the gravitational readings do not name their source, so the answer has the shape of a source term with a mass function and a discriminator, not the shape of a single name. PBHs are a reachable candidate inside current physics, but "some" and "all" are different questions: "all" must clear every observational slot, while "some" needs one surviving mass window. The instruments that could settle this already exist in partial form, microlensing, gamma-ray background, CMB and gravitational-wave mergers, and each exclusion depends on assumptions that must stay visible and reversible. What the framework offers is that shape, the unbundling of the question, and falsifiers written in advance. The slots stay open until the ground settles them.
