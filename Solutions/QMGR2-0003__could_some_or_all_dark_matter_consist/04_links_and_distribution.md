## Two findings in the wider literature that bear on this question

1. **A direct empirical proof of dark matter is not a proof of its identity.** Clowe et al. 2006 (https://arxiv.org/abs/astro-ph/0608407) report an *"8-sigma significance spatial offset of the center of the total mass from the center of the baryonic mass peaks"* in the Bullet Cluster and say this *"cannot be explained with an alteration of the gravitational force law."* That settles unseen mass against a modified force law in that system. It does not distinguish PBHs from particles. Cite it as a discriminator of one kind, never as evidence for PBHs.
2. **Underdetermination is a recognised result in a different field.** The Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy entry on underdetermination (https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/scientific-underdetermination/, first published 2009, substantively revised 2023) states that *"the evidence available to us at a given time may be insufficient to determine what beliefs we should hold in response to it."* This is a low-overlap frame (philosophy of science, not cosmology) that corroborates the figure claim that readings do not name a source. It is corroboration only; the verdict comes from the figures.
