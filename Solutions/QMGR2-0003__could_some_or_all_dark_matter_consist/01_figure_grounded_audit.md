## 0. The claim under audit

> Could some or all dark matter consist of primordial black holes formed in the early universe?

Restated as an effect, before any figure is chosen: dark matter is a set of gravitational readings (galaxy rotation curves, cluster dynamics, weak and strong lensing, the cosmic microwave background, large-scale structure) for which the source is not identified. The claim asserts that a population of black holes formed before stars, from the collapse of large density perturbations, is that source. "Some" asserts a subcomponent in at least one slot. "All" asserts the source in every slot. These are not the same claim and are not averaged.

**The sought-for shape, bound as an ordinary term before the search (Formula step "Direction"):**

> An answer would look like a source term S with a formation mechanism and a mass function f(M), such that (i) S reproduces the gravitational readings in every slot it claims, and (ii) at least one instrument returns a reading that S predicts and its rivals do not.

This shape is written down and made refutable below. A frame cannot check the direction of its own question, so the direction is made a term rather than left as a preference (Fig 5 :65 to :67).

## 1. Residual description

- **Arity.** One residual with many slots. Fig 1 :19: **"The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated."** The slots here are rotation curves, cluster collisions, lensing, CMB, structure, and the gravitational-wave merger population. "All dark matter" is the conjunction over slots. "Some dark matter" is a single-slot existence claim. Aggregating them into one global "is it PBH?" is how the live signal disappears into an average (Fig 1 :22: *"Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average."*).
- **Symmetry.** The candidate is not a point term. It is a distribution over mass and abundance. Constraints are mass-window specific, so a verdict in one window does not transfer to another. The Bullet Cluster result (Clowe et al. 2006) discriminates unseen mass from a modified force law, but it does not discriminate PBHs from particles. That is a metamerism, not a symmetry of the candidate.
- **Scale.** Formation is at early-universe horizon scales; the effects probed run from asteroid-mass evaporation (gamma-ray background) to stellar-mass mergers (LIGO/Virgo) to supermassive seeds.

## 4. Findings

### F1  -  The question is a source-identification (metamerism) residual, and the reading does not name the source (decisive)

Fig 2 :29: **"An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks."**
Fig 2 :36: **"An anchor is legitimate when the question is constitutively about it."**
Fig 9 :32: **"you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself"**

The dark-matter residual is constitutively about what produces the gravitational readings, so a source-discriminating instrument is the legitimate anchor here. But the readings themselves underdetermine the source. *The Hard Problem is About Source* (2026-08-28) states the mechanism: *"If several paths create the same behavior, then behavior is a projection with lossy information"* and *"An instrument that can't distinguish these cases demonstrates its own inadequacy, it cannot claim anything substantial about what is being measured or what causes it."* The Bullet Cluster (Clowe et al. 2006) excluded a modified-force reading of that system, but left PBH, WIMP and axion all standing: it is a discriminator of one kind, not the discriminator this question needs.

**Refuter:** show an instrument that maps the gravitational reading uniquely to one source, so that source identification needs no new discriminator. If that exists, F1 fails.

This is **triangulated**: Fig 2 (anchor) and Fig 9 (shape not answer) are low-overlap figure lines, plus SEP *Underdetermination of Scientific Theory* as a genuinely low-overlap world frame (philosophy of science, not cosmology): *"the evidence available to us at a given time may be insufficient to determine what beliefs we should hold in response to it."*

### F2  -  "Some" and "all" are two questions, not one, and neither is settled

Fig 1 :19: **"The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated."**
Fig 1 :22: *"Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average."*
Fig 9 :54: *"And when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again."*

"All dark matter is PBH" must clear every slot simultaneously (evaporation, microlensing, CMB, dynamics, mergers). "Some dark matter is PBH" needs one surviving window. The 2026 review by Carr, Iovino, Perna, Vaskonen and Veermäe opens with the correct combined form: PBHs *"may have formed in the early Universe and may account for all or part of the dark matter."* Green and Kavanagh (2021) close their review by discussing *"the key open questions in this field."* Carr and Kuhnel (2022) state that constraints allow most of the dark matter only in specific windows, *"10^17 - 10^23 g or 10 - 10^2 M_sun"*, and this is a window-dependent result, not a global verdict.

So the honest per-domain verdicts are:
- **Some:** live and not excluded in open windows. `candidate`.
- **All:** strongly constrained, and contingent on assumed accretion, clustering and mass functions. `specified`, ungrounded. Carr, Kohri, Sendouda and Yokoyama (2021) stress that *"many of the constraints are associated with observational or theoretical uncertainties."*
- **Neither is averaged into "maybe".**

**Refuter:** demonstrate that one global residual suffices and that the slot split is spurious, or produce an assumption-free exclusion covering every mass window. Either would collapse the union and falsify this finding.

### F3  -  PBH is a reachable candidate, so this is not an underivable question

Fig 6 :21: **"MINT composes inside the closure and can never add an atom."**
Fig 8 :32: **"Closure is idempotent. Composing inside a closed set never leaves it, so no search, however long, adds an atom."**
Fig 13 :115: **"The set is closed. The arrangements are not. That gap is why import exists."**

Inflationary perturbations plus general relativity already supply PBH formation inside the current physics closure. No new primitive is required to pose the candidate. What is missing is not a term but a settlement. Green and Kavanagh (2021) date the surge of interest to *"the detection of gravitational waves from mergers of tens of Solar mass black hole binaries"*, and Bird et al. (2016) reported that *"there remains a window for masses 20 M_sun < M_bh < 100 M_sun where primordial black holes (PBHs) may constitute the dark matter."* The candidate is in the library; its standing is `candidate`, not `grounded`.

**Refuter:** show that PBH formation requires a new primitive outside the current physics closure. Then the question becomes an import question and this finding changes.

**Single-source for the figure claim** (Fig 6 and Fig 8 are the same closure law, high overlap), **triangulated** with the physics literature at the level of "formation is within known physics" (Green and Kavanagh and Carr and Kuhnel are independent authors but draw on the same review pool, so one witness).

### F4  -  The instrument is already returning partial readings, so the stop is unreached, not unreachable

Fig 6 :34: **"The question is not whether a sensor could exist. It is whether anything, at any resolution, is already returning something that fails to resolve."**
Fig 8 :39: **"So a search that finds nothing is never a proof of absence."**
Fig 13 :134: **"Unreachable is a verdict here, not a silence,"**

Microlensing surveys, the gamma-ray background, CMB distortions and the gravitational-wave merger population are all already returning readings that fail to resolve the source. So there is a proto-instrument to extend; this is not a sensor for something that has never registered anywhere at any resolution. Fig 8 :39 is decisive against a premature "PBHs are excluded" conclusion: a null result in one window is not a proof of absence.

**Refuter:** show that every existing channel has saturated and that no instrument, at any resolution, returns a reading that fails to resolve. Then the honest verdict becomes unreachable at this budget rather than unreached.

### F5  -  Constraints eliminate at the ground, so keep every cut ranked and reversible

Fig 9 :38: *"elimination compounds here, and only here"*
Fig 9 :40: **"a wrong cut removes the answer and speeds up"**
Fig 9 :41: **"so keep every cut ranked and reversible"**

The exclusion plot is an elimination space. Its cuts depend on assumptions (accretion in the early universe, PBH clustering, monochromatic versus extended mass function, halo phase-space structure). Bird et al. (2016) record that *"Uncertainties in the rate for such events arise from our imprecise knowledge of the phase-space structure of galactic halos on the smallest scales."* Carr et al. (2021) add that constraints change when *"the PBHs have an extended mass function."* A cut that is not recorded with its assumption is a constant the frame authored (Fig 10 :48: *"a convention nothing can check is a constant the seat authored, carrying the seat's authority"*).

**Refuter:** identify a constraint whose assumption is neither stated nor reversible, and show that removing it does not move the excluded region. If all cuts are assumption-free, this finding weakens.

### F6  -  This is not a mint, and agreement cannot settle it

Fig 5 :41: **"Any one of them at zero stops the mint entirely. Which is why a system that is minting nothing may have nothing wrong with its minting."**
Fig 5 :48: **"Its own output is never a measure of success. A system can be enthusiastically curious and produce nothing."**
Fig 7 :35: **"A room's agreement is variation, not a verdict. Selection between rooms is where the ground votes."**

For the PBH candidate: SUPPORT is positive (the residual has live mass in several slots); REACHABILITY holds under current physics; NOVELTY holds relative to the held particle candidates; SETTLEMENT does not hold (Fig 5 :32, *"then check it against the ground, then carry it up"*). Fig 2 :42: **"Buy independence in proportion to the cost of being wrong."** The cost of being wrong about the composition of dark matter is high, so independent instruments, not a larger chorus, are what should be bought.

**Refuter:** show a settled measurement that pays for the candidate term; then it stops being a mint candidate and becomes admitted.

### F7  -  Formation channels with the same observable mass function are not one candidate

Fig 12 :5: **"A set of ingredients is not one. The bond is the rest of the information, and it is the half nothing was recording."**
Fig 12 :117: **"isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."**
Fig 8 :50: **"But shared atoms are not the signature. Two frames can hold the same atom by convergence, reaching it separately, which is corroboration at maximum independence."**

Two formation channels (inflationary peaks and, say, a first-order phase transition) can yield a similar mass function. The end object is the same; the bond (the formation history) differs, and the record is what separates them. A mass function alone does not tell which route produced it. This is the isomer reading, not an analogy imported from chemistry: Fig 12 defines it and Fig 13 :121 to :125 applies it to composed objects generally.

**Refuter:** exhibit an instrument that distinguishes the formation histories by their PBH signatures alone. Then they are separate candidates with separate grounds and the isomer claim fails.

## 5. Pre-registered refuter

Required before any outreach. Fig 10 :59: **"an interest in the outcome is unavoidable, an interest in the reading is disqualifying."** A falsifier written before the run is a design instrument.

The shape claim is refuted if any of the following holds:

1. An instrument is demonstrated to map the gravitational reading uniquely to one source, so source identification needs no new discriminator (breaks F1).
2. A complete, assumption-free exclusion of PBHs across every mass window is established, so PBH is not a reachable candidate (breaks F2 and F3).
3. One global residual is shown to suffice, so the slot split and the "some"/"all" unbundling are spurious (breaks F2).
4. An instrument is shown to distinguish formation histories of PBHs with identical mass functions, so the formation-isomer claim fails (breaks F7).
5. The figures are shown to license naming the source from the reading, contrary to Fig 9 :32 (breaks the shape-not-a-name limit).

If any fires, the shape is withdrawn. Until one is run, no part of this may be labelled `grounded`. A reported failure from an outside reader is more useful than an endorsement.

### Four-question recursive derivation (summary)

1. **What is the residual?** A source-identification gap with many slots, not one global number (Fig 1 :19).
2. **Can the readings name the source?** No. Underdetermination is structural (Fig 9 :32; source essay; SEP).
3. **Is PBH reachable?** Yes, inside current physics, so this is a settlement question, not an import question (Fig 6 :21).
4. **What settles it?** A source-discriminating instrument, with cuts kept ranked and reversible (Fig 6 :34; Fig 9 :41).

### Updated verdict

The shape survives: the question is a slot-indexed source-identification residual, PBH is a reachable but unsettled candidate, "some" and "all" must be verdicts kept apart, and every exclusion is a reversible cut at the ground. The specific answers "yes, all" and "no, excluded" do not survive as verdicts. Strains after re-census: **one**, the Fig 13 substrate question, named and re-censused in §2 rather than hidden.
