## What the wider literature bears on the question

1. **The field already has the separator.** Walker et al. 2004 distinguish engineering resilience (return time) from ecological resilience and explicitly say return time *"cannot be considered as the measure of resilience"* because multiple stable states mean it misses ways of failing. The Stockholm Resilience Centre dictionary says ecosystem resilience is both how much disturbance a system can handle *without shifting into a qualitatively different state* and the capacity *"to rebuild itself if damaged."* The resistance-ecology page reports Grimm and Wissel's three properties: staying essentially unchanged, returning to the reference state, and persistence through time. The business-continuity page keeps resistance to failure separate from RTO and RPO. This is the two-reading shape across four different vocabularies. It is corroboration, not the verdict; the verdict comes from the figures.
2. **A two-axis measurement already exists.** Boucher et al. and Bellingham et al., as reported on the resistance-ecology page, classified tree species by damage magnitude and post-disturbance response magnitude, yielding resistant, susceptible, usurper and resilient groups. That is a concrete two-axis instrument returning something, which is what Fig 6 :32 requires before an instrument can be improved.
3. **The recovery reading is instrumentable.** Scheffer et al. 2009 report generic early-warning signals and the lag-1 autocorrelation measure of critical slowing down. This strengthens F5 and F6 but does not settle the regime-shift discriminator, which remains live.
4. **The two readings can move against each other.** MathisonSlee et al. 2022 describe a rigidity trap where high connectivity and rigidity reduce novelty and adaptive responses. A system can be very resistant and very poor at reorganizing. That is the union signature of `01` section 1.3, corroborated from the field's own pool.
5. **The field's caution is real.** Walker et al. do not advocate separate measurement because of interdependencies; psychology calls the construct difficult to measure. Both are recorded as costs on the operational question, not as reasons to collapse the two readings (see `01` F8).

## Distribution: offer, do not push

Figure-grounded reasons:

- **Fig 4 :22**: *"Only methods cross up. Only head starts come down."* Sending a list of resilience indices before anyone has described a residual sends a recording, not a generator. Lead with the separator and its refuter; offer links after.
- **Fig 10 :42**: *"A verifier that can reconstruct the claim cannot verify it."* A recipient who has absorbed the framework before being asked for a verdict has joined the frame being checked.
- **Fig 9 :27-28**: *"describe it before you go looking, or any frame you pick will seem to fit."* Ask what they would expect to see if the two-reading separator were true before giving them the article.
- **Fig 2 :40**: *"disqualifying for a verdict: agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool"* (Fig 2 :40). A list selected for receptiveness is one witness wearing many names.

**Recommended order in a reply:** (1) the separator and its five failure conditions; (2) ask what the recipient would count as a failure; (3) record the verdict; (4) only then the three links, with the note that the figures, not the summary, are the authority.

## The two-stage offer

- **Stage 1 before stage 2** is Fig 10 :52: *"Bring a characterised residual, never the source."* plus Fig 2 :45: *"Frames range against the ground. They do not negotiate with it."* The recipient checks the separator without holding the framework's interior.
- **Stage 2 is opt-in by construction.** Once a recipient has absorbed the theory, their agreement stops being evidence. The stage-1 verdict is recorded before any theory is shown and is never pooled as corroboration.
- **Stage 3 sends a method**, which Fig 4 :22 permits. The formula is a generator, so giving it away does not give away an answer. Each user must state a ground and a refuter before running it.
- **Hard precondition:** stages 1 to 3 work only if the stage-1 output survives. The five refuters in `02_outreach_email.md` are the test.

## Practical constraint on submission

This is LLM-assisted work. Several venues in `03_public_contact_points.csv` have publication or AI-disclosure policies; the Ecology and Society submission page requires a data and code availability statement and an anonymised PDF, and the Stockholm Resilience Centre publishes a *Use of AI* policy. A human researcher must own and write anything submitted, and must declare the simulated status. This is a practical constraint on distribution, not evidence about resilience measurement.
