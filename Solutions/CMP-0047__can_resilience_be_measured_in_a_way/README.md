## The question restated as an effect

What is being decided is not the *size* of a resilience number but whether one measurement can keep two different effects apart. In effect terms: a system's library predicts its own trajectory; a disturbance arrives; the observed trajectory departs from prediction; R = |Γ(b,a) - o′| is measured per component slot and per time, never averaged (Fig 1 :19, Fig 1 :22). Two readings of that same post-disturbance residual trajectory are proposed:

- **Resistance:** the magnitude of the immediate departure. How far was the state knocked? This is an ordering or magnitude question, a comparison against a reference (Operators table on ⋛: *"a threshold relation between two quantities"*).
- **Recovery or reorganization:** the subsequent trajectory of R through time. Does the residual return toward the anchor (same basin), settle at a new nonzero value (reorganization), or never resolve? This is a duration and pathway question, a sequence (Fig 12 :30: *"A then B; B needs A to have happened"*).

The anchor is a measurement. Fig 2 :29 lists *"A measurement, an experiment, a counter"* as an anchor, and Fig 2 :36 says *"An anchor is legitimate when the question is constitutively about it."* The question is constitutively about measured response to disturbance, so it has a nameable anchor and is well formed, not yet a `gap`.

- Option A, "one resilience score can carry both effects": leads to the prediction that compression into a single number loses nothing that matters for either decision.
- Option B, "the two effects are two readings in different currencies": leads to the prediction that a single number loses something load bearing, and that the honest report keeps two readings side by side (Fig 12 :125, Fig 12 :130).

What an answer would look like, written down and bound as an ordinary term before the run (Formula, "Direction"): a two-axis measurement shape, with resistance read as displacement magnitude at the moment of disturbance and recovery read as the time-indexed trajectory of the residual toward or away from an unmoved reference, plus a discriminator that separates same-basin return from basin change. Until that shape is bound it predicts nothing and no check inside the loop can see it.

The verdict in `09_watson_answer.md` is Option B in `specified` mode, kept per domain and never averaged. A universal scalar index is declared `gap`.

## What is deliberately not here

- No personal emails of named individuals.
- No ranking of people by how likely they are to agree.
- No bulk-send plan.
- No claim that this derivation is `grounded`, no claim that it settles the resilience question, and no claim that one universal resilience index has been found or refuted empirically.

Three figure-grounded reasons, plus one practical one:

1. A room is not an anchor. *"A room's agreement is variation, not a verdict. Selection between rooms is where the ground votes."* (Fig 7 :35) and *"Alignment is triangulation, not negotiation. A mirror cannot correct you."* (Fig 2 :35). Gathering agreeable readers manufactures the correlation that disqualifies a verdict.
2. Correlation has valence. *"disqualifying for a verdict: agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool"* (Fig 2 :40). A list selected for receptiveness is one witness wearing many names.
3. A verifier must be outside the interior. *"A verifier that can reconstruct the claim cannot verify it."* (Fig 10 :42). The first contact therefore carries the residual and its refuter, not the framework.
4. Legally, unsolicited bulk email to scraped addresses breaches GDPR and CAN-SPAM style rules in many jurisdictions and gets the sending domain blacklisted. Public opt-in routes avoid that failure as well as the structural one.
