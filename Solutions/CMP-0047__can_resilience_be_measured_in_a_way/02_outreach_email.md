**Use:** one to one, or as a cover letter attached to a submission through the public venues in `03_public_contact_points.csv`. **Not** for bulk send.

**Hard precondition:** do not send until you have read the separator and its refuter in `01_figure_grounded_audit.md` sections 3 and 5 and can state them without the framework's vocabulary. The stage-1 output must survive on its own or the two-stage offer collapses on contact.

**Subject:** Check one measurement separator before you decide whether a resilience index is worth building

> Hi [name],
>
> I have one specific output I would like you to check against what you already know. I am not asking you to read the framework first, and I am not asking you to agree with it. If the output does not survive, there is nothing else to look at.
>
> **The output (`specified` on the separator, not `grounded`):** a disturbance produces two effects that a single measurement can keep apart. The first is resistance, the magnitude of the immediate departure of the system's state from its pre-disturbance reference. The second is recovery or reorganization, the subsequent trajectory of that departure: does it return toward the reference, settle into a different arrangement, or fail to resolve? These are not two resolutions of one quantity. One is an ordering, a comparison of how much; the other is a duration and pathway, a question of what happened next and where it ended. The figures put it as *"adjacency is not duration, an ordering is not a pathway"* (Fig 13 :36).
>
> The consequence is that a single resilience score that adds a resistance term and a recovery term is not a compact summary. It is the specific error the framework names: *"Summing them into one figure is the error this names, not the fix for it."* (Fig 12 :130) The two readings are reported side by side, per component, each with its own failure mode. A metric that drops the transient loses recovery; a metric that saturates loses resistance; a metric that pools readings loses both at the component level.
>
> **What I am not claiming:** that I have a universal resilience number, a universal recovery time, or a threshold that transfers across systems. The framework returns a shape, not a name. Where no figure line governs a universal scalar, I report `unreachable` rather than inventing a number. I am also not claiming that the two components should always be reported separately in practice. The resilience literature itself cautions that they are interdependent; the claim here is only that a measurement can, in principle, keep them distinct.
>
> **What would refute the separator:**
> 1. the two readings are related one to one, so carrying one carries the other with no information lost;
> 2. a single scalar exists whose round trip loses nothing on a ground where the resist decision and the recover decision can conflict;
> 3. per-component readings add nothing once averaged, because the aggregate preserves the basin decision;
> 4. no existing recovery reading registers at any resolution, and no better instrument can be built from one that already does;
> 5. a regime shift is indistinguishable from same-basin recovery at every achievable resolution and no discriminator can be derived.
>
> I wrote these down before sending this. If any one holds, I will narrow or retract the claim. A one-line "this fails because..." is more useful to me than agreement. If you would rather not engage, no reply is needed.
>
> **If it survives, and only then:** the sources are below. The figures are the authority, not my summary of them.
> - The figures and formula, SVG and markdown (branch `general-agent`): https://github.com/IsaiahN/tether/tree/general-agent/figures
> - The canonical usage rule: https://github.com/IsaiahN/tether/blob/general-agent/figures/README.md
> - The worked architecture article: https://adventuresinml.substack.com/p/arrival-of-the-fittest-the-tether
>
> **If you want to use the machinery:** the formula is in the figures link. Run it on a measurement problem you know well and can check against a ground. Write down what would refute your result *before* you run it, and state the mode of what you get. A reported failure is more useful to me than a reported success.
