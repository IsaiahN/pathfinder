**Subject:** Four things get called "the multiverse question." Which one would you test?

> Hi [name],
>
> I have one specific output I would like you to check against your own knowledge of cosmology. I am not asking you to read the theory first, and I am not asking you to agree with it. If the output does not survive, there is nothing else to look at.
>
> **The output (`specified`, not `grounded`):** "is eternal inflation and the multiverse testable or unfalsifiable" is a bundle of four different questions, and they have different answers because they have different grounds.
>
> 1. Inflation in our observable patch has a ground: the cosmic microwave background. It is testable and has been partly tested. That is not in dispute between the camps.
> 2. Other pocket universes in general have no nameable anchor. Nothing has registered from a causally disconnected region at any resolution. That is unreachable, which is a verdict, not a silence, and not a refutation.
> 3. One subclass does have an edge: a bubble collision with our bubble could leave a localized CMB signature. Searches have constrained that class without excluding the general claim.
> 4. The measure or typicality rule that turns the ensemble into predictions has no ground. Different measures give different answers. That is a convention conflict, not a measurement.
>
> So my claim is narrowly this: "unfalsifiable" is the right word for items 2 and 4, and the wrong word for items 1 and 3. "Tested" is the right word for item 1 and part of item 3, and the wrong word for 2 and 4. A single verdict over all four is a category error.
>
> **What I am not claiming:** that the multiverse exists, that it does not, that eternal inflation is established, or that this derivation settles any of them. I am not claiming that a null CMB search disproves other bubbles. A search that finds nothing proves nothing. I am also not claiming that a measure is a physical parameter. A declared meaning is not a grounded one.
>
> **What would refute the split, written before I ran it:**
> 1. a detected bubble-collision signature that survives model selection against ordinary fluctuations;
> 2. a prediction of eternal inflation that does not depend on any measure, typicality prior or observer proxy;
> 3. a ground reading incompatible with every slow-roll inflation model;
> 4. a proof that no observation could discriminate eternal inflation from a single-universe model even in principle;
> 5. an anchor for the ensemble whose answer does not move when a proponent pushes on it.
>
> If any one holds, I withdraw the affected item. A one-line "this fails because..." is more useful to me than agreement. If you would rather not engage, no reply is needed.
>
> **If it survives, and only then:** here are the sources. The figures are the authority, not my summary of them.
> - Figure text with line numbers (local framework extract): `FRAMEWORK/reference/03-FIGURES_TEXT.md`
> - Planck 2018 X, Constraints on inflation: https://arxiv.org/abs/1807.06211
> - Guth 2007, Eternal inflation and its implications: https://arxiv.org/abs/hep-th/0702178
>
> **If you want to use the machinery:** the formula is public. Run it on a problem you know well and can check against a ground. Write down what would refute your result *before* you run it, and state the mode of what you get. A reported failure is more useful to me than a reported success.

## Two-stage discipline

Stage 1: send the four-way split and the five refuters. Record the recipient's verdict **before** showing the framework. Stage 2, opt in only: offer the links. Later agreement is never pooled as corroboration, because Fig 2 :40 says agreement inside one evidence pool is the pool talking. Stage 3: offer the formula as a reusable method, with the instruction that the user writes their own refuter.
