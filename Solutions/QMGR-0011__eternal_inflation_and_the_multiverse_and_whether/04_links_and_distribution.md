## What the wider literature actually contributes

1. **The two sides of the same paper.** Guth 2007 says both that pocket universes are unobservable and that eternal inflation changes how predictions are extracted. Those two sentences are the whole D1/D2a distinction. Citing only the second makes eternal inflation look empirically ordinary; citing only the first makes it look empty. Both are quoted in `01` F3.
2. **The critic's own test program.** Ijjas, Steinhardt and Loeb 2013 calls the multiverse-unpredictability problem serious and still asks for decisive B-mode, non-Gaussianity and particle searches. That is an appeal to a ground, so by Fig 7 :35 the debate in D1 is selection between rooms, not a vote.
3. **A real null result with a real number.** Feeney et al. 2013 constrained bubble collisions to fewer than 4.0 expected sources at 95% confidence. This is what a falsifiable subclass looks like. It does not touch generic D2a.
4. **Why the measure is the hard part.** Linde and Noorbala show that the choice of regularization changes predictions drastically. SEP §4.4 adds that typicality principles make the theories "at least in principle testable" while the freedom to choose a measure makes the predictions "untrustworthy and susceptible to confirmation bias." Both halves are needed; neither alone settles D3.
5. **A live proposal that changes the shape.** Hawking and Hertog conjecture a finite, smooth exit, which would alter what the multiverse predicts and therefore what an instrument should look for. It is a conjecture, not a measurement.

## Distribution: offer, do not push

Figure-grounded reasons:

- Fig 4 :22 — *"Only methods cross up. Only head starts come down."* Lead with the four-way split and its refuter. The architecture is a generator, not an icebreaker.
- Fig 10 :52 — *"Bring a characterised residual, never the source."* A recipient who has absorbed the whole framework before being asked for a verdict has joined the frame being checked.
- Fig 9 :27-28 — *"describe it before you go looking, or any frame you pick will seem to fit."* Ask which of the four domains they would test first before offering any links.
- Fig 2 :40 — *"agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool."* Record the stage-1 verdict separately and never pool later agreement as corroboration.
- Fig 7 :35 — *"A room's agreement is variation, not a verdict. Selection between rooms is where the ground votes."* Do not convene a panel of people selected for likely agreement.

Recommended order in a reply: (1) the four domains and the five refuters; (2) ask which one they would test and what they would count as a failure; (3) record their answer; (4) only then the three links, with the note that the figures, not this summary, are the authority.

## The two-stage offer

Stage 1 carries the residual, the shape claim and the pre-registered refuter. Stage 2 is opt in and carries sources. Stage 3 offers the formula as a method for the recipient's own problem, with the instruction to write a refuter first and state a mode. The discipline follows Fig 10 :52 and Fig 4 :22. Stage 3 still needs a ground per problem, because the formula cannot check the direction of its own question.
