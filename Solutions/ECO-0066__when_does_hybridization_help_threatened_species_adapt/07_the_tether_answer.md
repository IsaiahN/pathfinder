# The Tether's answer: when hybridization helps, and when it threatens distinctiveness

**Mode: `specified` on the branch conditions, `general` on the structural rule, not `grounded`.** This is a shape and a route, not a result. Simulated derivation checked against the figures (`05_what_shape_means.md` section 7), not a ground reading. No fitness or ancestry measurement was taken.

## 1. The residual it answers

A threatened population has an adaptive shortfall in its habitat. Gene flow from a second lineage is available. Two outcomes are reported: fitness rises with the boundary intact, or the boundary dissolves. The residual is **two conditions in one compound**, not one hard question (Fig 9 :57 "a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either. It stops being a mixture and becomes a specific compound."). It is measured per population: Fig 1 :19 "The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated."

## 2. The answer

1. **Gene flow has no intrinsic valence.** It is a benefactor to the adaptive-capacity residual and a malfactor to the boundary-integrity residual, possibly at once. Fig 11 :49 "An actor's valence is relative to the residual, not a property it carries." Any single verdict on "hybridization" has averaged away the only signal that matters.

2. **The branch depends on the union type, not on the fact of admixture.** Fig 8 :14 "their union adds nothing" is the blend branch: ancestry is replaced, no adaptive atom crossed, distinctiveness is lost without compensation. Fig 8 :21 "nothing was aimed; the union is mute" is the undirected branch: a surplus may exist, but no residual was described, so no branch call can be scored. Fig 8 :26 "2: the atom crosses back" is the helpful branch: a low-overlap donor whose closure differs contributes a heritable atom against a stated residual.

3. **An import must be gated before the boundary moves.** Fig 6 :23 "No frame escapes its own closure. The bound moves only on contact with a frame whose closure differs." The source gate records where the allele came from; the shadow test asks whether it explains the adaptive gap. Fig 6 :24 "A wrong atom costs more than starting from primitives." In conservation the wrong atom can cost a lineage, so the price of an unvetted introduction exceeds the price of waiting.

4. **The habitat decides.** Fig 4 :34 "A seed carries no small tree: it carries what will grow one, and the conditions it lands in decide what it becomes." The allele is a head start. Disturbance, introduced taxa, and second-order contact can turn an aimed union into a blend. Habitat enumeration is mandatory, not background (Fig 11 :44).

5. **Prevention is an intervention, not a baseline.** Fig 11 :66 "Isolation is not removal of the habitat. It is substitution of one habitat for another." The two failure modes are invisible until they act: Fig 11 :68 "what you failed to reproduce is invisible until the goal fails" and Fig 11 :69 "what you unintentionally introduced is invisible until it acts." The asymmetry is decisive: introduction is available, exclusion is not (Fig 11 :61). A wrong branch call is not symmetric in cost.

6. **Distinctiveness is partly a convention, and the framework will not supply the cutoff.** Fig 10 :18 "a declared meaning is not a grounded one." Fig 9 :32 "you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself." What can be reported is the shape of a good boundary criterion: declared before the case, independent of the desired decision, and measurable on the ground. The numeric cutoff is a `gap`, reported as a verdict rather than a silence (Fig 13 :134 "Unreachable is a verdict here, not a silence.").

7. **The objective link is the weak link.** Sequencing and fitness monitoring are mature instruments. Stating what counts as progress, fitness gain **and** boundary persistence together, is the least instrumented link (Fig 3 :27 "A link with no instrument is not a link that works. It is a link nobody has looked at."). Effort spent on better sequencing cannot rescue an unset objective.

## 3. What it explicitly does not say

- Not that admixture is always a rescue. The empirical survey found rescue rare relative to swamping (Todesco et al. 2016), and this solution does not overrule that by assertion.
- Not that admixture is always extinction. Johnson et al. 2010 is a measured counterexample to that universal.
- Not that the Florida panther case is a recipe. Fig 5 :43 "A term that explains everything by saying nothing fails this, and so does a term that explains one occasion perfectly."
- Not a number for distinctiveness, and not a species concept. That is a declared convention, not a figure-derived result.
- Not `grounded`. No measurement here settles `left(R, φ)`.
- Not a determination that any named threatened species should or should not be introgressed. That requires a per-slot ground reading with a declared objective.

## 4. What would settle it - the ground

A source-discriminating instrument, not a further argument. From *The Hard Problem is About Source*, opened 2026-11-23: an instrument that reads green "cannot comment on the source"; the spectrometer resolved it. Here the readings and the required discriminators are:

- **Hybrid index or ancestry proportion** cannot comment on the source. The discriminator is provenance: donor identity, timing and direction of introgression, and whether the event was single or continuous.
- **Fitness gain** must be measured per population, not pooled. The discriminator is a survival or reproduction contrast in the actual habitat, with the habitat enumerated to second-order contact.
- **Boundary persistence** needs a metric declared before the intervention, not tuned after it.
- **Branch call:** does an aimed, traced, low-overlap union produce a heritable adaptive atom, or does a high-overlap union only replace ancestry? This is Fig 8's test, and it must be read against Fig 8 :32 "Closure is idempotent. Composing inside a closed set never leaves it, so no search, however long, adds an atom."

Standing refuters from `01` section 4 remain live: an aimed low-overlap union that loses the boundary without warning; a blend that preserves the boundary and raises fitness; a convention-free distinctiveness metric; a reversal of established introgression; and habitat-independent outcomes.

## 5. The honest stop

**Unreached, not unreachable.** The Figure 8 mapping onto biological branches is a `specified` fit, not a measurement. Fig 8 :56 "The triangulation claim fits four cases already known and has not been tested forward." The same caveat applies here. The framework narrows what an answer must look like and names what is owed: provenance, a shadow test, per-slot fitness, a declared boundary metric, and low-overlap review. It stops there and records the lineage so the stop is checkable.

## 6. One-paragraph version for outreach

> The Tether does not say hybridization is good or bad for threatened species. It says the question is two residuals, adaptive capacity and boundary integrity, measured per population, and that gene flow's effect is relative to which residual you are scoring. The outcome depends on the union type: a high-overlap blend replaces ancestry without importing adaptation, an undirected swarm has no stated objective so nothing can be scored, and only a low-overlap union described against a stated residual can cross an adaptive allele back. That import still needs a provenance gate and a shadow test, and the habitat decides what the allele becomes. Preventing contact is not a neutral baseline; it substitutes a habitat and cannot undo an established introgression. The framework will not supply a cutoff for distinctiveness, because it returns a shape, not a name. What it offers is the classifier, the gates, the declared convention, and refuters written in advance. The branch stays open until the ground pays for one of them.
