# Outreach draft: check the branch classifier before deciding about the intervention

**Use:** one to one, or as a cover letter attached to a submission through the public venues in `03_public_contact_points.csv`. **Not** for bulk send.

**Hard precondition:** do not send until the two residuals and the pre-registered refuter in `01_figure_grounded_audit.md` are written down in the recipient's own terms. The first stage collapses if the recipient is shown a conclusion before being asked what would count as a failure.

---

**Subject:** One question about hybridization: which branch is this population on?

> Hi [name],
>
> I have one specific output to check against what you already know. I am not asking you to read the framework first, and I am not asking you to agree with it. If the output does not survive, there is nothing else to look at.
>
> **The output (`specified`, not `grounded`):** hybridization is not one intervention with one valence. It is a union of two residuals that must be measured separately for each population. The first is adaptive capacity: does survival or reproduction rise in the actual habitat? The second is boundary integrity: does the lineage remain distinguishable under a criterion declared before the case is scored? Gene flow can help the first while threatening the second, in the same population at the same time. Fig 11 :49 "An actor's valence is relative to the residual, not a property it carries." Fig 1 :19 "The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated." A species-wide average hides the decision.
>
> The shape claim is that three union types separate the observed outcomes. A high-overlap blend replaces ancestry without importing adaptive variation. An undirected union produces a mix with no stated objective, so nothing was aimed and nothing can be scored. A low-overlap union, described against a stated residual, can cross an adaptive allele back into the recipient, and that is the branch where hybridization helps. An import also needs two gates before the lineage boundary moves: a provenance gate on where the allele came from, and a shadow test on whether it explains the adaptive gap.
>
> **What I am not claiming:** that admixture is always a rescue, that introgression is always extinction by swamping, that the Florida panther case is a general rule, or that I can supply a number for distinctiveness. The framework returns a shape, not a name. The cutoff for distinctiveness is a convention that has to be declared and kept from deciding the outcome.
>
> **What would refute the shape claim (written before this message):**
> 1. A low-overlap, aimed, traced, fitness-improving introgression in which the recipient boundary nevertheless disappears with no prior warning signal.
> 2. A high-overlap blend in which the boundary persists and fitness rises with no intervention.
> 3. A ground-anchored distinctiveness metric declared before interventions that settles both residuals on one axis.
> 4. A demonstrated removal of established introgression from a wild population without substituting a habitat.
>
> A one-line "this fails because..." is more useful to me than agreement. If you would rather not engage, no reply is needed. **If you do reply, please give your branch verdict on the classifier before reading anything below.**
>
> **If it survives, and only then:** the sources are below. The figures are the authority, not my summary of them.

> - Canonical usage rule (local): `FRAMEWORK/reference/FIGURES_README_canonical.md`

Write down what would refute your result *before* you run it, and state the mode of what you get. A reported failure is more useful to me than a reported success.
