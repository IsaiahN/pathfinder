# Isomers, functional groups, and order in the hybridization question

**Mode: `specified`.** This is a simulated derivation checked against the figures, not a ground reading (see `05_what_shape_means.md` section 7).

## RESIDUAL

Two lineages P (threatened) and D (donor) are in contact. Does the outcome depend on which genes they share, or on how those genes are arranged and expressed? And in what order must the decision be made: classify the union first, or assemble the atoms first? The supporting derivation owes (1) an isomer test, (2) a functional-group test, and (3) a dependency order, each with a falsifier.

## DERIVATION

### Q1 - Are the two lineages isomers?

Fig 12 :117 "isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."
Fig 12 :96 "Two entries can share every ingredient and be inverses of one another."

Write the two lineages over a shared allele set A. If P and D contain largely the same alleles but differ in regulatory arrangement, mating system, or which alleles are expressed under which conditions, then they are isomers in the figure's precise sense: same atoms, different bond, different substance. The practical consequence is that hybrid index alone cannot distinguish them, because index counts ingredients and not arrangement. This is the Figure 12 version of the metamerism warning in `05` section 4.

The weaker form is also available. If the allele sets do not match, the two are not isomers but share a **functional group**: Fig 12 :123 "functional group. A substructure whose presence fixes the outcome. The group says what kind; the qualifier says which member." Here the functional group is the adaptive-capacity structure: a donor-derived allele that is selected in the recipient habitat. The qualifier says which allele and which habitat.

**Falsifier, written before the run:** if two lineages with indistinguishable allele sets but different arrangements show identical hybrid fitness and identical boundary persistence across habitats, the isomer claim fails. The functional-group claim would then stand alone.

### Q2 - What order does the decision require?

Fig 3 :20 "The chain is ordered by dependency, not by importance. Each link consumes what the one above produces, so effort spent above the break arrives at a step that cannot use it."
Fig 9 :29 "matching is a one-pass check, not a search"
Fig 9 :27 "describe it before you go looking, or any frame you pick will seem to fit"

The dependency order is:

1. **Perceive**: measure the per-slot residual (fitness shortfall and ancestry) before anything else.
2. **Vocabulary**: have terms for what the situation affords, including "donor atom available", "barrier present", "disturbance regime".
3. **Objective**: state what counts as progress, the conjunction of fitness gain and boundary persistence. This is the middle link and is the least instrumented (Fig 3 :27).
4. **Plan**: choose the union type and the gate procedure under incomplete information.
5. **Learn and carry**: name what worked, verify it at the ground, and carry the method, not the recording, to the next population.

An intervention planned at step 4 before step 3 is fixed is effort above the break arriving at a link that cannot use it. A manager who first decides "we will introduce donors" and then defines distinctiveness to fit has reversed the order and has let a convention decide an outcome (Fig 10 :47).

**Falsifier:** if a documented program reliably reaches correct branch calls by choosing donors first and defining the objective afterwards, the dependency order claim is refuted and must be re-censused against Fig 3.

### Q3 - What is the analogue in other fields?

The figure's own analogues are used, not invented:

- **Import** (Fig 6 :14 "the only operator that moves the wall") is the general form of introgression. The source gate and shadow test are the general form of provenance and mechanistic checking.
- **Union surplus** (Fig 8 :45 "Two frames together reach what neither reaches alone.") is the general form of adaptive introgression, valid only where closures differ and a residual was described.
- **Blend** (Fig 8 :14 "their union adds nothing") is the general form of swamping without gain.
- **Seed and habitat** (Fig 4 :34 "A seed carries no small tree: it carries what will grow one, and the conditions it lands in decide what it becomes.") is the general form of an adaptive allele: a head start whose fate is decided by the receiving conditions.

Fig 9 :30 "your words are borrowed too, so describe the effect rather than the cause." The analogues are borrowed vocabulary. This derivation describes effects so a working biologist can check them without accepting the framework.

### Q4 - What is settled and what may compose further?

Fig 12 :104 "A settled molecule becomes an atom for whatever composes over it. So irreducible is a fact about a level and never about the world."

A single verified branch call (say: this aimed, provenanced event raised fitness and the boundary persisted) settles at the ground and can become an atom for the next management decision. It does not become a universal rule. The recursion is legitimate; the replay is not. Fig 4 :22 "Only methods cross up. Only head starts come down."

**Falsifier:** if a verified branch call transfers directly to a new species with no re-enumeration of habitat and no new gate, the level-relativity claim is refuted.

## CONCLUSION

The two lineages are isomers when their allele sets match and their arrangement differs; otherwise they share the adaptive-capacity functional group. The decision order is perceive, vocabulary, objective, plan, carry, with the objective link being the one most likely to be missing. The analogues are import, union surplus, blend, and seed-and-habitat, all drawn from the figures. Nothing here requires a new biological primitive. The strain is that arrangement is harder to measure than ingredient count, which is exactly the Figure 12 point: Fig 12 :14 "the ingredients are recorded and the arrangement is not".
