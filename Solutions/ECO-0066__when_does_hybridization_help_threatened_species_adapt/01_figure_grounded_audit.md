## 0. The residual, in effect terms

A threatened population P has an adaptive shortfall S in its current habitat: survival or reproduction is below what the changing conditions require. A second lineage D is in contact, voluntarily or because a maintainer introduced it. Gene flow from D into P is a candidate intervention. Two effects are reported in the literature:

- **Branch H:** P's fitness rises and P remains a distinguishable lineage.
- **Branch T:** P's boundary dissolves into the hybrid swarm, sometimes with a demographic cost from maladaptive hybrids.

The residual R is the unaccounted difference between prediction and observation about which branch a case is on. It is **indexed per population slot**: Fig 1 :19 "The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated." A species-wide average hides the only signal that says where to intervene. Fig 1 :22 "Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average."

Two immediate consequences follow and are kept separate throughout:

1. "Help" and "threaten" are not opposite ends of one scale. They are verdicts on two different residuals, adaptive capacity and lineage integrity. Fig 11 :49 "An actor's valence is relative to the residual, not a property it carries." Fig 11 :50 "The same actor is a benefactor to one residual and a malfactor to another, so mutualism and parasitism are relations rather than kinds." Gene flow is neither intrinsically good nor intrinsically bad.
2. The question is a **union compound**, not one hard question. Fig 9 :55 "A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones." Fig 9 :57 "a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either. It stops being a mixture and becomes a specific compound." Both conditions bind at once in a real management decision, so a single lever that clears one leaves the intersection standing.

The sought-for shape, written down before the run as an ordinary term so it can be refused: **a condition set that classifies a hybridization event by its union type, and a named per-slot measurement that would confirm or break the classification.** That is a shape, not an answer key.

## 1. Domain census

### 1.1 R by arity, symmetry, scale

- **Arity:** two conditions, adaptive shortfall and boundary integrity. Union, not partition. Fig 9 :54 "And when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again."
- **Symmetry:** the relation between P and D is not generally symmetric. Introgression is directional in the empirical record (rare-mother and rare-recipient asymmetries in Todesco et al. 2016). In figure terms, effect depends on operand order, which is an ordered bond, not a symmetric one: Fig 12 :46 "A bond is the typed relation between two operands. There are seven, they are not interchangeable, and which one holds is not recoverable from the operands."
- **Scale:** first order at the population and genome, second order at the lineage boundary and the management decision. Fig 12 :103 "Composition is recursive, and the level decides what is irreducible." A settled first-order outcome becomes an operand at the next level.

### 1.2 One line per domain

No domain is admitted without a nameable anchor.

| domain | actors | currency | anchor | question type |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **D1 Genomic and population frame** | two gene pools P and D, alleles, reproductive barriers, ancestry blocks | gene flow, heterozygosity, fitness, ancestry proportion | survival and reproduction measured in the habitat (the ground) | union of two closures; which union type is realized |
| **D2 Habitat and ecological frame** | habitat, conditions, relations (pollination, predation, competition), introduced taxa, disturbance | contact, selection, establishment | the same ground, read as fitness in that habitat | enumeration, not composition |
| **D3 Management and verification frame** | managers, reviewers, conventions, monitoring metrics | decisions, conventions, agreement, residual | the ground, never agreement | convention versus verdict; separation of seats |

D1, D2, and D3 are a **union**, not a partition: the same hybridization event is in contact with all three at once (Fig 11 :43 "The habitat is enumerated, never composed."). The overlap is a compound with its own loop.

### 1.3 Dependency order (Fig 3)

1. Enumerate the habitat and the per-slot residual before choosing any frame: Fig 11 :44 "List everything in contact with the residual, then what is in contact with those, and outward until the cascade stops mattering." and Fig 9 :27 "describe it before you go looking, or any frame you pick will seem to fit".
2. Fix the objective in the field's own terms: what counts as progress is fitness gain **with** a persisting boundary, not one or the other. This is the middle link of Fig 3, the one with the fewest instruments: Fig 3 :27 "A link with no instrument is not a link that works. It is a link nobody has looked at."
3. Classify the union type before sourcing an intervention: Fig 8 :45 "Two frames together reach what neither reaches alone."
4. Gate the imported atom: source gate and shadow test (Fig 6 :12, Fig 6 :13), before the wall moves (Fig 6 :14 "the only operator that moves the wall").
5. Settle at the ground: Fig 2 :45 "Frames range against the ground. They do not negotiate with it."

Refusal precedes mint (formula step 2): if "hybridization is always a threat" or "hybridization is always a rescue" is held as a term with a residual that persists and does not trend down, that term is refused before any new one is offered.

## 3. Findings

### F1 - Gene flow's valence is relative to the residual (decisive for the framing)

Fig 11 :49 "An actor's valence is relative to the residual, not a property it carries."
Fig 11 :51 "No standing list of good actors survives a change of residual."

The question assumes hybridization has a single valence that flips at some threshold. The figures deny that. Gene flow is a benefactor to the adaptive-capacity residual and a malfactor to the boundary-integrity residual, possibly in the same population at the same time. The two verdicts must be recorded separately and never averaged (Fig 1 :19). Any answer that returns one number for "is hybridization good for threatened species" has already committed the Fig 1 aggregation error.

**Refuter:** a case in which the same per-population measurement settles both residuals in one metric with no declared convention. That would collapse the two into one residual and break this finding. Not observed in the sources read.

### F2 - Three union types, three outcomes

Fig 8 :14 "their union adds nothing"
Fig 8 :49 "Two frames that overlap almost completely are one frame wearing two names."
Fig 8 :21 "nothing was aimed; the union is mute"
Fig 8 :29 "low overlap: B does not draw on A's evidence"

Figure 8 names three ways two frames combine:

1. **Blend** (collapse 4): high overlap, same atoms under different names. The union adds nothing. Mapped to genetic swamping without adaptive gain: ancestry is replaced and the boundary dissolves, but no new adaptive atom crossed. This is branch T.
2. **Undirected union** (collapse 5): the closures differ, so a surplus exists, but no residual is described and nothing is aimed. Mapped to an unmanaged hybrid swarm: a mix with no stated objective, where boundary loss and fitness effects are both unmonitored. This is the dangerous branch, because "there is a surplus somewhere" is not a verdict.
3. **Triangulation** (grounded panel): the residual is described first, a low-overlap frame is found, and an atom crosses back: Fig 8 :26 "2: the atom crosses back". Mapped to adaptive introgression or genetic rescue: a donor-derived allele enters the recipient closure and is selected in the habitat. This is branch H.

Fig 8 :45 "Two frames together reach what neither reaches alone." is the figure's permission for branch H, but only under the low-overlap, described-residual condition. It is not permission for every contact between two lineages.

**Refuter:** a documented case of a high-overlap blend that increases recipient fitness while preserving a distinct boundary, or a documented low-overlap, aimed union that produces no heritable adaptive atom. Either breaks the mapping.

### F3 - Provenance and shadow test decide whether the wall may move

Fig 6 :23 "No frame escapes its own closure. The bound moves only on contact with a frame whose closure differs."
Fig 6 :12 "source gate: provenance"
Fig 6 :13 "shadow test: explains a gap"
Fig 6 :24 "A wrong atom costs more than starting from primitives."

Hybridization is an import in the precise sense of Figure 6: an atom from a closure the recipient does not hold becomes available. Two gates apply. **Source gate:** where did the allele come from, and is the donor a real, characterizable frame rather than an unlabelled mixture? **Shadow test:** does the allele explain the adaptive gap it is supposed to fill, rather than merely correlate with ancestry? Without both, the wall moves on an unvetted atom. The cost asymmetry is severe here: a maladaptive introgression can be effectively irreversible, so the price of a wrong atom is higher than waiting for new mutation.

Fig 6 :22 "NOVEL means novel relative to atoms, not to the world." An allele already present in the donor is not new to the world; what is new is its availability in the recipient's closure. The unit of success is availability plus settlement, not the allele's existence.

**Refuter:** a case where introgression increases fitness but the donor allele cannot be traced and the adaptive mechanism is not identified, and the manager still calls it adaptive introgression. That is an import with no gates and it breaks this finding as a practical discriminator.

### F4 - Habitat decides what the imported atom becomes

Fig 4 :34 "A seed carries no small tree: it carries what will grow one, and the conditions it lands in decide what it becomes."
Fig 11 :43 "The habitat is enumerated, never composed."
Fig 11 :52 "Scoping the habitat to direct contact is how DDT reached the eagles"

An introgressed allele is a head start, not a stored adaptation. The recipient habitat selects it or discards it. Enumeration must include second-order contact: an introduced pollinator, a competitor, a disease vector, or a disturbance regime can decide the outcome even though none is part of the genome. Todesco et al. 2016 report that hybridization mainly occurred in native rather than novel environments and that disturbance is associated with hybrid establishment; their management conclusion is to restore mature and diverse habitats that resist hybrid establishment. That is the habitat-side branch condition.

**Refuter:** a case where the same donor allele produces the same fitness effect across two habitats with enumerated, different selection regimes. That would make the allele a context-free adaptation and break the habitat dependence.

### F5 - Prevention is substitution, not removal

Fig 11 :61 "The seat's power is asymmetric. Introduction is available; exclusion is not."
Fig 11 :66 "Isolation is not removal of the habitat. It is substitution of one habitat for another."
Fig 11 :68 "what you failed to reproduce is invisible until the goal fails"
Fig 11 :69 "what you unintentionally introduced is invisible until it acts"
Fig 11 :63 "Which is why a managed ecosystem is permanent work rather than a solved state."

A policy of "keep the species pure" is not a neutral baseline. It substitutes a managed habitat for the contact habitat and inherits actors that were not chosen. The two silent failure modes are exactly the ones conservation programs have: the conditions the lineage needed are not reproduced, and the actors of the new habitat act later. The asymmetry also means the two branches have unequal reversibility. Avoiding an introduction can be undone; an established introgression cannot be subtracted. Florida panther history before 1995 (Johnson et al. 2010: about 20 to 25 adults, predicted 95 percent extinction likelihood within two decades) is the empirical cost of the substitution branch.

**Refuter:** a demonstrated technique that removes an established introgressed allele from a wild population without substituting a habitat or importing further actors. None appears in the sources read.

### F6 - Distinctiveness is partly a convention, and that is a real gap

Fig 10 :18 "a declared meaning is not a grounded one"
Fig 10 :47 "a convention that decides an outcome has stopped being one"
Fig 2 :36 "An anchor is legitimate when the question is constitutively about it."

"Distinctiveness" depends on a species concept and a threshold chosen by a maintainer. If that threshold is tuned until the desired management outcome follows, the convention has decided an outcome and has stopped being a convention. The figures cannot supply the number. What they can supply is the **shape** of the missing thing: a measurable boundary persistence condition, stated before the case is scored, that the ground can pay or refuse. Fig 9 :32 "you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself."

This is recorded as `gap` on the numeric threshold, **not** on the shape. It is not silence: Fig 13 :134 "Unreachable is a verdict here, not a silence." The honest report is that the framework narrows what a distinctiveness metric must look like while declining to name its cutoff.

**Refuter:** a ground-anchored, community-accepted distinctiveness metric whose cutoff is independent of the management decision it is used for. If such a metric exists and settles both residuals, the gap closes and F1 may need re-census.

### F7 - The objective link is where the chain breaks

Fig 3 :27 "A link with no instrument is not a link that works. It is a link nobody has looked at."
Fig 3 :19 "Optimising a link that already works cannot rescue one that does not."

Genomic tools for detecting introgression are mature; survival monitoring is mature. The weakest link is the objective: what would count as progress here, in the field's own terms? Without an agreed objective, more sequencing effort lands above the break and cannot rescue it. The right objective, in effect terms, is a conjunction that must be written down before the run: per-slot fitness gain **and** boundary persistence, each with its own instrument.

**Refuter:** a field-wide, instrumented definition of successful genetic rescue that already includes boundary persistence and is used before interventions, not after. That would move the break.

### F8 - One case study is not a generator

Fig 5 :43 "A term that explains everything by saying nothing fails this, and so does a term that explains one occasion perfectly."
Fig 4 :22 "Only methods cross up. Only head starts come down."

The Florida panther case (Johnson et al. 2010) is a real ground reading: eight Texas females introduced in 1995, population tripled, heterozygosity doubled, fitness measures improved. It is a **single occasion**. Fig 5 :43 refuses a term that explains one occasion perfectly. Fig 7 :35 "A room's agreement is variation, not a verdict. Selection between rooms is where the ground votes." What may cross upward from that case is the method (source gate, single aimed event, habitat protection, monitoring), not the conclusion that admixture is always a rescue.

**Refuter:** a second, low-overlap case in which the same method produces the opposite outcome. That would not break the method; it would break any claim that the case itself is a rule.

### F9 - Independence must be bought in proportion to irreversibility

Fig 2 :42 "Buy independence in proportion to the cost of being wrong."
Fig 2 :31 "Independence, between two frames, is how little they draw on the same evidence."
Fig 7 :36 "The next room must differ from the last, and match the residual it surfaced."

The cost of a wrong branch call can be the loss of a lineage. That price requires reviewers from different closures: a population geneticist reading the ancestry data, a field ecologist reading the habitat enumeration, and a manager reading the convention declaration. They must not all be drawn from the same review literature. The four world sources used below are from one broad biological literature and are therefore one witness, not four (Fig 2 :40). This audit says so rather than hiding it.

**Refuter:** a decision process in which high correlation among reviewers demonstrably produced a correct, ground-paid branch call. Correlation is disqualifying for a verdict by rule, so this refuter would force a re-census of Fig 2 rather than a mere correction.

## 4. Pre-registered refuter

Written before the conclusion and before any outreach, per the seat map's rule that a falsifier written before the run is a design instrument. The shape claim is refuted if any of the following holds:

1. A case with a described per-slot residual, low donor-recipient overlap, traced donor ancestry, and a demonstrated fitness gain in which the recipient boundary nevertheless disappears with no warning signal. This breaks the triangulated-union branch.
2. A case of high-overlap blending in which the recipient boundary persists and fitness rises, with no aimed intervention. This breaks the blend branch.
3. A field-wide distinctiveness metric that is ground-anchored, declared before interventions, and settles both residuals on one axis without a convention. This breaks F6 and forces a re-census of F1.
4. A documented reversal of established introgression in the wild without habitat substitution or further import. This breaks F5.
5. An empirical regularity in which hybridization outcomes are independent of habitat enumeration. This breaks F4.

If any fires, the corresponding finding is retracted in the log below and the packet is withdrawn until repaired. Until one is run, no part of this may be labelled `grounded`.

## 5. Recommendation

1. Lead with the two-residual split and the pre-registered refuter, never with a verdict on hybridization in general (F1, F2).
2. Before any intervention, write down the objective as a conjunction with two instruments: per-population fitness and a boundary-persistence measure. Do not tune the boundary threshold to the desired decision (F6, F7).
3. Treat a proposed introgression as an import: record donor provenance, run a shadow test against the stated adaptive gap, and price a wrong allele above waiting (F3).
4. Enumerate the habitat out to second-order contact before predicting an outcome, including disturbance and introduced taxa (F4).
5. Treat isolation as an active habitat substitution with two silent failure modes, not as the absence of action (F5).
6. Buy low-overlap review in proportion to irreversibility. Do not pool agreement as corroboration (F9).
7. Fig 4 :25 "A recording carried upward looks like knowledge and is a description of one occasion." Send the classifier and the refuter, not the Florida panther story as a rule.

## 7. Strains and honest stop

- **Live strain:** the biological mapping of Figure 8's three panels onto swamping, undirected swarms, and adaptive introgression is a structural fit, not a measurement. Fig 8 :56 "The triangulation claim fits four cases already known and has not been tested forward." The same caution applies here. The mapping is kept because the figure's own conditions are checkable, and it is labelled `specified`.
- **Live gap:** the numeric distinctiveness threshold (F6). Unreachable is a verdict, not a silence.
- **No re-census owed** for the frame-internal findings F1, F3, F5, F9, which are direct readings of the figure text.
- **Honest stop:** the answer ends where the instruments end. A source-discriminating measurement: per-population fitness and ancestry together with a declared boundary metric, is what would move any part of this from `candidate` to `grounded`. Agreement, prestige, and the number of papers citing a case are not such an instrument.
