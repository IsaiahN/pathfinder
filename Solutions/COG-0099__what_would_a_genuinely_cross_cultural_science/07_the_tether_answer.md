# The Tether's answer: what a genuinely cross-cultural science of mind would look like

**Mode: `general` on the structural shape, `specified` on the per-domain verdicts, `gap` on the specific techniques and the universal concept list, not `grounded`.** This is a shape and a route, not a result. Simulated derivation checked against the figures (`05_what_shape_means.md` section 7), not a ground reading.

---

## 1. The residual it answers

A prediction formed in one frame is exported to another population and the reading fails to register against what is actually there: either a difference appears that the ground does not pay for, or no difference appears where one does. The measuring frame is silently a party to the measurement. The question asks what would remove that fault honestly, in methods, concepts and standards of evidence.

## 2. The shape and the per-domain verdicts

Kept separate and never averaged.

**D1, methods (`specified`, triangulated).** Fig 6 :32, "An instrument is not built from a description. It is improved from a worse instrument already returning something." An instrument is extended from a worse instrument that is already returning something in the new population, and the residual it produces is tracked. You do not design a culture-fair test from an account of fairness. Fig 1 :19, "The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated." Fig 1 :22, "Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average." Readings stay per person and per frame. Fig 10 :24, "the ground does not decay, the channel does"; Fig 10 :26, "stale percepts, dropped transients, saturated metrics, pooled readings". A national mean is a pooled reading.

**D2, concepts (`specified`, triangulated).** Categories are declared choices with recorded provenance, not neutral discoveries. Fig 13 :16, "it names what is being measured"; Fig 13 :26, "it decides what the measurement would mean". They decide both what is measured and what the reading means, so they belong inside the measurement, not before it. Fig 12 :111, "A vocabulary without bonds can name a state and cannot name a route to one." A trait list names states; it cannot say what a situation affords or how a person gets somewhere from it. Operators :58, "a refutation is a negation, which is why a vocabulary without it can only accumulate." The vocabulary must be able to refuse a universal claim. Over a fixed atom set, two applications differing only in their bond to the ground are isomers (Fig 12 :117, "isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."); as normally reported with different ingredient lists, they are only functional-group members (Fig 12 :123, "functional group. A substructure whose presence fixes the outcome. The group says what kind; the qualifier says which member.").

**D3, standards of evidence (`general`, triangulated).** Fig 2 :35, "Alignment is triangulation, not negotiation. A mirror cannot correct you." Agreement counts only when the frames draw on different evidence. Fig 2 :40, "disqualifying for a verdict: agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool". Fig 8 :48-49, "How much two frames draw on the same evidence decides both whether their agreement means anything and whether their union has any surplus. Two frames that overlap almost completely are one frame wearing two names." Fig 7 :35, "A room's agreement is variation, not a verdict. Selection between rooms is where the ground votes." Convergent derivation from separate experience is the strongest form: Fig 8 :50, "Two frames can hold the same atom by convergence, reaching it separately, which is corroboration at maximum independence." Fig 2 :42, "Buy independence in proportion to the cost of being wrong."

**D4, habitat and contact (`general`, triangulated).** Fig 11 :43, "The habitat is enumerated, never composed." List everything in contact with the residual: language, schooling, migration, media, ecology, institutions, and the testing situation itself; then what contacts those, outward until the cascade stops mattering. Fig 11 :39, "capability is a property of agent-and-habitat, never of the agent alone". A finding is a property of a person in a habitat, not of a disembodied mind.

**Across all domains (`general`).** Describe the effect, not the borrowed cause: Fig 9 :31, "your words are borrowed too, so describe the effect rather than the cause"; Fig 6 :30, "The description travels. Fails by being sharpened into terms only we can read. Cast it in effect terms." Cross-frame imports carry a source gate and a shadow test (Fig 6 :12, "source gate: provenance"; Fig 6 :13, "shadow test: explains a gap"), and a wrong atom costs more than starting from primitives (Fig 6 :24, "Import from a live peer is debited against that frame's independence. A wrong atom costs more than starting from primitives."). No final account: Fig 7 :6, "the limit: no last room".

## 3. The gap

No figure line names a statistical technique, a universal concept list, or a threshold for how many cultures is enough. Fig 9 :32, "you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself". Fig 13 :134, "Unreachable is a verdict here, not a silence,". Fig 8 :39, "So a search that finds nothing is never a proof of absence." Measurement invariance, multilevel modelling and registered reports are real world tools with their own grounds, including a live internal dispute over invariance testing. They are imports with provenance, not figure derivations. Declared `gap` of the unreachable-at-this-budget kind, not unreachable in principle.

## 4. What it explicitly does not say

- Not that cross-cultural differences are mostly measurement artifacts. That collapses the union into D1 and is the single-lever error the refuter tests.
- Not that culture-fair tests can be designed from first principles. Fig 6 :32 forbids it.
- Not that agreement between cultures is corroboration. Fig 2 :40 and Fig 7 :35 forbid it.
- Not that a universal concept list can be read off the framework. Naming an already-held atom is a provenanced binding, not a derivation; naming a missing primitive is forbidden.
- Not that measurement invariance (or any named technique) is the figure-derived standard.
- Not that the answer is a final theory. Fig 7 :6 and Fig 13 :90 leave no last room and no last route.
- Not `grounded`. No cross-cultural measurement was taken.

## 5. What would settle it: the ground

A source-discriminating instrument, not a further argument. From *The Hard Problem is About Source*: an instrument that reads green "cannot comment on the source"; it was "not by argument, but by the arrival of a better instrument" that the case closed. Here:

1. Does a score difference come from the named construct, from response style, from comprehension of the task wording, from the testing situation, or from a source not yet enumerated? An instrument that discriminates these would name the source.
2. **Independence test.** Are the two frames actually drawing on different evidence, or are they one pool wearing two names? If the latter, the corroboration collapses.
3. **Fixed-atom test.** Over a fixed atom set, do two applications with different bonds to the ground behave differently? If not, the isomer claim falls to the functional-group reading (`06`, Q2).
4. **Standing falsifiers.** A single lever that removes the residual in all conditions at once; a frame that can move the thing it measures and still predict better; an instrument built from pure description that works at first contact; a universal list derived from held atoms alone; agreement from a shared pool that moves the ground estimate; a closing frame that improves its predictions. Any one fires the retraction procedure in `01` section 4.

## 6. The honest stop

**Unreached, not unreachable.** Fig 6 :27, "Which is why some unreachability is depth and some is genuine absence, and from inside the frame the two look the same." The gap on a specific technique may be depth (a better instrument exists and has not been built), or genuine absence (no instrument has ever registered anything along that axis). From inside this derivation the two look the same, and the framework says so. The answer stops at the limit of the available instruments and records the lineage so the stop is checkable. "Indiscernibility is not identity."

## 7. One-paragraph version for outreach

> A genuinely cross-cultural science of mind would not be one method applied everywhere. It would unbundle into three conditions with different anchors: instruments grown from tasks already returning something in each population, with residuals kept per person and per frame rather than averaged into a culture mean; categories declared as choices with their provenance recorded and with a refusal available, because no vocabulary is neutral; and evidence that counts only when frames drawing on genuinely different evidence range separately against a ground none of them can move. Agreement among studies that share samples, scales and categories is one witness spelled many ways, not corroboration. It would enumerate the habitat around each residual, describe effects rather than export borrowed causes, import categories with a source gate and a shadow test, and keep the science open because there is no last room. The figures return that shape. They do not name the statistical package, the universal concept list, or the number of cultures, which is a declared gap, not a silence. What would close it is a source-discriminating instrument, not another argument. This is a simulated derivation checked against the figures, not a ground reading, and it cannot settle the question it reports on.
