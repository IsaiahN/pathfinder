# Outreach draft: check the output before deciding about the method

**Use:** one-to-one, or as a cover letter attached to a submission through the public venues in `03_public_contact_points.csv`. **Not** for bulk send.

**Hard precondition:** do not send until the two checks in section 1 of `08_expert_verification_packet.md` have been run by someone who does not hold this derivation. The output must survive validation or the two-stage offer collapses on contact.

**Subject:** One checkable claim about cross-cultural score differences

> Hi [name],
>
> I have one specific output I would like you to check against what you already know. I am not
> asking you to read the framework first, and I am not asking you to agree with me. If the
> output does not survive, there is nothing else to look at.
>
> **The output (`general` shape, `specified` application, not `grounded`):** a between-group
> score difference is a reading, not a source. The figures I work from say a transform between
> two frames is lossy, and that two entries with the same ingredients can be different things
> whose notation cannot tell them apart. Applied here: two groups can answer an identically
> worded item differently because the meaning, the response scale, or the contact changed, and
> nothing in the score announces which. So the observed difference by itself does not decide
> between a genuine construct difference, a translation artifact, a testing artifact and a
> sampling artifact. These four conditions overlap, so they have to be unbundled before a
> single lever is trusted.
>
> **What the output proposes as a procedure, not a result:** describe the residual in effect
> terms before choosing a method; measure the round-trip gap across the translation instead of
> assuming it is zero; install a check in each domain that can actually fail; test per item and
> per group rather than on the pooled mean; buy independence with methods that draw on
> different evidence; treat fit thresholds as budget filters, never as witnesses for the
> verdict; and stop with the lineage recorded where the instruments stop discriminating. The
> framework returns the shape of a procedure, not a named statistical test. I am explicitly not
> claiming it can tell you which test to use.
>
> **What I am not claiming:** that invariance testing, anchoring vignettes, back-translation
> or any other named method is certified by the figures; that a failure of scalar invariance
> proves a difference is an artifact; that a large, significant difference proves it is real;
> or that this derivation has settled any actual cultural comparison. No dataset was measured
> here. This is a simulated derivation, and it cannot settle the question it reports on.
>

> 2. a single lever that clears every intersection of translation, response style and sampling,

> 3. a figure line naming one specific statistical procedure as sufficient to certify genuineness;

## Why this version is ordered this way

| the move | figure-grounded reason |
|---|---|
| Output first, framework second | Fig 10 :52 "Bring a characterised residual, never the source." A recipient who absorbs the framework before giving a verdict joins the frame being checked. |
| Do not ask for agreement | Fig 2 :35 "Alignment is triangulation, not negotiation. A mirror cannot correct you." |
| Pre-registered refuter | Seat rule: a falsifier written before the run is a design instrument, not only an honesty device. |
| Mode stated on every substantive claim | `THE_FORMULA.md`: "Mode. general · specified · grounded. State which." |
| Specific tests withheld | Fig 9 :32 "you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself"; F8 records the choice of test as a `gap`. |
| Verdict recorded before links | Fig 2 :40: agreement from a shared evidence pool is one witness, not corroboration. |
| Split into four conditions before choosing a method | Fig 9 :54 "And when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again." |
| No request for trust or prestige | Prestige can fund an action but cannot settle it; only the ground settles it. |
| Offer the method as reusable | Fig 4 :22 "Only methods cross up. Only head starts come down." Sending a method does not give away an answer. |
| Ask the reader to write their own refuter | A frame cannot check the direction of its own question; the sought-for shape must be bound as an ordinary term. |

## Two-stage (in practice, three) offer

1. **Stage 1:** send the residual, the shape and the refuter. Ask what the recipient would count as a failure. Record the verdict before showing anything else.
2. **Stage 2, opt-in only:** if they are curious, offer the three local source paths. They read the figure text themselves; the summary is not the authority.
3. **Stage 3:** offer the formula as a method to reuse, with the instruction that each user states their own ground, writes their own refuter before the run, and labels the mode of the result.

Stage 2 is opt-in by construction because a recipient who has absorbed the interior can no longer serve as an outside frame. Later agreement is never pooled as corroboration. Stage 3 is permitted because a generator can be given away without giving away an answer, but each new problem still needs its own ground.

## Hard precondition

Stages 1 to 3 only work if the stage-1 output survives an outside check. As drafted, the output is a shape with a recorded `gap` and a nonzero round-trip gap (`01` section 6, `11_audit.md`). If a reader supplies the missing figure line or shows the union collapses, the email is withdrawn rather than softened.
