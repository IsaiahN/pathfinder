## Three things in the wider literature that bear directly on this question

1. **The trio is the field's own framing.** Bormashenko 2024 quotes Landauer asking how much energy, how long and how large a computation must be, "what are the physical limits of the process of computation?" This is exactly the question's trio, and it supports F1's reading that D1 is the baseline rather than the whole answer.
2. **The fundamental bounds are not one kind of thing.** The same review treats Abbe diffraction, c, Heisenberg, Bremermann, Mandelstam-Tamm, Margolus-Levitin, Landauer and Bekenstein as separate limiting principles, and notes that lowering temperature saves erasure energy but slows the computation. This supports the union verdict and the closing of any trade that would merge them into one resource.
3. **The formal and the physical are kept in one conversation without being merged.** The FQxI 2019-2020 contest framing lists Godel, Turing, quantum theory and chaos together as "rigorous arguments limiting what we can prove, compute, and predict," while the 2009 contest lists impossibility principles separately. That is the D5/D6 split: both are live, their anchors differ.
