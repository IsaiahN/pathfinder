## DERIVATION

### Q1 — Are D2, D3, D4 isomers?

Fig 12 :117: "Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."

Fig 12 :124: "A substructure whose presence fixes the outcome. The group says what kind; the qualifier says which member."

Written over the shared atoms {entropy, energy, time, space, information}, the three limits arrange differently:

- D2 Landauer binds **logical irreversibility** (an erasure) to **heat dissipated** at temperature T: `information − (reversible path) ⇒ entropy`. The decisive bond is production from absence, an irreversible step.
- D3 quantum speed limits bind **energy** to **orthogonalization time**: `energy ⋛ minimum time`. The decisive bond is a comparison between a resource and a rate.
- D4 Bekenstein binds **radius and energy** to **entropy**: `(space, energy) ⇒ entropy bound`. The decisive bond is production from a bounded region.

Same atoms, different bonds, different substance. They are **isomers** in Fig 12's exact sense, and the difference is decisive in behaviour: D2 is about erasure, D3 about rate, D4 about density.

**Falsifier, written before the run:** if D4 cannot be written without a seventh atom (gravity or horizon area is genuinely irreducible and not a composition of space, energy, entropy), the isomer claim fails for D4 and it moves to a separate domain. The same test applies to D3 if quantum orthogonality is not expressible in the held atoms. The weaker claim then is a **functional group**: all three are `resource ⇒ bound` arrangements with different qualifiers (Fig 12 :124). D5 and D6 are **not** isomers of these, because they hold formal/structural atoms the physical limits do not; they are a different kind, and grouping them would be a union error (F1 of `01`).

### Q2 — What is the order of the union?

Fig 3 :20: "The chain is ordered by dependency, not by importance."

Fig 12 :111: "A vocabulary without bonds can name a state and cannot name a route to one."

The dependency order derived in `01` §1.4 is re-derived here from bonds:

1. **Anchor bond first.** "Physically meaningful" is a comparison (⋛) against an unmoved ground, not a conjunction of properties. Operators :49: "a threshold relation between two quantities." Without this bond, no later limit can be ranged, so anything above it is effort spent above the break (Fig 3 :19).
2. **Budget/wall distinction second.** ⋛ is applied again: is the candidate a quantity you can increase (budget) or a categorical boundary (wall)? Fig 6 :27 governs this exact comparison.
3. **Per-domain verdicts third.** Only now are D1 to D6 searched, each against its own anchor.
4. **Import last, and debited.** Fig 6 :24: "Import from a live peer is debited against that frame's independence. A wrong atom costs more than starting from primitives."

A plan is a sequence, a goal a comparison, progress a subtraction. The order above is therefore a **route**, not a list of ingredients.

### Q3 — Catalyst and cycle analogues: why reversible computation is not the wall

Fig 12 :119: "A part that survives its own use." Fig 12 :121: "The output re-enters as input."

Reversible computing is a catalyst-like part: the gate survives its own use, so spent and regenerating parts behave differently across uses, and nothing in a resource ledger records which a term is. This is why the Landauer limit is not simply an energy limit (F7 of `01`): the erasure is the spent part, the reversible path is the regenerating part. A cycle whose output re-enters as input can close or not depending on the sign of the loop (Fig 12 :121), and a chain cannot close by construction.

The temptation is to call a reversible, cyclic computer a machine that evades the wall. Refused: surviving one's own use changes the budget accounting, not the closure of what can be composed. Fig 13 :115: "The set is closed. The arrangements are not. That gap is why import exists." A cycle re-arranges; it adds no atom.

**Falsifier:** a demonstrated cyclic computation that leaves its closure by rearrangement alone, measured, would refute this refusal and with it F3 of `01`.

### Q4 — The two currencies, restated as order

Fig 12 :126: "Bits price the description. Actions price finding out whether it holds." Fig 12 :125: "A composition costs in two currencies, and they do not add."

Time, memory, and energy are one accounting. The action cost of testing a candidate composition is a second accounting. A system holding only the first "will not notice the shortfall" and "proposes arrangements it cannot afford to test" (Fig 12 :127). For INF-0074 the consequence is specific: a claim that a limit is "beyond energy" because it is small in joules can be wrong because the finding-out cost is not priced at all. The wall verdict in `01` is about reach given atoms, and reach is driven by what can be settled, not by the size of the library (Fig 12 :104).

## CONCLUSION

D2, D3, and D4 are **isomers** over the shared physical atom set (same atoms, different bond, decisive behavioural difference), with a stated falsifier that would reduce them to a functional group. D5 and D6 are not isomers of them; they are a different kind and must stay in separate verdicts. The union's dependency order is anchor, then budget/wall comparison, then per-domain verdicts, then debited import. Reversible and cyclic computation change the accounting and do not cross the closure wall. One falsifier is live and named for the cycle case; none is hidden.
