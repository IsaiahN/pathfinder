# Structure and order: group isomers, the broken link, and two currencies

**Mode: `specified` on the structural claims, `general` on the bond definitions.** This is a simulated derivation checked against the figures, not a ground reading (see `05_what_shape_means.md` section 7). No firm was measured.

## RESIDUAL

The main audit returns a four-condition separator but leaves two questions open. (1) If two AI systems are deployed over the same customer and employee groups and differ only in which comparison bond attaches to which group, are they different substances or the same substance described twice? (2) The firm wants an ordering of work: what must be settled before what? The Formula says a vocabulary with only ingredients can name a state and not a route to one, so the order has to be derived rather than listed.

## DERIVATION

### Q1 - Are two group-aware systems isomers?

- Fig 12 :117 *"isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."*
- Fig 12 :5 *"A set of ingredients is not one. The bond is the rest of the information, and it is the half nothing was recording."*
- Let system S1 attach the comparison `⋛` between the AI-decided outcome and the baseline for customer group C, and use employee group E only as a context condition. Let S2 hold the same atoms and attach `⋛` between the AI-decided outcome and the baseline for E, with C as context. The ingredient lists are identical; the bond placement differs.
- Under Fig 12 :117 they are **isomers**: same atoms, different bond, different substance. Their performance reports can look identical in a flat formula that lists groups and metrics and omits where the bond was attached. That invisibility is the named failure of the flat formula (Fig 12 :14-17).
- Weaker claim if the atom sets differ: if S1 uses atoms C and E while S2 uses only C, they share a **functional group** rather than being isomers. Fig 12 :123 *"functional group. A substructure whose presence fixes the outcome. The group says what kind; the qualifier says which member."*
- **Condition:** the isomer reading requires a fixed atom set. If a group appears in one system and not the other because the habitat changed, the relation is composition plus import or substitution, not isomerism. The condition is live and named, not hidden.

### Q2 - What is the dependency order of a valid determination?

- Fig 3 :20 *"The chain is ordered by dependency, not by importance."* Each link consumes what the one above produces.
- The order for BUS-0085 is: **perception** (can the firm tell which group a case is in, and tell it apart from the last case?) -> **vocabulary** (does it have terms for what the decision affords each group?) -> **objective** (can it say what would count as progress for that group, in its own terms?) -> **planning** (a route under incomplete information) -> **learn and carry** (verify and carry to the next decision).
- The middle link is the one that breaks. Fig 3 :25-27 says the perceptual and planning links have obvious failures and obvious tests, while the link about *"what would count as progress"* has *"neither, so it attracts the least attention and the fewest instruments"*, and Fig 3 :27 *"A link with no instrument is not a link that works. It is a link nobody has looked at."*
- Fig 3 :19 *"Optimising a link that already works cannot rescue one that does not."* Model accuracy work lands above the break where instruments already exist; it cannot rescue a missing objective.
- Fig 3 :22 *"A step whose input never arrives cannot be diagnosed, only its predecessor can."* A per-group verdict taken before the objective is instrumented is a reading of nothing, not a weak reading.
- **Falsifier:** if a firm can show a working per-group objective instrument in place before any group-level perception or vocabulary, the order here is wrong for that firm and must be re-censused from its residual.

### Q3 - The two currencies of a per-group evaluation do not add

- Fig 12 :125 *"A composition costs in two currencies, and they do not add."*
- Fig 12 :126 *"Bits price the description. Actions price finding out whether it holds. They are in different units, so a system holding one accounting cannot compare them"*.
- A fairness criterion is cheap to state in bits and expensive to verify per group in actions. A large-sample per-group experiment is expensive to state and may take only one action to refute if a non-updating anchor contradicts it. A firm that reports only "we meet the criterion" holds the bits accounting and will propose arrangements it cannot afford to test. The budget decides how much of the group space is actually reached (Fig 12 :127).
- Because the units differ, a single "evaluation cost" number that adds them is a flat formula hiding a bond. This is the two-currency version of Fig 9 :60: filters for the budget, witnesses for the verdict.
- **Falsifier:** a demonstration that bits and actions are convertible at a fixed rate for group evaluation, so one accounting suffices. No figure line supplies such a rate.

### Q4 - Refusing a group verdict is a negation, not a subtraction

- Operators :58 *"a refutation is a negation, which is why a vocabulary without it can only accumulate"*.
- Fig 12 :112 *"A plan is a sequence. A goal is a comparison. Progress is a subtraction. A refutation is a negation, and that one is not a bond."*
- A firm that can only add fairness certificates cannot withdraw a group verdict when the ground turns against it. It can only stop reporting the old one and let it stand in the record. A vocabulary with `¬` can carry the correction: the group verdict loses its standing to be composed over while nothing is deleted from the record (Fig 6 :42-43).
- The practical form: the pre-registered refuter in `01` section 4 must be allowed to *refuse* a per-group claim, not merely to lower its score. A score that trends down without crossing a declared threshold is the term whose residual persists and does not trend down (Formula step 2), and that is the candidate to point the refusal at.
- **Falsifier:** a framework that represents withdrawal only by deleting the old claim and still preserves the provenance and reach properties of Fig 6 :42-47. The figures say deletion cannot do that; a counter-example would need a different storage rule.

### Q5 - Is the firm-level lift a composition or an import?

- Fig 4 :22 *"Only methods cross up. Only head starts come down."*
- Fig 4 :30 *"So take the round trip and measure the gap. Without it you are navigating by dead reckoning: confident about a position nobody checked."*
- A per-group method can be carried up to a firm-level evaluation rule only if it is verified and compressed; the recording of one group's success cannot. What comes back down is a head start for the next group's search, never a replay. The lift number is the coarse description, and the round trip from per-group residuals to a firm number and back again need not match. The gap `R_T` is the measurement of what the coarse description cannot hold (Fig 4 :31).
- If the firm adopts a metric from an outside frame (a vendor's fairness definition, a regulator's threshold), that is an import. Fig 6 :23 *"No frame escapes its own closure. The bound moves only on contact with a frame whose closure differs."* It needs a provenance gate and a shadow test: does it explain a gap the firm already had? A wrong import costs more than starting from primitives (Fig 6 :24).
- **Falsifier:** show that a firm-level lift number is losslessly invertible to the per-group residual vector for all group partitions. Then the round-trip gap is zero and the warning does not apply. This is the same falsifier as the round-trip check in `11_audit.md`.

## CONCLUSION

Two systems over the same customer and employee atoms that differ only in bond attachment are isomers under Fig 12 :117, with the fixed-atom condition stated and live. A valid determination is ordered by Fig 3, and the link that usually breaks is the objective link, because it is the one with no instrument. Stating a metric and testing it per group are two currencies that cannot be added, so a single cost number is a flat formula. Withdrawing a group verdict requires negation, which a certificate-accumulating vocabulary lacks. The firm-level lift is a lossy condensation of per-group residuals, and an outside metric is an import needing provenance and a shadow test. None of these results names a group, a metric or a number; they are the structure and order the answer must have.
