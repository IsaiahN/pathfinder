## QUESTION

Decide, in effect terms, what the Tether says about the question: how can firms determine whether AI systems improve performance across different customer and employee groups? Option A: a pooled before/after lift number answers it. Option B: the question is a union of per-group conditions, and a firm can determine the answer only through a four-condition separator with verdicts kept separate per group. What each leads to: A hides a live slot in an average, lets a moving outcome stand as an anchor, and lets a certificate decide an outcome; B returns a shape, a refuter written in advance, and the instruments that would close each per-group verdict, while leaving the firm-specific numbers open to the ground.

## DOMAIN CENSUS

R by arity, symmetry and scale, then one line per domain with actors, currency and anchor, partition or union, and order by dependency.

- **R (the residual).** The gap between what the AI system predicts for a case and what actually happens for that case, indexed by customer or employee group. Arity: at least two, with overlapping slots; a case can be both a customer and an employee. Symmetry: "improves" is an ordered threshold comparison `⋛`, not a symmetric `+`, so a correlation cannot choose the arrow. Scale: first order inside one group's decision loop; second order at the firm's cross-group comparison. Fig 9 calls this a union compound, not one hard question: a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either.
- **Domain 1, measurement and ground per group.** Actors: the AI system, a decision case, the group, the outcome observed. Currency: prediction versus observation per slot. Anchor: the per-group comparison whose outcome does not move because a frame thinks the system works. This is a partition boundary question about which components cross between prediction and observation.
- **Domain 2, group structure.** Actors: customer groups, employee groups, their intersections, the contact each has with the decision. Currency: contact and group membership read off the world. Anchor: the per-group ground reading. Union, not partition; groups overlap.
- **Domain 3, verification and audit.** Actors: internal evaluators, external auditors, affected groups, vendor reports. Currency: agreement and residual. Anchor: the same per-group ground, never agreement, with each frame corrected separately. In this simulated run all seats draw on one evidence pool, so their agreement is one pool talking, not corroboration.
- **Domain 4, the deployment decision.** Actors: the firm, managers, people subject to the decision. Currency: cost of being wrong and action budget. Anchor: the pre-registered failure condition plus the measured outcome. This is where the separator is spent or ignored.
- **Order by dependency.** Perceive the group, acquire terms for what it affords, state what counts as progress in its terms, plan a route, then verify and carry (Fig 3). Describe the residual before choosing a frame (Fig 8 :53). Settle each first-order per-group verdict before the second-order firm comparison (Fig 12 :104).

Strain test applied: the Fig 1 per-slot residual and the Fig 11 valence-relative claim govern different objects, so they do not conflict. The live tension between Fig 2 :33 (anchor does not update) and decision-dependent outcomes is re-census S1 in `01` section 7, not a smoothed reading.

## VERDICT (from the figures)

The question as posed cannot be answered by one pooled number. It is a union of per-group conditions with a verdict per group, kept separate and never averaged. A firm can determine the answer only if it indexes the residual per group, enumerates groups from contact, instruments what counts as progress in each group's own terms, uses a non-updating anchor with at least two independently ranged frames, and writes its failure condition before the run. Metrics and lift percentages are filters for the budget, never witnesses. The structure is a four-condition separator with five pre-registered refuters; isomers, the dependency order and the two-currency warning are worked out in `06`. No figure line returns the groups, the metric or the lift number, so those are declared `gap`, unreached at this budget, not silence. Mode `specified`, ungrounded, candidate. This is a simulated derivation checked for internal consistency against the figures, not a ground reading.

- **cost if wrong:** bounded and locatable. The packet is mode labeled, states refuters before the conclusion and records provenance, so an error is findable. The real cost is a firm making a deployment decision from a pooled number that hides one group's live residual, or from an outcome that moved when the decision moved. Fig 2 :42 *"Buy independence in proportion to the cost of being wrong."* The cost is avoidable by leading with the per-group residual and the anchor test.
- **undoable:** yes. The separator does not commit a firm to a group list, metric or number. A defective metric can be replaced without withdrawing the structure, and a group can be added when contact shows it matters. Nothing is deleted from the record when a verdict is refused; the reach falls because a refusal was added (Fig 6 :42-43).
- **reversed by:** a ground reading, not agreement. A per-group comparison whose label does not move under the decision, an instrument that discriminates real improvement from a change in who is observed, two independently ranged frames with the stage-1 verdict recorded before the framework was shown, or a standing group list that survives a change of residual would each change the verdict. Discovering a figure line that selects a group, metric or threshold after a genuine re-census would too. Until one of these fires, the verdict stands as `candidate`.
