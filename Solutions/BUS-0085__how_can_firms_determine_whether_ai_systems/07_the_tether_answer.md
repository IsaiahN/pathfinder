# The Tether's answer on AI performance across customer and employee groups

**Mode: `specified` on the separator and every per-domain claim, `general` on the structural shape, not `grounded`.** This is a shape and a route, not a result. Simulated derivation checked against the figures (`05_what_shape_means.md` section 7), not a ground reading. No firm's data was used.

## 1. The residual it answers

A firm asks whether an AI system improves performance across customer and employee groups. In effect terms the question is whether, for each group-slot, the gap between the system's prediction and the observed outcome shrinks under a comparison that does not move when the firm acts on its belief. The residual is indexed per slot (Fig 1 :19 *"The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated."*), and "across groups" is the instruction not to pool it (Symbols :43-44 *"R is measured per slot, never as a single global number."*). The question is a union of overlapping conditions, not one hard question (Fig 9 :4-5).

## 2. The shape: a four-condition separator

1. **Index the residual per group and enumerate the groups from contact.** Fig 1 :22 *"Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average."* Fig 11 :43 *"The habitat is enumerated, never composed."* A customer can be an employee; the intersection is its own condition. No standing group list survives a change of residual (Fig 11 :51). The firm reads the slots off the world.
2. **Instrument the middle link before judging the model.** The chain is perception -> vocabulary -> objective -> planning -> learn and carry, ordered by dependency (Fig 3 :20). The link that breaks is the objective, because it is the one for which "what would count as progress" has no instrument (Fig 3 :25-27). A firm that cannot say, in a group's own terms, what a better outcome would look like and how it would be read has no verdict; a reading below the break is a reading of nothing (Fig 3 :22).
3. **Use a non-updating anchor and independent frames.** Fig 2 :33 *"The anchor must not update."* A group outcome that is partly produced by the AI's own decision is an instrument, not an anchor (Fig 2 :29-30). The comparison must hold the outcome fixed against the firm's belief, and at least two frames must range against it separately (Fig 2 :46), buying independence in proportion to the cost of being wrong (Fig 2 :42). An internal team that has read the model's criterion and dashboard is no longer outside the frame it grades (Fig 10 :50-51).
4. **Write the failure condition before the run.** Improvement is an ordered comparison `⋛` (Fig 12 :87), so a symmetric "AI used" / "performance high" correlation cannot choose the arrow. The sought-for shape is made an ordinary, refusable term before the run (Formula, "Direction"; Operators :58 *"a refutation is a negation, which is why a vocabulary without it can only accumulate"*).

Verdicts are kept per domain and per group and never averaged into a single firm-wide lift. A pooled number is a filter for the budget, never a witness (Fig 9 :60 *"Use filters for the budget and witnesses for the verdict. Never let a filter hand you a verdict."*).

## 3. What it explicitly does not say

- Not that any named fairness metric is the verdict. Metrics are instruments and filters; the fairness literature's own statement is that passing a test is not a certificate.
- Not that a single percentage, a vendor benchmark, or a synthetic test harness answers the question. A harness is a substituted habitat and proves wiring, not capability (Fig 11 :70).
- Not that the pooled result and the per-group results share a sign. They can reverse, which is why the question is split rather than searched (Fig 9 :4-5).
- Not that internal evaluation is independent after it has read the model's interior, and not that agreement among reviewers is corroboration (Fig 2 :40, Fig 7 :35).
- Not a universal group list, a universal threshold, or a universal lift number. No figure line returns these (Fig 9 :32, Fig 13 :134-135).
- Not `grounded`. No measurement at any firm was taken, and `left(R, phi)` is unknown.

## 4. What would settle it - the ground

A discriminator, not a further argument. From *The Hard Problem is About Source*: an instrument that reads green *"cannot comment on the source"*; the spectrometer resolved it. Here:

- **Anchor test:** is there a per-group outcome comparison whose label does not change when the AI's decision changes? Randomized holdout, exogenous rule variation, or an audit sample with decisions deliberately varied are candidate forms. A before/after dashboard fails it.
- **Middle-link instrument:** what would count as progress for each group, in that group's own terms, and what reads it? If no instrument is named, the link is unexamined, not working (Fig 3 :27).
- **Independence test:** do at least two frames range against the same anchor separately, with separate evidence? If they share a report, their agreement is one witness (Fig 2 :31, Fig 8 :48).
- **Standing refuters:** a group list fixed across residuals; a single metric that is necessary and sufficient; an aggregate residual that is losslessly invertible to the per-group vector; an internal checker still independent after reading the dashboard. Any one of these firing would change the answer.
- **Second-order type question:** the comparison `⋛` is defined over two quantities (Operators :47-52); at firm level its operands are per-group verdicts now serving as atoms. Whether a verdict is a quantity at that level needs an instrument, not an argument (Fig 12 :104). Named in `06_isomers_and_order.md` and left open.

## 5. The honest stop

**Unreached, not unreachable.** The firm-specific groups, metrics, thresholds and lift numbers require a ground reading in that firm's habitat. The figures can say the shape any valid determination must have, can say what a missing instrument would have to discriminate, and can say what would reverse the verdict. They cannot name the number. Fig 13 :134 *"Unreachable is a verdict here, not a silence"*; Fig 8 :39 *"So a search that finds nothing is never a proof of absence."* The lineage is recorded so the stop is checkable. *Indiscernibility is not identity.*

## 6. One-paragraph version for outreach

> The Tether does not give a universal answer to whether AI improves performance across customer and employee groups. It says the question must be split into per-group slots because the residual is indexed per slot and an average hides a live signal; that the real bottleneck is not model accuracy but the missing instrument for what counts as progress in each group's own terms; that the anchor for "improvement" cannot be an outcome the AI's own decision changes; and that at least two independent frames must range against that anchor before a verdict counts. It refuses to name a metric or a percentage, because the framework returns a shape, not a name. What it offers is a four-condition separator, five failure conditions written in advance, and the kinds of instrument that would close it. The firm-specific numbers stay open until the ground settles them.
