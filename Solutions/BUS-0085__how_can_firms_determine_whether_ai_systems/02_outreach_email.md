# Outreach draft - check the separator first, then decide about the framework

**Use:** one to one, or as a cover letter attached to a submission through the public venues in `03_public_contact_points.csv`. **Not** for bulk send.

**Hard precondition:** do not send until the version of the shape claim below is stated with its mode and its refuter intact. If the recipient cannot tell what would fail, the two-stage offer collapses on contact.

---

**Subject:** One checkable claim about subgroup AI evaluation, before any theory

> Hi [name],
>
> I have one specific claim I would like you to check against what you already know about AI evaluation. I am not asking you to read the framework first, and I am not asking you to agree with anything. If the claim does not survive, there is nothing else to look at.
>
> **The residual, in effect terms:** a firm deploys an AI system; the system predicts an outcome for a case; the world returns what actually happened. The gap `R = |prediction - observation|` must be read per customer or employee group, not pooled. Fig 1 :19 *"The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated."* A pooled lift number can be near zero while one group's residual is live, because averaging is exactly how the signal disappears (Fig 1 :22).
>
> **The claim (`specified`, not `grounded`):** a firm can determine whether an AI system improves performance across groups only if four conditions hold together. (1) The residual is indexed per group, and group membership is enumerated from contact rather than taken from a standing list. (2) There is an instrument for what counts as progress in each group's own terms, which is the middle link most evaluations do not measure. (3) There is a non-updating anchor per group, with at least two frames ranging against it separately; an outcome that changes when the AI's decision changes is an instrument, not an anchor. (4) A failure condition was written down before the run. This is a shape and a route, not a measurement, and not a fairness certificate.
>
> **What is deliberately not claimed:** that any named fairness metric is the verdict, that a single percentage answers the question, that internal evaluation is independent once it has read the model's own dashboard, or that a passing audit certifies improvement. Fig 9 :60 *"Use filters for the budget and witnesses for the verdict. Never let a filter hand you a verdict."* Metrics are filters; the per-group verdict is the witness.
>
> **What would refute the claim:**
> 1. a single aggregate residual can replace the per-group vector under every group partition, including partitions chosen after seeing the result;
> 2. an outcome label that moves when the AI's decision moves still settles the improvement question without an independent comparison;
> 3. one fairness criterion is both necessary and sufficient for "improves this group" in all decision contexts, with its anchor declared;
> 4. an internal checker that has read the model's criterion and dashboard still returns a verdict independent of the frame it grades;
> 5. a standing group list survives a change of residual and a change of habitat.
>
> I wrote these down before sending this. If any one holds, the separator is wrong and I will retract it. A one-line "this fails because..." is more useful to me than agreement. If you would rather not engage, no reply is needed.
>
> **If it survives, and only then:** here are the public sources that bear on the claim. The figures, not my summary of them, are the authority.
> - NIST AI Risk Management Framework, public framework and engagement pages: https://www.nist.gov/itl/ai-risk-management-framework and https://www.nist.gov/itl/ai-risk-management-framework/ai-risk-management-framework-engage
> - NIST AI Resource Center, including its public metric-submission route: https://airc.nist.gov/ and https://airc.nist.gov/metrology/how-to-submit/
> - Fairness and Machine Learning (Barocas, Hardt and Narayanan, MIT Press 2023), especially the chapter on testing discrimination in practice: https://fairmlbook.org/testing.html
> - Underspecification Presents Challenges for Credibility in Modern Machine Learning (D'Amour et al., JMLR 2022): https://jmlr.org/papers/v23/20-1335.html
>
> **If you want to use the machinery:** the formula and the figures are public. Run them on an evaluation you know well and can check against a ground. Write down what would refute your result *before* you run it, and state the mode of what you get. A reported failure is more useful to me than a reported success.
>
> Thanks for reading,
> [name]

## Why this version is ordered this way

| the move | figure-grounded reason |
|---|---|
| Residual and shape first, theory last | Fig 10 :52 *"Bring a characterised residual, never the source."* Each reader ranges the claim against their own ground before holding any framework interior. |
| Do not ask for agreement | Fig 2 :35 *"Alignment is triangulation, not negotiation. A mirror cannot correct you."* |
| Refuter written before the run | Seat Map :287 *"a falsifier written before the run is a design instrument, not only an honesty device."* |
| Mode stated on the claim | `THE_FORMULA.md` *"Mode. general · specified · grounded. State which."* |
| No fairness certificate asserted | Fig 5 :48 *"Its own output is never a measure of success. A system can be enthusiastically curious and produce nothing."* |
| Metrics held as filters | Fig 9 :60; the fairness book's "passing a test is not a certificate" is world corroboration, not the source of the verdict. |
| Groups enumerated, not listed | Fig 11 :51 *"No standing list of good actors survives a change of residual."* |
| Non-updating anchor demanded | Fig 2 :33 *"The anchor must not update."* This is the condition most before/after dashboards fail. |
| Outside check required | Fig 10 :50 *"Looking inside the frame it grades is how the seat stops being outside."* |
| Prestige not used | *The Recursive Self-Improvement Trap*: *"Prestige can fund an action. It can give an action a budget to be tried. But it cannot settle an action. Only the ground can settle it."* |
| Offer the formula as a method | Fig 4 :22 *"Only methods cross up. Only head starts come down."* A generator can be given away without giving away an answer. |
| No "trust", no "true answer" | Fig 10 :42 *"A verifier that can reconstruct the claim cannot verify it."* |

## Two-stage discipline for the reply

1. **Stage 1.** Send only the residual, the four-condition separator, the mode and the five refuters. Ask: what would you count as a failure here? Record the answer before showing anything else.
2. **Stage 2, opt in only.** If the reader is curious, offer the four public links above, with the note that the figures are the authority. Their later agreement is never pooled as corroboration (Fig 2 :40).
3. **Stage 3.** Offer the formula as a method to reuse, with the instruction that each user writes their own refuter and states their own mode.

Nothing is sent by this agent. You host and send. The public venues in `03_public_contact_points.csv` are opt-in or institutional submission routes, not a list of people selected for likely agreement.
