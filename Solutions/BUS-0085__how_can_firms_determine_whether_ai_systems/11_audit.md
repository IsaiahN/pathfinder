## Independent domain census

I redid the census from the residual line alone, before relying on the primary's conclusions, using the WATSON order: R by arity, symmetry and scale; one line per domain with actors, currency and anchor; partition or union; order by dependency.

- **R (arity / symmetry / scale).** The residual is the gap between a predicted case outcome and the observed case outcome, indexed by customer or employee group. Arity: two or more overlapping slots, so a **union compound** (Fig 9 :4-5), not one hard question. Symmetry: "improves" is ordered and threshold-like, `⋛`, so a symmetric co-occurrence cannot decide direction. Scale: first order inside a group's decision loop, second order at the firm's cross-group verdict.
- **Domain 1, measurement and ground per group.** Actors: system, case, group, observed outcome. Currency: prediction against observation per slot. Anchor: the per-group comparison whose label does not move under the decision. Question type: partition boundary between prediction and observation.
- **Domain 2, group structure.** Actors: customer groups, employee groups, their intersection, contact. Currency: contact and membership. Anchor: per-group reading. Question type: union, so unbundle before searching.
- **Domain 3, verification and audit.** Actors: internal evaluators, external auditors, affected groups. Currency: agreement and residual. Anchor: the same ground, never agreement. In this simulated run domain 3 draws on the same pool as domains 1 and 2, so its independence is zero.
- **Domain 4, deployment decision.** Actors: firm, managers, subjects. Currency: cost of being wrong, action budget. Anchor: the pre-registered refuter plus the measured outcome. Question type: where the separator is acted on or ignored.

Order by dependency: perceive group, acquire vocabulary for what it affords, instrument the objective, plan, verify and carry (Fig 3); describe the residual before choosing a frame (Fig 8 :53); settle first-order per-group verdicts before the second-order firm comparison (Fig 12 :104).

My census matches the primary's four domains and its union reading. I reached it from Fig 9's union/partition split and Fig 12's level rule, while the primary derived from Fig 1's slot indexing and Fig 11's habitat rule. Different starting figures, same boundaries, low overlap between routes within the framework. It is still one evidence pool, so the agreement is expected and carries no independent weight.
