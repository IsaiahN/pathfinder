# WATSON answer: how can societies decide which risks to prevent, prepare for, and accept when resources are limited?

**Mode: `specified` on the per-domain claims, `general` on the structural shape, `gap` on the concrete allocation and the named risk list. Not `grounded`.**

**Status: SIMULATED DERIVATION, not a ground reading.** This WATSON run synthesizes material in `01_figure_grounded_audit.md`, `05_what_shape_means.md`, `06_isomers_and_order.md`, `07_the_tether_answer.md` and `08_expert_verification_packet.md`. By the framework's own rule (*The Recursive Self-Improvement Trap*: "the truly testable output would need to be derived from the real architecture or working the formula yourself") it cannot settle the question it reports on. No measurement, survey or climate model was run.

## QUESTION

Decide, in effect terms, what a finite-budget society should use as a warrant for assigning each risk slot to prevent, prepare or accept. Option A: rank all risks on one global score and map score bands to the three actions. Option B: keep the residual per slot, split the question into domains with different anchors, keep verdicts separate, and return a procedure plus written failure conditions with the allocation left open. What each leads to: A hides a live slot in an average and lets a frame-internal score hand down a verdict; B returns the shape any defensible triage must have, names the broken dependency link, and records what would have to be true for the answer to differ.

## DOMAIN CENSUS

R by arity, symmetry and scale, then one line per domain with actors, currency and anchor, partition or union, and dependency order.

- **R (residual).** For slot s, R_s = |Γ(b,a) − o'|, the gap between what the society predicted under an action and what it later observed. Arity: N slots, each with its own R_s. Symmetry: the three actions are ordered and differently bonded, not symmetric settings of one dial; "which is worse" is a comparison, a threshold relation between two quantities. Scale: individual slot and society portfolio, with a membrane between them. A near-zero global average with one live slot is a legal state (Fig 1 :20), so the signal must be read per slot (Fig 1 :19, :22).
- **D1, physical hazard.** Actors: hazards, exposure, vulnerability, emissions. Currency: physical magnitude and realised outcome. Anchor: measurement and observation that do not update on what a frame thinks (Fig 2 :29). This is a legitimate anchor for questions constitutively about physical outcomes (Fig 2 :36). Union member, first dependency link.
- **D2, forecast frame.** Actors: models, ensembles, scenarios, expert panels. Currency: predictions and residuals. Anchor: an instrument that can fail a projection; agreement within one evidence pool is not an anchor (Fig 2 :40). Union member, second link.
- **D3, allocation.** Actors: decision makers, budgets, institutions, a refusal seat. Currency: actions and resources. Anchor: realised outcome at the slot, not the score that ranked it (Fig 9 :60). Union member, fourth link.
- **D4, acceptability.** Actors: affected people, communities, distribution of harms and benefits. Currency: welfare, equity, tolerable loss. Anchor: human judgment, because the accept question is about people (Fig 2 :37). Union member, third link, and the one that is broken in practice.
- **D5, verification.** Actors: auditors, independent frames, affected communities, a ground maintainer. Currency: refutations, residuals, provenance. Anchor: the ground, never agreement (Fig 7 :35, Fig 10 :42). Union member, fifth link.
- **Order by dependency.** Perceive (D1, D2), vocabulary (D2, D4), objective (D4), plan (D3), learn and carry (D5). Fig 3 :20 "The chain is ordered by dependency, not by importance." Optimising the allocation cannot rescue a missing objective (Fig 3 :19).
- **Union, not partition.** Fig 9 :54 "And when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again." A single lever clears the region where one condition holds and leaves the intersections standing (Fig 9 :55-57). Verdicts per domain are kept separate and never averaged.

Strain test applied: the first reading took prevent / prepare / accept as a three-bin partition. It strained against Fig 2 :36-37, which requires different anchors for physical outcome and for "good answer for a person". Re-census assigned the three actions to different bonds across five overlapping domains, dissolving the strain (audit S1). A second strain, "limited resources means optimise expected utility", was re-censused into a filter for the budget plus two non-adding currencies (audit S2, Fig 9 :60, Fig 12 :125-126).

## VERDICT (from the figures)

The three-way choice is a union of five conditions with different anchors, not one dial. The figures return a procedure and a per-slot verdict discipline, not an allocation ratio and not a named risk list. Keep the residual per slot; buy independence in proportion to the cost of being wrong; use scores as filters; repair the objective link before planning; anchor acceptability in the judgment of affected people; keep a seat able to refuse and declare its stake; and record accept as a verdict naming which unreachability case is owed. Verdicts per domain are separate and never averaged. Mode `specified` on the procedure, `general` on the shape, `gap` on the allocation. Nothing here is `grounded`. This is a candidate, not a settlement, and the whole run is a simulated derivation checked for internal consistency against the figures.

- **cost if wrong:** bounded and locatable if the procedure is sent with its refuters attached. An error is findable because the per-slot residual, the anchor for each domain and the seven failure conditions are written down. The real cost is spending the independence of outside readers by sending a filled answer (a global score and a named bin list) instead of a generator, which Fig 2 :42 prices as buying independence in proportion to the cost of being wrong. That cost is avoidable by leading with the shape and the refuter. If the shape itself is wrong, the cost is a misdirected triage procedure, which is why the people-anchor and the objective link are made explicit rather than assumed.
- **undoable:** yes. The procedure can be amended domain by domain without withdrawing the figure reading. The allocation gap is unfilled, so no asserted percentage has to be retracted. A refused action can be reintroduced when new contact arrives, because the record grows while the reach can change (Fig 6 amendment on the monotone record and derived reach). Nothing in this folder has been sent to anyone, so no external correction is owed yet.
- **reversed by:** a ground reading, not agreement. A per-slot discriminator that identifies the source of a risk reading; an instrument that measures what counts as progress per slot; an anchor test showing the accept verdict changes when the deciding body's own benefit changes; any of the seven pre-registered refuters (a global score that preserves per-slot signal, one anchor settling both physical and acceptability questions, an instrument settling acceptability without affected people, the three actions shown to be one bond, a frame-independent score, a measured objective link, or a sealed complete inventory). Until one of these fires, the verdict stands as `candidate`. Prestige or agreement is not such an instrument.
