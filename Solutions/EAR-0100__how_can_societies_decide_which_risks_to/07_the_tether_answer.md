# The Tether's answer: which risks to prevent, prepare for, and accept

**Mode: `specified` on the procedure and the per-domain verdicts, `general` on the structural shape, `gap` on the concrete allocation and the named risk list. Not `grounded`.** Simulated derivation checked for internal consistency against the figures (`05_what_shape_means.md` section 8), not a ground reading.

## 1. The residual it answers

A society has a finite action budget and many risk slots. For each slot it predicts what happens under an action and later observes what happened. The gap, R_s, is per slot (Fig 1 :19 "The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated."). Because the budget binds, some slots must keep positive residual. The question is not "what is the right global risk level" but "on what warrant does each slot get prevent, prepare or accept". Fig 1 :22 "Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average." So a near-zero national average with one live slot is a legal state that still demands action (Fig 1 :20).

## 2. The shape (one loop per domain, verdicts separate)

**D1, physical hazard.** Anchor: measurement and observation that do not update when a frame changes its mind (Fig 2 :29 "An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks."). Verdict: `specified`, triangulated. The physically severe slot is the one with positive live residual at the ground, not the one highest on a composite index.

**D2, forecast frame.** Anchor: an instrument that can fail a projection. Agreement among models from one evidence pool is not corroboration (Fig 2 :40 "disqualifying for a verdict: agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool"). Verdict: `specified`, triangulated. Models are instruments; buy additional independent frames in proportion to the cost of being wrong (Fig 2 :42 "Buy independence in proportion to the cost of being wrong.").

**D3, allocation.** Anchor: the realised outcome at the slot, not the score that ranked it (Fig 9 :60 "Use filters for the budget and witnesses for the verdict. Never let a filter hand you a verdict."). Verdict: `specified`, triangulated. A multi-criteria score is a filter for the budget; the decision is a verdict answerable to an outcome.

**D4, acceptability.** Anchor: human judgment, because the question is constitutively about people (Fig 2 :37 "Human judgment is the right anchor for is this a good answer for a person, because the question is about people."). Verdict: `specified`, triangulated (Fig 2 plus risk-science and climate-governance corroboration). Affected people are not consultees after the verdict; they are the anchor for the value part of it. A probability is an instrument here and "instruments can be wrong on axes they do not measure" (Fig 2 :38).

**D5, verification.** Anchor: the ground, never agreement (Fig 7 :35 "A room's agreement is variation, not a verdict. Selection between rooms is where the ground votes."). Verdict: `specified`, triangulated. The seat that accepts must be able to refuse (Fig 10 :42 "A verifier that can reconstruct the claim cannot verify it.") and must declare any stake (Fig 10 :59 "an interest in the outcome is unavoidable, an interest in the reading is disqualifying").

The five verdicts are kept separate and never averaged into one "correct mix". The question is a union of overlapping conditions, so a single lever clears only the region where one condition holds and leaves the intersections standing (Fig 9 :55-57).

## 3. What the three actions actually are

- **Prevent** is a change of contact with the ground (Fig 11 :40 "An improvement that does not change contact changes nothing, however much it improves."). It cannot mean erasing the hazard, because "Introduction is available; exclusion is not" (Fig 11 :61). Its warrant is a changed physical residual at the slot.
- **Prepare** is a head start or method that makes the next search cheaper (Fig 4 :22 "Only methods cross up. Only head starts come down."). Its warrant is capability under surprise, which is what the low-overlap resilience sources call the distinction between known unknowns and unknown unknowns.
- **Accept** is a recorded verdict that no composed response is available within the budget, not a passive default and not a convention (Fig 10 :47 "a convention that decides an outcome has stopped being one"). Its value part is anchored in D4; its capability part is anchored in the unreachable-at-budget reading of Fig 8 :40-43.

These are different bonds, not three settings of one dial (Fig 12 :112 "A plan is a sequence. A goal is a comparison. Progress is a subtraction."). Description cost and action cost are two currencies that do not add (Fig 12 :125-126).

## 4. The dependency order and the broken link

Memory: perceive, vocabulary, objective, planning, learn and carry (Fig 3). The chain is ordered by dependency, not importance (Fig 3 :20). The break in practice is at the objective link: governance can name a hazard and can plan an allocation, but often has no stated, measurable "what would count as success here, for whom, by when" (Fig 3 :25-27). Optimising the allocation cannot rescue a missing objective (Fig 3 :19). Repair the objective link first.

## 5. What it explicitly does not say

- Not a percentage split between prevention, preparation and acceptance, and not a named list of risks for any society. No figure line returns either. Fig 9 :32 "you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself."
- Not that a risk score is the verdict. It is a filter (Fig 9 :60) and a frame-internal quantity (Fig 1 :27 "They are frame-internal. A frame cannot score itself with a quantity it also produces.").
- Not that model agreement settles the physical question (Fig 2 :40), and not that public agreement settles the acceptability question (Fig 7 :35).
- Not that "no signal found" means "no risk". Fig 8 :39 "So a search that finds nothing is never a proof of absence."
- Not a standing best-practice list of risk treatments. Fig 11 :51 "No standing list of good actors survives a change of residual."
- Not grounded. No measurement, survey or model was run here.

## 6. The honest gap

The allocation ratio and the named assignments are a `gap`, status unreachable at this budget rather than silence (Fig 13 :134 "Unreachable is a verdict here, not a silence"). To close it one needs, per slot: a measured residual, a non-updating anchor, a stated objective with an instrument, a people-anchor for acceptability, and a seat able to refuse. The `gap` is a verdict, and it is reported rather than filled with an invented number.

## 7. What would settle it - the ground

1. A per-slot discriminator that distinguishes the source of a reading (instrument-level, not another aggregate score). From *The Hard Problem is About Source*: a meter reading "cannot comment on the source"; only a discriminating instrument resolves it.
2. An instrument that measures "what counts as progress" per slot, repairing the Fig 3 objective link.
3. An anchor test for acceptability: would the accept verdict still hold if the deciding body changed its mind about its own benefit from it? Fig 2 :33 "The anchor must not update."
4. The seven pre-registered refuters in `01_figure_grounded_audit.md` section 5, any one of which would break the shape claim.

## 8. The honest stop

A society's risk taxonomy is a visible choice, not nature's own list (*They're Scanning Books*: "Declaring the primitives does not make them neutral. It makes the choice visible."). The figures return the shape and the lineage and then stop at the limit of the available instruments. This answer stops there too. Indiscernibility is not identity, and the record of the route is what keeps it checkable.

## 9. One-paragraph version for outreach

> The Tether does not tell a society which risks to put in which bin, and it does not return a percentage split. It says the residual must be read per risk slot, because aggregating slots is how a live signal disappears into an average. It says the question is a union of five conditions with different anchors, so the verdicts must be kept separate and never averaged: physical outcome is settled by measurement, acceptability by the judgment of the people who bear the loss, and scores are filters for the budget, never witnesses for the verdict. It says the chain usually breaks at the objective link, before any allocation is planned, and that "accept" must be a recorded verdict with a reason, not a default and not an assumption that a quiet search proves absence. What it offers is the shape any defensible triage must have, seven failure conditions written in advance, and the instruments that would close the remaining gap. The named allocation stays open until the ground settles it.
