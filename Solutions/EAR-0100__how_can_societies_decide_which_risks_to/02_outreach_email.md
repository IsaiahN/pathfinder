# Outreach draft: check one decision procedure before you decide whether the framework is worth reading

**Use:** one-to-one, or as a cover letter attached to a submission through the public venues in `03_public_contact_points.csv`. **Not** for bulk send.

**Hard precondition:** do not send until a recipient has been offered the residual and the refuter first. The procedure must survive that check or the two-stage offer collapses on contact.

---

**Subject:** One checkable claim about risk triage under a tight budget

> Hi [name],
>
> I have one specific output I would like you to check against what you already know. I am not asking you to read the framework first, and I am not asking you to agree with it. If the output does not survive, there is nothing else to look at.
>
> **The question in effect terms.** A society has a finite budget and many risk slots. For each slot there is a prediction about what happens under an action and an observation that arrives late. The residual is the gap between them, and it must be read per slot, never as one global risk score. Fig 1 :19 says "The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated." Fig 1 :22 adds "Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average." So a near-zero national average with one catastrophic live slot is a legal state that still requires action.
>
> **The output (`specified`, not `grounded`).** The three-way choice to prevent, prepare or accept is not one dial with three settings. It is a union of five conditions with different anchors: physical outcome, forecast frame, allocation, acceptability to the people who bear the loss, and verification. The anchors differ, so the verdicts must be kept separate and never averaged. For the accept decision specifically, the question is constitutively about people, and Fig 2 :37 says "Human judgment is the right anchor for is this a good answer for a person, because the question is about people." A probability of harm is an instrument here, not the anchor. Fig 9 :60: "Use filters for the budget and witnesses for the verdict. Never let a filter hand you a verdict." Scores are filters.
>
> **What I am not claiming.** I am not claiming a figure returns a percentage split between prevention, preparedness and acceptance, or a list of named risks for your country. Fig 9 :32: "you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself." The framework returns the shape any defensible triage must have, the broken dependency link, and the failure conditions. It does not settle which risk gets which action without per-slot ground contact.
>
> **What would refute the shape claim:** I wrote these down before sending this.
> 1. A single global risk score is shown to preserve the per-slot signal and drive correct per-slot action.
> 2. One anchor is exhibited that legitimately settles both physical outcome and acceptability to affected people.
> 3. An instrument is shown that settles acceptability without appeal to the people who bear the loss.
> 4. Prevent, prepare and accept are shown to be one action with different parameter values.
> 5. A score is shown to be independent of the frame that produces it.
> 6. The objective link (what counts as success per slot) is shown to be measured already in the field.
> 7. A sealed, complete risk inventory is demonstrated, so that a no-signal search establishes absence.
>
> If any one holds, I will retract or amend the claim in the record with the correction left visible. A one-line "this fails because..." is more useful to me than agreement. If you would rather not engage, no reply is needed.
>
> **If it survives, and only then:** the sources are below. The figures are the authority, not my summary of them.
> - Canonical figures and formula (SVG, branch `general-agent`): https://github.com/IsaiahN/tether/tree/general-agent/figures
> - The Society of Agents essay, which carries the per-slot and filters-not-verdicts rules: https://adventuresinml.substack.com/p/how-a-society-of-agents-would-work
> - Supporting Climate-Related Decisions Under Uncertainty (Lempert, open access): https://link.springer.com/chapter/10.1007/978-3-031-85542-9_4
>
> **If you want to use the machinery:** the formula is in the figures link. Run it on a risk decision you know well and can check against a ground. Write down what would refute your result before you run it, and label the mode of what you get. A reported failure is more useful to me than a reported success.

## Why this version is ordered this way

| the move | figure-grounded reason |
|---|---|
| Output first, theory second | Fig 10 :52 "Bring a characterised residual, never the source." Fig 2 :45 "Frames range against the ground. They do not negotiate with it." |
| Do not ask for agreement | Fig 2 :35 "Alignment is triangulation, not negotiation. A mirror cannot correct you." |
| Residual stated in effect terms before frames | Fig 9 :27-28 "describe it before you go looking, or any frame you pick will seem to fit"; Fig 8 :53 "Residual first, frame second." |
| Pre-registered refuter | Seat discipline: a falsifier written before the run is a design instrument. |
| Mode stated on every claim | `THE_FORMULA.md`: "Mode. general · specified · grounded. State which." |

| Independence proportional to the cost of error | Fig 2 :42 "Buy independence in proportion to the cost of being wrong." |
| No bulk send, no receptiveness ranking | Fig 2 :40; Fig 2 :41; a room that agrees is "variation, not a verdict" (Fig 7 :35). |

## Two-stage offer in one line each

1. **Stage 1:** here is the per-slot residual and the five-anchor separation, and here are seven written failure conditions. What would you count as a failure?
2. **Stage 2, opt-in only:** if it survives and you are curious, here are the three source links. The figures, not the summary, are the authority.
3. **Stage 3, opt-in only:** the formula is a method. Run it on your own problem, write your own refuter first, state your own mode.
