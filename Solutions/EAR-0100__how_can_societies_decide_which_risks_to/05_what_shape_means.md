## 1. A shape is a constraint, not a filler

The figures README states the limit: the framework returns "what a missing thing would have to look like (a shape, not a name)." Fig 9 :32 "you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself." Fig 13 :134 "Unreachable is a verdict here, not a silence" combines with the canonical rule that the framework returns a shape and then stops at the limit of the available instruments.

From *The Library of Babel* (carried quote): "You can cut your search short if the set of books returned is small and descriptive enough that it provides a general shape of the answer that satisfies your question."

So for EAR-0100 a **shape** is the form any defensible risk triage must have, stateable before we can name which risk goes in which bin. It constrains: per-slot residual, separate anchors, a written objective link, a refusal seat, independence bought in proportion to cost. It does not fill: it does not return "40 percent prevention" or "accept flooding in region X."

Concretely: *a five-anchor, per-slot verdict procedure with pre-registered failure conditions* is a shape. *A national risk register with a single ranked score* is a filled answer, and it is the thing the figures refuse (F1).

## 2. How the shape is obtained

- *Library of Babel* (carried): use the vocabulary already closest to the thing, look for new terms that overlap with it, recurse. "With each new term gained, you narrow the space and deepen your vocabulary."
- Fig 6 :30 "outward: does another frame already hold it? The description travels. Fails by being sharpened into terms only we can read. Cast it in effect terms." Fig 9 :30-31 "your words are borrowed too, so describe the effect rather than the cause."
- *Decoding Tacit Knowledge* (carried): "When contact isn't possible, the best you can send is a plain description of what you'd expect to see, because anyone can check that against the world."
- In this folder the residual was described in effect terms first (prediction versus observation per slot, finite budget, late ground, plural bearers of loss), and only then were figure lines and world fields selected (Fig 8 :53 "Residual first, frame second.").

*How a Society of Agents Would Work* (fetched) gives the operational form of the shape for a society: "separate agents, one ground", with methods shared upward and head starts handed down. It also states the exact budget rule used in F5: "Use filters for the budget and witnesses for the verdict. Never let a filter hand you a verdict." That essay is the closest thing to a canonical reading of "society decides" in the eight, and it governs section 6 of this file.

## 3. What licenses a name (which risk, which action)

Only two things, both outside the shape:

1. **The ground settles it.** `THE_FORMULA.md` step 5: "the ground settles it. Verified only by what pays. A gate is not the ground." For EAR-0100 that means a measured per-slot residual and a realised outcome, not a model score.
2. **A provenanced import from a frame whose closure differs**, with a source gate and a shadow test (Fig 6 :23 "No frame escapes its own closure. The bound moves only on contact with a frame whose closure differs."; Fig 8 :50-52 "Convergent derivation and adopted import look identical in the contents. Only the record of where each came from separates them, and the surplus lives only where the closures differ."). For EAR-0100 this is how a society imports a frame that can represent a hazard its own closure lacks.

*They're Scanning Books* (carried): "The real import is not a new term in the existing space. It is a new dimension of the space." Also "Declaring the primitives does not make them neutral. It makes the choice visible." A society's risk taxonomy is a visible choice, not nature's own list.

### Correction retained visibly (same correction as the exemplar's 05 section 5b / 06 Q1)

I initially read "shape, not a name" as forbidding the naming of any concrete action. That overstates it. The figures README's phrase governs a **missing** primitive, one not held in Γ. Naming prevent, prepare and accept is not inventing a primitive; those are bindings from the governance frame onto operations the society already holds (change contact, build capability, record a verdict). What the figures forbid is (a) inventing a seventh mechanism, (b) claiming that such a binding moves the wall (Fig 6 :21 "MINT composes inside the closure and can never add an atom."), and (c) claiming that the binding settles the per-slot question without ground contact. The correction is kept here rather than silently applied.

## 4. Why a single risk score is the specific forbidden error

*The Hard Problem is About Source* (carried) gives the mechanism. Metamerism: an instrument reads green and "cannot comment on the source". One green light, or yellow plus blue, or RGB, or CMYK, or a scheme not yet discovered: the reading does not determine the source. "If several paths create the same behavior, then behavior is a projection with lossy information." And "An instrument that can't distinguish these cases demonstrates its own adequacy." It was the spectrometer, not an argument, that resolved it.

A national risk score is a reading of many slots projected onto one number. Several different per-slot configurations produce the same score. Naming a triage decision from that score is the metamerism error: a decision derived from a projection without an instrument that discriminates among the sources. What is owed is a per-slot discriminator, not a better universal score.

Fig 1 :27 "They are frame-internal. A frame cannot score itself with a quantity it also produces." reinforces this: the score is produced by the same frame it ranks, so it cannot be its own anchor.

## 5. The people-anchor reading

For the acceptability part of the question, Fig 2 :37 is decisive and unusual: "Human judgment is the right anchor for is this a good answer for a person, because the question is about people." Fig 2 :36 "An anchor is legitimate when the question is constitutively about it." So the figures themselves authorise a people-anchor for D4, while forbidding that same anchor from settling D1 (Fig 2 :38 "It is only an instrument for is this code correct, and instruments can be wrong on axes they do not measure.").

This is why the two verdict streams in this folder are never averaged. A physically well-founded decision to prepare can still be unacceptable to those who bear the cost, and a publicly accepted risk can still be physically unmitigated. Both statements can be true at once because they range against different anchors (Fig 2 :46 "Each frame is corrected by it separately, and what makes the correction sound is that neither frame can move it.").

World corroboration from a different field: Aven, Glette-Iversen and Karatzoudi (2025) write that the decision of when to invoke the precautionary principle and how much weight to give it is "fundamentally a management or political issue, not science." IPCC AR6 WGII Chapter 17 adds with very high confidence that "Governance, especially when inclusive and context sensitive, is an important enabling condition for climate risk management and adaptation."

## 6. What "society decides" means under the figures

From *How a Society of Agents Would Work* (fetched) and the figures:

- A society is a group of separate frames ranging against one ground, not a single super-frame. "A group is only worth having while its members stay different." Independence is "how little they draw on the same evidence" (Fig 2 :31).
- The group's agreement is never the verdict. Fig 7 :35 "A room's agreement is variation, not a verdict. Selection between rooms is where the ground votes."
- Votes come from outcomes, not popularity; use success rate per try and per kind of situation, because "Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average." That is Fig 1 :22 applied to collective judgment.
- The maintainer of the shared store sets conventions, never verdicts; Fig 10 :47 "a convention that decides an outcome has stopped being one."
- The room (the decision body for one question) must change when the question changes: Fig 10 :72 "A room that stays fixed while the question moves is the failure; the correlation is only its symptom."
- "Introduction is available; exclusion is not" (Fig 11 :61). A society adds measures to a habitat it did not author; a managed risk landscape is permanent work, not a solved state (Fig 11 :63-64 "a managed ecosystem is permanent work rather than a solved state").

## 7. Prestige is not action

From *The Recursive Self-Improvement Trap* (fetched, verbatim): "Prestige must never be allowed to validate an action directly." And: "Prestige can fund an action. It can give an action a budget to be tried. But it cannot settle an action. Only the ground can settle it."

For climate risk governance this forbids treating an institution's reputation, a famous assessment, or a large consensus as the warrant for accepting or preparing. Those can fund a trial of a measure; only the realised outcome can settle it. It is also why `03_public_contact_points.csv` contains venues and opt-in channels rather than famous names, and why `02_outreach_email.md` asks for a refutation and never for agreement.

## 8. My own audit is a simulation, not a ground reading

Same essay (fetched, verbatim): "LLMs can quickly simulate an approximate answer/output with the formula found in the architecture post. It will often be better than the generic outputs that you will usually get otherwise too (intelligence is essentially arrangement). However, the truly testable output would need to be derived from the real architecture or working the formula yourself."

So `01_figure_grounded_audit.md`, `06_isomers_and_order.md`, `07_the_tether_answer.md`, `08_expert_verification_packet.md`, `09_watson_answer.md` and `11_audit.md` are simulated derivations checked for internal consistency against the figures. They are not tests of climate risk governance and cannot settle the question they report on. This must be stated on every substantive file and is restated in each one.
