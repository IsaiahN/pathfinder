# Expert verification packet: the Tether on risk triage under a tight budget

**What this is:** a claim in a form a climate-risk-governance researcher or risk scientist can check. Mode, derivation and refuters are stated before the conclusion, so a reader knows what to look for and what would settle it.

**Mode: `specified` on the sub-claims, `general` on the structural shape, `gap` on the allocation number. Not `grounded`.** This is a simulated derivation checked for internal consistency against the figures, not a ground reading. It cannot settle the question it reports on.

## 1. The claim, in one paragraph

For a society with a finite budget and many risk slots, the three-way choice to prevent, prepare or accept is not one dial with three settings. It is a union of five conditions with different anchors: physical outcome (measurement), forecast frame (a failing instrument), allocation (realised outcome), acceptability (human judgment of the people who bear the loss), and verification (the ground, never agreement). The residual must be read per slot, scores are filters for the budget rather than witnesses for the verdict, the decision chain usually breaks at the objective link before any allocation is planned, and "accept" must be a recorded verdict with a stated reason rather than a default or an inference from a quiet search. The framework returns this shape and its failure conditions, not a percentage split and not a named list of risks.

## 2. Three checkable sub-claims

### C1: per-slot residual and filters-not-verdicts (framework-internal; checkable by reading Figures 1 and 9)

- **Statement:** risk must be indexed per slot; a global score hides the live slot; and a score is a filter for the budget, never a witness for the verdict.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 1 :19 "The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated." Fig 1 :22 "Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average." Fig 1 :20 "Support is satisfied when some slot carries positive mass, so a global residual near zero with one live slot is a legal state and not an inert one." Fig 9 :60 "Use filters for the budget and witnesses for the verdict. Never let a filter hand you a verdict." Fig 1 :27 "They are frame-internal. A frame cannot score itself with a quantity it also produces."
- **Refuter:** exhibit a global score that preserves the per-slot signal and drives correct per-slot action, or show a score independent of the frame that produces it. Either fires, C1 falls.

### C2: the acceptability anchor is human judgment, not a model score (checkable by reading Figure 2, and against risk-science literature)

- **Statement:** "which risks to accept" is constitutively about people, so its anchor is human judgment; a probability estimate is an instrument for the physical question and can be wrong on axes it does not measure.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 2 :36 "An anchor is legitimate when the question is constitutively about it." Fig 2 :37 "Human judgment is the right anchor for is this a good answer for a person, because the question is about people." Fig 2 :38 "It is only an instrument for is this code correct, and instruments can be wrong on axes they do not measure." Fig 2 :33 "The anchor must not update."
- **World corroboration (secondary):** Aven, Glette-Iversen and Karatzoudi, *Journal of Risk Research* 27(11), 2025, write that when to invoke the precautionary principle and how much weight to give it is "fundamentally a management or political issue, not science." IPCC AR6 WGII Chapter 17 finds with very high confidence that "Governance, especially when inclusive and context sensitive, is an important enabling condition for climate risk management and adaptation."
- **Refuter:** an instrument that settles acceptability without appeal to the people who bear the loss, or an anchor that legitimately settles both physical outcome and acceptability. Either fires, C2 falls.

### C3: the union reading, the broken objective link, and the honest accept bin (framework-internal; checkable by reading Figures 3, 8, 9, 10, 11)

- **Statement:** the five conditions form a union, not a partition, so verdicts stay separate; the chain breaks at the objective link before planning; and "accept" must record which of three cases it holds (underivable in principle, unreachable at this budget, or genuine absence unprovable from inside).
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 9 :54 "And when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again." Fig 9 :57 "a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either. It stops being a mixture and becomes a specific compound." Fig 3 :27 "A link with no instrument is not a link that works. It is a link nobody has looked at." Fig 8 :39 "So a search that finds nothing is never a proof of absence." Fig 10 :47 "a convention that decides an outcome has stopped being one." Fig 11 :51 "No standing list of good actors survives a change of residual."
- **Refuter:** show that the five conditions are disjoint (partition), exhibit a measured objective link per slot in the field, demonstrate a sealed and complete risk inventory, or show a standing treatment list whose valence survives every change of residual. Any fires, C3 falls.

## 3. What is explicitly not claimed

- Not a percentage split between prevention, preparation and acceptance.
- Not a named list of risks for any society, country or organisation.
- Not that a risk score, cost-benefit number or multi-criteria ranking is the verdict. Those are filters for the budget.
- Not that model agreement settles the physical question, or that public agreement settles the acceptability question.
- Not that "no signal found" proves "no risk".
- Not a standing best-practice list of treatments; valence is relative to the residual.
- Not `grounded`. No measurement or model was run in this derivation.
- Not a claim that the precautionary principle is itself a decision rule. This packet records the risk-science position that it is not one.

## 5. What would settle it

1. A per-slot source-discriminating instrument, not another aggregate score. A meter reading "cannot comment on the source" (*The Hard Problem is About Source*); the discriminator, not the argument, resolves it.
2. An instrument that measures what would count as progress per slot, repairing the Fig 3 objective link. Lempert (2025) corroborates that "No universal criterion exists" and that framing comes first.
3. An anchor test for acceptability: would the verdict still hold if the deciding body's own benefit from it changed? Fig 2 :33 "The anchor must not update."
4. Any of the seven pre-registered refuters in `01_figure_grounded_audit.md` section 5.

## 6. What an expert can check in twenty minutes
