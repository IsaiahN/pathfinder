| what | URL | verified content |
|---|---|---|
| Ice shelf overview (Wikipedia) | https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ice_shelf | Defines the grounding line and ice front; states that in steady state about half of Antarctic ice shelf mass is lost to basal melt and half to calving, and that the relative importance varies significantly between shelves. Documents Larsen B hydrofracture by meltwater wedging, thinning before collapse, pinning points and the acceleration of feeder glaciers. A synthesis page, not a primary measurement. |
| Ice sheet and marine instability (Wikipedia) | https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ice_sheet | Grounded below sea level, MISI cannot occur while a stable ice shelf is in front; basal melt thins the shelf, reduces buttressing and pushes the grounding line back in a self-reinforcing loop. | The Science of Ice Shelves (NSIDC) | https://nsidc.org/learn/parts-cryosphere/ice-shelves/science-ice-shelves | Describes calving as near-cyclical on a stable shelf; warm air forms melt ponds that deepen crevasses (hydrofracture); warm water melts from below; sea-ice decline lets swell buckle an already fractured shelf; basal melting at 0.1 C above freezing can remove about 10 m of ice per year; glacier acceleration of three to eight times after Larsen B. |
| Why Ice Shelves Matter (NSIDC) | https://nsidc.org/learn/parts-cryosphere/ice-shelves/why-ice-shelves-matter | Ice shelves do not raise sea level directly when they break up; the glaciers behind accelerate. Describes the resisting pressure of islands and submerged mountains ("brakes on glacier flow are released"). Notes that the main cause of West Antarctic mass loss is ice shelf basal melt per IPCC AR6, while at Thwaites the intrusion of warm deep ocean water, not air temperature, is the main source of thinning. |

## Wider literature that bears on the shape

These are links found on the pages above and read there as descriptions, not opened as separate sources. They are named so a checker can find the actual instruments and models.

- **Hydrofracture theory:** the melt-pond and fracture-wedging study linked from the NSIDC science page (`doi:10.1029/AR079p0079`).
- **Sea-ice buffer:** the 2018 study on regional sea-ice retreat and Larsen A, B and Wilkins breakups linked from the same page (`doi:10.1038/s41586-018-0212-1`).
- **Basal melt sensitivity:** the 2002 ocean-temperature study linked there (`doi:10.1126/science.1070942`).
- **MISI and MICI:** DeConto and Pollard 2016 (`doi:10.1038/nature17145`), linked from the NSIDC science page, and the contested paleo record discussed in the ice sheet overview.
- **Post-breakup acceleration:** the 2004 Glacier acceleration study linked from the NSIDC "why they matter" page (`doi:10.1029/2004GL020670`).

No quote from any of these linked papers is used in this folder. They are pointers for the ground check, not witnesses for the verdict.

## Distribution: offer, do not push

Figure-grounded reasons:

- **Fig 4 :22 "Only methods cross up. Only head starts come down."** Sending the full architecture before anyone has described a residual sends a recording, not a generator. Lead with the shape claim and its refuter; offer links after.
- **Fig 10 :42 "A verifier that can reconstruct the claim cannot verify it."** A recipient who has absorbed the framework before being asked for a verdict has joined the frame being checked.
- **Fig 9 :27 "describe it before you go looking, or any frame you pick will seem to fit".** Ask what they would expect to see if the shape claim were true before giving them the article.
- **Fig 2 :40 "disqualifying for a verdict: agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool".** A mass send selected for people who already agree converts the claim into a convention, not corroboration (Fig 2 :31).
- **Fig 2 :42 "Buy independence in proportion to the cost of being wrong."** The wrong answer here sends field campaigns to the wrong shelf. Independence is worth paying for.

**Recommended order in a reply:** (1) the shape claim and its refuter; (2) ask what the recipient would count as a failure and record the answer; (3) only then the four public links, with the note that the figures, not the summary, are the authority.

## The two-stage offer

1. **Stage 1 before stage 2** is Fig 10 :52 "Bring a characterised residual, never the source." and Fig 2 :45 "Frames range against the ground. They do not negotiate with it." The recipient checks the output without holding the framework's interior.
2. **Stage 2 is opt-in.** Once a recipient has absorbed the theory, their agreement stops being evidence. The stage-1 verdict must be recorded first and never pooled later as corroboration.
3. **Stage 3 sends a method, which is permitted.** Fig 4 :22 "Only methods cross up. Only head starts come down." The formula is a generator, so giving it away does not give away an answer.
4. **Stage 3 still needs a ground per problem.** A frame cannot check the direction of its own question; each user must write down what would refute their result and state the mode of what they get.

Nothing is sent by this agent. You host and send. If you host the packet, prefer a link that lets a recipient decline contact, an opt-in download page rather than an attachment pushed to a list.
