## 1. A shape is a constraint, not a filler

The canonical rule says the framework reports *"what a missing thing would have to look like (a shape, not a name)."* Fig 9 :32 "you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself". Fig 13 :134 "Unreachable is a verdict here, not a silence". The metalaw note adds: "The figures are the ground for design questions; Isaiah is the anchor for what the figures genuinely cannot settle."

From the Library of Babel essay: *"This means that you can cut your search short if the set of books returned is small and descriptive enough that it provides a general shape of the answer that satisfies your question."* The same essay: *"With each new term gained, you narrow the space and deepen your vocabulary."*

For this question a **shape** is therefore the narrowed form of an answer we can state before we can rank the processes. It constrains: per-slot residual, union unbundled, contact enumerated outward, measurement as anchor, no single scalar, no name for a slot until an instrument settles it. It does not fill: it does not say "basal melting is the control."

- Shape: "the controlling term for shelf s is a condition in contact with a thinning residual, measured by an instrument that discriminates among co-producers."
- Name: "basal melting controls shelf s." The framework never returns this.

## 2. How the shape is obtained

- **Proximity and recursion.** Library of Babel: use the vocabulary you already hold that is closest to the thing, then find recurring new terms that overlap with it. That is exactly what happened in `01`: "stability" was the borrowed word; "thinning", "back stress", "hydrofracture", "flexure" and "grounding-line retreat" were the nearer terms found in the ice-science frame.
- **Effect terms.** Fig 6 :30 "The description travels. Fails by being sharpened into terms only we can read. Cast it in effect terms." Fig 9 :30 "your words are borrowed too, so describe the effect rather than the cause". The outreach email describes the effect, reduced restraint on a glacier, before naming any process.
- **Plain description when contact is impossible.** Decoding Tacit Knowledge: *"When contact isn't possible, the best you can send is a plain description of what you'd expect to see, because anyone can check that against the world."* This is why stage 1 of the offer carries a failure condition, not a theory.
- **Stopping.** Library of Babel: you are done when *"the finish line will also be the starting place"*, and the paradox is that by then you have *"composed the answer through the process of locating it."* Here the stop is the listed set of instruments that would close the `gap`, not a verdict about a winning process.

## 3. What licenses a name

Only two things, both outside the shape itself:

1. **The ground settles it.** Formula step 5: "the ground settles it. Verified only by what pays. A gate is not the ground." The canonical rule: the framework *"stops at the limit of what the available instruments can distinguish, and records the lineage so the stop is honest."*
2. **A provenanced import from a frame whose closure differs** (Fig 6 :23, Fig 8 :50), with a source gate and a shadow test that explains a described residual.

The process names in this folder, basal melt, hydrofracture, calving, cliff failure, sea-ice buffer, are **case 2**. They come from the glaciology frame, with author, venue, year and quote in `10_source_ledger.csv`. They are not figure-derived. Wekerle et al. 2026 even show two different dominant processes at two glaciers in one model, which is precisely why a universal name would be a category error.

From Library of Babel: *"If you don't have the answer to a question, it's because you are lacking certain things in the library (instruments, information) that would help, or perhaps you are asking the wrong question altogether."* Our missing thing is a source discriminator and a per-shelf threshold instrument, not another process name.

## 4. The instrument question, not the argument question

*The Hard Problem is About Source* gives the mechanism for F5 in `01`. Metamerism: an instrument reads green and *"cannot comment on the source"*. It might be one green light, yellow plus blue, RGB, CMYK, or a scheme not yet discovered. Two quotes are load-bearing here:

- *"If several paths create the same behavior, then behavior is a projection with lossy information"*
- *"An instrument that can't distinguish these cases demonstrates its own inadequacy, it cannot claim anything substantial about what is being measured or what causes it."*

Observed shelf thinning is that green reading. Basal melt, hydrofracture, wave flexure and dynamic thinning can all produce it. It was a spectrometer, not an argument, that resolved the colour case. Here the corresponding instrument is coincident basal melt rate, crevasse propagation and flexural strain at one shelf. Until it exists, naming a source from the reading is the metamerism error. Fig 6 :32 "An instrument is not built from a description. It is improved from a worse instrument already returning something." Altimetry and InSAR already return something, so the proto-instrument exists and this is a reachable instrument question, not a fantasy.

## 5. Q2 answered: what licenses "control"?

**Nothing in the framework licenses a particular process as the controller of a named shelf.** A process is licensed only by (i) a ground reading that discriminates it from co-producers at that shelf, or (ii) a provenanced import with a shadow test, recorded as such. Until one of those, the word "control" is the searcher's own contribution.

The record in this folder does **not** commit the error of naming the slot as the framework's answer. It reports what the world sources measure and labels those reports as imports. The one thing it refuses is the leap from "this process is measured here" to "the framework has identified the controller everywhere."

## 6. Two consequences

**Prestige is not action.** *The Recursive Self-Improvement Trap*: *"Prestige can fund an action. It can give an action a budget to be tried. But it cannot settle an action. Only the ground can settle it. The Ground Maintainer must be blind to prestige."* A campaign that gathers famous glaciologists to agree with the shape is the named collapse, not corroboration (Fig 2 :35 "Alignment is triangulation, not negotiation. A mirror cannot correct you."). The legitimate ask gives the claim a budget to be tried and lets the ground settle it.

**My own audit is a simulation, not a ground reading.** Same essay: *"LLMs can quickly simulate an approximate answer/output with the formula found in the architecture post. It will often be better than the generic outputs that you will usually get otherwise too. However, the truly testable output would need to be derived from the real architecture or working the formula yourself."* So `01`, `06`, `07`, `09` and `11` are simulated derivations checked for internal consistency against the figures. They are **not** tests of any glaciological claim and they cannot settle anything they report on.

**Contact and drift.** Decoding Tacit Knowledge: *"Two frames that overlap almost completely are 'one frame wearing two names'"* and *"a measurement with no question is noise, and a reading with no encounter is projection."* This is the reason the second seat in `11_audit.md` declares that its agreement is internal consistency only, and the reason the two synthesis web sources in `10_source_ledger.csv` are counted as one pool with several views.
