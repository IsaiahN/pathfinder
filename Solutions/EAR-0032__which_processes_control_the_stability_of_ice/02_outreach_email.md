# Outreach draft: check the shape first, then decide about the framework

**Use:** one-to-one, or as a cover letter attached to a submission through the public venues in `03_public_contact_points.csv`. **Not** for bulk send.

**Hard precondition:** do not send until the shape claim and its refuter below survive the recipient's own check. The offer collapses on contact if the stage-1 output does not hold.

---

**Subject:** One checkable claim about what "ice shelf stability" can mean

> Hi [name],
>
> I have one specific output I would like you to check against what you already know. I am not asking you to read the framework first, and I am not asking you to agree with it. If the output does not survive, there is nothing else to look at.
>
> **The output (`specified` on the applied shape, `general` on the structural claim, not `grounded`):** the question "which processes control ice shelf stability" is a union of overlapping conditions, not one question with one answer. The residual has to be indexed per shelf, per tributary glacier, per grounding-line sector and per season. A global stability number can look fine while one slot is live, because averaging across slots is exactly how the live signal disappears. The conditions, ocean heat and basal melt, surface meltwater and hydrofracture, mechanical calving and rifting, geometry and pinning with back stress, sea-ice loss and wave flexure, couple to one another. A lever that clears a shelf where only one condition holds leaves the intersections standing.
>
> **What the framework does not supply:** it does not name a winning process and it does not give a threshold number. Its own rule is that it returns a shape, not a name. The process names in my notes come from published glaciology, with dates and measurements, and I am recording them as imports rather than as things the framework derived. I am not claiming that basal melting is "the" control, nor that any of the other processes is.
>
> **What would refute the shape claim, written before this message:**
> 1. One scalar, with a stated instrument, predicts the response of every shelf under both ocean and surface forcing without hiding a live slot.
> 2. Two of the five condition families never co-occur and never change each other's effect on glacier acceleration, so that pair is a partition after all.
> 3. A model-only metric is found that every independent field observation fails to move, so agreement, not measurement, becomes the anchor.
> 4. Second- and third-order contacts, such as sea ice and swell, never change the residual once melt and meltwater are held fixed.
>
> If any one holds I will retract the claim. A one-line "this fails because..." is more useful to me than agreement. If you would rather not engage, no reply is needed.
>
> **If it survives, and only then:** here are the sources. The figures, not my summary of them, are the authority. I have also listed the public venues where this kind of claim can be checked properly.
>
> - Verbatim figure text with line numbers (local in this solution): `FRAMEWORK/reference/03-FIGURES_TEXT.md`
> - Canonical usage rule (local copy): `FRAMEWORK/reference/FIGURES_README_canonical.md`
> - The Cryosphere, open-access journal with public peer review: https://www.the-cryosphere.net/
> - International Glaciological Society, public contact and symposia: https://www.igsoc.org/contact
>
> **If you want to use the machinery:** the formula is in the figures folder. Run it on a problem you know well and can check against a ground. Write down what would refute your result *before* you run it, and state the mode of what you get. A reported failure is more useful to me than a reported success.
>
> [name]

## Two-stage offer, stated plainly

1. **Stage 1:** send the residual, the shape claim and the pre-registered refuter. Ask what the recipient would count as a failure. Record their verdict before showing any theory.
2. **Stage 2, opt-in only:** if they are curious, offer the three source links and the note that the figures, not the summary, are the authority.
3. **Stage 3, opt-in only:** offer the formula as a method to reuse, with the instruction that each user writes their own refuter and states their own mode.

A Track B reply, if one is ever sent, is a conversation and never corroboration. There is no bulk-send plan here.
