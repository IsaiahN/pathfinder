# The Tether's answer on ice shelf stability

**Mode: `general` on framework-internal shape claims, `specified` on the applied shape, `gap` on which process dominates a given shelf and on every threshold number. Not `grounded`.** This is a simulated derivation checked against the figures, not a ground reading. It cannot settle the glaciological question it reports on.

## 1. The residual it answers

An ice shelf restrains the glaciers behind it. When that restraint falls, the glaciers accelerate and move land ice into the ocean. The residual is indexed per shelf, per tributary, per grounding-line sector and per season:

```
R_s,t = | prediction of thickness, velocity, rift state and back stress - observation |
```

It is not one number called "ice shelf stability." Fig 1 :19 "The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated." Fig 1 :22 "Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average." The world record agrees from the ground: at 79 North Glacier mass loss is dominated by basal melting, while at Zachariae Isstrom it is dominated by iceberg calving, in the same 2008 to 2021 model (Wekerle et al. 2026). One ranking cannot cover both.

## 3. What it explicitly does not say

- Not that basal melting, hydrofracture, calving, cliff failure or wave forcing is "the" control. That is a `gap` from the framework and a per-shelf empirical finding from the ground.
- Not one global stability index, and not one ranked process list valid for every shelf.
- Not that marine ice cliff instability is established. Its observational support is contested in the world record; that is a ground disagreement (see `01` F3).
- Not that the six-term substrate contains a melt or heat primitive. It governs composition form only (see `01` S1).
- Not `grounded`. No measurement was taken here, and the process names are imported reports.

## 4. What would settle it, the ground

A source-discriminating instrument, not a further argument:

1. Coincident basal melt rate, crevasse propagation and flexural strain at one shelf in one season, so one thinning signal can be apportioned among its co-producers.
2. A per-shelf threshold determination: at what measured combination of melt, fracture and wave forcing does a trend become a disintegration, with the instrument named before the run.
3. A direct observation of the cliff-failure link at a named grounding line, or its refutation.
4. A demonstration that two of the five condition families are partitioned for a named shelf.
5. Standing falsifiers from `01` §4: a single universal scalar predictor; a partition proof; a model-only metric no observation can move; proof that third-order contacts never matter; a process name derived from the substrate; a quantity returning nothing at any resolution; disjoint instruments changing the verdict weight.

## 5. The honest stop

**Reached at the instrument limit, not at a final answer.** Sensors already register melt rates, velocities, rift propagation and sea-ice extent. What does not yet register is the apportionment among co-producers and the threshold schedule per shelf. The framework stops there and records the lineage so the stop is checkable. Indiscernibility is not identity: a shelf that looks stable by one instrument is not thereby stable. Verdicts stay separate per domain and are never averaged.

## 6. One-paragraph version for outreach

> The Tether does not name a winning process for ice shelf stability, because its rule is to return a shape, not a name. What it says is this: the residual must be tracked per shelf, per glacier and per season rather than folded into one stability number; the candidate processes form a union of overlapping conditions, so a single lever leaves the intersections standing; the habitat must be enumerated outward, because the term that matters, sea ice and swell for example, can be three contacts away; measurement is the only anchor, since models that share a parameter pool are one witness with several names; and since basal melt, hydrofracture and wave flexure can all produce the same observed thinning, a source-discriminating instrument is owed before any process is named as the control. What the world reports, with dates and measurements, is that different shelves are dominated by different processes. The answer stops there, with its refuters written in advance and the instruments that would close the gap listed, rather than pretending the slot is already filled.
