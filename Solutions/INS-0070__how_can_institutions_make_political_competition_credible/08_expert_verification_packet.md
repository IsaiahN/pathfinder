# Expert verification packet: How can institutions make political competition credible when losing power may carry severe personal or economic risks?

**What this is:** a claim in the form a specialist can check. Mode, provenance, and refuters are stated before the conclusion, so a reader knows what to look for and what would settle it.

**Mode: `specified` on the institutional application, `general` on the structural shape. Not `grounded`.** This is a shape and a route, not a result. It is a simulated derivation checked for internal consistency against the figures (`05_what_shape_means.md` §6), not a ground reading.

**Hard precondition for sending:** re-check every figure quote below against `FRAMEWORK/reference/03-FIGURES_TEXT.md` before using this packet in outreach. The figures, not this summary, are the authority.

## 1. The claim, in one paragraph

A contest over power is credible only if five constraints hold in effect terms, whatever country or period is examined: the body that certifies the result must not change its answer when a contender pushes on it; the contenders must each be corrected by that same unmoved settlement rather than negotiating over what the count means; the cost of losing must be named and bounded in advance, because a stake that survives the outcome is exactly what makes defeat unacceptable; removal from office must be writable as a refusal that sticks, not merely a subtraction of a person; and the habitat of retaliation must be enumerated two or three steps out, with the bonds between roles priced rather than recorded as a list of ingredients. These constraints form a union compound, not a blueprint. The framework returns a shape, not a name. No specific institution is derived and nothing here is grounded. Below are three sub-claims that can be checked against the figures and against a specialist's own knowledge, each with the refuter written before the run.

## 2. Three checkable sub-claims

### C1, framework-internal and political-science facing: the arbiter must not update

- **Statement:** the certifying settlement must be pinned, and a body that moves with the incumbent is a party listed as the ground, not an anchor.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 2 :33: *"The anchor must not update."* Fig 2 :47: *"A ground listed as one of the parties has already become a frame. Which is panel 1, arrived at by a different route."* Fig 2 :34: *"If it does, panel 3 turns into panel 1: the anchor becomes another frame, everything drifts together, and the drift is coherent, which is why nobody notices."*
- **What to check:** is the characterization faithful to how counting and certification actually work, and is the anchor a thing each side ranges against rather than a mediator that breaks ties (Fig 2 :46)? Does a count that tracks the incumbent's influence fail to close the contest?
- **Refuter:** a documented case of credible alternation where the certifying body demonstrably changed its ruling under incumbent pressure and no other constraint repaired the settlement. If found, C1 is withdrawn.

### C2, institutional-design facing: severe loss is a standing stake that must be bounded

- **Statement:** the cost of losing is not only a bad payoff. It is a position whose value survives the outcome, so it must be nameable and bounded before the contest. This is Fig 3's middle link, where the question supplies no instrument.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 10 :61: *"And standing is a stake that survives the outcome it was earned on."* Fig 10 :59: *"an interest in the outcome is unavoidable, an interest in the reading is disqualifying."* Fig 3 :27: *"A link with no instrument is not a link that works. It is a link nobody has looked at."* Fig 2 :42: *"Buy independence in proportion to the cost of being wrong."*
- **What to check:** do prison, exile, confiscation, and post-defeat prosecution behave as standing stakes in the relevant cases, and does independence of checking frames scale with severity? The political-science and institutional sources in `04_links_and_distribution.md` put the same items on the table: retribution after leaving office, wealth from office, criminal prosecution on leaving, and control of other branches.
- **Refuter:** a documented case of repeated alternation with severe personal risk of loss and no bound on that risk in any institution. If found, C2 is withdrawn as a necessary condition.

### C3, framework-internal: the arrangement and the habitat carry the result

- **Statement:** same actors under a different bond give a different political substance, refusal must be writable as negation, and the habitat cannot be subtracted, so the work is permanent.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 12 :5: *"A set of ingredients is not one. The bond is the rest of the information, and it is the half nothing was recording."* Fig 12 :112: *"A plan is a sequence. A goal is a comparison. Progress is a subtraction. A refutation is a negation, and that one is not a bond."* Fig 10 :82: *"Three panels, one move: arrange for refusal."* Fig 11 :61-62: *"The seat's power is asymmetric. Introduction is available; exclusion is not. You can add an actor and you cannot subtract the habitat, because contact is a fact about the world and does not consult you."*
- **What to check:** can two arrangements with the same actors and different bonds produce indistinguishable behaviour? Can a one-time reform permanently bind retaliation without maintenance? Can a designer genuinely exclude an actor from the habitat?
- **Refuter:** either result, a same-actors different-bond pair with indistinguishable behaviour, or a one-time change that permanently bounds retaliation, falsifies C3. A credible alternation that registers a refusal with subtraction alone, with no negation of a standing claim, also withdraws it.

## 3. What is explicitly not claimed

- Not a named institution. Fig 9 :32: *"you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself"*. Term limits, an independent electoral commission, and an independent judiciary are provenanced imports from political science, not figure derivations.
- Not loser's consent as the anchor, and not a tie-breaking third party. Consent is a frame state; the anchor is the settled transfer each side ranges against (Fig 2 :46-47).
- Not a single credibility score. Fig 1 :22: *"Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average."*
- Not that a search which finds nothing proves absence. Fig 8 :39: *"So a search that finds nothing is never a proof of absence."*
- Not a one-time fix. Introduction is available, exclusion is not, and a managed ecosystem is permanent work (Fig 11 :61-64).
- Not grounded. No election, court ruling, experiment, or dataset was used to settle the claim, and the verdict is a candidate.

## 5. What would settle it

A ground reading, not agreement. The refuters in §2 are the pre-registered failure conditions. Beyond them:

1. A case or measurement where a named binding, applied to a specific slot, is checked against whether the loser actually survived and whether the ruling moved.
2. A source-discriminating observation: a single "democracy score" cannot say which slot is live, so the discriminator, not a further argument, is owed.
3. Any of the five refuters in `07_the_tether_answer.md` §4 firing, each of which withdraws a specific finding.

Agreement from a shared evidence pool is one witness (Fig 2 :40), and a mirror cannot correct you (Fig 2 :35). A reply to this packet is a conversation, never corroboration.

## 6. What an expert can check in twenty minutes

1. Read Fig 2 :33, :46, :47 and Fig 12 :5, :112 (five short passages) in `FRAMEWORK/reference/03-FIGURES_TEXT.md`.
2. Check C1 against one case you know: did the certifying body move when the incumbent pushed, and did alternation continue anyway?

4. Check C3: find a same-actors different-bond pair with indistinguishable behaviour, or a one-time reform that ended retaliation without maintenance.
