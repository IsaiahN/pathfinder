## 1. Independent domain census

I redid this census from the residual line alone, before relying on the primary's conclusions, using the WATSON order: R by arity, symmetry and scale; one line per domain with actors / currency / anchor; partition or union; order by dependency.

- **R (arity / symmetry / scale).** The residual is the gap between a system that can register an accepted loser and one that cannot write that sentence or make it stick. Arity: two or more contending frames plus one settlement that is not a negotiation between them. Symmetry: winning and losing are ordered, not interchangeable; the consequence is a sequence, contest then settlement then transfer. Scale: first order at one contest and one arbiter, second order at a durable regime of repeated alternation where a settled contest becomes an atom for the next one.
- **Domain 1, contest.** Actors: incumbent, challenger, parties. Currency: votes, office, patronage. Anchor: none nameable yet, since who won is the dispute. Not a domain on its own.
- **Domain 2, settlement.** Actors: electoral body, courts, legislature, certifying officers. Currency: rulings, counts, certifications. Anchor: yes, a ruling that does not move when a contender pushes on it.
- **Domain 3, habitat / contact field.** Actors: military, bureaucracy, prosecutors, media, business, foreign governments, electorate. Currency: coercion, money, information, loyalty. Anchor: yes, the field of contact, which updates but is not authored by the designer.
- **Domain 4, verification / outside frame.** Actors: monitors, journalists, researchers, opposition inside the loop. Currency: credible reports, residuals. Anchor: yes, the ground, never agreement. In this simulated run all four domains draw on one evidence pool, so this domain's independence is zero.
- **Partition or union.** A union compound, not a partition. The conditions overlap: an unmoved arbiter, a bounded loss, a writable refusal, and an enumerated habitat. One lever clears the single-condition region and leaves the intersections standing.
- **Order by dependency.** Bind the sought-for shape as an ordinary term, pin the settlement, make refusal writable, name and bound the standing stake, enumerate the habitat, then expect alternation. Effort spent above a break arrives at a step that cannot use it.

My census matches the primary's four domains and its union reading. Where the primary derived from Fig 2 and Fig 10, I derived the union reading from Fig 9 :54-56 and the order from Fig 3 :20-21. Different starting figures, same domain boundaries. Low overlap between routes within the framework, but still one evidence pool.

## 4. Round trip gap R_T

The Symbols table defines R_T as the gap between what was sent up and what came back down, `gap(x, (T_E ∘ T_A)(x))`, measuring what the coarser description cannot hold. Fig 4 :30 requires it to be measured rather than assumed. I ran the round trip on the condensation the folder actually uses.

1. **Sent up (x):** the verbatim figure set, chiefly Fig 2 :33 and :47 (unmoved anchor), Fig 10 :61 (standing stake), Fig 11 :61-62 (asymmetric seat), Fig 12 :5 and :112 (arrangement and negation), and Fig 9 :32 (shape, not a name).
2. **Abstraction (T_A):** the shorthand carried in `07_the_tether_answer.md` §2, an unmoved settlement, a bounded loss, a writable refusal, an enumerated habitat, and priced bonds.
3. **Concretisation (T_E):** the concrete institutional claim a reader might draw, an independent electoral commission with fixed terms, an independent judiciary, and term limits.

**R_T is nonzero.** Three things the shorthand holds do not survive the trip back down:

- the **source gate and shadow test** for each imported institution. The shorthand says "provenanced import"; the concrete claim needs a named source, a differing closure, and a shadow test that explains a gap. Nothing in the figure text names an institution, so this constraint is dropped unless the ledger records it.
- the **operand distinction between `¬` and `−`**. The shorthand says "removal must stick"; the concrete claim can collapse into a reshuffle unless the refusal of the standing claim is written explicitly.
- the **per-slot indexing of the residual**. The shorthand says "credibility"; the concrete claim tends to a country score, which Fig 1 :22 forbids.

Conversely, two things survive cleanly: the requirement that the settlement not move under pressure, and the requirement that a severe loss is a standing stake that must be bounded. That is the residue worth sending. R_T was **not** measured numerically, because no ground reading exists; it is recorded qualitatively as the enumerated set of dropped constraints. Treating "the formula is the condensed version of the figures" as identity would set R_T to zero and would be the dead-reckoning error Fig 4 :30 names. The honest form is a lossy condensation with a measured gap, which is what `09_watson_answer.md` now states in its provenance and verdict.
