## Restatement as an effect

The question asks what must be true of an arrangement for a contest over power to be a competition rather than an existential struggle. Two options are visible:

- **Option A, norm first:** losing is safe because contenders are committed to accepting defeat. If the commitment fails under severe risk, the contest collapses into a fight over the arbiter, and the loss is no longer bounded.
- **Option B, ground first:** losing is safe because the settlement does not depend on what the loser wants. The outcome is pinned by something that does not move when a contender pushes on it, and the cost of losing is bounded before the contest starts.

An answer would look like a **shape**: a set of constraints any credible arrangement must satisfy, stated in effect terms, with the specific institutions left as provenanced imports from political science rather than as figure-derived names. Per `THE_FORMULA.md`: *"Mode. general · specified · grounded. State which. Accepted means the ground settled it; otherwise candidate."*

## What is deliberately not here

- No personal emails of named individuals.
- No ranking of people by how likely they are to agree.
- No bulk-send plan.
- No claim that the figures name a specific institution such as term limits or an independent judiciary. They return a shape, not a name.
- No claim that this simulated derivation settles the question it addresses.

Three reasons, in the figures' own words:

1. **Agreement is not a verdict.** Fig 2 :35: *"Alignment is triangulation, not negotiation. A mirror cannot correct you."* A list selected for people likely to agree manufactures the correlation that would make their agreement worthless.
2. **Correlation disqualifies a verdict.** Fig 2 :40: *"disqualifying for a verdict: agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool."*
3. **A verifier must not hold the interiors.** Fig 10 :42: *"A verifier that can reconstruct the claim cannot verify it."* So stage 1 of the offer carries only the residual and its refuter.

Legally: scraping personal emails for unsolicited bulk outreach breaches GDPR and CAN-SPAM style rules in many jurisdictions. Public institutional and opt-in channels avoid that failure as well as the structural one.
