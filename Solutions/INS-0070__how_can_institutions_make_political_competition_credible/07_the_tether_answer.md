# The Tether's answer: How can institutions make political competition credible when losing power may carry severe personal or economic risks?

**Mode: `specified` on the institutional application, `general` on the structural shape. Not `grounded`.** No election, court ruling, experiment, or dataset was used to settle this derivation.

**Status: SIMULATED DERIVATION, checked for internal consistency against the figures, not a ground reading.** Per `05_what_shape_means.md` §6, the figures are authority here but this run cannot settle the question it reports on. The verdict is a candidate, not an accepted result. All figure quotes are cited as `Fig N :line` against `FRAMEWORK/reference/03-FIGURES_TEXT.md`.

---

## 1. The residual it answers

A political order is supposed to convert disagreement into a contest with an accepted loser. When the cost of losing is unbounded, the loser has no reason to accept the outcome, and the contest does not close. It becomes a struggle over who controls the settlement itself.

- **Option A, norm first:** losing is safe because contenders are committed to accepting defeat. This relies on a frame state, and it fails exactly when severity rises.
- **Option B, ground first:** losing is safe because the settlement does not depend on what the loser wants. The outcome is pinned by something that does not move when a contender pushes on it, and the cost of losing is bounded before the contest starts.

The live signal is per slot, not one country score. Fig 1 :19: *"The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated."* Fig 1 :22: *"Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average."* The slots here are the contenders, the certifying body, the courts, the security services, the media, the economy, and the electorate.

This is a **union compound**, not one hard problem. Fig 9 :54-56: *"A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones. A single lever clears the region where only one condition holds and leaves the intersections standing, because the second condition still binds."* Credibility needs an unmoved arbiter **and** a bounded loss **and** a writable refusal **and** a known habitat. Term limits alone clear one region and leave the intersections standing.

## 2. The shape

The answer is a set of constraints in effect terms, not a named institution. Five constraints govern any credible arrangement.

1. **The settlement must not move when a contender pushes on it.** The body that certifies the result must not change its answer under incumbent pressure. Fig 2 :33: *"The anchor must not update."* Fig 2 :47: *"A ground listed as one of the parties has already become a frame. Which is panel 1, arrived at by a different route."* A count controlled by the incumbent is a party listed as the ground, and because the drift is coherent nobody involved sees it (Fig 2 :34). Mode: `general`, applied `specified`.

2. **Contenders must range separately against that same unmoved settlement, not negotiate over what the count means.** Fig 2 :45: *"Frames range against the ground. They do not negotiate with it."* Fig 2 :42: *"Buy independence in proportion to the cost of being wrong."* Where loss is severe, checking frames must not share the incumbent's evidence pool. Fig 2 :40: *"disqualifying for a verdict: agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool"*. Mode: `general`.

3. **The cost of losing must be named and bounded before the contest.** Prison, exile, confiscation, and prosecution after defeat are not just bad payoffs, they are a standing stake that continues to operate after the contest ends. Fig 10 :61: *"And standing is a stake that survives the outcome it was earned on."* Fig 10 :59: *"an interest in the outcome is unavoidable, an interest in the reading is disqualifying."* This is Fig 3's middle link. Fig 3 :27: *"A link with no instrument is not a link that works. It is a link nobody has looked at."* The question names the defect but supplies no bound on it. Mode: `specified`.

4. **Removal from office must be writable and must stick.** A system that can accumulate incumbents but cannot register that a tenure is over has not closed the contest. Fig 12 :112: *"A plan is a sequence. A goal is a comparison. Progress is a subtraction. A refutation is a negation, and that one is not a bond."* Removal of an incumbent is negation (refusing the claim), not subtraction (removing an operand). Fig 10 :82: *"Three panels, one move: arrange for refusal."* Mode: `general` for the operator, `specified` for the transfer.

5. **The habitat must be enumerated, and the bonds between roles priced.** Credibility is a property of the arrangement of contenders and habitat, not the character of one contender. Fig 11 :61-62: *"The seat's power is asymmetric. Introduction is available; exclusion is not. You can add an actor and you cannot subtract the habitat, because contact is a fact about the world and does not consult you."* Actors two or three steps out matter. Fig 11 :52-53: *"And contact cascades. Scoping the habitat to direct contact is how DDT reached the eagles: first-order enumeration, second-order effect, and the residual that mattered was three steps out."* Same actors under a different bond give a different substance. Fig 12 :5: *"A set of ingredients is not one. The bond is the rest of the information, and it is the half nothing was recording."* And the work never ends. Fig 11 :64: *"Which is why a managed ecosystem is permanent work rather than a solved state."* Mode: `general`, applied `specified`.

**Dependency order.** Fig 3 :20-21: *"The chain is ordered by dependency, not by importance. Each link consumes what the one above produces, so effort spent above the break arrives at a step that cannot use it."* Bind the sought-for shape (Fig 5 :65-66), pin the settlement (constraint 1), make refusal writable (constraint 4), name and bound the loss (constraint 3), enumerate the habitat (constraint 5), then expect alternation as a chain of differing handoffs (Fig 7 :36). Optimising alternation cannot rescue a broken settlement.

**Temporal addendum.** A credible competition is not one clean election. Fig 7 :35: *"A room's agreement is variation, not a verdict. Selection between rooms is where the ground votes."* Fig 7 :36: *"A chain of mirrors is one long room. The next room must differ from the last, and match the residual it surfaced."*

## 3. What it explicitly does not say

- Not a blueprint or an institutional name. Fig 9 :32: *"you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself"*. Term limits, an independent electoral commission, and an independent judiciary are bindings imported from political science and recorded as such in `10_source_ledger.csv`. They are not derivations from the figures and do not move the wall on their own.
- Not a claim that loser's consent or a national "democracy score" is the anchor. Consent is a frame state; it can be withdrawn and is not an anchor (Fig 2 :47). A global score discards the per-slot signal (Fig 1 :22).
- Not a claim that a third party breaks ties between contenders. Fig 2 :46: *"The anchor is not a third party to the disagreement and it is not the tie-breaker in a vote. Each frame is corrected by it separately, and what makes the correction sound is that neither frame can move it."*
- Not a claim that the figures select among countries or that a one-time reform solves the problem. Introduction is available, exclusion is not, and the work is permanent (Fig 11 :61-64).
- Not grounded. No observed transfer of power was used to settle any constraint, and no threshold for "severe" is derived.

## 4. What would settle it, the ground

The pre-registered refuter was written before the conclusion (see `01_figure_grounded_audit.md` §4 and `08_expert_verification_packet.md`). The shape claim is refuted if any of the following holds:

1. A documented case of credible alternation where the certifying body demonstrably moved under incumbent pressure and no other constraint repaired the settlement.
2. A documented case of repeated alternation with severe personal risk of loss and no bound on that risk in any institution.
3. A derivation of a specific institution from the six-term framework alone, with no import from political science.
4. An improvement that does not change anyone's contact with the ground (Fig 11 :40) and still moves the credibility outcome.
5. Two arrangements with the same actors and different bonds producing indistinguishable behaviour (which would falsify the isomer reading in `06_isomers_and_order.md`).

What would move the claim from `specified` to `grounded` is a ground reading, not agreement: a case or measurement in which a named binding, applied to a specific slot, is checked against whether the loser actually survived and whether the ruling moved. Fig 13 :138-139: *"you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself, and narrowing gets you close while the ground decides."* Agreement between this run and any other simulated seat is one evidence pool talking (Fig 2 :40), not a witness.

## 5. The honest stop

The derivation stops at the limit of what the figures can return. The figures narrow the shape of a credible arrangement, but they do not select among institutions, and no ground reading was taken. Fig 9 :60: *"Use filters for the budget and witnesses for the verdict. Never let a filter hand you a verdict."* A list of people chosen for being likely to agree is a filter, and it cannot hand a verdict.

The lineage is recorded so the stop is checkable: what was derived, what was imported, what was retracted, what would reverse it. The canonical limit is that the framework returns what a missing thing would have to look like, a shape and not a name (`FRAMEWORK/reference/FIGURES_README_canonical.md`). Unreached is reported as unreached, not as a silence. No part of this answer may be labelled `grounded` until one of the refuters in §4 has been run and has failed to fire.

## 6. One-paragraph version

> The Tether does not name an institution, and it does not claim a ground reading. It says that a contest over power is credible only if the body that certifies the result does not move when a contender pushes on it, the cost of losing is named and bounded before the contest, removal from office is writable as a refusal that sticks, the habitat of retaliation is enumerated two or three steps out, and the bonds between roles are priced rather than recorded as a list of ingredients. These are constraints in effect terms, a union compound, not a blueprint. Named mechanisms such as an independent judiciary or term limits are imports from political science, recorded with provenance, not derivations from the figures. What the answer offers is the shape any credible arrangement must have, five refuters written in advance, and the honest statement that nothing here is settled until a ground reading checks it. The verdict is `specified`, ungrounded, and a candidate.
