# Outreach draft: check one shape claim before you decide about the framework

**Use:** one-to-one, or as a cover note attached to a submission through the public venues in `03_public_contact_points.csv`. **Not** for bulk send. No personal addresses are used; the recipient must have an institutional or opt-in channel.

**Hard precondition:** do not send until the figure quotes in `08_expert_verification_packet.md` have been re-checked against `FRAMEWORK/reference/03-FIGURES_TEXT.md`. The claim must survive verification before the two-stage offer is made.

---

**Subject:** A checkable claim about competitive elections, and what would refute it

> Hi [name],
>
> I have one specific claim I would like you to check against what you already know. I am not asking you to agree with it, and I am not asking you to read any theory first. I am asking what you would count as a failure.
>
> **The residual, in effect terms:** a contest over power is supposed to produce an accepted loser. When losing may mean prison, exile, confiscation, or prosecution, the loser has no reason to accept the result, and the contest stops being a competition. It becomes a struggle over who controls the count. I am not treating "democracy" as the measure here; I am asking which specific slots carry the live signal.
>
> **The claim (`specified`, not `grounded`):** four constraints have to hold before competition is credible, whatever country or period is examined:
>
> 1. The body that certifies the result must not change its answer when a contender pushes on it. A count that moves with the incumbent is a mirror, not a ground.
> 2. The contenders must each be corrected by that same unmoved settlement, rather than negotiating with each other over what the count means.
> 3. The cost of losing must be bounded and nameable in advance. A stake that survives the outcome (office wealth, patronage, immunity from prosecution) is exactly what makes defeat unacceptable.
> 4. Removal from office must be writable and must stick. A system that can accumulate incumbents but cannot register "this tenure is over" has not closed the contest.
>
> This is a shape, not a blueprint. I am not claiming these constraints pick out one institution or one country, and I have not named a specific mechanism as the answer.
>
> **What I wrote down before sending this as a refuter:**
> 1. A documented case of credible alternation where the certifying body demonstrably moved under incumbent pressure and nothing else repaired the settlement.
> 2. A documented case of repeated alternation with severe personal risk of loss and no bound on that risk in any institution.
> 3. A derivation of a specific institution from the six-term framework alone, with no import from political science.
> 4. An improvement that does not change anyone's contact with the ground and still moves credibility.
>
> If any of these holds, the corresponding constraint is wrong and I will withdraw it. A one-line "this fails because..." is much more useful to me than agreement. If you would rather not engage, no reply is needed.
>
> **If the shape survives, and only then:** here are the sources. The figures, not my summary of them, are the authority.
> - The verbatim figure text with line numbers (local extract): `FRAMEWORK/reference/03-FIGURES_TEXT.md`
> - The canonical usage rule (local): `FRAMEWORK/reference/FIGURES_README_canonical.md`
> - Public figures repository: https://github.com/IsaiahN/tether/tree/general-agent/figures
>
> **If you want to use the machinery:** the formula is in the figures link. Run it on a problem you know well and can check against a ground. Write down what would refute your result before you run it, because the framework's own limit is that a loop cannot check the direction of its own question. A reported failure is more useful to me than a reported success.
>
> Thanks,
> [name]

## Contact rules

- One recipient at a time. No cc lists, no newsletters, no automation.
- Institutional or opt-in channels only, from `03_public_contact_points.csv`.
- An opt-out line in every message: "If you would rather not engage, no reply is needed."
- A reply is a conversation, never corroboration. Fig 2 :40: *"disqualifying for a verdict: agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool."*
- If a recipient sends a refutation, copy it with provenance into `11_audit.md` and re-evaluate it as if it came from any other source.
