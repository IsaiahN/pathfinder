## 0. The residual in effect terms

> How can institutions make political competition credible when losing power may carry severe personal or economic risks?

Stated as an effect, not a topic: a political order is supposed to convert disagreement into a contest with an accepted loser. When the cost of losing is unbounded, the loser has no reason to accept the outcome, and the contest does not close. It becomes a struggle over who controls the settlement itself. The gap is between a system that can register "this side lost" and a system that cannot write that sentence, or writes it and cannot make it stick.

- **What is being decided:** whether competition is a real mechanism (losers can lose and survive) or a label applied to a struggle that has not been settled.
- **Option A:** loss is safe because contenders are committed to accepting defeat. This relies on a frame state, and it fails exactly when severity rises.
- **Option B:** loss is safe because the settlement is pinned by something no contender can move, and the downside is bounded before the contest begins. This is the figure-derived shape.
- **What an answer would look like:** a set of constraints, stated in effect terms, that hold regardless of which country or period is examined, with named institutions recorded as imports rather than derivations. Bind this as an ordinary term before the run, per Fig 5 :65-66: *"make the sought-for shape an ORDINARY TERM. Write down what an answer would look like, bind it, and let it accrue a residual like anything else."*

The residual is indexed by slot, not aggregated. Fig 1 :19: *"The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated."* Fig 1 :22: *"Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average."* The live slots here are the contenders, the arbiter, the security services, the courts, the media, the economy, and the electorate. A single country score for "competitiveness" throws away the only signal that says where to look.

## 1. Domain census

### R by arity, symmetry, scale

- **Arity:** at least two contending frames plus one settlement that is not a negotiation between them. The question is not about one actor choosing well; it is about a relation among actors and a ground.
- **Symmetry:** winning and losing are ordered, not interchangeable. The ballot count is a comparison, but the consequence is a sequence: contest, then settlement, then transfer. The loss is a state change that must be writable as a refusal of the incumbent's tenure. Fig 12 :112: *"A plan is a sequence. A goal is a comparison. Progress is a subtraction. A refutation is a negation, and that one is not a bond."*
- **Scale:** first order at the level of a single contest and a single arbiter; second order at the level of a durable regime of repeated alternations, where a settled contest becomes an atom for the next one (Fig 12 :104).

### One line per domain

| domain | actors | currency | anchor (nameable?) |
|---|---|---|---|
| D1 contest | incumbent, challenger, parties | votes, office, patronage | **no, not yet.** Who won is exactly what is disputed. Not a domain until the settlement is separated out. |
| D2 settlement | electoral body, courts, legislature, certifying officers | rulings, counts, certifications | **yes:** a ruling that does not change when a contender pushes on it. |
| D3 habitat / contact field | military, bureaucracy, prosecutors, media, business, foreign governments, electorate | coercion, money, information, loyalty | **yes:** the actual field of contact, which updates but is not authored by the designer. |
| D4 verification / outside frame | monitors, journalists, researchers, opposition inside the loop | credible reports, residuals | **yes:** the ground, never agreement. In this simulated run all seats draw on one evidence pool, so independence is zero. |

No nameable anchor means it is not a domain yet. D1 fails the test on its own and must be coupled to D2. That coupling is a **compound with its own loop**, not a single domain, so it keeps its own verdict.

### Partition or union

This is a **union compound**, not a partition. Fig 9 :54-56: *"A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones. A single lever clears the region where only one condition holds and leaves the intersections standing, because the second condition still binds."* Credible competition requires an unmoved arbiter **and** a bounded cost of losing **and** a habitat that can register refusal. A single lever, for example term limits alone, clears the first region and leaves the intersections standing. Fig 9 :57-58: *"What remains is smaller and it is also categorically different: a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either. It stops being a mixture and becomes a specific compound."*

### Order by dependency

Fig 3 :20-21: *"The chain is ordered by dependency, not by importance. Each link consumes what the one above produces, so effort spent above the break arrives at a step that cannot use it."*

1. Bind the sought-for shape as an ordinary term (Fig 5 :65-66), including what loss would have to look like to be survivable.
2. Pin the settlement so an outcome can be registered at all (Fig 2 :33).
3. Make refusal writable and sticky (Fig 12 :112, Fig 5 :53).
4. Enumerate the habitat and introduce bounds (Fig 11 :43-48).
5. Expect repeated alternation only after the earlier links hold (Fig 7 :35-36).

Optimising link 5 cannot rescue a break at link 2. Fig 3 :19: *"Optimising a link that already works cannot rescue one that does not."* The middle link in this problem is link 3: having terms for what losing affords and an instrument for measuring it. Fig 3 :25-27: *"The link between them, having terms for what a situation offers and being able to say what would count as progress, has neither, so it attracts the least attention and the fewest instruments. A link with no instrument is not a link that works. It is a link nobody has looked at."*

## 3. Findings

Each finding is one loop in one domain. Verdicts are kept separate and never averaged. Mode is stated on every substantive claim.

### F1: The arbiter must not update (D2, decisive for settlement)

**Mode:** `general` structural, `specified` when applied to an electoral body or court.

Fig 2 :29-30: *"An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks. A measurement, an experiment, a counter, a market, a patient outcome. If it moves when you push on it, it is not an anchor."*

Fig 2 :33: *"The anchor must not update."*

Fig 2 :34: *"If it does, panel 3 turns into panel 1: the anchor becomes another frame, everything drifts together, and the drift is coherent, which is why nobody notices."*

Fig 2 :47: *"A ground listed as one of the parties has already become a frame. Which is panel 1, arrived at by a different route."*

If the body that certifies the result is controlled by the incumbent, it is a party listed as the ground. The count drifts toward whoever has the most influence, and because the drift is coherent nobody involved can see it. This is the structural reason that a contest over an arbiter the incumbent can move is not a competition.

**Refuter:** a documented case in which the certifying body demonstrably changed its ruling under pressure from the incumbent and credible alternation continued without any other constraint. If found, F1 is withdrawn.

**Triangulation:** `triangulated` across Fig 2 (anchor between frames) and the seat rules in `THE_SEAT_MAP_Field_Guide_amended.md` §THE GROUND MAINTAINER (a seat with no stake in which value the ground returns today), two different quantities with low overlap. Still one framework evidence pool.

### F2: Contenders must range against the ground separately, not negotiate with it (D1 plus D2)

**Mode:** `general`.

Fig 2 :45: *"Frames range against the ground. They do not negotiate with it."*

Fig 2 :46: *"The anchor is not a third party to the disagreement and it is not the tie-breaker in a vote. Each frame is corrected by it separately, and what makes the correction sound is that neither frame can move it."*

Fig 2 :35: *"Alignment is triangulation, not negotiation. A mirror cannot correct you."*

Fig 2 :40: *"disqualifying for a verdict: agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool"*

Fig 2 :42: *"Buy independence in proportion to the cost of being wrong."*

This is the seat-4 rule applied to elections. Monitors, courts, and media add nothing if they read the incumbent's own count. Their independence is the parameter, not a virtue. Where the cost of being wrong is severe, independence should be bought in proportion to that cost.

**Refuter:** a case in which two checking frames with high evidence overlap still changed the outcome, so their agreement was not the pool talking. That would contradict Fig 2 :40 and F2 falls.

**Triangulation:** `triangulated` across Fig 2 :40 and Fig 8 :47-48: *"Independence is one quantity read twice."* The first prices verdicts, the second prices surplus. Low overlap within the framework, still one pool.

### F3: The cost of losing must be bounded and nameable (D3, the middle link)

**Mode:** `specified`. The figure gives the shape of the requirement, not a threshold.

Fig 3 :25-26: *"The link between them, having terms for what a situation offers and being able to say what would count as progress, has neither, so it attracts the least attention and the fewest instruments."*

Fig 10 :59: *"an interest in the outcome is unavoidable, an interest in the reading is disqualifying."*

Fig 10 :61: *"And standing is a stake that survives the outcome it was earned on."*

Fig 10 :60: *"Where a stake cannot be removed, declare it. An undeclared interest is modelled anyway and an unnamed model runs unchallenged."*

The question itself names the defect: losing may carry severe personal or economic risks. That is precisely a standing stake, a position whose value survives the outcome it was earned on. Prison, exile, confiscation, or prosecution after defeat are not just bad payoffs; they are a stake that continues to operate after the contest ends. A vocabulary that cannot name that stake as a bound cannot say what would count as progress toward safety. This is Fig 3's middle link, and it has no instrument in the question as posed.

**Refuter:** a documented case of repeated alternation where losing carries severe personal risk and no bound on that risk exists in any institution. If found, F3 is withdrawn as a necessary condition.

**Triangulation:** `triangulated` across Fig 3 (dependency chain, middle link) and Fig 10 (seat stake and standing), different figures with different quantities. The world corroboration from the peaceful-transition and term-limit sources is secondary and shares no author with the figures, but it is one pool of political-science evidence, not a second figure.

### F4: Refusal must be writable and must stick (D2 plus D3)

**Mode:** `general` for the operator, `specified` for the transfer of office.

Fig 12 :112: *"A plan is a sequence. A goal is a comparison. Progress is a subtraction. A refutation is a negation, and that one is not a bond."*

Fig 5 :53: *"refuted: a term we hold is wrong; a term is owed that says so"*

Fig 5 :55: *"What it removes is not a term but a term's standing to be composed over."*

Fig 10 :82: *"Three panels, one move: arrange for refusal."*

A system holding only ingredients can say what a situation contains and cannot say that a claim is wrong. Applied here: a political order with no operator for removing an incumbent can accumulate incumbents and cannot register "this tenure is over." The operation needed is negation, not subtraction (removing a person from an arrangement is subtraction; refusing the claim that they still hold office is negation). Operators table :58: *"a refutation is a negation, which is why a vocabulary without it can only accumulate."*

**Refuter:** exhibit a political order that has no operator for removing an incumbent, or whose refusal does not survive the next contest, and still shows credible alternation. That would break the necessity claim.

**Triangulation:** `single-source` for the operator definition (Fig 12 and the Operators table are the same definition), `triangulated` for "arrange for refusal" across Fig 10 :82 and Fig 5 :53-55, two different figures.

### F5: The designer introduces but cannot subtract; the work is permanent (D3)

**Mode:** `general` structural, `specified` in application.

Fig 11 :39: *"capability is a property of agent-and-habitat, never of the agent alone."*

Fig 11 :40: *"An improvement that does not change contact changes nothing, however much it improves."*

Fig 11 :61-62: *"The seat's power is asymmetric. Introduction is available; exclusion is not. You can add an actor and you cannot subtract the habitat, because contact is a fact about the world and does not consult you."*

Fig 11 :64: *"Which is why a managed ecosystem is permanent work rather than a solved state."*

Fig 11 :52-53: *"And contact cascades. Scoping the habitat to direct contact is how DDT reached the eagles: first-order enumeration, second-order effect, and the residual that mattered was three steps out."*

Credibility is a property of the arrangement of contenders and habitat, not of the good character of one contender. A designer can introduce an electoral commission, a court, a term limit, or a free press. It cannot remove the security services, the patronage networks, or the electorate from contact. The relevant retaliation risk is often two or three steps out: not the candidate but the prosecutor, the judge, the party financier. Scoping to the two candidates is first-order enumeration and misses where the residual lives.

**Refuter:** a documented one-time institutional change that permanently bounded retaliation without continued maintenance, or a case where a designer genuinely excluded an actor from the habitat. Either contradicts Fig 11 :61-64.

**Triangulation:** `triangulated` across Fig 11 (habitat asymmetry) and Fig 1 :19-22 (per-slot residual), different figures and quantities.

### F6: The arrangement is the substance, not the ingredient list (D2 plus D3)

**Mode:** `general`.

Fig 12 :5: *"A set of ingredients is not one. The bond is the rest of the information, and it is the half nothing was recording."*

Fig 12 :117: *"isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."*

Fig 12 :104: *"A settled molecule becomes an atom for whatever composes over it. So irreducible is a fact about a level and never about the world."*

Two contenders, a court, and a law are the same atoms under several bonds. Under comparison (`⋛`) they make an election. Under production (`⇒`) they make an incumbent manufacturing a result. Under negation (`¬`) they make a removal from office. The atoms are identical; only the bond differs, and the behaviour differs decisively. Likewise, a settled contest becomes an atom for the next one, which is why alternation can become a habit rather than a fresh contest every time.

**Refuter:** a case in which two arrangements with the same actors and different bonds produce indistinguishable behaviour. That would falsify the isomer claim at this level.

**Triangulation:** `single-source` for the isomer definition (Fig 12), `triangulated` for "arrangement carries information" across Fig 12 :5 and Fig 4 :19-20: *"The kernel is the part that does not change with size: the same loop and the same rule for naming what worked, whether one agent is running it or a thousand. Only the actors, the currency and the anchor change."*

### F7: The framework returns a shape, not a name (direction limit)

**Mode:** `general`.

Fig 9 :32: *"you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself"*

Fig 9 :27-28: *"describe it before you go looking, or any frame you pick will seem to fit"*

Fig 8 :39: *"So a search that finds nothing is never a proof of absence."*

The figures cannot derive "term limits" or "an independent judiciary" as the answer. Those are bindings imported from political science. If a search is framed first by the institution and then by the residual, any institution will seem to fit, which is the projection error named in *The Hard Problem is About Source*: an instrument that reads a value cannot comment on its source. What can be returned is the shape: an unmoved settlement, a bounded loss, a writable refusal, an enumerated habitat, and an arrangement whose bonds are priced.

**Refuter:** a derivation that produces a specific institution from the six-term set alone, with no import from a political-science frame. If produced, F7 is withdrawn.

**Triangulation:** `triangulated` across Fig 9 :32 and the canonical figures README (*"what a missing thing would have to look like (a shape, not a name)"*), but the README shares author and evidence pool with the figures, so this is internal consistency, not independent corroboration.

### F8: Alternation is a chain of handoffs, not a one-time fix (temporal domain)

**Mode:** `specified`.

Fig 7 :35: *"A room's agreement is variation, not a verdict. Selection between rooms is where the ground votes."*

Fig 7 :36: *"A chain of mirrors is one long room. The next room must differ from the last, and match the residual it surfaced."*

Fig 7 :12-13: *"same closure, handed along; agreement compounds, evidence does not"*

A credible competition is not a single clean election. It is a sequence of handoffs in which each new contest is selected against the residual the last one surfaced. If the next contest resembles the last one, the chain has stopped travelling. The political-science source on peaceful transitions reports that once a country has an initial alternation it is very likely to keep doing so, a habit-forming property, and the figure rule explains why: each settled contest becomes an atom for the next one (Fig 12 :104).

**Refuter:** a case where alternation repeated identically for many cycles with no selection against the previous residual, and credibility persisted. That would contradict Fig 7 :36.

**Triangulation:** `triangulated` across Fig 7 (room chain) and Fig 13 :90 *"One chain is one route through a space that has no last route."* Different figures; the world corroboration is secondary.

## 4. Pre-registered refuter

Written before the conclusion, per `THE_SEAT_MAP_Field_Guide_amended.md`: *"And a falsifier written before the run is a design instrument, not only an honesty device."* The shape claim is refuted if any of the following holds:

1. A documented case shows credible alternation with a certifying body whose ruling moves when the incumbent pushes on it, and no other constraint repairs the settlement.
2. A documented case shows repeated alternation with severe personal risk of loss and no bound on that risk in any institution.
3. A derivation produces a specific institution from the six-term set alone, without an import from political science.
4. An improvement that does not change contact (Fig 11 :40) moves the credibility outcome.
5. Two arrangements with the same actors and different bonds produce indistinguishable behaviour, falsifying F6.

If any fires, the affected finding is withdrawn and the summary answer is retracted. Until one is run, no part of this may be labelled `grounded`.

## 5. Recommendation

1. State the shape, not a blueprint: an unmoved settlement, a bounded loss, a writable refusal, an enumerated habitat, and priced bonds.
2. Buy independence in proportion to the cost of being wrong (Fig 2 :42). Where loss is severe, checking frames must not share the incumbent's evidence pool.
3. Bind the cost of losing as an ordinary term before any institutional design is proposed (Fig 5 :65-66). Write down what a survivable loss would look like and let it accrue a residual.
4. Enumerate contact two steps out, including courts, prosecutors, security services, and financiers (Fig 11 :52-53).
5. Keep verdicts per domain and per slot. Do not average them into one credibility score (Fig 1 :22).
6. Record named institutions as imports with a source gate and a shadow test. Convergent derivation and adopted import look identical in the contents; only the record separates them (Fig 8 :50-52).
7. State mode on every sentence and offer the refuter before the theory.
