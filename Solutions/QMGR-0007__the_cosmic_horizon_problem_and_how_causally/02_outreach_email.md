# Outreach draft: check the shape before you decide about the mechanism

**Use:** one-to-one, or as a cover letter attached to a submission through the public venues in `03_public_contact_points.csv`. **Not** for bulk send.

**Hard precondition:** do not send until the shape claim and its refuter in `01_figure_grounded_audit.md` sections 4 and 5 are stable. The output must survive validation or the two-stage offer collapses on contact.

---

**Subject:** One checkable shape for the horizon problem, before we argue about inflation

> Hi [name],
>
> I have one specific output I would like you to check against what you already know. I am not asking you to read a theory first, and I am not asking you to agree with me. If the output does not survive, there is nothing else to look at.
>
> **The residual (`specified`, not a verdict about any model).** The CMB is near 2.725 K and uniform to about one part in 100,000. In a hot Big Bang without an early accelerated epoch, sky patches more than about 2 degrees apart were outside one another's particle horizons at last scattering, so no signal could have equalized their temperatures. The prediction "no contact, no shared temperature" fails against the reading "shared temperature." That gap, not the name of any theory, is the thing I would like checked.
>
> **The output: a shape, not a name.** The framework I am using returns only a constraint any acceptable answer must satisfy. Here is the shape: now-disconnected patches were earlier in causal contact and near thermal equilibrium; a structure mediated between the connected beginning and the smooth state; expansion then stretched the equilibrated patch beyond the horizon, so the equalized regions now appear disconnected. Anything that explains the uniformity without changing causal contact is excluded by the shape. The framework does not return the word "inflation." Calling this inflation is a binding from the physics literature onto the shape, offered with its provenance, not a derivation.
>
> **What I am not claiming:** that the framework proves inflation is correct, that it selects a model, that it returns a spectral index or a number of e-folds, or that it closes the question of initial conditions. Those are world facts established by instruments, not outputs of the method. The current measurements (near scale invariance, adiabatic perturbations, r below about 0.06) are consistent with the binding but do not by themselves discriminate it from varying-c, ekpyrotic/cyclic or big-bounce alternatives. I am also not asking you to trust or endorse the framework.
>
> **What would refute the shape:** I wrote these down before sending this.
> 1. A measurement showing that patches separated by more than 2 degrees at last scattering had large, causally structured temperature differences, so the uniformity residual is not a fact.
> 2. A mechanism demonstrated to produce the uniformity while leaving causal contact unchanged.
> 3. A derivation showing the standard non-inflationary Big Bang closure reaches uniformity from its own atoms.
> 4. A discriminator that excludes every prior-contact-plus-stretching mechanism and settles a non-contact one.
>
> If any holds, I will retract the shape. A one-line "this fails because..." is more useful to me than agreement. If you would rather not engage, no reply is needed.
>
> **If it survives, and only then:** the sources are below. The figures are the authority, not my summary of them.
> - Verbatim figure text with line numbers (local extract): `FRAMEWORK/reference/03-FIGURES_TEXT.md`
> - Canonical figures README: https://github.com/IsaiahN/tether/blob/general-agent/figures/README.md
> - The eight-step formula and mode rule (local): `FRAMEWORK/figures/THE_FORMULA.md`
>
> **If you want to use the machinery:** the formula is in the figures link. Run it on a problem you know well and can check against a ground. Write down what would refute your result *before* you run it, and state the mode of what you get. The framework's own rule is that a loop cannot check the direction of its own question, so an output can be contrived to fit. A reported failure is more useful to me than a reported success.
>
> Thanks for reading.
> [name]
