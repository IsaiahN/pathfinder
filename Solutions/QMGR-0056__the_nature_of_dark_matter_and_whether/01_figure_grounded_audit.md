## 0. The claim under audit

The source question (QM & GR Questions.txt line 72) offers three candidates:

> The nature of dark matter and whether it is a particle, a modification of gravity, or something else

Stated as an effect: Γ (GR plus the Standard Model plus the observed baryon inventory) predicts a gravitational signature from visible matter; the sky returns a different signature; R is the per-slot mismatch. The three candidates are three different *kinds of move* against that residual:

- **particle:** add an atom to Γ (an unseen source term). Currency: cross-section and relic abundance. Ground: detector or collider.
- **modified gravity:** change the law (the bond between mass and curvature). Currency: acceleration law. Ground: a force-law discriminator.
- **something else:** import a primitive from a frame with a different closure. Currency: a shadow test. Ground: a frame whose closure predicts R's shape.

The audit asks which of these moves the figures license, which they refuse, and which they leave as a gap. It does not ask which candidate is true; that is a ground question this simulation cannot answer.

## 1. Domain census (before choosing any figure line)

### 1.1 R by arity, symmetry and scale

- **Arity.** At least three overlapping conditions, not one binary: (i) a missing source in the energy budget; (ii) a law deviation at low acceleration or large scale; (iii) a frame not yet held (emergent gravity, primordial black holes, a modified-inertia scheme). Fig 9 :4-5: *"are not one hard question but several well-formed ones. Split it rather than search."*
- **Symmetry.** The three moves are not symmetric alternatives inside one closure. Adding an atom (particle) keeps the bond set fixed and changes the inventory; changing the law keeps the inventory and changes the bond; importing a primitive changes the closure itself (Fig 6 :14: *"the only operator that moves the wall"*). They cannot be ranked on one scale because they are different operators.
- **Scale.** Galaxy (kpc rotation curves), cluster (Mpc lensing and gas), horizon (CMB acoustic peaks and light-element abundances). A lever that clears one scale leaves the intersections at the others standing (Fig 9 :55-56: *"A single lever clears the region where only one condition holds and leaves the intersections standing, because the second condition still binds."*).

## 3. Findings

### F1 - The three options are a union of overlapping conditions, not a partition (decisive for the question's form)

Fig 9 :4-5:

> **"are not one hard question but several well-formed ones. Split it rather than search."**

Fig 9 :54-56:

> **"And when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again."**
> **"A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones."**

The menu "particle, modification of gravity, or something else" is a union. A modified-gravity model can absorb the cluster discriminator that particle advocates call decisive (Moffat 2007: *"Excellent fits to the 2D convergence κ-map data are obtained without non-baryonic dark matter"*). A primordial-black-hole candidate is a particle in the inventory but an object not in the Standard Model. "Something else" is not a third disjoint box; it is the label for the remainder after the two named closures are unbundled.

**Refuter:** show that the three conditions are mutually exclusive and jointly exhaustive over every slot, so that one lever clears the whole residual. If that were demonstrated, the union reading would fail and a single verdict would be legitimate. **Status: `triangulated`** (Fig 9 split rule plus Fig 1 per-slot rule, low overlap).

### F2 - The anchor is gravitational measurement, and it is legitimate

Fig 2 :29:

> **"An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks."**

Fig 2 :36:

> **"An anchor is legitimate when the question is constitutively about it."**

Fig 2 :45:

> **"Frames range against the ground. They do not negotiate with it."**

The question is constitutively about gravity, so the sky is a legitimate anchor. Two readings of it are load-bearing here. The Bullet Cluster lensing map puts the mass peak with the galaxies, not with the X-ray gas that carries most of the baryons (Clowe et al. 2006: *"the gravitational potential does not trace the plasma distribution, the dominant baryonic mass component, but rather approximately traces the distribution of galaxies"*). Planck fixes the cold-dark-matter density at Ω_c h² = 0.120 ± 0.001 with the acoustic scale measured to 0.03 percent (Planck Collaboration 2018/2020). Both readings are independent of which theory a frame prefers.

But the anchor is not a single number. Fig 2 :46: *"Each frame is corrected by it separately, and what makes the correction sound is that neither frame can move it."* The particle frame and the modified-gravity frame must each range against the same lensing and CMB maps separately. A fit inside one frame is frame-internal: Fig 1 :27 *"They are frame-internal. A frame cannot score itself with a quantity it also produces."*

**Refuter:** a measurement that changes its reported value with the interpreter's preferred theory, or a demonstration that the lensing map is not a reading of mass distribution at all. **Status: `triangulated`** (Fig 2 anchor rule plus Fig 1 frame-internal rule; world sources corroborate the ground exists but are secondary).

### F3 - The framework returns a shape, not a name; the three-way choice is a gap

Fig 9 :32:

> **"you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself"**

Fig 13 :134-135:

> **"Unreachable is a verdict here, not a silence,"**
> **"It cannot say which primitive is missing, because naming it would mean already holding it."**

The question asks for a name: particle, law, or something else. The figures license a shape: *whatever closes R must reproduce the measured gravitational potential at every slot where the baryonic inventory is insufficient*. They do not select the atom, the bond change, or the imported primitive. Naming one before the discriminator exists is the inversion Fig 9 :27-28 forbids: *"describe it before you go looking, or any frame you pick will seem to fit."* Fig 8 :53: *"Residual first, frame second."*

This is a declared `gap`, of the shape-not-a-name kind, not unreachable in principle. It is a verdict and not a silence.

**Refuter:** a figure re-census showing that the six-term set plus its bonds determines which of the three moves closes R, or a ground measurement that settles the name directly. **Status: `triangulated`** (Fig 9 plus Fig 13 plus the canonical figures README, which the exemplar logs as the same limit; low overlap between the leave/arrive figure and the minimum-set figure).

### F4 - The specific held term with a flat residual is owed a refusal, before a new mint

Fig 5 :53:

> **"refuted: a term we hold is wrong; a term is owed that says so"**

Fig 5 :59-60:

> **"a term whose residual PERSISTS AND DOES NOT TREND DOWN is the one to point the refusal at, which is the same shrinking test the selector already runs, read with the opposite sign."**

Operators table :58:

> **"a refutation is a negation, which is why a vocabulary without it can only accumulate"**

The WIMP is a held term that has accrued a prediction and returned null across increasing exposure. LZ reports: *"we find no evidence for an excess over expected backgrounds"* with 4.2 tonne-years (LZ Collaboration 2024/2025); XENONnT reports *"no significant excess above background"* with 3.1 tonne-years (XENONnT Collaboration 2025). Its residual persists and does not trend down. By the framework's own ranking rule, the term to point a refusal at is the one that keeps being wrong, not a fresh unseen particle minted to cover the same slot.

Two limits on this finding, kept visible: (1) refusing the WIMP is not refusing the particle *shape*; axions, sterile neutrinos and primordial black holes remain candidates in the same shape (Fig 9 :32); (2) a null result is not a proof of absence (F5). What the figures license is a **method verdict**, not a physics verdict: refuse first, then consider a mint.

**Refuter:** a positive recoil with the right annual modulation and a background-rejection that survives an independent analysis would make the WIMP term pay at the ground and would refute this finding. **Status: `single-source`** on the figure mechanism (Fig 5 plus Operators), with the world null result as corroboration of the flat residual, not of the method.

### F5 - A null result is a budget filter, never a witness for absence

Fig 8 :39:

> **"So a search that finds nothing is never a proof of absence."**

Fig 8 :40-43, on underivable versus unreachable at budget, issues a different instruction for each. LZ's exclusion of spin-independent cross-sections down to 2.1 × 10^-48 cm² at 36 GeV/c² says: *in this region of parameter space, at this exposure, nothing arrived.* It does not say *no particle exists*. Under Fig 6 :49-50, that is the SEARCHED question (*"is it in here and unfound?"*) or the budget question, not the OUTWARD question. More exposure at the same reach is more search, not a new atom: Fig 8 :32 *"Closure is idempotent. Composing inside a closed set never leaves it, so no search, however long, adds an atom."*

Fig 9 :60:

> **"Use filters for the budget and witnesses for the verdict. Never let a filter hand you a verdict."**

A cross-section limit is a filter for the budget. Treating it as a verdict for "not a particle" is the named error.

**Refuter:** a derivation from the figure set that an exhausted detector parameter space entails the absence of any unseen source term. No such derivation is held. **Status: `triangulated`** (Fig 8 plus Fig 9 plus Fig 6, three distinct figures).

### F6 - The owed object is a discriminator, not a better fit on the same axis

Fig 6 :29:

> **"inward: is our representation adequate to hold it? The instrument extends access. Fails by refining forever on an axis that cannot carry it. Sharpen in our own terms."**

Fig 6 :32:

> **"An instrument is not built from a description. It is improved from a worse instrument already returning something."**

Fig 3 :27:

> **"A link with no instrument is not a link that works. It is a link nobody has looked at."**

MOND fits galaxy rotation curves with one free parameter per galaxy and the same a₀ (Famaey and McGaugh 2012: *"As a mathematical description of the effective force law, MOND works remarkably well in individual galaxies."*). ΛCDM fits the CMB and large-scale structure and requires a non-baryonic component (Planck). Each framework scores well on the slots it is built for. What is missing is not another fit but an instrument that separates the two readings where they disagree, for example the behaviour of lensing mass where baryons and collisionless components separate. Fig 6 :34: *"The question is not whether a sensor could exist. It is whether anything, at any resolution, is already returning something that fails to resolve."* The cluster-lensing maps and the underground detectors are two such proto-instruments; neither yet closes both readings at once.

This is reported as **unreached**, not unreachable in principle. Fig 8 :39 applies.

**Refuter:** an existing instrument that already distinguishes missing-source from changed-law across all slots, if its reading were shown to be decisive and were ignored here. **Status: `single-source`** on which concrete instrument would do it (that is the gap); `triangulated` on the structural claim that a discriminator is owed (Fig 6, Fig 3, Fig 10 :52).

### F7 - The two frames largely share an evidence pool, so their agreement is not corroboration

Fig 2 :40:

> **"agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool"**

Fig 8 :50-52:

> **"Convergent derivation and adopted import look identical in the contents. Only the record of where each came from separates them"**

Particle and modified-gravity advocates both read the same rotation curves, the same lensing maps and the same CMB power spectrum. When a fit in one camp and a fit in the other reproduce the same curve, that is a projection with lossy information, not two witnesses. The discriminator must use readings with genuinely different anchors: the underground detector (D2 ground) against the sky (D1 ground) against a force-law test (D3 ground). Fig 2 :42: *"Buy independence in proportion to the cost of being wrong."*

The philosophical literature provides a low-overlap corroboration of the *shape* of this failure. The Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy records that when Uranus misbehaved, postulating Neptune saved the theory, but the same move with Vulcan for Mercury failed until general relativity arrived: *"the very same strategy failed when used to try to explain the advance of the perihelion in Mercury's orbit"* (SEP, Underdetermination of Scientific Theory, 2009/2023). Adding an unseen object and changing the law were both live moves against one residual; only a discriminator decided which. That is D1/D2/D3 in a different field.

**Refuter:** a demonstration that the detector, lensing and CMB readings are statistically independent and already point the same way at a single name. **Status: `triangulated`** on the figure side (Fig 2 plus Fig 8), with low-overlap world corroboration from philosophy of science (expected weight modest because the figure verdict does not depend on it).

### F8 - "Something else" is a request for an import with gates, not a catch-all list

Fig 6 :23:

> **"No frame escapes its own closure. The bound moves only on contact with a frame whose closure differs."**

Fig 8 :50-52 (quoted in F7) requires a provenance record and a shadow test. Emergent-gravity and primordial-black-hole proposals are imports from frames whose closures differ from the standard particle closure; they are legitimate only when they explain a gap the held atoms cannot (shadow test) and carry a record of where the primitive came from (source gate). Naming one as *the* answer without those gates is the apophenia inversion of Fig 9 :27-28.

The live literature shows such frames exist. A 2026 JCAP paper treats primordial black holes as a dark-matter component and computes their mass function (*"the PBH mass distribution spans several orders of magnitude"* for stochastic Inflation). Its existence is not evidence for that candidate; it is evidence that the "something else" slot is a real closure with its own instruments, which is exactly what the framework predicts should happen when a frame searches for a missing primitive.

**Refuter:** a figure line that enumerates the admissible "something else" frames and selects one. None is held. **Status: `single-source`** on the figure gate; world source used only to show the slot is occupied.

### F9 - Verdicts stay per slot and are never averaged into one winner

Fig 1 :19:

> **"The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated."**

Fig 1 :22:

> **"Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average."**

A single global verdict such as "dark matter is a particle" flattens four different slots. Galaxy rotation is well fitted by a modified law; cluster lensing is often read as requiring collisionless mass; the CMB acoustic peaks require an extra non-baryonic density at recombination; light-element abundances constrain the baryonic inventory independently (Fig 9 :55: *"A single lever clears the region where only one condition holds and leaves the intersections standing"*). No one lever clears all four. Verdicts are kept separate:

| slot | best-supported reading *at this budget* | mode | triangulated / single-source |
|---|---|---|---|
| galaxy rotation curves | modified-law fits are tight with one parameter; particle halos also fit | `specified` | single-source per frame, not averaged |
| cluster lensing and gas separation | mass peak offset from baryonic gas | `specified` | triangulated across independent instruments, but the interpretation is contested (Moffat 2007) |
| CMB acoustic peaks | extra non-baryonic density required in ΛCDM | `specified` | single-source for the ΛCDM fit; a different fit is not excluded |
| light-element abundances | baryon inventory fixed independently of the particle question | `specified` | triangulated with CMB baryon density |

**Refuter:** a single model that closes every slot with one parameter and no residual anywhere, confirmed by a discriminator. **Status: `triangulated`** (Fig 1 plus Fig 9).

### F10 - Dark matter is not a seventh primitive; the substrate strain dissolves on re-census

A first pass read "dark matter" as a candidate seventh member of the minimum set, which would break Fig 13 :116 (*"Six members, and no amount of arranging produces a seventh"*) if it were forced in. That was a strain and it was sent back to census.

Re-census: the substrate already holds **energy**, a state, described as *"conserved, finite, directionless alone"* (Fig 13 :13-14). Dark matter is a form of mass-energy whose *source* is unregistered, not a new kind of thing. A particle candidate is a new atom in the library Γ (an import), not a new substrate member; a modified law is a change of bond; an emergent scheme is a composition over the six. The exemplar's binding of gravity to the Gradient is an **unproven binding**, not a derivation, and this solution does not repeat it as a fact. Fig 13 :74: *"Everything else is a composition across the two kinds, never within one."*

**Refuter:** a derivation of a seventh member from outside the six, or a demonstration that mass-energy is not among the six. **Status: `triangulated`** (Fig 13 plus the canonical metalaws note, plus the exemplar's own correction, which is a framework-internal record and not independent).

## 4. Pre-registered refuter

Required before any outreach, per Seat Map :287: *"a falsifier written before the run is a design instrument, not only an honesty device."* Written here before the verdict in `07` and `09`.

The shape claim (union, shape-not-a-name, discriminator owed) is refuted if any of the following holds:

2. **Single-law closure of the modified-gravity reading.** One modified law that fits rotation curves, cluster lensing, CMB peaks and light-element abundances simultaneously with no unseen source term and no fitted parameter that shifts between slots.

6. **Non-updating anchor fails.** A demonstration that the lensing or CMB reading changes with the interpreter's preferred theory, which would refute F2.

If any fires, the shape claim is withdrawn or narrowed and the change is logged here rather than silently applied. Until one is run, no part of this may be labelled `grounded`.
