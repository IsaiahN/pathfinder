**Subject:** Check one output about inflation and firm pricing before you decide whether the method is worth reading

> Hi [name],
>
> I have one specific output I would like you to check against what you already know about firm pricing and inflation. I am not asking you to read the framework first, and I am not asking you to agree with it. If the output does not survive, there is nothing else to look at.
>
> **The output (`specified`, not `grounded`):** the question "how much of inflation depends on market power and pricing strategies" asks for a magnitude, but the derivation returns a shape and a refusal. Inflation's dependence on firms is a union of four per-slot conditions, not a percentage of a pooled index: a cost-and-expectation shock reaching a firm's pricing rule; the firm's position governing how much of that shock is passed on, sheltered or amplified; a changing cast of firms whose markups differ; and a measurement channel that pools the result into an index. Verdicts for these four conditions must be kept separate and never averaged.
>
> Three things follow that are checkable without my theory:
>
> 1. A firm-level inflation residual must stay indexed by firm and by time. An economy-wide average throws away the signal that says which slot is failing.
> 2. A markup is a reading, not a source. The same markup reading can come from a barrier to entry, overhead recovery, an expectation of future cost, or a composition shift. The reading does not select among them.
> 3. The effect of a high markup on pass-through is not a standing coefficient. In the BIS working paper on oil shocks, high markups cushion disinflationary shocks and barely affect inflationary ones. The sign flips with the residual.
>
> **What I am not claiming:** that market power explains a particular percentage of recent inflation; that a rising markup series proves rising monopoly power; that the price index is the ground rather than a pooled instrument pointed at it; or that this derives a number. The method returns a kind of thing, never the answer itself. A variance share is a filter for where to look, not a witness for a cause.
>
> **What would refute the shape claim:**
> 1. an aggregate share that predicts, prospectively and per named firm-slot, which slot's price residual will move, across a change in index composition;
> 2. an instrument that discriminates markup-from-barrier from markup-from-expected-cost at the slot, with readings stable under a change of cost classification;
> 3. a markup/pass-through effect whose sign and magnitude are invariant across inflationary and disinflationary shocks of comparable size;
> 4. a single intervention that restores all four conditions and leaves no categorically different residue.
>
> I wrote these down before sending this. If any one holds, I will retract the shape claim. A one-line "this fails because..." is more useful to me than agreement. If you'd rather not engage, no reply is needed.
>
> **If it survives, and only then:** the sources are below. The figure text, not my summary of it, is the authority.
> - Verbatim figure text with line numbers (local framework file): `FRAMEWORK/reference/03-FIGURES_TEXT.md`
> - Canonical usage rule (local framework file): `FRAMEWORK/reference/FIGURES_README_canonical.md`
> - Eight-step loop and mode rule (local framework file): `FRAMEWORK/figures/THE_FORMULA.md`
>
> **If you want to use the machinery:** the formula is in the figures link. Run it on a pricing problem you know well and can check against a ground. Write down what would refute your result *before* you run it, and state the mode of what you get. The framework's own rule is that a loop cannot check the direction of its own question, and its provenance essay warns that outputs can be *"contrived to fit the formula"*. A reported failure is more useful to me than a reported success.
>
> - [name]

## Two-stage offer, stated plainly

**Stage 1.** Send the shape claim and the four refuters. Ask what the recipient would count as a failure. Record their verdict before showing any framework material.

**Stage 2, opt-in only.** If they are curious, offer the three local figure files, with the note that the figure text and not the summary is the authority. Their later agreement is never pooled as corroboration (Fig 2 :40, *"agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool"*).

**Stage 3.** Offer the formula as a method to reuse. Each user writes their own refuter, states their own mode and names their own ground.

**Why the order matters:** stage 1 before stage 2 is Fig 10 :52 and Fig 2 :45; stage 2 is opt-in because a recipient who has absorbed the framework before being asked for a verdict has joined the frame being checked (Fig 10 :42, *"A verifier that can reconstruct the claim cannot verify it."*); stage 3 sends a generator, which does not give away an answer (Fig 4 :22).
