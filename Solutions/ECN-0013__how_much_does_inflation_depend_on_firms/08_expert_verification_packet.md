**Mode: `specified` on substantive claims, `general` on the structural shape. Not `grounded`.** No new measurement is claimed. This is a shape and a route, not a result.

## 1. The claim, in one paragraph

Inflation's dependence on firms' market power and pricing strategies is not a single percentage. It is a union of four per-slot conditions: a cost or expectation shock reaching a pricing rule, the firm's position governing pass-through or shelter, a changing cast of firms, and a measurement channel that pools the result into an index. The residual must stay indexed by firm and by time. A markup is a reading that cannot comment on its own source, and its effect changes sign with the residual, so there is no standing coefficient. A variance share is a budget filter, not a witness. The universal percentage is a `gap`: the instrument that would close it, a source-discriminating firm-level design, is characterized but not held here.

## 2. Three checkable sub-claims

### C1: framework-internal and methodological; checkable by reading Figure 1 and Figure 9

- **Statement:** the per-slot residual must not be aggregated into a single attribution without loss; the four conditions form a union with an unassignable shared fraction, not a partition of shares.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 1 :19: *"The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated."* Fig 1 :22: *"Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average."* Fig 9 :57: *"What remains is smaller and it is also categorically different: a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either."* Fig 9 :60: *"Use filters for the budget and witnesses for the verdict. Never let a filter hand you a verdict."*
- **World check:** Hasenzagl and Perez (Richmond Fed, 2026) report that *"A percentile marks a position rather than a firm, and the firm in the middle of the distribution in 2020 is not the firm that was there in 1960."* Does that composition fact change how one should read a pooled attribution?
- **Refuter:** an aggregate share that predicts, prospectively and per named firm-slot, which slot's price residual will move across a change in index composition; or a single intervention that restores all four conditions and leaves no categorically different residue.

### C2: economics-facing; checkable against the pass-through literature

- **Statement:** the effect of market power on pass-through is not a standing coefficient. Its sign depends on the residual, so a high markup is a cushion in one regime and neutral or amplifying in another.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 11 :49: *"An actor's valence is relative to the residual, not a property it carries."* Fig 11 :50: *"The same actor is a benefactor to one residual and a malfactor to another, so mutualism and parasitism are relations rather than kinds."*
- **What to check:** Kharroubi, Spigt, Igan, Takahashi and Zakrajsek (BIS Working Paper 1150, 2023) report that *"high markups mainly reduce the pass-through of disinflationary oil shocks, while they barely affect that of inflationary oil shocks."* Is that asymmetry robust to a different cost-shock family or a different sample? The claim is `specified`, not a general law.
- **Refuter:** a firm-level estimate whose markup/pass-through sign and magnitude are invariant across inflationary and disinflationary shocks of comparable size. If found, C2 is false.

### C3: framework-internal derivation with a world-side demonstration

- **Statement:** a markup is a reading, not a source, and the firm-to-index transform is lossy, so a headline attribution must report a round-trip gap rather than a share.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 9 :32: *"you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself."* Fig 4 :30: *"So take the round trip and measure the gap. Without it you are navigating by dead reckoning: confident about a position nobody checked."* Fig 10 :25: *"stale percepts, dropped transients, saturated metrics, pooled readings"*
- **What to check:** Glover, Mustre-del-Río and von Ende-Becker (Kansas City Fed, 2023) write that *"markups could account for more than half of 2021 inflation. However, the timing and cross-industry patterns of markup growth are more consistent with firms raising prices in anticipation of future cost increases, rather than an increase in monopoly power or higher demand."* Benkard, Miller and Yurukoglu (NBER WP 34513, 2025) find the original markup result *"sensitive to unreported sample restrictions that drop 27% of the available observations."* The Richmond Fed brief locates the disagreement in *"how firm-level markups are combined into an economy-wide average and how costs are classified, which are measurement choices."* Is a source-discriminating instrument available that would separate these readings?
- **Refuter:** an instrument that discriminates markup-from-barrier from markup-from-expected-cost at the slot, with readings stable under a change of cost classification; or an invertible firm-to-index transform for the quantities in question.

## 5. What would settle it

A source-discriminating instrument, not a further argument. From *The Hard Problem is About Source*: an instrument that reads green *"cannot comment on the source"*; the spectrometer resolved it, and *"The answer is unreached until a better instrument exists."*

1. Does a markup reading come from a barrier, overhead recovery, expected future cost, or a composition shift, with readings stable under a change of cost classification?
2. Does the markup/pass-through relation flip with the sign of the cost shock at comparable magnitude?
3. How much of an aggregate markup change is within-firm versus a change of cast, across a period with entry and exit?
4. Is the firm-to-index transform invertible for the quantities in question? If not, report `R_T` rather than a share.

## 6. What an expert can check in twenty minutes

1. Read Fig 1 :19 and :22, Fig 9 :57 and :60, Fig 10 :25, Fig 11 :49-50, and Fig 4 :30: eight short passages.
2. Check C2 against their own work: is the BIS asymmetry a sign flip or a measurement artefact in their view?
3. Name one design that would discriminate a barrier from expected future cost at the firm level, or say why none is possible.
4. Send the refutation. A failure is more useful than an endorsement.
