## 1. A shape is a constraint, not a filler

The figures README states the limit: the framework returns *"what a missing thing would have to look like (a shape, not a name)."* Fig 9 :32: *"you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself."* Fig 13 :134-135: the system *"can report that it lacks something rather than producing an answer anyway"*, and *"It cannot say which primitive is missing, because naming it would mean already holding it."*

From *The Library of Babel*: *"you can cut your search short if the set of books returned is small and descriptive enough that it provides a general shape of the answer that satisfies your question."*

So a **shape** is the narrowed form of an answer you can state before you can name it. It constrains; it does not fill. For this question:

- `a per-slot warning structure with separable perception and composition conditions` is a shape.
- `systemic risk = 0.73 next quarter` is a name, and not a licensed one.

## 2. How a shape is obtained

- *Library of Babel*: use the vocabulary you already have that is *closest* to the thing; *"With each new term gained, you narrow the space and deepen your vocabulary."* Steer by the question as tether: *"The books that you are returning in your searches need to be tested or ranged against the problem you want answered in order to steer the direction of the next search."*
- Fig 6 :30: *"The description travels. Fails by being sharpened into terms only we can read. Cast it in effect terms."* Fig 9 :30-31: *"your words are borrowed too, so describe the effect rather than the cause."* The question's phrase *"partly hidden"* is already an effect description; it should not be converted into a cause such as "fraud" or "model error" before the census.
- *Decoding Tacit Knowledge*: *"When contact isn't possible, the best you can send is a plain description of what you'd expect to see, because anyone can check that against the world."* This is the rule for the outreach file: state what a refuter would look like, not the theory.
- *Library of Babel* on stopping: *"you will know that you are done because the finish line will also be the starting place"*, and the paradox is that by then you have *"composed the answer through the process of locating it."*

## 3. What licenses a name

Only two things, both outside the shape itself:

1. **The ground settles it.** Formula step 5: *"the ground settles it. Verified only by what pays. A gate is not the ground."* For this question, the realized crisis pays. A stress-test pass is a gate.
2. **A provenanced import from a frame whose closure differs** (Fig 6 :23, Fig 8 :50-52), with a source gate and a shadow test. New exposure-level data is such an import, and its provenance (who collected it, at what date, at what granularity) is part of the claim.

A related point in *They're Scanning Books When They Should* is that a real import changes a dimension of the space rather than adding a term inside it (the essay is listed here as a cross-reference from the exemplar; I did not open it this session, so no quotation from it is used as evidence). Applied here, trade-level exposure data would be a new dimension of the model's space, not another column in the same table.

The six terms are a visible guess, not a discovery of nature's own list. Declaring the primitives makes the choice visible, not neutral. Naming a slot as *the* answer turns a visible choice into an invisible one.

## 4. Why this question's "number" is a source-discriminating problem

*The Hard Problem is About Source* gives the mechanism. An instrument reads green and *"cannot comment on the source"*: one green light, or yellow plus blue, or RGB, or CMYK, or a scheme not yet discovered. The reading does not determine the source. *"If several paths create the same behavior, then behavior is a projection with lossy information."* And: *"An instrument that can't distinguish these cases demonstrates its own inadequacy, it cannot claim anything substantial about what is being measured or what causes it."* It was the **spectrometer**, not an argument, that resolved it: *"The answer is unreached until a better instrument exists."*

A single pre-crisis score is exactly such a reading. A low score could come from genuinely low exposure, from a shared hidden source that the model cannot see, or from a topology that happens to damp the tested shock. The score cannot comment on the source. Correcting the defect therefore owes a source-discriminating instrument (exposure-level data with provenance), not a better argument about the score.

*The Recursive Self-Improvement Trap* confirms the limit on this document itself: *"the output is only as good as the validator. The agent, or the tool, or the frame, or the theory, cannot validate itself."* And: *"You may all agree and be wrong."*

## 5. Q2 answered: what licenses a particular hidden exposure?

**Nothing in the framework.** The framework returns a shape. A particular hidden exposure is licensed only by the ground settling it (a default trace, a settlement record, a disputed trade resolving one way) or by a provenanced import with a shadow test. Until then, naming it is the searcher's own contribution and must be recorded as such. This is why `01` marks the exact pre-crisis number as `gap`, not as a hidden answer.

## 6. Two consequences for outreach

**Prestige is not action.** From *The Recursive Self-Improvement Trap*: *"Prestige must never be allowed to validate an action directly."* and *"Prestige can fund an action. It can give an action a budget to be tried. But it cannot settle an action. Only the ground can settle it. The Ground Maintainer must be blind to prestige."* A campaign that gathers prestigious names to agree with the shape claim is the named collapse, not corroboration. The legitimate ask is the opposite: give the claim a budget to be tried, and let the ground (a held-out crisis, a settlement record, an instrument) settle it.

**My own audit is a simulation, not a ground reading.** Same essay: *"LLMs can quickly simulate an approximate answer/output with the formula found in the architecture post."* So `01`, `06`, `07`, `08`, `09` and `11` are simulated derivations, checked for internal consistency against the figures. They are **not** tests of the claim and must be labelled as such. They cannot settle anything they report on.

## 7. Correction to my own §3

I initially wrote that *"a source-discriminating import with provenance"* could license a name immediately. That overstates it. Fig 8 :50-52 says convergent derivation and adopted import *"look identical in the contents. Only the record of where each came from separates them"*, so a provenance record is necessary but not sufficient: the shadow test (does the imported atom actually explain the residual?) and eventual ground settlement are still owed. The correction is recorded here rather than silently applied to §3.
