**Subject:** One checkable claim about pre-crisis systemic risk measurement

> Hi [name],
>
> I have one specific output I would like you to check against what you already know. I am not asking you to read a theory first, and I am not asking you to agree. If the output does not survive, there is nothing else to look at.
>
> **The output (`specified`, not `grounded`):** a measurement framework indexes its residual per object slot, never as a global average. Applied here, that means per institution, per exposure edge and per correlated-position cluster. Three consequences follow, in effect terms:
>
> 1. A single systemic-risk score is a pooled reading. It can be self-consistent and still lose the signal that says which slot is failing contact.
> 2. Two institutions whose losses move together through an unobserved common exposure are one exposure wearing two names. Their agreement is the shared source talking, not two independent confirmations that diversification works.
> 3. More computation on the same reported positions cannot add the missing edges. What adds an edge is new exposure-level data from a frame whose information differs, such as trade-level records or a counterparty's books.
>
> I am **not** claiming that systemic risk is unknowable. The missing edges are unreachable at the current data budget, which is a different fact from underivable in principle, and it issues a different instruction: get the data, not a bigger model on the same data.
>
> **What I am not claiming:** a certified pre-crisis number, a forecast of any named crisis, or that models which agree must be wrong. Shared-pool agreement is disqualified as a verdict and retained as a budget filter. The realized crisis is the anchor that settles, and because it arrives after the prediction, every pre-crisis measure stays a candidate until the ground pays.
>
> **What would refute the shape:**
> 1. A scalar index that prospectively and per slot predicts which named institution or edge fails first in a held-out crisis, verified at the ground.
> 2. Correlated losses shown at the ground to come from independent shocks with no shared hidden exposure.
> 3. A missing exposure derived from reported data alone and verified without any new data import.
> 4. A crisis whose cascade stays confined to first-order direct contacts.
>
> I wrote these down before sending this. If any one holds, the shape is wrong and I will retract it. A one-line "this fails because..." is more useful to me than agreement. If you would rather not engage, no reply is needed.
>
> **If it survives, and only then:** the sources are below. The figures are the authority, not my summary of them.
> - Canonical figures README and usage rule (local framework): `FRAMEWORK/reference/FIGURES_README_canonical.md`
> - Verbatim figure text with line numbers (local framework): `FRAMEWORK/reference/03-FIGURES_TEXT.md`

> - May, Levin and Sugihara, *Ecology for bankers*, Nature 451, 2008: https://www.nature.com/articles/451893a

Write down what would refute your result *before* you run it, and state the mode of what you get. A reported failure is more useful to me than a reported success.
