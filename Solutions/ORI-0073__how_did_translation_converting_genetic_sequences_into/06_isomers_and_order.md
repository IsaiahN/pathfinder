# Are the code-origin hypotheses isomers? Structure, order and analogues

**Mode: `specified`.** A simulated derivation checked against the figures, not a ground reading (see `05_what_shape_means.md` §6).

## RESIDUAL

The audit left two questions open: (1) the three live hypotheses for how the triplet mapping got fixed (random freeze, stereochemical affinity, optimality) look like different causal stories over the same entities. Are they **isomers** under Fig 12's definition? (2) What order, if any, is licensed among the components of translation?

Atoms held in this derivation: triplet, anticodon, amino acid, adaptor RNA, synthetase, catalytic RNA, peptide, metal ion. Bonds held: the seven of Fig 12 plus negation, with production (⇒) and identity (≡) the relevant pair. No new primitive is available without import. ## DERIVATION

### Q1: Is the three-hypothesis set a set of isomers?

- Fig 12 :117: "Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."
- Fig 12 :124: "functional group. A substructure whose presence fixes the outcome. The group says what kind; the qualifier says which member."
- The three stories share the same atoms: codon, anticodon, amino acid, adaptor, catalyst. They differ in the bond they assert. Random freeze asserts a conventional identity (≡) that became frozen. Stereochemical affinity asserts a physical correspondence discoverable from the operands. Optimality asserts a comparison (⋛) over error rates that selected the assignment.
- Under Fig 12's definition they are therefore **isomers**: same atoms, different bond, different predicted history. The difference is invisible in the endpoint (all predict a near-universal, error-structured code) and decisive in the history.
- **Falsifier, written before the run:** if two of the three make indistinguishable predictions under every instrument that could be brought to bear, they are indiscernible rather than identical (local figures README: "Indiscernibility is not identity"), and the choice between them is a convention, not a discovery. The isomer claim then collapses into a functional-group claim: the group is "a fixed triplet mapping", the qualifiers are the stories.

### Q2: Chain or cycle?

- Fig 12 :121: "cycle. The output re-enters as input. The sign of the loop decides whether it closes, and a chain cannot close by construction."
- Fig 12 :119: "catalyst. A part that survives its own use. Spent and regenerating parts behave differently across uses, and nothing records which a term is."
- A supply-to-mapping-to-catalyst-to-product story is a chain. Its product is the protein that the catalyst is made of, so the chain cannot close. The licensed form is a cycle in which the peptide product re-enters as a stabiliser or replicator of the RNA carrier.
- **Falsifier:** a demonstration of coded translation in which no component is regenerated by the system's own products would break the cycle claim and restore the chain as a live option.

### Q3: What order is licensed among the components?

- World source (Genetic code page, fetched this session): "Transfer RNA molecules appear to have evolved before modern aminoacyl-tRNA synthetases." It also reports that synthetases may have replaced an earlier ribozyme system, or that amino acids were recognised by pockets in proto-tRNAs.
- World source (PTC review, fetched this session): the PTC and the CCA end of tRNA "likely coevolved together"; the anticodon section was added to an older fragment comprising the CCA stem and T arm.
- Fig 12 :104: "A settled molecule becomes an atom for whatever composes over it." A component that is settled can serve as an operand at the next level.
- The figure-licensed order is partial: a carrier must be available before a mapping can be implemented over it, and a mapping must be settled before it can be composed into a system. The figure does **not** license a total order such as "RNA first, then peptide" or "peptide first, then RNA". Those are competing imported stories.
- **Falsifier:** a lineage in which aminoacyl-tRNA synthetase function demonstrably precedes any tRNA-like adaptor would break the partial order above and require a re-census of D2.

### Q4: What is the analogue, and where does it stop?

The lowest-overlap analogue comes from chemistry and complexity, not molecular biology: autocatalysis. The Autocatalysis page defines it as a reaction whose product is also its catalyst, and collectively autocatalytic sets as reaction sets that "produce, as reaction products, catalysts for enough of the other reactions that the entire set of chemical reactions is self-sustaining given an input of energy and food molecules". That is the same shape as F4 of the audit, arrived at in a different field.

Fig 8 :50-52 warns: "Two frames can hold the same atom by convergence, reaching it separately, which is corroboration at maximum independence" and "Convergent derivation and adopted import look identical in the contents. Only the record of where each came from separates them." The autocatalysis frame is recorded as an **import for corroboration**, not as the source of the figure claim. The figure claim came from Fig 12 first.

The analogue stops at the code. Autocatalytic sets explain closure; they do not explain why a particular triplet maps to a particular amino acid. That remains the open bond of F5.

## CONCLUSION

The three code-origin hypotheses are figure-isomers over a shared atom set, with a stated falsifier based on indiscernibility. The licensed order is a cycle, not a chain, and only a partial component order follows; the total RNA first or peptide first order is an import, not a derivation. The autocatalysis analogue corroborates the closure shape from a low-overlap field but cannot name the mapping. All of this is `specified`, ungrounded.
