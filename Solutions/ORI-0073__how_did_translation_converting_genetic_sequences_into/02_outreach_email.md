**Subject:** Check one claim about the origin of translation before you decide whether the framework is worth reading

> Hi [name],
>
> I am [your name]. I have one specific output I would like you to check against what you already know about the origin of translation. I am not asking you to read the framework first, and I am not asking you to agree with it. If the output does not survive, there is nothing else to look at.
>
> **The output (`specified` on the shape, `gap` on the history):** the residual is not one question. It is a union of at least three conditions: a supply of prebiotic operands, a mapping from nucleotide triplets to amino acids, and a catalyst that implements the mapping. The figures say a set of ingredients is not the bond, and that the bond is the half nothing was recording. Making amino acids and nucleotides in the lab does not deliver the triplet-to-amino-acid relation.
>
> The second output is about the loop. Translation is production: the protein does not exist before the mapping fires. The first translating system therefore cannot have been a linear chain waiting for a finished product. The figures distinguish a catalyst, a part that survives its own use, from a cycle, where the output re-enters as input, and state that a chain cannot close by construction. The shape of an origin account must be a self-sustaining catalytic loop in which the product helps regenerate the thing that made it. At that stage there is no maintainer and no live frame in the seat, so selection ran.
>
> **What I am not claiming:** that I can name the molecule that first closed the loop, that the code was uniquely determined by stereochemistry, or that any particular lab scenario is the historical one. The framework returns a shape, not a name. The code is treated as a convention that froze once it decided which protein gets built, not as a table derived from chemistry. The historical route is recorded as unreachable at this budget, which is a verdict and not a silence.
>
> **Refuters, written down before this message:** (1) a linear, non-autocatalytic route is demonstrated that gives coded translation with no component regenerated by its own products; (2) the triplet-to-amino-acid mapping is shown to be uniquely fixed by stereochemical or physicochemical affinity, so it was never a live convention; (3) an instrument returns a direct record of the origin sequence; (4) a translation system works with no part surviving its own use. If any holds, I withdraw or narrow the claim. A one-line "this fails because..." is more useful to me than agreement. If you would rather not engage, no reply is needed.
>
> **If it survives, and only then:** the sources are below. The figures, not my summary, are the authority.
> - Verbatim figure text with line numbers (local): `FRAMEWORK/reference/03-FIGURES_TEXT.md`
> - Canonical usage rule (local): `FRAMEWORK/reference/FIGURES_README_canonical.md`
> - The eight-step loop (local): `FRAMEWORK/figures/THE_FORMULA.md`
>
> **If you want to use the machinery:** write down what would refute your own result *before* you run it, and state the mode of what you get. A reported failure is more useful to me than a reported success.
