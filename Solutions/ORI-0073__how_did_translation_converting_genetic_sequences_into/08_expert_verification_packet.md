# Expert verification packet: the Tether on the origin of translation

**What this is:** a claim in a form a researcher in translation origins can check. Mode, derivation and refuters are stated before the conclusion, so a reader knows what to look for and what would settle it.

**Mode: `specified` on the shape, `general` on the loop, `gap` on the history. Not `grounded`.** No historical measurement is claimed. This is a shape and a route, not a result.

**Status:** a simulated derivation checked for internal consistency against the figures, not a ground reading. It cannot settle the claim it reports on.

## 1. The claim, in one paragraph

The origin of translation is a union of at least three conditions: a supply of prebiotic operands, a mapping from nucleotide triplets to amino acids, and a catalyst that implements the mapping. Ingredients are not the bond. Translation is production, so the protein does not exist before the mapping fires, and a linear chain cannot close. The shape an origin account must have is therefore a self-sustaining catalytic cycle in which the product helps regenerate the carrier of the mapping, at a stage with no maintainer and with selection running. The code is a convention that froze once it decided which protein gets built; its particular assignment is not recoverable from the operands. The first molecule and the historical route are left as `gap`.

## 2. Three checkable sub-claims

### C1: framework-internal; checkable by reading Figure 12

- **Statement:** ingredients are not the mapping, and translation is production rather than sequence. A model that assumes ready-made functional proteins available before coded translation has used the wrong bond.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 12 :5: "A set of ingredients is not one. The bond is the rest of the information, and it is the half nothing was recording." Also Fig 12 :46-47: "A bond is the typed relation between two operands. There are seven, they are not interchangeable, and which one holds is not recoverable from the operands. A recipe that names only its ingredients has recorded half of itself." And Fig 12 :77: "A produces B; B did not exist before".
- **What to check:** is the characterisation faithful to how translation works? In modern cells the amino acid is attached to tRNA by a synthetase before the ribosome reads the codon, so the product is indeed absent before the mapping fires. Does any current origin model assume pre-existing functional proteins without accounting for them?
- **Refuter:** show that the triplet-to-amino-acid relation is recoverable from the operands, or that a well-formed model produces coded translation with a supply-only chain and no mapping bond.

### C2: the cycle claim; checkable by a chemist or origins researcher

- **Statement:** the origin must be a catalytic cycle, not a chain. A catalyst survives its own use; a cycle has its output re-enter as input; a chain cannot close by construction.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 12 :119: "catalyst. A part that survives its own use. Spent and regenerating parts behave differently across uses, and nothing records which a term is." Fig 12 :121: "cycle. The output re-enters as input. The sign of the loop decides whether it closes, and a chain cannot close by construction." Fig 11 :36: "Where nothing maintains anything, intelligence is a label applied afterwards to a lineage that met its ground. Nothing was intelligent; selection ran."
- **What to check:** do the known routes to a proto-ribosome, tRNA adaptors and collectively autocatalytic peptide-RNA networks actually close a loop? Is there a published route that closes without autocatalysis?
- **Refuter:** demonstrate coded translation in which no component is regenerated by the system's own products, or demonstrate a non-autocatalytic route that still reaches the mapping.

### C3: framework-internal, with a world-facing edge; the convention that froze

- **Statement:** the mapping began as a live convention and became a constraint when it decided which protein is built. Its particular assignment is not recoverable from the operands, so the three code-origin hypotheses stand as isomers over the same atoms until an instrument discriminates them.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 2 :41: "efficient for a convention: agreement about a shared meaning is the product, not a corruption of it." Fig 10 :47: "a convention that decides an outcome has stopped being one". Fig 12 :117: "Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."
- **What to check:** is the "frozen" description faithful to the near universality of the code and to the known variants? Can stereochemical or error-minimisation arguments derive the assignment from the operands alone?
- **Refuter:** show the mapping is uniquely fixed by stereochemical or physicochemical affinity, so it was never a live convention; or show an instrument that makes two of the three hypotheses indiscernible, which collapses the isomer claim into a functional-group claim.

## 3. What is explicitly not claimed

- Not a named origin scenario, and not RNA first, peptide first, or thioester first. The framework returns a shape, not a name.
- Not a derivation of the genetic code table from chemistry.
- Not a uniqueness verdict over the random-freeze, stereochemical and optimality hypotheses.
- Not "selection did it". Fig 11 :36 describes the mode at the origin, not the missing bond.
- Not `grounded`. The historical route is `gap`.
- Not a claim that the simulated second seat corroborates the primary. It shares the same evidence pool.

## 5. What would settle it

A discriminator, not an argument. From *The Hard Problem is About Source*: an instrument reading green "cannot comment on the source"; the spectrometer settled it. Here:

1. A minimal system in which a peptide product helps regenerate the RNA carrier of a triplet mapping, checked against a ground.
2. A test of whether the triplet assignment can be derived from stereochemical affinity alone.
3. An instrument that distinguishes the three code-origin isomers, or a lineage resolving the adaptor/synthetase order.
4. Any of the four standing refuters in `07_the_tether_answer.md` §4.

## 6. What an expert can check in twenty minutes

1. Read Fig 12 :5, :46-47, :77, :119, :121 and Fig 11 :36, six short passages.
2. Check C2 against the literature you know: is there a published coded-translation route that closes without autocatalysis?
3. Check C3 against the code-origin debate: is the assignment derivable from the operands, or is "frozen convention" the right description?
4. Name one instrument that would discriminate the three code-origin stories, or say why none is possible.
5. Send the refutation. A failure is more useful than an endorsement.
