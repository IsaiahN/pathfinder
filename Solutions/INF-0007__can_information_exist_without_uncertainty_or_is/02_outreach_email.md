**Subject:** One question about information and uncertainty, with a failure condition

> Hi [name],
>
> I have one specific output I would like you to check against what you already know. I am not asking you to read a theory first, and I am not asking you to agree. If the output does not survive, there is nothing else to look at.
>
> **The residual, in effect terms:** "information" is used in at least four frames that share a word but not an anchor. In the communication frame it is a reduction of entropy, so a message about an outcome already known carries zero new information. In the semantic frame it is well-formed, meaningful, truthful data, which can be certain and still carry information. In the algorithmic frame it is the length of the shortest program that produces a string, so a fully determined string has information. In the physical frame a bit is a physical state, and erasing it has a measured thermodynamic cost. My output is that this is a **union of frames**, not one hard question, and that "is uncertainty essential?" has a different answer in each. Mode: `specified` on the per-frame verdicts, `general` on the union shape.
>
> **What the framework adds:** in the figures, information is the typed relation between operands, the part a list of ingredients does not record. It is not entropy. It is also a derivative a composition leaves behind, not one of the six primitives. Which definition "wins" is a convention, not something the ground settles.
>
> **What I am not claiming:** that there is one true definition of information; that Shannon entropy is wrong; that uncertainty is irrelevant to communication. Within the communication frame, uncertainty is essential by theorem, and I say so.
>
> **What would refute this, written before I wrote to you:**
> 1. a grounded derivation reducing the four frames to one quantity with one anchor;
> 2. a demonstration that the figures' bond-information and Shannon uncertainty are the same quantity with the same unit;
> 3. a frame-independent measure of uncertainty that does not depend on any observer's library;
> 4. a case where a true datum, a determined string or a physical bit carries zero information in every frame;
> 5. a measurement that settles which definition of information is correct.
>
> If any holds, I retract the union shape. A one-line "this fails because..." is more useful to me than agreement. If you would rather not engage, no reply is needed.
>
> **If it survives, and only then:** the sources are below. The figures are the authority, not my summary of them.
> - Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy, Information: https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/information/
> - Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy, Semantic Conceptions of Information: https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/information-semantic/
> - Landauer's principle, with the primary sources linked in its references: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Landauer%27s_principle
> - Figures text with line numbers (local): `C:\Users\Admin\Desktop\Project Pathfinder\FRAMEWORK\reference\03-FIGURES_TEXT.md`
>

Write down what would refute your result *before* you run it, and state the mode of what you get. A reported failure is more useful to me than a reported success.
