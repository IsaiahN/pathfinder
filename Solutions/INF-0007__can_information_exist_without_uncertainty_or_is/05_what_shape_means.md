# What "shape" means for this question, canonical reading

Sources read on 2026-11-23: the canonical figures README (local), `THE_FORMULA.md` (local), and the two framework essays named below. The full eight-essay set is listed at the end with the reading status of each.

## 1. A shape here is a structure of the answer, not a yes or no

The figures README says the framework returns *"what a missing thing would have to look like (a shape, not a name)."* Fig 9 :32: *"you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself."*

For a question phrased as an either/or, the natural mistake is to treat "yes" or "no" as the answer. The shape discipline forbids that when the question is a bundle. From *The Library of Babel*, opened this session: *"Perhaps your problem was a bundle or conflation of several problems masquerading as one? You will need to search all of those problems individually to find their answers and then combine them in a satisfactory way."* The same essay states the stopping rule: *"you can cut your search short if the set of books returned is small and descriptive enough that it provides a general shape of the answer that satisfies your question."*

So the shape for INF-0007 is not "information requires uncertainty" or "information does not require uncertainty." It is **a union of four frames with a separate verdict in each, plus a fifth structural frame that says what the word means inside the framework, plus the statement that the definitional residue is a convention.** That is a describable object you can check. It is not a filled slot.

## 2. How the shape is obtained

- Describe the residual in effect terms first. Fig 9 :30-31, *"your words are borrowed too, so describe the effect rather than the cause."* The effect here is simple: a signal changes what a frame can predict, and uncertainty is the spread of possibilities before the signal.
- Do not pick a favourite definition and then read the question through it. Fig 9 :27-28: *"describe it before you go looking, or any frame you pick will seem to fit."*
- Unbundle. Fig 9 :54: *"And when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again."*
- From *The Library of Babel*: use the vocabulary you have that is closest to the thing, then recurse as new terms arrive. *"With each new term gained, you narrow the space by definition and deepen your vocabulary."*

## 3. What licenses a name in this question

Two things, both outside the shape:

1. **A frame's ground pays for it.** A theorem inside the entropy frame and a measurement of the Landauer bound are in-frame `grounded` claims. Formula step 5: the ground settles it.
2. **A provenanced import from a frame whose closure differs** (Fig 6 :23, Fig 8 provenance rule), with a source gate and a shadow test. Bringing "information" from physics into the figures is a binding onto a held vocabulary, not a new primitive.

From *The Hard Problem is About Source*, opened this session: an instrument that reads green *"cannot comment on the source,"* and *"If several paths create the same behavior, then behavior is a projection with lossy information."* That is the failure mode here. Reading a single frame's entropy value and declaring what "information is" names a source from a projection. What is owed is a source-discriminating instrument or an explicit convention, not a name.

## 4. Why "which definition is right" cannot be a shape that pays

Fig 2 :36: *"An anchor is legitimate when the question is constitutively about it."* The definitional question is constitutively about a shared meaning, so the legitimate anchor there is agreement about meaning, which is a convention. Fig 2 :41 calls that *"efficient for a convention"* while Fig 2 :40 calls agreement about the world *"disqualifying for a verdict"* when frames share an evidence pool. Fig 10 :46: *"A convention makes no claim about the world; a verdict and an atom both do."*

A shape can therefore say: here are the frames, here is each verdict, and here is the part that is a convention. It cannot say: here is the one true definition, because that would be a convention deciding an outcome (Fig 10 :47).

## 6. Prestige is not action, and this audit is a simulation

From *The Recursive Self-Improvement Trap*, as quoted in the exemplar package: *"Prestige can fund an action. It can give an action a budget to be tried. But it cannot settle an action. Only the ground can settle it."* Gathering famous names to agree with a definition is the named collapse. The legitimate ask is to give the claim a budget to be tried.

The same essay says LLM output is *"simulacra"* and that *"the truly testable output would need to be derived from the real architecture or working the formula yourself."* So `01_figure_grounded_audit.md` is a simulated derivation checked for internal consistency against the figures. It is **not** a test of the claim and must be labelled as such. It cannot settle anything it reports on.

## 7. What settles the shape

A source-discriminating instrument, or an explicit convention that is not allowed to decide an outcome. Within a frame, a theorem or a measurement settles the in-frame part. Across frames, nothing does, and saying so is the honest stop rather than a silence. Fig 13 :134: *"Unreachable is a verdict here, not a silence."*
