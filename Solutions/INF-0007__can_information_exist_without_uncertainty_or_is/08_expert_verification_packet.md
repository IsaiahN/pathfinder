# Expert verification packet — information and uncertainty

**What this is:** a claim in a form a specialist can check. Mode, derivation and refuters are stated before the conclusion, so a reader knows what to look for and what would settle it.

**Mode: `specified` on per-domain verdicts, `general` on the union shape, not `grounded` on the definitional question.** No measurement is claimed to settle which definition of information is correct.

## 1. The claim, in one paragraph

The question "can information exist without uncertainty?" is a union of four frames, not one hard question. In the entropy frame uncertainty is essential by theorem: at zero uncertainty a message carries no new information. In the semantic, algorithmic and physical frames information exists without an observer's uncertainty: a true datum, a shortest program and a physical bit all carry information at certainty. In the figures, information is the typed bond that a list of ingredients does not record, and a derivative a composition leaves behind, not one of the six primitives. Which meaning wins is a convention about a shared word, not a verdict the ground settles. The output is the split, a verdict per frame, and five refuters written in advance.

## 2. Three checkable sub-claims

### C1 — framework-internal; checkable by reading Figure 12 and Figure 13

- **Statement:** in the figures, information is the bond and the derivative, not entropy and not a primitive.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 12 :5 *"A set of ingredients is not one. The bond is the rest of the information, and it is the half nothing was recording."* Fig 12 :47 *"recipe that names only its ingredients has recorded half of itself."* Fig 13 :124 *"Smoothness, symmetry, chaos and discreteness are each what a particular composition leaves behind, and none is a seventh member."*
- **Refuter:** show that the figures' bond-information and Shannon uncertainty are the same quantity with the same unit and anchor, or derive information as a seventh substrate member from the six.

### C2 — frame-internal theorem; checkable by an information theorist

- **Statement:** in the entropy frame, information is the reduction of uncertainty, so at zero uncertainty a message carries zero new information. This is the only domain where the answer to the headline question is "uncertainty is essential."
- **Source (world, secondary):** SEP Information entry: *"Information reduces uncertainty. The amount of information we get grows linearly with the amount by which it reduces our uncertainty until the moment that we have received all possible information and the amount of uncertainty is zero."*
- **What to check:** is the theorem stated correctly, and is it being applied only inside D1? Check that it is not being extended to semantic or algorithmic information.
- **Refuter:** exhibit positive Shannon information at zero uncertainty under the standard definition, or show a frame-independent measure of uncertainty that does not depend on any observer's probability assignment.

### C3 — cross-frame; checkable by a philosopher of information or a physicist

- **Statement:** in the semantic, algorithmic and physical frames, information exists without uncertainty; and the choice among definitions is a convention, not a ground verdict.
- **Source (world, secondary):** SEP Semantic Information quotes Shannon (1950): *"It is hardly to be expected that a single concept of information would satisfactorily account for the numerous possible applications of the general field."* The Landauer page states *"the minimum energy needed to erase one bit of information is proportional to the temperature at which the system is operating."* Fig 10 :46: *"A convention makes no claim about the world; a verdict and an atom both do."*
- **What to check:** is Floridi's factive definition (`well-formed, meaningful, and truthful data`) being represented fairly, and does the measured Landauer bound support the claim that a bit is a physical state rather than an observer's uncertainty?
- **Refuter:** show a true datum, a determined string or a physical bit carrying zero information in every frame, or produce a measurement that settles which definition of information is correct.

## 3. What is explicitly not claimed

- Not that Shannon was wrong, and not that uncertainty is irrelevant to communication.
- Not that there is one true definition of information. Fig 10 :47 disqualifies a convention that decides an outcome.
- Not that the four frames are isomers as written. They share a word, not an atom set (`06` Q2).
- Not `grounded` on the whole question. Only the D1 theorem and the measured Landauer bound carry that label.
- Not a ground reading. This packet is a simulated derivation checked for internal consistency against the figures. It cannot settle the claim it reports on.

## 5. What would settle it

1. A grounded derivation reducing the four frames to one quantity with one anchor (would refute the union).
2. A demonstration that bond-information equals Shannon uncertainty, same unit and anchor (would refute the re-census).
3. A frame-independent measure of uncertainty with no Γ dependence (would refute frame relativity).
4. A case with zero information in every frame (would refute "information exists without uncertainty").
5. A measurement settling the correct definition (would refute the convention verdict).

Until one fires, the shape stands as `candidate`.

## 6. What an expert can check in twenty minutes

1. Read Fig 12 :5, :47 and Fig 13 :124, three short passages. Is "information" there a bond and a derivative?
2. Check C2 against the standard definition. Is the zero-uncertainty limit stated correctly and confined to D1?
3. Check one non-entropy frame you know well (semantic, algorithmic or physical). Does information persist there when no observer is uncertain?
4. Name one instrument or derivation that would collapse the frames to one anchor, or say why none is available.
5. Send the refutation. A failure is more useful than an endorsement.
