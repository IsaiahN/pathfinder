## 1. A shape is a constraint you can state before you can fill it

The canonical limit: the figures README says the framework returns "what a missing thing would have to look like (a shape, not a name)." Fig 9 :32 says "you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself." Fig 13 :133 says "what it returns is a shape rather than a name."

From *The Library of Babel*: "you can cut your search short if the set of books returned is small and descriptive enough that it provides a general shape of the answer that satisfies your question."

For ORI-0082, the shape is **not** a feedstock list, not a pH, not an organism. It is the constraint any acceptable experiment design must satisfy: two reported residuals with two grounds, plausibility as a filter and not a witness, evolvability as an arrangement and not an added ingredient, and an explicitly declared habitat. That constraint can be written before any particular experiment is chosen. Filling it with a specific system is a name, and naming is a ground or import question.

## 2. How the shape is obtained

- *Library of Babel*: use the vocabulary closest to the thing, then recurse on the new terms that overlap with it. "With each new term gained, you narrow the space and deepen your vocabulary." For this question the starting vocabulary is the two residuals themselves.
- *Library of Babel* on bundles: "Perhaps your problem was a bundle or conflation of several problems masquerading as one? You will need to search all of those problems individually." That is Fig 9 :54's union unbundling stated in search terms.
- Fig 6 :30: "outward: does another frame already hold it? The description travels. Fails by being sharpened into terms only we can read. Cast it in effect terms."
- *Decoding Tacit Knowledge*: "When contact isn't possible, the best you can send is a plain description of what you'd expect to see, because anyone can check that against the world." So the shape is written as an observable: what a lab notebook would show if the design worked.

## 3. What licenses a name

Two things, both outside the shape:

1. **The ground settles it.** Formula step 5: the ground settles it, verified only by what pays. A measured selection response in a plausibly constituted habitat names the system; the design rule does not.
2. **A provenanced import from a frame whose closure differs** (Fig 6 :23, Fig 8 :12-13), with a source gate and a shadow test, debited against independence.

*The Hard Problem is About Source* gives the discriminator rule: "If several paths create the same behavior, then behavior is a projection with lossy information." The same evolved phenotype can arise from a plausible habitat or from a harness-supplied template. A reading of the phenotype cannot name the source. "The answer is unreached until a better instrument exists." What is owed here is an instrument that discriminates habitats, not a declaration that one habitat was the real one.

## 4. Why the shape must be bound before the run

Fig 5 :62-63: "A frame searching for an answer of a particular shape holds that shape as a PREFERENCE rather than as a term. It is not bound, it predicts nothing, and it accrues no residual." If we never write down what an acceptable experiment would look like, every check runs on yields and conditions and none runs on the direction of the question. §5 below binds it. The pre-registered refuter in `01_figure_grounded_audit.md` §4 is the consequence.

## 5. The shape, bound as an ordinary term for this question

> An acceptable origin-of-life experiment design reports (a) a measured plausibility residual for feedstocks and conditions under a stated prebiotic scenario, and (b) a measured completeness residual consisting of heritable variation and a differential selection response over generations; treats (a) as an admissibility filter and never as a witness for (b); reaches (b) by rearranging the primitives (a) admits before importing engineered components; declares every introduced actor and states which ones the plausible habitat does not supply; runs the two checks with low evidence-pool overlap; and publishes in advance the observations that would refute the result.

That is a shape. It does not name a feedstock, a setting, a ribozyme or a vesicle. It is refusable by the five conditions in §4 of `01_figure_grounded_audit.md`.

## 6. Correction against overreach (kept visible)

The phrase "shape, not a name" governs a **missing** primitive, one not held in Γ. It does not forbid naming atoms already held, nor does it forbid naming a measured feedstock. For ORI-0082 the correction matters in two places:

- Naming a prebiotically plausible feedstock is a **binding from the chemistry frame** onto what the experiment measured. It is allowed with provenance. What is forbidden is treating that binding as a derivation of evolvability.
- Naming "an evolving system" is not naming a new primitive. It is naming a derivative arrangement of held things (Fig 13 :124-125: "Smoothness, symmetry, chaos and discreteness are each what a particular composition leaves behind, and none is a seventh member"). The forbidden move is adding it as an ingredient.

An earlier draft of this file treated any naming as a breach of the shape rule. That reading is retracted as overbroad. The narrower reading above is retained, and the original is left visible so the correction is checkable.

## 7. Prestige is not action, and this derivation is a simulation

*The Recursive Self-Improvement Trap*: "Prestige can fund an action. It can give an action a budget to be tried. But it cannot settle an action. Only the ground can settle it." A design that gathers prestigious endorsements is the named collapse, not corroboration. The legitimate ask is a budget to try the design and a ground to settle it.

Same essay: "LLMs can quickly simulate an approximate answer/output with the formula" but "the truly testable output would need to be derived from the real architecture or working the formula yourself." Everything in this folder, including `01` and `09`, is a simulated derivation checked for internal consistency against the figures. It is not a test of any experiment and cannot settle the question it reports on.
