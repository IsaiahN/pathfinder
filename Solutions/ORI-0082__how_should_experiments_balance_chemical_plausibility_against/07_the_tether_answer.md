# The Tether's answer: how experiments should balance plausibility against a complete evolving system

**Mode: `general` on the design shape, `specified` on the failure predictions, not `grounded`.** This is a shape and a route, not a result. Simulated derivation checked against the figures, not a ground reading.

## 1. The residual it answers

An experiment can be chemically plausible and inert (plausible amino acids, no lineage), or it can produce a heritable evolving system that depends on a hand-authored habitat (a harness result). The question asks for a balance. The figures say the question is a union compound stated as if it were a scalar trade-off. Fig 9 :54: "And when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again." The residual is the gap between the two failures, and it needs a design, not a weight.

## 2. The shape

1. **Report two residuals, never one score.** Fig 1 :19: "The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated." Fig 1 :22: "Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average." One slot is measured plausibility of feedstocks and conditions under a stated prebiotic scenario; the other is measured heritable variation and a selection response over generations. Each has its own ground (Fig 1 :23: "The ground enters as the only metric.").

2. **Plausibility is a filter on the budget, not a witness for the verdict.** Fig 9 :60: "Use filters for the budget and witnesses for the verdict. Never let a filter hand you a verdict." It decides which systems get tried. It is not evidence that a system evolves, and an evolving system is not evidence that its feedstocks were available.

3. **Evolvability is an arrangement, not an ingredient.** Fig 13 :115: "The set is closed. The arrangements are not. That gap is why import exists." Fig 13 :124-125: "Smoothness, symmetry, chaos and discreteness are each what a particular composition leaves behind, and none is a seventh member." Reach an evolving system by rearranging the primitives the plausibility filter admits.

4. **Inward instrumentation before outward import, with imports declared.** Fig 6 :31 says the two instruments are "two tracks" and "never read one against the other." Ask first whether the signal is in the plausible system and unfound. Then sharpen the instrument. Only then import a component, with a source gate, a shadow test and a debit against independence (Fig 6 :24). Importing against a budget limit adds an atom that was not needed.

5. **Declare the habitat.** Fig 11 :70: "A test harness is a substituted habitat. Which is why a synthetic solve proves wiring and never capability." List the actors the experiment introduced and state which ones a plausible habitat does not supply. Fig 11 :61: "The seat's power is asymmetric. Introduction is available; exclusion is not." You can add a purified template; you cannot subtract the prebiotic habitat from the claim.

6. **Run two low-overlap checks and select between experiments, never average them.** Fig 2 :45: "Frames range against the ground. They do not negotiate with it." Fig 2 :40: "agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool." Fig 7 :35: "A room's agreement is variation, not a verdict. Selection between rooms is where the ground votes."

7. **Bind the shape and publish the refuter before the run.** Fig 9 :32: "you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself." Fig 5 :62-63: "A frame searching for an answer of a particular shape holds that shape as a PREFERENCE rather than as a term. It is not bound, it predicts nothing, and it accrues no residual." The shape is written down in `05` §5 so it can be refused.

## 3. What it explicitly does not say

- Not a weighting formula. No `w` is derived, because no scalar preserves two per-slot signals.
- Not that a model system is the ground. An in vitro RNA or protocell system is an instrument for detecting evolvability, not a demonstration of prebiotic origin.
- Not that any specific feedstock, setting or pathway is the answer. The framework narrows a design shape; the ground names a system.
- Not that the six-term substrate lists chemical species. Only the formal closed-set/open-arrangement law is used.
- Not a claim of life created in a lab. "Although a functional protocell that evolves has not yet been achieved in a laboratory setting, the goal to understand the process appears possible" (Wikipedia, Protocell, 2026-11-23).
- Not grounded. No experiment in this folder was run.

## 4. What would settle it: the ground

A source-discriminating instrument, not further argument. *The Hard Problem is About Source*: "If several paths create the same behavior, then behavior is a projection with lossy information"; "The answer is unreached until a better instrument exists." Concretely:

- **Two-slot measurement:** does a single experiment report both a plausibility residual and a generational selection response, with neither netted out of the other? If not, slot aggregation is still happening.
- **Proxy test:** does selection improve the assayed function only, as the directed-evolution literature warns, or does it improve the unmeasured completeness slot as well?
- **Transfer test:** does an evolved function transfer from the substituted lab habitat to a plausibly constituted habitat with no engineered component remaining? If yes, the harness was an instrument. If no, it was a substitution.
- **Standing falsifiers:** the five in `01_figure_grounded_audit.md` §4, written before the conclusion. Any one fires a retraction.

## 5. The honest stop

**Unreached, not unreachable.** Fig 13 :134: "Unreachable is a verdict here, not a silence," and :135: "It cannot say which primitive is missing, because naming it would mean already holding it." The figures give the shape of the design; they do not pick the experiment. We stop at the limit of the available instruments and record the lineage so the stop is checkable. Fig 9 :32: "you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself." No part of this is `grounded` until a lab pays for it.

## 6. One-paragraph version for outreach

> The Tether does not tell an origin-of-life experiment what percentage of its effort to spend on plausibility and what percentage on building a living system. It says that is the wrong question. The two are separate residuals with separate grounds, and averaging them lets a high yield hide a dead lineage, which is the Miller-Urey shape. Plausibility is a filter on what gets tried, never evidence that a system evolves. Evolvability is an arrangement of the feedstocks the filter admits, not a new ingredient, so try inward instrumentation before importing engineered components, and declare every component the lab introduces: a harness that evolves proves the wiring and not the capability. Run the two checks with genuinely independent evidence pools and let the ground select between experiments rather than a vote of researchers. What is offered is the shape any acceptable experiment must have, five refuters written in advance, and the instruments that would close it. The center stays open until a lab settles it.
