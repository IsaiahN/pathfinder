# Outreach draft: check one claim about schooling before you decide about the frame

**Use:** one-to-one, or as a cover letter attached to a submission through the public venues in `03_public_contact_points.csv`. **Not** for bulk send.

**Hard precondition:** do not send until the live strain in F3 of `01_figure_grounded_audit.md` is stated in the message. The strain is that real institutions must decide outcomes, so the claim is a question about keeping the ground reachable, not an absolute prohibition. A message that hides it will collapse on contact.

---

**Subject:** Would you check one claim about how schools confer standing on knowledge?

> Hi [name],
>
> I have one specific claim I would like you to check against what you already know about the history of education. I am not asking you to read the framework first, and I am not asking you to agree with it. If the claim does not survive, there is nothing else to look at.
>
> **The claim (`specified`, not `grounded`):** schooling does not merely deliver knowledge whose standing is fixed elsewhere. It acts as a membrane between a single learner and a population. What crosses down should be a head start that makes the next search cheaper, not a recording of a past success. On that reading, "basic" names the knowledge that has been compressed far enough to make later searches cheaper, and "useful" is a property of the knowledge together with the habitat it lands in, not of the knowledge alone.
>
> A second claim is about authority. An institution legitimately authors conventions: grading scales, canons, definitions of a field. A convention makes a disagreement statable. The failure begins when a convention decides an outcome (who is employable, which claim counts as authoritative) while remaining unchecked against anything the institution did not author. At that point the institution's decree has become a verdict about the world without a ground. The strain I want you to test is this: real universities must decide outcomes, so is an authority that keeps the ground reachable actually possible, or is the absolute form of the rule describing nobody?
>
> **What I am not claiming:** that credentials are an import that adds new knowledge, that any canon is final, that scholars who agree on a canon have thereby corroborated a claim about the world, or that exams measure capability rather than the wiring of a test harness. I am also not claiming a measurement I did not make. This is a structural claim checked for internal consistency, not a ground reading.
>
> **What would refute the claim:**
> 1. knowledge attains "basic" standing in a society with no formal transmission institution and the same search cost as in a schooled society;
> 2. a credential keeps its authority across a change of institution while being continuously checked against an anchor that does not move when the institution pushes;
> 3. an item called useful transfers to a novel habitat with no loss of capability and no unenumerated introduced actor, or an exam result transfers to capability with no residue loss;
> 4. a canon is final and successfully closes further inquiry.
>
> I wrote these down before sending this. If any one holds, the corresponding part of the claim is withdrawn. A one-line "this fails because..." is more useful to me than agreement. If you would rather not engage, no reply is needed.
>
> **If it survives, and only then:** the figure text is below. The figures are the authority, not my summary of them.
> - Verbatim figure text with line numbers (local extract): `FRAMEWORK/reference/03-FIGURES_TEXT.md`. Look at Fig 4 :11-12 and :22-25, Fig 10 :45-49, Fig 11 :61, :66 and :70, and Fig 2 :29-41.
> - Canonical usage rule (local copy): `FRAMEWORK/reference/FIGURES_README_canonical.md`.
> - The eight-step loop: `FRAMEWORK/figures/THE_FORMULA.md`.
>
> **If you want to use the machinery:** run it on a problem you know well and can check against a ground. Write down what would refute your result *before* you run it. The framework's own rule is that a loop cannot check the direction of its own question, and the source essay warns that outputs could always be contrived to fit the formula. A reported failure is more useful to me than a reported success.
>
> Thank you for reading this far.
