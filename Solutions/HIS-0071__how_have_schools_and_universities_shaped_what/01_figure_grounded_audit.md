## 0. The residual stated in effect terms

Question: how have schools and universities shaped what societies regard as basic, authoritative, or useful knowledge?

Stated as a residual, not a topic:

> **R = the standing-conferral gap.** On one side, the standing a body of knowledge would have in a society with no formal schooling. On the other, the standing it has once schools and universities select, credential, and transmit it. What failed to resolve is the mechanism by which an institution changes the standing of knowledge rather than merely moving it around.

Three options, with what each leads to:

| option | what it claims | what it leads to |
|---|---|---|
| A, neutral conduit | schooling only delivers knowledge whose standing is already fixed elsewhere | schools are a recording channel; "basic" is a discovery about the world, not an institutional effect |
| B, membrane | schooling lowers the search cost of some knowledge, making later searches cheaper | "basic" becomes a property of a head start that makes a next generation capable |
| C, seat that confers | schooling authors conventions that decide standing and quietly become verdicts | "authoritative" becomes a credential effect, and "useful" becomes a habitat effect |

An answer would look like: for each of the three predicates, a named mechanism, a figure line that governs it, a refuter that would break it, and a statement of what an anchor would have to show. This binds the sought-for shape as an ordinary term before the search (Fig 5 :65-66: "make the sought-for shape an ORDINARY TERM. Write down what an answer would look like, bind it").

## 1. Domain census, before choosing any figure line

**R by arity, symmetry, scale.** Arity: three predicates, basic / authoritative / useful. Fig 9 :54-57 warns that a bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones, so treat them as a candidate union until each has its own loop. Symmetry: none. Each is a directed conferral from institution to knowledge to society; swapping operands does not preserve the claim. Scale: two scales are in play. The individual learner runs a step-by-step loop and a university runs a population-level library with something that ranks what it produces. Fig 4 is the figure of that two-scale machine: "The same machine at two sizes, and the rule about what may pass between them."

**One line per domain. A domain needs a nameable anchor.**

| domain | actors | currency | anchor | partition or union |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| D1 Transmission across the membrane | one learner, the population, the curriculum as library, the teacher as membrane keeper | head starts versus recordings; search cost for the next agent | what the learner can reapply in a new habitat, with the ground for any settlement | partition: what crosses up (methods) versus what crosses down (head starts) |
| D2 Conferral of standing | the institution as seat, credentials, canons, peer frames, examiners | authority and credibility, which are frame-internal quantities | the ground, which does not move when the institution pushes | partition: convention versus verdict |
| D3 Selection in the habitat | ideas, methods and instruments introduced; conditions; relations; the ambient habitat | arrival and survival of ideas, cascades | the ground, which decides what survives contact | union: the classroom is a substituted habitat laid over an unauthored one |

**Order by dependency (Fig 3).** Fig 3 :20-21: "The chain is ordered by dependency, not by importance. Each link consumes what the one above produces." The order here is: describe the standing-conferral residual in effect terms before naming a frame (Fig 9 :27-28); settle what may cross the membrane (D1) before asking who has standing to confer (D2); keep the convention from becoming a verdict before asking what is useful in a habitat (D3). Refusal precedes mint (Formula step 2): before claiming schooling *confers* anything, check the conduit reading that there is standing already fixed outside the school. A link with no instrument is not a working link (Fig 3 :27), so each domain is asked what instrument could show its effect.

**Partition or union verdict.** The three predicates form a union, not three independent questions. They share the institution but not the anchor: D1 is answered by reapplicability, D2 by an unmoving ground, D3 by survival in contact. Where they overlap (a credential that also makes a search cheaper) that is a compound with its own loop, to be handled in `06_isomers_and_order.md`, not averaged into one verdict.

## 3. Findings

### F1 - Schooling is a membrane, and what crosses decides whether knowledge is basic (D1, triangulated)

Fig 4 :11-12, in the "send down" panel:

> **"a head start, never a recording"**
> **"it makes the next agent's search cheaper"**

Fig 4 :22-24:

> **"Only methods cross up. Only head starts come down."**
> **"upward: a method that can be reapplied. Never the recording of a particular success."**
> **"downward: something that makes the next search cheaper. Never a replay of what worked once."**

Fig 4 :25:

> **"A recording carried upward looks like knowledge and is a description of one occasion."**

Before concluding, state the effect: if a school hands a learner a recording of a past success, the learner can repeat that success and cannot produce a new one. If it hands a head start that makes the next search cheaper, the learner can reapply it. So "basic" is not a rank in a list of facts. It is the name for knowledge that has been compressed far enough to make subsequent searches cheaper. Fig 1 :19 supports the per-slot reading: "The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated." A curriculum that aggregates all knowledge into one "core" throws away the signal about which slot is live.

Triangulation: Fig 4 (the membrane rule) and Fig 11 :39 "capability is a property of agent-and-habitat, never of the agent alone" (the capability claim). These govern different quantities, so the overlap is low within the framework. Both are framework sources, so this is internal triangulation, not independent corroboration. Mode: `general` on the shape, `specified` on the claim that a head start lowers search cost.

**Refuter:** show a curriculum made of pure recordings that still produces novel capability in a new habitat with no access to a method, or show that "basic" status is identical in two societies with identical search costs but different schooling. Either fires and F1 falls.

### F2 - The seed carries no small tree, so usefulness is not carried by the item alone (D1, triangulated)

Fig 4 :34-35:

> **"The level a method lands on remakes it. A seed carries no small tree: it carries what will grow one, and the conditions it lands in decide what it becomes."**

Fig 11 :40:

> **"An improvement that does not change contact changes nothing, however much it improves."**

Effect: what a society calls "useful" is a property of the knowledge *and the habitat it lands in*. Transferring the same item into a different habitat (a different economy, a different language, a different institution) does not transfer its usefulness. The world literature calls the corresponding observation transfer of learning and notes that it remains contested, including whether it exists at all. That is a secondary witness, not the verdict.

Mode: `general` (structural), with a `specified` prediction that usefulness covaries with habitat contact rather than with the item.

**Refuter:** an item of knowledge that is equally useful in every habitat, with the same cascade and the same capability, independent of conditions. That would break the seed law and the capability claim together.

### F3 - Authority is a convention until it decides an outcome, then it is a verdict with no ground (D2, triangulated)

Fig 10 :45-47 gives the seat's office and its failure:

> **"It does author conventions, because nothing else can see across a seam."**
> **"A convention makes no claim about the world; a verdict and an atom both do. The seat may author what has no truth value and nothing that does."**
> **"a convention that decides an outcome has stopped being one"**

Fig 10 :48-49:

> **"a convention nothing can check is a constant the seat authored, carrying the seat's authority"**
> **"Declaring a meaning does not ground it."**

Fig 10 :16-17:

> **"the convention does not fix the bug it makes the bug statable"**

Effect: a university legitimately authors conventions. A grading scale, a canon, a citation format, a definition of a field: these have no truth value and make disagreement statable. The failure is a convention that starts deciding outcomes (who is employable, which claim counts as authoritative) while remaining unchecked against anything it does not author. At that point the institution's decree has become a verdict about the world without a ground. The credentialism literature reports the same shape in different vocabulary: "Credentialism is a reliance on formal qualifications or certifications to determine whether someone is permitted to undertake a task, speak as an 'expert' or work in a certain field."

Mode: `specified`. This is a checkable structural claim about the moment a convention crosses into verdict territory.

**Refuter:** a credentialing practice whose authority survives a change of institution and is continuously re-grounded against an anchor that does not move when that institution pushes. If such a practice exists and remains a convention while deciding outcomes, F3 is wrong.

**Strain, named not hidden:** Fig 10 :53-54 warns that "a prohibition nothing can satisfy is a prohibition stated wrong" and asks what would have to be true for a real occupant to meet an absolute. Real institutions must decide outcomes (admission, graduation, hiring). Read literally, "a convention that decides an outcome has stopped being one" describes almost every real university. So this is a live strain, not a clean rule. The honest form is a question: can a credential decide an outcome while the ground stays reachable? That is an institutional-design question, not something this derivation settles. Carried to `11_audit.md`.

### F4 - Credibility is frame-internal, so a school cannot score its own authority (D2, triangulated)

Fig 1 :24-27:

> **"coverage, and compression achieved"**
> **"predicates minted, and credibility accrued"**
> **"They are frame-internal. A frame cannot score itself with a quantity it also produces."**

Fig 1 :23: **"The ground enters as the only metric."**

Effect: "authoritative" cannot be established by pointing at the institution's own credentials, prestige, or list of degrees awarded. Those are quantities the frame produces, so using them to score the frame is self-scoring. The cultural-capital literature names the institutional form of this: "Institutionalized cultural capital comprises an institution's formal recognition of a person's cultural capital, usually academic credentials or professional qualifications." That is a description of the frame-internal currency, not a ground for it.

Mode: `general` (the disqualification is structural), `specified` on the claim that credentials are such a quantity.

**Refuter:** identify a measure of authority that an institution does not itself produce and that still tracks "authoritative" across institutions. If that measure exists and is reachable, the frame-internal objection narrows to the subset of self-produced measures.

### F5 - Consensus inside a canon builds a convention, not a verdict (D2, triangulated)

Fig 2 :39-41:

> **"Correlation has valence."**
> **"disqualifying for a verdict: agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool"**
> **"efficient for a convention: agreement about a shared meaning is the product, not a corruption of it"**

Fig 2 :35: **"Alignment is triangulation, not negotiation. A mirror cannot correct you."**

Effect: when a discipline agrees on what its basic texts are, that agreement is the product of a convention doing its job (shared meaning). It is not two independent frames corroborating a claim about the world. The failure is to read canonical consensus as a verdict. The hidden-curriculum literature supplies the parallel at the level of outcomes: "the hidden curriculum reinforces existing social inequalities through the education of students according to their class and social status." That describes a convention that has begun to decide outcomes, which is F3's crossing. Two different literatures, one shape; they share an evidence pool only loosely (sociology of education and critical pedagogy), so this is weak corroboration, not a second witness.

Mode: `specified`.

**Refuter:** a canon whose agreeing members demonstrably draw on different evidence and range separately against an unmoving ground. Then the agreement is triangulation, and the disqualification does not apply to it.

### F6 - The curriculum is a budget filter, and a filter must not hand a verdict (cross-cutting, single-source for the figure line)

Fig 9 :60:

> **"Use filters for the budget and witnesses for the verdict. Never let a filter hand you a verdict."**

Fig 1 :19: **"The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated."**

Effect: no society can teach everything. Selection is a budget filter over finite attention and time, and the figures permit it. The failure occurs when exclusion from the curriculum is read as non-knowledge, that is, when a budget filter is promoted to a witness. The National Defense Education Act is a documented historical instance of the filter at work: "In total, over a billion dollars was directed towards improving American science curricula," and "Latin and Greek programs are not funded under this title, on the grounds that they are not modern foreign languages, and thus do not support defense needs." The filter was explicit and tied to a goal; treating its output as a verdict about what is worth knowing is the failure the figure names.

Mode: `specified`. The world instance is historical evidence, not the verdict.

**Refuter:** a society in which exclusion from the curriculum leaves the excluded knowledge's public standing unchanged, with a separate functioning witness channel. If that is observed, the filter-to-verdict promotion is not universal and F6 narrows.

### F7 - The classroom is a substituted habitat: introduction available, exclusion not (D3, single-source for the habitat law)

Fig 11 :66, :61, :32:

> **"Isolation is not removal of the habitat. It is substitution of one habitat for another."**
> **"The seat's power is asymmetric. Introduction is available; exclusion is not."**
> **"introduce, never subtract"**

Fig 11 :65: **"Introducing an actor for its cascade is ecosystem engineering, after Jones, Lawton and Shachak, 1994. Cane toads and kudzu are the same move with the cascade unenumerated."**

Fig 11 :70: **"A test harness is a substituted habitat. Which is why a synthetic solve proves wiring and never capability."**

Effect: a school substitutes a habitat (classroom, timetable, syllabus, credential structure) for the ambient one. It can introduce ideas, methods and instruments, but it cannot exclude the ambient habitat, because contact is a fact about the world that does not consult it. What is "useful" is therefore a cascade property in the combined habitat, including what the school unintentionally introduced. A test or exam is a substituted habitat: passing it proves the wiring of that harness, never capability in the unmaintained habitat. The transfer-of-learning literature describes the bridge explicitly: "Bridging is when the teacher encourages transfer by helping students to find connections between learning and to abstract their existing knowledge to new concepts."

Mode: `general` on the asymmetry, `specified` on the claim about exams.

**Refuter:** an examination result that transfers to capability in a novel habitat with no residue loss and no unenumerated introduced actor. That would refute the harness claim.

### F8 - Handing knowledge down compounds agreement, not evidence (cross-cutting, single-source for the figure line, corroborated on outcome)

Fig 7 :12-13:

> **"same closure, handed along; agreement compounds, evidence does not"**

Fig 7 :35: **"A room's agreement is variation, not a verdict."**

Fig 7 :36: **"A chain of mirrors is one long room. The next room must differ from the last, and match the residual it surfaced."**

Effect: a tradition handed from one generation to the next is a room chain. If each generation is a mirror of the last, the canon's authority is compounded agreement, not accumulated evidence. Progress per handoff needs both bracket fidelity and ground pressure, and either at zero stops the chain. The trivium is a documented historical example of a canon that organised "basic" knowledge for centuries: "The trivium is the lower division of the seven liberal arts and comprises grammar, logic, and rhetoric." Its persistence is a fact about transmission, not a measurement that the arrangement was optimal.

Mode: `general` on the chain law, `specified` on the claim that a given canon is a mirror chain.

**Refuter:** show a scholarly tradition in which each generation was selected against the residual the last one surfaced, with low overlap between rooms and evidence compounding across handoffs. Then F8's mirror diagnosis does not apply to it.

### F9 - The ground, not the institution, decides what is authoritative (D2, triangulated)

Fig 2 :29-30, :33:

> **"An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks."**
> **"The anchor must not update."**

Fig 2 :46: **"Each frame is corrected by it separately, and what makes the correction sound is that neither frame can move it."**

Effect: "authoritative" knowledge is that which survives an anchor that does not move when the institution pushes. A credential, a citation count, or a canon's longevity are not such anchors; each moves when the institution pushes. The Stanford Encyclopedia entry on philosophy of education gives the institutional side of the same picture: "Educational institutions play key roles in systems of public knowledge, and the trust vested in them is foundational to the enterprise." Trust is exactly what must not be confused with the anchor, because trust moves with reputation.

Mode: `general` (the anchor rule), `specified` on which concrete instruments qualify in a given field.

**Refuter:** an authority that survives a deliberate attempt by the institution to move the supposed anchor, where the anchor demonstrably changed its answer. That would show it was a frame all along and confirm F9, or, if the authority survives with an anchor that genuinely does not move, F9's identification of authority with the ground would need narrowing.

### F10 - A canon of ingredients cannot name a route, and a vocabulary without negation can only accumulate (D1 and D2, triangulated)

Fig 12 :111-112:

> **"A vocabulary without bonds can name a state and cannot name a route to one."**
> **"A plan is a sequence. A goal is a comparison. Progress is a subtraction. A refutation is a negation, and that one is not a bond."**

Fig 5 :69-70: **"settling is necessary for a term to be composed over, and it is not sufficient"** and **"A term is settled when the ground pays for it. A term is usable when something can compose over it."**

Operators table :57-58: **"a refutation is a negation, which is why a vocabulary without it can only accumulate"**

Effect: when schools transmit a list of facts without the bonds between them (sequence, comparison, subtraction, negation), a learner can state what a situation contains and cannot state how to get anywhere from it or what would refute it. A curriculum that grows without a way to refuse a term produces a library that grows and a reach that does not. Fig 5 :71 states the insufficiency: "a library that grows is not a reach that grows."

Mode: `general` (structural), `specified` on the claim about a given curriculum.

**Refuter:** a body of "useful" knowledge consisting purely of states with no routes, comparisons, progress measures or refusals, which nonetheless supports novel action in a new habitat. That would break the bond claim.

## 5. Pre-registered refuter (written before the conclusion)

Required before any outreach; Fig 5 :65-66 makes the sought-for shape an ordinary term, and the seat-reasoning rule says "a falsifier written before the run is a design instrument, not only an honesty device."

The shape claim is refuted if any of the following holds:

1. **Transmission:** a body of knowledge attains "basic" standing in a society with no formal transmission institution and with the same search cost as in a schooled society. This breaks F1 and F2 together.
2. **Conferral:** a credential keeps its authority across a change of institution while being continuously checked against an anchor that demonstrably does not move when the institution pushes. This breaks F3, F4, F5 and F9.
3. **Habitat:** an item called useful transfers to a novel habitat with no loss of capability and no unenumerated introduced actor, or an exam result transfers to capability with no residue loss. This breaks F7.
4. **Closure:** a canon is final and successfully closes further inquiry, or a curriculum grows without bound yet its reach grows with it. This breaks F8, F10 and Fig 7's no-last-room rule.

If any fires, the corresponding domain verdict is withdrawn and the package is re-censused. Until one is run, no part of this may be labelled `grounded`.

## 6. Recommendation

1. Lead with the residual and the refuter, not with a theory of education. Fig 10 :52: **"Bring a characterised residual, never the source."**
2. Keep the three predicates separate in any outreach. They have different anchors, and averaging them produces exactly the aggregate that Fig 1 :19 discards.
3. State mode on every sentence. An unmeasured historical claim is a specification of what to check, not a verdict.
4. Offer the figure lines, not this summary, as the authority. Fig 8 :50-52: "Convergent derivation and adopted import look identical in the contents. Only the record of where each came from separates them."
5. Do not ask for agreement. Fig 2 :35 and Fig 2 :40 make agreement about the world worthless when it shares a pool. Ask what would refute the shape instead.
6. Send the generator (the derivation and the refuter), not the conclusion. Fig 4 :25: "A recording carried upward looks like knowledge and is a description of one occasion."

## 8. Updated verdict

The shape survives across all three domains: schooling shapes standing by controlling what makes a next search cheaper (D1), by authoring conventions that can silently become verdicts (D2), and by substituting a habitat in which usefulness is a cascade property (D3). What does not survive is the conduit reading (A), the claim that any single predicate explains the other two, and any final-canon ambition. One strain remains live and undissolved: the absolute form of Fig 10 :47 describes almost every real institution, so the operative question is whether a credential can decide outcomes while the ground stays reachable. That is an instrument question, not an argument. Strains after re-census: one, named.
