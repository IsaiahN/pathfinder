# Solution HIS-0071

**Question:** How have schools and universities shaped what societies regard as basic, authoritative, or useful knowledge?

**Source:** history of science and knowledge.txt line 88

**Section:** 8. Transmission, education, and public knowledge

**Mode:** `specified` on the substantive claims, `general` on the structural shape. No measurement settles the conferral of standing in this derivation, so nothing here is `grounded`.

**Status:** done

## What this package answers, in effect terms

The residual is the gap between the standing knowledge would have without formal schooling and the standing it has with schooling. Three candidate readings are held apart: schooling is a neutral conduit (a recording passed down), schooling is a membrane that lowers the search cost of some knowledge (a head start), or schooling is a seat that authors conventions which quietly become verdicts about the world. The derivation keeps per-domain verdicts separate and never averages them.

## Boundary and honesty

This is a simulated derivation checked for internal consistency against the figures, not a ground reading. Per *The Recursive Self-Improvement Trap*, LLM output is "simulacra" and "the truly testable output would need to be derived from the real architecture or working the formula yourself." This package cannot settle the historical question it reports on. It states what a check would have to look like. No figure quote in this folder was taken from a summary; all were checked against `FRAMEWORK/reference/03-FIGURES_TEXT.md`.

Sources used: the local canonical figure text and figures README, `THE_FORMULA.md`, and the public world sources listed in `10_source_ledger.csv`, each opened and read on 2026-11-23. No source was cited that was not read. All world sources are secondary corroboration; the figures decide.

## What is deliberately not here

- No personal emails of named individuals.
- No ranking of people by how likely they are to agree with the claim.
- No bulk-send plan.

Three figure-grounded reasons: Fig 2 :35 "Alignment is triangulation, not negotiation. A mirror cannot correct you."; Fig 2 :40 "disqualifying for a verdict: agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool"; Fig 10 :42 "A verifier that can reconstruct the claim cannot verify it." Selecting recipients for receptiveness manufactures the disqualifying correlation, and sending the full frame before asking for a verdict spends the only independence a reader contributes.
