## 1. A shape is a constraint, not a filler

The canonical figures README states the limit: the framework returns *"what a missing thing would have to look like (a shape, not a name)"*. Fig 9 :32: *"you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself."* And from Fig 13: "It can say the answer is out of reach with the library as it stands... It cannot say which primitive is missing, because naming it would mean already holding it."

From *The Library of Babel*: "you can cut your search short if the set of books returned is small and descriptive enough that it provides a general shape of the answer that satisfies your question."

So a **shape** is the narrowed form of an answer you can state before you can name the final thing. It constrains; it does not fill. For this question, the shape is: *schooling controls the cost of the next search, authors conventions that can turn into verdicts, and substitutes a habitat in which usefulness is a cascade property.* The detailed historical mechanism, with dates and places, is not supplied by the framework.

## 2. How a shape is obtained

- *Library of Babel*: bootstrap vocabulary by proximity. "With each new term gained, you narrow the space and deepen your vocabulary." Use the words you already have that are closest to the thing, then let recurring new terms narrow the search.
- Fig 6 :30: "The description travels. Fails by being sharpened into terms only we can read. Cast it in effect terms." Fig 9 :30-31: "your words are borrowed too, so describe the effect rather than the cause."
- *Decoding Tacit Knowledge*: "When contact isn't possible, the best you can send is a plain description of what you'd expect to see, because anyone can check that against the world." This is why the outreach packet leads with refuters stated as observable effects.
- *Library of Babel* on stopping: "the finish line will also be the starting place", and the paradox is that "you have already composed the answer through the process of locating it."
- *How Intelligence Actually Works*: "the mapping is the intelligence." A shape is a mapping carved down until "the recursion converges toward indiscernibility, and indiscernibility is not identity." The lineage, not the artifact, is what makes it checkable.

Applied here: the residual was stated in effect terms (the standing gap), the three predicates were separated before any frame was chosen, and only then were figures quoted. That order is required by Fig 9 :27-28: "describe it before you go looking, or any frame you pick will seem to fit."

## 3. What licenses a name

Two things, both outside the shape itself:

1. **The ground settles it.** `THE_FORMULA.md` step 5: "the ground settles it. Verified only by what pays. A gate is not the ground." The figures README says the framework "stops at the limit of what the available instruments can distinguish, and records the lineage so the stop is honest."
2. **A provenanced import from a frame whose closure differs** (Fig 6 :23, Fig 8 :50-52), with a source gate and a shadow test.

*They're Scanning Books* sharpens the second: "The real import is not a new term in the existing space. It is a new dimension of the space." And: "A minimum viable set of six terms is a human's careful guess at reality's compression axes; another sensorium's minimum set need not be those six." Also: "Declaring the primitives does not make them neutral. It makes the choice visible."

### Correction to my own reading of "shape, not a name"

I initially treated that rule as forbidding any named mechanism in this answer. That was an overstatement. The README's phrase governs a **missing** primitive, one not held in the library. Here the mechanisms named (convention, habitat, head start, filter) are already figure vocabulary. Naming them is a **binding** from a held set, not the invention of a new primitive. What remains forbidden is claiming that the binding moves the wall (Fig 6 :21: "MINT composes inside the closure and can never add an atom") or that it is a measurement. Both the earlier and corrected readings are recorded here so neither is quietly lost.

## 4. Why the "authoritative" question is the exact source error the essays warn about

*The Hard Problem is About Source* gives the mechanism. Metamerism: an instrument reads green and "it cannot comment on the source." One green light, or yellow plus blue, or RGB, or CMYK, or a scheme not yet discovered can produce the same reading. "If several paths create the same behavior, then behavior is a projection with lossy information." And: "An instrument that can't distinguish these cases demonstrates its own inadequacy." It was the spectrometer, not an argument, that resolved it: "It was proven wrong not by argument, but by the arrival of a better instrument."

Applied here: a credential, a canon's longevity, and a citation count are all readings of a projection. None comments on the source of authority. Reading authority off the credential is the metamerism error. What is owed is a discriminator that would distinguish an institution's decree from a claim that has actually paid against an unmoving ground. This is the figure verdict F4 and F9 in a different register; the essay corroborates, it does not decide.

## 5. What the population-level essays add

- *How a Society of Agents Would Work*: "capability is a property of agent-and-habitat, never of the agent alone." Also: "The group shares methods, never recordings." And a recording is "press this button at this spot." This is the D1 verdict in plain language.
- Same essay: "The shared store means the next agent does not pay again for what its predecessors already learned." This is why a curriculum is a head start, not an expense.
- *Decoding Tacit Knowledge*: "what passes down is ways of doing things and head starts, never the experience itself." And: "Writing carries the context well and the subtext partly, but it almost never carries the pretext." A textbook can carry the conclusion and cannot carry the history that made the conclusion settle. That is the F10 bond claim in cognitive vocabulary.
- *How Intelligence Actually Works*: "It can be inherited or taught, so the recipient need not pay the bill themselves, but something, somewhere, already paid it." A canon is a record of bills paid by someone else. Treating it as self-justifying loses that provenance.

## 6. Two consequences for outreach

**Prestige is not action.** From *The Recursive Self-Improvement Trap*: "Prestige can fund an action. It can give an action a budget to be tried. But it cannot settle an action. Only the ground can settle it." Also: "You may all agree and be wrong." A list of prestigious educators selected for likely agreement is the named collapse, not corroboration. The legitimate ask is a budget to be tried and an outside check against a ground.

**My own audit is a simulation, not a ground reading.** Same essay: "the output is only as good as the validator. The agent, or the tool, or the frame, or the theory, cannot validate itself." And: "the truly testable output would need to be derived from the real architecture or working the formula yourself." *Arrival of the Fittest* is blunter: the outputs generated by an LLM prompt "must be seen as simulacra" and "could never be verified if the solutions presented were not contrived to fit the formula." So `01_figure_grounded_audit.md` is a simulated derivation checked for internal consistency against the figures. It is not a test of the historical claim and must be labelled as such. It cannot settle anything it reports on.

## 7. Answer to the question this file exists to answer

For this question, "shape" means: the form any adequate answer must take before the historical detail is supplied. It must say which mechanism lowers search cost, which convention can become a verdict, which habitat makes knowledge useful, and what an anchor would have to show. It must not pretend the institution's own credential is that anchor, and it must not present a final canon. The shape binds an ordinary term, as `THE_FORMULA.md` "Direction" requires, so that it can accrue a residual and be refused.
