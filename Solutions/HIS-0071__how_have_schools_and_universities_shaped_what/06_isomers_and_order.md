## DERIVATION

### Q1 - Partition or union?

- A partition would require three disjoint mechanisms with three disjoint anchors. They are not disjoint: a credential (D2 currency) also makes a learner's next search cheaper (D1 currency), and the habitat a school substitutes (D3) is also where its conventions get tested.
- Fig 9 :56-58: "A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones. A single lever clears the region where only one condition holds and leaves the intersections standing, because the second condition still binds."
- ⇒ The three predicates form a **union**. The overlap (a credential that is also a head start, a canon that is also a habitat actor) is a compound with its own loop. Fig 9 :57-58: "What remains is smaller and it is also categorically different: a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either. It stops being a mixture and becomes a specific compound."
- **Mode:** `general`, from the figure's union rule; the specific overlaps are `specified` and unmeasured.

### Q2 - Are the three predicates isomers under Fig 12?

- Fig 12 :117: **"isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."**
- Fig 12 :124: **"functional group. A substructure whose presence fixes the outcome. The group says what kind; the qualifier says which member."**
- As the predicates are ordinarily written, they share only the institution and the knowledge item: "basic" adds the head start, "authoritative" adds the convention, "useful" adds the habitat cascade. By Fig 12's definition they are **not** isomers. They are members of the same **functional group**, standing-conferral, with different atoms in the remaining slots.
- The stronger version: write all three over the full shared atom set `{institution, learner, knowledge, habitat, ground, curriculum}` and let them differ only in which bond joins which pair. Then they are a real isomer claim: same atoms, different arrangement, different standing. That is the checkable form.
- **Falsifier, written before the run:** if any predicate requires an atom the others do not (for example, if "authoritative" cannot be written without a credential atom that "basic" and "useful" never use), the isomer claim fails and the functional-group reading stands. Recorded here before any conclusion is drawn from it.

### Q3 - Which bond governs each predicate, and is the bond forced?

Using the bond table in Fig 12 :55-89 and the Operators table:

| predicate | candidate bond | why | status |

**Is any bond forced by the figures?** No. The figures license the bonds; they do not select among them for this domain. Selecting one is a binding from the history-of-education frame, provenanced as such (Fig 8 :50-52: "Convergent derivation and adopted import look identical in the contents. Only the record of where each came from separates them."). This is a real limit, not a gap: the isomorphism claim in Q2 is checkable even though the individual bond bindings are not forced.

### Q4 - Dependency order

Fig 3 :20-21: **"The chain is ordered by dependency, not by importance. Each link consumes what the one above produces, so effort spent above the break arrives at a step that cannot use it."**

The order for this question is:

1. **Describe R in effect terms** (standing gap) before choosing a frame. Fig 9 :27-28: "describe it before you go looking, or any frame you pick will seem to fit."
2. **Refuse the conduit reading** before minting a conferral mechanism. `THE_FORMULA.md` step 2 owes a refusal first; negation is not a bond.
3. **Settle the transmission link (D1)** before asking who may confer (D2). A head start must be reapplicable before a credential can be assessed for what it makes cheaper.
4. **Keep the convention from becoming a verdict (D2)** before asking what is useful in a habitat (D3). Otherwise "useful" is just the institution's preference wearing a ground's clothes.
5. **Measure the round trip** across each handoff. Fig 4 :30: "So take the round trip and measure the gap. Without it you are navigating by dead reckoning: confident about a position nobody checked."

Fig 3 :27 warns: **"A link with no instrument is not a link that works. It is a link nobody has looked at."** The middle link here is step 3 to step 4: whether a convention has become a verdict. It has no instrument in this derivation, so it is named as unmeasured, not assumed working.

### Q5 - Analogues from the wider literature (corroboration only)

- **Trivium / quadrivium.** "The trivium is the lower division of the seven liberal arts and comprises grammar, logic, and rhetoric." A historical instance of a fixed set declared basic. Its endurance is transmission, not a measurement that the set was optimal (Fig 7 :35: "A room's agreement is variation, not a verdict.").
- **National Defense Education Act.** "Latin and Greek programs are not funded under this title, on the grounds that they are not modern foreign languages, and thus do not support defense needs." A budget filter in action, with the goal stated (Fig 9 :60: "Use filters for the budget and witnesses for the verdict. Never let a filter hand you a verdict.").
- **Bridging in transfer research.** "Bridging is when the teacher encourages transfer by helping students to find connections between learning and to abstract their existing knowledge to new concepts." The pedagogical form of Fig 4 :11, "a head start, never a recording."
- **Hidden curriculum.** "the hidden curriculum reinforces existing social inequalities through the education of students according to their class and social status." The outcome-side form of Fig 10 :47, "a convention that decides an outcome has stopped being one."

These four analogues draw on different literatures (history of curriculum, education policy, cognitive psychology, critical sociology). They are not four witnesses to the figure claim; they are four cases the shape fits. Where they would conflict with a figure, the figure governs.

## CONCLUSION

The three predicates form a **union**, not a partition, with a compound at each intersection. They are a **functional group** as ordinarily written and a real **isomer claim** when written over the full shared atom set; the isomer claim has a stated falsifier. No bond is forced by the figures, so each binding is provenance-bearing work by the history-of-education frame. The dependency order runs residual, refusal, transmission, conferral, habitat, with the convention-to-verdict link explicitly unmeasured. The analogical cases fit the shape and do not raise its status above `specified`.
