## Round trip gap R_T

The Symbols table defines R_T as the gap between what was sent up and what came back down, `gap(x, (T_E ∘ T_A)(x))`, measuring what the coarser description cannot hold. Fig 4 :30 requires it to be measured rather than assumed zero. I ran the round trip on the condensation this folder actually uses.

1. **Sent up (x):** the verbatim figure set, chiefly Fig 4 :11-12, :22-25, :34-35; Fig 10 :45-49; Fig 11 :61, :66, :70; Fig 9 :60.
2. **Abstraction (T_A):** the shorthand in `07_the_tether_answer.md`: basic equals head start, authoritative equals convention becoming verdict, useful equals habitat cascade, selection is a filter.
3. **Concretisation (T_E):** a historical claim about how actual schools and universities conferred standing.

**R_T is nonzero.** Four things the shorthand holds do not survive the trip back down:

- the **anchor condition** (Fig 2 :29-33): a convention is only distinguishable from a verdict by something that does not move when the institution pushes. The shorthand says "unchecked", which hides the instrument question;
- the **asymmetry** (Fig 11 :61): introduction yes, exclusion no. The shorthand says "habitat substitution", which can be read as full control;
- the **filter-witness distinction** (Fig 9 :60): the shorthand says "filter", which can be read as a harmless selection rather than a failure mode when promoted;
- the **no-last-room limit** (Fig 7, Fig 13): the shorthand says "not final", which can be read as a preference rather than a structural rule.

Conversely, one thing survives cleanly: a head start makes a next search cheaper, and usefulness depends on the habitat. That is the residue worth sending. R_T was **not** measured numerically, because no ground reading exists; it is recorded qualitatively as the enumerated set of dropped constraints. Treating "the formula is a condensed version of the figures" as identity would set R_T to zero and would be the dead-reckoning error Fig 4 :30 names.
