## 0. The claim under audit

> Option A: startups are more innovative than established companies.
>
> Option B: established firms have different strengths and constraints, not less innovation on one scale.

Stated as effects. Option A commits to one axis, puts both classes on it, ranks one above the other, and leads to a league table and to funding one class over the other. Option B commits to two structures in two habitats, each settled against its own ground, and leads to a division of labour plus the warning that a lever helping one kind of innovation leaves the intersections standing. The sought-for shape is bound before the run: an answer must not aggregate per-slot residuals, must attach capability to firm-plus-habitat, and must name features with opposite valences. That is Formula step "Direction" made into an ordinary term so it can be refused.

## 1. Domain census, done before any figure line is chosen

### R, the residual, by arity, symmetry and scale

- **Arity:** two conditions. "Startups" and "established companies" are not two values on one variable until one axis has been shown to settle both. The observed disagreements in the innovation literature do not shrink with effort and each measurement rule works well on a different subset, which is the figure's signature for several well-formed questions rather than one (verified below).
- **Symmetry:** the comparison is ordered, not symmetric. Asking "which is larger" is the comparison bond, but applying it here presumes the operands are two quantities on one axis. That presumption is exactly what is owed, so it cannot be assumed by the comparison.
- **Scale:** first order at the firm and its immediate habitat; second order at the population of firms and the selection between them. The membrane between scales is where "a firm is innovative" becomes "a class of firms is more innovative". Fig 4 governs the transform.

R is therefore a **union compound**, not one hard question and not a partition. Split before searching.

### One line per domain: actors, currency, anchor

| domain | actors | currency | anchor | question type |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| D1, measurement of innovation | patents, R&D counts, new products, initiation versus implementation | counts, citations, description length | a defined metric that pays on the ground for both classes | partition or union? is there one quantity? |
| D2, organizational size and habitat | hierarchy, slack, process discipline, specialization, team size | coordination cost, exploration versus exploitation | survival and adaptation in that habitat | habitat-relative capability |
| D3, verification of the comparison | researchers, meta-analyses, peer reviewers | agreement and residuals | the ground, never agreement | independence of the two frames |
| D4, outreach, after a verdict exists | expert readers of innovation studies | a correction on shape | the ground | contact, never evidence |

**No nameable anchor means it is not a domain yet.** D1 has an anchor only when a metric is defined and pays for both classes. Until then D1 is a candidate domain with an unbound anchor, and any ranking that issues from it is premature. D3's anchor is the ground, and its agreement is disqualified when its frames share an evidence pool, which the meta-analyses in this field do (they cite the same 20 to 53 empirical studies).

### Partition or union

Union. D1 splits into at least two conditions (initiation and implementation; radical and incremental; disruption and development) that do not collapse under one lever. D2 is the habitat in which each condition sits. D3 and D4 are not further domains of the claim but the checking and contact arrangements around it.

### Order by dependency (Fig 3)

1. Split the union before searching (Fig 9 :54). A reading taken before the split is a reading of nothing (Fig 3 :18 "Where a given chain stops is a reading, never a property of the chain").
2. Describe R in effect terms before choosing a frame (Fig 9 :27-28).
3. Settle the first-order condition in each habitat before attempting the second-order class comparison (Fig 4 membrane).
4. Refuse the scalar ranking before minting a policy lever (Formula step 2).

**Optimising a link that already works cannot rescue one that does not** (Fig 3 :19). Adding more firm-level measurements cannot rescue a comparison whose middle link, "one quantity called innovation", was never measured.

## 2. Findings

### F1 — Option A aggregates per-slot residuals and is refused (decisive against the scalar)

Fig 1 :19 "The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated."
Fig 1 :22 "Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average."
Fig 9 :4-5 "Disagreements that do not shrink with effort, where each rule keeps working well on a different subset, are not one hard question but several well-formed ones. Split it rather than search."

"Are startups more innovative?" reads two object classes as slots and collapses them into one global score. That is the exact operation Fig 1 forbids. The empirical literature behaves as the figure predicts: Damanpour's meta-analysis finds a positive size-innovation relation overall but reports that "types of innovation do not have a considerable moderating effect" while "size is more strongly related to the implementation than to the initiation of innovations in organizations", and Camisón-Zornoza et al. report that "the contradictory results obtained in previous studies are due to divergences in the methods used to operationalize one, or more, of the variables to be analysed." The disagreement is a split, not a single quantity measured more carefully.

**Refuter:** supply a single innovation measure that the ground settles for both classes on the same per-slot basis and on which one class dominates across the relevant habitat conditions, with residuals that trend down rather than persist. If such a measure is supplied, the aggregation was legitimate and F1 falls. Until then Option A is a category error at the level of the comparison, mode `specified`, not a false empirical finding.

### F2 — The union must be unbundled before a lever is proposed

Fig 9 :54 "And when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again."
Fig 9 :57 "a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either"
Fig 8 :39 "So a search that finds nothing is never a proof of absence."

The two conditions are initiation (new, radical, disruptive) and implementation (developing, refining, scaling). Wu, Wang and Evans, writing in *Nature* in 2019 from 65 million papers, patents and software products, report that "across this period smaller teams have tended to disrupt science and technology with new ideas and opportunities, whereas larger teams have tended to develop existing ones" and conclude that "both small and large teams are essential to a flourishing ecology of science and technology". Damanpour's 1992 abstract says the same in organizational language: size is more strongly related to implementation than to initiation. These are two conditions in one habitat, which is a union. A single lever, "fund startups more", clears the region where only initiation holds and leaves the intersection standing, because implementation still binds.

**Refuter:** show that one measure predicts both initiation and implementation with the same sign and the same ground pressure, so that a single lever moves both. Then the union collapses to a partition and F2 falls.

### F3 — Innovative capacity is firm-plus-habitat, not firm class

Fig 11 :39 "capability is a property of agent-and-habitat, never of the agent alone"
Fig 11 :40 "An improvement that does not change contact changes nothing, however much it improves."
Fig 4 :19 "Only the actors, the currency and the anchor change."

The kernel of innovating is the same at two sizes: bet, observe, mint, settle. What changes is the actors, the currency and the anchor. A startup's habitat rewards many cheap bets and high variance; an incumbent's habitat rewards refinement and the defense of an installed base. The Wikipedia account of disruptive innovation, drawing on Christensen, states that "good firms are usually aware of the innovations, but their business environment does not allow them to pursue them when they first arise, because they are not profitable enough at first". That is a claim about habitat and contact, not about the intelligence or virtue of the people in the firm. Option B's phrase "different strengths and constraints" is the figure-licensed reading of this; "less innovative" is not.

**Refuter:** show that moving the same people and the same resources between a startup habitat and an incumbent habitat leaves innovative capability unchanged. That would make capability a property of the class alone and break Fig 11 :39. Not run. Mode `specified`.

### F4 — Valence is relative to the residual, so the same feature cuts both ways

Fig 11 :49 "An actor's valence is relative to the residual, not a property it carries."
Fig 11 :51 "No standing list of good actors survives a change of residual."
Fig 11 :61 "Introduction is available; exclusion is not."

Hierarchy, slack, process discipline and specialized roles are not "good" or "bad" for innovation in general. They are benefactors to implementation and malfactors to initiation in most of the habitats studied. Hannan's research page records that "organizations face strong inertial pressures on their core features which makes selection a prime motor of population change", and that the liability of newness "found that the hazard declines with age" but that controlling for age-varying size "reverses the pattern". The same organizational feature changes sign with the residual. Fig 11 :61 also explains why the comparison is hard to fix by management decree: a seat can introduce an actor and cannot subtract the habitat, because contact is a fact about the world.

**Refuter:** show that a given structural feature has the same valence for initiation and for implementation in both habitats, measured on the same ground. Then "different strengths" collapses to "different amounts of the same strength" and F4 falls.

### F5 — The empirical magnitude is a gap here, not a silence

Fig 9 :32 "you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself"
Fig 9 :42 "narrowing gets you close, the ground decides"
Fig 13 :134 "Unreachable is a verdict here, not a silence"
Fig 13 :135 "It cannot say which primitive is missing, because naming it would mean already holding it."
Fig 8 :40 "Underivable-in-principle and unreachable-at-this-budget are different facts, and they issue different instructions."

No figure line returns the number of patents per employee at which a startup becomes "more innovative". The figures return the shape: refuse the aggregate, split the union, attach capability to habitat, name the opposite valences. The magnitude is a **gap**, and it is a gap of kind, not of budget. It is reachable by a ground reading once a metric is defined and pays for both classes. It is not unreachable in principle. Importing a metric from innovation studies to close it would need a source gate and a shadow test (Fig 8 :51-52 "Convergent derivation and adopted import look identical in the contents. Only the record of where each came from separates them, and the surplus lives only where the closures differ"), and it is debited against independence.

**Refuter:** a figure line that does return a scalar ranking of the two classes would falsify this finding by making the magnitude figure-governed after all. I ran the census and found none. "A search that finds nothing is never a proof of absence" (Fig 8 :39), so this is a recorded gap, not a theorem of absence.

### F6 — Agreement between the two literatures is one pool, not two witnesses

Fig 2 :31 "Independence, between two frames, is how little they draw on the same evidence."
Fig 7 :13 "agreement compounds, evidence does not"
Fig 2 :42 "Buy independence in proportion to the cost of being wrong."

The management literature and the science-of-science literature appear to agree that small units initiate and large units develop. But the Wu et al. paper cites March 1991 and Christensen, and the business literature cites the same meta-analyses. They are two readings of a related evidence pool, so their agreement is weak corroboration. The truly low-overlap witness is the ecological r/K literature, which was not derived from management theory at all. Wikipedia's r/K article states that "r-selected species are those that emphasize high growth rates, typically exploit less-crowded ecological niches, and produce many offspring, each of which has a relatively low probability of surviving to adulthood" and that "In stable or predictable environments, K-selection predominates as the ability to compete successfully for limited resources is crucial." That is a different closure, reached separately. It corroborates the shape, never the business number.

**Refuter:** show that the ecology frame was imported from the management frame rather than derived separately, and the independence claim collapses. Provenance is recorded in `10_source_ledger.csv`.

### F7 — Firm size is a filter for the budget, never a witness for the verdict

Fig 9 :60 "Use filters for the budget and witnesses for the verdict. Never let a filter hand you a verdict."
Fig 1 :27 "They are frame-internal. A frame cannot score itself with a quantity it also produces."

"Small" and "large" are excellent filters for where to point the next instrument: they tell you where to look for initiation and where to look for implementation. They are not the verdict. A policy that treats size as the verdict is a frame scoring itself with a quantity it also produces, because the size category was chosen before the measurement.

**Refuter:** a demonstration that the size filter itself pays on the ground for the same residual it was used to locate, with no circularity. Not supplied.

### F9 — The r/K and Nature analogies are corroboration, not derivations

Fig 8 :53 "Residual first, frame second."
Fig 9 :27 "describe it before you go looking, or any frame you pick will seem to fit"
Fig 6 :32 "An instrument is not built from a description. It is improved from a worse instrument already returning something."

The residual was described before the ecology and bibliometrics frames were chosen. r/K selection is an analogy across a different substrate and a different scale, so it carries a round-trip gap R_T. It is not a business result and must not be presented as one. Its value is precisely that it was not derived from management theory, giving it the low overlap F6 needs.

**Refuter:** if the ecology frame had been chosen first and the business residual mapped onto it afterwards, this would be echo without shadow, and F9 would be an apophenia finding rather than a corroboration. The order is recorded in the ledger.

## 3. Pre-registered refuter, written before the conclusion

Per Seat Map :287 "And a falsifier written before the run is a design instrument, not only an honesty device."

The shape claim, Option B, is refuted if any of the following holds:

1. A single measure of innovation is produced that the ground settles for both firm classes on the same per-slot basis, and on which one class dominates across the relevant habitat conditions, with a residual that trends down rather than persisting. This would make the aggregation legitimate and break F1 and F2.
2. Innovative capacity is shown to be a property of the firm class alone, so that relocating the same people and resources between habitats leaves capability unchanged. This breaks Fig 11 :39 and F3.
3. A structural feature is shown to carry the same valence for initiation and for implementation in both habitats, measured on one ground. This breaks Fig 11 :49 and F4.

If any fires, the shape claim is withdrawn and Option A is reinstated for that measure. No measurement has been run, so no part of this document is labelled `grounded`.

## 4. Recommendation

1. Split the question into at least two loops before any answer is written: initiation and implementation (F1, F2).
2. State Option B as a `specified` shape, not as an empirical verdict (F3, F5).
3. Name the structural features with opposite valences and say which residual each valence is relative to (F4).
4. Treat firm size as a filter for where to look, never as a witness for the verdict (F7).
5. Keep the r/K and Nature analogies as low-overlap corroboration with a recorded round-trip gap, not as business results (F6, F9).
6. Ask an outside reader for a failure of the shape before offering any source links. Fig 10 :52 "Bring a characterised residual, never the source."

### Seven-question recursive derivation, compressed

1. **One axis?** No. Fig 9 :4-5 and Fig 1 :19. Split the union.
2. **Which conditions?** At least initiation and implementation, licensed by Damanpour 1992 and Wu et al. 2019, which are low overlap with each other.
3. **Is capability in the class?** No. Fig 11 :39. It is in firm-plus-habitat.
4. **Do features carry fixed valence?** No. Fig 11 :49. Valence is relative to the residual.
5. **Can ⋛ rank the classes?** Not before one condition settles against a ground. Fig 12 :87, F2 strain.
6. **Is the magnitude figure-governed?** No. Gap (F5). Reachable by a ground reading, not figure-derivable.
7. **Route?** REFUSE the scalar before minting a policy lever (Formula step 2). No atom is minted; the reach falls because a refusal was added to the record.

### Updated verdict

The shape survives: the question is a union, capability is firm-plus-habitat, valence is relative to the residual, and both kinds are needed on different conditions. The scalar claim "startups are more innovative" does not survive as a figure-licensed verdict; it is refused as an aggregation, and a numerical ranking is a recorded `gap`. After re-census, one strain is named and kept (⋛'s operand type before settlement), none hidden. See `07_the_tether_answer.md`, `09_watson_answer.md`, and the independent re-census in `11_audit.md`.
