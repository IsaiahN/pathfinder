# Solution BUS-0049

**Question:** Are startups more innovative than established companies, or do established firms simply have different strengths and constraints?

**Source:** business.txt line 65

**Section:** 5. Entrepreneurship and startups

**Mode:** `specified` on the substantive claims, `general` on the structural shape. No innovation metric settles the comparison for both firm classes in one ground reading here, so nothing in this folder is `grounded`.

**Status:** done

**Registry status:** pending

## What this question decides, stated as an effect

Option A says "startups are more innovative": this commits to one scalar axis, puts both firm classes on it, and ranks one above the other. It leads to a league table and to policy that funds one class over the other. Option B says "different strengths and constraints": this commits to two structures in two habitats, each with its own ground and its own valence, and refuses the single ranking. It leads to a division of labour and to a warning that a lever which helps one kind of innovation will leave the other standing (Fig 9 :54 "And when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again").

What an answer would look like, bound as an ordinary term before the run (Formula step "Direction"): a shape that (a) does not aggregate per-slot residuals into one number, (b) attaches innovative capacity to firm-plus-habitat rather than to firm class alone, and (c) names which structural features have opposite valences for initiation versus implementation. That shape is written down and then tested, so the preference is refusable rather than hidden.

## What is deliberately not here

- No personal emails of named researchers.
- No ranking of people by how likely they are to agree with Option B.
- No bulk-send plan.

Three reasons, in the figures' own words:

1. **A room does not upgrade the label.** Fig 7 :13 "same closure, handed along; agreement compounds, evidence does not" and Seat Map :283 "A room does not upgrade the label". Adding agreeable readers raises the vocabulary and does not add an anchor.
2. **Selecting for agreement manufactures the disqualifying correlation.** Fig 2 :40 "agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool". A list ranked by receptiveness is a list selected to agree.
3. **Asking for trust before checking spends independence.** Fig 10 :42 "A verifier that can reconstruct the claim cannot verify it." The first contact carries the residual and its refuter, never the framework's interiors.

Legally, scraping personal emails for unsolicited bulk outreach breaches GDPR and CAN-SPAM-style rules in many jurisdictions. That is a practical failure on top of the structural one.

## Honest standing of this folder

This is a simulated derivation, checked for internal consistency against the figures, NOT a ground reading. Fig 11 :36 "Where nothing maintains anything, intelligence is a label applied afterwards to a lineage that met its ground. Nothing was intelligent; selection ran." And from the author's own essay: "LLMs can quickly simulate an approximate answer/output... the truly testable output would need to be derived from the real architecture or working the formula yourself." This folder therefore cannot settle whether startups are in fact more innovative. It says what shape a checkable answer must have, and where the ground would have to be read.
