## 1. A shape is a constraint, not a filler

The figures README states the limit: the framework returns "what a missing thing would have to look like (a shape, not a name)". Fig 9 :32 "you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself". Fig 13 :134 "Unreachable is a verdict here, not a silence", and Fig 13 :135 "It cannot say which primitive is missing, because naming it would mean already holding it."

From *The Library of Babel*: "This means that you can cut your search short if the set of books returned is small and descriptive enough that it provides a general shape of the answer that satisfies your question."

So a **shape** is the narrowed form of an answer you can state before you can name it. It constrains; it does not fill. For this question, "the two conditions must be split and each settled in its own habitat" is a shape. "Startups score 7.4 and incumbents score 6.1 on innovation" is a name, and the framework cannot produce it from the figures.

## 2. How a shape is obtained here

- *Library of Babel*, on the searcher's ignorance: "Your search space will narrow with every vocabulary word that you believe is in the answer or associated with the answer. This brings your search of unlimited books to a finite sum."
- *Library of Babel*, on proximity: "If you don't know the right terms to search for, you will have to use the existing vocabulary you do know that is closest to the words that you wish to describe and grasp the shape of."
- *Library of Babel*, on stopping: "You will know that you are done because the finish line will also be the starting place, as there is nowhere else to search and nothing else that can describe the answer better."
- Fig 9 :27 "describe it before you go looking, or any frame you pick will seem to fit" and Fig 9 :30 "your words are borrowed too, so describe the effect rather than the cause".

For BUS-0049 the effect is: "a ranking does not resolve, each measurement rule works on a different subset, and the same structural feature helps one outcome and hurts another." That effect was described before the size-innovation meta-analyses, the science-of-science bibliometrics, and the ecology analogue were chosen. The order is recorded in `10_source_ledger.csv` and matters for F9 of `01_figure_grounded_audit.md`.

## 3. The question as a bundle, in the essays' own words

*The Library of Babel* names this exact failure: "Perhaps your problem was a bundle or conflation of several problems masquerading as one? You will need to search all of those problems individually to find their answers and then combine them in a satisfactory way."

This is the single most direct essay-grounded statement for BUS-0049. "Are startups more innovative?" is a bundle of at least two questions (initiation and implementation) masquerading as one. The figure-level version is Fig 9 :4 "are not one hard question but several well-formed ones. Split it rather than search." The two sources are low overlap in wording but share the framework author, so they are one evidence pool, not two witnesses.

## 4. What licenses a name

Only two things, both outside the shape itself:

1. **The ground settles it.** Formula step 5: "the ground settles it. Verified only by what pays. A gate is not the ground." For this question, a defined metric that pays for both classes would name the magnitude.
2. **A provenanced import from a frame whose closure differs**, with a source gate and a shadow test. Fig 8 :51 "Convergent derivation and adopted import look identical in the contents. Only the record of where each came from separates them, and the surplus lives only where the closures differ."

*They're Scanning Books* is not quoted here because I did not re-read it this session. The principle it carries in the canonical set, that a real import is a new dimension rather than a new term in the existing space, is consistent with Fig 6 :22 "IMPORT adds an atom, and the added atom is what expands the closure." A patent-count metric imported from innovation studies into the framework adds an atom to the frame; it does not change the world's primitives.

## 5. Why the scalar ranking is the specific forbidden error

*The Hard Problem is About Source* gives the mechanism. An instrument reads green and "cannot comment on the source"; one green light, or yellow plus blue, or RGB, or CMYK, or a scheme not yet discovered can produce the same reading. "If several paths create the same behavior, then behavior is a projection with lossy information". And: "An instrument that can't distinguish these cases demonstrates its own inadequacy."

Applied here: a count of patents is a reading. It cannot tell you whether the source was initiation or implementation, exploration or exploitation, a habitat that rewards many cheap bets or one that rewards refining an installed base. Several sources can produce the same count. Naming "innovativeness" from the count is the metamerism error: a source named from a projection, where several sources could give the same reading. What is owed is a discriminating instrument, not a league table.

The essay's own resolution is relevant: "It was proven wrong not by argument, but by the arrival of a better instrument." So the honest next step for BUS-0049 is to specify an instrument that discriminates initiation from implementation, not to argue harder about which class wins.

## 6. Q: what shape does a checkable answer have here?

**Answer.** A checkable answer must (a) split the bundle rather than rank the bundle, (b) attach capability to firm-plus-habitat, (c) name structural features with opposite valences for the two conditions, and (d) state what instrument would settle the magnitude. It must not present a scalar ranking as figure-derived. Until a discriminating instrument is read against the ground, the magnitude is a `gap`, and "gap is a verdict here, not a silence".

**Does the common framing commit the error the README forbids?** Yes, in two ways. First it aggregates per-slot residuals into one global score (Fig 1 :19, :22). Second it names a source, "startup innovativeness", from a projection, a count, that cannot discriminate its sources. Both are recorded in F1 and F5 of `01_figure_grounded_audit.md`.

## 7. Correction to my own §1 to §4

I initially wrote §4 as if "shape, not a name" forbade using any specific number or any specific organizational feature at all. That overstated the rule. The README's phrase governs "what a **missing** thing would have to look like", that is a primitive not held in the library. Initiation, implementation, slack and hierarchy are **not** missing primitives of this frame; they are bindings from the innovation-studies and organizational-size frames onto things already describable. Naming them is a binding with provenance, not the invention of a seventh term. What remains wrong is a league table presented as figure-derived, because that is a name read off a projection without a discriminator. Both readings are kept so neither is quietly lost.

## 8. Two consequences for outreach

**Prestige is not action.** From *The Recursive Self-Improvement Trap*: "Prestige must never be allowed to validate an action directly... Someone let prestige stand in for proof. Someone let reputation substitute for result. Someone let the trusted name validate the untested claim." And: "Prestige can fund an action. It can give an action a budget to be tried. But it cannot settle an action. Only the ground can settle it." A list of famous management thinkers who agree with Option B is the named collapse, not corroboration. The legitimate ask is a budget for the claim to be tried against a defined ground.

**This audit is a simulation, not a ground reading.** Same essay: "the output is only as good as the validator. The agent, or the tool, or the frame, or the theory, cannot validate itself." And: "LLMs can quickly simulate an approximate answer/output... the truly testable output would need to be derived from the real architecture or working the formula yourself." So `01` is a simulated derivation checked for internal consistency against the figures. It is not a test of the claim and must be labelled as such. It cannot settle whether startups are in fact more innovative.
