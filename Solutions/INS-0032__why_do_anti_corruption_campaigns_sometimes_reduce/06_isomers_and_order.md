## Q1 - Are two campaigns with the same formal atoms isomers?

> "isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour." (Fig 12 :117)
> "functional group. A substructure whose presence fixes the outcome. The group says what kind; the qualifier says which member." (Fig 12 :124)
> "Symmetry is what decides whether an arrangement is a new thing." (Fig 12 :92)
> "A set of ingredients is not one. The bond is the rest of the information, and it is the half nothing was recording." (Fig 12 :5)

Two campaigns with an identical statute book, an identically named agency and an identical budget have the same atoms. If their bonds differ, that is, if one bonds its agency to an independent evidence source and a court that can refuse the executive, while the other bonds it to the appointing executive itself, they are **isomers** under Fig 12 :117: same atoms, different bond, different behaviour, with the difference invisible in the formal description. This is the figure-grounded version of the intuition that "it is not what is on the statute book, it is who can check whom", but it is stronger: the arrangement is itself a substance and carries information the ingredient list does not.

If the campaigns share only a pattern (`law -> investigation -> sanction`) while differing in which atoms occupy the slots, the weaker claim holds: they are members of one **functional group** (Fig 12 :124), the qualifier saying which member. The presence of the pattern fixes the kind, not the outcome.

The distinction is testable. A statute-book comparison that finds no difference between two campaigns cannot distinguish isomer from functional-group; the bond has to be recorded as its own datum. In the Chinese case the relevant bond is named by the record: *"Investigations by the party's disciplinary bodies are not part of the state judicial system"*, while Hu Xingdou's proposed second stage would add *"independent anti-corruption organs"* (Wikipedia, Due process and Positive change sections, cited from Deutsche Welle 2015). The first is a bond to the appointing body; the second is a bond to a differing closure. U4's Benin record makes the same contrast: agencies *"placed under the authority of the executive branch"* and *"susceptible to political influence and capture"* (U4 2026).

**Falsifier, written before the run:** if two campaigns with identical atoms and identical bonds nevertheless diverge in outcome with no difference in ground contact or imported evidence, the isomer framing is insufficient and the difference must be habitat (D2), which would move the explanation out of D3.

**Status:** `specified`, triangulated for the legality of the reading (Fig 12 and Fig 11 :50, different figures), single-source for the institutional application. The world corroborations are from anti-corruption research, one pool, so they count as one witness for the binding.

## Q2 - What order do the conditions bind in?

> "The chain is ordered by dependency, not by importance. Each link consumes what the one above produces, so effort spent above the break arrives at a step that cannot use it." (Fig 3 :20-21)
> "Optimising a link that already works cannot rescue one that does not." (Fig 3 :19)

Order for a campaign facing a corrupt habitat:

1. **Perception.** Can it read corruption at all? Failure here is visible and has obvious tests: complaint data, audit findings, procurement anomalies.
2. **Vocabulary.** Does its Γ have terms for what kind of corruption it is facing, and can it say what would count as progress there? This is the middle link. Fig 3 :26: "The link between them, having terms for what a situation offers and being able to say what would count as progress, has neither, so it attracts the least attention and the fewest instruments."
3. **Objective.** Can it state a comparison that would count as getting somewhere? Fig 12 :112: "A plan is a sequence. A goal is a comparison. Progress is a subtraction." A vocabulary with only arrest counts cannot state a goal about corruption incidence.
4. **Planning.** A route to that objective under incomplete information.
5. **Learn and carry.** Name what worked, verify it, carry it to the next campaign.

The two conditions from the primary audit map onto this chain: C1 (anchor independence) is mostly a failure of links 2 and 3, where a campaign can count arrests but cannot say what would count as corruption reduction; C2 (reach renewal) is mostly a failure of link 5, where the campaign names what worked but never imports a new atom from a differing closure. That mapping is why a single lever cannot clear both (Fig 9 :55).

The order also decides which instrument to buy first. Fig 3 :27: "A link with no instrument is not a link that works. It is a link nobody has looked at." The arrest-count link (1) has abundant data; link 5 has almost none. So budget piles up at links 1 and 4 while the break sits at 2 and 5. The U4 Ukraine review states the same practical asymmetry: practitioners prioritise tools observable to external observers, such as transparency measures and citizen engagement, while inward-oriented tools such as audit and risk management tend to be neglected even though they may be just as important (U4 2026).

**Falsifier:** a campaign whose chain demonstrably works at 1, 2 and 3 and fails only at 4. That would falsify the "usually middle" claim of F7 in the primary audit.

No candidate is discarded by prestige or familiarity. Each is ranged against the same residual, in the order Fig 8 :53 requires. The point is not to pick one; it is to notice that the first two are in one evidence pool (Fig 2 :31) and that the low-overlap fields corroborate the mechanism, not the campaign-level fact.

## Q4 - Does the shape generate the literature's answers?

Partly. The shape can generate:

- the purge reading, as C1 failure with a self-authored definition that decides outcomes (Fig 10 :47);
- the institution-building reading, as C2 when the reform genuinely imports a differing closure with a source gate and shadow test;
- the state-capture concern, as the habitat that makes the metric updatable;
- the metric-gaming finding, as channel decay under a single saturated target (Fig 10 :26);
- the "no universal laws" practitioner finding, as the union's refusal of a single lever (Fig 9 :55).

It cannot generate the *name* of the campaign that is a purge, or the identity of the institution that will serve as an independent ground in a named country. That is the `gap` recorded in F10 of `01`. It is a gap at the instrument boundary, not a silence: Fig 13 :134 ("Unreachable is a verdict here, not a silence,") and Fig 8 :39 apply. The source essay states why: an instrument that reads a result *"cannot comment on the source"*, and *"the answer is unreached until a better instrument exists"*.

## CONCLUSION

Two campaigns with the same formal atoms but different bonds are isomers under Fig 12 :117; two sharing only a `law -> investigation -> sanction` pattern are members of one functional group under Fig 12 :124. The conditions bind in dependency order, with the middle links (vocabulary and objective) and the carry link the usual breaks, which is why C1 and C2 need different instruments. The standard explanations are mostly bindings of one evidence pool and are generated by the shape only up to the point where a name would be required. The isomer claim has a written falsifier; the order claim has one; the naming gap is declared rather than filled.
