## The question restated as an effect

What is being decided is not whether anti-corruption campaigns work on average. It is why the same class of action produces two opposite regime outcomes. In effect terms: a governing authority holds a belief b about what enforcement will return, takes action a (investigations, arrests, seizures, institutional reform), its institutional library predicts Γ(b,a), and the world returns o′. R = |Γ(b,a) - o′| is measured per campaign and per targeted office, never averaged into a global verdict (Fig 1 :19, Fig 1 :22).

- Option A, "the campaign's effect is a property of the campaign itself": predicts that a campaign with enough laws and investigators reduces corruption wherever it is applied.
- Option B, "the campaign's effect is a relation between the instrument and the habitat it is ranged against": predicts that the campaign reduces corruption when its success measure is a ground that does not move when the regime pushes on it and when its reach includes contact with a closure that differs from the regime's own, and that it becomes a purge when either condition fails (Fig 2 :29, Fig 6 :23, Fig 11 :39).

- What an answer would look like, written down and bound as an ordinary term before the run (Formula, "Direction"): a pair of conditions whose conjunction keeps a campaign on the reduction side of the divide, plus an instrument that discriminates a genuine reduction from a factional removal when both produce the same observable reading (arrests, approval, falling perception scores). Until bound, that shape predicts nothing and no check in the loop can see it. It is now bound, so the refuter in `01` section 4 can fire on it.

The verdict recorded in `09_watson_answer.md` is Option B in `specified` mode, kept per domain and never averaged.

## What is deliberately not here

- No personal emails of named individuals.
- No ranking of people by how likely they are to agree.
- No bulk-send plan.
- No claim that this derivation is `grounded`, no claim that it settles the anti-corruption question, and no claim that the framework is the only true account of corruption and cleanup.

Three figure-grounded reasons, plus one practical one:

1. A room is not an anchor. *"A room’s agreement is variation, not a verdict. Selection between rooms is where the ground votes."* (Fig 7 :35) and *"Alignment is triangulation, not negotiation. A mirror cannot correct you."* (Fig 2 :35). Gathering agreeable readers manufactures the correlation that disqualifies a verdict.
2. Correlation has valence. *"disqualifying for a verdict: agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool"* (Fig 2 :40). A list selected for receptiveness is one witness wearing many names.
3. A verifier must be outside the interior. *"A verifier that can reconstruct the claim cannot verify it."* (Fig 10 :42). The first contact therefore carries the residual and its refuter, not the framework.
4. Legally, unsolicited bulk email to scraped addresses breaches GDPR and CAN-SPAM style rules in many jurisdictions and gets the sending domain blacklisted. Public opt-in routes avoid that failure as well as the structural one.
