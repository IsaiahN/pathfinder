**Subject:** One checkable claim about atom interferometry and gravity, and what would refute it

> Hi [name],
>
> I have one specific output I would like you to check against what you already know. I am not asking you to read any theory first, and I am not asking you to agree with it. If the output does not survive, there is nothing else to look at.
>
> **The residual, in effect terms.** An atom interferometer splits a matter wave into two spatially separated arms, lets gravity act differently on the two arms, and recombines them. The output fringe is a relative phase attributable to gravity. Two different questions hide inside that sentence: (1) is the phase a genuine measurement of gravity acting on a quantum state, and (2) does the phase decide whether gravity itself is quantum, rather than a classical background for quantum matter? I keep these two verdicts separate.
>
> **The output, stated with its mode.** `(grounded)` For question 1, yes. The fringe is a real, non-updating anchor: atom interferometers measure a gravitational phase on quantum matter, and atomic tests of the weak equivalence principle now approach the precision of classical tests while using genuinely quantum probes such as internal-state superpositions and bosons against fermions. `(gap)` For question 2, no. The measured phase does not identify its own source. The experimenters themselves write that they "will not be excluding all possible models for which the EP does not hold, as such models may also predict the same phase" (Dobkowski et al, 2025/2026). A review puts it plainly: "the same experiment can have different interpretations and physical implications depending on the results and on the underlying theoretical model" (Tino, 2021).
>
> **The shape I am offering.** The framework I use returns a shape, not a name. The shape of what is missing here is a **source-discriminating interferometric observable**: a measured quantity whose predicted value differs between a classical gravitational channel and a quantum one while the phase-only readings agree. I am not claiming that any existing interferometer has measured one. I am claiming that the phase measurement has a proto-instrument we can sharpen from, and that the missing observable is reachable rather than unreachable. A Snowmass white paper records the same open question: "If we observe entanglement generation through the Newton potential, is there a precise sense in which this actually demonstrates that the gravitational field is quantized?"
>
> **What I am not claiming.** Not that atom interferometry has already shown gravity is quantum. Not that gravitationally induced entanglement alone proves quantised gravity: a 2026 clocks proposal states the converse, that "introducing quantized gravitational degrees of freedom is neither required nor implied by gravity induced entanglement." Not a theory of everything, not a derivation of general relativity from quantum mechanics, and not a new primitive. Nothing here is asserted as settled by experiment except the phase readings themselves.
>
> **What would refute the shape claim.** I wrote these down before sending this:
> 1. A measured observable separates a classical gravitational channel from a quantum one while the phase agrees, with both predictions stated in advance. My `gap` closes and I withdraw the claim.
> 2. Two interferometers with different internal quantum states but identical trajectories return different gravitational phase per unit mass. The equivalence-principle reading fails.
> 3. A non-entangling local classical model reproduces every proposed entanglement observable, including clock-energy proposals. The discriminator shape collapses.
> 4. One observable is shown to both measure the phase and identify its source. My two-condition split is wrong.
>
> A one-line "this fails because..." is more useful to me than agreement. If you would rather not engage, no reply is needed.
>
> **If it survives, and only then:** the source links are below. The figures, not my summary, are the authority.
> - Canonical figure text with line numbers (local extract): `FRAMEWORK/reference/03-FIGURES_TEXT.md`
> - Figures and formula for direct download (branch `general-agent`): https://github.com/IsaiahN/tether/tree/general-agent/figures
> - Review of the experimental field: https://iopscience.iop.org/article/10.1088/2058-9565/abd83e

Write down what would refute your result *before* you run it, and state the mode of what you get. From the architecture essay: "the truly testable output would need to be derived from the real architecture or working the formula yourself." A reported failure is more useful to me than a reported success.

## Two-stage offer, explicit

1. **Stage 1:** send the residual, the two verdicts, and the refuter. Ask what the recipient would count as a failure. Record their verdict before showing any framework material.
2. **Stage 2, opt-in only:** if they are curious, offer the links. Their later agreement is never pooled as corroboration (Fig 2 :40).
3. **Stage 3:** offer the formula as a method to reuse, with the instruction that each user writes their own refuter and states their own mode.

**Hard precondition:** stages 1 to 3 only work if the stage-1 output survives. The grounded/gap split is that output. If it is blurred, the offer collapses on contact.
