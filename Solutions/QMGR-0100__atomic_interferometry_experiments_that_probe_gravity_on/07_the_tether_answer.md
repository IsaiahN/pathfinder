# The Tether's answer on atom interferometry and gravity on quantum states

**Mode: `grounded` on the phase measurements, `specified` on the structural shape, `gap` on the quantum-gravity inference.** Simulated derivation checked against the figures, not a ground reading (`05_what_shape_means.md` §6). Verdicts are kept per domain and never averaged.

## 1. The residual it answers

An atom interferometer splits a matter wave, lets gravity act differently on the two arms, and recombines them. The fringe is a relative phase. Hidden inside the question are two conditions: (A) is the phase a real measurement of gravity acting on a quantum state; (B) does the phase decide whether gravity itself is quantum. Fig 9 names this a union compound: a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either. One loop measures a fringe; a different loop identifies the source of the fringe.

## 2. The shape, per domain

**D1, measurement (verdict: grounded).** The fringe is a genuine anchor. Fig 2 :29 states the rule: "An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks. A measurement, an experiment, a counter, a market, a patient outcome." Atom interferometers measure a gravitational phase on quantum matter, and the QGI experiment confirmed the free-fall gauge phase while explicitly declining to exclude models that predict the same phase. This is a real, non-updating reading. Verdict: `grounded`.

**D2, composition (verdict: general structural).** Fig 13 :74: "Everything else is a composition across the two kinds, never within one." Fig 13 :75: "Two states never bond directly. It is not space-time but space, a structure, time; and two structures join only through a state." The interferometer parses as `Space → Gradient → Time` for the arms, `Energy → Spectrum → Time` for the internal-state readout, with a Network geometry and a ⋛ comparison of two phase quantities at the output. No direct state-state or structure-structure bond. Verdict: `general`, a legal cross-kind composition; which structure occupies the slot at the source level is not named by the framework.

**D3, verification (verdict: specified, bounded).** Fig 2 :31: "Two people reading the same report are not two frames; two instruments pointed at the same thing are." Atomic WEP tests and classical gravimeters are two instruments, so their agreement on the equivalence principle is meaningful triangulation. But agreement among physicists is not an anchor, and in this simulated audit all seats draw on one evidence pool, so their agreement carries no independent weight. Verdict: `specified` for the triangulation, `single-source` for the simulation.

**D4, the discriminator (verdict: gap, reachable).** The phase cannot comment on its own source. Fig 9 :32: "you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself." The missing thing is a **source-discriminating interferometric observable**: a measured quantity whose value differs between a classical gravitational channel and a quantum one while the phase-only readings agree. Fig 6 :32 gives the reachability rule: "An instrument is not built from a description. It is improved from a worse instrument already returning something." Atom interferometry already returns a phase that fails to resolve the source, so there is an edge to extend from. Verdict: `gap` on the inference, `specified` on the reachability of the shape.

The three discriminator programs in the literature (atom-interferometer GIE, massive-object GIE, clock-energy GIE) are not isomers as written; they are a functional group, which is a structural result in `06_isomers_and_order.md`. None has yet produced the discriminating reading.

## 3. What it explicitly does not say

- Not that atom interferometry has already shown gravity is quantum. The measured phase does not identify its source, and the experimenters say so.
- Not that gravitationally induced entanglement alone proves quantised gravity. A 2026 proposal states that introducing quantised gravitational degrees of freedom is "neither required nor implied by gravity induced entanglement."
- Not that the equivalence principle is violated. Current atomic tests are consistent with it; a violation would be a refuter, not a prediction.
- Not a theory of everything. No final room, per Fig 7 and the canonical README.
- Not a derivation of one theory from another, and no new primitive.
- Not grounded for condition B. Only the phase readings themselves are grounded.

## 4. What would settle it — the ground

A source-discriminating instrument, not a further argument. From *The Hard Problem is About Source*: an instrument that reads green "cannot comment on the source"; it was the spectrometer, not an argument, that resolved the case. Here the corresponding questions are:

1. **Channel test:** is there a measured observable on which classical and quantum gravitational channels give different predictions while the phase agrees? This closes the `gap`.
2. **Quantum universality of free fall:** do two interferometers with different internal quantum states but identical trajectories return the same gravitational phase per unit mass? A difference refutes the grounded equivalence-principle reading.
3. **Entanglement interpretation:** does an observed entanglement signal survive the non-entangling classical-channel accounts that Carney et al and Tobar et al keep open? If it does not, the discriminator shape collapses.
4. **Substrate test:** derive one of the six terms from the other five, or admit an unrefusable state-state or structure-structure composition. That breaks the structural shape.

## 5. The honest stop

**Unreached, not unreachable.** The missing thing has a nameable shape but no name: an observable, not a theory. The six-term set is "a human's careful guess at reality's compression axes"; declaring it makes the choice visible, not neutral. The answer stops at the limit of what the available instruments distinguish and records the lineage so the stop is checkable. "Indiscernibility is not identity." Importing a theory name against a budget limit would add an atom that was not needed (Fig 8 :40-43).

**Round trip.** Fig 4 :30: "So take the round trip and measure the gap. Without it you are navigating by dead reckoning: confident about a position nobody checked." The condensation from "we measure a phase" to "we test quantum gravity" drops the source-discrimination requirement. R_T is nonzero and enumerated in `11_audit.md`.

## 6. One-paragraph version for outreach

> The Tether does not say that atom interferometry has shown gravity is quantum. It says two separate things. First, the fringe is a genuine measurement: an interferometer splits a quantum matter wave, gravity acts differently on the two arms, and the recombined output is a real, non-updating phase reading, so gravity does act on quantum states in a measurable way. Second, that phase does not identify its own source: classical, semiclassical and quantum gravitational couplings can predict the same phase, and the experimenters themselves say they are not excluding models that do. So the honest verdict is grounded for the measurement and a gap for the quantum-gravity inference. What the framework returns is a shape, not a name: the missing thing is an observable on which classical and quantum channels separate while the phase-only readings agree. That instrument is reachable, because interferometry already returns a phase that fails to resolve the source; the current entanglement and clock proposals are attempts to build it. The answer stops there, with the falsifiers written down in advance, until a source-discriminating measurement settles it.
