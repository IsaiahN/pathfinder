## DERIVATION

### Q1 — What is the composition an atom interferometer performs?

Fig 13 :74: "Everything else is a composition across the two kinds, never within one." Fig 13 :75: "Two states never bond directly. It is not space-time but space, a structure, time; and two structures join only through a state."

Read the interferometer as a set of molecules:

- **Gravimeter arm sequence:** `Space (arm separation) → Gradient (potential difference) → Time (free-fall interval)`. One crossing, legal. Fig 13 :76: "A gradient over space and one over time are different terms, because which state it is bonded to is part of what it is." A gravimeter and a clock interferometer are different molecules, not two names for one.
- **Internal-state readout:** `Energy (internal state) → Spectrum (laser or microwave frequency) → Time (pulse timing)`. One crossing, legal. This is what makes an atom a clock as well as a test mass.
- **Geometry:** the beam splitters and mirrors form a `Network` structure that holds the arms in relation; the pulse order is a `→` sequence, and the final population difference is a `⋛` comparison of two quantities (the two arm phases).

No state bonds directly to a state, and no structure to a structure without a state between. The composition is legal under Fig 13. The important consequence is that the phase is **not** a primitive and not a direct space-time bond: a structure sits in the slot, exactly as in the exemplar's spacetime reading, but here the structure is not named by the framework. Whether it is ordering-like (spectrum) or directedness-like (gradient) at the source level is what the discriminator would settle.

**Falsifier, written before the run:** exhibit an interferometer geometry admitted by the framework in which two states or two structures bond with no intervening opposite-kind term and cannot be refused. Then Fig 13 does not govern this domain and the derivation fails.

### Q2 — Is the readout a comparison bond?

Operators table: ⋛ is "a threshold relation between two quantities." Fig 12 :46: "A bond is the typed relation between two operands. There are seven, they are not interchangeable, and which one holds is not recoverable from the operands."

At recombination the interferometer compares two path phases. The operands are quantities (phases), the relation is threshold-like (which output port, which population sign), and the bond is ⋛. This is a clean first-order type. Contrast with the exemplar, where ⋛ was applied to two structures and strained; here it is applied to two quantities as the table requires.

**Strain, named:** if one tries to lift ⋛ to compare two settled molecules-now-atoms at a higher level (for example a whole interferometer against another whole interferometer as quantities), Fig 12 :104 permits the demotion to atom, but whether an atom counts as a quantity at that level needs an instrument. This strain is recorded, not hidden. It does not bear on the first-order readout.

### Q3 — Are the discriminator proposals isomers or a functional group?

Fig 12 :117: "Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour." Fig 12 :124: "functional group. A substructure whose presence fixes the outcome. The group says what kind; the qualifier says which member."

Consider the three proposals read in this folder:

| proposal | atoms used | what occupies the structure slot | bond emphasis |
|---|---|---|---|
| Atom-interferometer GIE (Howl et al) | two atom interferometers, gravity, atom number | gravitational potential between the arms of different interferometers | ⋛ on number correlations at output ports |
| Levitated-mass GIE (Bose et al / Carney et al, as reviewed) | massive superposed objects, gravity, spin | Newton potential between superposed masses | → interaction then ⋛ Bell-type witness |
| Clock-energy GIE (Tobar et al) | levitated particle, array of nuclear clocks, internal energy | relativistic mass-energy contribution to active gravitational mass | ⋛ on clock visibility or population |

As the proposals are written they do **not** share one atom set: the first uses atom number and no internal clock energy; the second uses spin and macroscopic mass; the third uses internal energy and active gravitational mass. By Fig 12's definition they are **not isomers** as written. They are members of one **functional group**: each is a `state → structure → state` composition that produces a source-discriminating comparison, where the group says what kind (a cross-kind composition ending in ⋛ on a non-phase observable) and the qualifier says which member (atom number, spin, or internal energy).

The stronger isomer claim would require writing all three over the full shared atom set `{Space, Time, Energy, Gradient, Spectrum, Network}` and showing that they differ only in which structure mediates which state pair. That is a real checkable claim, but it is not what the papers currently assert. Tobar et al in fact argue that the clock proposal probes a genuinely relativistic effect unavailable to the spatial-superposition proposals, which is precisely a different substance, not a different spelling of one.

**Falsifier, written before the run:** if any two proposals can be written with identical atoms and identical bonds and still give different source-discrimination predictions, the functional-group reading fails and the isomer reading must be attempted. If all three can be written over the full shared atom set with only the mediating structure varying and no behavioural difference, the functional-group reading is withdrawn in favour of the isomer reading.

### Q4 — What order is the search?

Fig 12 :104: "A settled molecule becomes an atom for whatever composes over it. So irreducible is a fact about a level and never about the world." The phase measurement is settled against the ground, so it can serve as an atom at the next level. The GIE proposals are compositions **over** that settled atom, not imports that add a primitive (Fig 6 :26). That is why they are reachable.

Dependency order (Fig 3 :20: "The chain is ordered by dependency, not by importance"): settle the phase (link 1 to 2), then say what would count as progress (link 3, the source-discriminating observable), then plan the platform (link 4). Reading the proposals before binding the shape is the apophenia the exemplar names: Fig 9 :27-28, "describe it before you go looking, or any frame you pick will seem to fit."

Fig 8 :50: "Two frames can hold the same atom by convergence, reaching it separately, which is corroboration at maximum independence." The atom-interferometer and clock proposals reach the same comparison atom by different closures, so if either succeeds it is corroboration at high independence, provided provenance is recorded and the evidence pools are not shared.

## CONCLUSION

The interferometer is a legal cross-kind composition: space and time are joined through a gradient, internal energy is read through a spectrum, the geometry is a network, the pulse order is →, and the readout is ⋛ between two phase quantities. The readout bond is well typed at first order; the higher-level ⋛ strain is named.

The three discriminator proposals are **not isomers as written**; they are a **functional group** of `state → structure → state` compositions ending in a comparison on a non-phase observable. Their union is second-order, licensed by Fig 12 :104 rather than by any new primitive. This rescues what is defensible in the field's language without the part that fails: the shape (a source-discriminating comparison) needs no new atom and no claim that a phase already proves quantised gravity. Whether any member of the group actually discriminates the source is a ground question, still open.
