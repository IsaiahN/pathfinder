## Restating the question as an effect

An atom interferometer splits a matter wave along two spatially separated paths, lets gravity act differently on the two paths, and recombines them. The output fringe is a phase difference attributable to gravity. Two things are being decided, and they must not be averaged:

1. Does a measured gravitational phase on a quantum matter wave count as gravity acting on a quantum state? (It does, and the measurement is a genuine anchor.)
2. Does such a measurement distinguish a quantum gravitational coupling from a semiclassical one? (It does not, and the instrument that would is not yet built.)

What an answer would look like is bound as an ordinary term before the run: a **source-discriminating interferometric observable**, meaning a measured quantity whose value differs between a classical gravitational channel and a quantum one while the phase-only readings agree. That bound term then accrues a residual like anything else and is refusable.
