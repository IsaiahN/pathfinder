# Outreach draft: validate the output first, then decide about the theory

**Use:** one-to-one, or as a cover note attached to a submission through the public venues in `03_public_contact_points.csv`. **Not** for bulk send.

**Hard precondition:** do not send until the shape claim and its refuter in `01_figure_grounded_audit.md` §3 have been checked against the verbatim figure lines. The offer collapses on contact if the stage-1 output does not survive.

---

**Subject:** Check one planning output before you decide whether the framework is worth reading

> Hi [name],
>
> I have one specific output about planning for climate conditions with no historical analogue. I would like you to check it against what you already know. I am not asking you to read the theory first, and I am not asking you to agree with it. If the output does not survive, there is nothing else to look at.
>
> **The output (`general` on the shape, `specified` on the failure modes, not `grounded`):**
>
> 1. The condition is a **refutation**, not a data gap. The regularity a community's past plans relied on is false on the live slots, so the first move is to refuse that term, not to add another contingency plan. Refusal and new plan are different operators: a refutation is a negation, which is unary and joins nothing.
> 2. Do not average. The residual is indexed by place and by system. A town-wide or national risk score can be near zero while the water slot, the grid slot and the health slot are each fatal. Plan per slot.
> 3. Searching the historical record harder cannot produce the term for a no-analogue condition. Composition inside a closed set never leaves it. Run two separate tracks: extend an instrument that is already returning something, and import from a frame whose closure differs, after describing the residual in effect terms.
> 4. Vocabulary and objective come before the plan. If nobody has terms for what the new conditions afford, nobody can say what progress would be, and the plan is a reading of nothing. Write down what a good answer would look like and bind it as a checkable term before adopting anything.
> 5. Capability lives in the agent **and** the habitat. Enumerate actors, conditions, relations and the cascade out to three steps. You can add an actor; you cannot subtract the habitat. Moving away substitutes one habitat for another and inherits actors nobody chose.
> 6. Keep every cut ranked and reversible. Use cost-benefit as a budget filter and the ground, meaning what actually happens after the condition arrives, as the witness.
>
> **What this is not claiming:** which specific hazards will hit you, that relocation is the answer, that local knowledge alone is enough, or that outside expertise is superior. It does not name the structure in the slot. It returns a shape any plan must have, not the plan.
>
> **What would refute it, written before this message:**
> 1. a community's own historical record generates a term that predicts a genuinely no-analogue condition at the ground;
> 2. a plan is confirmed at the ground while the community had no vocabulary for the condition and no stated objective;
> 3. capability rises while contact with the ground is unchanged;
> 4. agreement or prestige is shown to be a legitimate anchor for whether a plan is good;
> 5. a relocation removes all prior contact and inherits none of the new habitat's unchosen actors, yet still meets the original goal.
>
> If any one holds, I withdraw the corresponding claim. A one-line "this fails because..." is more useful to me than agreement. If you would rather not engage, no reply is needed.
>
> **If it survives, and only then:** here are the sources. The figures are the authority, not my summary of them.
> - Verbatim figure text with line numbers: `FRAMEWORK/reference/03-FIGURES_TEXT.md` (local copy in the Tether repo on branch `general-agent`)
> - Canonical usage rule: `FRAMEWORK/reference/FIGURES_README_canonical.md`
> - The public peer-reviewed grounding for the residual: Williams, Jackson and Kutzbach 2007, *Projected distributions of novel and disappearing climates by 2100 AD*, PNAS: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC1851561/
> - The planning-method analogue: U.S. Climate Resilience Toolkit, *Decision-Making Under Deep Uncertainty*: https://toolkit.climate.gov/course-lesson/decision-making-under-deep-uncertainty
>
> **If you want to reuse the machinery:** the formula is in the figures. Run it on a planning problem you know well and can check against a ground. Write down what would refute your result *before* you run it, and state the mode of what you get. A reported failure is more useful to me than a reported success.
>
> — [name]
