## QUESTION

Decide, in effect terms, how a community should plan when its historical experience no longer predicts conditions. Option A: treat the situation as a data gap, extend the historical record, and produce a more detailed plan from past return periods. Option B: treat the held past-as-guide regularity as refuted, refuse it per slot, unbundle the compound, run inward and outward as separate tracks, acquire vocabulary and an objective before planning, enumerate the habitat and its cascade, and keep cuts reversible until the ground settles. What each leads to: A refines on an axis that cannot carry the novel term and spends the independence of the people asked to endorse it; B yields a checkable shape, written refuters, and an honest stop at the instruments.

## DOMAIN CENSUS

R by arity, symmetry and scale, then one line per domain with actors / currency / anchor, partition or union, and order by dependency.

- **R (the residual).** A community's held term (past conditions predict future design loads) is persistently false on specific slots. Arity: multi-slot, one residual per place and system (water, heat, grid, health, housing, supply chains). Symmetry: asymmetric, the arriving condition does not negotiate with the plan. Scale: household / neighbourhood / municipality / region, with a membrane (Fig 4). Question type: a union compound, not one hard question, because heat, flood, water stress and sea level overlap in the same habitat.
- **Domain 1, physical climate ground.** Actors: atmosphere, ocean, ice, land, greenhouse forcing, basins. Currency: energy, gradients, flows. Anchor: measurement and observed outcomes. Partition boundary: what is real regardless of any plan.
- **Domain 2, community held frame (Gamma).** Actors: planners, codes, design standards, infrastructure, local and Indigenous knowledge, budgets. Currency: predictions inherited from history, and plans. Anchor: none inside the frame; it ranges against Domain 1's ground. Question type: frame under audit, not a settling domain.
- **Domain 3, habitat of contact.** Actors: conditions, infrastructure networks, supply chains, health services, neighbours, ecosystems. Currency: contact and cascade. Anchor: the ground. Question type: union, enumerated never composed.
- **Domain 4, outside peer frames.** Actors: modellers, other communities already in novelty, restoration ecologists, engineers, insurers, knowledge holders from different regions. Currency: imports, head starts, independence. Anchor: the ground, never agreement. Question type: union of differing closures.
- **Order by dependency.** Refuse before mint (Formula step 2); perceive the per-slot residual; acquire vocabulary; state an objective; then sequence the plan; then carry methods up and head starts down (Fig 3, Fig 4 :22). Describe the residual before choosing a frame (Fig 9 :27-28).

Strain test applied: Fig 13's cross-kind composition rule governs the six substrate terms; community actors are not substrate terms. Fig 4 :19-20 resolves it (only actors, currency and anchor change with size), so Fig 13 is used only as a `general` analogue for scale structure, with the strain recorded in `06`. ## VERDICT (from the figures)

The answer is a shape, not a plan. The condition refutes a held term, so refuse it per slot before accumulating new plans. The residual is a multi-slot union compound: unbundle it, and give each compound its own loop and ground. Historical search cannot yield the novel term, so run inward instrument work and outward imports as separate tracks, with the residual described in effect terms before any frame is selected. Vocabulary and an objective precede the plan, which must be a bonded sequence with ranked, reversible cuts. Capability is agent-and-habitat; enumerate contact to third order, add what is needed, and treat relocation as substitution. The ground, not agreement and not prestige, settles any application. One strain remains and is named: Fig 13's cross-kind rule is a `general` analogue at a social scale, bounded by re-census in `06`. This is a `general` and `specified`, ungrounded, candidate result.

- **cost if wrong:** bounded and locatable. The shape keeps cuts ranked and reversible and keeps the residual per slot, so an error is local rather than system-wide. The real cost is spending the independence of outside reviewers by sending a recording (a finished plan) instead of a generator (the residual, the refuter and the shape). Fig 2 :42 prices that: "Buy independence in proportion to the cost of being wrong." It is avoidable by leading with the shape claim and its refuter.
- **undoable:** yes. No adaptation measure is prescribed, so nothing concrete has to be withdrawn if the shape fails. Methods can be re-imported, cuts can be reversed, and the claim can be superseded by a corrected packet. The refusal itself is recorded rather than deleted, so a later reader can see the lineage.
- **reversed by:** a ground reading, not agreement. A community's own historical record generating a term that predicts a no-analogue condition, a plan confirmed at the ground with no vocabulary or objective, capability rising with contact unchanged, consensus demonstrated to be a legitimate anchor, or a substitution that removes all contact and inherits no unchosen actors while still meeting the goal, would each fire a standing falsifier. A source-discriminating instrument at the habitat level would also settle which structure sits in the slot. Until one of these fires, the verdict stands as `candidate`.
