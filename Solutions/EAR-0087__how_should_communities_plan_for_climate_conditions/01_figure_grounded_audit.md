## 0. The residual under audit, stated in effect terms

The question is not "which adaptation measure works". It is this: a community holds a library Γ built from its own historical experience (normal temperatures, return periods, familiar hazards, design standards, local knowledge, budgets). It acts on a prediction from that library. Conditions arrive that the library did not predict, and the miss is persistent rather than a transient. In effect terms, the regularity the community composed its plans on is **false** under the new conditions.

This is Figure 5's fifth bin, added by amendment, not a NOVEL gap:

> Fig 5 :53 **"refuted: a term we hold is wrong; a term is owed that says so"**

And the term most worth refusing may not be in the library at all:

> Fig 5 :61 **"BUT THE TERM MOST WORTH REFUSING MAY NOT BE IN THE LIBRARY AT ALL."**

So the residual is not "we lack data about the future". It is "a term we hold is wrong". Formula step 2 orders the response: REFUTED before mint, a refusal (¬, unary, not one of the seven bonds) before a new plan. A community that starts by drawing a new plan has skipped the step that owes the refusal.

**R by arity, symmetry and scale.** Arity: multi-slot, one live residual per place and per system (water, heat, power, health, housing, supply chains), so it is indexed, not global. Symmetry: the relation between a community's held frame and the new condition is asymmetric, the condition does not negotiate with the plan. Scale: household / neighbourhood / municipality / region / nation, with a membrane between sizes (Fig 4). Question type: a **union compound**, not one hard question (Fig 9 :54-58), because heat, flood, water stress and sea level overlap in the same place.

### Domain census (actors / currency / anchor, before any figure line is chosen)

| domain | actors | currency | anchor | partition or union |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| D1 physical climate ground | atmosphere, ocean, ice, land, forcing, basins | energy, gradients, flows (Fig 13 states and structures) | measurement: gauges, reanalysis, observed outcomes | partition boundary: what is real regardless of any plan |
| D2 community held frame (Γ) | planners, codes, design standards, infrastructure, local and Indigenous knowledge, budgets | predictions inherited from history, and plans | **none inside the frame**; it ranges against D1's ground | frame under audit, not a settling domain |
| D3 habitat of contact | conditions, relations, supply chains, grids, health, governance neighbours, ecosystems | contact and cascade | the ground (what actually happens) | union; enumerate, never compose (Fig 11 :43) |
| D4 outside peer frames | modellers, other communities already in novelty, ecologists, engineers, insurers | imports, head starts, independence | the ground, never agreement | union; low-overlap closures are where the surplus lives |

No nameable anchor means D2 is not a domain that can settle anything by itself. That is the finding, not a defect in the census.

**Order by dependency (Fig 3).** 1 perception (register the per-slot residual at the ground) → 2 vocabulary (terms for what the new conditions afford) → 3 objective (what would count as progress) → 4 planning (a route) → 5 learn and carry. Refuse before mint (Formula step 2); describe the residual before choosing a frame (Fig 9 :27-28); settle a first-order term before composing over it (Fig 12 :106).

## 2. Findings

### F1 — The condition is REFUTED, not merely NOVEL (governs the first move)

> Fig 5 :53 **"refuted: a term we hold is wrong; a term is owed that says so"**
> Fig 5 :61 **"BUT THE TERM MOST WORTH REFUSING MAY NOT BE IN THE LIBRARY AT ALL."**

The historical regularity ("next season resembles the last hundred") is a held term generating a prediction. It is persistently wrong. Formula step 2 puts REFUTED before BROKEN and before mint, and the Operators table :53-58 makes negation unary: *"a refutation is a negation, which is why a vocabulary without it can only accumulate."* A community that adds a sixth contingency plan to five failed ones is accumulating, not refusing.

**Refuter:** show that the residual is fully a NOVEL bin (perception extension only) and that no held term is generating a false prediction, that is, that historical design assumptions still pay on every live slot. Then F1 is withdrawn and the response is perception extension, not refusal.

**Status:** triangulated across Fig 5 :53 (kind of gap) and Fig 8 :32 (what closure can produce). Low overlap: one sorts the gap by what is wrong, the other by what composition cannot add.

### F2 — Searching history harder cannot produce the novel term (governs the budget)

> Fig 8 :32 **"Closure is idempotent. Composing inside a closed set never leaves it, so no search, however long, adds an atom. This one is definitional: it is what closure means."**
> Fig 6 :21 **"MINT composes inside the closure and can never add an atom."**

A community that responds to novelty with a longer historical record, finer downscaling of the same variables, or another round of "lessons learned" from its own plans is searching inside the same closure. Fig 8 :39: **"So a search that finds nothing is never a proof of absence."** This is a budget instruction, not a proof that no answer exists. Formula step 7 distinguishes *unreachable at this budget* (more search) from *underivable in principle* (go outside).

**Refuter:** exhibit a case where a community, searching only its own historical record, generated a term that subsequently predicted a genuinely no-analogue condition at the ground. That breaks idempotence as applied here.

**Status:** triangulated across Fig 8 :32 and Fig 6 :21, with the world source Williams et al. 2007 corroborating (secondary only). The two figures overlap on the closure concept but differ in what they price: idempotence versus the mint operator. Marked triangulated with that caveat recorded.

### F3 — The refusal and the new plan run as two tracks, never read against each other

> Fig 6 :29 **"inward: is our representation adequate to hold it? The instrument extends access. Fails by refining forever on an axis that cannot carry it. Sharpen in our own terms."**
> Fig 6 :30 **"outward: does another frame already hold it? The description travels. Fails by being sharpened into terms only we can read. Cast it in effect terms."**
> Fig 6 :31 **"Neither answer tells you much about the other. Run them as two tracks, and never read one against the other."**

INWARD: extend an instrument that is already returning something (a gauge, a health surveillance signal, a damage report), from a worse instrument, not from a description (Fig 6 :32-34). OUTWARD: describe the residual in effect terms, then find a frame whose closure differs (another basin already in novelty, a restoration ecologist, an insurer, an Indigenous knowledge holder from a different region). A community that does only inward refines forever on an axis that cannot carry the novel term. A community that does only outward imports terms it cannot read or test.

**Refuter:** demonstrate an instrument built from a pure description with no prior instrument returning anything (breaks Fig 6 :32-34), or show that an inward and an outward result are the same quantity so reading one against the other is harmless.

**Status:** triangulated across Fig 6 :29-31 and Fig 9 :27-28 (describe before looking). Different quantities, low overlap.

### F4 — Vocabulary and objective precede the plan; below that break a reading is of nothing

> Fig 3 :25-26 **"having terms for what a situation offers and being able to say what would count as progress, has neither, so it attracts the least attention and the fewest instruments."**
> Fig 3 :27 **"A link with no instrument is not a link that works. It is a link nobody has looked at."**
> Fig 3 :19 **"Optimising a link that already works cannot rescue one that does not."**

A community faced with a no-analogue condition usually has planning instruments (budgets, capital programmes, hazard maps) but no vocabulary for what the new condition affords, and therefore cannot say what progress would even be. That is the middle link of Fig 3. Effort spent on the plan (link 4) lands at a step that cannot use it. Fig 3 :22-23: *"A step whose input never arrives cannot be diagnosed, only its predecessor can. Which is why a reading taken below the break is not a weak reading. It is a reading of nothing."*

**Refuter:** show a community that stated a checkable objective and a plan for a no-analogue condition while having no terms for what that condition affords, and whose plan was later confirmed at the ground. That breaks the dependency order.

**Status:** triangulated across Fig 3 :25-27 (chain) and Fig 5 :65-66 (bind the sought shape as a term). Low overlap: one is a dependency claim, the other a method discipline.

### F5 — Bind the sought-for shape as an ordinary term before running the plan

> Fig 5 :65-66 **"make the sought-for shape an ORDINARY TERM. Write down what an answer would look like, bind it, and let it accrue a residual like anything else."**

A community's preferred future ("a resilient town") is a preference, not a term. It predicts nothing, accrues no residual, and is invisible to the loop. Committing a prediction before a run makes it refusable by the same test as everything else (Formula "Direction"). This is the pre-registered refuter in §3: write down what an answer would look like and what would kill it before the plan is adopted.

**Refuter:** produce a community process whose stated goal accrued no residual and was never refusable, yet whose outcome was settled by a ground reading and could not have been otherwise. That breaks the Direction rule as applied here.

**Status:** single-source for the figure (Fig 5 :65-66) plus the Formula Direction section, which is the same framework family. Marked single-source, not corroborated.

### F6 — Aggregate risk scores throw away the only signal that says where to look

> Fig 1 :19 **"The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated."**
> Fig 1 :22 **"Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average."**

A single town-wide or national "climate risk score" is a global R near zero with one live slot, which Fig 1 :13 says is a legal state. The heat slot in one neighbourhood, the water slot in another and the grid slot at the edge can each be fatal while the average looks manageable. Fig 1 :6-7: the ground is the only metric, and frame-internal quantities such as "coverage achieved" and "predicates minted" do not count. Public engagement metrics and plans produced are frame-internal for this question.

**Refuter:** exhibit a case where averaging across places and systems preserved, rather than destroyed, the signal that determined which place failed first at the ground.

**Status:** triangulated across Fig 1 :19-22 and Fig 2 :29-30 (an anchor settles, an aggregate does not). Moderate overlap, recorded.

### F7 — The habitat must be enumerated from the world, including the cascade

> Fig 11 :43 **"The habitat is enumerated, never composed."**
> Fig 11 :48 **"You do not invent the list. You read it off the world, and what you cannot perceive or measure yet is the residual."**
> Fig 11 :52 **"And contact cascades."**

Fig 11 :44-47 gives the enumeration: actors (what acts on the residual and what it acts on), conditions (light, water, temperature, season, terrain), relations (pollination, predation, competition, dependency). For a community these are the grid, the water system, supply chains, health services, shelter, mutual aid and governance. Scoping to direct contact is how DDT reached the eagles: *"first-order enumeration, second-order effect, and the residual that mattered was three steps out"* (Fig 11 :52-53, Carson 1962 cited in the figure).

> Fig 11 :39 **"capability is a property of agent-and-habitat, never of the agent alone."**
> Fig 11 :40 **"An improvement that does not change contact changes nothing, however much it improves."**

A training programme or a new plan that leaves contact with the ground unchanged changes nothing. This is why "community capacity" cannot be assessed by counting plans.

**Refuter:** show a community whose measured capability increased while its contact with the ground (actors, conditions, relations, and the cascade out to third order) was unchanged. That breaks Fig 11 :39-40.

**Status:** single-source for the figure (Fig 11). IPCC AR6 WGII B.5 and D.2, and the DDT case in the figure itself, corroborate at the world level but share no measure with the figure. Still `single-source` in the verdict.

### F8 — Introduction is available, exclusion is not; relocation substitutes a habitat

> Fig 11 :61 **"The seat's power is asymmetric. Introduction is available; exclusion is not."**
> Fig 11 :63 **"Which is why a managed ecosystem is permanent work rather than a solved state."**
> Fig 11 :66 **"Isolation is not removal of the habitat. It is substitution of one habitat for another."**

A community can add an actor (a cooling centre, a mutual-aid network, an import from a peer frame), but it cannot subtract the habitat, because contact is a fact about the world. Fig 11 :67: *"Move the residual and you must reproduce everything its goal required, and you inherit the new habitat's actors, which you did not choose."* Retreat or relocation is therefore not a clean fix; it inherits an unauthored actor set and what was failed to reproduce is invisible until the goal fails (Fig 11 :68-69). The novel-ecosystem literature reaches the same practical limit from ecology: *"Attempts to 'force' stable novel systems 'back' to a historical state by traditional restoration and conservation approaches might result in systems collapse"* (British Ecological Society, 2024).

**Refuter:** exhibit a habitat substitution that removed all prior contact, reproduced none of the unchosen actors, and still met the original goal at the ground. That breaks the isolation law.

**Status:** single-source for the figure (Fig 11 :61-69), with the BES article as a low-overlap world corroboration.

### F9 — Outward imports need provenance, not prestige

> Fig 9 :27-28 **"describe it before you go looking, or any frame you pick will seem to fit"**
> Fig 8 :50 **"Convergent derivation and adopted import look identical in the contents. Only the record of where each came from separates them"**
> Fig 6 :23 **"No frame escapes its own closure. The bound moves only on contact with a frame whose closure differs."**

The community must describe the residual in effect terms before selecting the outside frame. Otherwise any fashionable framework seems to fit, which is apophenia (echo without shadow). Fig 7 :42: **"the next room you book is the direction you travel, and booking a room like the last one is how a chain stops travelling at all."** Select rooms with low overlap with the community's own evidence pool. Fig 2 :42: **"Buy independence in proportion to the cost of being wrong."** For irreversible adaptation decisions the cost is high, so independence is worth paying for.

**Refuter:** show that a frame selected after hearing the proposed solution gave the same independent correction as a frame selected from the residual alone, across several cases. That breaks the residual-first ordering.

**Status:** triangulated across Fig 9 :27-28, Fig 8 :50-52 and Fig 7 :42. Three figures, different quantities (selection order, provenance, room choice), low overlap.

### F10 — A plan needs bonds; an ingredient list cannot name a route

> Fig 12 :111 **"A vocabulary without bonds can name a state and cannot name a route to one."**
> Fig 12 :112 **"A plan is a sequence. A goal is a comparison. Progress is a subtraction. A refutation is a negation, and that one is not a bond."**
> Fig 12 :126 **"Bits price the description. Actions price finding out whether it holds. They are in different units"**

An asset inventory (shelters, pumps, staff, budget) names a state. It cannot write the sequence, the threshold that counts as success, the subtraction that measures progress, or the refusal of a failed assumption. Fig 9 :41: **"so keep every cut ranked and reversible"**, and Fig 9 :60: **"Use filters for the budget and witnesses for the verdict. Never let a filter hand you a verdict."** A cost-benefit ranking is a filter for the budget; the ground (what happens after the novel condition) is the witness.

**Refuter:** exhibit a plan written as a pure inventory, with no sequence, comparison, subtraction or negation, that was nevertheless refutable and settled at the ground.

**Status:** triangulated across Fig 12 :111-112 and Fig 9 :41, :60. Low overlap: notation versus decision discipline.

### F11 — Scale transfer carries methods and head starts, never a recording

> Fig 4 :19-20 **"Only the actors, the currency and the anchor change."**
> Fig 4 :22 **"Only methods cross up. Only head starts come down."**
> Fig 4 :34-35 **"A seed carries no small tree: it carries what will grow one, and the conditions it lands in decide what it becomes."**

A method that worked in one city crosses up as a method, verified and compressed. The particular plan crosses as a recording and produces a copy of a past success in conditions that remake it (Fig 4 :25-26). The membrane is a filter with a direction, not a wall. Fig 4 :30: **"So take the round trip and measure the gap. Without it you are navigating by dead reckoning: confident about a position nobody checked."**

**Refuter:** show a specific municipal plan transplanted unchanged into a different habitat that performed identically at the ground. That breaks the lossy-transform rule.

**Status:** triangulated across Fig 4 :22, :30 and Fig 7 :35-36 (agreement is variation, not a verdict). Low overlap.

## 3. Pre-registered refuter (written before the conclusion)

Required before any outreach, per seat reasoning: a falsifier written before the run is a design instrument, not only an honesty device. The shape claim is refuted if any of the following holds:

1. A community's own historical closure generates a term that predicts a genuinely no-analogue condition at the ground (breaks Fig 8 :32 and Fig 6 :21, F2).
2. A plan is confirmed at the ground while the community had no terms for what the new condition affords and no stated objective (breaks Fig 3 :25-27, F4).
3. Capability is shown to be a property of the agent alone, with contact unchanged (breaks Fig 11 :39-40, F7).
4. A "good plan" anchor is found that moves when planners push on it, that is, agreement or prestige is allowed to settle the verdict (breaks Fig 2 :29-30, :35, F9/F10).
5. A habitat substitution removes all prior contact and inherits none of the new habitat's unchosen actors while still meeting the original goal (breaks Fig 11 :66-69, F8).

If any fires, the corresponding figure-derived claim is withdrawn. Until one is run, no part of this may be labelled `grounded`.

## 4. Recommendation

1. Lead with the refusal, not the plan. State which held regularity is false, per slot, before proposing anything (F1, F6).
2. Unbundle the union. Heat, water, power and health are overlapping conditions, so each compound gets its own loop and its own ground (Fig 9 :54-58).
3. Run inward and outward as two tracks. Keep a live instrument in each; do not read one against the other (F3).
4. Write down the sought-for shape as an ordinary term and bind the refuter before adopting the plan (F5, §3).
5. Enumerate the habitat to at least three steps of cascade, including what is currently unmeasurable; that unmeasurable remainder is the residual (F7).
6. Keep every cut ranked and reversible. Treat cost-benefit as a budget filter and the ground as the witness (F10).
7. Select outside frames from the residual, not from the solution. Prefer low overlap and declare provenance (F9).
8. Transfer methods, not recordings, across scales, and measure the round trip gap R_T (F11, `11_audit.md` §4).

### Final reading

The shape survives: refuse the held regularity per slot, unbundle the compound, run inward and outward as separate tracks, acquire vocabulary and an objective before planning, enumerate the habitat and its cascade, keep cuts reversible, and let the ground settle. What is refused: any specific adaptation prescription, any aggregate risk verdict, consensus as an anchor, and any claim that this derivation is `grounded`. **Unreached, not unreachable.** Strains after re-census: one (Fig 13 applied to a social scale), named and bounded in `06`.
