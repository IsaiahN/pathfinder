# Supporting derivation: is a novel climate an isomer, and what order is a plan?

**Mode: `general` for the structural analogue, `specified` for the falsifier. Not `grounded`.** This is a simulated derivation checked against the figures (`05_what_shape_means.md` §6), not a ground reading.

## DERIVATION

### Q1 — Is the novel condition an isomer of the historical one?

> Fig 12 :117 **"isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."**
> Fig 12 :124 **"functional group. A substructure whose presence fixes the outcome. The group says what kind; the qualifier says which member."**

A historical climate and a no-analogue climate in the same place can be written over the **same atoms**: the same Space (the basin), the same Time (a season), the same Energy (heat content), the same Gradient (a temperature-moisture difference), the same Network (a watershed or supply chain). What changes is the **bond**: which state the gradient is attached to, whether the sequence → holds between the same events, whether the comparison ⋛ crosses the same threshold, whether the network relation still carries the same influence. On Fig 12's definition that is an isomer: same atoms, different arrangement, different substance, and the difference is invisible in an ingredient list ("hotter and drier") and decisive in behaviour.

The weaker reading is the **functional group**: both climates share a substructure (`state → structure → state`) but with different members in the slots. Fig 13 :76 supports the distinctness: *"A gradient over space and one over time are different terms, because which state it is bonded to is part of what it is."*

**Falsifier, written before the run:** if the novel condition requires an atom the historical one did not hold (a process, actor, or relation absent from the community's Γ and not derivable from the six), the isomer claim fails. The condition is then a new substance owed to an import (Fig 6 :23), not a rearrangement. An instrument, not an argument, decides which case holds.

**Status:** triangulated for the definition (Fig 12 :117 and Fig 13 :76, two different figures with low overlap); `specified` and unmeasured for the application to any particular community.

### Q2 — What order must a plan have?

> Fig 3 :20 **"The chain is ordered by dependency, not by importance."**
> Fig 12 :111 **"A vocabulary without bonds can name a state and cannot name a route to one."**
> Fig 12 :112 **"A plan is a sequence. A goal is a comparison. Progress is a subtraction. A refutation is a negation, and that one is not a bond."**

An asset inventory is a flat formula. It records ingredients (staff, pumps, shelters, budget) and not arrangement, so nothing downstream can tell a route from a list (Fig 12 :4-17). The dependency order for a plan under novelty is:

1. **Refuse** the false held term (¬, unary; Formula step 2). This is not a bond and produces no new plan by itself.
2. **Perceive** the per-slot residual at the ground (Fig 1 :19).
3. **Vocabulary** for what the new conditions afford (Fig 3 :7).
4. **Objective**: say what would count as progress, using ⋛ comparison and − subtraction (Fig 12 :112).
5. **Plan**: a → sequence over the branches, with cut points kept ranked and reversible (Fig 9 :41).
6. **Carry**: methods up, head starts down (Fig 4 :22).

The order is by dependency. Effort at step 5 cannot rescue a missing step 3 or 4 (Fig 3 :19).

> Fig 12 :126 **"Bits price the description. Actions price finding out whether it holds. They are in different units"**

A plan therefore has two prices that do not add: bits to state it and actions to test it at the ground. A community holding only a bits accounting "will not notice the shortfall" (Fig 12 :127). This is why an elaborate plan can be cheap to state and unaffordable to refute.

**Falsifier:** if a plan can be shown to work at the ground without a sequence, a comparison, a subtraction and a refusal (that is, as a pure inventory), the bond requirement fails and the flat form is sufficient.

### Q3 — What analogues are licensed, and where is the strain?

- **Cascade analogue (primary, in the figure).** Fig 11 :52: **"And contact cascades."** The figure's own case is DDT reaching eagles: first-order enumeration, second-order effect, residual three steps out (Fig 11 :52-53, Carson 1962). A community that scopes its habitat to direct contractors and direct hazards repeats the first-order error.
- **Novel-ecosystem analogue (world, low overlap).** Perring, Standish and Hobbs (2013) describe systems whose historical reference is no longer a usable target, and the British Ecological Society (2024) says forcing such systems back "might result in systems collapse." These corroborate F1 and F8 from ecology, a different closure. Overlap between these two world sources is high for the novelty concept, so they count as one witness, not two.
- **Decision analogue (world, low overlap).** The U.S. Climate Resilience Toolkit's DMDU lesson replaces "predict-then-act" with stress tests over many futures and adaptive pathways. That is the world-level form of keeping cuts ranked and reversible.
- **Scale strain, named.** Fig 13 :74: **"Everything else is a composition across the two kinds, never within one."** Fig 13 :75: **"Two states never bond directly. It is not space-time but space, a structure, time; and two structures join only through a state."** Applied literally to a community's actors (planners, grids, budgets), these lines describe the six substrate terms, not social actors. Fig 4 :19-20 resolves the apparent conflict: the kernel does not change with size, but actors, currency and anchor do. So Fig 13 is used here **only** for the claim that composition crosses kinds and that a state and a structure alternate in the description of a condition, not to name a community's actors as states or structures. This is a `general` structural analogue. Re-census, not a smoothed-over contradiction.

> Fig 12 :104 **"A settled molecule becomes an atom for whatever composes over it. So irreducible is a fact about a level and never about the world."**

Once a first-order response is settled at the ground, it becomes an atom for the next level; a neighbourhood's settled cooling arrangement can be an operand for the municipality. Irreducible is a fact about that level, not about the community.

## CONCLUSION

1. A no-analogue condition is **provisionally an isomer** of the historical one: same six atoms, different bond, decisive behavioural difference. Falsifier: an atom is required that the historical frame did not hold, which sends the question to import.
2. A plan must be written as a bonded arrangement in a dependency order: refuse, perceive, acquire vocabulary, state an objective, then sequence, then carry. An inventory cannot name a route.
3. The analogue is safe at the level of structure and cascades, and strained if Fig 13's six terms are read as social actors. Re-census by Fig 4 :19-20 resolves it, and the strain is recorded rather than hidden.
4. Two prices (bits and actions) are held apart; summing them into one "plan score" is the error Fig 12 :125-130 names.
