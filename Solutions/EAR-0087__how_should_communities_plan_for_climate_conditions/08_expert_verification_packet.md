# Expert verification packet: planning for no-analogue climate conditions

**What this is:** a claim in a form a community-climate-planning researcher can check. Mode, derivation and refuters are stated before the conclusion, so a reader knows what to look for and what would settle it.

**Mode: `general` on the shape, `specified` on the failure modes, not `grounded`.** No community outcome is claimed as settled. This is a shape and a route, not a result, and it is a simulated derivation checked for internal consistency against the figures, not a ground reading.

## 1. The claim, in one paragraph

A climate condition unlike anything in a community's historical experience is a **refutation** of a held term, not a data gap, so the first move is to refuse that term rather than add another plan. The residual is indexed by place and system, never averaged. Search inside the historical closure cannot yield the novel term; inward instrument work and outward imports from frames with different closures must run as two separate tracks. Vocabulary and a checkable objective precede the plan, which must be written as a bonded sequence with ranked, reversible cuts. Capability is a property of the agent and its habitat together; enumerate actors, conditions, relations and the cascade, because introduction is available and exclusion is not. The ground, meaning what actually happens after the condition arrives, decides. The framework returns this shape, not a named measure.

## 2. Three checkable sub-claims

### C1 — framework-internal; checkable by reading Figures 5, 8 and 1

- **Statement:** the condition is a REFUTED gap and its residual is slot-indexed, so averaging hides the live signal and searching history cannot add the missing term.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 5 :53 **"refuted: a term we hold is wrong; a term is owed that says so"**; Fig 8 :32 **"Closure is idempotent. Composing inside a closed set never leaves it, so no search, however long, adds an atom. This one is definitional: it is what closure means."**; Fig 1 :19 **"The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated."**
- **What to check:** does the community actually hold a prediction from its past that is now persistently false on specific slots? If yes, is the first proposed action a refusal or an accumulation of new plans?
- **Refuter:** exhibit a community whose own historical record generated a term that predicted a genuinely no-analogue condition at the ground; or show that averaging across places and systems preserved the signal that determined which place failed first.

### C2 — planning-facing; checkable by a practitioner against real planning sequences

- **Statement:** vocabulary and objective are dependencies upstream of the plan, and the plan needs bonds (sequence, comparison, subtraction, negation), not an ingredient list.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 3 :27 **"A link with no instrument is not a link that works. It is a link nobody has looked at."**; Fig 12 :112 **"A plan is a sequence. A goal is a comparison. Progress is a subtraction. A refutation is a negation, and that one is not a bond."**
- **What to check:** in a real no-analogue case, was a plan adopted before the community had terms for what the condition affords and a stated objective? Did the plan document contain a route, a threshold and a progress measure, or only an asset inventory?
- **Refuter:** a plan confirmed at the ground while the community had no vocabulary for the condition and no objective; or a pure inventory that was nevertheless refutable and settled at the ground.

### C3 — habitat-facing; checkable by an adaptation researcher against cases

- **Statement:** capability is agent-and-habitat, contact must be enumerated outward through the cascade, and relocation substitutes a habitat rather than removing one.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 11 :39 **"capability is a property of agent-and-habitat, never of the agent alone."**; Fig 11 :43 **"The habitat is enumerated, never composed."**; Fig 11 :66 **"Isolation is not removal of the habitat. It is substitution of one habitat for another."**
- **What to check:** did measured capability move with contact or with internal plan counts? In a relocation case, what was failed to reproduce and what unchosen actors were inherited?
- **Refuter:** capability rising with contact unchanged; or a substitution that removed all prior contact, inherited none of the new habitat's unchosen actors, and still met the original goal.

## 3. What is explicitly not claimed

- Not a specific adaptation measure, a hazard forecast, a probability, or a timetable.
- Not that relocation is the answer; isolation substitutes a habitat and inherits actors (Fig 11 :66-69).
- Not that agreement among stakeholders is the anchor. Fig 2 :29-30 requires an anchor that does not move when frames push on it.
- Not that local knowledge alone suffices or that external expertise is superior; provenance, not prestige, decides (Fig 8 :50-52).
- Not `grounded`. The figures are the authority for the shape; the ground settles any application.
- Not that Fig 13's six substrate terms are social actors. The cross-kind rule is a `general` analogue bounded by re-census in `06`.

## 5. What would settle it

1. **Per-slot test:** after a novel condition, does failure concentrate in slots the aggregate score called healthy?
2. **Vocabulary test:** do communities that acquired terms for what the condition affords before setting objectives outperform matched communities on a ground-readable outcome?
3. **Contact test:** does capability move only when contact with the habitat changes (Fig 11 :39-40)?
4. **Substitution test:** what was failed to reproduce and what was unintentionally introduced after a relocation (Fig 11 :67-69)?
5. **Standing falsifiers:** `01_figure_grounded_audit.md` §3, items 1 to 5.

## 6. What an expert can check in twenty minutes

1. Read Fig 5 :53, Fig 8 :32, Fig 1 :19, Fig 3 :27, Fig 12 :112, Fig 11 :39 and :43. Seven short passages.
2. Check C2 against one planning process you know: was vocabulary and objective present before the plan, and does the plan contain a route, a threshold and a progress measure?
3. Check C3 against one relocation or managed-retreat case you know: what was failed to reproduce, and which unchosen actors were inherited?
4. Name one instrument that would discriminate the source of a damage reading, or say why none exists (Hard Problem is About Source).
5. Send the refutation. A failure is more useful than an endorsement.
