# The Tether's answer: how should communities plan for novel climate conditions?

**Mode: `general` on the structural shape, `specified` on the named failure modes, not `grounded`.** This is a shape and a route, not a result. Simulated derivation checked against the figures (`05_what_shape_means.md` §6), not a ground reading.

## 1. The residual it answers

A community's plans are composed from a library built out of its historical experience. A condition arrives that the library did not predict and the miss persists. The regularity the plans relied on is false. That is not a data gap; it is Figure 5's REFUTED bin:

> Fig 5 :53 **"refuted: a term we hold is wrong; a term is owed that says so"**

The residual is indexed by place and system, not global (Fig 1 :19-22), and it is a **union compound**: heat, water, power and health overlap in the same habitat, so a single lever clears only the regions where one condition holds and leaves the intersections standing (Fig 9 :54-58). It has its own loop and its own ground.

## 2. The shape

1. **Refuse before planning.** The first owed move is a refusal of the past-as-guide term, not another plan. A refutation is a negation, unary, joining nothing (Operators table :53-58). Refusal and plan are different operators; adding a sixth contingency plan to five failed ones is accumulation, not correction.
2. **Never average the signal.** Fig 1 :19: **"The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated."** Fig 1 :22: **"Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average."** A town-wide risk score near zero with one fatal water slot is a legal state. Plan per slot.
3. **Search cannot manufacture the novel term.** Fig 8 :32: **"Closure is idempotent. Composing inside a closed set never leaves it, so no search, however long, adds an atom. This one is definitional: it is what closure means."** Run two tracks: extend an instrument already returning something (inward), and import from a frame with a different closure (outward). Fig 6 :31: **"Neither answer tells you much about the other. Run them as two tracks, and never read one against the other."**
4. **Vocabulary, then objective, then plan.** Fig 3 :27: **"A link with no instrument is not a link that works. It is a link nobody has looked at."** If nobody has terms for what the new condition affords, nobody can state progress, and the plan is a reading of nothing. Bind the sought-for shape as an ordinary term with a refuter before adopting the plan (Fig 5 :65-66).
5. **Enumerate the habitat, including the cascade.** Fig 11 :43: **"The habitat is enumerated, never composed."** Fig 11 :39: **"capability is a property of agent-and-habitat, never of the agent alone."** Map actors, conditions and relations outward until the cascade stops mattering; what cannot yet be perceived is the residual. Introduction is available, exclusion is not (Fig 11 :61), so a managed habitat is permanent work, not a solved state.
6. **Write the plan as a bonded arrangement.** Fig 12 :112: **"A plan is a sequence. A goal is a comparison. Progress is a subtraction. A refutation is a negation, and that one is not a bond."** An asset inventory names a state and cannot name a route.
7. **Keep cuts ranked and reversible.** Fig 9 :41: **"so keep every cut ranked and reversible"**; Fig 9 :60: **"Use filters for the budget and witnesses for the verdict. Never let a filter hand you a verdict."** Cost-benefit ranks the budget; the ground decides.
8. **Transfer methods, not recordings, across scales.** Fig 4 :22: **"Only methods cross up. Only head starts come down."** A plan copied from another city with its conditions changed is a replay, not a generator.
9. **Select outside frames from the residual.** Fig 9 :27-28: **"describe it before you go looking, or any frame you pick will seem to fit"**; Fig 7 :42: **"the next room you book is the direction you travel, and booking a room like the last one is how a chain stops travelling at all."** Buy independence in proportion to the cost of being wrong (Fig 2 :42).

## 3. What it explicitly does not say

- Not which measure to adopt. That needs a ground reading in this habitat or a provenanced import with a shadow test. The framework returns a shape, not a name.
- Not a prediction of which hazards will arrive, when, or with what probability.
- Not that relocation is the answer. Isolation substitutes a habitat (Fig 11 :66-69) and inherits unchosen actors.
- Not that local knowledge alone suffices, or that outside science is superior. Provenance decides, not prestige (Fig 8 :50-52).
- Not that stakeholder agreement is an anchor. Fig 2 :29-30 requires an anchor that does not move when frames push on it; agreement moves.
- Not `grounded`. No community outcome settles this derivation.

## 4. What would settle it — the ground

A discriminating instrument and observed outcomes, not a further argument. *The Hard Problem is About Source*: an instrument that reads green "is speaking only about what it measured, it cannot comment on the source"; "The answer is unreached until a better instrument exists." Here:

- **The per-slot test:** after a novel condition, does the failure concentrate in slots the aggregate score called healthy? A confirmed concentration supports the slot-indexing claim.
- **The vocabulary test:** do communities that acquired terms for what the condition affords before setting objectives outperform matched communities that went straight to plans, on an outcome the ground can read (damage, continuity of service, health)?
- **The contact test:** does capability move only when contact with the habitat changes, as Fig 11 :39-40 predicts?
- **The substitution test:** what was failed to reproduce and what was unintentionally introduced after a relocation, read at the ground (Fig 11 :67-69)?
- **Standing falsifiers from `01` §3:** a community's own history generating a predicting term for a no-analogue condition; a plan confirmed with no vocabulary or objective; capability rising with contact unchanged; consensus acting as an anchor; a substitution with no inherited actors and no lost goal.

## 5. The honest stop

**Unreached, not unreachable.** The six-term set is a visible guess at compression axes, and the kernel is unchanged with size while actors, currency and anchor change (Fig 4 :19-20). This derivation stops where the available instruments stop distinguishing, and records the lineage so the stop is checkable. Fig 8 :39: **"So a search that finds nothing is never a proof of absence."** Indiscernibility is not identity. One strain remains: applying Fig 13's cross-kind rule to a social scale is a `general` analogue bounded by re-census in `06`, not a claim about social actors as substrate terms.

## 6. One-paragraph version for outreach

> The Tether does not hand a community an adaptation plan, and it does not claim to know which hazard will arrive. It says that a no-analogue climate refutes a term the community already holds, so the first move is a refusal rather than another plan; that the residual is indexed by place and system, so a single average risk score hides the live signal; that searching the historical record harder cannot produce the new term, so inward instrument work and outward imports must run as separate tracks; that vocabulary and an objective have to exist before a plan can mean anything; that capability lives in the agent and the habitat together, with cascades out to several steps and no power to subtract the habitat; and that every cut should stay ranked and reversible while the ground, not agreement, decides. What it offers is the shape any plan must have, the falsifiers written in advance, and the instruments that would close it. The center stays open until the ground settles it.
