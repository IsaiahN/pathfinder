# Outreach draft: check the shape claim before you decide about the theory

**Use:** one-to-one, or as a cover letter attached to a submission through the public venues in `03_public_contact_points.csv`. **Not** for bulk send.

**Hard precondition:** do not send until the three cells in §1 are clear to you. The offer collapses on contact if the recipient cannot tell what would count as a failure.

---

**Subject:** One checkable claim about the experience of choice, and what would refute it

> Hi [name],
>
> I have one specific output I would like you to check against what you already know. I am not asking you to read the theory first, and I am not asking you to agree with it. If the output does not survive, there is nothing else to look at.
>
> **The residual, in effect terms.** A person facing a choice can read its source two ways: "I authored this" and "this was shaped by an obligation or a relationship." The question asks when each reading is experienced, and who is entitled to settle it. Because the question is about people's experience, the anchor is the person's own attended report. A researcher's classification is an instrument here, not the ground.
>
> **The output (`specified`, not `grounded`).** Three claims:
>
> 1. The two readings are not ends of one scale. They are two conditions that can both hold. A choice can be experienced as fully mine *because* it is relational. That is a third cell, not a midpoint.
> 2. What differs between the cells is the *bond arrangement*, not the ingredients. A want that produced the action, a want that the obligation produced, a choice made despite the obligation, and a choice that is an expression of the relationship all contain the same cast. They are different experiences because the relation between the parts differs.
> 3. An obligation is not autonomy-supporting or autonomy-thwarting as a kind. Its valence depends on what the person is trying to do and on the relation they stand in to the other person. "Choosing alone" does not remove the social setting; it replaces it with another one you did not choose.
>
> **What I am not claiming:** that autonomy is the absence of obligation; that there is a universal percent-autonomy threshold; that a researcher can read someone's felt source off their behavior; or that any relationship can be ranked as autonomy-supporting in general. The framework returns a shape and stops where an instrument must take over.
>
> **What would refute the output, written before this message:**
> 1. A unidimensional scale with a stable threshold that fully explains all three cells, where the "both" case has no distinct prediction.
> 2. Holding the ingredients fixed, changing the bond arrangement (production, subtraction, conjunction, identity) produces no systematic change in first-person reports.
> 3. An internal-only intervention that leaves contact with relationships unchanged reliably shifts felt self-direction.
> 4. A behavior-only or neural-only instrument reads the felt source across the three cells and validates against first-person report above chance.
>
> If any holds, I withdraw the affected claim. A one-line "this fails because..." is more useful to me than agreement. If you would rather not engage, no reply is needed.
>
> **What I would like to know:** what would you expect to see if the three-cell claim were true, and what would make you abandon it? I am recording that answer before showing any derivation.
>
> **If it survives, and only then:** the sources are below. The figures are the authority, not my summary of them.
> - Canonical figures and formula (local to this session): `FRAMEWORK/reference/03-FIGURES_TEXT.md`, `FRAMEWORK/figures/THE_FORMULA.md`
> - Canonical usage rule: `FRAMEWORK/reference/FIGURES_README_canonical.md`
> - Sense of agency and its measurement (open access): https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC5002400
> - Relational autonomy (Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy): https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/feminism-autonomy/
>
> **If you want to use the machinery:** the formula is in the figures. Run it on a problem you know well and can check against a ground. Write down what would refute your result *before* you run it, and state the mode of what you get. A reported failure is more useful to me than a reported success.
>
> [name]

## Two hard rules for sending

1. **One to one.** No bulk send, no forwarding to a list, no shared thread that lets recipients see each other's verdicts. Correlated verdicts are one witness (Fig 2 :40).
2. **No pooling.** A later agreement with the theory is not corroboration of the stage-1 check. Record it in a separate column if it happens, and give it zero verdict weight.
