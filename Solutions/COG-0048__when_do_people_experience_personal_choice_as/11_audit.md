## Round trip gap R_T

The Symbols table defines R_T as `gap(x, (T_E ∘ T_A)(x))`, the gap between what was sent up and what came back down. Fig 4 :30 requires it to be measured rather than assumed. I ran the round trip on the condensation the folder actually uses.

1. **Sent up (x):** the verbatim figure set, chiefly Fig 2 :36-37 (anchor), Fig 9 :54-57 (union), Fig 12 :117 (isomer), Fig 11 :39-66 (habitat), Symbols :24 (weighting).
2. **Abstraction (T_A):** the shorthand in `07_the_tether_answer.md`: per-slot first-person anchor, three cells, bond not ingredients, valence relative to residual, agent-and-habitat, no number.
3. **Concretisation (T_E):** a concrete claim about a person, say "she chose to stay because of her family, and this was not her own choice."

**R_T is nonzero.** Three things the shorthand holds do not survive the trip back down:

- the **bond provenance**. Whether the wanting preceded the act or was produced by the relation is not in the concrete description of the act; the shorthand needs a record the description does not carry.
- the **anchor scope**. The shorthand says first-person report is the anchor for the experience; the concrete claim easily slides into using the report as evidence about the cause, which Fig 8 :50 forbids.
- the **weighting values**. Symbols :24 defines `w` and says it is learnable, but supplies no number, so the concrete claim cannot be assigned a weight without an instrument.

Conversely, one thing survives cleanly: a choice can be both authored and related, and the distinction between the cells is a bond distinction rather than an ingredient distinction. That is the residue worth sending. R_T was **not** measured numerically, because no first-person instrument exists in this folder; it is recorded qualitatively as the enumerated set of dropped constraints. Treating the formula as identical to the metalaws would set R_T to zero and would be the dead-reckoning error Fig 4 :30 names.
