## 1. A shape is a constraint, not a filler

The figures README states the limit: the framework returns *"what a missing thing would have to look like (a shape, not a name)."* Fig 9 :32 "you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself." Fig 13 says the system *"can say the answer is out of reach with the library as it stands... It cannot say which primitive is missing, because naming it would mean already holding it."*

From *The Library of Babel* [opened]: *"You can cut your search short if the set of books returned is small and descriptive enough that it provides a general shape of the answer that satisfies your question."*

For this question, a **shape** is: per choice slot, first-person report is the anchor; the two readings are two conditions of a union compound with a real intersection; the difference between readings is a bond arrangement over shared ingredients; obligation valence is relative to the residual. That constrains any answer. It does not tell you which person in which room is self-directed, and it does not give a number.

A **name** would be: "this person's choice was autonomous because score X > threshold Y," or "obligations are autonomy-thwarting." Both go beyond what the derivation holds.

## 2. How a shape is obtained

- *The Library of Babel* [opened]: use the vocabulary you already have that is closest to the thing, then look for recurring new terms that overlap or correlate with it. *"With each new term gained, you narrow the space and deepen your vocabulary."*
- Fig 6 :30 "outward: does another frame already hold it? The description travels. Fails by being sharpened into terms only we can read. Cast it in effect terms." Fig 9 :30 "your words are borrowed too, so describe the effect rather than the cause."
- *Decoding Tacit Knowledge* [carried]: *"When contact isn't possible, the best you can send is a plain description of what you'd expect to see, because anyone can check that against the world."*
- *The Library of Babel* [opened] on stopping: the answer is reached when *"the finish line will also be the starting place"*, and the paradox is that by then you have *"composed the answer through the process of locating it."*

Applied here: the effect described first is "what the person reports about the source of this choice," not "what caused the choice" and not "how autonomous the person is." Describing the cause first would pick a frame (individualist or relational) and make any result fit it (Fig 8 :53 "Residual first, frame second.").

## 3. What licenses a name

Only two things, both outside the shape itself:

1. **The ground settles it.** Formula step 5: *"the ground settles it. Verified only by what pays."* Here the ground for the experience is the person's attended first-person report, because the question is about people (Fig 2 :36-37). A per-slot instrument that discriminates the three cells would close the identification gap in `01` F9.
2. **A provenanced import from a frame whose closure differs** (Fig 6 :23, Fig 8 :50-52), with a source gate and a shadow test. SEP relational autonomy and SDT internalization are such frames, but they license the *bond vocabulary*, not a verdict about any individual.

*They're Scanning Books* [carried] sharpens the limit: *"The real import is not a new term in the existing space. It is a new dimension of the space."* And: *"Declaring the primitives does not make them neutral. It makes the choice visible."* The six-term set and the seven bonds are a visible guess, not nature's own list. Turning one bond into *the* explanation of autonomy would make that choice invisible.

## 4. The source error is the specific forbidden move here

*The Hard Problem is About Source* [opened] gives the mechanism. Metamerism: an instrument reads green and *"cannot comment on the source."* One green light, or yellow plus blue, or RGB, or CMYK, or a scheme not yet discovered, all give the same reading. *"If several paths create the same behavior, then behavior is a projection with lossy information."* And: *"An instrument that can't distinguish these cases demonstrates its own inadequacy."* It was the **spectrometer**, not an argument, that resolved it.

Fig 8 :50 "Convergent derivation and adopted import look identical in the contents. Only the record of where each came from separates them." The question's central trap is reading the source of a choice off its content: a want that was produced by an obligation is content-identical to a want the person generated. The same behavior is compatible with several sources. So the derivation asks for the provenance record (which bond operated, with what weighting `w`), not for a judgment read off the act.

*The Recursive Self-Improvement Trap* [opened]: *"The output is only as good as the validator. The agent, or the tool, or the frame, or the theory, cannot validate itself."* This derivation cannot certify its own shape.

## 5. Does the framework return a name here? Answered, with a correction

**Answer:** no numeric name, and no verdict about an individual. It returns the three-cell structure, the bond/ingredient distinction, the relativity of valence, and the agent-and-habitat claim. It does not say which cell a given act occupies.

**Correction to my own first pass.** I initially wrote that the "shape, not a name" rule forbids saying *which* bond is operative. That overstates it. The README's phrase governs a *missing* primitive. The bonds are already held in the Operators table. Naming a bond arrangement in a particular case is a **binding** from the first-person frame onto held vocabulary, exactly as the exemplar corrected its own naming of Gradient. What remains forbidden is inventing a new operator for "autonomy" or claiming that the binding moves the wall (Fig 6 :21 "MINT composes inside the closure and can never add an atom"). Both the original claim and its narrowing are recorded here so neither is quietly lost.

## 6. Prestige is not action

From *The Recursive Self-Improvement Trap* [opened]: *"Prestige must never be allowed to validate an action directly."* Prestige *"can fund an action. It can give an action a budget to be tried. But it cannot settle an action. Only the ground can settle it."*

For outreach this means: a well-known autonomy theorist agreeing with the three-cell claim gives it a budget to be tried, not a verdict. The verdict must come from a per-slot check against first-person experience. And *"an interest in the outcome is unavoidable, an interest in the reading is disqualifying"* (Fig 10 :59), so a researcher's attachment to an individualist or relational frame must be declared.

## 7. My own audit is a simulation, not a ground reading

Same essay [opened]: *"LLMs can quickly simulate an approximate answer/output with the formula found in the architecture post. It will often be better than the generic outputs that you will usually get otherwise too. However, the truly testable output would need to be derived from the real architecture or working the formula yourself."*

So `01_figure_grounded_audit.md`, `06_isomers_and_order.md`, `07_the_tether_answer.md`, `09_watson_answer.md` and `11_audit.md` are simulated derivations checked for internal consistency against the figures. They are **not** tests of the claim, and they cannot settle anything they report on. No first-person data were collected for this solution.
