# Expert verification packet: the Tether on the experience of choice

**What this is:** a claim in a form a researcher in autonomy and self-determination can check. Mode, derivation and refuters are stated before the conclusion, so a reader knows what to look for and what would settle it.

**Mode: `specified` on the substantive claims, `general` on the structural shape. Not `grounded`.** No first-person measurement is claimed here. This is a shape and a route, not a result. It is a simulated derivation checked for internal consistency against the figures, not a ground reading.

## 1. The claim, in one paragraph

"Self-directed" and "shaped by obligation or relationship" are not two ends of one scale. They are two conditions that can both hold of a single choice, and the intersection is a distinct condition with its own loop. What separates the conditions is the bond arrangement over the same ingredients, not the ingredients: a want that produced the action, a want the obligation produced, a choice made against the obligation, and a choice that expresses the relationship are isomers, same atoms and different substance. Obligations have no standing valence; an obligation supports or crushes self-direction relative to what the person is trying to do. Capability is a property of person-and-habitat, and "choosing alone" substitutes a habitat rather than removing one. The framework returns this shape, writes its refuters in advance, and stops where a first-person instrument must take over.

## 2. Three checkable sub-claims

### C1: framework-internal, checkable by reading Figure 9
- **Statement:** the two readings are two conditions of a union compound, not a partition, and the "both" cell is categorically different from either alone.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 9 :54 "And when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again." Also Fig 9 :57 "a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either." And Fig 9 :4 "First: is there a fact here? Disagreements that do not shrink with effort, where each rule keeps working well on a different subset, are not one hard question but several well-formed ones. Split it rather than search."
- **Refuter:** a unidimensional measure with a stable threshold that fully explains all three cells, where the "both" case generates no distinct prediction.

### C2: framework-internal plus empirical, checkable by reading Figure 12 and by designing a study
- **Statement:** over the same cast of person, desire, obligation and alternative, four bond arrangements give different first-person readings of the source. Production (`⇒`), chained production, subtraction (`−`), and conjunction or identity (`+` / `≡`) are the arrangements.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 12 :117 "Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour." Also Fig 12 :46 "A bond is the typed relation between two operands. There are seven, they are not interchangeable, and which one holds is not recoverable from the operands."
- **What to check:** do the four arrangements, holding the cast fixed, produce systematically different source attributions at the nominated choice? Is the effect carried by the arrangement rather than by which desire or which obligation was used? SDT's internalization and SEP's constitutive relational autonomy are the world analogues of two of the four cells, but they do not substitute for the check.
- **Refuter:** changing the arrangement produces no systematic change in report, or changing the ingredients with the arrangement fixed does. Either breaks the isomer claim.

### C3: empirical, checkable against a manipulation of contact
- **Statement:** obligation valence is relative to the residual, and self-direction is agent-and-habitat; an internal-only intervention that leaves contact fixed does not move the felt source.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 11 :49 "An actor's valence is relative to the residual, not a property it carries." Also Fig 11 :39 "capability is a property of agent-and-habitat, never of the agent alone." And Fig 11 :66 "Isolation is not removal of the habitat. It is substitution of one habitat for another."
- **What to check:** take a fixed obligation and vary the person's current aim, or the closeness of the relation. Does the source attribution change as the valence prediction says? Then change only belief, hold contact fixed, and check whether the attribution moves.
- **Refuter:** a ranking of obligations as autonomy-supporting that is stable across changed aims and persons; or a reliable shift in felt self-direction from an internal-only intervention with contact held fixed.

## 3. What is explicitly not claimed

- Not a numeric autonomy threshold. No figure line carries one; this is a `gap` at the instrument level, reported as such.
- Not that the framework names who is free. It returns a shape. Fig 9 :32 "you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself."
- Not that autonomy is independence, or that isolation removes social context. Isolation substitutes a habitat.
- Not that obligations can be ranked in the abstract. Valence is relative to the residual.
- Not that a researcher can read felt source from behavior. Fig 8 :50 "Convergent derivation and adopted import look identical in the contents. Only the record of where each came from separates them."
- Not `grounded`, and not a claim that any specific person's choice has been classified here. The simulation cannot validate itself.

## 5. What would settle it

A per-slot first-person protocol that presents the three cells separately, records the bond provenance and the weighting history, and demonstrates that it discriminates the cells rather than relabeling them. Fig 8 :39 "So a search that finds nothing is never a proof of absence" applies to the current absence of such an instrument. A behavior-only or neural-only instrument that reads source across the cells and validates against first-person report above chance would also close the identification gap, and would refute the provenance claim.

4. Name one internal-only intervention you would treat as a genuine test of C3.
5. Send the refutation. A failure is more useful than an endorsement.
