## QUESTION

Decide, in effect terms, what the Tether says about the lived source of personal choice. Option A: treat "self-directed" and "obligation-shaped" as two ends of one continuum and locate a person or a choice on it. Option B: treat them as two conditions of a union compound, keep the verdicts per domain separate, and identify the bond arrangement over shared ingredients that distinguishes the experiences. What each leads to: A delivers a number the figures do not license, ranks obligations as autonomy-supporting in the abstract, and lets an observer's classification stand in for the person's experience. B delivers a checkable shape, an anchor for the experience, refuters written in advance, and an honest `gap` where a first-person instrument is required.

## DOMAIN CENSUS

R by arity, symmetry and scale, then one line per domain with actors, currency and anchor; partition or union; order by dependency.

- **R (the residual).** The gap, per choice slot, between the person's prediction about who or what authored the choice and the felt source when the choice is made. Arity: at least four slots (chooser, alternatives, relations in contact, timing). Symmetry: not symmetric under swap, because the bonds are ordered (production, subtraction, sequence, comparison). Scale: first order in one choice, second order in narrative identity, third order in the cultural habitat. Fig 1 :19 "The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated."
- **Domain 1, first-person phenomenal frame.** Actors: the person, belief `b`, weighting `w`, felt volition. Currency: first-person report of the felt source. Anchor: the person's own judgment, legitimate because the question is constitutively about people (Fig 2 :36). This is a partition question inside D1: which reading closes `R` for this slot.
- **Domain 2, social habitat.** Actors: relations, obligations, trusted others, norms, institutions. Currency: contact and introduced constraints. Anchor: the ground still decides, and valence is relative to the person's residual (Fig 11 :49). A union-within-domain: self-direction and relation can both be present.
- **Domain 3, observer and theory frame.** Actors: researchers, instruments, rival theories (individualist autonomy, relational autonomy, SDT, cross-cultural choice theory). Currency: agreement and residuals. Anchor: the ground, never agreement (Fig 2 :35). In this simulated run all seats draw on one evidence pool, so agreement here is one pool talking, not corroboration.
- **Order by dependency.** Settle the anchor (D1) before enumerating the habitat (D2) before reading the observer frames (D3), Fig 3 :20. Refuse the term 'autonomy equals absence of obligation' before minting any new account of autonomy (Formula step 2). Describe the residual before choosing a frame (Fig 8 :53).

Strain test: Fig 2 :36-37 makes judgment the anchor for questions about people, while Moore 2016 shows agency experience can diverge from causal facts. Re-census resolves this: the report is the anchor for what the experience was, an instrument and a lossy one for what caused the choice. Different quantities, no strain after re-census. A second apparent conflict between Iyengar and Lepper 1999 and SDT is handled in WORLD below.

Predicts that agency is a subjective, first-order experience, separable from ownership. **Corroborates D1.** Overlap: moderate with Moore 2016, shared literature.
- **Cross-cultural social psychology.** Iyengar and Lepper, "Rethinking the Value of Choice: A Cultural Perspective on Intrinsic Motivation", *JPSP* 76(3), 1999. Quote: "personal choice generally enhanced motivation more for American independent selves than for Asian interdependent selves." Predicts that the effect of who chooses depends on self-construal, supporting the relativity claim. **Corroborates the relativity claim, conflicts with SDT's universality reading.**
- **Cross-cultural motivation, the reply.** Bao and Lam, "Who Makes the Choice? Rethinking the Role of Autonomy and Relatedness in Chinese Children's Motivation", 2008, selfdeterminationtheory.org PDF. Quote: "freedom of choice is not equivalent to autonomy." Predicts that endorsement, not who chose, is the operative variable, and that closeness of relation conditions the effect. **Conflicts with the Iyengar and Lepper reading; corroborates the bond/ingredient distinction.**

Conflict handling. The genuine conflict is between Iyengar and Lepper 1999 and Bao and Lam 2008. Per the standing rule, it sends us back to the census and never flips a figure verdict. The census separates 'who authored the choice' (bond, Fig 12 :46) from 'does the person endorse it' (first-person anchor, Symbols :24). Iyengar and Lepper equated the two; Bao and Lam distinguish them. The conflict therefore confirms the need for the bond/ingredient distinction rather than altering the verdict. The two papers share a question and part of a literature, so their disagreement is one pool arguing with itself, not two independent witnesses.

The essays by Isaiah N (hard problem of source, library of Babel, recursive self-improvement trap, and others listed in `05_what_shape_means.md`) share the author and evidence pool of the figures, so their agreement is expected and carries little independent weight. They are used to read "shape" correctly, not as witnesses.

## VERDICT (from the figures)

The shape survives and the framing is refused. "Self-directed" and "relationship-shaped" are two conditions of a union compound, and the "both" cell is a distinct condition, not a midpoint. The felt source is anchored in first-person report per choice slot; it is a weighted construction; and the difference between cells is the bond arrangement over shared ingredients, not the ingredients. Obligations have no standing valence, self-direction is a property of person-and-habitat, isolation substitutes rather than removes, and provenance is invisible in content. What does not survive: the either/or framing, the ranking of obligations in the abstract, any claim that internal change alone moves the felt source, and any numeric threshold (a declared gap). This is a `specified`, ungrounded, candidate result. The strongest single point is the union and bond result, which is figure-derived and checkable in seven passages.

- **cost if wrong:** bounded and locatable. The packet states mode, refuters and provenance before the conclusion, so an error is findable rather than invisible. The avoidable cost is spending the independence of outside readers by sending a theory before a shape claim, which Fig 2 :42 prices: "Buy independence in proportion to the cost of being wrong." Leading with the shape claim and its refuter avoids it. A second cost is treating an observer's classification as the ground for a question about experience, which substitutes an instrument for the anchor.
- **undoable:** yes. The defective framings (one continuum, standing valence, independence as subtraction, a numeric threshold) are removable without touching the figure derivation. `07_the_tether_answer.md` and `08_expert_verification_packet.md` already remove them. No primitive has to be withdrawn, and no claim has been sent to anyone.
- **reversed by:** a ground reading, not agreement. A per-slot first-person instrument that discriminates the three cells and validates its bond-provenance coding would close the identification gap; a unidimensional scale that fully explains the "both" cell would break the union finding; a reliable effect of bond arrangement on report would confirm C2 while a null effect would break it; an internal-only intervention that moves felt source with contact fixed would break the agent-and-habitat claim; a figure-grounded derivation of a numeric threshold would close F9. A derivation of one of the six terms from the other five, or of a seventh bond, would break the substrate and is not expected. Prestige or agreement is not any of these instruments.
