## The question in effect terms

A person facing a choice can read its source two ways: "I authored this" (self-directed) and "this was shaped by an obligation or a relationship." The residual is the gap, per choice slot, between the person's prediction about the source and what the choice actually feels like when made. The question asks which conditions close that gap on which reading, and who or what is entitled to settle it. The answer does not average the two readings into one autonomy score: it keeps them as separate verdicts per domain and names the bond arrangement that distinguishes them.

## What is deliberately not here

- No personal emails of named individuals.
- No ranking of people by how likely they are to agree with the claim.
- No bulk-send plan.

Three reasons in the figures' own words:

1. **Agreement is not a verdict.** Fig 2 :35 "Alignment is triangulation, not negotiation. A mirror cannot correct you." A list selected for receptiveness manufactures the correlation that disqualifies agreement as evidence.
2. **Interests must be declared.** Fig 10 :59 "an interest in the outcome is unavoidable, an interest in the reading is disqualifying." The researcher's stake in a preferred frame is structural and is recorded, not asserted as neutral.
3. **A verifier must not hold the interiors.** Fig 10 :42 "A verifier that can reconstruct the claim cannot verify it." The first contact carries the shape claim and its refuter, not the framework's derivation.

Legally: unsolicited bulk email to personal addresses breaches GDPR and CAN-SPAM style rules in many jurisdictions. Public institutional opt-in channels avoid that failure as well as the structural one.
