## 0. The residual, in effect terms

The question asks about the *lived source* of a choice, not about its causal history in the neural or social sense. Stated as an effect:

> A person facing a choice holds a prediction about who or what authored it, `Gamma(b,a)`, built from private history and inherited prior (Symbols table :24, *"How far the belief leans on private history versus inherited prior."*). When the choice is made and experienced, an observation `o'` arrives. The residual is `R = |Gamma(b,a) - o'|` per choice slot. The question asks: under which conditions does `R` close on the reading "I authored this" and under which on the reading "an obligation or relationship shaped this"? And, prior to that, what is entitled to settle which reading is correct?

The second part is decisive and is where most discussion of this question goes wrong. Because the question is constitutively about people's experience, the anchor is the person's own judgment. Fig 2 :36 "An anchor is legitimate when the question is constitutively about it." Fig 2 :37 "Human judgment is the right anchor for is this a good answer for a person, because the question is about people." An external observer's classification is an instrument here, not the ground. Instruments can be wrong on the axes they do not measure (Fig 2 :37).

**What an answer would look like, bound as an ordinary term before the run** (Formula step, "Direction"; Fig 5 :65 "make the sought-for shape an ORDINARY TERM"): an answer is a rule that maps, per choice slot, a *bond arrangement* between the person, the desire, and the relations in contact, onto a predicted first-person reading of the source, together with a statement of what would falsify that mapping. It is not a number on an autonomy scale and not a list of autonomy-supporting versus autonomy-thwarting relationships.

## 1. Domain census

R by arity, symmetry and scale, then one line per domain giving actors, currency and anchor. A domain with no nameable anchor is not a domain yet.

### R: arity, symmetry, scale

- **Arity.** The choice event is not one slot. At minimum the chooser, the alternatives, the relations and obligations in contact, and the timing are separate slots. Fig 1 :19 "The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated." So a person can be globally content with their autonomy while one specific choice feels compelled: Fig 1 :13 "a global R near zero with one live slot residual is a legal state."
- **Symmetry.** The two readings are not symmetric under swap. The bonds that produce them are ordered: production (`⇒`, "A produces B"), subtraction (`−`, "A without B"), identity (`≡`, "two names, one referent"), conjunction (`+`, "both hold at once"). "I chose despite the obligation" and "the obligation produced my wanting" have the same ingredients and are different terms. Fig 12 :46 "A bond is the typed relation between two operands. There are seven, they are not interchangeable, and which one holds is not recoverable from the operands."
- **Scale.** First order: one choice event. Second order: the person's narrative identity across choices, where a settled first-order molecule becomes an atom (Fig 12 :104 "A settled molecule becomes an atom for whatever composes over it"). Third order: the cultural and institutional habitat. Actors, currency and anchor change at each scale (Fig 10 :68 "Different actors, a different currency, a different ground, and a lossy transform between").

### One line per domain

| domain | actors | currency | anchor | nameable? |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **D1, first-person phenomenal frame** | the person, belief `b`, weighting `w`, felt volition | first-person report of the felt source | the person's own judgment, legitimate because the question is about people | yes, Fig 2 :36-37 |
| **D2, social habitat** | relations, obligations, trusted others, norms, institutions | contact, introduced constraints, available acts | the ground still decides; valence is relative to the person's residual | yes, Fig 11 :49-51 |
| **D3, observer and theory frame** | researchers, instruments, rival theories (individualist autonomy, relational autonomy, SDT, cross-cultural choice theory) | agreement and residuals | the ground, never agreement | yes, Fig 2 :35 |

### Partition or union

**Union compound, not a partition.** Fig 9 :4 "First: is there a fact here? Disagreements that do not shrink with effort, where each rule keeps working well on a different subset, are not one hard question but several well-formed ones. Split it rather than search." Fig 9 :54 "And when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again." Fig 9 :57 "a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either." A choice can be both self-directed and relationship-shaped. That is a specific compound with its own loop, not a midpoint between two poles. A single lever such as "remove the obligation" clears the cells where only one condition holds and leaves the intersection standing (Fig 9 :55-56).

The three cells are: self-direction only, obligation-shaping only, and the intersection where a person experiences an act as fully theirs *because* it is relational. The third cell is the one that breaks the framing of the question as an either/or.

### Order by dependency (Fig 3)

Settle the anchor question first (D1), else there is no way to say which reading is correct. Then enumerate the habitat in contact with the choice (D2), because contact is what the habitat contributes (Fig 11 :43). Then read the observer frames against the ground (D3). Fig 3 :20 "The chain is ordered by dependency, not by importance." Describe the residual before choosing a frame (Fig 8 :53 "Residual first, frame second."): if the frame (individualist or relational) is named first, any result will seem to fit it.

## 3. Findings

Each finding carries a mode and a refuter. World sources are secondary corroboration only and are recorded with overlap in `10_source_ledger.csv`.

### F1: the anchor of this question is first-person experience (general, triangulated)

Fig 2 :36 "An anchor is legitimate when the question is constitutively about it."
Fig 2 :37 "Human judgment is the right anchor for is this a good answer for a person, because the question is about people."
Fig 2 :40 "agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool."

The question "when do people *experience* choice as X" is constitutively about experience. So the person's report of the felt source is the anchor. An experimenter's classification of whether the choice "really was" free is an instrument, and can be wrong on the axis of felt source (Fig 2 :37). Agreement among researchers who share the same theoretical and cultural evidence pool is one witness, not corroboration.

**Refuter.** Show that for a question constitutively about experience, a third party's classification settles the case better than the subject's own report, under conditions where the subject is attending to the choice. Until then, first-person report is the anchor for the experience, not an instrument for the cause.

**World note (secondary).** Moore 2016, *Frontiers in Psychology*, distinguishes Feeling of agency from Judgment of agency and warns that *"our experiences of agency can be quite divorced from the facts of agency."* This supports the anchor claim at the level of *what is being measured* while warning that the report is not a readout of the neural cause. It does not flip the verdict.

### F2: the experience is per choice slot, not a global trait (general, triangulated)

Fig 1 :19 "The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated."
Fig 1 :22 "Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average."
Fig 1 :13 "a global R near zero with one live slot residual is a legal state."

A person can answer "yes, I generally choose freely" while one particular decision, say a care obligation, feels entirely externally shaped. A global autonomy score averages away exactly the live signal the question asks about. Per-slot measurement is not a preference here; it follows from how `R` is indexed.

**Refuter.** A validated global trait measure that predicts the experience of a specific nominated choice better than a measure taken at that choice.

**World note (secondary).** Sense-of-agency research measures agency at the single action (intentional binding, causal judgment tasks; Wikipedia, *Sense of agency*, 2026; Moore 2016). This is corroboration from a different method, not the same evidence pool as the figures.

### F3: the two readings form a union compound, not one axis (specified, triangulated)

Fig 9 :4 "First: is there a fact here? Disagreements that do not shrink with effort, where each rule keeps working well on a different subset, are not one hard question but several well-formed ones. Split it rather than search."
Fig 9 :54 "And when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again."
Fig 9 :57 "a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either."

"Self-directed" and "shaped by obligation" are not the ends of one ruler. The cell where both hold is categorically different from either alone. This is the single most important derivation in the audit, because it changes the form of the answer: the honest answer is a per-cell map, not a threshold on one scale.

**Refuter.** A unidimensional measure with a stable threshold that fully accounts for all three cells, where the "both" case is demonstrably just a midpoint and generates no distinct prediction.

**World note (secondary).** Bao and Lam 2008 (*Self Determination Theory* site PDF) report that freedom of choice mattered to Chinese children when the mother-child relationship was not close, but not when it was close. The same act (mother chooses) falls in different cells depending on the relation. Iyengar and Lepper 1999 report the complementary pattern for trusted in-group others. Both corroborate the union reading. See §6 for the genuine conflict between them and how the census handles it.

### F4: the difference is the bond, not the ingredients (specified, single-source on the figure definition)

Fig 12 :117 "Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."
Fig 12 :46 "A bond is the typed relation between two operands. There are seven, they are not interchangeable, and which one holds is not recoverable from the operands."
Fig 12 :111 "A vocabulary without bonds can name a state and cannot name a route to one."

Four bond arrangements over the same ingredients (a person, a desire, an obligation, an alternative) produce different first-person readings:

- `desire ⇒ action` (production): "I wanted it, so I did it." Experienced as self-directed.
- `obligation ⇒ desire ⇒ action` (chained production): the relation produced the wanting, which now feels like mine. Experienced as self-directed, with a provenance question.
- `action − obligation` (subtraction): "I chose despite the obligation." Experienced as self-directed by contrast.
- `obligation + desire` (conjunction) or `self ≡ relation` (identity): "I chose because this is who I am in relation." Experienced as both self-directed and related.

This is the figure's isomer claim applied to experience: same atoms, different bond, different substance. The framework does not need a new primitive to say this. All the bonds are already in the Operators table.

**Refuter.** An experiment that holds the bond arrangement fixed and still obtains systematically different felt-source reports by changing only the ingredients, or that changes the arrangement with no change in report. Either would break the mapping.

**World note (secondary).** SDT's Organismic Integration Theory describes internalization as the transformation of an external motive into a personally endorsed value, which is the production bond in the vocabulary. The SEP entry on relational autonomy describes constitutive accounts where the relation is part of the defining condition of autonomy, which is the identity or conjunction bond. The two are different bonds, not two degrees of one thing.

### F5: an obligation's valence is relative to the residual, not a property of it (specified, triangulated)

Fig 11 :49 "An actor's valence is relative to the residual, not a property it carries."
Fig 11 :50 "The same actor is a benefactor to one residual and a malfactor to another, so mutualism and parasitism are relations rather than kinds."
Fig 11 :51 "No standing list of good actors survives a change of residual."

(The figure text uses a curly apostrophe in "actor's"; the straight form is used here for the no-em-dash style.)

So there is no standing list of autonomy-supporting and autonomy-thwarting relationships. The same obligation, the same trusted other, the same norm can support self-direction for one person's current residual and crush it for another's. Any claim of the form "obligations reduce autonomy" is a claim about a kind, and Fig 11 :50 refuses kinds in favour of relations.

**Refuter.** A ranking of specific relationships or obligations as autonomy-supporting versus autonomy-thwarting that remains stable across changed residuals and persons without reference to what the person is trying to do.

**World note (secondary).** Bao and Lam 2008 find the mother's choice effect reverses with relationship closeness. Iyengar and Lepper 1999 find the trusted-other effect depends on independent versus interdependent self-construal. Both are relation-to-residual effects. Low overlap with the figures: these are empirical social psychology, not a framework text.

### F6: self-direction is agent-and-habitat, and isolation substitutes rather than removes (specified, triangulated)

Fig 11 :39 "capability is a property of agent-and-habitat, never of the agent alone."
Fig 11 :40 "A system cannot be made more capable by improving the agent in isolation. An improvement that does not change contact changes nothing, however much it improves."
Fig 11 :66 "Isolation is not removal of the habitat. It is substitution of one habitat for another."

The everyday picture of autonomy as independence is a picture of subtraction. The figures give a different account: the ability to experience a choice as one's own is a property of the person together with their habitat. "Choosing alone" does not remove social context; it replaces one set of actors and inherited priors with another, with actors you did not choose (Fig 11 :67). A direct prediction: an intervention that changes only internal belief, leaving contact with relations and obligations untouched, does not change the felt source.

Fig 11 :61 "The seat's power is asymmetric. Introduction is available; exclusion is not." The practical lever is introduction of a supporting frame or relation, not exclusion of an obligation.

**Refuter.** A controlled intervention that changes only internal belief, with contact held fixed, and reliably shifts the first-person reading of the source. Or a demonstration that an obligation can simply be subtracted from a habitat rather than replaced.

**World note (secondary).** SEP *Feminist Perspectives on Autonomy*, quoting Nedelsky 1989: *"if we ask ourselves what actually enables people to be autonomous, the answer is not isolation, but relationships."* SDT's *The Theory* page: *"the social context can either support or thwart the natural tendencies toward active engagement and psychological growth."* Both corroborate. Overlap with each other is moderate (both respond to an atomistic picture), so this is not two fully independent witnesses.

### F7: the same behavior does not determine the felt source (general, triangulated)

Fig 8 :50 "Convergent derivation and adopted import look identical in the contents. Only the record of where each came from separates them."
Fig 9 :32 "you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself"
Fig 2 :40 "agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool."

Two people can perform the same act for the same observed reason and report different felt sources. The content of the choice does not carry its provenance. So the derivation returns a *shape*: felt source is a function of the operative bond and the weighting `w`, per slot, with first-person report as ground. It does not name which specific person in a specific case is self-directed. That is a gap at the identification level, not a silence: Fig 8 :39 "So a search that finds nothing is never a proof of absence."

**Refuter.** An instrument that reads the felt source directly from behavior or neural activity with no first-person report and no provenance record, and validates against the report at above-chance rates across the three cells of F3.

**World note (secondary).** *The Hard Problem is About Source* gives the metamerism case: an instrument that reads green *"cannot comment on the source."* The spectrometer, not an argument, resolved it. Moore 2016 gives the agency version: the feeling of agency can be divorced from the facts of agency. Both corroborate the provenance limit from different fields.

### F8: the felt source is a weighted construction (general, single-source on the symbol)

Symbols table :24 "How far the belief leans on private history versus inherited prior."
Symbols table :25 "Two frames with identical architecture diverge because their histories and their w diverge."

`w` is learnable from the outcomes of past weightings (`w_A + w_B = 1`). Applied here: the felt source of a choice depends on how far the person's prediction leans on their own history versus the inherited prior of their relations and culture. This is not a new claim imported into the framework; it is the Symbols table's own definition applied to D1. It predicts that the same bond arrangement will be read differently by people with different weighting histories, which is F5 at the intrapersonal level.

**Refuter.** A person whose reported felt source is invariant under manipulations that shift the balance of private history and inherited prior, where those manipulations are verified to have shifted the balance.

**World note (secondary).** Moore 2016's cue integration theory: *"the relative influence of the different sources of information may be linked to their reliability, with the more reliable source of information dominating the agentic experience."* This is a low-overlap corroboration from cognitive neuroscience.

### F9: no figure line yields a numeric tipping point (gap at the measurement level, honest)

Fig 4 :30 "So take the round trip and measure the gap. Without it you are navigating by dead reckoning: confident about a position nobody checked."
Fig 8 :39 "So a search that finds nothing is never a proof of absence."

No figure line gives "at 60 percent autonomy a choice is experienced as self-directed." A genuine re-census finds no primitive, bond, or operator that carries a number of that kind. This is a **gap** at the identification level, stated plainly: the framework returns a shape and a route, and stops where the instrument must take over. The honest report is `unreached`, not `unreachable`: a per-slot instrument that discriminates felt source across the three cells would close it. Treating the absence of a number as a refusal to answer would be the dead-reckoning error Fig 4 :30 names.

**Refuter (against the gap claim).** A figure-grounded derivation of a numeric threshold for the felt-source flip, using only the six terms, the seven bonds and negation, with no import. If produced, F9 is withdrawn.

## 4. Pre-registered refuter

Written before the conclusion, per the standing rule that a falsifier written before the run is a design instrument. The shape claim is refuted if any of the following holds:

1. **Anchor failure.** For a question constitutively about experience, a third party's classification reliably outperforms the subject's own attended report at identifying the felt source (breaks F1).
2. **Cell collapse.** A unidimensional scale with a stable threshold fully accounts for self-only, obligation-only, and both, with the "both" case generating no distinct prediction (breaks F3).
3. **Bond failure.** Holding ingredients fixed, changing the bond arrangement (production, subtraction, conjunction, identity) produces no systematic change in reported felt source; or holding the arrangement fixed, changing ingredients does (breaks F4).
4. **Contact failure.** An internal-only intervention that leaves contact with the habitat unchanged reliably shifts felt self-direction (breaks F6).
5. **Instrument success.** A behavior-only or neural-only instrument reads felt source across the three cells without first-person report and validates against report above chance (breaks F7 and closes F9).

If any fires, the affected finding is withdrawn and the shape claim is revised. None has been run in this simulated derivation, so nothing here is `grounded`.

## 5. Recommendation

1. Lead with the union finding (F3) and the bond finding (F4). The either/or framing of the question is the thing to refuse.
2. Measure per choice slot (F2). Do not report a single trait score.
3. Record first-person report as the anchor and the researcher's classification as an instrument (F1). Declare the researcher's stake (Fig 10 :59 "an interest in the outcome is unavoidable, an interest in the reading is disqualifying").
4. State the provenance of the desire, not just its content (F7). A want that arrived by production from a relation is content-identical to a self-generated want; only the record separates them.
5. Do not rank relationships as autonomy-supporting in the abstract (F5). Ask which residual, for whom, now.
6. Offer introduction rather than exclusion as the practical lever (F6).
7. Say `gap` at the numeric threshold (F9), and name the instrument that would close it.

### Two apparent strains, sent back to census

**Strain A: first-person report as anchor versus first-person report as unreliable.** Fig 2 :36-37 makes judgment the anchor for questions about people; Moore 2016 and *The Hard Problem is About Source* show that experience can be divorced from the cause. Re-census: these govern different quantities. The report is the anchor for *what the experience was*; it is an instrument, and a lossy one, for *what caused the choice*. No figure is strained. **Retraction recorded below.**

**Strain B: Iyengar and Lepper 1999 versus SDT (Bao and Lam 2008).** Iyengar and Lepper conclude that autonomy may have less relevance in interdependent cultures; Bao and Lam reply that freedom of choice is not autonomy, and show the effect depends on relationship closeness. This is a genuine world-source conflict. The conflict rule sends us back to the census, never to a flipped verdict. Re-census separates two quantities that Iyengar and Lepper equated: *who authored the choice* (the bond, Fig 12 :46) and *whether the person endorses it* (the felt source, D1 anchor, Symbols :24). A mother making a choice can be a production bond for the person's wanting and still be fully endorsed. The figure census already has the vocabulary to say this, so the conflict does not change the verdict; it confirms the need for F4 and F8. The two papers also share a question and partly a literature, so their disagreement is one pool arguing with itself, not two independent witnesses.

## 7. Updated verdict

The shape survives: the felt source of a choice is per slot, measured by first-person report as anchor; "self-directed" and "relationship-shaped" are two conditions of a union compound with a real intersection; and the difference between readings is the bond arrangement over shared ingredients, not an amount of autonomy. Obligation valence is relative to the person's residual, capability is agent-and-habitat, and isolation substitutes a habitat rather than removing one. The provenance of a want is not recoverable from its content. What does not survive: the either/or framing, any ranking of obligations as autonomy-supporting in the abstract, any claim that internal change alone moves the felt source, and any numeric threshold. No part is `grounded`. The strongest single point is the union/bond result, which is figure-derived and checkable in twenty minutes (see `08_expert_verification_packet.md`).
