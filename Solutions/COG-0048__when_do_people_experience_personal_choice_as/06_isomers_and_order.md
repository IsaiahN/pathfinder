# Isomers and order: the bond arrangement of a choice

**Mode: `specified`.** This is a simulated derivation checked against the figures, not a ground reading (see `05_what_shape_means.md` §7).

## RESIDUAL

The first audit found that "self-directed" and "obligation-shaped" are two conditions of a union compound, and that the difference between their experiences is carried by the bond rather than the ingredients. Two things are still owed: (1) a precise structural account of the bond arrangements, and (2) the order in which the derivation must run, including which term is refused before anything is minted.

## DOMAIN CENSUS

Atoms held for one choice slot: the person (a state-like term at this level), a desire or reason, an obligation or relation, and the alternative not taken. Bonds held: all seven plus negation. The weighting `w` decides how far the prediction leans on private history versus inherited prior (Symbols :24). No new primitive is available without import.

## STRUCTURE: the four arrangements are isomers

Fig 12 :117 "Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."
Fig 12 :124 "functional group. A substructure whose presence fixes the outcome. The group says what kind; the qualifier says which member."
Fig 12 :46 "A bond is the typed relation between two operands. There are seven, they are not interchangeable, and which one holds is not recoverable from the operands."

Write the cast once as `{person, desire, obligation, alternative}`. Now vary only the bond:

| arrangement | figure bond | first-person reading | world analogue |
|---|---|---|---|
| `desire ⇒ action` | production, "A produces B; B did not exist before" | "I wanted it, so I did it" | intrinsic motivation (SDT, CET) |
| `obligation ⇒ desire ⇒ action` | chained production | "I now want what was asked of me, and it feels like mine" | internalization (SDT, OIT); adaptive preference (SEP) |
| `action − obligation` | subtraction, "A without B" | "I chose despite what was expected" | independence as contrast |
| `obligation + desire` or `person ≡ relation` | conjunction / identity | "I chose because this is who I am here" | relational autonomy, constitutive accounts (SEP) |

All four share the same atoms. Under Fig 12's definition they are **isomers**: the difference is invisible in the ingredient list and decisive in the experience. As a weaker fallback, if a given case shares only some atoms with another, the two are members of the same **functional group** (the `state → structure → state` choice motif), not isomers. This is the same move the exemplar made for GR and QM: keep the isomer claim only where the full shared atom set is present; otherwise assert the functional-group claim.

**Falsifier, written before the run:** if a change of bond across the four arrangements produces no systematic change in first-person source reports, or a change of atoms with the bond held fixed does, the isomer claim fails and only the functional-group claim survives.

## ORDER: what comes before what

Three distinct orders, all figure-derived, none to be conflated.

1. **Order of dependency (Fig 3).** Settle the anchor (who decides what the experience is), then enumerate the habitat in contact, then read the observer frames against the ground. Fig 3 :20 "The chain is ordered by dependency, not by importance." Effort spent above the break arrives at a step that cannot use it.
2. **Order of refusal before mint (Formula step 2).** The everyday term "autonomy equals absence of obligation" is a held term that predicts wrongly for the third cell. What is owed first is a refusal, not a new theory of autonomy. Fig 5 :53 "refuted: a term we hold is wrong; a term is owed that says so." A vocabulary without negation can only accumulate (Operators :58), so without this refusal the derivation would add a new autonomy concept on top of the wrong one.
3. **Order inside the bond.** `⇒` and `−` and `→` are ordered bonds; swapping operands changes the term (Fig 12 :74 "swap them. Does it become a different thing? Yes."). "The relation produced my wanting" is not "my wanting produced the act with the relation present." The order of production is part of the substance.

## ANALOGUES: three low-overlap corroborations

Analogues corroborate; they do not decide. Fig 8 :53 "Residual first, frame second." The residual was described before these frames were chosen.

- **Metamerism (perception / instrumentation).** *The Hard Problem is About Source* (2026-08-28): an instrument that reads green "cannot comment on the source." Several sources, one reading. Analogue for: why the content of a choice cannot tell you its felt source. Corroborates F7.
- **SoA / SoO dissociation ( cognitive neuroscience).** Moore 2016 and the Wikipedia entry on sense of agency: a person can feel ownership of a movement without feeling agency over it. Analogue for: why "I did it" and "I authored it" are separate slots, not one judgment. Corroborates F1 and F2.
- **Cue integration (neuroscience).** Moore 2016: *"the relative influence of the different sources of information may be linked to their reliability, with the more reliable source of information dominating the agentic experience."* Analogue for the weighting `w` between private history and inherited prior. Corroborates F8.

None of these is a seventh bond. A relation that fails all six bond tests is not an operator you are missing; it is a claim not yet made (Fig 12 :90 "Six tests, and they separate the seven bonds. Seven is a count of bonds, not of operators.").

## WHAT ORDER THE UNION HAS

Fig 9 :57 "a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either." The three-cell map is not a sequence from least to most autonomous. It is a set of overlapping conditions. Fig 9 :55 "A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones." The union is unbundled before search; each cell gets its own loop. Then, at second order, a settled first-order choice can become an atom for narrative identity: Fig 12 :104 "A settled molecule becomes an atom for whatever composes over it." That is why a person can tell a stable story about being self-directed while individual choices fall into different cells.

## STRAIN

One strain remains live and is recorded, not smoothed: the figure vocabulary can name the bond arrangement, but no figure line gives the reliability values that set `w` for a person. Symbols :24 defines `w` and says it is learnable; it does not give a number. That is the instrument-level gap already marked in `01` F9. Treating the analogy to cue integration as if it supplied the values would be an import with no source gate (Fig 6 :23 "No frame escapes its own closure. The bound moves only on contact with a frame whose closure differs.").

## CONCLUSION

The four arrangements are isomers over one cast when the full atom set is shared, and members of one functional group when it is not. Order matters in three separate senses: dependency of domains, refusal before mint, and operand order within a bond. Three low-overlap analogues (metamerism, SoA/SoO dissociation, cue integration) corroborate the provenance, slot and weighting claims, but none supplies a numeric threshold. The falsifier for the structure claim is stated above and was written before this conclusion.
