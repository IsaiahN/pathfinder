# The Tether's answer: when is choice experienced as self-directed?

**Mode: `specified` on substantive claims, `general` on the structural shape. Not `grounded`.** This is a shape and a route, not a result. It is a simulated derivation checked against the figures (`05_what_shape_means.md` §7), not a ground reading.

## 1. The residual it answers

A person facing a choice reads its source two ways: "I authored this" and "this was shaped by an obligation or relationship." The question asks when each reading is experienced, and who may settle it. Because the question is constitutively about people's experience, the anchor is first-person report; an observer's classification is an instrument (Fig 2 :36 "An anchor is legitimate when the question is constitutively about it."). The residual is per choice slot, not global (Fig 1 :19 "The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated.").

## 2. The shape

1. **The two readings are two conditions, not two ends of a scale.** They can both hold of one choice. A person can experience an act as fully theirs *because* it is relational. That third cell is categorically different from either alone (Fig 9 :54 "And when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again."). The question as asked, "when A and when B," is therefore mis-framed if it assumes a partition.
2. **What differs between the cells is the bond, not the ingredients.** Over the same cast of person, desire, obligation and alternative, the arrangements `desire ⇒ action`, `obligation ⇒ desire ⇒ action`, `action − obligation` and `obligation + desire` / `person ≡ relation` are isomers: Fig 12 :117 "Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour." The framework returns the shape of the mapping, not a verdict about any individual.
3. **The felt source is a weighted construction.** Symbols :24 "How far the belief leans on private history versus inherited prior." Two people with identical architecture diverge because their histories and weights diverge. So the same arrangement can be read differently by different people, and the same person at different times.
4. **An obligation has no standing valence.** Fig 11 :49 "An actor's valence is relative to the residual, not a property it carries." The same obligation supports self-direction for one person's current aim and crushes it for another's. Any claim that obligations reduce autonomy as a kind is refused.
5. **Self-direction is agent-and-habitat.** Fig 11 :39 "capability is a property of agent-and-habitat, never of the agent alone." Fig 11 :66 "Isolation is not removal of the habitat. It is substitution of one habitat for another." "Choosing independently" replaces one set of actors you did not choose with another. A prediction follows: internal change alone, with contact held fixed, does not move the felt source. The practical lever is introduction, not exclusion (Fig 11 :61 "The seat's power is asymmetric. Introduction is available; exclusion is not.").
6. **Provenance is not recoverable from content.** Fig 8 :50 "Convergent derivation and adopted import look identical in the contents. Only the record of where each came from separates them." A want produced by a relation is content-identical to a self-generated want. What is owed is the record of the bond, not a judgment read off the act.
7. **No number is owed and none is available.** No figure line gives a percent-autonomy threshold. That is a `gap` at the instrument level (Fig 8 :39 "So a search that finds nothing is never a proof of absence."). The framework returns a shape and stops where an instrument must take over.

## 4. What it explicitly does not say

- Not that autonomy is independence, or the absence of obligation. Isolation substitutes a habitat; it does not remove one.
- Not that obligations can be ranked as autonomy-supporting or autonomy-thwarting in general. Valence is relative to the residual.
- Not that a researcher can read felt source off behavior. The same reading has several possible sources.
- Not that a third cell means "half autonomous." It is a different condition with its own loop.
- Not that any specific bond has been shown to operate in any specific person here. That needs first-person data.
- Not `grounded`. No first-person measurement was taken in this derivation.

## 5. What would settle it

The ground, not agreement. The required instrument is a per-slot first-person protocol that:
- presents the three cells separately and asks for source attribution at the nominated choice;
- records the bond provenance (did the want precede the act, was it produced by the relation, was it chosen against the obligation, is the relation part of the self-description);
- records the weighting history before the choice;
- and can be shown to discriminate the cells rather than merely relabel them.

Four standing falsifiers, written before the run (full list in `01` §4): unidimensional cell collapse; bond arrangement with no effect on report; internal-only intervention shifting felt source with contact fixed; behavior-only instrument reading source above chance. A derivation of a numeric threshold from the six terms and seven bonds would close the `gap` in §2.7.

## 6. The honest stop

`Unreached`, not `unreachable`. The three-cell shape, the bond mapping and the habitat claims are figure-derived. The identification of any particular case, and the number, are not. The cavity of the question stays open until the ground pays. The lineage is recorded so the stop is checkable. Indiscernibility is not identity.

## 7. One-paragraph version for outreach

> The Tether does not give an autonomy score. It says that "self-directed" and "shaped by relationship" are two conditions that can both hold of one choice, so the third case, an act that feels fully mine *because* it is relational, is not a midpoint but a different condition. What separates the cases is not the people or the obligations involved but the bond between them: a want that produced the action, a want the relation produced, a choice against the obligation, and a choice that expresses the relation all feel different with the same cast. An obligation is not autonomy-supporting or autonomy-thwarting in general; its valence depends on what the person is trying to do, and choosing alone replaces one social habitat with another rather than removing it. The framework returns that shape, names the instruments that would close it, and writes its refuters in advance. It does not name who is free, and it gives no threshold.
