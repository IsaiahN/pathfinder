# Order, union and analogues in the five-condition answer

**Mode: `specified` on the ordering and on the union claim, `single-source` on the isomer claim, not
`grounded`.** This is a simulated derivation checked against the figures, not a ground reading
(`05_what_shape_means.md` section 7).

## RESIDUAL

The main audit left three open structural questions: (1) in what order do the five conditions depend
on one another, (2) is the compound a partition or a union, and (3) are the five conditions variants
of one thing (isomers) or members of a family (a functional group)?

## DOMAIN CENSUS, RESTATED BRIEFLY

Atoms held: frames, per-slot residuals, ground and anchor, negation, library, closure, reach, bond
types, rooms, membrane, habitat. Bonds held: the seven plus the unary negation operator. No new
primitive is available without import. Conditions D1 to D5 are arrangements over this shared set, not
new members of it. Anchor for each is a ground that does not update (Fig 2 :29).

## DERIVATION

### Q1 - What is the dependency order?

Fig 3 :20: "The chain is ordered by dependency, not by importance." Fig 3 :10 names the objective
link: "it can say what would count as progress here, in its own terms". Fig 3 :19: "Optimising a link
that already works cannot rescue one that does not." Fig 3 :24: "Why the middle link is where chains
usually break."

Ordering the five conditions by what each consumes from the one before:

1. **D1 contact.** Two frames, each generating a per-slot residual against its own ground. Without
   distinct residuals there is nothing to disagree about. Fig 1 :19: "The residual is indexed by
   object slot, not aggregated."
2. **D2 objective.** A check that can fail a frame and does not update. This is the middle link: a
   community can have rich disagreement and still lack a way to say what would count as a correction.
   Fig 3 :27: "A link with no instrument is not a link that works. It is a link nobody has looked at."
3. **D3 vocabulary.** Terms that can be refused, plus a checker that cannot author the claim. Fig 5
   :53: "refuted: a term we hold is wrong; a term is owed that says so".
4. **D4 reach.** Settlement changes what is composable; imports and instruments move the wall;
   refusals lower the reach by addition. Fig 6 :43: "So the closure and the reach are two quantities:
   one monotone, one not."
5. **D5 transmission.** Only after a condition has been settled can it become a method that crosses
   to another room. Fig 12 :104: "A settled molecule becomes an atom for whatever composes over it."
   Fig 4 :22: "Only methods cross up. Only head starts come down."

**Falsifier, written before the run:** if a community shows reliable ground-settled revision with D4
working while D2's anchor moves with opinion, the order is wrong and D2 is not upstream of D4. That
would break Fig 3 :20 and Fig 2 :29 without flipping the per-domain verdicts.

**Status: `triangulated`** across Fig 3 (dependency), Fig 5 (refusal) and Fig 12 (settled molecule as
atom). Low overlap between a chain diagram, a pipeline amendment and a composition rule.

### Q2 - Partition or union?

Fig 9 :54: "And when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search
again." Fig 9 :55: "A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of
separate ones." Fig 9 :57: "What remains is smaller and it is also categorically different: a case in
two conditions is not a harder case of either." Fig 9 :4: "Disagreements that do not shrink with
effort, where each rule keeps working well on a different subset, are not one hard question but
several well-formed ones. Split it rather than search."

Reasoning: a lever that fixes D2 (install an external check) leaves D3 standing, because a community
with an anchor but no refusal vocabulary can still only accumulate. A lever that fixes D3 (teach
criticism) leaves D5 standing, because a single excellent room is not a chain. Therefore no single
lever clears the compound, and a case missing both D2 and D3 is a specific compound, not a more
severe case of either.

**Falsifier:** a single documented intervention that simultaneously installs all five conditions and
whose effect on ground-settled revision is separable, with no residual condition remaining. That
would support a partition reading and break Fig 9 :57.

**Status: `triangulated`** across Fig 9 (union) and Fig 8 (surplus lives where closures differ).

### Q4 - Isomers or a functional group?

Fig 12 :117: "isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in
the formula and decisive in the behaviour." Fig 12 :123: "functional group. A substructure whose
presence fixes the outcome. The group says what kind; the qualifier says which member." Fig 12 :94:
"The arrangement is the substance."

Read over the full shared atom set {frames, residual, ground, negation, library, reach, bond, room,
membrane, habitat}, each condition is a `frame – structure – frame` arrangement:

- D1: frame – independence – frame.
- D2: frame – non-updating anchor – frame.
- D3: held term – negation – checker.
- D4: library – settlement/import – reach.
- D5: room – residual-matched selection – room.

Because all five are written over the same atom set and differ only in which structure mediates
which pair, the **isomer claim** is live under Fig 12 :117. If a condition instead needs an atom the
others do not, the weaker **functional-group** claim stands, since all five share the substructure
of a check between two frames.

**Falsifier:** show that one condition cannot be written without an atom another requires, for example
that refusal needs a primitive not present in the transmission arrangement, or that a non-updating
anchor cannot be expressed over frames and residuals. That kills the isomer claim and leaves the
functional-group claim.

**Status: `single-source`** for the figure definition of isomer and functional group, with the test
stated and not run. The analogue table is separate corroboration for individual conditions, not for
the isomer claim itself.

## CONCLUSION

The dependency order is D1 to D5, with D2 the usual broken middle link. The compound is a union, not
a partition, so verdicts stay per condition and are never averaged. Over the shared atom set the five
conditions are a real isomer claim with a written falsifier; the functional-group reading is the
fallback. No world source overrode a figure line, and the one apparent strain (consensus-oriented
deliberation) was dissolved by re-census into the convention layer.
