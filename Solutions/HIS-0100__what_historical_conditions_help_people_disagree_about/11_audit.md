## Round trip gap R_T

The Symbols table defines R_T as `gap(x, (T_E ∘ T_A)(x))`, the gap between what was sent up and what came back down. Fig 4 :30 requires it to be measured rather than assumed. I ran the round trip on the condensation the folder actually uses.

1. **Sent up (x):** the verbatim figure set, chiefly Fig 2 :29/:31/:33 (anchor and independence), Fig 5 :53/:55 (refusal), Fig 6 :42/:43 (reach), Fig 4 :22 (membrane) and Fig 7 :36 (room selection).
2. **Abstraction (T_A):** the shorthand in `07_the_tether_answer.md`: five conditions, five anchors, no averaging, agreement as convention, case reading as `gap`.
3. **Concretisation (T_E):** a claim about actual historical episodes, for example "the Royal Society was open because it had an anchor" or "a field revised because it admitted a refusal".

**R_T is nonzero.** Four things the shorthand holds do not survive the trip back down:

- **which concrete object was the anchor** in an episode (Fig 2 :29 states the form, not the name);
- **whether rooms actually differed** and matched a surfaced residual, rather than merely using different words (Fig 7 :36);
- **whether a reach change was by import, instrument or refusal**, each of which needs a different record (Fig 6 :14, :33, :42);
- **the case-specific contribution of each condition**, which is the declared `gap` itself.

One thing survives cleanly: a productive disagreement needs distinct evidence, a check that does not move, a refusal, a reach change and a non-echoing handoff. That is the residue worth sending. R_T was **not** measured numerically, because no ground reading exists; it is recorded qualitatively as the set of dropped constraints. Treating the formula as identical to the figures would set R_T to zero and commit the dead-reckoning error Fig 4 :30 names.
