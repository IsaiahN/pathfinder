## 0. The residual in effect terms

The question asks for historical conditions under which a community can hold three things at once without any of them defeating the others:

1. **Disagreement persists.** Two or more frames generate different per-slot residuals against their own grounds, and neither is absorbed into the other.
2. **Evidence still bites.** The thing appealed to changes what a frame may say, and does not change in response to what any frame says.
3. **Criticism and revision are possible.** A held term can be refused, a checker can check without being able to author the claim, and the reachable set can change when the ground pays for something new.

"Historical" adds a fourth requirement: the arrangement has to survive transmission, so what crosses from one generation or one room to the next must make the next search cheaper rather than merely repeat a past success.

Stated as R, the gap is between a community's current handling of disagreement and an arrangement that keeps those four properties. Arity: at least two frames plus an anchor plus a transmission path, indexed per slot, never aggregated (Fig 1 :19: "The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated."). Symmetry: independence between frames is symmetric and two-valued in effect (how much they draw on the same evidence), while the anchor's relation to a frame is asymmetric, because the anchor does not update (Fig 2 :33: "The anchor must not update."). Scale: one frame loop, one community arrangement, one intergenerational membrane.

**Options.** A: openness is a trait of individuals, so find open-minded people and disagreement will stay productive. B: disagreement is a defect to be removed by reaching consensus on causes, evidence and standards. C (the figure-derived third): disagreement stays productive only under a union of structural conditions, each with a nameable anchor, and no single one is sufficient.

What an answer would look like, bound as an ordinary term before the run: a list of conditions each with (i) actors, (ii) a currency, (iii) an anchor that does not move when a frame pushes on it, and (iv) a failure test that would show the condition absent. That is the shape we let accrue a residual, per `THE_FORMULA.md` step "Direction".

## 2. Findings

### F1 - The disagreement must be between independent frames, not between two names for one pool (D1, decisive)

Fig 2 :13-15 names the first collapse: "no anchor; each updates on the others", "they converge, and what they converge on is whoever had the most influence". Fig 2 :18-21 names the second: "one evidence pool", "they never talk, and still agree", "so the agreement is the pool talking, not two frames confirming each other". Against both, Fig 2 :31: "Independence, between two frames, is how little they draw on the same evidence." Fig 2 :32: "Independence is what makes agreement mean something."

Fig 8 :47: "Independence is one quantity read twice." Fig 8 :49: "Two frames that overlap almost completely are one frame wearing two names." Fig 13 :38: "independence is how little two frames draw on the same evidence, and it is what makes their agreement mean anything." Fig 1 :19 and :22 keep the signal per slot: "Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average."

**Verdict (`specified`).** Sustained, productive disagreement requires frames whose closures differ, meaning different instruments, different records and different derivation routes. Politeness, diversity of wording or a shared journal do not supply independence. If both sides read the same report, they are one frame with two names and their disagreement carries no surplus. The residue must be reported per frame and per slot; an "openness index" averaged over a community is a budget filter, not a witness (Fig 9 :60).

**Refuter.** A documented episode in which a joint result was ground-settled and independently checkable while both disagreeing parties demonstrably drew on one and the same evidence pool, with no separate instrument on either side. That would break Fig 2 :31, Fig 8 :47 and Fig 13 :38.

**Status: `triangulated`** across Fig 2 (alignment diagram), Fig 8 (union geometry) and Fig 13 (independence applied to the substrate set). Low overlap between three different objects.

### F2 - Openness to evidence requires an anchor that does not update (D2, decisive)

Fig 2 :29: "An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks." Fig 2 :33: "The anchor must not update." Fig 2 :34 warns what happens when it does: "the anchor becomes another frame, everything drifts together, and the drift is coherent, which is why nobody notices." Fig 2 :45: "Frames range against the ground. They do not negotiate with it." Fig 2 :46: "The anchor is not a third party to the disagreement and it is not the tie-breaker in a vote." Fig 2 :47: "A ground listed as one of the parties has already become a frame."

Fig 5 :70: "A term is settled when the ground pays for it." Fig 1 :23: "The ground enters as the only metric." Fig 6 :25: "An import adds no atom to the world." The ground is not enlarged by agreement; only a frame's access changes (Fig 6 :23).

**Verdict (`specified`).** "Open to evidence" is not a disposition. It is a structural relation: a check that a frame can fail and that cannot be moved by the frame's belief, reputation or vote. Where the check is a committee that revises its standard in response to the claim, the arrangement is Fig 2 panel 1, coherent drift, and openness has been replaced by negotiation. Historically this is why an institutional practice of external verification matters more than an exhortation to be open minded. The Royal Society's motto, from the fetched `Nullius in verba` page, is an instance of exactly this condition: an expressed determination to withstand the domination of authority and to verify statements by an appeal to facts determined by experiment.

**Refuter.** A case in which the supposed anchor changed its answer in response to what a participating frame believed or published, yet both frames were still independently corrected by it and a ground-settled joint result followed. That would break Fig 2 :29 and :33.

**Status: `triangulated`** across Fig 2 (the anchor panel), Fig 5 (settlement) and Fig 1 (ground as only metric), with the historical motto as a low-overlap world corroboration only.

### F3 - Criticism needs the refusal operator, and the checker must not be able to author the claim (D3)

The Operators table :58: "a refutation is a negation, which is why a vocabulary without it can only accumulate"; :73: "Negation is the refusal of a claim." Fig 5 :53: "refuted: a term we hold is wrong; a term is owed that says so." Fig 5 :55: "What it removes is not a term but a term's standing to be composed over." Fig 5 :56: "Nothing leaves the library. What falls is the reach." Fig 5 :59 to :60: a term whose residual "PERSISTS AND DOES NOT TREND DOWN" is the one to point the refusal at.

Fig 10 :42: "A verifier that can reconstruct the claim cannot verify it." Fig 9 :51: "evidence bearing on the residual is admissible; evidence bearing only on its maker is a stake-inducing channel, and the verifier is denied it". Fig 10 :59: "an interest in the outcome is unavoidable, an interest in the reading is disqualifying." Fig 10 :60: "Where a stake cannot be removed, declare it."

**Verdict (`specified`).** Being open to criticism means (i) holding terms that can be refused, (ii) ranking candidate refusals by a residual that persists and does not trend down, (iii) keeping the checker unable to reproduce the claim from the framework's interior, and (iv) admitting evidence about the residual but not about the maker's standing. A community that can only add terms, or that asks a reviewer to endorse a fully supplied theory, is not open to criticism in this sense. Pre-registration is the institutional form of the same move: the world source on Registered Reports describes peer review before results are known, so the protocol rather than the attractive outcome is what gets accepted.

**Refuter.** A case where criticism demonstrably corrected a held term although the checker could reconstruct the claim from the framework's own interiors, and no residual was recorded before the run. That would break Fig 10 :42 and Fig 5 :59 to :60.

**Status: `triangulated`** across Fig 5 (the fifth kind), the Operators table (unary negation) and Fig 10 / Fig 9 (the checker's offices). Low overlap between a pipeline amendment, an operator definition and a checking arrangement.

### F4 - Revision changes the reach, not the size of the library (D4)

Fig 6 :21: "MINT composes inside the closure and can never add an atom." Fig 6 :22: "NOVEL means novel relative to atoms, not to the world." Fig 6 :23: "No frame escapes its own closure. The bound moves only on contact with a frame whose closure differs." Fig 8 :32: "no search, however long, adds an atom". Fig 5 :72: "a library that grows is not a reach that grows."

Fig 6 :42: "A refutation adds a term that refuses; it removes nothing, and the reach falls because something was added rather than because anything was deleted." Fig 6 :43: "So the closure and the reach are two quantities: one monotone, one not." Fig 6 :14 names the only wall-moving operator: "the only operator that moves the wall". That operator is import, and imports carry a source gate and a shadow test (Fig 6 :11-13).

Instruments are extended from worse instruments already returning something. Fig 6 :34: "Where nothing is, there is no edge to extend from". Fig 6 :33: "every sharper instrument was built on the residual the last one produced." Fig 6 :29: "inward: is our representation adequate to hold it? The instrument extends access." Fig 6 :30: "outward: does another frame already hold it? The description travels." Fig 6 :31: "Neither answer tells you much about the other. Run them as two tracks, and never read one against the other."

**Verdict (`specified`).** Historical revision is not the accumulation of more commentary inside one frame. It requires either a new atom entering from a frame whose closure differs, or an instrument sharpened from one already returning an imperfect reading, or a refusal that lowers the reach by addition. Collecting more of the same evidence inside the same closure cannot produce revision; this is definitional (Fig 8 :32). The replication-crisis source supplies a low-overlap instance: replication failures are described there as the normal operation of science, a mechanism by which unsupported hypotheses are eliminated, which is a reach change, not a library change.

**Refuter.** A documented case in which a new atom was minted entirely inside one closure with no import and no instrument extension, was later settled by the ground, and the atom was not already present among that frame's primitives. That would break Fig 6 :21, :23 and Fig 8 :32. Or a case where a term genuinely disappeared from the record rather than being refused by addition (breaks Fig 6 :42).

**Status: `triangulated`** across Fig 6, Fig 8 and Fig 5, with the metascience source as a distinct-field corroboration only.

### F5 - Historical transmission is a membrane and a room chain, not a recording handed along (D5)

Fig 4 :22: "Only methods cross up. Only head starts come down." Fig 4 :25: "A recording carried upward looks like knowledge and is a description of one occasion." Fig 4 :30: "So take the round trip and measure the gap. Without it you are navigating by dead reckoning: confident about a position nobody checked." Fig 4 :31 to :32: the gap "is the only honest report of how much was given up".

Fig 7 :13: "same closure, handed along; agreement compounds, evidence does not". Fig 7 :35: "A room's agreement is variation, not a verdict. Selection between rooms is where the ground votes." Fig 7 :36: "A chain of mirrors is one long room. The next room must differ from the last, and match the residual it surfaced." Fig 7 :42: "the next room you book is the direction you travel, and booking a room like the last one is how a chain stops travelling at all."

Fig 11 :39: "capability is a property of agent-and-habitat, never of the agent alone." Fig 11 :40: "An improvement that does not change contact changes nothing, however much it improves." Fig 11 :61: "The seat's power is asymmetric. Introduction is available; exclusion is not." Fig 11 :66: "Isolation is not removal of the habitat. It is substitution of one habitat for another." Fig 11 :73: "The habitat is not the ground. The habitat is where actors come from and it updates; the ground is what settles, and it does not."

**Verdict (`specified`).** "Historical conditions" are habitat conditions. What a later generation can inherit is a method that can be reapplied and a head start that makes its search cheaper, never a recording of one past success. A tradition that merely agrees with its predecessor is one long room: motion, no progress. Progress across handoffs needs low overlap between rooms, a residual surfaced by the earlier room that the later room matches, and a habitat still in contact with a ground that does not decay. Because exclusion is not available and isolation substitutes a habitat, a community cannot simply remove the conditions that make its view unfalsifiable; it can only introduce new contact and pay the cost of what came with it.

**Refuter.** A documented chain of many agreeing rooms, each closing the same way as the last, that nevertheless produced a ground-settled, independently checkable advance in knowledge with no new instrument, no import and no residual-matched room. That would break Fig 7 :13, :35 and :36. Or a case where a capability survived in isolation with no ground contact and no substituted habitat (breaks Fig 11 :39, :66).

**Status: `triangulated`** across Fig 4 (the membrane), Fig 7 (the room chain) and Fig 11 (the habitat). Low overlap between a scale transform, a handoff chain and a contact enumeration.

### F6 - Agreement has valence: it is the product for a convention and disqualifying for a verdict

Fig 2 :39: "Correlation has valence." Fig 2 :40: "agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool". Fig 2 :41: "agreement about a shared meaning is the product, not a corruption of it". Fig 10 :46: "A convention makes no claim about the world; a verdict and an atom both do." Fig 10 :47: "a convention that decides an outcome has stopped being one". Fig 10 :17: "it makes the bug statable", and Fig 10 :18: "a declared meaning is not a grounded one". Fig 10 :19: "→ install what can be violated".

**Verdict (`general`).** In a historically open community, agreement is legitimate only where the thing agreed on is a convention, a declared meaning that makes a disagreement statable and carries no truth value. The moment that convention decides which knowledge claim wins, it becomes a frame and the arrangement collapses. This is the precise distinction that lets a community share standards of criticism without converting those standards into a verdict about the world.

**Refuter.** A case where a shared convention demonstrably decided a knowledge outcome yet both frames remained independently corrected by the ground. That would break Fig 10 :47 and Fig 2 :43.

**Status: `triangulated`** across Fig 2 (correlation valence), Fig 10 (the convention office) and Fig 3 (the objective link).

**Boundary, not a strain.** F1 and F2 disqualify agreement as a verdict; F6 permits it as a convention. These govern different objects and are not in conflict. A re-census was run (section 6).

### F7 - The conditions are a union, so verdicts stay separate and are never averaged

Fig 9 :4: "Disagreements that do not shrink with effort, where each rule keeps working well on a different subset, are not one hard question but several well-formed ones. Split it rather than search." Fig 9 :54: "And when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again." Fig 9 :57: "What remains is smaller and it is also categorically different: a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either." Fig 12 :126: "A composition costs in two currencies, and they do not add." Fig 12 :94: "The arrangement is the substance."

**Verdict (`general`).** The five conditions cannot be combined into a single "open inquiry score". A community may satisfy the anchor condition and fail the refusal condition; that is not a weaker case of the anchor condition, it is a different compound with its own loop. Fig 9 :60: "Use filters for the budget and witnesses for the verdict. Never let a filter hand you a verdict."

**Refuter.** A case where a single scalar measure of institutional openness predicted and explained ground-settled revision across all five conditions without losing the per-frame signal. That would break Fig 1 :19, :22 and Fig 9 :57.

**Status: `triangulated`** across Fig 9 (split and unbundle), Fig 12 (bond versus ingredient list) and Fig 1 (per-slot residual).

### F8 - The specific historical conditions of a named episode are a `gap`, not a silence

Fig 9 :32: "you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself". Fig 9 :44: "Only a sealed room can be searched to the end, and no frame is a sealed room." Fig 13 :134: "Unreachable is a verdict here, not a silence". Fig 13 :135: "It cannot say which primitive is missing, because naming it would mean already holding it." Fig 6 :27: "some unreachability is depth and some is genuine absence, and from inside the frame the two look the same." Fig 8 :39: "So a search that finds nothing is never a proof of absence."

**Verdict (`gap`).** The framework returns the shape of the conditions a productive disagreement must satisfy. It does not return, and this run did not measure, which of them obtained in a particular historical episode, how much each contributed, or which missing atom would have made an unproductive episode productive. That is unreached at this budget, reported as a verdict rather than omitted. The instrument owed is comparative: episodes varying one condition at a time while holding the others visible, with the ground outcome recorded separately from the community agreement.

**Refuter.** A dataset that varies one condition at a time across comparable episodes and reliably separates the contribution of each to ground-settled revision. That would close the gap and would move this finding from `gap` to `specified`.

**Status: `single-source` for the shape limit within the figures (Fig 9 and Fig 13), corroborated by the Library of Babel essay with maximal evidence-pool overlap, so not an independent witness.**

### F9 - Analogues: the five conditions form a functional group, not five instances of one thing

Fig 12 :123: "functional group. A substructure whose presence fixes the outcome. The group says what kind; the qualifier says which member." Fig 12 :117: "isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour." Fig 12 :104: "A settled molecule becomes an atom for whatever composes over it." Fig 12 :106: "Nothing composes over what was never admitted as a part."

**Verdict (`specified`).** Each condition is a `frame – structure – frame` arrangement over the same substrate: two frames and the independence relation (D1), frames and the non-updating anchor (D2), held terms and the refusal operator (D3), library and reach linked by settlement (D4), rooms and the membrane linked by a residual-matched handoff (D5). They share a functional group over a common atom set, so the stronger isomer claim is testable: if all five are written over the full shared atom set and differ only in which structure mediates which pair, they are isomers under Fig 12 :117. If a condition needs an atom the others do not, the functional-group reading stands instead.

**Refuter.** A condition that can be written without an atom another condition requires, for example refusal without a ground that pays, or transmission without a residual surfaced by the earlier room. That kills the isomer claim and leaves the functional-group claim.

**Status: `single-source` for the figure definition, with the test stated and not run.**

### F10 - Option A and Option B both fail, and Option C is the residue

Option A fails at D1 and D3: selecting people for likely agreement manufactures the correlation that disqualifies their agreement (Fig 2 :40), and a reader who has absorbed the whole framework before being asked for a verdict has joined the frame being checked (Fig 10 :42). Option B fails at D1 and D4: difference in closures is the union surplus and the only route to a new atom (Fig 8 :45, Fig 6 :23). Option C survives: keep disagreement, require a non-updating ground, make refusal cheap and provenanced, make revision a reach change, and select the next room against the residual the last one surfaced.

Fig 8 :45: "Two frames together reach what neither reaches alone." Fig 6 :53: "searchers drawing on the same evidence are one searcher wearing many names." Fig 2 :42: "Buy independence in proportion to the cost of being wrong."

## 3. Pre-registered refuter

Written before the conclusion and before the world search, per the framework's rule that a falsifier written before the run is a design instrument, not only an honesty device. The shape claim is refuted if any of the following holds:

1. **Shared-pool sufficiency.** A documented episode where sustained disagreement stayed open and produced a ground-settled, independently checkable result while both frames drew exclusively on one evidence pool, with no separate instrument.
2. **Moving anchor.** A documented episode where the purported anchor changed its answer in response to a frame, yet both frames were still independently corrected by it.
3. **Deletion-based revision.** A case where a held term disappeared from the record rather than being refused by addition, so the reach fell by deletion (breaks Fig 6 :42, Fig 5 :55).
4. **Reconstructible verifier.** A case where criticism corrected a term although the checker could reconstruct the claim from the framework's own interiors (breaks Fig 10 :42).
5. **Mirror-chain progress.** A chain of many agreeing, similar rooms that nevertheless produced ground-settled, independently checkable advance with no new instrument, import or residual-matched room (breaks Fig 7 :35, :36).
6. **Internal atom mint.** A new primitive minted entirely inside one closure with no import and no instrument extension and later settled by the ground (breaks Fig 6 :21, Fig 8 :32).
7. **Substrate breach.** One of the six substrate members derived from the other five, or a seventh admitted (breaks Fig 13 :115).

If any fires, the corresponding condition is withdrawn. Until one is run, no domain verdict is `grounded`.
