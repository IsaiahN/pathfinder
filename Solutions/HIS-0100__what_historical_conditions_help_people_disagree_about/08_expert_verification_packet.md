# Expert verification packet: five conditions for disagreement that stays correctable

**What this is:** a claim in a form a historian, philosopher of science or science-studies researcher
can check. Mode, derivation and refuters are stated before the conclusion.

**Mode: `general` on the shape, `specified` on the five condition verdicts, `gap` on which episodes
satisfied them. Not `grounded`.** This is a simulated derivation checked for internal consistency
against the figures, not a ground reading. It cannot settle the question it reports on.

## 1. The claim, in one paragraph

Sustained disagreement about knowledge remains open to evidence, criticism and revision only under a
union of five structural conditions: independent frames with per-slot residuals; an anchor that can
fail a frame and does not update; a vocabulary with the unary negation operator plus a checker that
cannot author the claim; revision as a change of reach rather than of library size; and transmission
in which methods cross and the next room differs from the last and matches the residual it surfaced.
Each condition has a different anchor, so verdicts are kept separate and never averaged. Agreement is
legitimate as a convention, not as a verdict about the world. Which historical episodes satisfied the
conditions is a declared `gap`.

## 2. Three checkable sub-claims

### C1 - The anchor condition (framework-internal, checkable by reading Figure 2)

- **Statement:** an external check that does not move when a frame pushes on it is necessary for
  disagreement to remain evidence-responsive. A check that moves becomes another frame.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 2 :29, "An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its
  answer because of what any frame thinks." Fig 2 :33, "The anchor must not update." Fig 2 :45,
  "Frames range against the ground. They do not negotiate with it." Fig 2 :35, "Alignment is
  triangulation, not negotiation. A mirror cannot correct you."
- **What to check:** is the anchor condition distinct from independence, and does the first collapse
  (mutual update) really follow when it fails?
- **Refuter:** a documented episode where the purported anchor changed its answer in response to a
  frame and both frames were nevertheless independently corrected by it. That breaks Fig 2 :29 and
  :33.
- **Status: `triangulated`** across Fig 2 (the alignment diagram), Fig 5 :70 (settlement) and
  Fig 1 :23 (ground as only metric). Low overlap between three objects.

### C2 - The refusal condition (framework-internal plus a public-method check)

- **Statement:** criticism is possible only where a held term can be refused by addition to the
  record, and where the checker cannot reconstruct the claim from the framework's own interior.
- **Source, verbatim:** Operators table :58, "a refutation is a negation, which is why a vocabulary
  without it can only accumulate". Fig 5 :53, "refuted: a term we hold is wrong; a term is owed that
  says so". Fig 5 :55, "What it removes is not a term but a term's standing to be composed over."
  Fig 10 :42, "A verifier that can reconstruct the claim cannot verify it."
- **What to check:** is negation genuinely unary, so that a vocabulary with only bonds cannot express
  refusal? Does a checker who has been shown the full framework still have independence left?
- **Refuter:** a case where criticism corrected a held term although the checker could reconstruct the
  claim from the interior, and no residual was recorded before the run. That breaks Fig 10 :42 and
  the Operators table :58.
- **Status: `triangulated`** across the Operators table, Fig 5 (the fifth kind) and Fig 10 (the
  verifier's office).

### C3 - The transmission condition (framework-internal plus a historical check)

- **Statement:** what crosses generations is a method or a head start, never a recording of one past
  success; a chain of rooms progresses only when the next room differs from the last and matches the
  residual the last one surfaced.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 4 :22, "Only methods cross up. Only head starts come down." Fig 4 :25,
  "A recording carried upward looks like knowledge and is a description of one occasion." Fig 7 :13,
  "same closure, handed along; agreement compounds, evidence does not". Fig 7 :36, "A chain of
  mirrors is one long room. The next room must differ from the last, and match the residual it
  surfaced." Fig 11 :39, "capability is a property of agent-and-habitat, never of the agent alone."
- **What to check:** do the Royal Society's motto and the Registered Reports protocol count as
  methods rather than recordings? Is a shared standard a convention (Fig 2 :41) or a verdict that
  decides outcomes (Fig 10 :47)?
- **Refuter:** a chain of many agreeing, similar rooms producing ground-settled, independently
  checkable advance with no new instrument, no import and no residual-matched room. That breaks
  Fig 7 :13 and :36. Or a capability that survives in isolation with no ground contact and no
  substituted habitat (breaks Fig 11 :39, :66).
- **Status: `triangulated`** across Fig 4 (membrane), Fig 7 (room chain) and Fig 11 (habitat).

## 3. What is explicitly not claimed

- Not that openness is a personality trait or that selecting receptive people creates evidence.
- Not that the disagreement should be resolved by consensus on causes and evidence.
- Not that more data or longer search closes an open disagreement (Fig 8 :32).
- Not that survival in the historical record is settlement by the ground.
- Not that the framework names the episodes that satisfied the conditions. That is a `gap`.
- Not `grounded`. No measurement in this packet settles any of the five conditions.

## 5. What would settle it

1. **Independence test:** separate instruments and records, or one shared pool?
2. **Anchor test:** does the check move when a frame pushes on it?
3. **Refusal test:** is there a provenanced refusal written before the outcome was known?
4. **Reach test:** did the reachable set change, or only the library?
5. **Room test:** did the next room differ and match the surfaced residual, or simply agree?
6. **Gap test:** a comparative design varying one condition at a time across episodes, with ground
   outcomes recorded separately from community agreement.

## 6. What an expert can check in twenty minutes

1. Read Fig 2 :29, :31, :33, :35, :45 and Fig 10 :42, six short passages.
2. Check C2 against the Operators table :58 and Fig 5 :53, :55, :56.
3. Name one historical episode where an anchor moved with opinion and disagreement nonetheless stayed
   correctable, or say why none exists. If one exists, C1 fails.
4. Name one chain of agreeing rooms that produced a ground-settled advance with no new instrument or
   residual-matched room. If one exists, C3 fails.
5. Send the refutation. A failure is more useful than an endorsement.
