# The Tether's answer: historical conditions for disagreement that stays correctable

**Mode: `general` on the structural shape, `specified` on the five per-domain verdicts, `gap` on which
conditions obtained in a named episode. Not `grounded`.** This is a simulated derivation checked for
internal consistency against the figures (`05_what_shape_means.md` section 7), not a ground reading.

## 1. The residual it answers

A community is to disagree about knowledge and remain open to evidence, criticism and revision, over
time. The tension is that the three desiderata pull against each other: strong agreement destroys the
disagreement, an anchor that bends to opinion destroys evidence, and a vocabulary that can only add
terms destroys criticism. "Historical" adds transmission, so the arrangement must survive with its
ground contact intact from one room to the next. The residual is a union of five overlapping
conditions (Fig 9 :55), not one hard question.

## 2. The answer: five conditions, five anchors, no averaging

**D1, independent frames.** Disagreement is productive only when the parties draw on different
evidence. Fig 2 :31: "Independence, between two frames, is how little they draw on the same evidence."
Fig 8 :49: "Two frames that overlap almost completely are one frame wearing two names." Residuals stay
per frame and per slot (Fig 1 :19). A shared pool is not two witnesses; it is one witness with two
names. What history can supply here is a plurality of differently situated inquirers with distinct
instruments and records, not a shared canon read by everyone.

**D2, a non-updating ground.** Openness to evidence is a structural relation, not a temperament. Fig 2
:29: "An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any
frame thinks." Fig 2 :33: "The anchor must not update." Fig 2 :45: "Frames range against the ground.
They do not negotiate with it." If the check moves when a frame pushes on it, the arrangement is the
coherent drift of Fig 2 panel 1, and nobody notices because the drift looks like agreement. A
community is open to evidence to the degree that its claims can fail an external check.

**D3, a vocabulary that can refuse.** Criticism requires a term that refuses another. The Operators
table :58: "a refutation is a negation, which is why a vocabulary without it can only accumulate."
Fig 5 :53: "refuted: a term we hold is wrong; a term is owed that says so." The checker must be unable
to author the claim: Fig 10 :42, "A verifier that can reconstruct the claim cannot verify it." Fig 9
:51 admits evidence about the residual but denies evidence about the maker. Stakes that cannot be
removed must be declared. Historically this is the work of pre-registration, open protocols and
public refuters, not of an attitude described as open mindedness.

**D4, revision as a reach change.** More commentary inside one closure cannot revise a claim:
Fig 8 :32, "no search, however long, adds an atom". Fig 5 :72: "a library that grows is not a reach
that grows." Reach changes in three ways: a provenanced import from a frame whose closure differs
(Fig 6 :23), an instrument sharpened from a worse one already returning something (Fig 6 :33), or a
refusal that lowers the reach by addition (Fig 6 :42). Fig 6 :43 keeps the two quantities separate:
"So the closure and the reach are two quantities: one monotone, one not." Revision can look like a
loss of claims, which is why a community that only counts publications will not see it.

**D5, transmission by method and residual-matched rooms.** What crosses generations is a method or a
head start, never a recording. Fig 4 :22: "Only methods cross up. Only head starts come down." Fig 4
:25: "A recording carried upward looks like knowledge and is a description of one occasion." Fig 7
:13: "same closure, handed along; agreement compounds, evidence does not." Fig 7 :36: "A chain of
mirrors is one long room. The next room must differ from the last, and match the residual it surfaced."
Capability is agent-and-habitat (Fig 11 :39), introduction is available and exclusion is not
(Fig 11 :61), and isolation substitutes a habitat rather than removing it (Fig 11 :66). Historical
conditions are therefore habitat conditions, kept in contact with a ground that does not decay
(Fig 10 :24).

**The union rule.** These five are not additive. Fig 9 :57: "What remains is smaller and it is also
categorically different: a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either." Verdicts stay per
domain. Fig 9 :60: "Use filters for the budget and witnesses for the verdict. Never let a filter hand
you a verdict." No openness score is a witness.

**Agreement has valence.** Fig 2 :40 disqualifies agreement about the world when frames share a pool;
Fig 2 :41 makes agreement about shared meaning the product. Fig 10 :47: "a convention that decides an
outcome has stopped being one". A community can share a convention for what counts as a fair test,
but that convention must not choose which claim wins.

## 3. What it explicitly does not say

- Not that openness is a personality trait. Person-level openness is a contact-cost estimate, never a
  verdict criterion (X1 in the census). Fig 11 :39 makes capability agent-and-habitat.
- Not that disagreement should end in consensus. Fig 9 :4 splits non-shrinking disagreement into
  several well-formed questions; the task is to keep it correctable, not to silence it.
- Not that more data or more commentary closes an open question (Fig 8 :32).
- Not that a long-surviving view is therefore correct. Unmaintained survival is pure contact
  (Fig 11 :56), and a substituted habitat reproduces what it can (Fig 11 :66).
- Not which historical episodes satisfied the conditions, and not how much each contributed. That is
  a declared `gap`.
- Not `grounded`. No measurement in this run pays for any of the five conditions as historical facts.

## 4. What would settle it

A source-discriminating instrument, not a further argument. From *The Hard Problem is About Source*,
opened this session: an instrument that reads green *"cannot comment on the source"*, and the
spectrometer, not an argument, resolved which source produced the reading. Here the corresponding
instruments are:

1. **Independence test.** Do the disagreeing parties use separate instruments and records, or one
   shared pool? A correlation of citations is not independence.
2. **Anchor test.** Does the purported check change its answer when a frame pushes on it? If yes, it
   is a frame, not an anchor.
3. **Refusal test.** Is there a record of a held term being refused by addition, with provenance, and
   was the refusal written before the outcome was known?
4. **Reach test.** Did the reachable set change (import, instrument or refusal), or did only the
   library grow?
5. **Room test.** Did the next room differ from the last and match the residual the last surfaced,
   or did it simply agree?
6. **Gap test.** A comparative design varying one condition at a time across episodes, with the
   ground outcome recorded separately from community agreement, would close the case-specific `gap`.

## 5. The honest stop

**Unreached, not unreachable.** Fig 9 :32: "you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself". Fig 13
:134: "Unreachable is a verdict here, not a silence". Fig 6 :27 says that depth and genuine absence
look the same from inside the frame. The framework can say what an open disagreement must look like;
it cannot say which missing thing would have made a particular episode productive, because naming that
would mean already holding it (Fig 13 :135). The lineage is recorded so the stop is checkable.

## 6. One-paragraph version for outreach

> The answer is five conditions, not one virtue. Disagreement stays productive only when the parties
> draw on different evidence (Fig 2 :31); when the check they appeal to cannot be moved by what they
> believe (Fig 2 :29, :33); when their vocabulary can refuse a held term and the checker cannot
> reconstruct the claim (Operators :58, Fig 10 :42); when revision changes what is reachable rather
> than merely enlarging the library (Fig 5 :72, Fig 6 :42); and when what crosses to the next
> generation is a method and a residual-matched room, not an echo of the last one (Fig 4 :22,
> Fig 7 :36). These are a union of overlapping conditions, so they are reported separately and never
> averaged (Fig 9 :57, :60). Agreement is legitimate only as a convention, never as a verdict about
> the world (Fig 2 :40, :41; Fig 10 :47). Which historical episodes actually satisfied the conditions
> is an honest `gap`; the instrument owed is comparative, and this derivation is a simulation, not a
> measurement.

## 7. Seven refuters, written before the run

Carried verbatim in purpose from `01_figure_grounded_audit.md` section 3:

1. one shared evidence pool sufficient for ground-settled, independently checkable open disagreement;
2. a moving anchor that still corrected both frames;
3. deletion-based revision;
4. effective criticism by a checker who could reconstruct the claim;
5. a chain of agreeing, similar rooms producing ground-settled advance with no new instrument, import
   or residual-matched room;
6. a primitive minted inside one closure with no import, later settled by the ground;
7. one of the six substrate members derived from the other five.

None fired in this simulated run. None was tested at the ground.
