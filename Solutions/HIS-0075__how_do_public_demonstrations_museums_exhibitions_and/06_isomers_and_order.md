# Union, bonds and order in the transmission of science

**Mode: `specified`.** This is a simulated derivation checked against the figures, not a ground reading (see `05` §7).

## RESIDUAL

The question bundles four transmission conditions and at least three belief-slot kinds. Two questions are owed: (1) is this a partition or a union, and what does each yield; (2) what bond types relate a transmission to a belief, and in what order do the four channels act?

## DOMAIN CENSUS

Actors: the four channels (demonstration, museum, exhibition, popular writing), audiences, scientific ground, researchers. Currency: recordings versus methods, agreement, residuals. Anchor: measurement for factual slots, human judgment for trust and identity slots (Fig 2 :36-38). The four channels are not disjoint; they overlap, so the object is a union compound (Fig 9 :54-58).

## DERIVATION

### Q1 - Partition or union?

- Fig 9 :54: **"And when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again."**
- Fig 9 :55-56: **"A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones. A single lever clears the region where only one condition holds and leaves the intersections standing, because the second condition still binds."**
- A museum exhibition that hosts a live demonstration is in two conditions at once. Popular writing that reports an exhibition is in two. A lever that only improves "the museum" leaves the intersection standing.
- **Conclusion:** union, not partition. Each condition gets its own loop and its own ground; the intersections are specific compounds, not harder cases of either (Fig 9 :57).
- **Falsifier, written first:** if the four channels can be defined so that no audience event falls in more than one, the union claim fails and a partition search is licensed. The exemplar description of a museum hosting exhibitions and demonstrations makes that definition unavailable without artificial stipulation.

### Q2 - What bond does each channel use?

The Operators table defines the relevant operators. Fig 12 :71: **"sequence: A then B; B needs A to have happened."** Fig 12 :77: **"production: A produces B; B did not exist before."** Fig 12 :87: **"comparison: a threshold relation between two quantities."** Operators table :57: **"a refutation is a negation, which is why a vocabulary without it can only accumulate."**

| channel-to-belief relation | bond | figure basis | what it means here |
|---|---|---|---|
| a display creates a competence the visitor did not have | production ⇒ | Fig 12 :77 | a new term was minted in the audience's library |
| a visit precedes a later conversation or booking | sequence → | Fig 12 :71 | order matters; swapping changes the thing |
| a visitor weighs a dramatised account against the real one | comparison ⋛ | Fig 12 :87 | a threshold relation, not a join |
| a held belief is given up after correction | negation ¬ | Operators :57 | a refusal, not an addition |

- **Strain, named:** ⋛ is defined as a threshold relation between two quantities. At the belief level the operands are a dramatised account and a real account, which are readings, not obvious quantities. Whether they count as quantities at that level needs an instrument, not an argument. This is the same kind of strain the exemplar recorded for second-order ⋛, and it is kept visible.
- **Falsifier:** if all four channel effects can be modelled as additive contributions to one belief score, the bond typology fails and a flat formula suffices. That would also collapse the per-slot claim, so it is a real test.

### Q3 - Are the four channels isomers?

- Fig 12 :117: **"isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."**
- Fig 12 :124: **"functional group. A substructure whose presence fixes the outcome. The group says what kind; the qualifier says which member."**
- The four channels do not share an atom set in any strict sense: a live demonstration involves a phenomenon and a demonstrator, popular writing involves a text and a publisher, a museum involves a collection and a curator. As actually constituted they are **not isomers**. They are four members of a functional group: each is a transmission arrangement that sends a representation down to an audience (Fig 12 :124).
- The stronger form would be: hold the atom set fixed (a claim, a representation, an audience, a ground) and vary only the bond and order. Then the four are isomers in the figure's sense, and the difference in belief outcome would be carried entirely by the arrangement. That is the claim worth testing, because it is checkable.
- **Falsifier:** if any channel requires an atom the others do not (for example, a phenomenon only a demonstration can supply), the isomer claim fails and the functional-group reading stands.

### Q4 - What order do the four channels have?

Order is dependency, not importance (Fig 3 :20). The dependency chain for a single slot is:

```
perceive the transmission
  -> have terms for what it affords        (middle link, where chains break)
    -> say what would count as progress
      -> mechanism / route
        -> name, verify, carry to next slot
```

Across scales,Fig 4 :22 gives the membrane rule: **"Only methods cross up. Only head starts come down."** Across rooms, Fig 7 :36 gives the selection rule: **"A chain of mirrors is one long room. The next room must differ from the last, and match the residual it surfaced."**

So the four channels are not one linear funnel from demonstration to book. They are overlapping rooms, and the only licensed ordering is: which room is selected against the residual the previous one surfaced. A chain that selects the next room because it resembles the last is a mirror chain, and its agreement compounds without evidence (Fig 7 :12-13).

**Falsifier:** if a fixed sequence of the four channels produces the same per-slot residuals regardless of which residual the previous room surfaced, the selection-order claim fails.

## CONCLUSION

The object is a union compound with its own loop, not four independent effects. Its channels are functional-group variants as actually constituted, and isomers only under the stricter held-atom-set reading, which has a stated falsifier. The bond between a transmission and a belief is not uniformly additive: production, sequence, comparison and negation are distinct, and a vocabulary that cannot represent negation can only accumulate beliefs, never correct them. Order follows dependency (Fig 3) and room selection (Fig 7), not a fixed funnel. One strain is live and named: ⋛'s operand type at the belief level.
