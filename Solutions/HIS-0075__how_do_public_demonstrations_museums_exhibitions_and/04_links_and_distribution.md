## Four things in the wider literature that bear directly on this question

1. **The deficit model is the historical shape this question inherits.** The Public awareness of science article says early work *"adhering to the principles of the information deficit model"* has been *"largely disregarded."* The Information deficit model article gives the mechanism: more information does not necessarily change views. This corroborates the figure claim that a recording is not a method (Fig 4 :22-26), but it does not replace the figure derivation. The world source is secondary.
2. **Perceived knowledge is not factual knowledge.** The Public awareness of science article reports that self-reported and factual measures *"do not reflect the same underlying knowledge structures"* and had low correlations. This is the metamerism case in the field's own instruments: a survey reads green and cannot comment on the source. It corroborates F4.
3. **The museum study shows contact, not just content.** Duckett et al. report that visitors valued *"hands-on"* and *"blended art-science activities,"* that venue choice mattered for reaching new audiences, and that the shift was toward *"two-way dialogue between the 'experts' and 'non-experts'."* This corroborates F7, F8 and F9. It is one study in one city, so it is a witness with a narrow field, not a general verdict.
4. **Reach is a budget quantity.** The Royal Society's description of the Bodmer report's legacy is about expansion, popularisation and links between science and society. That is reach. Fig 9 :60 - *"Use filters for the budget and witnesses for the verdict"* - says not to promote it into evidence of belief change.

No world source conflicts with the figure verdict, so the conflict rule was not triggered. The essays share an author with the figures, so their agreement is expected and carries little independent weight. The PUS and museum sources are from a different frame and have low overlap with the framework, so they are the more useful corroboration.

## Distribution: offer, do not push

Figure-grounded reasons:

- **Fig 10 :52** - *"Bring a characterised residual, never the source."* Sending the full architecture before anyone has described a residual sends a source, not a check.
- **Fig 10 :42** - *"A verifier that can reconstruct the claim cannot verify it."* A recipient who has absorbed the framework before being asked for a verdict has joined the frame being checked.
- **Fig 9 :27-28** - *"describe it before you go looking, or any frame you pick will seem to fit."* Ask what they would expect to see in each slot before giving them the article.
- **Fig 7 :35** - *"A room's agreement is variation, not a verdict. Selection between rooms is where the ground votes."* Do not select recipients for likelihood of agreement.
- **Fig 9 :60** - *"Use filters for the budget and witnesses for the verdict."* Opening a venue is a budget filter; it is never a witness.

**Recommended order in a reply:** (1) the per-slot shape claim and its four refuters; (2) ask what they would count as a failure for each slot; (3) record their verdict; (4) only then the three links, with the note that the figures, not the summary, are the authority.

## The two-stage offer

- **Stage 1 before stage 2:** the recipient checks the shape claim without holding the framework's interior (Fig 10 :52, Fig 2 :45).
- **Stage 2 is opt-in by construction:** once a recipient has absorbed the theory, their agreement stops being evidence. Their stage-1 verdict must be recorded before any theory is shown, and later agreement is never pooled as corroboration (Fig 2 :40).
- **Stage 3 sends a method:** the formula is a generator, so giving it away does not give away an answer (Fig 4 :22). Each user still needs a ground of their own and must write their own refuter before running it.

**Hard precondition:** stages 1 to 3 only work if the stage-1 output survives. As drafted it is a shape with an effect-size gap, stated openly. That is survivable precisely because the gap is declared rather than filled with a number.
