# Expert verification packet - the Tether on public transmission and beliefs about science

**What this is:** a claim in a form a science-communication researcher or museum evaluator can check. Mode, derivation and refuters are stated before the conclusion, so the reader knows what to look for and what would settle it.

**Mode: `general` shape, `specified` field predictions, not `grounded`.** No measurement of an audience is claimed. This is a shape and a route, not a result. Simulated derivation checked for internal consistency against the figures, not a ground reading.

## 1. The claim, in one paragraph

"Public understanding of science" is one phrase for at least three kinds of belief slot (factual knowledge, structural knowledge of how science works, and trust or identity), acted on by four overlapping transmission conditions (demonstrations, museums, exhibitions, popular writing). Each slot must be ranged against its own ground: measurement for facts, human judgment for trust and identity, because that question is constitutively about people. Agreement about the world from a shared evidence pool carries no verdict weight, while agreement about shared meaning is the product. Most public transmission sends down a recording of a result rather than the method that produced it, so beliefs tilt toward science as facts. Channels decay while the ground does not, chains of retelling compound agreement without evidence, and contact and habitat, not polish, change what an audience can do. Effect sizes are a declared gap.

## 2. Three checkable sub-claims

### C1 - framework-internal, checkable against Fig 2 and Fig 1

- **Statement:** per-slot residuals with slot-specific grounds, not a single aggregate; and the two valences of agreement must be kept separate.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 2 :37 - *"Human judgment is the right anchor for is this a good answer for a person, because the question is about people."* Fig 1 :19 - *"The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated."* Fig 2 :40 - *"disqualifying for a verdict: agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool"*; Fig 2 :41 - *"efficient for a convention: agreement about a shared meaning is the product, not a corruption of it"*.
- **Refuter:** one aggregate public-understanding score predicting intervention outcomes as well as per-slot residuals; or two low-overlap channels converging on a factual belief by non-conventional agreement.

### C2 - field-facing, checkable against the research literature and an evaluator's own data

- **Statement:** what public channels send down is usually a recording, not a transferable method; the audience's prior conditions decide what grows; and measured "perceived knowledge" cannot comment on its source.
- **Source, verbatim (figure):** Fig 4 :22 - *"Only methods cross up. Only head starts come down."* Fig 4 :25 - *"A recording carried upward looks like knowledge and is a description of one occasion."* Fig 4 :34-35 - *"A seed carries no small tree: it carries what will grow one, and the conditions it lands in decide what it becomes."*
- **What to check:** in a channel you know, can recipients generate a novel prediction afterwards, or only repeat a reported result? Do self-reported knowledge and factual knowledge behave as distinct constructs in your data? The PUS article reports that they *"do not reflect the same underlying knowledge structures,"* and Duckett et al. report perceived knowledge increases at a museum event. Are you measuring the first, the second, or a projection of both?
- **Refuter:** a public transmission that reliably transfers a reusable method and whose recipients generate novel predictions with it.

### C3 - framework-internal derivation with a field-facing consequence

- **Statement:** the channel decays while the ground does not, so confidence can persist without contact; the route is INWARD to better instruments, not OUTWARD to a new theory.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 10 :24 - *"the ground does not decay, the channel does"*; Fig 10 :25-26 - *"stale percepts, dropped transients, saturated metrics, pooled readings"*; Fig 6 :32 - *"An instrument is not built from a description. It is improved from a worse instrument already returning something."*
- **Refuter:** a channel whose agreement with the scientific ground is measured at each hop and does not decay over time or re-telling.

## 3. What is explicitly not claimed

- Not a measured effect size, not a ranking of the four channels, not a claim that one channel is best.
- Not that reach, attendance, coverage or impressions are evidence of belief change. They are budget filters (Fig 9 :60: *"Use filters for the budget and witnesses for the verdict. Never let a filter hand you a verdict."*).
- Not that the deficit model is the whole story. The field has largely moved past it, and the figure reason is that more information is a recording, not a method.
- Not that shared public vocabulary is a failure. For shared meaning, agreement is the product.
- Not a verdict about science communication. Nothing here is `grounded`.
- Not that the PUS literature is part of the framework. It is a low-overlap world frame used as corroboration only.

## 5. What would settle it

An instrument, not a further argument. From *The Hard Problem is About Source*: an instrument reading green *"cannot comment on the source,"* and the case was settled *"not by argument, but by the arrival of a better instrument."* Owed here:

1. A slot discriminator separating factual, structural, perceived and trust readings in the same respondents.
2. A method-transfer test: novel prediction after exposure, not repetition of a result.
3. A hop test: per-slot accuracy and freshness at each re-telling.
4. An affordance instrument: can the audience say what a display affords before being told?
5. A cascade measure: follow visitors into later conversation and media choice.

## 6. What an expert can check in twenty minutes

1. Read Fig 2 :37 and :40-41, Fig 1 :19, Fig 4 :22-25, Fig 10 :24-26. Six short passages.
2. Check C2 against your own data or a study you know: is perceived knowledge being treated as factual knowledge? If so, the metamerism point stands.
3. Name one instrument that would discriminate the slots, or say why none is possible. That is the ground question.
4. Send the refutation. A failure is more useful than an endorsement.
