## 1. A shape is a constraint you can state before you can name the value

The figures README states the limit: the framework returns *"what a missing thing would have to look like (a shape, not a name)."* Fig 9 :32: **"you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself."**

From *The Library of Babel*: *"you can cut your search short if the set of books returned is small and descriptive enough that it provides a general shape of the answer that satisfies your question."*

For HIS-0075 a shape is therefore not an effect size and not a ranking of channels. It is the constrained answer form:

> Per person and per belief slot, name the ground that slot must range against; separate agreement about the world from agreement about shared meaning; say whether the channel sends a recording or a method; keep the habitat and its cascades in view; and leave the effect size unfilled until an instrument measures it.

That is a shape. "Museums raise trust by 12 percent" is a name where the framework has no instrument.

## 2. How the shape is obtained

- *Library of Babel*: use the vocabulary already closest to the thing, then *"With each new term gained, you narrow the space and deepen your vocabulary."* For this question the bootstrap vocabulary is the field's own: deficit model, perceived knowledge, science capital, two-way dialogue, trust. Those are the nearby terms.
- *Library of Babel* on bundles: *"Perhaps your problem was a bundle or conflation of several problems masquerading as one? You will need to search all of those problems individually to find their answers and then combine them in a satisfactory way."* This is the essay-level twin of Fig 9 :54 and is the reason `01` treats the question as a union.
- Fig 6 :30: **"The description travels. Fails by being sharpened into terms only we can read. Cast it in effect terms."** So the shape is described as residuals, anchors and contact, not as "epistemic asymmetry" or any other term borrowed from a frame the respondent may not share.
- *Decoding Tacit Knowledge*: *"When contact isn't possible, the best you can send is a plain description of what you'd expect to see, because anyone can check that against the world."* This is why the outreach email asks what a failure would look like before offering sources.
- *Library of Babel* on stopping: you know you are done when *"the finish line will also be the starting place."* Here that means the shape stops when the four refuters are stated and the effect-size gap is declared, not when a satisfying story about science communication has been assembled.

## 3. What licenses a name

Only two things, both outside the shape itself:

1. **The ground settles it.** Formula step 5: *"the ground settles it. Verified only by what pays."* In this field that means a per-slot measurement under a declared protocol, not a general impression.
2. **A provenanced import from a frame whose closure differs** (Fig 6 :23, Fig 8 :50-52), with a source gate and a shadow test. The PUS literature is such an import when it is used as corroboration; it is not a substitute for the figure derivation.

*They're Scanning Books When They Should* is not quoted here (not re-read), but the exemplar records its rule that *"The real import is not a new term in the existing space. It is a new dimension of the space."* The honest reading for this question is that PUS research supplies instruments and field data, not new primitives for the framework.

## 4. Why the field's standard measure is a metamerism error

*The Hard Problem is About Source* gives the mechanism: an instrument reading green *"cannot comment on the source."* Several sources can produce the same reading. Metamerism: *"If several paths create the same behavior, then behavior is a projection with lossy information."* And the fix is not another argument: it was *"proven wrong not by argument, but by the arrival of a better instrument."*

Applied here: the public understanding survey reads "I am well informed about science." That reading can come from factual knowledge, from structural knowledge, from familiarity, from identity, or from a desire to give the expected answer. The Duckett et al. study reports that perceived and actual understanding are not interchangeable, and the PUS article records that self-reported and factual measures *"do not reflect the same underlying knowledge structures."* Naming the source (science capital, deficit, trust) from the reading alone is exactly the error the essay names. What is owed is a discriminating instrument, not a nicer label.

## 5. The two-valence rule belongs here too

*Decoding Tacit Knowledge* states it in the field's own language: *"Shared pretext is good for one thing and ruins another. Agreement is the whole point when you're settling what words mean. It's worthless for settling what's true when both sides learned from the same sources."* This is Fig 2 :40 and :41 restated. It is the single most useful bridge between the framework and this question, and it is why `01` F2 refuses to average the two products.

The same essay adds the drift mechanism: *"Two people who only check against each other drift, and they drift together, so neither notices."* A public that gets its science only from popular accounts of other popular accounts is a drifting pair at scale. Fig 7 :36 - **"A chain of mirrors is one long room."**

## 6. Prestige is not action

From *The Recursive Self-Improvement Trap*: *"Prestige can fund an action. It can give an action a budget to be tried. But it cannot settle an action. Only the ground can settle it. The Ground Maintainer must be blind to prestige."*

A famous science communicator, a well-endowed museum or a bestselling popular book can give an outreach claim a budget to be tried. None of them can settle whether a belief changed. The outreach in `02` therefore asks for a check, not an endorsement, and `03` contains no ranking by likely agreement.

## 7. My own audit is a simulation, not a ground reading

Same essay: *"the output is only as good as the validator. The agent, or the tool, or the frame, or the theory, cannot validate itself."* And: *"LLMs can quickly simulate an approximate answer/output... However, the truly testable output would need to be derived from the real architecture or working the formula yourself."*

So `01`, `06`, `07` and `09` are simulated derivations checked for internal consistency against the figures. They are **not** tests of any claim about science communication and must be labelled as such. They cannot settle anything they report on. That is a limit of this run, not a defect in the question.

## 8. Correction to my own §1 (kept visible)

I first drafted §1 as "the framework never names a value before measurement." That is too broad. The README's phrase governs a **missing** thing: *"what a **missing** thing would have to look like (a shape, not a name)."* Naming a slot's anchor (human judgment for a trust belief) is not inventing a missing primitive; it is reading the rule that the question is constitutively about people (Fig 2 :36-38). What remains forbidden is supplying the effect size, which would be naming a value the instruments have not returned. The corrected claim is narrower: **the framework returns the form of the answer and the ground for each slot, not the measured magnitude.** Both the overstatement and its correction are recorded so neither is quietly lost.
