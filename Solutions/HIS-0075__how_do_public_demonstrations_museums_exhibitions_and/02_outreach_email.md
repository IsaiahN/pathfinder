# Outreach draft - check the shape first, then decide about the framework

**Use:** one to one, or as a cover letter attached to a submission through the public venues in `03_public_contact_points.csv`. **Not** for bulk send.

**Hard precondition:** do not send until a respondent can check the shape claim without holding any of the framework's interior. If they have to read the figures to answer, the offer is misordered.

---

**Subject:** One checkable claim about how public science communication changes beliefs

> Hi [name],
>
> I have one specific output I would like you to check against what you already know from your own work. I am not asking you to read the framework first, and I am not asking you to agree with anything. If the output does not survive, there is nothing else to look at.
>
> **The output (`general` shape, `specified` field predictions, not `grounded`):** "public understanding of science" is not one thing. It is at least three kinds of belief slot (factual knowledge, structural knowledge of how science works, and trust or identity), acted on by four overlapping transmission conditions (demonstrations, museums, exhibitions, popular writing). Two consequences follow before any measurement is taken.
>
> 1. The slots have different anchors. A factual belief ranges against measurement. A trust or identity belief ranges against human judgment, because that question is constitutively about people. A single score blends two products: agreement about the world, where agreement from a shared evidence pool is worthless as a verdict, and agreement about shared meaning, where agreement is the product.
> 2. What these channels most often send down is a recording of a scientific result (a fact, a spectacle, an image), not the method that produced it. That biases beliefs toward science as a body of facts rather than science as a way of finding out. And the audience's prior vocabulary decides what the transmitted seed becomes.
>
> **What I am not claiming:** that any of the four channels has a measured effect size; that I can rank them; that museums are better than popular writing; that coverage, attendance or "reach" are evidence of understanding. Those numbers are budget quantities. The figures return a shape, not a name, and I am not filling in the slot with a number I did not measure.
>
> **What would refute the shape claim:**
> 1. one aggregate "public understanding of science" score predicts intervention outcomes as well as per-slot residuals;
> 2. two transmission channels with demonstrated low overlap converge on the same factual belief change, and the convergence is not a convention about wording;
> 3. a public transmission reliably transfers a reusable method and its recipients generate novel predictions with it;
> 4. a channel's agreement with the scientific ground is shown not to decay over time and across re-tellings, with freshness measured at each hop.
>
> I wrote these down before making the offer. If any one holds, the shape is wrong and I will retract it. A one-line "this fails because..." is more useful to me than agreement. If you would rather not engage, no reply is needed.
>
> **If it survives, and only then:** the sources are below. The figures are the authority, not my summary of them.
> - The figures with line numbers (local extract): `FRAMEWORK/reference/03-FIGURES_TEXT.md`
> - The canonical usage rule: `FRAMEWORK/reference/FIGURES_README_canonical.md`
> - The eight essays, especially "Decoding Tacit Knowledge: Why Context, Subtext, and Pretext Matter" and "The Hard Problem is About Source": https://adventuresinml.substack.com/p/decoding-tacit-knowledge-why-context
>
> **If you want to use the machinery:** the formula is in the figures link. Run it on a problem you know well and can check against a ground. Write down what would refute your result *before* you run it, and label the mode of what you get. A reported failure is more useful to me than a reported success.
>
> Thanks,
> [name]

## Why this version is ordered this way

| the move | figure-grounded reason |
|---|---|
| Output first, theory second | Fig 10 :52 - *"Bring a characterised residual, never the source."* Fig 2 :45 - *"Frames range against the ground. They do not negotiate with it."* |
| Do not ask for agreement | Fig 2 :35 - *"Alignment is triangulation, not negotiation. A mirror cannot correct you."* |
| Pre-registered refuter | Standing rule: *"a falsifier written before the run is a design instrument."* |
| Mode stated on every claim | `THE_FORMULA.md`: *"Mode. general · specified · grounded. State which."* |
| Effect sizes withheld | Fig 9 :32 - *"you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself."* |
| Reach numbers not treated as evidence | Fig 9 :60 - *"Use filters for the budget and witnesses for the verdict. Never let a filter hand you a verdict."* |
| The two agreement valences separated | Fig 2 :40 and :41; the Decoding Tacit Knowledge essay: *"Shared pretext is good for one thing and ruins another."* |
| Recording versus method named | Fig 4 :22 - *"Only methods cross up. Only head starts come down."* |
| No request for prestige or endorsement | *The Recursive Self-Improvement Trap*: *"Prestige can fund an action. It can give an action a budget to be tried. But it cannot settle an action. Only the ground can settle it."* |
| Offer the formula as a method to reuse | Fig 4 :22 again: a generator can be given away without giving away an answer. Each user still needs their own ground and their own refuter. |

**What is deliberately absent:** no personal email, no purchased list, no receptiveness ranking, no request to trust the framework, and no claim that agreement with the shape would count as corroboration.
