# The Tether's answer on public transmission and beliefs about science

**Mode: `general` for the shape, `specified` for the field-level predictions, not `grounded`.** It is a shape and a route, not a result. Simulated derivation checked against the figures (`05` §7), not a ground reading.

## 1. The residual it answers

"Public understanding of science" is one phrase for several things: a factual belief, a structural belief about how science works, and a trust or identity belief. Demonstrations, museums, exhibitions and popular writing act on these through overlapping conditions, not as four independent causes. The residual is per person and per slot: the gap between the belief held and the belief that would be held under unimpeded contact with the relevant ground. Fig 1 :22: **"Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average."**

## 2. The shape

1. **The slots have different grounds, and the question is about people.** A factual belief ranges against measurement. A trust or identity belief ranges against human judgment, because the question is constitutively about people (Fig 2 :36-38). Do not settle the second with the first. The Science communication source records that knowledge levels show *"little, if any, correlation"* with attitudes toward science, which is the world-level shadow of this split.

2. **Two products must be kept apart.** Fig 2 :40: **"disqualifying for a verdict: agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool."** Fig 2 :41: **"efficient for a convention: agreement about a shared meaning is the product, not a corruption of it."** A public that has read the same popular accounts agrees about the world for reasons that carry no verdict weight; a public that shares a working picture of what "science" means has produced the convention it was asked to produce. Blending them makes the measurement uninterpretable.

3. **What is sent down is usually a recording, not a method.** Fig 4 :22: **"Only methods cross up. Only head starts come down."** Fig 4 :25: **"A recording carried upward looks like knowledge and is a description of one occasion."** So the easiest belief to move is "science is a set of facts," not "science is a way of finding out." Fig 4 :34-35: **"A seed carries no small tree: it carries what will grow one, and the conditions it lands in decide what it becomes."** The audience's prior vocabulary and history decide what grows.

4. **The channel decays; the ground does not.** Fig 10 :24: **"the ground does not decay, the channel does"**, with Fig 10 :25-26 listing **"stale percepts, dropped transients, saturated metrics, pooled readings."** Beliefs can therefore be confidently wrong in a way that looks exactly like being right. The PUS literature's distinction between self-reported and factual knowledge is the measured version of this.

5. **Rooms and chains compound agreement, not evidence.** Fig 7 :12-13: **"same closure, handed along; agreement compounds, evidence does not"**; Fig 7 :36: **"A chain of mirrors is one long room. The next room must differ from the last, and match the residual it surfaced."** Popular writing that cites popular writing is such a chain. Reach is a budget quantity, never a verdict (Fig 9 :60).

6. **Contact and habitat beat polish.** Fig 11 :40: **"An improvement that does not change contact changes nothing, however much it improves."** A better exhibit design that does not change what the audience is in contact with changes nothing about understanding. Effects cascade beyond the visitor: Fig 11 :52-53 names the DDT case where *"the residual that mattered was three steps out."* The Duckett study's reports of increased discussion and more querying after a museum event are such second-order effects.

7. **The middle link is where to look.** Fig 3 :24: **"Why the middle link is where chains usually break."** The first instrument owed is not a better attitude survey. It is an instrument for whether the audience has terms for what the display affords (Fig 3 :27: **"A link with no instrument is not a link that works. It is a link nobody has looked at."**).

8. **The effect sizes are a gap.** Fig 9 :32: **"you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself."** The framework cannot say which of the four channels is strongest or by how much. Anyone who supplies such a number here is naming what the instruments have not returned.

## 3. What it explicitly does not say

- Not that any channel has a measured effect size, and not that the four can be ranked.
- Not that "reach," attendance, coverage or media impressions are evidence of belief change. They are budget filters (Fig 9 :60).
- Not that the deficit model is the whole story. The field's own literature has moved past it, and the figures corroborate the reason: more information is a recording, not a method (Fig 4).
- Not that a shared public vocabulary is a failure. For shared meaning, agreement is the product (Fig 2 :41).
- Not that this is a verdict about science communication. It is a `general` and `specified` derivation, simulated and internally consistent only.
- Not a claim that PUS research is part of the framework. It is a low-overlap world frame used as corroboration.

## 4. What would settle it - the ground

An instrument, not a further argument. From *The Hard Problem is About Source*: an instrument reading green *"cannot comment on the source,"* and the case was resolved *"not by argument, but by the arrival of a better instrument."* Here the owed instruments are:

- **Slot discriminator:** a measure that separates factual knowledge, structural knowledge, perceived familiarity and trust in the same respondents, so a reading can be attributed to a source instead of named from a projection.
- **Method-transfer test:** does a channel leave recipients able to generate a novel prediction, or only to repeat a reported result? This distinguishes ⇒ from a recording (F3).
- **Hop test:** measure per-slot accuracy and freshness at each re-telling, from research paper to exhibit label to popular article. This tests decay (F4) and mirror chains (F5).
- **Affordance instrument:** can the audience say what the exhibit affords before being told? This reads the middle link (F7).
- **Cascade measure:** follow visitors to later conversation and media choice, not just the exit questionnaire (F9).

Standing falsifiers from `01` §5: an aggregate that predicts as well as per-slot residuals; low-overlap channels converging on a factual belief by convention-free agreement; reliable method transfer; a non-decaying channel. Any of these fires, and the shape is retracted.

## 5. The honest stop

**Unreached, not unreachable.** Fig 8 :39: **"So a search that finds nothing is never a proof of absence."** The instruments in this field already return something, so the route is INWARD (sharpen them), not OUTWARD (import another grand theory of communication). Fig 6 :32: **"An instrument is not built from a description. It is improved from a worse instrument already returning something."** The record stops here with a declared gap and four written refuters. *"Indiscernibility is not identity."*

## 6. One-paragraph version for outreach

> The Tether does not rank science museums against popular books, and it does not produce an effect size. It says that "public understanding of science" is a union of at least three kinds of belief slot acted on by four overlapping channels, and that each slot must be ranged against its own ground: measurement for facts, human judgment for trust and identity. It says that most public transmission sends down a recording of a result rather than the method that produced it, so beliefs tilt toward science as facts; that channels decay while the ground does not; that chains of popular retelling compound agreement without adding evidence; and that contact and habitat, not polish, change what an audience can do. It keeps agreement about shared meaning, which is the product, separate from agreement about the world, which needs independent frames. What it offers is that shape, four refuters written in advance, and the list of instruments that would close the gap. The effect sizes stay open until the ground settles them.
