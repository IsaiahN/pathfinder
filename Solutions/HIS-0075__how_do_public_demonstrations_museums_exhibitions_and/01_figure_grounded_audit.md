## 0. The residual, in effect terms

R is the gap, per person and per belief slot, between the belief held about science and the belief that would be held under unimpeded contact with the relevant ground. Three things about that sentence do work:

- **Per person and per slot, not one number.** Fig 1 :19: **"The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated."** Fig 1 :22: **"Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average."** "Public understanding of science" as a single percentage is exactly the aggregate that hides where the live signal is.
- **The relevant ground differs by slot.** A factual belief (does the Earth orbit the Sun?) ranges against measurement. A structural belief (what counts as evidence in science?) ranges against the practice of science. A trust belief (are scientists trustworthy?) ranges against human judgment, because the question is constitutively about people. Fig 2 :36: **"An anchor is legitimate when the question is constitutively about it."**
- **The four channels are conditions, not one cause.** Demonstration, museum, exhibition, and popular writing overlap (a museum hosts exhibitions and demonstrations; popular writing reports on both). Fig 9 :4-5: **"Disagreements that do not shrink with effort, where each rule keeps working well on a different subset, are not one hard question but several well-formed ones. Split it rather than search."**

The effect to be decided: which of these conditions moves which slot, in which direction, and under what contact.

## 1. Domain census (done before any figure line was chosen)

### R by arity, symmetry, scale

- **Arity:** multiple and mixed. At least three slot kinds (factual, structural, trust or identity) and four overlapping transmission conditions. This is not a scalar residual. Fig 9 :54: **"And when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again."**
- **Symmetry:** the transmission-to-belief relation is not a symmetric join. It is production (⇒) when a demonstration creates a new competence, sequence (→) when a museum visit precedes a later conversation, comparison (⋛) when a visitor weighs a dramatised account against a real one, or refusal (¬) when an acquired belief is given up. Fig 12 distinguishes these; the Operators table states that *"a vocabulary without [negation] can only accumulate."* A model that only adds facts cannot represent a corrected belief.
- **Scale:** three levels. One demonstrator and one audience member (one frame contacting another); a museum or exhibition (a room with many frames and a shared narrative); popular writing (a chain of rooms handed along). Fig 7 :12-13: **"same closure, handed along; agreement compounds, evidence does not"**.

### One line per domain: actors / currency / anchor

A domain needs a nameable anchor. Where a candidate has no anchor of its own, it is a frame that must range against someone else's ground, not a domain yet.

| domain | actors | currency | anchor (nameable?) |
|---|---|---|---|
| **D1 scientific ground** | claims, instruments, methods, measurements | settlement by what pays | **yes.** Fig 2 :29: **"An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks."** A measurement, an experiment, a counter. |
| **D2 transmission frames** (demonstrators, curators, exhibit designers, writers, media) | the four channels | representations sent down: recordings versus methods; attention, coverage, credibility | **no anchor of its own.** It is a channel that decays and must range against D1 and D3. Its own quality numbers are frame-internal: Fig 1 :27 **"They are frame-internal. A frame cannot score itself with a quantity it also produces."** |
| **D3 public belief frame** | audiences with existing vocabularies, histories and identities | agreement, trust, perceived knowledge | **yes.** Human judgment: Fig 2 :37 **"Human judgment is the right anchor for is this a good answer for a person, because the question is about people."** Operationally, measured beliefs under a declared protocol. |
| **D4 research / verification frame** (public understanding of science researchers) | researchers, surveys, interviews, focus groups | residuals and independent evidence | **yes, in principle.** The measured record under a fixed protocol. In this simulated run all four domains draw on one evidence pool, so D4's independence is zero and its agreement is not corroboration. |

### Partition or union

**Union, not partition.** The four channels overlap and the slot kinds overlap. A union compound has its own loop and its own ground (Fig 9 :54-58). Treating it as a partition would send us looking for four independent effects where there are overlapping conditions. The Library of Babel essay states the same rule in different words: *"Perhaps your problem was a bundle or conflation of several problems masquerading as one? You will need to search all of those problems individually to find their answers and then combine them in a satisfactory way."*

### Order by dependency (Fig 3)

Fig 3 :20: **"The chain is ordered by dependency, not by importance."** The order for this question:

1. **Perception:** can the audience perceive the transmission at all?
2. **Vocabulary and affordances:** does the audience have terms for what the exhibit affords? This is the middle link.
3. **Objective:** what would count as progress here, in the audience's own terms?
4. **Mechanism / planning:** a route from the current belief to that objective.
5. **Learn and carry:** name what worked, verify it, carry it to the next visit or the next topic.

Fig 3 :24: **"Why the middle link is where chains usually break."** Fig 3 :25-26: *"having terms for what a situation offers and being able to say what would count as progress, has neither, so it attracts the least attention and the fewest instruments."* So the first instrument owed in this field is not a better attitude survey. It is an instrument for whether the audience can read what the display affords.

## 3. Findings F1 to F10

### F1 - The anchor for this question is human judgment, not scientific correctness (decisive for method)

Fig 2 :36: **"An anchor is legitimate when the question is constitutively about it."**
Fig 2 :37: **"Human judgment is the right anchor for is this a good answer for a person, because the question is about people."**
Fig 2 :38: **"It is only an instrument for is this code correct, and instruments can be wrong on axes they do not measure."**

The question asks how beliefs are shaped. That is constitutively about people, so the settled record of what people believe under a declared protocol is the anchor. Scientific correctness is an instrument here, not the anchor: a communication can be factually exact and still fail on the axis of belief formation. The world literature corroborates this. The Science communication article reports that *"Many outreach initiatives focus solely on increasing the public's knowledge, but studies have found little, if any, correlation between knowledge levels and attitudes towards scientific issues."*

**Mode:** `general` (structural), triangulated across Fig 2 and the world source.
**Refuter:** show that beliefs about science are not constitutively about people, or that a non-human measure such as content accuracy settles whether a belief changed. If a factually perfect exhibit reliably settles the belief question without any measurement of a person, F1 fails.

### F2 - Two different products must be separated: agreement about the world and agreement about shared meaning

Fig 2 :39: **"Correlation has valence."**
Fig 2 :40: **"disqualifying for a verdict: agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool"**
Fig 2 :41: **"efficient for a convention: agreement about a shared meaning is the product, not a corruption of it"**
Fig 2 :31: **"Independence, between two frames, is how little they draw on the same evidence."**

"Beliefs about science" mixes two things. When the slot is factual or structural (is the claim true, how does inquiry work), agreement among people who all read the same popular accounts is the shared pool talking, not two frames confirming each other. When the slot is shared meaning (what "science" names in public life), agreement is the product being manufactured, so correlation is efficient rather than disqualifying. A single "public understanding" score blends them. The Decoding Tacit Knowledge essay states the same distinction: *"Shared pretext is good for one thing and ruins another. Agreement is the whole point when you're settling what words mean. It's worthless for settling what's true when both sides learned from the same sources."*

**Mode:** `general`, triangulated across Fig 2 :39-41, Fig 2 :31 and the essay. This is the live strain that drives the unbundling in §1.
**Refuter:** show two transmission channels with demonstrated low overlap (independent evidence pools) converging on the same factual belief change, where the convergence is not a convention about wording. That would make shared-pool agreement unnecessary to explain the result.

### F3 - What crosses is usually a recording, not a method, so beliefs tilt toward science as facts

Fig 4 :22: **"Only methods cross up. Only head starts come down."**
Fig 4 :23: **"upward: a method that can be reapplied. Never the recording of a particular success."**
Fig 4 :25: **"A recording carried upward looks like knowledge and is a description of one occasion."**
Fig 4 :26: **"Replayed downward, it produces a system that repeats a past success and cannot produce a new one."**
Fig 4 :34-35: **"A seed carries no small tree: it carries what will grow one, and the conditions it lands in decide what it becomes."**

A demonstration, a diorama, a "wow" exhibit and a popular article typically send down a recording of a scientific result: a fact, a spectacle, an image. What they rarely send is the method that produced it and can be reapplied. So the belief most easily moved is "science is a body of established facts," not "science is a way of finding things out." The conditions the seed lands in (the audience's prior vocabulary, identity and pretext) decide what grows. The Duckett et al. museum study corroborates the conditional part: participants compared *"real research that's going on here"* with a CSI programme and reported *"a bit more querying as opposed to just believing everything you see,"* which is a change in the conditions, not the transmission of a new fact.

**Mode:** `specified` (a shape prediction for this field), single-source for the figure line, corroborated by the museum study.
**Refuter:** demonstrate a public channel that reliably transfers a reusable method and whose recipients generate novel predictions with it. That breaks the claim that what crosses is usually a recording.

### F4 - The channel decays while the ground does not, producing stale, pooled, saturated beliefs

Fig 10 :24: **"the ground does not decay, the channel does"**
Fig 10 :25-26: **"stale percepts, dropped transients, saturated metrics, pooled readings"**
Fig 10 :27-28: **"the output is not a verdict, it is you have lost touch"**

The scientific ground does not rot, but the path from it to a museum label or a popular book does. By the time a claim reaches a public, it can be stale, shorn of its transient conditions, saturated into a headline, or pooled with other claims into a single reading. The PUS literature corroborates the mechanism: the Public awareness of science article records that self-reported familiarity and factual knowledge *"do not reflect the same underlying knowledge structures"* and had low correlations in a nanotechnology study. A survey that reads "I know a lot about science" cannot comment on the source of that reading, which is the metamerism case from *The Hard Problem is About Source*: the instrument *"cannot comment on the source."*

**Mode:** `specified`, triangulated across Fig 10 and the two world sources.
**Refuter:** exhibit a channel whose agreement with the scientific ground is measurable and does not decay over repeated tellings and across time (freshness and per-slot accuracy measured at each hop).

### F5 - Handing science along a chain of agreeing rooms compounds agreement, not evidence

Fig 7 :12-13: **"same closure, handed along; agreement compounds, evidence does not"**
Fig 7 :35: **"A room's agreement is variation, not a verdict. Selection between rooms is where the ground votes."**
Fig 7 :36: **"A chain of mirrors is one long room. The next room must differ from the last, and match the residual it surfaced."**

Popular writing that cites other popular writing rather than the research is a mirror chain. A museum that repeats the same narrative as every other museum is one long room. Both can produce an impression of consensus while the evidence pool stays fixed. Fig 8 :32: **"Closure is idempotent. Composing inside a closed set never leaves it, so no search, however long, adds an atom."** The Royal Society's own history of the Bodmer report describes the intended effect as *"an expansion of science communication, the popularisation of science in culture and media and greater links between science and society,"* which is a claim about reach, not about the ground. That is the right mode for it: reach is a budget quantity, not a verdict.

**Mode:** `general`, triangulated across Fig 7 and Fig 8.
**Refuter:** show a transmission chain in which each room is selected against the residual the previous room surfaced, draws on the ground directly, and has low overlap with its neighbours, with measured belief change at each handoff.

### F6 - Residuals must be read per slot, not aggregated into "the public's understanding"

Fig 1 :19: **"The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated."**
Fig 1 :22: **"Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average."**
Fig 1 :27: **"They are frame-internal. A frame cannot score itself with a quantity it also produces."**

Coverage, attendance, click-through, "predicates minted, and credibility accrued" are all frame-internal. The field's standard instrument, the large-scale public opinion survey, aggregates across people and across slot kinds. The contextualist turn described in the Public awareness of science article moved *"Instead of large scale public opinion surveys"* toward micro-sociological studies of expert-lay interaction precisely because the aggregate lost the live signal. The Science communication article quotes the journal editor Edna Einsiedel: *"We have clearly moved from the old days of the deficit frame and thinking of publics as monolithic to viewing publics as active, knowledgeable, playing multiple roles, receiving as well as shaping science."*

**Mode:** `general`, triangulated across Fig 1 and two world sources.
**Refuter:** show that one aggregate score directs interventions as well as per-slot residuals, meaning no slot ever carries a signal the aggregate misses.

### F7 - The middle link (vocabulary and affordances) is where transmission chains break

Fig 3 :24: **"Why the middle link is where chains usually break."**
Fig 3 :27: **"A link with no instrument is not a link that works. It is a link nobody has looked at."**
Fig 3 :19: **"Optimising a link that already works cannot rescue one that does not."**

A display that assumes the visitor already has terms for what it affords optimises a link above the break. The Duckett et al. study found that visitors valued hands-on activities and informal discussion and that *"it was good that it was very understandable; it was relatable and not like you were being talked down to,"* which is a report about affordances being readable. The study also reports that public engagement events are often criticised for *"preaching to the converted,"* which is a selection effect at the perception link, not a vocabulary failure. Keep the two distinct.

**Mode:** `specified`, triangulated across Fig 3 and the museum study.
**Refuter:** produce an instrument showing the audience already holds the terms for what a given exhibit affords, and that measured understanding is unchanged when those terms are made explicit.

### F8 - Capability is agent-and-habitat; a slicker exhibit that does not change contact changes nothing

Fig 11 :39: **"capability is a property of agent-and-habitat, never of the agent alone"**
Fig 11 :40: **"An improvement that does not change contact changes nothing, however much it improves."**

A more beautiful installation, a better-written popular book, a more charismatic demonstrator: none of these changes what the audience is in contact with. The Decoding Tacit Knowledge essay makes the same point for teaching: *"Writing carries the context well and the subtext partly, but it almost never carries the pretext, the 'you had to be there' part."* What is owed is a change in contact (two-way correction, direct encounter with the phenomenon, a channel that can answer back), not a better recording. The Duckett study describes precisely this shift: *"Events are no longer seen as merely an occasion for scientists to 'educate' the lay person, but an opportunity for two-way dialogue between the 'experts' and 'non-experts'."*

**Mode:** `specified`, triangulated across Fig 11, the essay and the museum study.
**Refuter:** show a presentation redesign that improves measured per-slot understanding while the audience's contact with the ground is held constant.

### F9 - Contact cascades beyond the direct audience

Fig 11 :43: **"The habitat is enumerated, never composed."**
Fig 11 :49: **"An actor's valence is relative to the residual, not a property it carries."**
Fig 11 :52-53: **"contact cascades. Scoping the habitat to direct contact is how DDT reached the eagles: first-order enumeration, second-order effect, and the residual that mattered was three steps out."**

A museum visitor is not the end of the chain. The Duckett focus group reports participants *"had increased discussion of science in their lives since the event"* and *"a bit more querying as opposed to just believing everything you see."* Those are second-order effects (conversation, scepticism) that an exit questionnaire aimed at the visitor alone will miss. The audience's valence is relative to the residual: the same exhibit is a benefactor for a trust slot and irrelevant for a factual slot.

**Mode:** `specified`, triangulated across Fig 11 and the museum study.
**Refuter:** show that direct-contact measurement at the exhibit captures every consequential effect, with no measurable cascade to family, peers or later media choices.

### F10 - What the figures cannot give: an effect size or a ranking of the four channels

Fig 9 :32: **"you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself"**
Fig 8 :39: **"So a search that finds nothing is never a proof of absence."**
Fig 6 :32: **"An instrument is not built from a description. It is improved from a worse instrument already returning something."**

The figures return the shape (per-slot, anchor-separated, union-unbundled, contact-sensitive, method-versus-recording aware). They do not return "museums move trust by X" or "popular writing is stronger than demonstration." That needs an instrument, and Fig 6 :34 makes clear that instruments are improved from worse instruments already returning something. In this field, the existing instruments are the surveys and the museum exit questionnaires. They are already returning something, so the route is INWARD (sharpen them), not OUTWARD (import a new theory of communication).

**Mode:** `general` for the shape, `gap` for the effect sizes. The gap is a verdict, not a silence.
**Refuter of the gap:** an instrument that measures per-slot belief change under each channel with low-overlap frames and a fixed ground would close it. Until then, no ranking is licensed.

## 4. Strain test and re-census

Two apparent strains were found. Both are resolved by re-census rather than by softening a reading.

**Strain A: anchor versus change.** Fig 2 :29-30 says an anchor must not change its answer because of what any frame thinks. Yet the whole question is about beliefs that change when a museum acts on them. Is the anchor moving? Re-census: the object of study (a person's belief) changes; the anchor (the measured record of that belief under a declared protocol) does not move because the researcher wants a different result. These are different quantities. The figures' README rule applies: *"a reading that makes one contradict another has misidentified its domain, not found a flaw."* No strain after re-census. F1 and F4 both survive.

**Strain B: agreement disqualifying and agreement productive.** Fig 2 :40 and :41 appear to say opposite things about agreement. Re-census: :40 governs agreement about the world across frames sharing an evidence pool; :41 governs agreement about a shared meaning, where the agreement is the product. The two govern different slots in D3. This is not a contradiction to be resolved away. It is evidence that D3 is a union that must be unbundled (Fig 9 :54). I keep this as a **live, named strain** because it is exactly the thing the field calls a measurement problem, and I do not pretend it has been measured: the PUS article's distinction between factual knowledge, self-reported knowledge and trust, and its note that these *"do not reflect the same underlying knowledge structures,"* is the world-level shadow of the same split.

**Fig 13 out of scope:** the six-term substrate governs what composes in physical systems. It does not govern how a museum changes a mind. Listing it as strained would be forcing a fit. It is recorded as not bearing, and that is an honest census result.

## 5. Pre-registered refuter

Written before the conclusion, per the standing instruction: *"a falsifier written before the run is a design instrument."*

The shape claim is refuted if any of the following holds:

1. One aggregate "public understanding of science" score predicts intervention outcomes as well as per-slot residuals, so no slot ever carries a signal the aggregate misses (breaks F6 and Fig 1 :19-22).
2. Two transmission channels with demonstrated low overlap (independent evidence pools) converge on the same factual belief change, and the convergence is not a convention about wording (breaks F2 and Fig 2 :31, :40).
3. A public transmission is shown that reliably transfers a reusable method, not a recording, and its recipients generate novel predictions with it (breaks F3 and Fig 4 :22-26).
4. A channel is shown whose agreement with the scientific ground does not decay over time and across re-tellings, with freshness and per-slot accuracy measured at each hop (breaks F4 and Fig 10 :24-28).

If any fires, the shape is wrong and I will retract it. If none fires, that is not confirmation, because this run shares one evidence pool and cannot supply an outside ground.

## 6. Recommendation

1. Stop reporting one aggregate. Report per-slot residuals (F6).
2. Split the two products: factual and structural belief versus shared meaning and identity. They have different anchors and different validity rules (F2).
3. Build the middle-link instrument before buying a better attitude survey (F7).
4. Ask what crosses: recording or method. Design for method transfer if the goal is structural understanding, and say honestly when the goal is only reach (F3).
5. Treat reach, coverage and attendance as budget quantities, never as verdicts (Fig 9 :60: **"Use filters for the budget and witnesses for the verdict. Never let a filter hand you a verdict."**).
6. Enumerate the habitat outward, because the consequential residual is often three steps from the exhibit (F9).
7. Offer the packet as a check, not as a theory to believe (Fig 10 :52: **"Bring a characterised residual, never the source."**).

## 8. Round-trip check (short form, full version in `11_audit.md`)

The condensation used in `07` and `09` is: per-slot residuals, two anchors, union unbundled, recording versus method, contact over redesign, gap for effect sizes. Carrying that back down to a concrete museum or popular article loses at least three things: the type of the bond (⇒ versus → versus ⋛), the ordering of refusal before mint when a held belief must be given up, and the distinction between a convention and a verdict. R_T is therefore nonzero and is recorded qualitatively, not numerically, because no ground reading exists here. Treating the formula as identical to the figures would set R_T to zero and would be the dead-reckoning error Fig 4 :30 names.

## 9. Final verdict of this audit

The shape survives and the empirical ranking does not exist yet. Beliefs about science are moved by transmission conditions that act per slot, on different grounds, through channels that mostly carry recordings, through habitats where contact and cascade matter more than polish. The two valences of agreement must be kept apart. The figures cannot say which of the four channels is strongest or by how much; that is a `gap` owed to a better instrument, and Fig 8 :39 says a search that finds nothing is not a proof of absence. All claims here are `general` or `specified` and none is `grounded`. The simulated second seat in `11_audit.md` shares this evidence pool, so its agreement is internal consistency, not corroboration.
