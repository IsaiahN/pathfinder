## Independent domain census

I redid the census from the residual line alone, before comparing with the primary's conclusions, using
the WATSON order: R by arity, symmetry and scale; one line per domain with actors, currency and
anchor; partition or union; order by dependency.

- **R (arity / symmetry / scale).** The residual is the gap between standing accorded and judgments
  that pay at a non-updating ground. Arity: four conditions, so a union compound, not one hard
  question. Symmetry: a comparison of a claim against a ground, not a symmetric join between two
  people; the title-checker relation cannot be read off the two people. Scale: first order in one
  slot, second order in a recognized lineage.
- **Domain 1, credential and institution.** Actors: issuers of documents. Currency: credentials,
  titles, prestige. Anchor: the issuer's own standard, which changes with the issuer's frame, so the
  anchor test fails. Question type: a currency, not an anchor.
- **Domain 2, subject-matter ground.** Actors: instruments, datasets, markets, boards. Currency:
  residuals settled or not. Anchor: the ground, pinned. Question type: the settling domain.
- **Domain 3, verification community.** Actors: peer frames. Currency: agreement and residuals.
  Anchor: the ground, never agreement. Question type: corroboration only at low overlap.
- **Domain 4, lineage and history.** Actors: lineages of work, provenance records. Currency: standing.
  Anchor: the ground, retrospectively. Question type: retrospective recognition plus a declared stake.

Order by dependency: ground and residual before claim; claim before independent check; check before
recognition; recognition before credential. A credential added above a break cannot repair it.

My census matches the primary's four domains and its union reading. I reached it from Fig 3's

from Fig 1's per-slot residual and Fig 2's anchor test. Different entry figures, same boundaries: low
overlap between routes within the framework, but still one evidence pool. The agreement is weak and

## Round trip gap R_T

The Symbols table defines `R_T` as the gap between what was sent up and what came back down,
`gap(x, (T_E ∘ T_A)(x))`, measuring what the coarser description cannot hold. Fig 4 :30 requires it to

3. **Concretisation (T_E):** the everyday claim the question asks about, namely "an expert is someone
   whose title and training make their judgment authoritative".

**R_T is nonzero.** Three things the shorthand holds do not survive the trip back down:

- the **scope of the stake rule**, which governs the checker and not the person checked; carried down
  naively it becomes a blanket condemnation of earned standing;
- the **legitimacy versus truth limit** at Fig 10 :65, which the everyday claim collapses into a
  trust judgment;
- the **union structure**, which the everyday claim flattens into one global authority score.

Conversely, one thing survives cleanly: a credential is frame-internal and cannot by itself settle a
claim, and recognition attaches to a lineage at a ground. That is the residue worth sending. `R_T` was

`R_T` to zero and would be the dead-reckoning error Fig 4 :30 names.
