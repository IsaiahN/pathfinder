## Three findings in the wider literature that bear directly on this question

1. **Title power and expert power are different objects.** French and Raven separate legitimate power
   (position, easily lost when the position is lost) from expert power (knowledge, experience, skill,
   bounded in scope, not everlasting). This is the world-language version of the figure distinction
   between a frame-internal credential and ground contact. Cite it when explaining why the question
   asks what is earned *beyond* titles.
2. **Recognition is social, but settlement is not a vote.** Shapin and Schaffer show that the
   credibility of experimentally produced facts was built with material, literary and social
   technologies, including "virtual witnessing" and replication. The figures do not deny this; they
   separate it: "the ground does not decay, the channel does" (Fig 10 :24). A community's assurance
   is a channel that can fail, not an anchor. Note also that the book's historical thesis is
   contested in its own field, so cite it as a case study, not as consensus.
3. **Consensus can be wrong; leadership style is habitat-dependent.** The animal-behaviour source
   reports that the answer to whether experienced leaders or consensus decides "probably depends on
   the species", and that a quorum rule can cascade into the incorrect decision. This is a
   low-overlap corroboration of Fig 7 :35: "A room's agreement is variation, not a verdict. Selection
   between rooms is where the ground votes."

## Sources that conflict, and what the conflict did

- Shapin and Schaffer appear to make credibility a social product; the figures insist the ground, not
  agreement, settles. Re-census: different quantities (channel versus ground). No verdict flipped.
  Recorded in `01_figure_grounded_audit.md` §6.
- French and Raven say expertise "does not have to be genuine" for expert power to work. Re-census:
  they describe influence, not settlement. No verdict flipped.
- Both conflicts are in `10_source_ledger.csv` with `overlap_with_other_sources` and status recorded.

## Distribution: offer, do not push

Figure-grounded rules:

- **Fig 4 :22**: "Only methods cross up. Only head starts come down." Sending the full framework
  before anyone has described a residual sends a recording, not a generator. Lead with the claim and
  its refuter; offer links after.
- **Fig 10 :42**: "A verifier that can reconstruct the claim cannot verify it." A recipient who has
  absorbed the interior before giving a verdict has joined the frame being checked.
- **Fig 9 :27-28**: describe the residual before choosing a frame, or any frame will seem to fit.
  Ask what the recipient would expect to see if the claim were true.
- **Fig 2 :42**: "Buy independence in proportion to the cost of being wrong." Seek readers whose
  public work touches the residual in a different closure; do not rank them by likely agreement.
- **Fig 2 :40**: later agreement is never pooled as corroboration when the evidence pool is shared.

**Recommended order in a reply:** (1) the claim, its slot and its refuter; (2) ask what they would
count as a failure, and record the answer before any theory; (3) only then the three links, with the
note that the figures, not the summary, are the authority; (4) only then the formula as a method to
reuse, with the instruction to write a refuter first.
