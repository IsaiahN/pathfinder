# Expert verification packet — the Tether on expert authority beyond titles

**What this is:** a claim in a form a researcher of expertise, authority or credibility can check.
Mode, derivation and refuters are stated before the conclusion, so a reader knows what to look for and
what would settle it.

**Mode: `specified`, not `grounded`.** No controlled comparison is claimed. This is a shape and a
route, not a result. It is a simulated derivation checked for internal consistency against the figures.

## 1. The claim, in one paragraph

Formal training and titles are third-party documents, frame-internal scores that can fund a trial but
cannot settle a claim. Authority beyond them is a relation, not a possession: a judgment in one slot,
a ground that does not move when opinion changes, checkers whose evidence pools do not overlap with
the claim's, and a record of the route by which the judgment was reached. Recognition is retrospective
and attached to a lineage, never global. Standing is a stake the checker must declare. The framework
returns this shape, not a name, and the claim stays a candidate until a pre-registered prediction is
scored against a fixed ground.

## 2. Three checkable sub-claims

### C1 — Framework-internal; checkable by reading Figures 1, 2 and 11

- **Statement:** credentials and titles are frame-internal; the anchor for authority is a ground that
  does not update; recognition is retrospective.
- **Source, verbatim:**
  - Fig 1 :27: "They are frame-internal. A frame cannot score itself with a quantity it also produces."
  - Fig 2 :29: "An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of
    what any frame thinks."
  - Fig 11 :36: "Where nothing maintains anything, intelligence is a label applied afterwards to a
    lineage that met its ground."
- **What to check:** does any of these lines govern a different quantity than the one claimed? If the
  institution's own standard is a genuine anchor, Fig 2 :29 is misapplied.
- **Refuter:** a title or credential that settles a claim in a slot where the holder's judgment has no
  contact with a non-updating ground, and the credential is not itself the question.

### C2 — World-facing; checkable against the literature on expertise and power

- **Statement:** authority is per-slot and independent of title in the relevant sense. Expert power
  is knowledge and experience bounded to a domain; legitimate power is a position that can be lost; a
  person can be expert in one field and a layperson in another.
- **Source, verbatim:**
  - Fig 1 :19: "The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated."
  - Fig 2 :40: "disqualifying for a verdict: agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing
    frames share an evidence pool"
  - Wikipedia *Expert*: "A person may well be an expert in one field and a layperson in many other
    fields."
  - Wikipedia *French and Raven's bases of power*: expert power is "based on what one knows,
    experience, and special skills or talents" and legitimate power "can easily be lost and the leader
    does not have his position or title anymore."
- **What to check:** is the mapping from figure `slot` to world `domain` faithful? Does the literature
  contain a controlled result that undermines the per-slot claim?
- **Refuter:** a global authority measure that predicts ground settlement across unrelated slots
  without aggregating away the per-slot signal; or evidence that a shared evidence pool adds genuine
  evidence to a verdict.

### C3 — Framework-internal derivation, with a world check on the stake rule

- **Statement:** provenance separates earned from borrowed standing but does not establish truth;
  standing is a stake that must be declared; a ground with a reputation to protect is disqualified.
- **Source, verbatim:**
  - Fig 8 :51: "Convergent derivation and adopted import look identical in the contents. Only the
    record of where each came from separates them"
  - Fig 10 :65: "Provenance shows a ruling was reached legitimately. It never shows it was right: the
    ground does that"
  - Fig 10 :61: "And standing is a stake that survives the outcome it was earned on."
  - Fig 10 :93: "A ground with a reputation to protect is a ground someone benefits from, and its
    keeper is thereby disqualified."
  - The essay *The Recursive Self-Improvement Trap*: "someone let prestige stand in for proof.
    Someone let reputation substitute for result."
- **What to check:** does Fig 10 :61 govern the checker's stake rather than the legitimacy of the
  person checked? If not, the finding was mis-scoped and must be withdrawn.
- **Refuter:** a checker with declared standing who refuses their own prior claims at the same rate as
  a checker with no such stake, in the same slot.

## 3. What is explicitly not claimed

- Not that training and titles are worthless. They fund a trial; they do not settle it.
- Not that any named person is or is not an authority. No name or ranking is returned.
- Not that reputation is always false. It is a stake to declare, not a verdict to trust.
- Not a global authority score. Per-slot only.
- Not that provenance establishes truth. It establishes the legitimacy of the route only.
- Not `grounded`. No measurement in this packet scores a prediction against a fixed ground.
- Not an independent witness. This packet is one simulated seat reading one evidence pool.

## 5. What would settle it

A ground reading, not agreement. The instrument is a pre-registered prediction scored against a
ground that does not move:

1. **Prediction test:** register a judgment in one slot, score it against the ground, and compare
   title-holders with non-holders who have a recorded lineage of ground contact.
2. **Anchor test:** move community opinion while holding the ground fixed and see whether the
   authority claim moves with it.
3. **Independence test:** use two checkers with different evidence pools and see whether agreement
   adds evidence beyond the shared pool.
4. **Source discrimination:** name the instrument that would distinguish a title from a ground-contact
   record in a reading of "this person is trusted". A title and a reputation produce the same reading;
   as with metamerism, the reading cannot comment on the source.

## 6. What an expert can check in twenty minutes

1. Read Fig 1 :27, Fig 2 :29, Fig 7 :35, Fig 8 :51 and Fig 10 :61, :93, :65. Six short passages.
2. Check C2 against what you know about expertise and institutional authority: is `slot` a faithful
   rendering of domain-specific expertise?
3. Name one ground that would settle the prediction test in your field, or say why none exists.
4. State what standing you bring to the check, if any.
5. Send the refutation. A failure is more useful than an endorsement.
