## 0. The residual in effect terms

A credential is "a piece of any document that details a qualification, competence, or authority
issued to an individual by a third party with a relevant or de facto authority or assumed competence
to do so" (Wikipedia, *Credential*). It is a record of what an institution granted. A title is a
credential that "identify[ies] a person as belonging to a specific group" and "may or may not be
associated with specific authority" (same source). Neither is a settlement of any claim the holder
later makes.

The residual is the gap between:

- **the standing a person is accorded**, that is credentials, titles, reputation; and
- **whether that person's judgments actually pay at a ground that does not move when people change
  their minds.**

Option A: authority is a possession conferred in advance by training and title. It leads to a
frame-internal score with no anchor and to a prestige loop in which a name validates an untested
claim. Option B: authority is a relation among a claim, a non-updating ground, independent checkers,
and a recorded lineage. It leads to a checkable shape, written refuters, and an honest stop.

**Direction is bound as an ordinary term now, before the run.** What an answer would look like:
"authority is a per-slot, provenance-bearing, independently checkable record of contact with a
non-updating ground, recognized retrospectively." Binding it makes the sought shape refusable by the
same test as everything else (formula step Direction; Fig 5 :62-66). It is not assumed true.

## 1. Domain census, before choosing any figure line

### R by arity, symmetry and scale

- **Arity:** authority is indexed per claim or slot, not a single global number. Fig 1 :19:
  "The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated." A person can be authoritative in one slot
  and a layperson in another. Aggregating a career into one authority score is the named failure:
  Fig 1 :22 "Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average."
- **Symmetry:** authority is a threshold relation between a claim and a ground, not a symmetric join
  between two peers. It is not recoverable from the two people compared. The checker cannot be the
  claim's author (Fig 10 :42).
- **Scale:** one agent's contact with a ground, and a population of checkers. The membrane between
  them passes methods, not the recording of a particular success (Fig 4 :22).

### One line per domain: actors / currency / anchor

| domain | actors | currency | anchor | status |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1. Credential and institution | institutions, departments, licensing boards, title-granting bodies | credentials, titles, prestige | nominal anchor is the issuer's own standard, which moves when the frame moves; fails the anchor test | not a ground; a frame-internal currency |
| 2. Subject-matter ground | measurements, experiments, datasets, markets, patients, boards | residuals that settle or do not settle | the ground, pinned, does not update on anyone's opinion | the only legitimate anchor |
| 3. Verification community | independent peer frames, expert checkers, reviewers | agreement and residuals | the ground, never agreement | corroborates only at low overlap |
| 4. Lineage and history | lineages of work, provenance records, predecessors, successors | standing and provenance | the ground, retrospectively | authority is a retrospective label; standing is a declared stake |

A domain without a nameable anchor is not yet a domain that can settle anything. Domain 1 has a
nameable issuer, but it fails the anchor test: Fig 2 :29-30 "An anchor is whatever settles the
question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks." and "If it moves when you
push on it, it is not an anchor." So domain 1 is a currency, not an anchor. It is kept in the census
because it is the thing the question asks us to set aside, not because it settles.

### Partition or union

This is a **union compound**, not a partition. A person can hold a title (domain 1) and also have
ground contact (domain 2), and the same person can be a checker (domain 3) in one slot while being
checked in another. The four domains overlap in one biography, so "authority" bundles legitimacy,
credential and reputation. Fig 9 :54-56: "And when the split lands on a union rather than a partition,
unbundle before you search again." Each domain therefore gets its own verdict; the verdicts are never
averaged into one authority score.

### Order by dependency (Fig 3)

1. A ground and a residual must exist and be perceptible (links 1 to 3: perception, vocabulary,
   objective).
2. An agent acts and produces a checkable claim (link 4).
3. Independent frames range that claim against the ground (link 5).
4. The community recognizes a lineage retrospectively (domain 4).
5. Institutions may codify recognition into credentials (domain 1).

The credential layer is last in dependency order. Fig 3 :19: "Optimising a link that already works
cannot rescue one that does not." Adding credentials to a broken ground-contact chain spends effort
above the break. Fig 3 :27: "A link with no instrument is not a link that works. It is a link nobody
has looked at." The middle link here is the one from a claim to an independent check.

## 3. Findings

### F1 — Credentials are frame-internal; a frame cannot score itself with them

Fig 1 :26-27: "predicates minted, and credibility accrued" and "They are frame-internal. A frame
cannot score itself with a quantity it also produces."

A degree count, a title list and a publication count are quantities the institution's frame produces
and then uses to score its own members. By this figure they cannot be the ground of a verdict about
whether the holder's judgments pay. Wikipedia *Credential* corroborates mechanically: credentials are
"issued to an individual by a third party with a relevant or de facto authority or assumed competence
to do so." French and Raven's account of legitimate power agrees on the fragility: "People
traditionally obey the person with this power solely based on their role, position or title rather than
the person specifically as a leader. Therefore, this type of power can easily be lost and the leader
does not have his position or title anymore."

- **Mode:** `general` (structural).
- **Status:** triangulated across Fig 1 and two independent social-science sources (low overlap:
  institution-issued documents versus influence taxonomy).
- **Refuter:** show a credential that settles a claim without any contact between the holder's
  judgment and the ground, in a slot where the credential itself is not the question. If such a
  credential exists, F1 fails.

### F2 — The anchor for authority is the ground, and it must not update

Fig 2 :29-30: "An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of
what any frame thinks." and "If it moves when you push on it, it is not an anchor."
Fig 2 :33: "The anchor must not update."

Authority beyond titles is therefore earned when a claim pays at something that does not move with
opinion: a measurement, a dataset, a market, a patient outcome, a board. Reputation is the wrong
kind of thing: it is exactly what updates when people think differently. Fig 2 :45: "Frames range
against the ground. They do not negotiate with it."

History-of-science corroboration is direct. Shapin and Schaffer's *Leviathan and the Air-Pump* set out
"to discover the origins of the credibility that we give experimentally produced facts today", and
record that Boyle's matter of fact depended "not only upon their actual performance but essentially
upon the assurance of the relevant community that they had been so performed." The air pump was the
instrument; the community's assurance was the channel, and the book documents how easily that
channel broke (replication troubles, "virtual witnessing"). The essay states the rule in one line:
"Only the ground can settle it."

- **Mode:** `general` for the anchor requirement, `specified` for any particular person's authority.
- **Status:** triangulated across Fig 2 and the history-of-science source (different evidence pool).
- **Refuter:** show a case where authority is genuinely settled by a vote or a reputation that
  changes with the vote, with no non-updating ground in the loop. Then the anchor requirement fails.

### F3 — Authority is retrospective, not a credential held in advance

Fig 11 :36: "Where nothing maintains anything, intelligence is a label applied afterwards to a
lineage that met its ground."
Fig 4 :23: "upward: a method that can be reapplied. Never the recording of a particular success."

The word "expert" does no work before the ground has paid. It is applied afterwards to a lineage that
met its ground. What can travel from one person to another is a method that can be reapplied, not a
recording of a success. Fig 11 :40: "An improvement that does not change contact changes nothing,
however much it improves." More training that does not change contact with the ground does not, by
itself, add authority.

Wikipedia *Expert* agrees on recognition: an expert is someone "accorded authority and status by
peers or the public in a specific well-distinguished domain", and "it is not always necessary for
individuals to have a professional or academic qualification for them to be accepted as an expert",
illustrated by "a shepherd with fifty years of experience". French and Raven: expert power is "based
on what one knows, experience, and special skills or talents."

A strain was recorded and re-censed: French and Raven add "The expertise does not have to be genuine -
it is the perception of expertise that provides the power base." That is a claim about **influence**,
not about **settlement**. It does not conflict with F3 once the domains are separated: a perceived
expert can move people (domain 3) without paying at the ground (domain 2). No verdict was flipped.

- **Mode:** `specified`.
- **Status:** triangulated (Fig 11 plus Fig 4 for the transfer rule; two world sources for recognition).
- **Refuter:** exhibit a person whose judgment pays repeatedly at a non-updating ground before any
  community recognition or training exists, and show that the recognition did not follow the ground
  contact. Then the retrospective claim fails.

### F4 — Authority is per-slot, never global

Fig 1 :19: "The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated."
Fig 13 :34: "And within a kind the members are distinct too, which is what makes them separate frames."

An authority claim is always a claim about a slot: this domain, this question, this measurement. A
global "authority" score drops the only signal that says where to look (Fig 1 :22). Wikipedia
*Expert*: "A person may well be an expert in one field and a layperson in many other fields." French
and Raven name both failure modes: "an expert in antique vases may have little expertise in antique
lamps" and "one's expertise is not everlasting."

- **Mode:** `general` for the per-slot rule, `specified` for any named slot.
- **Status:** triangulated (Fig 1 and Fig 13 are different figures with different structures; the
  two world sources are separate pools).
- **Refuter:** produce a working global authority measure that predicts ground settlement across
  unrelated slots without aggregating away the per-slot signal.

### F5 — Agreement is a room's variation, not a verdict

Fig 7 :35: "A room's agreement is variation, not a verdict. Selection between rooms is where the
ground votes."
Fig 7 :13: "same closure, handed along; agreement compounds, evidence does not"
Fig 2 :40: "disqualifying for a verdict: agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing
frames share an evidence pool"
Fig 2 :42: "Buy independence in proportion to the cost of being wrong."

A reputation built by being agreed with is a chain of mirrors. The next checker must differ from the
last and match the residual it surfaced. The animal-behaviour source makes the same point without
sharing the framework's vocabulary: in collective decision making, "Do stronger or more experienced
'leaders' exert more influence than other group members, or does the group make a decision by
consensus? The answer probably depends on the species." A quorum rule "generally resulted in the
'correct' decision but occasionally cascaded into the 'incorrect' decision." Consensus is a device,
not an anchor, and which device works is a property of the habitat, not of the person.

- **Mode:** `general`.
- **Status:** triangulated across Fig 7, Fig 2 and a low-overlap animal-behaviour source.
- **Refuter:** show that a population of checkers drawn from one shared evidence pool adds genuine
  evidence to a verdict, beyond the evidence already in the pool.

### F6 — Provenance is the whole difference between earned and borrowed standing

Fig 8 :50-52: "But shared atoms are not the signature." and "Convergent derivation and adopted
import look identical in the contents. Only the record of where each came from separates them"
Fig 10 :62: "A ruling carries provenance like an atom."
Fig 10 :65: "Provenance shows a ruling was reached legitimately. It never shows it was right: the
ground does that"

A title and a demonstrated judgment can look identical from the outside. What separates them is the
record of the route: which claim, which ground, which prediction, which date. Fig 10 :65 also limits
the claim: clean provenance shows legitimacy, not truth. Shapin's bibliography includes the volume
*Never Pure: Historical Studies of Science as if It Was Made by People with Bodies, Situated in Space,
Time, and Society, and Struggling for Credibility and Authority*, and Hartelius, quoted in Wikipedia
*Expert*, calls expertise "fundamentally contingent on a struggle for ownership and legitimacy", with
"attributed expertise... a performance that may or may not indicate genuine knowledge." Both name the
same seam: a performance and a record are different objects.

- **Mode:** `general` for the provenance requirement, `specified` for a given person's record.
- **Status:** triangulated (Fig 8 and Fig 10 are different figures; the world sources are separate pools).
- **Refuter:** show a case where a claim's origin record is absent yet a checker can still determine
  whether the claim was derived against the ground or merely adopted. Then provenance is not the
  discriminator.

### F7 — Standing is a stake; protect it and the ground is lost

Fig 10 :61: "And standing is a stake that survives the outcome it was earned on."
Fig 10 :93: "A ground with a reputation to protect is a ground someone benefits from, and its keeper
is thereby disqualified."
Fig 10 :59: "an interest in the outcome is unavoidable, an interest in the reading is disqualifying."
Fig 12 :119: "catalyst. A part that survives its own use. Spent and regenerating parts behave
differently across uses, and nothing records which a term is."

A title is a catalyst-like part: it survives the achievement it was earned on, and from the outside a
surviving part is indistinguishable from one that keeps regenerating by renewed contact. The figures
do not say standing is bad. They say it is a stake that must be declared, and that a checker who
protects a reputation has stopped being a checker. The essay states the same collapse: "someone let
prestige stand in for proof. Someone let reputation substitute for result." Fig 10 :47: "a convention
that decides an outcome has stopped being one."

A strain was recorded: read naively, Fig 10 :61 seems to say all earned authority is disqualifying.
Re-census shows it governs the **checker's** stake in the seat, not the legitimacy of the person
whose claim is being checked. Governing quantity: which value the ground returns today must not serve
the checker. The finding stands with that scope, not as a general condemnation of reputation.

- **Mode:** `general` for the stake rule, `specified` for any named checker.
- **Status:** triangulated (Fig 10, Fig 12, the prestige essay; three different framings).
- **Refuter:** exhibit a checker whose declared standing predicts the ground's answer in their own
  slot and who still produces correct refusals of their own prior claims. Then standing is not
  disqualifying in the way claimed.

### F8 — The answer is a test, not a name

Fig 9 :32: "you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself"
Fig 6 :23: "No frame escapes its own closure. The bound moves only on contact with a frame whose
closure differs."
Fig 13 :134: "Unreachable is a verdict here, not a silence,"

The figures return a shape: the evidence any legitimate authority claim must have. They do not return
a list of authorized people, and a search that finds no such person is not a proof that none exists
(Fig 8 :39: "So a search that finds nothing is never a proof of absence."). The practical test is a
source-discriminating instrument, not a further argument: pre-register a prediction in a slot, let it
pay or fail against a ground that does not move, keep the record, and use checkers whose evidence
pools do not overlap with the claim's.

- **Mode:** `general`.
- **Status:** single-source for the shape limit within the framework (Fig 9, Fig 6, Fig 13 agree and
  overlap heavily), corroborated by the essay's statement that a metatheory cannot validate itself.
- **Refuter:** produce from the figures a name or a ranked list of authoritative persons rather than
  a test. That would falsify the shape limit.

## 4. Pre-registered refuter

Written before the conclusion and before any outreach, per the seat rule that a falsifier written
before the run is a design instrument. The shape claim is refuted if any of the following holds:

1. A credential or title settles a claim in a slot where the holder's judgment has no contact with a
   non-updating ground, and the credential itself is not the question.
2. Authority is preserved after the supposed ground is shown to move with community opinion, that is
   the anchor fails Fig 2 :29-30.
3. A global authority score predicts ground settlement across unrelated slots without aggregating
   away the per-slot residual.
4. A checker whose standing is a declared stake still produces correct refusals of their own prior
   claims at the same rate as a checker with no such stake (breaks F7).
5. The figures yield a name or a ranked list of authoritative people rather than a test (breaks F8).

Until one of these is run, no part of this may be labelled `grounded`.

## 5. Recommendation

1. Repair the question's own framing before any outreach: "earn authority" is a relation to a ground,
   not the accumulation of credentials (F1 to F3).
2. Assess one slot at a time. Never aggregate a person into one authority number (F4).
3. Use independent checkers and record their evidence pools. Buy independence in proportion to the
   cost of being wrong (F5).
4. Record the route by which a judgment was reached. Treat the record as legitimacy only, never as
   truth (F6).
5. Declare standing as a stake. Do not let a ground with a reputation to protect sit in the checker
   seat (F7).
6. Send the test and the refuter, not the conclusion. Use the public venues in
   `03_public_contact_points.csv` and ask what would refute the shape claim (Fig 4 :22).

**Updated verdict.** The shape survives: authority beyond titles is a per-slot, provenance-bearing,
independently checkable record of contact with a ground that does not update, recognized
retrospectively, with standing declared as a stake. What does not survive is the common framing of
authority as a possession, a global score, or an anchor that can be created by agreement. Mode:
`specified`, ungrounded, candidate. No measurement moves it to `grounded`.
