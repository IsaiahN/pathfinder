# The Tether's answer: how experts earn authority beyond formal training and titles

**Mode: `specified`, not `grounded`.** This is a shape and a route, not a result. Simulated derivation
checked against the figures (`05_what_shape_means.md` §7), not a ground reading.

## 1. The residual it answers

Formal training and titles are third-party documents. They are evidence about what an institution
granted, not a settlement of any claim the holder later makes. The residual is the gap between the
standing a person is accorded and whether their judgments pay at a ground that does not move when
people change their minds. It is a **union compound**, not one hard question: it bundles a credential
(domain 1), a ground (domain 2), a community check (domain 3), and a lineage (domain 4). Fig 9 :56:
"it stops being a mixture and becomes a specific compound." Each domain keeps its own verdict.

## 2. The shape

1. **Credentials are frame-internal.** Fig 1 :27: "They are frame-internal. A frame cannot score
   itself with a quantity it also produces." A degree count or title list is a quantity the granting
   frame produces and then uses to score its own members. It cannot settle a claim. It can fund a
   trial (the essay: "Prestige can fund an action. It can give an action a budget to be tried.").
2. **The anchor is the ground, and it must not update.** Fig 2 :29: "An anchor is whatever settles the
   question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks." Reputation is the wrong
   kind of thing, because it is precisely what updates with opinion. Fig 2 :45: "Frames range against
   the ground. They do not negotiate with it."
3. **Recognition is retrospective.** Fig 11 :36: "Where nothing maintains anything, intelligence is a
   label applied afterwards to a lineage that met its ground." The word expert does no work before
   the ground has paid. What transfers between people is a method: Fig 4 :22 "Only methods cross up.
   Only head starts come down."
4. **It is per-slot.** Fig 1 :19: "The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated." A person
   is authoritative here and a layperson there. Fig 13 :34: "And within a kind the members are
   distinct too, which is what makes them separate frames."
5. **Agreement is not corroboration.** Fig 7 :35: "A room's agreement is variation, not a verdict.
   Selection between rooms is where the ground votes." Fig 2 :40: "disqualifying for a verdict:
   agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool."
   Independence is the parameter, and the cost of being wrong sets how much of it to buy (Fig 2 :42).
6. **Provenance separates earned from borrowed standing.** Fig 8 :51: "Convergent derivation and
   adopted import look identical in the contents. Only the record of where each came from separates
   them." But provenance is legitimacy, not truth: Fig 10 :65 "Provenance shows a ruling was reached
   legitimately. It never shows it was right: the ground does that".
7. **Standing is a declared stake.** Fig 10 :61: "And standing is a stake that survives the outcome
   it was earned on." Fig 10 :93: "A ground with a reputation to protect is a ground someone benefits
   from, and its keeper is thereby disqualified." A checker who protects a reputation has stopped
   being a checker.
8. **The framework returns a test, not a name.** Fig 9 :32: "you get a kind of thing, never the answer
   itself." It cannot rank people; it can say what the evidence must look like.

## 3. What it explicitly does not say

- Not that training and titles are worthless. They are a budget for a trial, not a settlement.
- Not that any named person is or is not an authority. No name is returned.
- Not that reputation is always false. It is a stake to be declared, not a verdict to be trusted.
- Not a global authority score. Per-slot only.
- Not that provenance establishes truth. It establishes legitimacy of route only.
- Not `grounded`. No measurement scores a prediction against a ground in this package.

## 4. What would settle it — the ground

A source-discriminating instrument, not a further argument. From *The Hard Problem is About Source*:
an instrument that reads green "cannot comment on the source"; the spectrometer resolved it. Here
the corresponding questions are:

- **Prediction test:** pre-register a judgment in one slot, then score it against a ground that does
  not move. Compare holders of the credential and non-holders who have a recorded lineage of ground
  contact. If the groups do not differ, F1/F3 fail.
- **Anchor test:** deliberately move community opinion while holding the ground fixed. If authority
  moves with opinion, the anchor requirement fails.
- **Independence test:** have two checkers range the same claim against the ground from different
  evidence pools. If agreement adds nothing over one pool, F5 is confirmed; if it does, F5 fails.
- **Stake test:** compare refusals of prior claims by checkers with and without declared standing.
  If rates are equal, F7 fails.
- Standing falsifiers from `01_figure_grounded_audit.md` §4: a credential that settles a claim with no
  ground contact; a global authority score that works across unrelated slots; the figures yielding a
  name instead of a test.

## 5. The honest stop

**Unreached, not unreachable.** The claims above are `specified` and candidate. No controlled
comparison was run, no person was named, and no ground reading was taken. Fig 8 :39: "So a search that
finds nothing is never a proof of absence." The answer stops at the limit of the available
instruments, and the lineage is recorded so the stop is checkable. The figures README:
"Indiscernibility is not identity."

## 6. One-paragraph version for outreach

> The Tether does not rank experts and does not say titles are worthless. It says that a credential is
> a third-party document and a frame-internal score, so it can fund a trial but cannot settle a claim.
> Authority beyond titles is a relation, not a possession: a judgment in one slot, a ground that does
> not move when opinion changes, checkers who do not share the claim's evidence pool, and a record of
> the route by which the judgment was reached. It is recognized afterwards, attached to a lineage,
> and indexed per slot. Standing is a stake that the checker must declare, and a ground with a
> reputation to protect is no longer a ground. What the framework offers is the shape any legitimate
> authority claim must have, the refuters written in advance, and the instrument that would close it:
> a pre-registered prediction scored against a fixed ground. The slot stays open until that happens.
