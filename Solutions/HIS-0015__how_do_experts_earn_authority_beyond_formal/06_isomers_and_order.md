# Isomers, catalysts and order: why authority is per-slot and why credentials are not ground contact

**Mode: `specified`.** This is a simulated derivation checked against the figures, not a ground
reading (see `05_what_shape_means.md` §7).

## RESIDUAL

Two things are being conflated in the question. A person can hold a credential (a document issued by
a third party) and separately have a record of judgments that paid at a ground. From the outside the
two produce the same reading: "this person is trusted". The supporting derivation owes: (1) whether
they are the same thing under the composition rules, (2) why authority must be indexed per slot, and
(3) what order the four domains must be assessed in.

## DOMAIN CENSUS

Atoms held in this derivation: a claim, a ground, a checker, a lineage, a credential. Bonds held:
conjunction, sequence, production, subtraction, comparison, negation. The question asks whether
`credential` and `ground-contact record` are the same molecule under a different name.

## DERIVATION

### Q1 — Is a title a ground-contact record under a different name?

Fig 12 :117: "isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible
in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."
Fig 12 :119: "catalyst. A part that survives its own use. Spent and regenerating parts behave
differently across uses, and nothing records which a term is."

A title is a catalyst-like part. It survives the achievement on which it was earned. From the outside
a surviving part and a regenerating part look identical, and "nothing records which a term is" unless
a provenance record is kept. The two are **not** isomers in the strict sense, because they do not
share the same atom set: a credential contains an issuer and a grant; a ground-contact record
contains a claim, a ground and a settlement. They share only the person they attach to, which is the
weaker **functional group** relation (Fig 12 :124): "functional group. A substructure whose presence
fixes the outcome. The group says what kind; the qualifier says which member."

The stronger isomer claim can be made only if both are rewritten over the full shared atom set
`{person, claim, ground, checker, lineage, credential}` and differ only in arrangement. That rewrite
is available as a test, not as a fact. As commonly written, a title and a demonstrated judgment are
different substances with a shared qualifier, not two spellings of one.

**Falsifier, written before this conclusion:** if a controlled comparison finds that a title predicts
ground settlement as well as a recorded history of predictions, in the same slot, with the title not
itself the question, then the two are behaviourally one substance and the isomer/functional-group
distinction fails.

### Q2 — Why authority is per-slot, not global

Fig 1 :19: "The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated."
Fig 13 :34: "And within a kind the members are distinct too, which is what makes them separate frames."

A person in two slots is two frames, because what each can express and what ground each ranges
against differ. Fig 13 :38-39: "independence is how little two frames draw on the same evidence, and
it is what makes their agreement mean anything. Six members that overlapped would be fewer members
wearing six names." The same applies to a career: a global authority number is one name worn over
several non-overlapping frames, and it throws away the per-slot signal.

Wikipedia *Expert* gives the world-side check: "A person may well be an expert in one field and a
layperson in many other fields." French and Raven give the boundary: "an expert in antique vases may
have little expertise in antique lamps" and "one's expertise is not everlasting."

**Falsifier:** produce a global authority measure that predicts ground outcomes in unrelated slots
without aggregating away the residual. If it works, the per-slot rule is wrong.

### Q3 — Order of the four domains

Fig 3 :20-21: "The chain is ordered by dependency, not by importance. Each link consumes what the one
above produces, so effort spent above the break arrives at a step that cannot use it."

The dependency order derived in `01_figure_grounded_audit.md` §1 is:

```
ground and residual  ->  agent's claim  ->  independent check  ->  recognized lineage  ->  credential
```

Fig 12 :106: "Which is why a system that cannot settle has one level however large its library.
Nothing composes over what was never admitted as a part."

- A claim cannot be checked before the ground that would settle it exists.
- A lineage cannot be recognized before checks have settled.
- A credential cannot be a legitimate record of authority before a lineage exists to record.

This is why adding credentials to a broken chain spends effort above the break (Fig 3 :19:
"Optimising a link that already works cannot rescue one that does not."). The credential link is not
a stronger link; it is a later one.

**Falsifier:** show a credential that reliably settles a claim in a slot before any ground contact or
lineage exists in that slot, with the credential not itself the question. Then the order is wrong and
the credential link is not downstream.

### Q4 — What kind of bond joins a claim to its checker?

Fig 12 :46: "A bond is the typed relation between two operands. There are seven, they are not
interchangeable, and which one holds is not recoverable from the operands."

The claim-checker relation is `⋛` comparison against a third term, the ground, not `≡` identity
between two people and not `+` conjunction of two agreeing opinions. Fig 12 :87: "is it about which
is larger, rather than both being present? Yes." The check asks how the claim stands relative to the
ground, not whether the checker and the claimant are both present and agree.

Fig 10 :42: "A verifier that can reconstruct the claim cannot verify it." If the checker could
produce the claim from their own library, the relation would collapse into identity and the surplus
would vanish. Fig 8 :45: "Two frames together reach what neither reaches alone."

**Falsifier:** show that a checker who can reconstruct the claim from their own model still produces
independent refusals at the same rate. Then the non-identity requirement is too strong.

### Q5 — Does a new ground instrument change the level or add a primitive?

Fig 12 :104: "A settled molecule becomes an atom for whatever composes over it. So irreducible is a
fact about a level and never about the world"

A pre-registered prediction that pays at a ground settles a molecule. That settled molecule becomes
an atom for the lineage at the next level. No new primitive is introduced. What changes is the level
at which irreducibility is assessed, not the set of atoms. This is the recursion the framework
names, and it is why the answer to "who is an authority" is always level-relative: authoritative at
one level is an atom for the next composition, not a final rank.

**Falsifier:** show a settled judgment that cannot serve as an atom for a later lineage, or a
ground-contact measurement that requires a seventh primitive beyond the ones already held.

## CONCLUSION

A title is a catalyst-like part that survives its own use; a ground-contact record is a regenerating
part. They are not the same substance, and only a provenance record discriminates them. Authority is
indexed per slot because distinct slots are distinct frames. The dependency order runs from ground to
claim to check to lineage to credential, so credentials cannot repair a break upstream. The
claim-checker relation is a comparison against a ground, not an identity or a conjunction, and a
settled judgment becomes an atom for the next level rather than a new primitive.

This rescues the part of the question that is answerable without naming anyone: the shape any
legitimate authority claim must have. It does not produce a ranked list of experts, which the
framework explicitly cannot return (Fig 9 :32).
