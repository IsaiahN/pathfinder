# Outreach draft - validate the separator first, then decide about the framework

**Use:** one to one, or as a cover letter attached to a submission through the public venues in `03_public_contact_points.csv`. **Not** for bulk send.

**Hard precondition:** do not send until the stage-1 claim below survives the refuter you write for yourself. The output must survive validation or the two-stage offer collapses on contact.

---

**Subject:** Check one separator about ecosystem service recovery before you decide whether the theory is worth reading

> Hi [name],
>
> I have one specific output I'd like you to check against what you already know about [their service or system]. I am not asking you to read a theory first, and I am not asking you to agree with it. If the output does not survive, there is nothing else to look at.
>
> **The output (`specified`, not `grounded`):** "can this service recover" is not answered by a species count or by habitat area alone. The residual belongs to a service flow, and it must be read per service, never pooled into an ecosystem-health average. What governs the return is a union of conditions: (1) the contact between the remaining assemblage and the habitat, enumerated outward including second- and third-order relations; (2) whether the measured flow at the beneficiary is a ground that does not move when the manager changes their mind; (3) whether there is an instrument for what "recovered" means in that service's own terms; and (4) if an actor must be introduced, whether it arrived with provenance and a shadow test. Recovery is ordered, not reversible: you can add an actor, you cannot subtract the habitat, and moving the service to another site substitutes a habitat and inherits actors you did not choose. A managed service is permanent work, not a solved state.
>
> **What I am not claiming:** I am not naming a species, a redundancy threshold, or a recovery time. The framework returns a shape, not a name: it narrows what any candidate answer must satisfy and stops where the instruments stop. Which actor fills a slot is a binding from your field onto a held category, with provenance, not a derivation from the figures. If several paths produce the same recovery reading, the reading does not tell you which path produced it.
>
> **What would refute the separator:**
> 1. a service that reliably recovers with the actor set and the habitat contact both unchanged;
> 2. a first-order contact enumeration that predicts recovery for every service at every scale, with no second- or third-order relation carrying the outcome;
> 3. an actor whose valence is fixed across two different service residuals;
> 4. a relocated or synthetic service that reproduces every condition its goal required and inherits no unchosen actor, checked against the ground over the relevant lag;
> 5. a recovery decision that changes when the responsible agency changes its stated preference, with identical flow readings;
> 6. a pooled ecosystem index that predicts each individual service's recovery with no live residual on any one slot.
>
> I wrote these down before sending this. If any one holds, I will retract the corresponding claim. A one-line "this fails because..." is more useful to me than agreement. If you'd rather not engage, no reply is needed.
>
> **If it survives, and only then:** the sources are below. The figures, not my summary of them, are the authority.
> - Canonical figure text extract (local framework file): `FRAMEWORK/reference/03-FIGURES_TEXT.md`, cited as `Fig N :line`.
> - Canonical usage rule (local framework file): `FRAMEWORK/reference/FIGURES_README_canonical.md`.
> - Grounded comparison from your own literature: Eppinga et al. 2025, https://doi.org/10.1098/rspb.2025.1744 ; Bahlai and Landis 2026, https://doi.org/10.1093/pnasnexus/pgag063 ; Mori, Furukawa and Sasaki 2013, https://doi.org/10.1111/brv.12004 .
>
> **If you want to use the machinery:** the formula is in the framework files. Run it on a service you know well and can check against a flow you can measure. Write down what would refute your result *before* you run it, and state the mode of what you get. A reported failure is more useful to me than a reported success.
>
> Thanks,
> [name]
