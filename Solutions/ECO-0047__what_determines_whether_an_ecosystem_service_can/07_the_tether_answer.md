## 1. The residual it answers

A service is a slot s (pollination, water purification, nutrient cycling, pest suppression, carbon storage). Before a perturbation the ecological frame predicts a flow Γ(b,a); the world returns o′; R = |Γ(b,a) − o′| is indexed per slot and never pooled (Fig 1 :19 *"The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated."*; Fig 1 :22 *"Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average."*). "Can it recover" asks whether R for that slot returns toward zero and stays there, read at a ground that does not move because a manager wants it to (Fig 2 :29 *"An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks."*). Species loss and habitat degradation are perturbations to the actors, conditions and relations in contact with that slot. The residual is a union of conditions with an unassignable shared fraction, not a two-option partition (Fig 9 :55 *"A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones."*).

## 3. What this explicitly does not say

- Not a species name, a count, a density or a number of years. Those are bindings from an ecological frame onto held categories, with provenance and a shadow test; they are not derived from the figures.
- Not a recovery percentage. Verdicts stay per service slot and are never averaged into an ecosystem-health index (Fig 1 :22).
- Not "restore by reversing the degradation." Isolation is substitution; the new habitat's actors are inherited, not chosen.
- Not "recovery is a solved state once the metric returns." Managed recovery is permanent work, and a returning metric may be a stale or saturated channel.
- Not a claim that the framework is the only account of ecosystem recovery, and not `grounded`. No measurement was taken by this derivation.
- Not a claim that a synthetic or mesocosm success establishes field capability.

## 4. Falsifiers, written before the conclusion

From `01_figure_grounded_audit.md` section 4. Each is a direct test of a separator condition.

1. A service recovers reliably with the actor set and the habitat contact both unchanged, in a case where the pre-perturbation prediction was wrong. Breaks condition 1.
2. A first-order contact enumeration predicts recovery for every service at every scale, with no second- or third-order relation carrying the outcome. Breaks condition 2.
3. An actor's valence is demonstrably fixed across two different service residuals. Breaks condition 3.
4. A recovery decision is invariant under a change in the responsible agency's stated preference, with identical flow readings and channel. Breaks condition 4.
5. A managed site attains a permanently recovered state with no further maintenance of the contact that delivered the service. Breaks condition 5.
6. A managed site removes an unwanted actor or subtracts a habitat relation as cleanly as it adds one. Breaks condition 6.
7. A relocated or synthetic service reproduces every condition its goal required and inherits no unchosen actor, verified against the ground over the relevant lag. Breaks condition 6.
8. A pooled index predicts each slot's recovery with no live residual on any one slot. Breaks the per-slot rule.
9. A species list fully specifies the delivered flow, with no bond, gradient or network relation carrying information the list lacks. Breaks the structural reading in `06_isomers_and_order.md`.

If any fires, the corresponding claim is withdrawn. Until one is run, nothing here is `grounded`.

## 5. What would settle it

A source-discriminating instrument, not a further argument. The world-side instance is Eppinga et al. 2025: *"biodiversity-dependent ecosystem service debts may drive initial phases of apparent recovery after disturbance, still followed by system collapse"* and *"social-ecological systems' safe operating spaces cannot be reliably inferred from recent observations of apparent system stability"*. What is owed for a slot:

- an instrument for the flow at the beneficiary over a stated lag that does not update on the manager's preference;
- the capacity to discriminate among sources of the same reading (restored contact, dilution, moved sampling point, saturated metric), because several paths can produce one green light;
- an enumeration of the habitat outward far enough that the second- and third-order relations are on the record;
- a shadow test for any actor introduced, with provenance.

See `08_expert_verification_packet.md` for the twenty-minute expert check and `10_source_ledger.csv` for the sources read.

## 7. One-paragraph version

> The Tether does not name the species that make an ecosystem service recover, and it does not give a recovery time. It says that the answer must be a separator read per service, never pooled into an ecosystem-health average: capability belongs to the assemblage and the habitat in contact, the habitat is enumerated outward until the cascade stops mattering, which actor helps depends on which service residual is named, the flow at the beneficiary is the ground and must be read through a channel that can fail, the objective needs an instrument before "recovered" can be said, a managed site is permanent work rather than a solved state, and you can add an actor but cannot subtract the habitat, while moving the service substitutes a habitat and inherits actors you did not choose. The framework returns this shape, not a name; the species list is a `gap` reported honestly. It offers falsifiers written in advance and the instrument that would close the gap. The verdict stays open until the ground settles it.
