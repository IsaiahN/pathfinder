# Expert verification packet: the Tether on ecosystem service recovery

**What this is:** a separator in a form an ecosystem-service ecologist can check against a system they know. Mode, provenance and refuters are stated before the conclusion, so a reader knows what to look for and what would settle it.

**Mode: `specified` on the separator and the per-domain verdicts, `general` on the structural shape, `gap` on a species list, a redundancy count or a recovery time. Not `grounded`.** No ecological measurement was taken by this derivation. It is a simulated derivation checked for internal consistency against the figures, not a ground reading.

## 1. The claim, in one paragraph

"Can this service recover" is not answered by a species count or by habitat area alone. The residual belongs to a service flow and must be read per service, never pooled into an ecosystem-health average. What governs the return is a union of conditions: the contact between the remaining assemblage and the habitat, enumerated outward including second- and third-order relations; the delivered flow at the beneficiary as a ground that does not move when a manager changes their mind; an instrument for what "recovered" means in that service's own terms; and, if an actor must be added, provenance and a shadow test for the import. Actor valence is relative to the named service, so there is no standing good-species list. Recovery is ordered rather than reversible: you can add an actor, you cannot subtract the habitat, and moving the service to another site substitutes a habitat and inherits actors you did not choose. A managed service is permanent work, not a solved state. The framework returns this shape, not a name, and stops where the instruments stop.

## 2. Three checkable sub-claims

### C1: the determinant is assemblage and habitat contact, not the species list

- **Statement.** A service's capacity to recover is a property of the assemblage and the habitat it is in contact with. The habitat is enumerated outward, and the controlling relation can be several steps out. A count or a trait list is an ingredient list, not the delivered flow.
- **Source, verbatim.** Fig 11 :39 *"So the answerable question is not what intelligence is, but what makes a system capable. And capability is a property of agent-and-habitat, never of the agent alone."* Fig 11 :40 *"A system cannot be made more capable by improving the agent in isolation. An improvement that does not change contact changes nothing, however much it improves."* Fig 11 :43 *"The habitat is enumerated, never composed."* Fig 11 :44 *"List everything in contact with the residual, then what is in contact with those, and outward until the cascade stops mattering."* Fig 11 :52-53 *"And contact cascades. Scoping the habitat to direct contact is how DDT reached the eagles: first-order enumeration, second-order effect, and the residual that mattered was three steps out."*
- **What to check.** In a service you know, is recovery tracked by the count and the direct contacts, or by a relation three steps out? Does a count restoration with unchanged contact fail to move the measured flow?
- **Refuter.** A service that recovers reliably with the actor set and the habitat contact both unchanged in a case where the pre-perturbation prediction was wrong; or a complete first-order enumeration that predicts recovery for every service at every scale. If either holds, C1 is withdrawn.

### C2: the reading needs a non-updating ground and an objective instrument

- **Statement.** The anchor is the delivered flow at the beneficiary, read through a channel that can decay. "Recovered" is a comparison against a reference flow in the service's own terms. Most monitoring breaks at the objective link, not at perception, because that link has the fewest instruments. Verdicts stay per slot.
- **Source, verbatim.** Fig 2 :29 *"An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks."* Fig 10 :24 *"the ground does not decay, the channel does"* and Fig 10 :26 *"saturated metrics, pooled readings"*. Fig 1 :19 *"The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated."* and Fig 1 :22 *"Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average."* Fig 3 :19 *"Optimising a link that already works cannot rescue one that does not."* and Fig 3 :27 *"A link with no instrument is not a link that works. It is a link nobody has looked at."*
- **What to check.** Does the site have a non-updating measure of the service flow over a stated lag, or only an index that can saturate or pool? When the metric returns, can you say which path produced the return?
- **Refuter.** A recovery decision invariant under a change in the responsible agency's stated preference with identical flow readings and channel; or a pooled ecosystem index that predicts each slot's recovery with no live residual on any one slot. If either holds, C2 is withdrawn.

### C3: valence is relative, introduction is asymmetric, isolation substitutes

- **Statement.** Which actor helps depends on which service residual is named, so no standing good-actor list survives a residual change. A seat can add an actor but cannot subtract the habitat; an import needs provenance and a shadow test because a wrong atom is costly. Relocation, ex-situ propagation and test harnesses are substituted habitats, not removals, and they inherit unchosen actors. Managed recovery is permanent work.
- **Source, verbatim.** Fig 11 :49 *"An actor's valence is relative to the residual, not a property it carries."* and Fig 11 :51 *"No standing list of good actors survives a change of residual."* Fig 11 :61-62 *"The seat's power is asymmetric. Introduction is available; exclusion is not. You can add an actor and you cannot subtract the habitat, because contact is a fact about the world and does not consult you."* Fig 6 :24 *"A wrong atom costs more than starting from primitives."* Fig 11 :66 *"Isolation is not removal of the habitat. It is substitution of one habitat for another."* Fig 11 :70 *"A test harness is a substituted habitat. Which is why a synthetic solve proves wiring and never capability."* Fig 11 :63 *"Which is why a managed ecosystem is permanent work rather than a solved state."*
- **What to check.** Does the same species help one service and harm another at your site? When an actor was introduced, was the cascade enumerated first, and was there a source gate and a shadow test? Did a restoration plot inherit conditions or actors it did not choose?
- **Refuter.** An actor whose valence is fixed across two service residuals; a managed site that removes an unwanted actor or subtracts a habitat relation as cleanly as it adds one; a relocated or synthetic service that reproduces every condition its goal required and inherits no unchosen actor over the relevant lag; or a managed site that attains a permanently recovered state with no further maintenance. If any holds, C3 is withdrawn.

## 3. What is explicitly not claimed

- Not a species name, a density, a redundancy threshold or a recovery time. Those are bindings from an ecological frame onto held categories, with provenance, not derivations from the figures.
- Not a recovery percentage, and not a pooled ecosystem-health verdict.
- Not "restore by reversing the degradation." Isolation is substitution, not removal.
- Not "recovery is a solved state once the metric returns." A returning metric may be a saturated or stale channel.
- Not a claim that the framework is the only account of ecosystem recovery, and not a claim that ecology is foreign to it (Fig 11 itself discusses DDT, Carson, ecosystem engineering and managed ecosystems).
- Not `grounded`. Nothing here is asserted as settled by measurement.

## 5. What would settle it

A source-discriminating instrument, not more agreement. For a named service slot the following would close the open part: a flow at the beneficiary over a stated lag read by something that does not update on the manager's preference; the ability to distinguish restored contact from dilution, a moved sampling point or a saturated metric when the reading returns; and a shadow test for any actor introduced. The world-side warning is Eppinga et al. 2025: *"biodiversity-dependent ecosystem service debts may drive initial phases of apparent recovery after disturbance, still followed by system collapse"*. Agreement with this packet is not such an instrument.

## 6. What an expert can check in twenty minutes

1. Read Fig 11 :39-40, :43-44, :49-51 and Fig 2 :29, Fig 10 :24. Six short passages.
2. Take one service you know. Name the flow, the beneficiary and the lag. Say whether an instrument for "recovered" exists, or whether only a count or an index exists.
3. Test C1: is there a second- or third-order relation that carries the outcome and that a direct-contact enumeration would miss?
4. Test C2: does your recovery reading change if the agency's stated preference changes? Can you name the source of a returning metric?
5. Test C3: name one actor whose valence flips between two services, and one introduction where the cascade was not enumerated first.
6. Send a refutation, not an endorsement. A failure is more useful than agreement, and stage-2 material is only sent after the stage-1 verdict is recorded.
