## 0. The residual in effect terms

A service is a slot s. Before a perturbation the frame's library Γ predicts a delivered flow Γ(b,a); the world returns the observed flow o′; R = |Γ(b,a) − o′| is measured per slot, never pooled (Fig 1 :19, Fig 1 :22). Species loss and habitat degradation are perturbations that change the habitat's actors, conditions and relations (Fig 11 :45-47). "Recovery" is the claim that R for a named slot trends back toward zero and stays there, read at a ground that does not move because a manager wants it to (Fig 2 :29).

Two distractors must be named before the census, because both are frame-internal readings of the same quantity:

- **Species richness as a proxy for the flow.** A count is an ingredient list. It is not the delivered service (Fig 12 :5, Fig 12 :111).
- **An apparent return of the metric** while the channel is stale, saturated or pooled (Fig 10 :24, Fig 10 :26). "Recovered" said by the same seat that introduced the actors is a verdict by a frame about itself, not a reading of the ground.

The residual is therefore **a union of conditions with an unassignable shared fraction** (Fig 9 :54-57), not a two-option partition. One lever clears the region where only one condition holds and leaves the intersections standing.

## 1. Domain census, before choosing any figure line

R by arity, symmetry and scale, then one line per domain with actors, currency and anchor. A candidate with no nameable anchor is not a domain yet.

- **R (arity / symmetry / scale).** Arity: several overlapping conditions (contact, ground reading, objective, seat, import provenance), so a union. Symmetry: broken. Recovery is ordered and effectively non-invertible: addition of an actor differs from subtraction of the habitat (Fig 11 :61-62), and a substituted habitat is not the removed one (Fig 11 :66). Scale: per service-slot at first order (Fig 1 :19), with second- and third-order contact cascades (Fig 11 :52-53).

| domain | actors | currency | anchor | question type |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| D1 Habitat and contact | remaining species, conditions (light, water, season, terrain), relations (pollination, predation, competition, dependency) | contact and the service flow | the measured flow at the beneficiary (does not update) | enumerate the habitat, never compose (Fig 11 :43-48) |
| D2 Reading and objective | monitor, metric, "what counts as recovered" | observation versus prediction | the ground, reached through a channel that can decay (Fig 10 :24) | dependency chain, break between objective and planning (Fig 3 :19, :27) |
| D3 Maintenance and import | manager seat, introduced actors, instruments, provenance | actions, source gate, shadow test | the ground, never the manager's opinion | two modes, unmaintained versus maintained; introduction not exclusion (Fig 11 :55-65; Fig 6 :23) |
| D4 The specific list | (candidate species, counts, recovery times) | none bound | none nameable | **not a domain yet**; no figure line returns a name (Fig 9 :32, Fig 13 :135) |

**Partition or union.** D1 through D3 form a union of overlapping conditions, not a partition (Fig 9 :55-57). A robust service can fail on the reading link alone; a damaged habitat can be unrecoverable even with a perfect reading; a maintained site can fail by an unenumerated introduction. D4 is excluded from the union as `gap`, because it has no anchor and no line (Fig 11 :48: what cannot be measured is the residual).

**Dependency order (Fig 3 :20).** Describe R in effect terms (Fig 9 :27-28) -> enumerate the habitat outward until the cascade stops (Fig 11 :44) -> check that the objective has an instrument (Fig 3 :27) -> refuse before minting a new actor (Formula step 2) -> import with provenance and shadow test if an atom is genuinely absent (Fig 6 :23, Fig 8 :53) -> settle at the ground (Fig 12). Effort spent above a broken link arrives at a step that cannot use it (Fig 3 :19).

## 3. Findings F1 to F12

### F1 - Capability is agent-and-habitat, not the species list (decisive against Option A)

Fig 11 :39-40:

> **"So the answerable question is not what intelligence is, but what makes a system capable. And capability is a property of agent-and-habitat, never of the agent alone."**
> **"A system cannot be made more capable by improving the agent in isolation. An improvement that does not change contact changes nothing, however much it improves."**

A service's capacity to recover is a property of the assemblage *and* the habitat it is in contact with. Restoring a species count while leaving the contact absent changes nothing, however large the count. This is the same move as separating lift from flapping: *"The lift move is Cayley's, 1799: separating the forces from the flapping is what made powered flight a problem anyone could work on."* (Fig 11 :42).

Mode `specified`; triangulated (Fig 11 plus Fig 1 :19, since the residual is per slot and the slot is a service, not a species). **Refuter:** a service that reliably recovers with the actor set and the habitat contact both unchanged, in a case where the pre-perturbation prediction was wrong. That breaks the contact claim.

### F2 - The habitat is enumerated, never composed, and the cascade runs outward

Fig 11 :43-44:

> **"The habitat is enumerated, never composed."**
> **"List everything in contact with the residual, then what is in contact with those, and outward until the cascade stops mattering."**

Fig 11 :45-48:

> **"actors: what acts on it, and what it acts on"**
> **"conditions: light, water, temperature, season, terrain"**
> **"relations: pollination, predation, competition, dependency"**
> **"You do not invent the list. You read it off the world, and what you cannot perceive or measure yet is the residual."**

The list for a recovering service is not derived from the species present at the point of damage. It is read off the world outward. Fig 11 :52-54 makes the scale point decisive:

> **"And contact cascades. Scoping the habitat to direct contact is how DDT reached the eagles: first-order enumeration, second-order effect, and the residual that mattered was three steps out."**

Habitat degradation that looks local can have the controlling relation three steps out. A restoration scoped to direct contact is a first-order reading of a third-order problem.

Mode `specified`; triangulated (Fig 11 enumeration plus Fig 1 :19-22 per-slot indexing). **Refuter:** a case where a complete first-order contact enumeration predicts recovery for all services at all scales, with no second-order relation carrying the outcome.

### F3 - There is no standing list of recovery-promoting species

Fig 11 :49-51:

> **"An actor's valence is relative to the residual, not a property it carries."**
> **"The same actor is a benefactor to one residual and a malfactor to another, so mutualism and parasitism are relations rather than kinds."**
> **"No standing list of good actors survives a change of residual."**

Which actor helps depends on which service (which residual) is being recovered. An introduced predator is a benefactor to pest suppression and a malfactor to pollination if it also visits flowers. The question "which species determines recovery" is answered only after the residual is named; it is not a property the species carries. This is the figure's own statement of Fig 9 :27-28: *"describe it before you go looking, or any frame you pick will seem to fit."*

Mode `specified`; triangulated (Fig 11 :49-51 plus Fig 9 :27-28, low overlap between habitat enumeration and residual-first lookup). **Refuter:** an actor whose valence is demonstrably fixed across two different service residuals.

### F4 - Two modes: unmaintained selection, maintained garden

Fig 11 :55-59:

> **"Two modes, and only one of them has a seat."**
> **"Unmaintained. No maintainer and no live frame in the seat. The habitat is pure contact, nothing selects, and what survives is what the ground kept."**
> **"Maintained. A seat exists and can act: choose the instruments, distil the impactful actors, introduce what was not there. That is the garden, and it is how arrival is worked on rather than waited for."**

Fig 11 :63:

> **"Which is why a managed ecosystem is permanent work rather than a solved state."**

So the determinant changes with the mode. In unmaintained systems, recovery is whatever the ground kept; no one is choosing. In maintained systems, recovery is worked on by a seat that enumerates, ranks by cascade, introduces actors and reads the ground. "Recovered" as a solved state is a category error: the maintenance is the thing that persists. The world literature corroborates the shape with a direct instance: Bahlai and Landis report that *"Habitat perenniality moderates these patterns, with communities in annual crops exhibiting large changes and those in perennial crops following similar but less pronounced trajectories, while communities in forested habitats remain stable or exhibit gradual, nearly linear declines."* (see `10_source_ledger.csv`).

Mode `general` on the two-mode split, `specified` on which mode a given site is in. Triangulated (Fig 11 plus Fig 7 :35-36, because a maintained site that repeats the same room is a mirror chain). **Refuter:** a managed site that attains a permanently recovered state with no further maintenance of the contact that delivered the service.

### F5 - The seat's power is asymmetric: introduction yes, exclusion no

Fig 11 :61-65:

> **"The seat's power is asymmetric. Introduction is available; exclusion is not."**
> **"You can add an actor and you cannot subtract the habitat, because contact is a fact about the world and does not consult you."**
> **"Introducing an actor for its cascade is ecosystem engineering, after Jones, Lawton and Shachak, 1994. Cane toads and kudzu are the same move with the cascade unenumerated."**

Introduction is an import, and Fig 6 :23: *"No frame escapes its own closure. The bound moves only on contact with a frame whose closure differs."* The source gate and shadow test are required (Fig 8 :50-52), and a wrong atom is expensive: Fig 6 :24, *"A wrong atom costs more than starting from primitives."* Restoration by adding an actor whose cascade was not enumerated is the cane-toad/kudzu move, not a repair.

Mode `specified`; triangulated (Fig 11 asymmetry plus Fig 6 :23-24 import price). **Refuter:** a managed site that removes an unwanted actor or subtracts a habitat relation as cleanly as it adds one, with the contact genuinely reduced rather than substituted.

### F6 - Isolation is substitution, not removal

Fig 11 :66-70:

> **"Isolation is not removal of the habitat. It is substitution of one habitat for another."**
> **"Move the residual and you must reproduce everything its goal required, and you inherit the new habitat's actors, which you did not choose."**
> **"what you failed to reproduce is invisible until the goal fails"**
> **"what you unintentionally introduced is invisible until it acts"**
> **"A test harness is a substituted habitat. Which is why a synthetic solve proves wiring and never capability."**

Translocation, ex-situ propagation, a restored plot at a different site, or a laboratory demonstration all substitute a habitat. Two failure modes are silent: the missing condition shows up only when the goal fails, and the unchosen actor shows up only when it acts. A synthetic or mesocosm "recovery" proves wiring, never the capability of the original service in its habitat. Eppinga et al. 2025 supply the matching warning at the social-ecological scale: *"biodiversity-dependent ecosystem service debts may drive initial phases of apparent recovery after disturbance, still followed by system collapse"* (`10_source_ledger.csv`).

Mode `specified`; triangulated (Fig 11 substitution plus Fig 10 :24 channel decay). **Refuter:** a relocated service that reproduces every condition its goal required and inherits no unchosen actor, verified against the ground over the relevant time lag.

### F7 - Recovery is indexed per service, never averaged

Fig 1 :19:

> **"The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated."**

Fig 1 :22:

> **"Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average."**

An index of "ecosystem health" that pools pollination, water quality, carbon storage and pest suppression can go up while one slot's flow is still far from the ground. This is the trade-off phenomenon the contact venues study, read as a figure rule: a gain in one slot is a different bond, not an addition to a common stock (Fig 12 :125-127). Bahlai and Landis 2026 report that *"these gross trends mask apparent periods of stability, recovery, and, in some cases, very sharp declines lasting many years across individual species and groups"* (`10_source_ledger.csv`), which is the same per-slot warning in empirical form.

Mode `specified`; triangulated (Fig 1 plus Fig 12 :125-127 two currencies). **Refuter:** a pooled index that predicts each individual service's recovery with no residual on any one slot.

### F8 - The anchor is the delivered flow, not the manager's reading of it

Fig 2 :29:

> **"An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks."**

Fig 10 :24, :26:

> **"the ground does not decay, the channel does"**
> **"stale percepts, dropped transients, saturated metrics, pooled readings"**

Fig 10 :28: **"you have lost touch"** is the correct output when the channel decays, not a verdict that the service recovered or failed. A "recovered" label produced by the seat that introduced the actors is a mutual-update collapse (Fig 2 :34-35): the anchor has become another frame. Eppinga et al. 2025: *"social-ecological systems' safe operating spaces cannot be reliably inferred from recent observations of apparent system stability"* (`10_source_ledger.csv`).

Mode `specified`; triangulated (Fig 2 plus Fig 10, different figures, low overlap). **Refuter:** a recovery decision that is invariant when the responsible agency's stated preference changes, with the same flow readings and the same channel.

### F9 - The chain usually breaks at the objective, not at perception

Fig 3 :19:

> **"Optimising a link that already works cannot rescue one that does not."**

Fig 3 :27:

> **"A link with no instrument is not a link that works. It is a link nobody has looked at."**

Fig 3 :29-30:

> **"Before asking whether a system is good, ask which link it currently stops at, and whether the answer was measured or assumed."**

Most monitoring effort lands where instruments already exist: species counts and habitat area (perception and vocabulary). "Recovered" needs an objective stated in the service's own terms: which flow, at which beneficiary, over which lag. Without that instrument the chain stops between objective and planning, and a reading taken below the break is *"a reading of nothing"* (Fig 3 :23). This is the structural reason the ecosystem-service trade-off literature exists: one service's gain is another's loss, and a single metric cannot state both objectives.

Mode `general`; triangulated (Fig 3 plus Fig 1 per-slot, plus Fig 12 :52 on a goal being a comparison). **Refuter:** a site with a calibrated, non-updating measure of "recovered" for every service slot that still cannot say which link its chain stops at.

### F10 - The question is a union, not a partition

Fig 9 :55:

> **"A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones."**

Fig 9 :57-58:

> **"What remains is smaller and it is also categorically different: a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either."**

A service with lost habitat contact *and* a saturated recovery metric is not a harder case of either; it is a specific compound. A single lever (say, restocking a pollinator) clears the region where only the actor condition holds and leaves the intersection with the broken reading standing. Verdicts are kept per domain and never averaged into a recovery percentage (Fig 9 :60: *"Use filters for the budget and witnesses for the verdict. Never let a filter hand you a verdict."*).

Mode `general` on the union, `specified` per domain. Triangulated (Fig 9 plus Fig 11 :61 asymmetry, different objects). **Refuter:** a single lever that closes R on every slot of a case in two conditions, with no residual left on either.

### F12 - Structure: a service is a flow over a network, not an ingredient list

Fig 13 :76-77:

> **"Energy and a gradient compose a flow. A gradient over space and one over time are different terms, because which state it is bonded to is part of what it is."**

Fig 13 :24: **"that entities relate, and influence travels"** (the network structure). Fig 12 :111: **"A vocabulary without bonds can name a state and cannot name a route to one."** A species list names what a situation contains; it cannot name the route by which a service is delivered or restored. Fig 12 :119 adds the catalyst distinction: **"catalyst. A part that survives its own use. Spent and regenerating parts behave differently across uses, and nothing records which a term is."** A pollinator population that regenerates and a one-off introduced pollinator that is spent behave differently across seasons, and most monitoring records only the count. `06_isomers_and_order.md` develops the isomer and functional-group reading: two services over the same actor set differ by bond, not by ingredients (Fig 12 :117, :123).

Mode `specified`; triangulated for the flow claim (Fig 13 plus Fig 12 :111). **Refuter:** a service whose delivered flow is fully specified by its species list, with no network relation, gradient or bond carrying information the list lacks.

## 4. Pre-registered refuter

Written before the conclusion and before any outreach, per Seat Map :287: *"a falsifier written before the run is a design instrument, not only an honesty device."*

The separator is refuted if any of the following holds:

1. A service reliably recovers with the actor set and the habitat contact both unchanged, in a case where the pre-perturbation prediction was wrong (breaks F1, Fig 11 :39-40).
2. A first-order contact enumeration predicts recovery for every service at every scale, with no second- or third-order relation carrying the outcome (breaks F2, Fig 11 :52-53).
3. An actor's valence is fixed across two different service residuals (breaks F3, Fig 11 :49-51).
4. A relocated or synthetic service reproduces every condition its goal required and inherits no unchosen actor, verified against the ground over the relevant lag (breaks F6, Fig 11 :66-70).
5. A recovery decision is invariant under a change in the responsible agency's stated preference, with identical flow readings and channel (breaks F8, Fig 2 :29).
6. A pooled ecosystem-health index predicts each slot's recovery with no live residual on any one slot (breaks F7, Fig 1 :22).
7. A species list fully specifies the delivered flow, with no bond or network relation needed (breaks F12, Fig 12 :111).

If any fires, the corresponding claim is withdrawn. Until one is run, nothing here is `grounded`.

## 5. Recommendation

1. Lead with the separator, not with a species list. The species list is a `gap`; asking for it before the residual is named guarantees that any frame picked will seem to fit (Fig 9 :27-28).
2. State the mode on every claim (F4, F8, F11) and keep the verdicts per domain (F10).
3. Put the enumeration before the intervention. Enumerate habitat outward, then rank by cascade, then introduce with provenance and a shadow test (Formula step 7, Fig 11 :58, Fig 8 :53).
4. Build the objective instrument before judging recovery (F9). A metric that saturated or pooled is a lost channel, not a verdict (Fig 10 :26-28).
5. Figure 4 :25: *"A recording carried upward looks like knowledge and is a description of one occasion."* Send the separator and its refuter, not a case-study success story.
6. Route the packet through the public opt-in venues in `03_public_contact_points.csv`, especially the ecosystem-service trade-off and restoration working groups. Ask for a refutation, not belief.

### Seven-question recursive derivation (summary)

1. **What is the slot?** A service flow, not a species count. Fig 1 :19, Fig 11 :73.
2. **What determines its return?** Contact between assemblage and habitat, not the agent alone. Fig 11 :39-40.
3. **How far out does the habitat go?** Until the cascade stops mattering; DDT was three steps out. Fig 11 :44, :52-53.
4. **Which actors help?** Relative to the named residual; no standing list. Fig 11 :49-51.
5. **Who decides recovery?** The ground, through a channel that can decay; not the managing frame. Fig 2 :29, Fig 10 :24.
6. **Can a missing actor be added?** Yes, by import with source gate and shadow test; exclusion is not available, and a wrong atom is costly. Fig 11 :61-62; Fig 6 :23-24; Fig 8 :50-52.
7. **What closes the residual?** Nothing in the figure set names a species, count or time; report `gap` and the instrument that would close it. Fig 9 :32, Fig 13 :134-135.

### Updated verdict

The separator survives: recovery is a property of assemblage and habitat, indexed per service slot, read at a non-updating ground, with the habitat enumerated outward, valence relative to the residual, two modes, asymmetric introduction, and substitution rather than removal under isolation. The species-list answer does not survive, and neither does a recovery percentage or a standing good-actor list. Strained after re-census: **none**; one `gap` (the specific list) and one live type question (what instrument counts as the service-flow ground at a given lag), both named rather than hidden.
