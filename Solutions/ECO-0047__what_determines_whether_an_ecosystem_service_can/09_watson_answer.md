## QUESTION

Decide, in effect terms, what determines whether an ecosystem service can recover after species loss or habitat degradation, and record what the framework cannot return. Option A: a determinant is the species that remain, expressible as a count, a trait list or a redundancy threshold, with a recovery time. Option B: the determinant is a separator read per service flow, with the habitat enumerated outward, the reading anchored at a non-updating ground, valence relative to the named service, two modes, asymmetric introduction and substitution under isolation; the specific list is a `gap`. A sends an ingredient list as if it were a delivered flow and spends the independence of the people checking it. B sends a checkable shape, refuters written in advance, and an instrument specification where the list would be.

## DOMAIN CENSUS

R by arity, symmetry and scale, then one line per domain with actors / currency / anchor, partition or union, and order by dependency.

- **R (the residual).** Before a perturbation Γ(b,a) predicts a service flow; the world returns o′; R = |Γ(b,a) − o′|. Arity: several overlapping conditions (contact, ground reading, objective, seat, import provenance), so a **union**, not a partition (Fig 9 :55 "A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones."). Symmetry: broken. Recovery is ordered and effectively non-invertible; adding an actor differs from subtracting the habitat. Scale: per service slot at first order, with second- and third-order contact cascades.
- **D1 Habitat and contact.** Actors: remaining species, conditions (light, water, season, terrain), relations (pollination, predation, competition, dependency). Currency: contact and the service flow. Anchor: the measured flow at the beneficiary, which does not update. Question type: enumerate the habitat outward, never compose it.
- **D2 Reading and objective.** Actors: monitor, metric, the stated meaning of "recovered". Currency: observation against prediction. Anchor: the ground through a channel that can decay. Question type: dependency chain, usually broken between objective and planning.
- **D3 Maintenance and import.** Actors: manager seat, introduced actors, instruments, provenance. Currency: actions, source gate, shadow test. Anchor: the ground, never the manager's opinion. Question type: two modes, introduction not exclusion.
- **D4 The specific list.** Actors: candidate species, counts, recovery times. Currency: none bound. Anchor: none nameable. **Not a domain yet.** No figure line returns a name.
- **Partition or union.** D1 through D3 form a union of overlapping conditions; D4 is excluded as `gap`. A robust service can fail on the reading link alone; a damaged habitat can be unrecoverable with a perfect reading; a maintained site can fail by an unenumerated introduction.
- **Order by dependency.** Describe the residual in effect terms; enumerate the habitat outward until the cascade stops; check that the objective has an instrument; refuse before minting a new actor; import with provenance and a shadow test only if an atom is genuinely absent; settle at the ground. Effort above a broken link arrives where it cannot be used (Fig 3 :19 "Optimising a link that already works cannot rescue one that does not.").

Strain test: Fig 11 governs habitat substitution, while the everyday word "restoration" governs a management intention. Different objects, so no strain after re-census. The mapping of physical substrate terms onto service components in `06_isomers_and_order.md` is a declared grammar binding, recorded as a strain rather than hidden.

## VERDICT

From the figures, not from the world sources: the separator survives and the species-list answer does not. Recovery is a property of assemblage and habitat, indexed per service slot, read at a ground that does not update, with the habitat enumerated outward, valence relative to the named service, two modes, asymmetric introduction, substitution rather than removal under isolation, and a managed site as permanent work. The structural reading in `06_isomers_and_order.md` holds: a service is a flow over a network written by bonds, not an ingredient list. The literal request for a species, a redundancy count or a recovery time is a `gap`, reported honestly rather than filled with a plausible number. Verdicts are kept per domain and never averaged. This is a `specified`, ungrounded, candidate result. The strongest outreach point is the pre-registered refuter, not agreement with the framework.

- **cost if wrong:** bounded and locatable. The packet is mode-labeled, states refuters before the conclusion and records the lineage, so an error is findable rather than invisible. The real cost is spending the independence of outside readers by sending the theory before the separator, which Fig 2 :42 prices as "Buy independence in proportion to the cost of being wrong." That cost is avoidable by leading with the shape claim and its refuter.
- **undoable:** yes. The species-list framing and the recovery-percentage framing are removable without touching the derivation; `07_the_tether_answer.md` and `08_expert_verification_packet.md` already remove them. A sent claim can be superseded by the corrected packet, and no primitive has to be withdrawn.
- **reversed by:** a ground reading, not agreement. A source-discriminating instrument at a named service slot showing which path produced a returning flow; a service that recovers with actor set and contact unchanged where the pre-perturbation prediction was wrong; a first-order enumeration that predicts every service at every scale; an actor with fixed valence across two residuals; or a relocated or synthetic service that reproduces every condition its goal required and inherits no unchosen actor. Any of these moves the corresponding verdict. Prestige, a room of agreeable readers or a shared evidence pool is not such an instrument.
