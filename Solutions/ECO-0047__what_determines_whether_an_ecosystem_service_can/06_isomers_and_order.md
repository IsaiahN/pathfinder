# Structure, order and isomers across ecosystem services

**Mode: `specified` on the structural claims, `general` on the bond grammar. Simulated derivation checked against the figures, not a ground reading (see `05_what_shape_means.md` section 7).**

## RESIDUAL

The primary audit says recovery is assemblage-and-habitat, enumerated outward, per service, with valence relative to the residual. Two supporting questions are owed: (1) what is the *structure* of a service, such that a species list cannot write it, and (2) if two services use overlapping actors, are they the same thing differently named, different things with the same ingredients, or a union of conditions? This file answers those and states a falsifier.

## DERIVATION

### Q1 - What kind of thing is a service?

Fig 13 :76-77:

> **"Energy and a gradient compose a flow. A gradient over space and one over time are different terms, because which state it is bonded to is part of what it is."**

Fig 13 :24 names the network structure: **"that entities relate, and influence travels"**. Fig 12 :111: **"A vocabulary without bonds can name a state and cannot name a route to one."**

A pollination service is not a list of bees. It is a flow over a network, with a gradient (a needing crop, a rewarding flower) in a particular space and season. The same bees over a different crop, or in a different season, compose a different term, because which state the gradient is bonded to is part of what the term is. This is why a species list is structurally incapable of answering the recovery question: it can name the ingredients and not the route (Fig 12 :5, :111).

**Falsifier, written before the run:** if a service's delivered flow is fully specified by the set and abundance of species present, with no network relation or gradient carrying information the list lacks, this claim fails.

### Q2 - Which bonds matter for recovery?

From the Operators table and Fig 12:

- `⇒` production: *"A produces B; B did not exist before"*. A flower produces nectar; a colony produces pollination. Recovery of production is not the return of an ingredient.
- `→` sequence: *"A then B; B needs A to have happened"*. A restoration sequence is ordered. Fig 13 :34-35: *"A seed carries no small tree: it carries what will grow one, and the conditions it lands in decide what it becomes."*
- `⋛` comparison: *"a threshold relation between two quantities"*. "Recovered" is a comparison against a reference flow, not an ingredient (Operators table :51: *"a goal is a comparison, which is why a vocabulary without it cannot state one"*). This is the broken link in Fig 3 :27 when no instrument states it.
- `−` subtraction: *"A with B removed or absent"*. Species loss is a subtraction from an arrangement, not from a stock; the bond that held the arrangement may be lost with the species.
- `+` conjunction: *"both hold at once"*. The union of conditions in the primary audit is not a simple sum; see Q4.

Because the arrangement is carried by the bond, two services can share actors and differ entirely in what they are (Fig 12 :94: *"The arrangement is the substance."*).

### Q3 - Are two services isomers, a functional group, or something else?

Fig 12 :117:

> **"isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."**

Fig 12 :123:

> **"functional group. A substructure whose presence fixes the outcome. The group says what kind; the qualifier says which member."**

Take two services over the same recorded actor list, say a set of insects that visits flowers and a set that preys on pests.

- **As the literature usually writes them** (different species lists, different habitats), they share few atoms. They are not isomers; they are two instances of the same **functional group**: a `state -> structure -> state` motif (a needing state, a mediating structure, a supplied state). Fig 13 :75 licenses the motif: *"It is not space-time but space, a structure, time; and two structures join only through a state."* Substrate terms are being used here as a grammar for service composition, not as a claim that a bee is a gradient; that mapping is a binding and is declared as such.
- **Over the full shared habitat set** (all actors, conditions and relations enumerated for the site, Fig 11 :43-48), the two services are **isomers**: same atoms, different bond arrangement, different substance. Pollination and pest suppression then differ by which relation is active, not by which species exist.

**Falsifier, written before the run:** if one service requires an atom the other does not even after the full habitat enumeration, the isomer claim fails and only the functional-group claim stands. This is the same shape as the exemplar's isomer test and is kept as a live condition, not a decoration.

### Q4 - What is the order of the determinants?

Not a list to be ticked off in any order. The dependency chain is ordered (Fig 3 :20: *"The chain is ordered by dependency, not by importance. Each link consumes what the one above produces, so effort spent above the break arrives at a step that cannot use it."*):

1. **Describe the residual** (Fig 9 :53: *"Nobody chooses. The gap does, and the gap is only as good as its description."*). Name the service flow and the lag.
2. **Enumerate the habitat** outward until the cascade stops mattering (Fig 11 :44, :52-53). This is `SEARCHED` first: is the actor already in the closure and unfound? Formula step 7 asks that before INWARD or OUTWARD because it is cheapest.
3. **Check the objective instrument** (Fig 3 :27). If "recovered" has no non-updating measure, the chain breaks here and later work is unreadable.
4. **Refuse before mint** (Formula step 2). If the held reading of the service is false, an import is not the first move; a refusal is owed. Negation is not a bond (Operators :70-74).
5. **Import only if an atom is genuinely absent**, with source gate and shadow test (Fig 6 :23, Fig 8 :53), debited against independence.
6. **Settle at the ground** (Fig 12 :104: *"A settled molecule becomes an atom for whatever composes over it"*); only then can the recovered service become an operand for the next service or the next season.

The determinants are a **union**, not a sequence of independent switches (Fig 9 :55-57), so this order is about diagnosis, not about which condition "causes" recovery.

### Q5 - Catalyst versus spent part: a second order effect across seasons

Fig 12 :119:

> **"catalyst. A part that survives its own use. Spent and regenerating parts behave differently across uses, and nothing records which a term is."**

A regenerating pollinator population and a one-off introduced cohort can produce the same first-season reading and differ in the second. Since the record usually holds only the count, the difference is invisible until the service fails. Bahlai and Landis 2026 report that *"these gross trends mask apparent periods of stability, recovery, and, in some cases, very sharp declines lasting many years across individual species and groups"* (`10_source_ledger.csv`), which is the empirical shape of this distinction. The catalyst test is a discriminator to build, not a name to assert.

### Q6 - Why repeated restoration can be motion without progress

Fig 7 :36:

> **"A chain of mirrors is one long room. The next room must differ from the last, and match the residual it surfaced."**

Fig 7 :37-39: progress per handoff has two factors, *"the bracket's fidelity across the handoff"* and *"the ground's pressure on the room"*. A restoration program that repeats the same intervention because it worked once is one long room. Each attempt must differ and must match the residual the last attempt surfaced. This is the structural reason a fixed protocol is not a recovery theory.

## CONCLUSION

A service is a flow over a network, written by bonds, not by an ingredient list. Two services over a shared habitat are isomers; as usually reported with different actor lists they are members of one functional group. The determinants are ordered by dependency for diagnosis but form a union for verdict, and repeated intervention is progress only when each room differs and matches the surfaced residual. The catalyst distinction names a discriminator that most monitoring lacks. None of these claims returns a species name, a count, or a recovery time; that remains the `gap` recorded in `01_figure_grounded_audit.md` F11.
