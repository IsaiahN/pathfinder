## The question restated as an effect

What is being decided is not the *level* of a firm's performance but *which shape of scope keeps the per-slot residual small over time*. In effect terms: a firm's library Γ predicts a market return Γ(b,a) for each product-market slot s; actions a are scope choices (enter one narrow slot, or enter many); the market returns o′; and R = |Γ(b,a) - o′| is measured per slot, never averaged across slots (Fig 1 :19 "The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated."; Fig 1 :22 "Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average.").

- Option A, specialize: concentrate Γ on one narrow slot. Predicted consequence: a deep capability and a small R in that slot, but a single channel to a single ground. If that ground's residual changes, the capability's valence flips (Fig 11 :49, Fig 11 :50) and R grows.
- Option B, diversify: spread Γ across many slots. Predicted consequence: multiple channels to multiple grounds and reuse of shared atoms across slots (economies of scope), but coordination cost and import risk when entering slots whose closure differs (Fig 6 :24).
- What an answer would look like, written down and bound as an ordinary term before the run (Formula, "Direction"): a *shape* naming the two conditions that must both hold for scope (narrow or broad) to keep the union of slot-residuals small, plus an instrument that discriminates reuse-within-closure (related diversification) from import-across-closure (unrelated diversification). That shape is the sought-for term. Until it is bound it predicts nothing and no check in the loop can see it.

The verdict recorded in `09_watson_answer.md` is: scope is not the primary variable; it is a union of two conditions (a maintained channel to a paying ground, and renewed reach through contact with a differing closure), kept per domain and never averaged, in `specified` mode.

## What is deliberately not here

- No personal emails of named individuals.
- No ranking of people by how likely they are to agree.
- No bulk-send plan.
- No claim that this derivation is `grounded`, no claim that it settles the business question, and no claim that one scope is universally best.

Three figure-grounded reasons, plus one practical one:

1. A room is not an anchor. *"A room’s agreement is variation, not a verdict. Selection between rooms is where the ground votes."* (Fig 7 :35) and *"Alignment is triangulation, not negotiation. A mirror cannot correct you."* (Fig 2 :35). Gathering agreeable readers manufactures the correlation that disqualifies a verdict.
2. Correlation has valence. *"disqualifying for a verdict: agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool"* (Fig 2 :40). A list selected for receptiveness is one witness wearing many names.
3. A verifier must be outside the interior. *"A verifier that can reconstruct the claim cannot verify it."* (Fig 10 :42). The first contact therefore carries the residual and its refuter, not the framework.
4. Legally, unsolicited bulk email to scraped addresses breaches GDPR and CAN-SPAM style rules in many jurisdictions and gets the sending domain blacklisted. Public opt-in routes avoid that failure as well as the structural one.
