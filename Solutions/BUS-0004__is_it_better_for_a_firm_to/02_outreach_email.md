# Outreach draft - validate the output first, then decide about the theory

**Use:** one-to-one, or as a cover letter attached to a submission through the public venues in `03_public_contact_points.csv`. **Not** for bulk send.

**Hard precondition:** do not send until the shape claim and its refuter in `01_figure_grounded_audit.md` are checked for internal consistency. The output must survive validation or the two-stage offer collapses on contact.

---

**Subject:** Check one output before you decide whether the specialization verdict is worth reading

> Hi [name],
>
> I am working on a narrow question in corporate strategy: is it better for a firm to specialize in one market or diversify across products and industries? I have one specific output I would like you to check against what you already know. I am not asking you to read the theory first, and I am not asking you to agree. If the output does not survive, there is nothing else to look at.
>
> **The output (`specified`, not `grounded`):** the question is not one choice but two conditions that must both hold, and they are routinely bundled. First, a firm must keep a working channel to a ground that actually pays, meaning realized customer return and survival, not internal agreement. Second, it must renew its reach through contact with a capability set different from its own. Narrow scope can satisfy the first and fail the second when its market changes. Broad scope can satisfy the second and fail the first in any single unit, because pooled averages hide a live loss. This is a prediction of a **shape**, not a measurement.
>
> From that shape follow three checkable claims. Related breadth, entering a new product built from capabilities the firm already holds, is a rearrangement of the same atoms, which is cheap and legal. Unrelated breadth, entering an industry whose capabilities the firm does not hold, requires importing a new atom, which has a price and must be checked against a source before it is paid for. And the advantage of a specialist can flip sign when its market's residual changes, so there is no standing list of good capabilities.
>
> **What I am not claiming:** that one scope is always better, that there is an optimal number of businesses, that "core competence" is a term the theory derives, or that the diversification discount proves focus wins. The discount reverses sign under better instruments and selection controls, so it is reported as instrument-dependent, not as a verdict. The framework returns a shape, not a name.
>
> **What would refute the shape claim:**
> 1. one scope lever that restores both a paying channel and renewed reach with no categorically different residue left over;
> 2. a pooled firm return shown to be a sufficient statistic for its per-business residuals, so per-slot measurement is unnecessary;
> 3. related breadth reaching a genuinely new capability with no rearrangement of held atoms and no import;
> 4. a capability keeping identical value across a documented change of market;
> 5. an instrument or ground reading that directly fixes one optimal breadth across firms.
>
> I wrote these down before sending this. If any one holds, the shape is withdrawn or narrowed. A one-line "this fails because..." is more useful to me than agreement. If you would rather not engage, no reply is needed.
>
> **If it survives, and only then:** the sources are below. The figures are the authority, not my summary of them.
> - Figures and formula for direct download: https://github.com/IsaiahN/tether/tree/general-agent/figures
> - Worked narrative and the shape method: https://adventuresinml.substack.com/p/how-intelligence-actually-works
> - The source/provenance essay: https://adventuresinml.substack.com/p/the-hard-problem-is-about-source
>
> **If you want to use the machinery:** the formula is in the figures link. Run it on a scope question you know well and can check against a ground. Write down what would refute your result *before* you run it, and state the mode of what you get. A reported failure is more useful to me than a reported success.
>
> - [your name]
