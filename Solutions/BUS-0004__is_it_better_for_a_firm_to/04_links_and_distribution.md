## What the wider literature bears on the question

1. **The empirical conflict is about the instrument, not the scope.** Berger and Ofek report a 13 to 15 percent diversification loss; Villalonga (2004) and Campa and Kedia (2002) show the discount shrinks or reverses under better data and selection controls. Villalonga's own resolution, a discount to unrelated and a premium to related diversification, is exactly the figure-derived distinction between an import across closures and a rearrangement inside one. The conflict was returned to the census and sharpened the verdict; it did not flip it.
2. **Shared capabilities, not shared products, are what travel.** Prahalad and Hamel's core competence and Panzar and Willig's economies of scope both name a shared input reused across product lines. In figure terms this is Fig 4 :22 and Fig 12 :117: a method crosses, a recording does not, and same atoms under a different bond are a different substance.
3. **Local feedback tilts toward exploitation.** O'Reilly and Tushman and March show that established organizations specialize in what they already know, which is the valence-flip and channel-decay mechanism of Fig 11 and Fig 10 at the organizational stratum.
4. **The specialist/generalist answer is habitat-dependent in ecology.** Optimal foraging theory predicts broad diets when food is scarce and narrow diets when food is abundant, and the niche-breadth literature's "jack of all trades is a master of none" matches Fig 11 :49-51. This is a genuinely low-overlap witness for the shape: there is no universally best breadth; it depends on the ground.
