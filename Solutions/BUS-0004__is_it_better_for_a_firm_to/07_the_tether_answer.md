# The Tether's answer: specialize or diversify?

**Mode: `specified` - not `grounded`.** This is a shape and a route, not a result. Simulated derivation checked against the figures (`05_what_shape_means.md` section 7), not a ground reading.

## 1. The residual it answers

A firm's library Γ predicts a market return for each product-market slot. "Better" is not the level of that return but the persistence of a low per-slot residual R = |Γ(b,a) - o′| over time and across slots. The question bundles two conditions that must both hold (Fig 9 :55, Fig 9 :57). It is a union compound, not one hard choice, and it has its own ground: the market.

## 2. The shape

1. **Scope is not the primary variable.** The primary variables are C1, a maintained channel to a paying ground, and C2, renewed reach through contact with a differing closure. Narrow scope can satisfy C1 and fail C2 when its market changes; broad scope can satisfy C2 and fail C1 in a unit whose loss is hidden by pooling. Fig 9 :32: *"you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself."*
2. **"Better" settles at the market, not in internal agreement.** Fig 2 :29: *"An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks."* Fig 2 :35: *"Alignment is triangulation, not negotiation. A mirror cannot correct you."* Realized return and survival, not a boardroom consensus, decide.
3. **Measure the vector, not the average.** Fig 1 :19: *"The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated."* A healthy pooled return can sit on top of one failing unit.
4. **Related breadth is legal rearrangement; unrelated breadth is a priced import.** Fig 12 :117: *"isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance."* Fig 13 :115: *"The set is closed. The arrangements are not. That gap is why import exists."* A new product from held capabilities is an isomer; a new industry needing new capabilities requires an import with a source gate and a shadow test. Fig 6 :24: *"A wrong atom costs more than starting from primitives."*
5. **What travels between slots must be a method, not a recording.** Fig 4 :23: *"upward: a method that can be reapplied. Never the recording of a particular success."* Reusing a capability works; replaying a past product does not.
6. **A capability can flip sign.** Fig 11 :49-51: valence is relative to the residual, and there is no standing list of good capabilities. The specialist's strength can become its rigidity when the market's residual changes.
7. **The numerical breadth is not given.** Fig 13 :134: *"Unreachable is a verdict here, not a silence,"* and Fig 8 :39: *"So a search that finds nothing is never a proof of absence."* The framework returns the conditions and an honest stop, not an optimal number of businesses.

## 3. What it explicitly does not say

- Not "specialize" and not "diversify" as universal verdicts.
- Not that there is an optimal breadth, nor that "two or three related businesses" is derived.
- Not that core competence is a term the framework derives. It is a binding from the business frame with no source gate.
- Not that the diversification discount proves focus wins. The sign flips with the instrument (segment data versus establishment data, and selection controls), so it is instrument-dependent.
- Not `grounded`. No ground reading of a firm's slot-residual vector was used.
- Not a theory of everything. The union keeps its own loop and its own ground (Fig 9 :55).

## 4. What would settle it - the ground

A source-discriminating instrument and a ground reading, not a further argument. From *The Hard Problem is About Source*: an instrument that reads green *"cannot comment on the source"*; the spectrometer resolved it. Here the corresponding questions are:

- **Per-slit measurement:** does each business unit's residual stay small, or is the pooled return masking one that grows? An instrument that reports residuals per slot would settle C1.
- **Relatedness discriminator:** does a new product reuse held capabilities (rearrangement, no new atom), or does it require an import? An instrument that discriminates these would separate related from unrelated scope.
- **Valence test:** does the capability that produced the old advantage keep its value after a documented change of market residual?
- **Falsifiers from the record:** one lever restoring both conditions; pooled return as sufficient statistic; related breadth with no rearrangement; invariant capability valence; an instrument fixing one optimal breadth.

## 5. The honest stop

**Unreached, not unreachable.** The question's residual is well formed (the market is a nameable anchor, Fig 2 :29), so this is not a `gap` at the root. But the specific numerical answer is a declared `gap`: the figures yield conditions and a shape, and the missing instrument is characterized in effect terms but not held. Stop at the limit of the available instruments and record the lineage so the stop is checkable. *"Indiscernibility is not identity."*

## 6. One-paragraph version for outreach

> The Tether does not say specialize and does not say diversify. It says the question bundles two conditions that must both hold: a firm must keep a working channel to a market that actually pays, and it must renew its capabilities through contact with a set it does not already have. Narrow scope tends to keep the first and lose the second when its market changes; broad scope tends to keep the second and lose the first in a unit whose loss is hidden by a pooled average. Related breadth is cheap because it rearranges capabilities the firm already holds, the same atoms in a different arrangement; unrelated breadth is an import with a price and a provenance check. The advantage of any capability can flip sign when the market changes, so there is no permanent list of good capabilities. What the framework offers is the shape any answer must have, the falsifiers written in advance, and the instruments that would close it. It does not name an optimal number of businesses. The center stays open until the ground settles it.
