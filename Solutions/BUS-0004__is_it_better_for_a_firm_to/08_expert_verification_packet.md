# Expert verification packet - the Tether on specialize vs diversify

**What this is:** a claim in a form a strategy or industrial-organization researcher can check. Mode, derivation and refuters are stated before the conclusion, so a reader knows what to look for and what would settle it.

**Mode: `specified` - not `grounded`.** No ground reading of a firm's per-slot residuals is claimed. This is a shape and a route, not a result. Simulated derivation, not a ground reading.

## 1. The claim, in one paragraph

The question whether a firm should specialize or diversify bundles two conditions that must both hold, and no single scope lever clears both. The first condition is a maintained channel to a ground that pays, meaning realized customer return and survival, not internal agreement. The second is renewed reach through contact with a capability set different from the firm's own. Narrow scope keeps the first and tends to lose the second when its market changes; broad scope keeps the second and tends to lose the first in a business unit whose loss is hidden by a pooled average. Related breadth is a rearrangement of capabilities the firm already holds, which is cheap and legal; unrelated breadth is an import of new capabilities, which has a price and needs a source gate. The framework returns this shape, states falsifiers in advance, and does not name an optimal breadth.

## 2. Three checkable sub-claims

### C1 - framework-internal; checkable by reading Figures 9, 1 and 2

- **Statement:** the question is a union of two conditions, judged per slot against a market anchor; "better" is not internal agreement.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 9 :55 - "A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones." Fig 9 :57 - "a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either." Fig 1 :19 "The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated." Fig 2 :35 "Alignment is triangulation, not negotiation. A mirror cannot correct you."
- **Refuter:** show one scope lever restoring both a paying channel and renewed reach with no categorically different residue; or show pooled return is a sufficient statistic for per-slot residuals; or show a scope choice persisting while realized return declines with no market correction registering.

### C2 - business-facing; checkable against the diversification and scope literature

- **Statement:** related breadth is a rearrangement of held capabilities (an isomer, Fig 12 :117); unrelated breadth is an import across closures (Fig 13 :115, Fig 6 :23). The empirical discount-versus-premium conflict resolves into exactly this distinction.
- **What to check:** Berger and Ofek (1995) find a 13 to 15 percent value loss from diversification, smaller for segments in the same two-digit SIC code. Villalonga (2004) and Campa and Kedia (2002) find the discount shrinks or reverses under establishment-level data and selection controls, and Villalonga attributes the difference to a discount to unrelated and a premium to related diversification. Prahalad and Hamel (1990) and Panzar and Willig (1981) name the shared capability/input that makes related scope cheap.
- **Refuter:** show that related breadth reaches a genuinely new capability with no rearrangement of held atoms and no import; or show that the discount/premium sign does not track relatedness under any instrument.

### C3 - framework-internal derivation

- **Statement:** a business unit settled against the ground becomes an atom for the portfolio-level composition; a capability's valence can flip when the residual changes; what travels between units must be a method, not a recording.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 12 :104 - "A settled molecule becomes an atom for whatever composes over it. So irreducible is a fact about a level and never about the world." Fig 11 :49 "An actor’s valence is relative to the residual, not a property it carries." Fig 4 :23 "upward: a method that can be reapplied. Never the recording of a particular success."
- **Refuter:** show a settled unit cannot serve as an atom for a portfolio composition; or show a capability keeping identical value across a documented change of market; or show a transferred capability reproduces an old product but cannot generate a new one.

## 3. What is explicitly not claimed

- Not a universal verdict for specialization or for diversification.
- Not an optimal breadth, nor a derived number of businesses.
- Not that core competence is derived by the framework; it is a binding from the business frame with no source gate.
- Not that the diversification discount proves focus wins; it is instrument-dependent.
- Not `grounded`. Nothing here is asserted as settled by experiment.
- Not a theory of everything; the union keeps its own loop and ground.

## 5. What would settle it

A source-discriminating instrument and a ground reading, not a further argument:

1. A per-slot residual instrument that shows whether pooled return is masking a failing unit.
2. A relatedness discriminator that shows whether a new product reuses held capabilities or imports new ones.
3. A valence test across a documented change of market residual.
4. Any of the five pre-registered falsifiers in `01_figure_grounded_audit.md` section 4 firing.

## 6. What an expert can check in twenty minutes

1. Read Fig 9 :55-57, Fig 12 :104 and :117, Fig 1 :19, Fig 2 :35, Fig 4 :23 (six short passages).
2. Check C2 against the scope literature: does the discount/premium sign really track relatedness?
3. Name one instrument that would report a per-business residual or discriminate reuse from import, or say why none is possible.
4. Send the refutation. A failure is more useful than an endorsement.
