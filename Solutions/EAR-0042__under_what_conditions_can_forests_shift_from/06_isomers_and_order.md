# Islands of the same forest: isomers, functional groups and the order of disturbances

**Mode: `specified`.** A simulated derivation checked against the figures, not a ground reading (see `05_what_shape_means.md` section 5).

## RESIDUAL

Two kinds of confusion recur in forest sink/source work: (1) two forests with the same species list and the same climate behave oppositely; (2) the same disturbances in a different order produce different outcomes. The figure vocabulary has separate structures for both. Which ones apply, and what would falsify the mapping?

## DOMAIN CENSUS

Atoms held (Fig 13): states space, time, energy; structures spectrum, gradient, network. For this question the "atoms" at the ecological level are the habitat items of Fig 11: canopy uptake, autotrophic respiration, heterotrophic respiration, deadwood, soil stock, fire, drought, stand age, edge. Bonds held: the seven of Fig 12, including sequence (→), subtraction (−), comparison (⋛), and the cycle structure. No new primitive is available without import.

## DERIVATION

### Q1: Are two forests with the same species list isomers?

Fig 12 :117: "isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."
Fig 12 :98: "This is not an analogy. Chemistry hit it first: silver fulminate and silver cyanate have the same composition and different properties, which is what forced structure into the notation."

**Reading.** A species list plus a climate summary is the flat formula. Two stands can share it and still differ because the **return path** is bonded differently: in one, deadwood is retained and decomposes slowly; in the other, a recent fire short-circuits the return. Same atoms, different bond, different carbon balance. The difference is invisible in the species list and decisive in `F`. So the isomer claim is well formed at the stand level.

**Falsifier:** if two stands with identical habitat items and identical return-path bonds differ in `F`, the isomer mapping fails and an unenumerated atom is owed (a gap, not a softened claim).

### Q2: What is the functional group?

Fig 12 :124: "functional group. A substructure whose presence fixes the outcome. The group says what kind; the qualifier says which member."
Fig 12 :121: "cycle. The output re-enters as input. The sign of the loop decides whether it closes, and a chain cannot close by construction."

**Reading.** The functional group here is the **signed return loop**: uptake → biomass → litter and deadwood → respiration → CO2 → uptake. Its presence fixes whether the system can be a sink or a source at all; the qualifier (fire, drought, pests, harvest) says which member of the group it is. A forest described as a chain (tree grows, carbon banked, end) does not contain the group and cannot represent a source state. This is why "stock of carbon" is the wrong unit for the question (F2 in `01_figure_grounded_audit.md`).

**Falsifier:** exhibit a forest whose source state is fully described without any return path, with no lateral export to carry the flux. Then the functional-group claim fails.

### Q3: Does order matter?

Fig 12 :74: "swap them. Does it become a different thing? Yes." This is the test for the sequence bond →, whose meaning is "A then B; B needs A to have happened" (Fig 12 :72).
Fig 13 :76: "A gradient over space and one over time are different terms, because which state it is bonded to is part of what it is."

**Reading.** Sequence is priced as an ordered bond because the two spellings differ. Drought → fire (mortality adds fuel, then ignition) and fire → drought (canopy opened, then water stress) are not the same compound. The first is a fuel-mediated flip; the second is an exposure-mediated one. Fig 13 :76 adds that a gradient bonded to space and one bonded to time are different terms; likewise a soil-moisture deficit in the dry season and one in the wet season are different habitats for the same trees.

**Falsifier:** if swapping the order of two disturbances never changes the sign or persistence of `F` at any site, the sequence claim is unsupported and should be struck.

### Q4: The catalyst structure and legacy

Fig 12 :119: "catalyst. A part that survives its own use. Spent and regenerating parts behave differently across uses, and nothing records which a term is."

**Reading.** Old soil carbon and coarse deadwood survive their own use as a stock; after a fire, the regenerating forest carries a different term than the unburned one even with the same species list. The framework names the distinction but says "nothing records which a term is", so a registry of burn history is an instrument requirement, not a theoretical add-on. This is the structural reason Q1's isomer claim is checkable only with history.

### Q5: What is the order of the union?

Fig 3 :20: "The chain is ordered by dependency, not by importance."
Fig 9 :55: "A single lever clears the region where only one condition holds and leaves the intersections standing, because the second condition still binds."

**Reading.** The dependency order for this question is: measure the signed flux (perception), name the habitat items that could change it (vocabulary), define what counts as a persistent crossing (objective), choose a lever (planning), then carry what worked (learn and carry). The union order is not a list of equal causes: drought is upstream of fuel, fuel is upstream of fire probability, deforestation is upstream of edge dryness, and all of them converge on the signed loop. Removing the last link in a chain cannot be repaired by optimizing an earlier one.

**Falsifier:** if a single lever clears the entire union including the intersections, the union/order claim fails and the problem is a partition after all.

## CONCLUSION

The two structures the question needs are supplied by Fig 12: **isomers** for stands that share a species list but differ in return-path bonding, and a **functional group** (the signed return loop) that fixes sink versus source kind. **Sequence** is a real ordered bond, so disturbance order is a different object, not a replay. The **catalyst** structure explains why legacy stock and burn history must be recorded. The union is ordered by dependency, not by importance. Every mapping has a stated falsifier.
