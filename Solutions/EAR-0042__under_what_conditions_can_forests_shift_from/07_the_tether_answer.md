# The Tether's answer: when a forest becomes a carbon source

**Mode: `general` for the shape, `specified` for the per-domain verdicts, `gap` for exact thresholds. Not `grounded`.** Simulated derivation checked for internal consistency against the figures (`05_what_shape_means.md` section 5), not a ground reading.

## 1. The residual it answers

A forest's net carbon balance is a signed flux: uptake minus release by respiration, decomposition, fire and export. The question asks when that sign flips from sink to source, and what makes the flip a persistent shift. Bound the answer shape first: a signed crossing, a persistence window, and an alteration of the stocks and return paths. The phrase "under what conditions" is a **union** of overlapping habitat conditions, not a single threshold (Fig 9 :55). Verdicts below are kept per domain and never averaged.

## 2. The shape, domain by domain

1. **The flip is the sign of a return loop, not the exhaustion of a stock.** Fig 12 :121: "cycle. The output re-enters as input. The sign of the loop decides whether it closes, and a chain cannot close by construction." Uptake becomes biomass, biomass becomes litter and deadwood, deadwood and soil respire back, and fire or harvest can short-circuit the return. Release exceeding uptake for long enough is the source state. *(Domain 1, `specified`, triangulated with a flux-partitioning study.)*
2. **The conditions are habitat items enumerated from the world, out to second order.** Fig 11 :43: "The habitat is enumerated, never composed." Fig 11 :46: "conditions: light, water, temperature, season, terrain". Water, temperature, VPD, fire, pests, stand age, soil stocks and edges are read off the world; mortality adding fuel and canopy opening drying the stand are second-order contacts. *(Domain 1 to 3, `general` rule plus `specified` list, triangulated.)*
3. **The conditions form a union with compounds.** Fig 9 :57-58: "a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either. It stops being a mixture and becomes a specific compound." Drought plus fire plus edge is a different object from drought alone. *(Union, `general`, triangulated.)*
4. **The flip is per slot.** Fig 1 :22: "Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average." A near-neutral basin can contain a clear source subregion. *(Domain 1 and 2, `specified`, triangulated.)*
5. **Actor valence is relative to the residual.** Fig 11 :50: "The same actor is a benefactor to one residual and a malfactor to another, so mutualism and parasitism are relations rather than kinds." Fire is a regeneration condition in some forests and a flip trigger in others. *(Domain 3, `general` rule plus `specified` assignment, triangulated.)*
6. **The anchor is measured signed flux, triangulated across independent instruments.** Fig 2 :29: "An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks." Fig 2 :37-38: "instruments can be wrong on axes they do not measure." Greenness, models and credits are frames, not anchors. *(Domain 1 and 5, `general`, triangulated.)*
7. **Management is a maintainer, not a ground.** Fig 11 :62: "You can add an actor and you cannot subtract the habitat, because contact is a fact about the world and does not consult you." A managed sink is permanent work. *(Domain 6, `general`, single-source for the asymmetry.)*
8. **Exact thresholds are a `gap` at this budget.** Fig 3 :27: "A link with no instrument is not a link that works. It is a link nobody has looked at." The instruments exist; the cross-network convention for "persistent shift" does not. This is underivable at this budget, not unreachable in principle. *(Gap, triangulated.)*

## 3. What it explicitly does not say

- No numerical tipping point, no critical VPD, no temperature or drought index value.
- No claim that a single driver causes all flips, and no claim that fire is always a source condition.
- No claim that a global or continental mean is the unit of a shift.
- No claim that satellite greenness measures net carbon flux.
- No claim that management can switch off decomposition or drought.
- No claim that the figure set is a forest model. It constrains the shape of an answer and the conditions under which an answer could be settled; it does not supply a biophysical threshold.

## 4. What would settle it, and what would refute it

**Settle (move from candidate toward grounded):** an instrument-backed, cross-network definition of persistent shift (a crossing threshold, a duration, a stock-change criterion), applied to flux towers, permanent plots and atmospheric inversions separately, with the results compared rather than averaged (Fig 2 :45 "Frames range against the ground. They do not negotiate with it.").

**Refute, written before the conclusion in `01_figure_grounded_audit.md` section 5:** (a) a flip with no habitat change; (b) a persistently net-source forest whose return loop still has an uptake sign and no lateral export; (c) a single condition's threshold that partitions everything with no interaction terms; (d) flips always spatially synchronous; (e) a proxy mapping invariantly to net flux across biomes. Any one fires, the corresponding claim is withdrawn.

## 5. The honest stop

**Unreached, not unreachable.** Fig 8 :39: "So a search that finds nothing is never a proof of absence." The missing thing is an operational definition with a shape (signed crossing plus duration plus stock change), not a number the framework can name. Fig 9 :39 warns that "a wrong cut removes the answer and speeds up", so any threshold adopted before the convention is settled should be kept ranked and reversible. The lineage is recorded in `01_figure_grounded_audit.md` sections 4 and 7, including the retracted readings, so the stop is checkable. A simulated derivation changes no contact and settles nothing.

## 6. One-paragraph version for outreach

> A forest becomes a carbon source when the sign of its return loop flips: release by respiration, decomposition, fire and export exceeds uptake for long enough that the stocks and return paths are altered, not merely for one dry season. The conditions that produce that crossing are not one threshold but a union of overlapping habitat items read off the world: water, temperature, fire, pests, stand age, soil stocks and edges, with second-order cascades such as drought-driven mortality adding fuel. The flip is per stand, so a near-neutral basin can hide a source subregion, and fire's role depends on the forest it acts on. The anchor is the measured signed flux from towers, plots and inversions, triangulated rather than averaged; greenness is only a proxy. A managed forest is maintained work, not a solved state. The figures return the shape of that answer and five written refuters, not a numerical tipping point: that number is still owed to an instrument and a stated convention for what counts as persistent.
