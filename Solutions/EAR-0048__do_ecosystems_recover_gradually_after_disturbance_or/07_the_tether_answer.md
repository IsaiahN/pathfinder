# The Tether's answer: do ecosystems recover gradually or settle differently?

**Mode: `specified` on the shape and per-domain verdicts, `general` on the barrier claims. Not `grounded`.** This is a simulated derivation checked for internal consistency against the figures (`05_what_shape_means.md` §6), not a ground reading. No measurement of any ecosystem was taken.

## 1. The residual it answers

After a disturbance, R(t) = |Γ(b,a) - o'| per slot (Fig 1 :19). The two offered outcomes are a monotonic decline back to S0 and a settlement at S1 not equal to S0. Fig 9 :54 requires unbundling before searching: this is a union of conditions, not a partition of the world into two kinds of ecosystem. The answer is a decision rule selecting a regime, not a choice between two slogans.

## 2. The shape, per domain

**D1, habitat.** Gradual return is not a default of the framework. In the unmaintained mode, Fig 11 :14-15: "nothing selects; what survives is what the ground kept". The habitat is enumerated from the world (Fig 11 :48), and the conditions decide what a perturbation becomes: Fig 4 :34-35 "A seed carries no small tree: it carries what will grow one, and the conditions it lands in decide what it becomes." So disturbance size alone cannot predict the outcome.

**D2, feedback structure.** The discriminator is the sign of the loop. Fig 12 :121: "cycle. The output re-enters as input. The sign of the loop decides whether it closes, and a chain cannot close by construction." A chain returns; a closing cycle holds a new state. Two systems with the same species and different bonds are isomers (Fig 12 :117), so the difference is in the arrangement, not the inventory.

**D3, reach and basin.** Gradual recovery is composition inside one closure. A different stable state is a change of closure, and internal composition cannot produce it. Fig 6 :23: "No frame escapes its own closure. The bound moves only on contact with a frame whose closure differs." Fig 8 :32: "Closure is idempotent. Composing inside a closed set never leaves it, so no search, however long, adds an atom. This one is definitional: it is what closure means." This is the structural reading of hysteresis: reversing the driver is not the same as importing the change needed to cross back.

**D4, observation.** A global recovery index can hide a live slot. Fig 1 :19 "The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated"; Fig 10 :24 "the ground does not decay, the channel does". The check is a per-slot hysteresis test against the ground, not agreement among monitors.

**Decision rule.** Regime is selected by (a) loop sign, (b) whether closure changed, (c) the enumerated habitat conditions, and (d) channel fidelity. The instrument that discriminates is the return-path test: if reversing the driver does not reverse the state under identical conditions, the alternative-state regime is admitted.

**Honest stop.** Which basin a given ecosystem reaches is a declared `gap`. Fig 9 :32 "you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself"; Fig 13 :134 "Unreachable is a verdict here, not a silence,". The framework gives the decision rule, not the name of the state.

**Management consequence.** Fig 11 :61-62 "Introduction is available; exclusion is not. You can add an actor and you cannot subtract the habitat, because contact is a fact about the world and does not consult you." Fig 11 :63 "Which is why a managed ecosystem is permanent work rather than a solved state." A wrong import is costly and partly irreversible.

## 3. What it explicitly does not say

- Not that gradual recovery never happens. It is one regime of the union.
- Not that any named ecosystem is in either regime. That needs a measurement.
- Not that "resilience" is a single number that settles the question (Fig 1 :22).
- Not that positive feedback in general is the answer. Fig 12 :121 gives a sign test as a shape; naming the loop is ground work.
- Not that the six-term substrate is nature's own list. It is a visible guess (`05_what_shape_means.md` §3).
- Not `grounded`. Nothing here is settled by experiment.

## 4. What would settle it - the ground

A source-discriminating instrument, not a further argument. Fig 9 :27-28: "describe it before you go looking, or any frame you pick will seem to fit." The tests are:

1. **Return-path test:** measure the trajectory under increasing then decreasing driver. A different return path at the same driver value confirms hysteresis and the alternative-state regime.
2. **Feedback-sign test:** measure the sign of the dominant re-entering loop. Does it predict which regime occurs under identical external conditions?
3. **Per-slot test:** is R(t) zero on every slot, or is one slot still live while the aggregate looks recovered (Fig 1 :13)?
4. **Import test:** does crossing back require a changed condition or introduced actor, rather than simply reversing the driver?
5. **Standing falsifiers from `01_figure_grounded_audit.md` §4:** a unique attractor under every disturbance; a crossing by internal composition alone; a feedback sign that does not predict regime; an averaged index that suffices; a figure line that names the basin.

## 5. The honest stop

**Unreached, not unreachable.** The six-term set and the bond list are a visible guess at the compression axes, not a final vocabulary. The answer stops at the limit of what the available instruments distinguish, and records the lineage so the stop is checkable. "Indiscernibility is not identity" (canonical README). A search that finds no figure line for the basin identity is not a proof of absence (Fig 8 :39).

## 6. One-paragraph version for outreach

> The Tether does not pick one side of the question. It says the two outcomes are different regimes of one union: gradual return is composition inside a single basin, while settlement in a different stable state is a change of basin that internal dynamics cannot cross and that needs an import to reverse. What selects the regime is the sign of the loop that closes, whether the closure changed, the enumerated habitat conditions, and whether the monitoring channel still touches the ground. The same species under a different feedback arrangement are isomers, so the difference is in the arrangement and invisible in a species count. The framework states the decision rule and the hysteresis test that would discriminate the cases, and it refuses to name which basin a particular ecosystem occupies. That name is a gap, to be closed by measurement, not by agreement.
