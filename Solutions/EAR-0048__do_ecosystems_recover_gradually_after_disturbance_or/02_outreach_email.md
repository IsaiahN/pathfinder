# Outreach draft: check one prediction about ecosystem recovery

**Use:** one-to-one, or as a cover note attached to a submission through a public venue in `03_public_contact_points.csv`. **Not** for bulk send.

**Hard precondition:** do not send until the shape claim and its refuter in `01_figure_grounded_audit.md` survive your own check. The output must survive validation or the two-stage offer collapses on contact.

---

**Subject:** One checkable prediction about recovery after disturbance

> Hi [name],
>
> I have one specific prediction I would like you to check against work you already know. I am not asking you to read a theory first, and I am not asking you to agree with anything. If the prediction does not survive, there is nothing else to look at.
>
> **The prediction (`specified`, not `grounded`):** after a disturbance, two different things can happen, and which one happens is not decided by the size of the disturbance alone. Recovery back to the original state is what a system does when it stays inside one basin of attraction. Settlement into a different stable state is what happens when the feedback loop closes with the opposite sign and the system crosses into another basin. The distinguishing test is the return path: if reversing the driver does not reverse the state, the system has hysteresis, and you are in the second case. The second case also requires an import, a changed condition or an introduced actor, to cross back; internal dynamics alone cannot do it.
>
> I am deliberately **not** claiming that any particular ecosystem is in either regime. That needs a measurement, and the framework I am using returns a shape, not a name. I am also not claiming that a single resilience number settles anything: the relevant signal is per species or per patch, and an average can hide a slot that has not recovered.
>
> **What would refute the prediction, written before this message:**
> 1. A per-slot measurement showing every unmaintained ecosystem returns to a unique prior state after every disturbance, so no alternative settlement exists.
> 2. A documented basin crossing by internal dynamics alone, with no import and no changed external condition.
> 3. A measurement showing feedback sign does not predict which regime occurs under identical conditions.
>
> If any one holds, I will withdraw the claim. A one-line "this fails because..." is more useful to me than agreement. If you would rather not engage, no reply is needed.
>
> **If it survives, and only then:** I can send the sources behind it. The figures, not my summary of them, are the authority. I will not send them before your verdict on the prediction is recorded, because once you have the framework in front of you, your agreement stops being an outside check.
>
> If you want to use the method yourself: write down what would refute your result *before* you run it, and state whether your result is `general`, `specified`, or `grounded`.

## Distribution rule

Send to one person at a time through a public, opt-in channel. Never send the same unsolicited message to a list, and never rank recipients by how likely they are to agree. The figure reason is Fig 9 :60: "Use filters for the budget and witnesses for the verdict. Never let a filter hand you a verdict."
