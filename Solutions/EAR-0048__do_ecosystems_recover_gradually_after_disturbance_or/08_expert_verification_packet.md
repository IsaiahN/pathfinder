# Expert verification packet: EAR-0048, ecosystem recovery after disturbance

**What this is:** a claim in a form an ecosystem ecologist can check. Mode, derivation and refuters are stated before the conclusion.

**Mode: `specified` on the shape, `general` on the barrier claims. Not `grounded`.** No measurement at any site is claimed. This is a shape and a route, not a result. It is a simulated derivation checked for internal consistency against the figures, not a ground reading.

## 1. The claim, in one paragraph

Gradual recovery and settlement in a different stable state are not two answers to one question. They are two regimes of a union of conditions. Gradual return is composition inside one basin of attraction; settlement in a different state is a change of basin that internal dynamics cannot produce and that needs an import (a changed condition or an introduced actor) to reverse. What selects the regime is the sign of the loop that closes, whether the closure changed, the enumerated habitat conditions, and whether the monitoring channel still touches the ground. Two ecosystems with the same species but different feedback relations are isomers: same atoms, different bond, different substance. The framework states the decision rule and the hysteresis test that would discriminate the cases and refuses to name which basin a particular ecosystem occupies. That name is a declared gap.

## 2. Three checkable sub-claims

### C1 - The discriminator is loop sign, not species count
- **Statement:** a chain returns toward one state; a cycle whose sign closes holds a self-maintaining state. Same species, different bond, different outcome.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 12 :121 - "cycle. The output re-enters as input. The sign of the loop decides whether it closes, and a chain cannot close by construction." Fig 12 :117 - "isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."
- **What to check:** in a system you know well, does the return trajectory follow the sign of the dominant re-entering feedback loop rather than the species list? Are two sites with the same taxa but different interaction structure actually different regimes?
- **Refuter:** identical feedback sign under identical conditions producing both regimes, or two same-species sites with different sign and identical return trajectories.
- **Mode:** `specified`, ungrounded. Triangulated (Fig 12 plus Fig 13 :121).

### C2 - Crossing basins requires an import
- **Statement:** within one basin, internal composition cannot reach a different basin. Reversing the driver is not the same as crossing back.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 6 :23 - "No frame escapes its own closure. The bound moves only on contact with a frame whose closure differs." Fig 8 :32 - "Closure is idempotent. Composing inside a closed set never leaves it, so no search, however long, adds an atom. This one is definitional: it is what closure means." Fig 6 :14 - "the only operator that moves the wall".
- **What to check:** after a documented regime shift, did recovery require a changed external condition or an introduced actor, or did reversing the driver alone restore the original state?
- **Refuter:** a documented crossing by internal dynamics alone, with no import and no changed external condition, measured per slot.
- **Mode:** `general` barrier, `specified` basin application. Triangulated (Fig 6 plus Fig 8, distinct roles).

### C3 - The signal is per slot, and the channel can fail
- **Statement:** a global recovery index can be near zero while one species or patch is still far from the reference state; apparent recovery can be a saturated metric rather than a state change.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 1 :19 - "The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated." Fig 1 :22 - "Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average." Fig 10 :24 - "the ground does not decay, the channel does".
- **What to check:** in a recovering site, is the per-species or per-patch residual zero on every slot when the aggregate index is zero? Is the monitoring instrument saturated while an independent ground measurement still moves?
- **Refuter:** per-slot residual zero whenever the aggregate is zero, across all slots and disturbances, with an independent ground check.
- **Mode:** `specified`. Triangulated (Fig 1 plus Fig 10).

## 3. What is explicitly not claimed

- Not that gradual recovery never happens. It is one regime of the union.
- Not that any named lake, forest, or reef is in either regime. That needs a measurement.
- Not that a single resilience number settles the question.
- Not that positive feedback in general is the answer. Fig 12 :121 gives a sign test as a shape; naming the loop in a particular system is ground work.
- Not that the six-term substrate is nature's own list. It is a visible guess.
- Not `grounded`, and not a claim that the figures have named the settlement state. Fig 9 :32 - "you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself".

## 5. What would settle it

A source-discriminating measurement, not an argument. Fig 9 :27-28 - "describe it before you go looking, or any frame you pick will seem to fit."

1. **Return-path test:** increasing then decreasing driver. Different return path at the same driver value confirms hysteresis.
2. **Feedback-sign test:** does measured loop sign predict the regime under identical conditions?
3. **Per-slot test:** is R(t) zero on every slot, or is one slot still live behind a recovered aggregate?
4. **Import test:** did crossing back need a changed condition or introduced actor?
5. The five pre-registered falsifiers in `01_figure_grounded_audit.md` §4.

## 6. What an expert can check in twenty minutes

1. Read Fig 12 :117 and :121, Fig 6 :23, Fig 1 :19 and :22 (five short passages).
2. Check C1 against one system you know: is the outcome governed by feedback sign or by the species list?
3. Name one measurement that would distinguish a return path from a forward path in that system, or say why none is possible.
4. Send the refutation. A failure is more useful than an endorsement.
