# Isomers, order and analogues: replication and boundary in ORI-0040

**Mode: `specified` on the structural derivation, `gap` on historical timing. Nothing here is `grounded`.** This is a simulated derivation checked for internal consistency against the figures (see `05_what_shape_means.md` §6). No chemistry was measured and no rock was dated.

## RESIDUAL

The source question bundles two conditions and a timing index. Write them in effect terms before looking for any frame:

- (i) a replicating informational polymer whose output re-enters as input and whose catalyst survives its use;
- (ii) a boundary that bounds the reproduction of that polymer, so a benefit stays with the lineage and a parasite cannot freely enter;
- (iii) an index for when (ii) appeared relative to (i), which the figures carry no instrument for.

Conditions (i) and (ii) are co-present, so this is a union, not one hard problem: Fig 9 :55 "a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either". Condition (iii) is a partition away from them, because asking when presupposes knowing what. Fig 9 :27-28 "describe it before you go looking, or any frame you pick will seem to fit" governs the order of the whole derivation.

## DOMAIN CENSUS

- **D1 molecular replication.** Actors: RNA strands, activated nucleotides, ribozymes. Currency: replication rate, fidelity, heritable sequence. Anchor: in vitro replication and selection experiments, nameable. Question: is replication a legal cycle without a boundary?
- **D2 boundary formation.** Actors: fatty acid vesicles, coacervate droplets, mineral pores, mineral surfaces. Currency: containment, local concentration, growth and division. Anchor: vesicle and droplet experiments, nameable. Question: what does a boundary do in effect terms?
- **D3 selection on the compound.** Actors: variants, environment, the bounded unit. Currency: differential reproduction. Anchor: population and competition experiments, nameable. Question: what is the unit of selection once cooperation and parasites bind?
- **D4 historical timing.** Actors: geological record, chronology of encapsulation relative to replication. Currency: temporal order. Anchor: the rock record and chronological experiment, not nameable at this budget. Question: when?

Arity: two conditions plus an index. Symmetry: non-symmetric at first order, a boundary contains a replicator while the replicator does not bound the boundary at the same level; at the next level the two co-produce a unit, which is recursion rather than symmetry. Scale: molecular and protocellular in contact. Per Fig 1 :19 "The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated", the three slots keep separate verdicts. D1 and D2 are a union upstream of D3; D4 is a partition downstream of D3 (Fig 3 :19 "Optimising a link that already works cannot rescue one that does not.").

## DERIVATION

### Q1 - Is a membrane a seventh primitive?

Fig 13 :10 "near, far, between, contained" lists containment under the space state. Fig 6 :21 "MINT composes inside the closure and can never add an atom." Fig 12 :5 "A set of ingredients is not one. The bond is the rest of the information, and it is the half nothing was recording."

A boundary is a spatial condition composed with the other kinds, not a new atom. The framework already has the slot "contained". Naming fatty acid vesicle, coacervate or mineral pore is a binding from the chemistry frame onto that slot, provenanced as such, and not a derivation of a new term. Fig 13 :115 "The set is closed. The arrangements are not. That gap is why import exists." explains why the chemical name has to come from outside the figure set. **Result:** containment is licensed at `general` mode; the chemical identity stays `gap` at figure level.

### Q2 - Are replication and boundary isomers?

Fig 12 :117 "isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour." Fig 12 :123 "functional group. A substructure whose presence fixes the outcome. The group says what kind; the qualifier says which member."

Under the figure's own definition the two conditions are **not isomers**. They do not share one atom set with a different bond: a cycle and a catalyst are structures and relations (Fig 12 :119, :121), while containment is a state of space (Fig 13 :10). They are two conditions of a union, and the surplus is in their compound: Fig 8 :45 "Two frames together reach what neither reaches alone." Naked replication reaches copying but lets products diffuse; bare containment reaches a place but no heredity. Together they reach differential reproduction of a bounded lineage, which is categorically different from more of either alone (Fig 9 :55).

Where the isomer idea does apply is **within** the boundary condition. Vesicle, coacervate and mineral pore are functional-group variants: same effect (a boundary that changes contact), different chemical member. The group says what kind, the qualifier says which member. That is why the field can argue about which compartment came first without touching the shape claim. **Result:** union for the two conditions, functional group for the boundary class, not isomerism between replication and containment.

### Q3 - What is the order of the two conditions?

Fig 3 :19 "Optimising a link that already works cannot rescue one that does not." Fig 12 :104 "A settled molecule becomes an atom for whatever composes over it. So irreducible is a fact about a level and never about the world." Fig 9 :55 "a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either"

The order is **dependency order, not chronology**. The replication cycle and the boundary condition are upstream of the compound; the compound is upstream of the timing question. You cannot date encapsulation relative to replication until you know what encapsulation does. Once the compound settles against the ground, it can serve as an atom at the next level (Fig 12 :104), so a protocell is composed over a settled replicator plus a settled boundary, not minted as a third primitive.

This is where the question's wording does its damage. "From the start" asks for geological order, but the figures return only the dependency order. The two are different quantities. Treating Fig 3 order as a date is a category error, not a finding. **Result:** D1 and D2 before D3 before D4; the first three are structural, D4 is `gap`.

### Q4 - What do the analogues license?

Fig 12 :119 "A part that survives its own use." Fig 12 :121 "The output re-enters as input. The sign of the loop decides whether it closes, and a chain cannot close by construction." Fig 11 :66 "Isolation is not removal of the habitat. It is substitution of one habitat for another."

A ribozyme that copies RNA is the figure's catalyst: a part that survives its own use. Replication is the figure's cycle: output re-enters as input, provided the sign is right. Neither line requires a wall. The boundary enters as a **substituted habitat** (Fig 11 :66): it changes contact, concentrates reagents, keeps catalyst near product, and makes a group of relatives a unit a parasite cannot freely enter. Fig 11 :61 "Introduction is available; exclusion is not." matches the field's point that a boundary can be added but cannot subtract the chemistry running inside it.

The world literature supplies three live members of the boundary functional group: fatty acid and phospholipid vesicles, membraneless coacervate droplets, and porous rocks or mineral surfaces (Joyce and Szostak 2018; Slootbeek et al. 2022; see `04_links_and_distribution.md`). Fig 9 :32 "you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself" forbids promoting one of these to a figure-derived name. **Result:** the analogy licenses the effect and refuses the name.

## FALSIFIER (written before the conclusion)

The order and union claim is refuted if any of the following holds:

1. A multi-function, parasite-resistant, open-ended RNA evolutionary system is demonstrated in well-mixed solution with no boundary of any kind, so differential reproduction of a bounded lineage is not required. This breaks Q3 at the ground (D1 and D2 are not a union).
2. Replication and containment are shown to be the same atoms under a different bond, making them isomers rather than a union, so the surplus of the compound is only a spelling effect.
3. Containment is shown not expressible by the space term as given, or a seventh primitive called membrane is forced into the set. This breaks Q1.
4. A ground reading dates encapsulation unambiguously relative to the origin of heritable replication. This closes D4 and converts the timing verdict from `gap` to `grounded`, without refuting the structural claim.

Item 4 is a closure of a gap, not a refutation of the shape. Keep that distinction visible (Fig 13 :134 "Unreachable is a verdict here, not a silence").

## CONCLUSION

The two conditions are **not isomers**. They are a union compound whose own loop and ground must be settled separately, and the several chemical compartments are functional-group variants of one boundary effect. The order among them is dependency order (cycle and boundary upstream of the compound, timing downstream), not geological order. The figures license a boundary of some kind, refuse to name one, and carry no instrument to date one. That is the whole of what this supporting derivation adds to `01_figure_grounded_audit.md`.
