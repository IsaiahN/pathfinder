## 0. The claim under audit, restated as an effect

The source question (item 40, section 4) asks: **Did RNA-based evolution require a membrane or some other form of compartment from the start?**

Stated as an effect, with the options and what each leads to:

- **Option A, yes from the start.** Darwinian evolution needs a bounded unit that ties a genotype to the products that benefit it and keeps parasites out. Without a boundary, benefits diffuse and selection cannot hold a unit. Leads to: the unit of selection is the protocell; naked RNA is not a full evolutionary individual.
- **Option B, no, the compartment came later.** Naked RNA replicators can undergo Darwinian evolution in well-mixed solution; a boundary is a later improvement in evolvability. Leads to: the unit of selection is the molecule at first, and the compartment is a derived innovation.

This is not one question. It bundles a **functional** question (is a boundary required for Darwinian evolution at all?) with a **historical** question (was it present "from the start"?). Fig 9 :55 "a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either". The two are answered separately, and one of them comes back `gap`.

**Direction, made an ordinary term before the run.** Following the formula's "Direction" discipline, here is what an answer would look like, bound so it can accrue a residual: a shape with (i) a unit whose reproduction is bounded, (ii) a currency of heritable variation plus differential reproduction, (iii) a nameable ground that settles whether evolution occurred, and (iv) a timing index that the ground must date. The sought-for shape is written down now so a later preference for "membrane" cannot install itself as an unrefusable term.

## 1. Residual and domain census

### 1.1 R by arity, symmetry and scale

- **Arity:** two co-present conditions (a replicating informational polymer, a boundary that bounds its reproduction) plus a timing index. This is a **union compound**, not one hard problem (Fig 9 :55).
- **Symmetry:** the relation is not a symmetric join. A boundary acts on the replicator's dispersion by containing it; the replicator does not bound the boundary at the same level. At the next level the two co-produce a new unit, which is recursion rather than symmetry (Fig 12 :104 "A settled molecule becomes an atom for whatever composes over it.").
- **Scale:** molecular (RNA strands) and protocellular (boundary plus contents). Two scales in contact, so the framework's cross-scale rule is relevant but, as F1 shows, it governs a different quantity than a lipid bilayer.
- **Slot index:** per Fig 1 :19 "The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated." The three slots, replication, boundary and timing, keep separate verdicts and are never averaged into one yes/no.

Dependency order (Fig 3): D1 and D2 upstream of D3, and D3 upstream of D4. You cannot ask when encapsulation happened until you know what encapsulation does. Refusal before mint (formula step 2) also applies: refuse the bundled "yes/no from the start" before trying to answer it.

## 3. Findings

### F1 - The figure called "The Membrane" is not a physical membrane (strain, re-census)

Fig 4 :21 "The membrane is the boundary between the sizes, and the rule about what may cross it. Not a wall: a filter with a direction."
Fig 4 :22 "Only methods cross up. Only head starts come down."

The figure's membrane sits between one agent and a population. It filters what crosses a **scale boundary**: methods upward, head starts downward. A lipid bilayer sits between a protocell and its environment. The two share a word and nothing else at first reading. Pressing Fig 4 into service as evidence that a physical membrane was required would be a strain, and the strain test says the line governs another quantity, so re-census.

**Re-census:** the physical boundary belongs to D2 and is governed by Fig 11 (habitat substitution, contact) and Fig 13 (containment as a state of space). Fig 4 stays in the census for the outreach question (what may cross between a derivation and a reader), not for the chemistry question.

**Verdict:** Fig 4 does not bear on whether RNA evolution needed a membrane. Single-source reading retracted; see §6. Mode `general`.

**Refuter:** show that the SVG's arrow directions (`carry up` / `send down`) denote material transport across a physical lipid boundary rather than method/head-start flow between an individual and a population.

### F2 - Naked replication is a legal cycle; the figures do not require a compartment for replication as such

Fig 12 :121 "The output re-enters as input."
Fig 12 :119 "A part that survives its own use."
Fig 12 :121 "The sign of the loop decides whether it closes, and a chain cannot close by construction."

A self-replicating RNA is exactly these two structures: a cycle, whose output re-enters as input, and a catalyst, a part that survives its own use. The figure says the loop closes when the sign is right and a chain cannot close by construction. It does not say the loop needs a wall. Containment may help the loop close in a messy world, but that is a claim about the habitat (F3), not about the cycle's legality.

**Verdict:** `general`, structural. Nothing in the six-term set forbids evolution in solution at the level of a single self-replicating cycle. Corroborated, not decided, by Joyce and Szostak 2018 ("Simple processes of natural selection can occur in solution") and Slootbeek et al. 2022 ("In theory, compartmentalization is not required for Darwinian evolution"). Triangulated across Fig 12 and two low-overlap world sources.

**Refuter:** derive from the figures a rule that every cycle closing under a catalyst requires an enclosing boundary. No such rule exists in the text; if one is produced, F2 falls.

### F3 - Capability belongs to the replicator together with its habitat

Fig 11 :39 "capability is a property of agent-and-habitat, never of the agent alone"
Fig 11 :40 "An improvement that does not change contact changes nothing, however much it improves."
Fig 11 :66 "Isolation is not removal of the habitat. It is substitution of one habitat for another."
Fig 11 :61 "Introduction is available; exclusion is not."

The figure's unit of capability is agent-and-habitat. "RNA alone evolves" is a truncated unit in the same sense that "an agent alone is capable" is truncated. A compartment is a **substituted habitat**: it changes local concentration, keeps a catalyst near its products, and makes a group of relatives a unit that a parasite cannot freely enter. Fig 11 :61 says introduction is available and exclusion is not, which matches the field's point that a membrane excludes parasites only by being a boundary that can be added, while it cannot subtract the chemistry that runs inside it.

**Verdict:** `specified`, triangulated (Fig 11 plus Joyce and Szostak 2018 and Slootbeek et al. 2022, which are low-overlap with the framework). The shape is licensed: a boundary that changes contact is part of the unit of capability. **The chemistry is not licensed.** The figures supply no term for lipid, coacervate or pore.

**Refuter:** exhibit an agent-only improvement in RNA replication that changes the ground (increases differential reproduction in a closed system with no boundary change). Fig 11 :40 says such an improvement changes nothing; a real counterexample would refute the capability claim and with it this finding.

### F4 - Replicator plus boundary is a union compound with its own loop

Fig 9 :55 "a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either"
Fig 8 :45 "Two frames together reach what neither reaches alone."
Fig 12 :5 "A set of ingredients is not one."

The two conditions, replication and boundedness, are not additive. A lever that only improves replication clears the region where replication is the binding condition and leaves the boundary condition standing (Fig 9 :55). The union is where the surplus lives: a protocell reaches differential reproduction of a *bounded* lineage, which neither naked replication (diffusing products) nor bare containment (no heredity) reaches alone. Fig 12 :5 is the notation-level version: the ingredients are not the arrangement, and the boundary is part of the arrangement.

**Verdict:** `specified`, triangulated across Fig 8, Fig 9 and Fig 12. The compound has its own loop and its own ground, so it must be settled separately and never averaged with D1.

**Refuter:** show that boundedness and replication are independent conditions whose effects add linearly, so a single lever clears both and the residue is not categorically different.

### F5 - Containment is in the six-term set; no seventh primitive is licensed

Fig 13 :10 "near, far, between, contained"
Fig 6 :21 "MINT composes inside the closure and can never add an atom."

Space, a state term, already includes "contained". A compartment is therefore a spatial condition composed with the other kinds, not a new primitive. The framework returns a shape (a bounded unit); naming the chemical boundary is a binding from the chemistry frame onto a held term, provenanced as such, not a derivation and not a new atom.

**Verdict:** `general` that containment is licensed, `gap` at figure level for which chemical boundary. Single-source for the figure definition (Fig 13); the chemical claim is explicitly left to the ground.

**Refuter:** derive a seventh primitive for "membrane" from the other six, or show that containment cannot be expressed by the space term as given.

### F6 - Describe the residual before naming the frame

Fig 9 :27-28 "describe it before you go looking, or any frame you pick will seem to fit"
Fig 9 :32 "you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself"

If we begin with "lipid membrane" and then map the residual onto it, any field will seem to fit, because the frame was chosen first. The correct order is to describe the effect: *differential reproduction of a bounded heritable unit, protected from diffusion and parasites*. Only then look for frames whose closure predicts that shape. The figures return a **kind of thing** (a boundary that changes contact), never a chemical name.

**Verdict:** `general` structural limit, triangulated (Fig 9 plus the canonical figures README). Mode noted because the finding constrains method, not chemistry.

**Refuter:** produce a figure line that names a specific prebiotic compartment chemistry. There is none; the set is domain-agnostic.

### F7 - "From the start" is a genuine `gap`, not a silence

Fig 6 :34 "Where nothing is, there is no edge to extend from, and the search for a proto-instrument is a search for one that is already there."
Fig 8 :39 "So a search that finds nothing is never a proof of absence."
Fig 13 :134 "Unreachable is a verdict here, not a silence"

The historical question asks for temporal order: did encapsulation precede, coincide with, or follow the origin of heritable RNA replication? The figures contain no temporal index for this event and no instrument that could date it. Formula step 7 distinguishes underivable-in-principle from unreachable-at-this-budget; here the answer is reachable in principle by geology but unreachable at this budget from the figures. Fig 6 :34 says there is no edge to extend from when nothing is already returning a reading. The ground is the rock record plus chronological experiment, and the figures do not supply it.

The world sources **conflict** here, which is expected and is recorded rather than resolved by voting: Ma 2017 places higher-level Darwinian evolution after membrane enclosure; Le Vay and Mutschler 2019 say encapsulation may have occurred at any stage; Slootbeek et al. 2022 say compartmentalization is not strictly required. Per the canonical rule, a conflict sends us back to the census, not to a flipped verdict. The census says D4 is a `gap`.

**Verdict:** `gap`. "Unreachable is a verdict, not a silence."

**Refuter:** a ground reading that dates encapsulation relative to the origin of heritable replication (a rock-record marker, or an experiment that fixes the order under plausible conditions) would close this gap. Until one exists, no timing claim may be labelled `grounded`.

## 4. Pre-registered refuter (written before the conclusion)

Required before any outreach: a falsifier written before the run is a design instrument.

The shape claim is refuted if any of the following holds:

1. A multi-function, parasite-resistant, open-ended RNA evolutionary system is demonstrated in well-mixed solution with **no boundary of any kind**, so that differential reproduction of a bounded lineage is not required (breaks F3 and F4 at the ground).
2. The framework must admit a membrane as a new primitive, or containment is shown not expressible by the space term (breaks Fig 13 and F5).
3. A ground reading dates encapsulation unambiguously relative to the origin of heritable replication (closes F7 and converts the timing verdict from `gap` to `grounded`).

If (1) or (2) fires, the structural claim is withdrawn. If (3) fires, the timing gap closes and the answer is updated. Until one is run, no part of this may be labelled `grounded`.

## 7. Updated verdict

The residue is a shape with three separate domain verdicts and one gap. Naked RNA replication is a legal cycle and needs no boundary as such (D1, `general`). A compartment is a substituted habitat that changes contact, so capability for higher-level Darwinian evolution belongs to the replicator together with a boundary (D2 and D3, `specified`, triangulated). The figures return that shape, not a chemical name, and do not date the event (D4, `gap`). The literal question, "did it require a membrane or other compartment **from the start**", is therefore not a yes/no the figures can give: functional necessity for higher-level evolution is supported as a shape, chemical identity and timing are owed to the ground. Unreachable is a verdict, not a silence.
