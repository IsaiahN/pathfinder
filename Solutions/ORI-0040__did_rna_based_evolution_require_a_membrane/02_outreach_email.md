**Use:** one-to-one, or as a cover note attached to a submission through the public venues in `03_public_contact_points.csv`. **Not** for bulk send.

**Hard precondition:** do not send until the four domain verdicts in `01_figure_grounded_audit.md` are kept separate in your own copy. If the note collapses into "yes, a membrane was needed from the start", the two-stage offer collapses on contact, because that is a claim the figures do not make.

**Subject:** One checkable shape about RNA evolution and compartments, before any theory

> Hi [name],
>
> I have one specific output I would like you to check against what you already know. I am not asking you to read a theory first, and I am not asking you to agree. If the output does not survive, there is nothing else to look at.
>
> **The residual, in effect terms.** Early RNA work shows that individual reactions can happen in the lab. The open question is what makes *sustained* Darwinian evolution possible: heritable variation plus differential reproduction that does not leak away. Two conditions bind at once, a replicating informational polymer and something that bounds its reproduction. A single lever that improves replication leaves the boundary condition standing.
>
> **The shape (`specified`, not `grounded`).** A self-replicating RNA is a legal cycle whose output re-enters as input and whose catalyst survives its own use, so nothing in the framework requires a compartment for replication as such. But capability belongs to the replicator together with its habitat, and a compartment is a substituted habitat that changes contact: it changes local concentration, keeps a catalyst near its products, and makes a lineage something a parasite cannot freely enter. So the shape is that a **boundary of some kind** becomes part of the unit of selection once cooperation and parasites matter. The framework returns a shape, not a name. It does not say fatty acid vesicle, coacervate, or mineral pore, and it does not date when the boundary appeared.
>
> **What I am not claiming.** I am not claiming that a lipid membrane was required from the very first replicating molecule. I am not claiming the figures settle the geological order. I am not claiming a new primitive called a membrane. And I am not asking you to accept the framework as a theory of everything.
>
> **What would refute the shape:**
> 1. a multi-function, parasite-resistant, open-ended RNA evolutionary system in well-mixed solution with no boundary of any kind, so differential reproduction of a bounded lineage is not required;
> 2. a demonstration that boundedness is not expressible as a spatial condition and must be added as a new primitive;
> 3. a ground reading that dates encapsulation unambiguously relative to the origin of heritable replication (this would close the timing gap rather than refute the functional shape).
>
> I wrote these down before sending this. A one-line "this fails because..." is more useful to me than agreement. If you would rather not engage, no reply is needed.
>
> **If it survives, and only then:** the sources are below. The figures, not my summary of them, are the authority.
> - Verbatim figure text with line numbers (local framework file): `FRAMEWORK/reference/03-FIGURES_TEXT.md`
> - Canonical usage rule (local framework file): `FRAMEWORK/reference/FIGURES_README_canonical.md`
> - Origins of life and compartment literature, peer reviewed and open access: Joyce and Szostak 2018, https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6120706/ ; Slootbeek et al. 2022, https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9536485/ ; Le Vay and Mutschler 2019, https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7289000/
>

Write down what would refute your result *before* you run it, and state the mode of what you get. A reported failure is more useful to me than a reported success.
