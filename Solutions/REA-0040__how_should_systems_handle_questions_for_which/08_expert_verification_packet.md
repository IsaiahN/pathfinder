# Expert verification packet: unanswered questions

**What this is:** a claim about how a system should respond when no available evidence settles a question, stated in a form a specialist can check. Mode, derivation and refuters are given before the conclusion.

**Mode: `general` on the structural shape, `specified` on the protocol, not `grounded`.** No measurement settles an "ought". This is a simulated derivation checked for internal consistency against the figures, not a ground reading. It cannot settle the question it reports on.

## 1. The claim, in one paragraph

"No evidence supports a definite answer" is not one condition but a union of at least three: the answer is unfound within the system's current reach, no instrument currently returns anything that fails to resolve, or the premise is false. A defensible protocol first splits the union, then runs the cheapest operation (independent search at the same reach, justified by the idempotence of closure), then tests the two opposed routes separately (sharpen an instrument that already returns a failing reading, or import a description from a frame whose closure differs), and owes a refusal rather than an explanation when the premise is false. The output is a mode-labelled status with a refuter and a lineage, never a filler name. Agreement among frames does not supply the missing evidence.

## 2. Three checkable sub-claims

### C1: the no-answer question is a union and must be split before search
- **Statement:** treating the three conditions as one hard question wastes budget and silently loses the distinction that selects the next operation.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 9 :4-5, "Disagreements that do not shrink with effort, where each rule keeps working well on a different subset, are not one hard question but several well-formed ones. Split it rather than search." Also Fig 9 :54-55, "when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again."
- **What to check:** are the three conditions genuinely different in their actors and currency, as `01` §1's census claims? Or does one rule cover every subset, making it a partition?
- **Refuter:** show a partition, or show that the three conditions co-vary so that clearing one clears all. Then a single search is right and C1 fails.

### C2: SEARCHED is first; new reach needs either an edge or an import, and "not found" is not "absent"
- **Statement:** repeated search inside the same closure adds no atom, so more search is owed only for unreachable-at-budget, not for underivable-in-principle. INWARD requires a proto-instrument already returning something that fails to resolve; OUTWARD requires the residual in effect terms plus source gate and shadow test. The two tracks are opposed and not to be read against one another.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 8 :32, "Closure is idempotent. Composing inside a closed set never leaves it, so no search, however long, adds an atom." Fig 6 :49, "more search at the same reach: is it in here and unfound? The closure does not change. Same atoms, same wall, more of the space actually visited." Fig 6 :34-35, "The question is not whether a sensor could exist. It is whether anything, at any resolution, is already returning something that fails to resolve. Where nothing is, there is no edge to extend from, and the search for a proto-instrument is a search for one that is already there." Fig 8 :39, "So a search that finds nothing is never a proof of absence."
- **What to check:** does the idempotence claim hold in the intended formalisation? Is there any case where sharpening an instrument answers whether another frame holds the term, which would collapse the two tracks?
- **Refuter:** a frame whose closure changes under repeated search with the same atoms; an instrument specified from a pure description with no prior resolution-failing reading; or a case where failing to find X proves X absent. Any one breaks C2.

### C3: a false premise is refused, and the honest output is a status (not a filler)
- **Statement:** REFUTED is a fifth bin that sorts by what is wrong, not by what is missing. What it owes is a negation, a unary operator that removes a term's standing to be composed over. The report is a verdict with a mode and a lineage, not a guess.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 5 :53, "refuted: a term we hold is wrong; a term is owed that says so". Fig 5 :55, "What it removes is not a term but a term's standing to be composed over. Nothing leaves the library. What falls is the reach." Fig 13 :134, "Unreachable is a verdict here, not a silence,". Also the Operators table: "a refutation is a negation, which is why a vocabulary without it can only accumulate" (table, not a numbered figure line).
- **What to check:** can a false premise be withdrawn without a unary refusal operator? Is "unreachable" actually a verdict in the framework's output layer, or only a silence described after the fact?
- **Refuter:** a working system that withdraws a false premise's standing with no negation operator; a figure line that places REFUTED among the four missing-thing bins; or a one-word status that preserves the bin, the instrument and the reversal condition so the round-trip gap is zero. Any one breaks C3.

## 3. What is explicitly not claimed

- Not that a system should always produce a definite answer, and not that "unknown" is a forbidden user-facing word. The claim is about preserving distinctions, not about vocabulary.
- Not that more search eventually settles everything. Idempotence and the proto-instrument rule say otherwise.
- Not that any frame that answers is a legitimate import. A differing closure, a provenance record and a shadow test are required.
- Not that agreement among many recipients corroborates anything. Fig 2 :35, "Alignment is triangulation, not negotiation. A mirror cannot correct you."
- Not that this derivation is `grounded`. No measurement of an "ought" was taken, and no ground reading was used.
- Not a ruling from Isaiah. `60_FIGURES_AS_METALAWS.md`: the figures are the ground for design questions; the author is the anchor for what they genuinely cannot settle.

## 5. What would settle it

For any specific instance, apply the six ground checks in `07_the_tether_answer.md` §4: is there an anchor that does not move; is the term in closure and unfound; is an instrument already returning a failing reading; does a differing frame predict the residual's shape with provenance; is the premise false; is the channel stale. The general protocol itself is not settled by a measurement, and this packet does not claim otherwise.

## 6. What an expert can check in twenty minutes

1. Read Fig 9 :4-5, Fig 8 :32 and :39, Fig 6 :34-35 and :49, and Fig 5 :53. Six short passages.
2. Check C1: are the three conditions a union, or is one rule enough?
3. Check C2: does closure idempotence hold in your formalisation, and can an instrument be built with no prior failing reading?
4. Check C3: is a unary refusal operator necessary to withdraw a false premise?
5. Name one case where a crowd of agreeing frames predicted the ground as well as an anchored instrument, or say why none exists.
6. Send the refutation. A failure is more useful than an endorsement.
