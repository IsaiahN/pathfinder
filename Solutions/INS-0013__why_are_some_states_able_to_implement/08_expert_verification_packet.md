# Expert verification packet - the Tether on consistent policy implementation

**What this is:** a claim in a form a public-administration researcher or empirically minded practitioner can check. Mode, derivation and refuters are stated before the conclusion, so a reader knows what to look for and what would settle it.

**Mode: `specified` - not `grounded`.** No prospective delivery measurement is claimed. This is a shape and a route, not a result. It is a simulated derivation checked for internal consistency against the figures, not a ground reading (`05_what_shape_means.md` section 7).

**Boundary of the check.** The first three sections are the whole stage-1 ask. Provenance, links and the method offer come later and only if a verdict on stage 1 is recorded first.

## 1. The claim, in one paragraph

Consistent implementation is a relation between a state and the field it acts on, not a stock the state owns. Two conditions have to hold at once: the state's channel to what actually happens in the field stays reachable, meaning its readings of delivery, compliance and case outcomes are not stale, saturated, pooled into a national average, or replaced by a report that measures only the state's own activity; and its reach keeps moving outward through contact with situations whose ways of working differ from its own, so it is not simply re-running the same standard procedure at a larger scale. These are two different failure modes with two different instruments, not two degrees of one problem. A vocabulary that can name cases but cannot say what would count as progress leaves the dependency chain broken at the middle link, which is why even basic decisions can fail. The procedure that produced earlier capacity can change sign when the field changes, so a strength and a rigidity can be the same object. Realized delivery per case is the anchor, not internal agreement or a governance ranking. The claim is a shape that any candidate answer must have; it does not name which reform, staffing rule or institutional design wins.

## 2. Three checkable sub-claims

### C1 - Framework-internal; checkable by reading Figures 11, 10 and 2

- **Statement:** capability is a property of state-and-habitat, so consistency cannot be explained by the state alone; quick collapse is a failure of the channel, not a decay of the field; the anchor is realized delivery, not internal agreement or a perception index.
- **Source, verbatim:**
  - Fig 11 :39 - *"So the answerable question is not what intelligence is, but what makes a system capable. And capability is a property of agent-and-habitat, never of the agent alone."*
  - Fig 11 :40 - *"A system cannot be made more capable by improving the agent in isolation. An improvement that does not change contact changes nothing, however much it improves."*
  - Fig 10 :24 - *"the ground does not decay, the channel does"*; Fig 10 :26 - *"saturated metrics, pooled readings"*
  - Fig 2 :29 - *"An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks."* The same line names *"a measurement, an experiment, a counter, a market, a patient outcome"* as instances. Fig 2 :37-38: *"instruments can be wrong on axes they do not measure."*
- **Refuter:** a state whose consistent delivery is unchanged across a habitat substitution that removes what the field pays for or permits; or a state that loses the ability to carry out basic decisions while both bracket fidelity and field pressure measure high and non-declining. Either fires against C1.

### C2 - Framework-internal derivation plus one field-facing check

- **Statement:** internal agreement is not evidence, and a state that keeps the same closure stops progressing; renewal of reach requires contact with a closure that differs, because composition inside a closure cannot add an atom; the middle link of the dependency chain is where translation into local objectives fails.
- **Source, verbatim:**
  - Fig 7 :13 - *"agreement compounds, evidence does not"*; Fig 7 :36 - *"A chain of mirrors is one long room. The next room must differ from the last, and match the residual it surfaced."*
  - Fig 8 :32 - *"Closure is idempotent. Composing inside a closed set never leaves it, so no search, however long, adds an atom. This one is definitional: it is what closure means."*
  - Fig 6 :23 - *"No frame escapes its own closure. The bound moves only on contact with a frame whose closure differs."*
  - Fig 3 :26 - *"The link between them, having terms for what a situation offers and being able to say what would count as progress, has neither, so it attracts the least attention and the fewest instruments."*
- **What a researcher can check against their own knowledge:** Scott reports that state legibility projects *"overlay, and often supersede, local practices"* and that *"such local knowledge, however quaint, is illegible"*. Lipsky reports that *"policy implementation in the end comes down to the people [the street-level bureaucrats] who actually implement it"*. Do these two observations not already name the channel and the import described in Fig 6 :23 and Fig 3 :26? A "no" to both questions is a real refutation of the mapping.
- **Refuter:** a state that keeps the same closure across two decades and keeps producing new, field-verified readings without any import from a differing closure; or a demonstration that a state renewed its reach purely by internal composition with no new atom. Either breaks idempotence and C2 with it.

### C3 - Structure and competing explanations

- **Statement:** the union of conditions must be unbundled before a lever is chosen; two states with identical atoms but different bonds are isomers, so an institutional inventory alone cannot predict consistency; the strength-to-rigidity flip follows from valence being relative to the residual; and a perception index is an instrument, not the ground.
- **Source, verbatim:**
  - Fig 9 :57 - *"What remains is smaller and it is also categorically different: a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either."*
  - Fig 12 :117 - *"isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."*
  - Fig 11 :50 - *"The same actor is a benefactor to one residual and a malfactor to another, so mutualism and parasitism are relations rather than kinds."*
  - Fig 12 :119 - *"catalyst. A part that survives its own use. Spent and regenerating parts behave differently across uses, and nothing records which a term is."*
- **Refuter:** one intervention that restores both conditions in both failure types and leaves no categorically different residue; or two states with identical atoms and identical bonds whose consistency diverges with no difference in contact or imported atoms, which would move the explanation out of composition and into habitat alone.

## 5. What would settle it

A prospective, per-case study that measures C1 and C2 separately, records bonds as well as atoms, and reports which condition failed before the decline. Concretely:

1. **Channel test:** does the state's reading of delivery degrade while the field keeps paying someone else? Indicators: stale percepts, saturated metrics, pooled readings (Fig 10 :25-26).
2. **Pressure and fidelity test:** are bracket fidelity and field pressure both nonzero at each handoff? Fig 7 :37-39 says zero either stops the chain.
3. **Import test:** did any new atom enter the state's reach from a closure that differs, before the decade of consistency? If not, C2 is refuted.
4. **Vocabulary test:** does the administrative vocabulary name cases only, or can it state what would count as progress in the changed situation (Fig 12 :111, :115)?
5. **Source-discriminating instrument:** can the study distinguish a paying habitat from a maintained channel from a closed reach from a reporting artefact when all produce the same compliance reading? The source essay says a reading *"cannot comment on the source"*.

1. Read Fig 11 :39-40, Fig 10 :24, Fig 2 :29, Fig 7 :13 and :36, Fig 8 :32, Fig 6 :23, Fig 3 :26, Fig 9 :57 and Fig 12 :117. Nine short passages, about nine minutes.

4. Send the refutation. A failure is more useful than an endorsement.
