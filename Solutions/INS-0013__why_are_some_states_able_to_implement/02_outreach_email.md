# Outreach draft - validate the output first, then decide about the theory

**Use:** one to one, or as a cover note attached to a submission through a public venue in `03_public_contact_points.csv`. **Not** for bulk send. No personal emails are used; the recipient must have an opt-in or public institutional route.

**Hard precondition:** the stage-1 output must survive the recipient's check before any theory is shown. The output here is a `specified` shape with no delivery measurement, so the legitimate ask is a refutation of the shape, never endorsement of it. If the recipient cannot be shown a failure condition they can actually test, do not send.

---

**Subject:** Check one claim about why states implement consistently before you decide whether any of this is worth reading

> Hi [name],
>
> I have one narrow claim about policy implementation that I would like you to check against what you already know. I am not asking you to read a theory first, and I am not asking you to agree with anything. I am asking for a failure. If the claim does not survive, there is nothing else to look at.
>
> **The claim (`specified`, not `grounded`):** consistent implementation is not a stock a state owns. It is a relation between the state and the field it acts on, and two conditions have to hold at once:
>
> 1. the state's channel to what actually happens in the field stays reachable, meaning its readings of delivery, compliance and case outcomes are not stale, saturated, pooled into a national average, or replaced by a compliance report that only measures the state's own activity;
> 2. the state's reach keeps moving outward through contact with situations whose ways of working differ from its own, frontline staff, local knowledge, courts, citizens and other agencies, so it is not simply re-running the same standard procedure at a larger scale.
>
> The claim is that these are two different failure modes with two different instruments, not two degrees of one problem. A state that has lost contact needs a different remedy from a state that has exhausted its reach. The further claim is that the procedure which produced earlier capacity can change sign when the field changes, so a strength and a rigidity can be the same object. The anchor is realized delivery per case, not a governance ranking or a ministerial claim.
>
> **What I am not claiming:** that I can name which reform, which staffing rule or which institutional design produces consistency, that any particular country is a case in point, that this is settled by evidence, or that it is the only account of state capacity. I am offering a shape any candidate answer would have to have. A shape is not a name.
>
> **What would refute it, written down before I sent this:**
> 1. a state's consistent delivery is unchanged when the field stops paying for or permitting what the state does;
> 2. a state loses the ability to carry out basic decisions while its reading fidelity and the field's pressure on it both measure high and are not declining;
> 3. a state keeps the same administrative closure for two decades and keeps producing new, field-verified readings without taking in anything new from outside;
> 4. a routine's measured value stays the same across a documented change in what the field rewards;
> 5. one intervention restores both conditions in both failure types and leaves nothing categorically different behind.
>
> If any one holds, I withdraw the claim. A single line saying which one holds and why is more useful to me than agreement. If you would rather not engage, no reply is needed.
>
> **If it survives, and only then:** I can send the three public sources below. The figures, not my summary of them, are the authority.
>
> - Figure text with line numbers (I can point to the exact lines): the local canonical figure text in this solution folder.
> - Formula and usage rule: the `figures` folder on the public `general-agent` branch of the `tether` repository.
> - The essays on source, search shape and the prestige/action separation: my Substack archive.
>
> I will send URLs only after you tell me what you would count as a failure.
>
> **If you want to use the machinery:** the formula is public. Run it on a problem you know well and can check against a real ground. Write down what would refute your result *before* you run it and state the mode of what you get. A reported failure is more useful to me than a reported success.
>
> Thanks,
> [name]

## Why this version is ordered this way

| the move | figure-grounded reason |
|---|---|
| Residual and refuter first, theory last | *"Bring a characterised residual, never the source."* (Fig 10 :52). The recipient checks the claim without holding the framework's interior |
| Do not ask for agreement | *"Alignment is triangulation, not negotiation. A mirror cannot correct you."* (Fig 2 :35) |
| Two failure modes kept separate | *"a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either"* (Fig 9 :57); a blended capacity score is a pooled reading (Fig 10 :26) |
| Pre-registered refuter | *"a falsifier written before the run is a design instrument, not only an honesty device"* |
| Mode stated on the claim | *"Mode. general · specified · grounded. State which."* (`THE_FORMULA.md`) |
| No name for the winning reform | *"you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself"* (Fig 9 :32) |
| Delivery, not a governance ranking, as the anchor | *"An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks."* (Fig 2 :29); *"instruments can be wrong on axes they do not measure."* (Fig 2 :37-38) |
| Links only after a verdict | Fig 10 :42, *"A verifier that can reconstruct the claim cannot verify it."* Once the recipient has the framework, their later agreement is not corroboration (Fig 2 :40) |
| Prestige kept out of the ask | *"Prestige can fund an action. It can give an action a budget to be tried. But it cannot settle an action. Only the ground can settle it."* (*The Recursive Self-Improvement Trap*, read 2026-11-23) |
| Offer the formula as a method, not an answer | *"Only methods cross up. Only head starts come down."* (Fig 4 :22) |
| Ask the user for their own refuter | Formula, "Direction": a frame cannot check the direction of its own question; make the sought-for shape an ordinary term |

## Sending rules

- One recipient at a time. No distribution lists, no mail-merge, no forwarding chain.
- Use only a public institutional contact, a call for submissions, or an address the person has published themselves. Record the source and date.
- A Track B style reply is a conversation, never corroboration. If a recipient returns a refutation, copy it into `11_audit.md` with provenance and have the auditor seat re-evaluate it as if it came from any other source.
- Do not follow up with the theory after a "no". An interest in the outcome is unavoidable; an interest in the reading is disqualifying (Fig 10 :59).
