## 2. What the wider literature bears on the question

**One evidence pool, one witness.** The state-capacity reference page and the governance-indicators page both sit in the institutional and measurement literature. They agree that capacity and governance can be described and scored, and they draw on overlapping traditions (World Bank indicators, comparative politics, public administration). Per Fig 2 :31, *"Independence, between two frames, is how little they draw on the same evidence."* Their agreement is one witness, not two.

- The state-capacity page defines capacity as *"the ability of a government to accomplish policy goals"* and distinguishes territorial, economic, infrastructural and symbolic types. Read as an agent-only stock, that **conflicts** with Fig 11 :40. It was re-mapped to Γ and the channel domain rather than used as the verdict (F1, F9 in `01_figure_grounded_audit.md`).
- The governance-indicators page records that WGI is *"a compilation of the perceptions of a very diverse group of respondents"* and quotes the critique that the indexes *"do not discriminate usefully among different aspects of governance"* and *"merely reflects perceptions of the quality of governance more broadly"*. This **corroborates** F6: a perception index is an instrument, not the ground, and instruments *"can be wrong on axes they do not measure"* (Fig 2 :37-38). It also records the WGI authors' rebuttal, which is noted rather than hidden.

**Low-overlap witnesses**, each from a different field and each read directly:

1. **Public administration and street-level implementation.** Lipsky's work, via the Wikipedia article, reports that *"policy implementation in the end comes down to the people [the street-level bureaucrats] who actually implement it"* and that resource pressure produces *"simplified routines for processing cases"*. This corroborates F2 and F8 at an individual and organisational stratum. Fig 4 :20 requires the anchor to be re-named at state scale, so it corroborates the mechanism shape, not a state-level measurement.
2. **Political anthropology and development.** Scott's *Seeing Like a State* (1998) and his 2010 Cato essay report that states *"seek to force administrative legibility on their subjects by homogenizing them and creating standards"* and that *"such local knowledge, however quaint, is illegible"*. This corroborates F3: a state's administrative categories are a dictionary that must become a grammar, and legibility has a cost. It is a different field and evidence pool from governance measurement.
3. **Cognitive science and decision theory.** Simon's bounded rationality, via the Wikipedia article, holds that decision makers *"select a decision that is satisfactory rather than optimal"* and describes the scissors of cognitive limits and environment structure. This corroborates F4: satisficing inside a fixed library needs an import from a differing closure to move the wall.
4. **Sociology of complex systems and safety science.** Perrow's *Normal Accidents* (1984) holds that *"multiple and unexpected failures are built into society's complex and tightly coupled systems"* and that *"many failures relate to organizations rather than technology"*. This corroborates F4 and F5: tight coupling plus a closed library is the configuration that fails, and small beginnings cascade.

**Framework-internal essays** are used for the canonical reading of "shape" in `05_what_shape_means.md` only. They are not independent world witnesses, because their author wrote the figures. That overlap is recorded in the ledger.

## 3. Distribution rules

Figure-grounded reasons for how material is offered:

- **Fig 4 :22:** *"Only methods cross up. Only head starts come down."* Send the generator, the residual and the refuter, not the conclusion. A recording carried upward *"looks like knowledge and is a description of one occasion"* (Fig 4 :25).
- **Fig 10 :42:** *"A verifier that can reconstruct the claim cannot verify it."* A recipient who has absorbed the framework before being asked for a verdict has joined the frame being checked.
- **Fig 9 :27-28:** *"describe it before you go looking, or any frame you pick will seem to fit."* Ask what they would expect to see if the shape claim were true before showing the source.
- **Fig 2 :40:** agreement from a shared pool is worthless as a verdict. Later agreement about the framework is never pooled as corroboration.
- **Fig 10 :59:** *"an interest in the outcome is unavoidable, an interest in the reading is disqualifying."* Declare the stake; do not ask for belief.

**A hard practical constraint.** The institutional venues in `03_public_contact_points.csv` are scholarly and professional bodies with their own publication rules. This whole solution is a simulated derivation produced with an LLM and is labelled as such everywhere. It must **not** be submitted as an authored empirical or theoretical article. Any submission must be rewritten and owned by a human author who can defend the derivation, with the AI contribution disclosed under the applicable policy. The legitimate use here is as a private checking instrument: post the residual and the refuter, invite a human to run the check, and let that human author whatever they conclude.

**Order in any reply:** (1) the shape claim and the pre-registered refuter; (2) the question "what would you count as a failure here?"; (3) only after a verdict is recorded, the source links with the note that the figures, not the summary, are the authority; (4) the formula as a reusable method, with the instruction that each user writes their own refuter and states their own mode.

## 4. The two-stage offer, compressed

**Stage 1.** Send `08_expert_verification_packet.md` sections 1 to 3 only: the one-paragraph claim, the three checkable sub-claims with their verbatim figure lines and refuters, and the non-claims. No provenance bundle, no essay links.

**Stage 2, opt-in only.** If the recipient is curious and has already recorded a verdict on stage 1, offer the three public links: the figure text, the formula and usage rule, and the essays. Their later agreement is a conversation, not corroboration (Fig 2 :40).

**Stage 3.** Offer the formula as a method, never as an answer (Fig 4 :22). Each user writes a refuter before running it and states the mode of what they get.

**Stop rule.** If stage 1 fails, the theory is not offered. A failure is more useful than an endorsement.

**Hosting.** Nothing here is hosted or sent by this agent. The human operator hosts the packet on an opt-in page and sends one to one. Prefer a download page that lets a recipient decline contact over an attachment pushed to a list.
