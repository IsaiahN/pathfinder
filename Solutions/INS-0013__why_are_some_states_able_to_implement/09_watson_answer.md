## QUESTION

Why are some states able to implement policies consistently while others struggle to carry out even basic decisions?

Restated as an effect: why does R = |Γ(b,a) - o′| stay small across many decisions in some state-slots and grow quickly in others? R is indexed per case and per policy, never averaged (Fig 1 :19). Anchor: realized delivery per case, with a measurement, an experiment, a counter or a patient outcome named as anchors in Fig 2 :29.

## DOMAIN CENSUS

Arity: a three-place relation (state library Γ, action, field return), indexed by slot and ordered by time; carries an ordered bond and a comparison bond, not symmetric ones. Symmetry: "consistent" is a threshold relative to a state's own record and to other states (Fig 12 :87). Scale: the single agency loop, the population of agencies and jurisdictions, and the time channel across years and regimes; the membrane between scales is lossy (Fig 4).

## VERDICT (from the figures)

**The figure-derived verdict:** consistent implementation holds when both C1 (a maintained channel to what actually happens in the field) and C2 (renewed reach through contact with a differing closure) hold, and collapses quickly when either fails; the routine that produced the earlier capacity can change sign when the residual changes. The verdict is the union kept separate per domain, never averaged. Mode `specified`. The institutional evidence pool corroborates the two-condition shape only after re-mapping the agent-only reading of state capacity to Γ and the information channel; the conflict was returned to the census and did not flip the verdict. Low-overlap fields corroborate the mechanism, not the state-level fact. Which reform wins is a declared `gap`, not a hidden answer.

- **Cost if wrong:** misdirected diagnosis. A state failing C2 would be sold channel instruments, or a state failing C1 would be told to import more, and a single lever would be applied to a union that needs two. In outreach terms, an expert's twenty minutes would be spent refuting a shape that was never the right shape. The cost is real but bounded and largely reversible.
- **Undoable:** yes. No term is deleted if the claim fails; a refutation is added, and the reach falls because *"something was added rather than because anything was deleted"* (Fig 6 :42-43). The shape and its five falsifiers are recorded in a provenance trail so a later reader can locate the error (Fig 10 :62-64).
- **Reversed by:** any of the five pre-registered falsifiers, in particular (1) consistent delivery unchanged after the field stops paying, (2) basic decisions lost while bracket fidelity and field pressure both measure high, (3) two decades of same closure with new field-verified readings and no import, (4) invariant routine valence across a change of residual, or (5) one intervention restoring both conditions with no categorically different residue. A source with a genuinely different evidence pool showing that per-case consistency is fully predicted by averaged governance scores would also reverse it.
