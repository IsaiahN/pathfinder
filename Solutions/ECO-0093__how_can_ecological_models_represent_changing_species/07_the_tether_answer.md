# The Tether's answer: how ecological models can represent changing interactions and stay testable

**Mode: `specified` — not `grounded`.** This is a shape and a route, not a result. It is a simulated derivation checked against the figures (`05_what_shape_means.md` §7), not a ground reading. No field dataset was analyzed here.

## 1. The residual it answers

An ecological model predicts per species, site and season. Fig 1 :19 "The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated." When interactions change, a fixed-interaction model leaves a live residual on some slots. Adding interaction terms can close those slots, but each term costs bits to state and field actions to settle. The question asks for the shape of a model that tracks change without becoming too expensive to test. It is a **union compound** (Fig 9 :4-5), not one hard problem, because representation and settlement have different grounds.

## 2. The shape

1. **Score the right quantity.** The residual is per species-site-season slot. A pooled fit may be near zero while one slot stays live (Fig 1 :20-21). Parameter count and training R² are frame-internal and cannot decide the verdict.

2. **Enumerate the habitat from contact.** Fig 11 :43 "The habitat is enumerated, never composed." Actors, conditions and relations are read off the world outward from the residual, including indirect cascades (Fig 11 :44, :52). The same actors constitute a different habitat when the residual changes, so the list is rebuilt rather than frozen. This is the figure's habitat, not an ecological niche; the article's "Three mappings offered and declined" explicitly refuses that identification (`05` §4).

3. **Treat changing interactions as relations, not fixed kinds.** Fig 11 :50 "The same actor is a benefactor to one residual and a malfactor to another, so mutualism and parasitism are relations rather than kinds." A coefficient indexed by the measured context is the relation written down. A context-free interaction type is the thing that fails when the residual moves (Fig 11 :51).

4. **Split the union before adding terms.** Fig 9 :54 "And when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again." Species turnover, interaction rewiring and environmental context are different conditions with different mechanisms. Yahaya et al. 2026 report exactly this split in plant-pollinator networks. One pooled rule for all three is the bundled case the figure refuses.

5. **Price both currencies, separately.** Fig 12 :125 "A composition costs in two currencies, and they do not add." Bits price the description; actions price finding out whether it holds (Fig 12 :126). A model that is cheap to write can be ruinous to settle. "It proposes arrangements it cannot afford to test, and the budget is what decides how much of the space is reached at all" (Fig 12 :127). This is the direct answer to "without becoming too complex to test": complexity is not one number, and a parameter count cannot stand in for the field budget.

6. **Admit a term only if it pays.** Fig 5 :43 "A new term is worth having when stating it, plus what remains unexplained after it, costs less than what was unexplained before." A term that fits one season perfectly fails if it does not help elsewhere (Fig 5 :44). The three guards (support, reachability, novelty) are a product, not a checklist (Fig 5 :33, :41).

7. **Keep the substituted-habitat caveat.** Fig 11 :70 "A test harness is a substituted habitat. Which is why a synthetic solve proves wiring and never capability." A simulation checks the wiring; only a field context never used to build it checks capability.

8. **Keep order and refusal in the vocabulary.** Same species and coefficients under a different order can behave differently. Fig 12 :117 "isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour." Abel and Lutscher 2026 find that different orders of emergence produce different instabilities. A vocabulary with only positive coefficients can lower a term and cannot refuse it; operators table :57 "a refutation is a negation, which is why a vocabulary without it can only accumulate."

9. **If the frame cannot hold the change, change the frame honestly.** Fig 13 :115 "The set is closed. The arrangements are not. That gap is why import exists." Fig 8 :32 "Closure is idempotent. Composing inside a closed set never leaves it, so no search, however long, adds an atom. This one is definitional: it is what closure means." The formula's step 7 keeps three questions apart: SEARCHED (more independent search at the same reach), INWARD (sharpen an instrument already returning something), OUTWARD (import a term from a frame whose closure differs, with provenance and a shadow test). Underivable in principle and unreachable at this budget are different facts with different instructions.

10. **The framework returns a shape, not a name.** It says which arrangements are admissible and what each costs. It does not say that a particular model class is the answer. That binding is the modeler's, and must be recorded as such.

## 3. What it explicitly does not say

- Not a theory of everything, and not a final model. There is no last room (Fig 7 :6, :30-31).
- Not that a single formalism wins. No model class is named or ranked.
- Not "all complexity is bad." Complexity in arrangements is expected; unpriced complexity is the failure.
- Not that a simulation is a field test. The substituted-habitat caveat stands (F7).
- Not that more parameters are the remedy for a persistent residual. The refusal branch may be owed before a mint (Formula step 2).
- Not `grounded`. No measurement at any field site was taken here.
- Not a claim that Fig 11's habitat *is* an ecological niche. The architecture essay declines that mapping.

## 4. What would settle it — the ground

A source-discriminating field design, not a further argument. *The Hard Problem is About Source* records that a reading cannot comment on its source and that "the answer is unreached until a better instrument exists." Here the concrete questions are:

- **Slot test:** does a context-relative relation model reduce held-out per-slot residual more than a fixed, context-free interaction matrix across independent seasons? This fires the refuter in `01` item 1.
- **Two-currency test:** record the field actions spent to settle each added interaction term. Does the action count grow with the term count, or stay flat? This fires `01` item 2.
- **Split test:** do separate turnover, rewiring and context loops beat one pooled model on held-out seasons? This fires `01` item 3.
- **Acceptance test:** is a season-specific term admitted by the selection rule when it fails to improve prediction elsewhere? This fires `01` item 4.
- **Substitution test:** does a synthetic-only calibration predict a novel real context as well as a field-calibrated model? This fires `01` item 5.
- **Order test:** do identical ingredients under different application orders behave differently in the field? This fires `01` item 6.
- **Standing falsifiers:** a closed vocabulary that generates a new kind of interaction by arrangement alone, with no import and no instrument change (`01` item 7).

## 5. The honest stop

**Unreached, not unreachable.** The six-term set is "a human's careful guess at reality's compression axes"; declaring it makes the choice visible, not neutral (*They're Scanning Books*). Another sensorium's minimum set need not be these six. The answer stops at the limit of the available field instruments and records the lineage so the stop is checkable. "Indiscernibility is not identity" (*How Intelligence Actually Works*). Fig 8 :39 "So a search that finds nothing is never a proof of absence." No source in this folder establishes which interaction form is right for a particular ecosystem; that is owed to the ground.

## 6. One-paragraph version for outreach

> The Tether does not offer a new ecological model class and does not claim that complexity is simply bad. It says to score the gap per species, site and season rather than as one pooled fit; to enumerate the habitat from contact instead of freezing a standing list of interaction kinds; to treat an interaction's sign as a context-relative relation; to split the compound into turnover, rewiring and context loops before adding terms; to price the bits needed to state a term separately from the field actions needed to settle it; and to admit a term only when it beats the acceptance bargain on held-out slots. It also says a synthetic test is a substituted habitat, so a simulated result proves wiring rather than field capability, and that same species under a different order can behave differently. What it returns is a shape with its failure conditions written in advance, not a name for the winning formalism. The center stays open until a field instrument closes it.
