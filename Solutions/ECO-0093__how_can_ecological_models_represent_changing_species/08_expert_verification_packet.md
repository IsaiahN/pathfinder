# Expert verification packet — the Tether on changing species interactions

**What this is:** a claim in a form an ecological modeler can check. Mode, derivation and refuters are stated before the conclusion, so a reader knows what to look for and what would settle it.

**Mode: `specified` — not `grounded`.** No field dataset is claimed to have settled anything. This is a shape and a route, not a result. It is a simulated derivation checked for internal consistency against the figures, not a ground reading.

## 1. The claim, in one paragraph

Score the residual per species, site and season, not as a pooled fit. Enumerate the habitat from contact with that residual, and treat an interaction's sign as a context-relative relation rather than a fixed kind. Split "changing interactions" into turnover, rewiring and context loops before adding terms. Price the bits needed to state a term separately from the field actions needed to settle it, and admit a term only when it beats the acceptance bargain on held-out slots. Treat a synthetic test as a substituted habitat that proves wiring rather than capability, and keep order and refusal in the vocabulary, because identical ingredients under a different order can behave differently. The framework returns this shape; it does not name a winning model class.

## 2. Three checkable sub-claims

### C1 — framework-internal and modeling-facing; checkable by reading the figure text and a model spec

- **Statement:** the residual must be indexed per slot, and the interaction list must be read off the world by enumeration from the residual, with context carried as part of the relation.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 1 :19 "The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated." Fig 11 :43 "The habitat is enumerated, never composed." Fig 11 :50 "The same actor is a benefactor to one residual and a malfactor to another, so mutualism and parasitism are relations rather than kinds." Fig 11 :48 "You do not invent the list. You read it off the world, and what you cannot perceive or measure yet is the residual."
- **What to check:** does the model under review report per-species, per-site, per-season residuals, or only a single fit statistic? Are interaction signs indexed by a measured context, or frozen as a fixed matrix?
- **Refuter:** a fixed, context-free interaction matrix predicts every slot as well as a context-relative relation model across independent seasons and sites; or a standing interaction list derived without reference to the focal residual predicts new sites without enumeration. If either holds, C1 is withdrawn for that system.

### C2 — the decisive testability claim; checkable against a model-selection report and a field budget

- **Statement:** description cost and settlement cost are two currencies in different units and must not be summed into one complexity score. A new interaction term is admitted only if it pays the acceptance bargain on held-out data.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 12 :125 "A composition costs in two currencies, and they do not add." Fig 12 :126 "Bits price the description. Actions price finding out whether it holds." Fig 5 :43 "A new term is worth having when stating it, plus what remains unexplained after it, costs less than what was unexplained before." Fig 5 :44 "fails this, and so does a term that explains one occasion perfectly."
- **What to check:** does the model-selection report record the number of field observations or experiments needed to estimate each added interaction term, alongside its description-length or parameter cost? Is the selection criterion evaluated on held-out seasons? A term that lowers training error only is the named failure.
- **Refuter:** show a class of interactions where the field actions needed to settle a term stay flat as terms are added, verified against a recorded budget; or show a season-specific term admitted by the rule without improving held-out prediction elsewhere. If so, C2 is narrowed or withdrawn.

### C3 — field-facing; checkable against a study design

- **Statement:** the compound must be split before search, a synthetic test is a substituted habitat, and order is a priced part of the arrangement.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 9 :54 "And when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again." Fig 11 :70 "A test harness is a substituted habitat. Which is why a synthetic solve proves wiring and never capability." Fig 12 :117 "isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."
- **What to check:** are species turnover and interaction rewiring partitioned rather than pooled into one "change" term? Is there at least one field context never used to build the simulator? Are two models with identical species and coefficients but different application order compared?
- **Refuter:** one pooled model with one rule predicts all subsets as well as separate loops on held-out seasons; or a synthetic-only calibration predicts a novel real context as well as a field-calibrated model; or identical ingredients under different order behave indistinguishably. Any one of these narrows C3.

## 3. What is explicitly not claimed

- Not a theory of everything, and no final model.
- Not a new model class. No formalism is named or ranked. "The habitat as an ecological niche" was offered and declined in the architecture essay; Fig 11's habitat is defined by contact with a moving residual, not by an organism's relation to its environment.
- Not that all complexity is bad. Unpriced complexity is the failure, not arrangement itself.
- Not that a field residual names its source. Several interaction mechanisms can produce the same trajectory; a source needs a discriminating instrument.
- Not `grounded`. No field measurement was taken by the author of this packet.
- Not a claim that a coefficients-only vocabulary can refuse a held interaction. The operators table records that a vocabulary without negation can only accumulate.

## 5. What would settle it

A field design, not a further argument. Concretely: (1) a held-out slot-level comparison of a relation model against a fixed matrix; (2) a recorded count of field actions per added term; (3) a partition test of turnover versus rewiring; (4) a field context never used in simulation; (5) an order comparison with identical ingredients. These are the instruments that would move any part of the claim from `specified` to `grounded`.

## 6. What an expert can check in twenty minutes

1. Read Fig 1 :19, Fig 5 :42-44, Fig 9 :54, Fig 11 :43, :48, :50, :70, Fig 12 :117, :125-127. Ten short passages.
2. Check C2 against one model-selection report you know: were actions and bits reported separately, and was the criterion evaluated on held-out data?
3. Name one field design that would distinguish rewiring from turnover for your system, or say why none is possible.
4. Send the refutation. A failure is more useful than an endorsement, and later agreement is never pooled as corroboration (Fig 2 :40 "disqualifying for a verdict: agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool").
