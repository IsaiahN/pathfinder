**Hard precondition:** do not send until the shape claim and its three testable sub-claims in `08_expert_verification_packet.md` survive your own check. The output must be checkable or the two-stage offer collapses on contact.

**Subject:** One checkable claim about changing interactions in ecological models

> Hi [name],
>
> I have one specific output I would like you to check against what you already know from ecological modeling. I am not asking you to read the framework first, and I am not asking you to agree with it. If the output does not survive, there is nothing else to look at.
>
> **The residual (`specified`, not `grounded`).** A model predicts for a species, a site and a season, and the gap between prediction and observation is tracked per slot, not as one pooled score. When interactions change, a fixed-interaction model leaves a live gap on some slots. Adding interaction terms can close those slots, but each term costs bits to state and field actions to settle. The two costs are in different units and do not add, so a model that is cheap to write down can still be too expensive to test. That is the decision the output addresses.
>
> **The shape claim (`specified`, not `grounded`).** Under the figures I work with, the habitat is enumerated from contact rather than composed from a standing list of interaction kinds; an interaction's sign is relative to the residual, so mutualism and parasitism are relations rather than fixed types; a problem whose disagreements do not shrink with effort is a union of conditions and should be split before more terms are added; and a new interaction term is admitted only if stating it plus what remains unexplained costs less than the confusion it removes. The framework returns a shape, not a name. It does not say which formalism is correct for your system.
>
> **What I am not claiming.** Not that this is a theory of everything, not that one model form wins everywhere, not that a synthetic test is a field test, and not that anything here is settled by measurement. The figures say a test harness is a substituted habitat, so a simulated solve proves wiring and never field capability. I am not claiming the model is simple; I am claiming the two costs must be reported separately.
>
> **What would refute it.** I wrote these down before contacting you:
> 1. A fixed, context-free interaction matrix predicts each species-site-season slot as well as a context-relative relation model across independent seasons.
> 2. The number of field actions needed to settle a model stays flat as changing interaction terms are added, against a recorded field budget.
> 3. One pooled model with one rule predicts all subsets as well as separate turnover, rewiring and context loops on held-out seasons.
>
> If any one holds, that part of the shape is wrong and I will retract it. A one-line "this fails because..." is more useful to me than agreement. If you would rather not engage, no reply is needed.
>
> **If it survives, and only then:** I will send three public sources. The figures are the authority, not my summary of them.
>
> **If you want to use the machinery:** the derivation method is in the public links. Run it on a problem you know well and can check against a ground. Write down what would refute your result *before* you run it, and state the mode of what you get. A reported failure is more useful to me than a reported success.
