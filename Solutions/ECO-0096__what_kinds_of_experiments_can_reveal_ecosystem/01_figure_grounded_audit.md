## 0. The residual, in effect terms

The question is usually heard as "which experimental design should an ecologist choose." That is the wrong first object. Stated as an effect:

- The quantity is `R = |Γ(b,a) − o′|` per slot `s`. Fig 1 :19: *"The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated."* A slot here is a species, a site, a trophic position, a region, a season. Fig 1 :22: *"Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average."* An "ecosystem-level effect" is a **scale** of description (Fig 4), not a permission to average slots.
- The normal anchor is an experiment. Fig 2 :29-30: *"An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks. A measurement, an experiment, a counter, a market, a patient outcome. If it moves when you push on it, it is not an anchor."*
- The question removes that anchor by premise. Manipulation is impractical (the system is too large, too slow, too connected, too irreversible) or unethical (one may not randomize a toxin, a logging regime, a disease, a famine onto a real ecosystem or community). This is a **price on actions**, not a change in logic. Fig 12 :125-127: *"A composition costs in two currencies, and they do not add."* *"Bits price the description. Actions price finding out whether it holds."* *"They are in different units, so a system holding one accounting cannot compare them"*. When the action price is prohibitive, the bargain forces designs that spend description and independence instead.
- The residual to close is therefore: **which arrangements can still range against a ground that does not update, with independent readings, an enumerated cascade, and per-slot verdicts, when direct manipulation is removed?**

What an answer would look like (Formula, "Direction"): a **design-shape separator**, four conditions plus a refuter, written down and bound as an ordinary term before any design is chosen. The separator is refusable; a preferred design name held as a preference is not (Fig 5 :62-63).

## 1. Domain census before choosing any figure line

**R by arity, symmetry, scale.**

- *Arity*. Multi-place and multi-relational: actors, conditions, relations, and the cascade among them. Fig 11 :45-47: *"actors: what acts on it, and what it acts on"*, *"conditions: light, water, temperature, season, terrain"*, *"relations: pollination, predation, competition, dependency"*.
- *Symmetry*. Asymmetric. Fig 11 :61: *"The seat's power is asymmetric. Introduction is available; exclusion is not."* And valence is not carried by the actor: Fig 11 :49-50: *"An actor's valence is relative to the residual, not a property it carries."* *"The same actor is a benefactor to one residual and a malfactor to another, so mutualism and parasitism are relations rather than kinds."* A comparison is also not symmetric: Fig 9 :22 names *"arity · symmetry · scale"* as the describing triple.
- *Scale*. Per slot and cross-scale. Fig 1 :19 again, and Fig 4 :20: *"Only the actors, the currency and the anchor change."* Ecosystem level is one scale of a lossy membrane, not a pooled number.

**One line per domain: actors, currency, anchor, nameable or not.**

**Partition or union.** Union, not partition. Fig 9 :54: *"And when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again."* Fig 9 :55-57: a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either. D2 to D6 overlap: a BACI design can use a model-based synthetic reference; a paleo record can be a natural experiment's before period; a mesocosm can import an exogenous perturbation. One lever clears the region where only one condition holds and leaves the intersections standing. So verdicts are kept per domain and never averaged into a single "best experiment."

**Dependency order (Fig 3).** Fig 3 :20: *"The chain is ordered by dependency, not by importance."* The order here is: perceive the slots and the cascade → acquire terms for what an ecosystem-level effect would be → state the objective (what counts as progress on `R`) → choose a route to it → carry the method, not the recording. The middle link is the one with no instrument: Fig 3 :25-26: *"The link between them, having terms for what a situation offers and being able to say what would count as progress, has neither, so it attracts the least attention and the fewest instruments."* Fig 3 :27: *"A link with no instrument is not a link that works. It is a link nobody has looked at."* Most ecology-methods effort lands in design statistics (above the break), while the objective term, "ecosystem-level effect at this slot," stays unmeasured.

## 2. Findings

### F1 — The premise is a price on actions, not a different logic (decisive for framing)

Fig 12 :125-127, quoted in §0. A manipulative field experiment spends actions: establish plots, randomize, apply treatment, hold constant, wait. Where the action is impractical or unethical, its price is effectively infinite, so the MDL bargain *"cost(φ) + left(R,φ) < cost(R)"* (Formula step 3) selects arrangements that spend description and independence instead. The action-price reading is triangulated with the habitat asymmetry (Fig 11 :61-62): introduction is available, exclusion is not, and you cannot subtract the habitat to make a clean control. What the question calls "impractical or unethical" is the ecological instance of a general structural constraint, not an exception to it. Verdict: `specified`, triangulated across Fig 12 and Fig 11, low overlap (one governs two currencies, the other governs contact asymmetry).

**Refuter:** show a case where manipulation is unavailable and the design logic is nevertheless identical in currency and anchor to a randomized trial, so no substitution of description for action occurs. That would dissolve the price reading.

### F2 — The anchor is whatever settles, not specifically randomization

Fig 2 :29-30 (quoted above) and Fig 2 :33: *"The anchor must not update."* The text names a measurement, an experiment, a counter, a market, a patient outcome. It does not name randomization as necessary. An exogenous event (a storm, a ban, a fire, a drought, a policy change) can be an anchor if the event did not move in response to the frame's own report. Fig 2 :35: *"Alignment is triangulation, not negotiation. A mirror cannot correct you."*

The world literature is careful here in a way that corroborates the figure rather than conflicting. Britannica's entry defines a natural experiment as an observational study useful *"when controlled experiments are impractical or unethical"* and warns that because there is no random assignment, natural experiments *"will never unequivocally determine causation in a given situation."* Craig et al. 2017 (Annual Review of Public Health) define natural experiments as *"any event not under the control of a researcher that divides a population into exposed and unexposed groups"* and stress *"Understanding and modeling the process(es) determining exposure to the intervention are therefore central to the design and conduct of NE studies."* Both are saying: the event qualifies as anchor only if its assignment process is understood and it did not update. Fig 2 :43: *"a convention that decides an outcome has stopped being one."* A model-based counterfactual that is tuned until it agrees is a convention deciding an outcome; it is not an anchor.

Verdict: `specified`, triangulated across Fig 2 and two low-overlap world fields (public-health evaluation and ecology methods; both correctly cited as secondary).

**Refuter:** exhibit a frame that moves its reference in response to its own report and still settles the question; or show that only randomized assignment can qualify as an anchor.

### F3 — Independence decides whether a design is one witness or two

Fig 2 :31: *"Independence, between two frames, is how little they draw on the same evidence."* Fig 2 :40: *"agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool"*. Fig 8 :48: *"How much two frames draw on the same evidence decides both whether their agreement means anything and whether their union has any surplus."* Fig 8 :50: *"Two frames can hold the same atom by convergence, reaching it separately, which is corroboration at maximum independence."*

Applied here: three observers scoring the same photographs are one frame with three names; a monitoring network and a model fitted to that network are one pool; a mesocosm and a field survey that share the same source community and the same analyst share evidence. The dissolution produce is the common-cause panel of Fig 2 :6: *"collapse 2: common cause (hidden shared source)"*, and the mutual-update panel of Fig 2 :4-5: *"collapse 1: mutual update (coherent drift)"*. Fig 2 :42: *"Buy independence in proportion to the cost of being wrong."* For irreversible ecosystem decisions, the cost of being wrong is high, so independence is worth buying with genuinely different instruments and different closures.

Verdict: `specified`, triangulated across Fig 2 and Fig 8, low overlap between the alignment frame and the union-surplus frame.

**Refuter:** show two readings with measurable zero independence whose agreement nevertheless increases the probability that the per-slot residual is correct. That would break the correlation rule.

### F4 — Enumerate the habitat cascade before naming the response variable

This is the decisive ecology-specific finding. Fig 11 :43-44: *"The habitat is enumerated, never composed."* *"List everything in contact with the residual, then what is in contact with those, and outward until the cascade stops mattering."* Fig 11 :48: *"You do not invent the list. You read it off the world, and what you cannot perceive or measure yet is the residual."*

Fig 11 :52-53: *"And contact cascades. Scoping the habitat to direct contact is how DDT reached the eagles: first-order enumeration, second-order effect, and the residual that mattered was three steps out."* Fig 11 :54: *"Carson, Silent Spring, 1962. The mechanism, biomagnification through the food chain to eggshell thinning, was established over the following decade."*

The world record corroborates the shape at low overlap. The US EPA CADDIS case study records that *"extensive toxicity testing of DDT on adult bird mortality revealed no relationship"* because *"lethality was not the impairment; decline of birds-of prey was the impairment"*, and that *"Together these findings provided lines of evidence by which DDT might cause eggshell thinning"* (opened 2026-11-23). That is exactly the two failures the figure predicts: scoping to a first-order endpoint (lethality) missed the third-order residual, and the conclusion came from multiple lines of evidence with different instruments, not from one randomized trial. Stanford's *DDT and Birds* (Ehrlich, Dobkin and Wheye) records the concentration steps, *"very slight environmental contamination can be turned into a heavy pesticide load in birds at the top of the food chain"*.

A design that measures only direct contact, or only the endpoint that is cheap to score, cannot reveal an ecosystem-level effect. It will report a clean null. Fig 9 :44: *"Only a sealed room can be searched to the end, and no frame is a sealed room. So searching and finding nothing proves nothing."*

Verdict: `specified`, triangulated across Fig 11 and two low-overlap world sources (EPA causal-assessment practice and an ecology textbook essay), both secondary.

**Refuter:** show an ecosystem-level effect whose causal chain is demonstrably first-order and whose residual is fully captured by direct-contact enumeration; that would narrow Fig 11 :52 to a special case rather than a design rule.

### F5 — Introduction is available, exclusion is not; "control" is not absence

Fig 11 :61 (quoted), Fig 11 :63: *"Which is why a managed ecosystem is permanent work rather than a solved state."* A treatment can be added; the habitat cannot be subtracted. So a "control plot" is never a habitat-free state, only a differently curated habitat, and any intervention imports actors the designer did not choose. Fig 11 :65: *"Introducing an actor for its cascade is ecosystem engineering, after Jones, Lawton and Shachak, 1994. Cane toads and kudzu are the same move with the cascade unenumerated."* The Jones, Lawton and Shachak 1994 paper is the figure's own cited source, and its abstract defines engineers as organisms that *"directly or indirectly modulate the availability of resources to other species, by causing physical state changes in biotic or abiotic materials."*

Isolation is substitution: Fig 11 :66: *"Isolation is not removal of the habitat. It is substitution of one habitat for another."* Fig 11 :68-69: *"what you failed to reproduce is invisible until the goal fails"*, *"what you unintentionally introduced is invisible until it acts"*. Fig 11 :70: *"A test harness is a substituted habitat. Which is why a synthetic solve proves wiring and never capability."*

Verdict: `specified`. On the asymmetry, triangulated (Fig 11 :61 and the engineering literature). On the harness limit, `single-source` on the figure side and strained against part of the mesocosm literature; see §5 S1 for the re-census that narrows it to capability transfer.

**Refuter:** exhibit a substituted habitat that reproduces field capability rather than merely mechanism wiring, with the field result arriving after the harness result and matching it per slot. That refutes Fig 11 :70 as applied here.

### F6 — Per-slot residual, never a global ecosystem average

Fig 1 :19 and :22 (quoted). Fig 8 :39: *"So a search that finds nothing is never a proof of absence."* Applied: a pooled ecosystem mean can be near zero while one species-slot carries a live residual, which is a *"legal state"* under Fig 1 :13 (*"a global R near zero with one live slot residual is a legal state"*). Ecosystem level denotes the scale at which the slots are arranged, not an average that erases them. A non-detection in a pooled index is not a non-effect.

Verdict: `general` on the structure (indexed, not averaged), `specified` on the ecology application. Triangulated across Fig 1 and Fig 8.

**Refuter:** show a biological integration quantity for which an ecosystem-level effect exists only in the pooled aggregate and disappears under every per-slot decomposition. That would require the effect to be a property of the averaging itself, which would move it out of the habitat and into the observer's summary.

### F7 — Instruments are extended from something already returning something

Formula step 7 separates three questions that are often conflated: SEARCHED (is it in here and unfound), INWARD (extend the instrument, sharpen in our own terms), OUTWARD (import a frame whose closure predicts the shape). Fig 6 :31: *"Neither answer tells you much about the other. Run them as two tracks, and never read one against the other."*

Fig 6 :32-33: *"An instrument is not built from a description. It is improved from a worse instrument already returning something."* *"Nobody specified a telescope from an account of Mars. A wandering point in the sky was already a reading, and every sharper instrument was built on the residual the last one produced."* Fig 6 :34-35: *"The question is not whether a sensor could exist. It is whether anything, at any resolution, is already returning something that fails to resolve."*

**INWARD** in this field: extend monitoring series, add sediment cores, improve remote-sensing resolution, densify temporal sampling. The long-view record is not a different kind of experiment; it is the same loop with a longer channel. Fig 10 :24: *"the ground does not decay, the channel does"*, and Fig 10 :26: *"stale percepts, dropped transients, saturated metrics, pooled readings"* name the failure modes: a monitoring programme can lose contact while the ecosystem keeps its own ground.

**OUTWARD** in this field: import a design frame whose closure differs, with a source gate and a shadow test (Fig 6 :12-14). Difference-in-differences and regression discontinuity enter ecology from econometrics; interrupted time series and synthetic controls enter from policy evaluation. Craig et al. 2017 record the machinery and its assumptions, including *"the parallel trends assumption"* for DiD. Butsic et al. 2017 (Basic and Applied Ecology) show regression discontinuity applied to a wildfire boundary, exploiting *"a discontinuity in either space, time, or policy to separate observations into treatment and control groups."* That is an import with a demonstrable shadow test: it explains a gap the local frame could not (no randomized burn is available), and its provenance is a different discipline. Fig 8 :32: *"Closure is idempotent. Composing inside a closed set never leaves it, so no search, however long, adds an atom."* Re-running more regressions inside the same observational data set is SEARCHED, not INWARD or OUTWARD.

Gotelli and Ellison's field primer gives the scale trade in plain terms: manipulative experiments *"allow for greater confidence in our inferences about cause and effect, but they are limited to relatively small spatial scales and short time frames"*, whereas *"Natural experiments can be conducted at virtually any spatial scale ... and over any time interval."* Blois et al. 2013 (PNAS) shows space-for-time substitution reaching roughly 72 percent of time-for-time accuracy, with a caution that it *"performed poorly during the Holocene when temporal variation in climate was small relative to spatial variation"*. That is the INWARD/OUTWARD instrument result stated in the world literature: a substitute axis works when the gradient it borrows is actually present, and fails quietly when it is not.

Verdict: `specified`, triangulated across Fig 6, Formula step 7, Fig 10 and four low-overlap world sources (statistics primer, econometrics-derived ecology methods, paleoecology, policy evaluation).

**Refuter:** show an instrument designed from a pure description with no prior instrument returning anything, or show that INWARD and OUTWARD are the same track so their disagreement carries no information.

### F8 — Describe the residual before choosing the design

Fig 9 :26-28: *"R's own predicates name the habitat that holds φ*"*, *"describe it before you go looking, or any frame you pick will seem to fit"*. Fig 9 :30-31: *"your words are borrowed too, so describe the effect rather than the cause"*. Fig 9 :53: *"Nobody chooses. The gap does, and the gap is only as good as its description."*

Concretely: state arity (how many actors and relations), symmetry (does the effect depend on direction, and is the anchor asymmetric), and scale (which slot and which crossing) before selecting BACI, a mesocosm, a paleo record, or a synthetic control. Schrodt et al. 2025 (Methods in Ecology and Evolution) draw the same distinction from the ecology side, contrasting models built around *"explicitly built ... causal hypotheses"* with predictive models, and noting that quasi-causal insight from predictive tools *"requires careful design and cannot be assumed in general predictive workflows."* Picking the design first and describing the residual afterwards is apophenia: any design will seem to fit.

Verdict: `specified`, triangulated across Fig 9 and a current ecology-methods source.

**Refuter:** exhibit a case where choosing the design first and describing the residual later produces the same per-slot verdict under an independent ground. That would show the description step is decorative.

### F9 — The question is a union compound, so verdicts stay per design class

Fig 9 :54-57 (quoted in §1) makes the union explicit. The design classes are not interchangeable options on one scale; they are different bonds to an anchor over a partly shared atom set. Fig 12 :46: *"A bond is the typed relation between two operands. There are seven, they are not interchangeable, and which one holds is not recoverable from the operands."* Fig 12 :117: *"Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."* `06_isomers_and_order.md` works the atom set out: `before`, `after`, `impact`, `reference`, `time series`, `exogenous event`. BACI and difference-in-differences are isomers over that set (same atoms, different contrast bond); regression discontinuity and synthetic control are further isomers; a mesocosm changes the habitat rather than the bond and is therefore a substitution, not an isomer. One lever (say "use BACI everywhere") clears the region where only before/after/reference conditions hold and leaves the substitution and long-view intersections standing.

Verdict: `general` on the union structure, `specified` on the ecology binding. Triangulated across Fig 9 and Fig 12; the specific isomer mapping in `06` is `single-source` for the figure definition and unmeasured at the ground.

**Refuter:** show that all design classes reduce to one bond over one fixed atom set with identical behaviour per slot, so the union collapses to a partition.

### F10 — No figure line names a design; that is a declared gap, not silence

After the census above, no line in `03-FIGURES_TEXT.md` selects BACI, regression discontinuity, a mesocosm, a paleo record, or any other named design for this ecosystem and this effect. Fig 9 :32: *"you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself"*. Fig 13 :134: *"Unreachable is a verdict here, not a silence"*. Fig 8 :39 (quoted) forbids converting a null result into a proof of absence. The canonical README states the limit as *"what a missing thing would have to look like (a shape, not a name)"*.

So the honest output is the separator and the refuter, not a top-three list. A named design is a binding from the ecology frame onto the separator, provenanced as such and held `candidate`. Fig 11 :51: *"No standing list of good actors survives a change of residual."* The same applies to a standing list of good designs: with a different residual, the same design changes valence (Fig 11 :49-50).

Verdict: `gap`, of the "shape, not a name" kind, reported as unreachable at this budget, not underivable in principle, and not silence.

**Refuter:** produce a figure line that, under a genuine re-census, selects a named design for a stated ecosystem effect. The gap would close and this finding would be withdrawn.

## 3. Pre-registered refuter

Written before the conclusion, per the standing rule that a falsifier written before the run is a design instrument. The design-shape separator is refuted if any of the following holds:

1. A design is exhibited with a non-updating ground, two genuinely low-overlap readings, and a cascade enumerated at least three steps out, whose per-slot residual disagrees with the ground when that ground is eventually measured.
2. The DDT and eagle cascade is shown to be first-order, so Fig 11 :52 does not govern what "ecosystem-level" means.
3. A substituted habitat is shown to reproduce field capability rather than mechanism wiring, with the field result arriving afterwards and matching per slot (refutes Fig 11 :70 as applied here).
4. An anchor is shown to update on the frame's own report and still settle the question (refutes Fig 2 :33).
5. The habitat is shown to be composable rather than enumerable in an ecosystem context (refutes Fig 11 :43).
6. A figure line is found that names a specific design for a stated ecosystem effect (closes F10).
7. A pooled ecosystem average is shown to carry a live effect that vanishes under every per-slot decomposition (refutes F6).

If any fires, the affected finding is withdrawn in the log below, not quietly edited. Until one is run, no part of this folder may be labelled `grounded`.

## 4. Recommendation

1. Lead with the separator (§0, `07`), not with a list of designs. Ask what would refute it before offering any source.
2. Enumerate the habitat cascade outward before choosing a response variable. Treat the DDT/eagles line as the standing example of why first-order scoping produces a clean, wrong null (Fig 11 :52-54).
3. Test the anchor: name the perturbation or measurement, then show it did not move in response to the analyst's model (Fig 2 :29-33). A model tuned to agree is a convention, not an anchor (Fig 2 :43).
4. Buy independence in proportion to the cost of being wrong (Fig 2 :42). Two readings from one data set are one witness.
5. Run INWARD and OUTWARD as separate tracks (Fig 6 :31). Extend the long-view instrument; separately import a quasi-experimental frame with provenance and a shadow test.
6. Use a substituted habitat for mechanism shape only, and state the two silent failures explicitly (Fig 11 :68-69).
7. Keep verdicts per slot and per domain. Do not average them into an ecosystem score (Fig 1 :22).
8. Host the two-stage offer through the public, opt-in venues in `03_public_contact_points.csv`. Ask for a refutation, never for agreement (Fig 2 :35).

## 6. Summary verdict

The shape survives: an ecosystem-level effect can be revealed without direct manipulation only by a design that names a non-updating anchor, buys low-overlap readings, enumerates the cascade past direct contact, and keeps `R` indexed per slot. Direct manipulation is unavailable because its action price is prohibitive, so the bargain shifts the spend to description and independence. The named designs in the literature are bindings onto that shape, not figure-derived answers, and the choice among them is a budget filter whose result is settled by the ground per slot. Verdicts per domain are kept separate and never averaged. Mode `specified` on the shape and per-domain claims, `general` on the union structure, `gap` on the named design, not `grounded`, and the whole folder is a simulated derivation checked for internal consistency, not a ground reading.
