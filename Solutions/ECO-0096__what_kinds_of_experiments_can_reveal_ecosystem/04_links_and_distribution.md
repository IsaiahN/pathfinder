## Wider literature that bears on the question

These are secondary witnesses. Each was read 2026-11-23; each is logged with author, venue, year and a quote in `10_source_ledger.csv`. Where a direct fetch was blocked, the content came from the public search index and the row says so.

1. **Design under unfeasible manipulation.** Stewart-Oaten, Murdoch and Parker 1986, *Ecology*, introduced BACI as repeated before/after sampling at control and impact sites. Chevalier et al. 2019, *Ecological Applications*, say BACI designs are *"powerful tools to derive inferences about environmental perturbations ... when controlled experimental designs are unfeasible"*. Christie et al. 2019, *Journal of Applied Ecology*, show that simpler designs lacking controls or pre-impact data produce *"inaccurate estimates of biodiversity responses"*. These corroborate the per-slot and anchor conditions; they are one broad ecology-methods pool, not three independent witnesses.
2. **Quasi-experimental imports.** Butsic et al. 2017, *Basic and Applied Ecology*, use regression discontinuity at a wildfire boundary; Schrodt et al. 2025, *Methods in Ecology and Evolution*, map BACI, difference-in-differences and RD onto ecology causal questions and note that predictive models do not yield causal claims without *"careful design"*. These are the OUTWARD track made concrete, and they come from a different discipline pool than the DDT case.
3. **What the scale price buys and costs.** Gotelli and Ellison's field primer states that manipulative experiments are limited to *"relatively small spatial scales and short time frames"* while natural experiments can be run *"at virtually any spatial scale"*. Blois et al. 2013, *PNAS*, test space-for-time substitution and find roughly 72 percent accuracy with a caution when temporal variation is small relative to spatial variation. Lovell et al. 2023, *Biological Reviews*, catalogue transferability limits of space-for-time substitution. These corroborate the INWARD instrument track and its failure mode.
4. **Substituted habitats.** Jones, Lawton and Shachak 1994, *Oikos*, is the figure's own cited source for ecosystem engineering. Macaulay et al. 2025, *Oikos*, document that mesocosm communities *"diverge from natural communities over time"* because of wall growth, restricted dispersal and artificial selection, while still recommending them where the research question's scale fits. Petersen and Kemp 2008 describe scale as *"a crucial issue"* in mesocosm research. This pool partially strains Fig 11 :70; the re-census that narrows it to capability is in §5 of `01` and §Strains of `11`.
5. **Definition of the constraint.** Britannica's natural-experiment entry states the design is useful *"when controlled experiments are impractical or unethical"* and that such studies *"will never unequivocally determine causation in a given situation"*. The 2025 MRC/NIHR natural-experiments framework defines the case as *"events or processes outside the control of a researcher that divide a population into groups with differing degrees of exposure"*. These are outside ecology, hence lower overlap, and they corroborate F2.

**Conflict rule.** No world source forced a figure verdict to flip. The apparent conflict between mesocosm utility and Fig 11 :70 was returned to the census and resolved as a difference of objects (mechanism isolation versus capability transfer). The conflict rule was therefore triggered once and ended in re-census, not in a changed verdict.

## Distribution: offer, do not push

Figure-grounded reasons, each with the primary line:

- **Fig 10 :52** — *"Bring a characterised residual, never the source."* Sending the whole framework before anyone has described the residual sends a source, not a characterised residual. Lead with the separator and the refuter.
- **Fig 4 :22** — *"Only methods cross up. Only head starts come down."* The formula is a generator, so offering it as a method is permitted. A recorded list of conclusions is not.
- **Fig 9 :27-28** — *"describe it before you go looking, or any frame you pick will seem to fit"*. Ask the recipient what they would expect to see if the shape claim were true before giving them the article.
- **Fig 2 :35** — *"Alignment is triangulation, not negotiation. A mirror cannot correct you."* Do not ask for agreement.
- **Fig 2 :40** — *"agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool"*. Do not select recipients for receptiveness and never pool later agreement as corroboration.
- **Fig 1 :19** — *"The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated."* Ask for a refutation per slot, not a general verdict on the framework.

**Recommended order in a reply:** (1) the separator and its four failure conditions; (2) ask what they would count as a failure and which slot they would test first; (3) record that verdict; (4) only then, the three links, with the note that the figures, not the summary, are the authority.

## The two-stage offer

1. **Stage 1.** Send the residual, the shape claim and the pre-registered refuter. Ask for a failure condition, not an endorsement. No framework material.
2. **Stage 2, opt-in only.** If the recipient is curious, offer the three source links. Their stage-1 verdict is already recorded; their later agreement is never added as evidence (Fig 2 :40).
3. **Stage 3, opt-in only.** Offer the formula as a method to reuse. Require each user to write their own refuter and mode before running it, because a frame cannot check the direction of its own question (Formula, "Direction"; Fig 5 :65).

**Hard precondition.** Stages 1 to 3 work only if the stage-1 output survives. `08_expert_verification_packet.md` is the artefact to send; `09_watson_answer.md` is the internal canonical record, not the first thing a stranger sees.
