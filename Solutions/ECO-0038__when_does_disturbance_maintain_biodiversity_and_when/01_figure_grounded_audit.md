## 0. The residual under audit

> When does disturbance maintain biodiversity, and when does it push ecosystems toward degradation?

Restated in effect terms. A disturbance is a relatively discrete event that changes demographic rates and resource availability (Dornelas 2010 defines D, B and K disturbance by mortality, reproduction and carrying capacity shifts). The same class of event is reported to raise local richness in one system and drive a degraded state in another (Connell 1978; Mackey and Currie 2001; Thom et al. 2016). The residual is the unexplained sign flip: *why does the same actor sometimes increase and sometimes decrease a community's diversity?*

What an answer would look like, written down and bound before the search (Formula "Direction"): a relational shape with a per-slot residual, a residual-relative valence for disturbance, cascading first-to-higher-order effects, a threshold comparison between two quantities, and a ground (measurement) that decides. That shape is the target. It is refutable; see section 3.

## 1. Domain census before choosing any figure line

### R by arity, symmetry and scale

- **Arity.** One disturbance actor pressed against a multiset of species slots. The effect is not on a single aggregate but on many slots at once, so R is indexed per slot, not per event (Fig 1 :11, :19).
- **Symmetry.** Not symmetric. The relation is a threshold comparison between two quantities (disturbance interval versus recovery or competitive-exclusion time), where which side is larger matters (Operators table :48-49: *"a threshold relation between two quantities"*). A symmetric join would lose the direction that decides the outcome.
- **Scale.** The event acts locally and briefly; the measured outcome may be alpha (plot), beta (turnover) or gamma (regional), and the residual that decides degradation can sit two or three steps out in the cascade (Fig 11 :52-53). The transform between scales is lossy (Fig 4 :27).

### One line per domain: actors, currency, anchor

| domain | actors | currency | anchor | question type |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| D1, disturbance as habitat actor | disturbance events (fire, windthrow, grazing, flood), species, conditions and relations | demographic shifts and resource release | measured community trajectory | enumeration of contact, not composition |
| D2, the diversity measurement | species slots, indices (richness, evenness, Shannon) | per-slot residual | counts and persistence | per-slot partition, never an aggregate verdict |
| D3, the threshold comparison | disturbance frequency and intensity versus recovery and competition rates | a comparison between two quantities | coexistence boundary in the data | threshold shape, not a named constant |
| D4, the management seat | manager, restoration acts, introduced or suppressed disturbances | contact | the ground, never agreement | introduction available, exclusion not; permanent work |

No nameable anchor means it is not a domain yet. D4 has one: the measured trajectory after a management act. D3 has one: the observed coexistence boundary. All four are domains.

### Partition or union

It is a **union compound**, not one fact. Disturbance type (D, B, K and their composites), the diversity measure (richness, evenness, compound indices), the scale (alpha, beta, gamma) and the environmental context (productivity, aridity, grazer type) are overlapping conditions, not alternative labels for one thing. Where conditions overlap, a single lever clears the region where only one binds and leaves the intersections standing (Fig 9 :55-56). The right move is to unbundle before searching again, not to hunt for one universal disturbance-diversity curve.

### Dependency order (Fig 3)

Enumerate the habitat and contact (D1) before measuring, measure per slot (D2) before comparing, compare the threshold (D3) before declaring an outcome, and let the ground settle it before the management seat acts (D4). Fig 3 :20: *"The chain is ordered by dependency, not by importance."* A reading taken below a missing measurement link is a reading of nothing.

### Strain test applied during census

My first pass tried to assign Fig 12's comparison bond to D1 as a kind of disturbance. That strained: comparison is a threshold relation between two quantities, not an event. Re-census put it in D3, where it governs the timing relation rather than the actor. Recorded in the retraction log, not smoothed over.

## 3. Findings F1 to F9

### F1 - disturbance has no fixed valence; it is relative to the residual (decisive, triangulated)

Fig 11 :49-51, verbatim:

> **"An actor's valence is relative to the residual, not a property it carries."**
> **"The same actor is a benefactor to one residual and a malfactor to another, so mutualism and parasitism are relations rather than kinds."**
> **"No standing list of good actors survives a change of residual."**

The Formula "Habitat" section says the same at the loop level: *"Valence is relative to R, not carried."*

So the question "when does disturbance maintain biodiversity" cannot be answered by describing disturbance. It is answered by describing the residual of the community it contacts. The same windthrow that opens substrate for saproxylic beetles can remove the seed bank that the herb layer needed. Fig 11 :45-47 enumerates what to read off the world: *"actors: what acts on it, and what it acts on"*, *"conditions: light, water, temperature, season, terrain"*, *"relations: pollination, predation, competition, dependency."*

World corroboration, low overlap with the figures. Thom et al. 2016 (Biological Reviews) report a "disturbance paradox": across 478 reviewed studies, disturbance on average decreased ecosystem carbon by 38.5 percent while increasing species richness by 35.6 percent. The same event is benefactor to the biodiversity residual and malfactor to the carbon residual, exactly the Fig 11 claim. Dornelas 2010 reaches the same structurally by defining D, B and K disturbances separately. **Corroborates.** Triangulated across Fig 11 and the Formula habitat rule.

**Refuter:** show a disturbance class whose sign on diversity is invariant across residuals, systems and measures, with no counter-case where it harms one residual while helping another. Thom's paradox is already a live counter-example to that refuter, so it would have to be overturned.

### F2 - the residual is per species slot, not a single diversity number (triangulated)

Fig 1 :19 and :22, verbatim:

> **"The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated."**
> **"Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average."**

A Shannon index or a single richness count collapses many species slots into one number. Fig 1 :13 adds: *"a global R near zero with one live slot residual is a legal state"*. So a stable-looking diversity index can coexist with one species in free fall. The figure predicts that studies using a single compound index will disagree with studies using richness, because they are reading different slices of R.

World corroboration, low overlap. Svensson et al. 2012 (Proc R Soc B) modeled both IDH and DEM and found richness gives a hump while evenness rises monotonically; their survey of 160 studies found dissimilar support across diversity measures in 70 percent of cases and between richness and evenness in over 75 percent. They conclude *"studies that use compound indices of diversity should present logical arguments, a priori."* This is the Fig 1 aggregation warning stated in ecological units. **Corroborates.**

**Refuter:** demonstrate a single diversity index that tracks degradation monotonically and unambiguously across taxa, scales and disturbance types, such that per-slot enumeration adds no checkable information.

### F3 - the question is a union compound; split it, do not keep searching for one curve (triangulated)

Fig 9 :4-5 and :54-56, verbatim:

> **"Disagreements that do not shrink with effort, where each rule keeps working well on a different subset, are not one hard question but several well-formed ones. Split it rather than search."**
> **"And when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again."**
> **"A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones."**

The fifty-year IDH literature is the exact failure this line names. Meta-analyses find peaked, increasing, decreasing and U-shaped diversity-disturbance relationships with no single shape dominant (Mackey and Currie 2001, reported in Miller et al. 2011); reviews report support rates from 16 percent to 46 percent depending on ecosystem and metric (Meyer et al. 2021 summarizing Mackey and Currie, Svensson et al. 2012 and Kershaw and Mallik 2013); Fox 2013 argued the hypothesis should be abandoned while Huston 2014 and Sheil and Burslem 2013 defended it. Each rule works on a different subset. Fig 9 says split rather than keep searching.

The Library of Babel essay states the same in search terms: *"Perhaps your problem was a bundle or conflation of several problems masquerading as one? You will need to search all of those problems individually to find their answers and then combine them in a satisfactory way."*

World corroboration, low overlap. Miller et al. 2011 (PNAS) show one two-species model generates peaked, U-shaped, increasing and decreasing DDRs depending on which aspect (frequency, intensity, rate, timing, duration, extent) is plotted, and conclude *"we have demonstrated how considering multiple aspects of disturbance can potentially reconcile apparently contradictory results."* Gao and Carmel 2020 (Oikos) find the hump holds in wet areas but not dry ones and depends on grazer type. Dornelas 2010 shows the unimodal pattern appears only when D, B and K are negatively correlated. These are three independent unbundlings (aspect, context, mechanism). **Corroborates.**

**Refuter:** exhibit a single diversity-disturbance relation (one shape, one metric, one threshold) that predicts outcomes across disturbance types, taxa, scales and productivity contexts, and that survives out-of-sample tests. Until then the union reading stands.

### F4 - the deciding relation is a threshold comparison; the figures give the shape, not the constant (triangulated)

Fig 12 :112 and Operators table :48-49, verbatim:

> **"A plan is a sequence. A goal is a comparison. Progress is a subtraction."**
> **"a threshold relation between two quantities"**

The two quantities are disturbance return interval on one side and the community's recovery or competitive-exclusion time on the other. The comparison ⋛ is ordered: which quantity is larger changes the outcome, matching Fig 12 :89's test *"is it about which is larger, rather than both being present? Yes."*

Crucially the figures give the shape (a threshold between two quantities) and not the name or the number. Fig 9 :32: *"you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself."* The canonical README says the framework reports *"a shape, not a name"*. So no derivation here can yield "disturb every 7 years" or "at 30 percent canopy removal." Those numbers are bindings from a specific system and must carry their provenance.

World corroboration. Dornelas 2010 found the unimodal pattern arises from community-wide negative correlations among D, B and K, not from species differences in competitive ability, which is a threshold-between-quantities mechanism. Janousek et al. 2020 (Diversity and Distributions) found avian richness peaked at intermediate fire severity only at lower latitudes and shifted with productivity, i.e. the threshold moves with context. **Corroborates for the shape; conflicts with any fixed universal constant.** This is a corroboration of relation, not an agreement from a shared pool: the figure line and the ecological result were derived in different domains.

**Refuter:** show the comparison is not a threshold between two quantities but a monotone linear addition, or produce a universal threshold constant that transfers across systems without provenance. Either would break the shape.

### F5 - effects cascade; the residual that decides may be third order (triangulated)

Fig 11 :52-54, verbatim:

> **"And contact cascades. Scoping the habitat to direct contact is how DDT reached the eagles: first-order enumeration, second-order effect, and the residual that mattered was three steps out."**

The immediate first-order effect of a disturbance (mortality, opening space, resource release) can be positive for local richness while a second- or third-order effect (loss of a mutualist, collapse of the seed bank, soil carbon loss, trophic cascade) drives degradation. Scoping to immediate contact is how the sign is misread. The Formula "Habitat" rule *"Everything in contact with R, then what contacts those, outward until the cascade stops mattering"* is the enumeration instruction.

World corroboration. Meyer et al. 2021 (Ecology and Evolution) tracked a beech windthrow for up to 24 years and found the hump for four taxonomic groups but with different peak times (beetles 3 years, herbs and trees about 5, fungi 8) and concluded *"the mechanisms driving species richness after disturbances are more complex than the IDH suggests and that these mechanisms vary with species group,"* invoking legacy effects, substrate heterogeneity and microclimate. Dornelas 2010 found carrying-capacity disturbance has the most lasting consequences because *"there is no recovery if community capacity is reduced"*, i.e. the slow variable, not the immediate mortality, decides. **Corroborates.**

**Refuter:** show that first-order contact enumeration predicts the long-term diversity outcome in a system where carrying capacity or mutualist loss also changes. If it does, the cascade clause adds nothing there.

### F6 - scale transform is lossy; alpha, beta and gamma must be read separately (triangulated)

Fig 4 :27 and :30, verbatim:

> **"The transform between scales loses information, and that is the point."**
> **"So take the round trip and measure the gap. Without it you are navigating by dead reckoning: confident about a position nobody checked."**

A richness gain at the plot scale can be a homogenization at the landscape scale. Treating local and regional diversity as the same quantity is a dead-reckoning error. Fig 4 :19-20: *"The kernel is the part that does not change with size: the same loop and the same rule for naming what worked, whether one agent is running it or a thousand. Only the actors, the currency and the anchor change."*

World corroboration. Meyer et al. 2021 found the hump-shaped course in alpha and gamma diversity but *"barely detectable"* in beta diversity, and explicitly noted that gamma was driven by plotwise richness rather than between-plot differences. Svensson et al. 2012 note the IDH/DEM predictions are used in the management of national parks and reserves, where a local gain can mask a regional loss. **Corroborates.**

**Refuter:** show alpha, beta and gamma move together under disturbance across systems so that one scale's measurement transfers without a round-trip gap.

### F7 - the ground settles it; agreement among reviewers is not an anchor (triangulated)

Fig 2 :29-30, :35 and :40, verbatim:

> **"An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks. A measurement, an experiment, a counter, a market, a patient outcome."**
> **"Alignment is triangulation, not negotiation. A mirror cannot correct you."**
> **"agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool"**

The appropriate anchor here is a measured community trajectory: counts, persistence, a before/after control, not a vote on whether the IDH is true. Fox 2013 and Huston 2014 reach opposite verdicts while citing overlapping reviews, which is one evidence pool read twice, not two independent witnesses. Fig 2 :42: *"Buy independence in proportion to the cost of being wrong."* Since wrong disturbance policy is costly (fire suppression, grazing bans, salvage logging), independence is worth buying with separate measurements.

**Refuter:** show a case where expert agreement, without a new measurement, correctly predicted the post-disturbance trajectory out of sample. That would make agreement an anchor, which Fig 2 forbids.

### F8 - where a seat manages, introduction is available and exclusion is not; this is permanent work (single-source for the figure rule, corroborated in practice)

Fig 11 :61-63, verbatim:

> **"The seat's power is asymmetric. Introduction is available; exclusion is not."**
> **"Which is why a managed ecosystem is permanent work rather than a solved state."**

A manager can add a disturbance (prescribed burn, managed grazing) but cannot subtract the habitat, because contact is a fact about the world. So "maintain biodiversity under disturbance" is not a solved state to be achieved once; it is ongoing work against a changing channel. Fig 10 :24: *"the ground does not decay, the channel does."*

World corroboration. Yuan et al. 2016 (Scientific Reports) found in Inner Mongolian grassland that richness peaked at intermediate anthropogenic grazing and concluded that *"the preservation of biodiversity depends on both encouraging and limiting disturbances within ecological thresholds."* Thom et al. 2016 found salvage logging and prescribed burning did not alleviate the disturbance paradox. Both are exactly the permanent, asymmetric management Fig 11 describes. **Corroborates.**

**Refuter:** exhibit a disturbance-management regime that achieves a durable, one-off restored state with no further intervention while the surrounding habitat continues to change.

### F9 - what actually survives: a shape and a route, not a curve (single-source for the figure limit)

The defensible residue, with mode attached:

| claim | mode | figure |
|---|---|---|
| Disturbance's sign is relative to a residual, not intrinsic to the event class | `general` (structural) | Fig 11 :49-51 |
| R must be read per species slot, not as one aggregate index | `general` (structural) | Fig 1 :19, :22 |
| The question is a union compound and must be unbundled before search | `general` (structural) | Fig 9 :4-5, :54-56 |
| The deciding relation is a threshold comparison between two quantities | `specified` (falsifiable) | Fig 12 :112; Operators :49 |
| Effects cascade; higher-order contact can override first-order gain | `specified` | Fig 11 :52-54 |

What does not survive: any universal "intermediate disturbance maximizes diversity" curve asserted as a result of the framework. That curve is a world candidate (Connell 1978) that fits the shape but is not figure-derived, and its own literature shows it is subset-dependent. Fig 8 :39: *"So a search that finds nothing is never a proof of absence."* A failure to find one universal curve is not proof none exists, but it is a verdict of `unreached` at this budget, not silence.

## 4. Pre-registered refuter (written before the findings were concluded)

Per the seat rule, *"a falsifier written before the run is a design instrument, not only an honesty device."* The shape claim is refuted if any of the following holds:

1. A disturbance class is shown to have an invariant sign on diversity across residuals, systems, scales and measures, with no case where it helps one residual and harms another (breaks F1, Fig 11 :49-51).
2. A single aggregate diversity index is shown to track degradation monotonically and unambiguously such that per-slot enumeration adds no checkable information (breaks F2, Fig 1 :19, :22).
3. One well-formed diversity-disturbance relation is shown to predict outcomes across disturbance types, taxa, scales and productivity contexts out of sample (breaks F3, Fig 9 :4-5).
4. The deciding relation is shown to be a monotone linear addition rather than a threshold between two quantities, or a universal threshold constant is shown to transfer across systems (breaks F4).
5. First-order contact enumeration is shown to predict long-term outcomes where carrying capacity or mutualist loss also changes (breaks F5).

If any fires, the shape is withdrawn. Until one is run, no part of this may be labelled `grounded`. The five refuters were recorded here before the world sources were interpreted into findings F1-F9; the findings are written to them, not fitted around them.

## 5. Recommendation

1. Lead with the shape and its refuter, not with a curve. Disturbance has no fixed sign; the sign belongs to the relation between the actor and a measured residual (F1).
2. Split before searching. Unbundle disturbance aspect, diversity measure, scale and environmental context (F3). A manager asking about grazing in a dry grassland and a manager asking about windthrow in a beech forest are asking two different, well-formed questions.
3. Measure per species slot and report richness, evenness and a compound index separately with an a priori argument (F2).
4. Enumerate contact out to the second and third order before calling an outcome degradation (F5), and measure alpha, beta and gamma with the round-trip gap named (F6).
5. Let the ground settle it. Ask what measurement would change the verdict, not how many reviews agree (F7).
6. Where a seat manages, treat it as permanent, asymmetric work (F8).
7. Offer the method, not a recording. Fig 4 :22-25: *"Only methods cross up. Only head starts come down."*

### Seven-question recursive derivation (summary)

1. **Is disturbance intrinsically good or bad for diversity?** Neither. Fig 11 :49-51 makes valence residual-relative. *Legality of the claim form*, not an empirical curve.
2. **What is the residual measured on?** Per species slot. Fig 1 :19, :22. A single index averages the live signal away.
3. **Does one curve govern?** No. Fig 9 :4-5, :54-56: it is a union compound; unbundle aspect, metric, scale and context.
4. **What decides the sign?** A threshold comparison between two quantities. Fig 12 :112, Operators :49. Ordered, not symmetric.
5. **Does the framework give the number?** No. Fig 9 :32 gives a kind of thing, never the answer itself. The number is a provenanced binding.
6. **What settles it?** The ground. Fig 2 :29-30, :35. A measured trajectory, not a vote.
7. **Is that the end?** No. Fig 11 :63 makes management permanent work; Fig 10 :24 says the channel decays, so the measurement must be repeated. Status `candidate`.

### Updated verdict

The shape survives: residual-relative valence, per-slot measurement, a union compound that must be split, a threshold comparison with a system-specific binding, cascading effects and a lossy scale transform, all settled by measurement rather than agreement. Every universal curve asserted as a framework result fails: the figures return a shape, not a number. Strains after re-census: none live. The one apparent strain (Fig 12 comparison assigned to an event) was a domain error, resolved by re-census and recorded above.
