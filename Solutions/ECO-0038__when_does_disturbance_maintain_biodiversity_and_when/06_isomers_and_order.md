# Disturbance types as isomers, and the order of the recovery loop

**Mode: `specified`.** This is a simulated derivation checked against the figures, not a ground reading (see `05_what_shape_means.md` section 6).

## RESIDUAL

The word "disturbance" names fire, windthrow, grazing, flood, harvest and disease as if they were one actor. They are not interchangeable: Dornelas 2010 shows mortality (D), reproductive (B) and carrying-capacity (K) disturbances have very different consequences, and that the unimodal pattern appears only when the three are negatively correlated. Two questions are owed: (1) under Fig 12's definition, are disturbance types **isomers** over a shared atom set, and (2) what order does the disturbance-to-recovery loop have, and what does that order forbid in restoration reasoning?

## DOMAIN CENSUS

Atoms held for D1: species slots, demographic rates (mortality d, birth b, carrying capacity J), resources, conditions (light, water, temperature, season, terrain), relations (competition, predation, pollination, dependency). Bonds held: the seven plus negation. No new primitive is available without import. ## DERIVATION

### Q1 - Are D, B and K disturbances isomers?

Fig 12 :117, verbatim:

> **"isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."**

Dornelas 2010 defines disturbance by which demographic parameter it shifts: D multiplies mortality, B multiplies birth, K multiplies carrying capacity. All three act on the same community atoms (the same species, the same J, the same resource pool). The bond differs. The behaviour differs sharply: K disturbance has the most drastic and lasting effects, D is intermediate, and B has almost no effect even at complete reproductive failure in that model. That is Fig 12's isomer definition applied directly: same atoms, different bond, different substance, invisible in the umbrella label "disturbance" and decisive in outcome.

The stronger case is the composites. DBKu varies all three in unison; DBKr makes K negatively correlated with D and B. Same three atoms, different arrangement between them, and only DBKr produced the unimodal diversity pattern. Fig 12 :94-96: *"The arrangement is the substance. Two entries with an identical ingredient set are not variants of one thing. They are different things whose notation cannot tell them apart."*

**Result:** disturbance types are isomers over the shared community atom set, not variants of one actor. **Triangulated** (Fig 12 definition plus Dornelas 2010 model result), but the world half is a single modelling study, so the empirical generality is `specified`, unmeasured.

### Q2 - Functional group rather than one kind

Fig 12 :124, verbatim:

> **"functional group. A substructure whose presence fixes the outcome. The group says what kind; the qualifier says which member."**

All disturbances share the functional group "a relatively discrete event that shifts demographic rates and resource availability" (Pickett and White definition, quoted in Dornelas 2010 and Miller et al. 2011). The qualifier decides the member: which rate, which aspect (frequency, intensity, timing, duration, extent, interval), which scale, which context. A review that reports "disturbance increases diversity" is stating the group and omitting the qualifier, which Fig 12 says fixes the outcome.

### Q3 - What order is the disturbance-to-recovery loop?

Fig 12's bond table distinguishes → sequence from ⇒ production:

- **→ sequence**: *"A then B; B needs A to have happened"* (Fig 12 :72; Operators :30).
- **⇒ production**: *"A produces B; B did not exist before"* (Operators :37).

A disturbance event followed by recovery is a **sequence**, not a production. The post-disturbance community is made of survivors, the seed bank and advance regeneration, all of which existed before the event. Meyer et al. 2021 found this directly: *"the regeneration layer remained relatively intact, and this legacy of the previous stand largely determined development after the disturbance."* Treating recovery as production (⇒) would erase the legacy, which is the substructure that actually fixes the trajectory.

Consequence for restoration: you cannot write a disturbed site as a fresh system. The sequence bond carries the survivors forward. Fig 12 :48: *"A chain is the degenerate case: one bond type, no branching."* A recovery model that records only "disturbance, then richness" is that degenerate chain; it cannot carry the parallel branches (legacy, substrate, microclimate, immigration) that Meyer et al. found decisive.

### Q4 - Why the IDH literature accumulates instead of refusing

Operators table :58, verbatim:

> **"a refutation is a negation, which is why a vocabulary without it can only accumulate"**

Fig 12 :112 adds: *"A plan is a sequence. A goal is a comparison. Progress is a subtraction. A refutation is a negation, and that one is not a bond."*

The disturbance-diversity literature has accumulated peaked, U-shaped, increasing and decreasing curves (Mackey and Currie 2001, reported in Miller et al. 2011) without a general refusal of the single-curve framing. Fox 2013 supplied a refusal ("should be abandoned"); Huston 2014 refused the refusal. Both moves are legitimate uses of ¬. What is not legitimate is accumulating another curve and calling it progress when the compound has not been split (Fig 9 :4-5).

## FALSIFIER, WRITTEN BEFORE THE RUN

The isomer and order claim fails if either of the following holds:

1. D, B and K disturbances are shown to be interchangeable over the shared atom set, so that the same arrangement of mortality, birth and carrying-capacity shifts produces the same diversity outcome regardless of which parameter is moved.
2. Recovery is shown to be genuinely productive (⇒) rather than sequential (→), so that the post-disturbance community is not composed of survivors and legacies and a fresh-system model predicts the trajectory as well as a legacy-aware one.

Neither fires on the sources read. Dornelas 2010 refutes (1) inside its model; Meyer et al. 2021 refutes (2) for the beech windthrow case. Both remain `specified`, not `grounded`, because neither is a measurement taken for this derivation.

## CONCLUSION

Disturbance types are isomers over a shared community atom set: same atoms, different bonds, different substances, with the difference invisible in the umbrella word and decisive in outcome. The functional group is "discrete demographic shift"; the qualifier (rate, aspect, scale, context) selects the member and fixes the outcome. Recovery is a sequence (→) over survivors, not a production (⇒) of a fresh community, which is why legacy effects decide trajectories. The order of the derivation is enumerate, measure per slot, split, compare, settle, act, with refusal before mint. The falsifier is stated above and has not fired.
