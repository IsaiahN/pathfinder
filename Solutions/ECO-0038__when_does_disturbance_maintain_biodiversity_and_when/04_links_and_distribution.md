## Two findings that bear directly on the pitch

1. **The disagreement is structural, not a shortage of data.** Fox 2013 and Huston 2014 reach opposite conclusions while reviewing overlapping literatures. Fig 9 :4-5 says disagreements that do not shrink with effort, where each rule works on a different subset, are not one hard question. So the pitch should not offer a verdict in the IDH debate. It should offer the unbundling.
2. **The same event has opposite signs on different residuals.** Thom et al. 2016 report carbon down and richness up for the same disturbances. That is Fig 11 :49-51 in ecological units, and it is the single most checkable part of the shape. Lead with it.

## Distribution: offer, do not push

Figure-grounded rules:

- **Fig 4 :22-25** - *"Only methods cross up. Only head starts come down. A recording carried upward looks like knowledge and is a description of one occasion."* Send the shape and the refuter, not a literature dump.
- **Fig 10 :42** - *"A verifier that can reconstruct the claim cannot verify it."* A recipient who has absorbed the framework before being asked for a verdict has joined the frame being checked.
- **Fig 9 :27-28** - *"describe it before you go looking, or any frame you pick will seem to fit."* Ask what they would expect to see if the shape claim were true before giving them any framework material.
- **Fig 2 :40** - *"agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool."* A mass send selected for people who already favour intermediate disturbance produces a convention, not corroboration.
- **Fig 2 :42** - *"Buy independence in proportion to the cost of being wrong."* Disturbance policy is costly, so seek readers in a different closure: fire ecologists, soil scientists, rangeland managers, statisticians who work on diversity indices.

**Recommended order in a reply:** (1) the shape claim and its five refuters; (2) ask what they would count as a failure and which measurement would change their mind; (3) only then the links, with the note that the figures, not this summary, are the authority.

## The two-stage offer

- **Stage 1 before stage 2** is Fig 10 :52 - *"Bring a characterised residual, never the source"* - and Fig 2 :45 - *"Frames range against the ground. They do not negotiate with it."* The recipient checks the shape against their own data without holding the framework's interior.
- **Stage 2 is opt-in by construction.** Once a reader has absorbed the theory, their agreement stops being evidence. Record their stage-1 verdict *before* any theory is shown, and never pool later agreement as corroboration.
- **Stage 3 sends a method, which is permitted.** Fig 4 :22 - *"Only methods cross up. Only head starts come down."* The formula is a generator, so giving it away does not give away an answer.
- **Stage 3 still needs a ground per problem.** Formula "Direction": a frame *"cannot check the direction of its own question, because the shape a frame is searching for is not a term it holds."* Each user must write down what would refute their result before running it and label the mode of what they get.

**Hard precondition.** Stages 1-3 only work if the stage-1 output survives. `01_figure_grounded_audit.md` states the five refuters; if any fires, stop and retract rather than send.
