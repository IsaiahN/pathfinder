# Expert verification packet - the Tether on disturbance and biodiversity

**What this is:** a claim in a form a disturbance ecologist can check. Mode, derivation and refuters are stated before the conclusion, so a reader knows what to look for and what would settle it.

**Mode: `specified` - not `grounded`.** No measurement at the disturbance site is claimed. This is a shape and a route, not a result.

**Status:** a simulated derivation checked for internal consistency against the figures, not a ground reading. It cannot settle the claim it reports on.

## 1. The claim, in one paragraph

Disturbance carries no fixed sign for biodiversity. Its valence is relative to the residual of the community it contacts, so the same event can raise one diversity component and degrade another. The residual must be read per species slot rather than as one compound index. The question is a union of overlapping conditions (disturbance type, diversity measure, scale, environmental context) and must be split before it is searched. The sign is decided by a threshold comparison between two quantities, the disturbance return interval and the community's recovery time, whose value is system-specific and must be measured. Effects cascade outward from first contact, and alpha, beta and gamma are lossy transforms of one another. The framework returns this shape, not a universal intermediate-disturbance number.

## 2. Three checkable sub-claims

### C1 - framework-internal and ecology-facing; checkable by reading Figure 11 and by looking at your own data

- **Statement:** disturbance's effect has no intrinsic sign; its valence is residual-relative.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 11 :49-51 - *"An actor's valence is relative to the residual, not a property it carries. The same actor is a benefactor to one residual and a malfactor to another, so mutualism and parasitism are relations rather than kinds. No standing list of good actors survives a change of residual."* Formula "Habitat": *"Valence is relative to R, not carried."*
- **What to check:** can you name a disturbance in your system that increased one component of diversity while decreasing another (for example richness up, soil carbon or a mutualist down)? Thom et al. 2016 report exactly this in a review: carbon down 38.5 percent, richness up 35.6 percent.
- **Refuter:** a disturbance class whose sign on diversity is invariant across residuals, systems, scales and measures, with no case where it helps one residual and harms another.

### C2 - measurement-facing; checkable by any ecologist who has used a diversity index

- **Statement:** a single compound diversity index can hide a per-slot decline; richness, evenness and compound indices are not interchangeable tests of the same claim.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 1 :19 - *"The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated."* Fig 1 :22 - *"Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average."*
- **What to check:** in a dataset you know, does a Shannon index stay flat while one rare species declines and the dominant becomes more even? Svensson et al. 2012 found richness and evenness gave different support for the IDH in over 75 percent of studies using both.
- **Refuter:** a single index that tracks degradation monotonically and unambiguously across taxa, scales and disturbance types, so that per-slot enumeration adds no checkable information.

### C3 - framework-internal derivation with an ecological test

- **Statement:** the sign is decided by an ordered threshold comparison between two quantities, not by a linear addition and not by a universal constant. Effects cascade, so the residual that decides may be second or third order.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 12 :112 - *"A plan is a sequence. A goal is a comparison. Progress is a subtraction."* Operators table :49 - *"a threshold relation between two quantities."* Fig 11 :52-53 - *"And contact cascades. Scoping the habitat to direct contact is how DDT reached the eagles: first-order enumeration, second-order effect, and the residual that mattered was three steps out."*
- **What to check:** does the sign in your system flip where return interval crosses recovery time for the slowest limiting variable (seed bank, soil carbon, a mutualist), rather than at a fixed intensity? Dornelas 2010 finds the unimodal pattern appears only when mortality, birth and carrying-capacity shifts are negatively correlated; Janousek et al. 2020 find the threshold moves with productivity and latitude.
- **Refuter:** show the deciding relation is a monotone linear addition rather than a threshold, or that first-order contact enumeration predicts the long-term outcome where carrying capacity or mutualist loss also changes.

## 3. What is explicitly not claimed

- Not a universal intermediate-disturbance curve, and no value for the threshold, the return interval or the intensity.
- Not "disturbance is good" or "disturbance is bad." Both are the intrinsic-valence error this packet refuses.
- Not a claim that the IDH is true or false. It is a world candidate that fits the shape and is subset-dependent; the packet offers an unbundling, not a verdict in that debate.
- Not that expert agreement settles anything. Fig 2 :35 - *"Alignment is triangulation, not negotiation. A mirror cannot correct you."*
- Not `grounded`. No measurement was taken for this derivation.
- Not that alpha, beta and gamma are the same quantity. Fig 4 :27 - *"The transform between scales loses information, and that is the point."*

## 5. What an expert can check in twenty minutes

1. Read Fig 11 :49-53 and Fig 1 :19, :22 and Fig 12 :112 (four short passages).
2. Check C1 and C2 against one dataset you know well: can you produce a case where a diversity index stays flat while a species declines, or where disturbance helps one metric and harms another?
3. Name one slow variable in your system (seed bank, soil carbon, mutualist, carrying capacity) whose recovery time could set the threshold, or say why none does.
4. Try to fire one of the three refuters above. A failure is more useful than an endorsement.
5. Send the refutation. If none fires, name the measurement that would decide the claim in your system.
