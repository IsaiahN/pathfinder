# The two scaling branches: structure, order, and analogues

**Mode: `specified` for the application, `general` for the structural claims.** Simulated derivation checked against the figures, not a ground reading (see `05_what_shape_means.md` §6).

## RESIDUAL

The answer to REA-0082 is a union of two conditions: budget-unfound and closure-missing. Two questions are owed: (1) what is the *structure* of the union, partition or compound, and (2) what is the *order* of the remedies, and can one be read against the other?

## DERIVATION

### Q1 - Partition or union?

Fig 9 :55: "A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones."
Fig 9 :57: "What remains is smaller and it is also categorically different: a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either."

The two branches are not two tiers of difficulty. A capability that is in closure but unfound and a capability that needs a new atom are categorically different cases: one is a search problem, the other is an import problem. They overlap in the same task slot, so they form a union compound with its own loop and its own ground, not a partition.

**Falsifier, written before the run:** if a single instrument assigns every observed flip to exactly one branch with no overlap and no third case, and the assignment is stable across two low-overlap model families, the union reading fails and the partition reading stands.

### Q2 - Are the branches isomers?

Fig 12 :117: "isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."
Fig 12 :124: "functional group. A substructure whose presence fixes the outcome. The group says what kind; the qualifier says which member."

Both branches hold the same atoms: `Γ`, `R_s`, a ground, a scale parameter. They differ in which operation is owed. That makes them **isomers** at the level of the remedy if and only if the operation is treated as a bond between the residual and the answer: one bond is "more search", the other is "import". On the weaker reading they share a **functional group** (a residual that persists and does not trend down) with different bonds attached.

This is a structural observation, not a measurement. **Falsifier:** show that search and import are not distinct bonds but two settings of one continuous parameter, so no bond difference exists. That collapses the isomer claim to the functional-group claim.

### Q3 - What order are the remedies?

Formula step 7, read from THE_FORMULA (not a figure quote): SEARCHED is asked first because it is cheapest and the other two assume its answer. Fig 6 :49 gives its shape: "more search at the same reach: is it in here and unfound? The closure does not change. Same atoms, same wall, more of the space actually visited." Fig 6 :30 gives INWARD: "The description travels. Fails by being sharpened into terms only we can read. Cast it in effect terms." Fig 6 :23 gives OUTWARD: "No frame escapes its own closure. The bound moves only on contact with a frame whose closure differs."

So the order is:
1. **SEARCHED** (scale): is the capability in here and unfound? Same atoms, same wall, more of the space visited.
2. **INWARD**: is our representation adequate to hold it? Extend the instrument from one already returning something.
3. **OUTWARD**: does another frame already hold it? Import, with a source gate and a shadow test.

Fig 6 :31: "Neither answer tells you much about the other. Run them as two tracks, and never read one against the other." A failure of scaling does not itself prove an import is owed; it may mean the instrument cannot see the slot. This is the exact place where a hasty "new principles are required" argument overreaches.

**Falsifier:** a case where increasing scale and importing a new atom are the same measured operation, so the three questions are not distinct. That breaks the order and the union at once.

### Q4 - What does scale transfer across a run boundary?

Fig 4 :22: "Only methods cross up. Only head starts come down."
Fig 4 :25: "A recording carried upward looks like knowledge and is a description of one occasion."

A larger model carries more recordings of occasions. What crosses to a new problem is a method, not the recording. So the benefit of scale is a cheaper search on the next problem only if a method was distilled and carried; raw parameter growth is not automatically a head start. This is a `specified` claim about model families, `general` for the membrane rule. **Falsifier:** show parameter growth alone transferring a new method to a held-out habitat with no distillation and no lossy round trip.

### Q5 - Analogues that bite

- **Grokking.** Power et al. (read 2026-11-24): "improving generalization performance from random chance level to perfect generalization, and that this improvement in generalization can happen well past the point of overfitting." This is a flip produced by optimization time on a structured dataset: branch (i), in closure and unfound. It corroborates the budget reading from an adjacent ML subfield, but shares the ML evidence pool, so it is one witness.
- **Inverse scaling.** McKenzie et al. (read 2026-11-24): "there are tasks for which increased model scale alone may not lead to progress, and that more careful thought needs to go into the data and objectives for training language models." A flaw in the objective or data is a frame-internal defect; changing it is an import from a different frame. Corroborates branch (ii), same pool.
- **No silver bullet.** Brooks (read 2026-11-24): "a scaling-up of a software entity is not merely a repetition of the same elements in larger sizes, it is necessarily an increase in the number of different elements." A genuinely low-overlap engineering witness for the claim that size alone does not change the kind of structure. It is an analogue, not evidence about neural networks.
- **Extended mind.** SEP (read 2026-11-24): "externalism is the view that what is going on inside an individual's body does not always, on its own, fix what is going on inside that individual's mind." Low overlap, corroborates Fig 11 :39-40 at the level of a philosophical analogue, not a measurement.

## CONCLUSION

The two branches form a union compound, not a partition. As remedy-types they are isomers under the bond that attaches the residual to its answer, or at minimum members of one functional group. Their order is fixed by cost: SEARCHED first, then INWARD and OUTWARD as opposed tracks that must never be read against one another. Scale transfers search, and only a distilled method crosses a run boundary. The falsifiers above are written before the result and are recorded in `01` §4 in their stronger form.
