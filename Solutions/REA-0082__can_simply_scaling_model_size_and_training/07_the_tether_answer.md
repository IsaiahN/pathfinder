# The Tether's answer: scaling and qualitatively new capabilities

**Mode: `specified` for the application to model families, `general` for the closure law. Not `grounded`.** Simulated derivation checked against the figures (`05_what_shape_means.md` §6), not a ground reading. No measurement was taken.

## 1. The residual it answers

Can raising the scale parameter k (parameters, tokens, compute) turn a held-out slot s with a persistent positive residual into a slot near zero, in a way that is a new *kind* of term? The question arrives as an "or": scale alone, or new learning principles. Fig 9 :55 says to check whether this is a bundled problem before searching: "A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones." It is. The two conditions are "in the closure but unfound" and "not in the closure". Fig 9 :57: "What remains is smaller and it is also categorically different: a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either."

## 2. The shape

1. **Scaling is more search at the same reach.** Fig 6 :49: "more search at the same reach: is it in here and unfound? The closure does not change. Same atoms, same wall, more of the space actually visited." Fig 8 :32: "Closure is idempotent. Composing inside a closed set never leaves it, so no search, however long, adds an atom." So scaling cannot, by itself, add a new atom or bond. `general`, structural.
2. **Where the capability is already in the closure, scaling is the right and sufficient remedy.** Fig 8 :41-42: "Underivable-in-principle and unreachable-at-this-budget are different facts, and they issue different instructions. Underivable: go outside. Unreachable-at-budget: the term is already in the closure and was not found, so the remedy is more search, not another atom." Importing a new atom here is a wrong atom, priced above starting from primitives (Fig 8 :43). `specified`.
3. **Where the capability needs a new atom or bond, new principles are required.** Fig 6 :14 names IMPORT as "the only operator that moves the wall", and Fig 6 :23: "No frame escapes its own closure. The bound moves only on contact with a frame whose closure differs." What such a principle must do is change the reach or change contact (new objective, new interaction, new instrument). `specified` for AI, with the gate from Fig 8 :9-13 (source gate: provenance, shadow test).
4. **A smooth aggregate curve cannot assign the branch.** Fig 1 :22: "Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average." Fig 1 :27: "They are frame-internal. A frame cannot score itself with a quantity it also produces." Fig 9 :60: "Use filters for the budget and witnesses for the verdict. Never let a filter hand you a verdict." Loss is a budget filter; per-slot ground residuals are the witness.
5. **Agent-only scaling does not change capability unless it changes contact.** Fig 11 :40: "A system cannot be made more capable by improving the agent in isolation. An improvement that does not change contact changes nothing, however much it improves." Fig 11 :39: "capability is a property of agent-and-habitat, never of the agent alone." This is the bridge from "more parameters" to "new habitat or new interaction".
6. **The specific new principle is not named.** Fig 9 :32: "you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself." Fig 13 :135: "It cannot say which primitive is missing, because naming it would mean already holding it." This is a `gap`, recorded as a verdict and not as silence.

## 3. What it explicitly does not say

- Not "scale is enough". That holds only for the budget branch.
- Not "scale can never produce something new". It can find what was already reachable; observers may reasonably call that new, but no atom was added.
- Not "new principles are required in every case". They are required only where the closure lacks the term, and SEARCHED must be asked first because it is cheapest.
- Not a name for the missing principle. No optimizer, architecture, or training scheme is asserted.
- Not a claim that a metric-induced flip is real. Fig 10 :26 names "saturated metrics, pooled readings" as channel failures.
- Not `grounded`. No instrument settles the branch assignment in this folder.

## 4. What would settle it

A per-slot ground instrument, not a further curve. Specifically:
- Does the flip survive a change of metric and a change of evaluator, with the anchor shown immovable (Fig 2 :29)?
- Does a source-discriminating instrument show that the new term was in `closure(Γ)` before, or that a new atom was imported? *The Hard Problem is About Source*: "the answer is unreached until a better instrument exists."
- Does the capability change when the habitat changes and stay fixed when only the agent changes (Fig 11 :40)?
- Standing falsifiers from `01` §4: a new atom produced by composition alone; a flip fully predicted by aggregate loss across two low-overlap families; capability improvement with independently unchanged contact; one operation closing both branches.

## 5. The honest stop

**Unreached, not unreachable.** The question is a union, and no measurement in this folder assigns a real capability to a branch. Fig 8 :39: "So a search that finds nothing is never a proof of absence." Fig 13 :134: "Unreachable is a verdict here, not a silence,". The answer stops at the limit of the available instruments and records the lineage so the stop is checkable. Which specific principle would be needed stays `gap`.

## 6. One-paragraph version for outreach

> The Tether does not answer "scaling" or "new principles" as a single choice. It splits the question into two cases. When a capability is already reachable from what the learner holds and was merely unfound, more scale is the right remedy: it visits more of the same reach, and calling the result new is a statement about our surprise. When a capability needs a primitive or a relation the learner does not hold, composition cannot manufacture it, however long the run, because closure is idempotent and only an import moves the wall. So new principles are required in that case, but they must be characterized in effect terms from the residual before being named, and the framework supplies only the shape of what they must do. Which case an observed capability belongs to cannot be read off a smooth loss curve, because aggregation hides the live slot; it needs a per-slot ground measurement and an instrument that discriminates the source. That measurement is not in this packet. The shape, the branch rule, and the refuters written in advance are.
