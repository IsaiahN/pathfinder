# Expert verification packet: the Tether on scaling and new capabilities

**What this is:** a claim in a form a machine learning researcher can check. Mode, derivation, and refuters are stated before the conclusion, so a reader knows what to look for and what would settle it.

**Mode: `specified` for the application, `general` for the closure law. Not `grounded`.** No capability measurement at a new scale is claimed. This is a shape and a branch rule, not a result.

## 1. The claim, in one paragraph

Scaling is more search at the same reach. It can find a capability already expressible from what the learner holds but not found at a smaller scale, and that is the correct remedy for that case. It cannot add a primitive or a new relation, because composing inside a closed set never leaves it. Where a capability needs a new atom or bond, an import from a frame whose closure differs is required, and the principle must be characterized in effect terms from the per-slot residual before it is named. Aggregate loss cannot tell which case is live, because it hides the live slot and is produced by the loop it is used to judge. Which branch an observed capability belongs to is a `gap` awaiting a per-slot ground instrument.

## 2. Three checkable sub-claims

### C1 - framework-internal; checkable by reading Figures 6 and 8

- **Statement:** scaling is the SEARCHED branch. It visits more of a fixed reach; it does not add an atom.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 6 :49: "more search at the same reach: is it in here and unfound? The closure does not change. Same atoms, same wall, more of the space actually visited." Also Fig 8 :32: "Closure is idempotent. Composing inside a closed set never leaves it, so no search, however long, adds an atom. This one is definitional: it is what closure means."
- **Refuter:** exhibit a term produced by composition inside `closure(Γ)` that requires an atom not in `atoms(Γ)`, with no import. Or show one operation that closes both the budget branch and the underivable branch without branch assignment.

### C2 - evaluation-facing; checkable by an ML researcher against their own benchmarks

- **Statement:** a qualitative flip is a claim about a held-out slot, not about a smooth average; metric choice and evaluator choice are part of the observation, not neutral windows.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 1 :22: "Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average." Fig 1 :27: "They are frame-internal. A frame cannot score itself with a quantity it also produces." Fig 10 :26: "stale percepts, dropped transients, saturated metrics, pooled readings".
- **What to check:** take one family with a claimed emergent ability. Replot with a continuous metric and per-slot error bars, and change the evaluator to a held-out set that was not used in training or metric design. Does the flip survive? Wei et al. (2022) claim it does; Schaeffer et al. (2023) claim it is metric-induced. They share a data pool, so their disagreement is one witness, not two.
- **Refuter:** a per-slot flip that is fully predicted by a continuous aggregate loss curve across two low-overlap families, with no new ground instrument. That makes the aggregate a witness and breaks this sub-claim.

### C3 - framework-internal plus a habitat claim

- **Statement:** where a new atom is needed, the remedy is an import; and capability changes only if contact changes.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 6 :14: "the only operator that moves the wall" (said of IMPORT). Fig 6 :23: "No frame escapes its own closure. The bound moves only on contact with a frame whose closure differs." Fig 11 :40: "A system cannot be made more capable by improving the agent in isolation. An improvement that does not change contact changes nothing, however much it improves."
- **What to check:** for a proposed new principle (objective, interaction, architecture), name the differing frame it came from, the gap it explains, and the contact it changes. If none of the three can be named, the principle is a relabeling of what the closure already held.
- **Refuter:** capability improves with scale while contact is independently measured as unchanged; or a new principle is derived from the current closure with no import and no binding.

## 3. What is explicitly not claimed

- Not that scale is sufficient in general. It is sufficient only for the budget branch.
- Not that scale can never surprise us. Finding a reachable term at a larger scale is a real change in the model, just not a new atom.
- Not that new principles are always required. SEARCHED is asked first because it is cheapest and the other two assume its answer.
- Not a name for the missing principle. The framework returns a shape, not a name.
- Not that a metric-induced flip is a capability change. Saturated metrics are channel failures.
- Not `grounded`. Nothing here is asserted as settled by experiment.

## 5. What would settle it

A per-slot ground instrument, not an argument. Fig 2 :29: "An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks. A measurement, an experiment, a counter, a market, a patient outcome." Here:

1. Does the flip survive a change of metric and evaluator, with the anchor shown immovable?
2. Does a source-discriminating instrument show the term was in the closure before, or that a new atom was imported? *The Hard Problem is About Source*: "the answer is unreached until a better instrument exists."
3. Does capability track habitat change rather than agent change alone?

## 6. What an expert can check in twenty minutes

4. Send the refutation. A failure is more useful than an endorsement.
