## Independent domain census

I redid the census before reading the primary's conclusions, using the WATSON order: R by arity, symmetry and scale; one line per domain with actors / currency / anchor; partition or union; order by dependency.

- **R (arity / symmetry / scale).** The residual is the gap between planned and delivered water for a river reach or a wetland in a season. Arity: more than one binding condition, because pumping and rainfall change act on the same store, so a **union compound**, not one hard question (Fig 9 :57). Symmetry: broken, because order matters and the response decays; capture precedes depletion, and a pump near a stream acts faster than one far away. Scale: reach and wetland, seasonal to decadal, across the membrane.
- **Domain A, groundwater and surface water.** Actors: wells, aquifers, gaining and losing streams, recharge, evapotranspiration. Currency: flux and head. Anchor: gauges, wells, pumped volumes. Question type: union of capture, storage loss and reduced discharge.
- **Domain B, rainfall and recharge.** Actors: amount, intensity, timing, season, snowmelt, land use. Currency: recharge flux. Anchor: gauges and recharge estimates. Question type: union, not one variable.
- **Domain C, river and wetland ecology.** Actors: groundwater-dependent wetlands, riparian and aquatic communities. Currency: hydroperiod and environmental flow. Anchor: measured inundation and flow thresholds. Question type: threshold comparison, not a stock.
- **Domain D, verification.** Actors: expert readers and instruments. Currency: agreement and residual. Anchor: the ground, never agreement. Independence zero in this simulated run.

Order by dependency: connection, capture, storage loss, ecological threshold. Describe the residual before choosing a frame (Fig 9 :27 to :28); record an outside verdict before showing the source (Fig 10 :52).

## Round trip gap `R_T`

The Symbols table defines `R_T` as `gap(x, (T_E ∘ T_A)(x))`, measuring what the coarser description cannot hold. Fig 4 :30 requires it to be measured rather than assumed. I ran the round trip on the condensation the folder actually uses.

1. **Sent up (`x`):** the verbatim figure set, chiefly Fig 1 :19 (per-slot residual), Fig 9 :54 to :57 (union), Fig 3 :20 and Fig 12 :72 (order), Fig 12 :87 (threshold).
2. **Abstraction (`T_A`):** the shorthand carried in `07` and `09`, "union, indexed, ordered, thresholded, with a gap."
3. **Concretisation (`T_E`):** a claim about a named reach, e.g. "pumping plus a dry sequence drops low flow past the environmental limit by X per cent."

**`R_T` is nonzero.** Four things the shorthand holds do not survive the trip back down:

- the **identity of the threshold** for that reach (the figure gives the comparison bond, not the operand value);
- the **fraction of the decline due to capture versus rainfall-driven recharge** (the source essay says the reading cannot comment on the source);
- the **lag time** from well to stream, which depends on distance and aquifer properties;
- the **instrument's detection limit**, without which a "no change" result cannot be interpreted.

One thing survives cleanly: a structure sits between the pressure and the outcome, the effects arrive in an order, and adequacy is a comparison at a threshold. That is the residue worth sending. `R_T` was **not** measured numerically, because no ground reading is held; it is recorded qualitatively as the enumerated set of dropped constraints. Treating "formula is the condensed version of the figures" as identity would set `R_T` to zero and commit the dead-reckoning error Fig 4 :30 names.
