# Expert verification packet — the Tether on groundwater, rainfall, rivers and wetlands

**What this is:** a claim in a form a hydrologist or ecohydrologist can check. Mode, derivation and refuters are stated before the conclusion, so a reader knows what to look for and what would settle it.

**Mode: `specified` on the structural claims, `general` on the shape, `gap` on basin numbers. Not `grounded`.** No measurement for a named reach is claimed. This is a shape and a route, not a forecast.

**Status:** a simulated derivation checked for internal consistency against the figures, not a ground reading. It cannot settle the question it reports on.

## 1. The claim, in one paragraph

Groundwater depletion and changing rainfall affect rivers and wetlands as a union of overlapping conditions indexed per reach, per wetland and per season, not as one trend. The effects arrive in a dependency order: hydraulic connection, then capture of water that would have discharged to the stream, then head and storage decline, then a threshold that changes the ecological outcome. The river response is a flow over a stored state with a decaying memory; adequacy is a comparison at a threshold, not a stock; the same head decline changes sign with the slot's dependence. The habitat is enumerated and cascades several steps out, and management can introduce but cannot subtract. Ground is measured flow, head and inundation, never reviewer agreement. No figure line yields a basin-specific number, and that is reported as a gap, not filled by an import.

## 2. Three checkable sub-claims

### C1 — hydrology-facing; checkable against pumping and streamflow records

- **Statement:** where a stream and aquifer are connected, pumping first captures water that would have reached the stream; storage depletion and the ecological threshold come later. The order is `connection → capture → head decline and storage loss → threshold crossing`, not one simultaneous average.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 12 :72 — "A then B; B needs A to have happened." Also Fig 3 :20, "The chain is ordered by dependency, not by importance." World anchor: Barlow and Leake, USGS Circular 1376, 2012, describes the cone of depression, reduced gradient and possible reversal to a losing stream; de Graaf et al. 2024 reports that "increased capture happens before groundwater depletion is experienced"; de Graaf et al. 2019 reports environmental flow limits reached "before substantial losses in groundwater storage are experienced."
- **What to check:** in a connected reach with a long record, does the capture component appear before measurable storage loss and before the environmental flow limit is crossed? Is the lag consistent with well-to-stream distance?
- **Refuter:** a connected system where environmental flow limits are crossed only after substantial storage loss, or where pumping produces no capture before depletion.

### C2 — ecohydrology-facing; checkable against wetland inundation and water-table records

- **Statement:** the wetland response is a threshold comparison, not a smooth stock. A small head decline can disconnect a shallow groundwater-fed wetland while the same decline elsewhere does nothing. Valence is relative to the residual.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 12 :87 — "a threshold relation between two quantities." Also Fig 11 :49, "An actor's valence is relative to the residual, not a property it carries." World anchor: de Graaf et al. 2019, "Only a small decline in groundwater level is needed to affect streamflow"; HESS 2025 mapping uses saturated area above 50% and water table depth below 5 m with a 0.5 to 3 m core-function band; the Victoria 2013 assessment says shallow wetlands relying on local flow systems are at most risk of disconnection.
- **What to check:** is there a head interval across which inundation duration or vegetation state changes categorically? Is the threshold site-specific rather than a universal depth?
- **Refuter:** a wetland whose ecological state changes smoothly and monotonically with head across the claimed transition, with no threshold at any resolution the instrument can resolve.

### C3 — framework-internal; checkable by reading Figures 1, 9 and 11

- **Statement:** the correct object is a union indexed per slot, so a global or national mean cannot serve as the verdict, and a single lever cannot clear the intersections. River and wetland outcomes over a shared atom set are isomers or at least functional-group relatives.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 1 :19 — "The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated." Fig 9 :55 — "A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones." Fig 12 :117 — "isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."
- **What to check:** do the figure lines say what is claimed? Do any two public datasets show opposite low-flow signs under the same global pressure, as de Graaf et al. 2024 report?
- **Refuter:** a single lever that restores both low flows and hydroperiod in the intersection cases; an aggregation that preserves the live slot; a measured case where river and wetland responses are driven by disjoint atom sets (which downgrades isomer to functional group).

## 3. What is explicitly not claimed

- Not a forecast for any named basin, reach, wetland, year or crop.
- Not that pumping always reduces river flow. Some regions show low-flow increases above 30% in the global model.
- Not that rainfall amount alone controls recharge. Timing and intensity can make intense rain run off rather than recharge.
- Not that 5 m is a universal wetland threshold. It is one mapping's operational choice with conditions of use.
- Not that the recession coefficient, the capture fraction or the environmental flow number is figure-derived. All are imported with provenance.
- Not that the hydraulic gradient of Darcy's law is the figure's gradient primitive. It is a structural analogue with a nonzero round trip gap.
- Not a derivation of one discipline from another, and no new primitive.
- Not `grounded`. Nothing here is asserted as settled by measurement.

## 5. What would settle it

A discriminating ground record, not another argument. From *The Hard Problem is About Source*: an instrument that reads a result "cannot comment on the source"; the spectrometer resolved the analogous case.

1. **Anchor:** paired streamflow, well heads, abstraction volumes and wetland inundation, per reach, per wetland, per season.
2. **Order test:** does capture precede storage loss, and does the ecological threshold precede large storage loss?
3. **Threshold test:** is there a categorical head interval, and at what depth for this wetland type?
4. **Source-discrimination test:** can pumping capture, rainfall recharge change and land-use effects be separated after a low-flow reading?

## 6. What an expert can check in twenty minutes

1. Read Fig 12 :72 and :87 and Fig 1 :19 — three short passages.
2. Check C1 and C2 against a system you know: is the order right, and is the flip a threshold?
3. Name one instrument that would separate pumping capture from rainfall-driven recharge in a single hydrograph, or say why none exists.
4. Send the refutation. A failure is more useful than an endorsement.
