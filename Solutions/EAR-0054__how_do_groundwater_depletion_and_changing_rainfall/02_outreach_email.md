# Outreach draft — check the shape before you decide about the framework

**Use:** one-to-one, or as a cover letter for a submission through a public venue in `03_public_contact_points.csv`. **Not** for bulk send.

**Hard precondition:** do not send until the refuter in section 1 of `01_figure_grounded_audit.md` is intact and the non-claims in `08_expert_verification_packet.md` are attached. The stage-1 output must survive validation or the two-stage offer collapses on contact.

**Subject:** Can you break one claim about groundwater, rainfall, rivers and wetlands?

> Hello [name],
>
> I have one specific output I would like you to check against what you already know. I am not asking you to read a theory first, and I am not asking you to agree with anything. If the output does not survive, there is nothing else to look at.
>
> **The output (`specified` on the structure, `gap` on numbers).** Groundwater depletion and changing rainfall do not combine into one smooth trend. They form a union of overlapping conditions whose intersection is a different kind of case, and the effect on rivers and wetlands depends on where you look and when:
>
> 1. The quantity to check is the gap between planned and delivered water, indexed per river reach, per wetland and per season. A basin or national mean can be near zero while one reach is in trouble. I am not offering a global number.
> 2. There is an order. Where the stream and aquifer are connected, pumping first captures water that would have reached the stream, then can reverse the gradient, then depletes storage. Environmental flow limits can be crossed before large storage losses appear. A well near a reach acts in days; one far away can act over decades.
> 3. The ecological flip is a threshold, not a smooth decline. A small head drop can disconnect a shallow groundwater-fed wetland or push a stream past its environmental flow limit; the same head drop elsewhere does nothing because dependence differs.
> 4. Rainfall change is not just "less water." Its timing and intensity matter: intense rain can run off instead of recharging, and pumping can matter less once storage is sustained by a mix of rain and groundwater discharge.
>
> **What I am not claiming:** that pumping is always bad for every river (some regions show large increases in low flow); that rainfall alone explains a decline; that I can give you a number for your basin; that the roughly 5 m wetland depth used in one global mapping is a universal threshold; or that the figure-derived shape settles any empirical question. The framework returns a shape, not a name. No measurement here is `grounded`.
>
> **What would refute it.** I wrote these down before sending this:
>
> 1. One lever (cutting pumping only, or adding recharge only) restores both low flows and wetland hydroperiod in the cases where both pressures act.
> 2. An aggregation across reaches and wetlands preserves the live slot and its sign.
> 3. A connected system crosses its environmental flow limit only after substantial groundwater storage loss.
> 4. A reach or wetland shows a memoryless, instantaneous response with no recession signal.
> 5. A wetland's ecological state changes smoothly with head across the connectivity transition, with no threshold.
> 6. A named reach under sustained connected pumping shows no measurable low-flow change, with the detection limit stated.
>
> A one-line "this fails because..." is more useful to me than agreement. If you would rather not engage, no reply is needed. If you do reply with a refutation, I will record it with provenance in the solution's audit and re-evaluate it, whatever your view of the framework.
>
> **If it survives, and only then:** here are the source links. The figures are the authority, not my summary of them.
> - The figures, with line numbers (local canonical extract, shared on request): `FRAMEWORK/reference/03-FIGURES_TEXT.md`
> - The canonical usage rule and the figures: https://github.com/IsaiahN/tether/tree/general-agent/figures
> - The worked derivation and the eight essays: https://adventuresinml.substack.com/p/how-intelligence-actually-works
>
> **If you want to reuse the machinery:** the formula is public at the figures link. Run it on a problem you know well and can check against a ground. Write down what would refute your result *before* you run it, and state the mode of what you get. A reported failure is more useful to me than a reported success.
>
> Thank you for your time,
> [your name]

## Two-stage offer, restated

1. **Stage 1.** Send the shape claim and the six refuters. Ask what the reader would count as a failure. Record their verdict **before** showing any theory.
2. **Stage 2, opt-in only.** If they are curious, offer the three links. The figures, not this summary, are the authority.
3. **Stage 3.** Offer the formula as a method to reuse, with the instruction that each user writes their own refuter and states their own mode.

Later agreement is never pooled as corroboration: Fig 2 :40 — "disqualifying for a verdict: agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool."
