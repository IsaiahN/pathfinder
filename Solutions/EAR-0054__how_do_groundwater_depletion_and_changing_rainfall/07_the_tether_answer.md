# The Tether's answer on groundwater depletion, rainfall, rivers and wetlands

**Mode: `specified` on the structural verdicts, `general` on the shape, `gap` on basin numbers. Not `grounded`.** This is a shape and a route, not a forecast for any named reach or wetland. Simulated derivation checked against the figures (`05_what_shape_means.md` section 7), not a ground reading.

## 1. The residual it answers

Water agencies plan river yield and wetland hydroperiod against a historical hydrograph. Pumping and rainfall change alter the water actually delivered. The gap `R = |Γ(b,a) − o'|` is indexed per river reach, per wetland and per season, never aggregated (Fig 1 :19: "The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated."). The low-flow season is part of the slot, because groundwater's share of river flow and a wetland's hydroperiod are set there. The question is a **union compound**, not one hard problem: a case with both pressures is not a harder case of either (Fig 9 :57: "What remains is smaller and it is also categorically different: a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either."). It has its own loop and its own ground.

## 2. The shape

1. **No single global answer.** The residual is per reach and per wetland (Fig 1 :13: "a global R near zero with one live slot residual is a legal state"). The sign can flip: de Graaf et al. 2024 report large low-flow decreases in the Indus, the Middle East, southern Europe, Mexico and California, and increases of 30% or more in parts of the lower Ganges and North China Plain. A national mean is a budget filter, not a verdict (Fig 9 :60: "Use filters for the budget and witnesses for the verdict. Never let a filter hand you a verdict.").

2. **The two pressures form a union, and a single lever will not clear it.** Fig 9 :55: "A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones." Connection, pumping, rainfall timing and ecosystem dependence overlap. Cutting pumping alone can leave a rainfall-driven recharge deficit; augmenting recharge alone cannot restore a disconnected stream.

3. **There is an order, and it decides what to measure first.** Connection precedes capture, capture precedes storage loss, and storage loss precedes the ecological threshold. Fig 12 :72: "A then B; B needs A to have happened." Fig 3 :19: "Optimising a link that already works cannot rescue one that does not." The de Graaf studies report capture before depletion and environmental flow limits before large storage losses. The river response is a flow over a stored state with a decaying memory (Fig 13 :76: "Energy and a gradient compose a flow. A gradient over space and one over time are different terms, because which state it is bonded to is part of what it is."). The recession form `Q(t) = Q0 exp(-a t)` is an imported hydraulic law with its own source gate, not a figure derivation.

4. **The ecological flip is a threshold, not a stock.** Adequacy is a comparison (Fig 12 :87: "a threshold relation between two quantities"). A small head decline can push a stream past its environmental flow limit or disconnect a shallow groundwater-fed wetland; the same decline elsewhere does nothing. The value of the threshold is a world import with a provenance record. Valence is relative to the residual (Fig 11 :49: "An actor's valence is relative to the residual, not a property it carries"): the same pumped water is a loss for one reach and a managed supply for another.

5. **The habitat is enumerated, not composed, and cascades outward.** Fig 11 :43: "The habitat is enumerated, never composed." Fig 11 :52: "And contact cascades." Well field, cone of depression, reduced baseflow, warmer and shallower water, loss of environmental flow, wetland disconnection, crop choice and income are read off the world, not assumed. Management can introduce but cannot subtract the habitat (Fig 11 :61: "The seat's power is asymmetric. Introduction is available; exclusion is not."), so a managed river-wetland system is permanent work, not a solved state (Fig 11 :63: "Which is why a managed ecosystem is permanent work rather than a solved state.").

6. **The ground is measured water, not agreement.** Fig 2 :35: "Alignment is triangulation, not negotiation. A mirror cannot correct you." Gauges, observation wells, recharge estimates and inundation maps are the anchor. Fig 10 :24: "the ground does not decay, the channel does". Stale gauges, dropped transients and pooled readings are live failure modes. A low-flow reading "cannot comment on the source" (*The Hard Problem is About Source*), so source separation needs a discriminating instrument, not another review.

7. **The numeric answer is a declared gap.** No figure line predicts the sign or size for a named reach under a named rainfall sequence. The instruments exist; what is missing is a convention for separating capture from rainfall-driven recharge in one hydrograph and a long enough record in data-scarce basins. Fig 8 :39: "So a search that finds nothing is never a proof of absence." Fig 13 :134: "Unreachable is a verdict here, not a silence,". The remedy order is SEARCHED first, then INWARD (sharpen an instrument already returning something), then OUTWARD (find the coupled frame whose closure predicts the shape). Importing a number against a budget limit adds an atom that was not needed.

## 3. What it explicitly does not say

- Not a forecast for any named basin, reach, wetland, year or crop.
- Not "pumping always reduces river flow." Some regions show low-flow increases of more than 30% under the same global model.
- Not "less rain means less recharge." Timing and intensity can make intense rain run off rather than recharge, and a wetter season can still leave a system stressed.
- Not that a single 5 m depth is a universal wetland threshold. It is a mapping choice with a source and conditions of use.
- Not that more storage is the general fix. A single lever clears only the region where one condition holds and leaves the intersections standing.
- Not that the recession coefficient or the capture fraction is figure-derived. Both are imports.
- Not `grounded`. No measurement here settles `left(R,φ)`.

## 4. What would settle it — the ground

A maintained, discriminating record, not a further argument. From *The Hard Problem is About Source*: an instrument that reads a result "cannot comment on the source"; the spectrometer resolved the analogous case. Here:

- **Anchor:** paired gauged streamflow, observation-well heads, abstraction volumes and wetland inundation, read per reach, per wetland and per season.
- **Order test:** does capture appear before storage loss, and does the ecological threshold appear before large storage loss?
- **Threshold test:** is there a head interval across which the outcome changes categorically, and at what depth for this wetland type?
- **Source-discrimination test:** after a low-flow reading, can pumping capture, rainfall recharge change and land-use effects be separated? If not, the instrument is inadequate, not the question answered.
- **Standing falsifiers from `01`:** one lever clears the intersection; aggregation preserves the live slot; the threshold comes only after large storage loss; the response is memoryless; the ecological change is smooth with head; a connected reach shows no measurable low-flow change with a stated detection limit.

## 5. The honest stop

**Unreached, not unreachable.** The figure-derived shape is checkable now with existing instruments. The basin numbers are not held here. "what a missing thing would have to look like (a shape, not a name)" governs the stop. Indiscernibility is not identity: a reach that has not been measured is not a reach without an answer. The lineage is recorded so the stop is checkable.

## 6. One-paragraph version for outreach

> The Tether does not give one answer to "how do groundwater depletion and changing rainfall affect rivers and wetlands," because the figures forbid aggregating the residual across slots. What it gives is a shape, indexed per reach, per wetland and per season. The two pressures form a union, not a single trend, so a lever that fixes one leaves the intersection standing. The effects arrive in an order: connection, then capture of water that would have reached the stream, then storage loss, then a threshold that flips the ecological outcome, with the river carrying a decaying memory of past recharge. Adequacy is a comparison at that threshold, not a stock of water, and the same head decline helps one slot and harms another depending on what it depends on. The habitat is enumerated and cascades outward, and management can add but cannot subtract, so a managed river-wetland system is permanent work. The ground is measured flow, head and inundation, not agreement among reviewers. The framework names no basin and no percentage; it offers the shape, the instruments that would close it, and refuters written in advance. The center stays open until the per-slot ground settles it.
