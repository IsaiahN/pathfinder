**Mode: `specified` on the applied claim, `general` on the structural shape, not `grounded`.** No measurement is claimed by this seat. This packet is a simulated derivation checked for internal consistency against the figures (`05_what_shape_means.md` §7); it cannot settle the claim it reports on.

## 1. The claim, in one paragraph

Climate change and human transport are not two independent, additive stresses on invasive species spread. They are two overlapping conditions of one union compound acting on a habitat. A single lever clears the region where only one condition holds and leaves the intersection standing, where the residue is categorically different: a novel propagule arriving by a human vector into a habitat whose conditions have shifted and whose regulators were left behind. The habitat must be enumerated from the world, transport is asymmetric introduction, climate changes conditions and substitutes habitats, and the impact arrives as a cascade several steps out. Invasiveness is a property of species-and-habitat, not of the species alone. The claim is a shape with a pre-registered refuter, not a species-level prediction or a measured rate.

## 2. Three checkable sub-claims

### C1: Framework-internal; checkable by reading Figure 9
- **Statement:** the two drivers form a union compound, not a partition or a list. One lever leaves the intersection standing.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 9 :55: *"A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones. A single lever clears the region where only one condition holds and leaves the intersections standing, because the second condition still binds."* And Fig 9 :57: *"What remains is smaller and it is also categorically different: a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either. It stops being a mixture and becomes a specific compound."*
- **What to check:** do climate and transport in fact overlap on the recipient habitat, so that clearing one leaves the other binding? The world sources cite warming shifting suitable habitat and climate opening new shipping pathways (IUCN 2025; IPBES 2023). Is that overlap real in the system you know?
- **Refuter:** an additive climate + transport model with no interaction term predicts observed spread as well as a compound model on held-out regions; equivalently, the intersection contributes no residual beyond the sum of single levers.

### C2: Ecology-facing; checkable against introduction and range-shift biology
- **Statement:** transport is introduction with asymmetric power, and climate substitutes habitats with two silent failure modes.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 11 :61: *"The seat's power is asymmetric. Introduction is available; exclusion is not."* Fig 11 :66: *"Isolation is not removal of the habitat. It is substitution of one habitat for another."* Fig 11 :68-69: *"what you failed to reproduce is invisible until the goal fails"* and *"what you unintentionally introduced is invisible until it acts."*
- **What to check:** is exclusion genuinely unavailable once an actor is established in a habitat, so that management is permanent work? In a range-shifting species, are the new habitat's actors inherited rather than chosen, and are the two failure modes (what the habitat fails to supply, what comes along unintentionally) actually invisible until the goal fails?
- **Refuter:** exhibit a reliable exclusion lever that removes an established actor without substituting a habitat; or show a range-shifting species that inherits none of the new habitat's actors and still succeeds; or show a "silent" failure mode that is visible before the goal fails.

### C3: Framework-internal derivation with a world-facing check
- **Statement:** the impact is a cascade, not an arrival event, and first-order contact enumeration misses it.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 11 :52: *"And contact cascades. Scoping the habitat to direct contact is how DDT reached the eagles: first-order enumeration, second-order effect, and the residual that mattered was three steps out."* World corroboration, Saebi et al. 2020: *"the first-order Markovian assumption limits accurate modeling of NIS spread risks through the global shipping network."*
- **What to check:** in the system you know, does the damage appear at the point of arrival or several relations away? Does a model that only counts direct contacts underpredict impact relative to one that includes indirect pathways?
- **Refuter:** a first-order-contact model predicts observed impacts without a cascade term, so the second-order term adds nothing out of sample.

## 3. What is explicitly not claimed

- Not a new ecological result, species-level prediction, or measured rate.
- Not that climate and transport are independent levers; that is the refused reading.
- Not that climate is a seat or an actor with intent. It changes conditions.
- Not that the habitat can be composed from a trait checklist (Fig 11 :43: *"The habitat is enumerated, never composed."*).
- Not that enemy release is universal, or that a single "invasive trait" exists (Fig 11 :50: valence is relative to the residual).
- Not `grounded`. No measurement at a ground is asserted.
- Not corroborated by agreement: the six world sources include two shared evidence pools, and this simulated second seat shares the primary's pool.

## 5. What would settle it

A discriminator instrument, not a further argument. From *The Hard Problem is About Source*: a reading *"cannot comment on the source"*, so several causal paths give the same spread curve. The decisive tests:

1. **Interaction Test:** additive model versus model with an explicit interaction term, scored on held-out regions. This is the primary refuter for C1.
2. **Cascade Test:** first-order-contact model versus higher-order/pathway model on impact data. Primary refuter for C3.
3. **Pathway / Substitution Test:** hold traits and conditions fixed and change a pathway; track inherited actors in a range shift. Primary refuter for C2.
4. Figure-level: derive one of the six substrate terms from the other five; show an agent-only improvement that moves the ground; exhibit an exclusion lever that removes an actor without substituting a habitat.
