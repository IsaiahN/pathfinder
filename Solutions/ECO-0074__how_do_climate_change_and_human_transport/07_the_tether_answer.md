# The Tether's answer: climate change x human transport x invasive spread

**Mode: `specified` on the applied compound claim, `general` on the structural shape. Not `grounded`.** Simulated derivation checked for internal consistency against the figures (`05_what_shape_means.md` §7), not a ground reading.

## 1. The residual it answers

The observed acceleration in invasive species spread must be explained, and the two named drivers must be decided as either independent additive stresses or one compound. It is a **union compound**, not one hard problem and not two separate ones: Fig 9 :57 *"a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either. It stops being a mixture and becomes a specific compound."* Each condition still binds at the intersection.

## 2. The shape

1. **The habitat is enumerated, not composed.** Fig 11 :43 *"The habitat is enumerated, never composed."* The relevant list is actors (propagule, natives, predators, competitors, mutualists, pathogens), conditions (Fig 11 :46 *"conditions: light, water, temperature, season, terrain"*) and relations (Fig 11 :47 *"relations: pollination, predation, competition, dependency"*), outward until the cascade stops mattering. What cannot yet be measured is the residual.

2. **Climate and transport are two overlapping conditions of one union.** Fig 9 :55: *"A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones. A single lever clears the region where only one condition holds and leaves the intersections standing, because the second condition still binds."* Clearing quarantine alone leaves climate-made suitable habitat; mitigation alone leaves the pathway open. The compound is a novel propagule arriving into freshly suitable habitat with regulators left behind.

3. **Transport is introduction, and the seat's power is asymmetric.** Fig 11 :61: *"Introduction is available; exclusion is not."* Humans, through shipping, trade and travel, act on the habitat by adding actors and pathways. They cannot subtract the habitat, because *"contact is a fact about the world and does not consult you"* (Seat Map :225). Managed ecosystems are therefore *"permanent work rather than a solved state"* (Fig 11 :63).

4. **Climate changes conditions and substitutes habitats.** A range shift is *"substitution of one habitat for another"* (Fig 11 :66), and the shifting species *"inherit[s] the new habitat's actors, which you did not choose"* (Fig 11 :67). The two failure modes are silent: *"what you failed to reproduce is invisible until the goal fails"* and *"what you unintentionally introduced is invisible until it acts"* (Fig 11 :68-69).

5. **The damage is a cascade, not an arrival event.** Fig 11 :52: *"And contact cascades. Scoping the habitat to direct contact is how DDT reached the eagles: first-order enumeration, second-order effect, and the residual that mattered was three steps out."* The world's higher-order shipping models make the same structural point with indirect pathways (Saebi et al. 2020).

6. **Actor valence is relative to the residual.** Fig 11 :50: *"The same actor is a benefactor to one residual and a malfactor to another, so mutualism and parasitism are relations rather than kinds."* Warming can flip a competitor into a facilitator; transport can remove a regulator. Enemy release is a relation with variable outcomes, not a universal law (Meijer et al. 2016).

7. **Invasiveness is species-and-habitat, not species alone.** Fig 11 :40: *"A system cannot be made more capable by improving the agent in isolation. An improvement that does not change contact changes nothing, however much it improves."* Trait-only risk assessment is agent-in-isolation and misses contact.

8. **The ground settles it, not the roster of actors.** Fig 11 :73: *"The habitat is not the ground. The habitat is where actors come from and it updates; the ground is what settles, and it does not."*

## 3. What it explicitly does not say

- Not a new ecological result, not a species-specific prediction, not a rate. The framework returns a shape, not a name (canonical README; Fig 9 :32).
- Not that climate and transport are independent levers. That is the refused Option A.
- Not that climate is a seat or an actor with intent. Climate changes conditions; transport is the introduction.
- Not that the habitat can be composed from a trait checklist.
- Not that enemy release is universal, or that any single "invasive trait" exists.
- Not `grounded`. No measurement at any ground was taken by this seat.

## 4. What would settle it: the ground

A discriminator instrument, not a further argument. From *The Hard Problem is About Source*: a color reading *"cannot comment on the source"*, and it was the spectrometer, not an argument, that resolved the source. Here:

1. **Interaction Test (decisive):** fit an additive climate + transport model and a model with an explicit interaction term to invasive spread on held-out regions. If the additive model matches out of sample, the compound claim falls.
2. **Cascade Test:** compare a first-order-contact model with a higher-order/pathway model on impact data. If first order suffices, F4 falls.
3. **Pathway Test:** hold species traits and habitat conditions fixed and change a transport pathway. If spread is unchanged, the asymmetric-introduction application falls.
4. **Substitution Test:** track a range-shifting species into a new habitat and record which actors it inherits and which silent failures become visible at goal failure.
5. Standing figure-level falsifiers: enumerate a habitat by composition rather than contact and predict a novel arrival; show an agent-only improvement that moves the ground; exhibit an exclusion lever that removes an actor without substituting a habitat.

## 5. The honest stop

**Unreached, not unreachable.** The world literature itself reports *"contradictory evidence, and no consensus"* (Bellard et al. 2018) on climate-driven range change. Fig 8 :39: *"So a search that finds nothing is never a proof of absence."* Fig 9 :44: *"Only a sealed room can be searched to the end, and no frame is a sealed room."* The answer stops where the instruments stop discriminating, and the lineage is recorded so the stop is checkable. Indiscernibility is not identity.

## 6. One-paragraph version for outreach

> The Tether does not give a new species-level result and does not claim to have measured anything. It says that climate change and human transport are not two independent stresses but two overlapping conditions of one union compound acting on a habitat: clearing one lever leaves the region where the other still binds. The habitat has to be enumerated from the world, not composed from a trait checklist. Transport is asymmetric introduction, so biosecurity is permanent work; climate changes the recipient conditions and substitutes habitats, with two failure modes that stay silent until the goal fails; and the damage arrives as a cascade several steps out, not at the point of arrival. Invasiveness is a property of species-and-habitat, not of the species alone, and actor valence is relative to the residual, so the same actor can help in one context and harm in another. What the Tether offers is that shape, a refuter written in advance, and the names of the instruments that would close it. The decisive one is an additive-versus-interaction test on held-out regions. Until that is run, the result is `specified` and ungrounded.
