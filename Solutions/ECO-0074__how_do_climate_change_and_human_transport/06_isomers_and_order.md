# Union, dependency order, and analogues

**Mode: `specified` on the applied claim, `general` on the structure. Simulated derivation checked against the figures, not a ground reading (`05_what_shape_means.md` §7).**

## RESIDUAL

The question asks how two drivers *interact*. Three things are owed: (1) is the two-driver problem a union or a partition; (2) in what dependency order do arrival, establishment and cascade sit; (3) what analogues from the figures and the world make the compound checkable. Each carries its own falsifier.

## DOMAIN CENSUS (carried from `01` §1)

Atoms/actors held: the propagule, the habitat (conditions: light, water, temperature, season, terrain; relations: pollination, predation, competition, dependency), the vector (ports, corridors, trade), and the changing conditions (warming, extremes). Bonds/operators held: `→` sequence, `⋛` comparison, and the six-term substrate's network and gradient structures. No new primitive is claimed.

## DERIVATION

### Q1: Union or partition?

- Fig 9 :54: **"And when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again."**
- Fig 9 :55: **"A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones. A single lever clears the region where only one condition holds and leaves the intersections standing, because the second condition still binds."**
- Fig 9 :57: **"What remains is smaller and it is also categorically different: a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either. It stops being a mixture and becomes a specific compound."**

Climate and transport overlap on the habitat: warming changes the recipient conditions *and* opens new routes (Arctic shipping, in the IUCN brief); transport delivers into freshly suitable habitat. This is a union. A partition model (two separate stresses) is Option A and is refused by the figure.

**Falsifier (written before the run):** an additive no-interaction model predicts observed spread as well as the compound model on held-out regions. If it does, the union reading fails and the partition reading stands.

### Q2: Dependency order of the chain

- Fig 3 :20: **"The chain is ordered by dependency, not by importance. Each link consumes what the one above produces, so effort spent above the break arrives at a step that cannot use it."**
- Fig 3 :25: **"Perception has obvious failures and obvious tests. Planning has obvious failures and obvious tests. The link between them, having terms for what a situation offers and being able to say what would count as progress, has neither, so it attracts the least attention and the fewest instruments."**
- Fig 3 :27: **"A link with no instrument is not a link that works. It is a link nobody has looked at."**

The chain for this question:

```
1 arrival (vector, propagule pressure)
        ↓  consumes: a pathway exists and is used
2 suitability (habitat conditions after climate change)
        ↓  consumes: a conditions slice that can support the propagule
3 establishment and local spread (relations begin to act)
        ↓  consumes: a self-sustaining population
4 cascade (impacts carried three steps out through relations)
```

The weak middle link is 2: whether a habitat's conditions now support establishment. The world source Bellard et al. 2018 reports that modelling studies *"have rarely considered the effects of climate change on transport, introduction success, or the resulting impacts,"* and that they often omit *"biotic interactions, life-history traits, or habitat fragmentation."* Effort spent improving arrival data (link 1) cannot rescue link 2.

**Falsifier:** demonstrate that link 2 has a working, widely used instrument that resolves establishment under changed conditions, so the "no instrument" claim is false. The audit would then move the break elsewhere, not withdraw the chain.

### Q3: Is the compound an isomer, a functional group, or something else?

Fig 12 :117: **"isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."**
Fig 12 :124: **"functional group. A substructure whose presence fixes the outcome. The group says what kind; the qualifier says which member."**

This question is **not** an isomer question. The two drivers do not share the same atom set arranged differently; they are different atoms in a union. The correct Fig 12 reading is a **bonded compound**: the ingredient list (climate, transport) is not the interaction, and *"A set of ingredients is not one. The bond is the rest of the information, and it is the half nothing was recording."* If someone tries to treat climate-only spread and transport-only spread as isomers of one another, that is a category error; they are different molecules, not rearrangements.

What *does* behave like a functional group is the invasion stage sequence: transport, introduction, establishment, spread (the IPBES stages). The group says what kind (an invasion stage); the qualifier says which member (arrival versus establishment). The compound's bond connects two stages that belong to different conditions.

**Falsifier:** show that the two drivers can be written as the same atoms under a different bond (a genuine isomer) with identical predictive content. That would collapse the union into a rearrangement and kill F2's categorical-difference claim.

### Q4: What order is the union?

Fig 12 :111: **"A vocabulary without bonds can name a state and cannot name a route to one."**
Fig 12 :112: **"A plan is a sequence. A goal is a comparison. Progress is a subtraction."**

The union is ordered, not symmetric. Arrival precedes suitability in the individual species' history (a propagule must get there before conditions can select it), but climate changes the conditions *before* arrival in the regional history (warming opens the route and the habitat first). So the compound is not a single `→` chain; it is two ordered lines that meet at the habitat. This is why a single-lever model fails: each line arrives at the meeting point from a different dependency direction.

**Falsifier:** exhibit a case where suitability is determined only after arrival and never before it (so the climate line has no upstream role), or a case where the transport line has no dependency on changed conditions. Either would collapse the two lines into one and make the union a sequence.

### Q5: Analogues that make the compound checkable

| analogue | source | what it licenses | caveat |
|---|---|---|---|
| DDT biomagnification | Fig 11 :52-54 | first-order enumeration misses a second-order effect three steps out | a known case, not a forward test of this compound (figure's own admission at :71) |
| Higher-order shipping networks | Saebi et al. 2020 (world) | first-order Markov models underpredict spread; indirect pathways matter | same Lloyd's data pool as Kaluza et al. 2010, so one witness |
| Cargo-ship small-world topology | Kaluza et al. 2010 (world) | network clustering raises vulnerability to spread even at low invasion probability | network structure, not climate coupling |
| Enemy release | Meijer et al. 2016 (world) | absence of regulators is a relation, with variable outcome | variability matches Fig 11 :50, does not prove universality |
| Spectrometer | *The Hard Problem is About Source* (essay) | a source cannot be named from a lossy reading; an instrument is owed | methodological analogue, not ecological evidence |

Each analogue is a corroborating frame, not a second witness where the evidence pools overlap (Fig 2 :40: *"agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool"*).

## CONCLUSION

The two drivers form a **union compound with a bond, not a list and not a partition**. The dependency chain runs arrival, suitability, establishment, cascade, and its weak link is suitability, where the instrument is missing. The compound is not an isomer; the invasion stages are a functional group. The order is two lines meeting at the habitat, which is exactly why one lever leaves the intersection standing. One falsifier per sub-derivation is recorded above and none has been run here.
