## 0. The residual, stated in effect terms

**Question as effect.** We are asked to decide what the interaction between climate change and human transport *does* to invasive species spread, and whether the two are independent levers or one compound. The effect to be explained: a species appears in a habitat it did not evolve in, survives, reproduces, and transforms the ecosystem; the rate is rising.

**Option A:** climate change and transport are additive stresses. Manage each on its own.
**Option B:** they are two overlapping conditions of one union compound acting on a habitat. A single lever leaves the intersection standing.

**What an answer would look like (bound as an ordinary term before the run).** A figure-licensed shape stating: (a) that the habitat is enumerated, not composed, and which actors, conditions and relations must be on the list; (b) whether the two drivers form a partition or a union; (c) which actor has a seat and which is only a change of conditions; (d) how first-order arrival becomes a second-order cascade; (e) where an instrument is missing; and (f) a refuter that would kill the shape. This is the "Direction" discipline from `THE_FORMULA.md`: write down what an answer would look like, bind it, and let it accrue a residual like anything else. The refuter in §3 was written before any world source was opened.

**R by arity, symmetry, scale.**
- *Arity:* at least three. The species (propagule), the habitat (conditions plus relations), and the vector (transport). Climate is a change of the conditions, not a fourth actor with a seat.
- *Symmetry:* not symmetric. Introduction is one-way: *"Introduction is available; exclusion is not"* (Fig 11 :61). Warming is directional. A shipping route has a source and a destination. A symmetric conjunction of two independent stresses is the wrong operator.
- *Scale:* first order inside one habitat (species meets conditions), second order at the cascade (impacts carried through relations), third order at the regional network (ports, corridors, range shifts). Fig 11 :52 calls the middle step *"second-order effect"* with the residual *"three steps out."*

## 1. Domain census (before any figure line is chosen)

One line per domain with actors / currency / anchor. A domain with no nameable anchor is not a domain yet.

| domain | actors | currency | anchor | status |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **D1 Recipient ecology (the habitat)** | propagule, natives, predators, competitors, mutualists, pathogens; conditions (light, water, temperature, season, terrain); relations (pollination, predation, competition, dependency) | survival, establishment, contact | field observation of what actually survives and the measured impact | domain (anchor nameable) |
| **D2 Climate as change of conditions** | temperature, water, season, terrain; extreme events (flood, drought, storm) | suitability, range position, establishment probability | measured climate variables and recorded range shifts | domain (instrument/ground) |
| **D3 Human transport as introduction** | ships (ballast, hull fouling), trade, roads, rail, canals, tourism, migrating people; ports and corridors | propagule pressure, introduction events, network connectivity | introduction records, vessel data, network topology | domain (records/ground) |
| **D4 Verification and outreach** | expert readers (invasion ecologists, biosecurity managers) | residuals and agreement | the ground (field data), never agreement | domain, independence approximately zero in this simulated run |

**Partition or union?** A **union**, not a partition. D2 and D3 overlap on D1: climate rewrites the recipient conditions *and* opens new introduction pathways (a warming Arctic shortens shipping routes); transport delivers propagules into habitats climate has made suitable. They are not separable boxes.

**Dependency order (Fig 3).** Arrival (D3, who gets where) precedes establishment (D1/D2, does the conditions slice support it), which precedes cascade (D1 relations carry the impact outward). Fig 3 :20: *"The chain is ordered by dependency, not by importance. Each link consumes what the one above produces, so effort spent above the break arrives at a step that cannot use it."* The weak middle link is the suitability step: the world source Bellard et al. 2018 reports that modelling studies *"have rarely considered the effects of climate change on transport, introduction success, or the resulting impacts"* (world, secondary; see `09`).

**Strain test applied before concluding.** Reading climate as a "seat acting on the habitat" strains: climate has no seat, it changes conditions. Re-census: climate belongs to the conditions/substitution reading of D2; transport belongs to the seat/introduction reading of D3. Different quantities, so no strain remains (see §6). An earlier draft tried to place extreme events under transport ("climate transports species"). That was rejected because it conflates the vector with the condition that opens the vector. Kept visible here as a correction.

## 3. Findings F1 to F9

Each finding carries its verbatim figure line(s), its mode, its triangulation status, and its refuter.

### F1: The habitat is enumerated, never composed (governs the whole residual)

Fig 11 :43: **"The habitat is enumerated, never composed."**
Fig 11 :44: **"List everything in contact with the residual, then what is in contact with those, and outward until the cascade stops mattering."**
Fig 11 :46: **"conditions: light, water, temperature, season, terrain"**
Fig 11 :47: **"relations: pollination, predation, competition, dependency"**
Fig 11 :48: **"You do not invent the list. You read it off the world, and what you cannot perceive or measure yet is the residual."**

Seat Map :215 (secondary framing): *"The habitat is enumerated, never composed. List everything in contact with the residual, actors, environment, relations, conditions, then what is in contact with those, and outward until the cascade stops mattering."*

**Reading.** The answer is not obtained by composing a species-trait list. It is obtained by reading off the world everything in contact with the spread: the propagule, the predators and competitors left behind, the mutualists gained, the conditions climate has shifted, and the vectors that deliver. What cannot yet be measured is the residual, not a reason to stop.

**Mode:** `general` (structural), triangulated across Fig 11 and the Seat Map (two framings, one evidence pool, so weak triangulation).
**Refuter:** produce the habitat by composing from a fixed trait list, with no enumeration of contacts, and show it predicts a novel arrival out of sample; or exhibit an actor in contact whose omission does not move the residual.

### F2: Climate and transport are a union compound, not two additive levers (decides the question)

Fig 9 :55: **"A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones. A single lever clears the region where only one condition holds and leaves the intersections standing, because the second condition still binds."**
Fig 9 :57: **"What remains is smaller and it is also categorically different: a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either. It stops being a mixture and becomes a specific compound."**
Fig 9 :54: **"And when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again."**

Fig 1 :19 (supporting): *"The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated."*

**Reading.** Option A is the "list of separate ones." Clearing transport alone (quarantine) leaves the region where climate alone makes a habitat suitable; clearing climate alone (mitigation) leaves the arrival pathway open. Where both bind, the residue is *categorically different*: a novel species arriving into a freshly suitable habitat with its regulators left behind. The two conditions must be unbundled before the compound is searched.

**Mode:** `general` (structural), triangulated across Fig 9 (union rule) and Fig 1 (no aggregation) plus the Seat Map :211 (union unbundling). Different figures, low overlap between them.
**Refuter:** an additive model of climate + transport with no interaction term predicts observed spread as well as a compound model on held-out regions; specifically the intersection contributes no residual beyond the sum of the single levers.

### F3: Transport is introduction, and the seat's power is asymmetric

Fig 11 :61: **"The seat's power is asymmetric. Introduction is available; exclusion is not. You can add an actor and you cannot subtract the habitat, because contact is a fact about"** (the SVG line continues onto the next extract line, *"the world and does not consult you."*)
Fig 11 :63: **"Which is why a managed ecosystem is permanent work rather than a solved state."**
Fig 11 :65: **"Introducing an actor for its cascade is ecosystem engineering, after Jones, Lawton and Shachak, 1994. Cane toads and kudzu are the same move with the cascade unenumerated."**

**Reading.** Humans, through transport, occupy the "maintained" seat: they introduce actors into habitats. They cannot subtract the habitat, because contact does not consult them. That is why biosecurity is permanent work, and why the figure's own examples (cane toads, kudzu) are introductions whose cascades were not enumerated. Climate is *not* this seat; it is the condition change (F5).

**Mode:** `general` to `specified` (the application to shipping is specified), triangulated with Seat Map :225.
**Refuter:** exhibit a reliable exclusion lever that removes an established actor from a habitat without substituting a habitat; or show a managed ecosystem reaching a solved state that stays solved with no further work.

### F4: The interaction is carried by contact cascades; first-order enumeration misses it

Fig 11 :52: **"And contact cascades. Scoping the habitat to direct contact is how DDT reached the eagles: first-order enumeration, second-order effect, and the residual that mattered was three"** (continues *"steps out."*)
Fig 11 :54: **"Carson, Silent Spring, 1962. The mechanism, biomagnification through the food chain to eggshell thinning, was established over the following decade."**

**Reading.** A species introduced for one reason acts through relations three steps out. The world source Saebi et al. 2020 makes the same point in network language: *"the first-order Markovian assumption limits accurate modeling of NIS spread risks through the global shipping network"* (world, secondary). The compound's damage is not at the point of arrival.

**Mode:** `general`, corroborated at the world by two independent fields (ecotoxicology case in the figure; transport-network modelling in the world source).
**Refuter:** a first-order-contact-only model predicts the observed impact without the cascade, so the second-order term adds nothing out of sample.

### F5: Climate changes conditions and substitutes habitats; two failure modes are silent

Fig 11 :46: **"conditions: light, water, temperature, season, terrain"**
Fig 11 :66: **"Isolation is not removal of the habitat. It is substitution of one habitat for another."**
Fig 11 :67: **"Move the residual and you must reproduce everything its goal required, and you inherit the new habitat’s actors, which you did not choose."**
Fig 11 :68: **"what you failed to reproduce is invisible until the goal fails"**
Fig 11 :69: **"what you unintentionally introduced is invisible until it acts"**

**Reading.** Warming shifts the conditions slice and drives range shifts, which are habitat substitutions, not habitat removals. A species tracking its climate envelope inherits actors it did not choose (new competitors, new mutualists, new enemies). The two silent failure modes are exactly what a range-shift model omits: what the new habitat failed to supply, and what came along that was not intended. The IUCN issue brief corroborates the pathway half: *"Climate change is also opening up new pathways of introduction of IAS"* (world, secondary).

**Mode:** `general` on the substitution shape, `specified` on its application to range shifts, triangulated across Fig 11 and the Seat Map :255.
**Refuter:** a range-shifting species arrives in a new habitat and inherits none of its actors (no new interactions at all), and the goal still succeeds; or a silent failure mode is visible before the goal fails.

### F6: Actor valence is relative to the residual; relations are not kinds

Fig 11 :49: **"An actor’s valence is relative to the residual, not a property it carries."**
Fig 11 :50: **"The same actor is a benefactor to one residual and a malfactor to another, so mutualism and parasitism are relations rather than kinds."**

**Reading.** Climate change can flip an actor's valence: a native competitor can become a facilitator under new conditions; a predator left behind by transport changes from regulator to absent relationship. This is why "enemy release" is a relation, not a fixed property of the species. Meijer et al. 2016 (world, secondary) support enemy release but report high variability and *"no simple relationship between enemy release and the success of non-native species,"* which is the context-dependence this figure predicts.

**Mode:** `general`, triangulated across Fig 11 and the Seat Map :217, with world corroboration that is explicitly variable rather than universal.
**Refuter:** exhibit a standing list of "good" and "bad" actors whose valence is unchanged for every member across a change of residual.

### F7: Capability is species-and-habitat, not species alone

Fig 11 :39: **"So the answerable question is not what intelligence is, but what makes a system capable. And capability is a property of agent-and-habitat, never of the agent alone."**
Fig 11 :40: **"A system cannot be made more capable by improving the agent in isolation. An improvement that does not change contact changes nothing, however much it improves."**
Fig 11 :71: **"How to read the claims here. Enumeration and asymmetry are rules this framework imposes on itself. The isolation law and the capability claim fit cases already known and have not been tested forward, so they"** (continues *"can still come back wrong: an agent-only improvement that moved the ground would refute the capability claim."*)

**Reading.** Invasiveness is not a property of the species alone. Trait-only risk assessment is agent-in-isolation and misses contact. The figure itself flags this as a standing candidate refuter, so it is recorded as live, not hidden. Bellard et al. 2018 corroborate that land use, habitat and biotic interactions are usually omitted from range models.

**Mode:** `general`, corroborated at the world, but explicitly *"not tested forward"* by the figure's own admission.
**Refuter:** an agent-only improvement that moves the ground, that is, a species' invasiveness changes with no change in contact; or a trait-only model predicts invasion across changed habitats with no loss of accuracy.

### F8: The habitat is not the ground; agreement is not corroboration

Fig 11 :73: **"The habitat is not the ground. The habitat is where actors come from and it updates; the ground is what settles, and it does not."**
Fig 2 :40: **"disqualifying for a verdict: agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool"**
Fig 2 :42: **"Buy independence in proportion to the cost of being wrong."**

**Reading.** Field data, not the roster of actors, settles the claim. The habitat updates; the ground does not. In this simulated run all four domains draw on one evidence pool, so their agreement is one pool talking. Three of the six world sources (IPBES, IUCN, the policy assessments) are themselves the same assessment lineage and count as one witness.

**Mode:** `general`, triangulated across Fig 11 and Fig 2.
**Refuter:** two genuinely independent instruments (different evidence pools) agree on the compound's contribution, which would be real corroboration; or the ground is observed to move when a frame pushes it.

### F9: Unreachable is a verdict, not a silence

Fig 8 :39: **"So a search that finds nothing is never a proof of absence."**
Fig 9 :44: **"Only a sealed room can be searched to the end, and no frame is a sealed room."**

**Reading.** The world literature's contradictory evidence on climate-driven range change (Bellard et al. 2018: *"contradictory evidence, and no consensus has been reached"*) is not a proof that no shape exists. It is a report that the current instruments do not resolve it. The honest stop is to name the missing instrument, not to manufacture a verdict.

**Mode:** `general`.
**Refuter:** demonstrate that the relevant closure is sealed (all admissible evidence enumerated and searched) and that absence therefore follows.

## 5. Pre-registered refuter

Written before any world source was opened, per Seat Map :287: *"a falsifier written before the run is a design instrument, not only an honesty device."*

The compound shape is **refuted** if any of the following fires:

1. An additive model of climate + transport with no interaction term predicts observed invasive spread as well as a compound model on held-out regions (kills F2).
2. A first-order contact enumeration predicts the observed impacts without a cascade term (kills F4).
3. The same species, with unchanged traits and unchanged habitat conditions, spreads at the same rate before and after a transport-pathway change (kills F3 applied to transport).
4. A range-shifting species inherits none of the new habitat's actors and the goal still succeeds, or a "silent" failure mode is visible before the goal fails (kills F5).
5. A trait-only model predicts invasion success across changed habitats with no loss of accuracy, or an agent-only improvement moves the ground (kills F7).
6. Two genuinely independent instruments agree that the intersection contributes nothing beyond the sum of single levers (kills the verdict at the ground, per F8).

Until one is run, no part of this may be labelled `grounded`.

## 7. Recommendation

1. Lead with the union claim and its refuter, not with a list of drivers (F2, F9).
2. Enumerate the habitat from the world before choosing any frame (F1; Fig 9 :27-28 *"describe it before you go looking, or any frame you pick will seem to fit"*).
3. Invest in the weak middle link: instruments that resolve suitability under changed conditions, since effort above the break cannot arrive where it is needed (Fig 3 :20-26).
4. Treat transport as asymmetric introduction and biosecurity as permanent work (F3).
5. Do not pool agreement from the same assessment lineage as corroboration (F8). Buy independence in proportion to the cost of being wrong.
