## QUESTION

Decide, in effect terms, what the Tether says about the observed acceleration in invasive species spread. Option A: climate change and human transport are independent, additive stresses, so each can be managed alone. Option B: they are two overlapping conditions of one union compound acting on a habitat, so managing one lever leaves the intersection standing. What each leads to: A applies single levers and leaves a categorically different residue at the intersection; B describes the compound, names the missing instrument, and carries a refuter written in advance. The sought-for shape is bound as an ordinary term in `01` §0 before any figure line is chosen.

## DOMAIN CENSUS

R by arity, symmetry and scale, then one line per domain with actors / currency / anchor, partition or union, and order by dependency.

- **R (the residual).** The accelerating spread of invasive species under two named drivers. Arity: three (propagule, habitat, vector; climate is a change of conditions). Symmetry: not symmetric, introduction is one-way and warming is directional. Scale: first order inside one habitat, second order at the cascade, third order at the regional network. Fig 9 names it a union compound, not one hard problem and not two separate ones.
- **Domain 1, recipient ecology (the habitat).** Actors: the propagule, natives, predators, competitors, mutualists, pathogens; conditions and relations. Currency: survival, establishment, contact. Anchor: field observation of what survives and the measured impact. Question type: enumerate the habitat, never compose it.
- **Domain 2, climate as change of conditions.** Actors: temperature, water, season, terrain, extreme events. Currency: suitability and range position. Anchor: measured climate variables and recorded range shifts. Question type: a change of the conditions slice and a substitution of habitats, not a seat.
- **Domain 3, human transport as introduction.** Actors: ships (ballast, hull fouling), trade, roads, rail, canals, tourism, people; ports and corridors. Currency: propagule pressure and introduction events. Anchor: introduction records, vessel data, network topology. Question type: the maintained seat, with asymmetric power.
- **Domain 4, verification and outreach.** Actors: expert readers. Currency: residuals and agreement. Anchor: the ground, never agreement. In this simulated run all four domains draw on one evidence pool, so independence is approximately zero.
- **Union, not partition.** Domains 2 and 3 overlap on Domain 1: warming changes recipient conditions and opens routes; transport delivers into freshly suitable habitat.
- **Order by dependency.** Arrival (Domain 3) precedes suitability (Domains 1 and 2), which precedes establishment, which precedes cascade. The weak link is suitability, where the instrument is missing (Fig 3 :20, :27). Fig 9 :54: unbundle the union before searching again.

Strain test applied: reading climate as a seat strains; re-census places climate in conditions/substitution and transport in introduction. Different quantities, so no strain after re-census. An earlier draft placed extreme events under transport; corrected and retained in `01` §6.

## VERDICT (from the figures)

The compound reading survives; the additive-list reading does not. Climate change and human transport are two overlapping conditions of one union compound acting on a habitat. Transport supplies asymmetric introduction with no exclusion lever; climate changes the recipient conditions and substitutes habitats with two silent failure modes; the impact is a cascade carried through relations, not an arrival event; actor valence is relative to the residual; and invasiveness is a property of species-and-habitat, not of the species alone. The habitat is enumerated from the world, never composed from a trait checklist. The verdict is `specified`, ungrounded, candidate. The decisive open instrument is an additive-versus-interaction test on held-out regions.

- **cost if wrong:** bounded and locatable. The packet states its mode, carries refuters before the conclusion and records the lineage, so an error is findable rather than invisible. The real cost is misallocating management effort: a single-lever programme leaves the intersection standing while appearing to act. Fig 2 :42 prices the remedy: *"Buy independence in proportion to the cost of being wrong."* That cost is reducible by running the Interaction Test with independent data.
- **undoable:** yes. The compound reading is added to, not substituted for, the single-lever readings; no primitive is withdrawn and no actor list is discarded. A superseding packet can be issued if a refuter fires, and the refuter stays in the record (Fig 6 :42: *"A refutation adds a term that refuses; it removes nothing, and the reach falls because something was added rather than because anything was deleted."*).
- **reversed by:** a ground reading, not agreement. An Interaction Test showing an additive model matches out of sample would kill the compound verdict; a first-order contact model predicting impacts would kill the cascade verdict; a reliable exclusion lever, a range shifter inheriting no new actors, or a trait-only model that loses no accuracy would kill the other sub-claims. A second seat drawing on the same evidence pool is not such a reading. Until one of these fires, the verdict stands as `candidate`.
