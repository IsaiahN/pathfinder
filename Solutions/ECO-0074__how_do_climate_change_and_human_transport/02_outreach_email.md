# Outreach draft: check the shape first, then decide about the framework

**Use:** one-to-one, or as a cover note attached to a submission through the public venues in `03_public_contact_points.csv`. **Not** for bulk send.

**Hard precondition:** do not send until the three sub-claims in `08_expert_verification_packet.md` have been checked by someone who has not read this folder's interiors. If the shape fails there, there is nothing else to look at.

---

**Subject:** One checkable claim about climate, transport, and invasive spread

> Hi [name],
>
> I work on a figure-grounded method for stating claims as shapes and testing them against a ground. I have one specific output about invasive species spread that I would like you to check against what you already know. I am not asking you to read the framework first, and I am not asking you to agree with it. If the output does not survive, there is nothing else to look at.
>
> **The output (`specified`, not `grounded`):** climate change and human transport are not two independent, additive stresses on invasive species. They are two overlapping conditions of one compound acting on a habitat. Clearing one lever leaves the region where the other still binds. The figure this comes from puts it this way: *"A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones. A single lever clears the region where only one condition holds and leaves the intersections standing, because the second condition still binds."* What is categorically different at the intersection is a species arriving, by a human vector, into a habitat whose conditions have shifted and whose regulators were left behind. This is a prediction of a **shape**, not a measurement.
>
> Three parts of the shape, each with its own check:
>
> 1. **Transport is introduction, and it is asymmetric.** *"Introduction is available; exclusion is not."* Biosecurity can add actors and pathways; it cannot subtract the habitat. That is why managed ecosystems are permanent work rather than a solved state.
> 2. **Climate changes conditions and substitutes habitats.** A range shift is not a habitat removed but one habitat swapped for another, and the species *"inherit[s] the new habitat's actors, which you did not choose."* The two failure modes are silent: what the new habitat failed to supply, and what came along that was not intended.
> 3. **The damage is a cascade, not an arrival event.** Direct-contact enumeration misses effects several steps out. The figure's own case is DDT reaching eagles through the food chain; the transport-network literature makes the same point with higher-order pathways in shipping.
>
> **What I am not claiming:** that this is a new ecological result, that trait-only risk assessment is useless everywhere, or that I have measured the interaction. It is a structural claim about the shape any adequate answer must have, with a refuter written in advance.
>
> **What would refute the shape (written before I looked at any data):**
> 1. An additive climate + transport model with no interaction term predicts observed spread as well as a compound model on held-out regions.
> 2. A first-order contact model predicts the observed impacts without a cascade term.
> 3. The same species with unchanged traits and unchanged habitat conditions spreads at the same rate before and after a transport pathway change.
> 4. A trait-only model predicts invasion across changed habitats with no loss of accuracy.
>
> If any one holds, I will retract the shape. A one-line "this fails because..." is more useful to me than agreement. If you would rather not engage, no reply is needed.
>
> **If it survives, and only then:** the sources are below. The figures are the authority, not my summary of them.
> - Figure text extract with line numbers (local framework reference): `FRAMEWORK/reference/03-FIGURES_TEXT.md`
> - Canonical usage rule (local): `FRAMEWORK/reference/FIGURES_README_canonical.md`
> - Public figures repository: https://github.com/IsaiahN/tether/tree/general-agent/figures
>
> **If you want to use the machinery:** the formula is in the figures link. Run it on a problem you know well and can check against a ground. Write down what would refute your result *before* you run it, and state the mode of what you get. A reported failure is more useful to me than a reported success.
