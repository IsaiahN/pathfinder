**Subject:** One checkable claim about the unique-species versus many-declines trade-off

> Hi [name],
>
> I have one specific output to check against what you already know. I am not asking you to read a framework first, and I am not asking you to agree with a conclusion. If the output does not survive, there is nothing else to look at.
>
> **The output (`general` on the shape, `specified` on the mechanisms, not `grounded`):** the question asks for a single weight that trades the loss of one unique species against the decline of many widespread species. The derivation says the requested number is not licensed. The two losses are different operators. Losing the unique species removes an atom from the held set, and no recombination of what remains can rebuild it. The many declines reduce abundance in atoms that are still held, so recovery there needs no new atom. A framework the figures define says two quantities in different units cannot be added, and that summing them is the error rather than the fix. A priority score may still decide what gets tried first under a budget; it must not decide what counts as the loss.
>
> Three further checks fall out of the same derivation. First, uniqueness is a relation to a function slot, not a standing label: a species is unique where no other held species performs that function, and its value flips if the function changes. Second, the two candidates are not a clean pair of alternatives. A widespread decline that crosses a viability threshold is itself an atom loss, so the case with both conditions must be unbundled before it is searched. Third, irreversibility raises the burden of proof before action; it does not supply the number the question asks for.
>
> **What I am not claiming:** that the unique species always wins, that the many declines always win, that any Red List category or phylogenetic score is the answer, or that the framework settles an ethical question. The framework returns a shape and a refuter, not a weight and not a species name. Any universal scalar is marked as a gap, owed to a measurement that puts both losses in one currency and does not move when an analyst disagrees with it.
>
> **What would refute the output:**
> 1. a measurement that supplies one common currency for atom loss and mass decline and passes the anchor test, meaning it does not move when a frame disagrees;
> 2. a standing list of unique species that keeps its rank across function slots and residuals;
> 3. an aggregate community metric shown to settle a live function slot;
> 4. a case where the two candidates are a clean partition with no viability threshold and no shared actor;
> 5. a recovery shown to restore the original habitat with no continuing maintenance.
>
> I wrote these down before sending this. If any one holds, the shape claim fails and I will retract it. A one-line "this fails because..." is more useful to me than agreement. If you would rather not engage, no reply is needed.
>
> **If it survives, and only then:** the sources are below. The figures, not my summary, are the authority.
> - Local figure text extract with line numbers: `FRAMEWORK/reference/03-FIGURES_TEXT.md`
> - Canonical figures README (the usage rule): `FRAMEWORK/reference/FIGURES_README_canonical.md`
> - The eight-step loop and mode rule: `FRAMEWORK/figures/THE_FORMULA.md`
>
> **If you want to use the machinery:** the loop is in the figures link. Run it on a decision you know well and can check against a ground. Write down what would refute your result *before* you run it. The framework's own rule is that a loop cannot check the direction of its own question, and the founding essay warns that outputs could "never be verified if the solutions presented were not contrived to fit the formula." A reported failure is more useful to me than a reported success.
