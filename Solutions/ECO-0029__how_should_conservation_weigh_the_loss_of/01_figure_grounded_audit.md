## 0. The residual, stated in effect terms

A conservation budget is finite. Two candidate losses are put on one scale to be weighed:

- **L_unique:** a species with no functional equivalent disappears. Before the loss, function `f` was performed at rate `r_f`; after it, the observed rate is `o_f`, and no other held atom composes `f`. The atom is gone from the habitat.
- **L_many:** many widespread species decline in abundance across many slots. The atoms remain held; their mass is reduced. A decline may cross a viability threshold and become a loss, so the two candidates are not disjoint.

The residual is `R_s = |r_s - o_s|` per function slot `s`, indexed by slot, never by community total. The question asks for a scalar `w` such that `w * L_unique` can be compared with `(1 - w) * L_many`.

Three properties of `R` must be fixed before any figure line is selected.

- **Arity.** `R` is indexed by function slot. More than one function can fail at once, and the same widespread species can be a compensator for one slot and a malfactor for another. Fig 1 :19: "The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated." Fig 1 :22: "Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average." When the losses share actors or functions, the case is a union compound and must be unbundled before search (Fig 9 :54).
- **Symmetry.** Non-symmetric. An actor's value is relative to the residual, not carried as a property. Fig 11 :49: "An actor's valence is relative to the residual, not a property it carries." Swapping the function slot can flip the verdict for the same species.
- **Scale.** L_unique is first-order at the species and can cascade several steps outward; Fig 11 :52: "Scoping the habitat to direct contact is how DDT reached the eagles: first-order enumeration, second-order effect, and the residual that mattered was three steps out." L_many is a first-order mass reduction in many atoms, but the aggregate can cross a threshold into atom loss. The decision-relevant object spans both scales.

The question supplies its comparison before it supplies the failed function. That ordering is a known hazard. Fig 9 :27-28: "describe it before you go looking, or any frame you pick will seem to fit." Any dataset will appear to support "weighing" once the frame is chosen first. So the audit describes `R_s` in effect terms first and asks only afterwards whether a weight can compose over it.

**Binding the sought-for shape as an ordinary term (Direction discipline).** Before any figure line is applied, the answer-shape is written down so it can accrue a residual and be refused: *a per-slot verdict of the form "for slot s, this commitment closed R_s and the pre-declared measurement agrees" or "for slot s, R_s persists and the commitment fails," with the two losses kept in separate columns and the universal weight marked unreachable unless a ground reading supplies it.* This is a candidate term, not a conclusion.

## 1. Domain census, before choosing any figure line

R by arity, symmetry and scale is stated above. One line per domain giving actors, currency and anchor follows. A domain without a nameable anchor is not yet a domain.

### Domain 1: the library and the operator that removes an atom (framework-internal)

- **Actors:** species as atoms, functions as molecules, functional groups as substructures, the seven bonds and negation.
- **Currency:** composition inside `closure(Γ)`, settlement against the ground, and the irreversible subtraction of an atom from the held set.
- **Anchor:** the figures for legality of composition, the ground for settlement. Both are nameable, so this is a domain.
- **Question type:** partition with a union overlap. Whether a function can be recomposed from held atoms is a partition question (Fig 13 :115). Whether a decline has already crossed into atom loss is a union question (Fig 9 :55).
- **Why it is a domain:** the two candidate losses are different operators here. L_unique is a subtraction: Fig 12 :82 "A with B removed or absent", with the identifying test at Operators :45 "is the second operand's absence the point? Yes." The absence is the point. L_many is a reduction of mass in atoms that remain in the closure, recoverable by rearrangement. Fig 6 :21: "MINT composes inside the closure and can never add an atom." An atom removed by extinction cannot be re-minted from the remaining ones.

### Domain 2: the habitat, contact and the decline-to-loss boundary

- **Actors:** the remaining species, conditions (light, water, temperature, season, terrain), relations (predation, pollination, competition, dependency), and viability thresholds.
- **Currency:** contact with the residual. A species counts only by what it does to `R_s`.
- **Anchor:** the measured function per slot, which does not update. Fig 11 :73: "The habitat is not the ground. The habitat is where actors come from and it updates; the ground is what settles, and it does not."
- **Question type:** enumeration, not composition, with a threshold boundary. The habitat is read off the world by contact cascades (Fig 11 :43, :44, :52). The boundary where decline becomes loss is a comparison: Operators :49 "a threshold relation between two quantities."
- **Why it is a domain:** uniqueness is not intrinsic. A species is unique only relative to a function slot where no other held atom composes the same function, which is Fig 12 :123: "functional group. A substructure whose presence fixes the outcome. The group says what kind; the qualifier says which member." A "unique species" is a functional group with one qualifier; a "widespread species" is one with many. But Fig 11 :51 says "No standing list of good actors survives a change of residual," so the verdict cannot be transferred.

### Domain 3: settlement and the budget

- **Actors:** field experiments, long-term datasets, instruments, independent review frames, and the budget convention.
- **Currency:** residual versus agreement. Agreement is a budget signal; residual is the verdict signal. Fig 9 :60: "Use filters for the budget and witnesses for the verdict. Never let a filter hand you a verdict."
- **Anchor:** a pre-declared measurement of the specific function per slot. A measurement moves only if the world changes, so it qualifies (Fig 2 :29-30).
- **Question type:** triangulation. Two frames with low overlap may both range against the same measurement. Two prioritization models using the same dataset are one frame wearing two names.
- **Why it is a domain:** a scalar weight is a convention, and a convention that decides an outcome has stopped being a convention (Fig 2 :43). The day-to-day budget must be set, but it cannot be promoted to the ground that settles the verdict.

### Partition or union overall

The three domains form a **union**, not a partition. The decision is simultaneously an operator question (was an atom removed or was mass reduced), a contact question (did the change touch the failed slot), and a settlement question (does the measurement agree). A single lever that clears one condition leaves the intersections standing (Fig 9 :55). So each domain gets its own loop and its own verdict, and the verdicts are never averaged into one score.

### Order by dependency (Fig 3)

1. **Perception:** index `R` by function slot. Without this, a live slot hides inside an aggregate total.
2. **Vocabulary:** have a term for the function and for the functional group that could perform it. This is the middle link that usually breaks, and a link with no instrument is not a link that works (Fig 3 :27: "A link with no instrument is not a link that works. It is a link nobody has looked at.").
3. **Objective:** say what would count as progress in the community's own terms, per slot, and bind "unique" as a relation to a slot rather than a standing label.
4. **Planning:** enumerate the habitat outward by contact from the failed function until the cascade stops mattering, and locate the viability threshold for each widespread species.
5. **Learn and carry:** settle each slot against a pre-declared measurement and record the lineage so a later reader can locate the error.

Effort spent above a break cannot rescue a link below it (Fig 3 :19: "Optimising a link that already works cannot rescue one that does not."). A more elaborate weighting formula is effort above the break if the slot-level function and the viability threshold were never measured.

## 3. Findings F1 to F11

### F1: the two losses are different operators, so the requested scalar is a category error (decisive for the form of the question)

Fig 6 :21: "MINT composes inside the closure and can never add an atom."
Fig 13 :115: "The set is closed. The arrangements are not. That gap is why import exists."
Fig 12 :125: "A composition costs in two currencies, and they do not add."
Fig 12 :126: "Bits price the description. Actions price finding out whether it holds."
Fig 12 :130: "Summing them into one figure is the error this names, not the fix for it. A price in bits and a price in actions are two readings, held apart."

L_unique removes an atom. No composition over the remaining atoms can recreate the missing function, so recovery needs an import (Fig 6 :23), which is a different operator with a source gate and a shadow test. L_many reduces mass in atoms still held, so recovery is a rearrangement inside the closure, which needs no new atom. One is irreversible under the held library; the other is reversible. Fig 12 :125-130 names the general rule: two quantities in different units cannot be added, and summing them is the error rather than the fix.

Applied carefully: the figure line governs description cost versus test cost. Its structural content is that incommensurable readings must be held apart. The two losses are incommensurable in the same way (atom removal versus mass reduction), so the line governs by its structure. This is an instance, not a forced fit. The honest caveat is recorded in §6 S1: if a measurement supplies one currency that does not move when frames disagree, the refusal fails and the weight becomes legitimate. No such measurement was read.

**Verdict:** `general` on the structural rule, `specified` on the application to this domain, triangulated across Fig 6, Fig 13 and Fig 12, which have low overlap (wall rule, atom-set rule, pricing rule). Refuter: show that the two losses are the same currency under a measurement that passes the anchor test of Fig 2 :29-30, or re-census Fig 12 :125-130 so that it no longer forbids summing incommensurable readings.

### F2: a weight is a filter for the budget, never a witness for the verdict

Fig 9 :60: "Use filters for the budget and witnesses for the verdict. Never let a filter hand you a verdict."
Fig 2 :43: "Guard, from Figure 10: a convention that decides an outcome has stopped being one."

Conservation prioritization indices are filters. They decide what gets tried first under scarcity. Myers et al. (2000) state the budget premise directly: "Conservationists are far from able to assist all species under threat, if only for lack of funding. This places a premium on priorities: how can we support the most species at the least cost?" That is a budget question, and the figures permit a convention to answer it. The failure mode is promotion: once the index decides whether the unique species or the many declines count as the loss, the convention has become an authored verdict and has stopped being a convention. Wiedenfeld et al. (2021) dispute the scarcity premise itself, arguing that "A goal of no human-induced extinctions is imperative given the irreversibility of species loss." This conflict is about the budget constraint, not about the figure rule. It is returned to the census in §6 S4 and does not flip the verdict.

**Verdict:** `general`, triangulated (Fig 9 and Fig 2, plus the world conflict which is assigned, not pooled). Refuter: exhibit a prioritization index whose output is a measurement that does not move when a frame disagrees with it; that would make the index an anchor rather than a filter.

### F3: uniqueness is relational, so there is no standing list of species to weight

Fig 11 :49: "An actor's valence is relative to the residual, not a property it carries."
Fig 11 :50: "The same actor is a benefactor to one residual and a malfactor to another, so mutualism and parasitism are relations rather than kinds."
Fig 11 :51: "No standing list of good actors survives a change of residual."
Fig 12 :123: "functional group. A substructure whose presence fixes the outcome. The group says what kind; the qualifier says which member."

The question treats "a unique species" and "many widespread species" as standing kinds with fixed values. The figures deny that. Uniqueness is a relation between an atom and a function slot, established by checking whether any other held atom composes that function. That is a functional group with a single qualifier. A widespread species may fill the same functional group with many qualifiers, and a species that is rare in abundance may be functionally redundant while a common species may be functionally unique. The Wikipedia page on Common species states the field's own version: "Common species are frequently regarded as being at low risk of extinction simply because they exist in large numbers, and hence their conservation status is often overlooked." Gaston and Fuller (2008), cited there, argue that conservation should look more closely at "the trade-off between species extinctions and the depletion of populations," which is this question in the field's words.

Isaac et al. (2007) supply the uniqueness side: "Many species that are both evolutionarily distinct and globally endangered (EDGE species) do not benefit from existing conservation projects or protected areas." That is a real relational property, measured against a phylogeny and a threat score. It is a provenanced binding, not a name the framework invents. But its valence is still relative to the function slot and the residual, so it cannot be read as a universal weight.

**Verdict:** `specified`, triangulated (Fig 11 clauses plus two low-overlap world sources). Refuter: exhibit an actor whose valence for a fixed residual flips with no change in residual and no change in context; that would break the relational claim in the other direction. Or exhibit a standing list that retains its rank across function slots and residuals, which would break Fig 11 :51.

### F4: index the residual per slot, or the many-decline case hides the unique loss and vice versa

Fig 1 :19: "The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated."
Fig 1 :22: "Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average."
Fig 1 :23: "The ground enters as the only metric."

The phrase "the decline of many widespread species" is an aggregate. A Living Planet figure cited on the Wikipedia Biodiversity page reports that wildlife populations declined by an average 73% since 1970, and that of 70,000 monitored species around 48% are declining. That is a real signal about a distribution of declines. It is not a measurement of any one slot. A community can lose total abundance while a specific pollination or nutrient-cycling slot stays live, and it can hold total abundance steady while a unique-species slot stays unperformed. Fig 1 forbids reading the aggregate as the verdict.

The converse error is equally live: one charismatic unique species can conceal many failed function slots. Keeping the two losses in separate columns is not a stylistic preference; it is the only way to keep a live slot from disappearing into an average.

**Verdict:** `general` on the indexing rule, `specified` on the slot measurement, triangulated (Fig 1 plus the world aggregate, which corroborates the existence of many declines but not the verdict). Refuter: show a case where a slot residual persists while the aggregate is statistically unchanged and the aggregate is still accepted as settling the case; that repeats the exact error Fig 1 :22 names.

### F5: decline and loss are a union with a threshold, not a partition

Fig 9 :54: "And when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again."
Fig 9 :55: "A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones."
Fig 9 :57: "a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either."
Fig 12 :87: "a threshold relation between two quantities"
Fig 12 :89: "is it about which is larger, rather than both being present? Yes."

A widespread decline that crosses a viability threshold is an atom loss. The Wikipedia page on Extinction debt states the deferred case: "future extinction of species due to events in the past," and adds that "the number of species currently present in a habitat may not be a good measure of the habitat's ability to support species in the future." Present abundance is therefore not the anchor for the verdict; a pre-declared viability threshold is. The comparison ⋛ is a threshold relation, and Operators :52 adds "a goal is a comparison, which is why a vocabulary without it cannot state one." Binding the goal requires naming the threshold before the measurement.

The two candidates in the question are therefore overlapping conditions. A single lever that saves the unique species can leave the widespread declines standing, and a lever that lifts many abundances can leave the threshold-crossed species lost. They must be unbundled, and the intersections get their own loop.

**Verdict:** `specified`, triangulated (Fig 9 union rule, Fig 12 comparison definition, Extinction debt world source). Refuter: show a decline that demonstrably cannot cross into atom loss at any abundance, or show a case where the two candidates are a clean partition with no shared actor or threshold, and the union treatment is wrong for that case.

### F6: the habitat must be enumerated outward; first-order contact undercounts

Fig 11 :43: "The habitat is enumerated, never composed."
Fig 11 :44: "List everything in contact with the residual, then what is in contact with those, and outward until the cascade stops mattering."
Fig 11 :52: "Scoping the habitat to direct contact is how DDT reached the eagles: first-order enumeration, second-order effect, and the residual that mattered was three steps out."

To decide whether a species is functionally unique, the function's habitat must be read off the world, not off the species list. Direct interactors come first, then their contacts, until the cascade stops mattering. This is why a uniqueness claim based only on the immediate role of a species is weak: the decision-relevant residual can be three steps out, as in the DDT example. The same applies to the many-decline side: a decline in a basal species can register as a loss three steps away in a pollinator or a predator.

**Verdict:** `specified`, triangulated (Fig 11 plus the extinction-debt and common-species world sources, which give the field's own account of deferred and distributed effects). Refuter: exhibit a complete enumeration of first-ring contacts where a second- or third-order residual still closes the gap, meaning the cascade rule was applied too late; or exhibit a cascade enumeration that is complete and still leaves an unassigned residual.

### F7: irreversibility sets a burden of proof, not a lexical priority

Fig 6 :21: "MINT composes inside the closure and can never add an atom."
Fig 11 :61: "The seat's power is asymmetric. Introduction is available; exclusion is not."
Fig 11 :63: "Which is why a managed ecosystem is permanent work rather than a solved state."
Fig 11 :66: "Isolation is not removal of the habitat. It is substitution of one habitat for another."
Fig 2 :42: "Buy independence in proportion to the cost of being wrong."

Extinction is permanent under the held library. A reintroduction is an import, not a re-mint, and it lands in a substituted habitat with two silent failure modes: what was failed to reproduce and what was unintentionally introduced. Managed recovery is permanent work, not a solved state. So the more irreversible option carries a heavier burden of proof: independent checking is bought in proportion to the cost of being wrong.

This is a **procedural asymmetry, not a scalar weight**. It says how much checking is owed before committing, not how many "units" of loss the unique species represents. An earlier reading of this audit said "unique species always outweighs many declines because extinction is irreversible." That reading is narrowed here and kept visible in §6: the union boundary of F5 means a widespread decline that crosses viability is itself an atom loss, so irreversibility cannot function as a lexical priority.

**Verdict:** `specified`, triangulated (Fig 6 plus Fig 11 clauses plus Fig 2 :42). Refuter: exhibit a managed recovery that reaches a stable solved state with no further maintenance and in the original habitat, which would break the asymmetry and substitution claims.

### F8: settlement belongs to a pre-declared measurement, not to agreement or prestige

Fig 2 :29: "An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks."
Fig 2 :30: "If it moves when you push on it, it is not an anchor."
Fig 2 :35: "Alignment is triangulation, not negotiation. A mirror cannot correct you."
Fig 7 :35: "A room's agreement is variation, not a verdict. Selection between rooms is where the ground votes."
Fig 9 :42: "narrowing gets you close, the ground decides"

A prioritization index, a phylogeny score, a Red List category and a stable time series are all frames. Each can be internally perfect and still wrong about the failed slot. Only a pre-declared measurement of that slot settles it, and the measurement must not move when a frame disagrees. Fig 11 :73 separates the habitat from the ground: a healthy-looking habitat is not the anchor. Fig 10 :59 requires the declaration of stake: "an interest in the outcome is unavoidable, an interest in the reading is disqualifying." A conservation manager who wants the unique species to win has a stake in the outcome, which is allowed; a stake in which reading of the data gets recorded is not.

The Recursive Self-Improvement Trap gives the related prestige rule: "Prestige must never be allowed to validate an action directly." A well-known reviewer or a flagship species cannot settle the decision. Prestige can give a claim a budget to be tried; it cannot decide it.

**Verdict:** `general` on the anchor rule, `specified` on the slot measurement, triangulated (Fig 2, Fig 7, Fig 9, Fig 10, plus the essay). Refuter: show a settlement in which the measured value was adjusted until the competing models agreed; the verdict is void by Fig 2 :30.

### F9: the question is frame-first, so the returned object is a shape, not a weight

Fig 9 :27-28: "describe it before you go looking, or any frame you pick will seem to fit"
Fig 9 :32: "you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself"
Fig 13 :135: "It cannot say which primitive is missing, because naming it would mean already holding it."

The question names the comparison before it names the function that failed. Any community will show both a unique-species risk and many declines, so if the frame is chosen first every case will appear to require the same weighing. The correct order is to describe `R_s` in effect terms, then ask which frame predicts its shape. What comes back is a kind of thing: a per-slot decision procedure with a union boundary and a declared gap. It is not the number `w`, and it is not a species name. The canonical usage rule states that the framework reports a shape, not a name, and stops at the limit of the instruments.

**Verdict:** `general`, triangulated (Fig 9 and Fig 13 plus the canonical README). Refuter: produce a verdict reached by first selecting a prioritization frame and only then describing the function, and treat it as a candidate rather than a result.

### F10: the universal weight is a genuine `gap`, unreachable from these figures

Fig 13 :134: "Unreachable is a verdict here, not a silence"
Fig 8 :39: "So a search that finds nothing is never a proof of absence."
Fig 9 :32: "you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself"
Fig 13 :118: "infinite effort inside a finite reach."

No figure line ranks atom subtraction against distributed mass reduction on a common scale. WATSON step 7 distinguishes SEARCHED, INWARD and OUTWARD. The mechanism is already in closure (functional groups, comparison, subtraction, per-slot residuals are all held), so further reading in conservation prioritization is SEARCHED and may be at a budget limit rather than a genuine absence. INWARD would be a slot-resolved instrument with a pre-declared viability threshold. OUTWARD would be an import from a frame whose closure differs, for example reliability engineering's single point of failure or clinical triage. These two tracks were not read against each other (Fig 6 :31: "Neither answer tells you much about the other. Run them as two tracks, and never read one against the other.").

The correct output is `gap` for the universal weight, with the vacancy characterized in effect terms: what is owed is a measurement that returns a common currency and does not move when a frame disagrees. Absence of such a line in the figures is not evidence that no answer exists.

**Verdict:** `gap` for the scalar, `general` for the honest-stop rule, triangulated (Fig 13, Fig 8, Fig 9). Refuter: exhibit a figure line that does rank the two losses, or a ground measurement that supplies the common currency; either would close the gap.

### F11: habitat is not ground, and the budget is not a seat above the ground

Fig 11 :73: "The habitat is not the ground. The habitat is where actors come from and it updates; the ground is what settles, and it does not."
Fig 10 :24: "the ground does not decay, the channel does"
Fig 10 :43: "A verifier that can reconstruct the claim cannot verify it."

"Community condition" and "ecosystem health" are habitat properties. They change with season, abundance and management. They are not the anchor. If the decision is settled by a habitat description alone, the anchor has been replaced by a frame and the loop has lost its reference. Fig 10 :24 adds that the ground does not decay while the channel does: a saturated monitoring indicator is a channel failure, not a change in what settles the question.

**Verdict:** `general`, triangulated (Fig 11 and Fig 10). Refuter: identify a verdict settled by habitat description with no independent pre-declared measurement; mark it `candidate`, not `grounded`.

## 4. Pre-registered refuter

Written before the conclusion, per `THE_SEAT_MAP_Field_Guide_amended.md` line 287: "And a falsifier written before the run is a design instrument, not only an honesty device." The shape claim under test is: *the two losses are different operators, so no universal scalar weight is licensed; the verdict is per slot, the two candidates form a union with a threshold, uniqueness is relational, irreversibility is a burden of proof, and the universal weight is a `gap`.* It is refuted if any of the following holds.

1. **Common-currency refuter.** A measurement supplies one currency for atom loss and mass decline that does not move when a frame disagrees, so the anchor test of Fig 2 :29-30 is passed and F1's incommensurability fails.
2. **Pricing re-census.** Fig 12 :125-130 is re-censused so that incommensurable readings may legitimately be summed; F1 and F2 fall with it.
3. **Standing-list refuter.** A list of "unique species" retains its rank across function slots and residuals, breaking Fig 11 :49-51 and F3.
4. **Aggregate refuter.** An aggregate community metric is shown to settle a live slot residual, breaking Fig 1 :19/:22 and F4.
5. **Partition refuter.** The two candidates are shown to be a clean partition with no shared actor and no viability threshold, breaking Fig 9 :55-57 and F5.
6. **Reversibility refuter.** A recovery is shown to restore the original habitat with no continuing maintenance, breaking Fig 11 :61/:66 and F7.
7. **Weight-from-figures refuter.** A verbatim figure line is produced that ranks the two losses, closing the F10 gap without a ground reading.

If any fires, the specific shape claim fails. None of these refute the framework; they refute this application of it. Until one is run, no part of this document may be labelled `grounded`.

## 5. Recommendation

1. **Restate the decision per function slot.** Name `R_s` and the measurement that will read it. Do not answer for "the community" or for "biodiversity" as a single slot.
2. **Keep the two losses in separate columns.** Record which atoms were removed, which atoms lost mass, which bonds changed, and whether any threshold was crossed. Convergence and import look identical in the contents (Fig 8 :50-52).
3. **Bind uniqueness as a relation, not a label.** For each candidate species, enumerate the habitat outward (Fig 11 :44) and name the function slot for which no other held atom composes its role.
4. **Unbundle before weighting.** Separate the pure unique-loss cases, the pure decline cases and the threshold-crossing intersections (Fig 9 :54-57). Each gets its own loop.
5. **Pre-declare the viability threshold before the measurement.** ⋛ is a comparison (Operators :52), and a goal must be stated as a comparison before the ground decides.
6. **Treat the scalar as a budget filter only.** Let it decide what gets tried first, never what counts as the loss (Fig 9 :60, Fig 2 :43).
7. **Buy independence where the cost is highest.** The irreversible option carries the heavier burden of proof (Fig 2 :42).
8. **Offer the generator, not the conclusion.** Fig 10 :52: "Bring a characterised residual, never the source." Lead with the slot residual and its refuter; offer links after a verdict is recorded.

Read after sections 0 to 5, per the method.
