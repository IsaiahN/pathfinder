# Supporting derivation: subtraction, negation, functional groups and the threshold

**Mode: `specified` on the structure, `general` on the operator distinctions, not `grounded`.** This is a simulated derivation checked against the figures, not a ground reading (see `05_what_shape_means.md` §6).

## RESIDUAL

The main audit leaves four structural questions open: (Q1) is extinction a subtraction or a negation; (Q2) are the unique-loss case and the many-decline case isomers under Fig 12; (Q3) what is the order of the union and the threshold; (Q4) does the recorded lineage carry the information the final species list does not. Each is answered below with verbatim figure lines and a falsifier.

## DOMAIN CENSUS

Atoms held: species, functions, conditions and relations as read off the habitat. Operators held: the seven bonds plus negation. Relevant held operators: the comparison ⋛, the subtraction −, and the unary negation ¬. No new primitive is available without import. Currency: composition inside closure and settlement against the ground. Anchor: a pre-declared per-slot measurement.

## DERIVATION

### Q1: Is extinction subtraction or negation?

- Operators :45, the subtraction row: "is the second operand's absence the point? Yes." Fig 12 :82: "A with B removed or absent." The species is an absent operand, so subtraction describes the change in the ledger.
- Operators :58, the negation row: "a refutation is a negation, which is why a vocabulary without it can only accumulate." Fig 12 :139-140 notes that without a unary operator a system can say what is missing and cannot say that an arrangement is wrong.
- The lost species is therefore both: the operand is absent (−), and the standing claim "function f is performed in this community" is false (¬). They operate at different levels. Subtraction edits the ledger; negation refuses the claim built on the ledger. Fig 6 :41-43 makes the consequence precise: a refusal does not delete a term, it removes that term's standing to be composed over, so the reach falls while the record grows.
- **Why it matters:** a conservation ledger that records only the subtraction can show a smaller species list without showing which functions lost their standing to be composed. A ledger that also records the negation shows the refused function. This is the F7 shape: irreversibility is a burden of proof recorded in the ledger, not a number.
- **Falsifier:** if a case is found where a species is absent but every function claim built on it remains composable from held atoms, the negation half is unnecessary and the claim collapses to plain subtraction. That would also weaken F1's incommensurability argument.

### Q2: Are the unique-loss case and the many-decline case isomers?

- Fig 12 :117: "isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."
- Fig 12 :123: "functional group. A substructure whose presence fixes the outcome. The group says what kind; the qualifier says which member."
- The two cases as the question writes them do **not** share the same atom set. The unique-loss case is a single atom with a single qualifier in a functional group; the many-decline case is many atoms with many qualifiers across several groups. By Fig 12's definition they are **not isomers**. They are two members of the same kind of structure (a function under threat) with different atoms in the slots.
- The stronger form: if both cases are written over the full shared atom set (the species present, their relations, the conditions and the viability thresholds), differing only in which atom is removed or which mass crosses the threshold, then they **are** isomers under Fig 12 :117: same atoms, different arrangement, different substance. This is the checkable structural claim worth testing.
- **Falsifier, written before the run:** if either case requires an atom the other does not (for example a threshold that exists only for the many-decline case and not for the unique case), the isomer claim fails and the weaker functional-group reading stands. The single point of failure analogue in systems engineering corroborates the unique case but is a designed system, so it cannot supply the missing atom for the ecological case.

### Q3: What is the order, and where does the threshold sit?

- Fig 12 :104: "A settled molecule becomes an atom for whatever composes over it. So irreducible is a fact about a level and never about the world."
- Fig 12 :87: "a threshold relation between two quantities." Operators :52: "a goal is a comparison, which is why a vocabulary without it cannot state one."
- The order of dependency is: enumerate the habitat, bind the function and its functional group, declare the viability threshold as a comparison, then measure. The threshold is not a tenth figure line; it is a ⋛ bond placed between the measured abundance and the pre-declared viability level. Before that bond is declared, "decline" and "loss" are not two states of one quantity; they are two different operators (F1).
- The union has three regions: (a) pure unique atom loss, (b) pure mass decline with all atoms above threshold, and (c) the intersection where a decline has crossed a threshold and an atom is lost. Fig 9 :54 says to unbundle the union before searching; Fig 9 :57 says the intersection is categorically different, not a harder case of either. Region (c) gets its own loop.
- **Falsifier:** if a species can cross from above-threshold to below-threshold and back with no change in the composition of the habitat, the comparison bond is not a real operator in this domain and the three-region split fails.

### Q4: Does the lineage carry what the final species list does not?

- Fig 13 :129: "the order is not recoverable from the artefact. It is recoverable from the lineage." Fig 13 :127: "Nothing about a composed term tells you, from looking at it, which route produced it or whether that route was available."
- Two communities with the same final species list and the same total abundance can have reached that state by losing a unique atom or by crossing many thresholds. The artefact (the list and the total) cannot discriminate. The record that can is the dated sequence of losses, declines, threshold crossings and introductions. The Wikipedia Extinction debt page makes the field-level point: the number of species present now may not measure the habitat's future capacity, so the current list is a projection with lossy information.
- This is why the packet in `08_expert_verification_packet.md` asks for the lineage, not just the outcome.
- **Falsifier:** if a final species list plus total abundance is shown to determine which losses were unique and which declines crossed thresholds, without any dated record, the lineage claim fails.

## CONCLUSION

Extinction is subtraction at the ledger level and negation at the claim level, and the two must both be recorded (Q1). The unique-loss and many-decline cases are not isomers as the question writes them, but a stronger fixed-atom-set version is a real isomer claim with a stated falsifier (Q2). The order is enumerate, bind, declare threshold, measure; the threshold is a ⋛ comparison creating three union regions, with the intersection unbundled (Q3). The dated lineage, not the final list, carries the discriminating information (Q4).

This framing keeps what is defensible in the question (a real trade-off under scarcity, a real asymmetry of reversibility, real relational uniqueness) without the part that fails (one scalar weight, a standing rarity-value list, a clean partition between decline and loss). The universal weight remains a `gap`.
