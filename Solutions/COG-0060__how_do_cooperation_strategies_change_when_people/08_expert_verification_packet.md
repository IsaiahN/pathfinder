# Expert verification packet - the Tether on cooperation across scale

**What this is:** a claim in a form a researcher in cooperation or institutional design can check. Mode, derivation and refuters are stated before the conclusion.

**Mode: `specified` (structural), not `grounded`.** No field measurement is claimed. This is a shape and a route, not a result. Simulated derivation checked for internal consistency against the figures.

## 1. The claim, in one paragraph

When cooperation moves from a small, repeated, face-to-face community into a large anonymous institution, the decision loop does not change; what changes is the cast, the currency and the anchor that settles cooperation. A small community supplies repeated contact, reputation and shared evidence; a large institution must reproduce or replace them with something that does not move with participants' opinions (an enforceable rule, a record, an audit, a court). Dense local trust is agreement from a shared evidence pool, a convention rather than corroboration, so independence must be bought at scale. Only the method for naming what worked crosses between settings, not the local recipe. The specific empirical roster of which strategies change and by how much is explicitly a gap.

## 2. Three checkable sub-claims

### C1 - framework-internal; checkable by reading Figures 4 and 11

- **Statement:** the cooperation loop is invariant across scale; only actors, currency and anchor change; cooperation is a property of agent and habitat.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 4 :19-20 "Only the actors, the currency and the anchor change." Fig 11 :39 "capability is a property of agent-and-habitat, never of the agent alone." Fig 11 :66 "Isolation is not removal of the habitat. It is substitution of one habitat for another."
- **Refuter:** show a cooperation behaviour invariant in actors, currency and anchor with the loop unchanged but behaviour unchanged; or an isolated individual-only improvement that moves the cooperation ground without changing contact; or a large institution that is a pure scaling of the community habitat with no substituted actors.

### C2 - institutional-design-facing; checkable against the anchor of cooperation

- **Statement:** reputation is a frame reading that updates with opinion, so it cannot be the legitimate anchor at scale; a non-updating anchor (enforceable rule, record, audit, market outcome) must replace it.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 2 :29 "An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks." Fig 2 :33 "The anchor must not update."
- **What to check:** in the setting you know, what actually settles whether cooperation succeeded? Does the putative anchor change when participants' opinions change? In small communities, does reputation play this role, and is it legitimate under the anchor test?
- **Refuter:** exhibit a large anonymous institution whose cooperation is stably settled by an anchor that shifts with participants' opinions, with no coherent drift. That breaks the anchor claim.

### C3 - verification-facing; checkable by an experimental economist or institutional scholar

- **Statement:** trust from shared evidence is correlation, not corroboration; formal independence (auditing, separation of roles, external checking) is the institutional substitute. What crosses scales is a method, not a local recipe.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 2 :40 "agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool." Fig 7 :13 "agreement compounds, evidence does not." Fig 4 :22 "Only methods cross up. Only head starts come down."
- **What to check:** does cooperation quality predicted by dense mutual knowledge hold up in anonymous settings without independent checking? Does the transplanted local norm survive without being re-derived?
- **Refuter:** shared-evidence agreement predicts cooperation at scale as well as independent checking; or a local norm transplanted unchanged keeps working at scale without re-derivation.

## 3. What is explicitly not claimed

- Not that people change internally at scale, and not that the kernel changes.
- Not that a named strategy (tit for tat, generalized or strong reciprocity) is the answer. Those are world-source names, not figure slots.
- Not that small communities always cooperate more than institutions.
- Not that formal rules automatically work; the new habitat brings unchosen actors and a nonzero round-trip gap (`06_isomers_and_order.md`).
- Not `grounded`. No experiment or dataset is asserted here.
- Not that the figure set explains the empirical content of the question. That is recorded as a `gap`.

## 5. What would settle it

A ground reading, not agreement. The instruments are listed in `07_the_tether_answer.md` section 4. The shortest version: disable the formal anchor in a large institution and see whether cooperation drifts with opinion (C2); change only isolated dispositions and see whether the ground moves (C1); compare shared-evidence trust with independent checking (C3); transplant a local norm unchanged (C3).

## 6. What an expert can check in twenty minutes

1. Read Fig 4 :19-22, Fig 11 :39/66 and Fig 2 :29/33/40 - six short passages.
2. Check C2 against one institution you know: name its anchor and say whether it updates with opinion.
3. Name one instrument that would discriminate an updating from a non-updating anchor in your setting, or say why none is possible.
4. Send the refutation. A failure is more useful than an endorsement.
