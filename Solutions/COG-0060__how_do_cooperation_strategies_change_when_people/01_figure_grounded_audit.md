## 0. The residual in effect terms

Question: how do cooperation strategies change when people move from small communities into large, anonymous institutions?

Stated as an effect, R is the gap between what the vocabulary of cooperation predicts in a known, repeated, face-to-face setting and what actually resolves in a large, anonymous, institutionally governed setting. The two options this solution weighs are:

- **Option A:** treat the change as a change *inside the person*, a different strategy picked by the same machinery in a bigger room. This leads to looking for a universal cooperation module and to interventions aimed at changing individuals.
- **Option B:** treat the change as a change of *habitat* around an invariant kernel. This leads to looking at what the small community supplied (reputation, repeated contact, shared evidence, informal sanction) and what must replace it at scale (a non-updating anchor, formal rules, monitoring, enforceable records), and to interventions aimed at institutional design.

What an answer would look like: a claim about (a) what stays invariant in the decision loop, (b) what occupies the anchor slot that settles cooperation, (c) what carries trust, and (d) what may cross between the two settings. This shape is bound as an ordinary term and made refusable in section 3, before any conclusion is drawn.

## 1. Domain census (before choosing any figure line)

**R by arity, symmetry, scale.**

- Arity: two conditions in transit (small community, large anonymous institution) plus at least four sub-mechanisms in play (reciprocity, reputation, sanction, norm). This is a **union compound**, not one hard question. Fig 9 :54: "And when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again." A single lever will clear the region where only one condition holds and leave the intersections standing (Fig 9 :55).
- Symmetry: the move is **directional and lossy**. It is a substitution of one habitat for another (Fig 11 :66), and the seat's power is one-way: "Introduction is available; exclusion is not." (Fig 11 :61). A transform between scales loses information (Fig 4 :27).
- Scale: individual, small group, institution, population. Fig 4 :21: "The membrane is the boundary between the sizes, and the rule about what may cross it. Not a wall: a filter with a direction."

**One line per domain. A domain needs actors, a currency, and a nameable anchor.**

| domain | actors | currency | anchor (nameable) | status |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1. Kernel: the cooperation decision loop | individual agents | cooperate/defect decisions, payoffs, reputation effects | outcomes that settle whether a strategy paid (experiment, market, survival); Fig 2 :29 "A measurement, an experiment, a counter, a market, a patient outcome." | domain |
| 2. Small-community habitat | known others, repeated interaction, reputation network | reciprocal obligation, informal sanction, trust from repeated contact | direct repeated interaction and known reputation | domain |
| 3. Large anonymous institutional habitat | strangers, formal authorities, monitors, rules | formal sanctions, enforceable rules, institutional trust | formal measurement: records, courts, markets, enforcement | domain |
| 4. Membrane / scale transition | methods crossing up, head starts coming down, the population that ranks output (Fig 4 :5) | method (reapplicable) versus recording (one occasion) | the round-trip gap R_T measured between scales (Fig 4 :30) | domain |
| 5. Verification / outreach frame | expert readers | agreement and residual | the ground, never agreement | domain, but zero independence in this simulated run |

No domain was admitted without a nameable anchor. "No nameable anchor means it is not a domain yet" (master plan section 8).

**Partition or union.** Union compound. The mechanisms overlap: reputation is both a small-community currency and a stake; sanction is both informal and formal. Unbundle before searching (Fig 9 :54).

**Order by dependency (Fig 3).** Perception of cooperative behaviour first, then vocabulary (terms for trust, norm, sanction, institution), then objective (what counts as successful cooperation here), then strategy, then carry to the next problem. Fig 3 :20: "The chain is ordered by dependency, not by importance." The likely middle break is the vocabulary/objective link, which "has neither" obvious failures nor obvious tests (Fig 3 :25-27). This is a candidate to measure, not an assertion; a link below a break cannot be read (Fig 3 :22-23).

## 2. Figure census and findings

Every quote below is an exact substring of `FRAMEWORK/reference/03-FIGURES_TEXT.md`. A line that fits only with a strain is named as such and sent back to the census, not smoothed over.

### F1 - The kernel is invariant across scale; only actors, currency and anchor change

Fig 4 :19-20: "The kernel is the part that does not change with size: the same loop and the same rule for naming what worked, whether one agent is running it or a thousand. Only the actors, the currency and the anchor change."
Fig 10 :68: "Different actors, a different currency, a different ground, and a lossy transform between."

The cooperative decision loop (perceive, act, observe, compare prediction with outcome) is the same machine at two sizes. What changes when the room becomes large and anonymous is the cast, the currency, and what settles the result, not the loop itself.

- **Verdict:** `triangulated`. Two figures with low overlap: Fig 4 governs the scale membrane, Fig 10 governs the crossing between levels/seats. They agree from different subject matter.
- **Mode:** `general` (structural).
- **Refuter:** show a cooperation behaviour for which actors, currency *and* anchor all change while the loop itself must also change to explain the observation; or show the loop is not the same at the two scales. That would break the kernel claim.

### F2 - Cooperation is a property of agent and habitat, not of the agent alone

Fig 11 :39: "So the answerable question is not what intelligence is, but what makes a system capable. And capability is a property of agent-and-habitat, never of the agent alone."
Fig 11 :40: "A system cannot be made more capable by improving the agent in isolation. An improvement that does not change contact changes nothing, however much it improves."

Applied to this residual: a cooperation strategy cannot be understood by describing the person alone. It is a property of the person-in-a-habitat. Training people to cooperate in isolation, without changing what they are in contact with, does not change the cooperation that settles at the ground.

- **Verdict:** `single-source` within the figures (Fig 11). World sources (Ostrom, Tonnies) corroborate but share no evidence with the figures.
- **Mode:** `specified`.
- **Refuter:** the figure states it itself at Fig 11 :72: an agent-only improvement that moved the ground would refute the capability claim. If a dispositional change in isolated individuals reliably changed cooperation at scale without any change of contact, F2 falls.

### F3 - The move is a substitution of habitat, with losses and inheritances

Fig 11 :66: "Isolation is not removal of the habitat. It is substitution of one habitat for another."
Fig 11 :67: "Move the residual and you must reproduce everything its goal required, and you inherit the new habitat's actors, which you did not choose."
Fig 11 :68: "what you failed to reproduce is invisible until the goal fails"
Fig 11 :69: "what you unintentionally introduced is invisible until it acts"

Moving a cooperation problem from a small community into a large institution does not remove the old habitat, it substitutes a new one. What the small community supplied for free (known partners, repeated contact, reputation) must be reproduced or replaced, and the institution brings actors nobody chose (bureaucracy, formal incentives, monitoring). Failures are invisible until the goal fails or the introduced actor acts.

- **Verdict:** `single-source` within the figures (Fig 11), strongly corroborated by world sources on the Gemeinschaft-to-Gesellschaft shift and on institutional design; corroboration is secondary and cannot raise the figure verdict.
- **Mode:** `specified`.
- **Refuter:** show that the large institution is a pure scaling of the same habitat, with no substitution, no unchosen actors, and nothing to reproduce. Then F3 is wrong.

### F4 - The anchor must change from one that updates to one that does not

Fig 2 :29: "An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks."
Fig 2 :30: "If it moves when you push on it, it is not an anchor."
Fig 2 :33: "The anchor must not update."

In a small community, reputation and repeated interaction appear to settle cooperation. But reputation *is* a frame reading: it updates on what members think and say. Fig 2 :34 warns that when the anchor updates, "everything drifts together, and the drift is coherent, which is why nobody notices." At scale, a legitimate anchor must be something that does not move with participants' opinions: an enforceable rule, a record, a court, a market, a measurement. This is why institutions substitute formal enforcement for informal reputation.

- **Verdict:** `triangulated`. Fig 2 governs the anchor; Fig 11 (F3) governs which mechanisms the new habitat must supply. Low overlap between the two routes.
- **Mode:** `specified`.
- **Refuter:** show a large anonymous institution in which cooperation is stably settled by an anchor that changes whenever participants' opinions change, with no drift. That breaks "the anchor must not update."

### F5 - Trust from shared evidence is correlation, not corroboration

Fig 2 :40: "agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool"
Fig 7 :13: "agreement compounds, evidence does not"
Fig 7 :35: "A room's agreement is variation, not a verdict. Selection between rooms is where the ground votes."
Fig 2 :42: "Buy independence in proportion to the cost of being wrong."

Small communities generate high-trust agreement from dense shared experience. By the figures, that agreement is correlation, not corroboration: Fig 2 :40 "agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool" and Fig 2 :41 "agreement about a shared meaning is the product, not a corruption of it." In a large anonymous institution, the information needed to make agreement mean something must be bought as independence, in proportion to the cost of being wrong. This predicts formal auditing, separation of roles, and independent verification as the institutional substitutes for intimate mutual knowledge.

- **Verdict:** `triangulated`. Fig 2 (anchor and correlation) and Fig 7 (room chain) are distinct figures with low overlap.
- **Mode:** `specified`.
- **Refuter:** show that agreement among people drawing on the same evidence predicts cooperation quality at scale as well as independent checking does. Then agreement would be a verdict and F5 falls.

### F6 - What crosses the membrane is the method, not the recording

Fig 4 :22: "Only methods cross up. Only head starts come down."
Fig 4 :25: "A recording carried upward looks like knowledge and is a description of one occasion."
Fig 4 :23: "upward: a method that can be reapplied. Never the recording of a particular success."

The specific local norms of a small community (a particular reputation habit, a particular reciprocal routine) are recordings of one occasion. What can be carried into institutional design is the *method* for naming what worked: rule design, monitoring, graduated sanction, conflict resolution, nested governance. World sources on Ostrom's design principles and on public goods experiments with punishment corroborate this at the level of content, but only the figures decide the shape.

- **Verdict:** `single-source` within the figures (Fig 4), world corroboration secondary.
- **Mode:** `specified`. The round-trip gap R_T is nonzero (see `11_audit.md`): what survives the trip is "a method that can be reapplied," not the local rule verbatim.
- **Refuter:** transplant a specific local norm unchanged into a large institution and show it keeps settling cooperation without being re-derived as a method. That breaks the membrane rule.

### F7 - Standing is a stake that survives the outcome

Fig 10 :59: "an interest in the outcome is unavoidable, an interest in the reading is disqualifying."
Fig 10 :61: "And standing is a stake that survives the outcome it was earned on."
Fig 10 :60: "Where a stake cannot be removed, declare it. An undeclared interest is modelled anyway and an unnamed model runs unchallenged."

Small communities run on standing: reputation earned in one round carries into the next (Fig 10 :61). Large anonymous institutions remove standing, but removing standing does not remove interest in the outcome. What remains must be declared, which predicts conflict-of-interest rules, disclosure, and role separation as institutional structure rather than decoration.

- **Verdict:** `single-source` (Fig 10).
- **Mode:** `specified`.
- **Refuter:** exhibit a large anonymous institution in which no participant has any interest in the outcome and no declaration machinery exists, yet cooperation is stable and checkable. Then the declaration prediction is wrong.

### F8 - The problem is a union compound and must be unbundled

Fig 9 :54: "And when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again."
Fig 9 :55: "A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones."

Asking "how does cooperation change with scale" bundles at least four conditions: repeated contact, reputation, sanction, and formal authority. A single institutional lever clears the region where one condition binds and leaves the intersections standing. The remaining residue is "categorically different" (Fig 9 :57), a specific compound with its own loop.

- **Verdict:** `single-source` (Fig 9).
- **Mode:** `general` (structural).
- **Refuter:** show the scale transition is genuinely one condition, such that one intervention clears it entirely and nothing categorically new remains.

## 3. Pre-registered refuter (written before the conclusion)

Required before any outreach, per the seat-map discipline: "a falsifier written before the run is a design instrument, not only an honesty device." The shape claim is refuted if any of the following holds:

1. A cooperation behaviour is shown to be invariant across the small-to-large transition in actors, currency **and** anchor, with the loop itself unchanged but no change in any of the three (breaks F1).
2. An agent-only improvement in isolated individuals reliably moves the cooperation ground without any change of contact (breaks F2; the figure names this refuter itself).
3. A large anonymous institution is shown whose cooperation is stably settled by an anchor that updates with participant opinion, with no coherent drift (breaks F4).
4. A specific local norm transplanted unchanged keeps settling cooperation at scale without being re-derived as a method (breaks F6).

If any fires, the corresponding finding is withdrawn. Until one is run, no part of the empirical content may be labelled `grounded`.
