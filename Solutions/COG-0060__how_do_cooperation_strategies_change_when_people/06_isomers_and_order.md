**Mode: `specified` (structural).** Simulated derivation checked against the figures, not a ground reading.

## RESIDUAL

The main audit leaves three structural questions open: (1) how the four bundled mechanisms are ordered, (2) what the round-trip between scales preserves and drops, and (3) whether small-community cooperation and large-institution cooperation are the same kind of thing under Fig 12's definitions, or merely members of one family. This file answers those three and states a falsifier for the third.

## DOMAIN CENSUS (supporting)

Atoms held: the cooperation kernel (perceive, act, observe, compare), actors, currency, anchor, method. Bonds held: the seven plus negation. No new primitive is available without import. The four mechanisms (repeated contact, reputation, sanction, formal authority) are conditions in a union (Fig 9 :54), not new atoms.

## DERIVATION

### Q1 - Order by dependency, not by importance

Fig 3 :20: "The chain is ordered by dependency, not by importance."
Fig 3 :19: "Optimising a link that already works cannot rescue one that does not."

The five links for this residual are:

1. **perception:** can the system tell cooperative behaviour apart from what was there before;
2. **vocabulary:** does it have terms for what a situation affords (trust, obligation, sanction, rule);
3. **objective:** can it say what would count as progress in cooperation here;
4. **strategy:** a route to that objective under incomplete information;
5. **learn and carry:** name what worked, verify it, carry it.

Fig 3 :25-27 names the middle link as the one with neither obvious failures nor obvious tests. Applied here: we have rich terms for cooperative *behaviour* and rich terms for *institutions*, but the objective link, what counts as successful cooperation when partners are anonymous, is the candidate break. Fig 3 :22-23 warns that a reading taken below a break is "a reading of nothing." So the first empirical instrument should be aimed at link 3, not at strategy training (link 4), where measurement already exists and effort usually lands (Fig 3 :29-30).

**Falsifier for Q1:** a ground reading showing that link 3 is already working (the objective is statable and agreed at scale) and that the break is at link 2 or link 4. Then the order above is wrong and the instrument should move.

### Q2 - The membrane transform and its round trip

Fig 4 :21: "The membrane is the boundary between the sizes, and the rule about what may cross it. Not a wall: a filter with a direction."
Fig 4 :25: "A recording carried upward looks like knowledge and is a description of one occasion."
Fig 4 :30: "So take the round trip and measure the gap. Without it you are navigating by dead reckoning: confident about a position nobody checked."

Sent up (x): the verbatim figure set, chiefly Fig 4 :19-22, Fig 11 :66-69, Fig 2 :29-33.
Abstraction (T_A): the working shorthand "the kernel is invariant; the anchor must not update; methods cross, recordings do not."
Concretisation (T_E): a concrete institutional proposal, e.g. "install monitoring, graduated sanctions and nested conflict resolution in the large institution."

**R_T is nonzero.** What the shorthand holds but the concrete proposal does not automatically carry:

- which specific actor in the new habitat will maintain the anchor (Fig 11 :67, actors you did not choose);
- whether the sanctioning monitor is itself accountable, which is a separate condition (Ostrom design principle 4, world corroboration only);
- whether the "method" was re-derived or merely copied as a local recipe (Fig 4 :23-25).

Conversely, what survives cleanly: the invariant kernel and the requirement that the substitute anchor not move with opinion. R_T is recorded qualitatively as the list of dropped constraints, not numerically, because no ground reading was taken. Treating the shorthand as identical to the figures would set R_T to zero and commit the dead-reckoning error Fig 4 :30 names.

### Q3 - Are the two cooperation regimes isomers or a functional group?

Fig 12 :117: "Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."
Fig 12 :124: "functional group. A substructure whose presence fixes the outcome. The group says what kind; the qualifier says which member."

Two readings:

- **Weak reading.** A small community and a large institution share only the kernel (`agent / currency / anchor` loop). They differ in the occupants of currency and anchor. Under Fig 12 they are **not isomers**; they are two members of a **functional group**, the pattern "a cooperation loop settled by something," with different qualifiers.
- **Strong reading.** Write both regimes over the same atom set (the kernel plus actors, currency, anchor and method), differing only in which bond connects anchor to currency (informal reputation versus formal enforcement). Then they are **isomers**: same atoms, different arrangement, different substance. This is the checkable structural claim, and it is the one worth taking to an expert.

**Falsifier, written before the run:** if either regime can be written without an atom the other requires, for example an institution with no anchor for cooperation or a community with no method for naming what worked, the isomer claim fails and the functional-group reading stands. A second falsifier: if the formal-enforcement bond and the reputation bond turn out to be the same bond under Fig 12's six tests, then there is no arrangement difference and the regimes are the same substance, which would break F4 of the main audit.

### Q4 - Order of the union

Fig 9 :57: "What remains is smaller and it is also categorically different: a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either."

After the general shape is applied, the residue is not "cooperation but harder." It is a specific compound: anonymous cooperation under a non-updating anchor with an unverified monitor. It needs its own loop and its own ground (a field experiment or institutional outcome), not more of the same intervention.

## CONCLUSION

The order is by dependency with the objective link as the candidate break (Q1, falsifiable). The scale transform is lossy with a nonzero, qualitatively recorded R_T (Q2). The two cooperation regimes are at minimum members of one functional group, and plausibly isomers over a shared atom set, with the isomer claim carrying an explicit falsifier (Q3). After unbundling, what remains is a categorically different compound, not a bigger version of the original (Q4).
