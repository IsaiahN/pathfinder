# The Tether's answer: cooperation across scale (COG-0060)

**Mode: `specified` (structural), not `grounded`.** A shape and a route, not a measurement. Simulated derivation checked for internal consistency against the figures, not a ground reading.

## 1. The residual it answers

People carry cooperation habits from small, repeated, face-to-face communities into large, anonymous institutions. What is the change? Option A says the person switches strategy with the same internal machinery. Option B says the machinery is invariant and what changes is the habitat around it. Fig 9 :54: "And when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again." The question bundles repeated contact, reputation, sanction and formal authority. It has to be unbundled before it can be searched.

## 2. The shape

1. **The decision loop is invariant.** The same loop runs at both sizes; Fig 4 :19-20: "Only the actors, the currency and the anchor change." What changes is who is in the room, what is exchanged, and what settles whether cooperation paid.
2. **Cooperation is a property of agent and habitat.** Fig 11 :39: "capability is a property of agent-and-habitat, never of the agent alone." Interventions that only change people, without changing contact, do not change the cooperation that settles at the ground (Fig 11 :40).
3. **The move is a substitution of habitat, not a scaling.** Fig 11 :66: "Isolation is not removal of the habitat. It is substitution of one habitat for another." The large institution must reproduce or replace what the community supplied for free, and it brings actors nobody chose (Fig 11 :67). What was not reproduced is invisible until the goal fails.
4. **The anchor must be replaced.** In a community, reputation and repeated contact appear to settle cooperation, but reputation updates with what members think. Fig 2 :33: "The anchor must not update." So the institutional substitute has to be something that does not move with opinion: an enforceable rule, a record, a court, a market, a measurement. This is the structural reason institutions install formal enforcement.
5. **Shared-evidence trust is not corroboration.** Fig 2 :40: "agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool." Fig 7 :13: "agreement compounds, evidence does not." Dense local trust is a convention, efficient for coordination but disqualifying as a verdict. At scale, independence has to be bought, which predicts auditing, separation of roles and external checking.
6. **Methods cross, recipes do not.** Fig 4 :22: "Only methods cross up. Only head starts come down." What is portable is the method for naming what worked (rule design, monitoring, graduated sanction, conflict resolution), not a particular village habit.
7. **Standing is a stake.** Fig 10 :61: "And standing is a stake that survives the outcome it was earned on." Removing reputation removes standing, not interest in the outcome. What remains must be declared, which predicts disclosure and conflict-of-interest rules as structure.
8. **Which strategies change, and by how much, is a `gap`.** Fig 9 :32: "you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself." The figures return the shape above, not an empirical roster of strategies. Fig 13 :134: "Unreachable is a verdict here, not a silence".

## 3. What it explicitly does not say

- Not that human nature changes at scale. The kernel is invariant.
- Not that any specific strategy (tit for tat, generalized reciprocity, strong reciprocity) is the known answer. Those are names from world sources, and naming one for a figure slot is an unproven binding.
- Not that small communities are cooperative and large institutions are not. Both can be; the figure claim is about what settles cooperation, not its amount.
- Not that formal rules automatically work. The new habitat brings unchosen actors and a nonzero round-trip gap (see `06_isomers_and_order.md`).
- Not `grounded`. No experiment, dataset or field measurement was taken.

## 4. What would settle it - the ground

A source-discriminating instrument, not a further argument. From *The Hard Problem is About Source* (opened 2026-11-22): a reading that "cannot comment on the source" does not tell you which mechanism produced it. Here the corresponding tests are:

1. **Anchor test.** In a large anonymous institution, disable the formal record or enforcement channel and see whether cooperation collapses or drifts with opinion. If it drifts, the anchor claim is corroborated; if cooperation is unchanged, F4 of the audit is refuted.
2. **Contact test.** Change only individual dispositions in isolation and measure cooperation at scale. If it moves the ground, Fig 11 :40 is refuted.
3. **Independence test.** Compare cooperation quality under shared-evidence trust against independent auditing. If the former predicts as well as the latter, Fig 2 :40 is refuted for this quantity.
4. **Transplant test.** Move a specific local norm unchanged into a large institution. If it keeps settling cooperation without re-derivation as a method, Fig 4 :22 is refuted.
5. **Standing test.** Build a large anonymous setting with no standing and no declaration machinery. If cooperation is stable and independently checkable, F7 is wrong.

These are instruments, not rhetoric. Until one fires, the content stays `candidate`.

## 5. The honest stop

**Unreached, not unreachable.** The shape stops at the limit of what the figures can distinguish and records the lineage so the stop is checkable. The world sources (Ostrom's design principles, Axelrod's shadow of the future, public goods experiments, the Gemeinschaft/Gesellschaft shift) corroborate the shape at the content level, and in one case from genuinely different evidence. They do not license a name for the empirical slot. Prestige or agreement is not an instrument. "Indiscernibility is not identity" (canonical figures README).

## 6. One-paragraph version for outreach

> The Tether does not say that people become different when they enter a large anonymous institution. It says the decision loop is the same and the habitat is not. A small community supplies known partners, repeated contact and reputation; a large institution has to reproduce or replace these, and must substitute an anchor that does not move with participants' opinions, such as an enforceable rule, a record or an audit. Dense local trust is agreement from a shared evidence pool, which is a convention, not corroboration, so independence has to be bought at scale. What can be carried across is the method for naming what worked, not the local habit. The framework returns that shape, not a name for the specific strategies that change; those are a recorded gap, and the instruments that would close it are stated in advance along with the refuters. The center stays open until the ground settles it.
