## Wider literature that bears on the question

The world sources split cleanly across low-overlap fields, which is why their agreement is worth recording even though it is only corroboration:

1. **Institutional design (Nobel/Ostrom, Wikipedia Ostrom).** Rules established over time by users of a shared resource, and design principles for long-enduring institutions including monitoring, graduated sanctions, conflict resolution and nested enterprises. This is the content-level counterpart of the figure claim that methods, not recordings, cross the membrane (Fig 4 :22-25) and that the new habitat must reproduce what the old one supplied (Fig 11 :67).
2. **Game theory (Evolution of Cooperation).** Reciprocal cooperation depends on the probability of future interaction and on recognizing partners and tracking history. When that probability falls, direct reciprocity fails; indirect reciprocity depends on a reliable reputation history. This is the content-level counterpart of the anchor claim (Fig 2 :29-33).
3. **Experimental economics (Public goods game).** In large groups with weak returns, contributions decay; punishment and sanctions raise cooperation. This is the content-level counterpart of the claim that a non-updating anchor must be installed at scale.
4. **Sociology (Gemeinschaft/Gesellschaft).** The shift from personal, local ties to indirect, formal, impersonal ties. This is the content-level counterpart of habitat substitution (Fig 11 :66).
5. **Governance (Williamson).** Organizations and markets resolve conflicts differently; organizations can be more efficient when conflicts are simpler to solve. Content-level counterpart of replacing reputation with institutional conflict resolution.

Overlap warning: several of these are Wikipedia pages on a shared host, and the two Ostrom sources share the same author. They are one witness for the Ostrom result, not two. The figure verdict does not depend on them.

## Distribution rules: offer, do not push

- **Fig 10 :52 - "Bring a characterised residual, never the source."** Lead with the shape claim and its refuter. Send the links only after a reader has recorded a verdict on stage 1.
- **Fig 2 :35 - "Alignment is triangulation, not negotiation. A mirror cannot correct you."** Do not ask for agreement. A failure is more useful than an endorsement.
- **Fig 2 :40 - "agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool"** Later agreement is never pooled as corroboration.
- **Fig 9 :27-28 - "describe it before you go looking, or any frame you pick will seem to fit"** Ask what they would expect to see if the shape claim were true before supplying the framework.
- **Fig 4 :22 - "Only methods cross up. Only head starts come down."** Stage 3 sends a method (the formula), not an answer.

## The two-stage offer

**(1)** Send the residual, the shape claim and the pre-registered refuter. Ask what they would count as a failure. Record their verdict **before** showing any theory. **(2)** Only if curious, offer the links, with the note that the figures, not the summary, are the authority. **(3)** Offer the formula as a method to reuse, with the instruction that each user writes their own refuter and states their own mode.
