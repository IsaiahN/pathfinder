# Outreach draft - check the shape first, then decide about the theory

**Use:** one-to-one, or as a cover letter attached to a submission through the public venues in `03_public_contact_points.csv`. **Not** for bulk send. No request for trust, no request for agreement.

---

**Subject:** One checkable claim about cooperation at scale, before any theory

> Hi [name],
>
> I have one specific, checkable claim I would like you to test against what you already know. I am not asking you to read a theory first, and I am not asking you to agree with anything. If the claim does not survive, there is nothing else to look at.
>
> **The claim (`specified`, structural, not `grounded`):** when cooperation moves from a small, repeated, face-to-face community into a large anonymous institution, the decision loop a person runs does not change. What changes is the cast, the currency and the anchor that settles whether cooperation worked. A small community supplies known partners, repeated contact and reputation; a large institution has to reproduce or replace those with something that does not move when participants' opinions move, such as an enforceable rule, a record, an audit or a court. What can be carried across is the method for naming what worked, not the particular local habit. This is a shape, not a measurement. I am not claiming to know which specific strategies change or by how much; the figures do not return that, and I record it as an open gap.
>
> **What would refute it (written down before I ran this):**
> 1. a cooperation behaviour where the people, the currency and the anchor all change but the loop does not, yet behaviour is unchanged;
> 2. an isolated, individual-only improvement that reliably changes cooperation at scale without changing anyone's contact with the situation;
> 3. a large anonymous institution whose cooperation is stably settled by an anchor that shifts with participants' opinions, with no drift;
> 4. a local norm transplanted unchanged into a large institution that keeps working without being re-derived as a method.
>
> If any of these holds, the corresponding part is wrong and I will retract it. A one-line "this fails because..." is more useful to me than agreement. If you would rather not engage, no reply is needed.
>
> **If it survives, and only then:** here are the sources. The figures, not my summary of them, are the authority.
> - Verbatim figure text with line numbers (local framework extract): `FRAMEWORK/reference/03-FIGURES_TEXT.md`
> - Canonical usage rule (local): `FRAMEWORK/reference/FIGURES_README_canonical.md`
> - The eight-step loop and mode rule (local): `FRAMEWORK/figures/THE_FORMULA.md`
> - Canonical figures repository: https://github.com/IsaiahN/tether/tree/general-agent/figures
> - Institutional design of cooperation at scale (Ostrom, Nobel Prize 2009): https://www.nobelprize.org/prizes/economic-sciences/2009/ostrom/facts/
> - Experimental work on cooperation and sanctions: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Public_goods_game
>
> **If you want to reuse the machinery:** the formula is in the figures link. Run it on a problem you know well and can check against a ground. Write down what would refute your result *before* you run it and state the mode of what you get. A reported failure is more useful to me than a reported success.
>
> Thank you for the check,
> [name]
