# The Tether's answer on links that vary

**Mode: `specified`: not `grounded`.** This is a shape and a route, not a result. Simulated derivation checked against the figures (`05_what_shape_means.md` section 6), not a ground reading.

## 1. The residual it answers

A network model's Γ predicts system behaviour from its link representation; the system returns o′. R = |Γ(b,a) - o′| is measured per link slot, not per graph (Fig 1 :19, :22). The live residual is that two graphs with the same nodes and the same connected pairs can produce different observations because their links differ in strength, direction, duration, and meaning. A single weighted edge type cannot register the difference because it has recorded only one projection of a four-way distinction. The question is a **union compound**, not one hard problem and not four separate ones: *"What remains is smaller and it is also categorically different: a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either."* (Fig 9 :57)

## 2. The shape

1. **A link is a typed bond, not a scalar slot.** *"A bond is the typed relation between two operands. There are seven, they are not interchangeable, and which one holds is not recoverable from the operands."* (Fig 12 :46) The bond is *"the rest of the information"* (Fig 12 :5) and *"The arrangement is the substance."* (Fig 12 :94) A representation that records endpoints and a weight has recorded half of itself.
2. **Strength is an ordering and a comparison.** *"ordering, so more and less can be said"* (Fig 13 :20); *"a threshold relation between two quantities"* (Fig 12 :87). It is not the gradient: *"What makes a gradient a gradient is that it can be spent, and neither an ordering nor a span of space has directedness."* (Fig 13 :46-47)
3. **Direction is the symmetry class of the bond.** The ordered bonds → and ⇒ mean *"A then B; B needs A to have happened"* (Fig 12 :72) and *"A produces B; B did not exist before"* (Fig 12 :77); the symmetric bonds +, ∥, ≡ do not distinguish the order. *"a pathway is not a direction"* (Fig 13 :36), so an adjacency entry alone is not a directed link.
4. **Duration is a time relation on the bond, not a scalar.** Time is *"the ordered succession of differences"* (Fig 13 :12); a bond may be a catalyst that *"survives its own use"* (Fig 12 :119) or a cycle in which *"The output re-enters as input"* (Fig 12 :121). Nothing in the edge list records which a term is.
5. **Meaning is which bond holds, and it is not derivable.** *"a declared meaning is not a grounded one"* (Fig 10 :18). A type label is a convention until a provenanced import (Fig 6 :23, Fig 8 :51) or a ground test settles it. The framework returns a shape, not a name: *"you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself"* (Fig 9 :32).
6. **The default weighted adjacency is the flat formula.** *"the ingredients are recorded and the arrangement is not"* (Fig 12 :14); with one bond type the bond price term is zero and *"A bond that costs nothing is a bond that carries nothing, which is the flat case."* (Fig 12 :101)
7. **Two graphs with the same nodes and edges but different bonds are isomers.** *"Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."* (Fig 12 :117) A strength-only change within one bond is an ordering shift on the same substance, not an isomer shift.
8. **Diagnostics must be per link, never a graph average.** *"Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average."* (Fig 1 :22) Fit quality is frame-internal: *"A frame cannot score itself with a quantity it also produces."* (Fig 1 :27)

## 3. What it explicitly does not say

- Not that any particular empirical link has a particular bond. That is a `gap`, not a hidden answer (Fig 13 :134, Fig 8 :39).
- Not that a weighted or temporal or multiplex representation is wrong. Each is a binding of one or more figure terms onto a domain, and each is legitimate once its provenance is recorded. What fails is the claim that a single scalar edge type carries all four variations.
- Not that gradient is strength, or that adjacency is direction. Those are re-censused mappings (section 6 of `01_figure_grounded_audit.md`).
- Not that a convention is meaningless. A convention makes a failure statable; it becomes a defect when it decides the outcome (Fig 10 :16-17, :47).
- Not `grounded`. No external system trace was measured here.

## 4. What would settle it: the ground

A source-discriminating instrument, not a further argument. From *The Hard Problem is About Source*: an instrument that reads green *"cannot comment on the source"*; the spectrometer resolved it. Here the corresponding questions are:

- **Bond-discrimination test:** does an observation exist that separates a sequence bond from a production bond between the same two nodes, with the same strength and duration? If yes, the bond is grounded as a name.
- **Symmetry test:** *"swap the operands. Does the meaning change?"* (Fig 12 :59) Applying this to a measured relation, not to the modeller's intuition, would settle D.
- **Catalyst test:** does the link survive its own use, and is that difference recorded and measurable across uses? This settles T where timestamps alone do not.
- **Threshold test:** does a ⋛ comparison hold between two link strengths, or are the values merely ordered without a threshold? This settles S at the ground.
- Standing falsifiers from `01` section 4: recover all four variations from operands alone; produce a nonzero bond term with one bond type; make two bond-different graphs indistinguishable; predict link-level failure from a graph average; restore all four conditions with one lever; derive a bond name from the figures without import or ground.

## 5. The honest stop

**Unreached, not unreachable.** The seven bonds and the six-term set are a visible grammar, not a discovery of nature's own list. Declaring them makes the choice visible, not neutral. The answer stops where instruments can no longer distinguish one bond from another, and records the lineage so the stop is checkable. *"Indiscernibility is not identity."* (figures README)

## 6. One-paragraph version for outreach

> The Tether does not tell you which edge type to put in your dataset. It says that a link is a typed bond between two operands, and which bond holds is information the operands do not carry. The four ways a link can vary are four different things in the figures: strength is an ordering and a comparison, direction is the symmetry class of the bond, duration is a time relation that can survive a use or close a loop, and meaning is the bond itself, licensed from outside the model rather than declared inside it. A weighted adjacency with one edge type is what the framework calls the flat formula: the arrangement has not been recorded and the bond carries nothing. Two graphs with the same nodes and edges but different bonds are isomers, and diagnosing them requires per-link residuals, not a graph-level fit. What the framework will not do is name the bond for a particular empirical link; that needs an import with provenance or an instrument that discriminates the source, and until then the slot stays open.
