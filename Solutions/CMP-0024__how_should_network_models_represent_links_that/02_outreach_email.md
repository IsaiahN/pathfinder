**Subject:** Check one output before you decide whether the representation claim is worth reading

> Hi [name],
>
> I have one specific output I'd like you to check against what you already know about networks. I am not asking you to read the framework first, and I am not asking you to agree. If the output does not survive, there is nothing else to look at.
>
> **The output (`specified`, not `grounded`):** a link is a typed bond between two operands, and which bond holds is information the operands do not carry. The four ways a link can vary are four different things, not four dials on one edge type:
>
> - strength is an ordering and a threshold comparison, not a gradient;
> - direction is the symmetry class of the bond, not an adjacency entry;
> - duration is a time relation on the bond, which may survive its own use or close a loop;
> - meaning is the bond itself, and a declared type is a convention until something outside the model grounds it.
>
> From this it follows that a weighted adjacency matrix with one edge type is what the framework calls the flat formula: the arrangement has not been recorded, so nothing downstream can tell a strong tie from a directed one, or a transient contact from a signed relation. Two graphs with the same nodes and the same connected pairs but different bond types are, in the framework's term, isomers. Diagnostics have to be indexed per link, not by a graph-level fit.
>
> **What I am not claiming:** that any particular edge in your data is a production bond rather than a sequence bond. The framework returns a shape, not a name. A particular bond needs a provenanced import or a measurement that discriminates between bonds. Until then the slot stays open, and I am recording it as open rather than filling it.
>
> **What would refute the shape claim:**
> 1. showing that strength, direction, duration, and meaning are all recoverable from the operands alone, with no bond record, and that a measurement cannot distinguish two graphs that differ only in bond type;
> 2. exhibiting a one-bond-type representation whose bond term is provably nonzero and that discriminates all four variations at the ground;
> 3. showing that two graphs with identical nodes and edges but different bond types are indistinguishable under an instrument that is otherwise sensitive;
> 4. an averaged graph-level statistic that predicts, prospectively and per link, which named slot will fail when a bond type is substituted.
>
> I wrote these down before sending this. If any one holds, I will retract the claim. A one-line "this fails because..." is more useful to me than agreement. If you'd rather not engage, no reply is needed.
>
> **If it survives, and only then:** the figures are the authority, not my summary of them. The relevant ones are Figure 12 on bonds, Figure 13 on ordering, time, and cross-kind composition, and Figure 9 on unbundling a union. I can send the exact passages.
>
> **If you want to use the machinery:** the formula is a method, not an answer. Run it on a link representation you know well and can check against a trace. Write down what would refute your result *before* you run it, and state the mode of what you get. A reported failure is more useful to me than a reported success.
