## QUESTION

How should network models represent links that vary in strength, direction, duration, and meaning?

Restated as an effect: why does R = |Γ(b,a) - o′| stay small for some link representations and grow for others, when the node set and the connected pairs are unchanged? R is indexed per link, not per graph (Fig 1 :19). Anchor: the observed system dynamics, a trace or removal experiment, named as the kind of anchor that settles a question in Fig 2 :29.

Option A: a link is one edge type with four scalar dials, which leads to a flat formula with no recorded arrangement. Option B: a link is a typed bond, and the four variations belong to four figure terms with four instruments, which leads to a checkable shape and a declared naming gap. The verdict below is Option B in `specified` mode.

## VERDICT (from the figures)

**The figure-derived verdict:** a link is a typed bond between two operands, and the bond is not recoverable from the operands. Strength is an ordering and a threshold comparison; direction is the symmetry class of the bond; duration is a time relation that may survive a use or close a loop; meaning is which bond holds, licensed by provenance or a ground test rather than declared inside the frame. A weighted adjacency with one edge type is the flat formula with a zero bond term. Two graphs with the same nodes and edges but different bonds are isomers. Diagnostics are per link, never a graph average. The four conditions form a union and are kept separate per domain, never averaged. Which bond a named empirical link has is a declared `gap`, not a hidden answer. Mode `specified`, ungrounded. Low-overlap ecology corroborates the instrument point; network-facing sources corroborate that each variation is a real dimension, but they share a broad pool and are one witness on that claim.

- **cost if wrong:** misdirected modelling effort. A team failing on meaning would be told to add more weight columns, or a team failing on duration would be told to add another layer, and one lever would be applied to a union that needs four. In outreach terms, an expert's twenty minutes would be spent refuting a shape that was never the right shape. The cost is real but bounded and largely reversible, because the claim carries its own refuter.
- **undoable:** yes. No term is deleted if the claim fails; a refutation is added, and the reach falls because *"something was added rather than because anything was deleted"* (Fig 6 :42). The shape and its seven falsifiers are recorded in a provenance trail so a later reader can locate the error (Fig 10 :62-64). A sent claim can be superseded by a corrected packet, and no bond type has to be withdrawn from the framework.
- **reversed by:** any of the seven pre-registered falsifiers, in particular (1) all four variations recovered from operands alone, (2) a nonzero bond term from one bond type, (3) bond-different graphs indistinguishable at the ground, (4) a graph average predicting link-level failure, or (5) one lever restoring all four conditions. Also reversing: a bond-discriminating observation or a provenanced import that names the bond for a specific empirical link, which would close the `gap`; or a figure re-census that recovers a nameable anchor for the latent-embedding candidate excluded at X1. Prestige or agreement is not such an instrument.
