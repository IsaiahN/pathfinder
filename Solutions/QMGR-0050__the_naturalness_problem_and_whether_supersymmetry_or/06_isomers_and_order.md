## DERIVATION

### Q1 - Are SUSY and composite Higgs isomers?

- Fig 12 :117 *"isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."*
- SUSY atoms include scalar superpartners of the known fermions. Composite-Higgs atoms include a new strong sector whose bound state is the Higgs. Extra-dimensional atoms include compact spatial dimensions. These are **not the same atom set**.
- Therefore, as written, they are **not isomers**. They differ in ingredients, not only in arrangement. The isomer test fails at the first condition.

### Q2 - Are they a functional group?

- Fig 12 :124 *"functional group. A substructure whose presence fixes the outcome. The group says what kind; the qualifier says which member."*
- The common substructure is: **new degrees of freedom coupled to the Higgs at the weak scale whose contribution softens or cancels the quadratic sensitivity to the cutoff.** Its presence fixes the outcome (the residual stops growing with Λ²). The qualifier selects the member: sparticles, new strong dynamics, extra dimensions, relaxion field.
- So the candidate resolutions form a **functional group**, not an isomer set. This is a `specified` structural claim; the physics content comes from Wikipedia (Hierarchy problem, 2026) and Wikipedia (Little hierarchy problem, 2026), which list the candidates and their mechanisms.
- **Falsifier, written before the run:** if two members of the proposed group share no common substructure that fixes the cancellation outcome, the functional-group claim fails and the mechanisms are an unrelated list.

### Q3 - Can the stronger isomer claim be made?

- If both mechanisms are written over the **full shared atom set** of the Standard Model plus the six-term substrate, differing only in which structure mediates which state pair, then they become Fig 12 isomers: same atoms, different bond, different substance. This is the claim worth testing, and it is the same move the exemplar used for GR and QM.
- The analogue is *not* asserted here. It is a reachable stronger form with a stated cost: it requires committing to a binding of physics fields onto the six-term atoms, which `05` §6 says must be recorded as a choice, not a discovery.
- **Falsifier:** if either mechanism requires an atom the other provably does not (for example extra dimensions requiring a compact spatial dimension that SUSY in four dimensions does not), the strong isomer claim fails and the functional-group reading stands.

### Q4 - What is the order of composition?

- Fig 12 :104 *"A settled molecule becomes an atom for whatever composes over it. So irreducible is a fact about a level and never about the world."*
- The Standard Model effective field theory is settled at its own level: it is confirmed across decades of experiment. It can therefore serve as an atom for the next level.
- The resolution is a **second-order composition**: `(settled SM EFT) → (imported new atom) → (protected weak scale)`. It is legal only because the first-order theory settled. This is the recursion Fig 12 names, not a new primitive and not an import that adds an atom to the world.
- **Fig 4 round trip:** the move from the weak scale to the cutoff and back is a crossing between scales. Fig 4 :30 *"So take the round trip and measure the gap. Without it you are navigating by dead reckoning: confident about a position nobody checked."* The tuning measure is exactly that gap, and it is a price in bits, not a measurement of the ground.
- **Fig 3 dependency:** perceive the residual (link 1), have terms for what a mechanism affords (link 2), say what would count as progress (link 3), then plan a search (link 4). Fig 3 :19 *"Optimising a link that already works cannot rescue one that does not."* Most effort in naturalness research lands at link 4 (building bigger colliders) while the unresolved link is 3 (what would count as a resolution).

### Q5 - Which bond carries the cancellation? (strain test)

- The loop contributions of a fermion and its scalar partner enter with opposite signs; the total quadratic divergence cancels. Wikipedia (Hierarchy problem, 2026) writes the fermion contribution with a minus sign and the scalar contribution with a plus sign, and states that the total is zero when both are included.
- In the figure grammar this would superficially resemble a symmetric bond whose operands cancel. Strain test: Fig 12 :59 *"swap the operands. Does the meaning change? No."* applies to conjunction; it carries no sign reversal. Calling the cancellation `+` or `≡` forces a reading the Operators table does not support.
- **Re-census:** the cancellation governs a relation in the physics frame, not a figure bond. Withdrawn as a bond identification (recorded in `01` §7). Kept as a `specified` physics description. Fig 12 :91 *"A relation that fails all six is not a bond you are missing. It is a claim that has not been made yet."*

### Q6 - Union or partition?

- Fig 9 :54 *"And when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again."* The mechanisms overlap: SUSY can be combined with compositeness, and extra dimensions can be combined with SUSY. This is a union of overlapping conditions.
- A single lever (for example, excluding the simplest MSSM) clears the region where only one condition holds and leaves the intersections standing. Fig 9 :57 *"A case in two conditions is not a harder case of either. It stops being a mixture and becomes a specific compound."*
- Consequence: the question "which mechanism?" is not one search over a list. It is a compound with its own loop and its own ground (Fig 9 :43).

## CONCLUSION

The mechanisms are **not isomers as written** (different atom sets). They are a **functional group** (Fig 12 :124) sharing the substructure "new weak-scale degrees of freedom that soften the quadratic divergence." The stronger isomer claim is available if both are written over a full shared atom set, with a stated falsifier. The order is second-order: the settled SM EFT becomes an atom over which the imported mechanism composes (Fig 12 :104). The cancellation bond identification was withdrawn under strain; the union reading is retained under Fig 9 :54.

This rescues what is defensible without claiming the figures name a mechanism. "SUSY is a candidate member of a functional group whose bargain is unmeasured" is checkable. "SUSY is the resolution" is not a figure-derived verdict.

## FALSIFIERS (written before the run)

1. Two proposed members share no substructure that fixes the cancellation outcome: functional-group claim fails.
2. One mechanism requires an atom another provably lacks: strong isomer claim fails, functional-group claim stands.
3. A settled first-order EFT cannot serve as an atom for a second-order composition: the order claim fails (breaks Fig 12 :104).
4. A figure bond is found whose six tests actually match the sign-reversing cancellation: the Q5 withdrawal is reversed and the bond identification is restored with provenance.
