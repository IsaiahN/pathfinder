**Use:** one-to-one, or as a cover letter attached to a submission through the public venues in `03_public_contact_points.csv`. **Not** for bulk send.

**Hard precondition:** do not send until the union/mute-union distinction in F1 and F3 of `01_figure_grounded_audit.md` is checked against the verbatim figure text. If that distinction collapses, the two-stage offer collapses on contact.

**Subject:** Check one failure pattern in coupled Earth-system prediction before you decide what to call it

> Hi [name],
>
> I have one specific output I would like you to check against what you already know about coupled Earth-system work. I am not asking you to read a framework first, and I am not asking you to agree with anything. If the output does not survive, there is nothing else to look at.
>
> **The output (`specified`, not `grounded`):** the question "which Earth-system processes are still studied too separately" asks for a list. The figures I work from do not return a list. They return a separator. In effect terms, the residual is a joint prediction that fails when the contributing processes are represented separately, even though each separate representation predicts its own slot well. Three things follow:
>
> 1. It is a **union compound**, not one hard question and not a list of independent entries. A lever applied to one process clears the region where only that condition holds and leaves the intersections standing.
> 2. The **combined effect lives in the coupling bond**, the typed relation between the processes, which an ingredient list does not record. A forcing-then-response, a feedback cycle and a threshold comparison are different coupled outcomes over the same processes.
> 3. **Studying processes together is not itself the fix.** A union of frames whose closures differ has a possible surplus, but if no residual is described the union is mute: nothing was aimed. The residual has to be described before the frame is chosen.
>
> So the operational answer is a discriminator, not a name: a process pair is "studied too separately" when a coupled prediction for that slot keeps failing and does not trend down under more component-only work, and the two process frames do not draw on the same evidence at source. If they share an evidence pool, their agreement is one pool talking and there is no surplus to find.
>
> **What I am explicitly not claiming:** that any figure line names a specific pair such as carbon-climate or ice-albedo. It does not. The literal list is a `gap`, which is a verdict and not a silence. Naming a coupling from an already-settled component model is a provenanced binding from the Earth-system frame, not something the figures derived. I am also not claiming that a consensus statement among component specialists settles anything: agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool.
>
> **What would refute this:**
> 1. The combined effect is fully recovered by a sum or list of the separate process predictions, with no bond-dependent remainder.
> 2. For every putatively coupled pair, the two frames draw on the same evidence at source, so there is no surplus.
> 3. The decisive residual always lies in direct first-order contact, with no cascade outward.
> 4. A figure line or rule selects a specific process pair from the residual alone.
>
> I wrote these down before sending this. If any holds, I retract the shape claim. If you find a per-slot discriminating measurement that closes a named coupling, that is not a defect, it is the ground firing, and I will narrow the gap accordingly. A one-line "this fails because..." is more useful to me than agreement. If you would rather not engage, no reply is needed.
>
> **If it survives, and only then:** the sources are below. The figures, not my summary of them, are the authority.
> - Canonical figure text with line numbers (local extract): `FRAMEWORK/reference/03-FIGURES_TEXT.md`
> - Figures README and citation rule (local): `FRAMEWORK/reference/FIGURES_README_canonical.md`
> - The eight-step loop and mode rule (local): `FRAMEWORK/figures/THE_FORMULA.md`

> **If you want to use the machinery:** the formula is in the figures. Run it on a coupled problem you know well and can check against a ground. Write down what would refute your result *before* you run it, and state the mode of what you get. The framework's own warning is that a frame cannot check the direction of its own question, and that outputs can be contrived to fit the formula. A reported failure is more useful to me than a reported success.
