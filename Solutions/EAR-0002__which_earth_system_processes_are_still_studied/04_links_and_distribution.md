## Three things in the wider literature that bear directly on this question

1. **Multiple stressors exceed single stressors.** The Planetary Boundaries article, citing the coral-reef literature, says combinations of temperature, UV and acidity *"may well cause perturbations in the abundance and diversity of marine biological systems that go well beyond the effects of a single stressor acting alone."* This is the world-facing statement of Fig 9 :57, *"a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either."* Use it when explaining why a list of processes is the wrong object, not when claiming the figures predicted the result.
2. **Aggregation loses the slot.** Zoller et al. 2026 report that indirect effects of human pressures on one component show high spatial variation and that their comparison *"highlight[s] the risk of information loss inherent in commonly used aggregation approaches."* This is a low-overlap corroborator for Fig 1 :19 and :22. It is a present-day research finding, not a figure line, so it is corroboration only.
3. **Emergence is interaction, not addition.** The Emergence article distinguishes weak emergence, where a property *"is the result of interaction rather than aggregation,"* from strong emergence, which is not deducible even in principle. The Earth-system cases at issue are weak emergence. This corroborates the bond reading (Fig 12 :5) and dissolves a potential conflict with the framework's compositional stance. Note that the Lewes quote about incommensurable components reads like strong emergence if taken alone; the article's own weak/strong distinction is why no conflict is declared.

None of these sources conflicts with the figure verdict. The conflict rule was not triggered. The Wikipedia articles share a platform and many editors and are one broad evidence pool, not three independent witnesses; the essays share the framework author and are maximal overlap with the figures.
