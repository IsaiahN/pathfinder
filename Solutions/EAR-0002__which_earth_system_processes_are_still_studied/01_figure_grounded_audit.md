## 0. The claim under audit

The ordinary-language question asks for a **list**: which processes are studied too separately. Stated as an effect, the residual is:

> **R (in effect terms).** A prediction of a joint Earth-system outcome fails when the contributing processes are represented separately, even though each separate representation predicts its own slot well. The joint outcome is not recovered by listing, summing or averaging the separate predictions. What is owed is an explanation of the failure that is indexed per slot: which coupled outcome, in which place and season and time window, under which pairing of processes, and what was missed that only appears when the processes touch.

An answer would look like this, and is bound as an ordinary term before the run, per `THE_FORMULA.md` "Direction": *"make the sought-for shape an ORDINARY TERM. Write down what an answer would look like, bind it, and let it accrue a residual like anything else."*

- **Option A (the list reading):** name the processes (atmosphere, ocean, cryosphere, biosphere, carbon and nitrogen cycles, land use, freshwater, aerosols, and so on) and rank them by how separately they are studied. This leads to a ranking of names.
- **Option B (the separator reading):** describe the shape that any member of that list must satisfy before it can count as "studied too separately," and report the literal list as a `gap`. This leads to a discriminator an outside frame can apply to a named pair, plus a refuter.

What each leads to: A hands a filter a verdict (Fig 9 :60) and produces a list whose members cannot be checked from the figures. B produces a checkable shape and an explicit stop, at the cost of not naming the processes.

The census below is written before any figure line is chosen, so the figures cannot hand us the answer by selection.

## 1. Domain census (before choosing figure lines)

### 1a. R by arity, symmetry and scale

- **Arity: two or more conditions.** Even the simplest case (a temperature response to a carbon perturbation) has two processes. The question's "combined effects" implies at least a pair, and the assessment literature works with nine interacting processes. Arity is therefore a union, not a single slot (this is checked against figure text below; at this stage it is a structural reading of the question).
- **Symmetry: mostly ordered, sometimes cyclic, never a plain symmetric conjunction.** In a coupled pair one process often produces a change that the other responds to (a forcing-then-response order), while in others the loop closes (warming reduces albedo, which warms further). A symmetric "both hold" reading loses the direction that carries the combined effect.
- **Scale: multi-rate and multi-extent.** Atmospheric adjustments are fast, ocean heat uptake slow, ice-sheet and carbon-cycle responses millennial, tectonic and weathering responses geological. The residual for a coupled pair can sit at a different scale from either component, which is why it is missed by first-order enumeration.

### 1b. One line per domain: actors, currency, anchor

A domain is admitted only if a nameable anchor exists. Where none is nameable, it is not yet a domain.

- **D1, component-process science.** Actors: discipline instruments, process models, researchers in oceanography, atmospheric chemistry, cryosphere science, biogeochemistry, ecology. Currency: within-slot prediction error. Anchor: a measurement or reanalysis in that discipline (a flux tower, a float, a core, a spectrometer). Nameable: yes. This is a **partition boundary** question inside each discipline: which compositions are legal within the held vocabulary of that discipline.
- **D2, coupled Earth-system modelling and assessment.** Actors: coupled Earth-system models, scenarios, assessment bodies, intercomparison projects. Currency: skill at predicting the joint state and reproducing feedbacks. Anchor: the observed combined outcome (global temperature, ocean heat content, atmospheric carbon burden) which does not move when a model changes its mind. Nameable: yes. This is a **union compound** with its own loop and its own ground, built out of D1 components.
- **D3, the habitat / field of contact.** Actors: conditions (light, water, temperature, season, terrain), relations (predation, competition, dependency, pollination), cascades. Currency: contact and cascade, not prediction. Anchor: the ground; the habitat is read off the world rather than composed. Nameable: yes, as the world itself (the thing that settles whether a cascade happened). This is where "processes" are enumerated, not assembled.
- **D4, the verification / outreach frame.** Actors: Earth-system scientists acting as outside frames. Currency: agreement and residual. Anchor: the ground, never agreement. Nameable: yes (an instrument, dataset or published record). In this simulated run all seats draw on one evidence pool, so D4's independence is zero. That is recorded, not hidden.

### 1c. Partition or union

**Union.** D1 and D2 overlap: a coupled model is built from component models, so the two frames are not disjoint; a scientist can work in both. D3 is the habitat that both D1 and D2 sample, so it is not a third box beside them but the field beneath both. This is a union of overlapping conditions, not a partition. The operational consequence is that a single-discipline lever clears only the region where one condition holds and leaves the intersections standing.

### 1d. Order by dependency

1. Enumerate the habitat of contact outward before composing anything (Fig 11, checked below).
2. Settle each component process before composing the pair; an unsettled component cannot serve as an operand.
3. Describe the coupled residual before choosing a frame to explain it.
4. Refuse a mistaken term before minting a new one (`THE_FORMULA.md` step 2).

Dependency order matters because effort spent above a broken link arrives at a step that cannot use it (Fig 3).

Operators table :4:

This is the structural core of the answer. A list of processes is an ingredient list. "Studied too separately" means the typed relation between them was not recorded: a forcing-produced-response is a production bond (⇒), a feedback loop is a cycle (Fig 12 :121), a comparison of two states is a threshold relation. **Mode:** `specified` on the bond reading; `general` on the cross-kind composition rule. **Status:** `triangulated` across Fig 12, Fig 13 and the Operators table, three low-overlap routes (notation, substrate, operator table). **Refuter:** demonstrate that the combined effect is fully recovered by a sum of the separate process predictions, with no bond-dependent remainder, or that two different coupling arrangements over the same processes produce identical joint outcomes in every measurable case. **World note:** Wikipedia, Emergence: *"emergence occurs when a complex entity has properties or behaviors that its components do not have on their own, and emerge only when they interact in a wider whole."* Also G. H. Lewes, quoted there: *"The emergent is unlike its components insofar as these are incommensurable, and it cannot be reduced to their sum or their difference."*

### F3  -  An undirected union is mute: studying them together is not enough

Fig 8 :7 and :19-21:

> **"interdisciplinarity, no mint"**
> **"closures differ, so a surplus exists"**
> **"but no residual is described"**
> **"nothing was aimed; the union is mute"**

Fig 8 :53:

> **"Residual first, frame second."**

Fig 9 :27-28:

> **"describe it before you go looking, or any frame you pick will seem to fit"**

This is the decisive finding against the obvious answer. The naive response to "studied too separately" is "study them together." The figures distinguish two things. A union of frames whose closures differ has a **surplus** (a possible new coupling term), but if no residual is described the union is **mute**: nothing was aimed, so no term is minted. Merely adding an interdisciplinary room, a working group or a coupled model does not close the residual; it only creates the condition under which a described residual could be closed. The residual must be described first, and the frame selected against it, not the frame selected first and the residual read off it.

**Mode:** `specified`. **Status:** `triangulated` across Fig 8 :19-21/:53 and Fig 9 :27-28, two figures with different subjects (union surplus versus leaving/arriving). **Refuter:** show a coupled study that produced a new, ground-settled coupling term without first describing a residual that persistently failed under the separate frames; or show that the union of differing closures mints without a described residual. **World note:** the ESD journal scope statement says it *"solicits contributions that investigate these various interactions and the underlying mechanisms, ways how these can be conceptualized, modelled, and quantified"* (Earth System Dynamics, homepage, fetched 2026-11-22), which is the aim, not the result.

### F4  -  The residual is per slot; aggregation hides it

Fig 1 :19 and :22:

> **"The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated."**
> **"Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average."**

Symbols table :43-44:

> **"R is measured per slot, never as a single global number."**

Fig 9 :60:

> **"Use filters for the budget and witnesses for the verdict. Never let a filter hand you a verdict."**

"Which processes are studied too separately" cannot be answered by one global coupling score. A process can be well-coupled in one region, season or variable pair and badly coupled in another. A global average of "integration effort" is a frame-internal quantity: *"They are frame-internal. A frame cannot score itself with a quantity it also produces."* (Fig 1 :27). **Mode:** `specified` on per-slot indexing; `general` on the filter/witness separation. **Status:** `triangulated` across Fig 1, Symbols table and Fig 9. **Refuter:** show a coupled residual that is correctly represented by a single global scalar with no slot index, such that the per-slot readings add no information. **World note:** Zoller, Lade, Gotangco Gonzales, Fetzer, Chaudhary and Rocha 2026, Earth System Dynamics 17, 1719-1764: *"Human pressures on one Earth system component typically entail indirect effects on other components"* and their results *"highlight the risk of information loss inherent in commonly used aggregation approaches."*

## 4. Pre-registered refuter

Written before the conclusion and before any outreach, per the seat map rule that *"a falsifier written before the run is a design instrument, not only an honesty device."*

The shape claim is refuted if any of the following holds:

1. **Bond recovery.** The combined effect is fully recovered by a sum or list of the separate process predictions, with no bond-dependent remainder and no persistent residual (breaks F1, F2, F4).
2. **Shared pool.** For every putatively coupled pair, the two frames draw on the same evidence at source, so the union has no surplus (breaks F7, Fig 8 :48-49).
3. **Direct-contact sufficiency.** The decisive residual always lies in direct first-order contact, with no cascade outward (breaks F5).
4. **Instrument closure.** A per-slot discriminating instrument is produced and closes the named coupling, so that pair is no longer "still studied too separately" (this is the ground firing, not a defect; it narrows the gap).
5. **Figure-derived list.** A figure line or rule selects a specific process pair from the residual alone, with no world import (breaks F6).
6. **Re-census failure.** A genuine re-census shows that the union/bond rules govern a different object than the study of processes, so the domain was misidentified.

If any of 1, 2, 3, 5 or 6 fires, the shape claim is withdrawn or re-censused. If 4 fires, the gap shrinks and the answer is updated with provenance. Until one is run, no part of this may be labelled `grounded`.

## 5. Recommendation

1. **Lead with the separator, not the list.** Send the residual description and the refuter first. State clearly that no figure line names a process (F6).
2. **Split before searching.** Treat the question as a union compound; unbundle it into overlapping conditions and keep the intersections visible (F1).
3. **Describe the residual before naming a frame.** A coupled model or a working group is a necessary condition, not a mint (F3).
4. **Index per slot and report the habitat enumeration.** Do not hand a global integration metric a verdict (F4, F5).
5. **Use filters only for the budget.** Impact, novelty and publication counts decide where to look, never which process is causally responsible (Fig 9 :60).
6. **Ask for a failure, not an endorsement.** Record the outside verdict before showing the figures (Fig 10 :52).
7. **Carry methods, not recordings.** *"Only methods cross up. Only head starts come down."* (Fig 4 :22) A recording of one successful coupling is a description of one occasion (Fig 4 :25).

### The three-question close (formula step 7)

- **SEARCHED.** Is the coupled residual in the current closure and merely unfound? For the per-slot discriminator, yes: Fig 5, Fig 8 and Fig 9 already hold it. More component-process search at the same reach does not add an atom (Fig 6 :49). This is asked first because it is cheapest.
- **INWARD.** Sharpen the per-slot instrument from one already returning something: a coupled measurement that fails to resolve a specific slot is the edge to extend (Fig 6 :32). This is where the gap lives.
- **OUTWARD.** Import a frame whose closure predicts the residual's shape, with a source gate and shadow test (Fig 8, Fig 10 :34-35). For Earth-system coupling, the low-overlap frame is the complex-systems/emergence literature, used only to corroborate shape, never to name a process. Underivable in principle and unreachable at this budget are kept distinct.
