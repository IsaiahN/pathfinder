## 1. The claim, in one paragraph

The question "which Earth-system processes are still studied too separately to understand their combined effects?" asks for a list. The figures return a separator. The residual is a union compound, not one hard question and not a set of independent entries, so a single-discipline lever leaves the intersections standing. The combined effect lives in the typed coupling bond, which a list of processes does not record; a feedback cycle and a forcing-then-response are different coupled outcomes over the same processes. An undirected interdisciplinary union is mute until a residual is described, so "study them together" is not itself a fix. Operationally, a process pair is "studied too separately" when a coupled prediction for that slot keeps failing and does not trend down under component-only work, and the two process frames do not draw on the same evidence at source. The habitat must be enumerated outward because the decisive residual can be third order. The literal list is a `gap`, with a per-slot discriminating instrument owed. Refuters were written before the run.

## 5. What would settle it

A per-slot discriminating instrument, not a further argument. From *The Hard Problem is About Source*: a reading *"cannot comment on the source"*; *"If several paths create the same behavior, then behavior is a projection with lossy information."* Here:

1. **Discriminator:** a measurement that fails when a named coupling is absent and succeeds when it is present, for one place, season and variable pair. This closes one member of the `gap`.

3. **Independence test:** do the two frames draw on different evidence at source, or are they one pool with two names?
4. **Cascade test:** where does the contact cascade stop mattering for that residual?
