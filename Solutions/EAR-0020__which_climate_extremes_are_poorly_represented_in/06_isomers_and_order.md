## RESIDUAL

The main audit (`01`) leaves two questions open. (1) Is the three-way split rare / local / compound a partition, or a union requiring separate loops? (2) If compound events are made of the same drivers as single extremes, what makes them a different substance, and in what order do the remedies apply? This file answers those two questions only.

## Q1 — Partition or union? (decisive for the shape)

- Fig 9 :54: **"When the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again."**
- Fig 9 :55: **"A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones. A single lever clears the region where only one condition holds and leaves the intersections standing, because the second condition still binds."**
- Fig 9 :57-58: **"a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either. It stops being a mixture and becomes a specific compound."**

The three named causes overlap in the world: a compound hot-dry event can be rare, and a flash flood can be both compound and local. So they cannot be a partition. The correct topology is a union with three overlapping regions and at least one triple intersection. Each region gets its own loop and its own refuter; the intersections are not averaged into the marginals.

Consequence for the remedy: a lever that raises resolution clears the local-only region. A lever that lengthens the record clears the rare-only region. Neither clears the intersection, because the second condition still binds there. This is the exact clause Fig 9 :55 predicts.

**Falsifier, written before the run:** if a single intervention removes the residual in all three regions at the same rate, the union reading fails and a partition reading stands. Status: `general`, triangulated across Fig 9 and Fig 12 (different quantities: when to split a search vs what makes an arrangement a new substance).

## Q2 — Are compound events isomers of single extremes?

- Fig 12 :46: **"A bond is the typed relation between two operands. There are seven, they are not interchangeable, and which one holds is not recoverable from the operands."**
- Fig 12 :117: **"Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."**
- Fig 12 :124: **"A functional group. A substructure whose presence fixes the outcome. The group says what kind; the qualifier says which member."**

Hot and dry are atoms (first-order extremes settled against the ground, per Fig 12 :104). Their joint occurrence under conjunction (+) is a different term from either alone, and the type of the bond matters:

| compound class (world name, imported) | figure bond | what the bond changes |
|---|---|---|
| multivariate hot-dry, wet-windy | + conjunction, both hold at once | depends on a dependence parameter, not on either marginal |
| preconditioned (antecedent soil moisture then flood) | → sequence, B needs A to have happened | order is decisive; swapping operands gives a different event |
| temporally compounding (two dry summers in a row) | → sequence at a higher order | the output re-enters as input |
| spatially compounding (breadbasket heat) | + over distinct slots, then a comparison ⋛ at the aggregate | the operands are in different places, not the same place |

This is Fig 12's isomer claim one level up: the same drivers under a different typed bond are a different substance. Fig 12 :94: **"The arrangement is the substance."**

**Important limit.** Bond assignment is a `specified` hypothesis about the world frame, not a figure derivation. The seven bonds are figure-internal; the mapping of Zscheischler's four classes onto them is an import with a shadow test (it explains why marginal skill fails). Zscheischler et al. (2020) warn that *"the complex nature of compound events results in some cases inevitably fitting into more than one class, necessitating soft boundaries within the typology."* That is exactly the figure's warning that the bond type is not recoverable from the operands. Double-bonded cases must be recorded as such rather than forced into one class.

**Falsifier:** if two imported compound classes can be shown to share both operands and bond yet differ in behaviour, the bond typing is insufficient and a new distinction (world-frame, provenanced) is owed. Status: `specified`, single-source for the bond set (Fig 12), unmeasured for the mapping.

## Q3 — What is the order of the union?

- Fig 12 :104: **"A settled molecule becomes an atom for whatever composes over it. So irreducible is a fact about a level and never about the world"**
- Fig 13 :76: **"A gradient over space and one over time are different terms, because which state it is bonded to is part of what it is."**

A single extreme that has been settled against the ground (a 10-year hot day, a 99th-percentile precipitation event) becomes an atom at the next level. A compound event composes over those settled atoms, so it is second order. This is why the remedy order in `01` §4 is not arbitrary: SEARCHED must test whether the second-order molecule is already in the closure before INWARD or OUTWARD is tried.

Dependency order (Fig 3 :20): **"The chain is ordered by dependency, not by importance. Each link consumes what the one above produces, so effort spent above the break arrives at a step that cannot use it."** For this question the order is:

```
perception (is anything returning at the slot?)
   -> vocabulary (is there a term for the joint or the sequence?)
      -> objective (per-slot skill, not mean skill)
         -> planning (SEARCHED, then INWARD, then OUTWARD)
            -> learn and carry (record the provenance and the refuter)
```

Fig 3 :5, :7, :10 are quoted in `01` §1. A modelling group that jumps to planning while perception is missing has spent effort above the break.

**Falsifier:** if a remedy at the objective or planning link removes the residual with perception and vocabulary held fixed, the dependency order is wrong. Status: `specified`, single-source (Fig 3).

## Q4 — What the order is not

The order is not a ranking of the three causes by importance, and not a claim that rare happens before local before compound. Fig 13 :4-5: **"There is no order of rank here. The two kinds are in lockstep, a crossing is a change of role rather than a step upward, and no member of the pair is senior to the other."** Applied here: rarity, locality and compoundness are crossings of the same loop at different scales, not rungs. This blocks the tempting but wrong claim that compound events are "harder" versions of single extremes. Fig 9 :57 already refuses that: a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either.

## CONCLUSION

The three named causes form a **union**, not a partition (Q1, triangulated). Compound events are figure-level **isomers** of single extremes when the same settled drivers are bound by a different typed bond, with the bond mapping kept as `specified` and provenanced (Q2, single-source). The remedies are **ordered by dependency** from perception through vocabulary to planning, and SEARCHED precedes INWARD and OUTWARD (Q3, single-source). The order carries no rank among the causes (Q4, Fig 13).

What survives re-census: the union law and the order. What remains open: the exact bond assigned to each world compound class, and whether a case fitting two classes needs a compound bond the seven do not contain. That is recorded as a strain, not hidden.
