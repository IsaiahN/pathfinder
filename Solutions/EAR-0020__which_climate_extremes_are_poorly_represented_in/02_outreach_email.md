# Outreach draft: check one shape claim before you decide whether the derivation matters

**Use:** one-to-one, or as a cover letter attached to a submission through the public venues in `03_public_contact_points.csv`. **Not** for bulk send.

**Hard precondition:** do not send until the stage-1 output below survives a reader's own check. If the shape claim fails its refuter, there is nothing else to look at.

---

**Subject:** One claim about which extremes models miss, and what would refute it

> Hi [name],
>
> I have one specific output I would like you to check against what you already know about extreme-event modelling. I am not asking you to read the framework first, and I am not asking you to agree with it. If the output does not survive, there is nothing else to look at.
>
> **The residual, in effect terms.** A climate model predicts per grid cell and per event slot. The gap to the observation is indexed by slot, not averaged. Fig 1 :19: "The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated." Fig 1 :22: "Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average." The question asks which slots carry a positive residual that the model systematically misses, and groups the causes as rare, local, or compound.
>
> **The shape claim (`specified`, not `grounded`).** Those three causes are not three independent puzzles. They are three structural conditions:
>
> 1. **Rare** is a live slot hidden by an average. A global mean skill near zero is compatible with one event slot carrying all the error. Fig 1 :13: "a global R near zero with one live slot residual is a legal state".
> 2. **Local** is a lossy scale transform. Fig 4 :31: "The gap is not an error to eliminate. It is the measurement of what the coarser description cannot hold, and it is the only honest report of how much was given up." What is owed is a measured round-trip gap, not a claim that the coarse field represents the local event.
> 3. **Compound** is a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones. Fig 9 :57: "a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either. It stops being a mixture and becomes a specific compound." A model that represents heat and drought separately can still miss hot-dry.
>
> **What the shape claim does not say.** It does not name the specific extremes. The framework returns a shape, not a name (Fig 9 :32: "you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself"). The membership list, rare heavy precipitation and unprecedented heat, tornadoes and sub-daily flash floods, hot-dry events, fire weather and compound flooding, comes from published climate assessments with provenance, not from the derivation. Convergent derivation and adopted import look identical in the contents and only the record separates them, so I keep the record.
>
> **What would refute the shape claim.** I wrote these down before sending this:
> 1. Per-slot, equally weighted skill shows no excess residual at rare-event slots.
> 2. A downscaling round trip has a round-trip gap indistinguishable from zero for the tested local quantity.
> 3. A driver-independent model reproduces the joint frequency and impacts of a compound event.
> 4. Multi-model agreement that draws on one evidence pool is treated here as a verdict (it is not; the observed anchor decides).
>
> If any of the first three holds, the corresponding part of the shape fails and I will retract it. A one-line "this fails because..." is more useful to me than agreement. If you would rather not engage, no reply is needed.
>
> **If it survives, and only then:** the sources are below. The figures are the authority, not my summary of them.
> - Verbatim figure text with line numbers (local framework extract): `FRAMEWORK/reference/03-FIGURES_TEXT.md`
> - Canonical usage rule (local framework copy): `FRAMEWORK/reference/FIGURES_README_canonical.md`
> - The eight-step loop (local framework file): `FRAMEWORK/figures/THE_FORMULA.md`
> - World corroboration actually read: IPCC AR6 WG1 Chapter 11, https://www.ipcc.ch/report/ar6/wg1/chapter/chapter-11/
> - Compound-event typology: https://www.nature.com/articles/s43017-020-0060-z
>
> **If you want to use the machinery:** the formula is in the figures link. Run it on a problem you know well and can check against a ground. Write down what would refute your result *before* you run it. The framework's own limit is that a loop cannot check the direction of its own question, and its author warns that outputs *"could never be verified if the solutions presented were not contrived to fit the formula."* A reported failure is more useful to me than a reported success.
>
> Thanks for your time,
> [name]
