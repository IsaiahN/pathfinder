## Wider literature that bears on the question

1. **IPCC AR6 WG1 Ch 11** is the anchor for the field's own answer. It says models reproduce the sign of temperature-extreme change but not always the magnitude, that resolution helps in high-topography regions, that sub-daily studies are scarcer, and that compound-event probability has increased. It is a secondary witness for the shape, and the primary source for the membership list.
2. **Zscheischler et al. 2020** gives the four-class typology. Its statement that classes have soft boundaries is a direct, independent corroboration of Fig 9 :54 (union rather than partition). The two sources share no evidence pool with the figures, so this agreement is genuinely useful, though it is still corroboration and never a figure derivation.
3. **Bevacqua et al. 2023** supports the SEARCHED branch: part of the deficit is budget-limited sampling, not a missing mechanism. It also supplies the curse-of-dimensionally framing that matches Fig 3's perception/vocabulary break in effect terms.
4. **Ridder et al. 2022** supports the D4 warning that an unweighted multi-model median can hide skill-dependent bias. It does not say the models are wrong about everything; it says the ensemble summary can be biased.

No world source conflicts with the figure verdict. The conflict rule was not triggered. Overlap among the four world sources is partial (they cite one another and share the CMIP/IPCC pool), so their agreement is treated as one climate-science evidence pool, not four independent witnesses.

## Distribution: offer, do not push

The figure-grounded reasons are the same as in the exemplar package:

- **Fig 4 :22**, *"Only methods cross up. Only head starts come down."* Sending the full architecture before a reader has described a residual sends a recording, not a generator. Lead with the shape claim and its refuter; offer the links after.
- **Fig 10 :42**, *"A verifier that can reconstruct the claim cannot verify it."* Seat 4 must not hold the interiors. A recipient who has absorbed the whole framework before being asked for a verdict has joined the frame being checked.
- **Fig 9 :27-28**, *"describe it before you go looking, or any frame you pick will seem to fit."* Ask what they would expect to see at a rare/local/compound slot before giving them the article.
- **Fig 2 :40**, *"disqualifying for a verdict: agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool"*. A mass send selected for people already sympathetic produces a convention, not corroboration.

**Recommended order in a reply:** (1) the shape claim and its refuter; (2) ask what the reader would count as a failure; (3) record that verdict; (4) only then the three framework links and the four world links, with the note that the figures, not the summary, are the authority.

## The two-stage offer

1. **Stage 1, before any theory.** Send the residual, the three structural conditions and the pre-registered refuter. The recipient checks the output without holding the framework's interior. Fig 10 :52, *"Bring a characterised residual, never the source."*
2. **Stage 2, opt-in only.** If curious, the reader opens the links. Their stage-1 verdict must already be recorded. Later agreement is never pooled as corroboration, because the moment they have read the framework they share its evidence pool (Fig 2 :40).
3. **Stage 3, offer the formula as a method.** Fig 4 :22 permits sending generators. Each user must write their own refuter and state their own mode before running it, because a frame *"cannot check the direction of its own question"* (formula, Direction).

**Hard precondition:** stages 1 to 3 work only if the stage-1 output survives. The honest status of that output is `specified`, ungrounded, candidate. It is offered for checking, not for belief.
