## 0. The residual, stated in effect terms

Observation to be explained. Public attention and public money are not distributed as a function of truth or importance alone. Four selections happen, not one.

1. Before any allocation, a **problem-definition** step decides which uncertainties count as unanswered public questions at all. The James Lind Alliance framework, as described by Millum (2024), gathers "uncertainties" from guidelines, systematic reviews and surveys of patients and clinicians, removes questions that already have answers, and produces a long list, then a top ten. The CHNRI method, as reviewed by Rudan et al. (2017), lists up to a few hundred competing questions and scores them against defined criteria.
2. An **allocation** step decides which of those questions receive a budget, time, infrastructure and eligible participants. Millum (2024) notes that resources include not only funding but "the time allotted, available research infrastructure (e.g., MRI machines), eligible research participants, and even trainee places."
3. An **attention-durability** step decides which questions hold attention across years rather than weeks. Downs (1972): "AMERICAN public attention rarely remains sharply focused upon any one domestic issue for very long." He adds that each problem "suddenly leaps into prominence, remains there for a short time" and then "gradually fades from the center of public attention."
4. A **historical-inheritance** step carries past criteria, past awards and past narratives into the next round. Rudan et al. (2017) report that by 2016 more than two thirds of CHNRI exercises had departed from the original five criteria, and they explicitly ask "what time horizon(s) grant agencies adopt and how these differ across agencies" and "whether this is explicit or implicit and how this is decided." Tomlinson et al. (2011) found that "none of the countries surveyed had a systematic or operational appeals process for outlined priorities" and that "in all countries (except South Africa) the priorities that were outlined described broad disease categories rather than specific research questions."

Stated as an effect: there is a gap between the allocation a ground-pressured selection would keep, meaning a per-slot residual that trends down against something that does not move when a frame thinks, and the allocation the societal system actually keeps, meaning a distribution that tracks prior attention, inherited criteria and the influence of whoever set the last agenda. The actors are experts, advocacy groups, patients, publics, media, funders, panels, ministries, indexers and later panels reading past awards. The currencies are salience, public money, attention and legitimacy. The question is whether the selection ranges against a ground that does not update, or whether it updates on its own previous decisions and converges on whoever had the most influence.

An answer would look like this, written down and bound as an ordinary term before the run (Formula step "Direction"): a **selection shape**, that is, a list of the selection relations, the currency of each, the location of the anchor for each, the conditions under which the residual index is per slot rather than aggregate, and the dependency order among them. What an answer would *not* be: a universal priority rule, a budget percentage, a rank order of societies, or a named list of questions that every society ought to fund. Those are preferences, not terms, and the loop cannot check its own direction (Fig 5 :62-63).

## 1. Domain census, before any figure line is chosen

### R by arity, symmetry and scale

- **Arity.** At least four live quantities: the ex ante problem definition, the allocation of public resources, the durability of attention over time, and the historical inheritance of criteria and narratives. "Which question deserves resources" and "which question holds long-term attention" are not one variable with two values.
- **Symmetry.** The coupling is ordered, not symmetric. Attention makes a question salient, salience enters an agenda, an agenda enables an allocation, results feed later criteria. This is a `→` sequence in the operators table ("A then B; B needs A to have happened", Operators :30), not a `+` conjunction. Swapping the order gives a different process: funding first and agenda second is a different society from agenda first and funding second.
- **Scale.** Per question, per grant, per career slot; then field and portfolio; then generational and societal. Fig 1 :19 says the residual is indexed by slot. A headline such as "society funds the right questions" aggregates across slots and throws away the signal about where to look (Fig 1 :22).

### One line per domain: actors, currency, anchor

A domain is admitted only if a nameable anchor can be stated, even if that anchor is currently unreached. Where no anchor can be named, the object is not yet a domain and the question must be re-censused.

- **D1, the problem-definition and agenda frame.** Actors: subject experts, patients and carers, advocacy groups, journalists, elected representatives, programme officers. Currency: salience, admitted uncertainties, problem definitions. Anchor: the unresolved question's contact with a ground, that is, an observation or experiment that would change its status. Nameable, so D1 is a domain. The admitted uncertainties are an instrument for that anchor, and instruments can be wrong on axes they do not measure (Fig 2 :37-38).
- **D2, the allocation frame.** Actors: funders, ministries, panels, reviewers, applicants. Currency: public money, time, infrastructure, trainee places. Anchor: the eventual ground contact of the funded question, something that does not move because a panel liked the proposal. Nameable, so D2 is a domain. Its operational criterion (score, ranking, award) is a budget instrument, not the anchor.
- **D3, the attention-durability frame.** Actors: media, institutions created after a peak of concern, indexers, later panels reading past awards, historical narratives. Currency: sustained attention, repetition, cumulative credit. Anchor: an external ground introduced by a room that differs from the last one. Inside the loop alone no anchor is present, which is exactly the mirror-chain failure. The anchor is nameable as what selection between rooms over time would reach, so D3 is a domain.
- **D4, the public seat and stake.** Actors: the public, governance bodies, agenda setters, groups whose voices are routinely absent. Currency: legitimacy, trust, voice, accountability. Anchor: the consequences of a selection for knowledge or health, read by a checker who cannot author those consequences. A ground with a reputation to protect is disqualified (Fig 10 :93), so this anchor must be sought outside the selecting body. Nameable, so D4 is a domain. Tomlinson et al. (2011) make the operational need concrete: a fair process requires "revision/appeals" and "leadership" as separate criteria, and "none of the countries surveyed had a systematic or operational appeals process."
- **Rejected as a domain:** "the public mood" or "the zeitgeist." No anchor can be named that does not itself update on the system's own reports. It is habitat, not a verdict-bearing domain. Millum (2024) reports that priority setting is often indirect, so a journal encouraging "high-priority" topics or an advocacy campaign counts as habitat pressure, not as an anchor.

### Partition or union

D1, D2 and D3 are a **union of overlapping conditions**, not a partition. Past attention is an input to the agenda (prior citations and prior awards are read as merit evidence); the agenda constrains which findings can exist and be seen; the allocation determines which questions can be worked on at all; inherited criteria weight all three. Fig 9 :55: "A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones." Fig 9 :54: "And when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again." One lever, such as more money, double-blind review, a longer time horizon or a new survey of patients, clears the region where only one condition holds and leaves the intersections standing. Verdicts therefore stay separate per domain and are never averaged into a single "society got it right" score.

### Order by dependency (Fig 3)

1. Perception: can the selection see the residual per slot? (Fig 3 :4-5)
2. Vocabulary: does it have terms for what a question affords? (Fig 3 :6-8)
3. Objective: can it say what would count as progress, in its own terms? (Fig 3 :9-11)
4. Planning: is there a route to that objective under incomplete information? (Fig 3 :12-14)
5. Learn and carry: name what worked, verify it, carry it to the next problem. (Fig 3 :14-16)

The chain is ordered by dependency, not importance: Fig 3 :20: "The chain is ordered by dependency, not by importance." Effort spent above a break arrives at a step that cannot use it (Fig 3 :19). The suspected break in societal priority setting is between links 2 and 3: the system has a rich vocabulary of criteria (answerability, effectiveness, deliverability, equity, feasibility, cost) and of inputs and outputs (money, papers, citations, scores), but typically no instrument for "did this funded question change contact with a ground that does not update on the process." Rudan et al. (2017) expose exactly this: the time horizon "clearly influences research prioritisation," yet which horizon a grant agency adopts is often implicit. That link attracts the least attention and the fewest instruments (Fig 3 :25-26), and "A link with no instrument is not a link that works. It is a link nobody has looked at" (Fig 3 :27). This is a reading of the chain, not a property of it; Fig 3 :18 says where a chain stops is a reading, never a property of the chain.

## 3. Findings

### F1  -  Public resources are a budget, not a ground (decisive for the shape; `general`)

Fig 2 :29: "An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks." Fig 2 :33: "The anchor must not update." Fig 12 :127: "the budget is what decides how much of the space is reached at all." Fig 6 :21: "MINT composes inside the closure and can never add an atom."

Public resources buy the attempt. They determine which questions enter the reachable set, how much of the arrangement space is searched, how many people can work on a slot, and for how long. They do not determine which finding is true or which question mattered. The essay makes the same separation in non-figure language: prestige can fund an action but cannot settle it (Recursive Self-Improvement Trap). Bush (1945, as quoted in the National Academies 2020 volume) gives the historical public-policy form: basic research is funded because "new products and processes are not born full-grown. They are founded on new principles and new conceptions that result from basic scientific research." The payoffs of a particular investigation cannot be predicted, which is precisely why a budget cannot be a verdict.

Applied to D1 and D2, this gives the first half of the answer: the societal selection shapes **reach**, not **settlement**. Cost if wrong: if this is false, a panel could certify a question as important by funding it, which no instrument in the ledger supports.

**Mode:** `general` for the structural separation, `specified` when applied to a named funding or agenda system. **Status:** triangulated across Fig 2 (anchor), Fig 12 (budget) and Fig 6 (closure), which use different objects.

**Refuter:** exhibit a public-selection criterion that settles a slot without moving when the selecting body is pushed, and whose per-slot residual trends down as resources increase while attention tracks that same non-updating measurement rather than prior attention.

### F2  -  The four selections form a union, not one decision (`general`, `specified` when applied)

Fig 9 :55: "A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones." Fig 9 :54: "And when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again." Fig 9 :57: "a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either."

Problem definition, allocation, attention durability and historical inheritance are not four boxes that can be counted and ranked. They overlap: past attention enters the agenda, the agenda constrains the allocation, the allocation determines which findings can become visible, and the criteria are inherited from earlier rounds. Millum (2024) makes the overlap institutional: funders define the context, experts list and rank questions, journals signal what will be published, advocacy groups press the agenda, and governments act both directly and indirectly. Tomlinson et al. (2011) show the same union operationally: their framework needs "Documentation and legitimacy", "Stakeholder involvement", "Revision/Appeals" and "Leadership" as four separate criteria, and a failure in one is not repaired by effort in another.

A single lever clears only the region where one condition holds. More money does not repair an agenda that cannot see the slot; a new survey does not repair a room chain that books the same reviewers; a longer time horizon does not by itself create an appeal mechanism.

**Mode:** `general` structural, `specified` for a named society. **Status:** triangulated across Fig 9 and Fig 3, two distinct figures with different objects (union splitting versus dependency ordering), with world corroboration from research priority setting and national priority review. The priority-setting world sources share one evidence pool, so they count as one witness for the union shape.

**Refuter:** show a single lever that clears all four selections including their intersections, so that the compound is a partition rather than a union, tested at the ground and not inside a shared evidence pool.

### F3  -  The effect is per slot, never aggregate (`general`)

Fig 1 :19: "The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated." Fig 1 :22: "Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average." Fig 9 :4-5: "are not one hard question but several well-formed ones. Split it rather than search."

"Which questions deserve public resources?" is not one question. The answer differs by field, career stage, question type, disease burden, population and time horizon. Rudan et al. (2017) show that discovery research was prioritised where the time horizon exceeded ten years and few effective interventions existed, while delivery research dominated where the horizon was short and interventions existed but were not implemented. An average across all slots is a budget filter, not a witness (Fig 9 :60). This is why the priority-setting literature can report opposite results in different settings without either being wrong: the disagreement does not shrink with effort because each rule works well on a different subset.

**Mode:** `general` structural, `specified` for any named slot. **Status:** triangulated across Fig 1 and Fig 9, two distinct figures with different objects (slot indexing versus union splitting).

**Refuter:** show that a global attention average predicts per-slot question choice as well as or better than per-slot residuals, with the comparison made at the ground and not inside a shared evidence pool.

### F4  -  Self-updating criteria are mirrors, not triangulation (`specified`)

Fig 2 :34: "If it does, panel 3 turns into panel 1: the anchor becomes another frame, everything drifts together, and the drift is coherent, which is why nobody notices." Fig 2 :35: "Alignment is triangulation, not negotiation. A mirror cannot correct you." Fig 2 :40: "agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool". Fig 10 :47: "a convention that decides an outcome has stopped being one"

A panel that treats prior awards as evidence of merit, an indexer who ranks by past citation counts, and a historical narrative that cites previous narratives are each ranging against a record produced by the same process. Their agreement is the pool talking, not two frames ranging against a ground. Downs (1972) supplies the low-overlap world shape: public perception of a crisis "does not reflect changes in real conditions as much as it reflects the operation of a systematic cycle of heightening public interest and then increasing boredom with major issues." Viergever et al. (2010) find that priority exercises commonly overlook good-practice elements and that "one gold standard or best practice is therefore not attainable, nor appropriate"; their checklist nevertheless insists on transparency and evaluation, which are the two ways a process can be ranged against something outside itself.

**Mode:** `specified`. **Status:** triangulated (Fig 2 plus Fig 7 plus Fig 10), with world corroboration from public-policy attention research and from research priority setting. The two world fields are low overlap; the priority-setting papers share one pool and count once.

**Refuter:** show a public selection whose criterion is an external measurement, not a prior decision of the same process, with independence renewed by separate experience (Fig 10 :80), and show that the per-slot residual trends down.

### F5  -  Long-term attention is a room chain that runs in place unless the next room differs (`specified`)

Fig 7 :12-13: "same closure, handed along; agreement compounds, evidence does not". Fig 7 :35: "agreement is variation, not a verdict. Selection between rooms is where the ground votes." Fig 7 :36: "A chain of mirrors is one long room. The next room must differ from the last, and match the residual it surfaced." Fig 8 :32: "no search, however long, adds an atom"

This is the finding that the historical section of the question makes decisive. A society that books the same kind of room each round, selected by the same criteria, travels in place. The room's internal agreement is variation, not a verdict. Downs (1972) names the five stages (pre-problem, alarmed discovery, realising the cost, decline, post-problem) and notes that institutions created during a peak "almost always persist and often have some impact even after public attention has shifted elsewhere." Those persistent institutions are the physical residue of a room that was booked once. Progress over time requires the next room to differ from the last and to match the residual the last one surfaced. The two factors in Fig 7 :38-39, "the bracket's fidelity across the handoff" and "the ground's pressure on the room", are the quantities to instrument; zero either and the chain runs in place.

**Mode:** `specified`. **Status:** triangulated (Fig 7 plus Fig 6 plus Fig 8, all different objects), with world corroboration from Downs 1972 in political science and Tomlinson et al. 2011, who found that Malaysia's repeated priority exercise was the only case allowing comparison across time. These are two genuinely different fields, but agreement on a shape is still one witness pool per field, not a merged proof.

**Refuter:** show a sequence of societal rooms with low overlap whose next room is chosen against the residual surfaced by the previous one, and whose per-slot residuals trend down across handoffs. That would refute the claim that a reused chain necessarily runs in place; it would not refute Fig 7 itself.

### F6  -  There is no standing list of good priority rules (`general`)

Fig 11 :43: "The habitat is enumerated, never composed." Fig 11 :49: "An actor's valence is relative to the residual, not a property it carries." Fig 11 :51: "No standing list of good actors survives a change of residual." Fig 11 :61: "The seat's power is asymmetric. Introduction is available; exclusion is not." Fig 11 :40: "An improvement that does not change contact changes nothing, however much it improves."

The same actor, for example a prestigious reviewer, a patient advocate or a media campaign, is a benefactor to a residual that needs discrimination and a malfactor to a residual that needs novelty. So the honest answer to "which priority method is best?" is not a list. It is a rule for enumerating the habitat (actors, conditions and relations) and for re-reading valence whenever the residual changes. Viergever et al. (2010) reach the same practical limit from inside the field: "There are as many approaches to health research prioritization as there are priority setting exercises. One gold standard or best practice is therefore not attainable, nor appropriate." Millum (2024) reports that the methods "are deliberately incomplete" and that "all defer to some extent to the organizers of and/or participants in a priority setting exercise with respect to the criteria used." What a seat can do is introduce actors, for example a lottery, a registered report, a patients-and-clinicians partnership, an appeal mechanism or an independent room. It cannot subtract contact, because contact is a fact about the world and does not consult it (Fig 11 :61).

**Mode:** `general`. **Status:** single-source on the figure side (Fig 11), corroborated in a different field by Viergever et al. 2010 and Millum 2024, which are themselves one priority-setting pool. Reported as single-source-plus-corroboration, not upgraded to triangulated across independent pools.

**Refuter:** exhibit a priority rule that improves per-slot question choice across several changed residuals without changing valence and without adding contact.

### F7  -  The ground does not decay, the channel does (`specified`)

Fig 10 :24: "the ground does not decay, the channel does". Fig 10 :26: "stale percepts, dropped transients, saturated metrics, pooled readings". Fig 11 :36: "Where nothing maintains anything, intelligence is a label applied afterwards to a lineage that met its ground. Nothing was intelligent; selection ran."

What fails in a societal priority system is the channel, not the truth. Attention saturates, pooled readings replace per-slot readings, transients are dropped, and a broad agenda category stands in for a specific research question. Tomlinson et al. (2011) found exactly this: priorities were mostly "broad disease categories rather than specific research questions," documentation varied markedly, and feedback to participants was limited. When the output stops reporting contact, the system says "you have lost touch" rather than a verdict (Fig 10 :27-28). The remedy is to keep the anchor reachable, not to negotiate a new definition of importance.

**Mode:** `specified`. **Status:** triangulated (Fig 10 plus Fig 11 plus Fig 2, different objects: channel versus selection versus anchor).

**Refuter:** show a public-priority metric that does not saturate under increased use, does not pool across slots, and whose failures are detected at the ground rather than by the selecting body's reputation.

### F8  -  The selecting body's stake is structural and must be declared (`general`)

Fig 10 :59: "an interest in the outcome is unavoidable, an interest in the reading is disqualifying." Fig 10 :60: "Where a stake cannot be removed, declare it. An undeclared interest is modelled anyway and an unnamed model runs unchallenged." Fig 10 :93: "A ground with a reputation to protect is a ground someone benefits from, and its keeper is thereby disqualified." Seat Map :171: "an interest in the outcome is unavoidable, an interest in the reading is disqualifying."

A body that sets public priorities cannot be its own anchor. Its interest in the outcome (a successful portfolio, a healthier population, a credible nation) is unavoidable; its interest in the reading (which questions count as important, whose uncertainty counts as an uncertainty) is disqualifying. Declaring the stake does not remove it, but it makes the model visible so it can be challenged. Millum (2024) gives the world-side form: stakeholder involvement has instrumental and intrinsic reasons, and "without attention to power dynamics, their engagement can often lead to presence without voice and voice without influence" (Pratt, as quoted by Millum). Tomlinson et al. (2011) operationalise the challenge as an appeals mechanism and dispute resolution, which they found absent in every surveyed country.

**Mode:** `general`. **Status:** single-source on the figure side (Fig 10 plus seat map, one framework), with low-overlap world corroboration from research ethics and national priority review. Reported as single-source, not upgraded.

**Refuter:** exhibit a selecting body whose reading is demonstrably unaffected by its own reputation across changed residuals, checked by a party that cannot reconstruct the criterion.

### F9  -  Metrics are filters for the budget, witnesses only if ranged against the ground (`general`)

Fig 9 :60: "Use filters for the budget and witnesses for the verdict. Never let a filter hand you a verdict." Fig 2 :42: "Buy independence in proportion to the cost of being wrong." Fig 1 :27: "They are frame-internal. A frame cannot score itself with a quantity it also produces." Fig 12 :125: "A composition costs in two currencies, and they do not add."

Coverage counts, grant counts, citation counts, impact factors and "number of prioritised questions" price the budget. They tell you how much of the space a field has visited. They do not tell you which question was true or which mattered unless they are separately ranged against a ground that does not update on them. Fig 12 :126 adds the type warning: "Bits price the description. Actions price finding out whether it holds." A short priority list is cheap to state and may take a whole budget to settle. Simon (1971, quoted in Ko, Foundations of Information) gives the low-overlap cognitive framing: "a wealth of information creates a poverty of attention and a need to allocate that attention efficiently among the overabundance of information sources that might consume it." Attention is the scarce currency, so the filter is necessary; the error is promoting it to a witness.

**Mode:** `general`. **Status:** triangulated (Fig 9 plus Fig 2 plus Fig 12 plus Fig 1, different quantities: filter, independence, currencies, frame-internal score), with low-overlap corroboration from information science and human cognition.

**Refuter:** show a metric that functions as a witness at the ground without a non-updating anchor and without low-overlap frames.

### F10  -  The broken link is the objective (`specified`)

Fig 3 :25-26 describes the middle link: "having terms for what a situation offers and being able to say what would count as progress, has neither, so it attracts the least attention and the fewest instruments." Fig 3 :27: "A link with no instrument is not a link that works. It is a link nobody has looked at." Fig 3 :19: "Optimising a link that already works cannot rescue one that does not." Fig 5 :72: "a library that grows is not a reach that grows."

Priority systems have precise terms for inputs and outputs: burden of disease, cost effectiveness, answerability, deliverability, equity, number of awards, number of papers. They typically have no term, or no instrument, for "did this funded question change contact with a ground". Rudan et al. (2017) report that the time horizon is set by convention (a standard ten years in 74% of the first 50 CHNRI exercises) and that whether this is explicit or implicit is itself an open question. So effort lands above the break, where measurements already exist (Fig 3 :29-30), and the system optimises link 4 while link 3 remains unread. More criteria, a bigger library of priorities, and a longer list of scored questions do not move the reach.

**Mode:** `specified`. **Status:** triangulated (Fig 3 plus Fig 5 plus Fig 12 plus Formula step 7, different objects).

**Refuter:** exhibit a societal priority system with a working instrument at the objective link, show the measurement, and show that improving the planning link afterwards changes the per-slot residual rather than only the reported count.

## 4. Pre-registered refuter, written before the conclusion

A falsifier written before the run is a design instrument (Seat Map :287). The shape claim is withdrawn if any of the following fires:

1. **Anchor refuter.** A societal selection is found whose criterion settles a slot, does not move when the selecting body is pushed, and whose per-slot residual trends down as resources rise while attention tracks that same non-updating measurement rather than prior attention. This would break F1 and F4.
2. **Union refuter.** A single lever is shown to clear all four selections, including their intersection, so that the compound is a partition rather than a union. This would break the census and F2.
3. **Mirror-chain refuter.** A sequence of societal rooms with low overlap is shown whose next room is chosen against the residual surfaced by the previous one, with per-slot residuals trending down across handoffs. This would break F5.
4. **Standing-list refuter.** A priority rule is shown to improve per-slot question choice across several changed residuals without changing valence and without adding contact. This would break F6.
5. **Aggregation refuter.** A global attention average predicts per-slot question choice as well as per-slot residuals, tested at the ground. This would break F3.
6. **Sufficiency refuter.** A societal selection is shown to change contact with an external ground while that ground demonstrably moves in response to the selecting body. That would break the anchor definition in Fig 2 :29 and leave the claim without a ground.
7. **Instrument refuter.** An instrument is shown that measures whether a selected question changed contact with a ground, moving the objective link from unreached to reached. This would break F10 in its current form and would be a result, not a defect.

Until one of these is run, nothing here is `grounded`.

## 5. Recommendation

1. State the shape, not the rule. The answer is a set of selection relations and anchor locations, not a universal priority design. Fig 9 :32: "you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself."
2. Split the question into its four selections and keep the verdicts separate. Do not average them into "society got it right" or "society got it wrong" (Fig 9 :54-57).
3. Instrument the objective link before adding budget, criteria or reviewers. Fig 3 :27 applies; optimising above the break cannot rescue it.
4. Keep the channel reachable: per-slot readings, a non-updating ground, an appeal mechanism, and at least one room drawn from a closure that differs from the last (Fig 7 :36, Fig 10 :24, Fig 2 :42).
5. Declare the selecting body's stake and refuse the claim that it settles importance (Fig 10 :59-60, :93).
6. Treat counts and scores as filters for the budget. Never let a count hand you a verdict (Fig 9 :60).
7. Send the residual and the refuter, not the theory. Fig 10 :52: "Bring a characterised residual, never the source." Fig 4 :22: "Only methods cross up. Only head starts come down."

## 7. Strains, re-census and what remains open

**Strain S1, live and named.** Fig 11 :51 refuses a standing list of good actors, while public policy necessarily asks for a standing rule. Re-census: the figure governs valence across changed residuals; a rule at a fixed residual is a candidate binding. Not dissolved into a rule; named as the limit of what the framework can return.

**Strain S2, live and named.** Fig 12 :126 says description cost and action cost do not add, while priority policy routinely sums them into a single "cost of a research investment" or a single score. Re-census: the figure governs two currencies that a system holding one accounting cannot compare. The policy sum is a convention, not a measurement (Fig 10 :18). Kept visible.

**Strain S3, live but narrowed.** Fig 2 :29 requires an anchor that does not update, while Millum (2024) and Tomlinson et al. (2011) show that stakeholder values legitimately enter the criteria. Re-census: values may set the convention (Fig 2 :41, agreement efficient for a convention) but may not serve as the verdict (Fig 2 :40). Whether a given weight is a convention or a verdict is an instrument question. The tension is narrowed, not erased.

**Strain S4, dissolved by re-census.** Fig 2 :40 and Fig 2 :41 appear to contradict each other. Re-census assigns them to verdict and convention respectively (Fig 2 :39). No contradiction, one parameter read twice.

**Strain S5, dissolved by re-census.** Fig 6 :14 calls import the only wall-moving operator, while lotteries, longer horizons and appeal mechanisms are described here as interventions that do not add atoms. Re-census: those interventions add actors to a habitat (Fig 11 :61), they do not claim to move the closure wall. An import would have to add a primitive with provenance and a shadow test; none is claimed here.

**Strain S6, world-source warning.** General summaries of agenda-setting were avoided as witnesses; the Downs quote is taken from the original 1972 text republished by National Affairs, and the issue-attention cycle's normative status is recorded as a world prediction, not a figure line. The Simon quotation is taken from a textbook page that attributes it explicitly to Simon 1971, because the direct PDF fetch returned an unsupported content type. No quote is cited from a page that was not opened.

**What remains open.** No figure line returns which concrete question wins, what share of public resources is correct, which time horizon is right, or which priority method dominates. That is a `gap` of the shape-not-a-name kind (Fig 9 :32, Fig 13 :134), reported as unreached, not as silence (Fig 8 :39). It is unreached at this budget with these instruments, not underivable in principle: the CHNRI scoring datasets, the WHO checklist, appeal records and registered-report infrastructure are instruments already returning something from which an INWARD instrument could be built. A direct Simon PDF fetch returned an unsupported content type, the James Lind Alliance site returned 405 GET and COHRED returned a transport error; those venues were not cited as read.

### Updated verdict

The figures return a shape, not a priority rule. Public resources set reach, not settlement. Agenda definition, allocation, attention durability and historical inheritance form a union that must be unbundled and read per slot. Criteria that draw on their own past decisions are mirrors, and their agreement is variation rather than a verdict. A metric that decides an outcome has stopped being a convention; counts price the budget and are not witnesses. No standing list of good priority rules survives a change of residual, and a selecting body cannot certify its own importance. Every domain verdict is kept separate and none is averaged. Mode: `specified` on the domain mechanisms, `general` on the structural shape, not `grounded`. After re-census, three strains remain live and named (S1, S2, S3), three are dissolved (S4, S5, S6), and no figure quote was found to have drifted.
