## Two things in the world literature that bear directly on this question

1. **The four selections are described separately by different fields.** Research priority setting describes the agenda and allocation steps (CHNRI, WHO checklist, JLA, ethics review). Political science describes attention durability (Downs 1972). Information science and cognitive science describe the scarcity of the attention currency (Simon 1971). These are genuinely low-overlap fields, but they are secondary witnesses for the shape, not the source of the verdict. The figures decide.
2. **The field's own "no gold standard" result is a corroboration, not a conflict.** Viergever et al. (2010) say one best practice is not attainable and not appropriate, and Millum (2024) says formal methods are deliberately incomplete. That is what Fig 11 :51 would predict for actor valence across changed residuals. It does not license a conclusion that all methods are equally good, which would be a verdict the figures do not return.

## Distribution: offer, do not push

Figure-grounded reasons:

- **Fig 4 :22-25** - "Only methods cross up. Only head starts come down. A recording carried upward looks like knowledge and is a description of one occasion." Sending the full architecture before anyone has described a residual sends a recording, not a generator. Lead with the refuter and the shape claim; offer the links after.
- **Fig 10 :42** - "A verifier that can reconstruct the claim cannot verify it." Seat 4 must not hold the interiors. A recipient who has absorbed the entire framework before being asked for a verdict has joined the frame being checked.
- **Fig 9 :27-28** - "describe it before you go looking, or any frame you pick will seem to fit." Ask what they would expect to see if the shape claim were true before giving them the article.
- **Seat Map :163** - "High correlation is disqualifying for a verdict and efficient for a convention." A mass send selected for people who already agree produces a convention, not corroboration.

**Recommended order in a reply:** (1) the shape claim and its five refuters; (2) ask what they would count as a failure, and record the answer; (3) only then the sources, with the note that the figures, not the summary, are the authority.

## The two-stage offer (and the optional third)

- **Stage 1 before stage 2** is Fig 10 :52, "Bring a characterised residual, never the source", and Fig 2 :45, "Frames range against the ground. They do not negotiate with it." The recipient checks the output without holding the framework's interior.
- **Stage 2 is opt-in by construction.** Once a recipient has absorbed the theory, their agreement stops being evidence. Their verdict on stage 1 must be recorded before any theory is shown, and later agreement is never pooled as corroboration (Fig 2 :40, "agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool").
- **Stage 3 sends a method, which is permitted.** Fig 4 :22, "Only methods cross up. Only head starts come down." The formula is a generator, so giving it away does not give away an answer.
- **Stage 3 still needs a ground per problem.** `THE_FORMULA.md` "Direction": a frame "cannot check the direction of its own question, because the shape a frame is searching for is not a term it holds." Each user must write down what would refute their result before running it, and label the mode of what they get.

**Hard precondition:** stages 1 to 3 only work if the stage-1 output survives. That is why the five refuters lead the email. A reported failure is the desired outcome of the check.
