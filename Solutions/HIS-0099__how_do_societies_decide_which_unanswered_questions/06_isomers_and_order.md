# Are priority mechanisms isomers? Union, order and analogues

**Mode: `specified`.** This is a simulated derivation checked against the figures, not a ground reading (see `05_what_shape_means.md` section 7).

## RESIDUAL

The record describes societal priority setting as four overlapping selections. Two questions are owed: (1) can the four selections be ordered by dependency without flattening the union, and (2) are two different priority mechanisms **isomers** under Fig 12's definition, or merely members of a functional group?

## DERIVATION

### Q1  -  Is the union ordered by dependency?

- Fig 3 :20: **"The chain is ordered by dependency, not by importance."**
- Fig 3 :19: **"Optimising a link that already works cannot rescue one that does not."**
- Fig 3 :27: **"A link with no instrument is not a link that works. It is a link nobody has looked at."**

The dependency order is perception, vocabulary, objective, planning, learn and carry. In this domain that reads: can the system see the question slot (perception); does it have a term for what an unanswered question affords (vocabulary); can it say what would count as contact with a ground (objective); can it choose a route under incomplete information (planning); can it name what worked, verify it and carry it to the next round (learn and carry). The order is not a ranking of importance. Money is usually treated as the most important lever, but money spent before the objective link exists has nowhere to land.

The four selections are not a single chain: they are a union that overlaps the chain at different links. Problem definition sits mostly at perception and vocabulary; allocation at planning; attention durability at learn and carry; historical inheritance at the transform between rounds. Re-census check: ordering the links does not dissolve the union, because overlap remains at each link. This is the Fig 9 :54-57 reading, not a partition forced into a line.

**Falsifier, written before the run:** if a single lever improves all four selections at once, including their intersections, the union reading fails and the four selections are a partition or a single quantity with four names.

### Q2  -  Are two priority mechanisms isomers?

- Fig 12 :117: **"Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."**
- Fig 12 :124: **"functional group. A substructure whose presence fixes the outcome. The group says what kind; the qualifier says which member."**
- Fig 12 :5: **"A set of ingredients is not one. The bond is the rest of the information, and it is the half nothing was recording."**

Take two concrete mechanisms described in the world sources: CHNRI (criteria scored by a large expert pool, then weighted by stakeholders) and the James Lind Alliance framework (patients, carers and clinicians identify and rank unanswered questions, commercially interested parties excluded). Their ingredient lists differ, so as written they are not isomers under Fig 12's strict definition. The honest reading is the weaker one: they are members of the same **functional group**, a selection that lists candidate questions and ranks them against stated criteria. The group says what kind (a ranking selection); the qualifier says which member (expert-scored versus patient-and-clinician-scored).

The stronger isomer claim is available only over a shared atom set. If both mechanisms are written over the same six selection atoms (list candidates, state criteria, score independently, weight by values, decide, appeal), differing only in which bond joins which atoms, then they are isomers: same atoms, different arrangement, different substance. CHNRI uses a metrics-based pooling bond at the score step; JLA uses a consensus bond at the ranking step and an exclusion bond at the membership step. Different bonds, different substance. This is the claim worth testing.

**Falsifier, written before the run:** if either mechanism can be written without an atom the other requires (for example, JLA with no independent scoring step, or CHNRI with no stakeholder weighting step, in a way that changes the outcome rather than only the name), the isomer claim fails and the functional-group reading stands instead.

### Q3  -  What is the order of the room chain over time?

- Fig 12 :104: **"A settled molecule becomes an atom for whatever composes over it. So irreducible is a fact about a level and never about the world"**
- Fig 7 :36: **"A chain of mirrors is one long room. The next room must differ from the last, and match the residual it surfaced."**
- Fig 10 :80: **"Frames whose independence is renewed by separate experience can be maintained"**

Each settled priority round becomes an atom for the next round: the earlier list, the earlier criteria and the institutions created after the last attention peak are all operands. They are settled in the sense of having happened, not in the sense of having been settled by a ground, so they carry no verdict weight on their own. The next round must differ from the last and match the residual the last one surfaced. Tomlinson et al. (2011) give the world-side instance: Malaysia's repeated priority exercise was the only one that allowed comparison across time, so only that case had a chance to be a chain rather than a single room. A chain that re-books the same room produces motion without progress (Fig 7 :12-13: **"same closure, handed along; agreement compounds, evidence does not"**).

The transform between rounds is lossy. Past criteria cannot be carried up as a recording and replayed down as a new decision (Fig 4 :22, "Only methods cross up. Only head starts come down."). What must cross is the method for enumerating the habitat and the criterion for what would count as a ground.

**Falsifier, written before the run:** if a repeated priority exercise can reproduce the same list across changed residuals without a ground reading and still improve per-slot choice, the mirror-chain claim fails for that case.

### Q4  -  What analogues does the question license, and which are refused?

- **Downs 1972, issue-attention cycle.** A low-overlap political-science analogue for attention durability. It predicts a five-stage cycle and the persistence of institutions created at a peak. It corroborates the shape of D3; it does not supply the verdict, because the figures decide.
- **Simon 1971, scarcity of attention.** A low-overlap information-science and cognition analogue for the currency. It predicts that attention, not information, is the scarce resource and must be allocated. It corroborates the filter-not-witness reading (Fig 9 :60).
- **Bush 1945, basic research as an open-ended public commitment.** A historical policy analogue for why a society funds questions whose payoff cannot be predicted. It corroborates budget-not-verdict (F1). Its "endless frontier" framing is a world metaphor, not a seventh primitive or a last room; the figures forbid a last room (Fig 7 :6), so the metaphor is used only where it fits.
- **Refused analogue:** "the market for ideas" as an automatic selector. No anchor is nameable that does not update on market prices generated by the same process, so it is habitat, not a domain (Fig 2 :34). Refused analogue 2: "public opinion" as the anchor for importance. Human judgment is the right anchor only for a question constitutively about people; for whether a question changed contact with a ground it is an instrument, and instruments can be wrong on axes they do not measure (Fig 2 :37-38).

**Falsifier for the analogues:** a source that supplies a non-updating anchor for a market or opinion selection, with a per-slot residual that trends down at that anchor, would turn the refused analogue into a domain and force a re-census.

## CONCLUSION

The four selections are a union crossed by a dependency chain, not a flat list. Two concrete priority mechanisms are, as written, members of a functional group; over a shared atom set they become a real isomer claim with a stated falsifier. A repeated priority round is a room chain whose settled products become atoms for the next round, and it runs in place unless the next room differs and matches the surfaced residual. The low-overlap analogues (attention cycles, scarcity of attention, open-ended public commitment) corroborate mechanism shapes; the market and public-opinion analogues are refused as domains because no non-updating anchor is nameable for them.

This framing keeps what is defensible without inventing a seventh primitive, a universal rule or a last room. The specific mechanism that wins in a given field, its magnitude and its time horizon remain a `gap` of the shape-not-a-name kind.
