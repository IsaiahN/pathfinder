**Subject:** Check one output about research priority setting before you decide whether the framework is worth reading

> Hi [name],
>
> I have one specific output I would like you to check against what you already know about research priority setting. I am not asking you to read the theory first, and I am not asking you to agree with it. If the output does not survive, there is nothing else to look at.
>
> **The output (`specified`, not `grounded`):** public priority setting is not one decision. It is a union of four overlapping selections. First, a problem-definition step decides which uncertainties count as unanswered questions at all. Second, an allocation step decides which of those get public resources. Third, an attention-durability step decides which hold attention across years rather than weeks. Fourth, a historical-inheritance step carries past criteria and past awards into the next round. These overlap, so a single lever clears only the region where one condition holds and leaves the intersections standing. The effect has to be read per question slot, not as a national average.
>
> The structural claim is that public resources buy the attempt, not the verdict. Money determines which questions enter the reachable set, not which answer is true or which question mattered. A criterion that draws on its own past decisions is a mirror, and its agreement is variation rather than a verdict. A metric that decides an outcome has stopped being a convention. And no standing list of good priority rules survives a change of residual, which is consistent with the field's own finding that one gold standard is not attainable.
>
> **What I am not claiming:** that this derives a budget percentage, a time horizon, a rank of agencies, or a list of questions every society should fund. The framework returns a shape, not a name. Naming a lottery, a registered report, a patients-and-clinicians partnership or an appeal mechanism is a binding from the world frame, with provenance, not a derived primitive. Agreement from a shared pool is one witness, not corroboration.
>
> **What would refute the shape claim:**
> 1. a public selection shown to settle a slot against a measurement that does not move when the selecting body is pushed, with the per-slot residual trending down while attention tracks that measurement rather than prior attention;
> 2. a single lever shown to clear all four selections, including their intersection, so the compound is a partition rather than a union;
> 3. a sequence of independently chosen rooms whose next room is picked against the residual the last one surfaced, with per-slot residuals trending down across handoffs;
> 4. a priority rule shown to improve per-slot question choice across several changed residuals without changing valence or adding contact;
> 5. an instrument that measures whether a selected question changed contact with a ground, which would move the currently broken objective link from unreached to reached.
>
> I wrote these down before sending this. If any one holds, I will retract the claim. A one-line "this fails because..." is more useful to me than agreement. If you would rather not engage, no reply is needed.
>
> **If it survives, and only then:** the sources are below. The figures are the authority, not my summary of them.
> - The figures and formula (local canonical extract): `FRAMEWORK/reference/03-FIGURES_TEXT.md` in this project
> - Rudan et al. 2017, CHNRI review, Journal of Global Health: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5481891/
> - Millum 2024, Ethics and health research priority setting, Wellcome Open Research: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11234074/
> - Viergever et al. 2010, checklist for health research priority setting: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3018439/
>
> **If you want to use the machinery:** the formula is in the figures. Run it on a problem you know well and can check against a ground. Write down what would refute your result *before* you run it. The framework's own rule is that a loop cannot check the direction of its own question, and the author warns that outputs could never be verified if the solutions presented were not contrived to fit the formula. A reported failure is more useful to me than a reported success.
