## 1. A shape is a constraint, not a filler

The figures README states the limit: the framework returns *"what a missing thing would have to look like (**a shape, not a name**)."* Fig 9 :32: *"you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself."* Fig 13: *"It can say the answer is out of reach with the library as it stands... It cannot say which primitive is missing, because naming it would mean already holding it."*

From *The Library of Babel*, opened this session: *"you can cut your search short if the set of books returned is small and descriptive enough that it provides a general shape of the answer that satisfies your question."*

So a **shape** is the narrowed form of an answer you can state before you can name it. It constrains; it does not fill. For this question, "four overlapping selections, per-slot residuals, a non-updating anchor, and a chain that must change rooms" is a shape. "All societies should fund X percent of basic research for ten years" is a name, and a preference presented as a term.

## 2. How a shape is obtained

- *Library of Babel*: use the vocabulary you already have that is *closest* to the thing, look for recurring new terms that overlap or correlate with it, recurse. *"With each new term gained, you are able to narrow the space by definition and deepen your vocabulary."*
- Fig 6 :30: *"The description travels. Fails by being sharpened into terms only we can read. Cast it in effect terms."* Fig 9 :30-31: *"your words are borrowed too, so describe the effect rather than the cause."*
- *Decoding Tacit Knowledge*: *"When contact isn't possible, the best you can send is a plain description of what you'd expect to see, because anyone can check that against the world."*
- *Library of Babel* on stopping: the answer is reached when *"the finish line will also be the starting place"* - and the paradox is that by then you have *"composed the answer through the process of locating it."*

Applied here: the ordinary language of "deserves", "public resources" and "long-term attention" is the starting vocabulary. Recursion through the figure lines narrows it to selection relations, currencies and anchors. The bundle warning in the same essay is directly relevant: *"Perhaps your problem was a bundle or conflation of several problems masquerading as one? You will need to search all of those problems individually"* - which is the Fig 9 union verdict.

## 3. What licenses a name

Only two things, both outside the shape itself:

1. **The ground settles it.** Formula step 5: *"the ground settles it. Verified only by what pays."* The README: the framework *"stops at the limit of what the available instruments can distinguish, and records the lineage so the stop is honest."*
2. **A provenanced import from a frame whose closure differs** (Fig 6 :23, Fig 8 :50-52), with a source gate and a shadow test.

*They're Scanning Books* sharpens this: *"The real import is not a new term in the existing space. It is a new dimension of the space."* And: *"A minimum viable set of six terms is a human's careful guess at reality's compression axes; another sensorium's minimum set need not be those six."* Also: *"Declaring the primitives does not make them neutral. It makes the choice visible."*

The same caution applies to naming a priority mechanism. A patients-and-clinicians partnership, a lottery, a registered-report option or an appeal mechanism is an available binding from the world frame, not a new primitive and not a derived answer. Naming one without provenance is the searcher's own contribution.

## 4. Why a metric's reading does not determine the source

*The Hard Problem is About Source*, opened this session, gives the mechanism. Metamerism: an instrument reads green and *"cannot comment on the source"* - one green light, or yellow+blue, or RGB, or CMYK, or a scheme not yet discovered. The reading does not determine the source. *"If several paths create the same behavior, then behavior is a projection with lossy information."* And: *"The answer is unreached until a better instrument exists."* It was the **spectrometer**, not an argument, that resolved it.

Here the corresponding error is reading a high citation count, a high priority score or a well-attended question and naming its source as importance. Several paths create that reading: genuine importance, prior attention, prestige of the proposer, a peak in the issue-attention cycle, and a criterion that updates on its own awards. The score cannot discriminate. What is owed is a discriminator (an appeal record, a post-award ground audit, an independent room), not a name.

## 5. Q2  -  ANSWERED, no further question needed

**What licenses a particular priority rule or time horizon?** Nothing in the framework. The framework returns a shape. A particular rule is licensed only by the ground settling a minted term, or by a provenanced import with a shadow test. Until one of those, the rule is the searcher's own contribution and must be recorded as such.

**Does the world literature commit the error the README forbids?** Not when it reports a checklist or a method as a candidate. Viergever et al. (2010) explicitly refuse a gold standard: *"One gold standard or best practice is therefore not attainable, nor appropriate."* Millum (2024) reports that methods are deliberately incomplete and defer to organisers about criteria. Those are honest candidate bindings. The error would be converting one of them into the answer the framework derived.

## 6. Correction to my own §3 and §4

I initially drafted §3 as if "shape, not a name" forbade naming any concrete mechanism. That overstates the rule. The README's phrase is *"what a **missing** thing would have to look like (a shape, not a name)."* It governs a primitive not held in Γ. A registry, an appeal mechanism, a lottery and a patients-and-clinicians partnership are all already available in the world frame; selecting one is a binding, not the invention of a seventh primitive, and it does not move the closure wall (Fig 6: *"IMPORT adds an atom"* - a binding adds none).

My §4 metamerism point is **not** retracted: a priority score still does not discriminate among the several sources that could produce it, so a source-discriminating instrument is owed. Both claims are now recorded so neither is quietly lost.

## 7. Two consequences for outreach

**Prestige is not action.** From *The Recursive Self-Improvement Trap*, opened this session: *"Prestige must never be allowed to validate an action directly... Someone let prestige stand in for proof... Prestige can fund an action. It can give an action a budget to be tried. But it cannot settle an action. Only the ground can settle it. The Ground Maintainer must be blind to prestige."*

A campaign that gathers prestigious names to agree with a priority claim is the named collapse, not corroboration. The legitimate ask is the opposite: give the claim a budget to be tried, and let the ground, an instrument, an audit or a board settle it.

**My own audit is a simulation, not a ground reading.** Same essay: *"LLMs can quickly simulate an approximate answer/output... However, the truly testable output would need to be derived from the real architecture or working the formula yourself."* Also: *"the output is only as good as the validator. The agent, or the tool, or the frame, or the theory, cannot validate itself."* So `01` is a simulated derivation, checked for internal consistency against the figures. It is **not** a test of the claim and must be labelled as such. It cannot settle anything it reports on.
