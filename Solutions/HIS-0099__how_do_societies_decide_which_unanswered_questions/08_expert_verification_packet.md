# Expert verification packet  -  the Tether on societal research priority setting

**What this is:** a claim in a form a research-priority-setting scholar or science-policy researcher can check. Mode, derivation and refuters are stated before the conclusion, so a reader knows what to look for and what would settle it.

**Mode: `specified`  -  not `grounded`.** No measurement of a real priority system is claimed. This is a shape and a route, not a result.

## 1. The claim, in one paragraph

Public priority setting is not one decision but a union of four overlapping selections: which uncertainties count as unanswered questions, which of those receive public resources, which hold attention across years, and how past criteria and narratives are inherited. Public resources set reach, not settlement. The effect is indexed per question slot, never a national average. Criteria that draw on their own past decisions are mirrors, and their agreement is variation rather than a verdict; a metric that decides an outcome has stopped being a convention; and no standing list of good priority rules survives a change of residual. The framework returns this shape, not a rule, a percentage, a horizon or a list of questions. The concrete mechanism, share and horizon stay open until a ground settles them.

## 2. Three checkable sub-claims

### C1  -  framework-internal plus field-facing; checkable by reading Figures 9, 1 and 3 and by comparing with the cited priority-setting literature

- **Statement:** the four selections form a union of overlapping conditions, and the residual is per slot. Effort at one lever leaves the intersections standing. The broken link is the objective: systems have criteria for inputs and outputs but typically no instrument for whether a selected question changed contact with a ground.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 9 :55, "A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones." Fig 9 :57, "a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either." Fig 1 :19, "The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated." Fig 3 :27, "A link with no instrument is not a link that works. It is a link nobody has looked at."
- **What the field says:** Rudan et al. 2017 report that the time horizon "clearly influences research prioritisation" and that whether this is explicit or implicit is an open question. Millum 2024 reports that the formal methods are "deliberately incomplete" and defer to organisers about criteria.
- **Refuter:** show a single lever that clears all four selections including their intersection, or show that a global attention average predicts per-slot choice as well as per-slot residuals at the ground, or exhibit a priority system with a working objective-link instrument whose improvement changes the per-slot residual rather than only the reported count.

### C2  -  framework-internal; checkable by reading Figures 2, 7 and 10

- **Statement:** criteria that range against the process's own past output are mirrors, and their agreement is variation rather than a verdict. A criterion that decides an outcome has stopped being a convention. A reused room chain travels in place unless the next room differs and matches the residual it surfaced.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 2 :34, "If it does, panel 3 turns into panel 1: the anchor becomes another frame, everything drifts together, and the drift is coherent, which is why nobody notices." Fig 7 :35, "agreement is variation, not a verdict. Selection between rooms is where the ground votes." Fig 10 :47, "a convention that decides an outcome has stopped being one". Fig 7 :36, "A chain of mirrors is one long room. The next room must differ from the last, and match the residual it surfaced."
- **What the field says:** Downs 1972 reports that public perception of a crisis reflects an attention cycle more than changes in real conditions; Tomlinson et al. 2011 found no operational appeals process in any surveyed country and documented a single repeated exercise (Malaysia) as the only case with cross-time comparison.
- **Refuter:** show a public selection whose criterion is an external measurement that does not move when the selecting body is pushed, with independence renewed by separate experience and per-slot residuals trending down; or show a sequence of low-overlap rooms whose next room is picked against the surfaced residual with residuals trending down across handoffs.

### C3  -  framework-internal; checkable by reading Figures 11, 12, 9 and 2

- **Statement:** there is no standing list of good priority rules across changed residuals; counts price the budget and are not witnesses; the selecting body's stake is structural and must be declared.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 11 :51, "No standing list of good actors survives a change of residual." Fig 9 :60, "Use filters for the budget and witnesses for the verdict. Never let a filter hand you a verdict." Fig 1 :27, "They are frame-internal. A frame cannot score itself with a quantity it also produces." Fig 10 :59, "an interest in the outcome is unavoidable, an interest in the reading is disqualifying." Fig 10 :93, "A ground with a reputation to protect is a ground someone benefits from, and its keeper is thereby disqualified."
- **What the field says:** Viergever et al. 2010 conclude that "One gold standard or best practice is therefore not attainable, nor appropriate." Millum 2024 reports that engagement can produce "presence without voice and voice without influence" (Pratt, as quoted).
- **Refuter:** exhibit a priority rule that improves per-slot choice across several changed residuals without changing valence or adding contact; or show a metric that functions as a witness without a non-updating anchor and without low-overlap frames; or exhibit a selecting body whose reading is demonstrably unaffected by its reputation across changed residuals, checked by a party that cannot reconstruct the criterion.

## 3. What is explicitly not claimed

- Not a universal priority rule, budget percentage, time horizon, agency ranking or list of questions every society should fund.
- Not that all methods are equally good. A binding at a fixed residual is a candidate, not a forbidden invention.
- Not that public attention or a citation count determines importance. They are filters and habitat conditions; several paths produce the same reading, and a discriminator is owed.
- Not that a selecting body's stake can be removed. It is declared, not hidden.
- Not `grounded`. No measurement at the ground is asserted here.
- Not a theory of everything or a last ranking of methods. The framework refuses a last room (Fig 7 :6).

## 5. What would settle it

A source-discriminating instrument, not a further argument. From *The Hard Problem is About Source*: an instrument that reads green "cannot comment on the source"; the spectrometer resolved it. Here:

1. **Contact instrument:** does a selected question change contact with a ground that does not move when the selecting body is pushed?
2. **Union test:** does one lever clear all four selections, including the intersection?
3. **Room-chain test:** is the next round drawn from a differing closure and matched to the surfaced residual?
4. **Appeal test:** is there an operational revision mechanism with a checker who cannot author the decision?
5. **Instrument refuter:** an instrument that measures whether a selected question changed contact with a ground would move the objective link from unreached to reached and would be a result, not a defect.

## 6. What an expert can check in twenty minutes

1. Read Fig 9 :54-57, Fig 1 :19/:22, Fig 2 :29/:34, Fig 3 :27 and Fig 11 :51, six short passages.
2. Check C1 against one priority exercise you know: are the four selections separable, and is there an objective-link instrument?
3. Name one instrument that would distinguish genuine ground contact from prior attention in that exercise, or say why none is possible.
4. Send the refutation. A failure is more useful than an endorsement.
