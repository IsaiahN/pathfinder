# The Tether's answer on societal research priority setting

**Mode: `specified`  -  not `grounded`.** This is a shape and a route, not a result. Simulated derivation checked against the figures (`05_what_shape_means.md` section 7), not a ground reading.

## 1. The residual it answers

Societies do not allocate public resources and long-term attention as a function of truth or importance alone. The residual is the gap between an allocation a ground-pressured selection would keep (per-slot residuals that trend down against something that does not move when a frame thinks) and the allocation actually kept (a distribution tracking prior attention, inherited criteria and the influence of whoever set the last agenda). It is a union compound of four overlapping selections, not one hard problem (Fig 9 :55: *"A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones."*). Each selection has its own loop and its own anchor, so the verdicts stay separate.

## 2. The shape

1. **Public resources set reach, not settlement.** Fig 12 :127: *"the budget is what decides how much of the space is reached at all."* Fig 2 :29: *"An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks."* Money determines which questions enter the reachable set, not which answer is true or which question mattered. This is the prestige/action separation in non-figure language.
2. **There are four selections in a union, not one decision.** Problem definition, allocation, attention durability and historical inheritance overlap. Fig 9 :54: *"And when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again."* One lever clears only the region where one condition holds and leaves the intersections standing.
3. **The effect is per question slot, never a national average.** Fig 1 :19: *"The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated."* Fig 1 :22 warns that aggregation throws away the signal about where to look. The literature's opposite findings across settings are expected under a union, not a contradiction.
4. **Self-updating criteria are mirrors, not anchors.** Fig 2 :34: *"If it does, panel 3 turns into panel 1: the anchor becomes another frame, everything drifts together, and the drift is coherent, which is why nobody notices."* A criterion that reads its own past awards and a ranking built on past citations are ranging against a record produced by the same process. Their agreement is the pool talking.
5. **Long-term attention is a room chain that runs in place unless the next room differs.** Fig 7 :36: *"A chain of mirrors is one long room. The next room must differ from the last, and match the residual it surfaced."* Downs (1972) gives the world shape: attention peaks then fades while institutions created at the peak persist. Progress over time requires a low-overlap room chosen against the surfaced residual.
6. **A metric that decides an outcome has stopped being a convention.** Fig 10 :47: *"a convention that decides an outcome has stopped being one"*. Counts and scores price the budget. Fig 9 :60: *"Use filters for the budget and witnesses for the verdict. Never let a filter hand you a verdict."*
7. **No standing list of good priority rules survives a change of residual.** Fig 11 :51: *"No standing list of good actors survives a change of residual."* Viergever et al. (2010) reach the same limit inside the field: one gold standard is not attainable, nor appropriate. What can be returned is a rule for enumerating the habitat and re-reading valence.
8. **The selecting body cannot certify its own importance.** Fig 10 :59: *"an interest in the outcome is unavoidable, an interest in the reading is disqualifying."* Fig 10 :93 disqualifies a ground with a reputation to protect. The stake is declared, not hidden, and challenged through an appeal seat outside the body.
9. **The objective link is the broken one.** Fig 3 :27: *"A link with no instrument is not a link that works. It is a link nobody has looked at."* Priority systems have rich criteria but typically no instrument for whether a selected question changed contact with a ground. The time horizon that "clearly influences research prioritisation" is often implicit (Rudan et al. 2017).

## 3. What it explicitly does not say

- Not a universal priority rule, budget percentage, time horizon, agency ranking or list of questions every society should fund. The framework returns a shape, not a name (Fig 9 :32).
- Not that all methods are equally good. Fig 11 :51 refuses a standing list across changed residuals; it does not refuse a candidate binding at a fixed residual.
- Not that public attention is irrelevant. It is a filter and a habitat condition, not a witness.
- Not that a priority score determines the source of importance. Several paths produce the same reading; a discriminator is owed (*The Hard Problem is About Source*).
- Not grounded. Nothing here is settled by a measurement at the ground.

## 4. What would settle it  -  the ground

A source-discriminating instrument, not a further argument. From *The Hard Problem is About Source*: an instrument that reads green *"cannot comment on the source"*; the spectrometer resolved it. Here:

- **Contact instrument:** does a selected question change contact with a ground that does not move when the selecting body is pushed? An instrument that distinguishes genuine contact from prior attention would move the objective link from unreached to reached.
- **Union test:** does a single lever clear all four selections including their intersection, or does a second condition still bind? A case in two conditions is not a harder case of either (Fig 9 :57).
- **Room-chain test:** is the next priority round drawn from a closure that differs from the last and matched to the residual the last one surfaced? If not, the chain runs in place.
- **Appeal test:** is there an operational mechanism for revising a priority, with a checker who cannot author the decision? Tomlinson et al. (2011) found none in the surveyed countries.
- Standing falsifiers from `01`: derive a selection that settles a slot against a non-updating ground; show a partition; show a chain with declining per-slot residuals; show a rule stable across changed residuals; show a global average that predicts per-slot choice at the ground.

## 5. The honest stop

**Unreached, not unreachable.** The four selection atoms and the anchor rule are a shape, not a society-specific answer. Fig 13 :134: *"Unreachable is a verdict here, not a silence."* The stop is at the limit of the available instruments, and the lineage is recorded so the stop is checkable. *"Indiscernibility is not identity."* No figure line returns which question wins, what share is correct, or which horizon is right. Declaring a binding such as a lottery or an appeal mechanism makes the choice visible; it does not make it neutral.

## 6. One-paragraph version for outreach

> The Tether does not rank societies or hand out a budget percentage. It says that public resources set reach, not settlement, so money buys the attempt and the ground alone decides which question mattered. It says that problem definition, allocation, long-term attention and historical inheritance are four overlapping selections, not one decision, and that the effect must be read per question slot rather than as a national average. Criteria that feed on their own past awards are mirrors, so their agreement is variation rather than a verdict; a metric that decides an outcome has stopped being a convention; and no standing list of good priority rules survives a change of residual. What it offers is the shape any candidate answer must have, five refuters written in advance, and the instruments that would close it. The concrete mechanism, share and horizon stay open until a ground settles them.
