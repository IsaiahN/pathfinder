## QUESTION

Decide, in effect terms, what the Tether says about the claim that societies decide which unanswered questions deserve public resources and long-term attention. Option A: answer with a universal priority rule, a budget percentage, a time horizon, a ranking of agencies or a claim that public resources determine which question is important. Option B: keep the figure-derived shape, refuse the rule and the verdict claim, and leave the specific binding open until a ground settles it. Each leads somewhere different. A sends a preference where a term is owed, spends the independence of the people asked to check it, and confuses reach with settlement. B sends a checkable shape with refuters written in advance and an instrument request. The figures decide for B. The residual to be explained is the gap between an allocation a ground-pressured selection would keep and the allocation the societal system actually produces.

## DOMAIN CENSUS

R by arity, symmetry and scale, then one line per domain with actors, currency and anchor, then partition or union, then order by dependency.

- **R (the residual).** The gap between a ground-pressured allocation and the allocation actually kept. Arity: four live quantities, the ex ante problem definition, the allocation of public resources, the durability of attention over time, and the historical inheritance of criteria and narratives. Symmetry: ordered, since attention makes a question salient, salience enters an agenda, an agenda enables an allocation, results feed later criteria. Swapping the order gives a different society. Scale: per question, per grant, per career slot, then field, portfolio and generation. Fig 1 :19 "The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated."
- **D1, the problem-definition and agenda frame.** Actors: experts, patients and carers, advocacy groups, journalists, elected representatives, programme officers. Currency: salience, admitted uncertainties, problem definitions. Anchor: the unresolved question's contact with a ground, an observation or experiment that would change its status. Nameable, so this is a domain. Admitted uncertainties are an instrument for that anchor and can be wrong on axes they do not measure.
- **D2, the allocation frame.** Actors: funders, ministries, panels, reviewers, applicants. Currency: public money, time, infrastructure, trainee places. Anchor: the eventual ground contact of the funded question, which does not move because a panel liked the proposal. Nameable, so this is a domain. Score and ranking are budget instruments, not the anchor.
- **D3, the attention-durability frame.** Actors: media, institutions created after a peak, indexers, later panels reading past awards, historical narratives. Currency: sustained attention, repetition, cumulative credit. Anchor: an external ground introduced by a room that differs from the last one. Nameable as what selection between rooms over time would reach, so this is a domain.
- **D4, the public seat and stake.** Actors: the public, governance bodies, agenda setters, groups whose voices are routinely absent. Currency: legitimacy, trust, voice, accountability. Anchor: consequences for knowledge or health read by a checker who cannot author those consequences. A ground with a reputation to protect is disqualified, so this anchor must be sought outside the selecting body. Nameable, so this is a domain.
- **Rejected as a domain.** Public mood or zeitgeist. No anchor can be named that does not itself update on the system's own reports. It is habitat, not a verdict-bearing domain.
- **Partition or union.** D1 to D4 are a union of overlapping conditions, not a partition. Past attention enters the agenda, the agenda constrains the allocation, the allocation determines which findings become visible, and criteria are inherited. A single lever clears only the region where one condition holds. Verdicts stay separate per domain and are never averaged into one "society got it right" score.
- **Order by dependency.** Perception, vocabulary, objective, planning, learn and carry (Fig 3). The chain is ordered by dependency, not importance. The suspected break is between vocabulary and objective: priority systems have rich criteria and input/output metrics but no instrument for whether a selected question changed contact with a ground.

Strain test: a line that only fits with a strain governs another quantity. Fig 2 :40 and Fig 2 :41 were read as a contradiction and re-censused as one independence parameter on two quantities, verdict and convention. Fig 2 :34 and Fig 11 :51 were read as a contradiction and re-censused as anchor status versus actor valence. No figure line was found to have drifted; three live strains remain named in `01_figure_grounded_audit.md` section 7.

## VERDICT (from the figures)

The shape survives; the priority rule and the verdict claim do not. Public resources set reach, not settlement. Problem definition, allocation, attention durability and historical inheritance are a union that must be unbundled and read per slot, never averaged into a national score. A criterion that draws on its own past output is a mirror, and its agreement is variation rather than a verdict. A metric that decides an outcome has stopped being a convention; counts price the budget and are not witnesses. No standing list of good priority rules survives a change of residual, and a selecting body cannot certify its own importance. The historical clause adds one result beyond the sibling funding question: a room chain runs in place unless the next room differs and matches the surfaced residual. The audience's stake and this derivation's simulated status are declared rather than hidden. Mode: `specified` on the four domain mechanisms, `general` on the structural shape, not `grounded`. The specific mechanism, share, horizon and question are a `gap` of the shape-not-a-name kind, reported as unreached, not as silence.

- **cost if wrong:** bounded and locatable but not trivial. If reach and settlement are not distinct, a panel could certify a question by funding it, which no instrument in the ledger supports; if the union is actually a partition, the four per-domain verdicts collapse into one average and the intersections go unexamined; if actor valence is fixed rather than residual-relative, a standing rule becomes licit and the no-list verdict fails; if the room-chain clause is wrong, attention durability is just noise and the historical section of this question has no answer. The avoidable cost is spending the independence of outside readers by sending the full framework before the check. Fig 2 :42 "Buy independence in proportion to the cost of being wrong." Leading with the shape and its five refuters avoids that cost.
- **undoable:** yes. No primitive is withdrawn and no institution is committed by this derivation. `07_the_tether_answer.md` and `08_expert_verification_packet.md` already state the shape, the non-claims and the refuters. A sent packet can be superseded by a corrected one, and a named binding can be provenanced or retracted without touching the figure reading. Fig 9 :29-31 requires every cut to stay ranked and reversible, which this verdict does.
- **reversed by:** a ground reading, not agreement. The following would reverse or refine it: a selection criterion shown to settle a slot without moving when the selecting body is pushed, with per-slot residuals trending down (breaks D1 and D2); a single lever shown to clear all four selections and their intersection (breaks the union); a sequence of low-overlap rooms chosen against the surfaced residual with residuals trending down (breaks D3); a rule shown to improve per-slot choice across several changed residuals without changing valence or adding contact (breaks the no-list verdict); a global attention average that predicts per-slot choice as well as per-slot residuals at the ground (breaks per-slot indexing); an operational appeal with a checker who cannot author the decision (refines D4); or an instrument that measures whether a selected question changed contact with a ground, which would move the objective link from unreached to reached (breaks F10 in its current form and would be a result). A body of agreeing readers, or a list of prestigious names selected for likely agreement, is not such a reversal. Fig 2 :35 "Alignment is triangulation, not negotiation. A mirror cannot correct you." Fig 10 :42 "A verifier that can reconstruct the claim cannot verify it."
