## Two things in the world literature that bear directly on this question

1. **The ensemble mean is explicitly not sufficient where it matters.** IPCC AR6 Chapter 10 says in high confidence that multi-model mean and spread do not characterise low-likelihood, high-impact changes or cases where models simulate opposing changes, and that storylines aid interpretation. This is the closest external statement to the figure verdict that averaging across slots hides the live signal. It also limits the claim: the mean is not forbidden, it is demoted from verdict to description.
2. **Independence is the live methodological problem, not a solved correction.** Abramowitz et al. 2019 say there is no universally accepted methodology to define independence, and Brunner et al. 2020 show that weighting by independence and performance changes the projected mean warming and narrows the likely range by 13 percent in one scenario. Pathak et al. 2023 find roughly 11 independent models carry the information of 57 CMIP6 models. So the figure's demand for independence (Fig 2 :31-32) is not a rhetorical preference; it changes the number.

A cross-field check from survey statistics makes the same arithmetic visible: correlated observations reduce effective sample size (Kish's design effect). The figures make the point at Fig 2 :44. The statistics source is low overlap with climate science, so it is a genuine second witness for that one claim, not a pooled one.

**Overlap warning.** Knutti appears as an author on three of the climate sources (2010, 2013, 2020), and all climate sources draw on the CMIP literature and the same assessment record. Their agreement is partly one pool talking. Pathak et al. 2023 and IPCC AR6 are different institutional frames but still climate science. Only the statistics source is from a different field.
