## 1. A shape is a constraint, not a filler

The canonical figures README states the limit: the framework returns *"what a missing thing would have to look like (a shape, not a name)"*. Fig 9 :32: *"you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself"*.

From *The Library of Babel* (read directly): *"you can cut your search short if the set of books returned is small and descriptive enough that it provides a general shape of the answer that satisfies your question."*

So a **shape** here is the narrowed form of a *procedure* that can be stated before a particular model is named. It constrains; it does not pick a winner. The shape this solution returns is not "model X is right"; it is "a comparison must preserve per-slot residuals, unbundle the three conditions, keep dependent agreement from counting as corroboration, hold the anchor fixed, keep the axes in separate accounts, and report unreachable honestly."

## 2. How the shape is obtained

- *Library of Babel* (read directly): start from vocabulary you already have, look for terms that overlap or correlate with the thing you are describing, recurse. *"With each new term gained, you narrow the space and deepen your vocabulary."*
- Fig 9 :27-28: *"describe it before you go looking, or any frame you pick will seem to fit"*
- Fig 6 :30: *"Cast it in effect terms."*
- *Library of Babel* (read directly): *"The books that you are returning in your searches need to be tested or ranged against the problem you want answered in order to steer the direction of the next search."*
- *Library of Babel* on stopping: *"the finish line will also be the starting place"* and *"you have already composed the answer through the process of locating it."*

For this question the residual was described first (probability, timing, severity, per region and variable), then the figure lines were chosen. The alternative order, picking a preferred comparison statistic and then justifying it, is the apophenia the figure warns against.

## 3. The bundle is the key essay passage for EAR-0095

*The Library of Babel* (read directly) states the exact failure this question presents:

> *"Perhaps your problem was a bundle or conflation of several problems masquerading as one? You will need to search all of those problems individually to find their answers and then combine them in a satisfactory way."*

This is the essay-level version of Fig 9 :4-5 and Fig 9 :57. The question asks about probability, timing and severity as if they were one "disagreement." The essay says: that is a bundle; search each individually, then combine only in a satisfactory way. "Satisfactory" does not mean summed into one score. Fig 12 :130 says *"Summing them into one figure is the error this names, not the fix for it."*

This is the strongest single cross-source point in the package and it should lead any outreach based on it.

## 4. What licenses a name, and why this question needs none

Only two things license a name: the ground settling a minted term, or a provenanced import from a frame whose closure differs. Nothing in this question requires naming a winning model. The framework returns a shape. Selecting a particular model or weighting scheme is a binding from the climate-modelling frame, and must be recorded as such.

As the exemplar's mapping of *They're Scanning Books When They Should* records: *"The real import is not a new term in the existing space. It is a new dimension of the space."* And: *"Declaring the primitives does not make them neutral. It makes the choice visible."* A choice of ensemble statistic is a visible choice, not a discovery of nature's list.

## 5. Prestige is not action (relevant to who checks this)

From *The Recursive Self-Improvement Trap* (read directly):

> *"Prestige can fund an action. It can give an action a budget to be tried. But it cannot settle an action. Only the ground can settle it."*

The same essay: *"You may all agree and be wrong. You may set your clock to someone else's, who was setting your clock to yours, and be off by many deviations over time. You need ground truth to define where you stand."*

That is the model-dependence problem in one sentence. A set of models that calibrate against one another is a set of clocks set to one another. The observational record is the ground truth that defines where they stand. The legitimate ask is a budget to try the protocol, not a list of prestigious endorsements.

## 6. My own audit is a simulation, not a ground reading

From *The Recursive Self-Improvement Trap* (read directly): *"the output is only as good as the validator. The agent, or the tool, or the frame, or the theory, cannot validate itself."* And: *"LLMs can quickly simulate an approximate answer/output with the formula found in the architecture post... However, the truly testable output would need to be derived from the real architecture or working the formula yourself."*

So `01_figure_grounded_audit.md` and `09_watson_answer.md` are simulated derivations checked for internal consistency against the figures. They are **not** a test of the comparison protocol and must be labelled as such. They cannot settle anything they report on.

## 7. A correction to keep visible

I initially wrote §1 as if "shape, not a name" forbade naming any particular model. That overstates the rule, exactly as the exemplar's own §5b correction records. The README phrase governs a **missing** primitive, one not held in the library. Here nothing is missing: the models, the observations and the three axes are all already in the frame. The correct statement is narrower: the framework returns the shape of the comparison procedure; selecting a particular model or weighting is a binding from the climate frame and must be provenanced as such. The earlier absolute form is preserved here rather than erased.

## 8. Consequence for the answer

The shape is a procedure, not a verdict and not a winner. It is stated in `07_the_tether_answer.md` and checked in `08_expert_verification_packet.md`. Its refuters are written in advance. Whether it works on a real ensemble is a ground question that this derivation cannot answer.
