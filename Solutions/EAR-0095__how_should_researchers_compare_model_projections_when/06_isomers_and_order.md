# Why probability, timing and severity are different substances

**Mode: `specified`.** This is a simulated derivation checked against the figures, not a ground reading (see `05_what_shape_means.md` §6).

## RESIDUAL

The question treats "probability, timing or severity" as interchangeable flavours of one disagreement. The figure set says the arrangement carries meaning the ingredients do not. Two questions are owed:

1. Can the three axes be written as different bond arrangements over the same model outputs, so that they are different substances rather than different spellings of one?
2. Are models from one lineage isomers or functional-group members, and what does that imply for counting witnesses?

## DOMAIN CENSUS

Atoms held: a model projection is an output over a state (region, horizon) with a magnitude. Bonds held: the seven, including symmetric + and ordered →, −, ⋛. No new primitive is available without import. The axes are already in the question, so they are bound terms, not sought shapes.

## DERIVATION

### Q1: The three axes are different bonds over related ingredients

Fig 12 :46: *"A bond is the typed relation between two operands."*

Fig 12 :95-96: *"Two entries with an identical ingredient set are not variants of one thing. They are different things whose notation cannot tell them apart, and the difference is carried entirely by the bond."*

Mapping the question's own terms onto the bond table:

| axis | nearest bond | figure definition | effect |
|---|---|---|---|
| probability | ∥ disjunction | Fig 12 :62 *"either suffices; not both required"* | a measure over which future suffices |
| timing | → sequence | Fig 12 :72 *"A then B; B needs A to have happened"* | an ordering; swapping changes the thing |
| severity | ⋛ comparison or − subtraction | Fig 12 :87 *"a threshold relation between two quantities"*; Fig 12 :82 *"A with B removed or absent"* | a magnitude relative to a baseline or threshold |

These are not three names for one quantity. They are three different typed relations. Under Fig 12 the relation is *"not recoverable from the operands"* (Fig 12 :46). So a single score that records only the operands (the projections) has recorded half of itself. This is the structural reason to keep three accounts, and it is a `specified` transfer because the figure does not itself name probability, timing or severity.

### Q2: Are the axes isomers?

Fig 12 :117: *"Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."*

The chemistry analogue in the figure is concrete and cross-field: *"silver fulminate and silver cyanate have the same composition and different properties, which is what forced structure into the notation"* (Fig 12 :97-98). That is an instance from chemistry, a field with low overlap with climate science, and it is the figure's own evidence.

Applied here:

- Two projections built from the same model atoms but combined with different bonds (one ranged over ∥, one ordered by →) are **isomers** in the figure's sense: same atoms, different substance. They can both be legitimate and still disagree, because the difference lives in the arrangement, not the ingredients.
- The three axes themselves are **not** strictly isomers of one another, because they do not share an identical ingredient set: probability draws on an ensemble of futures, timing on an ordering, severity on a threshold or baseline. The weaker and correct claim is that they are different bond types over overlapping ingredients, which is why they cannot be summed.

**Falsifier, written before the run:** if two axes can be written with the same bond over the same atoms and the resulting term is invariant (same predictions under the same ground), the different-substance claim fails and a single score may be licensed.

### Q3: Models from one lineage are a functional group, not independent isomers

Fig 12 :123: *"functional group. A substructure whose presence fixes the outcome. The group says what kind; the qualifier says which member."*

A family of models sharing a dynamical core, evaluation data and tuning history is a functional group: the shared core says what kind, the parameterisation and resolution say which member. Counting members of a functional group as independent witnesses is the error Fig 2 :40 names. This is a structural reading, and it matches the world literature (model genealogy, effective number of models) that was read in `04_links_and_distribution.md`.

**Falsifier:** if two members of the same functional group can be shown to differ in the bond arrangement that governs the residual at issue, and to arrive at their output by separate routes, they qualify as convergent derivation rather than adopted import and may count separately (Fig 8 :50-52).

### Q4: What order is the union?

Fig 12 :92-93: *"Symmetry is what decides whether an arrangement is a new thing. Under a symmetric bond, k operands have k! spellings and one term."*

An ensemble mean combines models under a symmetric +. k models have k! spellings and one term. So the ensemble is one composed term, not k independent witnesses. Counting the k orderings as k candidates overcharges by log₂(k!) while the bond term undercharges (Fig 12 :93). This is the arithmetic beneath the room-chain warning in Fig 7.

Order by dependency: describe R per slot (Fig 9 :27-28), split the union (Fig 9 :54), establish which bond governs each axis (Q1), decide independence before interpreting agreement (Q3), and only then compose the verdict. The comparison bond ⋛ is reserved for the ground decision, not for merging the three axes into one number (Fig 9 :42).

## CONCLUSION

The three axes are not three flavours of one disagreement. They are different typed relations over overlapping model ingredients, so the difference is carried by the bond and is lost by a flat score. Two models can be isomers (same atoms, different arrangement, different substance) and both legitimate; models from one lineage are better read as a functional group, whose members are not independent witnesses. The ensemble mean is one symmetric term, not k witnesses. This is a `specified`, ungrounded structural claim with two stated falsifiers.
