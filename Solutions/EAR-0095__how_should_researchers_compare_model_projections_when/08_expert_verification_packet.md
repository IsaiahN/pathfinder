# Expert verification packet: comparing disagreeing model projections

**What this is:** a claim in a form a climate modeller or projection-methods researcher can check. Mode, derivation and refuters are stated before the conclusion, so a reader knows what to look for and what would settle it.

**Mode: `specified`, not `grounded`.** No measurement is claimed. This is the shape of a comparison procedure, not a result about any particular ensemble.

## 1. The claim, in one paragraph

A comparison of disagreeing model projections should keep the residual indexed by slot (region, variable, horizon) rather than collapse it to a global average; should unbundle probability, timing and severity into separate loops before combining them; should range each model separately against a fixed observational ground; should discount agreement between models that share lineage, components or evaluation data; should keep the three axes in separate accounts rather than sum them into one score; and should return "unreachable at this budget" with the residual described in effect terms where the current closure cannot resolve a slot. The ensemble mean is a description, not a verdict. The figures license the shape of this procedure; they do not name a winner or settle it against the ground.

## 2. Three checkable sub-claims

### C1: framework-internal; checkable by reading the figure text

- **Statement:** the residual must be indexed per slot, and global averaging loses the signal that says where to look.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 1 :19 *"The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated."* Fig 1 :22 *"Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average."* Fig 1 :28 *"The gap is measured per object, not per step. A system that averages across objects has thrown away the only signal that says where to look."*
- **Refuter:** show a global score that preserves a positive per-slot residual the indexed record would otherwise have lost, on a declared ground.

### C2: methods-facing; checkable by a researcher against model dependence practice

- **Statement:** agreement among models that draw on the same evidence is not corroboration, and adding members from one lineage adds spelling rather than evidence after a point.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 2 :31 *"Independence, between two frames, is how little they draw on the same evidence."* Fig 2 :40 *"agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool"* Fig 7 :35 *"A room's agreement is variation, not a verdict. Selection between rooms is where the ground votes."*
- **What to check:** do the models in your ensemble share components, tuning data or evaluation records? Abramowitz et al. 2019 report there is no universally accepted definition of independence; Pathak et al. 2023 estimate roughly 11 independent models carry the information of 57 CMIP6 models. Does your weighting change the conclusion? Brunner et al. 2020 found a 13 percent narrowing of the likely range under performance and independence weighting in one scenario.
- **Refuter:** show two models with documented shared ancestry, components and evaluation data carrying full independent corroborating weight on the residual at issue.

### C3: structural; checkable against the bond rules and the question's own terms

- **Statement:** probability, timing and severity are different typed relations over overlapping model ingredients, so a single score discards the difference that matters.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 12 :125 *"A composition costs in two currencies, and they do not add."* Fig 12 :130 *"Summing them into one figure is the error this names, not the fix for it. A price in bits and a price in actions are two readings, held apart."* Fig 9 :57 *"a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either. It stops being a mixture and becomes a specific compound."*
- **What to check:** in your decision context, is a one-number score lossless? IPCC AR6 WGI Chapter 10 states in high confidence that multi-model mean and spread are not sufficient where models simulate substantially different or opposing changes, and that storylines aid interpretation.
- **Refuter:** demonstrate a commensuration of the three axes in one score with no decision-relevant loss, on a declared ground. **Declared limit:** the figure names bits and actions, not probability, timing and severity. This sub-claim is a `specified` structural transfer, and an expert is entitled to reject the transfer without rejecting the figure.

## 3. What is explicitly not claimed

- Not that any named model or weighting scheme is wrong or right.
- Not that the ensemble mean is useless. It is demoted from verdict to description.
- Not that all disagreement is resolvable; some slots may be unreachable at this budget.
- Not that the three axes are the figure's own two currencies. See the declared limit in C3.
- Not `grounded`. Nothing here is asserted as settled by experiment.
- Not a claim that the framework can name the winning model. It returns a shape, not a name.

## 5. What would settle it

A ground reading, not agreement. In particular:

1. A per-slot check showing whether the indexed residual finds a live signal the global average hides.
2. An agreed, problem-specific metric for model independence, then a check of whether correlated models carry corroborating weight (the live strain in this solution).
3. A decision test of whether the three axes are losslessly commensurable.
4. A search test distinguishing "unreachable at this budget" from "in the closure and unfound".

## 6. What an expert can check in twenty minutes

1. Read Fig 1 :19, :22, :28, Fig 2 :31, :40, Fig 7 :35 and Fig 12 :125, :130. Eight short passages.
2. Name one case from your own work where a global ensemble mean hid a per-slot failure, or say why none exists.
3. Say whether the models you use share lineage or evaluation data, and whether that changes how much their agreement counts.
4. Say whether probability, timing and severity can be scored together in your application without loss.
5. Send the refutation. A failure is more useful than an endorsement.
