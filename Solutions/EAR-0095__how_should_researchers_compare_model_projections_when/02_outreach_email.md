# Outreach draft: check the comparison rule before you decide about the theory

**Use:** one-to-one, or as a cover letter attached to a submission through the public venues in `03_public_contact_points.csv`. **Not** for bulk send.

**Hard precondition:** do not send until the stage-1 claim survives the reader's own check. If it does not, the two-stage offer collapses on contact.

---

**Subject:** One checkable claim about comparing disagreeing model projections

> Hi [name],
>
> I am working on a small, checkable claim about how researchers should compare model projections when the models disagree about probability, timing or severity of change. I am not asking you to read a theory first, and I am not asking you to agree with anything. If the claim does not survive, there is nothing else to look at.
>
> **The claim (`specified`, not `grounded`):** a comparison should keep the residual indexed by slot (region, variable, horizon) instead of collapsing it to one global number; it should unbundle probability, timing and severity into separate questions before searching; it should range each model separately against the observational record, with that record held fixed; it should estimate how much the models draw on the same evidence before treating their agreement as corroboration; and it should keep the three axes in separate accounts rather than sum them into one score. If a slot cannot be resolved with the current models and instruments, the output should say "unreachable at this budget" with the residual described in effect terms, not a manufactured consensus.
>
> **What this predicts in your field:** an ensemble mean will be a useful description and a poor verdict in exactly the cases where models give opposing changes or a live residual sits in one region. Adding more models from the same lineage will add spelling rather than evidence after a point. A model that reproduces the historical record perfectly may still fail on the projection horizon.
>
> **What I am not claiming:** that any particular model is wrong, that one weighting scheme is the answer, or that this procedure is settled by a measurement. The figures from which this is derived return a shape of a procedure, not a name and not a number.
>
> **What would refute it, written before this message:**
> 1. a single global score that preserves a positive per-slot residual that the indexed record would have lost;
> 2. models with shared ancestry and shared evaluation data shown to carry full independent corroborating weight on the residual at issue;
> 3. the three axes shown to shrink as one question under sustained effort;
> 4. probability, timing and severity shown to be commensurable in one score with no decision-relevant loss;
> 5. an observational anchor that is legitimately updated by model consensus and still settles the question.
>
> If any one holds, I will retract the claim. A one-line "this fails because..." is more useful to me than agreement. If you would rather not engage, no reply is needed.
>
> **If it survives, and only then:** here are the sources I actually read. The figures are the authority, not my summary of them.
> - IPCC AR6 WGI Chapter 10, "Linking Global to Regional Climate Change": https://www.ipcc.ch/report/ar6/wg1/chapter/chapter-10/
> - Abramowitz et al. 2019, "Model dependence in multi-model climate ensembles": https://esd.copernicus.org/articles/10/91/2019/
> - Brunner et al. 2020, "Weighting models by performance and independence": https://esd.copernicus.org/articles/11/995/2020/
>
> **If you want to use the machinery:** the method is in the figures link in the packet. Run it on a comparison you know well and can check against an observational ground. Write down what would refute your result *before* you run it, and state the mode of what you get. A reported failure is more useful to me than a reported success.
>
> [name]

## Handling a reply

1. Record the reader's verdict on stage 1 *before* sending any framework material.
2. A refutation is copied into `11_audit.md` with provenance and re-evaluated by the auditor seat as if it came from any other source.
3. Later agreement is never pooled as corroboration (Fig 2 :40). It is a conversation, not a witness.
