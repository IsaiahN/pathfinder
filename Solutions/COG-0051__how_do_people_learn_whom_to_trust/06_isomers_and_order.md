# Bonds, order, and analogues in trust learning

**Mode: `specified`.** A simulated derivation checked against the figures, not a ground reading (see `05_what_shape_means.md` section 5).

## RESIDUAL

The shape claim says trust learning is per slot, anchored, weighted, habitat-bound, refutable, and composed into reputation. Three questions are owed: (1) which bonds build the reputation molecule, (2) what order the operations run in, and (3) are different institutional regimes **isomers** under Fig 12, or merely instances of one functional group.

## DOMAIN CENSUS (supporting)

Atoms held: trustor, trustee, observed outcome, reputation, enforcement record, prior, private history (states); ordering, gradient, network (structures from Fig 13). Bonds held: the seven plus negation. No new primitive is available without import. Analogues drawn from low-overlap fields are candidates for import, with provenance recorded, not identities.

## DERIVATION

### Q1 — Which bonds build the reputation molecule?

Fig 12 :46 "A bond is the typed relation between two operands. There are seven, they are not interchangeable, and which one holds is not recoverable from the operands."

- **Sequence (→), ordered.** A trusts B, then B may reciprocate. Swapping the operands gives a different thing: B trusting A is a different slot. Fig 12 :71 "A then B; B needs A to have happened."
- **Production (⇒), ordered.** A settled first-order outcome produces reputation; the reputation did not exist before the outcome fired. Fig 12 :76 "A produces B; B did not exist before."
- **Subtraction (−), ordered.** Withholding trust is trust with the exposure removed; progress toward a decision is a subtraction from uncertainty. Operators table: "progress is a subtraction, which is why a vocabulary without it cannot say how far along it is."
- **Comparison (⋛), ordered and threshold.** The decision rules in the world literature are all thresholds: direct reciprocity needs w > c/b, indirect reciprocity needs q > c/b, network reciprocity needs b/c > k. Operators table: "a goal is a comparison, which is why a vocabulary without it cannot state one." A trust decision is a comparison at a threshold, not a sum.
- **Negation (¬), not a bond.** Betrayal refuses a held term. Operators table: "a refutation is a negation, which is why a vocabulary without it can only accumulate." This is the eighth operator and is unary.

A list of "competence plus benevolence plus integrity" is the flat-formula error Fig 12 names: "A recipe that names only its ingredients has recorded half of itself." The arrangement is the substance.

**Falsifier for Q1:** if the decision can be written as one additive score with no threshold, no ordering, and no refusal, the bond analysis fails and the flat form stands.

### Q2 — Are kinship trust and institution-backed trust isomers?

Fig 12 :117 "isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."

- Take the full shared atom set: trustor, trustee, observed outcome, reputation, enforcement record, prior, private history.
- Under kinship regime, enforcement runs through repeated direct contact and in-group membership: mostly sequence and cycle.
- Under institution-backed regime, enforcement runs through a record neither party can move: production of reputation plus comparison against an external anchor.
- Same atoms, different arrangement, different behaviour. By Fig 12 :117 they are **isomers**, not two strengths of one substance.

Fig 12 :124 "functional group. A substructure whose presence fixes the outcome. The group says what kind; the qualifier says which member." Both regimes share the functional group `trustor → structure → trustee` with a comparison at the threshold, so a weaker form of the claim is that they are members of one functional group with different qualifiers.

The world record is consistent in effect terms: low-trust societies are "typically kinship-based" and may lack "a trusted civil court system" (Wikipedia, High-trust and low-trust societies). That is a bond difference, not a missing atom.

**Falsifier for Q2, written before the run:** if one regime can be written without an atom the other requires (for example, institution-backed trust with no reputation atom, or kinship trust with no prior), the isomer claim fails and the functional-group reading stands instead. If both regimes compose identically under the same bonds, both readings fail and they are one regime under two names.

### Q3 — What order do the operations run in?

Order is by dependency, not importance (Fig 3 :20 "The chain is ordered by dependency, not by importance."):

1. Perceive the outcome for this slot. Fig 3 :5 "the system can tell what is in front of it, and tell it apart from what was there before."
2. Have terms for what the trustee affords. Fig 3 :7 "it has terms for what things afford: what can be moved, opened, joined, blocked."
3. Say what counts as progress here, in the learner's own terms. Fig 3 :10 "it can say what would count as progress here, in its own terms."
4. Choose whether to extend trust, check first, or withhold.
5. Name what worked, verify it, and carry it to the next interaction. Fig 3 :15 "name what worked, verify it, and carry it to the next problem."

Two prior orders matter:

- **Refuse before mint** (Formula step 2): when a held trust term is contradicted, the residual is REFUTED and a negation is owed first; an explanation is not owed. Minting a new positive term over an unrefused one reverses the order.
- **Describe before frame** (Fig 9 :27 "describe it before you go looking, or any frame you pick will seem to fit"): characterize the per-slot residual in effect terms before selecting a literature or a discipline. Otherwise any field seems to fit.

**Falsifier for Q3:** if a learner can carry a verified term to the next interaction without first saying what counts as progress in the new habitat, the dependency order is wrong.

### Q4 — Cycle, catalyst, and the second-order step

Fig 12 :121 "cycle. The output re-enters as input. The sign of the loop decides whether it closes, and a chain cannot close by construction." Repeated interaction closes the trust loop; the world analogue is direct reciprocity with w > c/b. A one-shot trust game is a chain, not a cycle.

Fig 12 :119 "catalyst. A part that survives its own use. Spent and regenerating parts behave differently across uses, and nothing records which a term is." Institutions and reputational ledgers are catalyst-like: they survive the transactions they validate, which is why they are worth building and why a corrupted one is so costly.

Fig 12 :104 "A settled molecule becomes an atom for whatever composes over it. So irreducible is a fact about a level and never about the world." Once a first-order outcome is settled, it becomes reputation, an atom at the next level. This is recursion, not import (Fig 6 :21 "MINT composes inside the closure and can never add an atom").

**Falsifier for Q4:** show reputation operating without any settled first-order outcome, or a catalyst that is spent by its first use and still validates the second.

## CONCLUSION

Trust learning is built from ordered bonds: sequence for directed trust, production for reputation, subtraction for withholding and progress, comparison for the threshold decision, and negation for betrayal, with cycle and catalyst as structures. Kinship and institution-backed regimes are isomers over a shared atom set, or at minimum members of one functional group; the stronger claim has a stated falsifier. The operations run in dependency order with refusal before mint and description before frame. This rescues what is structural in the shape without naming a cognitive algorithm or importing a new primitive.
