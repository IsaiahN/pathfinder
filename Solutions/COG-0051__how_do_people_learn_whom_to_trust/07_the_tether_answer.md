# The Tether's answer on learning whom to trust

**Mode: `general` on the shape, `specified` on the per-domain predictions, not `grounded`.** A simulated derivation checked against the figures (`05_what_shape_means.md` section 5), not a ground reading.

## 1. The residual it answers

A learner must decide, separately for each candidate trustee, whether to extend trust. The gap is between what the learner's belief predicted and what the trustee actually did, indexed by candidate. Because networks and institutions change the habitat, the same decision rule does not transport unchanged. The question is a union compound of per-slot learning, a reputation cascade, and an institutional habitat, not one hard problem (Fig 9 :54 "a bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones"). Each domain gets its own loop and its own verdict.

## 2. The shape, per domain

**Domain A, per-slot learning.** Trust is not one global level. Fig 1 :19 "The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated." Fig 1 :22 "Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average." The anchor is what the trustee did, not the number of peers who endorse them: Fig 2 :29 "An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks." The weighting w between private history and inherited prior is learnable (Symbols table), and that is the axis along which networks and institutions differ. Betrayal is a refusal, not an accumulation: Operators table, "a refutation is a negation, which is why a vocabulary without it can only accumulate."

**Domain B, network and reputation.** Endorsement is evidence only at independence. Fig 2 :31 "Independence, between two frames, is how little they draw on the same evidence." Fig 2 :39 "Correlation has valence." Fig 2 :40 "disqualifying for a verdict: agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool." Fig 2 :41 "efficient for a convention: agreement about a shared meaning is the product, not a corruption of it." So a closed network produces a convention, not a verdict on trustworthiness. Reputation is a second-order atom: Fig 12 :104 "A settled molecule becomes an atom for whatever composes over it. So irreducible is a fact about a level and never about the world." A closed frame cannot add a reason to trust by internal search alone; Fig 6 :23 "No frame escapes its own closure. The bound moves only on contact with a frame whose closure differs." Imports are debited against independence (Fig 6 :24).

**Domain C, institutions and habitat.** Trustworthiness is not carried by the person. Fig 11 :49 "An actor's valence is relative to the residual, not a property it carries." Fig 11 :51 "No standing list of good actors survives a change of residual." Fig 11 :43 "The habitat is enumerated, never composed." Fig 11 :52 "contact cascades", so the relevant habitat reaches beyond direct partners to observers and institutions. Institutions matter as instruments at the middle of the dependency chain: Fig 3 :27 "A link with no instrument is not a link that works. It is a link nobody has looked at." A court that rules on evidence neither party can move is an anchor in ordinary language.

**Domain D, verification.** Fig 10 :42 "A verifier that can reconstruct the claim cannot verify it." Fig 2 :35 "Alignment is triangulation, not negotiation. A mirror cannot correct you." In this simulated run all seats share one evidence pool, so agreement is internal consistency, not corroboration.

Verdicts stay separate. They are never averaged into a single "trust level."

## 3. What it explicitly does not say

- Not a cognitive algorithm. The mechanism is a `gap` at this budget, stated as such, not hidden.
- Not a ranking of societies by virtue. A convention is efficient for shared meaning; that is a product, not a moral score.
- Not a claim that reputation is always worthless. It is worthless as a verdict when frames share a pool and useful as a coordinate for action when provenance is shown.
- Not grounded. No measurement here settles the empirical question of exactly how humans perform this learning.
- Not a derivation of one institution from another. Kinship and court-backed trust are isomers over a shared atom set (`06_isomers_and_order.md`), with a stated falsifier.

## 4. What would settle it: the ground

A source-discriminating instrument, not more agreement. From *The Hard Problem is About Source*, a colour instrument "cannot comment on the source", and the question was closed "not by argument, but by the arrival of a better instrument." Here the corresponding questions are:

- Does a per-slot residual carry predictive signal that a global trust score loses? A within-person design where the same person rates many specific trustees can test Fig 1 :19/:22 directly.
- Does independence change what agreement means? Compare two groups that agree about a trustee from the same report against two groups that agree from separate readings of the trustee's behaviour (Fig 2 :40).
- Does the same actor's trust rating change with habitat and enforcement, holding the actor fixed (Fig 11 :49-51)?
- Does recording a betrayal as a refusal track later behaviour better than averaging it into a score (Operators table negation entry)?
- Does second-order and institutional contact change a decision that first-order contact alone does not (Fig 11 :43-44)?

Standing falsifiers are in `01_figure_grounded_audit.md` section 4.

## 5. The honest stop

**Unreached, not unreachable.** Fig 8 :39 "So a search that finds nothing is never a proof of absence." Fig 9 :44 "Only a sealed room can be searched to the end, and no frame is a sealed room. So searching and finding nothing proves nothing." The framework returns a shape, not a name: Fig 9 :32 "you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself." The six-term substrate is a visible guess, not nature's own list; declaring it makes the choice visible, not neutral. We stop at the limit of the available instruments and record the lineage.

## 6. One-paragraph version for outreach

> The Tether does not produce one trust score, and it does not name the brain mechanism. It says that "whom to trust" is a per-person calculation: a global average throws away the signal. It says the anchor is what the trustee actually did, so a network that repeats one report has a convention, not corroboration; agreement about shared meaning is a real product, but it is not evidence about the world. It says the weighting between your own experience and inherited institutional prior is learnable, and that is what differs when networks and institutions differ. It says trustworthiness is a relation between a person and a habitat, not a property the person carries, so the same individual can be the right trustee under one set of rules and the wrong one under another. Betrayal is a refusal of a held term, not one more low observation. What it offers is that shape, refuters written in advance, and the instruments that would close it. The mechanism stays open until the ground settles it.
