# Expert verification packet: the Tether on learning whom to trust

**What this is:** a claim in a form a trust or social-cognition researcher can check. Mode, derivation and refuters are stated before the conclusion, so a reader knows what to look for and what would settle it.

**Mode: `general` on the structural shape, `specified` on the domain predictions, not `grounded`.** No measurement is claimed. This is a shape and a route, not a result.

## 1. The claim, in one paragraph

Learning whom to trust is a per-candidate calculation, not the formation of one global trust level. The anchor is what the trustee actually did, so agreement among frames that share an evidence pool is a convention about meaning, not a verdict about trustworthiness. The weighting between private experience and an inherited institutional prior is learnable and is the axis on which networks and institutions differ. Trustworthiness is a valence relative to the learner's residual and habitat, not a property carried by the person; betrayal is a refusal of a held term, not another observation to average in. Reputation is a second-order atom built from settled first-order outcomes, and a closed frame can only acquire a new reason to trust by contact with a frame whose closure differs. The framework offers that shape, refuters written in advance, and the instruments that would close it. It does not name the cognitive algorithm.

## 2. Three checkable sub-claims

### C1 — the per-slot claim (framework-internal, checkable by reading Figure 1)

- **Statement:** for each candidate trustee the residual is indexed by that candidate; aggregating across candidates into one scalar discards the signal the question asks for.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 1 :19 "The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated." Fig 1 :22 "Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average."
- **Prediction:** within-person trust judgments across many specific targets must carry structure that the same person's single "generalized trust" score does not.
- **Refuter:** show a trust decision where the residual is genuinely one scalar, the per-target structure carries no predictive signal, and aggregation loses nothing.

### C2 — the anchor-and-independence claim (checkable by a trust researcher)

- **Statement:** the anchor is revealed behaviour, not endorsement. The valence of correlation matters: agreement from a shared pool is disqualifying for a verdict and efficient for a convention.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 2 :31 "Independence, between two frames, is how little they draw on the same evidence." Fig 2 :40 "disqualifying for a verdict: agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool" Fig 2 :41 "efficient for a convention: agreement about a shared meaning is the product, not a corruption of it" And Fig 2 :35 "Alignment is triangulation, not negotiation. A mirror cannot correct you."
- **Prediction:** two groups that agree about a trustee from separate readings of that trustee's behaviour carry information; two groups that agree from one shared report do not, even when the reported agreement level is identical.
- **Refuter:** exhibit a case where a network agrees from strictly independent readings and independence changes nothing about what the agreement means.

### C3 — the habitat-and-instrument claim (checkable by a social or institutional researcher)

- **Statement:** trustworthiness is relative to the residual and habitat, not carried by the actor; institutions matter as instruments at the link where a trust system must say what counts as progress.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 11 :49 "An actor's valence is relative to the residual, not a property it carries." Fig 11 :51 "No standing list of good actors survives a change of residual." Fig 3 :27 "A link with no instrument is not a link that works. It is a link nobody has looked at."
- **Prediction:** holding the target actor fixed, trust ratings change when the enforcement regime changes; and adding an instrument at the vocabulary/objective link changes the decision where adding exhortation does not.
- **Refuter:** show the same actor rated identically under two habitats with different residuals and enforcement and no change in contact, or a system that builds correct trust with no instrument at that link where success there was measured and not assumed.

## 3. What is explicitly not claimed

- Not a cognitive algorithm or a brain mechanism. That is a `gap`, stated as such.
- Not a single trust score, and not a derivation of one from many.
- Not that reputation is always worthless. It is worthless as a verdict when the frames share a pool, and useful as a coordinate for action when provenance is shown.
- Not a moral ranking of high-trust versus low-trust societies. A convention is efficient for shared meaning; that is not a virtue score.
- Not `grounded`. Nothing here is asserted as settled by experiment.
- Not a claim that the world sources are independent witnesses. They are one encyclopaedia pool, declared as such.

## 5. What would settle it

A source-discriminating instrument, not further agreement. From *The Hard Problem is About Source*: an instrument that reads green "cannot comment on the source"; the case was closed "not by argument, but by the arrival of a better instrument." Here:

1. A within-person design testing whether per-target residuals predict later trust better than a global score (C1).
2. A design separating shared-pool agreement from independent-reading agreement about the same trustee (C2).
3. A habitat-shift design holding the actor fixed while changing enforcement, with an instrument at the vocabulary/objective link (C3).
4. A betrayal design testing whether recording a refusal predicts later behaviour better than averaging (supporting claim).

## 6. What an expert can check in twenty minutes

1. Read Fig 1 :19/:22, Fig 2 :31/:35/:40/:41, Fig 11 :49/:51 and Fig 3 :27, eight short passages.
2. Check C2 against what you know of reputation and corroboration: does a shared report really tell you nothing new about a trustee?
3. Name one instrument that would distinguish shared-pool agreement from independent agreement in your own data, or say why none is possible.
4. Send the refutation. A failure is more useful than an endorsement.
