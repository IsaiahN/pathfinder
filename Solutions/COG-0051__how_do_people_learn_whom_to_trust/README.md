## The residual stated as an effect

A learner faces many candidate trustees, one per object slot. For each slot the learner holds a belief about trustworthiness built from private history and an inherited prior (weight w), extends or withholds trust (action a), observes what the trustee actually did (o'), and registers a residual R = |Γ(b,a) minus o'| for that slot. The question asks what governs the per-slot decision rule as the habitat changes: network structure, reputation cascades, and the institutions that make outcomes visible and enforceable. What an answer would look like is a shape: the invariants any trust-learning system must satisfy, with the slots marked where the framework cannot name a mechanism. That sought-for shape is written down here as an ordinary term so it can accrue a residual and be refused (Formula step "Direction").

## What is deliberately not here

- No personal emails of named individuals.
- No ranking of people by how likely they are to believe the claim.
- No bulk-send plan.

Three reasons, in the figures' own words:

1. **A room does not upgrade the label.** Seat Map :283: "A room reaching a conclusion without the ground is agreement, and agreement among frames is a weighted average of influence. Adding people raises the vocabulary and does not add an anchor."
2. **Correlation disqualifies a verdict.** Seat Map :163: "High correlation is disqualifying for a verdict and efficient for a convention." A recipient list selected for receptiveness manufactures exactly the correlation that makes agreement worthless.
3. **Asking for trust before checking spends independence.** Fig 10 :42 "A verifier that can reconstruct the claim cannot verify it." Seat 4 must not hold the interiors.

Legally: unsolicited bulk email to harvested personal addresses breaches GDPR and CAN-SPAM style rules in many jurisdictions and gets the sending domain blacklisted. Public opt-in venues avoid that practical failure as well as the structural one.
