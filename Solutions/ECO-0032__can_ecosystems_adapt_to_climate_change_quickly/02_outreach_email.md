# Outreach draft: check the residual before you decide whether the framing is worth reading

**Use:** one to one, or as a cover note attached to a submission through the public venues in `03_public_contact_points.csv`. **Not** for bulk send.

**Hard precondition:** do not send until the habitat/niche mapping in `01_figure_grounded_audit.md` §6 is understood as retracted. The first contact must not imply that the framework's "habitat" is the ecological niche. That mapping is explicitly declined by the source.

---

**Subject:** One output to check about ecosystem adaptation rates

> Hi [name],
>
> I have one specific output I would like you to check against what you already know. I am not asking you to read a theory first, and I am not asking you to agree with it. If the output does not survive, there is nothing else to look at.
>
> **The residual (`specified`, not `grounded`):** "Can ecosystems adapt to climate change quickly enough through migration, evolution, or shifts in species composition?" asks for a comparison between two rates, the rate at which the climatic ground moves and the rate at which a response can track it. Migration, evolution and composition shift are not three alternative answers. They overlap: moving populations change composition, changed composition alters selection, and evolved dispersal changes migration. So the question is a union of loops, not one scalar. The figures say the residual is per object slot and must not be aggregated: a global percentage can be near zero while one interaction is failing.
>
> **The shape I would like checked:** an answer is a per-slot rate comparison between ground velocity and one named response velocity, with the habitat contact enumerated in actors, conditions and relations, introduced actors identified, and the persistence reading kept separate from the mechanism claimed to have produced it. The comparison is a threshold relation between two quantities, not a count of species and not an average.
>
> **What the figures do not supply:** they do not contain a climatic velocity, a dispersal rate, an additive variance or a turnover threshold. They say what the answer must look like and that the ground must settle it. So the empirical yes or no is recorded here as unreached, not as "no" and not as silence.
>
> **What would refute the shape:**
> 1. one global adaptation rate that predicts persistence as well as the per-slot comparison under every reweighting;
> 2. a range shift in which the old habitat is genuinely removed as a condition set, with no substituted habitat and no inherited actors;
> 3. a maintained intervention that excludes an unwanted actor rather than adding one, while the habitat stays the same field of contact;
> 4. a lineage increasing fitness with zero additive variance and unchanged contact;
> 5. a numeric threshold derived from the framework alone, with no imported measurement.
>
> I wrote these down before sending this. If any holds, I will retract the shape. A one-line "this fails because..." is more useful to me than agreement. If you would rather not engage, no reply is needed.
>
> **If you have a verdict, I would like it recorded before any theory is shown.** Then, and only if you are curious, the figures and the formula are below. The figures are the authority, not my summary of them.
> - Verbatim figure text with line numbers (local extract): `FRAMEWORK/reference/03-FIGURES_TEXT.md` in the project. Fig 11 covers habitat, contact, introduction and isolation; Fig 1 covers per-slot residuals.
> - Canonical figures README: https://github.com/IsaiahN/tether/blob/general-agent/figures/README.md
> - Arrival of the Fittest, the architecture and the formula: https://adventuresinml.substack.com/p/arrival-of-the-fittest-the-tether
>
> **If you want to reuse the machinery:** take the shape claim above, write down what would refute your own result before you run it, and state whether your output is general, specified or grounded. I am interested in failures, not endorsements.
