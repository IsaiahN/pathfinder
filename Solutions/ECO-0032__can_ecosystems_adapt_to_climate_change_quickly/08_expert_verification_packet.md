# Expert verification packet: the Tether on ecosystem adaptation rates

**What this is:** a claim in a form an ecologist or evolutionary biologist can check. Mode, derivation and refuters are stated before the conclusion, so a reader knows what to look for and what would settle it.

**Mode: `specified`, not `grounded`.** No ecological rate is claimed. This is a shape and a route, not a result.

## 1. The claim, in one paragraph

"Can ecosystems adapt to climate change quickly enough through migration, evolution, or shifts in species composition?" is not one scalar question. It is a union of overlapping loops, and the residual must be indexed per slot, a population, a range edge, an interaction or a management unit. The sought-for quantity is an ordered threshold comparison between the velocity of the climatic ground and the velocity of one named response. Unmaintained persistence is selection's output and does not identify the mechanism that produced it. Migration substitutes a habitat and inherits actors the mover did not choose. Managed composition work is permanent because introduction is available and exclusion is not. The figures give this shape and the ground must supply the rates. The empirical yes or no is therefore unreached by this derivation, not answered no.

## 2. Three checkable sub-claims

### C1: measurement structure; checkable by any ecologist against their own data

- **Statement:** a global adaptation percentage is a different question from the per-slot comparison, because a global residual near zero with one live slot is a legal state.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 1 :19 "The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated." Fig 1 :22 "Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average." Fig 9 :60 "Use filters for the budget and witnesses for the verdict. Never let a filter hand you a verdict."
- **What to check:** take any published adaptation rate. Does it preserve the ranking of live slots under reweighting? If not, the aggregate has hidden the live signal.
- **Refuter:** produce one aggregate statistic that predicts persistence as well as the per-slot comparison under every reweighting. That breaks C1.

### C2: mechanism structure; checkable by an ecologist or evolutionary biologist

- **Statement:** migration is substitution of a habitat, not removal of one, with two failure modes that are invisible until they act; unmaintained persistence is selection running, not an agent adapting.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 11 :66 "Isolation is not removal of the habitat. It is substitution of one habitat for another." Fig 11 :67 "Move the residual and you must reproduce everything its goal required, and you inherit the new habitat's actors, which you did not choose." Fig 11 :36 "Where nothing maintains anything, intelligence is a label applied afterwards to a lineage that met its ground." Fig 7 :35 "A room's agreement is variation, not a verdict. Selection between rooms is where the ground votes."
- **What to check:** in a range shift, does the recipient site reproduce the conditions and relations the source had, and which actors arrived unchosen? In a persistence study, can the mechanism be discriminated from the reading, or is it named from the reading alone?
- **Refuter:** exhibit a move with no substituted habitat and no inherited actors, or show the old habitat removed as a condition set. That breaks the substitution claim.

### C3: intervention structure; checkable by a conservation practitioner

- **Statement:** in the maintained mode the seat can introduce and cannot exclude, so managed adaptation is permanent work rather than a solved state.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 11 :61 "The seat's power is asymmetric. Introduction is available; exclusion is not." Fig 11 :63 "Which is why a managed ecosystem is permanent work rather than a solved state." Fig 11 :49 "An actor's valence is relative to the residual, not a property it carries."
- **What to check:** name an intervention that removed an actor rather than adding or changing one, and say what habitat remained. Check whether the introduced actor's valence flipped when the goal changed.
- **Refuter:** show a maintained intervention that excludes an unwanted actor while the habitat stays the same field of contact. That breaks the asymmetry claim.

## 3. What is explicitly not claimed

- Not yes, not no. The empirical verdict is `gap`, unreached by figures alone.

## 5. What would settle it

A source-discriminating measurement, not a further argument.

1. **Rate test:** both velocities reported in comparable units for the same named slot, under one protocol.
2. **Source test:** telemetry, genetics or turnover partitioning that discriminates migration from selection from replacement as the source of a persistence reading.
3. **Threshold test:** an instrumented criterion for "enough", with the person entitled to declare it named. Without it the objective link stays broken (Fig 3 :27).
4. **Introduction test:** in a managed case, which actors came with the substituted habitat and were not chosen?

## 6. What an expert can check in twenty minutes

1. Read Fig 1 :19, :22, Fig 11 :36, :61, :63, :66, :67 and Fig 7 :35. Eight short passages.
2. Check C2 against one range-shift study you know: was the shift a substitution with inherited actors?
3. Check C1 against one synthesis you know: does its headline rate preserve live slots under reweighting?
4. Name one instrument that would discriminate the three mechanisms from a persistence reading, or say why none is possible.
5. Send the refutation. A failure is more useful than an endorsement.
