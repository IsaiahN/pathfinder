## 0. The residual under audit

> **Question:** Can ecosystems adapt to climate change quickly enough through migration, evolution, or shifts in species composition?

Stated in effect terms: two things move. The climatic ground moves (temperature, rainfall, extremes, season). An ecosystem's possible responses move too (populations disperse, allele frequencies change, species are added or lost from an assemblage). "Quickly enough" names a comparison between those two movements. It is not a property of the climate alone and not a property of the ecosystem alone.

The residual is indexed by slot, not by ecosystem average. Fig 1 :19 "The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated." Fig 1 :22 "Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average." A slot here is a population, a range edge, an interaction, or a management unit. For each slot s: `R_s = |Γ(b,a)_s − o'_s|`, the predicted persistence under a response versus the persistence actually observed after the ground moved. The comparison that answers the question is a threshold relation between two rates, not a count of species and not a mean.

**The sought-for shape, made an ordinary term before the run** (Formula, "Direction"). An answer to this question is: a per-slot rate comparison, between the velocity of the climatic ground and the velocity of one named response mechanism, with (i) the habitat contact enumerated in actors, conditions and relations, (ii) every introduced actor identified, (iii) the mechanism that produced a persistence reading kept distinct from the reading itself, and (iv) the ground's verdict stated separately from the model's verdict. This shape is bound here and is allowed to accrue a residual like any other term, so it can be refused by the same test as everything else.

**What each option leads to.** Option A, a single global yes or no, spends the live slot in an average and asserts a settlement no instrument in this folder made. Option B, the unbundled per-slot shape, is checkable by an outside frame and stays open at the ground.

## 1. Domain census (before choosing any figure line)

### R by arity, symmetry and scale

- **Arity:** four things are in contact, one moving driver plus three named response mechanisms. Migration changes composition; composition change alters selection; evolution alters dispersal propensity. These overlap, so this is a union, not a list of separate questions. Fig 9 :54 "And when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again." Fig 9 :57 "a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either".
- **Symmetry:** the comparison is not symmetric. Swapping the ground's rate and the response's rate does not leave the same question: "too fast" and "too slow" are not the same failure. The bond is the ordered threshold comparison ⋛, Operators table :48 "a threshold relation between two quantities", not the symmetric conjunction +.
- **Scale:** the ground moves at regional to continental scale over decades; the responses occur per population, per generation, per interaction. Different scales imply a membrane and a round trip (Fig 4), and a global number cannot carry what the slot-level reading holds (Fig 1 :22).

### One line per domain

| # | domain | actors | currency | anchor (nameable, or not yet a domain) | question type |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| D1 | unmaintained habitat, wild systems with no seat | populations, species, conditions (light, water, temperature, season, terrain), relations (pollination, predation, competition, dependency) | persistence, births, deaths, dispersal events | measured persistence against the environmental filter; the ground does not update on what a model thinks | union member, pure contact |
| D2 | migration and range shift | dispersers, corridors, range edges, recipient ecosystems, climate velocity | distance per time versus climate velocity per time | observed range boundaries and measured climate velocity | union member, habitat substitution |
| D3 | evolution within populations | heritable variants, generation time, selection pressures | rate of change in mean fitness, additive variance | allele-frequency and fitness measurements | union member, selection between rooms |
| D4 | species-composition shift | species pool, introductions, disturbances, and a manager only if maintained | turnover, cascade effects, introductions | measured composition and function; the ground still decides | union member with a maintained/unmaintained split |
| D5 | measurement and verification frame | ecologists, monitoring networks, models, instruments | residual per slot between predicted and observed rates | observed persistence and rates, never agreement among models | the frame that ranges against the other four |

Every domain has a nameable anchor. D5 is a frame, not a fifth mechanism, but it is kept separate because its anchor rule governs what counts as a verdict. No domain is admitted without an anchor.

**Partition or union.** A union of D1 to D4 under D5. The overlaps are themselves compounds with their own loops (Fig 9 :58 "It stops being a mixture and becomes a specific compound"), so the union must be unbundled before any search. Fig 9 :4 "First: is there a fact here?" and :5 "are not one hard question but several well-formed ones. Split it rather than search."

**Order by dependency (Fig 3).** 1 perception: measure the two rates per slot. 2 vocabulary: have terms for what a habitat affords and what dispersal, variance and turnover can do. 3 objective: say what would count as "enough" in the system's own terms. 4 planning: choose which mechanism and which monitoring route. 5 learn and carry: name what worked, verify it, carry it to the next site. The chain is ordered by dependency, not importance; effort spent above a break cannot rescue a link below it (Fig 3 :20). The suspected break here is between 2 and 3, because the term "quickly enough" is usually assumed rather than measured (Fig 3 :24 "Why the middle link is where chains usually break.", :27 "A link with no instrument is not a link that works. It is a link nobody has looked at.").

**Strain test applied during the census.** Reading Fig 11's "habitat" as the ecologists' habitat or niche makes the figure strain: the canonical article explicitly lists "The habitat as an ecological niche" under "Three mappings offered and declined" and says "A habitat here is defined by contact with a residual". Re-census: Fig 11's habitat is a field of contact with a residual, the ecological habitat is a set of actors and conditions in the world. Different objects, so the line governs the contact structure and not the ecological place. The naive mapping is retracted in §6, not smoothed over.

## 3. Findings

### F1: The question is a union compound, not one hard question

Fig 9 :5 "are not one hard question but several well-formed ones. Split it rather than search." Fig 9 :54 "And when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again." Fig 9 :57 "a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either".

Migration, evolution and composition shift are not three alternative answers to one question. A migrating population changes the composition of both the source and the recipient; a changed composition alters selection; evolved dispersal alters migration. Each overlap is a specific compound with its own loop and its own ground. A single yes or no applied to the bundle is a search for a fact that was never constituted. Mode `general`, triangulated across Fig 9 and Fig 8 (low overlap: Fig 9 governs the split, Fig 8 governs why an undescribed union is mute).

**Refuter:** show that the three mechanisms can be measured by one scalar that predicts persistence equally well in every pairwise overlap, without loss. If so, the union reading fails and a single loop governs.

### F2: "Quickly enough" is a comparison the figures can state but not fill

Fig 12 :112 "A plan is a sequence. A goal is a comparison." Operators table :51 "a goal is a comparison, which is why a vocabulary without it cannot state one" and :48 "a threshold relation between two quantities". Fig 9 :32 "you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself".

The framework can write the comparison `rate_of_ground ⋛ rate_of_response` and can say which bond it is. It cannot supply either number, and it cannot declare a threshold. The figures return a shape, not a name, and certainly not a rate. The canonical README says the framework returns *"what a missing thing would have to look like (a shape, not a name)"*. Mode `specified` for the bond type, `general` for the limit.

**Refuter:** derive a numeric rate or a universal threshold from the figures alone, with no measurement imported. If that succeeds, F2 is wrong.

### F3: In unmaintained systems adaptation is selection running, not an agent adapting

Fig 11 :36 "Where nothing maintains anything, intelligence is a label applied afterwards to a lineage that met its ground. Nothing was intelligent; selection ran." Fig 11 :56 "Unmaintained. No maintainer and no live frame in the seat. The habitat is pure contact, nothing selects, and what survives is what the ground kept." Fig 7 :35 "A room's agreement is variation, not a verdict. Selection between rooms is where the ground votes."

In D1 the word "adapt" names an outcome applied after the fact. The ground (the environmental filter) keeps what it keeps. There is no seat choosing the response and no frame being credited. This is not a denial that evolution occurs; it is a refusal to treat persistence as a term earned by an agent. Mode `general`, triangulated across Fig 11 and Fig 7 with low overlap (habitat contact versus handoff selection).

**Refuter:** show a population persisting through a mechanism that changes the ground's own verdict without changing contact. That would break Fig 11 :40 "An improvement that does not change contact changes nothing, however much it improves."

### F4: Migration is substitution of a habitat, not removal of one

Fig 11 :66 "Isolation is not removal of the habitat. It is substitution of one habitat for another." Fig 11 :67 "Move the residual and you must reproduce everything its goal required, and you inherit the new habitat's actors, which you did not choose." Fig 11 :68 "what you failed to reproduce is invisible until the goal fails" and :69 "what you unintentionally introduced is invisible until it acts".

Migration as an adaptation mechanism is a change of habitat, not an escape from one. Two failure modes are structurally invisible in advance: conditions the target habitat does not reproduce, and actors the target habitat brings that were not chosen. This is why a range shift is not a clean fix and why the question cannot be settled by distance moved alone. Mode `specified`, single-source for the figure claim (Fig 11 only). World corroboration from Rubenstein et al. 2023 is secondary (see §9).

**Refuter:** exhibit a case where a species moves and the old habitat is genuinely removed as a condition set, with no substituted habitat and no inherited actors. Fig 11 :66 would then be wrong.

### F5: Evolution's rate is variance and generation limited, and the ground still decides

Fig 11 :40 "A system cannot be made more capable by improving the agent in isolation. An improvement that does not change contact changes nothing, however much it improves." Fig 7 :37 "Progress per handoff has two factors. Zero either and the chain runs in place:" with :38 "the bracket's fidelity across the handoff" and :39 "the ground's pressure on the room".

Selection can only spend the variance it has, and each generation is a handoff whose progress needs both fidelity (heritable transmission) and ground pressure. If either factor is zero, the lineage runs in place. The figures therefore license the shape of a rate limit, not the value. Mode `general` for the two-factor shape, `specified` for its application to D3; triangulated across Fig 11 and Fig 7.

**Refuter:** show a lineage increasing fitness under selection with zero additive variance and no change of contact. That breaks the two-factor claim and the world corroboration (Fisher's theorem) together.

### F6: Composition shifts are asymmetric work in the maintained mode

Fig 11 :61 "The seat's power is asymmetric. Introduction is available; exclusion is not." Fig 11 :63 "Which is why a managed ecosystem is permanent work rather than a solved state." Fig 11 :49 "An actor's valence is relative to the residual, not a property it carries." Fig 11 :52 "And contact cascades. Scoping the habitat to direct contact is how DDT reached the eagles: first-order enumeration, second-order effect, and the residual that mattered was three steps out."

Where a manager exists (assisted migration, restoration, rewilding), the response is work on a habitat the manager did not author. The manager can add an actor and cannot subtract the habitat. So a managed ecosystem cannot be "adapted" and finished; it is permanently maintained. The valence of an introduced actor flips with the residual, so no standing list of good species survives a change of question. Mode `specified`, single-source for the figure claim (Fig 11 only).

**Refuter:** show a maintained intervention that excludes an unwanted actor from a habitat rather than adding or changing one, with the habitat remaining the same field of contact. That breaks Fig 11 :61.

### F7: The verdict is per slot, and a global average is a different question

Fig 1 :19 "The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated." Fig 1 :22 "Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average." Fig 9 :60 "Use filters for the budget and witnesses for the verdict. Never let a filter hand you a verdict."

A single percentage of species "keeping pace" mixes range edges, long-lived trees, pollinators and microbes into one number. Fig 1 :20 says a global residual near zero with one live slot is a legal state and not an inert one. So an aggregate can look settled while a particular interaction is failing. A rate threshold used to allocate monitoring budget is a filter, never a witness. Mode `general`, triangulated across Fig 1 and Fig 9 (per-slot measurement versus budget/verdict separation).

**Refuter:** produce one aggregate statistic that preserves the ranking of live slots under every reweighting. If it exists, the per-slot requirement is an overconstraint.

### F8: The likely break is the objective link, not the data link

Fig 3 :24 "Why the middle link is where chains usually break." Fig 3 :27 "A link with no instrument is not a link that works. It is a link nobody has looked at." Fig 3 :19 "Optimising a link that already works cannot rescue one that does not."

Ecology has abundant perception (monitoring, remote sensing, palaeo records) and abundant vocabulary (dispersal kernels, heritability, turnover). What is often missing is a stated objective: what rate difference would count as "enough" for this slot, and who is entitled to declare it. Most effort lands above the break because that is where measurements already exist (Fig 3 :30). Mode `specified`, single-source for the figure claim (Fig 3 only); the world literature corroborates the diagnosis without deciding it.

**Refuter:** show a stated, instrumented threshold for "enough" that is used consistently across taxa and ecoregions and that settles a real case. Then the break is elsewhere, and F8 is wrong.

### F9: The empirical verdict is `gap`/unreached, and that is a verdict

Fig 8 :39 "So a search that finds nothing is never a proof of absence." Fig 13 :134 "Unreachable is a verdict here, not a silence,". Fig 2 :29 "An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks." Fig 8 :32 "no search, however long, adds an atom".

No figure line contains a climatic velocity, a dispersal rate, an additive variance or a turnover threshold. Composing the six-term set forever cannot produce them, because closure is idempotent. The empirical yes/no is therefore unreached by this method, not silently omitted and not answered "no". What is owed is an import from the ground: measurements, imported with a source gate and a shadow test, debited against independence. Mode `general` for the limit, `specified` for the claim that this particular budget cannot close it.

**Refuter:** an instrumented per-slot comparison that closes `R_s` for a named system would move the answer to `grounded` for that system. It would not retroactively make the figures contain rates.

### F10: Apparent conflict in the world evidence is a census error, not a flipped verdict

Rubenstein et al. 2023 report average poleward and upslope shifts but conclude that "most species are not shifting in expected directions". Ibáñez et al. 2013 say that "for most organisms, particularly long-live species, their evolutionary rates will be too low to keep up with the pace of climate change". Acosta Alamo and Manne 2025 find that "Rates of change in biodiversity were more strongly predicted by rates of change in land use land cover than by rates of change in climate." These look like conflicting answers. Re-census shows they are different slots, drivers and scales: range edges versus long-lived populations versus assemblage composition, climate velocity versus land cover velocity. Fig 9 :5 "Split it rather than search." Fig 1 :22 forbids averaging them. The conflict rule was not triggered; no figure verdict was flipped by a world source. Mode `specified`, world sources are secondary corroboration only.

**Refuter:** demonstrate that one measurement protocol, one driver and one slot definition reconciles all three findings into a single rate with the same threshold. If so, the union reading is too expensive and should be withdrawn.

## 4. Pre-registered refuter

Written before the conclusion, per Seat Map :287 "And a falsifier written before the run is a design instrument, not only an honesty device." The shape claim is refuted if any of the following holds:

1. A single monitor or model returns one global adaptation rate that predicts persistence as well as the per-slot comparison under every reweighting (breaks F7 and Fig 1 :19).
2. A species is shown to move such that the old habitat is removed as a condition set and no new habitat is substituted, with no inherited actors (breaks F4 and Fig 11 :66).
3. A maintained intervention excludes an unwanted actor from a habitat rather than adding to it, while the habitat remains the same field of contact (breaks F6 and Fig 11 :61).
4. A lineage increases fitness with zero additive variance and unchanged contact (breaks F5 and Fig 11 :40).
5. A numeric rate or universal threshold is derived from the figures alone with no imported measurement (breaks F2 and F9).
6. One driver, one slot definition and one protocol reconciles the three world findings in F10 into a single answer (breaks the union census in F1).

If any fires, the shape is withdrawn or re-censused. Until one is run, nothing here is `grounded`.

## 5. Recommendation

1. Unbundle before answering. Report migration, evolution and composition shift as three loops with a fourth measurement loop, and keep the pairwise compounds visible (F1).
2. Write the threshold as an ordinary term and measure it per slot. Do not let "quickly enough" remain a preference (F2, F8).
3. Treat persistence as a ground reading, not as evidence of a mechanism. An ecosystem that persists could have done so by migration, evolution or reassembly; the reading does not name the source (see `05_what_shape_means.md` §4).
4. In any managed case, say that the work is permanent and that introduction is available while exclusion is not (F6).
5. Import the rates from measurements with source gate and shadow test; debit the import against the independence of the frames supplying it (F9, Fig 6 :24 "Import from a live peer is debited against that frame's independence.").
6. Send the generator, not the conclusion. Fig 4 :22 "Only methods cross up. Only head starts come down."

### Round trip note

## 7. Updated verdict

The shape survives. The empirical answer does not, at this budget. What survives: the question is a union of at least four loops, not one rate; the sought-for quantity is a per-slot threshold comparison ⋛ between ground velocity and response velocity; unmaintained persistence is selection's output, not an agent's achievement; migration substitutes habitat and inherits actors; managed composition work is permanent and asymmetric; the objective link is the likely break; a global average is a different question. What does not survive: any global yes or no, any claim that the figures contain a rate, any mapping of the framework's habitat onto the ecological niche, and any claim that persistence identifies the mechanism that produced it. Strains after re-census: one named and resolved (habitat/niche mapping, retracted), none hidden.
