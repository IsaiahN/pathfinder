# Are migration, evolution and composition shift isomers? Order and structure in ECO-0032

**Mode: `specified`.** This is a simulated derivation checked against the figures, not a ground reading (see `05_what_shape_means.md` §7).

## RESIDUAL

The three named mechanisms are described in different vocabularies. The question asks whether they are three spellings of one term (symmetric isomers), different members of one kind (a functional group), or different substances built from different atoms. A second question is owed: in what order must they be checked, given that a chain that is strong at the top and broken in the middle produces nothing?

## DERIVATION

### Q1: What are the atoms of each mechanism?

- **Migration:** an actor moves from one habitat to another. Atoms: populations, conditions, relations, distance. Fig 11 :66 "Isolation is not removal of the habitat. It is substitution of one habitat for another." The bond is not plain subtraction; it is substitution with an inherited remainder (Fig 11 :67 "Move the residual and you must reproduce everything its goal required, and you inherit the new habitat's actors, which you did not choose."). Two failure modes ride along: Fig 11 :68 "what you failed to reproduce is invisible until the goal fails" and :69 "what you unintentionally introduced is invisible until it acts".
- **Evolution:** heritable variants are sifted across handoffs. Atoms: variants, generation time, selection pressure. Fig 7 :35 "A room's agreement is variation, not a verdict. Selection between rooms is where the ground votes." Bond: `→` across generations, with `⋛` at each handoff.
- **Composition shift:** the actor set of an assemblage changes. Atoms: species pool, relations, introductions. Fig 11 :61 "The seat's power is asymmetric. Introduction is available; exclusion is not." In the maintained mode the bond is `⇒` production plus `+` conjunction; in the unmaintained mode it is selection's output.

### Q2: Are they isomers or a functional group?

- Fig 12 :117 "Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."
- Fig 12 :123 "A substructure whose presence fixes the outcome. The group says what kind; the qualifier says which member."
- As usually described, the three mechanisms share only the habitat container: migrations move populations, evolution changes variants, composition shifts change the actor set. Different atoms, so they are **not isomers** under Fig 12 :117. They are three members of a **functional group**: each is a response that changes contact between a lineage and its conditions, and the qualifier says which member (Fig 12 :123).
- The stronger form: hold the full habitat set `{actors, conditions, relations}` fixed and vary only the bond and which atom is active. Then migration (`−` plus substitution), evolution (`→` plus `⋛`) and composition (`⇒` plus `+`) are **isomers** in the figure's sense: same atoms, different arrangement, different substance. The difference is decisive in behaviour, which is why a persistence reading alone cannot tell them apart (see `05` §4).
- **Falsifier, written before the run:** if any mechanism can be written without an atom the others require (for example, migration with no conditions, or evolution with no relations), the isomer claim fails and only the functional-group claim survives. If two mechanisms reduce to the same bond over the same atoms, the isomer claim also fails: they are one term with two spellings.

### Q3: What is the order of checking?

- Fig 3 :20 "The chain is ordered by dependency, not by importance. Each link consumes what the one above produces, so effort spent above the break arrives at a step that cannot use it."
- The dependency order is: perceive the two rates per slot; have terms for what the habitat affords and what each mechanism can do; state what would count as "enough"; choose a monitoring or intervention route; name what worked, verify and carry it.
- Fig 3 :24 "Why the middle link is where chains usually break." The objective link is the one without an instrument (Fig 3 :27 "A link with no instrument is not a link that works. It is a link nobody has looked at."). So the checking order is not "measure migration first". It is "state the threshold first, then measure the mechanism whose slot it governs".
- Fig 9 :54 "And when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again." Unbundling precedes mechanism choice.

### Q4: What composes over a settled mechanism?

- Fig 12 :104 "A settled molecule becomes an atom for whatever composes over it. So irreducible is a fact about a level and never about the world." A mechanism that has been settled for one slot becomes an atom for the next question, for example a proven dispersal kernel becoming a term in a regional forecast.
- Formula step 5: "Settling is necessary for a term to be composed over and it is NOT SUFFICIENT. A library that grows is not a reach that grows." So a mechanism being well studied does not make it the right operand for a new slot.
- Two currencies must be held apart (Fig 12 :125 "A composition costs in two currencies, and they do not add."): bits to describe the mechanism, actions to find out whether it holds at a site. A review can be cheap in bits and expensive in actions. Summing them into one score is the named error.

### Q5: Which bond is "quickly enough"?

- Operators table :48 "a threshold relation between two quantities" and :51 "a goal is a comparison, which is why a vocabulary without it cannot state one". Fig 12 :112 "A plan is a sequence. A goal is a comparison."
- So the bond is ⋛ and the comparison is ordered: ground velocity on one side, response velocity on the other. A vocabulary holding only `+` could say both are present and could not say which is larger. That is why "presence of adaptation" is not an answer to "quickly enough".
- **Falsifier:** if the threshold is shown to be a conjunction (both processes merely present) rather than an ordered comparison, the bond identification fails and the shape must be re-censused.

## CONCLUSION

As described in the literature the three mechanisms are a functional group, not isomers: different atoms, one kind of response. Under a fixed habitat set they are a real isomer claim, same atoms arranged differently, with a stated falsifier. The checking order is set by dependency, and the missing instrument is at the objective link, not at the observation link. "Quickly enough" is ⋛, an ordered threshold, which is why a presence/absence vocabulary cannot answer it.
