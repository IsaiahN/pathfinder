# Expert verification packet: the Tether on misinformation diffusion

**What this is:** a claim in a form a diffusion researcher can check. Mode, derivation and refuters are stated before the conclusion, so a reader knows what to look for and what would settle it.

**Mode: `general` on the figure laws, `specified` on the diffusion application. Nothing is `grounded`.** No cascade measurement is claimed. This is a shape and a route, not a result.

## 1. The claim, in one paragraph

A social network is already the structure whose meaning is "that entities relate, and influence travels." From the figure laws it follows that a claim outruns correction where evidence pools are correlated, where no anchor refuses to update, where handoffs stay inside one closure, where catalytic bridges carry content across closures, and where the effect sits two or three hops out. These are one union compound, so a single lever should not be expected to clear it. Bridges are valence-free: the same bridge carries corrections as readily as falsehoods. The framework does not say which feature dominates or why false content specifically wins; the content-side reason is an import from psychology, recorded as such. What it offers is the shape any candidate answer must have, falsifiers written in advance, and the instruments that would close it.

## 2. Three checkable sub-claims

### C1 - framework-internal; checkable by reading Figures 8 and 13

- **Statement:** frames that draw on the same evidence have no surplus to correct one another. Applied to diffusion, a dense like-minded cluster is the structural suspect.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 8 :47-49: "Independence is one quantity read twice. How much two frames draw on the same evidence decides both whether their agreement means anything and whether their union has any surplus. Two frames that overlap almost completely are one frame wearing two names." Also Fig 13 :38-39: "independence is how little two frames draw on the same evidence, and it is what makes their agreement mean anything."
- **What to check:** is the mapping from "shared evidence" to "homophilous cluster" faithful, or is it a strain? Fig 2 :18-21 says "they never talk, and still agree," which homophilious nodes do not; the correct figure for talking nodes is Fig 2 collapse 1 plus Fig 8 overlap. Both are recorded in `01`.
- **Refuter:** under matched content and anchoring, high-overlap clusters show no larger false-over-true reach or persistence differential than low-overlap clusters.

### C2 - framework-internal and application-facing; checkable against Figure 2

- **Statement:** with no non-updating anchor, mutual update converges on influence rather than truth, so persistence is the time the chain takes to run out of nodes.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 2 :13-15: "no anchor; each updates on the others / they converge, and what they converge on is whoever had the most influence." Fig 2 :33: "The anchor must not update." Fig 2 :35: "Alignment is triangulation, not negotiation. A mirror cannot correct you." Fig 7 :13: "same closure, handed along; agreement compounds, evidence does not."
- **What to check:** does a fact-check node that updates on the crowd still count as an anchor under the figure's definition? By Fig 2 :29-30 it does not: "An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks."
- **Refuter:** adding a non-updating anchor at fixed topology leaves the false-over-true persistence differential unchanged.

### C3 - application-facing; checkable against Figures 11 and 12

- **Statement:** bridges are catalytic and valence-free, and the differential is multi-hop. A bridge changes reach for any content; it is not a misinformation node.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 12 :119: "catalyst. A part that survives its own use. Spent and regenerating parts behave differently across uses, and nothing records which a term is." Fig 11 :49: "An actor's valence is relative to the residual, not a property it carries." Fig 11 :52: "first-order enumeration, second-order effect, and the residual that mattered was three steps out."
- **What to check:** in a real cascade, does the same bridge carry true and false content to comparable depth? If it does, the structural role survives but the false-specific reach claim does not; that distinction is the point of this sub-claim.
- **Refuter:** first-order degree alone accounts for the whole differential, with no second or third-order contribution; or a bridge reproducibly carries only false content across the closure under matched content.

## 3. What is explicitly not claimed

- Not a ranked list of features with effect sizes.
- Not that any node is inherently a misinformation node. Valence is relative to the residual (Fig 11 :49-51).
- Not that cross-cutting ties alone fix an echo chamber. That is one half of a union compound (Fig 9 :55-58).
- Not that the figures derive the content-side reason false wins. Novelty and identity are imported from the psychology frame and debited against independence (Fig 6 :24).
- Not `grounded`. Nothing here was settled by a measurement.
- Not that this simulated derivation can validate itself. "the output is only as good as the validator" (*The Recursive Self-Improvement Trap*).

## 5. What would settle it

A source-discriminating instrument, not a further argument. From *The Hard Problem is About Source*: an instrument that reads green "cannot comment on the source"; the spectrometer resolved it. Here:

1. a matched-seed cascade design that separates structural position from content properties;
2. an anchor test at fixed topology, measuring the change in persistence;
3. a hop test, comparing first, second and third-order contributions;
4. a bridge test, comparing true and false content on the same bridge.

## 6. What a diffusion researcher can check in twenty minutes

1. Read Fig 8 :47-49, Fig 2 :13-15 and :33-35, Fig 11 :49 and :52, Fig 12 :119. Five short passages.
2. Check C3 against their own cascade data: is a high-degree bridge carrying both true and false content, or only one?
3. Name one instrument that would separate structural position from content in a cascade, or say why none is available.
4. Send the refutation. A failure is more useful than an endorsement.
