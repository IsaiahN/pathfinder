**Subject:** One structural claim about misinformation diffusion, and what would refute it

> Hi [name],
>
> I have one specific output I would like you to check against what you already know. I am not asking you to read the framework first, and I am not asking you to agree with it. If this does not survive, there is nothing else to look at.
>
> **The output (`specified`, not `grounded`):** a social network is already, in the framework's vocabulary, the structure whose meaning is "that entities relate, and influence travels." From that and two other figure laws it follows that the structural conditions under which *any* claim outruns correction are: nodes drawing on one evidence pool rather than independent ones, no anchor that refuses to update when the crowd pushes on it, handoff along chains that share a closure, bridge nodes that carry content between closures, and effects that sit two or three hops out rather than at direct contact.
>
> The framework does **not** say which of these dominates, and it does not say why *false* content specifically wins. That last part is an import from the psychology frame, not a figure result. I am reporting it as a gap, not filling it.
>
> **What I am not claiming:** that a bridge node is a misinformation node. The figure says an actor's valence is relative to the residual, so the same bridge carries corrections as readily as falsehoods. I am also not claiming a single lever clears an echo chamber: the figure names two distinct collapses, and their union survives a one-sided fix.
>
> **What would refute the shape claim (written down before this check):**
> 1. with content and anchoring matched, a high-overlap, unanchored network shows no larger false-over-true reach or persistence differential than a low-overlap, anchored one;
> 2. adding a non-updating anchor at fixed topology leaves the persistence differential unchanged;
> 3. reposts inside one closure increase independent ground contact rather than only reach;
> 4. first-order degree alone accounts for the whole differential, with no second or third-order contribution.
>
> If any one holds, I retract the claim. A one-line "this fails because..." is more useful to me than agreement. If you would rather not engage, no reply is needed.
>
> **If it survives, and only then:** the figures are the authority, not my summary of them. The verbatim text is below, and the sources I actually read are listed in the folder.
