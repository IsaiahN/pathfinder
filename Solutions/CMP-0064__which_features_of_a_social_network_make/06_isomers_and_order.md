## DERIVATION

### Q1 - Are homophily, bridges and anchor absence isomers or functional groups?

- Fig 12 :117: **"isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."**
- Fig 12 :124: **"functional group. A substructure whose presence fixes the outcome. The group says what kind; the qualifier says which member."**
- Fig 12 :97-98: silver fulminate and silver cyanate share composition and differ in properties, "which is what forced structure into the notation."

The three suspects share atoms (nodes and edges) but differ in arrangement: a dense within-cluster edge set, a cross-cluster edge at one node, and the absence of an edge to a non-updating anchor. Under Fig 12 :117 they are **isomers** of the same atom set with different bonds and different diffusion behaviour. Under Fig 12 :124 they are also **functional groups**: the presence of a cross-closure bridge fixes the *kind* of outcome (reach beyond the cluster), and the qualifier says which member (which bridge, which content).

Which reading is safer? The isomer reading is stronger but assumes the features are composed terms with a recorded bond. The functional-group reading only assumes a substructure whose presence fixes an outcome. Since these features are emergent properties of an arrangement rather than composed molecules with lineage, the **functional-group reading is the defensible one**; isomer is the stronger claim worth testing if a lineage of compositions can be written. `specified`, `single-source` for the network case (Fig 12 definition), triangulated with the chemistry instance in the same figure.

**Falsifier:** if the same substructure (for example a cross-closure bridge under matched content and anchoring) can be shown to produce opposite diffusion outcomes in repeated measures, the functional-group claim fails and the feature is not outcome-fixing.

### Q2 - What bond connects the seed to its reach?

- Operators table :30: → sequence, "A then B; B needs A to have happened." Test: "swap them. Does it become a different thing? Yes."
- Operators table :38: ⇒ production, "A produces B; B did not exist before." Test: "is there a B before A fires? No."
- Fig 12 :42-44: the arrangement is priced, "A bond costs what it could have been," and "a bond that costs nothing is a bond that carries nothing."

Within one closure, a repost is → sequence: the next node already existed and the claim arrives at it in order. Across a closure boundary, the bond is ⇒ production: the nodes on the other side were not reachable from the seed's closure before the bridge fired. Fig 6 :23 confirms: **"No frame escapes its own closure. The bound moves only on contact with a frame whose closure differs."** That is a production statement, not a sequence statement.

This distinction is not cosmetic. Fig 12 :121: **"cycle. The output re-enters as input. The sign of the loop decides whether it closes, and a chain cannot close by construction."** Persistence inside a cluster is a loop whose sign closes; pure chain-like forwarding cannot close by construction. So the two quantities in the question are bonded differently: farther is ⇒ production across closures, longer is a closing cycle inside one.

**Falsifier:** if every cross-closure arrival can be shown to have been reachable by another path from the seed, the ⇒ reading collapses into → and the production claim is refuted.

### Q3 - In what order must the habitat be enumerated?

- Fig 11 :43: **"The habitat is enumerated, never composed."**
- Fig 11 :44-48: list everything in contact with the residual, then what is in contact with those, outward until the cascade stops mattering; actors, conditions, relations.
- Fig 11 :52: **"And contact cascades. Scoping the habitat to direct contact is how DDT reached the eagles: first-order enumeration, second-order effect, and the residual that mattered was three steps out."**
- Fig 3 :20-21: **"The chain is ordered by dependency, not by importance."**

Dependency order for this question:

1. adjacency (who contacts whom),
2. handoff (how the claim travels over adjacency),
3. anchor (whether anything non-updating is reachable),
4. differential (measured false-minus-true reach and persistence).

Effort at step 4 before step 1 is above the break. A study that reports a false-news reach advantage without an adjacency map has measured an effect whose habitat was never enumerated, which is exactly Fig 11's DDT failure. This also explains why first-order degree is incomplete: it enumerates direct contact only, while the residual that matters is three steps out.

**Falsifier:** a case where the differential is fully determined by first-order degree with no second or third-order contribution.

### Q4 - Analogues (supporting, not deciding)

- **Epidemiology, superspreading events.** "Some cases of superspreading conform to the 80/20 rule, where approximately 20% of infected individuals are responsible for 80% of transmissions" (Wikipedia, *Superspreading event*, read 2026-11-22). This is Fig 12 :119's catalyst in a different evidence pool: "A part that survives its own use."
- **Computational social science, echo chamber detection.** JECHO's three principles are "internal opinion homogeneity, opinion extremism, and structural isolation" (Dehkordi et al., 2026). That is Fig 8 :47-49's overlap measured with a Jaccard threshold.
- **Economics, chatroom models.** "when agents are highly sensitive to peer pressure and the network exhibits structural polarization, even if the majority does not genuinely believe in it, the message still can spread widely without encountering open resistance" (Liu, 2025). This is Fig 2 collapse 1 and collapse 2 in one sentence, arrived at from game theory.
- **Network science, community-aware influence.** Robson and Umrawal (2025/2026) "computes CDD to prioritize bridging nodes," the cross-closure production bond of Q2.
- **Chemistry, the figure's own instance.** Liebig and Wöhler, 1824-1826, silver fulminate and silver cyanate; Berzelius named isomerism in 1830 (Fig 12 :97-98).

These analogues corroborate structure, not verdict weight. They are different fields but several are preprints in one broad computational pool; the overlap is recorded in `10_source_ledger.csv`.

### Q5 - Union or partition?

Fig 9 :55-58: **"a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either."** The features are not additive. A high-overlap cluster with a reachable anchor is a different compound from a high-overlap cluster without one. A bridge into a closure that already holds an anchor carries corrections; the same bridge into an unanchored closure carries whatever the pool rewards (Fig 11 :49-51, valence is relative to the residual). So the answer must be reported as a union of conditions, and a single lever is not expected to clear the intersections.

## CONCLUSION

The suspect features are best read as **functional groups** whose presence fixes the kind of diffusion outcome, with the stronger isomer reading left open for a written lineage. Reach across closures is ⇒ production, persistence inside a closure is a closing cycle, and the habitat must be enumerated outward to at least three hops before any differential is claimed. The four falsifiers above were written before the conclusion and remain live. Nothing is `grounded`.
