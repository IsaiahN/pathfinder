## 0. The residual in effect terms

Let two claims, one true and one false, enter the same network. Let `reach` be cascade size and depth, and `persistence` be time until circulation stops. The residual R is the difference in reach and persistence between the false and the true claim. The question asks which features of the network produce or enlarge that difference.

This is not one hard question. Fig 9 :55-58: **"a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either."** The two conditions are structure (who is connected to whom) and content (what the claim is). A lever that changes only one condition clears the region where that condition alone holds and leaves the compound standing. So the answer will be a conjunction, not a single winner.

R must be kept per slot. Fig 1 :19: **"The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated."** The two content classes are two slots, not one average, and the whole point of the question is their difference. Averaging them would delete the signal.

The shape the answer would have: a set of structural predicates each of which, when present, predicts a larger false-over-true differential, each with a stated refuter, and a clear statement of which part of the differential the figures cannot see at all. That shape is now bound as an ordinary term so it can accrue a residual like anything else (formula step "Direction").

## 1. Domain census, before choosing any figure line

### 1.1 R by arity, symmetry, scale

- **Arity:** two content classes compared on one topology, so a union compound (Fig 9 :54-58), not one question.
- **Symmetry:** the comparison is a threshold relation between two quantities, reach and persistence (Operators table :46-52). It is ⋛, not a symmetric join. Swapping true and false does not give the same role.
- **Scale:** microscopic (node and edge), mesoscopic (community, cluster, bridge), macroscopic (whole cascade). The effect that matters is three steps out, not at direct contact (Fig 11 :52-54).

### 1.2 One line per domain: actors, currency, anchor

| domain | actors | currency | anchor (nameable? if not, not a domain yet) | partition or union |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| D1 topology | nodes, edges, communities, bridge nodes | adjacency and paths | measured adjacency, who actually connects to whom | partition boundary: which compositions exist at all |
| D2 diffusion | cascades, adopters, retweet handoffs | transmissions | observed cascade size, depth, time | union with content: a claim travels only if both structure and content allow it |
| D3 truth and correction | claims, fact-checkers, ground truth | truth value and corrections | the claim's ground truth, which does not move when the network pushes on it | partition boundary: anchor versus frame |
| D4 verification and outreach | expert readers | agreement and residual | the ground, never agreement | separate frame, zero independence in a simulated run |

All four have nameable anchors. D4's anchor is the ground but its independence in this run is zero because all seats draw on one evidence pool, so its agreement is not a witness (Fig 2 :40).

### 1.3 Partition or union

D1 and D3 are partition boundaries inside the wider question: a network either has a cross-kind path or it does not, an anchor either updates or it does not. D2 is a union with content. The overall residual is therefore a **union compound**. Per Fig 9 :54-58, it must be unbundled before search, and a single lever must not be expected to clear the intersections.

### 1.4 Dependency order (Fig 3)

Fig 3 :20-21: **"The chain is ordered by dependency, not by importance."** Order here:

1. Enumerate the topology (who contacts whom). Without adjacency there is no diffusion to diagnose.
2. Establish how content travels over that topology (the process).
3. Locate the anchor, or its absence (the correction channel).
4. Measure the differential against the ground.

Effort spent at step 4 before step 1 is effort above a break that arrives at a link that cannot use it. A claim about "virality" with no adjacency map is a reading below the break: "a reading of nothing" (Fig 3 :23).

## 3. Findings

### F1 - The network is already a named structure, and influence travel is its meaning (general, triangulated)

Fig 13 :23-24:

> **"network"**
> **"that entities relate, and influence travels"**

This is not an analogy imported from graph theory. The substrate names network as one of its three structures, and defines it by relation plus travel of influence. A social network is an instance of that structure, not a metaphor for it. So a question about which network features govern diffusion is a question the figures govern directly. Strain: none at this step.

Triangulation: Fig 13 :24 (network carries influence) and Fig 13 :38-39 (frames over that network may be more or less independent) are low overlap readings of the same structure, one about the pathway, one about the evidence behind the nodes. `triangulated`.

### F2 - Homophily is the figure's "shared evidence pool" (specified, triangulated)

Fig 13 :38-39:

> **"independence is how little two frames draw on the same evidence, and it is what makes their agreement mean anything"**
> **"Six members that overlapped would be fewer members wearing six names."**

Fig 8 :47-49:

> **"Independence is one quantity read twice."**
> **"How much two frames draw on the same evidence decides both whether their agreement means anything and whether their union has any surplus. Two frames that overlap almost completely are one frame wearing two names."**

A dense cluster of like-minded nodes is a set of frames with high overlap. Their agreement carries no information about the world and their union has no surplus to correct anything. This is the structural reading of an echo chamber. It matches the world literature exactly: JECHO's three principles include "internal opinion homogeneity" and "structural isolation" (Dehkordi et al., 2026, read 2026-11-22).

**Refuter:** a matched network in which high-overlap clusters show no larger false-over-true reach differential than low-overlap clusters, with content and anchoring held fixed. That would break this finding.

**Verdict: `triangulated`** (Fig 13 and Fig 8, different routes, low overlap).

### F3 - Two collapses, not one: the echo chamber is a union compound (general, triangulated)

Fig 2 names two distinct failure modes. Collapse 1, :13-15:

> **"no anchor; each updates on the others"**
> **"they converge, and what they converge on is whoever had the most influence"**

Collapse 2, :18-21:

> **"one evidence pool"**
> **"they never talk, and still agree"**
> **"so the agreement is the pool talking, not two frames confirming each other"**

A real echo chamber has both: nodes update on one another (collapse 1) and draw on the same prior pool (collapse 2). Fig 2 :34 warns what happens if only one is treated:

> **"If it does, panel 3 turns into panel 1: the anchor becomes another frame, everything drifts together, and the drift is coherent, which is why nobody notices."**

Fig 9 :54-58 forbids treating this as one lever:

> **"And when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again."**
> **"a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either"**

So adding cross-cutting ties (treating collapse 2) while leaving no anchor (collapse 1) leaves the compound standing. Coherent drift is exactly why nobody notices. The figure-grounded prediction is that single-lever interventions will underperform, which the world literature also reports for individual-level nudges that do not translate to collective effects (Furutani et al., 2026).

**Refuter:** introduce one lever only and observe the differential disappear in both halves of the compound. That would show this was one condition, not two.

**Verdict: `triangulated`** (Fig 2 and Fig 9).

### F4 - Absence of a non-updating anchor is the persistence enabler (general, triangulated)

Fig 2 :29-30 defines the anchor:

> **"An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks."**

Fig 2 :33-35:

> **"The anchor must not update."**
> **"Alignment is triangulation, not negotiation. A mirror cannot correct you."**

Fig 2 :45:

> **"Frames range against the ground. They do not negotiate with it."**

If a fact-check becomes just another node that updates on the crowd, it is panel 1 again. It stops being an anchor and becomes a frame. Persistence is then explained structurally: with no pinned ground, there is nothing for the cascade to range against, so the chain runs until attention runs out, not until the claim is corrected. Fig 10 :24 adds: **"the ground does not decay, the channel does"**, so the correctable object is the channel, not truth.

The world source with expert personas is the closest corroboration: "expert-driven personas preserve factual stability" (Maurya et al., 2025), where the expert node behaves like a non-updating anchor.

**Refuter:** a network with a non-updating anchor shows no reduction in false-over-true persistence relative to the same network without one.

**Verdict: `triangulated`** (Fig 2 and Fig 10).

### F5 - Handoff down a same-closure chain produces motion without progress (specified, single-source for the diffusion instance)

Fig 7 :12-13:

> **"same closure, handed along; agreement compounds, evidence does not"**

Fig 7 :36:

> **"A chain of mirrors is one long room. The next room must differ from the last, and match the residual it surfaced."**

A retweet or repost is a handoff. If each node forwards to a node in the same closure, the chain is a mirror chain: the claim moves, the evidence does not grow, and persistence is the time the chain takes to run out of nodes. Fig 7 :37-38: progress per handoff needs both bracket fidelity and ground pressure; zero either and the chain runs in place.

This is an application of a figure law to an instance, not a figure line that mentions social media. Marked `specified` and `single-source` (Fig 7 only) for the diffusion instance. The general law (agreement is not evidence) is triangulated by Fig 2 :40.

**Refuter:** show that a chain of same-closure handoffs increases the network's ground contact (new independent evidence) rather than only its reach.

**Verdict: `single-source` for the instance, `triangulated` for the underlying law.**

### F6 - Bridge nodes are catalytic, not "pro-false" (specified, triangulated)

Fig 12 :119:

> **"catalyst. A part that survives its own use. Spent and regenerating parts behave differently across uses, and nothing records which a term is."**

Fig 11 :49-51:

> **"An actor's valence is relative to the residual, not a property it carries."**
> **"The same actor is a benefactor to one residual and a malfactor to another, so mutualism and parasitism are relations rather than kinds."**

A bridge node is a catalyst: it survives its own use and can carry many claims across a closure boundary. Crucially its valence is relative to the residual. The same bridge can carry a correction as easily as a falsehood. So the structural claim is about **reach**, not about a node being inherently a misinformation node. Fig 6 :23:

> **"No frame escapes its own closure. The bound moves only on contact with a frame whose closure differs."**

Fig 6 :24: **"Import from a live peer is debited against that frame's independence."** Cross-cutting contact moves the wall, and that movement is exactly what a bridge provides. The world source on inter-community influence prioritises "bridging nodes" (Robson and Umrawal, 2025), corroborating the structural role without proving a false-specific bias.

**Refuter:** a bridge that carries false content across communities at the same rate and to the same depth as true content, under matched seeds and content, would refute any claim that bridges specifically amplify false reach. Note this does **not** refute the structural role of bridges, only the differential claim.

**Verdict: `triangulated`** (Fig 12, Fig 11, Fig 6).

### F7 - The residual is multi-hop; direct-contact enumeration misses it (general, single-source)

Fig 11 :52-54:

> **"And contact cascades. Scoping the habitat to direct contact is how DDT reached the eagles: first-order enumeration, second-order effect, and the residual that mattered was three steps out."**

Fig 11 :43: **"The habitat is enumerated, never composed."**

A one-hop neighbourhood is not the habitat of a cascade. The structural feature that governs reach is the cascade of contacts outward, and the effect that matters can be three steps from the seed. This is why degree alone is an incomplete predictor: degree is first-order enumeration.

**Refuter:** a case where the false-over-true differential is fully determined by direct-neighbour degree with no contribution from second or third-order paths.

**Verdict: `single-source` (Fig 11).**

### F8 - The figures cannot see why *false* specifically wins (limit, triangulated)

The figures give structural conditions under which any claim outruns the ground. They do not contain a term for the content-level reason a false claim attracts more handoffs than a true one. Formula, "Direction": a frame's sought-for shape is a preference, not a term, and "Every check here runs on the MECHANISM and none on the DIRECTION OF THE QUESTION." Fig 9 :30-31: **"your words are borrowed too, so describe the effect rather than the cause."** Fig 9 :32: **"you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself."**

The content-side reason (novelty, emotional arousal, identity alignment) is an **import** from the psychology frame. Fig 6 :23 says the bound moves only on such contact, and :24 debits it against independence. So the differential has two parts:

1. figure-governed: the structural conditions that let any claim travel far without ground contact;
2. imported and world-corroborated: the content property that makes false claims travel more in those conditions.

The figures decide part 1. They do not rank part 2, and they cannot. Reporting that as `gap` is the honest verdict, not a silence. Fig 8 :39: **"So a search that finds nothing is never a proof of absence."** The world literature supplies the missing atom (novelty and identity), but it is secondary corroboration, not a figure ruling.

**Refuter:** a figure line that names a content property as a network feature would break this limit. None was found after re-census.

**Verdict: `triangulated` for the limit (Fig 9, formula Direction, Fig 6 on import).**

### F9 - Filtering versus witnessing (general)

Fig 9 :60: **"Use filters for the budget and witnesses for the verdict. Never let a filter hand you a verdict."** A tier, a centrality score, or a publication venue is a filter on what to read. The verdict comes from the figures and, for the empirical ranking, from measured cascades. Fig 2 :42: **"Buy independence in proportion to the cost of being wrong."** The cost here is high, so independent frames are worth buying, but the world sources read for this solution mostly share a computational-social-science pool and that overlap is recorded in `10_source_ledger.csv`.

## 4. Pre-registered refuter

Written before the conclusion, per the method's rule that a falsifier written before the run is a design instrument.

The shape claim is refuted if any of the following holds:

1. Under matched content, a network with high-overlap clusters and no non-updating anchor shows no larger false-over-true reach or persistence differential than a low-overlap, anchored network. (breaks F2, F3, F4)
2. Adding a non-updating anchor at fixed topology leaves the false-over-true persistence differential unchanged. (breaks F4)
3. Reposts that stay inside one closure increase independent ground contact rather than only reach. (breaks F5)
4. The differential is fully accounted for by first-order degree, with no contribution from second or third-order paths. (breaks F7)
5. A figure line is found that names a content property, such as novelty, as a network feature. (breaks F8)

If any fires, the finding is withdrawn, not softened. Until one is run, nothing here is `grounded`.

## 5. Recommendation

1. Report the answer as a conjunction of structural conditions, not a ranked list. Fig 9 :54-58 says a union must be unbundled before search.
2. Keep the reach claim and the persistence claim separate. They are two quantities under one ⋛, not one score (Operators table :46-52).
3. Name the bridge role as catalytic and valence-free. Do not call a node a misinformation node (Fig 11 :49-51).
4. State the import explicitly: the content-side reason false wins comes from the psychology frame and is debited against independence (Fig 6 :24).
5. Treat any single-lever intervention claim with suspicion until both halves of the compound are addressed (Fig 9 :55-58).
6. Send the refuter before the theory (Fig 10 :52, "Bring a characterised residual, never the source").

## 7. Verdict of this audit

**The figure frame decides a shape, not a ranking.** The structural conditions that let a claim outrun the ground are: correlated evidence pools (low independence), no non-updating anchor, same-closure handoff, catalytic bridges, and multi-hop contact cascades. These are separate findings with separate verdicts, never averaged (Fig 1 :19). Which one dominates empirically, and the content-side reason false specifically wins, is a `gap` at the figure level, filled only by secondary world sources and by a ground that has not been read here. Mode: `general` and `specified`, nothing `grounded`. Status: `candidate`.
