# Outreach draft: check the residual before you decide about the theory

**Use:** one-to-one, or as a cover letter attached to a submission through the public venues in
`03_public_contact_points.csv`. **Not** for bulk send.

**Hard precondition:** do not send until the scope question in `01`, finding F11, is settled for your
audience. If they use "advantage" to mean a failure of quantum predictions rather than a classical
simulation barrier, the first stage below must be re-worded before it is sent.

---

**Subject:** What would a new physical principle have to predict that quantum mechanics does not?

> Hi [name],
>
> I have one specific question I would like you to answer from your own work, before I show you
> anything I have written. I am not asking you to read the theory first, and I am not asking you to
> agree with it. If the answer does not survive, there is nothing else to look at.
>
> **The residual, in effect terms.** A quantum device completes a task in resources that the best
> known classical procedure cannot match. Two very different claims are being read off that one
> event: first, that the device matches the predictions of standard quantum mechanics; second, that
> no efficient classical account of the same output is known. The first is settled by an experiment.
> The second is a search result plus assumptions. My question is whether anything in the observed
> gap is a failed physics prediction, rather than a classical difficulty.
>
> **What I would count as a failure of my reading:** any of the following, written down before I
> wrote this message.
>
> 1. A device output in the high-complexity regime that disagrees with standard quantum predictions
>    beyond what the accounted noise model permits, for example under a random-circuit-plus-inverse
>    echo test.
> 2. An unconditional, unrelativized proof that the observed behaviour cannot be produced by any
>    circuit in BQP.
> 3. A demonstration that "quantum advantage" is constitutively about quantum mechanics failing, so
>    that the experimental and computational halves are one condition rather than two.
>
> If any one holds, the claim I am about to describe is wrong and I will retract it. A one-line
> "this fails because..." is more useful to me than agreement. If you would rather not engage, no
> reply is needed.
>
> **My recorded verdict, stated only now:** on the figures I use, no new physical principle is owed by
> an advantage alone. Composition inside a closed set never adds an atom, and a search that finds
> nothing is never a proof of absence. An advantage is a new arrangement of the quantum account, not
> a new element in the physical inventory. New principles would be imports, and an import is licensed
> by a measured deviation, not by a new capability. The high-complexity question stays open, with a
> stated instrument. This is a `specified` shape, not a `grounded` result, and it is a simulated
> derivation rather than a measurement.
>
> **What I am not claiming:** that quantum mechanics is complete or final; that no classical
> simulator will ever be found; that quantum mechanics cannot be tested where classical simulation
> is hard; or that a specific replacement theory is right. The framework returns a shape, not a name.
>
> **If it survives, and only then:** the sources are below. The figures are the authority, not my
> summary of them.
> - Verbatim figure text with line numbers (local framework extract):
>   `FRAMEWORK/reference/03-FIGURES_TEXT.md`, cited as `Fig N :line`.
> - A public account of the architecture: https://medium.com/@IsaiahNwukor/arrival-of-the-fittest-the-tether-architecture-1de7edf641ec
> - The figures and formula for direct download (branch `general-agent`):
>   https://github.com/IsaiahN/tether/tree/general-agent/figures
>
> **If you want to use the machinery:** run it on a problem you know well and can check against a
> ground. Write down what would refute your result *before* you run it. A reported failure is more
> useful to me than a reported success.
>
> Thanks,
> [name]
