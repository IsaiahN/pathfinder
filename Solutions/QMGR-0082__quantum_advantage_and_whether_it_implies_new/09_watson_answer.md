## QUESTION

Decide, in effect terms, what the Tether says about the claim that quantum advantage implies new
physical principles beyond quantum mechanics. Option A: accept the implication, treat the speedup as
a failed prediction of the existing physical account, and owe a new principle. Option B: keep the two
halves of the event separate, treat the speedup as an arrangement inside the quantum closure, and
owe an import only if a measured deviation fires. What each leads to: A adds a physical atom on the
strength of a budget fact, which Fig 6 prices above starting from primitives and which spends the
independence of the people asked to check it; B sends a checkable shape, a dependency order and
pre-registered refuters, and keeps the high-complexity test open until an instrument registers
against the ground.

## VERDICT (from the figures)

No. A demonstrated quantum advantage does not by itself imply new physical principles beyond quantum
mechanics. It is a new arrangement inside the quantum closure: the set is closed and the arrangements
are not, so deeper circuits and new tasks rearrange what is already held. The hard part of the claim
lives in the classical half of the compound, where "no efficient simulator is known" is a search
result plus conjectures. The figures say a search that finds nothing is never a proof of absence, and
a filter for the budget can never hand a verdict. A new physical principle is an import, and imports
are licensed by a measured deviation from quantum predictions (the REFUTED bin), not by a new
capability (the NOVEL bin). The affirmative implication is therefore a `gap` after a genuine
re-census, not a silence: unreachable is a verdict. The honest companion claim is that whether
quantum mechanics fails at the complexity frontier is genuinely open, with a stated instrument, an
echo test that compares fidelity decay against the accounted noise model. This result is
`specified` on the shape and `general` on the framework topology, never `grounded`.

- **cost if wrong:** bounded and locatable. If an import was owed and we refused it, we keep a false
  theory, but the refuter is a measurement at a named frontier, so the error is findable. The larger
  cost runs the other way: importing a wrong principle on the strength of a speedup adds an atom that
  was not needed, which Fig 6 prices above starting from primitives, and it spends the independence
  of outside checkers by asking them to endorse a frame before they have tested its output.
- **undoable:** yes. Imports are stamped with origin and sequence, and a refusal removes a term's
  standing to be composed over without deleting anything from the record (Fig 6 :42-43), so a
  premature import can be superseded. No physical primitive has to be withdrawn to correct this
  answer; only the verdict changes when a deviation is registered.
- **reversed by:** (1) a device output that deviates from quantum predictions beyond the accounted
  noise model under an echo or verification protocol; (2) an unconditional, unrelativized proof that
  the observed behaviour lies outside BQP; (3) a demonstration that "quantum advantage" is
  constitutively about physics failing rather than classical simulation being hard, which would
  collapse the union compound and require a fresh census; (4) a figure re-census that admits a
  seventh substrate primitive by rearrangement. Until one fires, the verdict stands as `candidate`.
