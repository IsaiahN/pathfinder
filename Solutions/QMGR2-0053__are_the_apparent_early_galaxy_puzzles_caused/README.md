## Residual in effect terms

The residual R is the gap between what early-galaxy observations report and what galaxy-formation models predict: too many massive galaxies too early, too much mass in too little time, too much early quiescence, and a population of compact red objects whose source is disputed. The three options are not three answers to the same question. Each leads somewhere different.

- **Measurement.** The anchor (spectroscopy, rest-frame near-IR) moves the reported redshifts and masses; the puzzle shrinks as the channel is sharpened. If this is the whole story, no term is owed anywhere else.
- **Modeling assumptions.** The perception link works, but the vocabulary link (SED templates, IMF, dust law, mass-to-light ratio, AGN versus stars) mis-attaches the reading. A refit is owed, not a new term.
- **Real theory problem.** Both links work and a genuinely new term is owed in galaxy-formation physics, or a new primitive is owed. This is the expensive branch and the figures price it above the other two.

What an answer would look like: a per-slot sorting of R into Fig 5's bins, with the union unbundled, the anchor named, and a stated refuter. The sought-for shape is made an ordinary term here, in section 1 of `01_figure_grounded_audit.md`, so it can accrue a residual like anything else.
