# Which bond, and in what order? A supporting derivation

**Mode: `specified`.** This is a simulated derivation checked against the figures, not a ground reading (see `05_what_shape_means.md` section 6).

## RESIDUAL

The phrase "an institution caused a change in trust, growth, corruption, or conflict" does not say which bond is meant. Three questions are owed: (1) which of the figure's operators fits the claim, (2) what order the design must impose to test it, and (3) whether the four outcomes are isomers of one structure or members of a functional group.

## DOMAIN CENSUS

Atoms held for this derivation: institution I, outcome Y, and a source of external variation E. Bonds held: the seven of Fig 12 plus negation. No new primitive is available. The anchor must be a source that does not update on Y. The four outcomes are a union compound, so they are censused separately and never averaged.

## DERIVATION

### Q1 — Which bond does "caused a change" mean?

Fig 12 :72 lists **sequence** (→): *"A then B; B needs A to have happened"*. Fig 12 :77 lists **production** (⇒): *"A produces B; B did not exist before"*. Fig 12 :87 lists **comparison** (⋛): *"a threshold relation between two quantities"*. Fig 12 :121 lists **cycle**: *"The output re-enters as input. The sign of the loop decides whether it closes, and a chain cannot close by construction."*

- If the claim is that the reform created a level of trust that did not exist before, it is production (⇒). The test is: *"is there a B before A fires? No."*
- If the claim is that trust rose after the reform and would not have risen without it, the "before" already contains some trust. The claim is a **comparison** over time, or a sequence with a counterfactual, not production.
- If the claim is that the reform and trust mutually reinforced each other, it is a cycle. Fig 12 :121 warns that a chain cannot close by construction, so a linear before-and-after design cannot detect it.
- If the claim is that the reform is irrelevant, that is negation (¬). Operators table :58: *"a refutation is a negation, which is why a vocabulary without it can only accumulate"*. A vocabulary that only reports positive effects can never record this verdict.

**Conclusion Q1:** the word "caused" underdetermines the bond. The claim must specify it before a design is chosen, because different bonds require different admissible evidence. Fig 12 :90-91: *"A relation that fails all six is not a bond you are missing. It is a claim that has not been made yet."*

### Q2 — What order must the design impose?

Fig 12 :111: *"A vocabulary without bonds can name a state and cannot name a route to one."* Fig 12 :112: *"A plan is a sequence. A goal is a comparison. Progress is a subtraction. A refutation is a negation, and that one is not a bond."*

A design must therefore be a **route**, not a state description. Its order is fixed by dependency (Fig 3):

1. Source E must be fixed **before** Y is measured, or it is not an anchor.
2. The comparison group must be defined **before** the outcome is seen, or the cut is authored by the result.
3. The refuter must be written **before** E is examined (Fig 5 :65-66).

World corroboration (secondary): the NIST page says *"An Experimental Design is the laying out of a detailed experimental plan in advance of doing the experiment"*, and the AEA RCT Registry makes that plan public before the run. Both are bindings from outside the framework, not derivations from it.

**Falsifier, written before the run:** if a design can be assembled after seeing the outcome and still separate the bonds, the ordering requirement is false.

### Q3 — Are trust, growth, corruption and conflict isomers or a functional group?

Fig 12 :117: *"Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."* Fig 12 :123: *"functional group. A substructure whose presence fixes the outcome. The group says what kind; the qualifier says which member."*

- As written in the question, the four outcomes share only the substructure "institution I plus a change in Y". By Fig 12's definition they are **not isomers**: they do not share the same atom set. They are members of a **functional group**, the common shape "a bond from an institution to an outcome", with four different outcome atoms in the slot.
- The stronger version: if all four are written over a shared atom set (I, E, Y-before, Y-after, the institution's own report, an independent measure), differing only in which bond holds and which measure is used, then they **are** isomers over that shared set. That is the claim worth testing, because it predicts that a design which works for one outcome should transport to another with a measurable round-trip gap.
- Fig 13 :121: *"Different routes can arrive at the same substance."* Two different designs (a lottery and a cutoff) can identify the same bond; their agreement is corroboration only if their evidence pools are disjoint.

**Falsifier:** if any one outcome requires an atom the others do not (for example conflict requires a third-party actor that trust does not), the isomer claim fails and the functional-group reading stands. This is recorded before any case is examined.

### Q4 — What survives transport?

Fig 4 :30: *"So take the round trip and measure the gap. Without it you are navigating by dead reckoning: confident about a position nobody checked."*

A local estimate carried to another setting is a condensation. What typically fails to survive is (a) the manipulability of the source E, (b) the independence of the outcome measure, and (c) the per-slot definition of the effect. What often survives is the qualitative shape: a source that did not update, read against a separate outcome, produces a discriminating comparison. Fig 11 :70: *"A test harness is a substituted habitat. Which is why a synthetic solve proves wiring and never capability."*

## CONCLUSION

The four outcomes are a functional group as the question writes them, and a real isomer claim only over a shared atom set. The bond must be named before the design, the design must be ordered by dependency and registered before the run, and transport must report its round-trip gap. None of this requires a new primitive, an import that moves the wall, or a ground reading. "Institution X caused change Y in place Z" remains a candidate until the ground pays for that specific bond.
