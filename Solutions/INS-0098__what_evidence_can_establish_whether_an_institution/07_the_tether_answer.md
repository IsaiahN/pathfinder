# The Tether's answer: what evidence can establish institutional causation

**Mode: `specified` on the substantive gates, `general` on the structural shape, not `grounded`.** This is a shape and a route, not a result about any particular institution. Simulated derivation checked against the figures (`05_what_shape_means.md` section 6), not a ground reading.

## 1. The residual it answers

We observe an institution I and a change in an outcome Y (trust, growth, corruption or conflict). The residual R is the gap between the prediction "I produced that change" and the observation that I and Y co-occurred. Four bonds fit that observation: production (I ⇒ Y), reverse sequence (Y → I), a common cause (Z ⇒ I and Z ⇒ Y), and no bond (¬). Fig 12 :95-96: *"Two entries with an identical ingredient set are not variants of one thing. They are different things whose notation cannot tell them apart, and the difference is carried entirely by the bond."*

The four outcomes are a union compound. Fig 9 :57: *"a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either"*. Each outcome gets its own loop and its own ground; they are never averaged into one score.

## 2. The shape: four gates, kept separate

1. **A bond must be named before a method.** "Caused a change" may mean production, comparison over time, sequence with a counterfactual, or a cycle. Fig 12 :112: *"A plan is a sequence. A goal is a comparison. Progress is a subtraction. A refutation is a negation, and that one is not a bond."* Each bond admits different evidence. See `06_isomers_and_order.md`.
2. **The source of variation must not update on the outcome.** Fig 2 :29-30: *"An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks. A measurement, an experiment, a counter, a market, a patient outcome. If it moves when you push on it, it is not an anchor."* Concretely: an assignment, lottery, cutoff or eligibility shock that units cannot manipulate and that does not respond to trust, growth, corruption or conflict. Fig 2 :33: *"The anchor must not update."*
3. **The outcome must be outside the slice being graded.** Fig 10 :93: *"A ground with a reputation to protect is a ground someone benefits from, and its keeper is thereby disqualified."* An institution's own report of its effect is not an anchor. The measure must be something the institution cannot move.
4. **Effects must be read per slot and the refuter written before the run.** Fig 1 :22: *"Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average."* Fig 5 :65-66: *"The remedy is a discipline rather than a branch: make the sought-for shape an ORDINARY TERM. Write down what an answer would look like, bind it, and let it accrue a residual like anything else."*

A fifth rule governs how these are combined: **witnesses, not volume.** Fig 7 :13: *"agreement compounds, evidence does not"*. Two low-overlap designs, with disjoint evidence pools, beat ten replications of the same survey. Fig 2 :42: *"Buy independence in proportion to the cost of being wrong."*

## 3. What it explicitly does not say

- Not that any named estimator is derived by the framework. Randomization, RD, IV and DiD are bindings from the statistics frame onto the anchor shape, provenanced as such. Naming one does not add a primitive (Fig 6 :22).
- Not that correlation is worthless. It is a filter for where to look; Fig 9 :60: *"Use filters for the budget and witnesses for the verdict. Never let a filter hand you a verdict."*
- Not that "caused" always means production of a brand-new quantity. Trust and corruption exist before most reforms; the bond is usually a comparison or a cycle.
- Not that the four outcomes are one problem. They are four loops with four anchors.
- Not that a published, prestigious or well-cited study settles anything. Fig 2 :35: *"Alignment is triangulation, not negotiation. A mirror cannot correct you."*
- Not `grounded`. No measurement of any institution was made in this run.

## 4. What would settle it — the ground

A source-discriminating design, not more argument. From *The Hard Problem is About Source*: an instrument that reads green *"cannot comment on the source"*; the spectrometer, not a debate, resolved it. Here the corresponding tests are:

- **Anchor Test:** does the source of variation move with the outcome? If it does, the design cannot separate production from common cause.
- **Measure Test:** can the institution whose reform is studied move the outcome measure by changing its own reporting? If yes, that measure is not an anchor.
- **Slot Test:** does the effect survive disaggregation, with subgroup boundaries set before the outcome was seen?
- **Refuter Test:** was the failure pattern registered before the run, and did it fire?
- **Transport Test:** what is the round-trip gap when the estimate is carried to a second setting? Fig 4 :30: *"So take the round trip and measure the gap. Without it you are navigating by dead reckoning: confident about a position nobody checked."*
- **Standing refuters from the audit:** a design whose source responds to the outcome yet still separates the bonds; a bond recoverable from the operands alone; a global average preserving every live slot; same-pool replications counted as independent; a zero-gap transport.

## 5. The honest stop

**Unreached, not unreachable.** The figures allow a verdict of `gap`: Fig 8 :39 *"So a search that finds nothing is never a proof of absence."* This answer stops at the shape of the evidence, because no ground reading of a specific institution was taken. It records the lineage (which bonds were censused, which gates were derived, which methods were only bound) so the stop is checkable. *"Indiscernibility is not identity."* A specific causal claim about a specific reform stays `candidate` until a design with a real anchor pays for it.

## 6. One-paragraph version for outreach

> The Tether does not tell you which institution caused which trend, and it does not anoint a favourite estimator. It says that "caused" is a claim about a bond, and a before-and-after correlation carries no bond: the same pattern fits production, reverse causation, a hidden common cause, or nothing at all. So the evidence must have four properties: it must name which bond is meant; it must come from a source of variation that does not move when the outcome moves; the outcome must be measured by something the institution cannot move; and the effect must be read per unit, with the failure pattern written down before the run. Two designs with disjoint evidence are worth more than ten studies sharing one survey. What is offered here is the shape any admissible answer must have, five falsifiers written in advance, and the instruments that would close it. The specific case stays open until the ground pays.
