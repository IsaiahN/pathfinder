## What the wider literature bears on the question

1. **The four rival bonds are a standard problem, not a rhetorical device.** The Causality and Confounding pages both state that association alone does not determine causation and that common causes must be handled by design or by explicit assumptions. This corroborates F1 and F2 from a field that does not draw on the figures.
2. **The anchor shape is independently described.** Natural experiments generate exposure from factors outside the investigator's control; the NIST page requires the plan to be laid out before the experiment. Both are the external source of variation in F3, reached from engineering and epidemiology rather than from the framework.
3. **Falsification before the run has a public institution.** The AEA RCT Registry makes the pre-registered design public. This is F10 expressed as a venue rather than as an argument.
4. **Source discrimination needs an instrument.** Hill's nine viewpoints are explicitly *not* hard-and-fast rules; he ends with the question of whether there is any other way of explaining the facts. That is exactly the bond-identification residual, stated in epidemiology in 1965. The Hard Problem essay's spectrometer point corroborates it from measurement theory, but shares an author with the figures, so its weight is low.

## Distribution: offer, do not push

Figure-grounded reasons:

- **Fig 4 :22** *"Only methods cross up. Only head starts come down."* Sending the whole framework before a reader has described a residual sends a recording. Lead with the bond census and the refuter; offer the links after.
- **Fig 10 :42** *"A verifier that can reconstruct the claim cannot verify it."* A recipient who has absorbed the interiors before giving a verdict has joined the frame being checked.
- **Fig 9 :27-28** *"describe it before you go looking, or any frame you pick will seem to fit."* Ask what pattern they would expect if a production bond held, before showing the essay list.
- **Fig 2 :40** *"agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool"*; a mass send chosen for people who already agree produces a convention, not corroboration.
- **Fig 10 :59** *"an interest in the outcome is unavoidable, an interest in the reading is disqualifying"*; declare any funding or institutional stake in the first message.

**Recommended order in a reply:** (1) the shape claim and its five refuters; (2) ask what they would count as a failure; (3) record their stage-1 verdict; (4) only then the links, with the note that the verbatim figures, not the summary, are the authority.

## The two-stage offer

**(1)** Validate the shape claim against the ground. **(2)** Only if curious, read the framework that produced it. **(3)** Use the formula recursively on other problems. The order follows the figures:

- Stage 1 before stage 2 is Fig 10 :52 *"Bring a characterised residual, never the source"* and Fig 2 :45 *"Frames range against the ground. They do not negotiate with it."*
- Stage 2 is opt-in by construction. Once a recipient has absorbed the framework, their agreement stops being evidence. The stage-1 verdict must be recorded before any theory is shown, and later agreement is never pooled as corroboration.
- Stage 3 sends a method, which is permitted: Fig 4 :22 *"Only methods cross up. Only head starts come down."* The formula is a generator, so giving it away does not give away an answer.
- Stage 3 still needs a ground per problem. `THE_FORMULA.md` "Direction" says a frame *"cannot check the direction of its own question, because the shape a frame is searching for is not a term it holds."* Each user must write down what would refute their result before running it.
