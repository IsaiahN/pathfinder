**Subject:** One checkable claim about institutional causal evidence, before any theory

> Hi [name],
>
> I have one output I would like you to check against what you already know. I am not asking you to read the framework first, and I am not asking you to agree with it. If the output does not survive, there is nothing else to look at.
>
> **The output (`specified`, not `grounded`).** Saying an institution *caused* a change in trust, growth, corruption or conflict is a claim about a bond, not about two things that were observed together. The same before-and-after pattern fits at least four bonds: the institution produced the change; the change produced the institution; a third thing produced both; or nothing connected them. The bond is not recoverable from the operands, so more observations of the same operands cannot decide it. That much is a structural claim from a composition theory, written down before any case was examined.
>
> **What the shape requires.** Evidence earns the word "caused" only when it comes from a source of variation in the institution that does not move when the outcome moves: an assignment, a lottery, a cutoff, an eligibility shock that units cannot manipulate. Two further gates are separate from that one, not substitutes for it: the outcome must be measured by something outside the institution's own reporting, and the effect must be visible per unit and subgroup rather than only in a national average. Same-pool replication is not a fourth gate; ten studies using the same survey are one instrument counted ten times.
>
> **What would refute the shape claim:**
> 1. a design whose source of variation responds to the outcome, yet still separates the rival bonds;
> 2. a case where the bond is determined by the operands alone, with no design, timing or external record;
> 3. a global average shown to preserve every live slot's causal information;
> 4. two replications sharing one evidence pool counted as independent corroboration, with no ground objection;
> 5. a measured round trip between two genuinely different settings with zero gap in what the estimate can hold.
>
> I wrote these down before sending this. If any one holds, I will retract the claim. A one-line "this fails because..." is more useful to me than agreement. If you would rather not engage, no reply is needed.
>
> **What I am not claiming:** that any named estimator is *the* answer the framework derives. Randomization, cutoffs, instrumental variables and difference in differences are bindings from the statistics frame onto a shape the figures already return. They add no new primitive. I am also not claiming that "the institution caused it" always means production of a brand-new thing; often it is a comparison over time, and the claim has to say which bond it means. And I am not claiming this is settled by measurement. It is `specified`.
>
> **If it survives, and only then:** the sources are below. The figures are the authority, not my summary of them.
> - Verbatim figures and formula (local canonical extract): `FRAMEWORK/reference/03-FIGURES_TEXT.md`
> - Canonical usage rule: `FRAMEWORK/reference/FIGURES_README_canonical.md`

>
