## QUESTION

Decide, in effect terms, what evidence could establish that an institution caused a change in trust, growth, corruption or conflict. Option A: accept a before-and-after association, a prestigious evaluation, or a count of same-pool replications as establishing causation. Option B: require a named bond, a source of variation that does not update on the outcome, an outcome measure outside the institution's own reporting, per-unit effects with a pre-registered refuter, and witnesses from disjoint evidence pools. What each leads to: A spends the independence of the people checking it and mistakes a shared evidence pool for corroboration; B sends a checkable shape with stated falsifiers and an open slot until a ground pays for a specific case.

## DOMAIN CENSUS

R by arity, symmetry and scale, then one line per domain with actors, currency and anchor; partition or union; order by dependency.

- **R (the residual).** The gap between the prediction "institution I produced change Y" and the observation that I and Y co-occurred. Arity: four outcomes, so a **union compound**, not one hard question; Fig 9 :57 says a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either. Symmetry: the claimed bond is ordered (sequence or production), not a symmetric conjunction. Scale: per unit and per period first, then cross-setting transport. What cannot be read is the bond itself.
- **Domain 1, observational association.** Actors: variables, samples, estimators. Currency: covariance and fitted coefficients. Anchor: none nameable for causation; the measured data are frames, not the ground. Union with D2 when a design is added. Verdict: association only.
- **Domain 2, exogenous-variation design.** Actors: assignment, lottery, cutoff, policy shock, treatment and comparison groups. Currency: variation that does not respond to the outcome. Anchor: a source that does not move when the outcome moves. Question type: the frame whose closure predicts the shape of the bond. Verdict: decisive identification frame.
- **Domain 3, outcome measurement.** Actors: audits, surveys, administrative records, the institution's own reports. Currency: contact with the real outcome. Anchor: the actual trust, growth, corruption or conflict measured by something the institution cannot move. Verdict: necessary and load-bearing.
- **Domain 4, verification and replication.** Actors: expert readers, replications, registers. Currency: agreement and residual. Anchor: the ground, never agreement. Verdict: evidence only when closures differ.
- **Domain 5, transport.** Actors: settings, populations, time periods. Currency: a carried estimate. Anchor: the ground in the new setting. Verdict: nonzero round-trip gap.
- **Order by dependency (Fig 3).** Measure the outcome per slot; name the candidate bonds; say what would count as a refusal; choose a design with a non-updating anchor; register the refuter before looking; replicate in a different closure; measure the round trip.

Strain test applied: Fig 11 :40 says an improvement that does not change contact changes nothing. Read as a claim about an agent's capability, not about the value of a better design; a better design changes contact with the ground. No strain after re-census. The production-versus-comparison ambiguity in "caused a change" was resolved by unbundling the bond, not by smoothing it over.

## VERDICT (from the figures)

The shape survives; any claim about a specific institution stays `candidate`. Co-occurrence does not carry a bond, so four gates govern admissible evidence: name the bond; use a source of variation that does not update on the outcome; measure the outcome outside the institution's own reporting; read effects per unit with a pre-registered refuter. Witnesses from disjoint evidence pools count, same-pool volume does not, and transport carries a nonzero round-trip gap. Named estimators are bindings from the statistics frame onto this shape, not derivations from the figures. The four outcomes are a union compound with four grounds, never one averaged score. This is a `specified`, ungrounded result. The strongest presentation point is structural and external: Hill's 1965 warning that viewpoints help us study association before crying causation, echoed by the figure rule that a filter must never hand you a verdict.

- **cost if wrong:** bounded and locatable. The packet states its mode, names the rival bonds and writes its refuters before the conclusion, so an error is findable rather than invisible. The real cost is spending the independence of outside readers by sending a conclusion instead of a generator; Fig 2 :42 prices that as *"Buy independence in proportion to the cost of being wrong."* It is avoidable by leading with the shape and the five refuters.
- **undoable:** yes. The binding of a named method to the shape can be withdrawn without touching the derivation; `07_the_tether_answer.md` and `08_expert_verification_packet.md` already keep them separate. No primitive has to be withdrawn, and a sent claim can be superseded by a corrected packet.
- **reversed by:** a ground reading, not agreement. A design on a real institution whose source of variation demonstrably does not move with the outcome, with an independent outcome measure and a fired or unfired pre-registered refuter, would move that specific claim toward `grounded`. A design whose source moves with the outcome yet still separates the bonds, a bond recoverable from operands alone, a global average preserving every live slot, same-pool replications counted as independent, or a zero-gap transport would break the shape. Until one of these fires, the verdict stands as `candidate`.
