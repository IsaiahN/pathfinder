# Expert verification packet — institutional causal evidence

**What this is:** a claim in a form a researcher in causal inference, political methodology or evaluation can check. Mode, derivation and refuters are stated before the conclusion, so a reader knows what to look for and what would settle it.

**Mode: `specified` on the gates, `general` on the structural shape, not `grounded`.** No measurement of any institution is claimed. This is a shape and a route, not a result.

**Status:** a simulated derivation checked for internal consistency against the figures, not a ground reading. It cannot settle the claim it reports on.

## 1. The claim, in one paragraph

Saying an institution caused a change in trust, growth, corruption or conflict is a claim about a bond, and the bond is not carried by the operands. The same before-and-after pattern fits production, reverse causation, a hidden common cause, or no connection. Evidence earns the word "caused" only when four separate gates pass: the bond is named; the source of variation in the institution does not respond to the outcome; the outcome measure is outside the institution's own reporting; and effects are read per unit with the failure pattern registered before the run. Witnesses from disjoint evidence pools count; volume from one pool does not. This is a `specified` shape, not a finding about any real reform.

## 2. Three checkable sub-claims

### C1 — framework-internal; checkable by reading Figures 12 and 2

- **Statement:** co-occurrence of an institution and an outcome does not identify a causal bond. The rival bonds must be refused separately, not averaged into one association.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 12 :46-47 — *"A bond is the typed relation between two operands. There are seven, they are not interchangeable, and which one holds is not recoverable from the operands. A recipe that names only its ingredients has recorded half of itself."* Also Fig 12 :95-96 — *"Two entries with an identical ingredient set are not variants of one thing. They are different things whose notation cannot tell them apart, and the difference is carried entirely by the bond."* And Fig 2 :35 — *"Alignment is triangulation, not negotiation. A mirror cannot correct you."*
- **Refuter:** exhibit a case where the bond is determined by the operands alone, with no design, timing or external record; or show that two frames sharing one evidence pool still give independent corroboration.

### C2 — design-facing; checkable by a researcher against their own methods

- **Statement:** the decisive evidence has the shape of a source of variation that does not move when the outcome moves, paired with an outcome measure the institution cannot move. The named methods in the literature (random assignment, regression discontinuity at a non-manipulable cutoff, instrumental variables, difference in differences with a credible parallel trend) are bindings onto this shape, not things the figures derive.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 2 :29-30 — *"An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks. A measurement, an experiment, a counter, a market, a patient outcome. If it moves when you push on it, it is not an anchor."* Also Fig 10 :93 — *"A ground with a reputation to protect is a ground someone benefits from, and its keeper is thereby disqualified."*
- **What to check:** for a study you know, can the source of variation be moved by the outcome (for example, a reform adopted *because* trust rose)? Can the outcome measure be shifted by the institution's own reporting? If either is yes, the study has not passed the shape, whatever its estimate says.
- **Refuter:** exhibit a design whose source of variation responds to the outcome yet still separates production from common cause; or show an institution-controlled outcome measure that cannot be moved by its keeper.

### C3 — verification-facing; checkable against the record

- **Statement:** same-pool replication is not corroboration, and transport carries a nonzero round-trip gap. The lineage of a design is part of the evidence, not documentation of it.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 7 :13 — *"same closure, handed along; agreement compounds, evidence does not"*. Fig 8 :50-52 — *"Convergent derivation and adopted import look identical in the contents. Only the record of where each came from separates them, and the surplus lives only where the closures differ."* Fig 4 :30 — *"So take the round trip and measure the gap. Without it you are navigating by dead reckoning: confident about a position nobody checked."* Fig 13 :130 — *"A term carries the atoms it was built from and the bonds that joined them. That record is not documentation of the term. It is the term."*
- **Refuter:** show two replications with genuinely disjoint evidence that still add nothing; or measure a round trip between two different closures with zero gap.

## 3. What is explicitly not claimed

- Not that any named estimator is derived by the figures, or is uniquely correct. Naming a held method is a binding from the statistics frame onto an already-held shape; it adds no primitive (Fig 6 :22).
- Not that correlation is useless. It is a filter for where to look, not a witness for the verdict: Fig 9 :60 — *"Use filters for the budget and witnesses for the verdict. Never let a filter hand you a verdict."*
- Not that "caused" always means production of a new quantity. Trust and corruption pre-exist most reforms; the bond may be a comparison or a cycle.
- Not that the four outcomes are one question. They are four loops with four anchors and four grounds.
- Not that a famous or well-cited study settles anything. Prestige can fund a check; it cannot be the check.
- Not `grounded`. No specific institution was examined in this run.

## 5. What would settle it

A source-discriminating design on a real case, not a further argument.

1. **Anchor Test:** does the source of variation move with the outcome?
2. **Measure Test:** can the institution move the outcome measure by changing its own reporting?
3. **Slot Test:** does the effect survive disaggregation with prespecified subgroup boundaries?
4. **Refuter Test:** was the failure pattern registered before the run, and did it fire?
5. **Transport Test:** what is the round-trip gap in a second setting?

## 6. What an expert can check in twenty minutes

1. Read Fig 12 :46-47, Fig 2 :29-30, Fig 7 :13 and Fig 4 :30, four short passages.
2. Check C2 against one study you know well: name its source of variation and ask whether the outcome could move it.
3. Ask whether its outcome measure is controlled by the institution being evaluated.
4. Name one way in which two supposedly independent replications might actually share one evidence pool.
5. Send the refutation. A failure is more useful than an endorsement.
