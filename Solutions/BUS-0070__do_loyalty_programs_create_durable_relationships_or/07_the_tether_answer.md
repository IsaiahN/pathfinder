# The Tether's answer on loyalty programs and durable relationships

**Mode: `specified` - not `grounded`.** This is a shape and a route, not a result. Simulated derivation checked against the figures (`05_what_shape_means.md` section 8), not a ground reading. No program-removal measurement pays for any claim here.

## 1. The residual it answers

An answer would explain why the gap between what a program predicts a customer will do and what the customer actually does stays small for years in some customer-slots and grows quickly in others, and whether that small gap is maintained by contact with what the customer values or by the program's own points, tiers and discounts. R is indexed per customer, never averaged (Fig 1 :19, :22). The question is about **duration of contact and what kind of thing maintains it**, not about the level of repeat purchasing.

The either/or must be unbundled before it is searched (Fig 9 :54). It is two conditions, not one hard question:

- **C1:** the low residual would survive removal of the instrument and a change of habitat, because contact with what the customer values changed.
- **C2:** the low residual lasts only while the channel keeps the bracket matched to behaviour; points, tiers and discounts redirect demand, and purchases move when a rival offers a better bracket.

A third class, named by sibling question BUS-0064, is neither: habit, switching costs or a lack of alternatives. It is an honest `gap` here because no source-discriminating instrument is held.

## 2. The shape

1. **Program metrics are not the anchor.** Fig 1 :27: *"They are frame-internal. A frame cannot score itself with a quantity it also produces."* Enrollment, redemption and member repeat rate are produced by the program. Fig 2 :29 names *"a market"* and *"a measurement, an experiment, a counter"* as anchors. The anchor here is revealed choice under a discriminator, not the ledger.
2. **A repeat purchase is a reading, not a source.** Fig 9 :30-32: *"your words are borrowed too, so describe the effect"*, *"rather than the cause"*, *"you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself"*. The source essay's metamerism rule says an instrument *"cannot comment on the source"* and that *"the answer is unreached until a better instrument exists"*. The reading is consistent with genuine preference change, points response, habit, a switching cost or no alternative.
3. **Quick shifting is channel failure.** Fig 10 :24: *"the ground does not decay, the channel does"*; :26: *"saturated metrics, pooled readings"*; :29: *"→ keep the anchor reachable"*. Fig 7 :37-39 gives the two-factor form: bracket fidelity times ground pressure, and zero either stops the chain. Khodakarami et al. (2024) measure purchases following reward qualification at a competing firm; Klemperer (1995) describes introductory offers, price wars and the exploitation of locked-in customers. Different fields, same channel structure.
4. **Durability requires contact renewed from a differing closure.** Fig 11 :39: *"capability is a property of agent-and-habitat, never of the agent alone."* Fig 11 :40: *"An improvement that does not change contact changes nothing, however much it improves."* Fig 6 :23: *"No frame escapes its own closure. The bound moves only on contact with a frame whose closure differs."* A program that only recomputes next quarter's offer from this quarter's points ledger stays inside its own closure. Fig 8 :32: *"Closure is idempotent. Composing inside a closed set never leaves it, so no search, however long, adds an atom. This one is definitional: it is what closure means."*
5. **The retention device can change sign.** Fig 11 :49-51: valence is relative to the residual and no standing list of good actors survives a change of residual. Fig 12 :119: a catalyst is a part that survives its own use, and *"nothing records which a term is"*. The progress that binds a customer to one firm can be the thing that releases them once the reward is reached there.
6. **Two customers with the same program can still differ.** Under Fig 12 :117 they are isomers if the bonds differ: *"same atoms, different bond, different substance."* This is why a program inventory alone cannot predict persistence. Liu (2007) measures exactly that heterogeneity.
7. **Never answer with an average over customers.** Fig 1 :22: *"Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average."* Fig 9 :60: *"Use filters for the budget and witnesses for the verdict. Never let a filter hand you a verdict."* "Members spend 20 percent more" is a frame-internal, pooled reading.

## 3. What it explicitly does not say

- Not `grounded`. No program-removal measurement has paid for this shape.
- Not a name. It does not say which program, which feature, which tier design or which segment wins. Fig 9 :32: *"you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself."*
- Not an average. It makes no claim about the percentage of variance attributable to "loyalty", "program" or "relationship". Such numbers are filters for budget, not witnesses (Fig 9 :60).
- Not a claim that programs never create durable relationships. C1 is a real possibility; the claim is that it must be discriminated from C2 at the ground.
- Not a collusion claim. Whether reward programs facilitate interfirm coordination is a different question about firm conduct, not about customer-slot duration.
- Not corroboration by agreement. Leenheer, Liu and Khodakarami share one evidence pool (Fig 2 :31, :40). Klemperer (economics) and Wood and Neal (cognition) corroborate mechanism, not a program-level verdict.

## 4. What would settle it - the ground

A source-discriminating instrument, not a further argument. The source essay's metamerism rule: an instrument that reads a result *"cannot comment on the source"*, and *"the answer is unreached until a better instrument exists"*. Here the reading is repeat purchase; the candidate sources are genuine preference change, points response, habit, switching cost and lack of alternatives.

The falsifiers, pre-registered in `01` and repeated in `08`:

1. a program-internal metric predicts prospectively and per customer whose low residual survives removal, with provenance showing it is not derived from the program's own ledger;
2. one intervention restores both conditions in both failure types with nothing categorically different left over;
3. an instrument discriminates the sources per customer and its classifications survive program removal;
4. purchases shift among firms while the channel measures fresh, unsaturated and unmatched, and preference measurement is unchanged;
5. reach renews purely by internal composition, with no atom from a differing closure, yet the low residual survives removal across a change of habitat;
6. a program's measured effect is invariant across a documented change of residual.

A randomized or natural program-removal study that measures C1 and C2 separately per customer, records the bonds as well as the atoms, and reports which condition failed before the switch would turn this from `specified` toward `grounded`.

## 5. The honest stop

**Unreached, not unreachable.** Fig 13 :134: *"Unreachable is a verdict here, not a silence,"* and Fig 8 :39: *"So a search that finds nothing is never a proof of absence."* The shape stops at the limit of the available instruments and records the lineage so the stop is checkable. The missing thing is a discriminator of sources, characterized in effect terms but not held. Naming a winning program from a purchase reading would be the metamerism error the source essay forbids. The seven claims in section 2 are a shape; the eighth thing, which program actually works for which customer, is not held and is not pretended.

## 6. One-paragraph version for outreach

> The Tether does not rank loyalty programs or name the feature that wins. It says that "the program works" is two conditions that have to be told apart per customer. A durable relationship exists only where the low prediction error would survive removal of the program and a change of what competitors offer; that is where contact with what the customer values actually changed. Where the low error lasts only while points, tiers and discounts keep the bracket matched, the program is mostly shifting purchases and discounts among firms, and the shift tracks reward qualification and competitor matching rather than any change in what the customer values. Quick loss is the channel going stale, saturated, pooled into an average or simply matched by a rival. The same points balance can change sign: the progress that binds a customer to one firm can be the thing that releases them once the reward is reached there. Two customers with the same program can differ, because the arrangement carries information the ingredient list does not. The market, not the program ledger, is the anchor. What is offered is that shape, six falsifiers written in advance, and the instrument that would close it. Which program occupies which condition stays open until a source-discriminating instrument exists, and the stop is recorded rather than filled.
