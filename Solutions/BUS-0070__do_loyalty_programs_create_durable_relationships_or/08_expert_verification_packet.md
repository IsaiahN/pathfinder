## 1. The claim, in one paragraph

"Loyalty programs work" is not one thing. It is two conditions that have to be told apart per customer. **C1, contact maintained:** the customer's low prediction error would survive removal of the program and a change of competitive habitat, meaning the program changed contact with something the customer actually values. **C2, channel maintained:** the low error lasts only while points, tiers and discounts keep the bracket matched to behaviour; remove or match them and the purchase moves to a rival. These are two different failure modes with two different instruments, not two degrees of one problem. The same points balance can change sign: the progress that binds a customer to one firm can be the thing that releases them once a reward is reached there. Two customers with the same program can differ because the arrangement carries information the ingredient list does not. The market, not the program ledger, is the anchor. The claim is a shape any candidate answer must have; it does not name which program, feature or segment wins.

## 2. Three checkable sub-claims

### C1 - Framework-internal; checkable by reading Figures 1, 2 and 10

- **Statement:** program metrics are frame-internal and cannot be the anchor; the market and revealed choice under a discriminator are; quick purchase shifting is a failure of the channel, not a decay of the ground.
- **Source, verbatim:**
  - Fig 1 :27 - *"They are frame-internal. A frame cannot score itself with a quantity it also produces."*
  - Fig 2 :29 - *"An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks."* The same line names *"a measurement, an experiment, a counter, a market, a patient outcome."*
  - Fig 10 :24 - *"the ground does not decay, the channel does"*; Fig 10 :26 - *"saturated metrics, pooled readings"*; Fig 10 :29 - *"→ keep the anchor reachable"*
- **What a researcher can check against their own knowledge:** Leenheer et al. (2007) report a *"small positive yet significant effect"* of membership on share of wallet that is *"seven times smaller than is suggested by a naïve model that ignores the endogeneity of program membership."* Does that naive model not score the program with a quantity the program itself produces? Khodakarami et al. (2024) find that *"a customer’s purchase probability at the focal firm decreases after they qualify for a reward independent of redemption at a competing firm"*. Is that not a channel effect rather than a change in what the customer values? A "no" to both is a real refutation of the mapping.
- **Refuter:** a program-internal metric that predicts prospectively, per customer, whose low residual survives program removal, with provenance showing it does not derive from the program's ledger; or purchases shifting among firms while the channel measures fresh, unsaturated and unmatched and preference measurement is unchanged. Either fires against C1.

### C2 - Framework-internal derivation plus two low-overlap checks

- **Statement:** internal agreement and internal recomputation do not renew reach; durability requires contact with a closure that differs; the retention device changes valence when the residual changes.
- **Source, verbatim:**
  - Fig 8 :32 - *"Closure is idempotent. Composing inside a closed set never leaves it, so no search, however long, adds an atom. This one is definitional: it is what closure means."*
  - Fig 6 :23 - *"No frame escapes its own closure. The bound moves only on contact with a frame whose closure differs."*
  - Fig 11 :39 - *"So the answerable question is not what intelligence is, but what makes a system capable. And capability is a property of agent-and-habitat, never of the agent alone."*
  - Fig 11 :50 - *"The same actor is a benefactor to one residual and a malfactor to another, so mutualism and parasitism are relations rather than kinds."* Fig 12 :119 - *"catalyst. A part that survives its own use. Spent and regenerating parts behave differently across uses, and nothing records which a term is."*
- **What a researcher can check against their own knowledge:** Klemperer (1995), in economics, describes a choice between *"setting a low price to capture market share, and setting a high price to Harvest profits by exploiting its current locked-in customers"*. Is that not the channel half of C2 stated in another field? Delfabbro and King (2020), in behavioural public health, find that *"At least 40% of problem gamblers reported loyalty card use compared with only around 10% of gamblers in general"* while cautioning about causality. Does that not show the same device carrying a different valence at a different anchor? A "no" to both is a real refutation of the low-overlap corroboration.
- **Refuter:** a program that renews its reach purely by internal composition, with no atom from a differing closure, and whose low residual survives removal across a documented change of habitat; or a program whose measured effect is invariant across a documented change of residual. Either breaks idempotence or the valence claim and C2 with it.

### C3 - Structure and competing explanations

- **Statement:** the union of conditions must be unbundled before a lever is chosen; two customers with identical program exposure but different bonds are isomers, so a program report alone cannot predict persistence; a purchase reading does not name its source.
- **Source, verbatim:**
  - Fig 9 :57 - *"What remains is smaller and it is also categorically different: a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either."*
  - Fig 12 :117 - *"isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."*
  - Fig 9 :32 - *"you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself"*
  - Fig 1 :22 - *"Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average."*
- **What a researcher can check against their own knowledge:** Liu (2007) reports that heavy buyers claimed rewards without changing behaviour while light and moderate buyers *"gradually purchased more and became more loyal to the firm"*, and concludes there is a *"need to consider consumer idiosyncrasies when studying loyalty programs"*. Is a single program-level retention number not an average that discards exactly that difference? Wood and Neal (2007) write that *"Once a habit is formed, perception of contexts triggers the associated response without a mediating goal."* Can a repeat purchase, on that evidence, name the source a program report assigns to it?
- **Refuter:** one intervention that restores both conditions in both failure types and leaves no categorically different residue; or an averaged program-level statistic that predicts prospectively which named customer will lose contact next. Either collapses the union or defeats F9's methodological consequence.

## 3. What is explicitly not claimed

- Not `grounded`. No measurement has paid for any part of this.
- Not a theory of everything about marketing, and no final or unique theory claim.
- Not an average. No claim about the percentage of retention attributable to a program, a brand or a category.
- Not that points, tiers, discounts, culture or service quality are irrelevant. They are Γ, the library operand; the claim is that Γ alone does not settle durability.
- Not that any specific program or feature is the answer. That is a `gap` at the instrument boundary, not a hidden answer.
- Not that the marketing literature independently corroborates this. Leenheer, Liu and Khodakarami share one evidence pool. The low-overlap sources from economics, cognitive science and behavioural public health corroborate the mechanism shape, not the customer-level fact.

## 5. What would settle it

A randomized or natural program-removal study that measures C1 and C2 separately per customer, records the bonds as well as the atoms, and reports which condition failed before the switch. Concretely:

1. **Contact test:** does the customer's low residual persist after the program is removed or a rival enters, while an independently measured preference reading is unchanged? Fig 2 :29 requires an anchor that does not move with the frame.
2. **Channel test:** does the purchase follow reward qualification, tier proximity and competitor matching rather than any measured preference change? Indicators: stale percepts, saturated metrics, pooled readings (Fig 10 :25-26).
3. **Source-discriminating instrument:** can the study distinguish a genuine preference change from points response, habit, switching cost and lack of alternatives when all produce the same repeat-purchase reading? The source essay says a reading *"cannot comment on the source"*.
4. **Valence test:** does the same device help retention in one residual regime and hurt it in another, as Fig 11 :49-51 and Fig 12 :119 predict?

## 7. What an expert can check in twenty minutes

1. Read Fig 1 :27, Fig 2 :29, Fig 10 :24, Fig 8 :32, Fig 6 :23, Fig 11 :39 and :50, Fig 12 :117 and :119, Fig 9 :57 and Fig 1 :22. Ten short passages, about eight minutes.
2. Check C1 and C2 against your own knowledge of program effects: is there a documented case of a low residual surviving program removal across a change of habitat with no import from a differing closure? About five minutes.
3. Name one instrument that would discriminate a genuine preference change from points response, habit and lack of alternatives using observable customer data, or say why none exists. About five minutes.
4. Send the refutation. A failure is more useful than an endorsement.
