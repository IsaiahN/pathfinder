## Q1 - Are two customers with the same program isomers?

> **"isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."** (Fig 12 :117)
> **"functional group. A substructure whose presence fixes the outcome. The group says what kind; the qualifier says which member."** (Fig 12 :124)
> **"Symmetry is what decides whether an arrangement is a new thing."** (Fig 12 :92)

Two customers with the same points earned, the same tier and the same reward exposure have the same atoms. If their bonds differ, that is, if one composes those atoms into a long-run relationship and the other into a short-run reward chase, they are **isomers** under Fig 12 :117: same atoms, different bond, different behaviour, with the difference invisible in the program report. This is the figure-grounded version of "it is not what you offer, it is what the customer does with it", but stronger, because it says the arrangement is itself a substance and carries information the ingredients do not.

If the customers share only a pattern (both follow enroll, earn, redeem) while differing in which atoms occupy the slots, the weaker claim holds: they are members of the same **functional group** (Fig 12 :124), the qualifier saying which member. The presence of the pattern fixes the kind, not the outcome.

The distinction is testable. A program-level report that finds no difference between two customers cannot distinguish isomer from functional-group; the bond has to be recorded. Fig 12 :5: *"A set of ingredients is not one. The bond is the rest of the information, and it is the half nothing was recording."*

**Falsifier, written before the run:** if two customers with identical atoms and identical bonds nevertheless diverge in persistence with no difference in ground contact or imported atoms, the isomer framing is insufficient and the difference must be habitat (D3), which would move the explanation out of D1.

**Status:** `specified`, triangulated for the legality of the reading (Fig 12 and Fig 11 :50, different figures), single-source for the customer application. The world corroboration is Liu's finding that heavy buyers claimed rewards without changing behaviour while light and moderate buyers increased purchasing, plus Khodakarami's competitive reward effects, but these are the same broad marketing evidence pool.

## Q2 - What order do the conditions bind in?

> **"The chain is ordered by dependency, not by importance. Each link consumes what the one above produces, so effort spent above the break arrives at a step that cannot use it."** (Fig 3 :20-21)
> **"Optimising a link that already works cannot rescue one that does not."** (Fig 3 :19)

Order for a firm facing a changed competitive market:

1. **Perception.** Can it read what the customer actually does, rather than what the ledger says? Failure here is visible and has obvious tests.
2. **Vocabulary.** Does its Γ have terms for what the customer affords beyond points and tiers, and can it say what would count as progress there? This is the middle link. Fig 3 :26: *"The link between them, having terms for what a situation offers and being able to say what would count as progress, has neither, so it attracts the least attention and the fewest instruments."*
3. **Objective.** Can it state a comparison that would count as getting somewhere, for example "low residual survives instrument removal"? Fig 12 :112: *"A plan is a sequence. A goal is a comparison. Progress is a subtraction."* A vocabulary with only points and redemptions cannot state a goal.
4. **Planning.** A route to that objective under incomplete information.
5. **Learn and carry.** Name what worked, verify it, carry it to the next problem.

The two conditions from the primary audit map onto this chain: C1 (contact) is mostly a failure of links 1 and 2; C2 (channel) is mostly a failure of link 5, because the firm names what worked in points terms but never imports a new atom from a differing closure. That mapping is why a single lever, "more points", cannot clear both (Fig 9 :55).

The order also decides which instrument to buy first. Fig 3 :27: *"A link with no instrument is not a link that works. It is a link nobody has looked at."* The ledger-facing link (1) has earn and redemption data; link 5 has almost none. So budget will naturally pile up at links 1 and 4 while the break sits at 2 and 5.

**Falsifier:** a firm whose chain demonstrably works at 1, 2 and 3 and fails only at 4. That would falsify the "usually middle" claim of the primary audit's figure reading, though not the dependency ordering.

## Q3 - Competing explanations, and what each can and cannot reach

| candidate explanation | what it names | figure-line bearing | what it cannot reach |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Behavioural loyalty / share of wallet** (Leenheer et al. 2007) | the measured residual itself, corrected for self-selection | Γ is a real operand; the sevenfold naive gap is the frame scoring itself (Fig 1 :27) | It still reads a purchase and cannot say which source produced it. It needs the discriminator named in F3 |
| **Program long-term effects** (Liu 2007) | idiosyncratic response by starting patronage level | supports per-slot indexing (Fig 1 :19) and the union (Fig 9 :57) | A longitudinal association is not a removal experiment, so it cannot separate C1 from C2 at the ground |
| **Goal shielding / competitive reward qualification** (Khodakarami et al. 2024) | the competitor-dependent channel and the valence flip | close to C2 and F6 (Fig 10 :24, Fig 11 :49-51) | It is a marketing-pool binding. It predicts where purchases move, not whether contact with a valued ground changed |
| **Switching costs** (Klemperer 1995) | the lock-in instrument that makes market share pay later | close to Fig 10's channel and Fig 6's closure | Economics-pool binding with no source gate for this customer-level claim. Zero verdict weight until provenanced (Fig 8 :51) |
| **Habit / context cuing** (Wood and Neal 2007) | context-triggered response without a mediating goal | Fig 11 :48; the source essay's metamerism | Cognitive stratum; Fig 4 :20 requires the anchor to be re-named at the customer level. It corroborates the reading, not a program verdict |
| **Loyalty-program harm association** (Delfabbro and King 2020) | valence flip in a different field | Fig 11 :50, Fig 12 :119 at a different anchor | Self-selection is explicitly cautioned; it cannot be used as a causal program effect |
| **Attitudinal loyalty** (broad marketing literature) | a term in the customer's Γ | Fig 1 :27 says a frame cannot score itself with what it produces | A self-report moves when pushed, so it is not an anchor (Fig 2 :30) |
| **Luck / lack of alternatives** | an unearned early reading that persists while unfuted | Fig 8 :40: *"Underivable-in-principle and unreachable-at-this-budget are different facts"* | Cannot be excluded from inside a frame; Fig 8 :39: *"So a search that finds nothing is never a proof of absence."* Honest `gap`, not a rival verdict |

No candidate is discarded by prestige or familiarity. Each is ranged against the same residual, in the order Fig 8 :53 requires. The point is not to pick one; it is to notice that the first three, plus attitudinal loyalty, are all in one evidence pool (Fig 2 :31) and that the two low-overlap fields corroborate mechanism, not the customer-level fact.

## Q4 - Does the shape generate the literature's answers?

Partly. The shape can generate:

- the behavioural-loyalty emphasis on measured retention, as the residual slot under a frame that has not yet been discriminated (Fig 1 :19);
- the long-term-effects emphasis on heterogeneity, as C1 and C2 failing differently in different slots (Fig 9 :57);
- the competitive-program emphasis on reward qualification, as C2 and Fig 10 :24;
- the switching-cost emphasis on lock-in, as the channel component of C2;
- the valence-flip emphasis, as Fig 11 :49-51 and Fig 12 :119;
- the habit emphasis, as Fig 11 :48 and the source-discriminator limit.

It cannot generate the *name* of the specific program, feature or customer that will work next. That is the `gap` recorded in F11 of `01`. It is a gap at the instrument boundary, not a silence: Fig 13 :134 (*"Unreachable is a verdict here, not a silence,"*) and Fig 8 :39 apply. The essay's source rule states why: an instrument that reads a result *"cannot comment on the source"*, and *"the answer is unreached until a better instrument exists"*.

## CONCLUSION

Two customers with the same program but different bonds are isomers under Fig 12 :117; two customers sharing only an enroll-earn-redeem pattern are members of one functional group under Fig 12 :124. The conditions bind in dependency order, with the middle link (vocabulary and objective) and the carry link the usual breaks, which is why C1 and C2 need different instruments. The standard explanations are mostly bindings of one evidence pool and are generated by the shape only up to the point where a name would be required. The isomer claim has a written falsifier; the order claim has one; the naming gap is declared rather than filled.
