## QUESTION

Decide, in effect terms, what the Tether says about the claim that life could have begun in more than one environment with only one lineage surviving. Option A: read the single surviving lineage as evidence of a single origin in a single environment. Option B: read it as the output of a selection filter over an unknown number of arrivals, and refuse the count. What each leads to: A closes the search for a second genesis and forces a choice between venue hypotheses that the record may not discriminate; B gives a checkable shape, an open count, and a named instrument gap.

Before the run, bind the sought-for shape as an ordinary term: an answer must (i) say whether distinct arrival routes are composable, (ii) say why survivors are fewer than arrivals if they are, and (iii) say what reading would discriminate sources from one survivor. These three are the refusable object.

- **Ambiguity held in the census.** "More than one environment" means either one origin spanning several venues (M1) or several independent origins (M2). They share ingredients but differ in bond, so they are different terms. A third arrangement (M3), several origins followed by fusion or gene transfer, is a cycle.

## VERDICT (from the figures)

The shape survives; the count does not. Distinct arrival routes are composable, so more than one origin is not excluded. One survivor does not imply one arrival, because the unmaintained habitat keeps what the ground kept and a non-invertible transform turns a web into a tree. The count cannot be read from one survivor's record, and finding no second genesis is not proof of absence. "More than one environment" must be split: one origin using several venues is a different molecule from several independent origins, because the environment is part of the term. What is owed is a source-discriminating instrument described in effect terms, not a name and not another argument. This is a `specified`, ungrounded result with a `gap` at the count. The verdicts are kept separate by domain and are never averaged.

- **cost if wrong:** bounded and locatable. The packet states its mode, three checkable sub-claims and a pre-registered refuter, so an error is findable rather than invisible. The real cost is spending the independence of outside readers by sending a recording instead of a generator, which Fig 2 :42 prices: "Buy independence in proportion to the cost of being wrong." That cost is avoidable by leading with the shape and its refuter. A second, subtler cost is conflating M1 and M2, which would make a multi-venue chemistry result look like evidence for multiple independent origins.
- **undoable:** yes. The count claim was never made, so nothing factual must be withdrawn. The M1/M2 distinction and the union reading are derived additions and can be removed without touching the arrival or survival verdicts. A sent claim can be superseded by a corrected packet, and no primitive is withdrawn. A genuine refutation would be added to the record and would lower the reach, not be silently deleted.
- **reversed by:** a ground reading, not agreement. An assay that returns a definite number of independent sources from surviving molecular architecture would close D3. A demonstrably independent extant lineage would refute the single-survivor premise as a fact. A proof that early-Earth chemistry admits exactly one arrival route would break D1. A proof that several arrivals cannot collapse to one survivor would break D2. A demonstration that venue exchange changes no observable would break the M1/M2 distinction. Fig 2 :45: "Frames range against the ground. They do not negotiate with it." Until one of these fires, the verdict stands as `candidate` and the count as `gap`.
