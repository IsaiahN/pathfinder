# Supporting derivation: structure, order and analogues for multiple origins

**Mode: `specified` on the structure claim, `general` on the order claim.** Simulated derivation checked against the figures, not a ground reading (`05_what_shape_means.md` §6).

## RESIDUAL

The question's phrase "more than one environment" can be realised as at least three different arrangements. The figure set must be used to decide whether these are variants of one term or different terms, and whether an order can be assigned to them without importing a venue name.

## DOMAIN CENSUS

Atoms held: Space, Time, Energy (states); Gradient, Network, Spectrum (structures). Bonds held: the seven, including sequence →, production ⇒, conjunction +, and comparison ⋛. No new primitive is available without import. Venues (vent, hot spring, pond, pumice raft) are bindings from the geochemistry frame, not atoms of the six-term set.

## DERIVATION

### Q1: Is a venue part of the term or an external label?

- Fig 13 :76 "A gradient over space and one over time are different terms, because which state it is bonded to is part of what it is."
- Fig 12 :46 "A bond is the typed relation between two operands. There are seven, they are not interchangeable, and which one holds is not recoverable from the operands."
- ⇒ If an origin event is a molecule and an environment is one of its operands, the environment is part of the term. Two origins in two environments are two terms, not two spellings of one.
- **Falsifier:** show that the surviving molecular architecture is invariant under exchange of venue operands, and that no prediction distinguishes them. Then the venue is an external label and this claim fails.

### Q2: M1, M2, and the third arrangement

| arrangement | description | figure reading |
|---|---|---|
| M1 | one origin, several venues in one history | one molecule with several atoms; a conjunction + of venue operands under a single production ⇒ |
| M2 | several independent origins, several venues, one survivor | several first-order molecules, then a selection filter between them; Fig 7 :33 "variation inside, selection between" |
| M3 | several origins followed by fusion or gene transfer into one lineage | the output re-enters as input; Fig 12 :121 "cycle. The output re-enters as input." |

- Fig 12 :117 "isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."
- M1 and M2 need not have the same atoms, so they are not automatically isomers. They are different **molecules**: M2 contains a selection operator that M1 does not, and M3 contains a cycle that neither of the others contains.
- If all three are rewritten over the same full atom set (the six terms plus the venue bindings), then they become an isomer set: same atoms, different arrangement, different substance. That is a checkable structural claim, not an assertion about Earth history.
- **Falsifier:** if M2 or M3 can be written without an atom the others require, the isomer claim fails and the weaker "different molecules" claim stands instead.

### Q3: Can an order be assigned?

- Fig 12 :111 "A vocabulary without bonds can name a state and cannot name a route to one."
- Fig 12 :112 "A plan is a sequence. A goal is a comparison. Progress is a subtraction."
- → is sequence ("A then B; B needs A to have happened"), and ⇒ is production ("A produces B; B did not exist before"). An origin is ⇒, not →: the living thing did not exist before the event. A sequence of prebiotic stages may use →, but the transition itself is ⇒.
- The figures' own minimum-set derivation says order is *not* introduced at the level of the kinds. The canonical Substack article states that "we cannot say which state composed first, and we don't know if the order mattered." So writing an ordered chain of the six terms for an origin event adds an ordering the substrate disclaims.
- **Conclusion on order:** an order can be assigned to the *stages* of a proposed scenario under a geochemical instrument, but not to the six terms as such. The figure-grounded output is a partial order over licensed transitions, not a timeline. Mode `general`.

### Q4: Convergence versus common descent (the analogue that matters)

- Fig 8 :50 "But shared atoms are not the signature. Two frames can hold the same atom by convergence, reaching it separately, which is corroboration at maximum independence."
- Fig 8 :51 "Convergent derivation and adopted import look identical in the contents. Only the record of where each came from separates them, and the surplus lives only where the closures differ."
- Fig 13 :126 "WHY THE LINEAGE IS THE PROOF"
- The observed unity of biochemistry is a shared atom set across all extant life. Two readings fit it: inheritance from one ancestor, or convergence plus horizontal transfer among several ancestors. The contents do not separate them; only the record does, and the pre-LUCA record is gone.
- Kempes and Krakauer (2021) call life "homoplastic" rather than a universal homology, which is the external analogue of the convergence reading. Crapitto et al. (2022) describe LUCA as the sole survivor of multiple contemporaneous lineages, which is the external analogue of the filter. Agreement between these two is partly a shared pool, so it is counted once.
- **Falsifier:** a source-discriminating measurement of surviving molecular architecture that returns a unique ancestry. That would collapse convergence and common descent into one record and close the D3 gap.

### Q5: Two currencies again

- Fig 12 :125 "A composition costs in two currencies, and they do not add."
- Stating M3 (fusion and cycles) costs more bits than stating M1 (one origin, several venues). Settling M3 may cost fewer actions if a single gene-transfer signature is discoverable. The two readings must be reported side by side, not summed into a single "most likely" score. This is the same discipline as keeping the three domain verdicts separate in `01`.

## CONCLUSION

The three arrangements are different molecules under the bond rule, not variants of one term. They become isomers only under the stronger rewrite over a full shared atom set. An order can be assigned to stages under an instrument but not to the six terms themselves. Convergence and common descent are indistinguishable in content and separable only by the record, which has been lost; this is a shape verdict, not an event count. The falsifiers above are written before the run.
