## 0. The question under audit, stated as an effect

> Could life have begun in more than one environment, with only one lineage surviving?

**Effect statement.** We observe one surviving lineage. The question is whether that observation settles the number of arrival events or the number of environments in which they occurred. Option A: the unity of extant life (one genetic code, one chirality, ATP as energy currency, one LUCA) is proof of one arrival in one place. Option B: that unity is a reading from one survivor, and a filter over several arrivals can leave one descendant. A closes the search for a second genesis and forces a choice between venue hypotheses. B keeps the count open and names the instrument that would close it.

**Bind the sought-for shape as an ordinary term (Formula, "Direction").** The answer, whatever it is, must satisfy three conditions. (i) It must say whether distinct arrival routes are composable at all. (ii) It must say why survivors are fewer than arrivals, if they are. (iii) It must say what reading would discriminate one source from several. These three are written down here before the conclusion so they can accrue a residual and be refused.

**Ambiguity that must be censused before any figure is chosen.** "Begun in more than one environment" can mean two different things:

- **M1, one origin across several venues.** A single lineage whose prebiotic stages used several settings (for example, synthesis in one place and polymerisation in another). One arrival, several environments.
- **M2, several independent origins in different venues.** Several arrivals, several environments, one or zero survivors.

M1 and M2 share an ingredient set ("life", "environment") but differ in the bond between them. Treating them as one question would be the flat-formula error. Fig 12 :95 "Two entries with an identical ingredient set are not variants of one thing. They are different things whose notation cannot tell them apart, and the difference is carried entirely by the bond."

## 1. Domain census, before choosing any figure line

**R by arity, symmetry and scale.**

- **Arity.** The observation has arity one (one survivor). The question asks about a variable with arity one or many (arrivals). This is a many-to-one mapping, not a single number.
- **Symmetry.** Arrival events in different environments are non-interchangeable. Fig 13 :76 "A gradient over space and one over time are different terms, because which state it is bonded to is part of what it is." A vent arrival and a hot-spring arrival are different terms for the same reason: which environment it is bonded to is part of what it is. Under a symmetric bond k operands would have k! spellings and one term (Fig 12 :92); here the environment is part of the term, so the bond is ordered and the terms are distinct.
- **Scale.** An origin event is first order (local chemistry reaching a self-sustaining system). The surviving biosphere and LUCA are a higher-order composition. The question crosses a membrane: Fig 4 :21 "The membrane is the boundary between the sizes, and the rule about what may cross it. Not a wall: a filter with a direction." What crosses from arrivals to survivor is a filtered result, not a recording of every arrival.

**One line per domain: actors / currency / anchor.**

**Partition or union?** This is a **union compound**, not a partition. It bundles at least two conditions: (i) how many arrivals, and (ii) why one survivor. Fig 9 :54 "And when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again." Fig 9 :57 "a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either". A single lever, for example identifying LUCA's environment, clears the region where only one condition holds and leaves the intersection standing because the second condition still binds. The three domains are therefore not averaged into one verdict.

**Order by dependency (Fig 3).** Arrival (D1) before survival (D2) before evidence (D3). Each link consumes what the one above produces. Fig 3 :20 "The chain is ordered by dependency, not by importance." Reading the count of origins directly off the genes of one survivor, with no arrival model and no filter model, is a reading below the break: Fig 3 :22 "A step whose input never arrives cannot be diagnosed, only its predecessor can."

## 3. Findings

### F1: The question is a union compound and must be unbundled (governing finding)

Fig 9 :54 "And when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again."
Fig 9 :55 "A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones."
Fig 9 :57 "a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either"

The question as asked joins "more than one environment" with "only one lineage surviving". These are two conditions. A single lever, such as pinning down the environment of LUCA, answers the first condition in some readings and the second in none. The residue after that lever is categorically different: it is a compound of arrival count and filter, not a harder venue question. This is why the three domains above keep separate verdicts and why no average is taken.

**Refuter:** show that survivor count is a deterministic function of arrival count under all admissible conditions, so the two conditions are one variable. Then the union reading fails and a single loop is correct.

### F2: M1 and M2 are different terms, and conflating them is a re-census error

Fig 12 :95 "Two entries with an identical ingredient set are not variants of one thing. They are different things whose notation cannot tell them apart, and the difference is carried entirely by the bond."
Fig 12 :94 "The arrangement is the substance."

M1 (one origin, several venues) and M2 (several origins, several venues) share ingredients but differ in arrangement. M1 is a single molecule with several atoms in its history. M2 is several molecules with a filter between them and the survivor. The world literature makes the same distinction without the figure vocabulary: Mariscal et al. (2019) list one LUCA, multiple origins with extinction, and hybrid traces of a second origin as three currently indistinguishable scenarios; Pappas (2026) cautions that a last common ancestor is not the origin of life.

**Correction recorded, not erased.** My first reading treated "more than one environment" as already meaning M2. That was an item-sharing error. Re-census: the environment is part of the term (Fig 13 :76), and the bond decides whether we have one term or several. Both M1 and M2 remain live; the figures do not select between them.

**Refuter:** exhibit a single arrangement in which "one origin in several venues" and "several origins in several venues" make the same prediction for every observable. Then the distinction carries no information and should be dropped.

### F3: Distinct arrival routes are licensed, not forbidden (D1 verdict: triangulated)

Fig 13 :115 "The set is closed. The arrangements are not. That gap is why import exists."
Fig 13 :121 "Different routes can arrive at the same substance."
Fig 12 :117 "isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."
Fig 11 :43 "The habitat is enumerated, never composed."

The figure set licenses more than one distinct arrival route. It does not assert that more than one occurred, and it does not name a venue. The "shape, not a name" limit applies: the framework returns a kind of thing, not the answer itself (Fig 9 :32). Naming submarine vents or terrestrial hot springs as *the* cradle is a binding from the geochemistry frame onto an atom already in the library, not a derivation from the six-term set.

World corroboration at reduced independence: Kempes and Krakauer (2021) argue for multiple forms of life through multiple historical pathways; Omran and Pasek (2020) argue for a "yes and" mindset between dry-land hydrothermal fields and submarine vents. These are separate fields (theoretical biology and geochemistry), so their agreement has some independence, but both draw on the same early-E Earth literature, so it is one pool with two voices.

**Triangulation status: triangulated.** Two low-overlap figure lines (Fig 13 :115/:121 on arrangements, Fig 12 :117 on same-atoms-different-bond) plus the enumeration rule at Fig 11 :43. Verdict in D1: plurality is *composable*, not *observed*. Mode `general`.

**Refuter:** prove that under early-Earth boundary conditions the closure of plausible prebiotic chemistry admits exactly one arrival route. That would break the composition claim without needing any new instrument.

### F4: One survivor does not imply one arrival (D2 verdict: triangulated)

Fig 11 :56 "Unmaintained. No maintainer and no live frame in the seat. The habitat is pure contact, nothing selects, and what survives is what the ground kept."
Fig 11 :36 "Where nothing maintains anything, intelligence is a label applied afterwards to a lineage that met its ground. Nothing was intelligent; selection ran. The word does no work there."
Fig 7 :33 "variation inside, selection between"
Fig 7 :35 "A room's agreement is variation, not a verdict. Selection between rooms is where the ground votes."
Fig 10 :70 "If levels were copies there would be one lineage drifting and no branches. The tree of life is what a non-invertible transform looks like from outside."

This is the direct answer to the question as posed. A many-to-one mapping from arrivals to survivors is exactly the shape the figures predict in the unmaintained mode. Selection does not multiply surviving lineages to match arrivals; it keeps what the ground kept. Brunk and Marshall (2021) state that there are no data bearing directly on the number of times life originated, and that multiple origins have been argued likely if life was to have a reasonable chance of leaving descendants. Crapitto et al. (2022), following Fournier and Alm (2015), state that "the LUCA lineage was also the sole survivor of multiple contemporaneous lineages". These corroborate, from scenario methodology and phylogenomics respectively. The two sources share part of an evidence pool, so their agreement is counted once.

**Triangulation status: triangulated.** Three independent figure routes: habitat survival (Fig 11 :56), room selection (Fig 7 :33/:35), and non-invertible transform giving a tree from a web (Fig 10 :70). Verdict in D2: the observed single lineage is *not evidence of a single arrival*. Mode `specified` (a falsifiable prediction about the mapping), not `grounded`.

**Refuter:** show that several arrivals cannot collapse to one survivor, so that one extant lineage entails exactly one origin. That would break the survivorship step.

### F5: The count is not readable from one survivor's record (D3 verdict: gap for the count, general for the shape)

Fig 13 :128 "Nothing about a composed term tells you, from looking at it, which route produced it or whether that route was available. The space has no last route and every turn begins from a remainder the last one left, so the order is not recoverable from the artefact. It is recoverable from the lineage."
Fig 13 :126 "WHY THE LINEAGE IS THE PROOF"
Fig 10 :24 "the ground does not decay, the channel does"
Fig 2 :37 "Human judgment is the right anchor for is this a good answer for a person, because the question is about people. It is only an instrument for is this code correct, and instruments can be wrong on axes they do not measure."

The unity of the genetic code is a settled reading about *common descent among extant life*. It is not a reading about the number of origins. Several sources can give the same projected reading, which is the metamerism point in *The Hard Problem is About Source*: an instrument that reads green "cannot comment on the source". Mariscal et al. (2019) say the single-origin assumption "may simply be because this situation is currently indistinguishable from the scenario in which life originated several times". Cleland and Copley (2005) add that the standard detection tools "could not detect such organisms if they existed".

**Strain test.** Fig 13 :128 says the order is not recoverable from the artefact but is recoverable from the lineage. Does that conflict with Fig 10 :70's "tree of life" being a projection? Re-census: Fig 13 :128 concerns a composed term within a run, where the lineage is recorded; Fig 10 :70 concerns a transform between levels, where the web is flattened to a tree. Different objects, different quantities. No strain after re-census.

**Verdict in D3: `gap` for the count.** The figure set gives the shape of the problem (one reading, several possible sources, a lossy channel) but returns no count and no discriminating instrument. "Unreachable is a verdict here, not a silence" (Fig 13 :134). This is a genuine limit, not a failure to try. Mode `general`.

**Refuter:** produce an instrument already returning a reading that fails to resolve source, which then returns a definite count of origins from surviving molecular architecture. That closes the gap.

### F6: Failure to find a second genesis is not proof of absence

Fig 8 :39 "So a search that finds nothing is never a proof of absence."
Fig 9 :44 "Only a sealed room can be searched to the end, and no frame is a sealed room. So searching and finding nothing proves nothing."
Fig 6 :27 "Which is why some unreachability is depth and some is genuine absence, and from inside the frame the two look the same."

This is the sharpest practical consequence. If no shadow biosphere is found with the tools we have, that is consistent with one origin, with several origins whose other lineages went extinct, and with several origins whose descendants we cannot detect. Cleland and Copley (2005) make the same point from instrument design. The figures add the reason: closure is idempotent, so no amount of searching inside one frame adds the atom that would let it discriminate.

**Refuter:** a sealed-frame proof that the relevant search space is finite and exhaustively searched, which would turn "not found" into a real result. No frame here is sealed, so this refuter is stated but not currently satisfiable.

### F7: The missing thing is an instrument, not a seventh primitive

Fig 6 :32 "An instrument is not built from a description. It is improved from a worse instrument already returning something."
Fig 6 :33 "Nobody specified a telescope from an account of Mars. A wandering point in the sky was already a reading, and every sharper instrument was built on the residual the last one produced."
Fig 9 :27 "describe it before you go looking, or any frame you pick will seem to fit"

The honest answer to "what would settle it" is not "a better theory of abiogenesis". It is an instrument that already returns something which fails to resolve. Candidates in the world literature include source-discriminating biochemical assays and searches for nonstandard biochemistry in extreme environments, but naming one as *the* answer would be a preference masquerading as a term (Formula step 2: a sought-for shape is held as a preference, not a term). The shape is stated; the name is not.

**Refuter:** show an instrument built from a description alone, with no prior reading returning something, that resolves the count. That would break the instrument rule in this domain.

### F8: Prestige and agreement cannot settle the count

Fig 2 :35 "Alignment is triangulation, not negotiation. A mirror cannot correct you."
Fig 2 :40 "disqualifying for a verdict: agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool"

Seat Map :163 "High correlation is disqualifying for a verdict and efficient for a convention."
Seat Map :283 "A room does not upgrade the label. A room reaching a conclusion without the ground is agreement, and agreement among frames is a weighted average of influence."

A survey of researchers asked which origin story they find most plausible is a convention, not a verdict. The same applies to this solution: its agreement with the world literature is not a second witness where the sources share a pool. Fig 2 :42 "Buy independence in proportion to the cost of being wrong." The cost here is high (a false single-origin conclusion would suppress the search for a second genesis), so independence is bought by using low-overlap fields and by keeping the second seat's simulated status visible in `11_audit.md`.

### F9: One origin across many venues and many origins are different molecules, not degrees

Fig 13 :76 "A gradient over space and one over time are different terms, because which state it is bonded to is part of what it is."
Fig 12 :117 "isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."
Fig 12 :123 "functional group. A substructure whose presence fixes the outcome. The group says what kind; the qualifier says which member."

Under the figure's own rule, a venue is part of the term. Several venues in one history (M1) and several histories each with a venue (M2) differ in bond, not in ingredient count. This also means the world literature's multi-venue "hybrid" models (Brunk and Marshall's "multi-pot" chemistry) cannot be used as evidence for multiple independent origins without a separate argument. The re-census at F2 stands.

**Falsifier, written before the run:** if either M1 or M2 can be written without an atom the other requires, and the two make identical predictions for surviving molecular architecture, the distinction fails and they should be merged.

### F10: The two currencies of the decision must be held apart

Fig 12 :125 "A composition costs in two currencies, and they do not add."
Fig 12 :126 "Bits price the description. Actions price finding out whether it holds."

A compact story in which life arose once is cheap to state (few bits) and may still take a whole research budget to refute. A many-origins story is expensive to state and may be cheap to refute with one discriminating assay. Summing the two into one score would be the error Fig 12 names. This is also why the budget filters in `PLANS/00_MASTER_PLAN.md` section 6 are filters for what gets tried first, never witnesses for the verdict: Fig 9 :60 "Use filters for the budget and witnesses for the verdict. Never let a filter hand you a verdict."

## 4. Pre-registered refuter

Written before the conclusion, per Seat Map :287: "And a falsifier written before the run is a design instrument, not only an honesty device."

The shape claim is refuted if any of the following holds:

1. **Arrival refuter:** a demonstration that, under early-Earth boundary conditions, the closure of plausible prebiotic chemistry admits exactly one arrival route. Breaks F3.
2. **Survivorship refuter:** a demonstration that multiple arrivals cannot collapse to one surviving lineage, so that one lineage entails one origin. Breaks F4.
3. **Evidence refuter:** an instrument, already returning some reading that fails to resolve source, which returns a definite number of origins from surviving molecular architecture. Breaks F5 and closes the gap.
4. **Union refuter:** a demonstration that arrival count and survivor count are the same variable under all admissible conditions. Breaks F1 and collapses the three domains into one.
5. **Bond refuter:** a demonstration that M1 and M2 make identical predictions for every observable, so the environment is not part of the term. Breaks F2 and F9.

If any fires, the relevant finding is withdrawn and the retraction log below is updated. Until one is run, nothing in this folder is `grounded`.

### Round trip

A condensation of the figures into "many arrivals, one survivor, count unknown" loses three constraints on the way back down: the union structure (F1), the M1/M2 bond distinction (F2), and the requirement that the missing thing be an instrument already returning something (F7). Treating the shorthand as identical to the figure set would set the round-trip gap to zero and would be the dead-reckoning error Fig 4 :30 names. `11_audit.md` records R_T qualitatively and checks it from the second seat.
