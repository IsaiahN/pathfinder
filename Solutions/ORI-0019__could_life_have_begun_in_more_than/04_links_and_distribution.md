## Wider literature that bears on the question

1. **Arrival plurality.** Kempes and Krakauer (2021) put the position directly: there have been multiple origins of life on Earth and life is not a universal homology. This is a principle-level claim, not a measurement of a second genesis. It corroborates the figure verdict that distinct arrival routes are composable; it does not corroborate a count.
2. **Venue plurality.** Omran and Pasek (2020) argue for a "yes and" mindset between terrestrial hydrothermal fields and submarine vents rather than an either/or choice. Because they present the two as distinct settings, they corroborate the figure's type distinction between chemically different arrival terms (Fig 13 :76).
3. **Survivorship.** Crapitto et al. (2022), following Fournier and Alm (2015), describe LUCA as the sole survivor of multiple contemporaneous lineages. Brunk and Marshall (2021) note that multiple origins have been argued necessary for a reasonable chance of leaving descendants today. Both corroborate the per-domain survivorship verdict, from phylogenomics and from scenario methodology respectively. Their agreement is partly a shared evidence pool, so it is one witness with two voices, not two independent witnesses.
4. **Indistinguishability.** Mariscal et al. (2019) state that the single-origin assumption may simply reflect that the situation is currently indistinguishable from several origins, and display three indistinguishable scenarios. This is the closest external analogue to the figure's source-discrimination gap.
5. **Detection.** Cleland and Copley (2005) argue that standard microbial tools could not detect an alternative form of life if it existed, so failure to find one is not evidence of absence. This corroborates Fig 8 :39 and Fig 9 :44 from a separate field (instrument design and philosophy of biology).
6. **Report with a caution.** Pappas (2026) reports that Bacteria and Archaea may each have traversed the path to free-living cells independently. The article also quotes Betul Kacar: a last common ancestor is not the same thing as the origin of life. That caution matches the re-census in `01` F2: do not conflate one origin spanning many venues with many independent origins.

No world source contradicts the figure verdict. The conflict rule was not triggered. Sources 1 to 3 partly share an evidence pool (reviews and reconstructions of early-life research), so their agreement is recorded as corroboration at reduced independence.
