# The Tether's answer: could life have begun in more than one environment, with only one lineage surviving?

**Mode: `general` on the structural shape, `specified` on the survivorship prediction. Not `grounded`.** No measurement of the number of origins exists. This is a simulated derivation checked against the figures (`05_what_shape_means.md` §6), not a ground reading.

## 1. The residual it answers

One surviving lineage is observed. The question asks whether that observation settles how many origins and how many environments there were. The figure set answers by splitting the question: this is a **union compound**, not one hard question, because "how many arrivals" and "why one survivor" are two conditions with different anchors (Fig 9 :54 "And when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again."). Verdicts are kept per domain and never averaged.

## 2. The shape, per domain

**D1, arrival (triangulated, `general`).** Distinct arrival routes are composable. Fig 13 :115 "The set is closed. The arrangements are not. That gap is why import exists." Fig 13 :121 "Different routes can arrive at the same substance." Fig 12 :117 "isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance." The figures therefore do not forbid more than one origin. They do not say that more than one occurred, and they do not name a venue. "More than one environment" must also be split between one origin spanning several venues and several independent origins, because the environment is part of the term (Fig 13 :76 "A gradient over space and one over time are different terms, because which state it is bonded to is part of what it is.").

**D2, survival (triangulated, `specified`).** One survivor does not entail one arrival. Fig 11 :56 "Unmaintained. No maintainer and no live frame in the seat. The habitat is pure contact, nothing selects, and what survives is what the ground kept." Fig 7 :35 "A room's agreement is variation, not a verdict. Selection between rooms is where the ground votes." Fig 10 :70 "If levels were copies there would be one lineage drifting and no branches. The tree of life is what a non-invertible transform looks like from outside." A many-to-one map is the expected shape. The observation is therefore neutral on the count.

**D3, evidence (`gap` for the count, `general` for the shape).** The count cannot be read from one survivor's record. Fig 13 :128 "Nothing about a composed term tells you, from looking at it, which route produced it or whether that route was available." Fig 10 :24 "the ground does not decay, the channel does." And failure to find a second genesis proves nothing: Fig 8 :39 "So a search that finds nothing is never a proof of absence." The figure set returns the shape of the missing discriminator, not its name. "Unreachable is a verdict here, not a silence" (Fig 13 :134).

## 3. What it explicitly does not say

- Not "life arose once." That is a count claim requiring a ground reading this run does not have.
- Not "life arose many times." The figures license plurality; they do not assert an event.
- Not "it arose at vents" or "at hot springs." Venue names are bindings from geochemistry, not outputs of the six-term set.
- Not that the genetic code's universality is irrelevant. It settles common descent among extant life; it does not count arrivals.
- Not that a shadow biosphere exists, and not that one has been ruled out.
- Not `grounded`. No instrument settled `left(R,φ)` for the count.

## 4. What would settle it, the ground

A source-discriminating instrument, not another argument. Per Fig 6 :32 "An instrument is not built from a description. It is improved from a worse instrument already returning something." The candidate must already return a reading that fails to resolve source, so it can be sharpened. Concretely, the ground would move if:

1. An assay on surviving molecular architecture returned a definite number of independent sources rather than a common-descent reading compatible with all three arrangements.
2. An independent lineage were found whose biochemistry is demonstrably not derived from LUCA, which would refute "only one lineage surviving" as a fact.
3. A closed-set proof showed that early-Earth prebiotic chemistry admits exactly one arrival route, which would remove the D1 plurality.
4. A demonstration showed that multiple arrivals cannot collapse to one survivor, which would remove D2's filter.

Until one of these fires, the verdict remains `candidate` and the count remains `gap`.

## 5. The honest stop

**Unreached, not unreachable.** The figure set's own limit is "a shape, not a name" (canonical figures README). The six-term substrate is "a human's careful guess at reality's compression axes" (*They're Scanning Books*), not nature's own list. The answer stops where the available instruments stop discriminating, and records the lineage so the stop is checkable. Indiscernibility is not identity: one survivor's record that looks like a single origin is not thereby a single origin.

## 6. One-paragraph version for outreach

> The Tether does not count the origins of life, and it does not pick a cradle. It says that distinct arrival routes are composable, so more than one origin is not excluded; that in an unmaintained habitat selection keeps what the ground kept, so one surviving lineage does not imply one arrival; and that the count cannot be read off a single survivor, because the artefact does not carry which route produced it and a search that finds no second genesis proves nothing. "More than one environment" must also be split: one origin using several venues is a different molecule from several independent origins, because the environment is part of the term. What the framework offers is that shape, the falsifiers written in advance, and the description of an instrument that would close the count. It refuses to name the number until the ground returns it.
