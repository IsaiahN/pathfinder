# Union, cycle, and order: a supporting derivation

**Mode: `specified`.** Simulated derivation checked against the figures, not a ground reading (see `05_what_shape_means.md` §6).

## RESIDUAL

The phrase "predicted reliably before changes become difficult to reverse" carries two structural ambiguities that the main audit flags but does not resolve: is it one question or several (union versus partition), and is the driver-response relation a chain or a cycle? A third follows: if the four domains of the census are separate, are they isomers of one structure or different structures sharing a functional group? These decide what a single verdict could even mean.

## DOMAIN CENSUS (used here, not copied from the main audit)

Atoms held: a driver, a response, a threshold relation, an observer, a habitat, and a reversal path. Bonds held: the seven plus negation. No new primitive is available without an import. The question is which bonds compose these atoms and in what order.

## DERIVATION

### Q1 : One question or four? Union, not partition

- Fig 9 :54 "And when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again."
- Fig 9 :55 "a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either. It stops being a mixture and becomes a specific compound."

A partition would allow one verdict per disjoint sub-question and a clean aggregate. A union means the conditions overlap: an instrument can work only where the habitat has been enumerated; a reading can be per slot only where an instrument resolves slots; action can be timely only where the reversal path is known. A single lever clears the region where one condition holds and leaves the intersections standing. So the four domains must keep separate verdicts. Aggregating them would repeat Fig 1 :22 "Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average" at the level of domains.

**Falsifier:** demonstrate a single intervention that moves all four domains simultaneously on a real ecosystem, measured against the ground. That would collapse the union toward a partition.

### Q2 : Chain or cycle?

- Fig 12 :121 "cycle. The output re-enters as input. The sign of the loop decides whether it closes, and a chain cannot close by construction."
- Fig 13 :31 "a remainder compounding through a chain that amplifies is chaos, and the detail a crossing discards reappears above it as noise."

A tipping point is not a linear chain from stressor to response. It is a positive feedback loop: the response re-enters as input and amplifies, which is why the crossing can be abrupt and why the sign of the loop decides whether it closes. A chain model cannot close by construction, so it cannot represent a tipping point at all, only a smooth trend. Hysteresis (difficulty of reversal) is the same loop traversed with a different threshold; the return path is a different composition, not the chain run backwards.

**Falsifier:** a system whose abrupt shift is fully explained by a linear, open-loop response with no re-entry of output as input, verified against the ground. That would make the cycle reading wrong for that system and force a re-census of S2.

**Status:** triangulated (Fig 12 cycle and Fig 13 chaotic remainder, two figures with different subject matter).

### Q3 : Are the four domains isomers, or a functional group?

- Fig 12 :117 "isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."
- Fig 12 :124 "functional group. A substructure whose presence fixes the outcome. The group says what kind; the qualifier says which member."

The habitat domain (D1), instrument domain (D2), reading domain (D3) and decision domain (D4) do not share the same atoms. D1 has species and relations; D2 has statistics and time series; D4 has policy rates. By Fig 12's definition they are **not isomers**. They are members of a **functional group**: each has the shape "a monitor ranging against a ground through a channel", with different atoms in the slots. The group says what kind of thing each is; the qualifier says which member. This matters because a fix that works on one member (more data for D2) does not transfer to another (faster driver manipulation for D4) merely because they share the group.

The stronger isomer claim would require re-describing all four over a common atom set, for example as four different arrangements of observer, signal, threshold, driver and reversal path. That is a checkable structural hypothesis, not something the framework asserts.

**Falsifier:** exhibit all four domains over a common atom set with identical atoms and only the bond differing, and show the behavioural difference is decisive. If any domain requires an atom the others do not, the isomer claim fails and the functional-group reading stands.

### Q4 : What order do the domains run in?

- Fig 3 :20 "The chain is ordered by dependency, not by importance. Each link consumes what the one above produces, so effort spent above the break arrives at a step that cannot use it."
- Fig 3 :27 "A link with no instrument is not a link that works. It is a link nobody has looked at."
- Fig 3 :29 "Before asking whether a system is good, ask which link it currently stops at, and whether the answer was measured or assumed."

Dependency order: D1 habitat enumeration, then D2 instrument aiming, then D3 per-slot reading, then D4 decision and reversal. This is not a ranking of importance. It is the order in which each link consumes the previous one's output. Most practical effort lands at D2 (better statistics) because that is where the measurements already exist; the figure says that is where effort arrives, not where it is needed. The middle link in this chain is D3, the reading: it has neither the obvious failures of perception nor the obvious failures of planning, and so attracts the fewest instruments.

**Falsifier:** a demonstrated case where the decision link works while the habitat census has not been done, because the reversal path is known independently of the crossing mechanism. That would reverse the dependency order for that system and require a re-census.

### Q5 : Is there a last step?

- Fig 13 :90 "One chain is one route through a space that has no last route."
- Fig 13 :115 "The set is closed. The arrangements are not. That gap is why import exists."

There is no terminal arrangement of the four domains that ends the question. A reliability claim is a route through a space with no last route; each crossing leaves a residual that re-enters the next monitoring cycle. This is why the answer is a shape with refuters rather than a final verdict.

## CONCLUSION

The bundled question is a **union** of overlapping conditions, not a partition. The physical relation it describes is a **cycle** whose sign decides whether the threshold closes, not a chain. The four domains are **not isomers** as currently described; they are members of one functional group, each with different atoms in the monitor-and-ground slots. Their order is by **dependency**, not importance, and the least instrumented link is the reading itself. There is no last route. Each claim has a stated falsifier above.
