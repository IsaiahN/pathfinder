## 1. A shape is a constraint, not a filler

The figures README states the limit: the framework returns "what a missing thing would have to look like (a shape, not a name)." Fig 9 :32 "you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself." Fig 13 :134 "Unreachable is a verdict here, not a silence".

From *The Library of Babel*: "you can cut your search short if the set of books returned is small and descriptive enough that it provides a general shape of the answer that satisfies your question."

So a **shape** is the narrowed form of an answer you can state before you can name the thing that fills it. It constrains; it does not fill. For this question, the shape is: reliability is a relation between a monitor and a habitat, the residual is per slot and not pooled, absence of a warning is not proof of safety, and the statistic-to-state map carries a measurable round trip gap. A numeric claim such as "this indicator has a 10 percent false-alarm rate in all ecosystems" is a name where the framework returns a shape. A pre-registered test that reports the rate for one system against the ground is an attempt to earn the name.

## 2. How the shape is obtained

- *Library of Babel*: use the vocabulary you already hold that is closest to the thing, then look for recurring new terms that overlap or correlate with it. "With each new term gained, you narrow the space and deepen your vocabulary." This is bootstrapping by proximity, not by authority.
- Fig 6 :30 "The description travels. Fails by being sharpened into terms only we can read. Cast it in effect terms." Fig 9 :30-31 "your words are borrowed too, so describe the effect rather than the cause." The residual was therefore described in effect terms in `01` §0 before any figure line was chosen.
- *Decoding Tacit Knowledge*: "When contact isn't possible, the best you can send is a plain description of what you'd expect to see, because anyone can check that against the world." This is exactly the two-stage offer in `02_outreach_email.md`: give a description anyone can check, then offer the theory only if asked.
- *Library of Babel* on stopping: "the finish line will also be the starting place", and the searcher has "composed the answer through the process of locating it." For this question the stopping point is not a yes or a no; it is the four-domain shape with its five refuters.

## 3. What licenses a name

Two things, both outside the shape itself:

1. **The ground settles it.** Formula step 5: "the ground settles it. Verified only by what pays. A gate is not the ground." The figures README says the framework "stops at the limit of what the available instruments can distinguish, and records the lineage so the stop is honest." For ecological tipping points, the ground is the observed crossing and its reversibility, which is retrospective. A prospective reliability claim is therefore a candidate until a pre-registered test pays.
2. **A provenanced import from a frame whose closure differs** (Fig 6 :23 "No frame escapes its own closure. The bound moves only on contact with a frame whose closure differs."), with a source gate and a shadow test. Importing the vocabulary of early-warning statistics into the framework is such a contact, and the provenance is recorded in `10_source_ledger.csv`.

*The Hard Problem is About Source* sharpens the distinction. A color instrument reads green but "cannot comment on the source": one green light, or yellow plus blue, or RGB, or CMYK, or a scheme not yet discovered. "If several paths create the same behavior, then behavior is a projection with lossy information." And "An instrument that can't distinguish these cases demonstrates its own inadequacy." Applied here: a flat variance reading has several possible sources (a safe system, a saturated metric, a pooled average, a dropped transient). Fig 10 :25 names those channel failures directly. What is owed is a discriminating instrument and a reported gap, not a name assigned to the reading.

## 4. Why the question invites the specific forbidden error

The forbidden error here is treating the indicator as an anchor. Fig 2 :29 "An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks." An indicator is an instrument; instruments "can be wrong on axes they do not measure" (Fig 2 :37). A rising autocorrelation is a reading, not the crossing. The crossing settles the question, and it does so after the reading.

A second forbidden error is the bundled verdict. A single "yes, predictable" or "no, not predictable" averaged over four domains is the disaggregation failure Fig 1 :22 names: "Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average." Here the slots are the four domains of the census, and the average would hide which link actually works.

## 5. What shape means for the answer we give

- **The shape is checkable without the theory.** A recipient can be asked whether a pooled regional average can hide a local crossing, whether absence of a warning proves safety, and whether reversal time is independent of driver manipulation rate. All three are answerable against ordinary ecological knowledge before any framework is shown.
- **The shape does not name an indicator.** It says what any candidate reliability claim must satisfy. It does not say variance, or lag-1 autocorrelation, or skewness is the right statistic for a given ecosystem.
- **The shape carries its own refusal.** The five pre-registered refuters in `01` §3 are part of the shape, not an appendix. Fig 5 :65-66: "make the sought-for shape an ORDINARY TERM. Write down what an answer would look like, bind it, and let it accrue a residual like anything else."
- **The shape stops at the instrument limit.** It does not claim the framework can settle a numeric reliability question for real ecosystems. That would need a ground reading the figure set does not contain.

## 6. Two consequences for this package

**Prestige is not action.** From *The Recursive Self-Improvement Trap*: "Prestige can fund an action. It can give an action a budget to be tried. But it cannot settle an action. Only the ground can settle it. The Ground Maintainer must be blind to prestige." A list of eminent ecologists agreeing that early-warning signals work is a convention, not corroboration. The legitimate ask is to give the shape a budget to be tried against a ground.

**This audit is a simulation, not a ground reading.** Same essay: "LLMs can quickly simulate an approximate answer/output with the formula found in the architecture post... However, the truly testable output would need to be derived from the real architecture or working the formula yourself." The essay also states "Metatheories cannot validate themselves." So `01`, `07` and `09` are simulated derivations checked for internal consistency against the figures. They are **not** tests of whether ecological tipping points are predictable, and they cannot settle anything they report on.
