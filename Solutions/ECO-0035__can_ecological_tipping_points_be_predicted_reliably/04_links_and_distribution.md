## Three things in the wider literature that bear directly on this question

1. **False positives are structural, not just statistical noise.** Boettiger and Hastings 2012 show that selecting systems because they tipped, then measuring how often warning signals appeared, inflates the apparent hit rate. This is the prosecutor's fallacy. It is a world-side instance of Fig 2 :40 "agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool": the sample and the claim share an evidence pool, so the agreement is the pool talking.
2. **Lead time and reversal rate are separate conditions.** Biggs et al. 2009 find that "Large increases in the indicators only occur once a regime shift is initiated, often too late for management to avert a shift" when drivers can only be changed gradually. This is the world-side counterpart of Fig 3 :27 "A link with no instrument is not a link that works. It is a link nobody has looked at." The perception link (indicator) can work while the objective link (time to act) does not.
3. **Indicator validity depends on assumptions the indicator does not measure.** Layritz et al. 2025 show that rising variance and autocorrelation are "not supported by mathematical theory" under non-Gaussian noise and produce false positives; Zhang et al. 2015 show indicator performance depends on the specific model and access conditions. These are world-side instances of Fig 4 :30 "So take the round trip and measure the gap. Without it you are navigating by dead reckoning: confident about a position nobody checked."

These are corroboration only. The verdict is from the figures. No world source was found that conflicts with the figure verdict; the conflict rule was therefore not triggered. Agreement among Scheffer 2009, Dakos 2012 and Lenton 2008 is one evidence pool, not three witnesses.

## Distribution: offer, do not push

Figure-grounded reasons:

- **Fig 4 :22** "Only methods cross up. Only head starts come down." Sending a full framework before a recipient has described the residual sends a recording, not a generator. Lead with the four-domain shape and the five refuters; offer links after.
- **Fig 10 :42** "A verifier that can reconstruct the claim cannot verify it." A recipient who has absorbed the framework before giving a verdict has joined the frame being checked.
- **Fig 9 :27-28** "describe it before you go looking, or any frame you pick will seem to fit." Ask what the recipient would expect to see if the shape claim were true before giving them the source list.
- **Fig 7 :35** "A room's agreement is variation, not a verdict. Selection between rooms is where the ground votes." Do not select recipients for likely agreement.
- **Fig 2 :42** "Buy independence in proportion to the cost of being wrong." The cost of a wrong reliability claim is high, so independent checks are worth buying; receptiveness estimates are not.

**Recommended order in a reply:** (1) the four-domain shape claim and its five refuters; (2) ask what the recipient would count as a failure; (3) record that verdict; (4) only then, if curious, the three source links with the note that the figures, not the summary, are the authority.

## Hard precondition

Stages 1 to 3 work only if the stage-1 output survives. The output in `01_figure_grounded_audit.md` is a `general` and `specified` shape with refuters, not a numeric reliability claim. If it is modified into a yes or no verdict, the offer must be withdrawn until the mode and refuters are restored.
