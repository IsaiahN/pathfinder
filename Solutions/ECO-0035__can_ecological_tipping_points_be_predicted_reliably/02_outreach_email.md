# Outreach draft: check the output first, then decide about the framework

**Use:** one-to-one, or as a cover note attached to a submission through a public venue in `03_public_contact_points.csv`. **Not** for bulk send.

**Hard precondition:** do not send until the shape claim and its five refuters in `01_figure_grounded_audit.md` §3 are intact and the recipient has been asked for a verdict on stage 1 before any theory is shown. The two-stage offer collapses on contact if the verdict is recorded after the theory.

**Subject:** One checkable claim about predicting ecological tipping points, before any theory

> Hi [name],
>
> I have one output I would like you to check against what you already know. I am not asking you to read the framework first, and I am not asking you to agree with it. If the output does not survive, there is nothing else to look at.
>
> **The claim (`general` on the shape, `specified` on the parts, not `grounded`):** the question "can ecological tipping points be predicted reliably in time to act?" is not one question. It bundles at least four conditions: whether the actor that governs the crossing is in contact with the monitoring system; whether any instrument is already returning a signal that fails to resolve; whether the reading is per patch or pooled into a regional average; and whether the driver can be changed fast enough for a warning to be usable. Reliability is not a property an indicator carries. It is a property of the monitor plus the habitat it ranges against, and it has to be earned per system against the ground. A pooled regional average can be flat while one patch is about to cross. No warning found is not the same as no warning owed.
>
> **What I am not claiming:** that any named early-warning statistic is reliable or unreliable across the board, that a particular indicator thresholds a particular ecosystem, that a flat reading means safety, or that any of this is settled by measurement. The framework returns a shape and a route, not a number.
>
> **What would refute the shape claim.** I wrote these down before the search:
> 1. A closed, enumerated habitat is demonstrated for a real ecosystem in which every actor capable of moving the crossing is in contact with the instrument, and that closure is verified against the ground.
> 2. A pooled statistic is shown to carry the same decision-relevant information as per-patch residuals in a system with known spatial heterogeneity, verified against the ground.
> 3. An indicator is shown to have zero round trip gap between its abstraction and its concretisation on the relevant states.
> 4. Channel death is shown to be distinguishable from system quiet by construction in a deployed monitor.
> 5. The time available for management is shown to be independent of how fast the driver can be changed.
>
> If any one holds, I retract the corresponding part. A one-line "this fails because..." is more useful to me than agreement. If you would rather not engage, no reply is needed.
>
> **If it survives, and only then:** the public sources are below. The figures, not my summary of them, are the authority.
> - Verbatim figure text with line numbers (local framework file): `FRAMEWORK/reference/03-FIGURES_TEXT.md`
> - Figures and formula for direct download: https://github.com/IsaiahN/tether/tree/general-agent/figures
> - Canonical usage rule: https://github.com/IsaiahN/tether/blob/general-agent/figures/README.md
>
> **If you want to use the machinery:** the formula is in the figures link. Run it on a problem you know well and can check against a ground. Write down what would refute your result *before* you run it, and state the mode of what you get. A reported failure is more useful to me than a reported success.
>
> Thanks,
> [name]
