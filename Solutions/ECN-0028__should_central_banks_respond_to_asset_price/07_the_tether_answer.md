# The Tether's answer: should central banks respond to asset booms when inflation is low?

**Mode: `general` on the shape, `specified` on instrument choice, not `grounded`.** This is a simulated derivation checked for internal consistency against the verbatim figures, not a ground reading (`05_what_shape_means.md` section 6).

## 1. The residual it answers

A bank's rule predicts welfare from the consumer-price slot. The ground returns instability from the credit, leverage, and asset-price slots. The question asks whether a quiet first slot settles the second. Fig 1 :19: "The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated." Fig 1 :22: "Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average." The Symbols table adds: "R is measured per slot, never as a single global number." So the answer cannot be obtained by averaging inflation and asset prices into one index.

## 2. The shape

1. **The answer is conditional on the mandate, not universal.** Fig 2 :36: "An anchor is legitimate when the question is constitutively about it." If the question is only about prices, consumer-price inflation is the anchor and there is no figure-licensed direct response to asset prices. If the question also includes financial stability, Fig 2 :37-38 applies: "instruments can be wrong on axes they do not measure." A quiet CPI print is an instrument reading on the price axis, not an anchor for the balance-sheet axis. Since the question names financial instability, the second branch is the standing one. Both branches are recorded; neither is averaged.

2. **The three conditions form a union compound.** Fig 9 :5: "are not one hard question but several well-formed ones. Split it rather than search." Fig 9 :57: "a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either." The boom, the instability, and the low inflation overlap, so they must be unbundled before a lever is chosen (Fig 9 :54).

3. **Enumerate the habitat before choosing the instrument.** Fig 11 :43-44: "The habitat is enumerated, never composed." and "List everything in contact with the residual, then what is in contact with those, and outward until the cascade stops mattering." Fig 11 :52-53: "first-order enumeration, second-order effect, and the residual that mattered was three steps out." The relevant ground is the credit and leverage cascade, not the asset price alone. Fig 11 :40: "An improvement that does not change contact changes nothing, however much it improves." A policy rate contacts borrowing costs; prudential tools contact collateral, leverage, and funding. Which one contacts the live slot is a `specified`, economy-specific question.

4. **The target states a goal; it cannot decide the outcome.** Fig 10 :47: "a convention that decides an outcome has stopped being one." Fig 10 :49: "Declaring a meaning does not ground it." "Inflation is on target, therefore hold" converts the convention into the decision. The Goodhart's law source read in `09` gives the world-side version: a measure that becomes a target ceases to be a good measure.

5. **The two costs must stay in separate columns.** Fig 12 :126: "Bits price the description. Actions price finding out whether it holds." Fig 12 :129: "Neither figure predicts the other, so both are read or the choice is made blind." The cost of deciding whether the boom is dangerous and the cost of acting on that decision do not share a unit. The empirical result in `09` shows the two coming apart: some stability measures improve and others worsen when asset prices are added to a rule.

6. **No fixed rule is final.** Fig 7 :30-31: "every room closes some of the gap; no finite number of them ever arrives." The obligation is to re-census the habitat and to make each review room differ from the last, not to install a rule once.

## 3. What it explicitly does not say

- Not "always respond" and not "never respond." Both are `general`-mode verdicts over a union compound.
- Not that the policy rate is the wrong instrument. It is the instrument of the price slot; the claim is only that it does not, by itself, contact the credit slot.
- Not that macroprudential tools are proven to work here. That is a `specified` choice that needs a ground reading in the specific economy.
- Not that consumer prices and asset prices are unrelated. The claim is that they are separate slots, which is a structural claim about where the residual is indexed, testable against data.
- Not `grounded`. No experiment or natural experiment was read that settles the welfare effect of leaning against a boom.

## 4. What would settle it, the ground

- A source-discriminating observation: does a quiet CPI print with rising credit and leverage precede a bust more often than a quiet CPI print without them? If yes, the two-slot census is confirmed at the ground. If the two are indistinguishable at the decision horizon, F1 fails.
- A contact test: does the chosen instrument actually move the credit and leverage slot, and by how much? Fig 11 :40 makes this the criterion, not the instrument's reputation.
- A two-column cost ledger: description cost and action cost reported separately, never summed (Fig 12 :126, :129).
- Standing falsifiers from `01` section 5: collapse of the slots, a price-only mandate, a controlled experiment in either direction, decomposition of the union, or a single-unit calculus.

## 5. The honest stop

**Unreached, not unreachable.** The eight essays record that the six-term set is "a human's careful guess at reality's compression axes; another sensorium's minimum set need not be those six." Declaring the slots makes the choice visible, not neutral. The derivation stops at the limit of what the opened sources distinguish and records the lineage so the stop is checkable. *How Intelligence Actually Works* gives the exact caution: "The recursion converges toward indiscernibility, and indiscernibility is not identity." A rule that has never been wrong is not thereby settled.

## 6. One-paragraph version for outreach

> The Tether does not answer "always tighten" or "never tighten." It says the question is conditional on the mandate: if the object is only consumer prices, that is the anchor; if the object includes financial stability, a quiet consumer-price reading is an instrument on one axis and cannot settle the other. It says consumer prices, asset prices, and credit are separate slots, so a single average hides the live signal; that the boom, the instability, and the low inflation form a union that must be unbundled before any lever is picked; that the instrument must contact the credit cascade three steps out, which the policy rate alone does not; that the inflation target is a goal, not the thing that decides the outcome; and that the cost of deciding and the cost of acting must be reported in separate columns. It hands over the shape, the branch condition, and refuters written in advance, and it stops there until a ground reading settles which response pays.

## 7. Cost if wrong, undoability, reversal

- **Cost if wrong:** a missed buildup if the credit slot is ignored (the BIS source in `09`), or an induced recession and higher interest-rate volatility if a false positive is acted on (the empirical and debate sources in `09`).
- **Undoable:** yes. A rule or a buffer setting is revisable; no primitive is withdrawn. Output already lost is not recovered by the reversal, and that asymmetry is part of the action cost in section 2.5.
- **Reversed by:** a ground reading, not agreement. A source-discriminating observation, a contact test on the chosen instrument, or a mandate change would move the verdict. A room full of economists agreeing would not.
