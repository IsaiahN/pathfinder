## 0. The question in effect terms

> Should central banks respond to asset-price booms and financial instability, even when consumer-price inflation is low?

Restated as a residual: a bank's decision rule predicts welfare from the consumer-price slot. The ground returns financial instability from the credit, leverage, and asset-price slots. When the consumer-price slot is quiet and the other slots are not, the rule's prediction and the ground come apart. The residual is the gap, per slot.

- **Option A, respond.** Tighten or use macroprudential tools while CPI is quiet. Leads to a chance of stopping the imbalance early; also leads to false positives, a possible recession, and higher interest-rate volatility in the very indicators used to measure stability (Akram and Eitrheim, 2008, read below).
- **Option B, do not respond.** Treat a quiet CPI slot as sufficient. Leads to a legible anchor and no false-positive tightening; also leads to a live residual accumulating in a slot the rule does not read until the bust (Borio and Lowe, 2002, read below).

The sought-for shape is bound before the run: *a rule that keeps slots separate, states which slot the instrument contacts, and names the measurement that would settle the response.* A loop cannot check the direction of its own question (formula step 2, Direction), so the shape is made an ordinary term here and allowed to accrue a residual.

## 1. Residual census, before choosing any figure line

**R by arity, symmetry, scale.** Arity: at least three slots (consumer prices, asset prices, credit/leverage), so this is not a two-operand comparison. Symmetry: the slots are not interchangeable. A quiet CPI print and a rising credit-to-GDP gap are different quantities, not two names for one. Scale: the decision sits at the level of a policy rule, but the residual lives one to three links out in the habitat: credit growth, collateral values, leverage, funding, then real output. Fig 11 :52-53 names exactly this shape: "first-order enumeration, second-order effect, and the residual that mattered was three steps out."

**Dependency order (Fig 3).** Perception (do the credit and asset slots register at all?) precedes vocabulary (are there terms for financial imbalance, not just deviation of inflation from target?) precedes objective (what counts as progress: a quiet CPI, or a quiet CPI with a stable balance sheet?) precedes planning (which instrument contacts which slot?) precedes learn and carry. The chain usually breaks in the middle: Fig 3 :27, "A link with no instrument is not a link that works. It is a link nobody has looked at." Fig 3 :19, "Optimising a link that already works cannot rescue one that does not." A committee that has a precise inflation instrument and no credit instrument is optimising above the break.

## 3. Findings F1 to F8

### F1: A single-slot rule cannot see a live residual in another slot (decisive, structural)

Fig 1 :19: "The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated."

Fig 1 :22: "Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average."

Symbols table :43-44: "R is measured per slot, never as a single global number."

Fig 1 :13: "a global R near zero with one live slot residual is a legal state"

An inflation-targeting rule that reads only the consumer-price slot is in exactly the legal state Fig 1 :13 describes: global residual near zero, one live slot unread. Adding asset prices to the average does not fix this; it performs the aggregation the figure forbids. The remedy is a rule that keeps the slots as separate readings rather than folding them into one index.

**Mode:** `general`, structural. **Status:** triangulated (Fig 1 and Symbols table, low overlap: one is the agent loop, the other is the notation table).

**Refuter:** show that consumer prices, asset prices, and credit are one slot, that is, that no asset-price or credit movement can occur without a corresponding consumer-price movement at the decision horizon. If they are one slot, the multi-slot census is wrong and F1 is withdrawn. This is a factual claim about the economy and is testable; it is not asserted here.

### F2: CPI is an anchor for its own question and an instrument for the stability question

Fig 2 :29-30: "An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks."

Fig 2 :36: "An anchor is legitimate when the question is constitutively about it."

Fig 2 :37-38: "instruments can be wrong on axes they do not measure."

Fig 2 :25: "pinned: it does not update on A or B"

The phrase "even when consumer-price inflation is low" asks whether a quiet instrument on one axis can settle a question about another axis. Under Fig 2 :36, the answer is conditional on the mandate. If the question is constitutively about prices (a narrow price-stability mandate), CPI is the legitimate anchor and a direct asset-price response is not licensed by this derivation. If the question is also about financial stability, CPI is an instrument, and an instrument that is quiet on the price axis says nothing about the balance-sheet axis. The figures therefore do not hand down "yes" or "no"; they hand down the condition under which each applies.

This is the one genuine branch in the derivation, and it is recorded rather than smoothed. The question as posed names financial instability as part of the object, so the standing reading here is the second one. A pure price-stability mandate would flip it.

**Mode:** `general`. **Status:** triangulated (Fig 2 anchor lines plus the Goodhart's law world source, low overlap between a metatheory figure and an economic adage).

**Refuter:** a mandate document that makes price stability the sole constitutive object, plus evidence that financial stability is only a constraint on the inflation forecast. Under that condition the branch flips to "do not respond directly."

### F3: The question is a union compound, not one hard question

Fig 9 :5: "are not one hard question but several well-formed ones. Split it rather than search."

Fig 9 :54: "And when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again."

Fig 9 :57: "a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either."

The question bundles three conditions: an asset-price boom, financial instability, and low consumer-price inflation. These overlap. A boom can occur with or without instability; low inflation can accompany either. By Fig 9 :57 the overlap is a specific compound with its own loop, and a single lever will clear the region where one condition holds while leaving the intersections standing. This is why the literature disagrees without converging: different papers are well-formed on different subsets. Fig 9 :4-5 names that exact situation.

**Mode:** `general`. **Status:** triangulated (Fig 9 split/union rules plus Bordo and Jeanne 2002, whose abstract reports that optimal policy "cannot be summarized by a simple policy rule," and Akram and Eitrheim 2008, who report mixed effects).

**Refuter:** show that the three conditions never occur apart at the decision horizon, so the union collapses to one condition and one lever suffices.

### F4: The instrument must contact the slot where the residual lives, and the cascade is three steps out

Fig 11 :43-44: "The habitat is enumerated, never composed." and "List everything in contact with the residual, then what is in contact with those, and outward until the cascade stops mattering."

Fig 11 :52-53: "first-order enumeration, second-order effect, and the residual that mattered was three steps out."

Fig 11 :40: "An improvement that does not change contact changes nothing, however much it improves."

A policy rate contacts borrowing costs and the inflation forecast. It does not, by itself, contact collateral values, bank leverage, or funding structure. Those are separate actors in the habitat, enumerated from the world rather than composed from the price rule. Borio and Lowe (2002) give the world-side version: "sustained rapid credit growth combined with large increases in asset prices appears to increase the probability of an episode of financial instability." Posen (2006) gives the complementary constraint: "there is no monetary substitute for financial stability, and no market substitute for monetary ease during severe credit crunch." Together they name a two-instrument habitat: the rate for the price slot, prudential tools for the credit and leverage slots.

**Mode:** `specified` (the instrument choice needs a ground reading in the specific economy). **Status:** triangulated across Fig 11 and two world sources with different methods and venues (BIS working paper and a journal debate), though both are in the same broad economics pool.

**Refuter:** a controlled study showing that the policy rate alone contacts the credit and leverage slots sufficiently, so no separate prudential instrument changes contact. If that holds, the habitat collapses and the two-instrument recommendation is withdrawn.

### F5: Two incommensurable costs must be read separately

Fig 12 :126: "Bits price the description. Actions price finding out whether it holds."

Fig 12 :129: "Neither figure predicts the other, so both are read or the choice is made blind."

The cost of describing whether a boom is dangerous (data, models, forecasting) and the cost of acting on that description (tightening, a possible recession) are in different units. Summing them into a single "optimal rule" score is the error the figure names. The world record matches: Akram and Eitrheim (2008) find that adding house prices, equity prices, or credit growth improves some stability measures and worsens others "mainly because of higher interest rate volatility owing to a more active monetary policy." That is the two currencies coming apart, not a paradox.

**Mode:** `general`. **Status:** triangulated (Fig 12 pricing rule and an empirical central-bank model).

**Refuter:** a decision procedure that prices description and action in one unit without residue. The figures say the units do not add, so such a procedure would have to show what common currency it uses; absent that, the finding stands.

### F6: A target that decides the outcome has stopped being a convention

Fig 10 :47: "a convention that decides an outcome has stopped being one"

Fig 10 :49: "Declaring a meaning does not ground it."

Fig 10 :59: "an interest in the outcome is unavoidable, an interest in the reading is disqualifying."

"Inflation is on target, therefore do not respond" converts a convention (the target) into a decision rule that decides an outcome. Under Fig 10 :47 that conversion is the named failure. Goodhart's law, read from Wikipedia (1975 Goodhart quote reproduced there), states: "Any observed statistical regularity will tend to collapse once pressure is placed upon it for control purposes." The same point from a different field: once CPI is the target, it stops being a clean measure of the underlying stability. This does not say the target is useless. It says the target cannot be the thing that settles a question about a quantity it does not measure.

**Mode:** `general`. **Status:** triangulated (Fig 10 and the Goodhart adage, low overlap: metatheory figure versus economic/m organisational adage).

**Refuter:** evidence that making CPI the target does not alter its information content about balance-sheet risk, that is, that the regularity does not collapse under control pressure. Then the convention remains a convention and the finding is withdrawn.

### F7: No fixed rule is a last room

Fig 7 :30-31: "every room closes some of the gap; no finite number of them ever arrives"

Fig 13 :118: "infinite effort inside a finite reach."

Fig 8 :39: "So a search that finds nothing is never a proof of absence."

An augmented Taylor rule, a fixed inflation target, or a fixed prudential trigger is a room. Each closes part of the gap; none arrives. Bordo and Jeanne (2002) independently report that optimal policy "depends on the economic conditions in a complex, nonlinear way." So the answer is a standing obligation to re-census the habitat and to make review rooms differ from the last one (Fig 7 :36, low overlap between rooms), not a rule to be installed once.

**Mode:** `general`. **Status:** triangulated (Fig 7 plus Fig 13 plus a world result; the two figures are low-overlap in what they govern, the chain versus the substrate).

**Refuter:** a rule that closes the gap entirely across all three conditions. The figures forbid a last room, so such a claim would have to show which figure is wrong; it cannot be absorbed as a better rule inside the framework.

### F8: The verification frame must range against the ground, not pool agreement

Fig 2 :35: "Alignment is triangulation, not negotiation. A mirror cannot correct you."

Fig 2 :40: "agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool"

Fig 2 :42: "Buy independence in proportion to the cost of being wrong."

Fig 10 :52: "Bring a characterised residual, never the source."

This finding governs the outreach artifacts, not the policy verdict. The cost of being wrong about a boom is large and locatable (a missed crisis or an induced recession), so independence should be bought in proportion. First contact carries the residual, its refuter, and the branch condition, not the figures. A reviewer who has absorbed the framework before giving a verdict has joined the frame being checked and cannot correct it.

**Mode:** `general`. **Status:** single-source for the figure wording (Fig 2), corroborated by the seat map's correlation rule cited in `04_links_and_distribution.md`.

**Refuter:** a demonstration that reviewers drawn from the same literature and data pool can still supply independent evidence about the residual. Since independence is a quantity, not a job title, this is measurable; the figures would require the measurement before the claim.

## 5. Pre-registered refuter

Written before the conclusion and before outreach, per the standing rule that a falsifier written before the run is a design instrument.

The shape claim is refuted if any of the following holds:

1. Consumer prices, asset prices, and credit are shown to be one slot at the decision horizon, so no independent live residual exists (breaks F1 and the domain census).
2. The mandate is shown to be constitutively about price stability alone, so CPI is the legitimate anchor (flips F2 to the "do not respond directly" branch; this is a branch condition, not a refutation of the figure).
3. A policy experiment shows that responding to a boom reduces crisis losses with no material output cost, or alternatively that it raises total losses in every observed subset (the first grounds the specified instrument choice; the second refuses it).
4. The union decomposes into genuinely independent conditions, so one lever clears each region and the intersections are empty (breaks F3).
5. A single-unit decision calculus is demonstrated that prices description and action without residue (breaks F5).

If 1, 3 in its second form, 4, or 5 fires, the shape is withdrawn or repaired. Until one is run, nothing here is labelled `grounded`.

## 6. Recommendation

1. Do not answer the question as a binary. State the mandate branch first (F2), then enumerate the habitat (F4).
2. Keep the slots separate in the decision rule; do not build a single composite index (F1).
3. Unbundle the union before choosing a lever (F3).
4. Read the description cost and the action cost in separate columns (F5).
5. Treat the inflation target as a convention, not as the thing that decides the outcome (F6).
6. Buy independence for the check in proportion to the cost of being wrong (F8). Send the residual and the refuter first, the figures only after a verdict is recorded.
7. Fig 4 :25: "A recording carried upward looks like knowledge and is a description of one occasion." Send the generator (the derivation and the refuter), not a conclusion.
