# Expert verification packet: the Tether on asset prices and monetary policy

**What this is:** a claim in a form a monetary economist or central-bank researcher can check. Mode, derivation and refuters are stated before the conclusion, so the reader knows what to look for and what would settle it.

**Mode: `general` on the shape, `specified` on instrument choice, not `grounded`.** No welfare experiment on leaning against a boom is claimed. This packet is a simulated derivation checked for internal consistency against the figures (`05_what_shape_means.md` section 6), not a ground reading. It cannot settle the question it reports on.

## 1. The claim, in one paragraph

The figures do not return "always respond" or "never respond." They return a conditional shape. If the mandate is constitutively about prices, the consumer-price index is the anchor and no direct asset-price response is licensed. If the mandate also covers financial stability, a quiet consumer-price reading is an instrument on one axis and cannot settle the balance-sheet axis. Consumer prices, asset prices and credit are separate slots, so a composite index hides the live signal; the boom, the instability and the low inflation form a union that must be unbundled before a lever is chosen; the instrument must contact the credit and leverage cascade, which the policy rate alone does not; the inflation target states a goal but cannot decide the outcome; and the cost of deciding and the cost of acting must be reported in separate columns. The packet offers the shape, the branch condition and refuters written in advance. It stops there.

## 2. Three checkable sub-claims

### C1: Framework-internal, checkable by reading Figure 1 and the Symbols table

- **Statement:** a decision rule that reads only the consumer-price slot cannot see a live residual in the credit or asset slot. Folding the slots into one index performs the exact aggregation the figure forbids.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 1 :19 "The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated." Fig 1 :22 "Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average." Symbols table: "R is measured per slot, never as a single global number."
- **Mode:** `general`, structural.
- **Refuter:** show that consumer prices, asset prices and credit are one slot at the decision horizon, so no movement in one can occur without a corresponding movement in the others. If that holds, the per-slot census is wrong for this question and the claim is withdrawn.

### C2: Framework-internal with a mandate condition, checkable by reading Figure 2

- **Statement:** whether CPI is an anchor or an instrument depends on what the question is constitutively about. Since this question names financial instability, a quiet CPI print is an instrument on the price axis and cannot settle the stability axis.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 2 :36 "An anchor is legitimate when the question is constitutively about it." Fig 2 :38 "instruments can be wrong on axes they do not measure."
- **Mode:** `general`.
- **What to check:** the actual legal mandate. A price-stability-only mandate flips the branch to "do not respond directly." The claim is conditional on that text, not on the reader's preference.
- **Refuter:** a mandate document that makes price stability the sole constitutive object together with evidence that financial stability enters only as a constraint on the inflation forecast. Under that condition the claim's standing branch flips, and the flip is recorded rather than treated as a defeat.

### C3: Framework-internal, checkable by reading Figures 11 and 12

- **Statement:** the instrument must contact the slot where the residual lives. The relevant ground is the credit and leverage cascade, one to three links out, not the asset price alone. Description cost and action cost must be kept in separate columns.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 11 :43 "The habitat is enumerated, never composed." Fig 11 :53 "steps out." (the full passage is "first-order enumeration, second-order effect, and the residual that mattered was three steps out.") Fig 12 :126 "Bits price the description. Actions price finding out whether it holds."
- **Mode:** `specified` on the instrument list, `general` on the two-cost rule.
- **What to check:** does the proposed instrument actually move credit growth, leverage or collateral values, and by how much? Are the two costs reported in different units rather than summed into one score?
- **Refuter:** a controlled study showing the policy rate alone contacts the credit and leverage slots sufficiently, or a decision calculus that prices description and action in one unit without residue. Either result withdraws the specified claim; neither touches C1 or C2.

## 3. What is explicitly not claimed

- Not "always tighten" and not "never tighten." Both are `general`-mode verdicts over a union compound.
- Not that the policy rate is the wrong instrument. It is the instrument of the price slot; the claim is that it does not by itself contact the credit slot.
- Not that macroprudential tools are proven to work in this economy. That requires a contact test (Fig 11 :40).
- Not that consumer prices and asset prices are unrelated. The claim is structural: where the residual is indexed.
- Not `grounded`. Nothing here is settled by a welfare experiment.
- Not that agreement among reviewers counts as corroboration. The second seat in `11_audit.md` shares this packet's evidence pool, so its agreement is internal consistency (Fig 2 :40).

## 5. What would settle it

A ground reading, not a vote:

1. A source-discriminating observation: does a quiet CPI print with rising credit and leverage precede a bust more often than a quiet CPI print without them? This tests C1 at the ground.
2. A contact test: does the chosen instrument move the credit and leverage slot, and by how much? This tests C3 (Fig 11 :40).
3. A two-column cost ledger: description cost and action cost reported separately, never summed (Fig 12 :126, :129).
4. A mandate text check: is the question constitutively about prices alone? This selects C2's branch.

## 6. What an expert can check in twenty minutes

1. Read Fig 1 :19 and :22, Fig 2 :36 and :38, Fig 11 :43 and :53, and Fig 12 :126. Six short passages.
2. Check C2 against the mandate text you know. Which branch is live in your jurisdiction?
3. Check C3 against one instrument you know well. Does it contact the credit slot directly, or only through borrowing costs?
4. Name one observation that would distinguish a benign boom from a credit-fuelled one while CPI is quiet. If none exists, say so; that is a finding about the instrument, not about the framework.
5. Send the refutation. A failure is more useful than an endorsement, and agreement is not pooled as corroboration.
