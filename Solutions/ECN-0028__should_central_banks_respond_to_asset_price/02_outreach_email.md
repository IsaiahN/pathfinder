# Outreach draft: check one output before you decide whether the framework is worth reading

**Use:** one to one, or as a cover note attached to a submission through a public venue in `03_public_contact_points.csv`. **Not** for bulk send.

**Hard precondition:** the shape claim must stay in `general` mode and the refuter must stay visible. If a send-off version turns the conditional into "central banks should respond," the two-stage offer collapses on contact.

---

**Subject:** One checkable claim about asset booms and a quiet CPI print

> Hi [name],
>
> I have one specific output I would like you to check against what you already know. I am not asking you to read a framework first, and I am not asking you to agree with anything. If the output does not survive, there is nothing else to look at.
>
> **The output (`general` on the shape, `specified` on instrument choice, not `grounded`):**
>
> The question "should central banks respond to asset-price booms when consumer-price inflation is low?" bundles three conditions that overlap. A quiet consumer-price reading is an instrument on the price axis. It does not, by itself, comment on the balance-sheet axis. So the Tether returns a conditional shape instead of a yes or a no:
>
> 1. If the mandate is constitutively about prices alone, the consumer-price index is the anchor and no direct response to asset prices is licensed by this derivation.
> 2. If the mandate also covers financial stability, consumer prices, asset prices and credit are separate slots. A composite index aggregates away the live signal. The instrument has to contact the credit and leverage cascade, which sits one to three links beyond the asset price; the policy rate alone does not. The inflation target states a goal but cannot decide the outcome, and the cost of deciding whether the boom is dangerous and the cost of acting on that decision belong in two different columns.
>
> Since your question names financial instability, the second branch is the one I would ask you to check. The first branch is recorded, not discarded.
>
> **What I am not claiming:** that central banks should always tighten into a boom, or never do; that the policy rate is the wrong instrument; that macroprudential tools are proven to work in your economy; or that consumer prices and asset prices are unrelated. I am claiming a shape: separate slots, a named contact, and a named measurement that would settle the response. The framework returns a shape, not a name.
>
> **What would refute the shape claim (written before this email):**
> 1. Consumer prices, asset prices and credit are shown to be one slot at the decision horizon, so no independent live residual exists.
> 2. A price-stability-only mandate is shown to make consumer prices the sole anchor. That flips branch 2 to branch 1 and is recorded as a branch condition, not a defeat.
> 3. A controlled study shows the policy rate alone contacts the credit and leverage slots sufficiently, so no separate instrument changes contact.
> 4. A decision calculus prices the description cost and the action cost in one unit without residue.
>
> If any of these holds, I will withdraw or repair the claim. A one-line "this fails because..." is more useful to me than agreement. If you would rather not engage, no reply is needed.
>
> **Before you answer, what would you count as a failure?** I would like your verdict on the shape and the refuter recorded before I send you any theory, because once you have the framework in front of you, your agreement stops being a useful check.
>
> **If it survives, and only then:** here are the sources. The figures, not my summary of them, are the authority. I will send the links after your verdict, not with this note.
>
> **If you want to use the machinery:** I can send the formula as a method to reuse. Please write down what would refute your own result before you run it, and state the mode of what you get. A reported failure is more useful to me than a reported success.
