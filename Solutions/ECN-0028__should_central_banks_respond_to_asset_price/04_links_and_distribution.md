## Two findings in the wider literature that bear directly on this question

1. **The disagreement is a union, not a contradiction to be averaged.** Filardo (2000) concludes that folding asset prices into the target measure does not improve performance; Borio and Lowe (2002) conclude that in some circumstances a policy response is appropriate. Both are well-formed on different subsets. Fig 9 :5 says "are not one hard question but several well-formed ones. Split it rather than search." So the correct use of these two sources is to map which subset each one governs, not to split the difference and announce a centrist verdict.
2. **The empirical result is two-currency.** Akram and Eitrheim (2008) find that adding house prices, equity prices and credit growth helps some stability measures and hurts others because of higher interest-rate volatility. Fig 12 :126: "Bits price the description. Actions price finding out whether it holds." The figure does not predict the empirical result; the empirical result is a low-overlap corroboration of the figure's two-currency rule.

## Distribution: offer, do not push

Figure-grounded reasons:

- **Fig 10 :52** "Bring a characterised residual, never the source." Sending the full framework before a reader has described the residual sends a recording, not a generator. Lead with the conditional shape and its refuter.
- **Fig 2 :45** "Frames range against the ground. They do not negotiate with it." The reader's first job is to check the output against what they know, not to be persuaded by the architecture.
- **Fig 9 :27-28** "describe it before you go looking, or any frame you pick will seem to fit." Ask what they would expect to see in a credit-fuelled boom with a quiet CPI before mentioning the figures.
- **Seat Map :163** "High correlation is disqualifying for a verdict and efficient for a convention." A list selected for likely agreement produces a convention, not a check.
- **Fig 2 :42** "Buy independence in proportion to the cost of being wrong." A mistaken policy judgment is expensive, so spend effort on reviewers whose public record touches the credit and asset-price residual and who work in a different closure from this derivation.

**Recommended order in a reply:** (1) the conditional shape and the four refuters; (2) the question "what would you count as a failure here?"; (3) record the verdict; (4) only then the three source links, with the note that the figures, not this summary, are the authority; (5) the formula as a method to reuse, with the instruction to write a fresh refuter and state a mode.

## The two-stage offer, restated

1. **Stage 1:** send the residual in effect terms, the branch condition, and the refuters. Ask for a failure, not an endorsement. Record the verdict before showing theory.
2. **Stage 2, opt in:** if the reader is curious, send the figures text, the canonical README and one worked derivation. Their later agreement is never pooled as corroboration (Fig 2 :40).
3. **Stage 3, opt in:** offer the formula as a method. It is a generator, so giving it away does not give away an answer (Fig 4 :22). Each user writes their own refuter and states their own ground.
