# Union, dependency order, and instrument analogues

**Mode: `specified` on the ordering, `general` on the union claim.** This is a simulated derivation checked against the figures, not a ground reading (see `05_what_shape_means.md` section 6).

## RESIDUAL

The question bundles "asset-price boom," "financial instability," and "low consumer-price inflation." Is this one problem with three symptoms, a partition into three separate problems, or a union compound with its own loop? And if it is a union, in what order must the levers be applied?

## DOMAIN CENSUS

- **D1, the price-rule frame.** Actors: committee, policy rate, target. Currency: forecast minus target. Anchor: realised inflation, legitimate only for the price question (Fig 2 :36).
- **D2, the balance-sheet frame.** Actors: banks, borrowers, collateral, leverage, asset markets. Currency: credit growth, valuation gaps, funding spreads. Anchor: realised instability, a default or a bust.
- **D3, the checking frame.** Actors: reviewers. Currency: agreement and residuals. Anchor: the ground, never agreement. In this simulated run it shares the primary's evidence pool.

Order by dependency (Fig 3 :20): "The chain is ordered by dependency, not by importance." Perception of D2 comes before vocabulary for D2, which comes before an objective that includes D2, which comes before choosing an instrument that contacts D2.

## DERIVATION

### Q1: Partition or union?

Fig 9 :5: "are not one hard question but several well-formed ones. Split it rather than search."

Fig 9 :57: "a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either."

Fig 9 :54: "And when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again."

The three conditions are individually well-formed. A boom can be studied without instability; instability can arise without a boom; low CPI can accompany either. They overlap, so they do not partition the space. The overlap is a compound: a credit-fuelled boom with a quiet CPI. That compound has its own ground and its own loop. So the figure verdict is **union, not partition**, and the first move is unbundling, not lever selection.

**Falsifier, written before the run:** if records show the three conditions never occur apart at the decision horizon, the union collapses to one condition and a single lever suffices. This claim fails and the functional-group reading in Q3 stands instead.

### Q2: What is the dependency order of the levers?

Fig 3 :19: "Optimising a link that already works cannot rescue one that does not."

Fig 3 :27: "A link with no instrument is not a link that works. It is a link nobody has looked at."

The price chain is already strong: CPI is measured, forecast, and targeted. The balance-sheet chain is the weak middle link. Effort spent refining the inflation forecast cannot rescue the missing credit instrument. So the order is: enumerate the balance-sheet habitat first, decide what would count as progress there second, and only then choose the instrument. This reverses the common order, in which the rate decision is taken first and financial stability is treated as a constraint afterwards.

**Falsifier:** show that the balance-sheet link is already instrumented and working (credit aggregates are timely, decisive, and acted on), so effort above the break is no longer wasted. Then the order reverses.

### Q3: Are the two objectives isomers, or a functional group?

Fig 12 :117: "Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."

Fig 12 :124: "functional group. A substructure whose presence fixes the outcome. The group says what kind; the qualifier says which member."

Price stability and financial stability, as written in most mandates, do not share atoms: one reads CPI, the other reads leverage and credit. They are therefore not isomers. They are two members of a **functional group**, the group being "a comparison of a measured state against a threshold" (Operators :51: "a goal is a comparison, which is why a vocabulary without it cannot state one"), with different operands. The stronger isomer claim would require both objectives written over a full shared atom set (prices, credit, leverage, expectations), differing only in arrangement. That is a checkable structural claim, not the current mandate.

**Falsifier:** if both objectives are shown to use the same operands and the same threshold relation, they are the same term under two names, and the functional-group reading is withdrawn.

### Q4: The instrument analogues, and which one contacts the residual

Fig 11 :61: "Introduction is available; exclusion is not." A committee can add a macroprudential instrument; it cannot subtract the credit habitat.

Fig 11 :40: "An improvement that does not change contact changes nothing, however much it improves."

Two analogues are available from Fig 12, and they are not interchangeable.

1. **Comparison (⋛) / the target.** Operators :48: "a threshold relation between two quantities." The inflation target is a comparison term. Fig 10 :47 warns that "a convention that decides an outcome has stopped being one." So the target can state a goal; it cannot decide whether to lean against a boom.
2. **Subtraction (−) / the gap.** Fig 12 :112: "A vocabulary without bonds can name a state and cannot name a route to one." Formula composition section: "progress is a subtraction." A valuation gap or a credit-to-trend gap is a subtraction term, and it is what gives the credit instrument something to act on. A vocabulary with only the comparison cannot name the route from a quiet CPI to a risky balance sheet.

The policy rate is the instrument of the comparison; capital buffers, loan-to-value limits, and credit-growth ceilings are the instruments of the subtraction. Fig 11 :43-44 requires that these be enumerated from contact, not composed from the target.

**Falsifier:** show that the subtraction term is not reachable from the bank's instrument set (the bank cannot observe or act on the credit gap), in which case the habitat must be handed to a different seat and this derivation's instrument list is withdrawn.

### Q5: The round trip between the two venues

Fig 4 :30: "So take the round trip and measure the gap. Without it you are navigating by dead reckoning: confident about a position nobody checked."

A rule carried from a pure price-stability regime into a stability-inclusive regime is a head start, not a replay. Fig 4 :22: "Only methods cross up. Only head starts come down." The method that crosses is "keep slots separate and name the ground." The recording that does not cross is "this committee tightened by 50 basis points in 2006." The round-trip gap R_T is the set of constraints dropped in the move, and `11_audit.md` enumerates it.

## CONCLUSION

The question is a **union compound**, not a partition and not one hard problem. The dependency order runs from the balance-sheet habitat to the objective to the instrument, reversing the usual rate-first order. The two objectives are a **functional group**, not isomers, under the current mandates. The target (a comparison) cannot decide the outcome; the credit gap (a subtraction) is the reachable route. The instrument list is `specified`, contact-dependent, and not `grounded`.

What survives is the unbundling and the order. What does not survive is any single "should." The stronger isomer claim and the exact instrument list remain open, each with a falsifier stated above.
