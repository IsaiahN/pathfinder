# Are the candidate transport mechanisms isomers? Structure, order and analogues

**Mode: `specified` on the structural application, `general` on the figure definitions.** Simulated derivation checked against the figures, not a ground reading (see `05_what_shape_means.md` section 5).

## RESIDUAL

Mansy 2010 lists five permeability mechanisms for the same observable behaviour: solubility-diffusion, transient pore, packing defect, head-group gated and lipid carrier. If they are all routes by which "large polymer retained, small nutrient passed" happens, what is their structural relation, and which one does the framework license us to name? Two questions are owed: (1) are they **isomers** under Fig 12, and (2) what is the **dependency order** of the boundary conditions they serve?

## DERIVATION

### Q1 - Are the mechanisms isomers?

Fig 12 :117: *"isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."*

The five mechanisms do not share the same atoms in the chemical sense. Solubility-diffusion uses partitioning and diffusion through the hydrophobic core; a transient pore uses a water-lined defect; a carrier uses a flipping fatty acid. They share the **observed formula** ("a permeable boundary") but differ in what composes the route. Under a strict chemical reading they are **not isomers**.

The looser, level-relative reading is more useful: at the level of the observation they share a description and differ in arrangement, which is Fig 12's isomer shape applied to mechanisms rather than molecules. The framework is domain-agnostic (Fig 4 :19-20 *"Only the actors, the currency and the anchor change"*), so the shape can cross levels, but the crossing must be declared.

**Strain, named.** Mechanisms are not molecules. Applying Fig 12 to them is an analogue, not a derivation. The discriminator that would make the claim testable is an instrument, exactly as *The Hard Problem is About Source* requires (*"An instrument that can't distinguish these cases demonstrates its own inadequacy"*). I record the strain rather than smoothing it. Verdict: `specified`, triangulated for the shape (Fig 12 + the source-discrimination essay), strained for the operand type.

**Falsifier, written before the run:** if two mechanisms with the same ingredients and different internal arrangement produce identical permeability under every resolvable condition, the isomer distinction collapses into indiscernibility, and the honest report is "one mechanism under two descriptions," not two isomers.

### Q2 - Are they a functional group instead?

Fig 12 :124: *"functional group. A substructure whose presence fixes the outcome. The group says what kind; the qualifier says which member."*

"Semipermeable membrane" is the **group**: it fixes the kind of outcome (retain large, pass small). "Fatty acid vesicle with a carrier mechanism" and "phospholipid vesicle with a packing-defect mechanism" are **qualifiers**: they say which member. Mansy 2010 provides the qualifier contrast directly: *"Even subtle differences in partition coefficients can lead to large permeability differences"* and ribose *"crosses monoacyl and diacyl lipid membranes approximately fivefold faster than diastereomers of equal molecular weight"*.

So the most the figures license is the group claim plus an open member slot. Naming the member is a binding from the chemistry frame (F5 of `01`). Verdict: **functional group, not isomer, at the chemical level; isomer-shaped at the level of the observation.** Both kept, neither averaged.

### Q3 - What is the order of the boundary conditions?

Fig 3 :20: *"Optimising a link that already works cannot rescue one that does not."* The boundary chain is ordered by dependency:

1. **Containment** (Fig 13 :10 *"near, far, between, contained"*). Without a inside/outside distinction there is nothing to retain.
2. **A spendable difference across it** (Fig 13 :22 *"a difference that can be spent"*). Without a gradient there is no direction for passage.
3. **A typed filter** (Fig 4 :21 *"Not a wall: a filter with a direction."*). This is where retention and exchange are both served.
4. **Import of what the interior cannot compose** (Fig 6 :14 *"the only operator that moves the wall"*). Nutrients are imports; waste is the efflux that keeps the closure workable.
5. **Growth and division** (world layer). Chen and Walde 2010: *"the membrane forms a semipermeable barrier that permits small molecules to pass into the cellular space and traps modified (e.g., phosphorylated or polymerized) products."*

The permeability link (3 to 4) has instruments, so it is not Fig 3's invisible middle link (Fig 3 :27 *"A link with no instrument is not a link that works. It is a link nobody has looked at."*). The link that usually lacks an instrument in this field is the coupling from membrane composition to inherited phenotype, which Fig 11 :52 calls the contact cascade.

### Q4 - Which bond does growth write?

Fig 12 :70-79 distinguishes **sequence** (*"A then B; B needs A to have happened"*) from **production** (*"A produces B; B did not exist before"*).

Membrane growth does not merely precede a pH gradient; it produces one. Chen and Walde 2010 report that *"Fatty acid flip-flop into the inner leaflet will also transport protons ... so growth transduces the energy from the micelle-vesicle transition into a pH gradient across the membrane."* That is a production bond (`⇒`), not a sequence (`→`). Writing it as `growth → gradient` would record the ingredients and lose the relation, the flat-formula failure Fig 12 :5 names (*"A set of ingredients is not one. The bond is the rest of the information, and it is the half nothing was recording."*).

The chain is therefore: `containment + gradient ⇒ selective crossing ⇒ import/efflux ⇒ growth ⇒ (new) gradient`, with each `⇒` a production, not a mere order. Verdict: `specified` on the chemical binding, `general` on the bond distinction.

### Q5 - What about the union of retention and exchange?

Fig 9 :55-56: *"A single lever clears the region where only one condition holds and leaves the intersections standing, because the second condition still binds."* Tightening a membrane is the single lever: it improves retention and blocks import. The residue is the compound where both bind, and it is *"categorically different"* (Fig 9 :57). Ma and Yu 2025 model this: phospholipid content stabilises the membrane but *"the permeability of the membrane declines in proportion to the rise in phospholipid content"*, selecting for transport functions. The compound is the real object, not a harder version of either condition.

## CONCLUSION

The candidate transport mechanisms are **not chemical isomers**: they do not share atoms. They are members of a **functional group** ("semipermeable membrane"), and they are **isomer-shaped at the level of the observation**, where several arrangements give the same reading. That last claim is a declared analogue and carries the strain of applying Fig 12 to mechanisms; the discriminator must be an instrument. The dependency order is containment, gradient, typed filter, import, growth, with production bonds rather than mere sequence where growth creates a gradient. No new primitive and no import that moves the wall is required for any of this; the only import is the chemical cast itself, which is provenance-bearing world material.
