**Subject:** Check one boundary rule before you decide whether the framing is worth reading

> Hi [name],
>
> I have one specific output I would like you to check against what you already know. I am not asking you to read the framework first, and I am not asking you to agree with it. If the output does not survive, there is nothing else to look at.
>
> **The residual, in effect terms:** a boundary that retains everything starves and accumulates inhibitors. A boundary that passes everything cannot hold a concentrated interior. The question is what rule lets a boundary discriminate what crosses.
>
> **The output (`specified`, not `grounded`):** the framework's membrane figure states the shape as *"Not a wall: a filter with a direction."* Applied to a protocell, this predicts that a working boundary is typed and directional, that it keeps what composes inside while importing what the inside cannot make, and that "useful" versus "waste" is a role assigned by the compartment's state rather than a label carried by the molecule. It does not say which molecule does the job. The framework returns a shape, not a name.
>
> **What I am not claiming:** that the framework names fatty acids, phospholipids, transient pores or partition coefficients as the answer. Those bindings come from the chemistry frame, not from a figure derivation, and until provenance and a shadow test are shown they carry zero evidentiary weight. I am also not claiming this is settled by experiment.
>
> **What would refute the shape claim, written before this message:**
> 1. a boundary with no selective rule, passing everything or nothing, still sustains a composing and growing interior;
> 2. a compartment grows and replicates its genome with zero net import across its boundary;
> 3. a figure line names a specific molecule as the answer;
> 4. retention and exchange turn out to be one condition, with a single lever that optimises both and leaves no compound residue;
> 5. the round trip from the shape to the chemical description is lossless.
>
> If any one holds, the shape claim is wrong and I will retract it. A one-line "this fails because..." is more useful to me than agreement. If you would rather not engage, no reply is needed.
>
