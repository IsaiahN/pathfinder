## 1. The claim, in one paragraph

The input is common (an authoritarian regime falls) and the output varies. The figure-grounded shape is: accountability is a property of an anchor whose ruling does not change when the powerful push on it, not a property of a new label; old power is a habitat you can add actors to but cannot subtract, so personnel replacement alone changes nothing; new institutions must draw on evidence low-overlap with the old network, or they are one frame wearing two names; the wall moves only by import from a closure that differs, and that import is debited against independence; and the reform list is an ingredient list whose bonds and order carry the difference between outcomes. The whole is a union of overlapping conditions, so verdicts stay per domain and are never averaged. The framework returns this shape, not a country or a policy. Three checkable sub-claims follow.

## 2. Three checkable sub-claims

### C1: Accountability is an anchor property, not a label

- **Statement:** a new oversight body that has not been shown to be non-updating under pressure has not been shown to be accountable. Novelty of name, law or personnel is not evidence of the anchor property.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 2 :29-30 "An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks. A measurement, an experiment, a counter, a market, a patient outcome. If it moves when you push on it, it is not an anchor." Also Fig 2 :33 "The anchor must not update." And the second, low-overlap line: Fig 10 :24 "the ground does not decay, the channel does".
- **What to check:** is there any measure in your field of whether oversight rulings change when former power holders apply pressure? If yes, that is the instrument the claim needs. If no, the claim is unreached, not refuted.
- **Refuter (R1):** a transition in which the oversight body's rulings demonstrably do not change under such pressure, yet the old power structure fully reproduces. That breaks the anchor verdict.

### C2: Old power is a habitat that persists; you can add, you cannot subtract

- **Statement:** replacing personnel while leaving the network of relations, incentives and informal rules intact does not change contact, so does not change capability. Vetting, lustration and new agencies are acts of introduction into a habitat the transition did not author; isolation substitutes one habitat for another and inherits its actors.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 11 :40 "A system cannot be made more capable by improving the agent in isolation. An improvement that does not change contact changes nothing, however much it improves." Fig 11 :61 "Introduction is available; exclusion is not." Fig 11 :67 "Move the residual and you must reproduce everything its goal required, and you inherit the new habitat's actors, which you did not choose."
- **What to check:** in a transition you know, were the security services, patronage networks and economic dependencies enumerated before reform, or were new agencies introduced with the cascade unenumerated? Did the inherited officials survive their own use (Fig 12 :119, catalyst) or were they spent?
- **Refuter (R2):** a transition in which the old habitat is fully dismantled and rebuilt from different contact, yet old power reproduces. That breaks the habitat verdict.

### C3: Independence decides whether accountability means anything, and imports are debited

- **Statement:** oversight that draws on the same evidence pool as the network it oversees is not a second frame. External support can supply the missing atom and spend the independence at the same time.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 2 :31 "Independence, between two frames, is how little they draw on the same evidence." Fig 2 :40 "disqualifying for a verdict: agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool." Fig 6 :23 "No frame escapes its own closure. The bound moves only on contact with a frame whose closure differs." Fig 6 :24 "Import from a live peer is debited against that frame's independence."
- **Important counting rule:** Fig 8 :47 "Independence is one quantity read twice." Fig 2 and Fig 8 are two readings of one quantity, not two witnesses. The verdict is single-source in the figures and is corroborated in the world corpus only secondarily.
- **What to check:** did the new court or commission share personnel, funding, sources or career histories with the body it was meant to oversee? If external actors supplied the key atom, did the new body subsequently range against the donor rather than against the ground?
- **Refuter (R3/R4):** a transition with genuinely low overlap between new institutions and the old elite network but no accountability, or a transition that produced accountability entirely inside the old closure with no import from a differing frame. Either breaks this verdict.

## 3. What is explicitly not claimed

- Not a country case, a country ranking, or a prediction about any named transition.
- Not that elections, a free press, vetting, trials or a new constitution are sufficient or necessary. They are atoms; the bond and the order were not recorded.
- Not that "political will" or "elite commitment" is a cause. Neither has a nameable anchor.
- Not that old elites should be removed. Exclusion is unavailable; only introduction is.
- Not that external assistance is unconditionally beneficial. It carries a debit against independence.

## 5. What would settle it

A discriminating instrument, not a further argument. From *The Hard Problem is About Source*: a colour instrument that reads green "cannot comment on the source"; the spectrometer resolved it. Here:

1. An anchor test: a measure of whether oversight rulings change under pressure from former power holders.
2. A contact test: a before/after measure of inherited networks and dependencies, not a count of new agencies.
3. An independence test: a measure of overlap in personnel, funding and evidence between new institutions and the old network.
4. An import test: identification of the external frame that supplied the missing atom, and the debit against independence that followed.
