# Outreach draft: check the output before you decide about the theory

**Use:** one-to-one, or as a cover note attached to a submission through a public venue in `03_public_contact_points.csv`. **Not** for bulk send. Field: democratization research.

**Hard precondition:** the shape claim and its refuters below must survive your check before any theory is offered. If stage 1 fails, there is nothing else to look at.

---

**Subject:** One checkable output on transitions from authoritarian rule

> Hi [name],
>
> I have one specific output I would like you to check against what you already know about transitions from authoritarian rule. I am not asking you to read a theory first, and I am not asking you to agree with anything. If the output does not survive, there is nothing else to look at.
>
> **The question in effect terms.** The input is the same in both cases: an authoritarian regime falls. In one branch the new arrangement produces accountable institutions; in the other the old networks and informal rules persist under new labels. What distinguishes the two is the thing I am asking you to check.
>
> **The output (`specified`, not `grounded`).** Four claims, each with a failure condition:
>
> 1. Accountability is a property of an anchor, not of a new label. A body whose findings change when the former power holders push on it has not become accountable, however new it looks. The figure line is: an anchor "does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks" and "if it moves when you push on it, it is not an anchor" (Fig 2 :29-30).
> 2. Replacing personnel without changing contact changes nothing. The old power structure is a habitat of relations and incentives; you can add an actor to it and you cannot subtract it.
> 3. Oversight that draws on the same evidence as the network it oversees is one frame wearing two names. Independence is the deciding quantity.
> 4. A list of reforms is an ingredient list, not a route. The order and the relation between the reforms carry the information.
>
> **The refuter I wrote down before running this:**
>
> - A transition where the oversight body's rulings demonstrably do not change under pressure from former power holders, yet the old structure fully reproduces.
> - A transition where the old habitat is fully dismantled and rebuilt, yet old power reproduces.
> - A transition where new institutions have genuinely low overlap with the old elite network, yet no accountability results.
> - A transition that produced accountability entirely inside the old closure with no import from any frame whose closure differs.
>
> If any one holds, the corresponding claim is wrong and I will retract it. A one-line "this fails because..." is more useful to me than agreement. If you would rather not engage, no reply is needed.
>
> **What I am not claiming:** that this names a country or a policy. The framework returns a shape, not a name. Courts, elections, a free press and vetting are bindings from your field onto the shape, offered as hypotheses with provenance, not as derived conclusions. I am also not claiming that a new constitution or a new election law is an answer; those are atoms, not bonds.
>
> **If it survives, and only then:** the source material is below. The figures are the authority, not my summary of them.
> - Verbatim figure text with line numbers (local canonical extract): `FRAMEWORK/reference/03-FIGURES_TEXT.md` in the project folder.
> - The canonical usage rule: `FRAMEWORK/reference/FIGURES_README_canonical.md`.
> - The eight essays by Isaiah N, indexed in `05_what_shape_means.md` of the solution folder.
>
> **If you want to use the machinery:** the full derivation is in the folder, with the residual, the domain census and all six refuters. Run it on a transition you know well and can check against a ground. Write down what would refute your result *before* you run it. A reported failure is more useful to me than a reported success.

## Two-stage offer, stated plainly

1. **Stage 1:** the four claims and the four failure conditions above. Ask what the recipient would count as a failure. Record the verdict here, before any theory is shown.
2. **Stage 2, opt-in only:** if they are curious, offer the local figure extract, the canonical usage rule and the essays. Their later agreement is never pooled as corroboration, because by then they hold the framework's interior (Fig 2 :40).
3. **Stage 3:** offer the formula as a method to reuse, with the instruction that each user states their own ground and writes their own refuter first.

No stage asks for trust, agreement, or a public endorsement. No bulk send. One-to-one only.
