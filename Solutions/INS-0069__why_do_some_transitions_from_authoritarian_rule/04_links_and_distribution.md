## Wider literature that bears on the output

These were not opened as full texts this session and are not treated as witnesses. They are signposts for a checker who wants to test the shape against the field.

- Guillermo O'Donnell and Philippe Schmitter, *Transitions from Authoritarian Rule: Tentative Conclusions about Uncertain Democracies* (1986). The classic treatment of uncertainty and contingency in transitions; the natural first stop for a refuter of F1 and F6.
- Adam Przeworski, *Democracy and the Limits of Self-Reform* (1991). The claim that democracy institutionalizes uncertainty about who wins is the closest field analogue to an anchor that does not update on the powerful.
- James Mahoney and Douglass North on path dependence and increasing returns in institutions; Ruth and David Collier on critical junctures. These are the primary sources behind the Wikipedia path-dependence entry and bear on F2.
- Ruti Teitel, *Transitional Justice* (2000), and Roman David, *Lustration and Transitional Justice* (2011). The personnel versus structure debate maps onto the introduction-not-exclusion finding.
- Steven Levitsky and Daniel Ziblatt, *How Democracies Die* (2018), on mutual toleration and forbearance; Nancy Bermeo on executive aggrandizement. These bear on F3 and on the figure line that a convention which decides an outcome has stopped being one.
- Kathryn Sikkink and Carrie Booth Walling (2006, 2007) on the justice cascade and human rights trials. Their finding that countries with both a truth commission and trials improved more on the Political Terror Scale is a candidate ground for part of the shape.

## Distribution: offer, do not push

Figure-grounded reasons:

- Fig 4 :22 "Only methods cross up. Only head starts come down." Sending the full architecture before anyone has described a residual sends a recording, not a generator. Lead with the four claims and the refuter; offer the links after.
- Fig 10 :42 "A verifier that can reconstruct the claim cannot verify it." A recipient who has absorbed the framework before being asked for a verdict has joined the frame being checked.
- Fig 9 :27-28 "describe it before you go looking, or any frame you pick will seem to fit." Ask what they would expect to see if the shape claim were true before giving them the derivation.
- Fig 2 :40 "disqualifying for a verdict: agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool." A send selected for people who already agree produces a convention, not corroboration.
- Fig 1 :19 "The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated." Keep the four domain verdicts separate; do not offer a single success score.

**Recommended order in a reply:** (1) the four claims and the refuter; (2) ask what the recipient would count as a failure and record it; (3) only then the local figure extract, the canonical usage rule and the essays, with the note that the figures, not the summary, are the authority.

## The two-stage offer

- **Stage 1 before stage 2** is Fig 10 :52 "Bring a characterised residual, never the source." The recipient checks the output without holding the framework's interior.
- **Stage 2 is opt-in by construction.** Once a recipient has absorbed the theory, their agreement stops being evidence. The stage 1 verdict must be recorded first and later agreement never pooled (Fig 2 :40).
- **Stage 3 sends a method**, which is permitted under Fig 4 :22. The formula is a generator, so giving it away does not give away an answer.
- **Stage 3 still needs a ground per problem.** A frame "cannot check the direction of its own question, because the shape a frame is searching for is not a term it holds" (THE_FORMULA, "Direction"). Each user must write down what would refute their result before running it and label the mode of what they get.
