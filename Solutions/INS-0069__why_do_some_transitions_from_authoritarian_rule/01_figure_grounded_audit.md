## 0. The residual in effect terms

The input is held constant: an authoritarian regime loses power. The output varies: in one branch the new arrangement settles accountability (wrongs are named, power is constrained by something that does not bend to the powerful, turnover becomes routine); in the other the same networks, incentives and informal rules persist under new labels and new personnel.

R is not "why do democracies succeed." It is the variance in outcome given a common input. Stated as a gap: what does the new arrangement range against, such that a ruling changes when the old power holders push on it in one case and does not in the other?

- **Arity:** at least two conditions, accountability produced and old structure reproduced, and empirically a union of overlapping conditions (Fig 9 :55: "A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones."). A case in both is a compound, not a harder case of either.
- **Symmetry:** not symmetric. The transition is directed (before and after), and the bonds that carry it are ordered: sequence, production, comparison, subtraction, refusal (Fig 12). Reordering the reforms changes the substance.
- **Scale:** the single reform is first order; the regime-level arrangement is second order, and a settled reform becomes an atom for whatever composes over it (Fig 12 :104). Judging a reform before it has settled against the ground is a reading taken below the break (Fig 3).

**Bound ordinary term, per the formula's "Direction" step.** Before running, I write down what an answer would look like so it can accrue a residual like anything else: *an accountable transition is one whose oversight anchor does not update on the powerful, whose new institutions draw on evidence low-overlap with the old network, whose habitat has been enumerated and actively worked rather than merely relabelled, whose wall was moved by a verified import from a differing closure, and whose reforms are bonded in an order with an instrument at the middle link.* This is a shape, not a country and not a policy list. It is refusable by the tests in section 3.

## 1. Domain census

One line per domain: actors, currency, anchor. No nameable anchor means it is not a domain yet. Partition or union stated, then dependency order (Fig 3).

| domain | actors | currency | anchor | question type |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| D1 The accountability anchor | oversight bodies, courts, auditors, press, citizens as witnesses | whether a ruling changes when the powerful push on it | the ground of accountability itself (what happened, who did what), which must not move when pushed | anchor test; a domain only because the anchor is nameable |
| D2 The habitat of old power | inherited officials, security services, party cadres, business networks, patronage ties | contact and influence: who can act on whom, what flows | none (the habitat updates; it is explicitly not the ground) | enumeration, never composition |
| D3 The transition design | negotiators, drafters, reformers, election administrators | bonds and order: which reform precedes which, what is a goal, a comparison, a subtraction, a refusal | the ground settles each reform; a gate is not the ground | dependency chain, ordered |
| D4 The external frame | exiles, diaspora, international courts, foreign donors, conditionality bodies | imports | a frame whose closure differs from the old regime's | import with source gate and shadow test |

**Partition or union.** Union, not partition. The two conditions, accountable institutions produced and old structures reproduced, overlap; a case in both is a specific compound (Fig 9 :55). A single lever clears the region where only one condition holds and leaves the intersection standing. Unbundle before searching again.

**No domain for "political will" or "elite commitment."** There is no nameable anchor for either; they are re-descriptions of D1 and D2 outcomes, not independent domains. Listing them would be a name where the framework owes a shape.

**Dependency order.** D2 first (enumerate what is in contact with the residual), then D4 (find a closure that differs), then D3 (bond the reforms in order), then D1 (settle whether the resulting body is an anchor). Refusal before mint (Formula step 2): the old claim "we are now democratic" must be refused before any new term is admitted. The middle link of Fig 3 is the one to watch: having terms for what a situation affords, and saying what would count as progress, has neither an obvious failure nor an obvious test.

## 2. Findings

### F1: Accountability is an anchor property, not a label (decisive for D1)

Fig 2 :29-30: "An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks. A measurement, an experiment, a counter, a market, a patient outcome. If it moves when you push on it, it is not an anchor."

Fig 2 :33: "The anchor must not update."

Fig 2 :34: "If it does, panel 3 turns into panel 1: the anchor becomes another frame, everything drifts together, and the drift is coherent, which is why nobody notices."

Fig 10 :24: "the ground does not decay, the channel does"

Fig 10 :25-26: "stale percepts, dropped transients, saturated metrics, pooled readings"

A new oversight body whose findings change when the former power holders apply pressure has not produced accountability. It is panel 1: the anchor has become another frame and the whole arrangement drifts together. The drift is coherent, which is exactly why observers inside the transition do not notice it. Renaming the ministry, rewriting the constitution or holding an election does not update the anchor; those are changes in the frames, not in what the frames range against.

The second figure line governs a different quantity: the ground does not decay, the channel does. A transition can lose touch through stale percepts and pooled readings even while every formal institution is newly created. Accountability is therefore a property of contact, not of novelty.

**Verdict D1:** triangulated across Fig 2 (the anchor must not update) and Fig 10 (the ground does not decay, the channel does). Low overlap: one governs the anchor's invariance, the other governs channel degradation. Mode `specified`. Refuter R1 below.

### F2: The old power structure is a habitat that persists; you can add, you cannot subtract (decisive for D2)

Fig 11 :40: "A system cannot be made more capable by improving the agent in isolation. An improvement that does not change contact changes nothing, however much it improves."

Fig 11 :43: "The habitat is enumerated, never composed."

Fig 11 :61: "Introduction is available; exclusion is not."

Fig 11 :67: "Move the residual and you must reproduce everything its goal required, and you inherit the new habitat's actors, which you did not choose."

Fig 11 :65: "Introducing an actor for its cascade is ecosystem engineering, after Jones, Lawton and Shachak, 1994. Cane toads and kudzu are the same move with the cascade unenumerated."

Replacing personnel (the agents) while leaving the network of relations, conditions and incentives (the habitat) intact does not change contact, so it does not change capability. Vetting, lustration and institutional reform are acts of introduction into a habitat the transition did not author. Isolation is not removal: you substitute one habitat for another and inherit its actors, including the ones you did not choose. The power is asymmetric; you can add an actor and you cannot subtract the habitat, because contact is a fact about the world and does not consult you.

This is why "remove the old elites" is the wrong frame. Exclusion is not available. What is available is enumeration of the cascade, then introduction with the cascade accounted for. Introducing a new court into a patronage habitat with the cascade unenumerated is the kudzu move: the actor arrives and acts, and what it brings with it was never read off the world.

**Verdict D2:** single-source for the figure definition (Fig 11). World corroboration is genuinely low-overlap: path dependence in institutional economics, transitional-justice institutional reform, and ecosystem engineering in ecology all return the same shape from different evidence pools. Mode `specified`. Refuter R2 below.

### F3: Independence decides whether accountability means anything (D1/D2 overlap)

Fig 2 :31: "Independence, between two frames, is how little they draw on the same evidence."

Fig 2 :40: "disqualifying for a verdict: agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool"

Fig 8 :48: "How much two frames draw on the same evidence decides both whether their agreement means anything and whether their union has any surplus."

An oversight body staffed from, funded by, and socially continuous with the network it oversees is not a second frame. It is the same frame wearing a different name. Its findings will agree with the old network not because both checked the ground but because they share the pool. Buy independence in proportion to the cost of being wrong (Fig 2 :42).

**Retraction inside this finding.** I first counted Fig 2 and Fig 8 as two independent witnesses for the independence verdict. That was double counting. Fig 8 :47 says plainly: "Independence is one quantity read twice." The two figures are two readings of one quantity, so this verdict is **single-source**, not triangulated. It is corroborated in the world corpus by a 2011 study of 163 countries on judicial independence, which is a different evidence pool, but that corroboration is secondary and cannot promote the figure verdict to two witnesses.

**Verdict D1/D2 overlap:** single-source (figure), world corroboration. Mode `specified`. Refuter R3 below.

### F4: The wall moves only on contact with a differing closure (D4)

Fig 6 :23: "No frame escapes its own closure. The bound moves only on contact with a frame whose closure differs."

Fig 6 :14: "the only operator that moves the wall"

Fig 6 :24: "Import from a live peer is debited against that frame's independence."

Fig 8 :32: "Closure is idempotent. Composing inside a closed set never leaves it, so no search, however long, adds an atom."

An authoritarian closure cannot mint accountability from inside its own atoms. However long the internal reform process runs, composition inside the closure never leaves it. The wall moves only on contact with a frame whose closure differs: exiles, a diaspora, an international court, an external election observer, a foreign parliament. But imports are not free. Import from a live peer is debited against the importing frame's independence, so external support can buy the atom and spend the independence at the same time. That is the honest cost line, and it is where externally backed transitions commonly fail: the new anchor ranges against the donor rather than against the ground.

**Verdict D4:** single-source (figure), world corroboration from international factors in democratization and from hybrid courts in transitional justice. Mode `specified`. Refuter R4 below.

### F5: A list of reforms is a flat formula, not a molecule (D3)

Fig 12 :5: "A set of ingredients is not one. The bond is the rest of the information, and it is the half nothing was recording."

Fig 12 :111: "A vocabulary without bonds can name a state and cannot name a route to one."

Fig 12 :119: "catalyst. A part that survives its own use. Spent and regenerating parts behave differently across uses, and nothing records which a term is."

Fig 12 :117: "isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."

Fig 3 :19: "Optimising a link that already works cannot rescue one that does not."

"Elections, constitution, free press, trials, vetting" is an ingredient list. It records the atoms and not the bonds. The substance is in the order and the relation: which reform produces which, which is a comparison, which is a subtraction, which is a refusal of the old claim. Under a symmetric bond two spellings are one term; under an ordered bond they are different substances. Transition sequences are ordered, so re-ordering is not a cosmetic change.

The same personnel under a different bond can be an **isomer** (Fig 12 :117): same atoms, different arrangement, different substance. This is the figure-grounded reason why two transitions with the same formal ingredients can diverge. The inherited officials are also a **catalyst** (Fig 12 :119), a part that survives its own use; spent and regenerating parts behave differently across uses and the notation usually fails to record which is which.

**Verdict D3:** triangulated across Fig 12 (the bond carries the information) and Fig 3 (links are ordered and status is per link). Low overlap: one is a notation result, the other a dependency result. Mode `specified`. Refuter R5 (in `06_isomers_and_order.md` and section 3).

### F6: This is a union compound and must be unbundled before it is searched

Fig 9 :55: "A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones."

Fig 9 :27-28: "describe it before you go looking, or any frame you pick will seem to fit"

Fig 9 :32: "you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself"

The question bundles two conditions: an accountable institution was produced, and the old structure was not reproduced. Those overlap. A single lever clears the region where only one holds and leaves the intersection standing, which then becomes a specific compound rather than a harder instance of either. The order of work is residual first, frame second. If you pick the comparative-politics frame or the transitional-justice frame before describing the residual, any of them will seem to fit. What the figures return is a shape that any answer must have, never the answer itself; so the derivation cannot name Tunisia, Poland or South Africa as "the answer."

**Verdict (cross-domain):** single-source (Fig 9), with the canonical figures README confirming the shape-not-a-name limit. Mode `general` on the structural claim. Refuter: show the two conditions do not overlap in any observed transition (then it is a partition and may be searched as two questions).

### F7: The link that usually breaks is the middle one (D3)

Fig 3 :27: "A link with no instrument is not a link that works. It is a link nobody has looked at."

Fig 3 :29: "Before asking whether a system is good, ask which link it currently stops at, and whether the answer was measured or assumed."

Fig 3 :19: "Optimising a link that already works cannot rescue one that does not."

A transition can perceive the old regime and plan elections, yet have no terms for what accountability would count as, and no instrument for whether it arrived. That is the link between vocabulary and objective, the one with neither obvious failures nor obvious tests. Effort above the break (constitution drafting, election administration, international recognition) arrives at a step that cannot use it. A reading taken below the break is not a weak reading; it is a reading of nothing.

**Verdict D3:** single-source (Fig 3), world corroboration from transitional-justice literature on the difficulty of measuring effectiveness. Mode `specified`. Refuter: exhibit an instrument that measures whether an oversight body's rulings change under pressure, and show the link works.

## 3. Pre-registered refuter

Written before the conclusion, as required: a falsifier written before the run is a design instrument. If any of the following holds, the corresponding finding is withdrawn.

- **R1 (against F1):** a transition in which the accountability body's rulings demonstrably do not change when former power holders push on it, yet the old power structure fully reproduces. Accountability would then not be an anchor property as claimed.
- **R2 (against F2):** a transition in which the old habitat (networks, personnel, informal rules, security services, economic dependencies) is fully dismantled and rebuilt from different contact, yet old power reproduces. The habitat verdict would fail.
- **R3 (against F3):** a transition in which new institutions demonstrably draw on evidence low-overlap with the old elite network, yet no accountability results. Independence would not be the deciding quantity.
- **R4 (against F4):** a transition that minted accountability entirely inside the old closure with no import from any frame whose closure differs. The wall would have moved without the only operator that moves it.
- **R5 (against F5):** a transition where the same formal ingredients in a different order produce no difference in outcome, or where the inherited officials are demonstrably spent rather than regenerating and the model cannot record it. The bond and catalyst claims would fail.
- **R6 (against F6):** evidence that the two conditions never overlap. Then the problem is a partition, not a union, and the unbundling finding is wrong.

Until one of these is run, nothing above may be labelled `grounded`.

## 4. Recommendation

1. Lead with R1 to R6, not with a theory of democratization. Ask what a failure would look like before offering any derivation (Fig 10 :52: "Bring a characterised residual, never the source.").
2. Stop treating a new constitution, a new election law or a new anti-corruption agency as an answer. They are atoms, not bonds (F5). The checkable question is what the new body ranges against (F1) and what evidence it draws on (F3).
3. Enumerate the habitat before designing the reform. Read the cascade outward until it stops mattering (Fig 11 :44). A reform introduced with the cascade unenumerated is kudzu.
4. Price the import. External support that supplies the atom but spends the independence has bought a mirror, not an anchor (F4, Fig 2 :35 "Alignment is triangulation, not negotiation. A mirror cannot correct you.").
5. Do not average. Report each domain's verdict separately (F8, Fig 1 :19).
6. Send the generator, not a recording: the residual, the shape, the refuter. Figure 4 :22: "Only methods cross up. Only head starts come down."
