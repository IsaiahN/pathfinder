## Boundary and standing

This folder is the entire work product. No file outside it was modified. The figure authority is the local canonical extract at `FRAMEWORK/reference/03-FIGURES_TEXT.md`; every `Fig N :line` citation points there. World sources were fetched and are listed with author, venue, year, accessed date and a quote in `10_source_ledger.csv`.

**This is a simulated derivation checked for internal consistency against the figures, not a ground reading.** By the framework's own rule (*The Recursive Self-Improvement Trap*: LLM output is "simulacra" and "the truly testable output would need to be derived from the real architecture or working the formula yourself"), this package cannot settle the question it reports on. It reports the shape any answer must have and the instruments that would close it.

## Effect restatement of the question

The input is the same in both cases: an authoritarian regime loses power. In one branch the new arrangement settles accountability, in the other the same networks, incentives and informal rules persist under new labels. Asked in effect terms: what does the new arrangement range against, so that a ruling changes when the powerful push on it in one case and does not in the other? An answer would be a shape with a refuter, not a list of country names or a ranked set of policy recommendations.

## What is deliberately not here

- No personal emails of named individuals.
- No ranking of people by how likely they are to agree.
- No bulk-send plan.
- No claim that the figures name a country or a specific reform. They return a shape, not a name.

Reason, in the figures' own words: *"disqualifying for a verdict: agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool"* (Fig 2 :40). Selecting recipients for receptiveness manufactures the correlation that would make their agreement worthless. *"A verifier that can reconstruct the claim cannot verify it."* (Fig 10 :42). The first contact therefore carries the residual and its refuter, not the framework.
