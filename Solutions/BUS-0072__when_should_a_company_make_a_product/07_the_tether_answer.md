# The Tether's answer on make or buy

**Mode: `specified` - not `grounded`.** This is a shape and a route, not a result. Simulated derivation checked against the figures (`05_what_shape_means.md` section 7), not a ground reading. No contract or cost measurement pays for any claim here.

## 1. The residual it answers

An answer would explain why the gap between what a firm's routines predict about a component and what the market actually returns stays small when the component is made inside in some slots and bought in others. R is indexed by component, never averaged (Fig 1 :19, :22). The question is about **the location of the boundary**, not about the level of a firm's outsourcing percentage.

The union must be unbundled before it is searched (Fig 9 :54). It is three conditions, not one hard question:

- **C1:** the component is reachable and settles under internal composition.
- **C2:** an import from a supplier's closure produces a gated surplus that survives the independence debit.
- **C3:** the make-capability survives over time, so the channel to the make-ground stays reachable.

## 2. The shape

1. **Make is internal composition, and it cannot add a primitive.** Fig 6 :21: *"MINT composes inside the closure and can never add an atom."* So "we can make it" is a reachability question, and "we should make it" is a settlement question. A firm that keeps searching inside its own library forever never acquires a capability it did not already hold (Fig 8 :32, *"Closure is idempotent. Composing inside a closed set never leaves it, so no search, however long, adds an atom."*).
2. **Buy is the only operator that can expand reach, and it is gated and debited.** Fig 6 :23: *"No frame escapes its own closure. The bound moves only on contact with a frame whose closure differs."* Fig 6 :14: *"the only operator that moves the wall"*. But an import has a *"source gate: provenance"* and a *"shadow test: explains a gap"* (Fig 6 :12, :13), and Fig 6 :24: *"Import from a live peer is debited against that frame's independence. A wrong atom costs more than starting from primitives."* So the real cost of a buy includes the knowledge the firm stops holding.
3. **A supplier's surplus needs differing closures.** Fig 8 :45: *"Two frames together reach what neither reaches alone."* If the supplier's closure is identical to the firm's, the union adds nothing. Hammerstein and Noë 2016 state the same boundary in animal behaviour: *"We tend to refuse a particular trade if we know that a better deal can be obtained elsewhere, or if we can pick the cherries ourselves with little effort."*
4. **The boundary is per component, not a corporate posture.** Fig 1 :19: *"The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated."* A blended outsourcing ratio is a pooled reading (Fig 10 :26) and destroys the diagnostic.
5. **The contract and the market are the anchor, not internal agreement.** Fig 2 :29: *"An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks. A measurement, an experiment, a counter, a market, a patient outcome."* Fig 2 :35: *"Alignment is triangulation, not negotiation. A mirror cannot correct you."* Strategic-importance scores and board consensus are instruments, and instruments can be wrong on axes they do not measure.
6. **Losing the make-capability is channel failure, not ground decay.** Fig 10 :24: *"the ground does not decay, the channel does"*; :26: *"saturated metrics, pooled readings"*; :29: *"keep the anchor reachable"*. The market keeps paying the supplier; the firm's own readings of its capability went stale.
7. **A make-capability can change sign.** Fig 11 :50: *"The same actor is a benefactor to one residual and a malfactor to another, so mutualism and parasitism are relations rather than kinds."* Fig 12 :119: *"catalyst. A part that survives its own use. Spent and regenerating parts behave differently across uses, and nothing records which a term is."* The line that produced the advantage can become the thing that prevents the next one.
8. **Make and buy are bonds, not extra atoms.** Two firms with the same component list but different make-buy patterns are isomers under Fig 12 :117: *"isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."*

## 3. What it explicitly does not say

- Not `grounded`. No prospective ground measurement has paid for this shape.
- Not a name. It does not say which component a particular firm should make, which supplier to choose, or which activities are "core". Fig 9 :32: *"you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself."*
- Not an average. It makes no claim about what percentage of components should be outsourced.
- Not that transaction costs, capabilities, world-renowned frameworks or internal expertise are irrelevant. They are bindings of Γ or of the supply habitat. The claim is that none of them settles the boundary on its own (Fig 11 :39).
- Not a theory of everything about firm boundaries. It stops where the instrument stops.
- Not corroboration by agreement. Klein, Mazzoni, Coase, Williamson, Fine and Whitney and Reitzig and Wagner overlap in one evidence pool (Fig 2 :31, :40). Hammerstein and Noë is the independent low-overlap witness, and it corroborates the mechanism, not the firm-level fact.

## 4. What would settle it - the ground

A source-discriminating instrument, not a further argument. The source essay's metamerism rule: a reading of green *"cannot comment on the source"*, and *"The answer is unreached until a better instrument exists."* Here the readings are cost savings and delivery records; the candidate sources are a genuinely differing supplier closure, a temporary quote, an accounting allocation, or a firm that has quietly lost the ability to make.

The falsifiers, pre-registered in `01` and repeated in `08`:

1. a firm adds a genuinely new atom to its capability by internal composition alone, with no contact with a differing closure;
2. a supplier's component expands the firm's reach with zero independence debit and no coordination cost, or a wrong import costs less than starting from primitives;
3. an averaged make-or-buy ratio predicts prospectively and per component which named slot will fail;
4. one intervention restores all three conditions in all failure types with nothing categorically different left over;
5. a sourcing decision validated only by internal agreement survives an active-rivalry test with no correction;
6. a supplier whose closure is identical to the firm's still produces a durable surplus;
7. a firm loses the ability to make a component while its internal readings of cost and quality stay fresh and unpooled;
8. a make-capability's measured value stays the same across a documented change in what the market rewards.

A prospective, per-component study that measures C1, C2 and C3 separately, records the bonds as well as the atoms, and reports which condition failed before the boundary moved would turn this from `specified` toward `grounded`.

## 5. The honest stop

**Unreached, not unreachable.** Fig 13 :134: *"Unreachable is a verdict here, not a silence,"* and Fig 8 :39: *"So a search that finds nothing is never a proof of absence."* The shape stops at the limit of the available instruments and records the lineage so the stop is checkable. The missing thing is a discriminator of sources: an instrument that would tell a genuine supplier surplus from a temporary quote, an accounting choice, or a quietly lost internal capability. Naming a winner from a cost reading would be the metamerism error the source essay forbids. The eight conditions in section 2 are a shape; the ninth thing, which component a named firm should make next, is not held and is not pretended.

## 6. One-paragraph version for outreach

> The Tether does not rank components or name the supplier that wins. It says that the make-or-buy boundary is a relation, not a list: a component belongs inside the firm when internal composition is reachable and settles, and outside when an import from a supplier whose closure differs produces a gated surplus that survives the independence debit, with the make-capability retained over time so the channel stays reachable. Those are three conditions with three different failure modes, not three degrees of one problem. Making cannot add a capability you do not hold; buying can, but it costs independence and the knowledge you stop keeping. Losing the ability to make is the channel going stale, saturated or pooled into an average, while the market keeps paying someone. The strength that gave the advantage can change sign when the component's role changes, so the line that won yesterday can block the next win. The contract and the market, not internal agreement, are the anchor. What is offered is that shape, eight falsifiers written in advance, and the instruments that would close it. Which component occupies which slot stays open until a source-discriminating instrument exists, and the stop is recorded rather than filled.
