## 1. The claim, in one paragraph

The make-or-buy boundary is a relation between two ways of getting a component, not a ranked list of "core" versus "non-core" activities and not one cost threshold. Three conditions have to be handled at once. First, a component can be made only where the firm's library of routines, tools and knowledge can compose it and where doing so settles on the ground; internal search cannot add a capability the firm does not already hold. Second, buying is an import from a supplier's closure. It is the only route that can expand the firm's reach, but it is gated by provenance and a shadow test, and it is debited against the firm's independence, so the real cost includes the knowledge the firm stops keeping. Third, the make-capability has to survive over time; a firm that buys for years and loses the ability to make has not seen the market stop paying, its own channel went stale. These are three different failure modes with three different instruments, not three degrees of one problem. A make-capability can change sign when the component's role changes. The contract and the market, not internal agreement, are the anchor. The claim is a shape that any candidate answer must have; it does not name which component a particular firm should make.

## 2. Three checkable sub-claims

### C1 - Framework-internal; checkable by reading Figures 6, 8 and 13

- **Statement:** making composes inside the closure and cannot add a primitive; buying is the only operator that moves the wall, and a supplier's surplus requires closures that differ.
- **Source, verbatim:**
  - Fig 6 :21 - *"MINT composes inside the closure and can never add an atom."*
  - Fig 6 :23 - *"No frame escapes its own closure. The bound moves only on contact with a frame whose closure differs."*
  - Fig 8 :32 - *"Closure is idempotent. Composing inside a closed set never leaves it, so no search, however long, adds an atom."*
  - Fig 8 :45 - *"Two frames together reach what neither reaches alone."*
  - Fig 13 :115 - *"The set is closed. The arrangements are not. That gap is why import exists."*
- **Refuter:** a firm that adds a genuinely new atom to its capability by internal composition alone, with no contact with a differing closure; or a supplier whose closure is demonstrably identical to the firm's that nevertheless produces a durable surplus. Either fires against C1.

### C2 - Framework-internal derivation plus one low-overlap biological check

- **Statement:** an import is gated and debited against independence; a wrong atom costs more than starting from primitives; losing the internal capability is channel failure, not ground decay.
- **Source, verbatim:**
  - Fig 6 :12 - *"source gate: provenance"*; Fig 6 :13 - *"shadow test: explains a gap"*
  - Fig 6 :24 - *"Import from a live peer is debited against that frame's independence. A wrong atom costs more than starting from primitives."*
  - Fig 10 :24 - *"the ground does not decay, the channel does"*; Fig 10 :26 - *"saturated metrics, pooled readings"*
  - Fig 2 :29 - *"An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks."* The same line names *"a counter, a market"* as instances.
- **What an expert can check against their own knowledge:** Klein and Mazzoni 2025 report *"hidden costs" of outsourcing such as "loss of expertise within the firm, reduced ability to detect and respond to competition"* (Reitzig and Wagner 2010), and Fine and Whitney's framework distinguishes *"dependency for capacity"* from *"the most risky ... dependency for that knowledge"* (Weinstein 2004). Does either sentence not already name the independence debit in Fig 6 :24? A "no" to both is a real refutation of the mapping. Then the low-overlap test: Hammerstein and Noë 2016 report that under market competition *"mycorrhizal fungi would probably have lost the ability to directly acquire carbon"*. Is that not the same debit at a different stratum?
- **Refuter:** a supplier whose component expands the firm's reach with zero independence debit and no coordination cost; or a firm that loses the ability to make a component while its internal readings of cost and quality stay fresh, unpooled and ground-verified. Either breaks C2.

### C3 - Union, structure and competing explanations

- **Statement:** the boundary is a union of three conditions, per component, so one lever cannot clear it; two firms with identical component lists but different make-buy patterns are isomers, so a bill of materials alone cannot predict the boundary; the strength-to-rigidity flip follows from valence being relative to the residual.
- **Source, verbatim:**
  - Fig 9 :57 - *"What remains is smaller and it is also categorically different: a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either."*
  - Fig 1 :19 - *"The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated."*
  - Fig 12 :117 - *"isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."*
  - Fig 11 :50 - *"The same actor is a benefactor to one residual and a malfactor to another, so mutualism and parasitism are relations rather than kinds."*
  - Fig 12 :119 - *"catalyst. A part that survives its own use. Spent and regenerating parts behave differently across uses, and nothing records which a term is."*
- **Refuter:** one intervention that restores all three conditions in all failure types and leaves no categorically different residue; or two firms with identical atoms and identical make-buy bonds whose boundary outcomes diverge with no difference in ground contact or imported atoms, which would move the explanation out of composition and into habitat alone.

## 3. What is explicitly not claimed

- Not `grounded`. No measurement has paid for any part of this.
- Not a theory of everything about firm boundaries, and no final or unique theory claim.
- Not an average. No claim about the percentage of components that should be outsourced.
- Not a name. It does not say which component a particular firm should make, which supplier to pick, or which activities are core. That is a `gap` at the instrument boundary, not a hidden answer.
- Not that transaction costs, core competences, modularity or internal expertise are irrelevant. They are bindings of Γ or of the habitat; the claim is that none settles the boundary alone.
- Not that the economics and strategy literature independently corroborates this. Klein, Mazzoni, Coase, Williamson, Fine, Whitney and Reitzig and Wagner overlap in one evidence pool. ## 5. What would settle it

A prospective, per-component study that measures C1, C2 and C3 separately, records bonds as well as atoms, and reports which condition failed before the boundary moved. Concretely:

1. **Reachability and settlement test:** can the firm actually compose the component inside Γ, and do internal cost, quality and delivery converge on the ground? If not, C1 fails.
2. **Import gate and debit test:** does the supplier offer pass a source gate and explain a real gap, and what is the measured loss of internal knowledge after two years of buying? Fig 6 :12-13, :24.
3. **Channel test:** are the firm's readings of its own make-capability fresh and unpooled, or stale, saturated and averaged (Fig 10 :24-29)? Fig 7 :37-39 gives the two-factor form: bracket fidelity times ground pressure, zero either stops the chain.
4. **Source-discriminating instrument:** can the study distinguish a genuine supplier surplus from a temporary quote, an accounting allocation, or a quietly lost internal capability when all four produce the same cost reading? The source essay says a reading alone *"cannot comment on the source"*.

1. Read Fig 6 :21, :23, :24, Fig 8 :32, :45, Fig 13 :115, Fig 10 :24, Fig 1 :19, Fig 9 :57 and Fig 12 :117. Ten short passages, about eight minutes.
2. Check C1 and C2 against your own knowledge of sourcing: is there a documented case of a new capability acquired by internal composition alone, with no contact with a differing closure? Or a supplier with an identical closure producing a durable surplus? About five minutes.
3. Name one instrument that would discriminate a genuine supplier surplus from a temporary quote, an accounting allocation and a quietly lost internal capability using observable data, or say why none exists. About five minutes.
4. Send the refutation. A failure is more useful than an endorsement.
