# The Tether's answer: individual versus collective identity

**Mode: `specified` on the split verdict, `general` on the structural shape. Not `grounded`.** This is a shape and a route, not a result. Simulated derivation checked against the figures (`05_what_shape_means.md` section 6), not a ground reading.

## 1. The residual it answers

The question offers an exclusive or: universal, or dependent on social organization. The disagreement does not shrink when the same instrument is applied in different settings, and each rule keeps working well on a different subset. That is the signature of a union compound, not one hard question. Fig 9 :54 states: "And when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again." The residual is therefore split into form, weight and instrument before any verdict is given.

## 2. The shape, per domain (verdicts kept separate, never averaged)

**Domain 1, form (social cognition).** A boundary between person and group recurs at every scale. The loop does not change with size; only the cast does. Fig 4 :19-20 states: "Only the actors, the currency and the anchor change." So the *form* of the distinction is universal in the kernel sense. The poles are named from the world frame (independent and interdependent), not minted by the framework, and must be provenanced as a binding. Verdict: `general`, triangulated (Fig 4 kernel plus Fig 12 level-relativity).

**Domain 2, weight (social organization).** The importance of that boundary is not a property it carries. It is a relation between a boundary and a habitat. Fig 11 :39 states: "And capability is a property of agent-and-habitat, never of the agent alone." Fig 11 :50 states: "The same actor is a benefactor to one residual and a malfactor to another, so mutualism and parasitism are relations rather than kinds." These lines are about capability and valence, not identity by name, so they govern the *weight* quantity by analogy at the same level of organization. Verdict: `specified`, triangulated across Fig 11 and Fig 12, with the world case (rice versus wheat) contested at the measurement level by Roberts 2015.

**Domain 3, instrument (measurement).** A single universal score cannot be the anchor. Fig 2 :29 states: "An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks." An individualism score moves with translation, sample and frame (Soler-Anguiano et al. 2023), so it fails the anchor test. Verdict: `specified`, single-source from the figures for the anchor rule, with world corroboration from psychometrics and from the I-C critique.

**Not averaged.** Taking a mean of "universal in form" and "contingent in weight" produces a pseudo-verdict that answers nothing. Fig 1 :19 states: "The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated." The live slot is the habitat term; averaging across it is how the signal disappears.

## 3. The answer in one sentence

The distinction between individual and collective identity is universal in **form** (a boundary that recurs at every scale) but its **importance** is not universal: it is a derived quantity of the social organization, and any claim about importance is only as good as the instrument that discriminates its source. Mode `specified`, ungrounded.

## 4. What it explicitly does not say

- Not that individual identity is more real than collective identity, or vice versa.
- Not that any particular culture is individualist or collectivist as an anchor fact. Those are bindings from the world frame with contested provenance.
- Not that the rice/wheat finding is settled. Roberts 2015 raises shared-history non-independence; it is logged as a D3 conflict, not used to flip the verdict.
- Not that no boundary exists in a culture where none was found. Fig 8 :39 states: "So a search that finds nothing is never a proof of absence."
- Not that the framework names which pole occupies which slot. It returns a shape, not a name.
- Not `grounded`. No measurement settles the weight function here.

## 5. What would settle it, the ground

A source-discriminating instrument, not another argument. From *The Hard Problem is About Source*: a colour reading "cannot comment on the source," and it was the spectrometer that resolved the ambiguity. Here the corresponding tests are:

1. **Invariance Test:** does one identity-weight scale hold configural, metric and scalar invariance across the habitats compared, with culture-specific items declared rather than silently dropped?
2. **Habitat Test:** does adding an enumerated habitat term (coordination demand, relational mobility, kinship, cost of exit) change the predicted identity weight while the boundary form is held fixed?
3. **Source Test:** can two different habitats produce the same score, and can an instrument discriminate them? If not, metamerism stands and the score is not a verdict.
4. **Standing falsifiers from `01`:** a culture with no registrable boundary after a genuine inward search; a controlled cross-habitat invariance result that removes the weight dependence; a scale invariant everywhere with no culture-specific items; a re-census showing the kernel changes with size.

## 7. One-paragraph version for outreach

> The Tether does not answer "universal or contingent?" with a single word. It says the question is two questions bundled together. The form of the individual/collective boundary is a kernel invariant: it recurs at every scale while the actors, currency and anchor change. Its importance is not a property the distinction carries; it is a relation between a boundary and a habitat, so it changes as social organization changes. What cannot be asserted is a universal identity score, because such a score reads the same value for different sources and no instrument here discriminates them. What is offered is the split, the dependency order between form, weight and measurement, the falsifiers written in advance, and the tests that would close it. The ground stays open until an instrument settles it.
