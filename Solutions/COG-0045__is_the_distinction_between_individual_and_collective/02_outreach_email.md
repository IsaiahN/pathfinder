# Outreach draft: check the residual claim before deciding about the framework

**Use:** one-to-one, or as a cover note attached to a submission through the public venues in `03_public_contact_points.csv`. **Not** for bulk send.

**Hard precondition:** do not send until the split in `01_figure_grounded_audit.md` F1 and F3 is understood by the sender. A claim that averages universality and contingency collapses on contact, and the two-stage offer then has no stage 1.

---

**Subject:** One checkable claim about individual and collective identity, before any theory

> Hi [name],
>
> I have one specific output I would like you to check against what you already know. I am not asking you to read the framework first, and I am not asking you to agree with it. If the output does not survive, there is nothing else to look at.
>
> **The question as posed:** is the distinction between individual and collective identity universal, or does its importance depend on social organization? I am treating that "or" as a bundle of two questions, not one (Fig 9 :54: "And when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again.").
>
> **The output (`specified`, not `grounded`):** the form of the boundary is a kernel invariant across scales (Fig 4 :19-20: "Only the actors, the currency and the anchor change."). Its *importance* is a property of the person in a habitat, not a property the distinction carries (Fig 11 :39: "And capability is a property of agent-and-habitat, never of the agent alone."; Fig 11 :50: "The same actor is a benefactor to one residual and a malfactor to another, so mutualism and parasitism are relations rather than kinds."). So the honest answer is neither bare universal nor bare contingent: universal in form, contingent in weight. This is a shape and a route, not a measurement.
>
> **What I am not claiming:** that one national individualism score is an anchor; that a single scale travels unchanged across cultures; that the rice/wheat result is settled (Roberts 2015 raises a shared-history independence problem); or that the framework names which identity form occupies which slot. The framework returns a shape, not a name. Convergent derivation and adopted import look identical in the contents and only the record separates them.
>
> **What would refute the output, written before this email:**
> 1. a culture with no registrable individual/collective boundary at any resolution after a genuine instrument search;
> 2. a controlled cross-habitat comparison showing identity-weight invariance with scalar measurement invariance established;
> 3. a scale that is invariant in every tested culture with no culture-specific items and predicts behaviour equally in all of them;
> 4. a re-census showing the kernel changes with size rather than the actors, currency and anchor changing.
>
> If any holds, I withdraw the split. A one-line "this fails because..." is more useful to me than agreement. If you would rather not engage, no reply is needed.
>
> **If it survives, and only then:** the sources are below. The figures are the authority, not my summary of them.
> - Canonical figure text with line numbers (local extract in this project): `FRAMEWORK\reference\03-FIGURES_TEXT.md`
> - Figures and formula repository: https://github.com/IsaiahN/tether/tree/general-agent/figures
> - Cross-cultural measurement invariance and the I-C dichotomy: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10565350/
>
> **If you want to use the machinery:** the formula is in the figures link. Run it on a problem you know well and can check against a ground. Write down what would refute your result *before* you run it, and state the mode of what you get. A reported failure is more useful to me than a reported success.
