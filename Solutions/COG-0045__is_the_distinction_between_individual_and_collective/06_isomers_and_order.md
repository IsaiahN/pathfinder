### Q3 - Are the individual and collective poles isomers or a functional group?

- Fig 12 :117: "isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."
- Fig 12 :124: "functional group. A substructure whose presence fixes the outcome. The group says what kind; the qualifier says which member."
- As world literature normally writes them, the two poles differ in their atoms: an autonomous inner self versus a relational self-in-context. Under Fig 12's definition they are **not isomers**; they are two members of a functional group (identity boundary) with different atoms in the slots.
- The stronger form: if both are written over a shared atom set (boundary self/other, habitat, instrument) and differ only in which pole is bonded to which habitat term, then they **are** isomers: same atoms, different arrangement, different behavioural consequences. This is the claim worth testing, because it predicts that changing the bond while holding atoms fixed changes the score.
- World corroboration of the bond-dependence: Bower 2014 reports the same Han ethnic group producing different identity readings under rice versus wheat. The atoms (people, friends, self) are held; the bond to the habitat changes. Roberts 2015 contests the independence of those observations, which is a D3 problem, not a refutation of bond-dependence itself.
- **Falsifier, written before the run:** if the two poles can be written without a shared atom set, or if holding the bond fixed while changing the habitat leaves the reading unchanged, the isomer claim fails and the functional-group reading stands.

### Q4 - What is the dependency order?

- Fig 3 :20: "The chain is ordered by dependency, not by importance. Each link consumes what the one above produces."
- Fig 3 :25: perception and planning have obvious tests, but the middle link (terms for what a situation offers, and what would count as progress) "has neither, so it attracts the least attention and the fewest instruments."
- The order for COG-0045:
  1. **Perception:** can we register a boundary at all? If not, there is no residual to route.
  2. **Vocabulary:** do "individual" and "collective" travel between the psychology frame and the social-organization frame? Fig 9 :30-31 requires effect terms, not borrowed cause terms.
  3. **Objective:** what would count as the boundary being *important*? This is the missing link. Without a stated objective, no instrument can be built and no verdict can be measured.
  4. **Planning:** choose a cross-habitat comparison with an invariance test.
  5. **Learn and carry:** carry the method (effect description plus refuter), never a recording of one culture's score (Fig 4 :22-25).
- Fig 3 :23: "A step whose input never arrives cannot be diagnosed, only its predecessor can." Most of this literature operates at step 4 with step 3 unmeasured, which is why the universality disagreement does not shrink.

### Q5 - Is the kernel invariant with size?

- Fig 4 :19-20: "The kernel is the part that does not change with size: the same loop and the same rule for naming what worked, whether one agent is running it or a thousand. Only the actors, the currency and the anchor change."
- At one person: self-construal. At a village: coordination and kinship. At a nation: a score. The loop (register boundary, range against habitat, name what worked) is the same; the cast changes.
- **Falsifier:** a demonstration that the loop itself changes when the number of agents changes, i.e. that actors, currency and anchor do not exhaust what varies. That would break F1 in `01_figure_grounded_audit.md`.

## CONCLUSION

The question is a union compound (Q1). Naming independent and interdependent is a binding from the world frame, not a forbidden seventh primitive (Q2). The two poles are a functional group as normally written and a real isomer claim over a shared atom set (Q3). The dependency chain stops at the unmeasured objective "what counts as importance," which is where effort should go (Q4). The kernel is invariant with size while the cast changes (Q5). Each of these carries its own falsifier; none depends on treating a single universality score as an anchor.
