# Expert verification packet: the Tether on individual versus collective identity

**What this is:** a claim in a form a cultural psychologist or social cognition researcher can check. Mode, derivation and refuters are stated before the conclusion, so a reader knows what to look for and what would settle it.

**Mode: `specified` on the split verdict, `general` on the structural shape. Not `grounded`.** No measurement of identity weight is claimed here. This is a shape and a route, not a result.

## 1. The claim, in one paragraph

The question "is the individual/collective distinction universal, or does its importance depend on social organization?" is a union compound and must be split. The **form** of the boundary is a kernel invariant: the loop does not change with size, only the actors, currency and anchor do. The **importance** of that boundary is not a property it carries; it is a relation between a boundary and a habitat, so it varies with social organization. A single universal identity score cannot be the anchor because it reads the same value for different sources and no instrument here discriminates them. The verdict is universal in form, contingent in weight, and instrument-dependent in reading, with falsifiers written in advance.

### C2 - world-facing; checkable by a cultural psychologist

- **Statement:** identity weight is a relation to the habitat, not a fixed property of a person or a culture. Over a shared atom set the poles are a real isomer claim; as normally written they are a functional group.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 11 :39 "And capability is a property of agent-and-habitat, never of the agent alone." Fig 11 :50 "The same actor is a benefactor to one residual and a malfactor to another, so mutualism and parasitism are relations rather than kinds." Fig 12 :117 "isomer. A substructure whose presence fixes the outcome. The group says what kind; the qualifier says which member."
- **What to check:** is the analogy to capability and valence legitimate at the identity level, or does it govern another quantity (the strain test)? Does the rice/wheat case (Talhelm et al. 2014, Bower 2014) actually hold atoms fixed and vary the habitat bond? Does Roberts 2015 (shared-history non-independence) defeat the habitat reading or only the specific causal estimate?
- **Refuter:** a controlled cross-habitat comparison showing identity-weight invariance with scalar invariance established; or a result showing the habitat term adds no predictive variance once the residual is described; or a demonstration that the valence line is a false analogy here.

### C3 - measurement-facing; checkable by a psychometrician

- **Statement:** a single universal identity scale is not an anchor and exhibits metamerism: the same reading from different sources.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 2 :29 "An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks." Fig 2 :35 "Alignment is triangulation, not negotiation. A mirror cannot correct you."
- **What to check:** Soler-Anguiano et al. 2023 report scalar invariance for MXINDCOL across US and Mexican samples and degradation of INDCOL on translation; Wong, Wang and Klann 2018 argue the labels should be discontinued; Brewer and Chen 2007 show the "collective" in collectivism measures is often interpersonal networks. Do these support the metamerism reading, or is the score already source-discriminating?
- **Refuter:** a scale that is invariant in every tested culture with no culture-specific items, predicts behaviour equally in all of them, and survives a source-discrimination test.

## 3. What is explicitly not claimed

- Not a theory of everything; no final room. The habitat term stays a compound with its own loop.
- Not that either pole is more real, or that any culture is individualist or collectivist as an anchor fact.
- Not that rice farming causes collectivism. That world claim is contested by Roberts 2015 and is not the figure verdict.
- Not that the framework names which pole occupies which slot. It returns a shape, not a name.
- Not `grounded`. Nothing here is asserted as settled by experiment.
- Not that a null result proves absence. Fig 8 :39: "So a search that finds nothing is never a proof of absence."

## 5. What would settle it

A source-discriminating instrument and a habitat-controlled comparison, not a further argument.

1. **Invariance Test:** does one identity-weight scale hold scalar invariance across the habitats compared, with culture-specific items declared?
2. **Habitat Test:** does an enumerated habitat term change predicted identity weight while boundary form is held fixed?
3. **Source Test:** can an instrument discriminate two habitats that produce the same score?
4. **Standing refuters:** no registrable boundary after a genuine inward search; invariance across habitats; a universally invariant scale with no culture-specific items; a kernel that changes with size.
