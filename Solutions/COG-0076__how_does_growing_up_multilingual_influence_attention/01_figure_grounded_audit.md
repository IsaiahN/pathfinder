## 0. The residual under audit, in effect terms

The question is: *How does growing up multilingual influence attention, memory, identity, and social understanding?*

Stated as an effect, not as a topic:

- A child is raised in contact with two or more languages rather than one. Call that condition **M**.
- The question asks what difference M makes to four outcome slots: **attention**, **memory**, **identity**, **social understanding**.
- An answer would look like a **per-slot vector of effects**, each with a named instrument, a named mechanism, a stated direction, and a stated refuter. It would not look like one number called "the bilingual advantage".

Bind that description as an ordinary term before the run, per `THE_FORMULA.md` "Direction": *"make the sought-for shape an ORDINARY TERM - write down what an answer would look like, bind it, and let it accrue a residual like anything else."* The bound term here is:

> **SHAPE(COG-0076)** = four separate verdicts, one per slot, each anchored to an instrument or to structured human judgment as the question type requires, with the mechanisms kept as competing candidates rather than merged, and the direction of each effect left open until a ground settles it.

What each option leads to:

- **Option A, the umbrella reading:** treat "multilingual upbringing" as a single treatment producing a single general advantage. This aggregates across slots. Fig 1 :19 *"The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated."* Fig 1 :22 *"Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average."* It also selects the frame before describing the residual, which Fig 9 :28 forbids: *"any frame you pick will seem to fit"*.
- **Option B, the per-slot reading:** keep the four verdicts separate, anchor each to the right kind of ground, and let the mechanisms compete. This is the reading the figures govern and the one developed below.

The **R** being tracked is the gap between what a language-exposure frame predicts for a slot and what an instrument actually returns. Symbols table :38 *"It is the aim, not only the error signal, and it is always a slice: what cannot be perceived yet is residual and simply cannot be read."*

## 1. Domain census (before any figure line is chosen)

Describe R by arity, symmetry and scale, then one line per domain with actors, currency and anchor. A domain with no nameable anchor is not a domain yet.

### 1.1 R by arity, symmetry, scale

- **Arity:** four slots, not one. Fig 1 :19 *"The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated."* Symbols table :43 *"R is measured per slot, never as a single global number."* A global "bilingual advantage" is not a reading of R; it is the deletion of the only signal that says where to look.
- **Symmetry:** the relation between M and each slot is asymmetric (exposure is the condition, the slot is the outcome). The four slots are not interchangeable, and the valence of the same exposure differs by slot. Fig 11 :49 *"An actor's valence is relative to the residual, not a property it carries."* Fig 11 :50 *"The same actor is a benefactor to one residual and a malfactor to another, so mutualism and parasitism are relations rather than kinds."*
- **Scale:** developmental time. The loop closes within a child's lifetime; what crosses a generation is a method or a head start, not a recording (Fig 4 :22 *"Only methods cross up. Only head starts come down."*). Fig 4 :20 *"Only the actors, the currency and the anchor change."*

### 1.2 One line per domain

| domain | actors | currency | anchor | nameable? |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **D1, the developing child's frame** | the child, the two languages, caregivers, interlocutors, contexts of use | prediction residual per slot; new terms entering the library | behavioural and neural instruments for the slot, and the child's own record where the question is about the person | yes, instruments plus structured person-report |
| **D2, the measurement frame** (developmental cognitive science) | tasks (Simon, flanker, Stroop, false belief, vocabulary), samples, meta-analyses | effect sizes, precision, bias-corrected estimates | the instrument reading; the ground for this frame is whether the effect pays under a non-updating test | yes, experiment and pre-registered replication |
| **D3, the social and identity frame** | family, peers, institutions, language communities, cultural context | norms, recognition, self-concept | because the question is constitutively about people, structured human judgment, not consensus | yes, Fig 2 :36 *"An anchor is legitimate when the question is constitutively about it."* |

A candidate fourth domain, "general cognitive elegance", was considered and not admitted: it has no nameable anchor and no instrument, so by the census rule it is not a domain yet. This is a census decision, not a silence, and it is recorded here rather than hidden.

### 1.3 Partition or union

This is a **union**, not a partition. Fig 9 :54 *"And when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again."* Fig 9 :55 *"A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones."* The four outcomes overlap through three candidate mechanisms (executive function, metalinguistic awareness, sociolinguistic awareness). A single lever that clears the attention region can leave the identity and social-understanding intersections standing, because the second condition still binds. Fig 9 :57 *"a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either"*.

Consequence: the four slots get four loops, and the mechanisms are not averaged into one cause.

### 1.4 Order by dependency (Fig 3)

Fig 3 :20 *"The chain is ordered by dependency, not by importance."* The order is:

1. **perception**, the child discriminates the two languages (Fig 3 :4 "1 · perception");
2. **vocabulary**, the child has terms for what the situation affords, including that one object can have two names (Fig 3 :6 "2 · vocabulary");
3. **objective**, what counts as progress on a task, in the child's own terms (Fig 3 :9 "3 · the objective");
4. **planning**, a route under incomplete information;
5. **learn and carry**, name what worked, verify it, carry it.

The middle link is the one with the fewest instruments. Fig 3 :26 *"situation offers and being able to say what would count as progress, has neither, so it attracts the least attention and the fewest instruments."* Fig 3 :27 *"A link with no instrument is not a link that works. It is a link nobody has looked at."* This is why the metalinguistic and sociolinguistic accounts are the least measured in the literature (Yu, Kovelman and Wellman 2021, read), and why F7 below is a measurement finding, not a speculation.

Ordering rules that also bind: describe the residual before choosing a frame (Fig 9 :28); refuse before mint (`THE_FORMULA.md` step 2); settle a first-order composition before composing over it (Fig 12 :104 *"A settled molecule becomes an atom for whatever composes over it. So irreducible is a fact about a level and never about the world, and there is no bottom"*).

## 3. Findings

Each finding carries its mode, its verbatim figure lines, a refuter, and a triangulation mark. Verdicts are kept per domain and are never averaged.

### F1 — One residual per slot; the umbrella term aggregates the signal (decisive against Option A)

**Mode: `general` for the rule, `specified` for its application here. Domain: D1 and D2.**

Fig 1 :19 *"The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated."*
Fig 1 :22 *"Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average."*
Fig 1 :13 *"a global R near zero with one live slot residual is a legal state"*
Symbols table :43 *"R is measured per slot, never as a single global number."*

The four outcomes in the question are four slots. A claim of the form "multilingual children have better attention, memory, identity and social understanding" is one global score wearing four names. Fig 1 :13 says a near-zero global R with one live slot is a **legal state**, so an aggregate can be quiet while a slot is screaming. The world record shows exactly this shape: a meta-analysis of 152 studies (891 effect sizes) found no bilingual advantage in attention after bias correction, while the same corpus found a small **disadvantage** in verbal fluency (Lehtonen et al. 2018), and a 2024 preprint found multilingual children ahead on referential perspective-taking but **no** difference on emotion comprehension (OSF b3mht, 2024). One condition, different slots, different signs.

**Refuter:** a pre-registered analysis showing that a single aggregate factor predicts held-out developmental outcomes better than the four-slot decomposition, with the aggregate residual not smaller on any slot. If that fires, F1 is wrong.

**Status: triangulated.** Fig 1 (per-slot residual) and Fig 11 :50 (valence relative to residual) are two low-overlap figure lines, and Lehtonen 2018 and the 2024 preprint are two different methods. Not averaged.

### F2 — Multilingualism changes the habitat and the contact, not the child in isolation (decisive for the shape)

**Mode: `specified`. Domain: D1 and D3.**

Fig 11 :39 *"So the answerable question is not what intelligence is, but what makes a system capable. And capability is a property of agent-and-habitat, never of the agent alone."*
Fig 11 :40 *"A system cannot be made more capable by improving the agent in isolation. An improvement that does not change contact changes nothing, however much it improves."*
Fig 6 :23 *"No frame escapes its own closure. The bound moves only on contact with a frame whose closure differs."*

"Growing up multilingual" is not a property stored inside a child. It is a change in the habitat: another interlocutor set, another language community, another set of contexts in which a word must be chosen. The measurable consequence, if any, must be traced to changed contact. Bialystok's 2011 review supplies the sharpest natural test available: bimodal bilinguals (speech plus sign) can produce both languages at once and do not face the same selection conflict; in a flanker task they performed like monolinguals, while spoken-language bilinguals showed the advantage (Emmorey et al. 2008b, as reported in Bialystok 2011). A language *label* with no selection contact produces no effect. That is Fig 11 :40 in data.

Yu, Kovelman and Wellman 2021 put the same point in their own words: *"None of the three accounts claim that bilingualism induces extraordinary processes of ToM development; rather, bilingualism simply provides more opportunities to develop relevant skills."* More opportunities is a habitat description, not an agent upgrade.

**Refuter:** a study finding the same per-slot effect size for children who are bilingual by label but whose contact does not differ (for example, bimodal bilingualism, or classroom-only second language with no community use) as for children with documented differential contact. If that fires, F2 is wrong.

**Status: triangulated.** Fig 11 :40 and Fig 6 :23 are different figure lines (habitat capability and closure/contact), and Bialystok 2011 and Yu et al. 2021 are different research programmes.

### F3 — The second language is an import that adds terms to the child's library, and one referent can carry two names

**Mode: `specified`. Domain: D1.**

Fig 6 :14 *"the only operator that moves the wall"*
Fig 6 :22 *"NOVEL means novel relative to atoms, not to the world. IMPORT adds an atom, and the added atom is what expands the closure."*
Fig 6 :24 *"Import from a live peer is debited against that frame's independence. A wrong atom costs more than starting from primitives."*
Fig 12 :67 *"two names, one referent"* (the identity bond ≡)
Fig 8 :51 *"Convergent derivation and adopted import look identical in the contents. Only the record of where each came from separates them, and the surplus lives only where the closures"*

A second lexicon arrives from another frame (the language community) and enters the child's Γ. That is an import, and it is the only kind of move that expands what the child can compose. The identity bond is the exact structure the metalinguistic-awareness literature describes: a single pictured item can have two names, and discovering that is a measured precursor of theory-of-mind gains (Yu et al. 2021, reviewing Chan 2004 and Diaz and Farrar 2018b).

**Strain S1, named and kept.** Fig 6 :22 says IMPORT adds an atom. A vocabulary item is not one of the six substrate primitives; it is a molecule over held atoms (sound, meaning, context). Two readings compete: (a) the second language adds genuine primitives to the child's library, moving the wall; (b) it adds molecules and identities over primitives already held, expanding closure without adding a primitive. Re-census does not settle which, because the framework's atoms are defined at a different stratum from lexical items. I record this as a live strain, not a correction, and do not let it carry the verdict: F2 and F1 stand on contact and slot indexing, not on this atom count.

**Refuter:** show that a child's second language adds no reachable composition at all (no new term, no new bond, no new identity over an existing referent), so closure(Γ) is unchanged, and that the observed slot effects arise without any expansion of the library. If that fires, F3 is wrong.

**Status: triangulated for the identity-bond reading across Fig 6 and Fig 12, single-source for the atom-count strain.**

### F4 — Agreement inside the field is not corroboration (decisive for the literature reading)

**Mode: `general` for the rule, `specified` for the diagnosis. Domain: D2.**

Fig 2 :13 *"no anchor; each updates on the others"*
Fig 2 :14 *"they converge, and what they converge on"*
Fig 2 :15 *"is whoever had the most influence"*
Fig 2 :18 *"one evidence pool"*
Fig 2 :19 *"they never talk, and still agree"*
Fig 2 :20 *"so the agreement is the pool talking,"*
Fig 2 :21 *"not two frames confirming each other"*
Fig 2 :35 *"Alignment is triangulation, not negotiation. A mirror cannot correct you."*
Fig 7 :35 *"A room's agreement is variation, not a verdict. Selection between rooms is where the ground votes."*

The bilingual-advantage literature has hundreds of agreeing results. de Bruin, Treccani and Della Sala 2014 found that studies fully supporting the advantage were most likely to be published and studies challenging it least likely, with funnel-plot asymmetry. Lehtonen et al. 2018 found that the small advantages in inhibition, shifting and working memory disappeared after bias correction, and that null and negative findings were underrepresented in small samples. This is Fig 2's panel 2 (one pool) and panel 1 (coherent drift) at the level of a research field. Agreement here is a filter, not a witness.

Fig 9 :60 *"Use filters for the budget and witnesses for the verdict. Never let a filter hand you a verdict."* A meta-analysis that corrects for bias is a **filter** on the budget of what gets counted. It does not itself hand us the verdict on any child.

**Refuter:** two pre-registered, independently recruited, low-overlap samples using different task families returning the same nonzero per-slot effect after correction, with declared separate evidence pools. That would be a witness, not a pool, and would move the affected slot from `gap` toward `grounded`.

**Status: triangulated.** Fig 2 and Fig 7 are different figures; de Bruin 2014 and Lehtonen 2018 use different bias diagnostics but partly share the same literature pool, so they are counted as **one** world witness, not two.

### F5 — Anchors are question-specific; identity is anchored in persons, not in a task score

**Mode: `general`. Domain: D2 and D3.**

Fig 2 :29 *"An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks. A measurement, an experiment, a counter, a market, a patient outcome. If it moves when"*
Fig 2 :30 *"you push on it, it is not an anchor."*
Fig 2 :36 *"An anchor is legitimate when the question is constitutively about it."*
Fig 2 :37 *"Human judgment is the right anchor for is this a good answer for a person, because the question is about people. It is only an instrument for is this code correct, and instruments can be wrong on axes they"*
Fig 2 :45 *"Frames range against the ground. They do not negotiate with it."*

For **attention** and **memory**, the question is about information processing, so a task or neural measure is a legitimate anchor. For **identity**, the question is constitutively about a person and how that person is recognised, so structured human judgment (first-person report, interview, and judgment by people who know the child) is the legitimate anchor. Consensus is not a substitute: Fig 2 :41 *"efficient for a convention: agreement about a shared meaning is the product, not a corruption of it"* describes how a shared label works, not how a person's identity is settled.

This is also the strictest place to apply Fig 2 :33 *"The anchor must not update."* If the anchor for identity is a live opinion poll about what bilingual identity "should" be, it updates on the frame and stops being an anchor.

**Refuter:** an identity result claimed as settled from a single reaction-time or accuracy score with no person-level judgment, or an attention result claimed as settled from self-report alone. Either swap breaks the anchor rule.

**Status: triangulated across Fig 2 :36 and Fig 2 :37 (the two halves of the anchor-legitimacy rule). No world source was needed to establish it; world sources are silent on the anchor type, and silence is recorded as silence.**

### F6 — The four outcomes are a union with three competing mechanisms, not a partition with one

**Mode: `specified`. Domain: D1, D2, D3.**

Fig 9 :4 *"First: is there a fact here? Disagreements that do not shrink with effort, where each rule keeps working well on a different subset,"*
Fig 9 :5 *"are not one hard question but several well-formed ones. Split it rather than search."*
Fig 9 :54 *"And when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again."*
Fig 9 :57 *"a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either"*

Yu et al. 2021 review three candidate accounts of the social-understanding effect: executive function, metalinguistic awareness and sociolinguistic awareness. They conclude that the executive-function account, though most studied, has received the least empirical support, and that research measuring one account rarely measures the others. Buac and Kaushanskaya 2020 found that language ability, not EF, predicted theory of mind in simultaneous bilinguals, while EF, not language, predicted it in monolinguals and English-L1 bilinguals. Different routes to the same task, different predictors by acquisition history. Fig 8 :50 *"But shared atoms are not the signature. Two frames can hold the same atom by convergence, reaching it separately, which is corroboration at maximum independence."*

Fig 12 :117 *"isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."* The three mechanisms are isomers at the frame level when they are written over the same atom set (two languages, a referent, an interlocutor, a context) with different bonds: selection, identity, and matching-to-interlocutor. Which route is operative for a given child is a declared gap (see §6).

**Refuter:** evidence that the three mechanisms reduce to one latent factor that predicts all four slots equally across language histories, with no interaction by acquisition history. If that fires, the union reading collapses to a partition and F6 is wrong.

**Status: triangulated.** Fig 9 (union) and Fig 12 (isomer) are two figure lines; Yu et al. 2021 and Buac and Kaushanskaya 2020 are two different research groups with different samples.

### F7 — The middle link has no instrument, and that is the link that matters

**Mode: `specified`. Domain: D1.**

Fig 3 :25 *"Perception has obvious failures and obvious tests. Planning has obvious failures and obvious tests. The link between them, having terms for what a"*
Fig 3 :26 *"situation offers and being able to say what would count as progress, has neither, so it attracts the least attention and the fewest instruments."*
Fig 3 :27 *"A link with no instrument is not a link that works. It is a link nobody has looked at."*
Fig 3 :29 *"Before asking whether a system is good, ask which link it currently stops at, and whether the answer was measured or assumed."*

In the developmental chain, discrimination of two languages (perception) and performance on a task (planning) both have tests. Having terms for what the situation affords, that one object has two names and that different people know different languages, is the link with almost no instrument. Yu et al. 2021 found only three studies linking metalinguistic awareness to theory of mind in bilingual children and two linking sociolinguistic awareness, and note that almost all bilingual-ToM research is cross-sectional. The absence of a longitudinal instrument is not evidence that the link works; it is the Fig 3 :27 failure.

**Refuter:** a validated, pre-registered instrument that measures the affordance link directly in children before task performance is trained, and that shows the link does not mediate any slot effect. If that fires, F7 is narrowed rather than discarded: the middle link would then have an instrument and would be assessed.

**Status: single-source for the figure rule (Fig 3), with world corroboration on the measurement gap from Yu et al. 2021.**

**Mode: `specified`. Domain: D2.**

Fig 5 :53 *"refuted: a term we hold is wrong; a term is owed that says so"*
Fig 5 :54 *"A term that refuses another pays the same bargain. Stating the refusal, plus what remains unexplained after it, costs less than what was"*

If that fires, F8 is wrong and the correction is a rebinding.

**Status: triangulated across Fig 5 (refusal) and the Operators table (negation), with Lehtonen 2018 as world corroboration.**

## 4. Pre-registered refuter

Written before the conclusion, per the standing rule: a falsifier written before the run is a design instrument, not only an honesty device. The shape claim SHAPE(COG-0076) is refuted if any of the following holds:

1. **Aggregation fires:** a single aggregate factor predicts held-out per-slot outcomes better than the four-slot decomposition, with no quiet slot hidden by the average (breaks F1, Fig 1 :19 and :22).
2. **Contact does not fire:** children bilingual by label but with no differential contact show the same per-slot effects as children with documented differential contact (breaks F2, Fig 11 :40).
3. **Anchors are interchangeable:** identity is settled by a task score alone, or attention by person-report alone, and the swapped readings agree (breaks F5, Fig 2 :36-37).
4. **The union is a partition:** the three mechanisms reduce to one latent factor with no interaction by language history across all four slots (breaks F6, Fig 9 :54).
5. **The import adds nothing:** the second language adds no reachable composition to the child's library and the slot effects arise without any expansion of closure (breaks F3, Fig 6 :22).
6. **A genuine second witness fires:** independent, pre-registered, low-overlap frames converge on a nonzero per-slot effect after bias correction. This does **not** refute the shape; it refutes the specific `gap` verdict on direction in §6 and moves that slot toward `grounded`. It is included because a pre-registered refuter must be able to fire against the parts of the answer that are presently gaps.

If 1 to 5 fire, the shape claim is withdrawn and re-censused, not softened. Until one is run, nothing here is labelled `grounded`.

## 5. Recommendation

1. Lead with the shape claim and the refuter, not with any theory of bilingualism (Fig 10 :52 *"Bring a characterised residual, never the source."*).
2. Keep the four verdicts separate in any write-up. Never publish a single "bilingual advantage" headline (F1).
3. For each slot, name the anchor before the effect: instrument for attention and memory, structured human judgment for identity (F5).
4. Treat the second language as a habitat and contact change, and design the comparison around contact, not around the label (F2).
5. Treat the three mechanisms as competing isomers over a shared atom set, and measure more than one at a time (F6, F7).
6. Use the public submission and contact points in `03_public_contact_points.csv`. Ask for a refutation, never for belief (Fig 2 :35, Fig 9 :60).
7. Withhold the framework links until the recipient has recorded a verdict on stage 1 (Fig 10 :42).

## 7. Seven-question recursive derivation (summary)

1. **Is there one residual or four?** Four. Fig 1 :19 and the Symbols table :43. The umbrella term aggregates and hides a live slot.
2. **What changes when a child grows up multilingual?** Contact and habitat, not the isolated agent. Fig 11 :40. The bimodal case is the discriminator.
3. **Does the child's library change?** Yes, by import from the language community (Fig 6 :23), with an identity bond over one referent (Fig 12 :67). Whether a primitive is added is S1, open.
