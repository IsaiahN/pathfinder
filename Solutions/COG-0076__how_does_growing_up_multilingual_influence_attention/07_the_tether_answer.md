# The Tether's answer on multilingual upbringing and the four outcomes

**Mode: `specified` on the shape, `general` on the structural rules, not `grounded`.** This is a shape and a route, not an empirical result. Simulated derivation checked against the figures (`05_what_shape_means.md` §6), not a ground reading.

## 1. The residual it answers

A child is raised in contact with two or more languages. The question asks what difference that makes to attention, memory, identity and social understanding. The residual is not one gap; it is four, and a global score near zero is a legal state while one slot is live (Fig 1 :13 *"a global R near zero with one live slot residual is a legal state"*). The four outcomes are a **union compound**, not one hard problem: a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either (Fig 9 :57 *"a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either"*). It gets four loops and four grounds.

## 2. The shape (one loop per domain, verdicts kept separate)

**Domain 1, the developing child (`specified`).**

1. **Four slots, never one advantage.** Fig 1 :19 *"The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated."* and Symbols table :43 *"R is measured per slot, never as a single global number."* Attention, memory, identity and social understanding get separate verdicts. The umbrella term "the bilingual advantage" aggregates the signal and is refused (Fig 5 :53 *"refuted: a term we hold is wrong; a term is owed that says so"*).
2. **The change is in contact and habitat, not in the isolated child.** Fig 11 :40 *"A system cannot be made more capable by improving the agent in isolation. An improvement that does not change contact changes nothing, however much it improves."* The comparison that matters is between children whose contact differs. The bimodal spoken-plus-signed case is the natural discriminator: no selection conflict, no advantage in the flanker comparison (Bialystok 2011 reporting Emmorey et al. 2008b).
3. **A second language is an import that adds terms, with an identity bond over one referent.** Fig 6 :23 *"No frame escapes its own closure. The bound moves only on contact with a frame whose closure differs."* Fig 12 :67 *"two names, one referent"*. Whether that import adds a primitive is the live strain S1 and does not carry the verdict.
4. **The middle link is the least measured.** Fig 3 :27 *"A link with no instrument is not a link that works. It is a link nobody has looked at."* Having terms for what a situation affords, that one object has two names and that different people know different languages, is that link.

**Domain 2, the measurement frame (`specified` on rules, `gap` on direction).**

5. **Agreement in this literature is not corroboration.** Fig 2 :20 *"so the agreement is the pool talking,"*; Fig 7 :35 *"A room's agreement is variation, not a verdict. Selection between rooms is where the ground votes."* A meta-analysis that corrects for publication bias is a filter on the budget, not a witness for the verdict (Fig 9 :60 *"Use filters for the budget and witnesses for the verdict. Never let a filter hand you a verdict."*).
6. **The three mechanisms are candidates over a shared atom set, not a merged cause.** As written with different bonds they are Fig 12 :117 isomers *"isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."* Which one fires for which child is a gap.

**Domain 3, the social and identity frame (`general` on the anchor, `gap` on content).**

7. **Identity is anchored in persons.** Because the question is constitutively about people, Fig 2 :37 *"Human judgment is the right anchor for is this a good answer for a person, because the question is about people."* holds. A task score is an instrument for a different question. A shared label is a convention (Fig 2 :41), not a person.
8. **The framework returns a shape, not a name.** Fig 13 :135 *"It cannot say which primitive is missing, because naming it would mean already holding it."* It cannot fix the direction of any slot effect, the size of it, the mechanism, or the content of a child's identity. Those are gaps, reported as verdicts and not as silence: Fig 13 :134 *"Unreachable is a verdict here, not a silence."*

Verdicts are not averaged across domains. The domain-1 shape is `specified`; the domain-2 direction is `gap`; the domain-3 anchor is `general` and its content is `gap`.

## 3. What it explicitly does not say

- Not that bilingualism is good, bad, or neutral. Valence is relative to the residual, not a property of the exposure (Fig 11 :49 *"An actor's valence is relative to the residual, not a property it carries."*).
- Not that there is a bilingual advantage, a deficit, a critical period, or a specific identity outcome.
- Not that executive function, metalinguistic awareness or sociolinguistic awareness is *the* mechanism. They are provenanced candidates.
- Not that a reaction time or an accuracy score settles identity.
- Not that a large number of agreeing studies settles attention or social understanding.
- Not `grounded`. No instrument was read in this derivation, and the existing instrument record is mixed and bias-laden.

## 4. What would settle it — the ground

1. **A per-slot, pre-registered measurement** with bias correction and proper matching, reporting four verdicts rather than one. That would move G1 from `gap` toward `grounded`, slot by slot, never in aggregate.
2. **A contact discriminator:** compare children whose label matches but whose contact differs (including the bimodal case) and children whose contact matches but whose label differs. A label-only effect would break the shape.
3. **A mechanism discriminator:** measure all three candidate mechanisms in the same children, longitudinally, with an instrument for the affordance link. That would move G2.
4. **Person-level judgment for identity:** structured report and judgment by people who know the child, checked against held-out self-description, with the anchor kept from updating on the frame's preference (Fig 2 :33 *"The anchor must not update."*).
5. **Standing falsifiers** from `01` section 4: an aggregate factor that beats the four-slot decomposition; equal effects despite equal contact; interchangeable anchors; one latent mechanism factor; a genuine independent second witness (which closes a gap rather than killing the shape).

## 5. The honest stop

**Unreached, not unreachable.** Instruments exist and are improving; the problem is that the best current synthesis corrects the pool toward null while the mechanism question remains mostly cross-sectional. Fig 8 :39 *"So a search that finds nothing is never a proof of absence."* Fig 6 :27 *"Which is why some unreachability is depth and some is genuine absence, and from inside the frame the two look the same."* The answer stops at the limit of what the available instruments distinguish, and the lineage is recorded so the stop is checkable. *"Indiscernibility is not identity."*

One live strain is carried forward and not hidden: S1, whether a lexical import adds a primitive to the child's Γ. It does not carry any verdict.

## 6. One-paragraph version for outreach

> The Tether does not say that bilingual children have an advantage. It says that the question is four questions, not one: attention, memory, identity and social understanding have to be scored separately, because a global average can be quiet while one slot is live. It says that what changes when a child grows up multilingual is contact, a habitat of interlocutors and contexts, not the child in isolation; the bimodal spoken and signed case, where no language has to be suppressed, is the natural test. It says that attention and memory are anchored to instruments while identity, because it is about a person, is anchored to structured human judgment, and that three mechanisms, selection, two-names-for-one-referent, and matching language to the person in front of you, are competing routes over the same atoms rather than one merged cause. It refuses to name which route fires, which direction any effect goes, or what a bilingual identity contains; those are gaps reported as verdicts. What it offers is the shape any answer must have, five refuters written in advance, and the kinds of instrument that would close each gap. The slots stay open until the ground settles them.
