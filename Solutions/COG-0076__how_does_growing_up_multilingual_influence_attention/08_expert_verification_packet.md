# Expert verification packet — the Tether on multilingual upbringing and four outcomes

**What this is:** a claim in a form a developmental or cognitive scientist can check. Mode, derivation and refuters are stated before the conclusion, so a reader knows what to look for and what would settle it.

**Mode: `specified` on the shape, `general` on the structural rules, not `grounded`.** No measurement by this derivation is claimed. This is a simulated derivation checked for internal consistency against the figures, not a ground reading; it cannot settle the question it reports on.

## 1. The claim, in one paragraph

Growing up multilingual does not produce one general effect on a child. The residual is indexed by the outcome being tracked, so attention, memory, identity and social understanding require four separate verdicts, and a global average near zero is a legal state while one slot is live. What changes with multilingual upbringing is contact and habitat, the interlocutors and contexts a child meets, not the child considered in isolation; the discriminating case is one where the language label is present but the selection contact is absent. For attention and memory the anchor is an instrument; for identity, because the question is constitutively about a person, the anchor is structured human judgment, not a task score or a consensus. Three mechanisms, executive function, metalinguistic awareness and sociolinguistic awareness, are competing routes over the same atom set rather than one merged cause, and the framework does not select among them or fix a direction. The framework returns a shape, not a name.

## 2. Three checkable sub-claims

### C1 — framework-internal plus empirical; checkable by reading Figure 1 and by inspecting an analysis plan

- **Statement:** the four outcomes must be scored per slot, never aggregated into a single bilingual advantage.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 1 :19 *"The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated."* Also Fig 1 :22 *"Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average."* and Symbols table :43 *"R is measured per slot, never as a single global number."*
- **What to check:** does any reported "bilingual advantage" rest on a composite score across attention, memory and social tasks? The published record already contains a case where attention shows no advantage after bias correction while verbal fluency shows a small disadvantage (Lehtonen et al. 2018), and a case where perspective-taking moves while emotion comprehension does not (2024 OSF preprint). Those are the per-slot signatures.
- **Refuter:** a pre-registered analysis in which a single aggregate factor predicts held-out per-slot outcomes better than the four-slot decomposition, with no live slot hidden by the average. If that fires, C1 is wrong.

### C2 — empirical; checkable against the contact structure of a sample

- **Statement:** the operative variable is differential contact, not the bilingual label.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 11 :40 *"A system cannot be made more capable by improving the agent in isolation. An improvement that does not change contact changes nothing, however much it improves."* Also Fig 6 :23 *"No frame escapes its own closure. The bound moves only on contact with a frame whose closure differs."*
- **What to check:** does the design include a group whose label is bilingual but whose selection contact is absent, and a group whose contact differs? Bialystok 2011 reports that bimodal speech-plus-sign bilinguals, who need not choose between competing languages, performed like monolinguals on a flanker task while spoken-language bilinguals showed the advantage (Emmorey et al. 2008b). If the shape is right, that pattern should generalise.
- **Refuter:** equal per-slot effects for children whose contact does not differ as for children whose contact does, after matching and bias correction. If that fires, C2 is wrong.

### C3 — framework-internal; checkable by reading Figures 2 and 12

- **Statement:** identity is anchored in structured person-level judgment, and the three mechanisms are isomers over a shared atom set, not one latent cause.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 2 :37 *"Human judgment is the right anchor for is this a good answer for a person, because the question is about people. It is only an instrument for is this code correct, and instruments can be wrong on axes they"*. Fig 12 :117 *"isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."*
- **What to check:** is any identity conclusion resting on a task score alone, or on agreement about a label? Are executive function, metalinguistic awareness and sociolinguistic awareness measured together anywhere, with a longitudinal order? Yu et al. 2021 report that studies measuring one account rarely measure the others and that almost all evidence is cross-sectional; Buac and Kaushanskaya 2020 find different predictors by acquisition history.
- **Refuter:** task score and structured person-report converging on the same identity reading across contexts with held-out prediction; or one latent mechanism factor with no language-history interaction across all four slots. Either fires against C3.

## 3. What is explicitly not claimed

- Not a bilingual advantage, deficit, critical period or specific identity outcome.
- Not any effect size or effect direction; G1 in `01` is a reported gap.
- Not that a particular mechanism is *the* mechanism. The three are provenanced candidates from the literature, not framework derivations.
- Not that a shared label settles who a person is. A convention that decides an outcome has stopped being a convention.
- Not that agreement among many studies corroborates the claim. Fig 7 :35 *"A room's agreement is variation, not a verdict. Selection between rooms is where the ground votes."*
- Not `grounded`. Nothing here was settled by an instrument read in this derivation.

## 5. What would settle it

1. A four-slot, pre-registered, bias-corrected measurement reporting each verdict separately, moving G1 from gap toward grounded slot by slot.
2. A contact discriminator, including the bimodal case and a label-matched contact-differing group.
3. A longitudinal instrument measuring all three mechanisms together, including the affordance link (two names for one referent; matching language to interlocutor).
4. Structured person-level judgment for identity, with the anchor kept from updating on the frame's preference (Fig 2 :33 *"The anchor must not update."*).
5. The falsifiers in `01` section 4, written before the run and reproduced in `02`.

## 6. What an expert can check in twenty minutes

1. Read Fig 1 :19 and :22, Fig 11 :40 and Fig 2 :37. Four short passages.
2. Check C2 against the bimodal literature. Does the no-selection case behave like the shape predicts?
3. Check C3 against your own reading of the bilingual-ToM record. Are the three mechanisms ever measured together, longitudinally?
4. Name one instrument that would order the three mechanisms, or say why none is possible.
5. Send the refutation. A failure is more useful than an endorsement.
