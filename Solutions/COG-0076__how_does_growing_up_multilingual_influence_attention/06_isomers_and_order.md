# Isomers and order: the four slots, the three mechanisms, and what may be composed over what

**Mode: `specified`.** This is a simulated derivation checked against the figures, not a ground reading (see `05_what_shape_means.md` §6).

## RESIDUAL

The question names four outcomes and leaves the mechanism unspecified. Three candidate mechanisms circulate in the literature: executive function, metalinguistic awareness, and sociolinguistic awareness. Two questions are owed: (1) are the four outcomes a partition or a union, and (2) are the three mechanisms **isomers** under Fig 12's definition, or are they different atoms?

## DOMAIN CENSUS

Atoms in play from the discussion: two languages (L1, L2), a referent, an interlocutor, a context, an attention resource, a memory trace. Bonds in play: identity (≡, two names one referent), sequence (→), comparison (⋛), conjunction (+). No new primitive is available without import. The ground for the structural claims is the verbatim figure text; the ground for any empirical claim would be an instrument, which has not been read here.

## DERIVATION

### Q1 — Partition or union?

- Fig 9 :4 *"First: is there a fact here? Disagreements that do not shrink with effort, where each rule keeps working well on a different subset,"*
- Fig 9 :5 *"are not one hard question but several well-formed ones. Split it rather than search."*
- Fig 9 :54 *"And when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again."*
- Fig 9 :57 *"a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either"*

The four slots do not separate cleanly: social understanding depends on language ability in some bilingual groups and on executive function in others (Buac and Kaushanskaya 2020), and attention-task differences do not transfer to emotion comprehension (2024 OSF preprint). A lever that clears attention can leave social understanding standing. ⇒ **union**, not partition. Fig 9 :55 *"A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones."*

**Falsifier, written before the run:** a single latent factor with no slot-by-condition interaction that accounts for all four outcomes across language histories would collapse the union to a partition.

### Q2 — Are the three mechanisms isomers?

- Fig 12 :117 *"isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."*
- Fig 12 :124 *"functional group. A substructure whose presence fixes the outcome. The group says what kind; the qualifier says which member."*
- Fig 12 :67 *"two names, one referent"* (identity)
- Operators :30 *"sequence"*; :36 *"production"*; :48 *"comparison"*

Write the three accounts over the same atom set `{L1, L2, referent, interlocutor, context, attention}`:

| account | bond structure | what is decisive |
|---|---|---|
| executive function | attention ⋛ (L1 competing with L2) | a **comparison** at a threshold of competition; selection under load |
| metalinguistic awareness | (L1 ≡ referent) + (L2 ≡ referent) | an **identity** over two names for one referent; representation can misrepresent |
| sociolinguistic awareness | interlocutor → language choice | a **sequence**: the other person's knowledge precedes and fixes the choice |

Same atoms, different bonds, different predicted behaviour. That is the figure's definition of isomer. Under the weaker reading, the three mechanisms are members of one **functional group**: each is a `state → structure → state` composition over language experience that contributes to social understanding, with a different qualifier.

- **Stronger claim, checkable:** they are isomers over the shared atom set.
- **Weaker claim, already supported:** they are a functional group with different qualifiers.

The stronger claim is worth testing because the observed behaviour differs by account (Yu et al. 2021 report that executive function predicts ToM in monolinguals but not bilinguals, while metalinguistic awareness predicts it in bilinguals).

**Falsifier:** if each mechanism requires an atom the others do not, they are not isomers; they are different primitives and the functional-group reading stands instead. A study showing that executive function cannot be written without an attention-competition atom that metalinguistic awareness does not use would fire this.

### Q3 — What order may be composed over what?

- Fig 12 :104 *"A settled molecule becomes an atom for whatever composes over it. So irreducible is a fact about a level and never about the world, and there is no bottom"*
- Fig 3 :20 *"The chain is ordered by dependency, not by importance."*
- Fig 3 :19 *"Optimising a link that already works cannot rescue one that does not."*

The dependency order from `01` is perception → vocabulary/affordance → objective → planning → learn and carry. The metalinguistic identity (two names, one referent) is a **second-order** composition: it can only be formed once each language-name-to-referent binding has settled. It then becomes an atom that can compose into a ToM reading. Fig 12 :103 *"Composition is recursive, and the level decides what is irreducible."*

This is why the metalinguistic account is developmentally later than simple exposure, and why a cross-sectional design cannot order the chain. Yu et al. 2021 explicitly call for longitudinal work on the sequence, noting that almost all existing evidence is cross-sectional.

**Type strain S2, named.** Fig 12 :111 *"A vocabulary without bonds can name a state and cannot name a route to one."* The framework can write the route (sequence → identity → comparison) but cannot, from the figure text alone, say which route a child takes. That needs an instrument. The strain is in the *selection*, not in the *legality*, of the composition. Kept visible, not smoothed.

### Q4 — Does the identity slot compose like the others?

- Fig 2 :36 *"An anchor is legitimate when the question is constitutively about it."*
- Fig 2 :37 *"Human judgment is the right anchor for is this a good answer for a person, because the question is about people. It is only an instrument for is this code correct, and instruments can be wrong on axes they"*
- Fig 13 :135 *"It cannot say which primitive is missing, because naming it would mean already holding it."*

Identity is not a fourth task. It is a different anchor type. The composition that settles "who this child understands themself to be" runs against structured person-level judgment, not against a reaction time. Fig 2 :41 *"efficient for a convention: agreement about a shared meaning is the product, not a corruption of it"* describes the shared label "bilingual"; it does not settle the person. Conflating the two is the metamerism error of `05` §4: the label reading is being used as a source report.

**Falsifier:** a demonstration that structured person-report and a task score converge on the same identity reading across children and contexts, with the task score predicting held-out self-description. If that fires, the anchor distinction narrows and this part of the shape is revised.

### Q5 — Is refusal owed before a mint?

- Fig 5 :53 *"refuted: a term we hold is wrong; a term is owed that says so"*
- `THE_FORMULA.md` step 2 routes REFUTED before the other bins.
- Fig 12 :140 *"A vocabulary that cannot refuse can only accumulate."*

The held term "the bilingual advantage" has a residual that persists and does not trend down (Lehtonen et al. 2018). The order is therefore refuse first, then mint the four-slot decomposition. Writing a finer-grained advantage before refusing the general one leaves the general term standing and composable over, which is exactly the accumulation Fig 12 :140 names.

**Falsifier:** if the general term's residual trends down under better matching, it is mis-attached rather than false (Fig 5 :13 *"mis-attached: refit what you have, do not invent"*), and the order is rebinding, not refusal.

## CONCLUSION

The four outcomes form a **union** of overlapping conditions, not a partition. The three mechanisms are **isomers over a shared atom set** under the strong reading and a **functional group** under the weak one; the strong reading has a stated falsifier and is unmeasured. The metalinguistic identity is a **second-order composition** over settled name-to-referent bindings, which is why the order is developmental and must be tested longitudinally. The identity slot uses a person-level anchor, not a task score. Refusal of the umbrella term precedes the four-slot mint. All of this is structural; none of it fixes an empirical direction.
