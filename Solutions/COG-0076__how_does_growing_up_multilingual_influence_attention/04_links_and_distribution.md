## Sources read through the search integration

The following were read as full or near-full text supplied by the search integration. Direct publisher fetches were not obtained for all of them, and they are marked accordingly in `10_source_ledger.csv`.

1. **Bialystok, Craik, Klein and Viswanathan 2004**, *Bilingualism, Aging, and Cognitive Control: Evidence From the Simon Task*, Psychology and Aging 19(2):290 to 303. Three studies; bilingual groups showed smaller Simon effects and lower working-memory costs, larger effects in older adults. Direct PDF text was available at `https://www.sakkyndig.com/psykologi/artvit/bialystok2004.pdf`.
2. **Lehtonen, Soveri, Laine et al. 2018**, *Is bilingualism associated with enhanced executive functioning in adults? A meta-analytic review*, Psychological Bulletin 144(4):394 to 425. 891 effect sizes from 152 studies. *"No evidence for a bilingual advantage remained after correcting for bias."* Direct PDF text was available at `https://eprints.whiterose.ac.uk/id/eprint/157474/1/Bilingual_meta_analysis.pdf`.
3. **de Bruin, Treccani and Della Sala 2014**, *Cognitive advantage in bilingualism: an example of publication bias?*, Psychological Science. *"Studies challenging the bilingual advantage were published the least."* PubMed abstract read at `https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/25475825/`.
4. **Inhibitory Control or Social Skills? Two Accounts for a Bilingual Advantage**, 2024 preprint, `https://doi.org/10.31234/osf.io/b3mht`. 40 monolingual and 42 multilingual children aged 4 to 7. Multilinguals outperformed on Director and Camera perspective-taking and on inhibition, with **no** difference on the Test of Emotion Comprehension. Neither inhibition nor emotion comprehension accounted for the advantage; metalinguistic awareness was proposed as a third factor. Abstract-level text only.
5. **Buac and Kaushanskaya 2020**, *Predictors of theory of mind performance in bilingual and monolingual children*, International Journal of Bilingualism 24(3):339 to 359. Language ability predicted ToM in simultaneous bilinguals; executive function predicted it in monolinguals and English-L1 bilinguals. Abstract-level text.
6. **Early bilingualism enhances theory of mind in children from low-income households via executive function skills**, 2023, Cognitive Development, `https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cogdev.2023.101389`. 68 preschoolers; bilingual children showed greater ToM after controls, and working memory and attention shifting mediated the link. Abstract-level text.

**Dick et al. 2019**, *No evidence for a bilingual executive function advantage in the ABCD study*, Nature Human Behaviour, is mentioned only as reported inside Yu et al. 2021. It was not opened directly and is not listed as a read source.

## Wider literature that bears on the residual

- The **advantage line** (Bialystok 2004, Bialystok 2011) is a real ground reading of attention and working memory effects in selected tasks. It is not refuted by the bias correction as a phenomenon; what is refused is its promotion to a general, cross-slot term.
- The **bias line** (de Bruin 2014, Lehtonen 2018) is a different instrument pointed at the same pool. It tells us what the pool can and cannot witness. It does not measure any child.
- The **mechanism line** (Yu et al. 2021, Buac and Kaushanskaya 2020, the 2024 OSF preprint) is where the union and the isomer reading come from: three accounts, partial support, different predictors by history, and a slot that moves without another slot moving.
- The **context line** (2023 Cognitive Development study) shows the same exposure producing different outcomes under different socioeconomic conditions, which is Fig 11 :49 *"An actor's valence is relative to the residual, not a property it carries."* in empirical form.

## Distribution: offer, do not push

- **Fig 10 :52** *"Bring a characterised residual, never the source."* Send the four-slot shape and its refuter. Do not send the architecture first.
- **Fig 10 :42** *"A verifier that can reconstruct the claim cannot verify it."* A recipient who has absorbed the framework before the verdict has joined the frame being checked.
- **Fig 9 :28** *"any frame you pick will seem to fit"*. Ask what they would expect to see in each slot before giving them any theory.
- **Fig 2 :35** *"Alignment is triangulation, not negotiation. A mirror cannot correct you."* Do not ask for agreement.
- **Fig 7 :35** *"A room's agreement is variation, not a verdict. Selection between rooms is where the ground votes."* Do not gather a crowd of agreeing readers.
- **Fig 2 :40** *"disqualifying for a verdict: agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool"*. A later endorsement is never pooled as corroboration.

**Recommended order in a reply:** (1) the four-slot shape and the anchors; (2) ask what they would count as a failure in each slot, and record that answer; (3) only then the three links, with the note that the figures, not the summary, are the authority.

## The two-stage offer

1. **Stage 1.** Send the residual, the per-slot shape, the anchor rule and the pre-registered refuter. Ask for a failure, not a verdict on the framework.
2. **Stage 2, opt-in only.** If they are curious, offer the figure text, the canonical README and the figures folder. Their stage-1 verdict is already recorded and is never revised by what they read later.
3. **Stage 3.** Offer the formula as a method to reuse. Each user writes their own refuter, names their own ground and states their own mode. Fig 4 :22 *"Only methods cross up. Only head starts come down."*

**Hard precondition:** the offer only works if stage 1 survives. The four-slot split, the contact framing and the anchor rule are that stage; the umbrella claim is not sent at all.
