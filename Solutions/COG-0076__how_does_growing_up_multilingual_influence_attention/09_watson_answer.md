## QUESTION

Decide, in effect terms, what the Tether says about the claim that growing up multilingual produces a general influence on attention, memory, identity and social understanding. Option A: treat it as one treatment with one general effect. Option B: unbundle into four per-slot verdicts with question-specific anchors and competing mechanisms. What each leads to: A aggregates a live signal into an average, selects a frame before describing the residual, and anchors a person-level question to a task score; B sends a checkable shape, five written refuters, and open slots until a ground settles each one.

## DOMAIN CENSUS

R by arity, symmetry and scale, then one line per domain with actors, currency and anchor; partition or union; order by dependency.

- **R (the residual).** The gap between what a language-exposure frame predicts for an outcome and what an instrument returns. Arity: four slots, so four residual vectors, never a global number (Fig 1 :19; Symbols table :43). Symmetry: asymmetric exposure-to-outcome relation, with valence differing by slot (Fig 11 :49-50). Scale: developmental time, closing within a lifetime; only methods and head starts cross a generation (Fig 4 :22).
- **Domain 1, the developing child's frame.** Actors: child, two languages, caregivers, interlocutors, contexts. Currency: per-slot residual, new terms entering the library. Anchor: behavioural and neural instruments, plus the child's own record where the question is about a person. Union, not partition: the four outcomes overlap through three mechanisms.
- **Domain 2, the measurement frame.** Actors: tasks, samples, meta-analyses, publication filters. Currency: effect sizes and precision. Anchor: the instrument reading under a non-updating test. Agreement inside one evidence pool is one witness, not corroboration.
- **Domain 3, the social and identity frame.** Actors: family, peers, institutions, language communities. Currency: norms, recognition, self-concept. Anchor: structured human judgment, because the question is constitutively about people (Fig 2 :36-37). Consensus about a label is a convention, not an anchor.
- **Order by dependency.** Perception of the two languages, then vocabulary and affordance terms (two names for one referent; matching language to interlocutor), then objective, then planning, then learn and carry (Fig 3 :20). Refuse the umbrella term before minting the decomposition (Formula step 2). Describe the residual before choosing a frame (Fig 9 :28).
- **Candidate domain not admitted.** "General cognitive elegance" has no nameable anchor and no instrument, so it is not a domain yet. Recorded as a census decision, not a silence.

Strain test: the four-slot rule governs the residual index; the union rule governs how those slots overlap; the anchor rule governs which ground is legitimate per slot. They constrain different quantities, so no figure line was strained to fit.

## WORLD (secondary sources read)

World sources are corroboration only. Overlap is declared rather than hidden; most sources share the broad multilingualism-and-cognition literature, so their agreement carries limited independent weight. Sources read through the search integration are marked.

- **Cognitive psychology / attention.** Bialystok, Craik, Klein and Viswanathan 2004, *Bilingualism, Aging, and Cognitive Control: Evidence From the Simon Task*, Psychology and Aging 19(2):290 to 303. Quote: "controlled processing is carried out more effectively by bilinguals and that bilingualism helps to offset age-related losses in certain executive processes." Predicts a real slot-specific attention effect. Corroborates the existence of an attention slot; conflicts with any claim that all slots move together. Overlap: shares the Bialystok evidence pool with the 2011 review.
- **Cognitive psychology / review and bimodal discriminator.** Bialystok 2011, *Reshaping the Mind: The Benefits of Bilingualism*, Canadian Journal of Experimental Psychology 65(4):229 to 235 (PMC4341987, opened directly). Quote: "there was no executive control advantage for the bimodal participants, as shown in Figure 4." Predicts that contact, not the label, drives the effect. Corroborates Fig 11 :40 and C2. Overlap: same author as 2004.
- **Meta-science / publication bias.** Lehtonen, Soveri, Laine et al. 2018, *Is bilingualism associated with enhanced executive functioning in adults? A meta-analytic review*, Psychological Bulletin 144(4):394 to 425 (PDF text read via search integration). Quote: "No evidence for a bilingual advantage remained after correcting for bias." Predicts that agreement in the pool is a filter, not a witness. Corroborates Fig 2 :20, Fig 7 :35 and Fig 9 :60. Overlap: one research-pool witness with de Bruin 2014.
- **Meta-science / publication bias.** de Bruin, Treccani and Della Sala 2014, *Cognitive advantage in bilingualism: an example of publication bias?*, Psychological Science (PubMed abstract read via search integration). Quote: "Studies challenging the bilingual advantage were published the least." Predicts a directional filter on what gets counted. Corroborates the filter-not-witness reading. Overlap: same debate as Lehtonen 2018, counted as one witness.
- **Developmental psychology / social understanding.** Yu, Kovelman and Wellman 2021, *How Bilingualism Informs Theory of Mind Development*, Child Development Perspectives 15(3):154 to 159 (PMC8881004, opened directly). Quote: "None of the three accounts claim that bilingualism induces extraordinary processes of ToM development; rather, bilingualism simply provides more opportunities to develop relevant skills." Predicts a habitat and contact mechanism and three incomplete accounts. Corroborates Fig 11 :40 and Fig 9 :54; also supports G2. Overlap: different research programme from the Bialystok pool.
- **Developmental psychology / mechanism routes.** Buac and Kaushanskaya 2020, *Predictors of theory of mind performance in bilingual and monolingual children*, International Journal of Bilingualism 24(3):339 to 359 (abstract read via search integration). Quote: "children with distinct language acquisition histories approach verbal ToM problems differently." Predicts different routes to the same task. Corroborates the isomer and union reading. Overlap: cited inside Yu et al. 2021, so partly shared evidence.
- **Developmental psychology / per-slot divergence.** *Inhibitory Control or Social Skills? Two Accounts for a Bilingual Advantage*, 2024 preprint (OSF b3mht; abstract read via search integration). Quote: "No difference was recorded between multilinguals and monolinguals in the TEC." Predicts that perspective-taking moves while emotion comprehension does not. Corroborates Fig 1 :19 and F1. Overlap: distinct sample and authors.
- **Developmental psychology / context dependence.** *Early bilingualism enhances theory of mind in children from low-income households via executive function skills*, 2023, Cognitive Development (abstract read via search integration). Quote: "children's working memory and attention shifting accounted for the links between bilingualism and ToM." Predicts that the same exposure produces different outcomes under different habitat conditions. Corroborates Fig 11 :49. Overlap: distinct sample.

## VERDICT (from the figures)

The shape survives; the umbrella claim does not. Four per-slot verdicts, a contact-based mechanism story, three competing routes, and open empirical directions. The four-slot split, the contact requirement and the anchor rule are `specified` or `general` and figure-governed. The sign and size of each effect, which mechanism is operative for which child, and the content of bilingual identity are declared gaps, reported as verdicts rather than silence (Fig 13 :134, Fig 8 :39). Verdicts are kept per domain and never averaged. This remains a `specified`, ungrounded result from a simulated derivation.

- **cost if wrong:** bounded and locatable. The packet states mode, anchors and refuters before the conclusion, so an error is findable rather than invisible. The real cost is spending an outside reader's independence by sending a recording instead of a generator, or by treating a bias-filtered agreement as a witness. Fig 2 :42 *"Buy independence in proportion to the cost of being wrong."* Both costs are avoidable by leading with the four-slot shape and its refuter.
- **undoable:** yes. The umbrella term is removable without touching the derivation; `07_the_tether_answer.md` and `08_expert_verification_packet.md` already remove it. A sent claim can be superseded by the corrected packet, and no primitive has to be withdrawn. Refusal adds a term and removes nothing from the record (Fig 6 :42 *"What is recorded only grows. What is reachable is derived, and it can fall."*).
- **reversed by:** a ground reading, not agreement. A pre-registered four-slot measurement with bias correction would move G1 slot by slot. A contact discriminator, including the bimodal case and a label-matched contact-differing group, would break or confirm C2. A longitudinal instrument measuring all three mechanisms together would close G2. Structured person-level judgment with a non-updating anchor would close G3. An aggregate factor beating the four-slot decomposition, equal effects despite equal contact, or interchangeable anchors would break the shape itself. Until one of these fires, the verdict stands as `candidate`. Prestige and headcounts are not such instruments.
