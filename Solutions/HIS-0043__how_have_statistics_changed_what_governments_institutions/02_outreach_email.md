# Outreach draft: check the shape before you decide about the theory

**Use:** one-to-one, or as a cover letter attached to a submission through the public venues in `03_public_contact_points.csv`. **Not** for bulk send.

**Hard precondition:** do not send until the two live strains in `01_figure_grounded_audit.md` section 6 are held visible in your own answer. The output must survive a check or the two-stage offer collapses on contact.

**Subject:** One checkable claim about statistics and social problems, before any theory

> Hi [name],
>
> I have one specific output I would like you to check against what you already know. I am not asking you to read a theory first, and I am not asking you to agree with anything. If the output does not survive, there is nothing else to look at.
>
> **The residual (`specified`, not `grounded`):** how did counting change what a government, an institution or a public treats as a social problem? Stated as an effect: before enumeration a condition is a private trouble or unremarked; after enumeration it can appear as a rate, a deviation from a norm, and a category with a budget attached. The question is which part of that change is an instrument effect, which part is a category effect, and which part is an attention-channel effect.
>
> **The shape claim (before any theory):** these are three overlapping conditions, not one lever. (1) A counting instrument extends what can register, and it is improved from a worse instrument already returning something: the parish bill of mortality came before the census rate. (2) A category is a comparison over a distribution, and a convention that starts deciding the outcome it was built to report has stopped being a convention. (3) The channel from the condition to public regard can decay, so that ministries and publics read the rate off other readings of the rate rather than off the condition. The ground does not decay; the channel does.
>
> **What it does not say:** it does not say statistics invented problems out of nothing, and it does not say statistics merely discovered pre-existing problems. It does not name which category was the right one, and it does not return the list of problems that would have been recognised without counting. That list is a declared gap, reported as unreached, not as silence. A rate reads a projection; it does not by itself say what produced it.
>
> **What would refute it, written down before this message:**
> 1. One intervention that restores per-slot visibility, a correct category and a reachable outcome channel in one move, with no categorically different residue.
> 2. A counting instrument specified in full from a description that returned discriminable readings where nothing at any resolution had returned anything.
> 3. A published rate that preserves every slot's live mass with no loss, against a case where the aggregate currently hides a live slot.
> 4. A category whose definition can be changed whenever its reported outcome becomes inconvenient, with no loss of function.
> 5. An instrument that discriminates among the sources of one rate reading without additional information.
> 6. A reporting regime in which the published rate moves only when an independently measured condition moves, over two or more cycles, with the measuring instrument outside the reporting frame.
>
> If any one holds, the corresponding part of the claim is withdrawn. A one-line "this fails because..." is more useful to me than agreement. If you would rather not engage, no reply is needed.
>
> **If it survives, and only then:** the sources are below. The figures are the authority, not my summary of them.
> - Verbatim figure text with line numbers: `FRAMEWORK/reference/03-FIGURES_TEXT.md` in this folder tree (local, no account needed).
> - Canonical usage rule: `FRAMEWORK/reference/FIGURES_README_canonical.md`.
> - Daston's review of Desrosières in the London Review of Books, 2000: https://www.lrb.co.uk/the-paper/v22/n08/lorraine-daston/why-statistics-tend-not-only-to-describe-the-world-but-to-change-it
> - Little on Hacking's *The Taming of Chance*, 2013: https://understandingsociety.blogspot.com/2013/09/ian-hacking-on-chance-as-worldview.html
>

Write down what would refute your result *before* you run it, and state the mode of what you get. A reported failure is more useful to me than a reported success.
