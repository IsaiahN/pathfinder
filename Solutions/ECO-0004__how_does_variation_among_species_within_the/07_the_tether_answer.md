# The Tether's answer: variation within a functional group and ecosystem resilience

**Mode: `specified` on the per-domain claims, `general` on the structural shape, `gap` on any numerical amount.** Not `grounded`. This is a simulated derivation checked for internal consistency against the figures (`05_what_shape_means.md` section 7), not a ground reading. No ecosystem was measured.

## 1. The residual it answers

Two readings of the same field observation are being confused:

- Reading A: a functional group has N species; the group performs function F; resilience is a property of the group.
- Reading B: the group has N members with different responses; a perturbation removes or suppresses some; the persistence of F depends on which response is available when the condition changes.

The residual is the gap between A's prediction and B's outcome. Fig 9 :54: *"And when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again."* The question is a union of at least five overlapping conditions (kind, selector, library, measurement, habitat), not one dial. Verdicts stay per domain and are never averaged.

## 2. The shape, domain by domain

1. **The group fixes what kind of function is at stake; the qualifier says which member.** Fig 12 :123: *"functional group. A substructure whose presence fixes the outcome. The group says what kind; the qualifier says which member."* The question is literally about the qualifier. Fig 13 :34: *"And within a kind the members are distinct too, which is what makes them separate frames."* So within-group variation is the condition for members to be independent frames at all.

2. **The response of each member is a slot-level residual.** Fig 1 :19: *"The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated."* Fig 1 :22: *"Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average."* A richness count or a mean trait value is frame-internal to the group description (Fig 1 :27), a filter for the budget, not a witness for the verdict (Fig 9 :60).

3. **The perturbation is a selector between member rooms.** Fig 7 :33: *"→ variation inside, selection between"*; Fig 7 :35: *"A room's agreement is variation, not a verdict. Selection between rooms is where the ground votes."* With all members responding alike, Fig 7 :32 reads *"→ one long room: motion, no progress"*. Difference is necessary but not sufficient: the taking-over member must match the residual the failing member surfaced (Fig 7 :36).

4. **Response diversity is independence between member responses, not a count.** Fig 2 :31: *"Independence, between two frames, is how little they draw on the same evidence."* Fig 2 :40: *"disqualifying for a verdict: agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool"*. Two species using the same response mechanism are one frame wearing two names (Fig 8 :48-49). Fig 8 :32: *"Closure is idempotent. Composing inside a closed set never leaves it, so no search, however long, adds an atom."* If the group holds one response type, no internal rearrangement produces a second when a novel condition arrives.

5. **Convergence is the strong case; shared mechanism is the weak case.** Fig 8 :50: *"But shared atoms are not the signature. Two frames can hold the same atom by convergence, reaching it separately, which is corroboration at maximum independence."* Fig 8 :51-52: *"Convergent derivation and adopted import look identical in the contents. Only the record of where each came from separates them"*. A trait table cannot tell them apart; the lineage can.

6. **Member valence is relative to the perturbation.** Fig 11 :49-51: *"An actor's valence is relative to the residual, not a property it carries"*; *"The same actor is a benefactor to one residual and a malfactor to another, so mutualism and parasitism are relations rather than kinds"*; *"No standing list of good actors survives a change of residual."* A species that is redundant under one condition can be load-bearing under another.

7. **Resilience belongs to group-and-habitat, not the group alone.** Fig 11 :39-40: *"capability is a property of agent-and-habitat, never of the agent alone"*; *"An improvement that does not change contact changes nothing, however much it improves."* Fig 11 :43-44 requires the habitat to be enumerated, never composed, and Fig 11 :52-54 shows why the residual that matters can be three steps out.

8. **The two resilience currencies do not add.** Fig 12 :125: *"A composition costs in two currencies, and they do not add."* Pitzer et al. 2026 (opened via search index) find response diversity raising functional stability and lowering compositional stability. Fig 9 :4-5 says split rather than search, and Fig 12 :130: *"Summing them into one figure is the error this names, not the fix for it."*

9. **No amount, species or threshold is returned.** Fig 9 :32: *"you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself."* Fig 13 :134: *"Unreachable is a verdict here, not a silence,"*. The numerical form is a declared `gap`, not silence and not an invitation to invent a number.

## 3. What it explicitly does not say

- Not "more species means more resilience". Counts are filters; response independence is the witness.
- Not "functional redundancy is a buffer". Redundancy without independence is a mirror (Fig 2 :35: *"A mirror cannot correct you."*).
- Not "response diversity equals species richness". The Galápagos predator study (Chico-Ortiz et al. 2026) is a case with high functional redundancy and low response diversity.
- Not that function stability and compositional stability are one quantity.
- Not a species list, a trait threshold, an optimal variance, or a universal sign.
- Not `grounded`. A simulated derivation cannot settle the question it reports on.
- Not an ecological claim that the framework's "functional group" is the same object as ecology's. The words overlap; the effect description does the work (strain S1 in `01`).

## 4. What would settle it: the ground, not agreement

An instrument is owed, not another argument. Fig 6 :32: *"An instrument is not built from a description. It is improved from a worse instrument already returning something."* The existing instruments already returning a reading are: per-species thermal performance or drought-response curves, reciprocal transplants, and replicated perturbation experiments with a focal function measured before and after. What they currently fail to resolve is the source question (Fig 8 :51-52): do the differing members draw on separate response mechanisms, or one mechanism at different magnitudes?

Discriminators that would move the verdict:

- **Mirror-chain test:** compare groups with correlated versus uncorrelated member responses under several independent perturbations, with a focal function that does not update on group composition. If correlated groups persist equally, D2 and D3 fail.
- **Source-discriminating test:** measure the mechanism behind each member's response, not just its magnitude. If all members share one pathway, the surplus is zero and D3 collapses.
- **Two-currency test:** report function persistence and compositional turnover separately. If a single measure is supplied that legitimately combines them, F7 is withdrawn.
- **Order test:** run the same perturbations in different sequences. If order has no effect with the group fixed, the ordered-bond reading in `06` Q4 is dropped.
- **Census validity test:** try to cast the group/qualifier distinction in effect terms for a guild where it does not fit. If the strain cannot be resolved, the derivation is withdrawn (refuter 3 in `01` section 4).

Fig 2 :42: *"Buy independence in proportion to the cost of being wrong."* The cost of being wrong is a wrong restoration or management decision, so an independent frame is cheap.

## 5. The honest stop

**Unreached, not unreachable in principle.** The figure set returns the shape and the refuters; it does not return the ecological fact. The world sources largely agree with the shape, but that agreement is not independent corroboration of the framework: the insurance and response-diversity literatures were read *after* the effect description, and they share a broad ecology evidence pool among themselves. The reliability-engineering and portfolio-theory sources are genuinely lower overlap and corroborate the mechanism shape (identical redundancy defeated by common cause; covariance deciding portfolio risk), not this ecosystem.

The stop is recorded with its lineage so it is checkable: Fig 13 :140-141 requires *"not only what was concluded, but what could have been concluded, and what would have had to be true for the answer to differ."* Sections 3 and 4 of this file are that record.

## 6. One-paragraph version for outreach

> The figures do not say that more species always make an ecosystem resilient. They say that a functional group fixes *what kind* of function is at stake while the differences among its members say *which member* is performing when the condition changes. Those differences are the live, per-species signal; averaging them into one richness or trait number throws the signal away. The perturbation is then a selector between the differing member responses: if all members respond alike, the group is one long room and has no second response to offer a novel condition; if they respond independently, the ground can vote for the member that matches the failure that just surfaced. A member's value flips with the perturbation, so there is no standing list of good or redundant species, and the group's capability belongs to the group and its habitat together. What is returned is this shape, a fixed-atom isomer reading of within-group differences, five refuters written in advance, and the instruments that would close it. No species, number, or universal sign is claimed; the question stays open at that limit.
