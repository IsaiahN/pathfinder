# Expert verification packet: the Tether on within-group variation and resilience

**What this is:** a claim in a form a functional-diversity ecologist can check. Mode, derivation and refuters are stated before the conclusion, so a reader knows what to look for and what would settle it.

**Mode: `specified` on the sub-claims, `general` on the structural shape, `gap` on any numerical amount. Not `grounded`.** No ecosystem was measured. This is a simulated derivation checked for internal consistency against the figures.

## 1. The claim, in one paragraph

A functional group fixes the kind of function at stake; the differences among its members say which member is performing when a condition changes. Those within-group differences are the live, per-member residual, and their independence is the quantity that decides whether the perturbation has a different response to select. If members respond identically, the group has one response and no internal rearrangement creates a second when a novel condition arrives. If they respond independently, the ground can select a member that matches the failure just surfaced, and the function persists. Member valence is relative to the perturbation, so there is no standing list of good or redundant species, and the group's capability belongs to group-and-habitat. Function persistence and compositional turnover are different currencies and must not be pooled. The framework returns this shape and its refuters, not a species, a variance, or a universal sign.

## 2. Three checkable sub-claims

### C1. Group versus qualifier, and per-member residuals (framework-internal plus ecology-facing)

- **Statement:** the group fixes what kind of outcome is at stake; the qualifier says which member performs. The within-group difference is a per-slot residual, so averaging members removes the signal that identifies the failing member.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 12 :123, *"functional group. A substructure whose presence fixes the outcome. The group says what kind; the qualifier says which member."* Fig 1 :19, *"The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated."* Fig 1 :22, *"Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average."*
- **What to check:** in your system, does the group membership predict *which* function is performed while the member identity predicts *under which condition* it is performed? Does a mean trait or richness index hide a member whose residual is live?
- **Refuter:** per-member response residuals are flat and non-trending across perturbations while the function persists, or a pooled richness index predicts function-through-perturbation as well as a per-member index across independent perturbations with no shared evidence pool. Either fires F2 in `01` and this claim is withdrawn.

### C2. Selection between differing member responses (ecology-facing; the decisive empirical claim)

- **Statement:** a perturbation is a selector between member rooms, not a force applied to an averaged group. Identical responses leave one room; differing independent responses let the ground select a member that matches the surfaced residual.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 7 :33, *"→ variation inside, selection between"*; Fig 7 :35, *"A room's agreement is variation, not a verdict. Selection between rooms is where the ground votes."* Fig 7 :32, *"→ one long room: motion, no progress"*. Fig 2 :31, *"Independence, between two frames, is how little they draw on the same evidence."*
- **What to check:** can you name a perturbation, a focal function that does not update on group composition, and a per-member response measure? Yachi and Loreau 1999 (full text opened) predict that with perfect positive correlation of responses the buffering effect vanishes and the ecosystem behaves as one species; Chico-Ortiz et al. 2026 (full text opened) provide a direct case of four carnivores in one functional group with opposing thermal responses.
- **Refuter:** a replicated, anchored experiment in which perfectly correlated member groups maintain the focal function as well as response-diverse groups across several independent perturbations; or a source-discriminating result showing the apparent differences are one mechanism at different magnitudes and function is still maintained. Either fires refuters 1 and 3 in `01` section 4.

### C3. Independence, convergence and the no-standing-list rule (framework-internal, with a live measurement type)

- **Statement:** response diversity is independence between member responses, not a count. Two members reaching the same function by separate responses are corroboration at maximum independence; two members sharing one mechanism are one frame wearing two names. A member's contribution flips with the residual.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 8 :50, *"But shared atoms are not the signature. Two frames can hold the same atom by convergence, reaching it separately, which is corroboration at maximum independence."* Fig 8 :51-52, *"Convergent derivation and adopted import look identical in the contents. Only the record of where each came from separates them"*. Fig 11 :51, *"No standing list of good actors survives a change of residual."*
- **What to check:** does the trait data record the *mechanism* of each member's response, not only its magnitude? If not, convergence and shared mechanism are indiscernible and the claim about independence is unmeasured, not refuted.
- **Refuter:** a stable ranking of member contributions across independent perturbation regimes and habitats (contradicts residual-relative valence), or a demonstration that all members use a single response pathway and the function persists (zero surplus). A live type question remains open: whether a "response" is an atom or a bond to a condition. This file treats it as a bond (Fig 12 :46); an instrument, not an argument, would settle it.

## 3. What is explicitly not claimed

- Not "more species means more resilience", and not a universal positive sign.
- Not that functional redundancy buffers by itself; redundancy without independence is a mirror (Fig 2 :35, *"Alignment is triangulation, not negotiation. A mirror cannot correct you."*).
- Not that response diversity equals species richness; the two come apart in the Galápagos case and in coral-reef cases cited there.
- Not that function stability and compositional stability are one quantity. They can move in opposite directions (Pitzer et al. 2026); Fig 12 :130, *"Summing them into one figure is the error this names, not the fix for it."*
- Not a species list, an optimal variance, a species count, or a restoration prescription.
- Not `grounded`. No field measurement was taken for this derivation.
- Not a claim that the framework's "functional group" and ecology's are the same object. The words overlap; the effect description, written before the figures were consulted, does the work.

## 5. What would settle it

A source-discriminating instrument, not a further argument. Fig 6 :32: *"An instrument is not built from a description. It is improved from a worse instrument already returning something."* Existing instruments already returning a reading: thermal performance curves, reciprocal transplants, replicated perturbation experiments. What they fail to resolve is the source question: one mechanism or several. The specific discriminators are listed in `07_the_tether_answer.md` section 4 and the refuters in `01` section 4. ## 6. What an expert can check in twenty minutes

1. Read Fig 12 :123, Fig 1 :19/:22 and Fig 7 :32/:33/:35. Five short passages.
2. Check C2 against your own system: name one perturbation, one focal function, and one per-member response measure. Is there a second response available if the first fails?
3. Say what you would count as a failure of the shape, and name the instrument that would discriminate shared mechanism from convergence.
4. Send the refutation. A failure is more useful than an endorsement.
