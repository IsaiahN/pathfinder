**Subject:** One checkable claim about species within a functional group, before any theory

> Hi [name],
>
> I have one specific output about biodiversity and ecosystem resilience that I would like you to check against what you already know. I am not asking you to read the framework first, and I am not asking you to agree with it. If the output does not survive, there is nothing else to look at.
>
> **The output (`specified`, not `grounded`).** Take a functional group and ask what the differences *among* its species do to the persistence of the group's function under a perturbation. The claim separates two things that are usually pooled: the group fixes *what kind* of function is at stake, while the differences between members say *which member* is actually performing when the condition changes. From that split it follows that:
>
> 1. the per-species response is the live signal; averaging members into one richness or mean-trait number removes the signal that says where the failure is;
> 2. the perturbation is a selector between the differing member responses, not a disturbance applied to an averaged group;
> 3. if the members respond identically, the group has one response and no amount of internal rearrangement creates a second one when a novel condition arrives;
> 4. a member's contribution flips with the perturbation, so no standing list of "good" or "redundant" species survives a change of condition.
>
> This is a **shape and a mechanism**, not a result. It returns no species list, no optimal variance, and no number of species. It is a simulated derivation checked for internal consistency against a set of figures, not an ecosystem measurement.
>
> **What I am not claiming.** Not that more species always means more resilience. Not that functional redundancy is a buffer by itself: redundancy without response independence is a mirror. Not that response diversity is the same as species richness. Not that function stability and compositional stability are one quantity; they can move in opposite directions. Not `grounded`. No field measurement was taken for this.
>
> **What would refute it (written before this message was sent):**
> 1. a replicated, anchored experiment in which groups with perfectly correlated member responses maintain the focal function as well as response-diverse groups across several independent perturbations;
> 2. per-member response residuals that are flat across perturbations while function persists, so aggregation loses nothing;
> 3. a source-discriminating result showing the apparently different member responses are one mechanism at different magnitudes and function is still maintained;
> 4. a systematic sign reversal, with response diversity reducing function persistence across systems;
> 5. a demonstrated domain mismatch: that the group/qualifier distinction cannot be stated in effect terms for an ecological guild at all.
>
> If any one holds, I withdraw the corresponding claim. A one-line "this fails because..." is more useful to me than agreement. If you would rather not engage, no reply is needed.
>
> **What I would actually value from you:** what would you count as a failure of this shape in your system? Which perturbation, which function, and which measurement would discriminate? A named discriminator is worth more to me than an endorsement.
>
> **If it survives, and only then:** links are below. The figures are the authority, not my summary of them.
> - Local figure text extract with line numbers: `FRAMEWORK/reference/03-FIGURES_TEXT.md` (shared on request as a file, not as a claim of authority).
> - Canonical figures README and citation rule: `FRAMEWORK/reference/FIGURES_README_canonical.md`.
> - Public figures repository: https://github.com/IsaiahN/tether/tree/general-agent/figures
>
> **If you want to use the machinery:** it is a generator, not an answer. State the shape you are looking for as an ordinary term, write down what would refute it *before* the run, and label the mode of what you get. A reported failure is more useful to me than a reported success.
>
> Best,
> [name]
