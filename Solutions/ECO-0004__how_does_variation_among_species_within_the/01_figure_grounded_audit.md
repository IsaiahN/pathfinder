## 0. The residual, stated in effect terms

The question asks: what does a difference *among* species that play the *same* role do to the persistence of the system's function?

Restated as an effect, before any theory is chosen:

- **Two things are being separated.** (a) The group-level role, the thing the species are equivalent in, and (b) the member-level response when a perturbation changes which members can still perform. The residual is the gap between predicting the outcome from (a) alone and what happens when (b) is included.
- **What each option leads to.** If within-group variation is treated as noise, the group is described by one averaged actor and the model loses the signal that says which member is still performing when the ground changes. If it is treated as signal, the group is described per member and the perturbation becomes a selector between members. Those are different objects with different anchors, not two strengths of the same variable.
- **What an answer would look like, bound as an ordinary term before the run.** An answer is a claim of the form: *within-group variation supplies response independence; under perturbation the ground selects between the differing responses; a group whose members respond identically has one response and cannot carry the function across a novel condition.* That shape is written down here and allowed to accrue a residual like any other term (Fig 5 :65). It is not allowed to be treated as a preferred direction invisible to the loop.

The residual's own description, before frame selection:

- **Arity:** at least two conditions and at least three slots. The focal function, a perturbing condition, and multiple member species that are equivalent in role but not in response. A bundled union, not one hard question (Fig 9 :54: *"And when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again."*).
- **Symmetry:** the group relation is symmetric (members interchangeable at the level of role); the response relation is ordered and conditional (which member performs depends on which condition arrives). The question lives exactly at that asymmetry.
- **Scale:** within-group member slots versus system-level function. A membrane crossing, not one scale (Fig 4 :27: *"The transform between scales loses information, and that is the point."*).

**Partition or union.** Union, not partition. The conditions overlap: a species can be in the group by effect trait, differ by response trait, be suppressed by the perturbation, and be replaced by a member whose response was already present. A single lever clearing one condition leaves the intersections standing (Fig 9 :55: *"A single lever clears the region where only one condition holds and leaves the intersections standing, because the second condition still binds."*). Verdicts will be kept per domain and never averaged into one resilience score.

Fig 3 :19: *"Optimising a link that already works cannot rescue one that does not."* The link most likely to be unmeasured here is D4, the objective measurement link, because richness is easy to count and function-through-perturbation is not (Fig 3 :27: *"A link with no instrument is not a link that works. ## 3. Findings

### F1. The figure names the question's own distinction: group versus qualifier (decisive for D1)

Fig 12 :123, verbatim:

> **"functional group. A substructure whose presence fixes the outcome. The group says what kind; the qualifier says which member."**

This is the exact structure of the question. The functional group fixes *what kind* of outcome is at stake (the function that persists). The variation among species *within* it is the *qualifier*, the part that says which member is doing it. The question is not asking whether the group matters; the figure says it fixes the outcome. It is asking what the qualifier buys.

Fig 13 :34 adds the reason qualifiers can matter at all:

> **"And within a kind the members are distinct too, which is what makes them separate frames."**

So within-group variation is not a blemish on an otherwise clean kind. Within-kind distinctness is what makes members separate frames, and separate frames are the precondition for a surplus (Fig 8 :45). The framework therefore refuses to collapse the group into one averaged member on structural grounds.

**Refuter for F1.** Re-census shows Fig 12 :123 governs only chemical substructures and cannot be cast in effect terms for an ecological guild, or an empirical case shows the focal function is fixed by species identity rather than by group membership in a way that makes the qualifier the whole outcome (then the group line does not govern and D1 falls). Strain check: the phrase "functional group" is borrowed vocabulary shared with ecology, so this mapping carries an echo risk, recorded as S1 in section 7 and handled by describing the effect before naming the figure.

### F2. The residual is per member, not per group; aggregation throws away the live signal (decisive for D4)

Fig 1 :19 and :22, verbatim:

> **"The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated."**
> **"Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average."**

Fig 1 :28:

> **"The gap is measured per object, not per step. A system that averages across objects has thrown away the only signal that says where to look."**

A functional group treated as one slot (a richness count, a mean trait value, a single "functional diversity" number) is exactly the aggregation the figure forbids. The per-species response to the perturbation is the slot-level residual. If one member fails and another carries the function, the global function reading may stay near zero residual while a live slot residual exists. Fig 1 :20 says that is a legal state, not an inert one: *"a global residual near zero with one live slot is a legal state and not an inert"* [one]. The index that reports "function maintained" and the index that reports "one member is failing" are measuring different slices.

Fig 1 :24-27 disqualifies the easy substitute:

> **"Everything else either produces a residual or consumes the library. These do not count:"**
> **"predicates minted, and credibility accrued"**
> **"They are frame-internal. A frame cannot score itself with a quantity it also produces."**

A count of species in the group is frame-internal to the group description. It does not register against the perturbation. Fig 9 :60: *"Use filters for the budget and witnesses for the verdict. Never let a filter hand you a verdict."* Richness is a filter for which systems to sample; response residual is a witness for the mechanism.

**Refuter for F2.** A replicated study shows that per-species response residuals are flat (no persistent, non-trending residual) across perturbations while the group-level function varies, so the slot-level signal is not live and aggregation loses nothing. Or a study shows the pooled richness index predicts function-through-perturbation as well as the per-member response index across independent perturbations, with no shared evidence pool. Then aggregation is not throwing away the signal and F2 is withdrawn.

### F3. The perturbation is a selector between member rooms, and identical responses are one long room (decisive for D2)

Fig 7 :33 and :35, verbatim:

> **"→ variation inside, selection between"**
> **"A room's agreement is variation, not a verdict. Selection between rooms is where the ground votes."**

This is the cleanest figure statement of the mechanism. The differing responses inside the group are variation. The perturbation is the selection between them. The function's persistence is where the ground votes. If all members respond alike, there is no selection between rooms: Fig 7 :32 says *"→ one long room: motion, no progress"*, and Fig 7 :13 says of that chain *"agreement compounds, evidence does not"*. A group of identical responders can keep agreeing with itself through a perturbation and still not move toward a function that survives the new condition.

Fig 7 :36: *"A chain of mirrors is one long room. The next room must differ from the last, and match the residual it surfaced."* The word "match" matters: mere difference is not enough. The member that takes over must match the residual the failing member surfaced, meaning it must actually perform the same function under the new condition. Difference in an irrelevant trait is not response diversity.

Fig 2 :29 supplies the anchor test:

> **"An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks. A measurement, an experiment, a counter, a market, a patient outcome. If it moves when you push on it, it is not an anchor."**

The perturbation is a legitimate anchor only when the question is constitutively about persistence under that perturbation (Fig 2 :36: *"An anchor is legitimate when the question is constitutively about it."*). If "resilience" is left as an unmeasured feeling about the system, there is no anchor and D2 is not a domain yet.

**Refuter for F3.** A controlled, replicated perturbation experiment shows that groups whose member responses are perfectly correlated maintain the focal function as well as response-diverse groups across several independent perturbations, with an anchor that does not update on group composition. That fires the mirror-chain prediction and refutes D2.

### F4. Independence between member responses is the quantity, not the count of members (D3, triangulated)

Fig 2 :31 and :39-40, verbatim:

> **"Independence, between two frames, is how little they draw on the same evidence."**
> **"Correlation has valence."**
> **"disqualifying for a verdict: agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool"**

Two species in the same functional group that respond to drought through the same physiological mechanism are two frames reading one evidence pool. Their apparent redundancy is a mirror, not corroboration. Fig 13 :37-38 states the same law for the substrate's members: *"If two members drew on the same underlying fact, their agreement would carry no information, and one of them would be redundant rather than minimal."*

Fig 8 :50 sharpens it:

> **"But shared atoms are not the signature. Two frames can hold the same atom by convergence, reaching it separately, which is corroboration at maximum independence."**

Members can perform the same function by convergent, separately evolved responses. That is corroboration at maximum independence, and it is the strong form of within-group variation. Fig 8 :51-52 adds the provenance rule: *"Convergent derivation and adopted import look identical in the contents. Only the record of where each came from separates them, and the surplus lives only where the closures differ."* A trait table cannot tell convergence from shared mechanism; the lineage can.

Fig 8 :32 closes the argument:

> **"Closure is idempotent. Composing inside a closed set never leaves it, so no search, however long, adds an atom. This one is definitional: it is what closure means."**

If the group's members all draw on one response type, no amount of rearranging within the group produces a new response. When a novel condition arrives, the wall does not move from inside. Fig 6 :23: *"No frame escapes its own closure. The bound moves only on contact with a frame whose closure differs."* Response diversity is the group already holding more than one response closure before the perturbation arrives.

**Refuter for F4.** A source-discriminating experiment shows the apparently different member responses are the same mechanism expressed at different magnitudes (one evidence pool), and the group still maintains function. Then independence is absent, the surplus is zero, and D3 collapses into D2 with one room.

### F5. The group fixes the kind; the member fixes the route to it. Arrangement, not ingredient list (D1, supports F1)

Fig 12 :94 and :117, verbatim:

> **"The arrangement is the substance."**
> **"isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."**

Two species with the same effect traits but different response traits are the ecological analogue of isomers: same functional formula, different arrangement relative to the perturbation, different behaviour when the condition changes. This is why a list of the group's functions cannot predict resilience. The bond, here the response relation between member and condition, carries information the ingredient list does not (Fig 12 :46: *"A bond is the typed relation between two operands. There are seven, they are not interchangeable, and which one holds is not recoverable from the operands."*).

Fig 12 :119 is a second structure worth carrying:

> **"catalyst. A part that survives its own use. Spent and regenerating parts behave differently across uses, and nothing records which a term is."**

A member that performs and then recovers (a resprouter after fire, a recolonising pollinator) is not the same kind of term as one that is spent by performing. A trait table that records both as "present in the group" has lost the distinction the figure says is decisive across uses.

**Refuter for F5.** Show that within-group differences change no system-level outcome under any perturbation, so the arrangement is not the substance at this scale and the isomer reading is empty. See `06_isomers_and_order.md` Q3 for the fixed-atom condition attached to this claim.

### F6. A member's contribution is relative to the perturbation; there is no standing good list (D5, triangulated)

Fig 11 :49-51, verbatim:

> **"An actor's valence is relative to the residual, not a property it carries."**
> **"The same actor is a benefactor to one residual and a malfactor to another, so mutualism and parasitism are relations rather than kinds."**
> **"No standing list of good actors survives a change of residual."**

A species that is redundant under one perturbation can be the sole performer under another. The warm-tolerant whelk that compensates for a cold-sensitive sea star in the Galápagos study (Chico-Ortiz et al. 2026, opened this session) would be unremarkable under a cool regime and load-bearing under a heatwave. The figures forbid ranking group members by contribution independent of the residual.

Fig 11 :39-40:

> **"So the answerable question is not what intelligence is, but what makes a system capable. And capability is a property of agent-and-habitat, never of the agent alone."**
> **"A system cannot be made more capable by improving the agent in isolation. An improvement that does not change contact changes nothing, however much it improves."**

Transposed without naming a seventh primitive: resilience is a property of the group-and-habitat, not of the group alone. Adding species to a group without changing their contact with the perturbing condition changes nothing.

Fig 11 :43-44 requires enumeration, not composition:

> **"The habitat is enumerated, never composed."**
> **"List everything in contact with the residual, then what is in contact with those, and outward until the cascade stops mattering."**

Fig 11 :52-54 warns what happens at one step out: *"And contact cascades. Scoping the habitat to direct contact is how DDT reached the eagles: first-order enumeration, second-order effect, and the residual that mattered was three steps out."* A within-group result that stops at the group boundary is a first-order enumeration.

**Refuter for F6.** A stable ranking of member contributions to the focal function is found across independent perturbation regimes and habitats, which would contradict residual-relative valence. Or an agent-only improvement (for example, raising the performance of one member in isolation) moves the ground, which the figure's own note says would refute the capability claim (Fig 11 :72).

### F7. The two currencies must be held apart: group function versus compositional turnover (D4, world conflict returned to census)

Fig 12 :125-126 and :130, verbatim:

> **"A composition costs in two currencies, and they do not add."**
> **"Bits price the description. Actions price finding out whether it holds. They are in different units, so a system holding one accounting cannot compare them"**
> **"Summing them into one figure is the error this names, not the fix for it."**

"Resilience" in the ecological literature bundles at least two outcomes: persistence of function and persistence of composition. Pitzer et al. 2026 (abstract retrieved through the public search index) report that response diversity *"increases functional stability but decreases stability in terms of species composition"*. Read as a single score, that is a conflict. Read through Fig 12 :125-130, it is two currencies in different units, and a single resilience number is the error the figure names. Fig 9 :4-5 says what to do with a disagreement that does not shrink: *"are not one hard question but several well-formed ones. Split it rather than search."*

This does not flip the verdict; it returns it to the census. D1/D2/D3 are about function persistence. D5 is partly about composition. Keeping them separate is the derived instruction.

**Refuter for F7.** A single operational definition of ecological resilience is given that genuinely measures one quantity across function and composition and is accepted by the relevant measurement community. Then the split is unnecessary and this finding is withdrawn. No figure supplies such a definition, so it is a `gap` until supplied.

### F8. No figure returns an amount of variation. The numerical form is a declared gap

Fig 9 :32: *"you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself."* Fig 13 :134: *"Unreachable is a verdict here, not a silence,"*. Fig 8 :39: *"So a search that finds nothing is never a proof of absence."*

After the census, no line in the figure text gives a threshold, an optimal variance, a species count, or a Select one of these A or B. The framework returns the shape: group fixes kind, qualifier fixes member, variation supplies independence, perturbation selects, ground votes. It cannot name the species, set the variance, or choose the perturbation, because naming those would be holding the answer as a term and then pretending to derive it (Fig 5 :62-63). This is the shape-not-a-name limit, correctly applied. It is a `gap` of the unreachable-at-this-budget and partly underivable kind, not silence.

**Refuter for F8.** A figure line is produced that governs the numerical magnitude of within-group variation, or an instrument is shown that settles it against a ground without importing an external trait model. Then the gap closes. Until then, any number offered here would be the searcher's own contribution recorded as a finding.

## 4. Pre-registered refuter (written before the conclusion)

Required before outreach: "A falsifier written before the run is a design instrument," per the standing instruction and the seat-map discipline. The shape claim is refuted if any of the following holds:

1. **Mirror-chain failure.** A replicated, anchored perturbation experiment shows that groups with perfectly correlated member responses maintain the focal function as well as response-diverse groups across several independent perturbations. (Kills D2, D3.)
2. **Flat slot residual.** Per-member response residuals are flat and non-trending across perturbations while function persists, so the within-group signal is dead and aggregation loses nothing. (Kills F2.)
3. **Domain mismatch.** Re-census shows Fig 12 :123 and Fig 7 :33 cannot be cast in effect terms for an ecological guild without a strain that the figures' own self-consistency rule forbids. (Withdraws the whole derivation.)
4. **Sign reversal.** Response diversity systematically *reduces* persistence of the focal function across functions and perturbations, with a non-updating anchor. (Reverses the verdict.)
5. **One evidence pool.** A source-discriminating instrument shows the apparently different member responses are one mechanism at different magnitudes, and function is still maintained. (Collapses D3 to one room.)
6. **Measure collapse.** A single accepted operational definition of resilience is supplied that measures function and composition as one quantity. (Withdraws F7, simplifies D4.)

If any fires, the affected claim is withdrawn in a visible retraction entry below. Until one is run, nothing here is `grounded`.

## 5. Recommendation

1. Lead with the shape and the refuter, not with the ecological literature or the framework (F10).
2. Keep the four domain verdicts separate; never report a single "variation helps resilience" score (F9, F7).
3. Replace richness and mean-trait summaries with per-member response residuals where a perturbation is available (F2). Richness stays as a sampling filter only.
4. Measure the perturbation as an anchor that does not update on group composition (F3, F6).
5. Enumerate the habitat outward at least two steps before claiming a group-level effect (F6).
6. Record the provenance of each member's response mechanism so convergence can be told from shared mechanism (F4).
7. Report the `gap` plainly: no amount is returned, and inventing one is the forbidden error (F8).

Fig 4 :25: *"A recording carried upward looks like knowledge and is a description of one occasion."* Send the generator (the residual, the shape and the refuter), not a conclusion.
