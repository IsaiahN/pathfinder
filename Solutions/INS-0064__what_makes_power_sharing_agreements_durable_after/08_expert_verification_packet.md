# Expert verification packet - the Tether on power-sharing durability

**What this is:** a claim in a form a scholar of conflict and power sharing can check. Mode, derivation and refuters are stated before the conclusion, so a reader knows what to look for and what would settle it.

**Mode: `specified` on the three per-domain verdicts, `general` on the union shape, `gap` on the standing list and the number. Not `grounded`, not a measurement, not a ground reading.**

**Status:** simulated derivation checked for internal consistency against the figures. It cannot settle the claim it reports on.

## 1. The claim, in one paragraph

The question "what makes power-sharing agreements durable after civil conflict" is a union of three conditions with different anchors, not a list of provisions with a right setting. A signed allocation of power is a convention that makes a violation statable but cannot ground its own durability. Durability needs an anchor that does not update on what the signatories say, plus an outside reading imported from a frame whose closure differs and carrying provenance and a shadow test. Capability is a property of the agreement and its habitat together: monitors, guarantors, neighbouring states and actors that did not sign are introduced and cannot be subtracted, so a managed peace is permanent work. No standing provision list is residual-independent. Two settlements over the same atom set bonded by sequence rather than by threshold are isomers. The specific provisions and the numerical durability threshold are a declared gap.

## 2. Three checkable sub-claims

### C1 - framework-internal and conceptual; checkable by reading Figure 10 and Figure 2

- **Statement:** the text of an agreement cannot be the anchor for its own durability. A written allocation is a convention, not a verdict.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 10 :49 - "Declaring a meaning does not ground it." Fig 10 :47 - "a convention that decides an outcome has stopped being one." Fig 2 :29 - "An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks." Fig 2 :33 - "The anchor must not update."
- **What to check:** does the claim confuse "the text makes a violation statable" with "the text settles whether the violation was repaired"? If a signatory's own compliance report is treated as the anchor, does it move when that signatory pushes on it?
- **Refuter:** a written allocation with no contact with any non-updating ground and no external frame that determines recurrence out of sample; or a compliance report that demonstrably does not update on the reporting party and still settles the question.

### C2 - political-science facing; checkable against the literature and cases

- **Statement:** the question is a union of overlapping conditions, and durability is indexed per group, so verdicts cannot be averaged into one coefficient. No standing list of provisions has residual-independent valence.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 9 :55-56 - "A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones. A single lever clears the region where only one condition holds and leaves the intersections standing, because the second condition still binds." Fig 1 :19 - "The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated." Fig 11 :51 - "No standing list of good actors survives a change of residual." Fig 11 :49 - "An actor's valence is relative to the residual, not a property it carries."
- **What to check:** do the cases in the standard datasets fall into different combinations of weak institution, weak practice and weak habitat, and does a drafting lever leave some combinations standing? Does a mutual veto have opposite valence under "fear of exclusion" and "inability to govern"? Relevant opened records: Wikipedia "Power sharing" (institutions "have to be implemented to matter"), Wikipedia "Consociationalism" (conditions "neither indispensable nor sufficient"), Cederman, Hug and Wucherpfennig 2022 via Cambridge Core ("practice rather than merely formal institutions", "no panacea").
- **Refuter:** a coding that assigns the institution/practice overlap to one cause with the per-group residual preserved; or a provision whose valence does not flip under any plausible change of post-conflict residual.

### C3 - framework-internal derivation with one open type question

- **Statement:** two settlements over the same institutional atoms bonded by sequence versus by threshold are isomers, and the sharing-feedback loop is a cycle whose sign the figures do not supply.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 12 :117 - "isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour." Fig 12 :46 - "A bond is the typed relation between two operands. There are seven, they are not interchangeable, and which one holds is not recoverable from the operands." Fig 12 :121 - "cycle. The output re-enters as input. The sign of the loop decides whether it closes, and a chain cannot close by construction."
- **What to check:** can two real settlements be coded over one atom set with sequence versus threshold bonding? Does an independent ground reading show a behavioural difference? Is the sign of the sharing-to-fear-to-compliance loop positive or negative in the case?
- **Refuter:** if either settlement needs an atom the other does not, the isomer claim fails and the functional-group reading stands (Fig 12 :123). If the sign is zero or identical across cases, the cycle reading fails. If durability is identical after the bond difference is controlled, the "decisive in the behaviour" clause fails.

## 3. What is explicitly not claimed

- Not a ranked list of institutions and not a new provision.
- Not a pooled durability coefficient or a numerical threshold.
- Not that vetoes, rotation, proportionality, autonomy or monitoring are *the* causes. Naming available atoms is a provenanced binding, not a figure-derived name and not a wall-moving import.
- Not that outside guarantors always help. An import can be a wrong atom; "Import from a live peer is debited against that frame's independence. A wrong atom costs more than starting from primitives." (Fig 6 :24)
- Not a sign for the sharing-feedback cycle. That is a live, named strain.
- Not `grounded`. No measurement was taken.

## 5. What would settle it

A ground reading, not agreement. From *The Hard Problem is About Source*: a spectrometer, not an argument, distinguished sources that gave the same green reading. Here:

1. **Anchor test:** does the compliance reading move when a party pushes on it?
2. **Independence test:** are institution coding and recurrence read by frames that do not share an evidence pool? A study coding both from one government report is one instrument, not two.
3. **Import test:** does the outside guarantor carry provenance and explain a gap the parties' own terms could not?
4. **Per-slot test:** does the failure register in one group's residual before the country average moves?
5. **Cycle-sign test:** does sharing lower fear and raise compliance, or entrench identity and raise fear?
6. **Isomer test:** do sequence-bonded and threshold-bonded settlements over the same atoms differ at the ground under independent readings?
