## DOMAIN CENSUS

R by arity, symmetry and scale, then one line per domain with actors / currency / anchor, partition or union, and order by dependency.

- **R (the residual).** The gap between a written allocation (prediction: "this will hold") and what the ground pays (continued joint exercise of power or a return to organized violence). Arity: three binding conditions. Symmetry: "durable" is an ordered threshold (⋛ comparison), not a symmetric join. Scale: first order inside one group's fear calculation, second order at the regime where expectations feed one another. Fig 1 :19 "The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated." Fig 1 :22 "Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average."
- **D1, agreement text / institutional design.** Actors: negotiators, elites, provisions (grand coalition, veto, proportionality, autonomy, rotation). Currency: formal office shares and written rights. Anchor: **none inside the frame**. The text updates on the parties' signatures, so it is a mirror. It is a condition in the union, not a verdict source. Seat Map :163: "A convention that decides an outcome has stopped being one."
- **D2, commitment / expectations.** Actors: former belligerents, their armed capacities, their audiences, external guarantors. Currency: credibility of future conduct through costly signals. Anchor: observed recurrence and the post-settlement distribution of capacity, neither of which moves because a party speaks. Domain: yes, anchor legitimate for a question constitutively about future exclusion or retaliation.
- **D3, habitat / implementation.** Actors: monitors, peacekeepers, a High Representative, neighbouring states, diasporas, security sector, economic actors, media. Currency: contact and introduction of actors. Anchor: the recurrence ground reached through a channel that can decay. Domain: yes. Fig 11 :43 "The habitat is enumerated, never composed." Fig 11 :73 "The habitat is not the ground. The habitat is where actors come from and it updates; the ground is what settles, and it does not."
- **D4, verification / research.** Actors: dataset coders, peer readers. Currency: residuals and agreement. Anchor: the ground, never agreement. In this simulated run every seat draws on one evidence pool (same essays, figures and public sources), so independence is zero and agreement is not corroboration.
- **Partition or union: union.** Fig 9 :55-56 "A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones. A single lever clears the region where only one condition holds and leaves the intersections standing, because the second condition still binds." Verdicts are kept per domain and never averaged.
- **Order by dependency (Fig 3).** Perception (can each group read the other's capacity?), vocabulary (terms for veto, rotation, monitoring), objective (no recurrence, not signature), planning, carry. The break is between vocabulary and objective, where the progress measure is missing.

## WORLD (secondary sources read)

World sources corroborate only. Most share a broad political-science pool; overlap is declared rather than hidden.

- **Institutional design / practice.** Wikipedia "Power sharing" (2026, opened): "power-sharing institutions have to be implemented to matter." Predicts that text alone is insufficient; corroborates the D1/D3 split. Overlap: broad political-science pool.
- **Consociationalism / conditions.** Wikipedia "Consociationalism" (2026, opened): Lijphart's conditions are "neither indispensable nor sufficient"; criticisms are "institutionalising and deepening existing divisions", "severely dependent on external support for survival" and "temporarily freezing conflicts but not resolving them." Predicts residual-relative valence and the need for maintained habitat contact; corroborates Fig 11. Overlap: same pool.
- **Case record.** Wikipedia "Dayton Agreement" (2026, opened): the agreement "mandated a wide range of international organizations to monitor, oversight and implement components of the agreement" and was both "Praised for establishing lasting peace" and "criticized for creating an unduly complex political governance system". Predicts introduction without subtraction and a mixed verdict; corroborates Fig 11 :61-64. Overlap: same pool.
- **Comparative politics / book.** Cederman, Hug and Wucherpfennig, *Sharing Power, Securing Peace?* (Cambridge University Press, 2022; publisher page opened): "while there are many successful examples of power sharing in multi-ethnic states, such as Switzerland, South Africa and Indonesia, other instances show that such arrangements offer no guarantee against violent conflict"; the study is "focusing on practices rather than merely formal institutions", and a reviewer on the page says "the peace-inducing effects of the practice - rather than merely the promise - of sharing political power, while also noting that power-sharing institutions are no panacea." Predicts the practice/promise split and no universal lever; corroborates Fig 10 :49 and Fig 9 :55-56. Overlap: same pool.
- **Strategic studies / credibility.** Wikipedia "Deterrence theory" (2026, opened): "costly signals are required to communicate the credibility of a defending state's resolve"; "all defending states have an incentive to act as if they are determined to resist an attack". Predicts that the written promise is weak and costly contact is strong; corroborates Fig 2 :33. Overlap: partly shared with the game-theory source below.
- **Mathematics / game theory.** Wikipedia "Prisoner's dilemma" (2026, opened): "For cooperation to emerge between rational players, the number of rounds must be unknown or infinite"; Axelrod's conditions are "Nice", "Retaliating", "Forgiving", "Non-envious". Predicts that indefinite repeated contact and a retaliatory-but-forgiving stance matter; corroborates the cycle-sign question, leaves the sign itself open. Overlap: strategic-studies pool with the row above.
- **Ecology / resilience (genuinely low overlap).** Wikipedia "Ecological resilience" (2026, opened), quoting Walker, Holling, Carpenter and Kinzig 2004: "the capacity of a system to absorb disturbance and reorganize while undergoing change so as to still retain essentially the same function, structure, identity, and feedbacks"; the account presumes "multiple stable states or regimes" and a threshold. Predicts that durability is a maintained relation with thresholds, not a fixed state; corroborates the Fig 11 habitat verdict at the mechanism-shape level only.
- **Animal behaviour (genuinely low overlap).** Wikipedia "Frans de Waal" (2026, opened): his "research centered on primate social behavior, including conflict resolution, cooperation, inequity aversion, and food-sharing"; he "used the term 'reconciliation' to describe reunions after fights, which was initially challenged but came to be accepted with respect to animal behaviour." Predicts that post-conflict repair is an active relationship process, not a signed document; corroborates the cycle and habitat shapes, not the political conclusion.

## VERDICT (from the figures)

The shape survives; the standing list does not. The question is a union of text, credibility and habitat with different anchors, indexed per group and never averaged into one coefficient. A written allocation makes violations statable but cannot ground its own durability. Credibility needs a non-updating anchor and an import from a differing closure with provenance and a shadow test. Capability is agent-and-habitat; actors can be introduced, not subtracted, so a managed peace is permanent work. No standing provision list is residual-independent. Two settlements over the same atoms bonded by sequence versus threshold are an isomer claim with a stated falsifier; the sign of the sharing-feedback cycle remains an open instrument question. The provision list and the number are a declared `gap`. Mode: `specified` on the three domain verdicts, `general` on the union shape, not `grounded`.

- **cost if wrong:** bounded and locatable, because verdicts are kept per domain, mode is stated and five failure conditions were written before the run. The real cost is spending the independence of outside readers by sending a recording (a best-practice list) instead of a generator (the shape and its refuter). Fig 4 :25 says of the recording that it "looks like knowledge and is a description of one occasion"; Fig 2 :42 says "Buy independence in proportion to the cost of being wrong." That cost is avoidable by leading with the shape claim and its refuter.
- **undoable:** yes. The list claims are removable without touching the derivation; `07_the_tether_answer.md` and `08_expert_verification_packet.md` already remove them. A sent packet can be superseded by a corrected one, and no figure line has to be withdrawn.
- **reversed by:** a ground reading, not agreement. An instrument that shows a compliance reading does not move when a party pushes it, with independent coders reading institutions and recurrence from different sources, would settle the anchor question. A per-group measurement of recurrence before the country average moves would settle the slot question. A measurement of the sharing-to-fear-to-compliance loop's sign would close the cycle strain. A paired comparison of sequence-bonded and threshold-bonded settlements under independent readings would confirm or kill the isomer claim. A derivation of a standing list from the figures, or a round-trip measurement showing the condensation loses no constraint, would reverse the gap. Until one of these fires the verdict stands as `candidate`.
