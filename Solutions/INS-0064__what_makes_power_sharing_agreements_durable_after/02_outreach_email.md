# Outreach draft: check one shape before you decide whether the framework is worth reading

**Use:** one-to-one, or as a cover letter attached to a submission through the public venues in `03_public_contact_points.csv`. **Not** for bulk send.

**Hard precondition:** do not send until you have `08_expert_verification_packet.md` in front of you and can state the refuter without looking. The offer collapses on contact if the stage-1 output does not survive.

---

**Subject:** One shape claim about power-sharing durability, with a failure condition written in advance

> Hi [name],
>
> I have one specific output I would like you to check against what you already know about
> post-conflict power sharing. I am not asking you to read a theory first, and I am not asking
> you to agree with it. If the output does not survive, there is nothing else to look at.
>
> **The output (`specified` on the two domains, `general` on the shape, not `grounded`):**
> the question as posed asks for a standing list of durability institutions. That object does
> not survive the check. What survives is a separator with four parts:
> 1. A signed allocation of power is a convention, not an anchor. It makes a violation
>    statable, but it cannot be the thing that settles whether the settlement holds.
> 2. Durability needs an anchor that does not update on what the parties say. Observed
>    recurrence, and the post-settlement distribution of capacity, are such an anchor; the
>    signatories' own compliance report is not.
> 3. The habitat is enumerated from contact outward, not composed from a list of provisions.
>    Capability is a property of the agreement and its habitat together, so monitors,
>    guarantors, neighbouring states and actors that did not sign all count. Introduction is
>    available; subtraction is not. A managed peace is permanent work, not a solved state.
> 4. The question is a union of overlapping conditions, not a partition. A lever that fixes
>    the text leaves the credibility and habitat cases standing. The verdicts stay per
>    domain and are never averaged into one durability coefficient.
>
> **What I am not claiming:** that any particular provision, veto rule, rotation schedule or
> peacekeeping mission is *the* cause of durability. Valence is relative to the residual. A
> mutual veto helps the group that fears exclusion and hurts the settlement that cannot
> decide. No standing list of good institutions survives a change of residual. I am also not
> claiming that the framework returns a number, a ranked list or a final theory. The
> specific provisions and the numerical threshold are a declared gap.
>
> **What would refute the shape claim:**
> 1. a written allocation that, with no non-updating ground contact and no outside frame,
>    determines recurrence out of sample;
> 2. a purely verbal promise, with no costly signal, no repeated interaction and no outside
>    reading, that reliably prevents recurrence;
> 3. a settlement whose capability is unchanged after every monitor, guarantor and
>    second-order contact actor is removed, with text and signatories held fixed;
> 4. a list of provisions whose valence never flips under any plausible change of the
>    post-conflict residual;
> 5. a durability study that codes institutions and recurrence from the same source, with no
>    independent outside reading, and still predicts out of sample.
>
> I wrote these down before sending this. If any one holds, I will retract or narrow the
> claim in a dated entry. A one-line "this fails because..." is more useful to me than
> agreement. If you would rather not engage, no reply is needed.
>
> **If it survives, and only then:** the sources are below. The figures are the authority,
> not my summary of them.
> - Figure text with line numbers (local extract): `FRAMEWORK/reference/03-FIGURES_TEXT.md`
> - Canonical usage rule (local): `FRAMEWORK/reference/FIGURES_README_canonical.md`
> - Eight-step formula (local): `FRAMEWORK/figures/THE_FORMULA.md`
>
> **If you want to use the machinery:** the formula is in the figures link. Run it on a
> problem you know well and can check against a ground. Write down what would refute your
> result *before* you run it, and label the mode of what you get. The framework's own limit is
> that a loop cannot check the direction of its own question, so a flattering refuter written
> after the run proves nothing. A reported failure is more useful to me than a reported
> success.
>
> — [name]

## Two-stage offer, restated

1. **Stage 1:** send the four-part shape and its five failure conditions. Ask what the reader would count as a failure. Record their verdict *before* showing any framework material.
2. **Stage 2, opt-in only:** if they are curious, offer the three local source paths. Their later agreement is never pooled as corroboration, because the essays, the figures and this packet share one evidence pool (Fig 2 :40).
3. **Stage 3:** offer the formula as a method to reuse, with the instruction that each user writes their own refuter and states their own mode.

No stage proceeds on a "sounds right". Stage 2 proceeds on curiosity after a stage-1 verdict. That is the whole point of the ordering.
