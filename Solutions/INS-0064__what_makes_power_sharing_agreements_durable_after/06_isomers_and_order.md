# Union structure, order, and isomers of a power-sharing settlement

**Mode: `specified` on the structural claims, `general` on the union and order reading, `gap` on which atoms occupy the slots. This is a simulated derivation checked against the figures, not a ground reading (see `05` section 7).**

## RESIDUAL

The first-pass question asks for an ordered ranking of durability institutions. Two questions are owed before any ranking: (1) is the thing being ranked a partition or a union, and where does its dependency chain break; (2) if two settlements hold the same institutional ingredients yet last for different lengths of time, what is the figure-level relation between them.

Atoms available in the political-science frame: grand coalition, mutual veto, proportionality, segmental or territorial autonomy, rotation of office, third-party monitoring, external guarantee, demobilization, power-sharing in the security sector. Bonds available: the seven, including sequence →, comparison ⋛, conjunction + and subtraction −, plus negation ¬. No new primitive is available without import. ## DERIVATION

### Q1 - Partition or union? UNION

- Fig 9 :54: "And when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again."
- Fig 9 :55-56: "A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones. A single lever clears the region where only one condition holds and leaves the intersections standing, because the second condition still binds."
- Fig 9 :57-58: "a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either. It stops being a mixture and becomes a specific compound."

Applying this to the opened source definitions: power-sharing *institutions* are the formal provisions; power-sharing *practices* are the de facto joint decision-making; the Cambridge 2022 study finds that institutions "have to be implemented to matter" and that the effect is one of "practice rather than merely formal institutions". Those are not two levels of one variable with an assignable fraction. They are two conditions that overlap in some cases and not in others. A drafting reform clears the case where only the institution is missing; it leaves the case where practice is also missing standing, and that residue is categorically different (a compound, not a harder drafting case).

- **Falsifier, written before the run:** exhibit a coding in which the shared fraction of institution and practice effects can be assigned to one cause instead of left unassignable, with the per-group residual still indexed (Fig 1 :19). If that coding survives an independent ground reading, the union reading fails for that case.

### Q2 - Where does the dependency chain break?

- Fig 3 :4-5: link 1 is perception, "the system can tell what is in front of it, and tell it apart from what was there before".
- Fig 3 :6-8: link 2 is vocabulary, "it has terms for what things afford: what can be moved, opened, joined, blocked".
- Fig 3 :9-11: link 3 is the objective, "it can say what would count as progress here, in its own terms".
- Fig 3 :24-27: "Why the middle link is where chains usually break. Perception has obvious failures and obvious tests. Planning has obvious failures and obvious tests. The link between them, having terms for what a situation offers and being able to say what would count as progress, has neither... A link with no instrument is not a link that works. It is a link nobody has looked at."

Post-conflict settlements are rich in link 2 and poor in link 3. Negotiators have terms for vetoes, rotations, quotas and monitoring. What counts as progress is frequently recorded as the signature itself, which is a reading of the text through a saturated metric (Fig 10 :25-26: "stale percepts, dropped transients, saturated metrics, pooled readings"). Seat Map :271 names the genus: "Produced, recorded, and not read... This is the one found last, because the evidence exists and so nothing looks missing."

- Fig 3 :19: "Optimising a link that already works cannot rescue one that does not." Adding more veto language above the break cannot rescue a missing progress measure.
- **Falsifier:** show a settlement corpus in which a validated, non-updating progress measure exists at link 3 and the durability residual still persists per group after it is read. That would move the break elsewhere and refuse this reading.

### Q3 - Are two different settlements isomers?

- Fig 12 :117: "isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."
- Fig 12 :46-47: "A bond is the typed relation between two operands. There are seven, they are not interchangeable, and which one holds is not recoverable from the operands."
- Fig 12 :5: "A set of ingredients is not one. The bond is the rest of the information, and it is the half nothing was recording."

Take two settlements over the same atom set: {grand coalition, mutual veto, proportionality, autonomy, rotation, monitoring, external guarantee, demobilization}.

- Settlement A bonds them by **sequence** (→): first demobilize, then monitor, then rotate office, with the veto as a backstop. Fig 12 :71-73 defines sequence: "A then B; B needs A to have happened" and its test "swap them. Does it become a different thing? Yes."
- Settlement B bonds them by **comparison** (⋛): mutual vulnerability is held at a threshold and the provisions switch on or off as that threshold is crossed. Fig 12 :85-87 defines comparison: "a threshold relation between two quantities", with the test "is it about which is larger, rather than both being present? Yes."

Same ingredients, different bond, different substance. Under Fig 12 :117 they are isomers, and the difference is invisible in an ingredient list. That is exactly why an ingredient-level literature reports mixed findings: the coders record atoms and not bonds (Fig 12 :14-16: "the ingredients are recorded and the arrangement is not / nothing downstream can tell them apart, / and nothing signals that anything was lost").

- Weaker form: if the two settlements share only one or two atoms, they are not isomers but members of a **functional group**, Fig 12 :123: "functional group. A substructure whose presence fixes the outcome. The group says what kind; the qualifier says which member." Both would then be instances of "shared decision making with an outside reading", differing in which atoms fill the slots.
- **Falsifier, written before the run:** if either settlement requires an atom the other does not (for example one needs Space-like territorial autonomy and the other does not, in the domain analogue one needs territorial autonomy and the other needs only office sharing), the isomer claim fails and the functional-group reading stands. A second falsifier: if independent ground readings show identical durability after the bond difference is controlled, the "decisive in the behaviour" clause fails.
- **Mode:** `specified`, unmeasured. Figure-side triangulated (Fig 12 :117 plus Fig 12 :46 plus Fig 12 :5, one figure but three independent structural statements); the mapping onto real settlements is a binding from the political-science frame and is provenance, not a derivation.

### Q4 - Is a durable settlement a cycle, and what is its sign?

- Fig 12 :121: "cycle. The output re-enters as input. The sign of the loop decides whether it closes, and a chain cannot close by construction."

The ordinary story is a positive feedback: sharing lowers fear, lower fear makes compliance more credible, credibility invites more sharing. But the same arrangement can run the other way: sharing entrenches group identities, entrenched identities raise the fear of exclusion, and the loop unwinds. The figure does not supply the sign. It says the sign decides whether the loop closes, and that a chain (a one-off constitution with no re-entry) cannot close by construction.

This is a live, named strain, not a result. The opened consociationalism record notes both readings: critics argue consociational arrangements "institutionalising and deepening existing divisions" and "temporarily freezing conflicts but not resolving them", while the Cambridge 2022 book description says "while there are many successful examples... other instances show that such arrangements offer no guarantee against violent conflict". Those are competing sign readings from one broad evidence pool, not two witnesses.

- **Falsifier:** a per-slot measurement showing that the sign of the sharing-to-fear-to-compliance loop is zero (no re-entry) or is the same across all cases. Either would kill the cycle reading as an explanation of differing durability.

### Q5 - Catalyst and functional group in the habitat

- Fig 12 :119: "catalyst. A part that survives its own use. Spent and regenerating parts behave differently across uses, and nothing records which a term is."
- Fig 12 :123: "functional group. A substructure whose presence fixes the outcome. The group says what kind; the qualifier says which member."

An external monitoring mission that is still reading the ground after the first election is a catalyst; a mission that withdraws on a fixed schedule is a spent part. An ingredient list records both as "monitoring". The Dayton record opened in `04` describes multiple international bodies with different lifetimes (IFOR, the Office of the High Representative, OSCE), which is the kind of detail an atom-only coding loses.

- **Falsifier:** show that a part's surviving its own use makes no difference to per-group residual after its first reading, measured independently of the mission's own reports.

## CONCLUSION

The question is a union of conditions with an unassignable overlap, and the chain most often breaks between having terms for institutional ingredients and having a non-updating measure of progress. Two settlements with the same atoms bonded by sequence versus by threshold are isomers under Fig 12 :117; that is a checkable structural claim with a stated falsifier, and it explains why atom-level coding produces mixed findings. A durable settlement is a cycle whose sign the figures leave open; that is a named strain, not a conclusion. External monitors divide into catalysts and spent parts, and ingredient lists cannot tell them apart.

This framing rescues what is defensible in the ordinary question without the standing list: the order, the union, the isomer test and the catalyst test need no new primitive and no general ranking of provisions. Which atoms occupy the slots remains a `gap` and a provenanced binding, not a figure-derived name.
