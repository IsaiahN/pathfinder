**Subject:** Check one shape claim about institutional transfer before you decide about the framework

> Hello,
>
> I have one specific output I would like you to check against what you already know. I am not asking you to read the theory first, and I am not asking you to agree with it. If the output does not survive, there is nothing else to look at.
>
> **The output (`specified`, not `grounded`):** similar institutional designs do not produce the same outcome because the written design is a list of ingredients, not the arrangement. The framework's bond principle puts it plainly: *"A set of ingredients is not one. The bond is the rest of the information, and it is the half nothing was recording."* Two societies with the same statute, the same court structure or the same electoral rule can therefore be isomers, *"same atoms, different bond, different substance"*, where the difference is carried by enforcement sequence, informal norms and the actors each design actually recruits.
>
> The shape claim has two conditions that I keep separate rather than average:
>
> 1. **C1, channel contact.** The rule must remain connected to a ground that can refuse it. A written rule is a convention, and *"a declared meaning is not a grounded one."* When the channel saturates or drops readings, the ground still pays, but nobody is reading it.
> 2. **C2, valence and weighting fit.** The same actor has different valence in a different habitat, and two systems with the same formal architecture diverge because their histories and their weightings diverge. The framework's own notation says two frames *"with identical architecture diverge because their histories and their w diverge."*
>
> **What it predicts here:** a design transplant is not a plug-in. On import you cannot subtract the receiving habitat, and you cannot reproduce every condition the original goal required. What you failed to reproduce is invisible until the goal fails; what you unintentionally introduced is invisible until it acts. So the same formal design should produce a persistent, per-society residual, with sign reversals in some habitats, not a single average effect with implementation noise around it.
>
> **What I am not claiming:** that any particular design is good or bad, that culture or geography alone explains the divergence, that an imported model can be ranked by how prestigious its source is, or that this settles a country case. The framework returns a shape, not a name, and the specific instrument that would tell a C1 failure from a C2 failure is something I do not have. I am recording that as a gap, not filling it with a variable.
>
> **What would refute the shape claim. I wrote these down before sending this:**
> 1. outcomes stay the same across a documented habitat substitution that changes contact, actors and valence;
> 2. two societies matched on history, inherited prior, habitat contact and enforcement fidelity still show a persistent residual with no unenumerated actor left;
> 3. the formal atoms of the written design fully predict outcomes with no contribution from bonds or informal arrangements;
> 4. a cross-society average preserves the live per-society signal and predicts a single society out of sample;
> 5. a rule with a demonstrably saturated or dropped channel continues to constrain outcomes against a paying ground.
>
> If any one holds, I will retract the shape. A one-line "this fails because..." is more useful to me than agreement. If you would rather not engage, no reply is needed.
>
> **If it survives, and only then:** the sources are below. The figures are the authority, not my summary of them.
> - Verbatim figures with line numbers (local extract): `FRAMEWORK/reference/03-FIGURES_TEXT.md`
> - Canonical figures README: `FRAMEWORK/reference/FIGURES_README_canonical.md`
> - Public figures repository (branch `general-agent`): https://github.com/IsaiahN/tether/tree/general-agent/figures
>
> **If you want to use the machinery:** the formula is in the figures link. Run it on a problem you know well and can check against a ground. Write down what would refute your result *before* you run it, and state the mode of what you get. A reported failure is more useful to me than a reported success.
