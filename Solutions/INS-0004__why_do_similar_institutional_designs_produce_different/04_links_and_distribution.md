## 2. Wider literature, opened and used as secondary corroboration

These are grouped by field. Overlap is declared because several are tertiary summaries of the same canonical literature.

### Comparative institutional analysis and political economy (one witness, not six)

- **New institutional economics.** Wikipedia, *New institutional economics*, fetched 2026-11-23. Quote: *"Institutions are the 'rules of the game', both the formal legal rules and the informal social norms that govern individual behavior and structure social interactions."* Predicts that the written design is only part of the object; corroborates Fig 12 :5 and F1. Overlap: high with the institutional-theory and historical-institutionalism pages below, all drawing on North.
- **Institutional theory.** Wikipedia, *Institutional theory*, fetched 2026-11-23. Quote: *"rather than necessarily optimizing their decisions, practices, and structures, organizations look to their peers for cues to appropriate behavior."* Also: *"multinational corporations (MNCs) operating in different countries with varying institutional environments will face diverse pressures."* Predicts isomorphism under uncertainty and divergent local pressure on the same form; corroborates F2 and F7. Same pool as the previous item.
- **Why Nations Fail.** Wikipedia, *Why Nations Fail*, fetched 2026-11-23. Quote: *"They identify countries that are similar in many of the above-mentioned factors, but because of different political and institutional choices become more or less prosperous."* The North/South Korea and Nogales comparisons are the question's own natural experiments. Predicts per-slot divergence under similar initial conditions; corroborates F2 and F5. Note the page also records the reverse-causality objection (Subramanian) and the oversimplification objection (Fukuyama); both are refused as verdicts here because they are arguments about measurement, not ground readings.
- **Historical institutionalism.** Wikipedia, *Historical institutionalism*, fetched 2026-11-23. Quote: *"many outcomes are possible, small events and flukes can have large consequences, actions are hard to reverse once they take place."* Also: *"the formal rules and administrative structures of institutions were not accurately describing the behavior of actors and policy outcomes."* Predicts path dependence and the rule/practice gap; corroborates F1, F3 and F8.
- **Path dependence.** Wikipedia, *Path dependence*, fetched 2026-11-23. Quote: *"past events or decisions constraining or defining later events or decisions."* Also: *"the future development of an economic system is affected by the path it has traced out in the past."* Predicts that the same design entered at a different time lands on a different trajectory; corroborates F3. Strong claim from the same pool as historical institutionalism.
- **State capacity.** Wikipedia, *State capacity*, fetched 2026-11-23. Quote: *"State capacity is the ability of a government to accomplish policy goals, either generally or in reference to specific aims."* The page distinguishes capacity from political control. Predicts that a formally identical agency has different effect depending on the tax and information apparatus around it; corroborates F4. Same broad pool.

## 3. Distribution: offer, do not push

Figure-grounded reasons:

- **Fig 4 :22** - "Only methods cross up. Only head starts come down." Sending the full architecture before anyone has described a residual sends a recording, not a generator. Lead with the shape claim and the refuter; offer the links after.
- **Fig 10 :42** - "A verifier that can reconstruct the claim cannot verify it." A recipient who has absorbed the framework before giving a verdict has joined the frame being checked.
- **Fig 9 :27-28** - "describe it before you go looking, or any frame you pick will seem to fit." Ask what they would expect to see if the shape were true before giving them any theory.
- **Fig 2 :40** - "disqualifying for a verdict: agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool." Selecting recipients by receptiveness produces a convention, not corroboration.
- **Fig 7 :35** - "A room's agreement is variation, not a verdict." A conference panel is a selection site, not a validation site.

**Recommended order in a reply:** (1) the two-condition shape and the five refuters; (2) ask what they would count as a failure and record the answer; (3) only then the framework links, with the note that the figures, not the summary, are the authority.

## 4. The two-stage offer

1. **Stage 1.** Send the residual, the shape claim and the pre-registered refuter. Ask for a failure, not an endorsement. Record the verdict before any theory is shown. This is Fig 10 :52, "Bring a characterised residual, never the source," and Fig 2 :45, "Frames range against the ground. They do not negotiate with it."
2. **Stage 2, opt-in only.** If they are curious, offer the three framework links. Their later agreement is never pooled as corroboration, because they now hold the interiors (Fig 2 :40).
3. **Stage 3.** Offer the formula as a method to reuse. This is permitted because a generator is not an answer (Fig 4 :22). Each user must state their own ground and write their own refuter before running it, because a frame cannot check the direction of its own question.

**Hard precondition:** stages 1 to 3 only work if stage 1 survives. This folder is a simulated derivation checked for internal consistency, not a measurement on a real country pair. The stage-1 verdict is the first real ground contact on offer.
