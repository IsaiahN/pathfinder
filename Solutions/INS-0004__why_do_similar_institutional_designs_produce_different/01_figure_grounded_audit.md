## 0. Residual, in effect terms

The question is not "are institutions important" and not "which design is best." It is this: a written design D is installed as action a in society S, and the residual R_S = |Gamma_S(b_S,a) - o'_S| differs persistently across S even though the formal text of D is the same.

- **Arity.** At least three-place: design, society-slot, action, plus the observation. Two societies reading the same written rule form a **seam**, not two independent questions (Fig 10 :11-18, "two frames, one field, no shared reading").
- **Symmetry.** The written form is exchangeable between societies; the outcome is not. Symmetry is broken by habitat, belief weighting, anchor and channel. This is the isomer condition: same atoms, different bond, different substance (Fig 12 :117).
- **Scale.** Step (one official applying a rule), cycle (one agency's enforcement loop), level (one society), population (cross-society comparison), lineage (the history that made the belief). The membrane between these scales is lossy (Fig 4 :27-30).
- **The sought-for shape, bound before the run** (Formula, "Direction"). Write it down as an ordinary term: *a union of two conditions, C1 = channel contact maintained to a paying ground, C2 = actor valence and belief weighting fit the local habitat, with an instrument that discriminates a C1 failure from a C2 failure.* Until it is bound it predicts nothing and no check can see it. Committing to it here makes it refusable.

**Option A** (design quality is a portable property of the text) predicts low between-society variance once implementation is controlled. **Option B** (the effect is a property of design-and-habitat) predicts persistent per-slot variance, sign reversals of the same rule, and invisible imports when the design is transplanted. The derivation below decides between them from the figures.

### 1c. Partition or union

**Union, not partition.** D1 and D2 overlap in the enactment seam: a written rule and a local reading of it are two frames in one field with no shared reading until a convention is installed (Fig 10 :11-18). D2 and D3 overlap in the inherited-habitat compound: history is both an actor in the habitat and the prior that weights the belief. D4 runs across all domains because the channel is the only route from a convention to a grounded outcome. By Fig 9 :57, *"What remains is smaller and it is also categorically different: a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either."* So C1 and C2 are kept separate and never averaged. Fig 9 :54-55: *"A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones."*

### 1d. Order by dependency (Fig 3)

1. perception of local conditions,
2. vocabulary for what the design affords *here*,
3. objective: what counts as progress in this slot,
4. planning: implementation,
5. learn and carry: revision after the ground answers.

The chain usually breaks at link 2 or 3, which have neither obvious failures nor instruments (Fig 3 :24-27). Fig 3 :27: *"A link with no instrument is not a link that works. It is a link nobody has looked at."* Therefore: describe the residual before naming a frame (Fig 9 :27-28), refuse before mint (Formula step 2), enumerate the habitat before importing a design (Fig 11 :43-44), and settle before composing over the result (Formula step 5).

## 3. Findings F1 to F10

### F1 - The written design is a convention, not a ground (D1)

Fig 10 :18: *"a declared meaning is not a grounded one"*. Fig 10 :19: *"install what can be violated"*. Fig 12 :5: *"A set of ingredients is not one. The bond is the rest of the information, and it is the half nothing was recording."*

A design document lists ingredients: who may act, what counts as a vote, what a court can hear. The bond, how these are actually joined and enforced locally, is the half the document does not record. Fig 10 :47 warns what happens when the convention is promoted: *"a convention that decides an outcome has stopped being one."*

- **Mode:** `general` for the convention/ground distinction, `specified` for institutional designs.
- **Status:** triangulated (Fig 10 convention and Fig 12 notation, low overlap).
- **Refuter:** exhibit a written rule that constrains an outcome with no interpretation or enforcement channel and no ground contact. If it constrains, F1 fails.

### F2 - The effect belongs to design-and-habitat, not to the design alone (D2)

Fig 11 :39: *"So the answerable question is not what intelligence is, but what makes a system capable. And capability is a property of agent-and-habitat, never of the agent alone."* Fig 11 :40: *"A system cannot be made more capable by improving the agent in isolation. An improvement that does not change contact changes nothing, however much it improves."* Fig 4 :20: *"Only the actors, the currency and the anchor change."*

Replace "agent" with "institutional design." Rewriting the statute without changing contact, actors or anchor changes nothing that the ground pays for. This is Option B and it is the structural heart of the answer.

- **Mode:** `general` for the capability claim, `specified` for societies.
- **Status:** triangulated (Fig 11 and Fig 4, different figures and different quantities).
- **Refuter:** identical outcomes across a documented habitat substitution that changes contact, actors and valence, with no compensating change in the design.

### F3 - Identical architecture diverges because histories and weightings diverge (D3)

Symbols table :25-26: *"Two frames with identical architecture diverge because their histories and their w diverge."* Symbols table :24 defines w as *"How far the belief leans on private history versus inherited prior."*

This is the most direct figure statement of the question available. Two societies can hold the same formal design (identical architecture) and still diverge because b_S and w_S differ. History is not a decorative background variable; it is the weighting that maps the same action to a different prediction.

- **Mode:** `general` for the w mechanism, `specified` for the society-level application.
- **Status:** triangulated (Symbols :25-26 and Fig 4 :19-20, the kernel-invariance claim).
- **Refuter:** two societies matched on history, inherited prior, habitat contact and enforcement fidelity, with a persistent unexplained residual on both slots. If the residual persists with every enumerated condition equal, w as stated is insufficient.

### F4 - Enforcement is a channel, and channels decay (D4)

Fig 10 :24: *"the ground does not decay, the channel does"*. Fig 10 :26: *"stale percepts, dropped transients, saturated metrics, pooled readings"*. Fig 10 :47: *"a convention that decides an outcome has stopped being one"*. Fig 2 :29: *"An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks."*

A rule with a dead channel is a convention nobody can violate in practice. The failure mode is not that the ground moved; it is that reading stopped updating. Saturated metrics (a compliance score everyone scores well on) and pooled readings (country averages) are named channel failures, and both are endemic to comparative institutional measurement.

- **Mode:** `specified`.
- **Status:** triangulated (Fig 10 channel and Fig 2 anchor).
- **Refuter:** outcomes continue to track the written design after the channel is demonstrably saturated or dropped, with the ground still paying.

### F5 - Comparative agreement is not evidence (D5)

Fig 1 :19: *"The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated."* Fig 1 :22: *"Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average."* Fig 7 :35: *"A room’s agreement is variation, not a verdict. Selection between rooms is where the ground votes."* Fig 2 :35: *"Alignment is triangulation, not negotiation. A mirror cannot correct you."*

A panel of comparative-institution scholars who agree because they read the same canon is one evidence pool. Their agreement is efficient for a convention (what the field means by "inclusive institutions") and disqualifying for a verdict about the world.

- **Mode:** `general`.
- **Status:** triangulated (Fig 1, Fig 7, Fig 2; three distinct mechanisms).
- **Refuter:** a cross-society average that preserves the live slot signal and predicts an individual society's outcome out of sample.

### F6 - The residual is a union of two conditions, not one hard problem

Fig 9 :57: *"What remains is smaller and it is also categorically different: a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either."* Fig 9 :55: *"A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones."* Fig 3 :27 (above).

C1 (channel contact) and C2 (valence/belief fit) are independently necessary and independently falsifiable. A single lever that restores C1 in a society failing C2 will clear the intersection-looking pile and leave the compound standing.

- **Mode:** `general`.
- **Status:** triangulated (Fig 9 union rule and Fig 3 dependency order).
- **Refuter:** one intervention that restores both conditions with no categorically different residue left over.

### F7 - Same atoms, different bond, different substance (union / D1-D2)

Fig 12 :117: *"isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."* Fig 12 :95: *"Two entries with an identical ingredient set are not variants of one thing. They are different things whose notation cannot tell them apart, and the difference is carried entirely by the bond."* Fig 8 :50: *"Two frames can hold the same atom by convergence, reaching it separately, which is corroboration at maximum independence."*

Two societies with the same written rules (ingredients) but different enforcement sequences, informal norms and network bonds are isomers, not versions of one thing. Fig 8 :50 is the rare case where the same shape arrived twice from genuinely different evidence pools: the framework's author reports arriving at polycentric-governance conclusions from biology and game agents, and Ostrom arriving at them from committees, fisheries and forests. That is a documented convergent derivation, but it is **one case**, and it is reported by the framework's author, so it carries little independent weight for the verdict (see the retraction table).

- **Mode:** `specified` for the institutional application.
- **Status:** triangulated for the isomer rule (Fig 12 and Fig 8, low overlap); single-source for the Ostrom convergence case.
- **Refuter:** two societies with identical formal rules and demonstrably identical informal bonds and enforcement sequences that still diverge persistently. That would kill the arrangement claim.

### F8 - Transplant is substitution of a habitat, not removal of one (union / D2-D3)

Fig 11 :66: *"Isolation is not removal of the habitat. It is substitution of one habitat for another."* Fig 11 :68: *"what you failed to reproduce is invisible until the goal fails"*. Fig 11 :69: *"what you unintentionally introduced is invisible until it acts"*. Fig 6 :23: *"No frame escapes its own closure. The bound moves only on contact with a frame whose closure differs."*

An imported institutional model is not a plug-in. The importer cannot subtract the receiving habitat (contact is a fact about the world that does not consult them) and cannot reproduce every condition the original goal required. Both the omission and the unintended introduction are invisible until the goal fails or the new actor acts.

- **Mode:** `specified`.
- **Status:** triangulated (Fig 11 isolation law and Fig 6 import rule).
- **Refuter:** a transplant that reproduces the original goal in a new society with every required condition enumerated and no actor introduced that was not chosen.

### F9 - Which specific norm flips the outcome is a declared gap

No figure line names the discriminator that would tell a C1 failure from a C2 failure in a given pair of societies. Fig 9 :32: *"you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself"*. Fig 13 :134: *"Unreachable is a verdict here, not a silence"*. Fig 8 :39: *"So a search that finds nothing is never a proof of absence."*

What is owed is an instrument, not another variable name. The three questions of Formula step 7 stay distinct:
- **SEARCHED:** the comparative literature already holds candidate discriminators (trust, state capacity, legal origin, settler mortality, path dependence). Is one in here and unfound? No census in this folder establishes that it is, so SEARCHED remains open, and its searchers must be independent (Fig 6 :53-54).
- **INWARD:** sharpen our own measures, from instruments already returning something (repeat surveys, administrative microdata, court records).
- **OUTWARD:** find a frame whose closure predicts the residual's shape and import its instrument with a source gate and a shadow test.

- **Mode:** `general` for the limit, `specified` for this field.
- **Status:** `gap` for the specific discriminator; triangulated for the limit itself.
- **Refuter:** a grounded instrument that discriminates a C1 failure from a C2 failure in a documented pair of societies would close the gap.

### F10 - Prestige is not action in institutional reform

From *The Recursive Self-Improvement Trap* (essay, read 2026-11-23): *"Prestige can fund an action. It can give an action a budget to be tried. But it cannot settle an action. Only the ground can settle it."* Fig 7 :35 and Fig 2 :35 (above) give the figure-side rule.

A reform adopted because a famous designer or a famous country endorsed it has a budget to be tried, not a verdict. The check is whether the ground paid in the receiving slot.

- **Mode:** `general`.
- **Status:** triangulated (Fig 2 and Fig 7, with the essay as a framework-internal statement).
- **Refuter:** a reform that survives ground contact in a new society without any re-test of the binding between the imported label and the local mechanism.

## 4. Pre-registered refuter

Required before any outreach. Seat reasoning: a falsifier written before the run is a design instrument, not only an honesty device. Written here before the verdict in section 7.

The shape claim (Option B with C1 and C2 kept separate) is refuted if any of the following holds:

1. **Habitat invariance.** Outcomes stay the same across a documented habitat substitution that changes contact, actors and valence, with no compensating design change (breaks Fig 11 :40).
2. **Matched-society divergence.** Two societies matched on history, inherited prior, habitat contact and enforcement fidelity still show persistently different outcomes with no unenumerated actor left (breaks Symbols :25-26 and Fig 11 :43-44).
3. **Ingredients suffice.** The formal atoms of the written design fully predict outcomes with no contribution from bonds, informal arrangements or sequencing (breaks Fig 12 :5).
4. **Averaging preserves the signal.** A cross-society average is shown to preserve the live slot residual and to predict an individual slot out of sample (breaks Fig 1 :22).
5. **A dead channel still decides.** A rule with a saturated or dropped channel continues to constrain outcomes against a paying ground (breaks Fig 10 :24, :47).

If any fires, the shape is withdrawn. Until one is run, no part of this may be labelled `grounded`.

### Round-trip gap R_T

1. **Sent up (x):** the verbatim figure set, chiefly Fig 11 :39-40, Fig 10 :18-19, Fig 12 :5, Fig 9 :32, Symbols :25-26.
2. **Abstraction (T_A):** the shorthand carried in this folder: same design, different outcome because habitat and weighting differ; two conditions C1 and C2; the discriminator is a gap.

**R_T is nonzero.** Three things the shorthand holds do not survive the trip back down: the middle link vocabulary/objective (Fig 3 :27), the convention-not-ground distinction (Fig 10 :18-19), and the per-slot indexing (Fig 1 :19). Treating the formula as identical to the figures would set R_T to zero and would be the dead-reckoning error Fig 4 :30 names.

## 7. Updated verdict

The question is not answered by naming a factor. The figures return a shape and a split.

- Same formal design across societies is same atoms, not same substance. The bond is the unrecorded half (Fig 12 :5, :117).
- The effect is a property of design-and-habitat; improving the design alone does not change contact (Fig 11 :39-40).
- Identical architecture diverges because histories and weightings diverge (Symbols :25-26). This is the mechanism, stated in the figures' own notation.
- Enforcement is a decaying channel to an unmoving ground; a saturated metric is a lost channel, not a settled question (Fig 10 :24-26).
- The two conditions C1 and C2 form a union and must be kept separate (Fig 9 :57). Averaging across societies dissolves the only signal (Fig 1 :22).
- The specific instrument that discriminates them is a declared `gap`, not a hidden answer (Fig 9 :32, Fig 13 :134).

Verdict: **Option B, `specified`, ungrounded, candidate.** The shape survives every re-census performed here; the five pre-registered refuters are the route to reversing it. Strains after re-census: **none hidden**, one declared gap (F9). This is a simulated derivation and cannot settle the institutional question it reports on.
