# Are similar institutional designs isomers? Bonds, order and analogues

**Mode: `specified`.** This is a simulated derivation checked against the figures, not a ground reading (see `05_what_shape_means.md` section 6).

## RESIDUAL

Two societies hold the same written design, yet R_S = |Gamma_S(b_S,a) - o'_S| differs persistently. Two questions are owed: (1) are the two designs **isomers** under Fig 12's definition, and (2) in what **order** must the derivation and the transplant run so that the result is not an artifact of sequencing?

## DOMAIN CENSUS

Atoms held: the formal rules written in the design (position, boundary, choice, aggregation, information, payoff, scope after Ostrom's IAD rule types), plus actors, networks and informal norms already enumerated in the habitat. Bonds held: the seven, including sequence (→) and comparison (⋛), plus negation. No new primitive is available without import. Dependency order: perception, vocabulary, objective, planning, learn and carry (Fig 3).

## DERIVATION

### Q1 - Are two societies with the same design isomers?

- Fig 12 :117: *"isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."*
- Fig 12 :5: *"A set of ingredients is not one. The bond is the rest of the information, and it is the half nothing was recording."*
- Fig 12 :95-96: *"Two entries with an identical ingredient set are not variants of one thing. They are different things whose notation cannot tell them apart, and the difference is carried entirely by the bond."*

If the "same design" is the same written text, then the same atoms are present. What differs is the bond: which rule is sequenced before which, which informal norm attaches to which formal position, whether monitoring is accountable to the appropriators or to a central authority, how sanctions graduate. By Fig 12's definition these are **isomers**, and the coded indicator that records only the ingredient set cannot tell them apart.

- The claim is `specified`, not `grounded`: nobody has measured a matched pair of societies at the bond level in this folder.
- Triangulated (Fig 12 :5, :95, :117 are three statements of one definition, so this is really **single-source** at the figure level: the isomer rule comes from Fig 12 only). Corroborated by the IAD seven-rule-type account (world source, lower overlap) and by Scott's road-name example (world source, lower overlap).
- **Falsifier, written before the run:** exhibit two societies with identical formal rules *and* demonstrably identical informal bonds, sequencing and enforcement, that still show a persistent outcome difference. The isomer claim fails and a different explanation is owed.

### Q2 - Or are they a functional group instead?

- Fig 12 :124: *"functional group. A substructure whose presence fixes the outcome. The group says what kind; the qualifier says which member."*
- If the two designs share only a substructure (for example, "an independent court" or "a competitive election") and fill the remaining slots with different atoms, they are **not** isomers. They are members of a functional group.

This matters because the weaker claim is much easier to hold. A comparative study that matches on one institution and varies the rest is usually studying a functional group, not an isomer pair. Calling it an isomer overstates the design match.

- **Falsifier written before the run:** if the two designs share only one substructure, the isomer claim is withdrawn in favour of the functional-group claim. This is not a failure of the whole answer; it is a narrowing that keeps the verdict honest.

### Q3 - What is the dependency order inside a design?

- Fig 3 :20: *"The chain is ordered by dependency, not by importance."* Fig 3 :27: *"A link with no instrument is not a link that works. It is a link nobody has looked at."*
- Order: perception of local conditions → vocabulary for what the rule affords locally → objective (what counts as progress in this slot) → planning (implementation) → learn and carry (revision).
- Designs usually break at the middle link, having terms for what a situation offers and being able to say what would count as progress, because that link has neither obvious failures nor instruments (Fig 3 :24-27). A statute drafted without a local vocabulary for its own affordances is strong at the top and broken in the middle. Fig 3 :19: *"Optimising a link that already works cannot rescue one that does not."*
- **Falsifier:** show a design that fails at implementation and is repaired by adding more enforcement resources while perception, vocabulary and objective are unchanged and unmeasured. That would mean the break is not at the middle link, and Fig 3 was applied to the wrong quantity.

### Q4 - What is the order of a transplant?

- Fig 11 :43: *"The habitat is enumerated, never composed."* Fig 11 :44: *"List everything in contact with the residual, then what is in contact with those, and outward until the cascade stops mattering."*
- Fig 11 :66: *"Isolation is not removal of the habitat. It is substitution of one habitat for another."*
- Fig 11 :68: *"what you failed to reproduce is invisible until the goal fails"*; Fig 11 :69: *"what you unintentionally introduced is invisible until it acts"*.
- Fig 6 :23: *"No frame escapes its own closure. The bound moves only on contact with a frame whose closure differs."*

Correct order: (1) describe the residual in effect terms; (2) enumerate the receiving habitat and the cascade; (3) name what the source habitat supplied that is not present; (4) import with a source gate and a shadow test (Fig 8); (5) settle against the ground in the new slot; (6) only then compose over the result. Reversing 1 and 3 is the apophenia error Fig 9 :27-28 names: *"describe it before you go looking, or any frame you pick will seem to fit."*

- **Falsifier:** a transplant in which the receiving habitat was enumerated only after the source design was chosen, and the outcome was nevertheless predicted out of sample. That would mean the order was irrelevant here, and the prescription should be withdrawn for this case.

### Q5 - What kind of bond does the divergence turn on?

- Operators table :30-33 defines sequence (→): *"A then B; B needs A to have happened"*, with test *"swap them. Does it become a different thing? Yes."* Enforcement sequence is therefore an ordered bond: elect, then audit, versus audit, then elect, are different substances even with identical atoms.
- Operators table :47-51 defines comparison (⋛): *"a threshold relation between two quantities"*, with test *"is it about which is larger, rather than both being present? Yes."* A compliance threshold is a comparison, and a threshold that is never crossed is a saturated channel (Fig 10 :26), not a settled outcome.
- The divergence therefore turns on **ordered bonds** plus a **threshold bond**, not on the symmetric conjunction of "rules present." A coded design checklist records the conjunction and loses the order and the threshold, which is exactly Fig 12's *"the ingredients are recorded and the arrangement is not."*

- **Falsifier:** show that swapping two enforcement steps in a matched pair changes nothing measurable, or that the compliance threshold never binds. Then the ordered/threshold reading is wrong for that case.

## CONCLUSION

The strongest defensible form is: **same formal atoms under different ordered and threshold bonds are isomers, and the isomer difference is decisive in the outcome.** The weaker form, which is what most comparative designs actually support, is a **functional group**: a shared substructure with different atoms in the remaining slots. Both are checkable. Neither requires a new primitive, an import that moves the wall, or a name for the missing instrument.

The order of derivation is fixed by dependency: residual first, habitat enumerated, refuse before mint, settle before composing. The order of transplant is fixed by the same rule plus the import gates: enumerate, characterize, import, settle, then compose.

`metric design = single cause of outcome` remains an unproven binding. `two formally identical designs are isomers over a shared atom set, with a stated falsifier` is a checkable structural claim.
