## 1. A shape is a constraint, not a filler

The figures README states the limit: the framework returns *"what a missing thing would have to look like (a shape, not a name)."* Fig 9 :32: *"you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself."* Fig 13 :134: *"Unreachable is a verdict here, not a silence"*, and the figure adds that it cannot say which primitive is missing, because naming it would mean already holding it.

From *The Library of Babel*: *"You can cut your search short if the set of books returned is small and descriptive enough that it provides a general shape of the answer that satisfies your question."*

So a **shape** is the narrowed form of an answer you can state before you can measure it. It constrains; it does not fill. For this question the shape is:

> Same formal atoms under different bonds yield different substance; capability belongs to design-and-habitat; identical architecture diverges because histories and weightings diverge; the effect is a union of a maintained channel and a local valence fit; the discriminator between them is not yet held.

That is a shape. "Sweden succeeds because of trust" is a name, and it is also a wrong level of answer: it selects one observed outcome and turns it into a cause.

## 2. How a shape is obtained

- *Library of Babel*: use the vocabulary you already have that is closest to the thing, look for recurring new terms that overlap or correlate with it, recurse. *"With each new term gained, you are able to narrow the space by definition and deepen your vocabulary."*
- Fig 6 :30: *"The description travels. Fails by being sharpened into terms only we can read. Cast it in effect terms."* Fig 9 :30-31: *"your words are borrowed too, so describe the effect rather than the cause."*
- *Decoding Tacit Knowledge*: *"When contact isn't possible, the best you can send is a plain description of what you'd expect to see, because anyone can check that against the world."* This is why the outreach email asks what a failure would look like rather than asking for agreement.
- *Library of Babel* on stopping: *"you have already composed the answer through the process of locating it."* The recursion narrows until the sought-for shape and the composed answer become indiscernible, and *"indiscernibility is not identity"* (*How Intelligence Actually Works*), so the lineage must still be kept.

For this question the proximity vocabulary was: institution, rule, enforcement, informal norm, trust, valence, weighting, habitat, channel. The recurring new terms that overlapped were *bond*, *isomer*, *convention*, *seam* and *substitution*. The figure text was grepped only after those effects were described, per Fig 9 :27-28.

## 3. What licenses a name

Only two things, both outside the shape itself:

1. **The ground settles it.** Formula step 5: *"the ground settles it. Verified only by what pays. A gate is not the ground."* Here that means an actual measurement in an actual society, not a cross-country index with a nice confidence interval.
2. **A provenanced import from a frame whose closure differs** (Fig 6 :23, Fig 8 :50-52), with a source gate and a shadow test. The Ostrom convergence is the nearest available case: Fig 8 :50 says *"Two frames can hold the same atom by convergence, reaching it separately, which is corroboration at maximum independence."* But the record of who derived what is what makes it count, and here that record is one author's account of one case.

*They're Scanning Books When They Should Be Studying Nature* sharpens the limit: *"A minimum viable set of six terms is a human's careful guess at reality's compression axes; another sensorium's minimum set need not be those six."* And: *"Declaring the primitives does not make them neutral. It makes the choice visible."* So even the vocabulary used to describe institutions is a visible choice, not nature's own list.

## 4. Why "the same design works differently" is a source problem, not a list problem

*The Hard Problem is About Source* gives the mechanism. Metamerism: an instrument reads green and *"cannot comment on the source."* One green light, or yellow plus blue, or RGB, or CMYK, or a scheme not yet discovered, all give the same reading. *"If several paths create the same behavior, then behavior is a projection with lossy information."* And: *"An instrument that can't distinguish these cases demonstrates its own inadequacy."* It was the **spectrometer**, not an argument, that resolved it.

The institutional analogue is exact. Two societies can both score "has an independent judiciary" or "has a written constitution" on the same coded indicator. The indicator reads the same. It cannot comment on the source: different enforcement bonds, different histories, different weightings, different channels. Naming "trust" from that reading is the metamerism error. What is owed is a better instrument, not another variable name. This is why F9 in `01_figure_grounded_audit.md` is declared as a `gap` rather than filled.

*How Intelligence Actually Works* adds a second caution: *"very little about a composed term tells you, from looking at it alone, which route produced it."* Two institutions that look identical in a dataset may have arrived by convergence or by adoption, and only the record separates them (Fig 8 :50-52).

## 5. Q1 - Does "shape, not a name" forbid naming trust, culture or state capacity?

**No, with a condition.** The figures README's phrase governs *what a missing thing* would look like. The subject is a primitive not held in Gamma. Trust, informal norms, networks, enforcement and weighting are not missing primitives here; they are already inside the institutional vocabulary, just as Gradient and Spectrum were already among the six in the QMGR exemplar. Naming one as *the* cause is a **binding from the comparative-institution frame** onto a held item, not the invention of a seventh thing. What remains forbidden is claiming the binding moves the wall (Fig 6 :22: *"IMPORT adds an atom"* - this adds none) and treating the binding as settled by the framework rather than by a ground.

**Correction to my own §4.** I first wrote that naming trust from a survey reading is forbidden by "shape, not a name." That overstates the rule. The error is **metamerism**: naming the source of a reading without an instrument that discriminates among sources. The reason is a missing discriminator, not a ban on naming held items. Both claims are recorded so neither is quietly lost.

## 6. Two consequences for outreach

**Prestige is not action.** From *The Recursive Self-Improvement Trap*: *"Prestige must never be allowed to validate an action directly... Someone let prestige stand in for proof... Prestige can fund an action. It can give an action a budget to be tried. But it cannot settle an action. Only the ground can settle it. The Ground Maintainer must be blind to prestige."* A reform list ranked by famous adopters or famous designers is the named collapse, not corroboration. The legitimate ask is the opposite: give the shape a budget to be tried, let the ground settle it, and declare the interest that cannot be removed.

**My own audit is a simulation, not a ground reading.** Same essay: *"LLMs can quickly simulate an approximate answer/output with the formula"*, but *"the truly testable output would need to be derived from the real architecture or working the formula yourself."* And *Arrival of the Fittest* says the outputs *"could never be verified if the solutions presented were not contrived to fit the formula."* So `01` is a simulated derivation checked for internal consistency against the figures. It is **not** a test of the institutional question and must be labelled as such. It cannot settle anything it reports on.

## 7. Three further ideas from the essays that bear on this question

1. **The room and the commons** (*How a Society of Agents Would Work*). *"The group shares methods, never recordings."* A comparative research community shares methods; it must not share a single conclusion as a recording and then replay it in each country. Fig 4 :25: *"A recording carried upward looks like knowledge and is a description of one occasion."*
2. **Per-kind rates** (same essay). *"A method that is brilliant in one setting must not be drowned by an average across all of them."* This is Fig 1 :22 restated at the level of research practice, and it is the methodological reason C1 and C2 must be reported separately.
3. **Convergent derivation and adopted import look identical in the contents** (Fig 8 :50-52, quoted in the same essay). *"Only the record of where each came from separates them."* For this question that means a reform copied from a successful country and a reform independently arrived at must be labelled differently even if they have identical text.

## 8. Answer to the canonical question

**What does "shape" mean here?** It means the set of constraints any answer must satisfy before it has been measured: same atoms under different bonds, agent-and-habitat capability, history-driven divergence of identical architecture, a union of channel and valence conditions, and an explicitly unheld discriminator. It does **not** mean a list of country-specific causes, a ranking of designs, or a name for the missing instrument. The name is licensed only by a ground reading or by a provenanced import with a shadow test, and the framework's own rule is that its own author's stake in the verdict must be declared, not asserted as truth (Fig 10 :59-60).
