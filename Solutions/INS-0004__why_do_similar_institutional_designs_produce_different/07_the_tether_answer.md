# The Tether's answer on INS-0004

**Mode: `specified` - not `grounded`.** This is a shape and a route, not a result. Simulated derivation checked against the figures (`05_what_shape_means.md` section 6), not a ground reading.

## 1. The residual it answers

The same written design D, installed as the same action a in different society-slots S, yields persistently different observations o'_S. R_S = |Gamma_S(b_S,a) - o'_S| is indexed per slot, never averaged (Fig 1 :19, Fig 1 :22). The comparison across societies is a **union compound**, not one hard problem: two conditions are bound together, and a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either (Fig 9 :57). It has its own loop and its own ground.

## 2. The shape

1. **A written design is a convention, not a ground.** Fig 10 :18: *"a declared meaning is not a grounded one"*; Fig 10 :19: *"install what can be violated."* It becomes a constraint only when it is installed so a ground can refuse it. Fig 12 :5: *"A set of ingredients is not one. The bond is the rest of the information, and it is the half nothing was recording."*

2. **Same atoms, different bond, different substance.** Two societies with the same formal rules are isomers under Fig 12 :117: *"Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."* The bond is the enforcement sequence, the informal norm, the accountability relation, the threshold. A coded checklist records the ingredients and loses the arrangement.

3. **The effect belongs to design-and-habitat.** Fig 11 :39: *"capability is a property of agent-and-habitat, never of the agent alone."* Fig 11 :40: *"An improvement that does not change contact changes nothing, however much it improves."* Rewriting the statute without changing contact, actors or anchor changes nothing the ground pays for.

4. **Identical architecture diverges because histories and weightings diverge.** Symbols table :25-26: *"Two frames with identical architecture diverge because their histories and their w diverge."* This is the mechanism in the figures' own notation. History is the weighting that maps the same action to a different prediction, not a decorative background variable.

5. **The union is a channel condition and a valence condition, kept separate.** C1: the rule must stay connected to a ground that can refuse it; *"the ground does not decay, the channel does"* (Fig 10 :24), and a saturated metric is a lost channel. C2: the same actor has different valence in a different habitat (Fig 11 :49, :51), so the design's effect changes sign when the resident and the residual change. These are independently necessary and independently falsifiable, and they are never averaged into an "institutional quality" score.

6. **A transplant is substitution, not replication.** Fig 11 :66: *"Isolation is not removal of the habitat. It is substitution of one habitat for another."* What the original habitat supplied is invisible until the goal fails; what the new habitat introduces is invisible until it acts (Fig 11 :68-69).

7. **The discriminator is a declared gap.** No figure line names the instrument that would tell a C1 failure from a C2 failure in a given pair of societies. Fig 9 :32: *"you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself."* Fig 13 :134: *"Unreachable is a verdict here, not a silence."* The answer stops here with the gap recorded, not filled with a variable name.

## 3. What it explicitly does not say

- Not that any particular design is good or bad. The framework returns a shape, not a design ranking.
- Not that culture, geography, disease or elite power alone explains the divergence. Each is absorbed into the habitat or the weighting, and a solo cause with no separate anchor is not a domain.
- Not that the same written rule is the same institution. It is the same ingredient set, and the difference is in the bond.
- Not that agreement among comparative researchers settles anything. *"Alignment is triangulation, not negotiation. A mirror cannot correct you."* (Fig 2 :35)
- Not that a famous country's adoption validates a reform. Prestige can fund an action but cannot settle it.
- Not `grounded`. No measurement on a real society pair settles `left(R,phi)` in this folder.

## 4. What would settle it - the ground

A source-discriminating instrument, not another argument. From *The Hard Problem is About Source*: an instrument that reads green *"cannot comment on the source"*; the spectrometer resolved it by distinguishing sources. Here the corresponding questions are:

- **C1 test:** does an enforcement channel still return fresh readings after a demonstrable shock to the rule's formal text? An instrument that measures contact, not just compliance scores, would show whether the channel is live or saturated (Fig 10 :26).
- **C2 test:** does the same formal role recruit actors of different valence when the residual changes? A matched-pair design that records attendance, turnover and who actually fills the role would show the sign reversal (Fig 11 :49-51).
- **Isomer test:** are two societies' formal atoms identical while their ordered bonds (sequence of enforcement, threshold of sanction, accountability of monitor) differ? If the bonds match and outcomes still differ, the isomer claim fails.
- **Standing falsifiers from `01`:** habitat invariance; matched-society divergence with no actor left unenumerated; ingredients alone predicting outcomes; an average preserving the live slot signal; a dead channel still deciding.

## 5. The honest stop

**Unreached, not unreachable.** The institutional vocabulary is a visible guess at the compression axes of this domain, not nature's own list. Another research tradition's minimum set need not be these six rule types. The answer stops at the limit of the available instruments and records the lineage so the stop is checkable. Fig 8 :39: *"So a search that finds nothing is never a proof of absence."* Indiscernibility is not identity.

## 6. One-paragraph version for outreach

> The Tether does not rank institutional designs and does not name a single cause of cross-society divergence. It says that a written design is a list of ingredients whose bonds are the unrecorded half, so two societies with the same statute can be isomers: same atoms, different arrangement, different substance. The effect is a property of design-and-habitat, not of the design alone, and two systems with identical architecture diverge because their histories and their weightings diverge. The divergence is a union of two conditions that must be kept separate: a channel that stays connected to a ground that can refuse the rule, and a valence fit between the design and the actors it actually recruits. An imported model substitutes one habitat for another, inheriting actors nobody chose. What the framework does not have is an instrument that discriminates a channel failure from a valence failure in a given pair of societies. It returns that gap, the shape any answer must have, and five falsifiers written in advance. The comparison stays open until the ground settles it.
