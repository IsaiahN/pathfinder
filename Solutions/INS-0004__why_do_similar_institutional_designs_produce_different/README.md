## The question restated as an effect

What is being decided is not the *quality* of an institutional design but the *fate of a residual when the same design is installed in different slots*. In effect terms: a society S holds a library Gamma_S and a belief b_S (how far that belief leans on private history versus an inherited prior, Symbols table :20-26). The design D is an action a applied in that slot. The realized outcome is o'_S. The residual is R_S = |Gamma_S(b_S,a) - o'_S|, and it is indexed by society-slot, never averaged across societies (Fig 1 :19, Fig 1 :22). "Similar designs" means the written rules are the same atoms; "different outcomes" means R_S differs by slot.

- Option A, "the design is a portable object whose quality transfers": leads to the prediction that outcomes should track the design's formal content and that divergence is a failure of implementation.
- Option B, "the design is a convention whose effect is a property of the design and its habitat together": leads to the prediction that divergence is structural, that the effect changes sign when the habitat changes, and that a transplant inherits actors nobody chose (Fig 11 :39-40, Fig 11 :66-68).

What an answer would look like, written down and bound as an ordinary term before the run (Formula, "Direction"): a *shape* naming two conditions that must be discriminated, C1 (a maintained channel to a paying ground) and C2 (valence and belief fit with the local habitat), plus an instrument that can tell a failure of C1 from a failure of C2. Until that shape is bound it predicts nothing, and no check in the loop can see it.

The verdict recorded in `09_watson_answer.md` is Option B in `specified` mode, kept per domain and never averaged. C1 and C2 are a union, not two estimates of one quantity.

## What is deliberately not here

- No personal emails of named individuals.
- No ranking of people by how likely they are to agree.
- No bulk-send plan.
- No claim that this derivation is `grounded`, no claim that it settles the institutional question, and no claim that the framework is the only true account of institutions.

Three figure-grounded reasons, plus one practical one:

1. A room is not an anchor. *"A room’s agreement is variation, not a verdict. Selection between rooms is where the ground votes."* (Fig 7 :35) and *"Alignment is triangulation, not negotiation. A mirror cannot correct you."* (Fig 2 :35). A list selected for receptiveness manufactures the correlation that disqualifies a verdict.
2. Correlation has valence. *"disqualifying for a verdict: agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool"* (Fig 2 :40). Comparative researchers reading the same canonical literature are one witness wearing many names.
3. A verifier must be outside the interior. *"A verifier that can reconstruct the claim cannot verify it."* (Fig 10 :42). The first contact therefore carries the residual and its refuter, not the framework.
4. Legally, unsolicited bulk email to scraped addresses breaches GDPR and CAN-SPAM style rules in many jurisdictions and gets the sending domain blacklisted. Public opt-in routes avoid that failure as well as the structural one.
