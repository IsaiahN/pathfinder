# Isomeric routes and dependency order

**Mode: `general` on the structure, `specified` on the falsifier. Not `grounded`.** Simulated derivation checked against the figures; see `05_what_shape_means.md` §6.

## RESIDUAL

The endpoint artifact is the same, but historians may propose different routes to it. Two questions are owed: (1) how can distinct routes that produce the same endpoint be discriminated, and (2) what is the dependency order across observation, theory, institution and policy?

## DOMAIN CENSUS

Atoms held: the six terms of Fig 13 (Space, Time, Energy states; Spectrum, Gradient, Network structures) are not the atoms of a historical trace. Here the atoms are per-item readings, terms, bonds, provenance records, refusals, selection events, rulings and habitat actors. Bonds held: the seven, plus negation. No new primitive is needed to express a route; `→` (sequence), `⇒` (production), `−` (subtraction), `⋛` (comparison) and `¬` (negation) suffice.

## DERIVATION

### Q1: Can different routes arrive at the same endpoint?

- Fig 12 :117: **"isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."**
- Fig 13 :121: **"Different routes can arrive at the same substance."**
- Fig 13 :123-124: *"Two chains built from different links may compose functionally identical terms, and the converse holds too: the same ingredients under a different bond are a different substance."*
- Therefore two histories with the same documents but different bonds (one where the observation produced the theory, one where the theory selected the observation) are isomers. They are not the same history with a different emphasis. Inspecting the endpoint cannot distinguish them, which is the isomer claim's consequence for F1 in `01`.

### Q2: What distinguishes isomeric routes?

- Fig 12 :5: **"A set of ingredients is not one. The bond is the rest of the information, and it is the half nothing was recording."**
- Fig 12 :111: **"A vocabulary without bonds can name a state and cannot name a route to one."**
- The record of which bond held, with provenance and sequence, is the discriminator. This is the lineage. It is not an optional embellishment; without it the two isomers are literally the same description.

### Q3: What is the dependency order of the four stages?

- Fig 3 :20: **"The chain is ordered by dependency, not by importance."**
- Fig 3 :19: **"Optimising a link that already works cannot rescue one that does not."**
- Fig 3 :27: **"A link with no instrument is not a link that works. It is a link nobody has looked at."**
- Order: a reading must be settled before a term can compose over it (Formula step 5: *"the ground settles it. Verified only by what pays."*); a term must be admitted before an institution can select it; an institutional position must be selected before a policy can encode it. Where a link has no instrument (no archive, no record, no outcome measure), the trace stops there. A narrative written below that break is *"a reading of nothing"* (Fig 3 :22-23), not a weak reading.

### Q4: Is the chain a partition or a union?

- Fig 9 :54: **"And when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again."**
- Fig 9 :57: **"What remains is smaller and it is also categorically different: a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either."**
- If, in a particular case, the institution and the policy are the same event (a ruling that both establishes a doctrine and decides an outcome), the stages overlap. That is a union compound with its own loop. It must be unbundled before the trace continues. Treating it as one hard stage is the error Fig 9 names.

### Q5: Analogues that corroborate, and their limits

- **Chain of custody (law).** Wikipedia, *Chain of custody*: *"the chronological documentation or paper trail that records the sequence of custody, control, transfer, analysis, and disposition of materials."* A break in the chain makes evidence inadmissible. Corroborates the need for provenance, but a legal chain tracks objects, not the bonds between claims.
- **Provenance (archives).** SAA glossary: *"records of the same provenance should not be mixed with those of a different provenance."* Corroborates per-item separation, but says nothing about selection between rooms.
- **Black box opening (STS).** Wikipedia, *Actor-network theory*: *"depunctualization to the opening of a black box. When closed, the box is perceived simply as a box, although when it is opened all elements inside it become visible."* Corroborates why the endpoint must be opened. Its *"transportation without deformation... is not possible since any actor-network involves a vast number of translations"* corroborates the lossy membrane (F4 in `01`). Limit: ANT is a descriptive method, not a verdict procedure; by its own account *"explanation does not follow from description"* (Latour).

### Q6: Falsifier, written before the run

If either of the following holds, the isomer-and-order derivation fails:

1. Two proposed routes with the same endpoint and the same atoms are shown to be distinguishable by inspecting the endpoint alone, so the bond record is redundant.
2. The four stages are shown to be exchangeable (policy before theory before observation, with the same settlement status), so no dependency order applies.

Until one is run, the order claim remains `general` and the case-specific order remains `specified` and unmeasured.

## CONCLUSION

Routes to the same endpoint are isomers under Fig 12 :117: same atoms, different bond, different substance, with the difference invisible in the artifact. The discriminator is the bonded lineage. The four stages are ordered by dependency (Fig 3 :20), and a break anywhere means the trace stops there; a reading below the break is a reading of nothing. Where stages overlap they form a union compound and must be unbundled (Fig 9 :54). Three outside analogues corroborate the structure but none supplies the verdict.
