# Solution HIS-0092: figure-grounded package

**Question:** What evidence is needed to trace how a claim moved from observation to theory, institution, and public policy?

**Source:** `Questions/history of science and knowledge.txt` line 113 (question 92, section 10, Historical explanation and contemporary relevance).

**Mode:** `general` on the structural shape of the evidence, `specified` on the per-domain evidence classes. Nothing here is `grounded`: no particular claim's trajectory was traced and no measurement was taken.

**Status:** done.

**Registry status:** pending.

## What is being decided, in effect terms

An endpoint artifact (a theory, an institutional doctrine, a public policy) is in hand. We want the route by which a claim reached it. The residual is the gap between what the artifact can tell us and the trajectory we want to reconstruct. Option A: treat the endpoint and its surrounding documents as sufficient evidence. Option B: require lineage evidence, namely the atoms, bonds, provenance, ground settlements, refusals, selection events, and round-trip gaps at each crossing. A leads to a narrative that cannot discriminate among routes; B leads to a checkable claim about what a historian must collect. The verdict below is B, as a shape, not as a named archive.

## What is deliberately not here

- No personal emails of named individuals.
- No ranking of people by how likely they are to accept the answer.
- No bulk-send plan.

The reasons are figure-grounded. Fig 2 :35: *"Alignment is triangulation, not negotiation. A mirror cannot correct you."* Fig 2 :40: *"agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool."* Fig 10 :52: *"Bring a characterised residual, never the source."* Asking for agreement before a check manufactures the correlation that would make the check worthless. A failure is more useful than an endorsement.

## Honest standing of this package

This is a simulated derivation checked for internal consistency against the figures, not a ground reading. It cannot settle the historical question it reports on. What it can do is return the shape of the evidence a genuine trace would need, and state what would refute that shape.
