# Expert verification packet: what evidence traces a claim from observation to policy

**What this is:** a claim in a form a historian or STS scholar can check. Mode, derivation and refuters are stated before the conclusion, so the reader knows what to look for and what would settle it.

**Mode: `general` on the shape, `specified` on the per-domain evidence classes. Not `grounded`.** No particular claim's trajectory is asserted.

**Status: simulated derivation checked for internal consistency against the figures, not a ground reading.** It cannot settle the question it reports on.

## 1. The claim, in one paragraph

An endpoint artifact (theory, institutional position, public policy) does not carry the route that produced it, so tracing a claim requires lineage evidence rather than the endpoint and its surrounding documents alone. The evidence must be per-item and unaggregated, bonded and ordered, provenanced at each import, inclusive of refusals, based on selection between independent rooms rather than endorsement, carrying ruling provenance and the convention-to-outcome crossing, enumerating the habitat to the order where the effect lands, and accompanied by a measured loss at each membrane crossing. This is a shape of evidence, not a named archive, and no actual historical case is reconstructed here.

## 2. Three checkable sub-claims

### C1: framework-internal; checkable by reading Figure 13 and Figure 12

- **Statement:** the endpoint cannot disclose its route; the record is the evidence.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 13 :128: *"Nothing about a composed term tells you, from looking at it, which route produced it or whether that route was available."* Fig 13 :129: *"It is recoverable from the lineage."* Fig 12 :111: *"A vocabulary without bonds can name a state and cannot name a route to one."*
- **Refuter:** exhibit an endpoint artifact from which the route is uniquely recoverable by inspection, so the lineage adds nothing; or show that an unordered set of documents determines the same trajectory as the bonded record in every case.

### C2: history-facing; checkable by a historian against their own cases

- **Statement:** per-item, refusals-inclusive, low-overlap lineage discriminates among routes where a consensus narrative does not. Institutional agreement is not evidence of the claim's truth; what matters is selection between rooms.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 1 :19: *"The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated."* Fig 7 :35: *"A room's agreement is variation, not a verdict. Selection between rooms is where the ground votes."* Fig 2 :31: *"Independence, between two frames, is how little they draw on the same evidence."*
- **What to check:** take a case you know. Do the archives record which rooms differed and which residual each surfaced, or only that the claim was eventually accepted? Do the sources draw on independent evidence, or on one report? Are rejected versions and withdrawals in the record?
- **Refuter:** produce a correct trajectory from successes alone with every refusal omitted; or show that increasing the number of same-pool documents increases reliability; or exhibit an institution whose agreement with the ground is constitutive (Fig 2 :36: *"An anchor is legitimate when the question is constitutively about it"*).

### C3: policy-facing; checkable against a policy process

- **Statement:** a policy is a ruling plus a convention, and the trace needs both the ruling's provenance and the crossing point where the convention began to decide an outcome. A provenance record shows legitimacy, not correctness.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 10 :62: *"A ruling carries provenance like an atom."* Fig 10 :63: *"The residual it answered, the reasoning, the date, in a record the seats below can read."* Fig 10 :65: *"Provenance shows a ruling was reached legitimately. It never shows it was right: the ground does that, and a wrong ruling stays wrong however clean its record."*
- **What to check:** for a policy you know, is there a dated record of the residual, reasoning and decision, and can you identify when a neutral guideline became binding? Is the outcome record separate from the legitimacy record?
- **Refuter:** exhibit a policy whose correctness is established by its outcome record alone with no provenance of the decision; or show a lossless transform between institutional position and policy text, so no round-trip gap need be measured.

## 3. What is explicitly not claimed

- Not a named archive, and not a claim that any specific repository is required.
- Not a reconstructed history of any real claim.
- Not a theory of everything, and not a final account of historical method. Fig 7 :6 names *"the limit: no last room."*
- Not that agreement, consensus or prestige settles a route.
- Not that a complete record is attainable; silence is a described residual (Fig 8 :39: *"So a search that finds nothing is never a proof of absence."*).
- Not `grounded`. No archive, measurement or outcome was used to settle this claim.

## 5. What would settle it

A ground reading, not agreement. Concretely:

1. Attach a real case and run the five evidence layers. Do they discriminate among competing routes?
2. Measure R_T at one membrane crossing. Is the gap nonzero and enumerated?
3. Fire one of the refuters in §2. If one holds, narrow or withdraw.
4. Check that the witnesses are low-overlap. If not, they are one witness wearing many names.

## 6. What an expert can check in twenty minutes

1. Read Fig 13 :127-130 and Fig 12 :111: two short passages.
2. Check C2 against one case you know: does the archival record contain bonds, refusals and room selection, or only acceptance?
3. Check C3 against one policy: are the ruling's provenance and the convention crossing both recorded?
4. Name one instrument or record that would distinguish two isomeric routes in that case, or say why none exists.
5. Send the refutation. A failure is more useful than an endorsement.
