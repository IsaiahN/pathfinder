## DERIVATION

### Q1 - Partition or union?

- Fig 9 :55: *"A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones."*
- Fig 9 :54: *"And when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again."*
- The four conditions named in `01` (horizon boundary, exterior multipoles, ringdown spectrum, thermodynamic behaviour) overlap: the same Kerr metric fixes three of them at once. A single lever that clears one condition leaves the intersections standing.
- **Result:** the contact between theory and exterior is a **union compound** with its own loop. The individual instruments form a **partition** only when their evidence pools differ (Fig 2 :31). Two reductions of one data set are not two slots.
- **Triangulated** across Fig 9 and Fig 2. Low overlap: union/partition rule versus independence rule.

### Q2 - Is there an isomer claim here?

- Fig 12 :117: *"Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."*
- Two exterior configurations can share the same ingredients and differ only in arrangement. A ringdown measured at one detector and the same ringdown reconstructed from a network of three are not the same term: the bond to the instrument is part of the term, and the noise properties differ.
- More consequentially, two black-hole theories can be fitted to the same shadow diameter while differing in their ringdown spectrum. Same observable ingredient, different bond to the theory, different substance. Fig 12 :104: *"A settled molecule becomes an atom for whatever composes over it. So irreducible is a fact about a level and never about the world."* A theory settled at first order (shadow, orbits) becomes an atom for the second-order comparison (ringdown, absence of surface).
- **Result:** the useful isomer test is not "does each theory fit the shadow" but "do two theories built from the same settled exterior atoms differ in the arrangement of their boundary condition and dynamical spectrum". That is a checkable structural claim, `specified`, unmeasured here.
- **Single-source** for the figure definition (Fig 12); the physics-facing check is still owed.

### Q3 - What order do the probes run?

- Fig 3 :20: *"The chain is ordered by dependency, not by importance."*
- Fig 3 :27: *"A link with no instrument is not a link that works. It is a link nobody has looked at."*
- Dependency order: perception (an exterior reading already exists and fails to resolve) before vocabulary (terms for what it affords) before objective (what counts as progress on each slot) before planning (which probe to sharpen) before learn and carry (name what worked and carry it to the next system).
- A demand to perceive the interior sits below the break. Fig 3 :22: *"A step whose input never arrives cannot be diagnosed, only its predecessor can."* It is not a weak test of the interior; it is no test of it.
- **Result:** run SEARCHED, then INWARD, then OUTWARD. Effort spent above the break (more theory) cannot rescue a missing link below it (no reading).

### Q4 - What does the analogue laboratory add?

- Fig 8 :45: *"Two frames together reach what neither reaches alone."*
- Fig 8 :51: *"Only the record of where each came from separates them, and the surplus lives only where the closures differ."*
- The lab frame (BEC, superconducting qubit chain) has a sonic horizon and no gravity. It can show that the Hawking mechanism produces correlated pairs and entanglement, which is something the astrophysical frame cannot observe directly because the temperature is too low (Shi et al. 2023: *"it is quite difficult to directly observe the Hawking radiation of an astrophysical black hole"*).
- The surplus exists only where the closures differ. The lab frame does not contain an astrophysical interior, so it cannot return a term about that interior. It can return a mechanism term, with a source gate (who built the analogue and from which model) and a shadow test (what gap in our frame does it explain).
- Thebault (2019) asks *"in what sense can they provide evidence of novel phenomena such as black hole Hawking radiation?"* That question is the shadow test in someone else's words. If the lab result was generated from the same model it is claimed to support, the two frames share an evidence pool and the surplus is zero.
- **Result:** import the lab frame as mechanism corroboration, never as a reading of an astrophysical interior. **Triangulated** for the surplus (Fig 8 plus Fig 6 :23); **single-source and contested** for the evidential reach (Thebault).

## CONCLUSION

The exterior probes are a union compound whose slots partition only under an independence test. The dependency order is perception to vocabulary to objective to planning to carry, and a direct interior reading is below the break. Two theories can stand as isomers over the same settled exterior atoms, differing in boundary condition and spectrum, which is a checkable structural claim. The analogue laboratory frame contributes a mechanism term across a differing closure, with provenance and a shadow test, not an interior term.

This rescues what is defensible in the question's premise: researchers can test black-hole theories from outside. It removes what is not: that such a test sees the interior, or that agreement among frames substitutes for a ground.

## FALSIFIER, WRITTEN BEFORE THE RUN

If two probes declared independent are shown to share a calibration or data product that fully determines both readings, the partition fails and Q1 is withdrawn. If two theories fitted to the same exterior atoms cannot be distinguished by any arrangement of their boundary condition and spectrum, the isomer claim in Q2 fails. If a probe returns nothing at any resolution and is still used to constrain the interior, Q3 fails. If the analogue result is shown to be generated by the same model it is claimed to corroborate, the surplus in Q4 is zero and the import is retracted as corroboration.
