# Expert verification packet - the Tether on the main cause of financial crises

**What this is:** a claim in a form a financial crisis researcher can check. Mode, derivation and refuters are stated before the conclusion, so a reader knows what to look for and what would settle it.

**Mode: `specified` on the applied claims, `general` on the union/dependency structure. Not `grounded`.** No crisis dataset or counterfactual is claimed to have been read as an anchor in this derivation.

## 1. The claim, in one paragraph

The question "are financial crises caused mainly by excessive risk-taking, unstable credit growth, bad regulation, or structural features of finance?" is not one question with four answers. The four are overlapping conditions in a union. A lever against any one clears only the region where that condition alone binds and leaves the intersections standing. "Mainly" aggregates per-episode residuals into a single number, which is how a live signal disappears into an average. Each domain has its own anchor, so verdicts must be kept separate. Contact order runs from the directly visible risk-taker outward through credit and balance-sheet architecture; this is an ordering warning, not a claim that structure dominates. Regulation is a convention that can become an outcome variable, so its sign and stake must be declared. The figures return this shape; they do not name a dominant factor, which is owed to the ground.

## 2. Three checkable sub-claims

### C1 - framework-internal; checkable by reading Figures 9 and 1

- **Statement:** the four candidates form a union of overlapping conditions, and a single "main cause" statistic is an aggregation that discards per-episode information.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 9 :55 - *"A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones. A single lever clears the region where only one condition holds and leaves the intersections standing, because the second condition still binds."* Also Fig 9 :4-5 - *"Disagreements that do not shrink with effort, where each rule keeps working well on a different subset, are not one hard question but several well-formed ones. Split it rather than search."* And Fig 1 :22 - *"Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average."*
- **Refuter:** exhibit a partition of crisis episodes into four exclusive cells such that removing the dominant condition drives the residual to near zero on held-out episodes, and show that a single aggregate statistic loses no predictive power relative to per-case residuals.

### C2 - research-facing; checkable against the researcher's own results

- **Statement:** contact order runs from direct actors to further-out conditions; risk-taking is first order, credit flows and balance-sheet structure are second and third order, and this ordering is not a ranking of causal share.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 11 :52 - *"And contact cascades. Scoping the habitat to direct contact is how DDT reached the eagles: first-order enumeration, second-order effect, and the residual that mattered was three steps out."* Also Fig 11 :49-50 - *"An actor's valence is relative to the residual, not a property it carries."* and *"The same actor is a benefactor to one residual and a malfactor to another, so mutualism and parasitism are relations rather than kinds."*
- **What to check:** is the ordering faithful to what crisis research actually observes? The BIS working page read for this solution says banks *"could act either as absorbers or amplifiers of shocks originiating in the financial system"*, which is a second-order role. The financial-crisis Wikipedia page reports that herding models *"indicate that it is the internal structure of the market, not external influences, which is primarily responsible for crashes"*, which is a third-order claim. Do these corroborate the order, or do they show the order is wrong?
- **Refuter:** a held-out test in which truncating the habitat at first-order contact loses no predictive power. Also: evidence that the further-out conditions do not change sign with the residual, which would break the relative-valence claim.

### C3 - framework-internal derivation with a research-facing consequence

- **Statement:** regulation is a convention that can become an outcome variable, and its sign is not fixed by the figures.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 10 :47 - *"a convention that decides an outcome has stopped being one"*. Also Fig 10 :59 - *"an interest in the outcome is unavoidable, an interest in the reading is disqualifying."*
- **What to check:** the world literature records both directions. The Wikipedia financial-crisis page: *"Some financial crises have been blamed on insufficient regulation"* and *"excessive regulation has also been cited as a possible cause of financial crises."* Regulatory capture (Stigler 1971, via the Wikipedia page): *"as a rule, regulation is acquired by the industry and is designed and operated primarily for its benefit..."* Does any of this let a rule regime be treated as fixed background, or does it always become an outcome variable in the episode it is invoked to explain?
- **Refuter:** exhibit a rule regime whose sign and effect are invariant across episodes and whose makers have no interest in the reading, so it can be modelled as a fixed condition.

## 3. What is explicitly not claimed

- Not that risk-taking, credit growth, regulation, or structure is the main cause. No causal share is asserted for any of the four.
- Not that the four are equally important. Equal weighting is another global aggregate and is refused for the same reason as "mainly".
- Not that structural features dominate behavioural ones, or the reverse. Contact order is not importance order.
- Not that regulation is too strict or too lax. Its sign is left to the ground and its stake is declared.
- Not that crises are inherently unpredictable. The *"little consensus"* line from Wikipedia is a world observation, not a figure verdict.
- Not a ground reading. No dataset, counterfactual, or natural experiment was used in this derivation. Every substantive applied claim is `specified`, awaiting an anchor.
- Not a claim that the Tether framework is true, unique, or self-validating. The RSI Trap essay: *"Metatheories cannot validate themselves."*

## 5. What would settle it

A source-discriminating instrument, not a further argument. The relevant tests, each tied to a refuter above:

1. **Partition test:** do intersections still bind after one lever is applied? (C1)
2. **Aggregation test:** does a single aggregate statistic retain held-out predictive power with no live slot left? (C1)
3. **Truncation test:** does first-order-only modelling lose predictive power? (C2)
4. **Sign test:** can a rule regime be fixed across episodes, or does it always co-vary with the outcome? (C3)
5. **Independent anchor test:** do two low-overlap instruments converge, with routes recorded? Fig 2 :40 - *"disqualifying for a verdict: agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool"*.
