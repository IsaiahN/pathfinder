# Union, order, and analogues: the supporting derivation

**Mode: `specified`** on the applied claims, `general` on the structural moves. This is a simulated derivation checked against the figures, not a ground reading (see `05_what_shape_means.md` §6).

## RESIDUAL

The main audit (`01` F1) concluded that the four candidate causes form a union, not a partition, and that "mainly" aggregates away the per-case signal. Two supporting questions are owed:

1. **Structure.** If the four are conditions in a union, what is the structure of the compound? Is it a list, a chain, or something with a specific order?
2. **Order.** The question asks "mainly", which is an importance ranking. Fig 3 says chains are ordered by dependency, not importance. What order does the figures' structure actually license, and is it the same order the world literature observes?

## DOMAIN CENSUS (summary, full census in `01` §2)

Four domains with distinct actors, currency and anchor: A behaviour (decisions, incentives, default data), B credit (flows, growth rates, credit aggregates), C regulation (rules, conventions, rule-change outcomes), D structure (balance sheets, fragility, exposure data). No new primitive is available without import, and importing a physics six-term set is a refused strain (F10). The compound is a union of overlapping conditions.

## DERIVATION

### Q1 - What is the structure of the compound?

A list of four independent causes would be a partition with four cells. Fig 9 :55-56 rules that out: *"A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones. A single lever clears the region where only one condition holds and leaves the intersections standing, because the second condition still binds."*

The structure is therefore **a union with intersections**, not a list and not a chain of substitutes. Fig 9 :57: *"What remains is smaller and it is also categorically different: a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either."* A crisis in which a credit boom and a fragile balance sheet coincide is not "more of the same" as a crisis with only one; it is a different compound with its own loop.

Fig 9 :54: *"And when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again."* The operational consequence: four separate searches, then a recombination, not one search over a ranked list.

### Q2 - Dependency order is not importance order

Fig 3 :20-21: *"The chain is ordered by dependency, not by importance. Each link consumes what the one above produces, so effort spent above the break arrives at a step that cannot use it."*

Applied to this question, the dependency chain is:

1. perceive and measure the crisis (anchor: crisis data);
2. have terms for what the situation affords (credit, leverage, rules, exposures);
3. state what would count as "mainly" (the broken link: no instrument);
4. plan a lever;
5. name what worked and carry it to the next episode.

Fig 3 :27: *"A link with no instrument is not a link that works. It is a link nobody has looked at."* Link 3 has no instrument. So the "mainly" comparison is not a weak reading; it is a reading of nothing, in the figure's phrase. Effort spent improving the candidate list (link 2) cannot rescue it. This is the structural reason the question must be re-asked, not merely re-studied.

**Falsifier for Q2:** produce an instrument that returns a well-defined causal share for a condition and does not move when the four schools push on it. Then link 3 has an instrument and the dependency chain reaches a planning step.

### Q3 - Contact order, separate from dependency order

Fig 11 :52 (verbatim): *"And contact cascades. Scoping the habitat to direct contact is how DDT reached the eagles: first-order enumeration, second-order effect, and the residual that mattered was three steps out."*

In the financial habitat:

- **First-order contact:** the risk-taker's decision (domain A). Directly visible, the actor everyone names first.
- **Second-order contact:** the credit and funding channels through which that decision reaches others (domain B). The BIS reading says banks *"could act either as absorbers or amplifiers of shocks originiating in the financial system"*, which is a second-order role.
- **Third-order contact:** the balance-sheet network and market architecture that decide whether the shock closes or spreads (domain D). The financial-crisis Wikipedia page: *"herding models, based on complex systems science, indicate that it is the internal structure of the market, not external influences, which is primarily responsible for crashes."*
- **Frame acting on the habitat:** regulation (domain C), which is inside the system it grades. Fig 11 :61: *"The seat's power is asymmetric. Introduction is available; exclusion is not."* A rule can be added; the habitat cannot be subtracted.

Contact order is **not** a ranking of causal share. It is a warning about where a search will look first and what it will systematically under-weight. Fig 11 :49-50: *"An actor's valence is relative to the residual, not a property it carries."* Risk-taking is not "the bad actor" and credit is not "the good measure"; their valence depends on the residual being tracked.

**Falsifier for Q3:** a held-out test in which truncating the habitat at first-order contact loses no predictive power. Then the cascade claim is refused.

### Q4 - Isomers, cycles, functional groups: analogues with a limit

Fig 12 offers four structures, of which three are useful analogues here. They are analogues, not transfers: the figure governs typed bonds between atoms in a compositional system, not causal attribution in economics. The analogue is limited to the structural principle and is marked as such.

**Isomer.** Fig 12 :117: *"Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."* If two crises are written over the same set of conditions (a boom, a fragile balance sheet, a weak rule, a herd) but with a different condition binding first, they are isomer-like: same ingredients, different arrangement, different outcome. This is a sharper statement than "the causes interact". It predicts that a policy that works for one arrangement can fail for the other even though the ingredient list is identical.

**Cycle.** Fig 12 :121: *"The output re-enters as input. The sign of the loop decides whether it closes, and a chain cannot close by construction."* Credit growth is exactly such a loop: lending raises asset prices, which raises collateral, which permits more lending (Wikipedia, *Credit cycle*: *"During an expansion of credit, asset prices are bid up by those with access to leveraged capital."*). A linear list of causes cannot close this loop; a causal chain model that omits the feedback is not a weaker model of the same thing, it is a different kind of object.

**Functional group.** Fig 12 :123: *"A substructure whose presence fixes the outcome. The group says what kind; the qualifier says which member."* Each of the four domains can be read as a functional group: its presence fixes a kind of crisis, while the episode-specific detail says which member. This supports treating the four as a union of condition-kinds rather than four competing names for one quantity.

**Catalyst.** Fig 12 :119: *"A part that survives its own use. Spent and regenerating parts behave differently across uses, and nothing records which a term is."* A regulatory rule that survives the crisis it was written after is catalyst-like. It changes what can happen in the next episode, and nothing in a one-episode causal list records that it has been used before. This is why Fig 10 :47 (*"a convention that decides an outcome has stopped being one"*) applies to domain C.

**Limit of the analogue.** Fig 12's bonds (+, ∥, ≡, →, ⇒, −, ⋛) are typed relations between operands in a library. Which bond holds is not recoverable from the operands. There is no operation in the figure set that assigns a causal share to four overlapping conditions. So the isomer/cycle/group/catalyst reading is a way to *describe* the union's structure; it is not a derivation of "which factor dominates". Claiming otherwise would be the strain this derivation refuses.

**Falsifier for Q4:** show that the four conditions cannot be written over a shared condition set (that one is reducible to the others, or requires a primitive none of the others use). Then the isomer-style analogue fails, and the weaker union claim stands alone.

## CONCLUSION

The compound is a union with intersections, not a ranked list. Dependency order puts "mainly" at the uninstrumented middle link of the chain, so an importance ranking is attempted before the objective has a measure. Contact order runs from the visible risk-taker outward through credit and architecture, with regulation acting on the habitat from inside. Isomer, cycle, functional group and catalyst are useful structural analogues for describing the union, with the typed-bond mechanics explicitly not transferred. What none of this yields is a dominant factor. That is the stated gap, and it is owed to an anchor, not to a better analogy.
