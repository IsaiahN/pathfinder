# The Tether's answer on the main cause of financial crises

**Mode: `specified` on the applied claims, `general` on the union/dependency structure. Not `grounded`.** This is a shape and a route, not a result. It is a simulated derivation checked against the figures (`05_what_shape_means.md` §6), not a ground reading.

## 1. The residual it answers

The question asks which of four candidates is *mainly* responsible for financial crises. In effect terms: find the causal description whose crisis prediction leaves the smallest residual on held-out episodes. The figures do not deliver that description. What they deliver is a verdict on the *form* of the question: the four candidates are overlapping conditions in a union, and the comparison "mainly" aggregates away the per-case signal.

## 2. The shape

1. **It is a union, not a partition.** Fig 9 :4-5: *"Disagreements that do not shrink with effort, where each rule keeps working well on a different subset, are not one hard question but several well-formed ones. Split it rather than search."* Fig 9 :55: *"A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones."* A lever against one condition clears only the region where that condition alone binds.

2. **"Mainly" is an aggregation that discards the live signal.** Fig 1 :19: *"The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated."* Fig 1 :22: *"Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average."* A single dominant-cause number is compatible with one live slot still carrying the answer.

3. **Each domain has its own anchor; verdicts stay separate.** Fig 2 :29: *"An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks. A measurement, an experiment, a counter, a market, a patient outcome."* Behaviour, credit, rules and structure are settled by different instruments. Averaging them is not a synthesis, it is a category error.

4. **Contact order is not causal share.** Fig 11 :52: *"Scoping the habitat to direct contact is how DDT reached the eagles: first-order enumeration, second-order effect, and the residual that mattered was three steps out."* Risk-taking is first-order and directly visible; credit flows and balance-sheet structure sit further out. The figures warn that a direct-contact search will under-weight the further-out conditions. They do not say structure is the main cause.

5. **Regulation is a convention that can become an outcome variable.** Fig 10 :47: *"a convention that decides an outcome has stopped being one"*. A rule that decides which institutions survive is no longer background. Its sign is not fixed: the world literature read for this solution records both insufficient and excessive regulation as candidate causes.

6. **The figures name no winner.** After a genuine census there is no figure line that governs "which factor dominates in this episode". That is a `gap` in the precise sense: unreachable with the instruments held here is a verdict, not a silence. What is owed is an anchor that discriminates among four sources of the same crisis reading, not another argument.

## 3. What it explicitly does not say

- Not that any one of the four is the main cause. No causal share is asserted.
- Not that the four are equally important. Equal weighting would be another global aggregate and is refused for the same reason.
- Not that structural features beat behavioural ones. Contact order is not a ranking of causes.
- Not that regulation is good or bad, or that more of it is the answer.
- Not that crises are unpredictable or inherent. The Wikipedia page reports *"There is little consensus"*, but that is a world observation, not a figure verdict.
- Not `grounded`. No crisis dataset, counterfactual or natural experiment was read as an anchor in this derivation.
- Not a theory of everything. The framework returns a shape, then stops and records the lineage.

## 4. What would settle it - the ground

A source-discriminating instrument, not a further narrative. From *The Hard Problem is About Source* (essay, opened this session): an instrument that reads green *"cannot comment on the source"*, and *"If several paths create the same behavior, then behavior is a projection with lossy information."* The corresponding tests here are:

1. **Partition test.** Do the four conditions partition the episodes, or do intersections still bind after one lever is applied? This fires F1 in `01`.
2. **Aggregation test.** Does a single aggregate "main cause" statistic predict held-out crises as well as the per-case residuals, with no live slot left? This fires F2.
3. **Truncation test.** Does a model cut off at first-order contact (risk-taking alone) lose predictive power when credit and balance-sheet connectivity are removed? This fires F4.
4. **Prospective test.** Was a candidate factor fixed before the episodes it explains, and did it reduce the unexplained remainder on them? This fires F6.
5. **Independent anchors.** Do two low-overlap instruments (say a credit-gap indicator and a systemic-fragility indicator) converge on the same factor, with the provenance of each route recorded? Convergence from shared data is one witness, not two (Fig 2 :40).

## 5. The honest stop

**Unreached, not unreachable.** The four domains are a declared choice of compression axes, not nature's own list. From *They're Scanning Books*: *"Declaring the primitives does not make them neutral. It makes the choice visible."* The answer stops where the available instruments cannot distinguish among sources of the same crisis reading, and the lineage is recorded so the stop is checkable. *"you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself"* (Fig 9 :32). Indiscernibility is not identity.

## 6. One-paragraph version for outreach

> The Tether does not name a main cause of financial crises, and it does not claim the four candidates are rivals. It says the question is a union of overlapping conditions presented as a partition, so a single lever clears only the cases where one condition binds and leaves the intersections standing. It says "mainly" is an aggregation that can hide a live signal in a single episode, so per-case verdicts with separate anchors are owed instead of a league table. It says risk-taking is the first-order actor while credit flows and balance-sheet structure sit further out in the contact cascade, which is an ordering warning, not a claim that structure dominates. And it says regulation is a convention that can become an outcome variable, so its sign and its stake must be declared. What it offers is the shape any settled answer must have, refuters written in advance, and the instruments that would close it. The dominant factor stays open until the ground settles it.
