## The residual in one line

A list of four candidate causes joined by "or" invites a single winner. The figures treat that invitation as the thing to test: the question may be a union of overlapping conditions presented as a partition, and "mainly" may be an aggregation that throws away the live per-case signal.

## What is deliberately not here

- No personal emails of named individuals. The contact points are institutional and opt-in.
- No ranking of researchers by how likely they are to agree with this derivation.
- No bulk-send plan.
- No claim that the figures settle empirical financial crisis research. They narrow the shape an answer must have; the ground of crises (data, counterfactuals, natural experiments in institutional design) settles which factor dominates in a given episode.

Three reasons, in the figures' own words:

1. **Agreement is not an anchor.** *"Alignment is triangulation, not negotiation. A mirror cannot correct you."* (Fig 2 :35) A list of people selected for agreement produces a convention, not corroboration.
2. **A shared evidence pool disqualifies a verdict.** *"disqualifying for a verdict: agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool"* (Fig 2 :40). Four schools of crisis theory often read the same handful of crises; their agreement is one pool talking.
3. **A verifier must not be given the interior.** *"A verifier that can reconstruct the claim cannot verify it."* (Fig 10 :42) The first contact carries the residual and its refuter, not the framework.

Legally: unsolicited bulk email to personal addresses breaches GDPR/CAN-SPAM style rules in many jurisdictions. Public institutional channels avoid that failure and the structural one together.

## Honest standing

This is a **simulated derivation checked for internal consistency against the figures, not a ground reading.** From the RSI Trap essay, which was opened this session: *"LLMs can quickly simulate an approximate answer/output with the formula"* but *"the truly testable output would need to be derived from the real architecture or working the formula yourself."* This package cannot settle the question it reports on. It can only say what shape a settled answer would have to take, and what would refute that shape.
