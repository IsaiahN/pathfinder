# Outreach draft: check the output before you decide about the framework

**Use:** one-to-one, or as a cover note attached to a submission through a public venue in `03_public_contact_points.csv`. **Not** for bulk send.

**Hard precondition:** do not send until the shape claim in §1 survives the recipient's own check. If the output does not survive, there is nothing else to look at.

---

**Subject:** One checkable claim about the drought event/shift boundary

> Hi [name],
>
> I have one specific output I would like you to check against what you already know. I am not asking you to read a theory first, and I am not asking you to agree with anything. If the output fails, there is nothing else to look at.
>
> **The question in effect terms:** for a water store at a place, when does a drying excursion stop being a temporary event and become a persistent shift that does not return? The usual answer is a fixed index threshold held for a fixed duration. That makes the index the anchor, so the answer changes when the index changes. The output below makes the measurement the anchor instead.
>
> **The output (`specified`, not `grounded`):** a persistent shift has three tests, not a universal number. (1) At the scale of the catchment, aquifer or stand, recovery time from a perturbation exceeds the return interval of the forcing. (2) The dry state is maintained by a closed positive feedback across a fast and a slow variable, for example soil moisture and evapotranspiration reducing rainfall, or vegetation loss reducing infiltration and recharge. A linear chain cannot close, so a linear causal story cannot represent persistence. (3) Independent readings, meaning streamflow, storage, vegetation and paleo-context, agree that the state has held beyond the forcing. This is a shape with tests. The number is owed from measurement, and I am not claiming one.
>
> Three consequences follow and are checkable on their own: a regional mean index can be near zero while one aquifer is in a live shift, so persistence must be read per slot; meteorological, hydrological, ecological and socioeconomic persistence are overlapping conditions and must be split before they are searched; and a precipitation-only index refined forever will still miss a storage-driven shift, because that axis cannot carry it.
>
> **What I am not claiming:** not that a named index value defines persistence; not that every persistent shift is abrupt, since abrupt and smooth transitions both satisfy the comparison; not that the framework supplies a universal duration, since it returns a shape and not a number; not that agreement among drought indices is evidence, since they largely share the same inputs; and not `grounded`, since no measurement in this draft settles anything.
>
> **What would refute it. I wrote these before sending this:**
> 1. A persistent dry state with no closed positive feedback, where removing the candidate loop leaves persistence unchanged.
> 2. An aggregated index that classifies local persistence perfectly at every slot with no live residual left over.
> 3. A persistent shift predicted perfectly by precipitation alone, with no storage or vegetation state entering the prediction.
> 4. One rule that classifies meteorological, hydrological, ecological and socioeconomic persistence and their intersections without splitting.
>
> If any holds, I will retract the shape claim. A one-line "this fails because..." is more useful to me than agreement. If you would rather not engage, no reply is needed.
>
> **If it survives, and only then:** the sources are below. The figures are the authority, not my summary of them.
> - Figures and formula for direct download (SVG): https://github.com/IsaiahN/tether/tree/general-agent/figures
> - Canonical usage rule: https://github.com/IsaiahN/tether/blob/general-agent/figures/README.md
> - Hydrological drought reframing (Van Loon et al. 2016, open access): https://hess.copernicus.org/articles/20/3631/2016/
>
> **If you want to use the machinery:** the formula is in the figures link. Run it on a problem you know well and can check against a ground. Write down what would refute your result before you run it, and state the mode of what you get. A reported failure is more useful to me than a reported success.
