# Event and shift as different bond arrangements: structure, order, analogues

**Mode: `specified`.** Simulated derivation checked against the figures, not a ground reading (`05_what_shape_means.md` §6).

## RESIDUAL

The same set of ingredients is present in a temporary drought and in a persistent shift: low precipitation, low soil moisture, vegetation, groundwater, a store, a season and a threshold. The question is why they yield different substances. Two supporting questions are owed: (1) are event and shift isomers under Fig 12's definition, and (2) what is the dependency order of the four domains so that effort lands on the broken link?

## DERIVATION

### Q1: Are a temporary event and a persistent shift isomers?

Fig 12 :5: *"A set of ingredients is not one. The bond is the rest of the information, and it is the half nothing was recording."*

Fig 12 :117: *"isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."*

Fig 12 :121: *"cycle. The output re-enters as input. The sign of the loop decides whether it closes, and a chain cannot close by construction."*

- Temporary event: an open chain. Rainfall deficit lowers soil moisture, vegetation wilts, rain returns, the store refills. The return path is negative feedback and the chain terminates.
- Persistent shift: the same atoms with an added closed loop. Low soil moisture reduces evapotranspiration, which reduces local rainfall; vegetation loss reduces infiltration and recharge, which removes the groundwater buffer; the store no longer refills under the same rain.
- The ingredient lists are nearly identical. What differs is the bond: chain versus cycle, negative versus positive sign. By Fig 12 :117 that is isomerism, and by :121 a chain could not represent the shift even in principle.
- **Corroboration, not authority:** Rietkerk et al. 2004 report that catastrophic shifts "have been theoretically attributed to positive feedback and bistability of ecosystem states." Different field, same structural claim. Triangulated (Fig 12 plus Fig 11 cascades plus the world source).

**Falsifier, written before the run:** if event and shift can be separated by their atom sets alone, with no reference to loop closure or sign, the isomer claim fails and the two are merely different quantities of the same term.

### Q2: The buffer as catalyst: a part that survives its own use

Fig 12 :119: *"catalyst. A part that survives its own use. Spent and regenerating parts behave differently across uses, and nothing records which a term is."*

Groundwater is the candidate catalyst. In one catchment it is spent and regenerated between droughts, so each event begins from the same buffer. In another it is mined and does not regenerate, so the same drought produces a shift. An index that records rainfall and soil moisture but not the regeneration status of the store records the ingredient and not which kind of part it is. This sharpens test 3 in `01`: the anchor must include a storage reading that distinguishes spent from regenerating. **Falsifier:** a catchment where the store is demonstrably mined and the system still returns to its prior range under the same forcing, with no other import.

### Q3: Functional group across drought types

Fig 12 :124: *"functional group. A substructure whose presence fixes the outcome. The group says what kind; the qualifier says which member."*

Meteorological, hydrological, ecological and socioeconomic persistence all share the comparison substructure (recovery time against return interval). That shared substructure is a functional group, not identity. The qualifier says which state occupies the slot, and the four qualify differently. This is the figure-grounded reason F6 splits them rather than averaging them. **Falsifier:** a single state in the slot that makes all four classifications identical across known cases.

### Q4: Dependency order of the four domains

Fig 3 :20-21: *"The chain is ordered by dependency, not by importance. Each link consumes what the one above produces, so effort spent above the break arrives at a step that cannot use it."*

Operators table :30-32: *"sequence / A then B; B needs A to have happened / no"*

1. **D3 baseline** first. Without a characterised reference, "returned" has no predicate.
2. **D1 detection** second. Per-slot readings against the baseline establish whether recovery occurred.
3. **D2 mechanism** third. Attribution of a closed loop is only meaningful after the recovery failure is established.
4. **D4 decision** last. Transform or restore depends on whether the loop closed.

The break is most likely between D1 and D2, the same under-instrumented middle link Fig 3 :25-26 names: we can measure rainfall and we can build infrastructure, but "what counts as a new state" has neither obvious test nor instrument. Building better climate models before that link is fixed is effort above the break.

**Falsifier:** a case where management choice can be correctly made without first establishing recovery failure and loop closure, in which case D4 is not downstream.

### Q5: What order is the shift itself?

The shift is not "a later stage of the event." Under → it is a different term with the same atoms and a different bond (Q1). Under Fig 12 :104, *"A settled molecule becomes an atom for whatever composes over it,"* a documented persistent shift becomes an atom for the next level: land degradation, dust feedback, regional aridity. The IPCC AR6 Ch8 statement that "Positive land surface feedbacks, including vegetation and dust, can contribute to abrupt changes in aridity" is exactly that second-order composition. **Falsifier:** a persistent dry state that cannot serve as an operand for a land-degradation or regional-aridity composition.

## CONCLUSION

Event and shift are isomers over a nearly shared atom set, separated by bond type and loop sign, not by quantity of dryness. Groundwater is the catalyst-shaped buffer whose regeneration status must be recorded. The four drought types form a functional group, not one term. The dependency order is baseline, detection, mechanism, decision, with the likely break at the definition of "new state." These claims need no new primitive and no seventh term. The numeric boundary remains owed from the ground.
