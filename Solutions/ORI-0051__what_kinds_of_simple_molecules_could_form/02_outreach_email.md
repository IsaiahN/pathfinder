**Subject:** One checkable claim about what the first cell boundary had to be like

> Hi [name],
>
> I work on figure-grounded derivations of hard open questions. I have one output about the first cell-like boundaries that I would like you to check against what you already know. I am not asking you to read any theory first, and I am not asking you to agree. If the output does not survive, there is nothing else to look at.
>
> **The output (`specified` on the shape, `general` on one analogy, `gap` on identity):** the question "which simple molecules could form the first cell-like boundaries?" is a bundle of at least four conditions with different grounds, not one question with one answer. The figure-governed shape any candidate must satisfy is this: a boundary is a *directed filter, not a wall*. It must separate an inside from an outside while letting nutrients in and waste out, and it must be a bonded arrangement whose head-tail structure and mixing ratio carry behaviour that the ingredient list does not. It must form and persist in a habitat that has to be enumerated from the setting, and it must be able to grow and divide by intrinsic physical processes. The framework does not name the molecule that actually did it. It returns a shape, not a name.
>
> **What I am not claiming:** that the figures select fatty acids over sulfates or phosphates. They do not. That the first boundary was a pure species rather than a mixture. That it formed in seawater, or that any laboratory vesicle proves the historical event. Those are bindings and reports from the chemistry frame, provenanced as such, and they carry no weight until their source is shown.
>
> **What would refute the shape claim:**
> 1. a boundary impermeable to all solutes that still supports an interior chemistry, growth and division;
> 2. ingredient lists that predict formation, stability, permeability and pH tolerance with no loss from omitting head-tail structure and mixing ratio;
> 3. the same molecular kind behaving identically across tested pH and ionic regimes;
> 4. growth and division that require an encoded catalyst present at the start, with no physical pathway before it;
> 5. a derivation of a specific molecular identity from the figure set alone, with source gate shown (that would strengthen the framework and refute my refusal to name one).
>
> I wrote these down before sending this. A one-line "this fails because..." is more useful to me than agreement. If you would rather not engage, no reply is needed.

## Two-stage offer, recorded before any theory is shown

**Stage 1 (this email):** the shape claim, the four refuters, and the request for a failure. Record the recipient's verdict *before* anything else is sent.

**Stage 2, opt-in only:** if they are curious, the three links go out, with the note that the figures are the authority. Their later agreement is never pooled as corroboration, because a reader who has absorbed the framework now holds its interiors (Fig 10 :42; Fig 2 :40).

**Stage 3, if asked:** the formula as a reusable method, with the instruction that each user writes their own refuter and states their own mode.
