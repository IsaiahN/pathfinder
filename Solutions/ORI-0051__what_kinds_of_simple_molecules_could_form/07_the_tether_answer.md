# The Tether's answer on the first cell-like boundaries

**Mode: `specified` on the figure-governed shape, `general` on the Figure 4 analogy, `grounded` only on the experiments reported from papers read, `gap` on which composition came first and where.** Not a ground reading. Simulated derivation checked for internal consistency against the figures (`05_what_shape_means.md` §6).

## 1. The residual it answers

The question "which simple molecules could form the first cell-like boundaries?" bundles at least five slots: separation, permeability, persistence in a habitat, growth and division, and composition over ingredient list. These have different anchors and are not one hard problem (Fig 9 :55). The figures answer the shape and the order; experiment answers the chemical identity; the rock record would answer the history.

## 2. The shape

1. **A boundary is a directed filter, not a wall.** Fig 4 :21: *"The membrane is the boundary between the sizes, and the rule about what may cross it. Not a wall: a filter with a direction."* At `general` mode, the shape transfers across strata. Empirically, Deamer 2017 reports permeability varying over eight orders of magnitude across solute types. A seal that retains everything is not a cell-like boundary.
2. **The composition is the unit, not the ingredient list.** Fig 12 :5: *"A set of ingredients is not one. The bond is the rest of the information, and it is the half nothing was recording."* Headgroup, chain length, unsaturation and mixing ratio all carry behaviour. The robust reported systems are mixtures (Milshteyn et al. 2018; Ekosso et al. 2026).
3. **The habitat must be enumerated, not composed.** Fig 11 :43: *"The habitat is enumerated, never composed."* Fig 11 :39: *"capability is a property of agent-and-habitat, never of the agent alone"* Reported favourable conditions: low ionic strength, pH near the headgroup pK, temperature above the gel-fluid transition, concentrating wet-dry cycles. In seawater the tested amphiphiles failed to form vesicles (Milshteyn et al. 2018).
4. **Growth and division can be intrinsic physical processes.** Fig 12 :121: *"cycle. The output re-enters as input. The sign of the loop decides whether it closes, and a chain cannot close by construction."* Budin and Szostak 2011 report growth and shear-driven division without pre-existing machinery. Selection needs no author: Fig 11 :36 — *"Nothing was intelligent; selection ran. The word does no work there."*
5. **The candidate families form a functional group, not an isomer set.** Fig 12 :123: *"functional group. A substructure whose presence fixes the outcome. The group says what kind; the qualifier says which member."* Fatty acids, alcohols, monoglycerides, amines, sulfates and phosphates are members; the qualifier (chain length, headgroup, ratio) sets the thresholds.
6. **The identity is not named by the framework.** Fig 9 :32: *"you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself."* Fig 13 :134: *"Unreachable is a verdict here, not a silence"* The figures cannot say which composition crossed the line first. That is a ground fact.

## 3. What it explicitly does not say

- Not "the first membrane was a fatty acid." Fatty acids are candidates with provenance, not a figure output.
- Not "the first membrane was a pure substance." The robust reported systems are mixtures.
- Not "vesicles formed in seawater." The experiments read report the opposite; the site question remains live.
- Not "a laboratory vesicle proves the historical event." Fig 11 :70: *"A test harness is a substituted habitat. Which is why a synthetic solve proves wiring and never capability."*
- Not "the figures derive the chemistry." Fig 13 :75 governs the substrate kinds, not molecular species; the first-pass reading that applied it to head-tail composition is retracted in `01` §4.
- Not grounded. No measurement in this folder was taken; the word is used only for the authors' reports as read.

## 4. What would settle it — the ground

A source-discriminating instrument or a geochemical record, not a further argument. From *The Hard Problem is About Source*: a reading of green *"cannot comment on the source"*; the spectrometer settled it. Here the corresponding questions are:

- **Identity test:** is there a measurement that distinguishes which amphiphile family produced a given ancient boundary structure, rather than simply showing that a boundary existed?
- **Habitat test:** does the candidate composition form and persist in the actual ionic, pH and temperature regime of the proposed setting? This fires against the pure-fatty-acid-in-seawater claim already.
- **Permeability test:** does the composition retain useful solutes while admitting nutrients, over the temperature range of the setting (Fig 4 :21)?
- **Propagation test:** does growth and division occur without an encoded catalyst supplied at the start (Fig 12 :121)?
- **Standing refuters from `01` §5:** an impermeable boundary that still works; ingredient lists with no information lost by omitting arrangement; identical behaviour across all habitats; machinery-requiring division; a figure-derived molecular identity; one variable governing all five slots.

## 5. The honest stop

**Reached for the shape, unreached for the identity, and silent on the history only in the precise sense that the framework refuses a name.** Unreached is not unreachable: the identity is answerable by another frame, and the historical sequence by another instrument. "Unreachable is a verdict here, not a silence" (Fig 13 :134) is recorded, not used to pretend the question has been answered. The framework's own boundary is stated in the README: it returns *"a shape, not a name"* and stops where the instruments stop discriminating, recording the lineage so the stop is checkable. The candidate list from the literature is a provenanced import, not a composition derived from the six terms.

## 6. One-paragraph version for outreach

> The Tether does not name the molecule that made the first cell boundary, and it does not claim the laboratory has replayed the event. It says the boundary had to be a directed filter, not a seal: something that held an inside together while letting nutrients through. It says the chemically checkable unit is a composition, usually a mixture of a head-and-tail amphiphile with another amphiphile, not a single ingredient, and that the habitat has to be read off the setting rather than assumed: low salt and divalent cations, a pH near the headgroup's transition, and a temperature above the gel-fluid point. It says growth and division can be intrinsic physical properties of such vesicles, so no machinery is owed at that step. It refuses to pick a winner among fatty acids, sulfates and phosphates, because the observation of a vesicle does not comment on its source. What it offers is the shape any candidate must satisfy, five refuters written in advance, and the instruments that would close the identity question. The historical answer stays open until the ground settles it.
