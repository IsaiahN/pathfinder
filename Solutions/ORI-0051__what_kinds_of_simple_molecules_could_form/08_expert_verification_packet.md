## 1. The claim, in one paragraph

The question "which simple molecules could form the first cell-like boundaries?" is a union of conditions, not one chemical fact. The figure-governed shape any candidate must satisfy has four parts: a boundary is a directed filter rather than a seal; its behaviour is carried by arrangement and mixing ratio, not by the ingredient list; it must form and persist in an enumerated habitat; and it can grow and divide by intrinsic physical processes. The candidate families from the literature (fatty acids and their alcohol, monoglyceride and amine mixtures, alkyl sulfates and alkyl phosphates) stand to one another as members of a functional group, not as isomers. The framework does not name a winner. The mixture, not the pure species, is often the robust reported unit. The historical identity of the first boundary is left to experiment and the rock record.

## 2. Three checkable sub-claims

### C1 — a boundary is a filter with a direction, not a wall

- **Statement:** a cell-like boundary must separate an inside from an outside while permitting selective exchange. A seal that retains everything fails the shape.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 4 :21: *"The membrane is the boundary between the sizes, and the rule about what may cross it. Not a wall: a filter with a direction."* Marked `general`: Figure 4's own subject is the boundary between one agent and a population, so this is a cross-stratum analogue, not a lipid claim.
- **What to check:** is the directed-filter shape faithful to what a primitive boundary must do? Deamer 2017 reports permeability varying over eight orders of magnitude across solutes, from milliseconds for water and CO2 to hours for sodium and potassium.
- **Refuter:** a compartment impermeable to every solute that still supports an interior chemistry, growth and division. Also: a demonstration that Figure 4's membrane is only about information between scales and cannot be used as an analogue at all, in which case the claim is withdrawn rather than repaired.

### C2 — the unit is a composition, and the candidate families are a functional group, not isomers

- **Statement:** headgroup, chain length, unsaturation and mixing ratio carry behaviour the formula does not. The families are members of a functional group (Fig 12 :123: *"functional group. A substructure whose presence fixes the outcome. The group says what kind; the qualifier says which member."*), not isomers under Fig 12 :117: *"Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."*
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 12 :5: *"A set of ingredients is not one. The bond is the rest of the information, and it is the half nothing was recording."* Fig 12 :48: *"A molecule is a bonded arrangement. A chain is the degenerate case: one bond type, no branching."*
- **What to check:** DS (C10 sulfate), DDS (C12 sulfate) and DDP (C12 phosphate) differ in composition, so the isomer definition does not apply; the reported agreement rates of 61 to 66 percent across conditions are consistent with family-plus-qualifier. Milshteyn et al. 2018 report the 1:1 lauric-acid/monoglyceride mixture more robust than the pure acid across acidic and alkaline hot-spring water.
- **Refuter:** identical behaviour across all tested pH, ionic and temperature conditions for the families (kills the functional-group claim); or a pure species matching the mixture's stability (kills the composition-over-molecule claim); or a genuine isomer pair found among candidates (restores the isomer claim for that pair).

### C3 — habitat conditions are ordered before propagation, and growth and division can be intrinsic

- **Statement:** supply, concentration, medium and temperature bind before encapsulation and division. Growth and division then close a cycle without encoded machinery.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 3 :20: *"The chain is ordered by dependency, not by importance."* Fig 11 :43: *"The habitat is enumerated, never composed."* Fig 12 :121: *"cycle. The output re-enters as input. The sign of the loop decides whether it closes, and a chain cannot close by construction."* Fig 11 :36: *"Nothing was intelligent; selection ran. The word does no work there."*
- **What to check:** Budin and Szostak 2011 report phospholipid-driven growth into filamentous vesicles and division under gentle shear, *"without the need for preexisting cellular machinery."* Deamer 2017 and Milshteyn et al. 2018 report failure of the same amphiphiles in seawater, so the medium is load-bearing.
- **Refuter:** a candidate that forms and persists in a setting where supply, concentration, medium and temperature all fail (breaks the dependency order); or a division mechanism that requires an encoded catalyst present at the start with no physical pathway before it (breaks the intrinsic-propagation claim); or a harness-only result presented as a historical demonstration (Fig 11 :70).

## 3. What is explicitly not claimed

- Not that the figures name fatty acids, sulfates or phosphates. They return a shape.
- Not that the first boundary was a pure substance. The robust reported systems are mixtures.
- Not that vesicles formed in seawater. The experiments read report the opposite; the site is disputed.
- Not that a laboratory vesicle replays the historical event. A harness proves wiring, never capability.
- Not that the isomer definition applies to the candidate list. It is declined; the functional-group reading is used.
- Not `grounded` on the history. The word is used only for the authors' reports as read.
- Not a theory of everything, and not a final answer. The historical slot stays open.

## 5. What would settle it

A source-discriminating instrument or a geochemical record, not a further argument.

1. **Identity test:** can any measurement distinguish which amphiphile family produced a given ancient boundary structure, rather than only showing that a boundary existed?
2. **Habitat test:** does the candidate composition form and persist in the actual ionic, pH and temperature regime of the proposed setting?
3. **Permeability test:** does it retain useful solutes while admitting nutrients across the temperature range?
4. **Propagation test:** do growth and division occur without a catalyst supplied at the start?

4. Send the refutation. A failure is more useful than an endorsement.
