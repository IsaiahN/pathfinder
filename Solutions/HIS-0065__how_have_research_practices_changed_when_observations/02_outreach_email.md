**Use:** one-to-one, or as a cover letter attached to a submission through the public venues in `03_public_contact_points.csv`. **Not** for bulk send.

**Hard precondition:** do not send until the three checkable sub-claims in `08_expert_verification_packet.md` survive your own check. The offer collapses on contact if the stage-1 output does not hold.

**Subject:** One checkable claim about large-scale, automated and remote observation

> Hi [name],
>
> I work on a figure-grounded framework that makes a narrow claim about how research practice changes when observations become large-scale, automated or remote. I am not asking you to read the framework first, and I am not asking you to agree with it. I am asking you to check one output against what you already know. If the output fails, there is nothing else to look at.
>
> **The output (`specified`, not `grounded`).** The observation loop does not get replaced when it scales. The same perceive, plan, act, predict loop runs, and what changes is the cast, the currency and the access to the ground. Figure 4 states the kernel rule: "The kernel is the part that does not change with size: the same loop and the same rule for naming what worked, whether one agent is running it or a thousand. Only the actors, the currency and the anchor change."
>
> From that, four things follow that a historian or methodologist could check:
>
> 1. Bigger instruments extend what can be represented, not what can be settled. A larger closure is not a larger reach.
> 2. The failure moves into the channel. "the ground does not decay, the channel does", and the named failure modes are stale percepts, dropped transients, saturated metrics and pooled readings.
> 3. Volume of agreement is not volume of independent evidence. "agreement compounds, evidence does not". Ten thousand sensors on one calibration are one frame wearing many names.
> 4. Remote observation substitutes a habitat. What the field survey supplied but the platform does not is invisible until the conclusion fails.
>
> **What I am not claiming.** I am not claiming that science has entered a new epistemology that replaces observation and testing. I am not claiming that automated observation is more objective because it is automated. I am not claiming to know which practice changed first, in which field, on which date. The framework returns a shape, not a name, and the historical record has to be supplied by people like you.
>
> **What would refute the shape claim.** I wrote these before sending this:
>
> 1. A large-scale or automated observation that increases independent ground contact one-for-one with its volume, with no shared evidence pool.
> 2. An agent-only improvement at scale that moves the ground.
> 3. A channel at scale that never drops, pools or saturates a reading, with no new instrument and no provenance cost.
> 4. A large-scale reading whose source is recoverable from the reading alone, with no lineage and no source-discriminating instrument.
>
> If any one holds, I retract the corresponding claim and redraw the shape. A one-line "this fails because..." is more useful to me than agreement. If you would rather not engage, no reply is needed.
>
> **What I would like from you, at stage 1:** what would you expect to see if the shape claim were true, and what would you count as a failure? I will record your answer before showing any theory.
>
> **If it survives, and only then:** here are the sources. The figures are the authority, not my summary of them.
> - Verbatim figure text with line numbers (local copy in the solution folder): `FRAMEWORK/reference/03-FIGURES_TEXT.md`
> - Canonical usage rule: https://github.com/IsaiahN/tether/blob/general-agent/figures/README.md
> - Figures and formula for direct download (SVG, branch `general-agent`): https://github.com/IsaiahN/tether/tree/general-agent/figures
> - The FAIR principles paper, as an instance of machine-actionable provenance: https://www.nature.com/articles/sdata201618
>
> **If you want to use the machinery:** the formula is in the figures link. Run it on a problem you know well and can check against a ground. Write down what would refute your result *before* you run it and label the mode of what you get. A reported failure is more useful to me than a reported success.
>
> Thanks for reading,
> [name]

## Distribution rule

One-to-one only. Record the stage-1 verdict before any theory is shown. A later agreement is never pooled as corroboration (Fig 2 :40). "Correlation has valence" (Fig 2 :39), and the local seat-map field guide is blunter: "High correlation is disqualifying for a verdict and efficient for a convention." Do not rank recipients by likely receptiveness.
