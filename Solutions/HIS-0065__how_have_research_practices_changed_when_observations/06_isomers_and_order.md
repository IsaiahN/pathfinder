# Large-scale, automated, remote: one change or three? Structure and order

**Mode: `specified`.** This is a simulated derivation checked against the figures, not a ground reading (see `05_what_shape_means.md` §6).

## RESIDUAL

The question lists three adjectives as if they named one thing. Are "large-scale", "automated" and "remote" three spellings of one change, three different changes, or three members of one family? And do they come in an order that the framework licenses? The answer matters because a union must be unbundled before it is searched (Fig 9 :54), and because a code that charges for the sequence overcharges under a symmetric bond (Fig 12 :92).

## DERIVATION

### Q1: Is "large-scale" many terms or one term?

- Fig 12 :92: "Under a symmetric bond, k operands have k! spellings and one term".
- Fig 12 :27: "a bigger space, not a bigger vocabulary".
- A million slots under the conjunction bond `+` are one term with a million spellings. The search enumerates every spelling and counts each as a candidate, which inflates the space and not the vocabulary.
- ⇒ **Large-scale is not a new kind of observation.** It is the same term iterated. This is the exact reason Fig 1 :19 says the residual is indexed by slot and Fig 1 :22 says aggregation destroys the signal: the spellings carry the information the bond does not.
- **Falsifier:** if a larger collection introduces an atom that was not in the smaller one, the iteration reading fails and an import has occurred (Fig 6 :14). Record the new atom and re-census.

### Q2: What bond does "automated" add?

- Fig 12 :76, Operators table :37: "A produces B; B did not exist before". This is `⇒` production, an ordered bond.
- An automated instrument produces the observation as an output of its own operation. The observation was absent before the pipeline fired. That is production, not conjunction.
- Contrast `→` sequence: "A then B; B needs A to have happened" (Fig 12 :71). Automation is not merely a faster ordering of the same human steps; the observation is generated by the instrument's operation, so the bond type changes.
- **Falsifier:** if the automated observation can be shown to be a pre-existing reading merely relayed faster, the production bond is wrong and `→` or `+` governs. Re-census D1.

### Q3: What does "remote" remove, and what does it substitute?

- Fig 12 :81: "A with B removed or absent". This is `−` subtraction, the absence of an operand. Remote observation is the absence of co-presence, not a negation of the claim, so it is `−` and not `¬` (the Operators table distinguishes them explicitly).
- Fig 11 :66: "Isolation is not removal of the habitat. It is substitution of one habitat for another." So the absence is not a pure subtraction. The observer's habitat is replaced by the platform's habitat: bandpass, sampling, orbit, reduction.
- ⇒ Remote = `−` on co-presence **and** a habitat substitution. The two silent failures are "what you failed to reproduce is invisible until the goal fails" and "what you unintentionally introduced is invisible until it acts" (Fig 11 :68, :69).
- **Falsifier:** a remote observation whose substituted habitat preserves every condition and introduces no unchosen actor. Then the substitution half is refuted and only the `- co-presence` half stands.

### Q4: Are they isomers?

- Fig 12 :117: "Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."
- Fig 12 :123: "functional group. A substructure whose presence fixes the outcome. The group says what kind; the qualifier says which member."
- As the question writes them, the three adjectives do not share an identical atom set: scale acts on slots, automation on the producer of the observation, distance on the habitat. By Fig 12's definition they are **not isomers**. They are three members of a **functional group**: each is a modification of the observation link `state → structure → state`, with a different qualifier in the slot.
- The stronger form, worth testing: write all three over the full shared atom set `{instrument, observer, habitat, method, ground, slot}` and let them differ only in which bond occupies which position. Then they are isomers, same atoms, different arrangement, different substance. That is a structural claim with a falsifier, not a slogan.
- **Falsifier, written before the run:** if any one of the three can be written without an atom the others require (for example, remote observation with no habitat substitution, or automation with no instrument that produces the observation), the isomer claim fails and the functional-group reading stands instead.

### Q5: What is the order?

- Fig 12 :119: "catalyst. A part that survives its own use." A reusable method is a catalyst: it is spent and regenerating across uses, and a record of one success does not say which a term is.
- Fig 12 :121: "cycle. The output re-enters as input." The residual of one round of observation is what the next round composes against.
- The three adjectives can co-occur. They are joined by `+`, not necessarily by `→`. Asserting a historical order (scale first, then automation, then distance) adds an ordering the figure does not license. The figure writes these arrangements with commas; the arrow is a bond that must be identified, not assumed.
- **What would settle the order:** dated records from the ground. This is a `gap` for the derivation, not a silence. "Unreachable is a verdict here, not a silence" (Fig 13 :134).

## CONCLUSION

Large-scale is one term under a symmetric bond with many spellings. Automation adds a production bond. Remote adds a subtraction of co-presence plus a habitat substitution. The three are not isomers as written; they are members of a functional group, and the stronger isomer form over a shared atom set is a checkable claim with a stated falsifier. No historical order is licensed. What is licensed is the structure, the catalyst and cycle reading of method, and the honest stop where the historical record must take over.

This rescues what is defensible in the everyday phrasing without claiming that a new loop or a new epistemology appeared. Fig 12 :44: "the arrangement is priced or it is free". Naming the bonds pays for the arrangement; listing three adjectives leaves it free.
