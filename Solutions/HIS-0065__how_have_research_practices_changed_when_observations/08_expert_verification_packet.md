# Expert verification packet: the Tether on large-scale, automated and remote observation

**What this is:** a claim in a form a researcher in big data or research practice can check. Mode, derivation and refuters are stated before the conclusion, so a reader knows what to look for and what would settle it.

**Mode: `general` on the structural invariants, `specified` on the domain predictions. Not `grounded`.** No historical dataset is claimed to have been read. This is a shape and a route, not a result.

## 1. The claim, in one paragraph

The observation loop does not get replaced when it scales, automates or moves remote. The same perceive, plan, act, predict loop runs; what changes is the cast, the currency and the access to the ground. Bigger instruments extend what can be represented, not what can be settled. The failure moves into the channel, where stale percepts, dropped transients, saturated metrics and pooled readings live. Volume of agreement is not independent evidence, and remote observation is a habitat substitution with two invisible failure modes. Provenance becomes part of the claim. The framework returns that shape, not a historical narrative; which practice changed first is left to the ground.

## 2. Three checkable sub-claims

### C1: framework-internal; checkable by reading Figures 4, 6 and 10

- **Statement:** the loop is invariant under scale; representation grows; the channel decays. A larger closure is not a larger reach.
- **Source, verbatim:**
  - Fig 4 :19-20, "The kernel is the part that does not change with size: the same loop and the same rule for naming what worked, whether one agent is running it or a thousand. Only the actors, the currency and the anchor change."
  - Fig 6 :26, "instruments extend what can be represented at all."
  - Fig 10 :24, "the ground does not decay, the channel does"; Fig 10 :25-26, "stale percepts, dropped transients, saturated metrics, pooled readings".
- **Refuter:** demonstrate a scale at which the loop stops running and a different loop produces checkable ground contact; or a channel that never drops, pools or saturates a reading, with no new instrument and no provenance cost.

### C2: data-practice facing; checkable by anyone who has run a pipeline or a survey

- **Statement:** volume of agreement is not volume of independent evidence. A million slots under a symmetric bond are one term with a million spellings, and reporting n as evidence is the pooled-reading failure restated in statistics.
- **Source, verbatim:**
  - Fig 7 :13, "agreement compounds, evidence does not".
  - Fig 2 :40, "agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool".
  - Fig 1 :19, "The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated."
  - Fig 2 :44, "The effective-sample-size correction for correlated observations is standard in survey statistics and meta-analysis; the collapse is the same arithmetic read as a warning."
- **What to check:** in a system you know, does adding observations add independent ground contact, or does it replay one calibration under many names? Would the effective sample size rise one-for-one with the raw count?
- **Refuter:** show that effective sample size grows one-for-one with observation volume because each added observation carries independent ground contact, not a shared pool. If so, this sub-claim is withdrawn.

### C3: method and provenance; checkable by a methodologist or data steward

- **Statement:** capability belongs to the researcher and the habitat together; remote observation substitutes a habitat; only methods cross scales, and the lineage is part of the claim.
- **Source, verbatim:**
  - Fig 11 :39, "capability is a property of agent-and-habitat, never of the agent alone."
  - Fig 11 :40, "An improvement that does not change contact changes nothing, however much it improves."
  - Fig 11 :67, "what you failed to reproduce is invisible until the goal fails"; Fig 11 :69, "what you unintentionally introduced is invisible until it acts".
  - Fig 4 :22, "Only methods cross up. Only head starts come down."
  - Fig 10 :62, "A ruling carries provenance like an atom."
- **Refuter:** demonstrate an agent-only improvement at scale that moves the ground (the refuter the figure itself supplies); or a remote observation whose habitat substitution preserves every required condition and introduces no unchosen actor; or a large-scale reading whose source is recoverable from the reading alone with no lineage and no discriminating instrument.

## 3. What is explicitly not claimed

- Not a new epistemology that replaces observation and testing. The kernel does not change with size.
- Not that automation increases objectivity. It changes the channel, which can decay.
- Not that large n is independent evidence, and not a ranking of fields or venues by receptiveness.
- Not a historical narrative. Which practice changed first, in which field, on which date, is a `gap` here.
- Not `grounded`. Nothing here is asserted as settled by a historical record.
- Not that the framework returns a name. "what it returns is a shape rather than a name" (Fig 13 :133).

## 5. What would settle it

Historical ground from at least two closures with low overlap: instrument logs, data statements, protocol changes, preregistrations, replication outcomes. The discriminating instrument must tell whether the channel degraded or the ground moved (Fig 10 :24), whether independence grew with volume (Fig 2 :44), whether a method crossed a habitat substitution (Fig 4 :22, Fig 11 :67-69), and whether an agent-only improvement changed contact (Fig 11 :40).

## 6. What an expert can check in twenty minutes

1. Read Fig 4 :19-20, Fig 10 :24-26 and Fig 7 :13, three short passages.
2. Check C2 against one system you know: is n treated as evidence when the readings share a calibration?
3. Name one instrument that would distinguish channel decay from ground movement in your field, or say why none is possible.
4. Send the refutation. A failure is more useful than an endorsement.
