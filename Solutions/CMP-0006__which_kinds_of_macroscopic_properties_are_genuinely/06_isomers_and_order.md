# Isomers, catalysts, cycles and functional groups: the arrangement kinds

**Mode: `specified`, not `grounded`.** This is a simulated derivation checked against the figures, not a ground reading (see `05_what_shape_means.md` section 6).

## RESIDUAL

The question asks which *kinds* of macroscopic property are irreducible to components. The figures answer in terms of kinds of **arrangement**, not kinds of physical quantity. Two questions are owed: (1) do the four arrangement structures of Fig 12 actually separate whole-properties from component-inventory properties, and (2) can a physical example be bound onto each one without inventing a new primitive or moving the wall?

## DERIVATION

### Q1  -  What does "irreducible" mean when the level is held fixed?

- Fig 12 :103: *"Composition is recursive, and the level decides what is irreducible."*
- Fig 12 :104: *"A settled molecule becomes an atom for whatever composes over it. So irreducible is a fact about a level and never about the world, and there is no bottom"*
- Fig 4 :33: *"Which is why there is no top and no infinite regress."*
- ⇒ At fixed level L, a property P is irreducible to components C if the description of P under the held bonds requires a term not in the multiset of C. At level L+1, P may settle and become an atom. So "irreducible" is an indexed predicate `irred(P, L)`, never a bare predicate.

**Falsifier:** a property P with `irred(P, L)` true for every L. That breaks the recursion. Triangulated (Fig 12 plus Fig 4); mode `general`.

### Q2  -  Do the four arrangement kinds pass the separator?

Fig 12 :116: *"Four structures a compositional system exhibits, and each names something the flat form cannot write."*

| kind | verbatim figure line | why the component list cannot write it | world binding (candidate, `specified`) |
|---|---|---|---|
| isomer | Fig 12 :117 *"Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."* | ingredient multiset identical; only the typed bond differs | different phases of one material; states with the same symmetry and different long-range entanglement (Wikipedia Topological order, opened this session) |
| catalyst | Fig 12 :119 *"A part that survives its own use. Spent and regenerating parts behave differently across uses, and nothing records which a term is."* | the difference is across uses, so a one-shot inventory cannot record it | a defect or impurity whose role changes after a cycle; binding unproven |
| cycle | Fig 12 :121 *"The output re-enters as input. The sign of the loop decides whether it closes, and a chain cannot close by construction."* | closure is a property of the loop's sign, not of any part | feedback in a critical or self-organised system; binding unproven |
| functional group | Fig 12 :123 *"A substructure whose presence fixes the outcome. The group says what kind; the qualifier says which member."* | the group fixes kind while the qualifier selects member, two different descriptions | a symmetry class fixing a universality class while microscopic detail selects the material; binding `specified`, corroborated by Wikipedia Critical phenomena |

All four pass Fig 13 :47 (*"If the whole carries a property neither part has, the whole is not the parts arranged."*) in the following sense: each is a fact about the relation, and removing the relation removes the property while leaving the ingredients unchanged.

**Falsifier, written before the run:** if any kind's distinguishing information is recoverable from the ingredient multiset alone (isomer collapses), or if a part's spent and regenerating states are behaviourally identical (catalyst collapses), or if a closed loop can be written as a chain (cycle collapses), or if the outcome is fixed by something other than a substructure (functional group collapses), that kind is struck from the list of irreducible kinds.

**Mode:** `specified`; single-source on the figure definitions (Fig 12), with the world bindings as low-overlap corroboration and explicitly not as derivations.

### Q3  -  Is the isomer claim real for the condensed matter frame?

- Fig 12 :95-96: *"Two entries with an identical ingredient set are not variants of one thing. They are different things whose notation cannot tell them apart, and the difference is carried entirely by the bond."*
- Wikipedia Topological order, opened this session: chiral spin states with exactly the same symmetry required a new kind of order because *"symmetry alone was not enough to characterize different chiral spin states."* Different quantum Hall states likewise all have the same symmetry and are outside the Landau symmetry-breaking description.
- Wikipedia Critical phenomena, opened this session: critical exponents *"take the same values for very different physical systems"*, explained by the renormalization group. This is the converse of the isomer claim: different ingredients, same substance at the measured level.
- Fig 13 :121: *"Different routes can arrive at the same substance."* Fig 13 :124-125: *"the converse holds too: the same ingredients under a different bond are a different substance."*
- ⇒ Both directions are figure-licensed: same ingredients, different arrangement, different substance; different ingredients, different route, same substance. So "the same components" does not settle whether two macro properties are the same, in either direction.

**Falsifier:** a case where identical microscopic ingredients under different admissible arrangements yield identical macroscopic properties at the same level, with no hidden bond difference. That weakens the isomer reading for that level. Triangulated (Fig 12, Fig 13, world frame); mode `specified`, ungrounded.

### Q4  -  What is the dependency order among these kinds?

Fig 12 :104 settles a molecule before it can become an atom for the next composition. Fig 3 :19-20: *"Optimising a link that already works cannot rescue one that does not."* The order is:

1. Settle the arrangement at level L (isomer, catalyst, cycle, functional group are evaluated here).
2. Promote the settled molecule to an atom at L+1 (Fig 12 :104).
3. Only then ask whether a property irreducible at L is composable at L+1.

A cycle at L may be a component of a larger cycle at L+1; that does not make the L property reducible *at L*. This is the level index of Q1 applied.

**Falsifier:** a case where a property is irreducible at L but cannot be promoted to an atom at L+1 while remaining settled. Mode `specified`.

### Q5  -  Are the derived quantity names a sixth kind?

No. Fig 13 :43-44 removes continuum as a seventh member: *"A continuum was the seventh and was removed for failing exactly this: it is a spectrum without gaps, or smoothness over space and time. A generating set must not contain what its own members produce."* Fig 13 :124-125: *"Smoothness, symmetry, chaos and discreteness are each what a particular composition leaves behind, and none is a seventh member."* THE_FORMULA substrate section says the same: these are *"what a turn leaves rather than members of the set."*

They are still irreducible to a component list in the *route* sense: Fig 12 :111 says *"A vocabulary without bonds can name a state and cannot name a route to one."* A route is not an ingredient. So smoothness can be irreducible to components without being a new kind of primitive. That distinction is the whole point of the question's word "kinds."

**Falsifier:** a derivation of one of the six from the other five, or a genuine seventh member that passes Fig 13 :41-44. Mode `specified`.

## CONCLUSION

The answer to "which kinds" is a list of **arrangement kinds**, not a list of physical quantities: isomer, catalyst, cycle and functional group (Fig 12 :117-123), with the whole-not-parts test at Fig 13 :47 as the discriminator against aggregates, and with smoothness, symmetry, chaos and discreteness classified as turn-leftovers rather than primitive kinds. Each verdict carries a level index (Fig 12 :103-104) and each is `specified`, ungrounded. The physical properties that populate these slots are candidate bindings with provenance, not derivations. The strongest single comparison is the isomer pair: same ingredients, different bond, different substance (Fig 12 :95-96), corroborated in the condensed matter frame by states with identical symmetry and different long-range entanglement.

The context-free standing list of physical properties remains a `gap` (Fig 11 :51, Fig 9 :32, Fig 13 :134). This file does not fill it.
