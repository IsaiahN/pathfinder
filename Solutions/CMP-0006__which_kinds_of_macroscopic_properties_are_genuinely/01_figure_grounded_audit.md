## 0. The residual, stated in effect terms

The question asks: *Which kinds of macroscopic properties are genuinely irreducible to the behavior of individual components?*

Two candidate readings, stated as effects before any figure is chosen:

- **A, the list reading.** There exists a standing list of property kinds, call it L, such that every member of L is irreducible to components in every system and at every scale. Returning L settles the question. What it leads to: a context-free verdict with no level index, so any change of level leaves the verdict untouched, and a property that is a whole-property at one level would be called irreducible at another.
- **B, the separator reading.** "Irreducible" is a relation between a whole and a level, not a property of the world. Return a separator that a reader can apply per slot per level, keep verdicts per domain, and report the literal context-free L as a `gap`. What it leads to: a checkable criterion, a falsifier, and an honest stop at the instrument limit.

The load-bearing word is **genuinely**. In the figures' terms it is a *direction of the question*, a sought-for shape, not a term the loop holds. `THE_FORMULA.md` step 2 warns: *"a frame searching for an answer of a given SHAPE holds that shape as a PREFERENCE, not a term. Unbound, predicting nothing, no residual - so nothing above can see it."* The remedy is discipline: bind the sought-for shape as an ordinary term (Formula "Direction"), let it accrue a residual, and make it refusable. Sections 3 and 4 do that.

What an answer would look like, bound in advance: **a four-condition separator with a per-level index, plus the name of the condition no figure line governs.** That is the shape the derivation is aimed at. It is written down here before the census so a later reader can see whether the result was fitted to it.

## 1. Domain census (done before choosing figure lines)

### 1.1 Describe R by arity, symmetry and scale

Fig 9 :22 gives the three descriptors: *"arity · symmetry · scale"*.

- **Arity.** The relation is two-place, whole-to-components, with a hidden third term, the qualifier "genuinely" that asks for a level-free guarantee. A two-place relation plus an unbound qualifier is already a union signal, not a clean partition.
- **Symmetry.** "Irreducible to" is ordered, not symmetric. A symmetric bond would say "correlated with"; the question asks which way the dependence runs. Under the bond table, `→` (sequence) and `⇒` (production) are ordered while `+`, `∥` and `≡` are symmetric. The question therefore lives under an ordered bond, not a symmetric one.
- **Scale.** The relation is explicitly cross-scale: macro versus micro. That puts the membrane (Fig 4) and the level rule (Fig 12 :104) in play from the start.

### 1.2 One line per domain: actors, currency, anchor

No nameable anchor means it is not yet a domain. Each line below has one.

- **D1, framework composition and levels.** Actors: the six terms (Space, Time, Energy states; Spectrum, Gradient, Network structures), the seven bonds plus negation, atoms, molecules, levels, `closure(Γ)`. Currency: composition inside the closure and settlement against the ground. Anchor: the figures as metalaws, plus the ground for any measurement. Nameable: yes.
- **D2, bond-carried arrangement.** Actors: ingredients, typed bonds, molecules, the four arrangement structures (isomer, catalyst, cycle, functional group). Currency: description length in bits and the information the bond carries that operands do not. Anchor: Fig 12's six bond tests and the minimum-description-length bargain. Nameable: yes.
- **D3, whole versus aggregate.** Actors: parts, whole, the relation between them. Currency: whether the whole carries a property neither part has. Anchor: the gradient argument at Fig 13 :47, which is a test that can fail. Nameable: yes.
- **D4, the condensed matter and emergence frame.** Actors: spins, electrons, atoms, order parameters, critical exponents, topological invariants, phases, coarse-graining maps. Currency: physical prediction and measurement. Anchor: experiment, an instrument that does not update on the theory (a universality-class measurement, a topological degeneracy count). Nameable: yes. This is the field the question is normally asked in, and the field named for public contact points.
- **D5, verification and outreach.** Actors: expert readers. Currency: agreement and residual. Anchor: the ground, never agreement. Nameable: yes, but in this simulated run all seats draw on one evidence pool, so its independence is zero (Fig 2 :31).

### 1.3 Partition or union

The question is a **union of overlapping conditions, not a partition of properties into two boxes** (Fig 9 :54-58). A candidate property can satisfy several conditions and fail others:

| candidate | D2 bond-carried | D3 whole not parts | D4 cross-micro universal | verdict shape |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| total energy, particle count | no | no | trivially | reducible aggregate |
| critical exponent | yes | yes | yes | irreducible at the critical level |
| topological ground-state degeneracy | yes | yes | yes | irreducible at the ordered level |
| order parameter (magnetization) | partly | yes | no | level-bound, not universal |
| smoothness of a continuum | yes, derived | yes | no | composition leftover, not a member |

A single lever clears the region where only one condition holds and leaves the intersections standing (Fig 9 :55): *"A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones."* The right move is to unbundle, then search again. The table is illustrative, not a returned list: the framework returns a kind of thing, not a name (Fig 9 :32).

### 1.4 Order by dependency (Fig 3)

1. Settle what composes at a level (D1) before asking which properties are irreducible (D2, D3). A step whose input never arrives cannot be diagnosed (Fig 3 :22-23).
2. Refuse the absolute reading of "genuinely" before minting any list (Formula step 2: refuse before mint).
3. Describe the residual before choosing a frame (Fig 9 :27-28), so D4 is approached with the separator, not with a favourite phase-transition example.
4. Run the per-level verdicts before the outreach step (D5), because the first contact must carry the refuter rather than the source.

## 3. Findings

### F1  -  "Genuinely irreducible" is level-relative (decisive against reading A)

Fig 12 :103 and :104:

> *"Composition is recursive, and the level decides what is irreducible."*
> *"A settled molecule becomes an atom for whatever composes over it. So irreducible is a fact about a level and never about the world, and there is no bottom"*

Fig 4 :33 closes the other end: *"Which is why there is no top and no infinite regress."* There is no smallest and no largest level. So a property that is irreducible at one level becomes a composable atom at the next, and no property carries an absolute "genuinely irreducible" label across all levels.

This is not a semantic quibble about the word "genuinely." It is the framework's central recursion claim. Reading A asserts a property of the world where the figure says the fact is about a level. Under the mode rule, A is a `general`-mode verdict with no measurement, which is the named output-layer defect.

**Refuter for F1:** show a property whose irreducibility holds independently of every level, that is, a property that cannot become an atom for any composition over it, or show a bottom or a top level. Either breaks Fig 12 :104 or Fig 4 :33.

**Mode:** `general` on the structural claim, `specified` on the prediction that a given slot will be re-described as an atom one level up. Triangulated: Fig 12 plus Fig 4, two low-overlap passages (bond recursion versus membrane between scales).

### F2  -  The irreducible content is the bond, not the ingredient list

Fig 12 :5: *"A set of ingredients is not one. The bond is the rest of the information, and it is the half nothing was recording."* Fig 12 :94: *"The arrangement is the substance."* Fig 12 :95-96: *"Two entries with an identical ingredient set are not variants of one thing. They are different things whose notation cannot tell them apart, and the difference is carried entirely by the bond."*

This is the positive answer the question actually admits. The kinds of macroscopic property that resist reduction to components are the kinds whose content is carried by the typed relation rather than by the inventory of parts. The bond table makes this checkable: seven bonds, three symmetric (`+`, `∥`, `≡`) and four ordered (`→`, `⇒`, `−`, `⋛`), with negation as a unary eighth operator. A property that flips when operands are swapped under a symmetric bond was never a component property; a property that survives reordering under a symmetric bond was always a component inventory.

The world framing makes the same point: a phase transition changes the organisation of the same atoms, and topological order distinguishes states with the same symmetry and different long-range entanglement (Wikipedia Topological order, opened this session). The world source is corroboration only. The verdict comes from Fig 12.

**Refuter:** exhibit a macroscopic property fully determined by the multiset of components under every admissible bond, and show the framework calls it irreducible. Or exhibit a bond whose type cannot be recovered from the operands but that carries no macroscopic consequence (would break the pricing rule at Fig 12 :99-102).

**Mode:** `specified`; triangulated across Fig 12 :5/:94/:95-96 and the bond table, with the physics frame as a low-overlap corroborator.

### F3  -  Four arrangement kinds that the flat component list cannot write

Fig 12 :116 introduces *"Four structures a compositional system exhibits, and each names something the flat form cannot write."* The four are given verbatim at :117, :119, :121 and :123 (quoted in the census table). They are the closest thing the figures offer to an answer to "which kinds":

1. **Isomer** (Fig 12 :117): same atoms, different bond, different substance. In condensed matter this is the pattern behind different phases of the same material and behind topological order beyond symmetry breaking.
2. **Catalyst** (Fig 12 :119): a part that survives its own use. Spent and regenerating parts behave differently across uses, and a component list does not record which a term is. This is a property of the *history of the arrangement*, not of the part.
3. **Cycle** (Fig 12 :121): the output re-enters as input, and the sign of the loop decides whether it closes. A chain cannot close by construction. A feedback property is therefore invisible to a components-only description.
4. **Functional group** (Fig 12 :123): a substructure whose presence fixes the outcome; the group says what kind, the qualifier says which member. This is why a property can be universal in kind and specific in member at once.

These four are **kinds of arrangement**, not a standing list of physical properties. They are level-relative by F1: a settled molecule becomes an atom at the next level, so an isomer at one level is an ingredient at another.

**Refuter:** show an arrangement kind whose difference is recoverable from the ingredient multiset alone (breaks isomer), show a part whose spent and regenerating states are indistinguishable in behaviour (breaks catalyst), show a closed loop writable as a chain (breaks cycle), or show an outcome fixed by something other than a substructure (breaks functional group).

**Mode:** `specified`; single-source on the figure definition (Fig 12), corroborated in `06_isomers_and_order.md` by the physics frame. The mapping of a given physical property onto one of the four is a binding, provenanced as such, never a derivation.

### F4  -  The aggregate/whole test separates reducible from irreducible

Fig 13 :47: *"If the whole carries a property neither part has, the whole is not the parts arranged."* This is the gradient case: an ordering varying across space gives a distribution, and a distribution stays put; what makes a gradient a gradient is that it can be spent, and neither ordering nor span of space has directedness (Fig 13 :45-47).

Apply as a test per slot, not globally. Fig 1 :19: *"The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated."* Fig 1 :22: *"Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average."* A property that is a sum or count over parts passes straight through to the components; a property that requires a typed relation or a direction does not.

The test is not "surprising" or "unexpected." Those are frame-internal judgements. Fig 1 :24-27 disqualifies coverage, compression, predicates minted and credibility accrued as self-scores: *"A frame cannot score itself with a quantity it also produces."* Surprise is not an anchor.

**Refuter:** exhibit a property that passes Fig 13 :47 and is nevertheless fully derivable from the component inventory under the held bonds, or exhibit a whole that carries a property neither part has where the bond type is irrelevant.

**Mode:** `specified`; triangulated across Fig 13 :47 and Fig 1 :19/:22, two different objects (substrate test versus residual indexing).

### F5  -  Smoothness, symmetry, chaos and discreteness are turn-leftovers, not new primitive kinds

Fig 13 :43-44 removes the continuum as a seventh member: *"A continuum was the seventh and was removed for failing exactly this: it is a spectrum without gaps, or smoothness over space and time. A generating set must not contain what its own members produce."* Fig 13 :124-125: *"Smoothness, symmetry, chaos and discreteness are each what a particular composition leaves behind, and none is a seventh member."*

This matters because the natural response to the question is to name smooth macroscopic behaviour as an irreducible property kind. The figures say it is a **composition leftover**, a derived quantity. It is irreducible to components in the same sense that a route is irreducible to an ingredient list (Formula "Composition": a vocabulary with only ingredients can name a state, not a route to one), but it is not a new member of the set. The set is closed and the arrangements are not (Fig 13 :115).

Consequence: do not convert a derived quantity into a primitive to make the answer look substantial. That is the "seventh member" error, and Fig 13 :44 explicitly forbids it.

**Refuter:** derive one of the six from the other five, or add a seventh member that is not producible by the six and pass the minimality test at Fig 13 :41-44.

**Mode:** `specified`; triangulated across Fig 13 :43-44/:124-125 and the Formula substrate section.

### F6  -  A standing list is structurally refused, and the gap is a verdict

Fig 11 :51: *"No standing list of good actors survives a change of residual."* The valence of a property is relative to the residual, not carried by the property (Fig 11 :49-50). Combine with Fig 9 :32: *"you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself"* and Fig 13 :134: *"Unreachable is a verdict here, not a silence,"* and Fig 13 :135: *"It cannot say which primitive is missing, because naming it would mean already holding it."*

So the literal L is a `gap`. It is not a failure of the derivation and not a blank. The gap is of the **shape-not-a-name** kind, not the underivable-in-principle kind: the framework can state what any member of L would have to look like (bond-carried, whole-not-parts, level-indexed) but cannot enumerate a level-free member. Fig 8 :39: *"So a search that finds nothing is never a proof of absence."*

This is where the question's "genuinely" is finally discharged. It is bound as an ordinary term: `genuinely = passes the separator at every level in the habitat`. Bound that way it accrues a residual and is refusable. The falsifier is in section 4.

**Refuter:** produce a context-free list of irreducible property kinds that survives a change of residual and a change of level, with each member shown to be bond-carried and whole-not-parts at every level. That would break Fig 11 :51 and Fig 12 :104.

**Mode:** `general` on the structural refusal, `specified` on the prediction that any proposed list member will flip with the residual. Single-source on the figure side (Fig 11, Fig 9, Fig 13 agree but share one evidence pool); the world source on weak versus strong emergence corroborates without deciding.

### F7  -  World sources corroborate the shape but cannot settle it

Opened this session: Wikipedia Emergence (Lewes 1875 on the emergent that *"cannot be reduced to their sum or their difference"*; Chalmers on weak versus strong emergence; the Carroll and Parola type taxonomy; Kivelson on an emergent property that *"can only occur in the limit that the number of microscopic constituents tends to infinity"*; Anderson on new properties at each level; Gu et al. on non-computable macroscopic properties of infinite systems); Wikipedia Philip W. Each predicts, in effect, that a component-only description loses something. That is the figures' separator, arrived at in a different field. But the world sources are secondary: Fig 8 :50-52, *"Convergent derivation and adopted import look identical in the contents. Only the record of where each came from separates them."* Their agreement is convergent derivation, not an import that moves the wall, and it is recorded as such. They do not flip the verdict, and one apparent conflict (Gu et al.'s in-principle irreducibility) is returned to the census in section 5, not used to upgrade the claim to `grounded`.

**Refuter for the world layer:** an experiment or proof in condensed matter showing that a named macroscopic property is fully determined by the component inventory at the same level under the held composition rules. That would not refute the figure separator; it would refute the *binding* of that particular property onto a slot.

**Mode:** `specified` on each binding; `general` on the cross-field pattern. Overlap declared: all five world pages are one encyclopedia pool, so their agreement is one witness, not five.

### F8  -  Effects and correlations are filters, not witnesses

Critical exponents, entropy slopes and universal scaling functions are excellent **filters for the budget**: they tell a researcher which properties are worth trying to derive and which measurement to build. They are not witnesses for the verdict about irreducibility. Fig 9 :60: *"Use filters for the budget and witnesses for the verdict. Never let a filter hand you a verdict."*

A measured exponent shared by two very different materials tells you the property is micro-independent at that level; it does not tell you the property is irreducible at every level. The second claim would need the level index and the refusal test. This is the error of promoting a shared measurement to an absolute.

**Refuter:** show a case where a shared critical exponent or universal quantity is used as the sole premise for a level-free irreducibility claim. That claim is invalid by Fig 9 :60 regardless of how well the measurement reproduces.

**Mode:** `general` on the rule; `specified` on the per-case prediction. Triangulated across Fig 9 :60 and Fig 2 :42.

### F10  -  Levels are not a hierarchy with a top or bottom

Fig 12 :104 gives *"no bottom"* and Fig 4 :33 gives no top. So a search for the ultimate irreducible component set is a search for a bottom that the framework rules out definitionally, and a search for the all-explaining macroscopic theory is a search for a top that is equally ruled out. This is the same structure as the exemplar's no-last-room finding: the question "which properties are *genuinely* irreducible" inherits a tunnel vision from the assumption that there is a final level at which the answer is absolute.

Fig 8 :32 gives the arithmetic: *"Closure is idempotent. Composing inside a closed set never leaves it, so no search, however long, adds an atom."* More microscopy does not add a new irreducible kind; it changes the level at which the existing kinds are indexed.

**Refuter:** identify a terminal level, below which no composition and above which no composition is possible, with a nameable anchor that settles both claims.

**Mode:** `general`; triangulated across Fig 12 :104, Fig 4 :33 and Fig 8 :32.

## 3b. Pre-registered refuter (written before the conclusion)

Per Formula step 2 and the discipline note in section 0, the shape claim is refuted if any of the following fires. Written before the run; the result is not fitted to them.

1. **Level-relativity broke.** A macroscopic property is demonstrated whose irreducibility holds at every level, in the sense that it cannot become an atom for any composition over it. Breaks Fig 12 :104.
2. **A terminal level is found.** A bottom or a top level with a nameable anchor. Breaks Fig 12 :104 and Fig 4 :33.
3. **The bond is dispensable.** A macroscopic property whose descriptive content is fully recoverable from the component multiset under every admissible bond, yet the separator calls it irreducible. Breaks Fig 12 :5/:94/:95-96.
4. **The whole-not-parts test fails.** A property is shown to be a composition leftover while its bond type makes no difference to the outcome. Breaks Fig 13 :47 and Fig 13 :124-125.
5. **A seventh member is derived.** One of the six is derived from the other five, or a genuine seventh primitive is added and passes the minimality test. Breaks Fig 13 :41-44.
6. **A standing list survives.** A context-free list of irreducible property kinds is produced and survives both a change of residual and a change of level. Breaks Fig 11 :51 and Fig 9 :32.
7. **The gap was silence.** A figure-governed line is found that enumerates a level-free list. Then F6 is withdrawn and the list is reported.

If none fires, the separator stands as `candidate`, not `grounded`.

## 4. Recommendation

1. Lead with the separator and its refuter, never with a list of physical properties. The question is a union, so keep the four condition verdicts separate (Fig 9 :54-58).
2. State the level index on every "irreducible" claim. An unindexed irreducibility verdict is a `general`-mode verdict with no measurement, the output-layer failure genus.
3. Use the four arrangement kinds of Fig 12 as the answer to "which kinds," and say plainly that they are kinds of arrangement, not a standing list of physical quantities.
4. Report the literal context-free list as a `gap`: *"Unreachable is a verdict here, not a silence"* (Fig 13 :134).
5. Keep world sources in the corroboration column. Their convergence with the figures is convergent derivation, not a second witness (Fig 8 :50-52).
6. Send the generator, not the conclusion: Fig 4 :22, *"Only methods cross up. Only head starts come down."* The verification packet in `08` carries the method and the refuter.
