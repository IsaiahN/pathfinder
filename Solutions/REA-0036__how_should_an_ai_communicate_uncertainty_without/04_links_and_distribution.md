## Four findings in the wider literature that bear directly on this question

1. **Calibration is real but insufficient.** Guo et al. show modern networks are *"poorly calibrated"* and that temperature scaling helps make estimates *"representative of the true correctness likelihood."* This is a D1 result. It does not establish that a message carrying such a number is usable, which is F1's boundary.
2. **A ground-referenced guarantee is available, and it is an import.** Angelopoulos and Bates give prediction sets *"guaranteed to contain the ground truth with a user-specified probability."* Under the figures this is a legitimate import with a source and a shadow test (Fig 6 :23, Fig 8 :50-52). It is not a derivation from the framework, and must be recorded as foreign.
3. **More words can make a message more misleading.** Steyvers et al. found *"longer explanations increased user confidence, even when the extra length did not improve answer accuracy."* This is the direct non-figure witness for F4's two-currency claim: bits were spent, actions were not saved, and the contact reading got worse.
4. **The right shape can improve decisions when it changes the habitat.** HEAR's warning-plus-correction format produced a 13 percent success gain and 29 percent lower error distance with 80 users, and PredActBench shows current systems fail to make predictor uncertainty visible under noise. Together they say the question is empirical and settled at the ground, not by argument. Devic et al. add that the field optimizes *"metrics that are not necessarily indicative of downstream utility."*

No world source conflicts with the figure verdict. The conflict rule was not triggered. Note the overlap: the four essays and the canonical README share the framework's author and evidence pool with the figures, so their agreement is expected and carries no independent weight. The arXiv papers are from different author groups and venues; PredActBench and Devic et al. are the lowest-overlap witnesses for the human-facing half.

## Distribution: offer, do not push

Figure-grounded reasons:

- **Fig 4 :22** - *"Only methods cross up. Only head starts come down."* Sending the full architecture before a recipient has described a residual sends a recording, not a generator. Lead with the claim and its refuter; offer links after.
- **Fig 10 :42** - *"A verifier that can reconstruct the claim cannot verify it."* Seat 4 must not hold the interiors. A recipient who has absorbed the framework before giving a verdict has joined the frame being checked.
- **Fig 9 :27-28** - *"describe it before you go looking, or any frame you pick will seem to fit."* Ask what they would expect to see if the shape claim were false before giving them the links.
- **Fig 2 :40** - *"agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool"*; a send selected for likely agreement produces a convention.
- **Fig 7 :13** - *"agreement compounds, evidence does not."* A room of agreeable readers is one long room, not a check.

**Recommended order in a reply:** (1) the shape claim and its five falsifiers; (2) ask what they would count as a failure and record it; (3) only then the three links, with the note that the figures, not this summary, are the authority. One recipient at a time. No bulk send, no automation, no misleading subject line, and an explicit opt-out line in every message.

## Venue notes

- SRA has a current open special issue on risk communication and interpretation of uncertainty (deadline 2027-01-31 as stated on the homepage) and a Risk Communication specialty group. These are opt-in, ground-visible audiences for the human-facing half.
- SJDM and its open-access journal are the natural home for the claim that human judgment is the anchor for usability and that two costs must be kept apart.
- AUAI and UAI 2026 are the natural technical audience for the round-trip-gap and provenance claims, which are closer to representation and reasoning under uncertainty than to user-interface studies.
- arXiv is a preprint route, not a peer-reviewed venue. Its own homepage says materials are not peer-reviewed by arXiv. Use it to invite checks, not to claim settlement.
