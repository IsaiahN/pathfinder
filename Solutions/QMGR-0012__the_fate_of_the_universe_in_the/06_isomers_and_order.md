## RESIDUAL

The question offers heat death, Big Rip and Big Crunch as if they were entries in one list. Two derivations are owed: (1) are they a **union** of overlapping conditions or a partition of one question, and (2) what is their structural relation over the six-term set, isomer, functional group, or merely different bindings?

## DERIVATION

### Q1 : Union or partition?

- Fig 9 :4: *"First: is there a fact here? Disagreements that do not shrink with effort"* and Fig 9 :5: *"are not one hard question but several well-formed ones. Split it rather than search."*
- Fig 9 :55: *"A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones. A single lever clears the region where only one condition holds and leaves the intersections standing"*.
- Physical check (world source, not a figure line): the heat-death article states that *"In a theorized 'open' or 'flat' universe that continues expanding indefinitely, either a heat death or a Big Rip is expected to eventually occur"*, while the future-universe article states that a closed universe with insufficient dark energy *"will end in a Big Crunch"*. Different conditions, different levers, overlapping regions.
- ⇒ **Union.** Clearing the phantom-energy condition does not clear the recollapse condition. The three are not a partition.
- **Falsifier, written before the run:** if one parameter regime generates all three as mere value settings and no intersection survives a single lever, the union claim fails and the partition reading stands.

### Q2 : What is the structural relation among the three?

- Fig 12 :117: **"Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."**
- Fig 12 :124: **"functional group. A substructure whose presence fixes the outcome. The group says what kind; the qualifier says which member."**
- Over the **full shared atom set** `{Space, Time, Energy, Gradient, Spectrum, Network}`, the three scenarios are compositions with different arrangements. Under Fig 12 :117 they are **isomers**: same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in an ingredient list and decisive in the terminal behaviour.
- As the physics literature actually writes them, they do not share all six atoms in the same way. Heat death emphasises energy, gradient and time; Big Rip emphasises the expansion-driving term, space and a threshold crossing; Big Crunch emphasises space, energy and network re-contraction. That weaker reading makes them members of a **functional group** (terminal composition) with different qualifiers, not strict isomers.
- The stronger isomer claim is the one worth testing, because it is checkable: write all three over the same atom set and ask whether the difference is carried entirely by the bond.
- **Falsifier:** if any scenario requires an atom the others do not, or if a scalar multiplier alone converts one into another, the isomer claim fails and the functional-group reading stands.

### Q3 : What order composes them?

- Fig 12 :104: **"A settled molecule becomes an atom for whatever composes over it. So irreducible is a fact about a level and never about the world."**
- The order is dependency order, not list order (Fig 3 :20): first describe the residual, then read the ground, then classify, then refuse the final-room framing. Putting the framing first is effort above the break (Fig 3 :19 *"Optimising a link that already works cannot rescue one that does not."*).
- The `⋛` comparison bond is relevant only at the level where a threshold is crossed (Big Rip, `w < -1`). Operators table :51 calls it *"a threshold relation between two quantities"*. At first order the operands are quantities; at second order the operands are settled molecules now serving as atoms. Fig 12 :104 permits the demotion to atom, but whether an atom counts as a quantity at that level needs an instrument, not an argument. **This is a live type strain, named, not hidden.**

### Q4 : The round trip from the figure set to a fate claim

Fig 4 :30: *"So take the round trip and measure the gap. Without it you are navigating by dead reckoning: confident about a position nobody checked."*

1. **Sent up:** Fig 13 :22 *"a difference that can be spent"*, Fig 12 :117 *"Same atoms, different bond, different substance"*, Fig 9 :5 *"are not one hard question but several well-formed ones. Split it rather than search."*
2. **Abstraction:** the shape in `07`: union, ground decides, heat death as gradient exhaustion, different bond regimes, no last room.
3. **Concretisation:** the concrete fate claim: phantom energy with `w < -1` gives a Big Rip at a finite time.

The gap is nonzero. The shape carries none of the measured `w`, none of the discriminator among acceleration sources, and no timescale. Treating the shape as if it were the fate claim would set the round-trip gap to zero and would be exactly the dead-reckoning error Fig 4 :30 names. The honest form is a lossy condensation with a recorded gap `R_T`, which is what `09_watson_answer.md` and `11_audit.md` state.

## CONCLUSION

The three scenarios form a **union** of overlapping conditions (Fig 9), not a partition and not a ranking. Over a fixed atom set they are a real **isomer** claim (Fig 12 :117), with the weaker functional-group reading (Fig 12 :124) standing if the atom sets differ. The order is dependency order: residual, ground, classification, framing. The `⋛` operand type at second order is a live strain. No figure line selects a scenario, a value of `w`, or a timescale.
