# Are the three outcomes isomers, and what order do they run in?

## DERIVATION

### Q1 - Are the three outcomes a union or a partition?

- Fig 9 :55: *"A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones."*
- Fig 9 :57-58: *"a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either. It stops being a mixture and becomes a specific compound."*
- A new predator is both a food-web link and a competitor for a shared prey. A novel pathogen is both a parasitic food-web link and a disease risk. The intersections are populated, so a partition is wrong. The residue after addressing one outcome is a specific compound with its own loop.
- **Conclusion:** union compound. One loop over the substituted habitat, not three independent assessments. **Triangulated** between Fig 9 and the Becker et al. 2019 disease-ecology result that spillover is an emergent property of aligned factors, not a set of separable components.

### Q2 - What bond does each outcome carry?

From Fig 12 / Operators table (bond, test, bearing here):

| outcome | candidate bond | test from the figures | why |
|---|---|---|---|
| new competition | ⋛ comparison, *"a threshold relation between two quantities"* | *"is it about which is larger, rather than both being present?"* | competition is which species wins under limiting supply, not mere co-occurrence |
| new food-web connection | ⇒ production / → sequence | *"is there a B before A fires? No."* / *"A then B; B needs A to have happened"* | consumption produces a predator from a prey; order is real |
| new disease risk | contact path, a network propagation (Fig 13 :24, *"that entities relate, and influence travels"*) plus → sequence along the path | *"that influence travels"* and ordered transmission | a pathogen must contact a competent host before establishment |

- These three bonds are not interchangeable. The Operators table: *"A bond costs what it could have been"* and *"A relation that fails all [tests] is not an operator you are missing; it is a claim that has not been made yet."*
- A flat list of "new species present" records the ingredients and not the arrangement. Fig 12 :117: *"Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."*
- **Falsifier, written before the run:** if all three outcomes reduce to one bond over one actor set with identical behaviour, the bond-typing claim fails and the flat list is sufficient.

### Q3 - Are the three outcomes isomers over a fixed actor set?

- Fig 12 :117: **"isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."**
- Hold the recipient habitat's actor set fixed, say {arriving species, resident herbivore, resident predator, resident pathogen, shared resource}. If the live bond changes from comparison to production to transmission, the same set yields three different outcomes. That is an **isomer claim in the figure's strict sense**: same atoms, different bond, different substance.
- Low-overlap corroboration: Chamberlain, Bronstein and Rudgers (2014, *Ecology Letters*, fetched 2026-11-23) found by meta-analysis that interaction outcomes vary in sign and magnitude with context, with mutualism, parasitism and competition on a continuum. Fig 11 :50 (*"mutualism and parasitism are relations rather than kinds"*) and that result are two frames with low evidentiary overlap, so the relation-not-kind premise is triangulated.
- But the isomer claim is only **single-source** for the strict figure definition (Fig 12) plus the ecology meta-analysis; it has not been tested forward over a fixed habitat actor set. Keep it `specified`, candidate.
- **Falsifier:** if each outcome requires an atom the others do not (a pathogen present only for disease, a predator present only for food-web links), then the outcomes are not isomers over one set. The weaker **functional-group** claim (Fig 12 :123: *"functional group. A substructure whose presence fixes the outcome. The group says what kind; the qualifier says which member."*) stands instead: the contact cascade is the group, the particular actor is the qualifier.

### Q4 - What is the order?

Dependency order, from the figures:

1. **Substitution before contact.** Fig 11 :66-67. The range shift substitutes the habitat; the new actor set becomes live. Without this step there is no new contact.
2. **Contact before cascade.** Fig 11 :52-53. First-order contact is enumerated, then the second- and third-order effects. Scoping only the first order is the documented field error.
3. **Cascade before outcome label.** A "new disease risk" is named only after the transmission path is observed; the reading does not determine the source (`05` §4 metamerism).
4. **Refusal before mint.** Formula step 2: a claim held ("no new risk because none detected") that persists and does not trend down owes a refusal (¬), not an explanation. Fig 8 :39: *"So a search that finds nothing is never a proof of absence."*
5. **Settlement before composition.** Fig 12 :104: *"A settled molecule becomes an atom for whatever composes over it."* A recorded new interaction can serve as an atom for predicting the next shift only after the ground settles it, not merely after a model reports it.
6. **Introduction is one-way.** Fig 11 :61-62. Where a seat acts, it can add an actor and cannot subtract the habitat. The order of management matters irreversibly.

- **Falsifier for the order:** a documented case in which the third-order outcome is correctly named before first-order contact is enumerated, with no loss, would show the order is a recommendation rather than a dependency.

## CONCLUSION

The three outcomes are a **union compound** over a substituted habitat (Q1), each carried by a **different bond** so an ingredient list is insufficient (Q2), and they form an **isomer claim over a fixed actor set** with a functional-group fallback (Q3). Their order is fixed by dependency: substitution, contact, cascade, label, refusal, settlement, and one-way introduction (Q4). This rescues the checkable structure in the question without naming which species or pathogen occupies a slot. `metric-like source named from a reading` remains unlicensed; "the outcomes are bond-distinct isomers over one recipient actor set" is a checkable structural claim with a stated falsifier.
