# Expert verification packet - the Tether on ECO-0039

**What this is:** a claim in a form an ecologist or disease ecologist can check. Mode, derivation and refuters are stated before the conclusion, so a reader knows what to look for and what would settle it.

**Mode: `specified` - not `grounded`.** No field measurement is claimed. This is a shape and a route, not a result.

## 1. The claim, in one paragraph

A climate-driven range shift is not a species acquiring new properties; it is a habitat substitution. The species lands in a new contact set and inherits actors it did not choose, and the new competition, disease risk and food-web connection are those inherited relations propagating outward as contact cascades. Whether an actor helps or harms is relative to what is being tracked, not a fixed property, and the three outcomes are a union compound carried by different bonds, so a list of newly co-occurring species is not enough. The framework returns a shape, not a name: which competitor, pathogen or trophic link lights up in a given system is settled by a measurement. What it offers is that shape, falsifiers written in advance, and the discriminating instruments that would close it.

## 2. Three checkable sub-claims

### C1 - framework-internal; checkable by reading Figure 11
- **Statement:** a range shift is a habitat substitution; new interactions are inherited contact relations that cascade beyond first order.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 11 :66 - *"Isolation is not removal of the habitat. It is substitution of one habitat for another."* Fig 11 :67 - *"Move the residual and you must reproduce everything its goal required, and you inherit the new habitat's actors, which you did not choose."* Fig 11 :52-53 - *"And contact cascades. Scoping the habitat to direct contact is how DDT reached the eagles: first-order enumeration, second-order effect, and the residual that mattered was three steps out."*
- **What to check:** is the substitution framing faithful to how range shifts are actually observed in your system? Are the consequential interactions first-order, or do they appear two or three steps out?
- **Refuter:** a documented shift into an actor-free habitat that still produces all three outcome classes with no inherited contact set; or a verified case where first-order contact accounts fully for all three outcomes.

### C2 - ecology-facing; checkable against the interaction literature
- **Statement:** an actor's sign is relative to the residual; mutualism, parasitism and competition are relations rather than kinds.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 11 :49 - *"An actor's valence is relative to the residual, not a property it carries."* Fig 11 :50 - *"The same actor is a benefactor to one residual and a malfactor to another, so mutualism and parasitism are relations rather than kinds."*
- **What to check:** is the context-dependence claim faithful? External corroboration (Chamberlain, Bronstein and Rudgers 2014, *Ecology Letters* 17:881-890, meta-analysis of 247 articles) found *"Interaction outcomes are context-dependent when the sign and/or magnitude change as a function of the biotic or abiotic context."* Is that your experience in range-shift systems?
- **Refuter:** a demonstration that interaction signs are invariant species properties across residuals and contexts, with no mutualism-parasitism continuum.

### C3 - framework-internal derivation with an ecology-facing check
- **Statement:** competition, disease and food-web outcomes are a union compound carried by different bonds, so a flat species list is insufficient; report per slot.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 9 :55 - *"A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones."* Fig 12 :117 - *"Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."* Fig 1 :19 - *"The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated."*
- **What to check:** in your system, do the three outcome classes overlap in actors and mechanisms (a new predator that also competes, a pathogen that is also a food-web link)? If they are disjoint, the union claim fails. Would an aggregate richness or mean-interaction metric hide which slot is live?
- **Refuter:** show that the three mechanisms are disjoint in general with empty intersections, so three partitions are correct; or show an aggregate index preserves the per-slot location of novel interactions across systems.

## 3. What is explicitly not claimed

- Not a prediction of which species acquires which pathogen, competitor or predator in a named place.
- Not that every range shift produces all three outcomes; detection failure is not absence, and absence is not proof of no risk (Fig 8 :39).
- Not trait determinism; the recipient habitat must be enumerated (Fig 11 :43).
- Not that agreement among models establishes a risk; shared-data models are one evidence pool (Fig 2 :40).
- Not a reversible management recipe; introduction is available and exclusion is not (Fig 11 :61-62).
- Not `grounded`. Nothing here is asserted as settled by experiment.
- Not that "network" is *the* structure of food-web connections. Fig 13 :24 names it as one of six structures; selecting a particular link is a binding, not a derivation.

## 5. What would settle it

A discriminating measurement, not a further argument. From *The Hard Problem is About Source*: an instrument that reads green *"cannot comment on the source"*; the spectrometer resolved it. Here:

1. Does the new interaction appear only after an enumerated contact chain of the predicted order? A field time series or tracer study settles this.
2. Is the observed decline competition, apparent competition through a shared predator, or a vectored pathogen? An exclusion experiment, pathogen screen or trophic-tracer measurement names the source.
3. Is the actor's sign context-dependent? Reciprocal transplant or common-garden trials across residuals test C2.
