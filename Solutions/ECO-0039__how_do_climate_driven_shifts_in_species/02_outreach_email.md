**Subject:** One checkable claim about range shifts and new species interactions

> Hi [name],
>
> I have one specific output I would like you to check against what you already know about range shifts. I am not asking you to read any theory first, and I am not asking you to agree. If the output does not survive, there is nothing else to look at.
>
> **The claim (`specified`, not `grounded`):** a climate-driven range shift is not a species acquiring new properties. It is a **habitat substitution**. The species lands in a new contact set and inherits actors it did not choose: the resident competitor, the local pathogen, the resident predator or prey. The new competition, disease risk and food-web connection are those inherited relations. They propagate as **contact cascades**, so first-order enumeration at the new range edge routinely misses the effect that matters three steps out (the DDT-to-eagles pattern). Each actor's sign is relative to what you are tracking, not a property it carries: the same species is a resource to one residual and a threat to another. Competition, disease and food-web outcomes are therefore **one union compound**, not three separate impact categories.
>
> **What is deliberately not claimed:** that any particular species will gain any particular pathogen, competitor or predator. The framework returns a shape, not a name. The specific case is settled by a measurement, not by the derivation. I am also not claiming that model agreement establishes a risk; models fed the same occurrence records are one evidence pool, not two witnesses.
>
> **What would refute the shape claim (written before I sent this):**
> 1. a range shift into an actor-free habitat that still produces all three outcome classes with no inherited contact set;
> 2. a trait-only rule that predicts all three outcome classes across novel habitats with no enumeration of the recipient habitat;
> 3. a demonstration that mutualism, parasitism and competition signs are fixed species properties, with no context dependence;
> 4. a demonstration that competition, disease and food-web mechanisms are disjoint in general, with empty intersections.
>
> If any one holds, I will retract the claim. A one-line "this fails because..." is more useful to me than agreement. If you would rather not engage, no reply is needed.
>
> **If it survives, and only then:** the sources are below. The figures are the authority, not my summary of them.
> - Figures and formula with line numbers (local extract, branch `general-agent`): local: FRAMEWORK/reference/03-FIGURES_TEXT.md
> - Public figures folder: https://github.com/IsaiahN/tether/tree/general-agent/figures
> - Canonical usage rule: https://github.com/IsaiahN/tether/blob/general-agent/figures/README.md
>
> **If you want to use the machinery:** the formula is in the figures link. Run it on a range shift you know well and can check against a field record. Write down what would refute your result *before* you run it, and label the mode of what you get. A reported failure is more useful to me than a reported success.
