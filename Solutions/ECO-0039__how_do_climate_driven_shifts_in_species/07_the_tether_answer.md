# The Tether's answer on range shifts and new species interactions

**Mode: `specified` - not `grounded`.** This is a shape and a route, not a field result. Simulated derivation checked against the figures (`05_what_shape_means.md` §6), not a ground reading.

## 1. The residual it answers

A species range edge relocates. The old prediction of that species' relations no longer matches the new location. The residual is the set of relations missed per species-slot and per interaction-slot: a competitor not formerly met, a pathogen not formerly encountered, a predator or prey not formerly connected (Fig 1 :19 - *"The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated."*). The question asks by what structure these novel interactions arise, and the three named outcomes are a union compound, not one hard problem split into three easy ones (Fig 9 :55).

## 2. The shape

1. **A range shift is a habitat substitution.** The species lands in a new contact set and inherits actors it did not choose. Fig 11 :66 - *"Isolation is not removal of the habitat. It is substitution of one habitat for another."* Fig 11 :67 - *"Move the residual and you must reproduce everything its goal required, and you inherit the new habitat's actors, which you did not choose."* The "new competition, disease risks, food-web connections" are those inherited relations.
2. **They propagate as contact cascades.** First-order enumeration at the new edge routinely misses the effect three steps out. Fig 11 :52-53 - *"And contact cascades. Scoping the habitat to direct contact is how DDT reached the eagles: first-order enumeration, second-order effect, and the residual that mattered was three steps out."*
3. **Valence is relative to the residual.** Fig 11 :49 - *"An actor's valence is relative to the residual, not a property it carries."* Fig 11 :50 - *"The same actor is a benefactor to one residual and a malfactor to another, so mutualism and parasitism are relations rather than kinds."* A species is not intrinsically "a disease risk" or "a new competitor."
4. **Enumerate the habitat; do not compose it from traits.** Fig 11 :43 - *"The habitat is enumerated, never composed."* Read the recipient actor, condition and relation list off the world and extend it outward until the cascade stops mattering (Fig 11 :44).
5. **The three outcomes are a union compound, each with a different bond.** Competition is a threshold comparison, a food-web link is a directed production or sequence, disease is an ordered transmission along a contact path (Fig 12 :117, *"Same atoms, different bond, different substance"*). A flat list of arriving species records ingredients, not arrangement.
6. **Report per slot, never as an average.** Fig 1 :19 and :28 - *"The gap is measured per object, not per step. A system that averages across objects has thrown away the only signal that says where to look."* Because valence is relative, averaging cancels live positive and negative outcomes.
7. **The particular case is not named by the framework.** Fig 9 :32 - *"you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself."* Which competitor, pathogen or trophic link appears in a given shift is settled by a measurement, not by the derivation.

## 3. What it explicitly does not say

- Not a catalogue of predicted species impacts. The framework gives the shape, not the name.
- Not "every range shift creates all three." Whether a given shift has a live disease pathway, a competitor or a trophic rewire is a ground question. Absence of detection is not proof of absence (Fig 8 :39).
- Not trait determinism. A fixed species-trait table is not sufficient; the recipient habitat must be enumerated.
- Not that model agreement establishes a risk. Models fed shared occurrence records are one evidence pool (Fig 2 :40).
- Not a management recipe with recall. Introduction is available and exclusion is not (Fig 11 :61-62).
- Not `grounded`. No field measurement is claimed.

## 4. What would settle it - the ground

Fig 2 :29-30: *"An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks. A measurement, an experiment, a counter, a market, a patient outcome. If it moves when you push on it, it is not an anchor."*

- Does the new interaction appear only after an enumerated contact chain, and does the chain's length match the predicted first/second/third order? A field time series or a tracer study settles this.
- Is the observed decline competition, apparent competition through a shared predator, or a vectored pathogen? A discriminator (exclusion experiment, pathogen screen, stable-isotope or trophic-tracer work) names the source where the bare reading cannot.
- Is the actor's sign context-dependent? Reciprocal transplant or common-garden trials across residuals test the relation-not-kind claim.
- Standing falsifiers from `01` §4: an actor-free habitat producing all three outcomes; a trait-only rule that predicts all three with no enumeration; invariant signs with no continuum; disjoint mechanisms with empty intersections.

## 5. The honest stop

**Shape reached, case unreached.** The figures close the structural question: substitution, cascade, relative valence, per-slot residual, union compound, ground settlement. They do not close the empirical question of which species and which pathogen in which place. The framework *"stops at the limit of what the available instruments can distinguish, and records the lineage so the stop is honest."* The answer is `specified`, ungrounded and a candidate. Prestige or agreement is not an instrument (Fig 2 :35).

## 6. One-paragraph version for outreach

> The Tether does not predict that a named species will acquire a named pathogen or competitor. It says that a climate-driven range shift is a habitat substitution: the species lands in a new contact set and inherits actors it did not choose, and the new competition, disease risk and food-web connection are those relations propagating outward as contact cascades, so first-order scoping misses the effect that matters three steps out. Whether an arriving species helps or harms depends on what you are tracking, not on a fixed property it carries, and the three outcomes are a union compound carried by different bonds, so an ingredient list of new species is not enough. The framework reports the residual per species- and interaction-slot and returns a shape, not a name; which link lights up in a given valley is settled by a measurement, not by this derivation. What it offers is that shape, falsifiers written in advance, and the discriminating instruments that would close it.
