## 0. The residual under audit

> **Question (ecology.txt line 48):** How do climate-driven shifts in species ranges create new competition, disease risks, or food-web connections?

Stated as an effect: a species' range edge relocates. The library Γ predicted that species' relations in its old setting. In the new setting Γ(b,a) no longer matches o′. The positive part of the residual on each slot is the set of relations the old picture missed: a competitor not formerly met, a pathogen not formerly carried, a predator or prey not formerly connected, a mutualist not formerly available.

Arity, symmetry, scale (Fig 9 :22):

- **Arity.** Three outcomes are named (competition, disease risks, food-web connections). This is tested in F6 and in `06_isomers_and_order.md`: they are a **union of overlapping conditions**, not three independent questions.
- **Symmetry.** These relations are typed and ordered, not a symmetric "both hold." Predation is a directed production/sequence (one consumes the other), competition is a threshold comparison between two quantities, transmission runs along a contact path. The bond matters, not the ingredient list (Fig 12 :117).
- **Scale.** First order is direct contact at the new range edge. Second and third order are the cascade outward (Fig 11 :52-53). A scoping that stops at first order misses the residual that matters.
- **Indexing.** R is measured per species-slot and per interaction-slot, never as one global biodiversity impact (Fig 1 :19, :28). This is the first thing an averaged "net effect of range shifts" discards.

## 3. Findings F1 to F10

### F1 - A range shift is a habitat substitution, not a removal (decisive mechanism)

Fig 11 :66:
> **"Isolation is not removal of the habitat. It is substitution of one habitat for another."**

Fig 11 :67:
> **"Move the residual and you must reproduce everything its goal required, and you inherit the new habitat's actors, which you did not choose."**

Fig 11 :69:
> **"what you unintentionally introduced is invisible until it acts"**

The species that shifts does not delete its old habitat; it lands in a substitute and inherits actors it did not select. The "new competition, disease risks, or food-web connections" in the question are exactly these inherited actors: the resident competitor, the local pathogen, the resident predator or prey. The mechanism is therefore *not* "climate changes a species and new interactions appear." It is "the species changes address, the habitat is substituted, and the new habitat's contact set becomes live." This is the single claim the rest of the answer hangs on.

- **Mode:** `specified` (a structural claim, ungrounded for any particular species).
- **Refuter:** a documented range shift into a genuinely actor-free habitat (no resident competitors, pathogens, predators, prey or mutualists, and no second-order contact) would break the claim. Also broken if the new interactions can be shown to arise with no change of contact set.

### F2 - The new interactions are contact cascades, not direct contact

Fig 11 :52-53:
> **"And contact cascades. Scoping the habitat to direct contact is how DDT reached the eagles: first-order enumeration, second-order effect, and the residual that mattered was three steps out."**

Fig 11 :70:
> **"A test harness is a substituted habitat. Which is why a synthetic solve proves wiring and never capability."**

Direct-contact enumeration at the new range edge is first order. Competition, disease and food-web effects normally propagate further: the arriving species changes a resident's abundance, which changes a third species' resource, which changes a fourth. DDT reached eagles three steps out. A transplant or mesocosm that shows the wiring of one new pair proves the mechanism of contact, not the whole cascade. This is why single-pair interaction trials routinely under-predict what a range shift does (corroborated by Alexander, Diez and Levine 2015, fetched 2026-11-23: *"these direct responses can be overwhelmed by indirect effects mediated via other interacting community members"*).

- **Mode:** `general` for the cascade shape, `specified` for the claim that first-order scoping is the common field error.
- **Refuter:** a case where all three outcome classes are fully accounted for by first-order contact, with no cascade beyond direct neighbors, would narrow the claim to that system; a general proof of closure at first order would break it.

### F3 - Valence is relative to the residual, not a property of the arriving species

Fig 11 :49:
> **"An actor's valence is relative to the residual, not a property it carries."**

Fig 11 :50:
> **"The same actor is a benefactor to one residual and a malfactor to another, so mutualism and parasitism are relations rather than kinds."**

Fig 11 :51:
> **"No standing list of good actors survives a change of residual."**

"New disease risk" and "new food-web connection" are not kinds of species. They are relations that light up against a particular residual. The same arriving ungulate is a resource to one predator, a competitor to one herbivore, a host to one parasite and an irrelevance to a fourth. Calling the arriver "a disease risk" names a relation as if it were an intrinsic property. This is corroborated from a low-overlap field: Chamberlain, Bronstein and Rudgers (2014, *Ecology Letters*, fetched 2026-11-23) found by meta-analysis across 247 articles that interaction outcomes vary in sign and magnitude with context, with mutualism and parasitism on a continuum rather than fixed categories. Fig 11 :50 and that meta-analysis are two frames with low evidentiary overlap, so this verdict is **triangulated**.

- **Mode:** `specified`.
- **Refuter:** a demonstration that an actor's sign is invariant across residuals and contexts, with no continuum, would break it (the Chamberlain result already points the other way).

### F4 - The habitat is enumerated, never composed

Fig 11 :43-44:
> **"The habitat is enumerated, never composed."**
> **"List everything in contact with the residual, then what is in contact with those, and outward until the cascade stops mattering."**

Fig 11 :45-47:
> **"actors: what acts on it, and what it acts on"**
> **"conditions: light, water, temperature, season, terrain"**
> **"relations: pollination, predation, competition, dependency"**

Fig 11 :48:
> **"You do not invent the list. You read it off the world, and what you cannot perceive or measure yet is the residual."**

You cannot derive the new interactions from a table of the arriving species' traits. The list must be read off the recipient habitat and extended until the cascade stops mattering. This is the figure-level reason trait-based forecasts stumble precisely where interactions are novel (the "call for experimental macroecology" literature, fetched 2026-11-23, notes that observational inference fails when species compete under novel climates or with novel competitors). Fig 13 :24 (*"that entities relate, and influence travels"*) names network as one of six structures, but it does not select which observed interaction is "the network." Naming a particular link that would be a binding, not a derivation (see §5).

- **Mode:** `general` (enumeration rule), `specified` (its failure in novel communities).
- **Refuter:** a general function from a fixed trait list to all three outcome classes across novel habitats, with no habitat enumeration, would break the rule.

### F5 - Report per slot, never as an aggregate

Fig 1 :19:
> **"The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated."**

Fig 1 :28:
> **"The gap is measured per object, not per step. A system that averages across objects has thrown away the only signal that says where to look."**

A single headline number such as "net change in regional species richness" or "average interaction strength" is a legal summary statistic but it is not the residual. It tells you neither which species is newly exposed nor which pathway to probe. Because valence is relative to the residual (F3), aggregation is worse here than usual: positive and negative outcomes cancel in the mean while both remain live on their own slots.

- **Mode:** `general`.
- **Refuter:** a demonstration that an aggregate index preserves the per-slot ranking of where novel interactions appear, across systems, would show the warning is conservative here.

### F6 - The three outcomes are a union compound, not a partition

Fig 9 :55:
> **"A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones."**

Fig 9 :57-58:
> **"a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either. It stops being a mixture and becomes a specific compound."**

A new predator is at once a food-web connection and a source of competition; a novel pathogen is at once a parasitic food-web link and a disease risk. Running three independent impact assessments answers the easy, single-condition regions three times and leaves the intersections standing, because the second condition still binds. What survives a lever applied to only one category is smaller and *categorically different*: a specific compound with its own loop. This is worked out with a falsifier in `06_isomers_and_order.md`.

- **Mode:** `specified`.
- **Refuter:** show that competition, disease and food-web outcomes have disjoint mechanisms and disjoint actor sets in general, so the intersections are empty; then three partitions are correct.

### F7 - The anchor is the ground, never agreement among models

Fig 2 :29-30:
> **"An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks. A measurement, an experiment, a counter, a market, a patient outcome. If it moves when you push on it, it is not an anchor."**

Fig 2 :35:
> **"Alignment is triangulation, not negotiation. A mirror cannot correct you."**

Fig 2 :40:
> **"agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool"**

Fig 8 :39:
> **"So a search that finds nothing is never a proof of absence."**

Models of range shifts, occurrence databases and trait syntheses largely draw on the same occurrence and climate records. Their agreement is one evidence pool talking (Fig 2 :40). The anchor is a measurement: a new interaction observed, a pathogen detected in a novel host, a trophic link recorded. Absence of a detected disease jump does not establish absence of risk; the detection instrument may simply not resolve it (Fig 8 :39). Buy independence in proportion to the cost of being wrong (Fig 2 :42).

- **Mode:** `general`.
- **Refuter:** an anchor that moves when frames push on it is not an anchor; if the "observed new interaction" turns out to be an artifact of the sampling frame, the claim of settlement is void.

### F8 - Management has asymmetric power: introduction is available, exclusion is not

Fig 11 :61-62:
> **"The seat's power is asymmetric. Introduction is available; exclusion is not. You can add an actor and you cannot subtract the habitat, because contact is a fact about the world and does not consult you."**

Fig 11 :63:
> **"Which is why a managed ecosystem is permanent work rather than a solved state."**

Fig 11 :65:
> **"Introducing an actor for its cascade is ecosystem engineering, after Jones, Lawton and Shachak, 1994. Cane toads and kudzu are the same move with the cascade unenumerated."**

Assisted migration and rewilding are introductions. You can add an actor; you cannot subtract the recipient habitat or recall its contact set. When the cascade is not enumerated, the move is the cane-toad case: an introduction whose consequences were invisible until they acted. Where a human seat exists, this is Fig 11's *maintained* panel, and the work is permanent, not a solved state.

- **Mode:** `specified` for the asymmetry claim, triangulated between Fig 11 and the invasion/range-shift comparison literature (fetched 2026-11-23), which warns that assisted-migration decisions made without considering biotic interactions can make range shifts invasions.

### F10 - The honest stop

Fig 9 :32:
> **"you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself"**

The figures return the shape of the answer: habitat substitution, contact cascade, relative valence, per-slot residual, union compound, ground settlement. They do not name which competitor, which pathogen or which trophic link will appear in any given range shift. Naming those is a binding from the field frame, provenanced as such and settled only by the ground. The result is `specified`, ungrounded and a candidate. *"Unreachable is a verdict, not a silence"* (Fig 13), here in the milder form: the general shape is reached; the specific case awaits an instrument.

## 4. Pre-registered refuter

Written before the conclusion, per the standing instruction: a falsifier written before the run is a design instrument. The shape claim is refuted if any of the following holds:

1. A documented range shift into an actor-free habitat produces all three outcome classes with no inherited contact set (breaks F1).
2. A general trait-only function predicts all three outcome classes across novel habitats with no enumeration of the recipient habitat (breaks F4).
3. Interaction signs are shown to be invariant properties of species across residuals and contexts, with no mutualism-parasitism continuum (breaks F3).
4. Competition, disease and food-web mechanisms are shown to be disjoint in general, with empty intersections (breaks F6).
5. A single aggregate index is shown to preserve the per-slot location of novel interactions across systems (weakens F5).
6. A range shift is shown to generate new interactions with no change of contact at any order (breaks F1 and F2 together).

Until one of these is run, no part of this may be labelled `grounded`.

## 6. Recommendation

1. Lead with the shape claim and the pre-registered refuter, not with a list of predicted species impacts (Fig 10 :52, *"Bring a characterised residual, never the source."*).
2. Enumerate the recipient habitat per species-slot outward until the cascade stops mattering; report per slot, not as an average (Fig 11 :43-44; Fig 1 :19).
3. Treat competition, disease and food-web outcomes as one union compound with its own loop, not three independent assessments (Fig 9 :55).
4. Range every claim against a measurement. Absence of detection is not absence of risk (Fig 8 :39); agreement among shared-data models is one witness (Fig 2 :40).
5. Where a human seat is involved (assisted migration), name the cascade before the introduction; exclusion is not available afterwards (Fig 11 :61-65).
6. Send the generator (the derivation and the refuter), not a recording of one case (Fig 4 :22-25).

Verdict: **the shape survives, the specific bindings are not licensed.** Mode `specified`, ungrounded, candidate.
