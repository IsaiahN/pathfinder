# Expert verification packet: the Tether on identifiable versus distributed coding

**What this is:** a claim in a form a neural-coding researcher can check. Mode, derivation and refuters are stated before the conclusion, so a reader knows what to look for and what would settle it.

**Mode: `general` on the structural shape, `specified` on the union claim, not `grounded`.** No measurement is claimed. This is a shape and a route, not a result.

## 1. The claim, in one paragraph

The either/or in the question is a bundled union, not a partition. An adequate neural representation has identifiable relata and typed relations between them: a population pattern with no relata is an ordering over nothing, and a single unit with no relation to any other unit has recorded only half of what carries the content. Identifiability is level-relative, because a settled population pattern becomes a single operand at the next level. Two contents over the same unit set are isomers if the typed relation differs. The framework returns that shape, not a name, so it makes no claim about which coding scheme the brain actually uses. Four refuters are written below and were written before this packet.

## 2. Three checkable sub-claims

### C1: framework-internal; checkable by reading Figures 9, 12 and 13

- **Statement:** a representation needs both atoms and typed bonds; the union is not a partition to be decided.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 12 :5: **"A set of ingredients is not one. The bond is the rest of the information, and it is the half nothing was recording."** Also Fig 13 :29: **"A structure with no state is empty: an ordering over nothing, a network with nothing at the nodes."** Also Fig 9 :54: **"And when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again."**
- **Refuter:** exhibit a settled representation that is a single atom with no typed bond and carries content at the ground; or one that is pure relation with no identifiable relatum at any level; or show the two conditions are a partition in the brain.

### C2: neuroscience-facing; checkable against the coding literature

- **Statement:** the selective single-unit evidence and the population-code evidence are relata and relations respectively, not two rival mechanisms that exclude each other. The single-unit result is evidence for sparseness and invariance, not for a grandmother code, and population coding is a joint-activity scheme whose relations carry information.
- **What to check:** the "Grandmother cell" article states *"The opposite of the grandmother cell theory is the distributed representation theory, that states that a specific stimulus is coded by its unique pattern of activity over a large group of neurons widely distributed in the brain."* It also reports that the researchers *"believe that they have found evidence for sparseness, rather than for grandmother cells."* The "Neural coding" article defines population coding as *"a method to represent stimuli by using the joint activities of a number of neurons."* Are those characterizations faithful? Does any source actually establish mutual exclusivity?
- **Refuter:** a demonstration that the two conditions are mutually exclusive and exhaustive in the brain, so a code cannot have both relata and relations. Failing that, cite a result that settles one side at the ground with a source-discriminating instrument.

### C3: framework-internal derivation, level-relativity and isomers

- **Statement:** the level at which a representation is called irreducible is local, and two contents over the same unit set differ as isomers when the bond differs.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 12 :104: **"A settled molecule becomes an atom for whatever composes over it. So irreducible is a fact about a level and never about the world"**. Fig 12 :117: **"Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."**
- **Refuter:** show a settled representation that cannot be demoted to an atom for a higher composition; or show two distinct contents with the same atom set and the same bond set, which kills the isomer claim and leaves only a shared-substrate claim.

## 5. What would settle it

A source-discriminating instrument, not a further argument. From *The Hard Problem is About Source*: an instrument that reads green *"cannot comment on the source"*; the spectrometer resolved the analogous case. Here:

1. **Necessity test:** silence or lesion a unit claimed to carry a content while holding the population projection fixed. Behaviour unchanged refutes pure localist atoms; a selective change refutes pure distributed relations.
2. **Bond-discrimination test:** hold the active unit set fixed and vary the typed relation. A content change confirms the bond carries information the operands do not; no change refutes the isomer claim.
3. **Level test:** read the same physical state at two levels and ask whether the population pattern at one level serves as a single operand at the next without changing the ground reading.
