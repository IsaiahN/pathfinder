# Atoms, bonds, isomers and level order in neural coding

**Mode: `specified` on the isomer and level claims, `general` on the structural requirement.** This is a simulated derivation checked against the figures, not a ground reading (see `05_what_shape_means.md` §7).

## RESIDUAL

The literature reports two things at once: invariant single-unit responses, and population patterns in which no single unit is necessary. Are these different substances, two readings of one substance, or variants of one thing whose notation cannot tell them apart? Three questions are owed: (1) what is the atom and what is the bond here, (2) are two contents over the same cellular set isomers, and (3) what is the order of levels?

## DOMAIN CENSUS

Atoms held: identifiable relata (a unit, a small ensemble, a state at a level). Bonds held: the seven typed relations, including `+`, `→`, `−`, `⋛`, plus negation. ## DERIVATION

### Q1: Is the atom the neuron or the state? CORRECTION TO MY OWN §3 of `05`

- Fig 13 :9-14 lists space, time, energy as *states* and Fig 13 :19-24 lists spectrum, gradient, network as *structures*. A neuron is not one of the six. It is an instance read from the neuroscience frame onto an atom slot.
- Fig 12 :104: **"A settled molecule becomes an atom for whatever composes over it. So irreducible is a fact about a level and never about the world"**.
- Therefore the atom is **level-indexed**: a unit at one level, a settled population pattern at the next. Asking "is the atom the neuron?" is asking at which level the reading is taken.
- **I overstated §3 of `05`.** "Shape, not a name" governs a missing primitive, not the selection of a relatum already in the substrate. Retracted as applied to atoms and bonds. Triangulated (README + Fig 13).

### Q2: Are two contents over the same cells isomers?

- Fig 12 :117: **"Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."**
- Fig 12 :92: **"Symmetry is what decides whether an arrangement is a new thing."**
- If content X and content Y are implemented over overlapping unit sets and differ only in the typed relation among those units, then by Fig 12 they are **isomers**: same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in a list of firing units and decisive in behaviour.
- The weaker reading: if X and Y merely share the form `relata → relation → relata`, they share a **functional group**, Fig 12 :123: **"A substructure whose presence fixes the outcome. The group says what kind; the qualifier says which member."**
- **Falsifier, written before the run:** if X and Y can be written with the same atom set **and** the same bond set, the isomer claim fails and only the shared-substrate claim remains.

### Q3: What is the order of levels?

- Fig 13 :29: **"A structure with no state is empty: an ordering over nothing, a network with nothing at the nodes."** A connection matrix with no relata is not yet a representation.
- Fig 13 :30: **"So each step acts on one kind and yields the other, and every link in the chain is changed by the link before it."** States and structures alternate; the order is lockstep, not ranked.
- Fig 12 :104 again permits a settled arrangement to become an atom at the next level. So the order is: relata (state-like) then typed relation (structure-like) then a settled molecule, which becomes a relatum for the next composition.
- Applied to the world sources: a population code is described in the "Neural coding" article as noisy single neurons whose joint activity yields a value, and as constrained to low-dimensional manifolds. A low-dimensional manifold at one level is a single identifiable operand at the next. That is the recursion in the world's own vocabulary. `specified`, unmeasured.

### Q4: Does the binding problem name the missing bond?

- The "Grandmother cell" article names the binding problem: how features dissected into separate modules are re-integrated into a seamless whole. In the figures' terms this is a bond question, not an atom question. The ingredients are recorded; the typed relation between them is not.
- Fig 12 :111: **"A vocabulary without bonds can name a state and cannot name a route to one."** An atom-only readout can say what is present and cannot say how it composes.
- Fig 12 :83-89 gives six yes/no tests separating the seven bonds. Which bond holds is not recoverable from the operands. So the binding problem is not solved by listing more neurons; it needs an instrument that discriminates the bond.
- **Strain, named:** the figures' seven bonds are a closed set derived from composition, and neuroscience does not yet say which of them a given circuit instantiates. Mapping a neural mechanism onto a specific bond is a binding from the neuroscience frame, not a derivation from the six.

### Q5: Analogues that bear

- **Chemistry, isomerism.** Fig 12 :97-98: *"silver fulminate and silver cyanate have the same composition and different properties, which is what forced structure into the notation."* This is the load-bearing analogue: a formula listing ingredients could not distinguish two substances, so structure was written into the notation. A neural code listing active units has the same defect.
- **Chemistry, catalyst.** Fig 12 :119: **"A part that survives its own use. Spent and regenerating parts behave differently across uses, and nothing records which a term is."** A unit that remains selective across repeated trials and one that adapts away are different terms even with identical instantaneous activity. The record of use is part of the term.
- **Neuroscience, manifold.** The "Neural coding" article reports population activity constrained to low-dimensional manifolds and attractors. A manifold is a structure; the position along it is a state. Fig 13 :74: **"Everything else is a composition across the two kinds, never within one."** Triangulated for the shape; the specific identification of a manifold with a bond type is unmeasured.

## CONCLUSION

The atoms are level-indexed relata. The bonds are typed relations among them. Two contents over the same unit set are isomers if their bond sets differ, and share only a functional group if the form is shared but the relata differ. The order runs relata then relation then settled molecule, with that molecule becoming a relatum at the next level. The binding problem is a bond question and needs an instrument, not a longer list of units.

This rescues what is defensible in both sides of the debate without choosing either: selective units are real relata, population patterns are real relations, and the level at which either is called irreducible is a local fact. No seventh primitive, no import that moves the wall.
