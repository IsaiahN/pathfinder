# Outreach draft: check the shape claim before you decide about the framework

**Use:** one-to-one, or as a cover letter attached to a submission through a public venue in `03_public_contact_points.csv`. **Not** for bulk send.

**Hard precondition:** do not send until a reader can answer the refuter in stage 1 without seeing the figures. If the output cannot survive on its own, the two-stage offer collapses on contact.

---

**Subject:** One checkable claim about localist versus distributed coding

> Hi [name],
>
> I have one specific claim I would like you to check against what you already know. I am not asking you to read a theory first, and I am not asking you to agree with it. If the claim does not survive, there is nothing else to look at.
>
> **The residual, in effect terms.** The question I am working on asks whether the brain encodes information in identifiable neural representations, or whether information is distributed across dynamics and connections. There is a real observation on both sides: selective single-unit responses that survive transformation of the stimulus, and population patterns in which no single unit is necessary. What I need checked is whether these are two competing mechanisms or two readings of one composition.
>
> **The shape claim (`general` on the structure, `specified` on the union).** An adequate answer needs both identifiable relata and typed relations between them. A population pattern with no identifiable units is an ordering over nothing. A single unit with no relation to any other unit has recorded only half of what carries the content. So the either/or in the question is a bundled union, not a partition. Which level you call "identifiable" is a fact about that level, not about the world: a population pattern settled at one level becomes a single operand at the next. This is a shape any candidate answer must have. It is not a measurement, and it is not a claim that I know which coding scheme the brain uses.
>
> **What I am not claiming.** Not that grandmother cells are correct. Not that pure distributed dynamics are empty of information. Not that the six-term substrate is the brain's own list of primitives. Not that a new experiment was run. The framework returns a kind of thing, never the answer itself.
>
> **The refuter, written before this email was sent. The claim fails if:**
> 1. a settled representation is shown that is a single unit with no typed relation to any other unit, and it carries content at the ground;
> 2. a settled representation is shown that is pure relation with no identifiable relatum at any level;
> 3. the two conditions are shown to be a partition in the brain, mutually exclusive and exhaustive, rather than a union in the description;
> 4. a source-discriminating instrument gives the same reading for both encodings where the ground distinguishes them.
>
> If any of those holds, I will retract the claim. A one-line "this fails because..." is more useful to me than agreement. If you would rather not engage, no reply is needed.
>
> **What I would like from you:** what would *you* count as a failure of this shape claim? If you can name one I have not listed, that is the most useful reply. Please record that answer before reading anything below.
>
> **If it survives, and only then:** the sources are below. The figures, not my summary of them, are the authority.
> - Verbatim figures with line numbers (local project extract): `FRAMEWORK/reference/03-FIGURES_TEXT.md`. See Fig 12 :5, :104, :117 and Fig 13 :29, :74-75.
> - Canonical usage rule (local project copy): `FRAMEWORK/reference/FIGURES_README_canonical.md`.
> - The eight essays by Isaiah N: https://adventuresinml.substack.com/p/how-intelligence-actually-works
> - Neural coding background: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neural_coding
>
> **If you want to reuse the method:** the formula is in `FRAMEWORK/figures/THE_FORMULA.md`. Run it on a problem you know and can check against a ground. Write down what would refute your result *before* you run it, and state the mode of what you get. A reported failure is more useful to me than a reported success.
>
> Thanks,
> [name]
