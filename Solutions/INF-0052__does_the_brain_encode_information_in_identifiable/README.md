## Residual in effect terms

An observation is a pattern of neural activity (spikes, rates, timing, population states, connectivity). Two candidate encodings compete:

- **Option A, identifiable representations.** A specific unit or small set stands for a specific content. Effect: reading which unit fires names the content; silencing that unit should remove that content.
- **Option B, distributed dynamics and connections.** Content is carried by the joint activity of many units and by the relations among them. Effect: no single unit is necessary; content is recovered by decoding the population pattern and its dynamics.

The residual `R` is the gap between what each candidate predicts and what the observation shows, indexed per recorded unit or slot (Fig 1 :19, Fig 1 :22). The question asks which option the brain uses. The derivation finds that the `or` is a bundled union, not a partition, and that the distinction is level-relative.
