# Expert verification packet - statistical falsifiability

**What this is:** a claim in a form a statistician, philosopher of science or methodologist can check. Mode, derivation and refuters are stated before the conclusion, so a reader knows what to look for and what would settle it.

**Mode: `general` on the shape, `specified` on the three per-domain verdicts, `gap` on the threshold. Not `grounded`.** No statistical test was run for this derivation. This is a simulated derivation checked for internal consistency against the figures, not a ground reading, and it cannot settle the claim it reports on.

## 1. The claim, in one paragraph

"Falsifiable" for a statistical theory is a four-condition separator, not a binary property of its distribution and not a threshold crossing. The theory stakes a per-slot, low-probability refusal region before the run; the reading comes from an anchor the theory cannot move, through a channel that has not decayed or been cued; the refusal is checked by frames with different evidence pools; and non-refusal is reported as unreached at this budget, with the threshold treated as a decision convention rather than a truth verdict. Falsification is a negation, so "consistent with the data" does not carry it. No figure line selects a correct alpha, prior or test, so the numeric choice is a declared gap requiring a field-specific cost of error imported with provenance.

## 2. Three checkable sub-claims

### C1 - Framework-internal; checkable by reading Figures 1, 5 and the Operators table

- **Statement:** a statistical claim is falsifiable only if it carries a negation-capable, pre-committed refusal rule indexed per observable slot. A single global fit statistic and a fit-only vocabulary fail this.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 1 :19 - "The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated." Fig 1 :22 - "Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average." Fig 5 :53 - "refuted: a term we hold is wrong; a term is owed that says so". Fig 5 :66 - "Committing a prediction before a run is this, already working." Operators table :58 - "a refutation is a negation, which is why a vocabulary without it can only accumulate".
- **What to check:** is the per-slot indexing condition actually load-bearing for the theory you know, or does one scalar summary preserve the refusal information? Does the field's reporting vocabulary contain a refusal operator, or only degrees of consistency?
- **Refuter:** show that a single global statistic is sufficient for every bundle the theory predicts, or exhibit a genuine refutation of a theory that staked no low-probability region and was not rescued by any auxiliary. If either holds, C1 fails.

### C2 - Ground-facing; checkable by a methodologist against the test design

- **Statement:** the refusal region must be fixed before the data are seen, and the frames that check it must not draw on the same evidence pool as the prediction. Otherwise the test is a fit wearing a prediction's clothes.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 2 :29 - "An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks." Fig 9 :27-28 - "describe it before you go looking, or any frame you pick will seem to fit". Fig 8 :45 - "Two frames together reach what neither reaches alone." Fig 2 :40 - "disqualifying for a verdict: agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool". Fig 10 :24 - "the ground does not decay, the channel does".
- **What to check:** were the rejection region, statistic, exclusions and stopping rule declared before the data? Would a different dataset have produced the same region? Are the two instruments genuinely different evidence pools, or two analyses of one? In the Clever Hans analogue: was the questioner able to cue the reading?
- **Refuter:** exhibit a test whose refusal region is demonstrably data-independent and whose error rate is unchanged by contingent analyst choices, together with a case where two overlapping pools legitimately corroborate a verdict about the world. If overlap can be shown not to disqualify, C2 fails.

### C3 - Consequence-facing; checkable by anyone who reads a p-value

- **Statement:** a p-value is a comparison of data to a model, not a negation of the model; non-refusal is unreached rather than verified; the threshold allocates a decision budget and must not be promoted to a truth verdict.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 9 :60 - "Use filters for the budget and witnesses for the verdict. Never let a filter hand you a verdict." Fig 8 :39 - "So a search that finds nothing is never a proof of absence." Fig 10 :47 - "a convention that decides an outcome has stopped being one". Fig 12 :42 - "The acceptance test is a bargain, not a threshold." Fig 13 :134 - "Unreachable is a verdict here, not a silence".
- **What to check:** does the field report non-significance as support for the theory? Does crossing the threshold change a decision about publication or follow-up but not the truth claim? Is there an instrument that could discriminate among the sources of a mismatch (theory, channel, analysis choice, systematic, luck)?
- **Refuter:** show a statistical theory for which survival of all possible tests at a fixed budget entails truth with no further discriminating instrument, or show that crossing a declared threshold is a fact about the data-generating process with no convention left in the chain. If either holds, C3 fails.

## 3. What is explicitly not claimed

- Not a correct alpha, confidence level, Bayes factor threshold or prior. That is a declared `gap` (Fig 9 :32: "you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself").
- Not that frequentist testing is correct and Bayesian inference is not. They are different anchors and currencies; no figure line chooses between them.
- Not that probabilistic theories can never be refuted. They can be, relative to a fixed experimental context, as Vignero and Wenmackers show.
- Not that a non-significant result supports a theory. It reports a budget and a set of checked slots.
- Not that agreement among methodologists corroborates the claim. Fig 2 :40 disqualifies agreement from a shared pool as a verdict.
- Not that the six-term substrate decides any statistical question. The figures give the separator's shape only.
- Not grounded. Nothing here was settled by an experiment or a test.

## 5. What would settle it

A source-discriminating instrument, not a further argument. From *The Hard Problem is About Source*: an instrument that detects green "cannot comment on the source".

1. **Discriminator:** an instrument or design that separates a wrong theory from a decayed channel, a contingent analysis, an unmodelled systematic or luck at the live slot.
2. **Independent replication:** a dataset from a different evidence pool that refuses or fails to refuse the same staked region.
3. **Persistent residual:** a term whose residual "PERSISTS AND DOES NOT TREND DOWN" across new data, with the H0 tension as the live cosmological instance.
4. **Cost of error:** a field-specific cost imported with provenance and a shadow test, which would close the numeric gap without pretending the figures produced the number.

## 6. What an expert can check in twenty minutes

1. Read Fig 1 :19 and :22, Fig 5 :53 and :66, Fig 9 :60, Fig 10 :24 and :47, Fig 8 :39 and :45. Nine short passages.
2. Check C1 against the theory you know: are the residuals per observable, and is there a refusal operator in the reporting vocabulary?
3. Check C2 against one study you know well: were the rejection region, exclusions and stopping rule fixed before the data, and are the two instruments different pools?
4. Check C3: does the field report non-significance as support, and is the threshold being used as a decision or as a verdict?
5. Name one instrument that would discriminate the sources of a mismatch at the live slot, or say why none is possible.
6. Send the refutation. A failure is more useful than an endorsement.
