## QUESTION

Decide, in effect terms, what "falsifiable" requires of a theory whose prediction is a probability distribution rather than a point. Option A: a statistical theory is unfalsifiable because every finite sample has positive probability under it, so only degree-of-disconfirmation remains. Option B: a statistical theory is falsifiable whenever data fall below a pre-declared significance threshold, and non-significance confirms it. What each leads to: A dissolves the question into a verdict on mathematical form and makes almost all of physics unfalsifiable; B promotes a decision convention to a truth verdict, aggregates the residual into one number, spends the independence of the checker and reports non-refusal as verification. The figures route between them. Residual restated: what has to be in place for a statistical claim to come back wrong, and for the wrongness to be attributable to the claim rather than to the test, the channel or an auxiliary?

## DOMAIN CENSUS

R by arity, symmetry and scale, then one line per domain with actors / currency / anchor; partition or union; order by dependency.

- **R (the residual).** Arity: four conditions (pre-committed per-slot refusal region, non-updating anchor with reachable channel, independent frames, honest non-refusal). Symmetry: the distribution-to-observation comparison is ordered, not a symmetric join; the tail probability is an ordering (spectrum) and a threshold relation (Fig 12 comparison bond), not a conjunction. Scale: first order inside one experiment, second order across replications and instruments, with a lossy crossing between one reading and a distribution over readings (Fig 4 :27-32). Union, not a single hard question: a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either (Fig 9 :57).
- **D1, the statistical model frame.** Actors: parameters, priors, likelihoods, prediction distribution Γ, rejection region, sufficient statistic. Currency: probability mass and description length; what the theory stakes and what it costs to state. Anchor: the pre-committed test procedure, which must not move when the data move. Question type: partition boundary over the sample space (which outcomes are staked as refusal).
- **D2, the measurement frame.** Actors: instruments, datasets, trials, calibrations, systematics, pooled readings. Currency: observations o'. Anchor: the data-generating process, which does not update on what the theory thinks. Question type: union of signal plus channel decay plus auxiliary assumptions.
- **D3, the verification frame.** Actors: test protocol, replications, reviewers, conventions, journals. Currency: residuals and agreement. Anchor: the ground, never agreement. Question type: union of conditions with an unquantified shared fraction. In this simulated run all seats draw on one evidence pool, so this domain's independence is zero.
- **Order by dependency.** Bind the sought-for shape before the run (Fig 5 :66); keep the channel to the ground reachable (Fig 10 :24); refuse before minting an adjustment (Formula step 2); describe the residual before choosing a comparison frame (Fig 9 :53); settle before composing across replications (Fig 12 :104).

The apparent conflict between the threshold as a decision rule and Fig 10 :47 (a convention that decides an outcome has stopped being one) is kept live and narrowed: decision convention legitimate, truth verdict forbidden. ## VERDICT (from the figures)

"Falsifiable" for a statistical theory means the four-condition separator: a pre-committed, per-slot low-probability refusal region; a non-updating anchor reached through a channel that has not decayed or been cued; independent frames checking the refusal; and non-refusal reported as unreached at this budget rather than as verification. Falsification is a negation, so a fit-only vocabulary cannot carry it. Rival theories over a shared atom set are isomers (Fig 12 :117), and the shared test structure is a functional group (Fig 12 :123). The threshold is a filter for the decision budget, not a witness for the verdict, and no figure line returns a correct alpha, prior or test. D1 is `specified` and triangulated; D2 is `specified` and triangulated; D3 is `specified` and triangulated with a declared `gap`. The whole package is simulated and ungrounded.

- **cost if wrong:** bounded and locatable. The packet states its refuters before the conclusion and keeps verdicts per domain, so an error is findable rather than invisible. The real cost is spending the independence of outside readers by sending a framework recording instead of a generator, which Fig 2 :42 prices: "Buy independence in proportion to the cost of being wrong." That cost is avoidable by leading with the separator and its refuter.
- **undoable:** yes. A failed condition can be dropped without touching the others; `07_the_tether_answer.md` and `08_expert_verification_packet.md` already keep the conditions separate. A sent claim can be superseded by a corrected packet, and no figure term has to be withdrawn. The numeric gap stays open either way.
- **reversed by:** a ground reading, not agreement. An instrument that discriminates among the sources of a mismatch at the live slot would close the discriminator; a dataset from a different evidence pool refusing the same staked region would settle the replication; a term whose residual persists and does not trend down would name the refusal target; a field-specific cost of error imported with provenance and a shadow test would close the numeric gap. Until one of these fires, the verdict stands as `candidate`.
