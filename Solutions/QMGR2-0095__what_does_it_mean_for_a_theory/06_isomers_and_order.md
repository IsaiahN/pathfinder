# Are rival statistical theories isomers? Structure, order and analogues

**Mode: `specified`.** This is a simulated derivation checked against the figures, not a ground reading (see `05_what_shape_means.md` §6). No statistical test was run for this derivation.

## RESIDUAL

The question leaves two structural matters unsettled. (1) If two statistical theories are stated over the same observables and the same probability model family, what is their relation under Fig 12's definitions? (2) What is the order of operations, since a test can be designed, run, reported, replicated and only then used as a ground for a further claim? The frame census in `01` gave a four-condition union; this file derives the arrangement inside that union.

## DERIVATION

### Q1 - Are two rival statistical theories isomers?

- Fig 12 :117: "isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."
- Fig 12 :94: "The arrangement is the substance."
- Symbols table :63: "the typed relation between two operands. Seven of them, and which one holds is not recoverable from the operands, so a term naming only its ingredients has recorded half of itself."

Write two cosmological models over the same published atom set: the same observables (CMB power spectra, BAO, supernova distances, local H0 determinations), the same likelihood family and the same parameter bookkeeping, differing only in which structure mediates which observable, for example early-time sound-horizon physics versus late-time distance-ladder physics. They are **isomers** under Fig 12: same atoms, different bond, different substance. Two entries with an identical ingredient set are not variants of one thing, and "the bond is charged for what it could have been" (Fig 12 :99). The observational difference is decisive even though a parameter-count formula cannot see it.

Write them instead as the loose pair "ΛCDM fits everything" versus "new physics fits the tension": they share almost no atoms (one is a family of models, the other a direction of research). By Fig 12's definition they are **not isomers**; they are different kinds of object, and calling them rivals is a category error. The stronger isomer claim requires the shared atom set to be stated explicitly. That is why the cosmology review's long list of model classes matters: it enumerates the arrangements over one atom set rather than opposing two slogans.

**Falsifier, written before the run:** if either theory can be written only by adding an atom the other does not hold (a new observable, a new likelihood family with no shared parameters, a new instrument that measures something the other never claims), the isomer claim fails and the pair is a composition plus an import, not an isomer pair.

**Status:** `single-source` for the figure definition (Fig 12), with the physics-facing check still unmeasured in this simulated derivation. Triangulated for the *shape* of the test by the cosmology review, which enumerates model classes over shared data rather than opposing two names.

### Q2 - If they are not isomers, are they a functional group?

- Fig 12 :123: "functional group. A substructure whose presence fixes the outcome. The group says what kind; the qualifier says which member."
- Fig 5 :42: "The acceptance test is a bargain, not a threshold."

The weaker, always-available reading is that two statistical theories share a **functional group**: a pre-registered, per-slot refusal region with a non-updating anchor and independent frames. The group fixes the kind of thing (a testable statistical claim); the differing theory bindings say which member. This is the level at which the four-condition separator from `01` lives. It does not require the two theories to be isomers, only that they instantiate the same test structure.

The functional-group reading is what the outreach packet in `08` asks an expert to check. It is weaker than the isomer claim and correspondingly harder to refute, so both are kept separate and neither is used to prop up the other.

### Q3 - What is the order of the union?

- Fig 10 :60: "Where a stake cannot be removed, declare it. An undeclared interest is modelled anyway and an unnamed model runs unchallenged."
- Formula step 2: "REFUTED - a term held is false; what is owed is a refusal, not an explanation" and "no arrangement of the other four reaches it".
- Fig 9 :53: "Residual first, frame second."
- Fig 5 :69: "settling is necessary for a term to be composed over, and it is not sufficient."

The dependency order is not arbitrary:

1. **Bind the sought-for shape** before the run (Fig 5 :65-66), including which slots are live and what would count as a refusal at each.
2. **Declare the stake.** The analyst's interest in the outcome is unavoidable; an interest in the reading is disqualifying (Fig 10 :59: "an interest in the outcome is unavoidable, an interest in the reading is disqualifying").
3. **Refuse before mint.** If a held term's residual persists and does not trend down (Fig 5 :59), the refusal is owed before any parameter adjustment is offered as an explanation.
4. **Range against an anchor that does not update** (Fig 2 :29), with the channel kept reachable (Fig 10 :24: "the ground does not decay, the channel does").
5. **Settle, then compose.** Only after the first-order claim is settled can it become an atom for the next level (Fig 12 :104: "A settled molecule becomes an atom for whatever composes over it. So irreducible is a fact about a level and never about the world"). A replication that is not settled cannot be composed over; it is a recording, not a generator.

**Strain, named.** Steps 3 and 4 run in opposed directions if the analyst waits for the ground to "speak" before writing the refusal rule. Fig 6 :31: "Neither answer tells you much about the other. Run them as two tracks, and never read one against the other." The discipline is to bind the rule first and let the ground refuse it, not to let the ground choose the rule after the fact.

### Q4 - What do the two currencies price?

- Fig 12 :125: "A composition costs in two currencies, and they do not add."
- Fig 12 :126: "Bits price the description. Actions price finding out whether it holds."
- Fig 12 :128-129: "A long chain of cheap parts is expensive to describe and may cost one action to refute. A short arrangement over a costly operand is cheap to state and may take a whole budget to settle."

This is the structural reason a simple parameter count cannot decide falsifiability. A model with many parameters is expensive in bits; a model that stakes one narrow region on a scarce instrument is expensive in actions. They are in different units and "neither figure predicts the other, so both are read or the choice is made blind" (Fig 12 :129). Vignero and Wenmackers reach the same point from their own field: a theory testable only by an accelerator the size of the solar system is "quasi-impossible" to falsify in practice, so "the (degree of) falsifiability of a hypothesis co-depends on the severity of available experiments". Their point is world corroboration of the shape, not a figure line.

**Refuter.** Exhibit a case where description bits and settlement actions are commensurable under a declared exchange rate that holds across fields. The figures deny this; the burden is on the exchange rate.

### Q5 - Analogues as the question needs them

**Catalyst and spent parts (Fig 12 :119: "catalyst. A part that survives its own use.").** A pre-registered protocol that is reused across replications is a catalyst; a one-off analysis tuned to one dataset is a spent part. Nothing in the p-value records which it is. The MDL-amendment point in `01` F7 is the same: the arrangement must be recorded or every lineage pays again.

**Cycle (Fig 12 :121: "cycle. The output re-enters as input. The sign of the loop decides whether it closes, and a chain cannot close by construction.").** Replication is a cycle only when the next test is selected against the residual the last one surfaced (Fig 7 :36). Repeating the same test is a chain, and "A chain of mirrors is one long room" (Fig 7 :36). The H0 tension is a live cycle: each new determination is selected against the residual the previous ones surfaced, and the review reports the tension "has grown in significance with the improvement of the data".

**Clever Hans as a low-overlap analogue.** If the questioner knows the expected answer, the reading can be generated by the questioner's cues. Beran 2012: "if you know the answer, you should not ask the question and score the response given by the participant." The structural fix is a channel cut, the same operation as Fig 10 :26 "stale percepts, dropped transients, saturated metrics, pooled readings" and Fig 10 :42 "A verifier that can reconstruct the claim cannot verify it." This corroborates the shape of the independence condition; it does not corroborate any figure line by itself.

## CONCLUSION

Two rival statistical theories stated over the same atom set are Fig 12's **isomers**: same observables and model family, different bond, different behaviour, with the difference invisible in a parameter count. If the shared atom set cannot be stated, they are at best members of one **functional group** (the test structure), and that weaker claim is kept separate. The order of the union is: bind the shape, declare the stake, refuse before minting an adjustment, range against a non-updating anchor, settle, then compose. Description bits and settlement actions are two currencies and do not add. `metric tensor = Gradient`-style bindings have no place here; the only bindings made are the statistical ones, recorded as provenance from the statistics frame onto held figure terms. No figure line selects the numeric threshold, so the answer stays `specified`, ungrounded, with a declared `gap`.
