# Outreach draft: check the shape and its refuter before you decide about the theory

**Use:** one-to-one, or as a cover letter attached to a submission through the public venues in `03_public_contact_points.csv`. **Not** for bulk send.

**Hard precondition:** do not send until the mode labels and the five refuters in `01_figure_grounded_audit.md` §3 are in front of the text. If the stage-1 output cannot survive a check, the two-stage offer collapses on contact.

---

**Subject:** One checkable claim about emergent gravity, before any theory

> Hi [name],
>
> I have one specific output I would like you to check against what you already know. I am not asking you to read a theory first, and I am not asking you to agree with it. If the output does not survive, there is nothing else to look at.
>
> **The question in effect terms.** We have horizon entropy proportional to area, a horizon temperature, the equivalence principle and the Einstein field equations. Two bindings are on the table: gravity is fundamental and thermodynamics is a coarse-grained description of it, or gravity is a composed term and thermodynamics supplies the atoms. The framework I am using does not pick between these as a fact about the world. It says what shape any answer must have.
>
> **The output (`specified`, not `grounded`).** Its minimum set has six terms in two kinds, states (space, time, energy) and structures (spectrum, gradient, network), with the rule that composition runs across kinds and never within one. From that rule it follows that a spacetime term cannot be a primitive: if space and time compose at all, a structure must sit between them. The continuum was explicitly removed as a seventh member, and the four laws of thermodynamics are the load-bearing test the six terms are built to pass. So "is gravity emergent?" becomes a reachability question: does the Einstein equation compose from thermodynamic atoms inside a held closure, or does it require an imported microscopic atom (bits, cells, entanglement)? The framework returns the shape of that answer, not a name for the atom.
>
> **What I am not claiming.** Not that the framework proves gravity is emergent. Not that entropy is the Spectrum or temperature is the Gradient; those are bindings from physics onto held atoms and carry provenance, not derivation. Not that black-hole thermodynamics is a result the framework produced; it is a known fit, reported as such. Not that "emergent" means novel downward causal powers; the framework's level-relative irreducibility is the weak kind. And not that this settles the question: there are real discriminating instruments (dwarf-galaxy rotation curves, lensing, ultra-cold neutron levels, quantum coherence tests) and they currently disagree.
>
> **What would refute the shape claim, written before this message:**
> 1. derive one of the six from the other five, or exhibit a legitimate seventh member;
> 2. measure a direct space-time bond with no intervening structure;
> 3. find a law of thermodynamics for which none of the six is load-bearing;
> 4. show the field equations require an atom that is neither in the thermodynamic closure nor supplied by a provenanced import;
> 5. settle the world question decisively against composition with a source-discriminating instrument (this ends the question, it does not refute the shape).
>
> If any of 1 to 4 holds, I will retract the shape claim. A one-line "this fails because..." is more useful to me than agreement. If you would rather not engage, no reply is needed.
>
> **If it survives, and only then:** the figures are the authority, not my summary. Links are in the next message, or in `04_links_and_distribution.md` if this is a submission cover letter.
>
> **If you want to use the machinery:** write down what would refute your own result before you run it, and label the mode of what you get. A reported failure is more useful to me than a reported success.
>
> Thanks,
> [name]
