## DERIVATION

### Q1 : What is a first-order derivative here?

- Fig 13 :74: **"Everything else is a composition across the two kinds, never within one."**
- A first-order molecule is `state → structure → state`, one crossing.
- The gravitational core reads `Space → structure → Time`. The thermodynamic core reads `Energy → structure → Time` (temperature as the spendable difference, entropy as the ordering).
- Both are legal under the cross-kind rule. This is the sense in which each is a **first-order derivative** of the six-term set. The structure in each slot is not selected by the six alone; Fig 13 itself licenses more than one: **"A gradient over space and one over time are different terms, because which state it is bonded to is part of what it is."**
- **Mode:** specified for the legality, single-source for the physics binding.

### Q2 : What order is the Einstein equation if it composes from thermodynamics?

- If entropy and temperature are held atoms and the metric is the composed term, the derivation is a first-order or shallow composition inside one closure. Jacobson 1995 is the physics-facing instance: *"The Einstein equation is derived from the proportionality of entropy and horizon area together with the fundamental relation δQ=TdS"*.
- If a microscopic substrate is required, that term is an import (Fig 6 :23) and the composition is **second-order**: a settled molecule (horizon thermodynamics) becomes an atom for whatever composes over it, Fig 12 :104: **"A settled molecule becomes an atom for whatever composes over it. So irreducible is a fact about a level and never about the world."**
- The two orders are different facts. Underivable-in-principle and unreachable-at-budget are also different facts (Fig 8 :40-43). Do not read one as the other.
- **Falsifier, written before the run:** if the field equations can be composed with no imported atom other than ones already in the thermodynamic closure, the second-order reading is unnecessary; if they provably cannot compose from anything in that closure, the first-order reading is false. Either result is a real outcome, not a defeat.

### Q3 : Is the relation an isomer, a functional group, a catalyst or a cycle?

- Fig 12 :117: **"isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."**
- As the two cores are written above, gravitational `{Space, structure, Time}` and thermodynamic `{Energy, structure, Time}` share only Time. Under Fig 12's definition they are **not isomers**. They are two members of the same **functional group**, Fig 12 :124: **"functional group. A substructure whose presence fixes the outcome. The group says what kind; the qualifier says which member."**
- The stronger form: if both descriptions are written over the full shared atom set `{Space, Time, Energy, Spectrum, Gradient, Network}`, differing only in which structure mediates which state pair, then they **are** isomers, same atoms arranged differently. This is the checkable structural claim worth sending to an expert.
- Fig 12 :119: **"catalyst. A part that survives its own use."** Horizon entropy behaves catalyst-like in the derivations: it is retained across the compositional step rather than consumed. This is an analogy, not an identity; record it as such.
- Fig 12 :121: **"cycle. The output re-enters as input. The sign of the loop decides whether it closes, and a chain cannot close by construction."** Thermodynamic gravity proposals are cyclic in this sense: matter affects the screen, the screen's entropy changes, and the change reacts back as gravity. The sign of the feedback is what decides whether the account closes, and a linear derivation cannot close it.
- **Falsifier:** if either description can be written without an atom the other requires (for example, gravity with no Time, or thermodynamics with no Energy), the isomer claim fails and the functional-group reading stands. If the feedback loop is demonstrably open, the cycle reading fails.

### Q4 : Weak or strong emergence?

- IEP *Emergence* (opened 2026-11-23), low overlap with physics: weak emergence is *"a property is emergent if it is a systemic property of a system"* that is novel and unpredictable but derivable from the lower-level rules; strong emergence adds *"downward causal powers"* and *"in principle irreducibility"*.
- Fig 12 :104 makes irreducibility a fact about a level. That matches the **weak** reading. The figures do not assert novel downward causal powers as a new fundamental law, so the strong reading is an import from philosophy, not a framework output.
- The outreach consequence: do not say "the framework shows gravity is strongly emergent." Say "the framework shows that whatever composes gravity is a settled molecule at a level, and that the level decides what is irreducible here."
- **Falsifier:** a figure re-census that licenses a genuinely new causal power at the composed level, independent of the base. Until then, keep the strong reading out.

## CONCLUSION

The gravitational and thermodynamic cores are first-order cross-kind molecules over the six-term set. The Einstein equation, if it composes from thermodynamic atoms, is either a shallow composition inside one closure or a second-order composition over a settled horizon-thermodynamic molecule; which one is a reachability question, not a matter of taste. As written, the two cores share a functional group rather than being isomers; over the full shared atom set they are a real isomer claim with a stated falsifier. Horizon entropy plays a catalyst-like role and the feedback is cycle-like. The framework's emergence is the weak, level-relative kind. Nothing here names a microscopic substrate or picks Option A over Option B.
