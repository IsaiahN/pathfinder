## 0. The residual in effect terms

> **Option A:** some practices possess an intrinsic property, conservatism, tradition, or "cultural inertia", that makes them persist; other practices lack it and change. This option searches for a kind of thing inside the practice.
>
> **Option B:** persistence and rapid change are two regimes of one loop, decided per practice-slot by whether the transmission channel keeps contact with a ground and by whether the habitat's residual has changed. This option asks for a measurement per slot.

Stated as an effect: for a practice-slot `s` (a practice, a bearer population, a habitat) at generation `t`, let `R_s(t) = |Γ(b,a) − o′|` be the mismatch between what practitioners predict from their library and what the world returns when the practice is performed. Some `R_s(t)` stay small for many `t`; others grow within one or two. The question is what distinguishes those slots. `R` is indexed per slot, never averaged (Fig 1 :19). The anchor is not the researchers' agreement about what "tradition" means; it is whatever settles whether the practice still works in its habitat (Fig 2 :29: *"An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks. A measurement, an experiment, a counter, a market, a patient outcome."*).

What an answer would look like, bound as an ordinary term before the run (formula step "Direction"): a two-condition shape in which (C1) channel contact is maintained and (C2) reach is renewed by contact with a differing closure, each measurable per slot, with the specific practice that will persist left as `???`. Binding this in advance makes the preference refusable by the same test as everything else.

## 1. Domain census (before any figure line was chosen)

**R by arity, symmetry, scale (Fig 9 :22, :27-28).** Arity: at least three place (practice `Γ`, action `a`, observation `o′`), indexed by slot `s` and ordered by generation `t`, so a single number "how traditional is this practice" already discards the indexing. Symmetry: not symmetric in time, and "persists" is a threshold comparison of a duration against a standard (Operators table :48: ⋛ is *"a threshold relation between two quantities"*); the same practice can be a benefactor to one residual and a malfactor to another (Fig 11 :50). Scale: step (one handoff), cycle (one generation), level (population), lineage (many generations), with a lossy transform between scales (Fig 4 :27-29).

One line per domain: actors / currency / anchor. No nameable anchor means it is not a domain yet.

| domain | actors | currency | anchor | status |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| D1 Learner's loop | bearer, `Γ`, belief `b` with weighting `w`, action `a`, observation `o′` | per-slot residual reduction; inherited prior versus private history | the practice as actually observed in the world | admitted |
| D2 Transmission chain | teachers, learners, successive rooms | bracket fidelity `h²`, ground pressure `S`, agreement | selection between rooms; the ground votes | admitted |
| D3 Practice composition and reach | constituent acts, tools, rules as atoms; bonds between them | description length `abs(φ)`, reach, method versus recording | settlement against the ground (formula step 5) | admitted |
| D4 Habitat and ground | conditions, relations, other practices | survival of what the ground kept; valence relative to the residual | the ground, which does not decay | admitted |
| D5 Maintainers and seat | elders, institutions, ritual, enforcement | contact maintained; introduction, never subtraction | ground kept reachable (Fig 10 :29) | admitted |
| X1 "Intrinsic conservatism of a practice" | the practice alone | tradition | none nameable: valence is relative to the residual (Fig 11 :49) | **not a domain yet; refused** |
| X2 "Traditional versus modern" | two labelled periods | novelty | already inside D4 as a habitat change | absorbed |

**Partition or union: union, not partition.** D1 and D3 form the handoff compound with its own loop (the membrane, Fig 4 :21); D2 and D4 form the maintained-transmission compound; D4 and D5 form the managed-habitat compound. The whole is a union of C1 (channel contact maintained) and C2 (reach renewed by contact with a differing closure). Overlaps are "a specific compound" with its own loop, not a harder case of either condition (Fig 9 :55-57): *"A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones."* A single lever that clears only one condition leaves the intersections standing.

**Order by dependency (Fig 3 :20).** perception (can the learner see the practice?) → vocabulary (terms for what it affords) → objective (what counts as carrying it forward) → planning (a route to transmit it) → learn and carry (name what worked, verify, carry). Persistence or rapid change is decided at the last two links and at the membrane between step and lineage. *"A link with no instrument is not a link that works. It is a link nobody has looked at."* (Fig 3 :27). For most cultural practices, the link from vocabulary to objective has no instrument, which is why the question is usually answered above the break.

**Strain test applied before findings.** An early reading tried to make Fig 11 :14 (*"survival of the fittest"*) govern all persistence, which would imply that a persistent practice is currently useful. That strains against Fig 7 :12-13 (agreement handed along without evidence) and against Arthur 1989 (lock-in to a path that is not necessarily efficient). Re-census: Fig 11 :56-59 names two modes, unmaintained and maintained; "survival of the fittest" governs only the unmaintained panel. The strained reading is retracted in §5 and replaced by the two-mode finding F8. No figure quote was kept on a strain.

## 2. Findings, per domain, with refuters

### F1 - D1, persistence must be measured per slot (decisive for method)

Fig 1 :19: **"The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated."**
Fig 1 :22: **"Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average."**
Fig 11 :50: **"The same actor is a benefactor to one residual and a malfactor to another, so mutualism and parasitism are relations rather than kinds."**

A question of the form "why do cultural practices persist" invites a culture-level average. The figures forbid that move: the signal that says *where* to look lives in a slot, and a practice's value is relative to a residual, not carried by the practice. So the residual must be restated as a distribution over slots before any answer is attempted. **Mode `general`. Status: triangulated** (Fig 1 measurement indexing and Fig 11 valence relativity, two figures with low overlap). **Refuter:** a published analysis showing that a culture-level average predicts per-practice persistence as well as or better than per-slot measures on the same longitudinal data.

### F2 - D1, the learner's weighting is part of the persistence mechanism

Symbols table :24-26: `w` is **"How far the belief leans on private history versus inherited prior. w_A + w_B = 1, and w is learnable from the outcomes of past weightings. Two frames with identical architecture diverge because their histories and their w diverge."**

Fig 10 :24: **"the ground does not decay, the channel does."**

Two learner populations with identical architecture diverge because their histories and their `w` diverge. Applied to cultural transmission, a practice persists harder where learners lean more on inherited prior and less on private correction; it changes where the channel's percepts decay faster than the ground moves. This is a definition of a governing quantity, not a measurement of it. **Mode `specified` for the application, `general` for the definition. Status: single-source** (Symbols table; no second figure names `w` directly). **Refuter:** two populations with measured divergent `w` and divergent histories but statistically identical persistence rates for the same practice over the same generations.

### F3 - D2, handed-along agreement is a mirror chain, not evidence

Fig 7 :12-13: **"same closure, handed along; agreement compounds, evidence does not"**
Fig 7 :35: **"A room's agreement is variation, not a verdict. Selection between rooms is where the ground votes."**
Fig 2 :35: **"Alignment is triangulation, not negotiation. A mirror cannot correct you."**

A practice can persist for generations purely because each generation copies the last within one closure. That is the mirror-chain collapse: the agreement compounds and the evidence does not. This is the figure-grounded reason *conformist transmission* cannot be the whole answer even if it is real, and it explains why the two 2020 world sources that weaken the conformist-transmission claim do not move this verdict (see §2b). **Mode `general` for the collapse, `specified` for its application to traditions. Status: triangulated** (Fig 7 room chain and Fig 2 anchor rule, different figures and different failure mechanisms). **Refuter:** a documented practice whose persistence is fully accounted for by ground selection across generations while agreement among bearers measures at or near zero.

### F4 - D2, the handoff has two factors and either at zero stops progress

Fig 7 :37: **"Progress per handoff has two factors. Zero either and the chain runs in place:"**
Fig 7 :38: **"the bracket's fidelity across the handoff"**
Fig 7 :39: **"the ground's pressure on the room"**

This is the governing line for the question. A chain of generations yields `R = h² × S`. Persistence over many generations requires the bracket to survive the handoff (`h² > 0`) and low ground pressure (`S` small) so that the same practice keeps paying. Rapid change follows when `S` rises (the habitat's residual moves, so the old practice no longer settles the slot) or when `h²` falls (the channel loses fidelity and the practice is re-taught as a different thing). Zero either factor and the chain "runs in place": motion without progress, which is the mirror chain of F3. **Mode `specified`** (the figure states the law for rooms; applying it to generations is a binding). **Status: triangulated** (Fig 7 and Fig 10 :24 channel decay, low overlap). **Refuter:** a practice with both `h²` and `S` measured high that nevertheless changes rapidly, or with both measured near zero that nevertheless changes; either breaks the product.

### F5 - D3, what crosses a generation is a method or a recording, and the two persist differently

Fig 4 :22: **"Only methods cross up. Only head starts come down."**
Fig 4 :25: **"A recording carried upward looks like knowledge and is a description of one occasion."**
Fig 12 :107-109: *"Settling compresses the description and not what it cost to establish."*

A practice transmitted as a generator (a reapplicable method, with the correction loop intact) can be rebuilt when the habitat moves; a practice transmitted as a recording of one occasion repeats that occasion and fails when conditions change. This is a structural difference in what is carried, not a difference in how old the practice is. The tacit-knowledge essay read for this folder gives the human case directly: context and effect descriptions travel, lived experience does not, so each learner rebuilds part of the practice through contact. **Mode `general` for the membrane rule, `specified` for practices. Status: triangulated** (Fig 4 membrane and Fig 12 settlement cost). **Refuter:** a practice transmitted as a verbatim recording that stays reapplicable across every documented habitat change with no re-derivation by any bearer.

### F6 - D4, nothing about the practice alone fixes its persistence

Fig 11 :49: **"An actor's valence is relative to the residual, not a property it carries."**
Fig 11 :51: **"No standing list of good actors survives a change of residual."**
Fig 11 :14-15: **"nothing selects; what survives is what the ground kept"**

Persistence is a relation between a practice and the current residual, not an intrinsic property. When the ground's residual changes, the valence of an unchanged practice can flip from benefactor to malfactor, and the practice changes or disappears even though nothing "inside" it changed. This is the figure-grounded refusal of Option A. **Mode `general`** (structural), applied to anthropology as `specified`. **Status: triangulated** (Fig 11 valence and Fig 7 ground voting). **Refuter:** a practice whose measured valence is invariant across a documented, instrument-verified change of the habitat's residual.

### F7 - D4, the cause is often three steps out

Fig 11 :52-53: **"And contact cascades. Scoping the habitat to direct contact is how DDT reached the eagles: first-order enumeration, second-order effect, and the residual that mattered was three steps out."**
Fig 11 :48: *"You do not invent the list. You read it off the world, and what you cannot perceive or measure yet is the residual."*

A practice may change because of a condition two or three links away in the habitat (a change in a neighbouring practice, a resource, a migration route), not because of a direct cause on the practice itself. The habitat is enumerated, never composed. **Mode `general` for the cascade rule, `specified` for a given case. Status: single-source** (Fig 11; the DDT case is the figure's own example, not an independent witness here). **Refuter:** a practice that changed with a fully enumerated first-order habitat in which no second- or third-order relation can be found after the fact.

### F8 - D5, two modes of persistence, only one of which has a seat

Fig 11 :56-59: **"Unmaintained. No maintainer and no live frame in the seat. The habitat is pure contact, nothing selects, and what survives is what the ground kept."** and **"Maintained. A seat exists and can act: choose the instruments, distil the impactful actors, introduce what was not there. That is the garden, and it is how arrival is worked on rather than waited for."**
Fig 11 :61: **"The seat's power is asymmetric. Introduction is available; exclusion is not."**
Fig 11 :39: **"capability is a property of agent-and-habitat, never of the agent alone."**

An unmaintained practice persists because the ground kept it; a maintained practice persists because a seat keeps the channel reachable and introduces supporting actors. These are two different explanations and must never be averaged into "tradition". Introduction is available and exclusion is not, so a managed cultural habitat is permanent work, not a solved state. **Mode `specified`** (anthropological application of the two panels). **Status: triangulated** (Fig 11 habitat modes and Fig 10 ground maintainer). **Refuter:** a maintained practice whose persistence is unchanged after maintenance ceases and the habitat's contact structure is documented to have changed.

### F9 - D4/D3, change needs a differing closure

Fig 6 :23: **"No frame escapes its own closure. The bound moves only on contact with a frame whose closure differs."**
Fig 8 :32: **"Closure is idempotent. Composing inside a closed set never leaves it, so no search, however long, adds an atom. This one is definitional: it is what closure means."**
Fig 13 :115: **"The set is closed. The arrangements are not. That gap is why import exists."**

Rapid change that introduces a genuinely new atom (a new tool, rule, or practice that was not already in the population's library) requires contact with a differing closure: migration, trade, conquest, a new institution, a new instrument. Rearrangement of what is already held can run forever without adding a member. **Mode `general`. Status: triangulated** (Fig 6, Fig 8, Fig 13, three distinct roles). **Refuter:** a documented closed habitat generating genuinely new atoms generation after generation with no contact with any differing closure.

### F10 - the specific practice and the specific date: `gap`

No figure line governs which particular practice will persist, or when the switch will happen, after a genuine re-census. Fig 9 :32: **"you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself"**. Fig 13 :134: **"Unreachable is a verdict here, not a silence,"**. Fig 8 :39: **"So a search that finds nothing is never a proof of absence."**

What is missing is an instrument that measures `h²` and `S` per slot and discriminates source (method versus recording, F5) where a coarse reading of "the practice is still there" cannot. Characterized in effect terms: a measure that returns a different reading for a practice kept by handed-along agreement and one kept by ground payment, and that registers the third-order habitat link before the practice flips. The framework cannot name that instrument. Naming it would mean already holding it. **Mode `general`** for the limit. **Status: `gap`, declared, not hidden.** A search that finds no figure line here is not proof that none exists.

## 2b. Two additions after the world search

**The world pool conflicts with itself, and the conflict is reported rather than averaged.** Henrich and Boyd (1998) report that conformist transmission *"creates a directional force that tends to establish and maintain cultural norms"*. Denton, Ram, Liberman and Feldman (2020) report that *"the common claim that conformity can maintain between-group differences is not always true"*, and Morgan and Thompson (2020) report that *"when individuals are biased (even weakly) the capacity of conformist transmission to sustain traditions is reduced and, in many cases, removed entirely"*. These are three real sources read on 2026-11-22 and they do not agree about whether a transmission bias stabilizes traditions. Per the standing rule, a conflict sends the derivation back to the census and never flips a figure verdict. Re-census result: the figures never name conformist transmission as a mechanism. Fig 7 :12-13 describes handed-along agreement as a collapse; Fig 7 :37-39 gives the two factors. Whether a psychological conformity bias is one physical realization of `h²` is an unmeasured question in the world pool, not a figure question. The verdict from F3 and F4 therefore stands, and the world conflict is recorded as one witness, not as a refutation.

**An apparent strain between Fig 11 :14 and Fig 7 :12-13 was resolved by re-census.** "Survival of the fittest" governs only the unmaintained habitat panel (Fig 11 :56); the mirror chain governs a different failure, agreement without evidence. Different objects, so no strain remains on this pair. The light strain that does remain is the use of ⋛ (comparison) for "persists longer than X generations": the Operators table defines it as *"a threshold relation between two quantities"*, and treating a duration as one of those quantities is an application of the operator, not a derivation of it. It is named here and in `06_isomers_and_order.md` and not smoothed over.

## 3. Pre-registered refuter (written before the conclusion)

Required before any outreach: a falsifier written before the run is a design instrument. The shape claim (C1 plus C2, per slot) is refuted if any of the following holds:

1. A practice with both `h²` (bracket fidelity) and `S` (ground pressure) measured high that nevertheless changes rapidly, or one with both measured near zero that nevertheless changes rapidly. Breaks Fig 7 :37-39.
2. A practice whose valence is measured invariant across an instrument-verified change in the habitat's residual. Breaks Fig 11 :49-51.
3. Genuinely new atoms arising generation after generation in a documented closed habitat with no contact with a differing closure. Breaks Fig 6 :23 and Fig 8 :32.
4. One intervention that clears both conditions with no categorically different residue left standing. Breaks the union reading, Fig 9 :54-57.
5. A culture-level average predicting per-practice persistence as well as per-slot measures on the same data. Breaks Fig 1 :19 and :22.

If any fires, the two-condition shape is wrong in that domain and the affected claim is withdrawn. Until one is run, no part of this may be labelled `grounded`.

## 4. Recommendation

1. Restate the question per slot before answering it; drop any culture-level average of "persistence" (F1).
2. Treat the question as a union of two conditions, C1 channel contact and C2 renewed reach, and unbundle it before searching for a mechanism (Fig 9 :54-57).
3. Measure `h²` and `S` separately; never substitute agreement among practitioners for ground pressure (F3, F4).
4. Distinguish what is transmitted as a method from what is transmitted as a recording (F5). This is the part a fieldworker can often see even before an instrument exists.
5. Do not answer "why do some practices persist" with a list of intrinsic traits. Traits have valence only relative to a residual (F6).
6. Record the maintainer, if any, and declare its stake; a maintained practice is a garden, not a fact of nature (F8, Fig 10 :60).
7. Say `gap` for the specific practice and the specific date. Offer the shape and the refuters, not a forecast (F10).
