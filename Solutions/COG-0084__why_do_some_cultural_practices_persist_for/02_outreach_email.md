# Outreach draft: check one shape before deciding whether the framework is worth reading

**Use:** one-to-one, or as a cover note attached to a submission through the public venues in `03_public_contact_points.csv`. **Not** for bulk send.

**Hard precondition:** do not send until the three checkable sub-claims in `08_expert_verification_packet.md` survive your own check. The stage-1 shape must stand on its own or the two-stage offer collapses on contact.

---

**Subject:** One checkable claim about why cultural practices persist or change

> Hi [name],
>
> I have one specific output I would like you to check against what you already know about cultural transmission. I am not asking you to read a framework first, and I am not asking you to agree with it. If the output does not survive, there is nothing else to look at.
>
> **The residual.** For a given practice in a given population and habitat, some stay recognizably the same for many generations and others change within one or two. Measured per practice-slot, the question is why the mismatch between what practitioners expect and what the world returns stays small in some slots and grows fast in others. Averaging across practices throws that signal away, so I am not asking about "culture" as a whole.
>
> **The output (`specified`, not `grounded`; general on the structure):** persistence and rapid change look like two regimes of one handoff, not two kinds of practice. The handoff has two factors: how faithfully the bracket survives the transfer from one generation to the next, and how much pressure the ground puts on the receiving room. Persistence needs the bracket to survive and the ground pressure to stay low. Rapid change follows when the ground pressure rises, or when the channel loses fidelity and the practice is re-taught as a different thing. Either factor at zero and the chain runs in place, which is motion without progress. A second condition sits alongside it: genuinely new atoms need contact with a differing closure, so a habitat closed to new people, tools or rules can rearrange its practices indefinitely without producing a new one. The two conditions form a union, not a list, and the specific practice that will persist is left open. This is a shape and a route, not a forecast and not a measurement.
>
> **What I am not claiming:** that conformist transmission is the mechanism, that "tradition" is a thing inside a practice, that a persistent practice is currently useful, or that I can name which practice will change next. Three sources I read this week disagree about whether conformity stabilizes traditions. I am not asking you to resolve that here. I am also not offering a theory of everything, and I am not claiming the framework is the only one that could produce this shape.
>
> **What would refute the shape:**
> 1. A practice with both bracket fidelity and ground pressure measured high that nevertheless changes rapidly, or one with both measured near zero that nevertheless changes.
> 2. A practice whose value is measured invariant across a documented, instrument-verified change in its habitat.
> 3. Genuinely new practices arising generation after generation in a closed habitat with no contact with a differing closure.
> 4. One intervention that clears both conditions with no categorically different residue left standing.
> 5. A culture-level average that predicts per-practice persistence as well as per-slot measures on the same data.
>
> I wrote these down before contacting anyone. If any one holds, the shape is wrong in that domain and I will retract the affected claim. A one-line "this fails because..." is more useful to me than agreement. If you would rather not engage, no reply is needed.
>
> **If it survives, and only then:** the sources are below. The figure text, not my summary of it, is the authority.
> - Verbatim figures with line numbers (local framework extract): `FRAMEWORK/reference/03-FIGURES_TEXT.md`
> - The canonical usage rule (local framework copy): `FRAMEWORK/reference/FIGURES_README_canonical.md`
> - The eight essays behind the reading of "shape", especially the tacit-knowledge and source-discrimination essays: https://adventuresinml.substack.com/p/decoding-tacit-knowledge-why-context and https://adventuresinml.substack.com/p/the-hard-problem-is-about-source
>
> **If you want to use the machinery:** the formula is in the figures. Run it on a transmission problem you know well and can check against a ground. Write down what would refute your result *before* you run it, and label the mode of what you get. A reported failure is more useful to me than a reported success.
>
> - [your name]

## Stage discipline

1. **Stage 1:** send the residual, the shape and the five refuters. Ask what the recipient would count as a failure. Record their verdict **before** any theory is shown.
2. **Stage 2, opt-in only:** if they are curious, send the three links. Their later agreement is never pooled as corroboration (Fig 2 :40).
3. **Stage 3:** offer the formula as a method, with the instruction that each person writes their own refuter and states their own mode.

A reply that corrects the shape is worth more than a reply that endorses it. Nothing in this packet is sent by this agent; you host and send.
