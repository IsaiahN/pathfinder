# Bonds, order and analogues for a transmission chain

**Mode: `specified` on the application, `general` on the structural claims.** Simulated derivation checked against the figures, not a ground reading (`05_what_shape_means.md` §6).

## RESIDUAL

The primary derivation leaves three structural questions open: (1) what kind of composed object is a practice handed down across generations, (2) in what order do the two conditions C1 and C2 bind, and (3) which analogue from composition theory governs the observation that twoPractices can look the same while being kept by different mechanisms. Answering these does not fill the `gap` in F10; it makes the shape precise enough to refute.

## DOMAIN CENSUS (supporting, consistent with `01` §1)

- R: arity three or more, ordered by generation, scale = handoff to lineage. Symmetry: a transmission chain is ordered (→), not symmetric; the comparison "persists longer than" is ⋛, a threshold relation (Operators table :48).
- D1 handoff composition: actors = acts, tools, rules as atoms and the bonds between them; currency = description length and actions to settle; anchor = the ground.
- D2 condition order: actors = channel and reach; currency = what crosses the membrane; anchor = whether the new atom pays.
- D3 analogue register: actors = the two transmitted practices; currency = the reading "still present after N generations"; anchor = an instrument that discriminates source.
- Union: D1 and D2 overlap at the membrane; D3 is a register over both, not a new domain. Order by dependency: perception → vocabulary → objective → planning → learn and carry (Fig 3 :20).

## DERIVATION

### Q1 - What kind of object is a transmitted practice?

Fig 12 :48: **"A molecule is a bonded arrangement. A chain is the degenerate case: one bond type, no branching. Nothing about a chain is a different kind of object."**

Fig 12 :111-113: *"A vocabulary without bonds can name a state and cannot name a route to one. A plan is a sequence. A goal is a comparison. Progress is a subtraction."*

A practice handed down by imitation is a chain: one bond type (→ sequence), no branching, and no claim that this makes it a lesser object. But the vocabulary that describes it must also have ⋛ (a goal is a comparison: *"a goal is a comparison, which is why a vocabulary without it cannot state one"*, Operators table :51) and ⇒ for a genuinely new atom introduced from a differing closure (Operators table :37: *"A produces B; B did not exist before"*). A vocabulary with only ingredients can say what a practice contains; it cannot say what would count as carrying it forward. **Mode `general`. Status: triangulated** (Fig 12 composition and Operators table bond definitions). **Falsifier:** a transmission record in which every new practice is demonstrably a rearrangement of held atoms and no ⇒ is ever needed, across many documented imports.

### Q2 - In what order do C1 and C2 bind?

Fig 3 :20: **"The chain is ordered by dependency, not by importance. Each link consumes what the one above produces, so effort spent above the break arrives at a step that cannot use it."**

C1 (channel contact) is upstream of C2 (renewed reach). A new atom arriving through a differing closure cannot be carried to the next generation if there is no channel with fidelity `h² > 0`. Conversely, a perfectly maintained channel with no new atom produces the mirror chain of F3: agreement compounding, evidence not. The union is ordered: C1 first, C2 second, and neither alone is sufficient. Fig 7 :37-39 gives the product, and a product has no order; the dependency order is in the *arrival* of the atom, not in the arithmetic. **Mode `general` for the order, `specified` for the two conditions. Status: triangulated** (Fig 3 dependency order and Fig 4 membrane direction). **Falsifier:** a documented case where reach is renewed and a new atom is carried across generations while the bracket fidelity at the handoff measures zero.

### Q3 - Are two practices with the same atoms isomers?

Fig 12 :117: **"isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."**

Two practices built from the same elements, say the same tool, the same gesture and the same rule, but bonded differently (one as a method with correction, one as a recording to be reproduced) are isomers in the figure's sense: same atoms, different bond, different behaviour across a habitat change. This is a checkable structural claim. **But it is not the right analogue for the case that matters most here.** Two practices kept by different mechanisms (a maintained garden and a mirror chain) can produce the *same observable reading* while having different sources. That is metamerism, not isomerism: the reading does not determine the source (*The Hard Problem is About Source*: *"If several paths create the same behavior, then behavior is a projection with lossy information."*). **Correction to my own first reading:** I initially mapped the whole persistence question onto isomerism. Re-census: isomerism governs same atoms arranged differently; metamerism governs different sources producing one reading. Both are kept, scoped to their own objects. **Mode `specified`. Status: single-source for the figure definition of isomer (Fig 12), single-source for the metamerism essay.** **Falsifier:** an instrument that distinguishes maintained from handed-along practices from the observable record alone would collapse the metamerism reading for that case; two practices with identical atoms and different bonds behaving identically in every habitat would collapse the isomer reading.

### Q4 - Does the chain close?

Fig 12 :121: **"cycle. The output re-enters as input. The sign of the loop decides whether it closes, and a chain cannot close by construction."**

A maintained cultural habitat is a cycle: the output of one generation (a trained bearer, a repaired tool, an institution) re-enters as input to the next. An unmaintained chain of copies is a chain and cannot close, which is why it runs in place. Fig 11 :62-64: *"Which is why a managed ecosystem is permanent work rather than a solved state."* **Mode `specified`. Status: triangulated** (Fig 12 cycle and Fig 11 asymmetry of introduction). **Falsifier:** a practice that persists indefinitely through a maintained seat with no re-introduction of any actor, condition or correction, in a habitat whose ground pressure is documented as nonzero.

## CONCLUSION

A transmitted practice is a chain, the degenerate case of a molecule, and needs not only sequence but comparison and production in its vocabulary. The two conditions are ordered: the channel must carry before a new atom can arrive. Same atoms under different bonds give a real isomer claim for method versus recording; same reading from different sources is metamerism and is the harder problem, because it needs an instrument rather than an argument. The maintained case is a cycle, the unmaintained case a chain, and only the cycle can renew itself. None of this names which practice will persist; it only makes the shape refutable.
