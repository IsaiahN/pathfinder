# The Tether's answer: why some cultural practices persist and others change rapidly

**Mode: `general` on the structural laws, `specified` on the application to cultural practices, not `grounded`.** A shape and a route, not a result. Simulated derivation checked for internal consistency against the figures (`05_what_shape_means.md` §6), not a ground reading.

## 1. The residual it answers

The observed distribution: per practice-slot `s` (practice, bearer population, habitat), the mismatch `R_s(t)` between what practitioners predict and what the world returns stays small for many generations in some slots and grows within one or two in others. "Persistence" must not be averaged into a culture-level score: Fig 1 :19 *"The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated."* and Fig 1 :22 *"Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average."* The anchor is what settles whether the practice still works in its habitat (Fig 2 :29), not the agreement of people who already call it a tradition.

## 2. The shape

1. **Persistence is a two-factor handoff, not a property of a practice.** Fig 7 :37: *"Progress per handoff has two factors. Zero either and the chain runs in place:"* Fig 7 :38: *"the bracket's fidelity across the handoff"* Fig 7 :39: *"the ground's pressure on the room"* Persistence needs the bracket to survive transfer (`h² > 0`) and ground pressure to stay low. Rapid change follows a rise in `S` or a fall in `h²`. With zero either, the chain "runs in place": motion without progress.

2. **Agreement is not the same as ground payment.** Fig 7 :12-13: *"same closure, handed along;"* *"agreement compounds, evidence does not"*. Fig 7 :35: *"A room's agreement is variation, not a verdict. Selection between rooms is where the ground votes."* A practice can last centuries as a mirror chain, with agreement compounding and evidence static. That is why a referendum among practitioners cannot tell you why it lasted.

3. **What is carried matters.** Fig 4 :22: *"Only methods cross up. Only head starts come down."* Fig 4 :25: *"A recording carried upward looks like knowledge and is a description of one occasion."* A practice transmitted as a reapplicable method can be rebuilt after a habitat change; a recording of one occasion repeats that occasion and fails when conditions move.

4. **Valence is relative to the residual.** Fig 11 :49: *"An actor's valence is relative to the residual, not a property it carries."* Fig 11 :51: *"No standing list of good actors survives a change of residual."* When the habitat's residual changes, an unchanged practice can flip from useful to harmful. There is no intrinsic "conservatism" in a practice.

5. **There are two modes of persistence, and only one has a maintainer.** Fig 11 :56-59 names unmaintained survival (nothing selects, the ground kept what survived) and maintained arrival (a seat acts, introduces, and keeps the channel reachable). They must be kept separate, never averaged into "tradition". Fig 10 :24: *"the ground does not decay, the channel does."*

6. **New atoms need a differing closure.** Fig 6 :23: *"No frame escapes its own closure. The bound moves only on contact with a frame whose closure differs."* Fig 8 :32: *"Closure is idempotent. Composing inside a closed set never leaves it, so no search, however long, adds an atom."* A closed population can rearrange its practices indefinitely without producing a new one. Rapid change of that kind imports from migration, trade, conquest or a new instrument.

7. **The conditions form a union, not a partition.** Fig 9 :55: *"A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones."* The channel condition is upstream of the reach condition (Fig 3 :20), and the overlaps are compounds with their own loops. One lever clears a single condition and leaves the intersections standing.

8. **Which practice, and when: `gap`.** Fig 9 :32: *"you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself"*; Fig 13 :134: *"Unreachable is a verdict here, not a silence,"*. The framework returns the two-condition shape and a declared absence of the per-slot instrument that would name the next change.

## 3. What it explicitly does not say

- Not "tradition" as an intrinsic property of a practice. Valence is relative to a residual (Fig 11 :49-51).
- Not "conformist transmission is the cause". The figures never name that mechanism, and three world sources read on 2026-11-22 disagree about it.
- Not "a persistent practice is currently useful". Lock-in and rigidity traps are real alternatives in the world literature and are not excluded by the figures.
- Not a forecast of which practice will change or when. That is F10's declared `gap`.
- Not a derivation of one ethnographic case from another. The binding of `h²` and `S` to specific field measures is an import and needs a source gate and shadow test.
- Not `grounded`. No longitudinal dataset was read as a ground.

## 4. What would settle it

A per-slot instrument, not a further argument. *The Hard Problem is About Source* (opened 2026-11-22): an instrument that reads green *"cannot comment on the source"*; a spectrometer resolved it. Here the corresponding tests are:

- **Fidelity/pressure test:** measure `h²` and `S` separately per practice-slot. A practice with both high that still changes, or both near zero that still changes, refutes the governing line.
- **Source-discrimination test:** distinguish a practice kept by ground payment from one kept by handed-along agreement using an observation that does not already assume which it is. If no such observation can be built, the mirror-chain finding stands as a shape but cannot be applied to a case.
- **Method/recording test:** move the practice to a substituted habitat (Fig 11 :70, a test harness is a substituted habitat) and see whether bearers rebuild it or replay it.
- **Import test:** document a closed habitat across generations and check whether new atoms appear without contact with a differing closure.
- **Union test:** apply one intervention at a time; a single lever that clears both conditions with no categorically different residue breaks the union reading.
- **Standing falsifiers:** the five in `01_figure_grounded_audit.md` §3, written before this conclusion.

## 5. The honest stop

**Unreached, not unreachable.** The figures stop where the available instruments stop distinguishing, and they record the lineage so the stop is checkable. For COG-0084 the stop is at the source-discriminating instrument. The world literature offers candidates (conformist bias, niche construction, lock-in, rigidity traps) but they are secondary, partly conflicted, and none of them is licensed by the figures as the missing atom. *"Indiscernibility is not identity."* See `11_audit.md` for the measured round-trip gap `R_T`.

## 6. One-paragraph version for outreach

> The Tether does not say that some practices have a conservative essence. It says that persistence is a per-slot result of a handoff with two factors: how faithfully the practice survives transfer, and how much pressure the ground puts on the receiving generation. If either is zero, the chain runs in place; agreement can compound for centuries without adding evidence, so asking practitioners why their tradition lasted cannot settle it. What is carried matters too: a reapplicable method survives a habitat change that kills a recording of one occasion, and a practice's value flips when the habitat's residual changes. There are two modes of persistence, unmaintained survival and maintained arrival, and they must not be averaged. New practices need contact with a differing closure; a closed population can only rearrange what it already holds. These conditions form a union, and the framework refuses to name which practice will change next. It returns the shape, five refuters written in advance, and an honest stop at the instrument that would discriminate a kept practice from a merely repeated one.
