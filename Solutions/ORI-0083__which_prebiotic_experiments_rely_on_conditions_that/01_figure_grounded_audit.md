## 0. The residual, stated in effect terms

> Question: Which prebiotic experiments rely on conditions that may have been unrealistic for early Earth?

The residual is **not** "is origin-of-life chemistry true." It is narrower and per-slot:

> A compound is synthesized in the laboratory under a declared condition set C. The result is offered as evidence that the chemistry could have run on early Earth, whose condition set E is reconstructed with uncertainty. R is the mismatch between what the bench frame predicts when it applies C and what the Earth frame can witness about E. R is indexed by condition slot, not aggregated into a single plausibility score.

The two things being compared are not two versions of one question. They are two habitats. The lab is a **substituted habitat** built to make a reaction legible; early Earth is a habitat whose conditions are inferred from rocks, isotopes, and models. The figure that governs this residual is Figure 11, not a chemistry figure. The chemistry itself is often settled at the bench; what is unsettled is the bridge.

What an answer would look like, bound before the run as an ordinary term (Formula step "Direction"): **a ranked, reversible list of condition slots, each with (i) the value used in the experiment, (ii) the reconstructed early-Earth value with its uncertainty, (iii) the instrument or observation that would discriminate them, and (iv) a named failure class if they differ.** A list of experiment names without those four fields does not answer the residual.

## 1. Domain census

R by arity, symmetry and scale, then one line per domain giving actors / currency / anchor. No nameable anchor means it is not yet a domain.

- **R (arity / symmetry / scale).** Arity: two conditions, the bench habitat and the reconstructed Earth habitat, so this is a **union compound**, not one harder question: Fig 9 :57-58, *"a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either."* Symmetry: **asymmetric**. The bench stands in for the Earth; the Earth never stands in for the bench. Reversing the direction produces a different claim (a field observation used to explain an apparatus). Scale: first order inside each habitat (a reaction runs, a condition is reconstructed), second order at the transfer (a bench result is promoted to a claim about the Earth).
- **Domain 1, the bench frame.** Actors: purified reagents, vessels, energy sources (spark, UV, heat), buffers, analytical instruments. Currency: yield and detectability of product. Anchor: the instrument reading. It is a real anchor for the narrow claim "this product formed under these stated conditions," and it does not update on what anyone thinks the early Earth was like.
- **Domain 2, the early-Earth reconstruction frame.** Actors: atmospheric and ocean models, volcanic outgassing, impactors, zircons, mineral surfaces, UV flux. Currency: boundary conditions with error bars. Anchor: rocks and isotopic measurements. This ground is legitimate for the question (the question is constitutively about a past state of the Earth), but the channel to it is thin, so the anchor returns ranges, not point values.
- **Domain 3, the transfer / verification frame.** Actors: review authors, replication groups, modelers, expert readers. Currency: agreement and residuals. Anchor: the actual early-Earth habitat, reachable only through Domain 2's instruments. In this simulated run all three domains draw on one literature pool, so independence is low and agreement among them is not corroboration (Fig 2 :40).
- **Partition or union.** Union. The two conditions overlap (both involve chemistry, energy, and water) but neither reduces to the other. Fig 9 :54: unbundle before searching again. The overlap is a compound with its own loop: the transfer inference.
- **Order by dependency (Fig 3).** Describe R before choosing a frame (Fig 9 :27-28). Enumerate the habitat before selecting a lever (Fig 11 :43-48). Settle the first-order bench result before the second-order transfer claim (Fig 12 :104). Ask SEARCHED first, then INWARD (sharpen the Earth reconstruction), then OUTWARD (import a frame that already holds the missing condition); never read the latter two against each other.

Strain test applied during the census: the laboratory appears to *exclude* conditions (purification, isolation), which seems to contradict Fig 11 :61, *"The seat's power is asymmetric. Introduction is available; exclusion is not."* Re-census resolves it: the figure's asymmetry describes a seat acting on an existing habitat. A bench builds a **substituted** habitat, so what looks like subtraction from the Earth is addition to a new one (Fig 11 :66). Different objects, no contradiction. Recorded in §8, not smoothed over.

## 3. Findings

### F1 - The governing figure is the habitat, and it decides the shape (decisive, triangulated)

Fig 11 :66: **"Isolation is not removal of the habitat. It is substitution of one habitat for another."**

Fig 11 :70: **"A test harness is a substituted habitat. Which is why a synthetic solve proves wiring and never capability."**

A prebiotic bench setup is a substituted habitat. A successful synthesis in it proves **wiring**: that under C the mechanism can produce the compound. It does not prove **capability**: that under E the same pathway would run on early Earth. This is the residual in the figures' own terms.

Triangulation: Fig 4 :34-35, from a different figure with low overlap, says **"The level a method lands on remakes it. A seed carries no small tree: it carries what will grow one, and the conditions it lands in decide what it becomes."** Same conclusion (conditions decide what a composition becomes), reached from the membrane between scales rather than from the habitat. Mark `triangulated`. The specific identification of a bench with a test harness is `single-source` (Fig 11 :70) and is named as such.

**Refuter:** show a bench solve in which the substituted conditions are identical, in every load-bearing slot, to the reconstructed Earth conditions. Then substitution is not substitution for those slots and F1 does not apply to that experiment.

### F2 - The failures of a substituted habitat are invisible by construction (single-source, strong)

Fig 11 :68-69: **"what you failed to reproduce is invisible until the goal fails"** and **"what you unintentionally introduced is invisible until it acts."**

The two named failures map directly onto the two ways an early-Earth condition can be unrealistic:

- **Omitted condition:** the experiment leaves out something the Earth had (a mineral surface, a wet-dry cycle, an impact-derived reducing pulse). The result still looks clean.
- **Introduced condition:** the experiment includes something the Earth lacked at the required level (a purified starting material, a high concentration, a continuous UV source). The result looks cleaner than the Earth could have been.

This is why the familiar objection is about *conditions* and not about the chemistry: the chemistry is visible in the product, the habitat is not.

**Refuter:** an instrument that reports on the omitted or introduced condition at the time of the run, rather than after the goal fails. If such an instrument already exists and reads the condition, the "invisible" claim weakens for that slot.

### F3 - Conditions must be enumerated from the world, not composed from a theory (triangulated)

Fig 11 :43: **"The habitat is enumerated, never composed."**

Fig 11 :46: **"conditions: light, water, temperature, season, terrain"**

Fig 11 :48: **"You do not invent the list. You read it off the world, and what you cannot perceive or measure yet is the residual."**

So the answer to "which experiments" cannot be produced by ranking theories. It is produced by reading the habitat: atmosphere composition, UV environment, concentration mechanism, mineral catalysis, pH, temperature, cycling, delivery flux. A condition that cannot yet be measured is itself the residual, not a reason to leave it off the list.

Triangulation: Fig 2 :29, **"An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks."** The Earth's condition values are the anchor; the choice of which experiment is fashionable is not.

**Refuter:** a complete condition list generated by composing a theory rather than by enumeration. If such a list can be shown to be complete, the enumeration law fails for this domain.

### F4 - The bench frame cannot derive Earth conditions from its own closure (triangulated)

Fig 6 :23: **"No frame escapes its own closure. The bound moves only on contact with a frame whose closure differs."**

Fig 6 :12-13: **"source gate: provenance"** and **"shadow test: explains a gap."**

Fig 6 :30: **"outward: does another frame already hold it? The description travels. Fails by being sharpened into terms only we can read. Cast it in effect terms."**

A chemist cannot derive the Hadean atmosphere from a flask. The condition values must be imported from geochemistry and planetary science, with a source gate (where did the estimate come from) and a shadow test (does it explain a gap the bench could not). This is why the question is cross-disciplinary by structure, not by preference.

**Refuter:** a derivation of the disputed Earth condition value from inside the bench frame that predicts the rock record. Then no import is owed for that slot.

### F5 - The transfer link is usually uninstrumented (single-source)

Fig 3 :27: **"A link with no instrument is not a link that works. It is a link nobody has looked at."**

In practice the bench result is settled and the Earth reconstruction is actively measured, but the bridge between them ("this mechanism would have run there") is often asserted rather than instrumented. The bridge is the middle link of the dependency chain, the one with no obvious test, so it attracts the least attention.

**Refuter:** name a working instrument that reads the bridge directly, for a named experiment and condition slot. It must return a value that could have failed.

### F6 - "Unrealistic" is a filter until a witness is shown (triangulated)

Fig 9 :60: **"Use filters for the budget and witnesses for the verdict. Never let a filter hand you a verdict."**

Fig 2 :37-38: instruments, including the reconstruction instruments, **"can be wrong on axes they do not measure."**

A label such as "geochemically implausible" ranks which experiments get tried first. It is a filter for the budget. It becomes a witness only when a specific measurement bears on a specific slot. The honest form is: *this experiment assumes condition X at value v; the best reconstruction gives range w with uncertainty; here is the observation that would discriminate them.*

**Refuter:** show a case where the plausibility filter was used as the verdict and no measurement is attached. That is a defect in the reasoning, not a refutation of this finding; the finding is refuted only if a filter can settle a condition without a witness.

### F7 - Convergence is not provenance (single-source, high practical importance)

Fig 8 :51-52: **"Convergent derivation and adopted import look identical in the contents. Only the record of where each came from separates them, and the surplus lives only where the closures differ."**

A laboratory product that matches a compound found in a carbonaceous chondrite looks like corroboration. It may be convergence (two frames reached the same molecule separately, which is strong) or it may be an imported starting material (which is not). The contents alone cannot tell you. Only the dated record can.

**Refuter:** a case in which the provenance record is complete and the two frames are independent, in which case the convergence claim is earned. The finding survives; only the specific suspicion is removed.

### F8 - The figures return the shape of the check, not the list of experiments (triangulated)

Fig 13 :133: **"what it returns is a shape rather than a name."**

Fig 13 :134: **"Unreachable is a verdict here, not a silence"**

Fig 9 :27-28: **"describe it before you go looking, or any frame you pick will seem to fit"**

The Tether cannot produce the requested list from the figures. It can produce the shape the list must have, the refuter for each entry, and the instruments that would settle each entry. Naming experiments is a world-source task, secondary and revisable. This is a **gap at the figure level for the naming part**, stated plainly: unreachable is a verdict, not a silence.

**Refuter:** a figure line that names a specific prebiotic experiment or condition value. None exists; if one were produced, this finding fails.

### F9 - Settled is necessary and not sufficient (single-source)

Fig 12 :104: **"A settled molecule becomes an atom for whatever composes over it."**

The bench synthesis is settled by its instrument. That makes it usable as an operand at the next level. It does not make the next level true. Fig 5's amendment says the same thing in the loop's vocabulary: a library that grows is not a reach that grows. A catalogue of successful syntheses is not a catalogue of early-Earth pathways.

**Refuter:** a demonstration that every settled synthesis automatically composes over into the Earth frame. None is offered.

### F10 - The round trip is lossy and the gap must be measured (single-source)

Fig 4 :30: **"So take the round trip and measure the gap. Without it you are navigating by dead reckoning: confident about a position nobody checked."**

Send up the bench condition set C, abstract to "this reaction is prebiotically plausible," bring it back down to the Earth frame. What is lost: the concentration, the purity, the continuous energy input, the vessel surface, the absence of competing reactions. That loss is `R_T`, and it is the thing the question is actually asking about. Do not set it to zero.

**Refuter:** a round trip in which C survives unchanged when brought back down to E. That would require C = E in every load-bearing slot.

## 4. Pre-registered refuter (written before the conclusion)

The shape claim is: **a bench synthesis in a substituted habitat proves wiring under C and not capability under E; the conditions that "may have been unrealistic" are the unenumerated differences between C and E.**

It is refuted if any of the following holds:

1. **Habitat identity.** For a named experiment, C and E are shown identical in every load-bearing condition slot, with measured values and uncertainties. Then F1 does not apply to it.
2. **Direct transfer.** The same mechanism, at a sufficient rate, is demonstrated under reconstructed early-Earth conditions, with an instrument that could have returned failure. Then the gap for that experiment closes.
3. **Figure-level reversal.** Fig 11 :70 is shown to admit a synthetic solve that counts as capability at the same level, so substitution no longer separates wiring from capability.
4. **Specific witness.** A geochemical measurement shows the disputed condition was present at the required level for the required duration (for example, a transient post-impact reducing atmosphere lasting long enough for the pathway). The experiment moves from "may be unrealistic" to "supported" on that slot; the shape claim survives, the entry is removed from the list.

If any fires, the affected entry is withdrawn and the change is recorded here, not silently edited away. Until one is run, no part of this may be labelled `grounded` as a claim about early Earth.

## 5. Recommendation

1. Lead with F1 and F2: the question is about habitat substitution, and substitution hides both omissions and introductions.
2. Enumerate condition slots per experiment (F3), never rank experiments by theory family.
3. For every slot, attach a source for the Earth estimate and an instrument that could discriminate (F4, F5, F6).
4. Keep provenance for convergence claims (F7), with dates.
5. State the gap at the figure level honestly (F8): the figures give the shape and the refuter, not the list.
6. Use `03_public_contact_points.csv`, one-to-one, public and opt-in venues only, and ask for a refutation of a condition slot rather than agreement with a theory (Fig 10 :52: *"Bring a characterised residual, never the source."*).

## 7. Verdict in brief

The figure-grounded verdict is a **shape**, mode `specified`, ungrounded as a claim about early Earth:

> Any prebiotic experiment is a solve in a substituted habitat. Its success is evidence for wiring under its own conditions, not capability under reconstructed early-Earth conditions. The conditions that may have been unrealistic are precisely the slots that were omitted from, or introduced into, that habitat; those slots must be enumerated from the world, sourced with provenance, and witnessed with an instrument that could fail. The figures return that shape and its refuters. They do not return a list of experiments, and the list supplied in `09_watson_answer.md` is world-source corroboration, revisable and carries no figure authority.

Strains after re-census: **one named and dissolved** (lab exclusion versus habitat exclusion asymmetry, §1), **one live** (the Earth anchor returns ranges, not point values, so condition comparisons inherit irreducible uncertainty; recorded, not hidden).
