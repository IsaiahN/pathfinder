# Outreach draft - check the condition list before you decide about the framework

**Use:** one-to-one, or as a cover note to a public submission through a venue in `03_public_contact_points.csv`. **Not** for bulk send. Field: prebiotic experiment conditions research.

**Hard precondition:** do not send until the three sub-claims in `08_expert_verification_packet.md` survive your own check. The offer collapses on contact if the stage-1 artifact is wrong.

---

**Subject:** One checkable claim about prebiotic experiments and early-Earth conditions

> Hi [name],
>
> I am working on a narrow question in prebiotic experiment conditions research: when a compound is made in the lab under a declared set of conditions, what would have to be true for that result to count as evidence about early Earth? I have one specific output I would like you to check against what you already know. I am not asking you to read the theory first, and I am not asking you to agree with it. If the output does not survive, there is nothing else to look at.
>
> **The output (`specified`, not `grounded`):** a laboratory setup is a substituted habitat. A successful synthesis in it demonstrates **wiring**, that the mechanism runs under the conditions used. It does not by itself demonstrate **capability**, that the same pathway would have run on early Earth. The two failure modes are silent: what was left out of the setup is invisible until the goal fails, and what was put into it is invisible until it acts.
>
> So the answer to "which experiments rely on unrealistic conditions" is not a list of good and bad theories. It is a per-condition list. For each experiment I would write down: (1) the value used at the bench, (2) the best reconstructed early-Earth value with its uncertainty, (3) the observation that would discriminate them, and (4) what would count as a failure. A global verdict like "plausible" or "implausible" is a budget filter, not a witness.
>
> Concretely, the condition slots I would check first are: atmosphere redox state and the duration of any reducing episode; UV flux and whether the experiment's source was continuous; concentration mechanism (including evaporation or wet-dry cycling); mineral surfaces and vessel walls; pH and temperature; starting-material purity; and competing reactions that the bench omitted.
>
> **What I am not claiming:** that any named experiment is impossible; that Miller-Urey was simply wrong (transient post-impact reducing atmospheres are a live possibility); that the RNA world is refuted; or that a compound found in a meteorite proves convergence rather than import. Those are provenance questions, and the contents alone cannot settle them.
>
> **What would refute the output:**
> 1. For a named experiment, show that the bench condition set and the reconstructed Earth set are identical in every load-bearing slot, with measured values.
> 2. Show the same mechanism running at sufficient rate under reconstructed early-Earth conditions, with an instrument that could have returned failure.
> 3. Show that a bench solve counts as capability at the same level, so substitution no longer separates wiring from capability.
> 4. For a specific slot, produce a measurement showing the disputed condition was present at the required level and for the required time. That removes the entry from the list; it does not damage the shape claim.
>
> I wrote these down before sending this. If one holds, I will withdraw the affected claim. A one-line "this fails because..." is more useful to me than agreement. If you would rather not engage, no reply is needed.
>
> **If it survives, and only then:** here are the source links. The figures are the authority, not my summary of them.
> - Verbatim figures with line numbers (local extract): `FRAMEWORK/reference/03-FIGURES_TEXT.md`
> - Canonical figures README: https://github.com/IsaiahN/tether/blob/general-agent/figures/README.md
> - Worked prebiotic chemistry review (Cleaves 2012, open access): https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s12052-012-0443-9
>
> **If you want to use the machinery:** the formula is in the figures link. Run it on a condition slot you know well and can check against a ground. Write down what would refute your result *before* you run it, and state whether your claim is `general`, `specified`, or `grounded`. A reported failure is more useful to me than a reported success.
>
> Thanks,
> [name]
