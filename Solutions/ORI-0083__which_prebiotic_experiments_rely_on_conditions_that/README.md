# Solution ORI-0083

**Question:** Which prebiotic experiments rely on conditions that may have been unrealistic for early Earth?

**Source:** origins of life.txt line 115

**Section:** 9. Evidence, experiments, and competing explanations

**Mode:** `specified` on the substantive claim about which conditions are load-bearing and unverified; `grounded` only on the narrow bench fact that a stated product formed under the stated laboratory conditions, as reported by the instrument in the source; `general` on the structural shape (a bench result is a solve in a substituted habitat). Nothing about the early Earth is `grounded` here.

**Status:** done

**Registry status:** pending

## Restatement as an effect

A prebiotic experiment produces a compound under a condition set C and is then used as evidence that the chemistry would have run under the early-Earth condition set E. The question asks where C and E may differ in a load-bearing way. The answer that would count is not a verdict on a whole theory: it is a per-slot list of conditions (atmosphere, UV flux, concentration, mineral surface, pH, temperature, wet-dry cycling, starting-material purity), each with a stated bench value, a stated Earth estimate with its uncertainty, and a witness that would settle the difference. Bind that description as an ordinary term before the run, so it can accrue a residual rather than remain a preference.

## What is deliberately not here

- No personal emails of named individuals.
- No ranking of people by how likely they are to agree with the claim.
- No bulk-send plan.
- No claim that any named experiment is impossible. The verdict is that a laboratory success does not by itself settle capability under reconstructed early-Earth conditions, and that the conditions in question must be enumerated and witnessed before a verdict on each one is earned.
