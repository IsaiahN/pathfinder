# Same experiment or different experiment? Structure, order and analogues

**Mode: `specified`.** This is a simulated derivation checked against the figures, not a ground reading (see `05_what_shape_means.md` §6).

## RESIDUAL

Two supporting questions are owed before the main answer can be written:

1. When two prebiotic runs share starting materials but differ in conditions, are they **the same experiment under a different arrangement**, or are they different substances in the Figure 12 sense? This decides whether one run's result may be composed over into the other's claim.
2. In what **order** should the condition slots be enumerated, so that a first-order effect is not mistaken for the residual three steps out?

## DOMAIN CENSUS

Atoms held by the loop are the six of Fig 13 (three states, three structures); bonds held are the seven plus negation (Fig 12). **Conditions are not atoms.** Fig 11 :46 lists them as habitat factors: *"conditions: light, water, temperature, season, terrain"*, and Fig 11 :43 says the habitat *"is enumerated, never composed."* Any attempt to treat "UV flux" as a seventh primitive would be a strain and is refused at census. The comparison below is therefore an **analogue**, not a composition inside `closure(Γ)`, and it is marked `general`.

## DERIVATION

### Q1 - Are two condition-variants isomers? ANALOGUE ONLY, strained if read literally

Fig 12 :117: **"isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."**

Fig 12 :123: **"functional group. A substructure whose presence fixes the outcome. The group says what kind; the qualifier says which member."**

Take two runs of a spark-discharge experiment, one with a CH4/NH3/H2 atmosphere and one with CO2/N2/H2O plus a carbonate buffer. They share reagents and the energy source. Under Fig 12 they look like isomers: same ingredients, different arrangement, different product distribution. The chemistry literature supports the behavioural difference: the Miller-Urey page records that CH4 was one of the most important ingredients for high amino-acid yield, and that buffered, less-reducing mixtures can also produce amino acids by a different route.

**But the figure does not license calling them isomers.** Fig 12's atoms are states and structures; the thing that changed is the habitat. Re-census: the two runs are better described as **the same functional group** (`energy input + carbon source + nitrogen source`) with different habitat qualifiers. The functional-group reading is figure-derived at `general` mode; the isomer reading is an analogue from chemistry and is `single-source`, corroborated only by the world literature.

**Falsifier, written before the run:** if the two runs produce the same product set at the same yield, the behavioural difference disappears and even the functional-group distinction loses its point. If a third run with a different atmosphere reproduces the first run's products exactly, the isomer analogue is dead.

### Q2 - What is the order of enumeration?

Fig 3 :20-21: *"The chain is ordered by dependency, not by importance. Each link consumes what the one above produces, so effort spent above the break arrives at a step that cannot use it."*

Fig 11 :52-53: **"And contact cascades. Scoping the habitat to direct contact is how DDT reached the eagles: first-order enumeration, second-order effect, and the residual that mattered was three steps out."**

The condition list must therefore be ordered by cascade, not by familiarity:

1. **Atmosphere redox state and duration of reducing episodes.** This sets whether HCN and aldehydes can form at all. It is upstream.
2. **Energy source and its time profile** (spark, continuous UV, impact pulse). Downstream of atmosphere, upstream of intermediate production.
3. **Concentration mechanism** (evaporation, wet-dry cycling, mineral adsorption). Determines whether intermediates reach reactive levels.
4. **Mineral surfaces and vessel walls.** The 2021 borosilicate-versus-Teflon result makes the container a condition, not a detail.
5. **pH and temperature.** These set rates and stability; the RNA-world page gives the cytosine half-life example.
6. **Starting-material purity and enantiomeric enrichment.** Required by the Sutherland route and by homochirality arguments.
7. **Competing reactions and sinks.** What removed the product as it formed.

Ordering by cascade is the dependency order of Fig 3. A slot that is three steps out (for example, vessel surface affecting HCN yield) is invisible if only direct contact is scoped.

**Falsifier:** if a reordering of this list changes which experiment is flagged as condition-dependent, then the order was doing verdict work and the list is not a neutral enumeration. Filters must not hand down a verdict (Fig 9 :60).

### Q3 - What do the analogues contribute?

Three analogues are used in this folder, all secondary:

- **Test harness as substituted habitat** (Fig 11 :70) is figure-derived, not an analogue, and is primary.
- **Metamerism**, from *The Hard Problem is About Source*: a reading of green cannot comment on the source. Analogue for "product formed" versus "pathway on Earth". It corroborates F2 of `01` but does not decide it.
- **Isomer / functional group**, Fig 12, applied to condition variants as in Q1. Marked `general` and analogue.

Fig 8 :51-52 warns that convergent derivation and adopted import look identical in the contents. An analogue imported from chemistry or perception science is an import: it needs a source gate and a shadow test. It never becomes the verdict.

### Q4 - What is the union of the two conditions?

Fig 9 :54: *"And when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again."* The bench result and the Earth reconstruction overlap (both involve chemistry and energy) but do not reduce to one another. The overlap is a compound with its own loop: the transfer. Fig 12 :104 - **"A settled molecule becomes an atom for whatever composes over it."** The bench result, once settled, becomes an operand at the transfer level. That makes it usable, not sufficient: Fig 5's amendment says settling is necessary and not sufficient.

**Falsifier:** if the bench result can be composed over into the Earth claim with no additional atom and no measurement, then the transfer is not a compound and Fig 9 :54 was misapplied. Show that and the union reading is withdrawn.

## CONCLUSION

Two runs that differ only in conditions are best read as a **functional group with different habitat qualifiers**, not as Figure 12 isomers: conditions are enumerated habitat factors, not atoms of the six-term set. The isomer analogue is recorded, marked `general` and single-source, and given a falsifier. The condition list is ordered by dependency and cascade, from atmosphere redox to competing sinks, so that a third-order effect is not mistaken for the lead condition. The union of bench and Earth remains unbundled at the transfer.
