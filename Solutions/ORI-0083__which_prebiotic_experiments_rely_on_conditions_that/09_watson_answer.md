## DOMAIN CENSUS

- **R (the residual).** A compound forms in a lab under condition set C and is offered as evidence about early Earth's set E. Arity: two conditions, so a union compound, not one harder question, Fig 9 :57-58 "a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either". Symmetry: asymmetric, the bench stands in for the Earth and never the reverse. Scale: first order inside each habitat, second order at the transfer.
- **Domain 1, the bench frame.** Actors: reagents, vessels, spark/UV/heat, buffers, analytical instruments. Currency: yield and detectability of product. Anchor: the instrument reading. It settles the narrow bench fact and does not update on Earth reconstructions.
- **Domain 2, the early-Earth reconstruction frame.** Actors: atmospheric and ocean models, volcanic outgassing, impactors, zircons, mineral surfaces. Currency: boundary conditions with error bars. Anchor: rocks and isotopic measurements. Legitimate for this question, but the channel is thin, so it returns ranges.
- **Domain 3, the transfer frame.** Actors: reviewers, replication groups, expert readers. Currency: agreement and residual. Anchor: the actual early-Earth habitat, reached only through Domain 2. In this simulated run all three domains draw on one literature pool, so independence is low.
- **Partition or union.** Union. The overlap (chemistry, energy, water) is a compound with its own loop, the transfer inference, so it is unbundled before searching (Fig 9 :54).
- **Order by dependency.** Describe R before choosing a frame; enumerate the habitat before selecting a lever; settle the first-order bench result before the second-order transfer; ask SEARCHED first, then INWARD (sharpen the reconstruction), then OUTWARD (import the Earth values), never reading the last two against each other.

## VERDICT (from the figures)

The shape survives; the requested list of guilty experiments is not figure-derivable. A bench synthesis is a solve in a substituted habitat, so it proves wiring under its own conditions and not capability under reconstructed early-Earth conditions. The conditions that may have been unrealistic are the omitted and introduced habitat slots, enumerated from the world and witnessed one at a time. Each entry must carry a bench value, a reconstructed Earth range, a discriminating observation and a written failure. The named candidates in `07_the_tether_answer.md` section 3 are `specified`, revisable world-source hypotheses, with the Miller-Urey case explicitly unresolved rather than refuted. This is `specified` and ungrounded as a claim about early Earth. The figures return a shape and a refuter, not a list and not a verdict on any theory.

- **cost if wrong:** bounded and locatable. The artifact states modes, refuters and provenance, so an error is findable rather than invisible. The main cost is spending an outside reader's independence by asking them to judge a theory before they have checked a condition slot; Fig 2 :42 ("Buy independence in proportion to the cost of being wrong") prices that. It is avoidable by leading with the slot and its refuter. A second cost is the wrong atom: importing a condition estimate from a single model without provenance adds a premise the question did not need.
- **undoable:** yes. Every row in the condition list is ranked and reversible. A new measurement moves an entry from "may be unrealistic" to "supported" without withdrawing the shape claim, and no primitive is withdrawn. The artifact is a generator, not a conclusion.
- **reversed by:** a ground reading, not agreement. A constraint on the duration and timing of post-impact reducing atmospheres settles the Miller-Urey slot; mineral or evaporite evidence for local HCN concentration settles the concentration slot; reconstructed temperature and pH settle the stability slots; a demonstration that a bench condition set equals the Earth set in every load-bearing slot, or a figure-level result that makes a synthetic solve count as capability at the same level, reverses the shape claim itself. Until one of these fires, the verdict stands as `candidate`.
