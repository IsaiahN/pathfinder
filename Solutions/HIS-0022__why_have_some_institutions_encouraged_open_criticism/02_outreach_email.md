# Outreach draft, stage one before stage two

**Use:** one to one, or as a cover note attached to a submission through a public venue in `03_public_contact_points.csv`. Not for bulk send.

**Standing:** the content below is a `specified` shape, not a `grounded` historical finding. It is a simulated derivation checked for internal consistency against the figures.

---

**Subject:** One checkable shape about institutional criticism, before any theory

> Hello,
>
> I have one claim I would like you to check against your own knowledge of the history of science. I am not asking you to read a framework first, and I am not asking you to agree with it. If the claim fails, there is nothing else to look at.
>
> **The residual.** Some institutions have kept a channel open by which their own claims can be refused by outsiders. Others have discouraged exactly that. The question is not which is morally better. It is what structural condition makes the first possible and the second attractive. I wrote down the shape of the answer before running anything: a claim survives contact when a frame can range it against a ground that does not change its answer because the institution thought something, and when someone is seated who can refuse the institution's own held claim.
>
> **The claim, `specified`, not `grounded`.** Four conditions, kept separate and never averaged:
> 1. the claim is ranged against a reference that does not update on what the institution thinks about it;
> 2. there is a seat able to refuse a claim the institution already holds, with its interest declared;
> 3. the critic comes from a closure that differs from the institution's, not from a hand-picked set of agreed voices;
> 4. the institution's habitat is enumerated, so the same patron or journal can be a benefactor to one residual and a malfactor to another.
>
> Where all four hold, open criticism is a channel to ground. Where condition 1 fails, the institution's own claim becomes the reference, everything drifts together coherently, and nobody notices because it looks like agreement. Where condition 2 fails, the institution can only accumulate, because it has no operator for refusal. Where condition 3 fails, agreement is the pool talking, not two frames confirming each other. Where condition 4 is skipped, the same policy is praised in one case and condemned in another with no way to say why.
>
> **What this does not say.** It does not say open criticism is always right, or that every dissenter is a ground. It does not name a historical institution as the winner. It does not return a cause for why a particular academy in a particular decade chose one policy. No figure line returns that, so the historical attribution is recorded as a gap, not filled with a guess. Agreement from historians who share one evidence pool is one witness, not corroboration.
>
> **What would refute the shape.** I wrote these down before sending this.
> 1. An institution with a non-updating reference and a seated refuter that still suppresses criticism for a reason outside the four conditions.
> 2. An institution with no independent critic that corrects error at the same rate as one with an open channel.
> 3. A re-census of the figures showing that criticism composes as a symmetric bond rather than as a negation, so the refusal reading fails.
> 4. A historical study that assigns one cause to the policy across cases where the four-condition separator predicts different domains.
>
> If any holds, I withdraw the claim. A one-line "this fails because..." is more useful to me than agreement. If you would rather not engage, no reply is needed.
>
> **If it survives, and only then:** the framework and the verbatim lines are below. The figures are the authority, not my summary of them.
> - Figures text with line numbers (local framework file): `FRAMEWORK/reference/03-FIGURES_TEXT.md`
> - Figures and formula, branch `general-agent`: https://github.com/IsaiahN/tether/tree/general-agent/figures
> - Architecture article: https://medium.com/@IsaiahNwukor/arrival-of-the-fittest-the-tether-architecture-1de7edf641ec
>
> **If you want to reuse the method:** the formula is in the figures link. Run it on a case you know well and can check against a ground. Write down what would refute your result before you run it, and state the mode of what you get. A reported failure is more useful to me than a reported success.
