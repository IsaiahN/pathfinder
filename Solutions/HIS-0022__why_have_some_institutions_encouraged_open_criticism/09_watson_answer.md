## QUESTION

Decide, in effect terms, what the Tether says about two institutional policies. Option A: the policy is a fixed institutional character, open or closed. Option B: the policy is a relation among a reference, a refusal seat, a critic pool and a habitat, so the same institution can encourage in one slot and discourage in another, and the historical cause of a given choice remains unreported. What each leads to: A produces an "openness" score that aggregates away the signal and a prestige list of famous cases; B produces a four-condition separator with failure conditions written in advance and a declared gap where no figure line governs.

## DOMAIN CENSUS

R by arity, symmetry and scale, then one line per domain with actors, currency and anchor. Partition or union, and order by dependency.

- **R.** Arity: two policies, but a union of overlapping conditions rather than a partition, because both can hold in one institution across different slots (Fig 9 :55). Symmetry: "challenge" is ordered, and "criticism" as an operation is a refusal, so it is a unary operator rather than a symmetric bond (Fig 12 :112). Scale: a single claim and critic at first order, an institution's standing across many claims at second order (Fig 10 :61). Slot indexed, not aggregated (Fig 1 :19).
- **D1, the epistemic frame.** Actors: held claims, methods, vocabulary, instruments. Currency: settlement against ground versus internal coherence. Anchor: the ground, which must not update. Partition boundary: which references may settle a claim.
- **D2, the checking arrangement.** Actors: critic, verifier, ground maintainer, keeper of the claim. Currency: verdicts versus agreement. Anchor: the ground, never agreement. Partition boundary: whether a refusal seat exists, survives its own use and has a declared stake.
- **D3, the community room chain.** Actors: rooms, critic pool, next room. Currency: residual per handoff, independent variation. Anchor: ground pressure on the room. Partition boundary: mirror chain versus selection between rooms.
- **D4, the historical habitat.** Actors: patrons, rulers, funders, societies, journals, disciplines. Currency: resources, reputation, standing. Anchor: none nameable for the causal question, so this domain is where the `gap` sits. Union of overlapping conditions (Fig 9 :55).
- **Order by dependency.** Check the anchor first; refuse before mint; only then build a channel; then settle at the ground (Fig 3 :20, `THE_FORMULA.md` steps 2 to 5).

Strain test applied: the apparent conflict between Fig 2 :35 and Fig 7 :35 was dissolved by re-census. Fig 2 governs the anchor against which frames range; Fig 7 governs selection between rooms over time. Same independence parameter, different objects.

## VERDICT (from the figures)

The four-condition separator survives; a historical attribution does not. Encouragement is a channel to ground when the settling reference does not update on what the institution thinks, a refusal seat survives its own use with a declared stake, the critic comes from a differing closure, and the habitat is enumerated. Discouragement is the coherent-drift collapse in which the institution's own claim occupies the anchor, a convention decides an outcome, the negation operator is dropped, and the channel decays while the ground does not. Over a matched atom set the two policies are isomers: same atoms, different bond at the refusal slot. Verdicts are per domain and never averaged. D1 to D3 are `specified`, triangulated. D4 is a `gap` with only a `general` shape. Nothing here is `grounded`.

- **cost if wrong:** a wrong attribution buys a list of famous institutions and an "openness" score that aggregates away the per-slot signal (Fig 1 :22). The larger cost is spending the independence of outside readers by sending a prestige list instead of a refuter. Fig 2 :42 "Buy independence in proportion to the cost of being wrong."
- **undoable:** yes. The four conditions can be restated per case without withdrawing any figure line. The historical attribution can be added later if an instrument exists, and no claim has to be promoted to a verdict to make the separator usable.
- **reversed by:** a ground reading, not agreement. A comparative instrument measuring whether the reference updates, whether the refusal seat survives its own use, the critic pool's evidence overlap and the error-correction rate would move D1 to D3 toward `grounded` or refute them. A figure re-census placing criticism under a symmetric bond would break D2. A verbatim figure line assigning a historical cause would close D4. Agreement among historians sharing one evidence pool is none of these.
