# Isomers, order and analogues for institutional criticism

**Mode: `specified` on the isomer and order claims, `general` on the structural reading. Simulated derivation checked against the figures, not a ground reading** (`05_what_shape_means.md` section 7).

## RESIDUAL

The audit in `01` leaves two questions open that this file must answer. (1) If an encouraging institution and a discouraging one are built from the same institutional materials, are they different things or different spellings of one thing? (2) In what order must the four conditions be applied, and what analogue makes that order checkable?

## DOMAIN CENSUS

Atoms held for the comparison: a claim, a reference, a refusal seat, a critic from a differing closure, a habitat of patrons and journals. Bonds held: the seven plus negation. The atom set is fixed for this comparison; no new primitive is introduced. A historical case that imports a new actor is a different comparison and is recorded as such.

## DERIVATION

### Q1: Are the two policies isomers?

Fig 12 :117 "isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."

Fig 12 :5 "A set of ingredients is not one. The bond is the rest of the information, and it is the half nothing was recording."

Over the fixed atom set above, the two policies share every atom. What differs is the bond at the refusal slot:

- **Encouragement:** the seat is bonded to a held claim by negation, `refuse(claim)`, using the unary operator of Fig 12 :112.
- **Discouragement:** the seat is bonded to the claim by identity with the institution's standing, so the claim cannot be refused without refusing the standing. Fig 10 :61 "standing is a stake that survives the outcome it was earned on."

Same atoms, different bond, different behaviour. That is Fig 12's isomer, and the difference is invisible in a list of institutional parts. A catalogue that says both institutions had journals, academies and patrons has recorded the ingredients and not the arrangement.

**Falsifier, written before the conclusion:** if a case can be shown in which the refusal seat is bonded to the claim by negation and the institution still cannot refuse the claim, or in which no negation bond exists and the institution refuses as readily, the isomer claim fails and the difference is some other variable.

### Q2: The weaker reading is a functional group, not an isomer

Fig 12 :123 "functional group. A substructure whose presence fixes the outcome. The group says what kind; the qualifier says which member."

If the atom set is not fixed, if, for example, one institution has a reference instrument and the other has none, the two are not isomers. They share the functional group "an institution with a claim and a seat", and the qualifier says which member of the group each is. This is the weaker, safer claim and it is the one to use when the atom sets have not been matched. In `01` the four conditions are presented as a separator, which is the functional-group reading; the isomer reading is reserved for matched comparisons.

### Q3: What is the order?

Fig 3 :20 "The chain is ordered by dependency, not by importance."

`THE_FORMULA.md` step 2 sorts a residual before minting, and the fifth kind, REFUTED, is a refusal rather than an explanation. Fig 5 :53 "refuted: a term we hold is wrong; a term is owed that says so." The order therefore is:

1. **Check the anchor.** Is the reference something that does not change its answer when the institution thinks about it? Fig 2 :29 "An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks."
2. **Refuse before mint.** If the held claim is the thing that reports whether criticism is healthy, refuse that standing first; do not add a new openness committee on top of it (Fig 10 :47 "a convention that decides an outcome has stopped being one").
3. **Only then mint a channel.** A refusal seat, an independent critic pool and a published residual can be added, and the mint is gated by support, reachability and novelty (`THE_FORMULA.md` step 3).
4. **Settle at the ground, not at the gate.** A gate is not the ground (step 5).

Running the order backwards is the recorded failure mode: adding a criticism channel while the institution's own claim still occupies the anchor produces a channel that reports to the claim.

### Q4: Is the refusal seat a catalyst or a spent part?

Fig 12 :119 "catalyst. A part that survives its own use. Spent and regenerating parts behave differently across uses, and nothing records which a term is."

A maintained refusal seat survives its own use: the institution that refuses one of its claims can refuse the next one. A one-off public inquiry that is disbanded after its report is a spent part. A policy can advertise the same event, a criticism episode, and yet differ categorically in whether the capacity persists. This is a bond-level distinction, not a degree of openness.

**Falsifier:** show a maintained refusal seat that loses its capacity after one use, or a one-off inquiry whose capacity is demonstrably regenerated for later claims, with both measured across more than one claim.

### Q5: The habitat is enumerated, not composed

Fig 11 :43 "The habitat is enumerated, never composed."
Fig 11 :48 "You do not invent the list. You read it off the world, and what you cannot perceive or measure yet is the residual."
Fig 11 :51 "No standing list of good actors survives a change of residual."

The analogues that travel to a new case are patrons, funders, journals, societies, disciplines and instruments, read off that case. They are not combined into an "institutional character" and then used to predict which policy appears. That is the move that would turn a shape into a name and would breach Fig 9 :32 "you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself."

### Q6: Round trip gap

Fig 4 :30 "So take the round trip and measure the gap. Without it you are navigating by dead reckoning: confident about a position nobody checked."

Abstraction: the four-condition separator. Concretisation: a claim about a particular academy. Three constraints are dropped on the way down and must be named: which actor actually occupies the anchor in that case, whether the refusal seat can survive its own use, and which critic pool has genuinely different evidence. `11_audit.md` records the gap `R_T` qualitatively and does not set it to zero.

## CONCLUSION

Over a fixed atom set, encouragement and discouragement are isomers: same institutional atoms, different bond at the refusal slot, decisive difference in behaviour. Where the atom sets are not matched, the honest claim is a functional group with a qualifier. The order is anchor check, refusal before mint, then channel construction, then settlement at the ground. The refusal seat is a catalyst if it survives its own use and a spent part if it does not. The habitat is enumerated and never composed into a character. No figure line selects which historical institution lands in which cell, so the attribution stays a `gap`.
