## 4. Round trip gap R_T

The Symbols table defines R_T as the gap between what was sent up and what came back down, `gap(x, (T_E ∘ T_A)(x))`, measuring what the coarser description cannot hold. Fig 4 :30 requires it to be measured rather than assumed. I ran the round trip on the condensation this folder actually uses.

2. **Abstraction (T_A):** the shorthand carried in `07` and `08`: "concentration is a union of two conditions; owner valence is relative to the residual; the deciding anchor must be external; the threshold is a gap".
3. **Concretisation (T_E):** the concrete question, namely a percentage at which a named firm becomes more accountable or over-controlled.

**R_T is nonzero.** Three things the shorthand holds do not survive the trip back down:

- **Which residual is in view** when "accountable" is asserted. The shorthand says "relative to the residual" but does not carry the two residual names; a reader can collapse them back into one score.
- **Whether the channel is intact.** The shorthand says "external anchor" but drops the distinction between the unmoved ground and the decayed channel, which is the whole of F4 and F6.
- **The union's second condition when only the first is measured.** The shorthand says "union" but a concrete governance score will record only monitoring, leaving the intersection standing, exactly the Fig 9 :56 failure.

Conversely, one thing survives cleanly: the two conditions are separable and a single scalar erases them. That is the residue worth sending. R_T was **not** measured numerically, because no ground reading exists; it is recorded qualitatively as the enumerated set of dropped constraints. Treating the formula as identical to the figure set would set R_T to zero and would be the dead-reckoning error Fig 4 :30 names. The honest form is a lossy condensation with a measured gap, which is what `09_watson_answer.md` states.
