# Expert verification packet: the Tether on concentrated ownership

**What this is:** a claim in ownership structure and corporate governance, in a form a researcher can check. Mode, derivation and refuters are stated before the conclusion, so a reader knows what to look for and what would settle it.

**Mode: `specified` on the union claim, `general` on the shape, `gap` on the threshold. Not `grounded`.** No firm-level measurement is claimed. This is a shape and a route, not a result.

## 1. The claim, in one paragraph

Concentrated ownership does not have one effect to be weighed against another. Accountability gain and control excess are two conditions that can hold in the same firm, indexed per firm and per stakeholder, and they must be read separately rather than averaged into a "net effect of concentration". Whether a large owner is helpful or harmful is relative to the residual in view, so "powerful owners" names a relation, not a kind. Monitoring is a capability of owner and habitat together, and the anchor that decides either condition must be external to the party being measured. The framework licenses the shape of a threshold between cash-flow rights and control rights but no number, so the ownership percentage is declared `gap`. Four refuters are written below, before this packet is sent.

## 2. Three checkable sub-claims

### C1 — framework-internal and governance-facing; checkable by reading Figure 9 and Figure 11

- **Statement:** the two effects are a union of overlapping conditions, and the owner's sign is relative to the residual, not a fixed property.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 9 :55 *"A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones."* Fig 9 :56 *"A single lever clears the region where only one condition holds and leaves the intersections standing, because the second condition still binds."* Fig 11 :49 *"An actor's valence is relative to the residual, not a property it carries."* Fig 11 :50 *"The same actor is a benefactor to one residual and a malfactor to another, so mutualism and parasitism are relations rather than kinds."*
- **What to check:** does a single concentration lever change monitoring and private benefits in opposite directions in known cases, so that a one-scalar answer loses information? Can the same controller be a monitor in one respect and an expropriator in another?
- **Refuter:** a controlled comparison under one stated residual, one anchor and one measurement regime in which the same controller is a net benefactor for both management shirking and minority expropriation; or a ground-visible measurement in which one concentration lever moves both conditions in the same direction in every firm with no categorically different intersection.

### C2 — governance-facing; checkable against the blockholder literature

- **Statement:** accountability is a property of owner and habitat, not of the stake alone; a controlling block can also worsen governance through private benefits, and the two effects separate in the data when cash-flow rights and control rights are measured apart.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 11 :40 *"A system cannot be made more capable by improving the agent in isolation. An improvement that does not change contact changes nothing, however much it improves."* Fig 2 :29 *"An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks. A measurement, an experiment, a counter, a market, a patient outcome."* Fig 12 :87 *"a threshold relation between two quantities"*.
- **What to check:** Shleifer and Vishny (1986) model the monitoring benefit; Johnson, La Porta, Lopez-de-Silanes and Shleifer (2000) define tunnelling; Claessens, Djankov, Fan and Lang (2002) report value rising with cash-flow ownership and falling when control rights exceed cash-flow rights; Morck, Shleifer and Vishny (1988) find a nonmonotonic relation. Are those four findings being read here as two conditions and a threshold shape, rather than as a single optimum? If one of them is misread, say which and how.
- **Refuter:** a replicated case in which the same stake produces the same measured monitoring improvement after disclosure, enforcement and market payment are removed, so that contact did not change (breaks Fig 11 :40); or a demonstration that a single monotone scalar fully predicts both effects with no wedge term (breaks the two-condition reading).

### C3 — framework-internal; checkable by reading Figure 10 and Figure 2

- **Statement:** the deciding anchor must be external to the party being measured; a controller-authored disclosure or compliance rating is a degraded channel, not a moved ground, and cannot decide the outcome.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 10 :86 *"Whoever is measured by a reading cannot own it."* Fig 10 :93 *"A ground with a reputation to protect is a ground someone benefits from, and its keeper is thereby disqualified."* Fig 10 :24 *"the ground does not decay, the channel does"*. Fig 10 :47 *"a convention that decides an outcome has stopped being one"*. Fig 2 :30 *"If it moves when you push on it, it is not an anchor."*
- **What to check:** is the distinction between the market's payment (anchor) and the controller-managed disclosure (channel) drawn correctly? A related-party transaction is a first-order transfer whose effect on minority returns is second or third order; does the enumeration go outward far enough?
- **Refuter:** a case in which the market's payment itself changes when only the controller's opinion moves, with information and enforcement held fixed and no independent frame able to range against an unmoved ground; or a challenge channel that tests high and non-declining on independent instruments while the ground's payment declines.

## 3. What is explicitly not claimed

- Not that concentration is good or bad, and not that one condition wins.
- Not an ownership percentage. 5 percent is a disclosure convention, not a figure result.
- Not that any named owner, family, fund or firm type has a fixed sign.
- Not that board size, board independence, a governance rating or a compliance checklist settles the question. Fig 10 :18 *"a declared meaning is not a grounded one"*.
- Not a derivation of the finance findings from the figures, and no new primitive. The finance literature is a secondary import with declared overlap, not a witness that can move the verdict.
- Not `grounded`. Nothing here is asserted as settled by experiment.

## 5. What would settle it

A source-discriminating instrument, not a further argument. From *The Hard Problem is About Source*: a reading *"cannot comment on the source"*; the spectrometer resolved the case.

1. A measurement that separates cash-flow rights from control rights in the same firm, so monitoring and private benefits are read at once.
2. An anchor the controller cannot move, measured by a frame that does not hold the controller's disclosure.
3. A grounded threshold estimate with the wedge measured directly. Until then the percentage stays `gap`.
4. A channel test: can a challenge still reach the ground after a documented change in entrenching mechanisms?
