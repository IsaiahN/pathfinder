# The Tether's answer: concentrated ownership and accountability

**Mode: `specified` on the union claim, `general` on the shape, `gap` on the threshold and on any named firm's dominant condition. Not `grounded`.** This is a shape and a route, not a result. Simulated derivation checked against the figures (`05_what_shape_means.md` §6), not a ground reading.

## 1. The residual it answers

Concentrating ownership moves the residual claim and the control rights into fewer hands. The question asks which effect follows: better monitoring of management, or excessive power over the firm and its minority holders. The two are not ends of one scale. They are two conditions in the same firm, indexed per firm and per stakeholder slot. Fig 1 :19 *"The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated."* Fig 9 :55 *"A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones."*

## 2. The shape, per domain, kept separate

1. **The either/or is refused.** Fig 11 :49 *"An actor's valence is relative to the residual, not a property it carries."* and Fig 11 :50 *"The same actor is a benefactor to one residual and a malfactor to another, so mutualism and parasitism are relations rather than kinds."* For management shirking, a concentrated owner is a benefactor; for minority expropriation, the same owner is a malfactor. "Powerful owners" is a relation, not a kind.
2. **Accountability is owner and habitat, not owner alone.** Fig 11 :40 *"A system cannot be made more capable by improving the agent in isolation. An improvement that does not change contact changes nothing, however much it improves."* A stake becomes monitoring only where disclosure, enforceable duty, an able board and a paying market exist. Fig 2 :29 *"An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks. A measurement, an experiment, a counter, a market, a patient outcome."*
3. **Control excess is a distinct condition with a distinct bond.** It is the subtraction of the minority claim by the controlling stake (Fig 12 :78 *"A with B removed or absent"*), not the absence of monitoring. Fig 11 :52-54 *"And contact cascades. Scoping the habitat to direct contact is how DDT reached the eagles: first-order enumeration, second-order effect, and the residual that mattered was three steps out."* Enumeration must reach third-order effects.
4. **The governing anchor must be external to the measured party.** Fig 10 :86 *"Whoever is measured by a reading cannot own it."* and Fig 10 :93 *"A ground with a reputation to protect is a ground someone benefits from, and its keeper is thereby disqualified."* Where the controller writes the disclosure or controls the board that grades it, the channel is degraded; the ground has not moved (Fig 10 :24 *"the ground does not decay, the channel does"*). Conventions cannot do the work: Fig 10 :47 *"a convention that decides an outcome has stopped being one"*.
5. **Entrenchment is a time-channel failure, not a second kind of owner.** The same control persists while the challenge channel decays (Fig 7 :13 *"agreement compounds, evidence does not"*). It needs its own instrument.
6. **No percentage is given.** Fig 9 :32 *"you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself"*. The comparison bond is "a threshold relation between two quantities" (Fig 12 :87), which licenses a threshold shape and no value. Declared `gap`.

## 3. What it explicitly does not say

- Not "concentration is good" and not "concentration is bad". Both are `general`-mode verdicts with no ground.
- Not that one condition wins. They are a union; a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either (Fig 9 :57 *"What remains is smaller and it is also categorically different: a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either."*).
- Not a number. The 5 percent disclosure line is a convention, not a figure result.
- Not that any named owner or firm type has a fixed sign.
- Not that board structure, a governance rating or a compliance checklist settles the question. Fig 10 :18 *"a declared meaning is not a grounded one"*.
- Not `grounded`. No firm-level measurement was taken in this folder.

## 4. What would settle it: the ground

Source-discriminating instruments, not further argument. From *The Hard Problem is About Source*: a reading *"cannot comment on the source"*; the spectrometer resolved the case, not a debate.

- An instrument that separates cash-flow rights from control rights, applied to the same firm, so that the two conditions are measured at once rather than inferred from one score.
- An anchor the controller cannot move: a payment, a court ruling, a minority return, measured by a frame that does not hold the controller's disclosure.
- **Threshold Test:** a grounded estimate of where the comparison between incentive and entrenchment flips for a named population, with the wedge measured directly. Until such a reading exists the percentage stays `gap`.
- **Channel Test:** does a challenge still reach the ground after a documented change in control-entrenching mechanisms, with the ground's payment measured independently?
- Standing falsifiers from `01` §4: fixed owner valence across residuals; one lever moving both conditions in the same direction in every firm; a market payment that moves when only the controller's opinion moves; a challenge channel that tests high while the ground declines.

## 5. The honest stop

**Unreached, not unreachable.** Fig 8 :39 *"So a search that finds nothing is never a proof of absence."* Fig 13 :134 *"Unreachable is a verdict here, not a silence,"*. The figures give the union, the relative valence, the external-anchor requirement and the structure of a threshold. They do not give the number or the named firm. The stop is recorded with its lineage so a later reader can locate the error, and agreement is never substituted for the ground: Fig 2 :35 *"Alignment is triangulation, not negotiation. A mirror cannot correct you."*

## 6. One-paragraph version for outreach

> The Tether does not say concentrated ownership is good or bad. It says the question is mis-posed as an either/or. Monitoring and control excess are two conditions that can hold in the same firm, and they must be read separately: a controlling block can watch management and divert value through the same voting power in the same quarter. Whether an owner helps or harms is not a property of that owner; it is relative to the residual in view, so "powerful owners" is a relation, not a kind. Accountability belongs to owner and habitat together, and the thing that decides either condition must be an anchor the measured party cannot move, not a governance score that party helped author. The framework gives the shape of the answer: a union, a threshold between cash-flow rights and control rights, an external anchor, and a refused percentage. What it cannot give is the number or the verdict for a named firm; that needs a source-discriminating instrument, and four refuters are written down in advance to say when the shape is wrong.
